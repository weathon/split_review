## Summary

The paper introduces the Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE) as a diagnostic metric for quantifying semantic imbalance (e.g., trend dominating seasonality) in contrastive time-series representations, and proposes Asymmetric Perceptual Weighting (APW) to dynamically reweight contrastive losses during training. The approach is built on top of CoST and tested on ETT, Electricity, and Weather forecasting benchmarks.

## Strengths

1. **Novel diagnostic metric with clear synthetic validation.** SDE quantifies component-level recoverability in a principled way (Eq. 1, inspired by word embedding arithmetic). Table 1 provides compelling synthetic evidence that SDE and its asymmetry Δ systematically track amplitude-ratio-induced imbalance in TS2Vec — a concrete tool for exposing the semantic skew problem.

2. **Clear improvements on ETT benchmarks.** On all three ETT dataset variants (ETT1, ETT2, ETTm1), CoST+APW achieves the lowest MSE on the majority of forecast horizons, often by a non-trivial margin (e.g., ETTm1: 0.056 vs. 0.067 at horizon 288; ETT1 horizon 168: 0.101 vs. 0.107). These gains are consistent and meaningful.

3. **Useful negative-result ablation.** Table 2 shows that directly adding SDE as a regularizer to TS2Vec fails to improve forecasting, while Table 3 shows that incorporating the same SDE-derived signal through reweighting (APW) succeeds on several datasets. This contrast isolates the value of the design choice (diagnostic → reweighting signal rather than loss term) and is honestly reported.

4. **Plug-and-play framing.** The method integrates with CoST without modifying its encoder architecture, making it a practical add-on for existing frequency-aware contrastive frameworks.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **SDE values for the proposed method are never reported on real data.** The paper states that CoST+APW yields "consistently lower SDE values" and that "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics," but Table 3 contains only MSE and MAE — no SDE columns appear. Without this evidence, the paper's central mechanistic claim — that APW works by reducing semantic imbalance — is untested on the actual evaluation datasets. A reader cannot verify whether SDE decreases, stays flat, or even increases under APW. This is the single most important evidential gap.

2. **Forecasting results are mixed and routinely contradicted by the "consistent gains" narrative.** The paper claims "consistent gains in forecasting accuracy" (Abstract) and "superior forecasting accuracy" (Section 4.4.4), but the data tell a more complicated story:
   - On **Electricity** (horizons 168, 336, 720), CoST+APW is the **worst** method — MSE of 0.566 vs. CoST's 0.425 (horizon 168), 0.677 vs. 0.576 (336), and 1.010 vs. 0.911 (720).
   - On **Weather**, APW underperforms TNC on all five horizons and is the worst method on horizons 336 and 720.
   - On **ETT2** horizon 48 and 720, baselines outperform APW.
   
   These counterexamples are not acknowledged or explained. A paper claiming "consistent" improvement must address such failures. Without doing so, the conclusions are not warranted by the evidence presented.

3. **No variance or significance information.** All results are reported as point estimates without standard deviations, confidence intervals, or significance tests. Many comparisons are very close (e.g., ETT2 horizon 168: APW 0.199 vs. TS2Vec 0.198 vs. TNC 0.197), making it impossible to assess whether any observed difference is meaningful. This undermines the reliability of the experimental comparison.

### Minor

4. **SDE computation changes between definition and deployment without discussion.** Section 3.2 defines SDE using the raw composite signal encoding \(v(a+b)\). In the actual APW method (Section 4.4.2–4.4.3), the composite embedding is produced by an MLP that fuses separate component embeddings \(g_\phi([v(a)\|v(b)])\). How this change affects SDE values — whether the MLP inflates or deflates Δ, or whether the metric remains comparable — is never analyzed. The paper should clarify whether the SDE computed under the MLP fusion is the same construct as originally defined.

5. **No ablation isolating the MLP fusion from the weighting mechanism.** The method introduces two novelties simultaneously: an MLP to produce composite embeddings and adaptive weighting via Δ. Their individual contributions are not disentangled. An ablation comparing "CoST + MLP (no weighting)" and "CoST + weighting (no MLP, using a simple additive composite)" would clarify what drives the gains on ETT and the losses on Electricity/Weather.

6. **Forecasting evaluation protocol is underspecified.** The paper states models are trained for 100 epochs with Adam but does not describe how the learned embeddings are adapted for forecasting (linear probe? full fine-tuning? a separate forecaster?). This information is necessary for reproducibility and for assessing whether the comparison to baselines is apples-to-apples.

7. **SDE acronym has three different full names.** The paper refers to "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (Abstract), "Semantic Separability Error" (Section 3.2), and "Semantic Decomposition Error" (Sections 4.1, 4.3). These should be unified.

8. **Hyperparameter sensitivity for \(\gamma, \gamma'\) is not explored.** These scaling parameters control how aggressively the method reweights losses, yet no analysis of their effect is provided.

### Trivial

9. The naming inconsistency for SDE (point 7 above).

## Nice-to-Haves

- **Report SDE values for CoST+APW (and baselines) on real datasets.** This is the single most important addition to substantiate the core claim.
- **Ablate MLP vs. weighting separately** to attribute improvement sources.
- **Characterize when APW helps vs. hurts.** The Electricity and Weather failures suggest that on datasets where periodic components are already well-captured or the decomposition assumption is imperfect, the weighting may be harmful. Quantifying semantic skew per dataset and correlating with APW gains/losses would sharpen the paper's contribution.
- **Show training dynamics of Δ** (a simple plot) to demonstrate that the weighting mechanism actually adapts during training.
- **Test on at least one additional framework** beyond CoST to support the "pluggable" claim.
