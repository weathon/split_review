# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a novel approach to improve weight disentanglement in Task Arithmetic by using Kronecker-Factored Approximate Curvature (KFAC) for regularization. This method, called TAK, addresses the challenge of representation drift when composing multiple task vectors, which typically leads to degraded performance. The key innovation is leveraging KFAC to create an efficient regularizer that does not require external data, making it suitable for privacy-preserving applications. The paper demonstrates that TAK achieves state-of-the-art results in task addition and negation, while also promoting robustness to task vector rescaling and eliminating the need for held-out tuning.

## Strengths
- The paper presents a novel integration of KFAC with Task Arithmetic, addressing a key limitation in existing approaches that require external data for regularization.
- The method is rigorously developed, with clear mathematical connections between representation drift regularization and curvature approximation.
- The paper provides comprehensive empirical validation across multiple benchmarks, showing clear performance improvements over state-of-the-art methods.

## Weaknesses
- The method's reliance on KFAC introduces significant computational overhead, particularly for large-scale models and multiple tasks.
- The paper does not thoroughly explore potential limitations or failure cases of the proposed approach, such as specific architectural constraints.
- The method's performance in strictly non-linear fine-tuning regimes is less explored, leaving some uncertainty about its broad applicability.

## Questions
- Can the authors provide more insights into the computational overhead of KFAC estimation and its impact on training time?
- Are there specific architectural limitations or scenarios where TAK might not be as effective? If so, what are the authors' hypotheses?
- How does the method handle potential conflicts between task vectors in highly overlapping domains?
- Can the authors elaborate on the choice of KFAC over other curvature approximation methods, and whether this choice has limitations?
