# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a co-training framework, CaPT, which integrates the CLIP model into semi-supervised learning (SSL) to mitigate the label dependency of existing SSL methods. CaPT consists of three modules: the Unimodal Prediction Module (UPM), the Multimodal Prediction Module (MPM), and the Prediction Fusion Module (PFM). The UPM is a fully fine-tuned unimodal network, while the MPM is a parameter-efficiently fine-tuned multi-modal CLIP model. The PFM aggregates the weak predictions of the UPM and MPM, generating co-pseudo labels to guide the strong predictions in aligning with the co-pseudo labels for joint training. Experimental results on various datasets demonstrate that CaPT achieves state-of-the-art performance in low-label regimes.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-structured and clearly written, making it easy to follow the proposed method and experimental results.
2. The paper provides theoretical analysis to demonstrate the label dependency of existing SSL methods and the need for a label-independent SSL framework.
3. The proposed CaPT framework is novel and achieves state-of-the-art performance on various datasets in low-label regimes.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed method is similar to CLS, which also employs a co-training framework with two unimodal networks. While CaPT uses a unimodal network and a multimodal CLIP model, the core idea of bidirectional information flow between the two models remains the same. The authors should clarify the key differences between the two methods.
2. The paper lacks sufficient analysis of the experimental results. For example, in Table 2, CaPT shows a significant improvement on the ImageNet dataset compared to the other methods. The authors should provide an in-depth analysis of the results to better understand the reasons for this improvement.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational complexity or resource consumption of the proposed method compared to the other methods. This information would be helpful in assessing the practicality of the method.

## Questions
1. What are the key differences between CaPT and CLS? While CaPT uses a unimodal network and a multimodal CLIP model, how does this fundamentally differ from the two unimodal networks in CLS?
2. Can you provide an in-depth analysis of the experimental results, especially the significant improvement of CaPT on the ImageNet dataset compared to the other methods?
3. What is the computational complexity and resource consumption of the proposed method compared to the other methods? How does it scale with the size of the dataset and the number of classes?
