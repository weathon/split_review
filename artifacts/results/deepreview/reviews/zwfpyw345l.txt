# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to learning representations of code for reinforcement learning. The key idea is to learn representations at three levels: token-level, function-level, and module-level, and to integrate these representations using a graph attention network. The authors evaluate their approach on three code-related RL tasks: code completion, program repair, and algorithmic problem solving.

## Strengths
1. The paper addresses an important problem of learning effective representations of code for RL tasks.
2. The idea of learning representations at multiple levels of abstraction and integrating them using graph attention is interesting.
3. The authors evaluate their approach on a diverse set of tasks, including code completion, program repair, and algorithmic problem solving.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper is not well-written and is full of grammatical errors. It is hard to read and understand the main contributions.
2. The novelty of the proposed approach is limited. The use of multi-level representations and graph attention networks has been explored in previous work on code representation learning.
3. The evaluation is not very convincing. The authors compare their approach to a few baselines, but some of the baselines are not very strong. For example, the flat-GAT baseline applies uniform attention across all nodes, which is not a very strong baseline. Additionally, the evaluation metrics used in the paper are not very comprehensive. For example, the authors do not report the number of training steps required to train the models, which is an important metric for understanding the training dynamics. Furthermore, the evaluation is limited to relatively small programs (150 lines on average). It would be interesting to see how the approach scales to larger programs, such as full Python files or even entire codebases.
4. The authors do not compare their approach to any pre-trained language models (such as CodeLlama or CodeGen), which are widely used for code-related tasks. It would be interesting to see how the proposed approach compares to pre-trained models on the evaluated tasks.

## Questions
1. Can you provide more details on the implementation of the proposed approach, including the architecture used and the hyperparameters employed?
2. Can you provide more details on the training dynamics, including the number of training steps required to train the models?
3. How does the proposed approach scale to larger programs, such as full Python files or entire codebases?
4. How does the proposed approach compare to pre-trained language models on the evaluated tasks?
