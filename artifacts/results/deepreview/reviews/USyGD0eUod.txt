# Review

## Summary
This paper investigates whether sparse autoencoders (SAEs) can distinguish trained transformers from randomly initialized ones based on commonly used quality metrics and automatic explanation pipelines. The authors find that SAEs trained on randomly initialized transformers produce auto-interpretability scores and reconstruction metrics that are similar to those from trained models. They conclude that high aggregate auto-interpretability scores do not guarantee that learned, computationally relevant features have been recovered, and recommend treating common SAE metrics as useful but insufficient proxies for mechanistic interpretability.

## Strengths
- This paper raises an important question in the field of mechanistic interpretability: whether commonly used evaluation metrics can distinguish features learned through training from artifacts arising from the data or model architecture.
- The authors conduct extensive experiments across various model sizes and multiple randomization schemes, providing convincing evidence that SAEs trained on randomly initialized transformers can produce similar auto-interpretability scores and reconstruction metrics as those trained on trained models.
- The paper is well-written and clearly presents the methodology, results, and implications.

## Weaknesses
- The authors focus on a specific type of randomization scheme and a particular dataset. It would be valuable to investigate whether the findings generalize to other randomization schemes and datasets.
- While the authors acknowledge the limitation of using auto-interpretability scores as the sole evaluation metric, they do not provide concrete suggestions for alternative metrics or ways to address this limitation.
- The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion of the potential implications of the findings for the field of mechanistic interpretability, beyond the need for routine randomized baselines.

## Questions
- How do the findings generalize to other randomization schemes and datasets?
- What are some potential alternative metrics or approaches that could more accurately distinguish between features learned through training and artifacts?
- What are the potential implications of the findings for the field of mechanistic interpretability beyond the need for routine randomized baselines?
