# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces VideoJudge, a bootstrapped framework for training scalable MLLM-based evaluators specialized for video understanding. The core contribution lies in an iterative generator-evaluator pipeline that synthesizes training data and enforces quality control, creating over 100,000 training examples without costly human annotation. The experiments demonstrate that fine-tuned 3B and 7B VideoJudge models match or outperform much larger baselines in accuracy and alignment with human ratings.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces VideoJudge, a bootstrapping framework for training scalable MLLM-based evaluators across diverse video understanding tasks. 

2. The paper demonstrates that fine-tuned small models on the bootstrapped data can match or outperform much larger models in accuracy and alignment with human-specified ratings. 

3. The paper provides a suite of trained pointwise and pairwise judge models, meta-evaluation benchmarks, bootstrapped datasets, and other artifacts to support reproducible research in video understanding evaluation.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks a detailed comparison with existing evaluation methods, such as the traditional metrics (e.g., BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore) and other LLM-based evaluation methods. It would be beneficial to include a table or figure comparing the performance of VideoJudge with these existing methods on the same benchmarks. This would help readers understand the advantages and limitations of VideoJudge relative to other evaluation approaches.

2. The paper does not provide enough details about the computational resources required to train and evaluate VideoJudge. 

3. The paper could benefit from more visualizations or examples of the generated rubrics and how they improve interpretability. For instance, including a table or figure that compares the rubrics generated by VideoJudge-R-3B with those generated by other models or human annotators would provide concrete examples of the improved quality and interpretability.

## Questions
1. How does VideoJudge compare with existing evaluation methods, such as traditional metrics (e.g., BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore) and other LLM-based evaluation methods? Could you provide a detailed comparison of their performance on the same benchmarks?

2. What are the computational resources required to train and evaluate VideoJudge? Could you provide a detailed list of the hardware and software used, as well as the training time and inference time required for different model sizes?

3. How do the generated rubrics improve interpretability, and can you provide concrete examples? Could you include a table or figure comparing the rubrics generated by VideoJudge-R-3B with those generated by other models or human annotators?

4. How does VideoJudge perform on different types of video content, such as instructional videos, movies, or surveillance footage? Are there any specific domains where VideoJudge excels or struggles?
