# Review

## Summary
The paper addresses the challenge of making chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning in language models more monitorable, focusing on two key properties: faithfulness and conciseness. While CoT reasoning has shown improvements in task performance, its intermediate steps may not accurately reflect the model's decision-making process (unfaithful) or may be overly verbose, making them difficult to monitor. The authors propose a novel pipeline to generate high-quality CoT traces that satisfy monitorability constraints, ensuring both faithfulness and conciseness. This is achieved by transforming existing CoT traces using instruct models as efficient priors. The resulting dataset is then used to fine-tune the base model via supervised learning. The empirical results demonstrate that this approach improves CoT faithfulness by approximately 10% and reduces CoT lengths by up to 60% while maintaining task accuracy across several benchmarks.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a novel approach to enhance the monitorability of CoT reasoning in language models by framing it as a constraint optimization problem. The use of a prior-guided pipeline to transform CoT traces into high-quality, monitorable versions is an innovative solution to the challenges of sparse feedback and the performance-monitorability trade-off.

2. The paper is well-structured and clearly written. The authors provide a thorough explanation of the problem, including the limitations of existing approaches. The proposed solution is presented in a detailed and comprehensible manner, with clear descriptions of the methodology and algorithms used.

3. The significance of the paper lies in its contribution to making CoT reasoning more interpretable and transparent. By improving the faithfulness and conciseness of CoT traces, the proposed approach enhances the ability to monitor and understand the reasoning process of language models. This is particularly important for high-stakes applications where the ability to interpret and control the model's behavior is crucial.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper relies on a prior policy model to transform CoT traces into more monitorable forms. The quality of the transformed traces depends on the strength and reliability of this prior model, which could introduce biases or artifacts. 

2. The paper evaluates the proposed approach on a limited set of benchmarks. Expanding the evaluation to include a broader range of datasets and tasks would provide a more comprehensive assessment of the method's effectiveness.

3. The paper primarily focuses on improving the faithfulness and conciseness of CoT traces. However, other aspects of CoT monitorability, such as the clarity and comprehensibility of the reasoning process, are not extensively addressed. 

4. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational resources required for the proposed approach, such as the training time and memory usage. This information would be valuable for assessing the practical feasibility of the method.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed approach handle cases where the prior model fails to generate monitorable traces due to limitations in its capabilities?

2. What is the computational cost of the proposed approach, and how does it scale with larger datasets and more complex tasks?

3. How does the proposed approach handle cases where the monitorability constraints conflict with the task performance, and how is the balance between the two managed?

4. How does the proposed approach handle cases where the reasoning process is inherently complex and cannot be simplified into concise traces without losing accuracy?
