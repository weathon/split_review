# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a new metric called semantic disentanglement error (SDE) to quantify the imbalance between trend and periodic components in time series representation learning. The authors observe that existing methods like TS2Vec and CoST struggle to balance both semantics, leading to degraded performance. They introduce SDE as a directional measure of component recoverability and integrate it into an adaptive weighting strategy for view-specific contrastive objectives. The proposed approach can be plugged into existing frameworks like CoST without architectural changes. Experiments on benchmark datasets demonstrate consistent gains in forecasting accuracy and representational robustness, especially under semantic skew conditions.

## Strengths
1. The paper clearly identifies the semantic imbalance problem in existing time series representation learning methods and provides concrete evidence through experiments.
2. The proposed SDE metric offers a quantitative way to diagnose the imbalance between trend and periodic components.
3. The proposed asymmetric weighting integrates frequency-domain and asymmetry-aware mechanisms, leading to improved representation of weak components.
4. The experiments demonstrate consistent gains in forecasting accuracy and representational robustness, especially under semantic skew conditions.

## Weaknesses
1. The experiments focus on forecasting tasks. How does the proposed method perform on other time series tasks like classification and anomaly detection?
2. The paper could provide more details on the computational overhead introduced by the proposed SDE metric and asymmetric weighting.
3. The paper could benefit from a more extensive analysis of the sensitivity of the method to hyperparameters.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed method perform on other time series tasks besides forecasting?
2. What is the computational overhead of the proposed method compared to baseline approaches?
3. How sensitive is the method to the choice of hyperparameters?
