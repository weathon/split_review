# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a novel contribution to the field of causal discovery with latent variables, offering a structural-assumption-free approach for linear non-Gaussian models. The authors introduce the concept of edge rank constraints, which is a valuable addition to the toolkit for latent-variable causal discovery. They provide a clear graphical criterion for distributional equivalence and develop an algorithm to recover models from data up to this equivalence. The work is well-supported by theoretical derivations and empirical evaluations.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a new concept, "edge rank constraints," which is a significant contribution to the field of latent-variable causal discovery. This concept helps in characterizing distributional equivalence in linear non-Gaussian models and has the potential for use in other settings.
2. The paper provides a clear and graphical criterion for distributional equivalence, which is a notable advancement. This criterion allows for the identification of equivalent models without relying on structural assumptions, which is a step forward in the field.
3. The authors develop an algorithm that can recover the underlying causal model up to equivalence from observed data. This algorithm is a practical contribution that has the potential to be applied in real-world scenarios.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper primarily focuses on linear non-Gaussian models, which may limit its applicability to other types of models or data sets.
2. The proposed method relies on the OICA algorithm, which is known to have scalability issues. This could limit the practical applicability of the proposed method to large-scale data sets.

## Questions
1. Can the edge rank constraints be extended to other types of causal models, such as nonlinear models or models with selection bias?
2. How does the proposed algorithm handle large-scale data sets, and are there any plans to address the scalability issues?
