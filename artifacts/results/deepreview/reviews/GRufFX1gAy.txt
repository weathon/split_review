# Review

## Summary
The paper presents InnoGym, a benchmark and framework designed to evaluate the innovation potential of AI agents. It introduces two key metrics: performance gain and novelty, to assess both the correctness and methodological innovation of solutions. The benchmark includes 18 carefully curated tasks from real-world engineering and scientific domains, standardized through a rigorous filtering and augmentation process. The authors also provide iGym, a unified execution environment for reproducible and long-horizon evaluations. Extensive experiments reveal that while some agents produce novel approaches, their lack of robustness limits performance gains, highlighting a key gap between creativity and effectiveness.

## Strengths
1. The paper addresses a critical gap in AI evaluation by focusing on innovation potential, not just correctness. This is particularly relevant given the rapid advancement of LLMs and agents.
2. The framework is well-designed, with a clear separation of metrics (performance gain and novelty) and a comprehensive task curation process. The inclusion of iGym as a unified execution environment is a significant contribution to reproducible research.
3. The paper provides extensive experimental results, showcasing the limitations of current agents in achieving robust performance and highlighting the need for better evaluation metrics that balance creativity with effectiveness.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion on the reliability and generalizability of the evaluation metrics, particularly the novelty evaluation using Codex and GPT-5.
2. While the benchmark includes 18 tasks, the paper could explore more diverse task domains to ensure broader applicability.
3. The paper could provide more insights into the scalability of the iGym environment and its performance with increasingly complex tasks.

## Questions
1. How do the authors plan to address potential biases in the evaluation metrics, especially given the reliance on Codex and GPT-5 for novelty assessment?
2. Can the authors provide more details on the scalability of iGym and its performance with more complex tasks?
3. How might the framework be extended to evaluate innovation in non-coding domains, such as natural language understanding or reasoning?
4. What are the authors' thoughts on incorporating human evaluation alongside automated metrics to assess the creativity and innovation of AI agents?
