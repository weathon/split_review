# Review

## Summary
This paper studies the two-community stochastic block model (SBM). A well-known algorithm for this problem is the spectral algorithm, which has two steps: spectral partition and correction. The authors showed that the spectral partition step alone can achieve results that were previously thought to require the correction step. The key insight is that the step of zeroing out vertices with high degrees is not needed. This step destroys the statistical independence of matrix entries, but this property is needed for the analysis. The authors also showed that the error bound achieved by the spectral partition step is tight, and is close to the information-theoretic limit.

## Strengths
The paper is well-written. The theoretical analysis is solid and the experiments are convincing. The finding that the correction step is not needed is interesting, although it is a bit incremental since it is a relatively straightforward modification of the analysis of the original spectral algorithm.

## Weaknesses
The main weakness is that the model studied is very simple, and it is not clear how relevant the analysis is for more general settings. In particular, the analysis seems to heavily depend on the fact that the graph is a SBM and that the communities are equal-sized. It is not clear how much the techniques can be generalized to more general graphs and models.

## Questions
* The analysis seems to depend heavily on the fact that the communities are equal-sized. Is it possible to extend the analysis to the imbalanced case?

* The analysis also seems to depend heavily on the fact that the graph is a SBM. Is it possible to extend the analysis to more general graphs? For example, what if the graph comes from a SBM plus some random noise?
