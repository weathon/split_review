# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a new process-level creativity evaluation framework for human-LLM dialogues, focusing on evaluating the creative process and contribution attribution in multi-turn interactions. The authors collect a dataset of 1,273 dialogues from 81 undergraduates and develop the Innovation Tracing Atlas (ITA) to decompose and annotate the dialogues turn-by-turn. They train an instruction-tuned evaluator on this dataset to output scores and rationales along four creativity dimensions. Experiments show that the fine-tuned model achieves better performance than baselines in evaluating creativity.

## Strengths
- The paper addresses an important and timely problem in AI-assisted creativity evaluation, which is becoming increasingly relevant as LLMs are integrated into educational tools.
- The proposed CREDO evaluation framework and ITA method provide a novel and useful process-oriented approach for creativity assessment, shifting focus from final products to the dynamics of the creative process.
- The dataset, if released, would be a valuable resource for further research on creativity evaluation and human-LLM interaction.

## Weaknesses
- The paper lacks a clear and detailed explanation of how the ITA visually represents the cognitive process and how it helps in the evaluation process. Including examples or a more thorough description of the ITA structure and how it aids in creativity assessment would significantly improve understanding.
- The experimental evaluation is limited. The paper does not compare the proposed method with existing creativity evaluation approaches, making it difficult to assess its relative advantages. Additionally, the fine-tuning details and the impact of different training strategies on evaluation performance are not discussed.
- The paper does not provide a comprehensive error analysis, which would help in understanding the limitations and potential areas for improvement. A detailed examination of failure cases and the reasons behind them would be valuable.

## Questions
- Could you provide more details on the structure and interpretation of the Innovation Traceability Atlas (ITA)? Specifically, how does it visually represent the cognitive process, and how does it aid in the evaluation of creativity?
- How does the proposed method compare to existing creativity evaluation frameworks in terms of accuracy and effectiveness? Are there specific baselines or methods you considered for comparison?
- Could you elaborate on the fine-tuning process and the impact of different training strategies (e.g., LoRA, distillation) on the evaluation performance? How did you determine the optimal training configuration?
- Have you conducted an error analysis to understand the limitations of the proposed approach? Could you provide examples of failure cases and potential reasons for these failures?
