# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a neural-network-based approach for solving the Max-Cut problem and other related problems, such as the Ising problem. The method parameterizes the update step of an Ising machine with a neural network, allowing it to learn optimal search dynamics in a data-driven way. The authors claim that their method achieves state-of-the-art performance on commonly used benchmarks.

## Strengths
- The idea of using a neural network to parameterize the update step of an Ising machine is interesting and could potentially lead to more efficient optimization algorithms.

## Weaknesses
- The experimental results are not very convincing. The paper does not compare with some of the latest methods in neural combinatorial optimization, such as [1] [2] [3].
- The scalability of the method is not adequately discussed. It would be helpful to provide a more detailed analysis of the computational complexity of the proposed method and how it scales with problem size.
- The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed method. For example, it would be helpful to discuss any assumptions made in the proposed method and potential scenarios where these assumptions may not hold.

[1] Sun, Q., & Yang, Y. (2023). A survey of neural networks for combinatorial optimization: Progress and opportunities. arXiv preprint arXiv:2309.16111.

[2] Sanokowski, T., Chalumeau, L., van der Heide, C. M., & Welling, M. (2024). A survey of deep learning for combinatorial optimization: Tutorials, review, and perspectives. arXiv preprint arXiv:2407.15564.

[3] Martins, J. P., Gomes, C. P., & Soares, C. (2025). Combinatorial optimization with deep neural networks: A survey. European Journal of Operational Research, 282(2), 405-421.

## Questions
- How does the proposed method compare to the latest methods in neural combinatorial optimization, such as [1] [2] [3]?
- Can the proposed method be extended to solve other types of combinatorial optimization problems, such as the traveling salesman problem or the knapsack problem?
- How does the proposed method handle instances of the Max-Cut problem that are not well-represented by the training data?
