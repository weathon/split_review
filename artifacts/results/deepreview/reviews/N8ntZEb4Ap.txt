# Review

## Summary
The authors present a novel feature selection method that uses gumbel-sigmoid sampling to select features. The method is end-to-end differentiable and can select a variable number of features.

## Soundness
3

## Presentation
3

## Contribution
2

## Strengths
- The paper is well written and easy to follow.
- The authors evaluate their method on a variety of datasets and compare it to a variety of other methods.
- The method is novel and interesting.

## Weaknesses
- The authors do not compare to the most obvious baseline: selecting no features at all. This is important because the authors' method reduces the dimensionality of the data, which likely improves performance simply by reducing overfitting. The authors show that their method outperforms other dimensionality reduction methods, which is not that surprising given that their method is optimized to help a downstream classifier.
- The authors use a somewhat unusual evaluation scheme where 50% of the features are corrupted. This is not a standard scheme, and it is not clear why the authors chose this particular scheme. It would be good to see the performance under no corruption.
- The authors do not discuss the computational cost of their method. It seems like it would be quite expensive to train, and the authors do not compare its cost to the costs of the other methods that they use for comparison.
- The authors do not discuss the stability of their method. It would be good to see error bars on the performance across multiple runs and/or information about which runs failed.

## Questions
- Why did you use the 50% corruption scheme? Why not use the standard schemes?
- How does your method compare to not using any feature selection?
- How does your method compare to the other methods when there is no corruption?
