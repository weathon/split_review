# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a new allreduce algorithm that can work efficiently when one of the GPUs is a straggler, i.e., it takes longer to finish computation. The proposed algorithm has two phases. The first phase uses the time for straggler to catch up with other GPUs to do a reduce-scatter communication. In the second phase, it uses a specially designed communication schedule to do the allreduce. The authors evaluated the proposed algorithm on a DGX server with 8 GPUs and showed that it could outperform the state-of-the-art allreduce algorithms.

## Strengths
- The proposed algorithm is interesting and the evaluation results are promising. 

- The authors provide a communication complexity analysis for the proposed algorithm.

## Weaknesses
- The proposed algorithm is not very practical. It assumes that a straggler can be identified in advance and it relies on the straggler to complete the reduce-scatter communication and to provide a schedule for the allreduce communication. However, these assumptions are not always true in practice. In real applications, the straggler can change from time to time, and the algorithm may need to switch the straggler frequently. Moreover, it is unclear how to provide a schedule for the allreduce communication in the proposed algorithm. The authors did not provide sufficient details on this. 

- The proposed algorithm is only evaluated on a system with 8 GPUs. It is unclear if the proposed algorithm can scale to systems with a large number of GPUs. 

- The authors claimed that the proposed algorithm can achieve a $2\times$ speedup over the lower bound of bandwidth-efficient algorithms. However, this claim is not well supported by the evaluation results. It is unclear what the best and worst cases of the compared baseline algorithms are. Moreover, the authors only evaluated the algorithms on a system with 8 GPUs, which is relatively small.

## Questions
- Can the proposed algorithm work efficiently when there are multiple stragglers?

- How to provide a schedule for the allreduce communication in the proposed algorithm?

- How does the proposed algorithm perform on systems with a large number of GPUs?

- What are the best and worst cases of the compared baseline algorithms?
