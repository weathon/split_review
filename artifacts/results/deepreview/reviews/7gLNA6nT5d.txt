# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes incorporating n-gram induction heads into transformers for in-context reinforcement learning, aiming to improve data efficiency and reduce hyperparameter sensitivity. The method is evaluated on grid-world and pixel-based environments, showing comparable or superior performance to Algorithm Distillation in some cases.

## Strengths
The integration of n-gram induction heads into transformers for in-context RL is a novel approach that addresses the challenges of data efficiency and hyperparameter sensitivity.

The method is evaluated on both grid-world and pixel-based environments, demonstrating its applicability to different types of observation spaces.

The paper provides experimental evidence that n-gram induction heads can reduce the amount of data required for generalization and make models less sensitive to hyperparameters.

## Weaknesses
The paper only compares the proposed method to Algorithm Distillation. A broader comparison with other in-context RL methods or RL methods in general would provide a more comprehensive evaluation.

While the paper claims improvements in data efficiency, the experiments are limited to relatively simple environments. The scalability of the method to more complex environments is not demonstrated.

The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational overhead introduced by the n-gram induction heads. This could be an important consideration for practical applications.

## Questions
Can you provide a more comprehensive comparison with other in-context RL methods or RL methods in general?

Can you provide more experiments on more complex environments to demonstrate the scalability of the method?

Can you provide a detailed analysis of the computational overhead introduced by the n-gram induction heads?
