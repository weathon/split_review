# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces Ambig-SWE, an extension of SWE-Bench Verified designed to evaluate LLM agents' handling of underspecified instructions in interactive code generation settings. The study assesses models' performance in detecting underspecification, asking clarification questions, and leveraging interaction to improve outcomes. The authors find that while models often fail to recognize underspecified inputs, interaction significantly improves performance, with some models achieving up to a 74% increase in resolution rates over non-interactive settings. The paper highlights critical gaps in current models' handling of missing information in complex software engineering tasks and provides structured evaluation steps for targeted improvements.

## Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The study provides a thorough evaluation of multiple aspects of LLM agent performance, including detection of underspecification, question asking, and task completion. The breakdown of results by model type and size offers valuable insights into how different architectures handle interaction.
- The introduction of Ambig-SWE as an extension of SWE-Bench Verified specifically for interactive, underspecified tasks is a valuable contribution. The benchmark has potential to drive future research on more effective handling of underspecification in software engineering and beyond.

## Weaknesses
- The paper relies on GPT-4o as a user proxy, which may not fully capture the variability and unpredictability of real user interactions. While the authors acknowledge this limitation, further analysis of how real user behavior might differ would strengthen the study.
- The study is limited to 500 issues from SWE-Bench Verified, potentially affecting the generalizability of the findings. Expanding the dataset could provide more robust insights, especially for less common scenarios.
- While the paper identifies significant performance improvements through interaction, it doesn't deeply explore the efficiency implications. More analysis of how interaction affects task completion time and computational resources would be valuable.

## Questions
- How do you envision extending Ambig-SWE to evaluate more naturalistic user interactions? What additional challenges do you anticipate in simulating real-world user behavior compared to the current setup?
- Given the performance improvements observed with interaction, have you analyzed the cost-benefit trade-offs in terms of computational resources and task completion time? How do these costs compare between interactive and non-interactive settings?
- The study focuses on software engineering tasks. Do you anticipate similar patterns in other domains that also involve complex, multi-step tasks with potential underspecification? What adjustments would be necessary to adapt Ambig-SWE to these domains?
