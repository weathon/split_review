# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a benchmark for evaluating the spatial reasoning capabilities of LLMs, image models, and agents. The task is to convert a set of photographs of an apartment into a 2D floor plan. The authors evaluate several state-of-the-art models on this benchmark and find that most models perform at or below a random baseline.

## Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The proposed benchmark is well-defined, with clear rules and evaluation metrics.
- The paper evaluates a wide range of models, including LLMs, image models, and agents.

## Weaknesses
- The paper does not provide much insight into why the models fail at this task or how it could be improved.
- The benchmark is relatively small, with only 50 apartments, and the apartments are quite similar (e.g., they are all residential apartments).
- The paper does not consider the use of vector-based representations, which could potentially improve performance.

## Questions
- Have you analyzed the failure cases of the models? What are the most common mistakes they make?
- How might the performance of the models change if they were provided with vector-based representations of the apartment (e.g., a Matterport3D scan)?
- How might the performance of the models change if the apartments were more diverse (e.g., commercial buildings, museums, etc.)?
