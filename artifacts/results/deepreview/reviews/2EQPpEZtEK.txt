# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces DiSTAR, a zero-shot TTS framework that operates entirely in the RVQ discrete code space and tightly couples an autoregressive drafter with masked diffusion. DiSTAR achieves blockwise parallelism while modeling layer-time dependencies across RVQ levels and local temporal neighborhoods, without an explicit duration predictor. The paper further develops an RVQ-specific sampling method that enhances quality and stability, supports diverse decoding strategies, and enables on-the-fly bitrate and compute control without retraining.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces DiSTAR, a zero-shot TTS framework that couples an autoregressive drafter with masked diffusion entirely in the discrete RVQ domain, achieving patch-level parallelism and joint modeling of RVQ layer-time dependencies.

2. The paper develops an RVQ-specific sampling method that boosts quality and stability, supports diverse decoding strategies, and enables on-the-fly bitrate and compute control without retraining.

3. DiSTAR provides state-of-the-art robustness, speaker similarity, and naturalness in standard zero-shot benchmarks, with comparable or lower computational cost.

## Weaknesses
1. The authors do not provide an evaluation of the model's robustness to different levels of noise, which is crucial for assessing its performance under various conditions.

2. The authors do not provide an evaluation of the model's robustness to different speaking styles, such as different speaking rates, which is crucial for assessing its performance under various conditions.

## Questions
1. How does DiSTAR perform under different levels of noise? Can the authors provide an evaluation of the model's robustness to noise?

2. How does DiSTAR perform under different speaking styles? Can the authors provide an evaluation of the model's robustness to different speaking rates?
