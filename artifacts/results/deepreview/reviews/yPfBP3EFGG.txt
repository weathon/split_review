# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a two-track variant of Nesterov-accelerated Adam, called STNAdam, for solving nonconvex + weakly-convex composite optimization problems. Theoretical convergence results are established under the KL assumption, and numerical results on low-light image enhancement are presented to demonstrate the superior performance of STNAdam.

## Strengths
1. The proposed STNAdam algorithm is an extension of the existing Adam algorithm, and it combines the ideas of Nesterov acceleration and adaptive learning rates. The two-track iteration framework is a novel approach that allows for continuous exploration of better iteration directions.

2. The authors provide a general convergence result for STNAdam under the KL assumption. The stochastic gradient in STNAdam can be provided by arbitrary a variance-reduced gradient estimator, such as SVRG, SAGA, and SARAH. This makes the algorithm versatile and applicable to a wide range of optimization problems.

3. The authors present empirical results on low-light image enhancement to demonstrate the superior performance of STNAdam. The results show that STNAdam outperforms existing algorithms in terms of image quality and computational efficiency.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed algorithm is an extension of the existing Adam algorithm, but the paper does not provide a detailed discussion on the novelty of the proposed approach. It would be helpful to clarify how STNAdam differs from previous Adam variants and what specific challenges it addresses.

2. The theoretical analysis is based on the KL assumption, which is a common assumption in optimization theory. However, it would be helpful to provide more intuition on why the KL assumption holds in practice and what implications it has for the convergence result.

3. The paper presents empirical results on low-light image enhancement, but it does not provide a detailed discussion on why this particular application is suitable for the proposed algorithm. It would be helpful to explain how the algorithm's features, such as the two-track iteration framework, are beneficial for this application.

4. The paper does not provide a comparison of the proposed algorithm with other state-of-the-art algorithms for the considered problem class. It would be helpful to include such a comparison to demonstrate the superiority of STNAdam over existing approaches.

5. The paper does not provide a discussion on the limitations of the proposed algorithm. It would be helpful to clarify any assumptions or constraints that need to be satisfied for the algorithm to work effectively, as well as any potential drawbacks or challenges in practical implementation.
