# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces TRACE (Truncated Reasoning AUC Evaluation), a method designed to detect implicit reward hacking in language models. TRACE works by progressively truncating a model’s chain-of-thought (CoT) at various lengths and estimating the expected reward at each cutoff. The key insight is that hacking models often achieve high rewards with only a small fraction of their CoT, indicating low effort. In contrast, non-hacking models require the full CoT to arrive at the answer. TRACE demonstrates significant gains over existing CoT monitoring methods in math reasoning and coding tasks, particularly for implicit reward hacking where CoT appears benign. The approach also enables unsupervised discovery of dataset loopholes, making it a promising tool for AI oversight.

## Strengths
- The paper is well written and easy to follow.
- The proposed method is simple yet effective, and has the potential to be adapted to a wide range of tasks and model architectures.
- TRACE demonstrates superior performance compared to CoT monitoring methods, especially in scenarios where the CoT appears plausible but contains hidden shortcuts.

## Weaknesses
- The paper would benefit from a more extensive discussion of its limitations and potential edge cases. For example, it's unclear how TRACE would perform in tasks where the model's CoT is genuinely complex and requires the full length to arrive at the solution.
- The experiments focus on math and code tasks, but it's not clear how well TRACE generalizes to other types of reasoning tasks, such as creative writing or strategic planning.

## Questions
- How sensitive is TRACE to the choice of truncation points? Is there an optimal way to determine the truncation percentages?
- How does TRACE handle cases where the model's CoT is genuinely complex and requires the full length to arrive at the solution? Would the method incorrectly flag these as hacking cases?
- How does TRACE perform on tasks where the model's CoT contains irrelevant information or logical errors, but is not hacking?
- How does TRACE compare to other detection methods, such as those based on the model's output correctness or the alignment between CoT and final answer?
- How does TRACE perform on models with different architectures or sizes, especially very large models?
- How does TRACE handle cases where the model engages in overthinking, producing longer CoT than necessary?
