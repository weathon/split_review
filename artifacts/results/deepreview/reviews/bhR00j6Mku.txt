# Review

## Summary
This paper studies the contamination of benchmarks in LLMs. The authors focus on two scenarios: (1) contamination during supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL), and (2) contamination with chain-of-thought (CoT) applied to advanced LLMs. They find that existing detection methods are fragile and can be easily evaded. The authors argue that the PPO-style importance sampling and clipping objectives are the root cause of this detection concealment. They also suggest that model developers should release more intermediate training checkpoints to allow the community to monitor and regulate potential benchmark contamination.

## Strengths
1. The paper provides a comprehensive analysis of benchmark contamination in LLMs, covering two different scenarios and evaluating multiple detection methods.
2. The authors identify the root cause of detection concealment and provide theoretical analysis.
3. The paper highlights the urgent need for advanced contamination detection methods and trustworthy evaluation protocols tailored to LLMs.

## Weaknesses
1. The authors could provide more detailed explanations of the experimental setup and results, such as the contamination ratios used and the performance of the detection methods under different contamination ratios.
2. The authors could discuss the potential limitations and challenges of releasing intermediate training checkpoints, such as intellectual property concerns or computational overhead.
3. The authors could explore potential mitigation strategies, such as modifying the PPO-style importance sampling and clipping objectives or developing more robust detection methods that can account for the root cause of detection concealment.

## Questions
1. How does the contamination ratio affect the performance of the detection methods in both scenarios?
2. What are the potential limitations and challenges of releasing intermediate training checkpoints?
3. How can we develop more robust detection methods that can account for the root cause of detection concealment?
