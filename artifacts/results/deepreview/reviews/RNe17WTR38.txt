# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes generator-verifier games to improve language models. The same base model is used as the generator and the verifier. The generator generates multiple responses, which are verified by the verifier. The responses that pass the verification are used to construct preference data for fine-tuning the model. To reduce the noisy verification, the authors propose a thresholded majority voting method, where a response is considered as a positive sample if its correctness rate exceeds a threshold. The authors conduct experiments on both synthetic and realistic reasoning tasks to verify the effectiveness of the proposed method.

## Strengths
1. The authors study the problem of improving language models without external supervision, which is an interesting and important problem.
2. The authors propose generator-verifier games to improve language models, which is a simple yet effective method. The authors also propose a thresholded majority voting method to reduce the noisy verification, which is reasonable.
3. The authors conduct experiments on both synthetic and realistic reasoning tasks to verify the effectiveness of the proposed method.

## Weaknesses
1. The authors conduct experiments on relatively small models (up to 12B). It is not clear whether the proposed method is effective on larger models.
2. The authors only evaluate the proposed method on reasoning tasks. It is not clear whether the proposed method is effective on other types of tasks.
3. The authors use DPO to fine-tune the model. It is not clear whether the proposed method is effective when combined with other fine-tuning methods, such as RLHF.
4. The authors do not compare the proposed method with other self-improvement methods, such as TTRL and R-Zero.
