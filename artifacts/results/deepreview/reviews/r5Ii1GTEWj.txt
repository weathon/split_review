# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a text-to-motion generation pipeline with three main components: 1) a curated large-scale Motion2Motion benchmark dataset consisting of text-to-motion dialogues, 2) a reinforcement learning-based motion generation model trained with the dataset, and 3) a low-level reinforcement learning-based optimization framework to ensure kinematic feasibility and physical constraints. The authors show that the proposed method surpasses strong baselines in both accuracy and interpretability.

## Strengths
The authors curated a large-scale Motion2Motion benchmark dataset consisting of text-to-motion dialogues, which may benefit the community.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper is poorly written. It is hard to follow the paper due to the lack of details. For example, the details of the dataset, the training and inference procedure of the proposed method, and the implementation of the low-level optimization framework are missing. The reviewer strongly suggests the authors reorganize the paper and add more details.
2. The reviewer is confused about the main contribution of the paper. The reviewer thinks the main contribution is the dataset. However, the details of the dataset are missing in the paper. If the main contribution is the dataset, the reviewer thinks the paper is more suitable for the dataset track.
3. The authors claim that the proposed method can generate physically consistent motions. However, the reviewer cannot find any experiments to support the claim. The reviewer strongly suggests the authors add experiments to support the claim.
4. The authors claim that the proposed method can generate motions from multi-turn dialogues. However, the reviewer cannot find any results to support the claim. The reviewer strongly suggests the authors add results to support the claim.

## Questions
1. What is the main contribution of the paper?
2. Can the proposed method generate physically consistent motions? Can the authors provide experiments to support the claim?
3. Can the proposed method generate motions from multi-turn dialogues? Can the authors provide results to support the claim?
