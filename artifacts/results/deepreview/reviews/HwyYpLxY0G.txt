# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces the Aligned Scoring Rule (ASR) to align textual scoring with human preferences while maintaining provable properness. Building on Wu & Hartline (2024), which reduces textual to numerical elicitation with proper scoring rules, ASR further optimizes this framework to better align with human preferences, using mean squared error minimization. Experiments demonstrate that ASR outperforms previous methods in aligning with human preferences without losing properness, showing strong potential for applications like peer grading.

## Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The proposed method is simple and easy to understand, with clear explanations and effective visualizations of key concepts.
- ASR is provably truthful and maintains robustness even when the language model makes errors.
- Experimental results show that ASR effectively fits reference scores and outperforms baseline methods in aligning with human preferences.

## Weaknesses
- The primary weakness is that the proposed method relies heavily on the ElicitationGPT framework from Wu & Hartline (2024), with the only significant modification being the optimization of scoring rules to align with human preferences. This limits the theoretical and technical contributions of the paper.
- The experiments are conducted on a single peer grading dataset, which may not fully demonstrate the method's generalizability across different domains or types of textual scoring tasks.

## Questions
- Could you elaborate on how the optimization of scoring rules to align with human preferences might impact the interpretability of the final scoring outcomes? Specifically, are there cases where the optimized ASR might produce unexpected scoring results that differ significantly from human judgment?
