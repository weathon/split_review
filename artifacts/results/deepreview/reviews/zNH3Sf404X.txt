# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a semi-supervised learning framework for detecting illicit Bitcoin transactions, emphasizing the importance of data quality over quantity. The authors present a comprehensive dataset of 163 million Bitcoin transactions and propose novel features to improve detection accuracy.

## Strengths
1. The paper provides a new dataset for illicit Bitcoin transaction detection.
2. The authors propose a semi-supervised learning framework that leverages unlabeled data effectively.
3. The writing is clear and easy to follow.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks a comparison with existing methods, such as GNN-based approaches mentioned in the related work.
2. The evaluation metrics are limited to F1-score and ROC AUC, which may not fully capture the performance of the proposed method.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the limitations of the proposed method or potential areas for improvement.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed method compare with existing approaches in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency?
2. Can the authors provide more insights into the features that contribute most significantly to the improved performance?
3. Are there any potential biases in the dataset that could affect the generalizability of the findings?
