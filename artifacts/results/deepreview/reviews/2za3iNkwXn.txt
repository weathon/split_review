# Review

## Summary
The paper explores the impact of compression techniques—quantization, distillation, and pruning—on the reasoning capabilities of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). Through performance benchmarking and mechanistic interpretation, the authors investigate how these compression methods affect reasoning capabilities across various datasets. The study highlights specific model weights, such as the MLP up-projection in the final layer, as being particularly crucial for reasoning tasks, offering insights for improving LRM compression and emphasizing the need to protect certain weight modules from excessive compression.

## Strengths
1. The authors conduct comprehensive experiments to evaluate the performance of various compressed LRM models on reasoning tasks, providing a thorough benchmarking analysis.
2. The authors provide a detailed mechanistic interpretation by tracking changes in weight importance scores, offering a fine-grained view of how compression affects specific model components.
3. The authors' findings have practical implications for model compression, particularly in preserving critical weights for reasoning tasks.

## Weaknesses
1. The authors' focus is primarily on DeepSeek-R1 and variants, which may not fully represent the performance of other LRM models under different compression strategies. Additional experiments on a broader range of models could strengthen the generalizability of the findings.
2. The authors mainly test on four reasoning datasets. While these are diverse, additional reasoning tasks could further validate the robustness of the proposed methods.

## Questions
1. How do the authors ensure the robustness of their mechanistic interpretation methods, such as the reliability of GPT-4o in identifying token sequences?
2. How do the authors' findings generalize to non-R1 model families? Are there any limitations or differences in the compression effects observed with other LRM architectures?
3. The authors note that pruning affects knowledge retention more than reasoning capabilities. Could they provide more insights into why this is the case and how it might be mitigated?
