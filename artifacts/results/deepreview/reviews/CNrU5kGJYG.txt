# Review

## Summary
This paper investigates the security of Trajectory Optimization (TO) models used in offline reinforcement learning. The authors identify the vulnerability of TO models to backdoor attacks, which are largely unexplored in this context. Existing backdoor attacks in RL, which rely on reward manipulation, are ineffective against TO models due to their sequence modeling nature and large network size. The authors propose TrojanTO, the first action-level backdoor attack against TO models, which employs alternating training to forge a connection between triggers and target actions. TrojanTO utilizes trajectory filtering to preserve benign performance and batch poisoning for trigger consistency. The paper demonstrates the effectiveness of TrojanTO in implanting backdoors across diverse tasks and model architectures with a low attack budget.

## Strengths
- The paper presents a novel approach to backdoor attacks in RL, specifically targeting TO models. This is an innovative contribution to the field, as existing backdoor attacks are not effective against TO models.
- The proposed TrojanTO attack is a post-training attack, which decouples the attack from the training process. This makes it more practical and feasible, as it does not require access to the original training dataset or the ability to manipulate the training loop.
- The paper provides a comprehensive evaluation of TrojanTO across a variety of RL tasks and TO model architectures. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of TrojanTO in implanting backdoors while maintaining a low attack budget and minimal impact on benign performance.

## Weaknesses
- The paper does not provide a detailed explanation of the trigger design and how it is optimized. More information on the trigger generation process and the optimization algorithm used would be beneficial.
- The paper does not compare the proposed TrojanTO attack with existing backdoor attacks in RL. It would be valuable to see how TrojanTO performs in comparison to other attacks in terms of effectiveness, efficiency, and stealthiness.

## Questions
- Can you provide more details on the trigger design and optimization process? How do you ensure that the trigger is robust and can be effectively recognized by the model?
- How does TrojanTO compare to existing backdoor attacks in RL? Can you provide a comparative analysis of the effectiveness, efficiency, and stealthiness of TrojanTO against other attacks?
