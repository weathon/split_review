# Review

## Summary
The paper analyzes the behavior of the parameter $r$ (reciprocal of optimal step length) in the classical steepest descent method for convex quadratic optimization problems, introducing a step length coefficient $t$. The study shows that the behavior of $r$ can be influenced by different values of $t$, resulting in different system states: convergence to a fixed value, oscillation between regions, or chaotic behavior. A detailed analysis is provided for a two-dimensional case. The research highlights the impact of $t$ on the overall system convergence.

## Strengths
The paper introduces an approach to analyzing the steepest descent method by focusing on the parameter $r$ and its interaction with the step length coefficient $t$. This perspective offers a new way of understanding the behavior of the system.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper seems to lack a clear, well-articulated problem statement. It would benefit from a more explicit definition of the problem being addressed, including the specific challenges and objectives. This would help readers better understand the context and significance of the research.

2. The paper appears to be more of an investigative report than a comprehensive research study. While it explores the behavior of the parameter $r$ and its interactions with the step length coefficient $t$, it does not seem to provide a structured or in-depth analysis. A research study typically involves a more systematic approach, including the formulation of hypotheses, the design of experiments, and the rigorous testing and validation of these hypotheses. It would be helpful if the authors could clarify how their approach relates to or differs from traditional research methodologies.

3. The paper does not seem to provide a thorough review of relevant literature. A good research paper typically situates its contributions within the existing body of knowledge, demonstrating an awareness of prior work and how the current study builds upon, challenges, or complements previous findings. It would be beneficial if the authors could discuss how their work relates to or extends the current state of research in the field.
