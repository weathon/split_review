# Review

## Summary
This paper considers the problem of machine unlearning, which aims to adjust a trained model to approximate a retrained one that excludes a portion of training data. Unlike previous studies that assumed the coincidence of class labels and target concepts, this work expands the scope by considering the label domain mismatch and investigates three problems beyond conventional all-matched forgetting, namely target mismatch, model mismatch, and data mismatch forgetting. The authors systematically analyze the new challenges in restrictively forgetting the target concept and reveal crucial forgetting dynamics in the representation level. Based on these insights, they propose a general framework called TARget-aware Forgetting (TARF) to enable active forgetting of the target concept while maintaining the rest by simultaneously conducting annealed gradient ascent on the forgetting data and selected gradient descent on the hard-to-affect remaining data. Empirical experiments under the newly introduced settings demonstrate the effectiveness of TARF.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The authors provide a clear motivation for the problem and a detailed description of the proposed approach.

2. The authors introduce new settings that decouple the class label and the target concept, which is a novel and interesting perspective in the field of machine unlearning.

3. The authors systematically reveal the challenges of restrictive unlearning with the mismatched label domains and demonstrate the importance of representation gravity in forgetting dynamics.

4. The proposed TARF framework is general and effective, and can handle a wide range of unlearning tasks.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed TARF framework is a three-phase process, which may be more complex and computationally expensive than some existing methods.

2. The performance of TARF relies on the quality of target identification, which may not always be accurate or reliable.

3. The experiments are mainly conducted on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets, which are relatively small and may not fully represent the performance on larger and more complex datasets.

## Questions
1. How does TARF handle the case where the target concept is not well-defined or ambiguous in the representation space?

2. Can TARF be extended to handle more challenging scenarios such as multiple concept unlearning or weakly-supervised unlearning?

3. How does TARF compare to state-of-the-art methods in terms of computational cost and scalability?
