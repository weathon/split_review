# Review

## Summary
The authors investigate the geometrical structure of the latent space of the vision foundation model DINOv2 using a linear autoencoder. They show that different tasks exploit the latent space differently. Further, they find that the latent space is more structured than expected from a random or Grassmannian basis. They propose the Minkowski representation hypothesis, where they suggest that tokens are formed by combining convex mixtures of a few archetypes.

## Strengths
- The paper is very well written and structured.
- The topic is of high interest to the community.
- The proposed Minkowski representation hypothesis is very interesting and could have implications for future work in the field.

## Weaknesses
- The paper is not self-contained, as a lot of content is pushed to the appendix. I think the paper would benefit from cutting less important content and putting it into the appendix, and using the space to add more important content from the appendix to the main paper. For example, I think the discussion of the co-activation matrix $Z^TZ$ and the dictionary $DD^T$ (see lines 227-229) should be in the main paper, as it is an important result. 
- The authors should be more clear when they introduce the concept of “concepts”. It is not clear from the beginning that they refer to the learned atoms in the autoencoder. I think it would be better to introduce the term “concept” earlier, e.g., already in the abstract, together with the explanation that they refer to the learned atoms in the autoencoder. 
- The authors should state more clearly that their investigation is limited to DINOv2 and that it is not clear if their findings generalize to other models. 
- The authors should make clear earlier that they study the latent space of a linear autoencoder trained on DINOv2 (see lines 84-85). I think it would be better to state this already in the abstract.

## Questions
- Do the authors expect their findings to generalize to other models? 
- Did the authors investigate other models besides DINOv2? 
- Is the linear autoencoder trained on DINOv2? Or is it trained on the output of DINOv2? I think this should be stated more clearly in the paper. 
- How did the authors choose the number of 32,000 concepts? Is this number related to the dimension of the latent space of the autoencoder? 
- What is the reconstruction error of the autoencoder?
