# Review

## Summary
This paper investigates data contamination in LLMs, focusing on the Arabic language context. The authors fine-tune several LLMs on varying proportions of Arabic datasets and evaluate them on the original English benchmarks to assess potential memorization effects. They extend the Tested Slot (TS)-Guessing method with a choice-reordering strategy to differentiate between genuine reasoning and contaminated recall. The findings reveal that while translating benchmarks into Arabic obscures traditional contamination signals, models still benefit from exposure to contaminated data, particularly those with stronger Arabic capabilities. The paper highlights the need for multilingual, contamination-aware pipelines to ensure fair and transparent evaluation of LLMs.

## Strengths
1. The paper addresses a crucial issue in LLM evaluation: data contamination. By extending the investigation to a multilingual context, specifically Arabic, the authors contribute to a less explored area, shedding light on how translation affects contamination detection.
2. The authors employ the TS-Guessing method, adapted with a choice-reordering strategy, to disentangle genuine reasoning from contaminated recall. This approach enhances the understanding of memorization effects in multilingual settings.

## Weaknesses
1. The experimental setup and choice of datasets may not fully support the paper’s objectives. For example, the use of the MMLU dataset in both the fine-tuning and evaluation phases could introduce bias. It would be more appropriate to use a different dataset for fine-tuning to avoid this potential issue.
2. The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion of the implications of the findings. Specifically, the authors should elaborate on how their results contribute to the broader understanding of data contamination and its impact on LLM evaluation.
3. The paper does not adequately address the limitations of the study. For instance, the authors should discuss the potential impact of translation quality on the results and the implications of their findings for different types of LLMs.

## Questions
1. How might the findings of this study generalize to other multilingual contexts beyond Arabic? Are there specific characteristics of the Arabic language or the datasets used that make the findings applicable to other languages and datasets?
2. What are the potential limitations of the Translation-Aware Contamination Detection (TACD) framework proposed in the study? How feasible is it to implement in other languages and datasets, and what are the associated challenges?
3. How do the findings of this study contribute to the broader understanding of data contamination in LLMs? What are the implications for the development and evaluation of future LLMs?
