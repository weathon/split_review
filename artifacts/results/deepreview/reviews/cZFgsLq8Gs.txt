# Review

## Summary
The authors present DeepScientist, an LLM-based agent system designed to autonomously conduct scientific research. The system's architecture and exploration strategy are well-defined, and the authors have implemented a three-stage cycle for DeepScientist: Strategize & Hypothesize, Implement & Verify, and Analyze & Report. The authors evaluate DeepScientist on three tasks: Agent Failure Attribution, LLM Inference Acceleration, and AI Text Detection. The results demonstrate that DeepScientist can surpass human-designed SOTA methods. The paper also includes a thorough analysis of the system's performance, the quality of the generated papers, and the trajectory of autonomous exploration.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The authors provide a clear and detailed description of DeepScientist's architecture and exploration strategy.
3. The evaluation of DeepScientist on three different tasks provides evidence of the system's ability to surpass human-designed SOTA methods.
4. The analysis of the system's performance, the quality of the generated papers, and the trajectory of autonomous exploration adds depth to the understanding of DeepScientist's capabilities.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed comparison with other AI scientist systems, such as AI-Scientist-V2, CycleResearcher, and Zochi. It would be beneficial to include a comparison of the performance and capabilities of DeepScientist with these existing systems.
2. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the limitations of DeepScientist. It would be beneficial to include a discussion of the potential limitations and challenges that DeepScientist may face in conducting scientific research.

## Questions
1. How does DeepScientist compare to other AI scientist systems in terms of performance and capabilities?
2. What are the potential limitations and challenges that DeepScientist may face in conducting scientific research?
