# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a novel interpretability method for gated neurons in transformers, focusing on the cosine similarities between input and output weights. The authors introduce the concept of "weakening neurons," which are relatively few but activate frequently and have a disproportionate impact on model behavior. They demonstrate that these neurons, primarily found in late layers, exhibit distinct patterns compared to "strengthening" neurons, which are more prevalent in early-middle layers. The paper presents a new method for conditional ablation, allowing the identification of specific activations responsible for certain behaviors. The findings are validated across nine different LLMs, highlighting the consistency of the observed patterns.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a new method for analyzing gated neurons in LLMs, focusing on cosine similarities between input and output weights, which is a unique perspective in the field of mechanistic interpretability.

2. The discovery of "weakening neurons" and their surprising behavior challenges existing assumptions about neuron functionality in transformers, providing valuable insights into model behavior.

3. The methodology is rigorously applied across nine different LLMs, demonstrating the robustness and generalizability of the findings, which adds credibility to the results.

4. The introduction of a new method for conditional ablation allows for a deeper understanding of specific neuron contributions, which is a practical advancement in interpretability research.

## Weaknesses
1. The method's reliance on cosine similarities between input and output weights may not capture the full complexity of neuron interactions within the model, potentially limiting the depth of the analysis.

2. The paper could benefit from a more detailed comparison with existing interpretability methods to highlight the novelty and effectiveness of the proposed approach.

3. While the paper identifies the importance of negative gate values, it does not provide a comprehensive analysis of how these values influence model behavior across different layers and tasks, which could be crucial for understanding the model's functionality.

## Questions
1. How do the findings on weakening neurons compare with existing methods for interpretability in LLMs? Are there similar discoveries in the literature that support or challenge the findings here?

2. Can the proposed method for conditional ablation be applied to other types of neural networks or different architectures, or is it specifically designed for transformers?

3. How do the identified patterns in neuron behavior (e.g., weakening vs. strengthening) vary across different tasks or datasets? Are there specific tasks where these patterns are more pronounced?

4. What are the implications of the discovered weakening neurons for model training and optimization? Should these findings influence the design of future LLMs?
