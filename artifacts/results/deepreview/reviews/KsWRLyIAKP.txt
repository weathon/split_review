# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a temporal graph learning framework for lead-lag detection in financial markets. The authors formulate lead-lag relationships as temporal link prediction tasks on dynamic graphs and introduce a new benchmark dataset. They evaluate various deep learning models, including LSTMs and Temporal Graph Neural Networks (TGNNs), and perform ablation studies to assess the impact of key components.

## Strengths
1. The paper provides a novel perspective on lead-lag detection by framing it as a temporal link prediction task on dynamic graphs, which allows for the capture of time-evolving relationships between financial assets.
2. The authors introduce a new benchmark dataset comprising stocks and commodities, enriched with temporal, structural, and sentiment features, which can facilitate further research in this area.
3. The paper offers a comprehensive evaluation of both positive and negative lead-lag relationships, and includes an ablation study to assess the impact of key components of the models.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed comparison with traditional statistical methods for lead-lag detection, making it difficult to assess the advantages or disadvantages of the proposed approach compared to established techniques.
2. The authors do not discuss the scalability of the proposed framework when dealing with larger datasets or more complex financial networks, which could limit its applicability in real-world scenarios.
3. The paper does not address the robustness of the models to noise or outliers in the financial time series data, which is a common challenge in financial market analysis.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed temporal graph learning framework compare to traditional statistical methods for lead-lag detection in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency?
2. Can the authors provide more insights into the scalability of the framework when applied to larger datasets or more complex financial networks?
3. How does the framework handle noise or outliers in the financial time series data, and what is the impact on the lead-lag detection performance?
