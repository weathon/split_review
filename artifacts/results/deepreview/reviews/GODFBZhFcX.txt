# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a novel framework for addressing uncertainty in multi-agent planning for embodied agents. The Planner-Composer-Evaluator (PCE) framework leverages LLMs to convert implicit assumptions in reasoning traces into a structured decision tree, enabling more effective action selection without relying heavily on communication. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of PCE across two benchmarks and three LLM backbones, showing improvements in success rates and task efficiency while maintaining comparable token usage.

## Strengths
- The paper presents a novel approach to handling uncertainty in multi-agent planning by structuring implicit assumptions into a decision tree, which is a significant departure from traditional communication-centric methods.
- The methodology is well-detailed, with clear explanations of how each module functions within the PCE framework.
- The paper includes comprehensive experiments across multiple benchmarks and LLM backbones, providing robust evidence of the framework's effectiveness.
- The user study adds value by demonstrating that PCE's communication patterns are perceived as more efficient and trustworthy by human partners.

## Weaknesses
- The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion on the scalability of the PCE framework, particularly as the number of agents or the complexity of the environment increases.
- While the paper compares PCE with communication-centric baselines, it could provide a more in-depth analysis of its performance against other uncertainty-handling techniques in multi-agent planning.

## Questions
- How does the PCE framework perform with an increasing number of agents, and what are the potential challenges in scaling the decision tree structure?
- Could the authors elaborate on how the PCE framework might be adapted for more dynamic environments where agent interactions and uncertainties are more complex?
