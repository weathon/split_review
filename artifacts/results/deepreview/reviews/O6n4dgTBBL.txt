# Review

## Summary
This paper studies the problem of stabilizing the gradient descent (GD) algorithm, by formulating it as a second-order dynamical system and using control theoretic tools. Theoretical analysis and experimental results are provided to support the proposed algorithm.

## Strengths
1. The problem of stabilizing GD is interesting and important, as GD is the most basic algorithm in deep learning.
2. The paper is clearly written, and the proposed algorithm is easy to understand.

## Weaknesses
1. The theoretical analysis in this paper only guarantees the convergence of the continuous-time dynamical system, instead of the discrete-time GD algorithm. This is a very important distinction, as the discretization process can introduce additional sources of instability, and the theoretical results in this paper do not apply to the actual GD algorithm. In fact, the authors themselves acknowledge this gap in the last section of the paper. Therefore, I think the current theoretical results are not strong enough to support the main claim of this paper.

2. The theoretical analysis in this paper is limited to the convex case, and cannot explain the stabilizing effect of the proposed algorithm in the nonconvex case. However, it is in the nonconvex case that the GD algorithm really needs stabilization, as in the convex case the classical condition of $\eta<2/L$ is sufficient to ensure convergence.

3. The experimental results in this paper are only provided for some simple toy examples, and are not sufficient to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm in practice. In particular, the authors do not provide any experimental results on real-world datasets.

## Questions
1. Can the theoretical results in this paper explain the stabilizing effect of the proposed algorithm in the nonconvex case?

2. Can the authors provide some experimental results on real-world datasets to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm in practice?
