# Review

## Summary
This paper studies the role of depth in the neural tangent kernel (NTK) for overparameterized neural networks. The main contributions are:

1. The authors derive a closed-form expression for the limiting kernel $\Theta_\infty^{(L)}$ for infinitely wide fully-connected ReLU networks with no biases.
2. They show that as the depth $L$ increases, the normalized limiting kernel $\tilde{\Theta}_\infty^{(L)}$ converges to a matrix of ones for any fixed dataset $X$.
3. The corresponding closed-form solution for the output of the neural network converges to a fixed limit.
4. The authors provide a characterization of the limiting solution using rough differential equations.
5. They demonstrate the theoretical results through empirical experiments on synthetic and MNIST datasets.

## Strengths
1. The paper provides a novel analysis of the role of depth in the NTK for overparameterized neural networks, which is an important research question in the field.
2. The authors derive a closed-form expression for the limiting kernel and demonstrate its convergence as the depth increases, which is a non-trivial result.
3. The use of rough differential equations to characterize the limiting solution is innovative and provides new insights into the behavior of neural networks with increasing depth.
4. The paper is well-structured and clearly written, with detailed proofs and explanations in the appendix.

## Weaknesses
1. The analysis is limited to fully-connected ReLU networks with no biases, which may not capture the behavior of more practical architectures with skip connections or other components.
2. The paper focuses on the limiting behavior as the depth increases, but does not provide insights into the convergence rates for finite depths, which is important for practical applications.
3. The experiments are limited to synthetic and MNIST datasets, and it would be beneficial to include more diverse and challenging datasets to test the generalizability of the findings.
4. The paper does not provide a clear interpretation of the theoretical results in terms of the generalization capabilities of the neural networks, which is a key aspect of understanding overparameterized models.

## Questions
1. Can the findings be extended to architectures with skip connections or other components? How would the limiting behavior be affected?
2. What are the convergence rates for the limiting kernel and the corresponding closed-form solution for finite depths? How do these rates affect the practical performance of neural networks?
3. How do the theoretical results relate to the generalization capabilities of the neural networks? Can the limiting behavior be linked to improved generalization or enhanced representation power?
4. How do the findings compare to existing literature on the role of depth in neural networks, such as the work by Hanin and Nica (2020)? What are the key differences and similarities?
