# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a dual ANN-to-SNN conversion framework for large language models, eliminating the need for specialized ANNs. By leveraging quantized LLMs and integer-spiking neurons, the method reduces computational costs and enhances scalability. A layer-wise calibration strategy further minimizes conversion errors, including unevenness error, while maintaining low latency. Extensive experiments on LLaMA models demonstrate competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art quantization techniques.

## Strengths
1. The dual ANN-SNN conversion framework is a novel approach that removes the need for training tailored ANNs, making it more scalable for large models.
2. The layer-wise calibration method is theoretically grounded and effectively reduces conversion errors with minimal computational and memory overhead.
3. The proposed method achieves performance comparable to leading quantization techniques, as shown through extensive experiments on LLaMA models.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed method does not seem to address the high latency issue in SNNs, which is a significant limitation for practical deployment.
2. The paper lacks a detailed comparison with other ANN-to-SNN conversion methods, particularly regarding accuracy and efficiency.
3. The proposed method may not be suitable for non-quantized LLMs, limiting its applicability to models that are not already quantized.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed method perform on other types of LLMs beyond the LLaMA model family?
2. What are the specific energy savings estimates for deploying the spiking LLMs on edge devices compared to traditional LLMs?
3. How does the layer-wise calibration method impact the overall model performance in terms of accuracy and latency?
4. Can the proposed method be extended to other types of neural networks beyond LLMs, such as CNNs or vision transformers?
5. How does the method handle the trade-off between accuracy and latency, and are there any strategies to further optimize this balance?
