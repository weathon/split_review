# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a novel framework for understanding forgetting in machine learning algorithms. The authors propose a definition of forgetting based on the consistency of a learner's predictive distribution over future experiences. They argue that forgetting occurs when a learner's predictive distribution changes in an inconsistent way, indicating a loss of previously acquired knowledge. The paper also introduces a measure of an algorithm's propensity to forget and validates the theory through experiments across various learning settings, including classification, regression, generative modeling, and reinforcement learning. The authors claim that their theory provides a principled understanding of forgetting and lays the foundation for analyzing and improving the information retention capabilities of general learning algorithms.

## Strengths
- The paper introduces a novel framework for understanding forgetting in machine learning algorithms, which is a significant contribution to the field.
- The theory is algorithm- and task-agnostic, making it broadly applicable across different learning settings.
- The paper provides a principled understanding of forgetting, distinguishing it from task performance and backward transfer.
- The authors validate their theory through comprehensive experiments across various learning tasks, including classification, regression, and generative modeling.
- The paper offers practical insights into the trade-off between forgetting and training efficiency, which can inform the development of future learning algorithms.

## Weaknesses
- The paper does not provide a clear and actionable definition of forgetting. While the authors propose a definition based on the consistency of predictive distributions, it is not immediately clear how this definition can be operationalized in practice. The authors should provide a more detailed and concrete definition of forgetting, along with a clear methodology for measuring it.
- The paper does not provide a comprehensive review of existing literature on forgetting in machine learning. While the authors mention some existing studies, they do not provide a thorough discussion of how their framework relates to or builds upon previous work. A more comprehensive review of the literature would help to contextualize the contribution of this paper.
- The paper does not provide a clear explanation of how the proposed measure of propensity to forget can be used in practice. The authors should provide a more detailed explanation of how this measure can be used to evaluate and compare different learning algorithms, and how it can inform the development of new algorithms.
- The paper does not provide a thorough discussion of the limitations of the proposed framework. The authors should clearly outline the scope and boundary of the validity of their theory, and discuss potential limitations and challenges in applying it to real-world learning algorithms.

## Questions
- How can the definition of forgetting based on predictive consistency be operationalized in practice? What are the concrete steps that can be taken to measure forgetting in a given learning algorithm?
- How does the proposed framework relate to existing literature on forgetting in machine learning? Can the authors provide a more thorough discussion of how their framework builds upon or differs from previous work?
- How can the measure of propensity to forget be used to evaluate and compare different learning algorithms? Can the authors provide a more detailed explanation of its practical utility?
- What are the potential limitations and challenges of applying the proposed framework to real-world learning algorithms? How can these limitations be addressed in future work?
