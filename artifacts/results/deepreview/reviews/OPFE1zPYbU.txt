# Review

## Summary
The paper investigates the limitations of diffusion models in high-dimensional data generation, challenging the conventional belief that these models can effectively learn statistical quantities of the underlying probability distribution. The authors argue that in high-dimensional sparse scenarios, the diffusion model's objective function degrades, making it difficult to learn essential statistical properties. They propose a new inference framework that simplifies the complex statistical processes into a more intuitive and consistent form, focusing on predicting the original data sample from noisy samples.

## Strengths
1. The paper provides a detailed analysis of the objective function and inference methods of diffusion models, offering a deeper understanding of their limitations in high-dimensional settings.
2. The authors introduce a novel inference framework that unifies most existing inference methods, providing a simpler and more intuitive perspective on the inference process.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper's structure is not well-organized, making it difficult for readers to follow the arguments and understand the contributions. The introduction is too lengthy, and the authors should provide a more concise overview of the paper's content and contributions. Additionally, the background section includes excessive mathematical details that are not directly related to the paper's main arguments. The authors should only include necessary mathematical formulations and move the rest to the appendix. The authors should clearly state the paper's main contributions and provide a roadmap for the paper's structure.
2. The paper's argument is not well-supported by the provided evidence. While the authors claim that diffusion models do not learn statistical quantities in high-dimensional settings, they only provide limited empirical evidence through tables showing the degradation phenomenon. The authors should provide more comprehensive empirical evidence, such as visualizations of the sampling process, to support their argument.
3. The paper's contribution is not significant enough to warrant acceptance. While the authors propose a new inference framework that unifies existing methods, they do not provide any new insights or perspectives on the underlying mechanisms of diffusion models. The authors should provide a more detailed analysis of how their framework differs from existing methods and how it leads to better performance.
