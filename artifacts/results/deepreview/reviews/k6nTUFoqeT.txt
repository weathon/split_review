# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces an efficient autoregressive VLA modeling framework that integrates a learnable action tokenizer (FASTERVQ) with an autoregressive policy (FASTERVLA). The action tokenizer encodes action chunks as single-channel images, capturing global spatio-temporal dependencies while maintaining a high compression ratio. The autoregressive policy is built upon this tokenizer with block-wise autoregressive decoding and a lightweight action expert, achieving faster inference and higher task performance. Extensive experiments across simulated and real-world benchmarks demonstrate that FASTERVQ delivers superior reconstruction quality, high token utilization, and strong cross-task and cross-embodiment generalization. FASTERVLA further improves overall capability, surpassing previous state-of-the-art VLA models in both inference speed and task performance.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The paper introduces a novel method for action tokenization, which is a significant contribution to the field of robotics.
3. The paper provides a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed method, demonstrating its effectiveness across various benchmarks and tasks.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational complexity of the proposed method, which could be a limitation for real-time applications.
2. The paper does not discuss the potential for overfitting, especially given the high compression ratio and the complexity of the model.
3. The paper could benefit from a more detailed comparison with other state-of-the-art methods in the field, especially those that also claim to achieve a balance between reconstruction fidelity and inference efficiency.

## Questions
1. How does the computational complexity of FASTER compare to other state-of-the-art methods in the field?
2. What are the potential limitations of the proposed method, and how can they be addressed in future work?
3. Can you provide more details on the potential for overfitting and how you mitigated it in your experiments?
