# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a method to incorporate the class covariance matrix into the process of finding the maximum margin hyperplane in SVM. It argues that the margin of the maximum margin hyperplane should depend on the class variance. The proposed method uses Cholesky decomposition to estimate the class covariance matrix from the training data and then uses this matrix to transform the data into a Euclidean space where a standard SVM is trained. The method is evaluated on 5 binary classification tasks and compared to standard SVM with various kernel functions.

## Strengths
The paper addresses an important problem of extending SVM to non-Euclidean spaces.

## Weaknesses
The paper has several major weaknesses. 

The writing is poor. The paper is full of vague statements and unclear descriptions. Here are some examples:
- The paper claims that the margin should depend on the class variance, but does not explain why. The only explanation provided is that "the entire dataset (through variance) should influence the calculation of decision boundary". This is a vague statement that does not explain why the margin should depend on the variance.
- The paper claims that "SVM is valid in Euclidean space only", but does not explain why. The only explanation provided is that "the equation of the linear SVM hyperplanes for both the decision boundary and margin hyperplanes, and the margin calculations, are derived from the Euclidean distance formula". This is a vague statement that does not explain why SVM is only valid in Euclidean space.
- The paper claims that "the margin of the classifier is dependent on the intra-class data covariance (variance is essentially covariance of a random variable with itself)", but does not explain why. 
- The paper does not explain how the SM algorithm works. It only provides a list of steps without any explanations.
- The paper does not explain how the convergence of the SM algorithm is checked.

The paper also contains several mathematical errors:
- It claims that "the new vector space has an inner product, hence it is an inner product space", but this is not true. The space with the Mahalanobis distance is an inner product space, but the space with the Euclidean distance is not an inner product space.
- It claims that "the margin maximization problem in the input space for y = 1 becomes", but the problem does not become what is shown in equation (10). The problem should be minimizing the norm of $\theta$ subject to the margin constraint, which is different from what is shown in equation (10).

The experimental evaluation is also problematic. It only compares the proposed method to standard SVM with various kernel functions, but does not compare it to any of the existing methods that incorporate class variance into SVM, such as the ones cited in the paper. This makes it impossible to assess the effectiveness of the proposed method compared to existing methods.
