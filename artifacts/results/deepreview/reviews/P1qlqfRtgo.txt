# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a comparison of three neural network architectures for modeling thermal explosions in hydrogen-oxygen mixtures. The authors generated a dataset using a reduced 11-species kinetic mechanism under various initial conditions. The architectures compared are a standard multi-layer perceptron (MLP), a DeepONet-inspired model, and a U-Net-style residual network. The results show that the U-Net architecture outperformed the other models in terms of mean squared error (MSE) and standard deviation (STD) on the test set.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a thorough comparison of three neural network architectures for modeling thermal explosions in hydrogen-oxygen mixtures. 
2. The authors generated a detailed dataset using a reduced 11-species kinetic mechanism under various initial conditions, which adds value to the study.

## Weaknesses
1. The study's scope is too narrow. The authors only investigate a specific chemical reaction (thermal explosion in hydrogen-oxygen mixtures). The findings may not be applicable to other reactions or broader combustion problems. The authors should consider extending their approach to other combustion regimes, such as non-explosive hydrogen-oxygen mixtures or other fuel-air mixtures. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the effectiveness of the proposed neural network architectures in combustion modeling.
2. The paper lacks novelty. The authors only compare three existing architectures (MLP, DeepONet, and U-Net) without introducing any new techniques or modifications specific to the combustion problem. The authors could consider proposing novel modifications to the architectures or integrating physical knowledge about combustion into the neural network design.
3. The authors do not compare their results with any existing benchmarks or state-of-the-art methods in combustion modeling. This makes it difficult to assess the significance of their contributions. The authors should consider comparing their results with existing combustion models or CFD codes to validate the accuracy and efficiency of their proposed approach.
4. The authors do not provide any ablation studies or sensitivity analyses to investigate the impact of different hyperparameters or design choices in their neural network architectures. This could help to better understand the effectiveness of each component and improve the robustness of the proposed approach. The authors should consider conducting ablation studies on different hyperparameters, such as the number of layers, hidden units, learning rates, or activation functions, and analyzing their impact on the performance of the model.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed approach compare to existing state-of-the-art methods in combustion modeling? The authors should provide a detailed comparison with relevant literature to demonstrate the significance of their contributions.
2. What are the limitations of the proposed approach, and how could it be extended to other combustion problems or different fuel-air mixtures? The authors should discuss potential challenges and provide suggestions for future work to address these limitations.
3. How sensitive is the performance of the proposed approach to different hyperparameters or design choices? The authors should conduct ablation studies to investigate the impact of various components and provide guidelines for selecting optimal settings.
