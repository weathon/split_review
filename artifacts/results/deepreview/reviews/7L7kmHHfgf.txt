# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces PIRN, a prototype-driven reconstruction framework designed to address challenges in few-shot multimodal anomaly detection (MAD). PIRN leverages a compact set of learnable prototypes to capture diverse normal patterns and constrain feature reconstruction. The framework incorporates three key innovations: Balanced Prototype Assignment (BPA) to ensure uniform prototype utilization and prevent codebook collapse; Adaptive Prototype Refinement (APR) to dynamically expand the model’s knowledge of unseen normal variations during inference; and Multimodal Normality Communication (MNC) to exchange high-level normal cues between modalities via gated cross-attention. Extensive experiments on benchmarks such as MVTec 3D-AD, Eyecandies, and Real-IAD demonstrate that PIRN consistently outperforms existing baselines in challenging few-shot settings.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a novel approach to multimodal anomaly detection (MAD) by introducing a prototype-driven reconstruction framework that addresses the challenges of few-shot scenarios. This is an innovative contribution to the field.
2. The paper is well-structured and clearly written. The authors provide a detailed explanation of their methodology, including the key innovations of Balanced Prototype Assignment (BPA), Adaptive Prototype Refinement (APR), and Multimodal Normality Communication (MNC). The figures and tables are informative and support the textual content well.
3. The proposed PIRN framework is a significant contribution to the field of multimodal anomaly detection, especially in few-shot settings. The ability to outperform existing baselines on challenging benchmarks is a strong indicator of the quality and significance of the work.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper could benefit from a more detailed comparison with existing methods, particularly in terms of highlighting the specific limitations of current approaches that PIRN addresses. This would strengthen the argument for the necessity of the proposed framework.
2. While the paper provides extensive experimental results, it would be beneficial to include a more detailed analysis of the performance of the individual components of the PIRN framework. This would provide deeper insights into the contribution of each component to the overall performance.
