# Review

## Summary
The paper addresses the challenge of deploying vision-and-language navigation (VLN) agents in diverse scenes and environments while preventing catastrophic forgetting. It introduces the all-day multi-scenes lifelong VLN (AML-VLN) problem and proposes Tucker Adaptation (TuKA), a method that uses high-order tensors and Tucker decomposition to manage multi-hierarchical navigation knowledge. The authors also present a VLN agent, AllDayWalker, which demonstrates superior performance over state-of-the-art baselines in lifelong VLN.

## Strengths
1. The introduction of Tucker Adaptation (TuKA) represents an innovative approach to handling multi-hierarchical knowledge in VLN, leveraging high-order tensors for more effective knowledge representation and decoupling.
2. The development of AllDayWalker, a VLN agent capable of lifelong learning across multiple scenes and environments, is a significant contribution, showcasing robust performance and adaptability.
3. The formalization of the AML-VLN problem is a valuable addition to the VLN research community, providing a framework for addressing real-world challenges in deploying VLN agents.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks detailed information on the experimental setup, particularly regarding the number of seeds used for the main experiments. This omission may impact the reproducibility and reliability of the reported results.
2. The evaluation is conducted in a limited number of environments, which may not fully capture the diversity and complexity of real-world scenarios. Expanding the evaluation to include a wider range of environments would provide a more comprehensive assessment of the proposed method's effectiveness.
3. The paper does not adequately address the scalability of the proposed method when applied to more complex and larger-scale environments. Discussing the potential limitations and challenges in scaling the approach would strengthen the paper's contributions.

## Questions
1. Could you provide more details on the number of seeds used for the main experiments? How does varying the number of seeds impact the reported results?
2. How does the proposed method perform in a wider range of environments that were not included in the evaluation? Are there any specific environments where you anticipate the method might struggle?
3. Can you provide insights into the scalability of your approach when applied to larger-scale and more complex environments? What are the potential challenges you foresee?
