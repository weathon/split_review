# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces Long-tailed Test-Time Adaptation (L-TTA), a method designed to address test sets with imbalanced class distributions in Vision-Language Models (VLMs). L-TTA incorporates three key components: Synergistic Prototypes (SyPs), Rebalancing Shortcuts (RSs), and Balanced Entropy Minimization (BEM). These mechanisms enhance tail class representation, achieve dynamic adaptation, and improve entropy minimization by focusing on uncertain classes. Experimental results demonstrate that L-TTA outperforms existing methods across various benchmarks, showing significant robustness and efficiency in handling long-tailed distributions.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.

2. The paper introduces a novel approach to address the long-tail distribution problem in test-time adaptation for VLMs.

3. The paper provides a comprehensive set of experiments, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed method.

## Weaknesses
1. The motivation is not clear. The authors claim that "text embeddings themselves carry biases from pre-training" and "Applying unimodal LT-TTA methods to VLMs progressively amplifies the inherent mismatch between visual and textual representations." However, the proposed method does not seem to address these issues.

2. The authors claim that the proposed method can address the long-tail distribution problem. However, the proposed method does not seem to be specifically designed for this purpose. It would be beneficial to provide more detailed explanations of how the proposed method solves this problem.

3. The authors claim that the proposed method can address the modality-bias amplification problem. However, the proposed method does not seem to be specifically designed for this purpose. It would be beneficial to provide more detailed explanations of how the proposed method solves this problem.

4. The paper lacks an analysis of the learned prototypes.

5. The paper lacks an analysis of the learned Rebalancing Shortcuts.

6. The paper lacks a comparison of the computational complexity of the proposed method with existing methods.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed method address the issues of text-induced tail erosion and modality-bias amplification?

2. What is the rationale behind the design of the Synergistic Prototypes, and how do they contribute to the overall performance of the method?

3. What is the rationale behind the design of the Rebalancing Shortcuts, and how do they contribute to the overall performance of the method?

4. What is the computational complexity of the proposed method, and how does it compare to existing methods?

5. Can the proposed method be applied to other types of models, such as text-only models or image-only models?
