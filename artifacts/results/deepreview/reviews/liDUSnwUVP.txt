# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a history-guided deep compartmental model (HG-DCM) for early-stage pandemic forecasting. The authors address the challenge of sparse data in emerging pandemics by leveraging a deep learning architecture that integrates historical pandemic data with epidemiological and demographic metadata to infer robust parameters for compartmental forecasting. The proposed method is evaluated on COVID-19 forecasting tasks, demonstrating improved stability and reduced overfitting compared to standard compartmental models and data-isolated deep learning approaches.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces an innovative approach to early-stage pandemic forecasting by leveraging historical data through a deep learning framework. The integration of compartmental modeling with a deep learning backbone is a novel contribution that addresses the limitations of traditional compartmental models in the context of sparse data.
2. The authors provide a comprehensive dataset that includes time-series case and death data, as well as associated pandemic- and country-level metadata, from major global outbreaks since 1990. This dataset is a valuable resource for the research community and contributes to the transparency and reproducibility of the proposed method.
3. The paper is well-structured, with a clear and logical flow of ideas. The authors effectively communicate the motivation, methodology, and results of their research, making it accessible to a wide audience. The use of figures and tables to illustrate key concepts and results enhances the clarity of the presentation.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks a detailed discussion on the computational complexity of the proposed method. Given the increasing emphasis on real-time forecasting and the potential scalability issues of deep learning models, an analysis of the computational requirements and scalability of HG-DCM is crucial for practical applications.
2. The paper does not provide a thorough comparison with a wide range of state-of-the-art methods in pandemic forecasting. While the authors compare HG-DCM with the DELPHI model and two deep learning-only models, there are numerous other approaches that could be considered, such as reinforcement learning, Bayesian inference, and hybrid models. A more comprehensive evaluation would strengthen the claims made in the paper.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the sensitivity of the proposed method to its hyperparameters. Understanding how different hyperparameter settings affect the performance of HG-DCM is crucial for practical applications and for ensuring robustness across different scenarios.
4. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the interpretability of the parameters and forecasts generated by HG-DCM. While the authors claim that their method produces interpretable parameters, a more thorough analysis and discussion of the interpretability of the model's outputs would enhance the understanding of its strengths and limitations.

## Questions
1. Can you provide more details on the computational complexity of your method and how it scales with the size of the dataset?
2. Can you provide a more comprehensive comparison with a wider range of state-of-the-art methods in pandemic forecasting, including reinforcement learning, Bayesian inference, and hybrid models?
3. Can you provide a detailed analysis of the sensitivity of your method to its hyperparameters and how they affect its performance?
4. Can you provide a more detailed analysis and discussion of the interpretability of the parameters and forecasts generated by your method?
