# Review

## Summary
This paper explores the scaling law of training loss curve (TLC) in LLM pretraining. Building upon previous work on TLC scaling, this study focuses on practical scaling recipes that consider hyperparameter scaling across model width, depth, learning rate, batch size, and weight decay. The authors introduce a novel LLM family called Celerity, demonstrating that by fixing the tokens-per-parameter (TPP) ratio and optimizing the AdamW timescale ($\tau$) for each TPP setting, TLCs can effectively collapse onto a single, predictable curve. This collapse is shown to be a reliable indicator of compute-efficient training and enables applications such as early diagnostics of training issues and enhanced hyperparameter tuning. The paper also proposes a functional form for normalized TLCs, allowing for early stopping in large-scale hyperparameter tuning.

## Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The empirical study is comprehensive and well-designed.
- The empirical findings are novel and have the potential to significantly improve the efficiency of LLM pretraining.

## Weaknesses
- The paper lacks a theoretical analysis explaining why fixing $\tau$ and TPP leads to TLC collapse.
- The proposed predictor for normalized TLCs relies on manually selected control variables, which may limit its generalizability to other models and training settings.

## Questions
- Why do you use the normalized timescale $\tau$ instead of the "real" timescale $\tau_{\text{iter}}$? Is it because the latter is not scale-invariant?
- How do you determine the "corresponding" $\tau$ for different TPP values in step 2 of the procedure for predicting the normalized universal TLCs? Is there a general power-law relationship between $\tau$ and TPP?
