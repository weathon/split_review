# Review

## Summary
The authors introduce a new type of jailbreak attack that can bypass safety guardrails of LLMs. The attack does not have a specific target, but rather prompts the model to generate an unsafe prompt and answer itself. The attack is successful against a variety of state-of-the-art models.

## Strengths
* The proposed attack is effective against a wide range of models
* The authors perform a wide range of ablations to better understand the attack
* The attack does not rely on a specific harmful topic, but is more general

## Weaknesses
* The presentation of the paper could be improved, specifically the use of figures
* The attack is not well motivated
* It would be interesting to see how the attack performs against defenses

## Questions
Thank you for submitting this interesting paper to ICLR! I have a few comments and questions that I would like to discuss with the authors:

* The presentation of the paper could be improved. Specifically, Figures 1 and 2 are hard to parse and understand. I think the authors should consider redesigning these figures to make the information more clear. Figure 5 has a lot of clutter and the authors should consider removing the lines for models that are not of interest (e.g., o1, o3, DeepSeek R1). Figure 6 is hard to read because the colors are very similar and the authors should consider a different visualization method. It would be helpful if the authors could include the model names in the captions of the figures so that they are easier to understand.
* The attack is not well motivated. The authors should consider adding a discussion on why an attacker would want to use this attack compared to a targeted attack. The authors mention that the attack is "less trivial" than other jailbreaks, but I think the authors should consider adding a more thorough discussion on this.
* The authors should consider evaluating the attack against existing defenses. This would help to better understand the risk posed by the attack.
