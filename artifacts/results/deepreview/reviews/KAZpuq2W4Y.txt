# Review

## Summary
The authors proposed a new approach, Higher-Order Multi-Instance Learning (HOMIL), for whole-slide image (WSI) classification. HOMIL is based on multi-instance learning (MIL) and extends it by incorporating second-order moments and adaptive clustering. The second-order moments are used to capture the variability and structural relationships among patches, while the adaptive clustering is employed to reduce computational cost and enhance classification accuracy. The authors evaluated HOMIL on two WSI datasets, CAMELYON16 and TCGA-NSCLC, and showed that it outperformed several state-of-the-art MIL methods.

## Strengths
- The authors proposed an extension of MIL by incorporating second-order moments and adaptive clustering.
- The authors evaluated the proposed method on two WSI datasets and showed that it outperformed several state-of-the-art MIL methods.
- The authors conducted an ablation study to evaluate the contribution of the different components of the proposed method.

## Weaknesses
- The proposed method is based on MIL, which assumes that the instances (patches) are independent and identically distributed. However, this assumption may not hold for WSI data, where the patches are highly correlated and form complex patterns. The proposed method does not take into account the spatial relationships between the patches, which may limit its ability to capture the full complexity of the WSI data.
- The proposed method relies heavily on the CONCH model for patch feature extraction. The authors did not compare the performance of HOMIL with different feature extraction methods, such as other pre-trained models or handcrafted features. It is unclear how the choice of feature extraction method affects the performance of HOMIL.
- The proposed method uses PCA for dimensionality reduction, which may lose important information in the high-dimensional feature vectors. The authors did not compare the performance of HOMIL with different dimensionality reduction techniques, such as t-SNE or UMAP. It is unclear how the choice of dimensionality reduction technique affects the performance of HOMIL.

## Questions
- How does the proposed method capture the spatial relationships between the patches?
- How does the choice of feature extraction method affect the performance of HOMIL?
- How does the choice of dimensionality reduction technique affect the performance of HOMIL?
