# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes the Copy-Paste method to enhance the contextual faithfulness of LLMs by directly embedding contextual fragments into responses. The authors observed that a high copying degree from the context to the response is inversely correlated with hallucination density, leading them to develop a two-stage pipeline. In the first stage, they use copy-paste prompting to generate a high-copying response. In the second stage, they train the model to prefer such responses using DPO. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of this method.

## Strengths
1. The proposed method is simple and effective, and it is supported by a clear motivation.
2. The authors conducted extensive experiments to demonstrate the effectiveness of the method.
3. The authors also provided an analysis of the underlying mechanisms of the method, which is insightful.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed method may not be suitable for open-ended generation tasks, such as writing a scientific report or a story, as it can only be applied to extractive tasks.
2. The proposed method may increase the length of the generated response, which can lead to additional inference costs.
3. The proposed method may not be suitable for scenarios where the retrieved context is very long, such as the RAFT dataset.

## Questions
1. How does the method perform on the RAFT dataset?
2. How does the method perform on open-ended generation tasks?
3. How does the method compare to the CoCoLex method in terms of response length and inference time?
4. How does the method perform on the RAG benchmark?
