# Review

## Summary
The authors present Latent Stochastic Interpolants (LSI), a novel framework for generative modeling in the latent space of a VAE, allowing for joint optimization of the encoder, decoder, and latent generative model. LSI addresses the limitation of simple priors in traditional diffusion models and reduces computational demands by operating in a lower-dimensional latent space. The framework is trained using a continuous-time Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) objective, providing a principled and scalable approach. Experiments on the ImageNet benchmark demonstrate LSI's competitive generative performance and efficiency, offering a promising alternative to existing methods in the field.

## Strengths
- The paper is well written and easy to follow.
- The proposed method is novel and interesting. It is well motivated and addresses a relevant problem.
- The experimental evaluation is thorough and supports the claims made by the authors.

## Weaknesses
- The experiments are only conducted on ImageNet. It would be interesting to see how the model performs on other datasets.
- The paper could benefit from a more extensive discussion of the limitations of the proposed approach.

## Questions
- How does the performance of LSI vary with different choices of the prior distribution $p_0(z_0)$?
- Can the proposed method be extended to incorporate multiple observations $x_{t_i}$ at arbitrary times $t_i$?
- How does LSI compare to other methods in terms of training time and convergence rate?
