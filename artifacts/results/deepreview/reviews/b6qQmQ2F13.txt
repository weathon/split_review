# Review

## Summary
The paper explores the memory-performance trade-off in reasoning models, focusing on how to allocate memory between model weights and the KV cache to maximize performance under various constraints. The authors conduct an empirical study on the Qwen3 model family across different model sizes (0.6B to 32B), weight precisions (4-bit to 16-bit), generation lengths, parallel scaling, and KV cache compression strategies. The study covers over 1,700 scenarios, evaluating the impact of different factors on accuracy and memory footprint across four challenging benchmarks: AIME25, GPQA-Diamond, LiveCodeBench, and MATH500.

## Strengths
- The paper provides a comprehensive analysis of the memory-performance trade-off in reasoning models, covering various factors such as model size, weight precision, generation length, parallel scaling, and KV cache compression. The extensive experimental study, spanning over 1,700 scenarios, offers valuable insights into the behavior of reasoning models and informs practical guidelines for deploying them.
- The paper is well-structured, with each section building on the previous one to present a coherent narrative. The use of figures and tables to illustrate key points is effective, and the overall writing is clear and concise.
- The paper's contributions are significant for both academic and practical reasons. It advances the understanding of memory optimization strategies in reasoning models, which is crucial for the deployment of large language models in resource-constrained environments. The findings provide practical guidelines that can inform the design and deployment of future reasoning models, making it a valuable resource for the research community.

## Weaknesses
- The paper's focus on a single model family (Qwen3) and a limited number of benchmarks (AIME25, GPQA-Diamond, LiveCodeBench, and MATH500) may limit the generalizability of the findings. While the authors acknowledge this limitation and provide some results on other models, a more comprehensive evaluation across a wider range of models and tasks would strengthen the paper's claims.
- The paper primarily focuses on weight-only quantization andKV cache quantization, but does not explore other compression techniques such as weight-activation quantization or mixed-precision approaches. Including these methods in the analysis would provide a more complete picture of the memory-performance trade-off.
- The paper primarily focuses on the Qwen3 model family and a limited number of benchmarks. While the authors acknowledge this limitation and provide some results on other models, a more comprehensive evaluation across a wider range of models and tasks would strengthen the paper's claims.

## Questions
- How do the findings generalize to reasoning models beyond the Qwen3 family? Have you considered evaluating the proposed strategies on other models to assess their applicability?
- The paper focuses on weight-only quantization and KV cache quantization. How do weight-activation quantization or mixed-precision approaches fit into the proposed framework, and could they offer better memory-performance trade-offs in certain scenarios?
- The paper primarily focuses on a limited number of benchmarks. How do the proposed strategies perform on other challenging reasoning tasks not covered in the paper?
