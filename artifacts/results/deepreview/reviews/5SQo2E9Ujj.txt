# Review

## Summary
The paper investigates curriculum learning in the context of goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (GCRL). Instead of uniformly sampling all goals, the authors propose to bias the sampling towards underachieved goals. They conduct experiments in GridWorld using universal value function approximators (UVFAs) and potential-based reward shaping. The results show that the proposed curriculum learning approach improves goal coverage, reduces approximation error, and enhances success rates, particularly for difficult edge goals.

## Strengths
The paper introduces a novel perspective on curriculum learning by framing it as selective data acquisition in GCRL. This reframing highlights the structural role of curricula in reshaping the training data distribution, which can have implications for the inductive biases of the learned function approximator. The authors conduct experiments in GridWorld using UVFAs and potential-based reward shaping, providing empirical evidence for the effectiveness of their approach.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper's contributions are not clearly articulated. While the authors claim that their approach improves the success rate on difficult edge goals, the results show only a marginal improvement. Furthermore, the paper lacks a comprehensive comparison with existing methods, making it difficult to assess the true value of the proposed approach.

2. The paper suffers from poor writing quality, with numerous grammatical errors and typos. For example, in the abstract, the phrase "using universal value function approximators (UVFAs) with potential-based reward shaping in GridWorld" is awkwardly phrased. Additionally, the term "underachieved goals" is not clearly defined, leading to confusion. These issues detract from the overall clarity and professionalism of the paper.

3. The paper's approach is overly simplistic and lacks novelty. The authors propose a curriculum learning strategy that biases sampling towards underachieved goals, but this idea has been previously explored in the literature. For example, the concept of focusing on underachieved goals is similar to the idea of "novelty-seeking" in intrinsic motivation, which encourages agents to explore goals that have not been achieved before [1]. The paper does not offer a significant advancement beyond these existing ideas.

[1] Curious Exploration Networks, Deepak Pathak, Pulkit Agrawal, Alexei A. Efros, Trevor Darrell

## Questions
1. Can the authors provide a more detailed explanation of how their approach differs from existing methods that focus on underachieved goals or novelty-seeking in intrinsic motivation? What specific advancements does their approach offer?

2. Can the authors provide a more comprehensive comparison with existing methods to better highlight the contributions of their approach?
