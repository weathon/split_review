# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces SWIREASONING, a training-free framework designed to improve reasoning in large language models (LLMs) by dynamically switching between explicit and latent reasoning modes. This approach aims to balance exploration and exploitation, addressing challenges such as the diffusion of probability mass and overthinking in latent reasoning. By using entropy trends to guide mode transitions and capping the number of switches, SWIREASONING effectively improves accuracy and token efficiency across various benchmarks in mathematics, STEM, coding, and general reasoning tasks.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The proposed method is simple and effective.
3. The experiments are comprehensive and demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed method relies on entropy to trigger mode switching, but the relationship between entropy and confidence is not well established. It is unclear why entropy can serve as a reliable indicator of confidence.
2. The proposed method introduces several hyperparameters, such as $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $C_{max}$. However, the paper does not discuss how to tune these hyperparameters in practice.
3. The paper does not provide a theoretical analysis of the proposed method. It is unclear why the proposed method can improve the performance of LLMs.

## Questions
1. How to determine the optimal values of the hyperparameters in practice?
2. Can the proposed method be generalized to other LLMs?
