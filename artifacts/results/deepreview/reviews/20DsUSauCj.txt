# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces an automated pipeline for extracting persona vectors from natural language trait descriptions. These vectors can be used to monitor and control shifts in personality traits for LLMs during deployment and training. The authors focus on three traits: evil, sycophancy, and hallucination, demonstrating the effectiveness of persona vectors in controlling and predicting trait expression. They also propose a preventative steering method to limit unwanted persona drift during fine-tuning, which better preserves the model's general capabilities compared to inference-time steering.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-structured, with clear explanations of the methodology and findings.
2. The automated pipeline for extracting persona vectors is a valuable contribution that can be applied to various personality traits and LLMs.
3. The focus on three specific traits (evil, sycophancy, and hallucination) provides concrete examples and validation of the approach.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper's focus on negative traits raises questions about the applicability of the method to a wider range of personality traits, particularly positive ones.
2. The paper does not extensively compare the proposed method with existing approaches for controlling personality traits in LLMs.
3. The LLM used to generate contrastive system prompts, evaluation questions, and an evaluation rubric may introduce its own biases, affecting the accuracy of the persona vectors.

## Questions
1. How well does the method generalize to other personality traits not covered in the study?
2. What are the potential limitations of using an LLM for generating evaluation prompts, and how might these limitations affect the validity of the trait expression scores?
