# Review

## Summary
This paper presents Vidar, a method that uses a pre-trained video diffusion model as a prior for robot manipulation tasks. Vidar first pre-trains a video diffusion model on large-scale robotic videos from different embodiments, then fine-tunes it on a small amount of robot-specific data. It also proposes a Masked Inverse Dynamics Model (MIDM) to map video to action. Vidar achieves state-of-the-art results on the RoboTwin benchmark and generalizes well to unseen tasks and backgrounds.

## Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The paper conducts extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world settings.

## Weaknesses
- **Unclear motivation**: The paper argues that existing VLA models require large-scale, task-specific datasets. However, recent methods like RT-2, OpenVLA, and RDT-1B leverage large-scale internet data and require no task-specific data. Moreover, these VLA models are trained with multi-task loss, enabling them to generalize to unseen tasks. The authors should compare Vidar with these methods and highlight the advantages of using a video diffusion model as a prior for robot manipulation tasks.
- **Lack of novelty**: The proposed method appears to be a straightforward application of video diffusion models to robot manipulation tasks. The paper should clarify the key technical challenges and innovations in this application.
- **Inaccurate statements**: The paper claims that "Vidu 2.0 (Bao et al., 2024) for the more diverse and challenging real-world tests." However, Vidu 2.0 has only been tested in simulation environments. The authors should correct this statement and explain how Vidu 2.0 was adapted for real-world experiments.
- **Limited generalization ability**: The paper evaluates the video generation quality of the original Vidu 2.0 model and the pre-trained Vidu 2.0 in Table 3. However, the pre-trained Vidu 2.0 model still performs poorly on the unseen target domain. This raises concerns about the generalization ability of the pre-trained video diffusion model. The authors should provide more analysis and explanations on why the pre-trained model can enhance video generation quality in the unseen domain.
- **Missing details**: The paper does not provide details on how the video diffusion model is adapted for robot manipulation tasks. For example, how is the video diffusion model conditioned on language instructions and robot embodiments? How are the generated videos aligned with the robot's action space? The authors should provide more details on these aspects.
- **Real-world experiments**: The paper does not provide real-world experiment videos. It would be helpful to include videos showing the real-world experiments to better evaluate the method's performance and generalization ability.
