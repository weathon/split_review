# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a method for language-based audio retrieval, which is a task to retrieve audio recordings from a database based on a given text query. The proposed method is based on a dual encoder architecture that separately encodes audio and text and aligns them in a joint embedding space. The authors propose three techniques to improve the performance of the model: (1) soft-label distillation from an ensemble of retrieval teachers, (2) LLM-driven caption augmentation (back-translation and caption mix for mixed audio), and (3) cluster-guided auxiliary classification. The proposed method is evaluated on the CLOTHO dataset and achieves a mAP@16 of 46.6 on the development test split. The authors also show that a weighted ensemble of models can further improve the performance.

## Strengths
1. The paper proposes a novel method for language-based audio retrieval that combines soft-label distillation, LLM-driven caption augmentation, and cluster-guided auxiliary classification.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the individual components of the proposed method and how they contribute to the overall performance improvement. For example, it would be helpful to see an ablation study that shows the impact of each component on the model's performance.
2. The paper does not provide a comparison of the proposed method with other state-of-the-art methods for language-based audio retrieval. This makes it difficult to assess the significance of the proposed method and how it compares to other existing approaches.
