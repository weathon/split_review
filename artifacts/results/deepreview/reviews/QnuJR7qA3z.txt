# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a framework for replacing various non-linearities in a model with a single, unified non-linearity. This is motivated by the goal of having hardware-friendly models that can be implemented with shared hardware blocks, reducing the hardware footprint. The authors propose a three-stage pipeline for training the unified non-linearity. They evaluate their method on four models and seven non-linearities.

## Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The proposed method is simple yet effective.
- The problem of hardware efficiency is important.

## Weaknesses
- The authors do not compare their method to prior work on efficient non-linearities. For example, [1] proposes a hardware-efficient non-linearity that is also based on LUTs. 
- The authors do not provide any latency numbers. Instead, they estimate the area and power consumption of the non-linearities. This is problematic because the area and power consumption are heavily dependent on the specific hardware implementation, and it is unclear how the proposed non-linearities will perform in real hardware. For example, the power consumption of the LUT-based implementation in [1] is significantly lower than the power consumption of the proposed non-linearity.
- The authors do not provide any real hardware results, such as measured latency on an FPGA or a GPU. This makes it difficult to assess the practicality of the proposed method.
- The authors do not provide a detailed description of the hardware implementation. Specifically, it is unclear how the LUTs are implemented and how the inputs are routed to the LUTs.

[1] Huang et al., "Hardware-Efficient Accurate Approximation of Deep Neural Network Non-linearities," ICCAD 2019.

## Questions
- How does the proposed method compare to prior work on efficient non-linearities?
- Can the authors provide real hardware results, such as measured latency on an FPGA or a GPU?
- Can the authors provide a more detailed description of the hardware implementation?
