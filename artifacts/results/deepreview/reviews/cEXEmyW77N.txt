# Review

## Summary
This paper explores the differences between citation graphs generated by LLMs and those created by humans. Using a dataset of 10,000 papers, the authors constructed paired citation graphs, comparing the structures and embeddings of LLM-generated references against ground truth. The study finds that while LLM-generated citation graphs resemble human-made ones in structure, they can be distinguished by their semantic embeddings.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-structured, with a clear presentation of the experimental pipeline, making it easy to follow.
2. The authors conduct extensive experiments to validate their approach, enhancing the reliability of the findings.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper’s motivation could be more clearly articulated. While detecting LLM-generated references is important, it’s unclear what actionable insights this detection provides. The practical implications of this distinction are not fully explored, which may limit the paper’s impact.
2. The analysis focuses solely on citation graphs without considering the full text of the papers, which limits the depth of the semantic evaluation. Examining the semantic fingerprints at a more granular level (e.g., sentence or paragraph embeddings) could reveal additional differences between LLM-generated and human-written references.
