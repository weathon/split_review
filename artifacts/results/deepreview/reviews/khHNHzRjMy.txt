# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a new dataset for emotion recognition in American Sign Language (ASL) called EmoSign. This dataset includes 200 videos with annotations for sentiment and emotion labels, as well as open-ended descriptions of emotion cues, all collected from Deaf ASL signers. The authors also provide baseline benchmarks using multimodal models, highlighting the challenges these models face in integrating visual cues for emotional reasoning and their bias towards positive emotions.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The dataset is carefully curated, with detailed annotations and descriptions that provide a rich resource for future research.
3. The baseline benchmarks using multimodal models offer valuable insights into the current limitations in emotion recognition for sign languages.

## Weaknesses
1. While the dataset is a significant contribution, the paper lacks novel methods or architectures to fully leverage it. Developing and proposing a new method for emotion recognition in sign language would strengthen the paper's contribution.

2. The dataset's size is relatively small, with only 200 videos, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. While the authors acknowledge this limitation, expanding the dataset would be beneficial.

## Questions
1. Have you considered developing a new method specifically for emotion recognition in sign language, leveraging the unique characteristics of the EmoSign dataset?

2. Are there plans to expand the EmoSign dataset? Additional data would help improve the generalizability of the findings.
