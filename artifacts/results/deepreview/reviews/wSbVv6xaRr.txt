# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a novel algorithm, FedMPDD, which utilizes the projected directional derivative to reduce communication costs and enhance privacy in federated learning. Specifically, each client computes the directional derivative of its gradient along multiple random vectors and transmits these values to the server. The server then aggregates this information and updates the global model. The authors provide theoretical analysis, demonstrating that FedMPDD converges at a rate of $O(1/K)$. Additionally, they conduct experiments on benchmark datasets to validate the performance of FedMPDD.

## Strengths
1. This paper introduces a novel algorithm, FedMPDD, which utilizes the projected directional derivative to reduce communication costs and enhance privacy in federated learning.
2. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of the convergence rate of FedMPDD.
3. The paper is well-organized, and the proposed algorithm is easy to follow.

## Weaknesses
1. The convergence analysis in this paper relies on the assumption of a fixed learning rate. However, it is well known that selecting an optimal learning rate is essential to achieve the best convergence rate. It would be beneficial if the authors could relax this assumption and derive the convergence upper bound with an arbitrarily selected learning rate.
2. The theoretical analysis in this paper focuses solely on the convergence rate. However, as a communication-efficient algorithm, it is also important to analyze the reduction in communication costs.
3. The experimental results in this paper are not sufficient. Firstly, the authors should validate the performance of FedMPDD on larger datasets, such as ImageNet, to demonstrate its applicability. Secondly, the authors should compare FedMPDD with more baselines, such as FedEF [1] and FedSVRG [2], to highlight its advantages.
4. The authors claim that FedMPDD can protect against gradient inversion attacks. However, there is a lack of experimental results to support this claim. It would be beneficial if the authors could conduct experiments on datasets with sensitive attributes, such as FEMNIST, and evaluate the performance of FedMPDD against gradient inversion attacks.

[1] Li, Xiang, et al. "Efficient federal learning with sketching." Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 33 (2020): 6812-6821.

[2] Woodworth, Blake E., Kumar Kshitij Patel, and Nati Srebro. "Minibatch vs local sgd for heterogeneous distributed learning." Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 33 (2020): 6281-6292.
