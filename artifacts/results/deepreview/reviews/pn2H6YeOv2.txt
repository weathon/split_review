# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a geometry-first approach called Prompt-Invariant CCA Certificates (PI-CCA) to address the challenges of continual updates for foundation vision-language models on non-stationary data streams. The proposed method aims to preserve the pre-trained cross-modal generalization under domain and prompt shifts without accessing past data. It achieves state-of-the-art performance among replay-free methods across several benchmarks.

## Strengths
- The paper introduces a novel geometry-first approach, PI-CCA, which summarizes image-text alignment with a compact certificate capturing the top-k canonical spectrum and subspace. This method is innovative in preserving pre-trained cross-modal generalization under domain/prompt shifts without accessing past data.
- The proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance among replay-free methods across multiple benchmarks, demonstrating its effectiveness.
- The paper provides a theoretical explanation for the correlation between geometry preservation and performance, adding depth to the understanding of the method's effectiveness.

## Weaknesses
- The proposed method introduces additional computational overhead. It would be beneficial to compare the computational cost with other methods and provide a detailed analysis of the trade-off between performance improvement and computational cost.
- The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the sensitivity of the method to hyperparameters. It would be valuable to understand how sensitive the method is to the choice of hyperparameters and whether tuning is required for different datasets or tasks.
- The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed method. Are there any scenarios or datasets where PI-CCA may not perform as well? Identifying and discussing these limitations would provide a more balanced view of the method's applicability.

## Questions
- The paper mentions that the method is compatible with parameter-efficient adaptation methods like LoRA. It would be interesting to see a detailed analysis of how the method performs when combined with different parameter-efficient adaptation techniques.
- The paper could explore the potential of applying the proposed method to other foundation models beyond vision-language models, such as foundation models for audio or other modalities. This would help to understand the generalizability of the approach.
