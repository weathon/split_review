# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes Guided Hybrid Policy Optimization (GHPO) to address the reward sparsity issue in RLVR training. GHPO includes two core components: Automated Difficulty Detection and Adaptive Prompt Refinement with Multi-Stage Guidance. Experimental results demonstrate that GHPO outperforms GRPO and GRPO-CL on the MATH dataset.

## Strengths
1. This paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The proposed method is well-motivated. The authors first point out the limitations of previous methods and then introduce their method to address these issues.
3. The authors provide the implementation code, enhancing the reproducibility of their work.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed method appears to be a heuristic combination of GRPO and SFT. The authors propose using ground-truth hints to supplement training when the model's performance is low, which seems more like an SFT approach. However, the SFT baseline is missing in the paper.
2. The experiments are not comprehensive. The authors only evaluate their method on the MATH dataset, which is insufficient to demonstrate its effectiveness. Previous work [1] suggests that GRPO is effective for both mathematical and coding tasks. Therefore, I recommend that the authors include additional experiments on coding benchmarks, such as HumanEval and MBPP, to provide a more thorough evaluation.
3. The authors claim that their method can improve training stability. However, there are no experiments to support this claim. I suggest that the authors include training curves to demonstrate the stability of their method.

[1] Guo, Daya, et al. "Deepseek-r1: Incentivizing reasoning capability in llms via reinforcement learning." arXiv preprint arXiv:2501.12948 (2025).
