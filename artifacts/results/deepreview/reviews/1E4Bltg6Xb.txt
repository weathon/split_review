# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a framework to address the dynamic path planning problem in urban road networks using reinforcement learning. The key idea is to use a hierarchical approach to represent dynamics features, which involves extracting task-relevant subgraphs based on policy attention and further decoupling them into local feature sequences using n-hop neighborhoods. The paper demonstrates that the proposed framework outperforms existing methods in terms of computational efficiency and effectiveness in capturing essential dynamic information.

## Strengths
1. The proposed hierarchical approach for representing dynamics features is a novel contribution. The combination of policy attention for task-aware graph sparsification and the n-hop neighborhood method for agent-centric feature decoupling is a creative solution to address the challenges of dynamic path planning.
2. The paper provides a clear motivation for the problem and a well-structured explanation of the proposed framework. The figures and diagrams help to illustrate the concepts and the overall workflow.
3. The paper addresses an important practical problem in urban transportation. The proposed framework has the potential to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of path planning in dynamic environments, which can have significant impacts on various applications such as on-demand delivery and intelligent transportation systems.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed framework is evaluated on a limited set of urban road networks in China. It would be beneficial to test the framework on a more diverse set of scenarios, including different urban layouts, traffic conditions, and geographic locations.
2. The paper does not provide a thorough analysis of the sensitivity of the framework to the choice of hyperparameters, such as the number of hops (n) and the number of top shortest paths (k). A more comprehensive sensitivity analysis would help to understand the robustness and generalizability of the proposed method.
3. The paper does not compare the proposed framework with a wide range of state-of-the-art methods in dynamic path planning. A more extensive comparison with existing methods would help to demonstrate the superiority of the proposed approach.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed framework perform on different types of urban road networks, such as those with different layouts, traffic conditions, and geographic locations?
2. How sensitive is the framework to the choice of hyperparameters, such as the number of hops (n) and the number of top shortest paths (k)? What is the recommended approach for selecting these parameters?
3. How does the proposed framework compare with other state-of-the-art methods in dynamic path planning, such as those based on graph neural networks, reinforcement learning, and hybrid approaches?
