# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a theoretical framework to explain the "less is more" phenomenon observed in recent works. The authors study data curation strategies where an imperfect oracle selects the training examples according to their difficulty and correctness. The authors provide exact scaling law curves for test error under both label-agnostic and label-aware curation rules, revealing when and why keeping only a subset of data can improve generalization.

## Strengths
- The paper provides a novel theoretical framework to explain the "less is more" phenomenon observed in recent works. The theoretical results are insightful and provide a principled explanation for this phenomenon.
- The paper is well-written and the theoretical results are clearly presented. The authors provide a clear explanation of the key concepts and results.
- The paper has significant implications for the field of machine learning. The results challenge the traditional scaling laws and provide a principled explanation for why keeping a subset of data can improve generalization. This has important implications for data curation strategies and can potentially lead to more efficient training of machine learning models.

## Weaknesses
- The paper focuses on a specific type of data curation strategy and it is unclear how generalizable the results are to other strategies.
- The paper does not provide a detailed comparison with other theoretical frameworks or empirical studies on the "less is more" phenomenon.
- The paper does not provide a detailed discussion on the practical implications of the theoretical results, such as how to effectively implement data curation strategies in real-world scenarios.

## Questions
- How does the proposed framework compare to other theoretical frameworks or empirical studies on the "less is more" phenomenon? Are the results consistent with previous findings?
- What are the practical implications of the theoretical results? How can practitioners effectively implement data curation strategies in real-world scenarios based on the proposed framework?
