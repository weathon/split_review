# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces Quantized Zeroth-order Optimization (QZO), a method designed to reduce memory usage during the fine-tuning of large language models (LLMs) by leveraging zeroth-order optimization to eliminate gradients and optimizer states. QZO approximates gradients by perturbing the continuous quantization scale, which avoids the need for de-quantization and re-quantization. Additionally, a directional derivative clipping method is proposed to stabilize training. The authors demonstrate that QZO can reduce memory costs by more than $18\times$ compared to full-parameter fine-tuning in 16 bits, enabling the fine-tuning of Llama-2-13B within a single 24GB GPU.

## Strengths
1. QZO effectively reduces memory usage by eliminating gradients and optimizer states while avoiding the need for de-quantization and re-quantization, which is a significant improvement over traditional methods.
2. The paper provides theoretical evidence that the directional derivative clipping method reduces the variance of the gradient estimate, which helps in stabilizing training.
3. QZO is compatible with both scalar-based and codebook-based post-training quantization methods, making it a versatile solution for memory-efficient training.

## Weaknesses
1. QZO's performance is dependent on the quality of the quantization method used. If the quantization results in a significant error, the gradient estimation might be less accurate, potentially affecting the model's performance.
2. The performance of QZO on tasks beyond NLP, such as image generation, is not extensively explored. The paper focuses primarily on NLP tasks, leaving questions about its applicability to other domains.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the impact of QZO on the convergence rate of the models. Understanding how QZO affects convergence is crucial for assessing its practicality in various applications.

## Questions
1. How does QZO impact the convergence rate of the models compared to traditional fine-tuning methods?
2. Can QZO be extended to other types of neural networks beyond LLMs, such as those used in computer vision or reinforcement learning?
3. How does the quantization method used in QZO affect the accuracy of gradient estimation? Have different quantization methods been compared to determine the most effective one?
4. What are the potential limitations of QZO in terms of task generalization? Does QZO perform equally well across different NLP tasks and datasets?
