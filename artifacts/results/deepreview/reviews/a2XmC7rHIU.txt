# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces the Open Proof Corpus (OPC), a large-scale dataset containing over 5,000 human-evaluated proofs generated by state-of-the-art large language models (LLMs). The primary contribution of the paper is the creation of this dataset, which aims to address the lack of high-quality, large-scale datasets for evaluating LLMs in proof generation tasks. The authors also provide insights into the limitations of current LLMs in generating correct proofs, despite their strong performance in generating correct final answers.

## Strengths
- The paper introduces a novel and valuable dataset, the Open Proof Corpus (OPC), which addresses the lack of large-scale, high-quality datasets for evaluating LLMs in proof generation tasks. The dataset comprises over 5,000 human-evaluated proofs, making it a significant contribution to the field.

- The paper provides insights into the limitations of current LLMs in generating correct proofs, despite their strong performance in generating correct final answers. This is a critical observation, as it highlights the need for more advanced techniques to improve the proof generation capabilities of LLMs.

- The paper demonstrates the effectiveness of the OPC in training models for proof evaluation, with the fine-tuned R1-QWEN3-8B model achieving 88.1% accuracy in proof correctness, matching GEMINI-2.5-PRO and approaching the performance of GPT-5. This shows the potential of the OPC to advance the field of proof evaluation and generation.

## Weaknesses
- The paper acknowledges that the majority of problems in the OPC are derived from high-school competitions, which limits the coverage of more advanced mathematical domains such as research-level mathematics.

- The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the common mistakes made by LLMs in generating incorrect proofs. A more in-depth analysis of these mistakes could provide valuable insights for improving the proof generation capabilities of LLMs.

## Questions
- Could you provide more details on the common mistakes made by LLMs in generating incorrect proofs? An analysis of these mistakes would be valuable for understanding the limitations of current LLMs and for improving their proof generation capabilities.

- How do you plan to expand the OPC to include more advanced mathematical domains, such as research-level mathematics?
