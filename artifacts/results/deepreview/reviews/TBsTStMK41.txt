# Review

## Summary
The paper addresses the issue of paper desk rejections at AI conferences due to author submission limits. The authors propose a novel optimization-based approach to minimize desk rejections while maintaining submission limits. They develop a mathematical framework, formulate the problem as a discrete optimization task, and design an efficient algorithm based on linear programming relaxation and randomized rounding. The method is evaluated on 11 years of ICLR data, showing a reduction in desk rejections by up to 19.23% compared to existing policies.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a well-defined mathematical framework that formalizes the submission-limit-based desk-rejection policies used by AI conferences. This framework allows for a more principled approach to the problem, moving beyond the straightforward policies currently in use.

2. The authors propose a novel optimization-based desk-rejection mechanism that satisfies submission limits while maximizing author welfare by preserving more submitted papers. This approach is a significant departure from the existing "first-come, first-served" or "last-come, first-served" desk-rejection policies.

3. The authors conduct extensive empirical evaluations on 11 years of ICLR submission data and show that their method consistently outperforms existing policies in all cases, reducing the number of desk-rejections by up to 19.23%. This demonstrates the practical effectiveness of their approach.

4. The proposed algorithm is highly efficient, with all results on ICLR data obtained within no more than 53.64 seconds. This suggests that the method can be easily integrated into current conference workflows without significant computational overhead.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper focuses exclusively on ICLR data for evaluation. While ICLR is a large and representative conference, evaluating the method on other conferences would provide a more comprehensive assessment of its generalizability and robustness.

2. The paper does not provide a thorough comparison with other potential approaches or baselines beyond the existing desk-rejection policies. For example, one could consider an approach that takes into account the quality of the submission (e.g., the number of citations, the venue of previous papers) when deciding whether to reject a paper.

3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the types of papers that are desk-rejected under the existing policies versus the proposed method. A deeper analysis could provide insights into the potential impact on the composition of the conference program, such as whether more high-quality papers from underrepresented groups are accepted under the new approach.

## Questions
1. Have you considered evaluating your method on other AI conferences to assess its generalizability and robustness? If so, what were the results?

2. Have you considered comparing your method with other potential approaches or baselines, such as taking into account the quality of the submission when deciding whether to reject a paper? If so, how did your method perform compared to these alternatives?

3. Can you provide a detailed analysis of the types of papers that are desk-rejected under the existing policies versus the proposed method? How does this affect the composition of the conference program, particularly with respect to papers from underrepresented groups?
