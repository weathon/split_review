# Review

## Summary
The authors propose a theoretical framework to understand the simplicity bias in neural networks trained with gradient descent. They show that various architectures, including linear, ReLU, convolutional, and self-attention models, exhibit simplicity bias due to saddle-to-saddle learning dynamics. The theory predicts how data distribution and weight initialization affect the learning process, providing insights into the emergence of stage-like dynamics in neural network training.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a novel theoretical framework that provides a unified explanation for simplicity bias across different neural network architectures.
2. The authors offer a detailed analysis of saddle-to-saddle dynamics, fixed points, and invariant manifolds, which contributes to our understanding of gradient descent learning processes.
3. The theory predicts the impact of data distribution and weight initialization on learning dynamics, which is validated by experimental results.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper focuses on one-layer networks, and it is unclear how well the findings generalize to multi-layer networks.
2. The theory assumes gradient flow dynamics, which may not perfectly capture the behavior of gradient descent with larger learning rates.
3. The analysis is limited to specific activation functions (linear, ReLU, quadratic) and does not cover a wide range of nonlinear activations.

## Questions
1. How do the results generalize to multi-layer networks, and what modifications would be needed to apply the theory to networks with multiple hidden layers?
2. The paper assumes small learning rates. How do the results change with larger learning rates, and what modifications would be needed to apply the theory to more realistic training settings?
3. The analysis is limited to specific activation functions (linear, ReLU, quadratic). How do the results generalize to other activation functions, such as sigmoid or tanh, and what modifications would be needed to apply the theory to a wider range of activations?
