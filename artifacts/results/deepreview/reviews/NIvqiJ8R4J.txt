# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces an adaptive tutoring framework aimed at enhancing personalized education by leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs). The proposed approach integrates collaborative cognitive diagnosis with dynamic instructional adaptation to address the limitations of one-size-fits-all teaching strategies in personalized learning environments. The framework is evaluated on the Gaokao dataset, demonstrating improvements in critical thinking stimulation and task completion rates compared to baseline models.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a novel framework that combines collaborative cognitive diagnosis with dynamic instructional adaptation, addressing the limitations of standard LLM responses in personalized education.
2. The framework is evaluated on the Gaokao dataset, showing significant improvements in critical thinking stimulation and task completion rates compared to baseline models.
3. The paper is well-structured and clearly presents the problem, the proposed solution, and the evaluation results.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks a thorough discussion of the limitations of the proposed framework and potential areas for future research.
2. The evaluation is limited to a single dataset, which may not fully represent the diversity of personalized education scenarios.

## Questions
1. Can the authors provide more details on the scalability of the framework and its performance with larger datasets?
2. How does the framework handle potential biases in the diagnostic process, and what measures are taken to ensure fairness in adaptive tutoring strategies?
