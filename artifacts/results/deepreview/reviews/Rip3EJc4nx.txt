# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a new pruning method for large language models that uses the information entropy of the model's output distribution to efficiently evaluate importance scores of neurons. This approach avoids the limitations of traditional methods that rely on one-hot cross entropy loss and eliminates the need for a separate teacher model. The authors demonstrate that their method consistently outperforms existing pruning techniques across various benchmarks and model series, effectively preserving the model's predictive capabilities while reducing computational and memory requirements.

## Strengths
1. The proposed information entropy-based criterion for neuron importance evaluation is a novel and effective alternative to traditional methods, offering a more holistic approach to model pruning.
2. The method demonstrates superior performance compared to existing pruning techniques across multiple benchmarks and model series, highlighting its effectiveness and robustness.
3. The paper provides extensive experimental results and comparisons, showcasing the method's advantages and validating its claims.
4. The authors plan to release the source code and trained weights, promoting transparency and enabling further research and application of the method.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks a detailed analysis of the computational overhead and runtime associated with the proposed pruning method, which is crucial for assessing its practicality.
2. The experiments primarily focus on LLaMA and Qwen series models. Including other types of models would strengthen the generalizability of the findings.
3. The paper could benefit from a more in-depth discussion on the selection of the calibration dataset and its impact on the pruning performance.
4. The paper does not provide a detailed comparison of the pruning method's effectiveness on different types of tasks or language use cases, which could provide further insights into its applicability.

## Questions
1. Can you provide more details on the computational overhead and runtime associated with your pruning method? How does it compare to existing techniques?
2. How does the proposed method perform on other types of models not included in the paper? Have you tested it on any non-transformer-based models?
3. Can you elaborate on the process of selecting the calibration dataset? How sensitive is the pruning performance to the choice of this dataset?
4. How does the proposed method handle different types of language tasks or domains? Are there specific tasks where it excels or struggles compared to other methods?
