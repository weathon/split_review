# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a method to evaluate the quality of concept annotations in a self-supervised manner. The authors propose a pipeline that iteratively prompts a model for annotations and measures the accuracy of the generated concepts in predicting the correct class. The authors argue that this pipeline simulates a slow reasoning process and that a higher accuracy gap between fast and slow annotations indicates better annotation quality.

## Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The authors propose a novel method for self-supervised evaluation of concept annotations.
- The authors provide a comprehensive analysis of the proposed method across multiple datasets and models.

## Weaknesses
- The authors argue that the gap between fast and slow annotations is an indicator of annotation quality. However, it is not clear why this is the case. In fact, the results in Figure 3 show that the gap is consistently negative across all datasets and models, suggesting that the slow annotations are worse than the fast annotations. The authors should provide more justification for the proposed metric.
- The authors motivate the need for a self-supervised method for evaluating concept annotations due to the high cost of human evaluation. However, the proposed method still requires human annotations to compute the metric (i.e., the ground truth class label is required). The authors should clarify this point.
- The authors argue that the proposed method provides a more rigorous evaluation of concept annotations than accuracy on a downstream task. However, the results in Table 4 show that the proposed metric is highly correlated with downstream accuracy (i.e., the fuse mode has almost the same CRI as the fast mode). The authors should provide more evidence that the proposed method provides a more rigorous evaluation.
