# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a training-free sampling algorithm for LLMs to improve their reasoning abilities. The authors claim that by sampling from a power distribution, the LLM can generate responses with higher likelihoods, which correspond to better reasoning capabilities. The paper provides theoretical justifications and experiments on several reasoning benchmarks to support their claims.

## Strengths
1. The idea of improving LLM reasoning without additional training is interesting and practical.
2. The paper provides a theoretical analysis of the difference between the power distribution and the low-temperature distribution, which is insightful.
3. The experimental results on several reasoning benchmarks are promising.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational overhead of the proposed method. It would be helpful to compare the inference time and computational resources required for this method versus standard sampling and RL-based methods.
2. The authors only conduct experiments on three LLMs, all based on the Qwen architecture. It would be beneficial to include experiments on a wider range of LLMs to demonstrate the generalizability of the proposed method.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed method affect the inference time and computational resources compared to standard sampling and RL-based methods?
2. How does the proposed method perform on LLMs other than Qwen?
