# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a new benchmark, BuilderBench, for evaluating RL algorithms' ability to learn general principles about the environment through open-ended exploration. BuilderBench consists of a robotic agent interacting with blocks in a circular environment, and the agent must learn to build structures without external supervision during training. The benchmark includes a task-suite of 42 diverse target structures that test understanding of physics, mathematics, and long-horizon planning during evaluation. The paper also provides a "training wheels" protocol for training and evaluating agents to build a single target structure. The paper argues that current AI models struggle to solve problems beyond the limits of existing data and that BuilderBench can help accelerate research into agent pre-training that focuses on open-ended exploration and learning through experience.

## Strengths
1. The paper proposes a new benchmark, BuilderBench, which requires agents to learn general principles about the environment through open-ended exploration without external supervision. This is an interesting and novel approach that could help accelerate research into agent pre-training.
2. The benchmark includes a diverse set of 42 target structures that test understanding of physics, mathematics, and long-horizon planning. This provides a comprehensive evaluation of agents' abilities and offers a range of challenges for agents to solve.
3. The paper also provides a "training wheels" protocol for training and evaluating agents to build a single target structure. This can help researchers to easily evaluate their agents and compare them to others.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a clear definition of what it means for an agent to "learn general principles about the environment" or to have "efficient exploration abilities." These concepts are central to the paper's argument, but they are not clearly defined, which makes it difficult to understand what the benchmark is measuring and how success or failure is determined.
2. The paper does not provide a clear explanation of how the tasks in the task-suite are generated or how their difficulty is varied. It says that the tasks are "carefully curated", but it's not clear what criteria are used to determine the difficulty of a task or how a task is created. This makes it difficult to understand the scope of the benchmark and how it can be extended or modified.
3. The paper does not provide a clear explanation of how the agent interacts with the environment or how it learns through exploration. It says that the agent must "explore and run experiments to extract knowledge about the environment", but it's not clear what form this exploration takes or how the agent extracts knowledge from its interactions. This makes it difficult to understand the mechanisms by which the agent learns.
4. The paper does not provide a clear explanation of how the evaluation of an agent's performance is conducted. It says that the agent is "evaluated by running its task-conditioned policy on these tasks and measuring the reward obtained by it", but it's not clear what the reward is or how it is determined. This makes it difficult to understand how an agent's performance is assessed or how it can be compared to other agents.

## Questions
1. What is the definition of "general principles about the environment" and how do you measure an agent's ability to learn them?
2. How are tasks generated and how do you determine their difficulty?
3. How does the agent explore the environment and how does it learn through this exploration?
4. How is an agent's performance evaluated and what is the reward used for this evaluation?
