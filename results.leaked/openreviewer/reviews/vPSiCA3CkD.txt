# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces an adaptive gradient method for solving convex optimization problems with differential and Lipschitz continuous objective functions. The proposed algorithm incorporates Nesterov acceleration and adapts its step size to the local curvature at a geometric rate, achieving near-optimal iteration complexity. The authors provide a comprehensive convergence analysis and validate their approach through numerical experiments.

## Strengths
1. The proposed algorithm is the first adaptive gradient method to achieve near-optimal iteration complexity for $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth functions.
2. The algorithm eliminates the need for hyperparameter tuning and line search procedures, which are typically required in other methods.
3. The paper provides a thorough convergence analysis and includes comparisons with existing methods, highlighting the advantages of the proposed approach.
4. The algorithm is validated through numerical experiments, demonstrating its effectiveness and practical applicability.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper assumes that the objective function is convex and differentiable, which may limit the applicability of the proposed method to a narrower class of problems compared to some existing methods.
2. While the algorithm achieves near-optimal complexity, it introduces additional complexity in its step size adaptation mechanism compared to other methods like AdaGrad and its variants.
3. The algorithm's performance may be sensitive to the choice of parameters, and the paper does not provide extensive guidance on parameter selection.
4. The numerical experiments are limited to specific benchmark problems, and the paper could benefit from a broader range of experimental validation, especially on real-world applications.

## Questions
1. Can the proposed method be extended to non-smooth or stochastic settings? If so, what modifications would be necessary?
2. How does the algorithm perform on a broader range of convex optimization problems beyond the ones tested in the numerical experiments?
3. Can you provide more detailed guidelines for selecting the parameters in the proposed algorithm? How sensitive is the performance to these parameters?
4. How does the algorithm compare with other state-of-the-art methods in terms of practical implementation and computational efficiency?
