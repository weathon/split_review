# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a framework for building equivariant machine learning models for tensors. It provides a theoretical characterization of polynomial functions that are equivariant to the diagonal action of classical Lie groups, including the orthogonal group $O(d)$, the indefinite orthogonal group $O(s, k−s)$, and the symplectic group $Sp(d)$. The authors propose a method for constructing equivariant functions by combining tensor products of input tensors with isotropic tensors, followed by contractions. They further extend their results to include analytic functions with globally convergent Taylor series. The framework is applied to three different problems: stress-strain tensors in materials science, path signatures in time series analysis, and sparse vector estimation in theoretical computer science. Experimental results demonstrate that the equivariant models outperform non-equivariant baselines and other static methods in these applications.

## Strengths
- The paper provides a rigorous theoretical foundation for constructing equivariant functions, drawing on invariant theory results. This approach differs from existing methods that use representation theory and Clebsch-Gordan coefficients.

- The proposed method has broad applicability to various tensor-valued problems in scientific domains, including materials science, time series analysis, and theoretical computer science. The generalization to indefinite orthogonal and symplectic groups extends the scope beyond just $O(d)$.

- The numerical experiments demonstrate clear performance gains of the equivariant models over non-equivariant baselines in various tasks.

- The paper is well-structured and clearly written, with detailed explanations of the theoretical framework and applications. The use of Einstein summation notation and clear definitions helps in understanding the tensor operations.

## Weaknesses
- The proposed method, while general, can be computationally intensive for higher-degree polynomials and larger input tensors. The authors acknowledge this limitation and suggest considering low-degree polynomials or alternative approximations.

- The method is primarily restricted to low-rank output tensors, which may limit its applicability in some higher-dimensional problems.

- The paper lacks a detailed analysis of the computational complexity and scalability of the proposed method for large-scale problems.

## Questions
- The paper focuses on low-degree polynomial and analytic functions. Have you considered or explored other types of equivariant functions, such as exponential functions or trigonometric functions?

- The current method is primarily restricted to low-rank output tensors. Are there any potential approaches or modifications to extend this method to higher-rank output tensors?

- The paper uses invariant theory to construct equivariant functions. Can you provide more intuition or motivation for why this approach was chosen over representation theory and Clebsch-Gordan coefficients, which are commonly used in equivariant machine learning?

- The paper demonstrates strong performance in the experimental results. Can you provide any theoretical insights or analysis on why imposing equivariances improves the learning performance and generalization?
