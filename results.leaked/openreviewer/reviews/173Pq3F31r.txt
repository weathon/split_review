# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a joint framework for expressive piano performance rendering (EPR) and automatic piano transcription (APT), which disentangles note-level score content and global performance style representations from both paired and unpaired data. The framework adopts a transformer-based sequence-to-sequence (Seq2Seq) architecture and is trained using only sequence-aligned data, without requiring fine-grained note-level alignment. An independent diffusion-based performance style recommendation (PSR) module is introduced to generate style embeddings directly from score content, supporting both style transfer and flexible rendering across a range of expressive styles.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The idea of jointly modeling EPR and APT is interesting, and the disentanglement of score content and performance style representations is well-motivated.
3. The PSR module is a novel contribution, allowing for automatic style generation from score content and improving accessibility for non-expert users.
4. The paper provides comprehensive experimental results, including both objective and subjective evaluations, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed framework.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed framework is only evaluated on classical piano music, and it is unclear how well it generalizes to other genres, such as popular music.
2. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational resources required for training and inference, which could be a limitation for practical applications.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed comparison of the proposed framework with other state-of-the-art methods in terms of computational efficiency and scalability.

## Questions
1. How well does the proposed framework generalize to other genres of music, such as popular music?
2. What are the computational resource requirements for training and inference, and how do they compare to other state-of-the-art methods?
3. How does the proposed framework handle different levels of expressiveness in the input scores? Can it adapt to the desired level of expressiveness?
