# Review

## Summary
This paper studies change points in dynamic multilayer networks. The authors proposed a two-stage algorithm, which first uses seeded binary segmentation to generate a coarse set of candidates, and then refines the locations via low-rank tensor estimation. Theoretically, they proved the consistency of the proposed algorithm in estimating the number and locations of change points. They further derived the limiting distributions of the refined estimators under both vanishing and non-vanishing jump regimes. Moreover, a data-driven procedure for constructing confidence intervals is proposed. Extensive numerical experiments demonstrate the superior performance and practical utility of the proposed methods compared to existing alternatives.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written. The notations are clean and the presentation is clear.
2. The proposed two-stage algorithm is reasonable and the theoretical analysis is solid.
3. The limiting distributions of the refined estimators under both vanishing and non-vanishing jump regimes are interesting.
4. The numerical experiments are comprehensive and the real data analysis is insightful.

## Weaknesses
1. The authors mentioned that the proposed method can be extended to allow latent positions to change at the change points. Can you provide more details?
2. The authors only provided the results for the vanishing jump regime when $T$ goes to infinity. What about the non-vanishing jump regime? If $T$ is finite, can you provide the corresponding result for the non-vanishing jump regime?
