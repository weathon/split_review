# Review

## Summary
The paper presents a training-free method for conditional generation using pretrained flow-based generative models. It leverages elliptical slice sampling (ESS) in the source space to generate samples with desired target properties, without requiring gradients through the generative model or the potential function. The authors demonstrate ESS-Flow’s effectiveness on materials design and protein structure prediction tasks.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a novel, training-free approach to conditional generation in flow-based models, which is interesting.
2. The paper is well-structured, with clear explanations and logical flow.
3. The paper addresses a significant challenge in generative modeling—controlled generation without requiring paired data or gradient computations.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks a theoretical analysis of the convergence properties of ESS-Flow, particularly in high-dimensional settings. A deeper exploration of its convergence behavior would strengthen the theoretical foundation of the method.
2. While ESS-Flow is designed to be gradient-free, the paper does not thoroughly address how it performs in high-dimensional spaces. High-dimensional sampling can be challenging for any method, and the lack of empirical results or analysis in this regard raises concerns about the method's scalability and effectiveness in high-dimensional settings.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the impact of different source distributions on ESS-Flow's performance. Understanding how the choice of source distribution affects sampling accuracy and convergence would be valuable for practical applications.
4. The paper lacks a comprehensive comparison with other recent gradient-free methods for controlled generation. Including such comparisons would help contextualize ESS-Flow's advantages and limitations relative to existing approaches.
