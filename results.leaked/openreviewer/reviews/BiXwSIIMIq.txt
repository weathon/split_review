# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a new framework that embeds a three-stage denoiser into ADMM for solving inverse problems. The denoiser consists of auto-correction via additive Gaussian noise, directional correction using conditional Langevin dynamics, and score-based denoising. Theoretical analysis is provided to guarantee the convergence of the proposed method. Experiments on a range of inverse problems demonstrate that the proposed method improves solution quality over a variety of baselines.

## Strengths
1. The proposed AC-DC denoiser effectively mitigates the manifold mismatch issue between ADMM iterates and the geometry of the noisy manifolds used to train the score functions.
2. Theoretical analysis is provided to guarantee the convergence of the proposed method under both fixed and adaptive step size schedules.
3. Experiments on a range of inverse problems demonstrate that the proposed method consistently improves solution quality over existing baselines.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed method requires multiple score evaluations at each iteration, which can be computationally expensive. It would be helpful to provide the time complexity analysis and compare the running time with other baselines.
2. The noise schedules used in the AC and DC stages are currently guided by empirical heuristics. It is desirable to design problem-adaptive scheduling strategies that can further improve both convergence speed and robustness.

## Questions
1. The theoretical analysis focuses on the convergence of the proposed method. Can the authors provide some insights into why the AC-DC denoiser leads to high-quality recovery?
2. How does the proposed method perform on inverse problems with non-smooth or non-convex objectives, such as compressed sensing?
3. How does the proposed method compare with other state-of-the-art methods in terms of computational efficiency and scalability to large-scale problems?
