# Review

## Summary
The paper critiques the quality of research in empirical machine learning, using a case study to illustrate common issues. The authors re-examine the methodology of the min-p sampling paper, showing that the original conclusions are not supported by the data. They identify several issues, including omitted data, incorrect statistical analysis, and inconsistent reporting. The authors conclude by outlining a set of principles for more rigorous empirical machine learning research.

## Strengths
- The paper tackles an important problem in the field: the lack of rigor in empirical machine learning research. The authors provide a clear overview of the issues and a concrete example of where things went wrong.
- The case study is thorough, using a variety of methods to demonstrate the problems with the original min-p sampling paper. The authors provide detailed analysis of the data, code, and methodology.
- The authors do a good job of outlining a set of principles for more rigorous science. These principles are concrete and actionable, and could be useful for researchers and reviewers alike.

## Weaknesses
- The paper focuses on a single case study, which makes it difficult to generalize the findings. It would be useful to see similar critiques applied to other papers in the field.
- The authors could provide more detail on how the principles for more rigorous science might be implemented in practice. What concrete steps can researchers take to improve the quality of their own work and the work of others?

## Questions
- How do the principles for more rigorous science proposed in this paper compare to existing guidelines for empirical machine learning research? Are there overlaps or divergences?
- What are some common pitfalls to avoid when applying these principles in practice? Are there specific scenarios where these guidelines might not be applicable?
- How can researchers balance the need for rigor with the need for innovation and creativity in their work? Are there ways to foster a more rigorous scientific culture without stifling progress?
