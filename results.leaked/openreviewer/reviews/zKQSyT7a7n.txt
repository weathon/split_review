# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces multi-task Visuo-Tactile World Models (VT-WM), which integrate fingertip tactile sensing with vision to jointly model global context and local contact dynamics. The authors demonstrate that VT-WM achieves 33% better performance at maintaining object permanence and 29% better compliance with the laws of motion in autoregressive rollouts. Moreover, experiments show that grounding in contact dynamics also translates to planning. In zero-shot real-robot experiments, VT-WM achieves up to 35% higher success rates, with the largest gains in multi-step, contact-rich tasks.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-organized and easy to follow.
2. The proposed method is technically sound. Integrating tactile signals with visual inputs to enhance the world model is intuitive and well-motivated.
3. The experiments are thorough, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed method in improving object permanence and physical compliance, as well as its success in real-world planning and data-efficient learning.

## Weaknesses
1. The novelty of the proposed method is limited. The main contribution is the integration of tactile signals with visual inputs, which is relatively straightforward. The authors should provide a more detailed explanation of the challenges involved in combining these modalities and how their approach addresses them.
2. The paper lacks a comparison with existing methods. The authors should include a quantitative comparison with other state-of-the-art world models to demonstrate the advantages of their proposed method.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed method handle the time delay between tactile and visual signals? Given that tactile signals are received 100ms after the visual signals, how does this time delay impact the performance of the proposed method? Have the authors considered any techniques to mitigate the negative effects of this delay?
2. How does the proposed method perform when the objects are of different shapes or sizes? The experiments primarily focus on cube-stacking tasks. How does the proposed method generalize to other objects, especially those with complex shapes or varying sizes?
3. How does the proposed method handle different surface textures or materials? The experiments are conducted on smooth surfaces. How does the proposed method perform on surfaces with different textures or materials, especially when the tactile sensor has limited resolution?
4. How does the proposed method perform in the presence of noise or artifacts in the tactile signals? Given that tactile signals are often noisy, how does the proposed method handle such noise or artifacts? Have the authors considered any techniques to improve the robustness of the method in the presence of sensor noise?
