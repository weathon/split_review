# Review

## Summary
The paper addresses the challenge of Multi-modal Entity Alignment (MMEA) in heterogeneous multi-modal knowledge graphs (MMKGs), where entities are described by attributes from various modalities. The authors identify and explore a practical but under-studied issue in MMEA termed Dual-level Noisy Correspondence (DNC), which encompasses misalignments in intra-entity (entity-attribute), inter-graph (entity-entity), and inter-attribute (attribute-attribute) correspondences. To tackle the DNC problem, they propose a robust MMEA framework named RULE. RULE first evaluates the reliability of both intra-entity and inter-graph correspondences using a dedicated two-fold principle. Leveraging these estimated reliabilities, RULE mitigates the negative effects of intra-entity noise during attribute fusion and prevents overfitting to noisy inter-graph correspondences during inter-graph discrepancy elimination. Additionally, RULE incorporates a correspondence reasoning module that uncovers underlying attribute-attribute connections across graphs, enhancing the identification of equivalent entities. Extensive experiments on five benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of the RULE method compared to seven state-of-the-art techniques.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The authors have made an important contribution by introducing the concept of Dual-level Noisy Correspondence (DNC) in Multi-modal Entity Alignment (MMEA). This new problem formulation addresses a significant challenge in the field and provides a fresh perspective on the limitations of existing methods.
3. The proposed RULE method is robust and effective, demonstrating its ability to handle noisy correspondences at both intra-entity and inter-graph levels. The extensive experimental results on five benchmarks against seven state-of-the-art methods show the superiority of the proposed method.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed method is somewhat complicated. The authors are encouraged to provide a more detailed and clearer description of the methodology.
2. The authors are encouraged to discuss the computational complexity and runtime of the proposed method.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed method perform in terms of computational efficiency, and what are the computational requirements for implementing the method?
2. Can the proposed method be adapted or extended to handle other types of noise or inaccuracies in multi-modal data?
