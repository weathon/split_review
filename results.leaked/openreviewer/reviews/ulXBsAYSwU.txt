# Review

## Summary
This paper presents MolMiner, a fragment-based generative model for molecular design. It supports multi-property conditional generation, incorporates symmetry-aware 3D modeling with dynamic geometry updates using force fields, and employs an order-agnostic generation strategy to enhance flexibility and diversity. The authors also propose improved benchmarking methods, including Wasserstein-based distributional metrics and calibration plots, to evaluate both unconditional and conditional generation performance. MolMiner aims to provide a comprehensive solution for controllable and interpretable molecular design.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a novel fragment-based, order-agnostic autoregressive model that supports multi-property conditional generation, which is a significant advancement over existing methods that typically focus on single-property conditioning.
2. MolMiner incorporates 3D geometry awareness through dynamic updates using force fields, which is crucial for capturing structure-dependent properties and enhancing the realism of generated molecules.
3. The model proposes a new sampling method that allows for flexible and efficient generation of molecules with desired properties, making it useful for high-throughput screening and drug discovery applications.
4. The authors provide a comprehensive evaluation of MolMiner using both unconditional and conditional generation benchmarks, including distributional comparisons and calibration plots, which validate the model's performance and provide insights into its limitations.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed comparison with existing state-of-the-art methods, particularly in terms of the diversity and novelty of the generated molecules. 
2. The model's performance is evaluated on a single dataset (ZINC), and it is unclear how well MolMiner generalizes to other datasets or chemical spaces. 
3. The paper does not provide a thorough analysis of the computational requirements and efficiency of MolMiner, which is important for practical applications.
4. The model's ability to handle large-scale datasets and generate molecules with very specific property requirements is not fully explored.

## Questions
1. How does MolMiner compare to other state-of-the-art generative models for molecules in terms of the diversity and novelty of the generated molecules? Can you provide more detailed metrics or evaluations to demonstrate the superiority of your model in this regard?
2. How does MolMiner handle large-scale datasets? Have you tested the model's performance on datasets significantly larger than ZINC, and what are the computational requirements and efficiency like in such cases?
3. Can you provide more insights into the computational requirements and efficiency of MolMiner? How does the model's performance scale with the size of the dataset and the complexity of the desired molecules?
4. How does MolMiner handle very specific property requirements? Can you provide examples or case studies where the model successfully generates molecules with very specific and challenging property profiles?
