# Review

## Summary
The paper discusses the false statements and flawed arguments made in a recent TPAMI publication (Palazzo et al., 2024) regarding EEG classification. The authors address the misleading claims about signal bleeding, subject attentiveness, session length, cross-subject variability, and the effects of supertrials on the signal spectrum. They provide evidence to refute these claims and correct the misunderstandings presented in the TPAMI publication.

## Strengths
1. The authors thoroughly analyze and refute the false claims made in Palazzo et al. (2024), providing evidence and context to support their corrections.

2. The paper helps to clarify misunderstandings in the field of EEG classification, promoting accuracy and rigor in future research.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper primarily focuses on correcting the errors made in a single publication (Palazzo et al., 2024). While this is important, it may not have a broad impact beyond the specific community working on EEG classification.

2. The paper does not introduce new methods or significantly advance the state-of-the-art in EEG classification. The contributions are primarily corrective rather than innovative.

3. The authors could have taken the opportunity to discuss broader implications or advancements in EEG classification beyond the specific corrections needed. The paper would benefit from a more forward-looking perspective on the field.
