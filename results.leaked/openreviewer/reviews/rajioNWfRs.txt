# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces TNT, a two-stage training framework for deep memory modules that addresses the inefficiencies in training and inference for these models. TNT uses a hierarchical memory architecture with periodic state resets, enabling massive parallelization during pre-training. This is followed by a fine-tuning stage to optimize performance for smaller chunk sizes used in inference. The authors demonstrate that TNT achieves up to a 17x speedup in training compared to the most accurate RNN baselines while also improving model accuracy. They highlight the potential of TNT to make deep memory modules more practical and scalable, which could help close the performance gap with Transformers.

## Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow. 
- The authors identify and address three key challenges faced by deep memory modules, which are critical for the development of these models. 
- The proposed TNT framework is novel and addresses the inefficiencies in training and inference for deep memory modules. 
- The authors provide extensive experimental results that demonstrate the effectiveness of TNT in terms of both training efficiency and model accuracy. 
- TNT achieves up to a 17x speedup in training compared to the most accurate RNN baselines while also improving model accuracy. This is a significant contribution, as it makes deep memory modules more practical and scalable.

## Weaknesses
- The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed approach. 
- The evaluation of TNT is primarily focused on language modeling tasks. It would be beneficial to see how well the framework generalizes to other types of tasks, such as image or speech processing. 
- The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational resources required for implementing TNT. This information would be valuable for assessing the feasibility of using TNT in resource-constrained environments.

## Questions
- Can you provide more details on the limitations of TNT and potential areas for future research? 
- How well does TNT generalize to other types of tasks beyond language modeling? Have you conducted any preliminary experiments in this direction? 
- Can you provide more details on the computational resources required for implementing TNT?
