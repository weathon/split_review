# Review

## Summary
The paper compares the learning dynamics of Transformers and State-Space Models (SSMs) on tasks related to associative recall and copying. It argues that while Transformers are robust to optimization hyperparameters, SSMs suffer from critical instabilities, requiring a narrow window of learning rates for optimal performance. This instability, the authors suggest, is a key differentiator between Transformers and SSMs, impacting their practical applications and scalability.

## Strengths
The paper offers a detailed comparison between Transformers and SSMs, focusing on their optimization dynamics and learning stability across various tasks. By highlighting the sensitivity of SSMs to learning rates, the authors provide insights into the models' behavior, which could inform future research on recurrent models.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper primarily relies on synthetic benchmarks for its findings, which may not fully capture the complexity of real-world language tasks. This limits the generalizability of the conclusions.
2. While the authors emphasize the importance of optimization stability, the paper lacks a rigorous theoretical analysis of why SSMs are more brittle in this regard compared to Transformers. A deeper theoretical framework would strengthen the claims.

## Questions
1. How do the findings on optimization instability and learning rate sensitivity generalize to more complex, real-world tasks beyond synthetic benchmarks?
2. The paper suggests that SSMs benefit more from increased width than depth. How does this scaling behavior impact the architectural design of SSMs in practical applications?
