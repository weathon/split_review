# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a continual learning method that is based on null space adaptation. The idea is to decompose the weight matrix of a model using SVD and find the null space, i.e. the space where the weights have minimal impact. Then, the weight update is constrained to this space to prevent interference with previous tasks. The method is evaluated on a standard continual learning benchmark and compared with several baselines.

## Strengths
- The paper is well written and easy to follow. The idea of using null space adaptation for continual learning is novel and interesting. 
- The paper includes a theoretical analysis that provides a bound on the interference between the different tasks. 
- The method is evaluated on a standard benchmark and several baselines are provided.

## Weaknesses
- The paper lacks a discussion on the computational overhead of the method. For each task, the method requires SVD decomposition of the weight matrix, which can be computationally expensive. In contrast, some of the baselines do not require any additional computation at training time. 
- The method is evaluated on the MTIL benchmark, which is designed for multimodal models. However, it seems that the method is general and can be applied to standard continual learning benchmarks. It would be interesting to see how the method compares with the baselines on such benchmarks.

## Questions
- What is the computational overhead of the method? How does it compare with the baselines?
- The paper claims that the method is memory-free. However, the method requires SVD decomposition of the weight matrix, which requires some memory. Can you please clarify this point?
- The method is evaluated on the MTIL benchmark, which is designed for multimodal models. How does the method compare with the baselines on standard continual learning benchmarks?
