# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces Direct Optimal Action Learning (DOAL), a framework for policy extraction from Q-value functions in offline reinforcement learning. DOAL utilizes behavior losses native to the policy's distribution, such as flow matching loss, to imitate an optimized action based on Q-values. The authors reinterpret the traditional balancing factor between Q-loss and behavior-loss as a mechanism for selecting a trust region for the optimal action, yielding a Batch-Normalizing Optimizer. The DOAL framework is shown to be effective when applied to Gaussian, Diffusion, and Flow policies, outperforming strong baseline models on 9 OGBench tasks and simplifying hyperparameter search.

## Strengths
The paper introduces a novel framework, DOAL, which provides a new approach to policy extraction from Q-value functions. The reinterpretation of the balancing factor as a trust region selection mechanism is a creative solution that simplifies hyperparameter search. The proposed method is evaluated on a wide range of tasks and compared against strong baselines, demonstrating its effectiveness. The DOAL framework is versatile and can be easily integrated with existing Q-value-based offline RL methods.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper is not well-written. For example, Figure 1 is not referenced or explained in the text. The caption of Figure 2 is not clear. The paper also contains some typos, such as "an efficient, effective, and a versatile" (should be "versatile framework") and "the DOAL learned policies achieved improvement over the strong baselines on OGBench" (should be "the DOAL framework learned policies"). The authors should carefully proofread and edit the paper to improve the writing and clarity.

2. The paper lacks a clear explanation of the motivation behind DOAL. While the authors mention that DOAL is more efficient and effective, they do not provide a detailed explanation of why it is more efficient and effective. It would be helpful if the authors could provide a more in-depth discussion of the motivation and explain the specific advantages of DOAL in comparison to other methods.

3. The paper lacks a detailed discussion of the limitations of DOAL. While the authors mention that DOAL is versatile, it would be helpful to provide a more detailed discussion of the scenarios where DOAL may not be suitable or may require adjustments. For example, the authors could discuss the performance of DOAL in environments with high-dimensional action spaces or in real-time applications where computational efficiency is critical. 

4. The paper lacks a detailed discussion of the computational complexity of DOAL. While the authors mention that DOAL is efficient, it would be helpful to provide a more detailed analysis of the computational requirements of DOAL, including the time and memory complexity of the training process. This would allow for a more comprehensive comparison of DOAL with other methods in terms of computational resources.

## Questions
1. Can you provide more details on the specific advantages of DOAL in comparison to other methods? How does DOAL improve efficiency and effectiveness, and why is it more versatile?

2. Can you provide a more detailed discussion of the limitations of DOAL? Are there specific scenarios where DOAL may not be suitable or may require adjustments? How does DOAL perform in environments with high-dimensional action spaces or in real-time applications where computational efficiency is critical?

3. Can you provide a more detailed analysis of the computational requirements of DOAL? What are the time and memory complexity of the training process of DOAL? How does DOAL compare to other methods in terms of computational resources?
