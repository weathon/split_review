# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces LoLoRA, a novel memory-efficient fine-tuning method for large language models (LLMs) that builds upon the popular LoRA approach. LoLoRA aims to reduce memory usage by employing gradient-free updates to the matrix $A$ during the forward pass, using only the immediate input of the layer. This approach allows LoLoRA to adapt to input distribution shifts without storing activations for the backward pass, further minimizing memory requirements compared to standard LoRA. The authors provide theoretical insights into optimal initializations for the LoRA matrices and validate their method through experiments on various NLP tasks.

## Strengths
- **Theoretical Contributions**: The paper provides a solid theoretical foundation for the proposed method, including proofs that justify the optimal initialization of the $A$ matrix based on input covariance.
- **Memory Efficiency**: LoLoRA successfully reduces the memory requirements for fine-tuning LLMs, which is a critical issue in the field.

## Weaknesses
- **Limited Experimental Scope**: The experimental validation is primarily focused on NLP tasks, with limited diversity in the types of datasets and models tested. Expanding the experiments to include more diverse data modalities and model architectures would strengthen the claims about LoLoRA's general applicability.
- **Performance Trade-offs**: In some cases, LoLoRA's performance is comparable to or slightly worse than LoRA-FA with EVA initialization. The paper does not thoroughly explore the reasons behind these trade-offs or provide clear guidelines on when to prefer LoLoRA.
- **Limited Baselines**: The paper compares LoLoRA primarily with LoRA and LoRA-FA. Including comparisons with other recent memory-efficient fine-tuning methods would provide a more comprehensive evaluation.

## Questions
1. Can you provide more detailed ablation studies to analyze the impact of different components of LoLoRA, such as the choice of update method for $A$ and the rank of the adaptation matrices?
2. How does LoLoRA perform on non-NLP tasks, such as computer vision tasks or reinforcement learning tasks?
3. What are the potential limitations of LoLoRA, and in what scenarios might it not be the most suitable choice? A more balanced presentation of the strengths and weaknesses would be beneficial.
4. Can you provide more insights into the decision-making process behind the choice of update methods for $A$ (e.g., HPCA, AE, etc.)? How do these choices impact performance across different tasks and model architectures?
