# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a new metric for self-supervised learning of time-series data. The proposed metric is a variant of Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) which is widely used in segmentation tasks. The authors argue that the popular MSE metric is not suitable for time-series as it is amplitude based and does not capture shape or structure. The proposed metric is structure aware and is bounded between 0 and 1. The experimental results show that the proposed metric is more robust to noise and phase shift compared to MSE. Furthermore, the authors show that using the proposed metric in self-supervised learning leads to better performance in forecasting and classification tasks.

## Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The proposed metric is novel and is a good alternative to MSE.
- The experimental results show that the proposed metric is more robust to noise and phase shift compared to MSE.
- The proposed metric leads to better performance in forecasting and classification tasks.

## Weaknesses
- The proposed metric is not shift invariant which is a major drawback for time-series data. This is mentioned in the paper but not properly addressed. It is not clear how much this affects the performance of the metric. It would be useful if the authors could provide some empirical results to support this.
- The experimental results are not very convincing. In most of the experiments, the proposed metric performs only slightly better than MSE (in some cases it is even worse). It would be useful if the authors could provide some statistical significance tests to show that the proposed metric indeed performs better than MSE.
- The authors compare their metric to only a few other metrics (MSE, PCC, SI-SNR). It would be useful if they could provide some justification for this choice. For example, they could explain why they did not include other popular metrics such as MAE, RMSE, MAPE etc.

## Questions
- How does the proposed metric perform compared to other popular metrics such as MAE, RMSE, MAPE?
- How does the proposed metric perform on datasets with large shifts?
