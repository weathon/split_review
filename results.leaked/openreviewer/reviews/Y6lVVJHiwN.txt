# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a unified framework for federated constrained optimization that addresses four major challenges in federated learning (FL): functional constraints, communication bottlenecks, local updates, and partial client participation. The authors propose FEDSGM, a switching gradient method that provides projection-free, primal-only updates, avoiding expensive dual-variable tuning or inner solvers. The paper incorporates bidirectional error feedback to handle communication limits and introduces a soft switching version of FEDSGM to stabilize updates near the feasibility boundary. The authors provide convergence guarantees and validate their theoretical findings through experiments on Neyman-Pearson classification and constrained Markov decision process (CMDP) tasks.

## Strengths
1. The paper addresses an important problem in the field of federated learning by extending the concept of SGM to handle functional constraints in a federated setting. This is a novel contribution that has the potential to improve the applicability of federated learning to real-world problems.
2. The paper provides a rigorous theoretical analysis of the proposed algorithm, including convergence guarantees and high-probability bounds. The authors also analyze the effect of compression, switching, and local updates, which adds depth to the understanding of the algorithm's behavior.
3. The paper is well-structured and clearly written. The authors provide a clear motivation for their work and explain the challenges they aim to address. The proposed algorithm is described in detail, and the theoretical results are presented in a clear and concise manner.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper's main theoretical result, Theorem 1, appears to be incorrect. The proof in Appendix C relies on the assumption that $\hat{G}(w_t) \leq \epsilon$ for all $t \in [T]$, which is not a valid assumption. This assumption is used in Lemma C.1, which in turn is used to prove the main result. Without this assumption, the proof breaks down, and the correctness of Theorem 1 is questionable.
2. The paper lacks empirical validation of the proposed algorithm. While the authors provide theoretical guarantees, it would be valuable to see empirical results that demonstrate the effectiveness of FEDSGM in practical scenarios. The paper would be strengthened by including experiments on real-world datasets and comparing the proposed algorithm with existing methods for federated constrained optimization.
3. The paper does not provide a discussion of the computational complexity of the proposed algorithm. It would be helpful to understand the scalability of FEDSGM for large-scale problems. Additionally, the paper could benefit from a comparison of the computational cost of FEDSGM with other existing methods for federated constrained optimization.

## Questions
1. Can you provide empirical results that demonstrate the effectiveness of FEDSGM in practical scenarios? Specifically, can you include experiments on real-world datasets and compare the proposed algorithm with existing methods for federated constrained optimization?
2. Can you provide a discussion of the computational complexity of FEDSGM? Specifically, can you analyze the time and space complexity of the proposed algorithm and compare it with other existing methods for federated constrained optimization?
3. Can you provide a detailed derivation of the proof of Theorem 1, specifically addressing the assumption that $\hat{G}(w_t) \leq \epsilon$ for all $t \in [T]$? Without this assumption, how does the proof of Theorem 1 hold?
