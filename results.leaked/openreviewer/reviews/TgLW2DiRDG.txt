# Review

## Summary
The paper studies the polyhedral complexes formed by ReLU networks. The main results are bounds on the average connectivity of the complex as well as its graph-theoretic diameter. The main idea is to use the sign sequence representation of the polyhedral complex and use it to define a graph where each node is a cell in the complex and two nodes are connected if their corresponding sign sequences differ by a single entry. The paper then proves bounds on the average degree of this graph and its diameter. The paper also provides experiments computing the connectivity graph for some networks and analyzing the distribution of the number of neighbors of the cells containing the training data.

## Strengths
The paper provides interesting new theoretical results on the connectivity of the polyhedral complexes of ReLU networks. These results are likely to be of interest to researchers in the area of explainability and geometry of neural networks. The proofs of the theoretical results are easy to follow. The paper also provides experimental results that help illustrate the theoretical results.

## Weaknesses
The motivation for the theoretical results is lacking. It would be good to explain how the theoretical results can be used to address problems in explainability and other areas related to the geometry of neural networks. It would also be good to explain the significance of the experimental results. The experiments seem to be largely preliminary and it is not clear what can be learned from them. It would also be good to provide some details about the experiments, such as the architecture of the networks used. The paper mentions that the code for the experiments is provided but I was unable to locate it.
