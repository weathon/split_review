# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a KV cache compression method inspired by quantum computing. The method divides the KV cache into four parts: anchor tokens, recent tokens, critical tokens, and non-critical tokens. The anchor tokens, recent tokens, and critical tokens are stored in classical memory, while the non-critical tokens are encoded into a quantum state through amplitude encoding. The attention computation includes two parts: a hard attention part that sums the values of anchor tokens, recent tokens, and critical tokens, and a soft attention part that sums the values of non-critical tokens weighted by their probability amplitudes. The method achieves 7x memory reduction and maintains 92-97% of baseline performance across five models and six benchmarks.

## Strengths
1. The idea of using quantum computing for KV cache compression is interesting.
2. The method achieves 7x memory reduction and maintains 92-97% of baseline performance across five models and six benchmarks.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not report the runtime of the method. A 7x reduction in memory usage is less meaningful if it is accompanied by a significant increase in runtime.
2. The method is only evaluated on models with a maximum size of 8B. It would be more convincing if the method could be evaluated on larger models, such as 70B models.

## Questions
1. What is the runtime of the method?
2. How does the method perform on larger models, such as 70B models?
