# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a new RL algorithm for LLMs, called ARPO. The main contribution of ARPO is an entropy-based adaptive rollout mechanism that selectively expands sampling based on token-level entropy. The authors found that after tool-call steps, the entropy of the LLM's token generation increases, indicating uncertainty. ARPO leverages this by branching the sampling process when entropy exceeds a threshold, leading to more efficient exploration of tool-use behaviors. Additionally, ARPO uses advantage attribution estimation to help the LLM internalize the advantages of different stepwise tool-use behaviors. The authors evaluate ARPO on 13 benchmarks and demonstrate that it outperforms traditional trajectory-level RL algorithms, achieving better performance with half the tool-use budget.

## Strengths
- ARPO introduces a novel entropy-based adaptive rollout mechanism that effectively balances global and step-level sampling, addressing the limitations of trajectory-level RL methods for tool-use agents.
- The authors conduct extensive experiments across 13 challenging benchmarks, demonstrating ARPO's superior performance compared to existing RL algorithms.
- ARPO achieves better performance with only half the tool-use budget of other methods, showing improved efficiency and scalability.

## Weaknesses
- The paper lacks a detailed discussion on the selection of hyperparameters, particularly the entropy threshold for branching, which could impact the reproducibility and practical application of the method.
- The theoretical analysis provided is limited in scope and does not fully explain the empirical results, leaving some gaps in the understanding of ARPO's effectiveness.

## Questions
- How is the entropy threshold for branching determined, and how sensitive is ARPO's performance to this hyperparameter?
- Could the authors elaborate on the potential limitations of using only half the tool-use budget, particularly in cases where more extensive tool usage might be required for certain tasks?
