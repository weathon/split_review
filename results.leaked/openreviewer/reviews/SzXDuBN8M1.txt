# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes TD-JEPA, a method for learning representations that are predictive of long-term latent dynamics across multiple policies/tasks from offline, reward-free transitions. TD-JEPA trains a state encoder, a policy-conditioned multi-step predictor, and a set of parameterized policies directly in latent space, enabling zero-shot optimization of any reward function at test time. The authors provide theoretical guarantees that TD-JEPA avoids collapse with proper initialization and learns encoders that capture a low-rank factorization of long-term policy dynamics. Empirically, TD-JEPA matches or outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on a variety of tasks.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The proposed method is well-motivated and theoretically grounded.
3. The empirical results are strong, with TD-JEPA matching or outperforming state-of-the-art baselines on a wide range of tasks.

## Weaknesses
1. The method is only evaluated on simulated tasks, it would be interesting to see how it performs on real-world robotics tasks.
2. The method is only evaluated on goal-reaching tasks, it would be interesting to see how it performs on other types of tasks, such as manipulation tasks.

## Questions
1. How does the method perform on real-world robotics tasks?
2. How does the method perform on other types of tasks, such as manipulation tasks?
3. How sensitive is the method to the choice of hyperparameters?
4. How does the method compare to other methods in terms of computational efficiency?
