# Review

## Summary
The paper investigates whether the Task-Method-Knowledge (TMK) framework can improve the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) on procedural and planning tasks. The TMK framework, borrowed from cognitive and educational science, decomposes tasks into explicit goals, methods, and domain knowledge, enabling structured reasoning. Experiments on the PlanBench benchmark, specifically in the Blocksworld domain, show significant performance improvements with TMK prompting, particularly for opaque symbolic tasks where traditional prompting falters. The study claims that TMK acts as a "symbolic scaffold," steering LLMs toward formal, code-like reasoning pathways, and suggests potential for broader application of TMK in enhancing LLM performance on complex, reasoning-intensive tasks.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a novel application of the TMK framework, borrowed from cognitive and educational science, to improve LLM reasoning on planning tasks. This interdisciplinary approach is innovative and provides a fresh perspective on enhancing LLM capabilities.
2. The paper is well-structured, with a clear explanation of the TMK framework, its components, and how it is integrated into LLM prompts. The use of Blocksworld as a test domain is well-chosen and allows for clear illustration of the framework's benefits.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper's focus on only the Blocksworld domain, while valuable, limits the generalizability of the findings. Testing the TMK framework on a wider range of planning tasks and domains would strengthen the claims of its broad applicability.
2. While the paper shows improvements over plain-text prompts, it lacks direct comparison with other advanced prompting techniques (e.g., chain-of-thought, tree-of-thoughts) in the experiments. Including such comparisons would provide a clearer picture of the TMK framework's relative effectiveness.
3. The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion on the limitations of the TMK framework, particularly in terms of scalability to more complex planning tasks and potential challenges in generalizing to other domains.

## Questions
1. How does the TMK framework perform on other planning tasks and domains beyond Blocksworld? Are there specific domains where its effectiveness might be limited?
2. The paper mentions that the TMK framework acts as a "symbolic scaffold" and steers LLMs toward formal, code-like reasoning pathways. Could the authors elaborate on how this mechanism operates and why it leads to improved performance?
3. Could the authors provide a more detailed comparison between the TMK framework and other advanced prompting techniques, such as chain-of-thought or tree-of-thoughts, on the same set of tasks?
