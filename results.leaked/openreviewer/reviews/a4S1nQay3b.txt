# Review

## Summary
This paper focuses on multi-view clustering (MVC) and aims to address the noisy correspondence (NC) problem in real-world multi-view datasets. The authors identify two critical forms of NC, category-level mismatch and sample-level mismatch, and propose a generative framework called CorreGen to tackle them. CorreGen formulates noisy correspondence learning in MVC as maximum likelihood estimation over underlying cross-view correspondences and solves it via an Expectation-Maximization algorithm. The paper demonstrates the effectiveness of CorreGen through extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world noisy datasets, showing significant improvements in clustering robustness.

## Strengths
1. The paper identifies and formalizes two types of noisy correspondence in MVC, category-level mismatch and sample-level mismatch, which are prevalent in real-world multi-view datasets and harmful to clustering. This problem is important and worth studying.
2. The paper proposes CorreGen, a novel generative framework that models latent cross-view correspondences through maximum likelihood estimation and solves it via an EM algorithm. This approach is different from existing discriminative approaches and shows better performance.
3. The paper introduces a principled E-step solution that jointly models category-level correspondences and suppresses sample-level noise by leveraging GMM-guided marginals. The extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world noisy datasets validate the effectiveness of the approach.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper could provide more detailed explanations and derivations for some technical steps in the EM algorithm, such as the choice of the auxiliary distribution Q and the optimization process in the E-step.
2. The paper could discuss the computational complexity of CorreGen and compare it with other methods, especially for large-scale datasets.

## Questions
1. Can the authors provide more detailed explanations for some technical steps in the EM algorithm?
2. What is the computational complexity of CorreGen and how does it compare with other methods?
