# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a neural neighborhood search method with Graph-aware Multi-modal Attention model (GAMA) for vehicle routing problems. The proposed model encodes the problem instance and its evolving solution as distinct modalities using graph neural networks, and models their intra- and inter-modal interactions through stacked self- and cross-attention layers. A gated fusion mechanism further integrates the multi-modal representations into a structured state, enabling the policy to make informed and generalizable operator selection decisions.

## Strengths
1. The proposed graph-aware multi-modal attention encoder that independently encodes the VRP instance and current solution as distinct semantic modalities using graph convolutional networks. 
2. The incorporation of a gated fusion module to integrate the multi-modal representations, providing the policy network with rich, structured state features that improve generalization and decision-making quality across diverse problem instances.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed method is only evaluated on the CVRP problem, while there are many other variants of VRPs that have been widely studied in the literature, such as the dynamic VRP, time-window-constrained VRP, split-deliver VRP, and so on. 
2. The scale of the problem instances is relatively small, with the number of customers ranging only from 20 to 100. 
3. The proposed method is not compared with some of the latest learning-based methods for VRPs, such as [1-3].

[1] Huang, Shengjie, et al. "Learning to Route with Thought: A Neural Network Approach for Vehicle Routing Problems." arXiv preprint arXiv:2405.14819 (2024).
[2] Bi, Jieyi, et al. "Learning to handle complex constraints for vehicle routing problems." Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 37 (2024): 93479-93509.
[3] Liu, Xin, et al. "Learning to Perform Local Rewriting for Combinatorial Optimization." arXiv preprint arXiv:2409.19149 (2024).

## Questions
1. How does the proposed method perform on other variants of VRPs? 
2. How does the proposed method perform on problem instances with a larger number of customers, such as those with 500 or 1000 nodes? 
3. How does the proposed method compare to the latest learning-based methods for VRPs?
