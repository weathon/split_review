# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a novel approach for learning Boltzmann generators, i.e. sampling methods that approximate the Boltzmann distribution of a given physical system at a given temperature. The authors introduce a constrained optimization framework that generates intermediate distributions by constraining both the KL divergence and the entropy decay between successive steps. This approach aims to address common issues in existing methods, such as mode collapse and mass teleportation, by promoting smooth distributional overlap and stability throughout the sampling process. The proposed method is evaluated on standard BG benchmarks and an introduced ELIL tetrapeptide system, demonstrating superior performance in terms of effective sample size and mode diversity compared to state-of-the-art methods.

## Strengths
The paper proposes a novel constrained optimization framework for learning Boltzmann generators that addresses key challenges in sampling from high-dimensional, multimodal distributions. The introduction of both trust-region and entropy constraints in a unified approach is an original contribution to the field. The theoretical foundation is well-developed, with clear mathematical formulations and proofs. The experimental evaluation is thorough, covering multiple benchmarks and including ablation studies that validate the effectiveness of both constraints. The paper is generally well-written and structured, with clear explanations of the methodology and results.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper would benefit from a more detailed discussion of the practical implications of the theoretical results, particularly in terms of computational efficiency and scalability to higher dimensions.
2. The paper does not provide a comprehensive comparison with other recent methods in the field, such as those using diffusion models for Boltzmann generator learning.
3. The experimental evaluation, while thorough, is limited to a relatively small set of molecular systems. The scalability of the method to significantly larger systems is not adequately addressed.
4. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational complexity or runtime performance of the proposed method compared to existing approaches.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed method scale to significantly larger molecular systems or more complex physical models? Are there any computational bottlenecks or limitations that might affect its applicability to such systems?
2. How does the method perform compared to state-of-the-art diffusion-based approaches for Boltzmann generator learning? What are the advantages or disadvantages of the proposed method in terms of sample quality and computational efficiency?
3. How sensitive is the performance of the method to the choice of hyperparameters, such as the trust-region bounds and entropy constraints? Is there a systematic way to determine optimal values for these parameters?
4. How does the method perform on systems with significantly higher dimensions (e.g., 1000+ dimensions)? What are the potential challenges in such high-dimensional settings?
5. How does the method handle systems with multiple metastable states? Is there a risk of mode collapse in such cases, and if so, how can it be mitigated?
