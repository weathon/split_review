# Review

## Summary
The authors present a language model-driven framework that transforms cluster annotation into a quantitatively optimizable task. The framework generates a ranked list of GO-based hypotheses and calculates calibrated confidence scores. The predicted descriptions are then embedded using a sentence-embedding model, and the framework evaluates the internal consistency of predictions and their external distinctiveness. The authors validate their method using a public K562 CRISPRi Perturb-seq dataset.

## Strengths
The authors propose a framework that combines large language models with single-cell clustering, introducing a novel approach to automate the process of hypothesis generation and experimental guidance.

## Weaknesses
1. The authors' prompt design lacks a clear, specific example, making it difficult to understand how the prompt should be constructed.
2. The authors validate their method using only the K562 CRISPRi Perturb-seq dataset, which is relatively small in scale. To better demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach, it would be beneficial for them to validate the method on larger datasets.
3. The authors' experiments are limited to gene ontology prediction, and it would be valuable for them to explore the performance of their method on other tasks, such as gene knockout effect prediction, to further demonstrate its versatility.

## Questions
1. How can we ensure the accuracy of the information retrieved from the large language model?
2. How can we evaluate the quality of the generated hypotheses?
3. How does the framework handle cases where there is no clear biological explanation for a particular cluster?
