# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a new variant of the Adam optimizer called Ano. The key idea is to decouple the update direction and the step size. The update direction is determined by the sign of the momentum, while the step size is scaled by the gradient norm. The authors also propose Anolog, which removes the sensitivity to the momentum coefficient by expanding its window over time via a logarithmic schedule. The paper provides non-convex convergence guarantees for Ano and evaluates its performance on computer vision, natural language processing, and deep reinforcement learning tasks.

## Strengths
1. The paper addresses an important problem in the field of deep learning, namely the robustness of optimizers to gradient noise and non-stationarity. The proposed Ano and Anolog optimizers are well-motivated and innovative solutions to this problem.

2. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of Ano in the non-convex setting, establishing a convergence rate of $\mathcal{O}(k^{-1/4})$ under standard assumptions. This analysis is a valuable contribution to the field and helps to understand the behavior of the proposed optimizers.

3. The experimental evaluation of Ano and Anolog is comprehensive and covers a wide range of tasks and domains. The results demonstrate that the proposed optimizers provide substantial gains in noisy and non-stationary regimes, such as reinforcement learning, while remaining competitive on low-noise tasks.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed comparison of the computational complexity of Ano and Anolog with other optimizers. It would be useful to know how the proposed optimizers affect the computational cost of training deep learning models.

2. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the sensitivity of Ano and Anolog to hyperparameters. It would be useful to know how the performance of the proposed optimizers varies with different hyperparameter settings.

3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the stability of Ano and Anolog. It would be useful to know how the proposed optimizers affect the stability of training deep learning models.

## Questions
1. How does the computational complexity of Ano and Anolog compare to other optimizers, such as Adam and SGD?

2. How sensitive are Ano and Anolog to different hyperparameter settings, such as the learning rate and the momentum coefficient?

3. How do Ano and Anolog affect the stability of training deep learning models?

4. How do Ano and Anolog compare to other state-of-the-art optimizers, such as Adan and Lion, in terms of performance and robustness?
