# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a novel approach to generating realistic large-scale human mobility trajectories. The key idea is to factorize the generation process into spatial occupancy priors and temporal dynamics. The spatial priors represent where people move, while the temporal dynamics capture how people move. This separation allows for better generalization across different geographic regions. The model is evaluated on three major urban datasets and shows improved performance compared to existing methods.

## Strengths
1. The paper addresses an important practical problem of generating realistic large-scale human mobility trajectories. The proposed method has potential applications in various domains, including urban planning, transportation, and public health.
2. The paper is well-organized and clearly written. The authors provide a thorough review of existing methods and clearly explain the motivation and design choices for their proposed approach.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the limitations of the proposed method. It would be valuable to discuss potential scenarios where the method may not perform well, such as highly dynamic environments or areas with limited mobility data.
2. The paper could benefit from a more extensive comparison with existing methods. While the authors compare their approach with several baseline methods, there are other recent works in the field that could be included in the evaluation.

## Questions
1. How does the computational complexity of TDDM compare to other trajectory generation methods? Is it feasible to use TDDM for real-time trajectory generation or on-device applications?
2. How does TDDM handle highly dynamic environments or areas with limited mobility data? Are there any specific challenges or limitations in such scenarios?
3. How does TDDM perform on more complex urban mobility patterns, such as those involving public transportation, ride-sharing, or car-sharing? The current evaluation focuses on individual trajectories; how would TDDM handle aggregated mobility patterns?
