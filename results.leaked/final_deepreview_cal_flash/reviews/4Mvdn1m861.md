## Final Review

## Summary
This paper proposes TokenCount, a training-free object counting framework that works entirely with the off-the-shelf Segment Anything Model (SAM) without fine-tuning or auxiliary models. It introduces two components: (1) a probabilistic prompt generation stage that models the likelihood of object locations as a probability distribution and iteratively samples prompts, and (2) an output token-based verification stage that uses SAM's decoder output tokens with the TS-SS similarity metric to distinguish positive from negative detections. Experiments on FSC-147 and CARPK show the method achieves competitive results among training-free approaches, with MAE of 16.25 on FSC-147 (best among SAM-only methods) and MAE of 4.68 on CARPK (beating all supervised methods listed).

## Strengths

1. **Novel repurposing of SAM output tokens for counting without architectural modifications.** The idea of interpreting SAM's internal output tokens (which jointly encode semantic and positional information) as a verification signal for counting is genuinely novel. The paper shows that output tokens separate positive from negative instances more cleanly than image embeddings (Figure 5, Table 5), and that the TS-SS metric tailored to their dual nature outperforms cosine similarity, dot product, and L2 distance (Table 3). This is a clean, principled contribution.

2. **Probabilistic prompt generation is well-motivated and ablated.** The iterative probability-guided sampling (Section 3.1) is a reasoned improvement over uniform grid or superpixel prompting. The ablation study (Table 4) systematically explores the trade-off between iteration count, prompts per iteration, mean prompts used, and accuracy, giving practitioners actionable guidance. The early stopping mechanism (IoU-based with patience) is a practical addition.

3. **Strong CARPK performance.** On CARPK, the method achieves MAE 4.68 / RMSE 6.13, outperforming all seven listed supervised methods (including BMNet+ at 5.76 and SAFECOUNT at 5.33) and all training-free methods except TFCAC. This is a genuinely impressive result for a training-free approach.

4. **Comprehensive ablation structure.** Tables 3–5 isolate three key design choices (verification metric, iterative sampling parameters, feature space), providing clear causal evidence that each component contributes to overall accuracy. This level of ablation is a strength of the paper.

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **Overclaiming in the abstract.** The abstract states that the method achieves "superior accuracy … outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods." This is contradicted by Table 1: on FSC-147, training-based methods PseCo (MAE 13.05), LOCA (10.79), SAFECOUNT (14.32), and BMNet+ (14.62) all beat the reported 16.25, as does the training-free TFCAC (12.26). The body of the paper is more measured (e.g., "best performance among training-free SAM only methods" in Section 4.2), but the abstract misrepresents the contribution. This must be corrected for the paper to be credible.

2. **No variance reporting for a stochastic method.** The prompt generation stage samples from a multinomial probability distribution (Section 3.1), making the entire pipeline stochastic. Yet every reported number is a single point estimate with no measure of variability (standard deviation, confidence intervals, or multiple runs). This is critical because (a) the claimed improvement over cosine similarity is only 0.23 MAE (16.48 → 16.25), and (b) Table 4 shows non-monotonic behavior (16-iter/128-prompt: 16.25; 32-iter/128-prompt: 16.65) that could reflect instability. Without variance, the significance of any claimed improvement cannot be assessed.

3. **Efficiency claims are unsupported by comparative data.** The paper repeatedly claims computational efficiency and asserts "more than twice as fast" as TFCAC (Section 4.2), yet provides no inference-time measurements for any other method under the same hardware. Only a single absolute number (1.69s/image on FSC-147) is reported. Without a direct timing comparison on identical hardware, the efficiency advantage remains an unsubstantiated assertion.

4. **Probability distribution update rule is underspecified.** Section 3.1 states that when a prompt is verified as negative, "the value of the probability distribution at that mask region is decreased" — but provides no quantitative factor or rule. Is the value halved? Set to a fraction? Scaled by similarity? Re-normalized after updates? Figure 3 gives a concrete example but no formula. This vagueness prevents exact replication and is a meaningful reproducibility gap.

### Minor

1. **Temperature parameter ambiguity.** The implementation mentions a "t-scale factor of 0.066" (Section 4.1) but does not explain how this relates to τ in Equation (1) or how it was selected beyond "validation experiments." The range of values explored and the specific validation split should be reported.

2. **Verification threshold not justified.** The TS-SS threshold of 300 is said to be "based on validation experiments" (Section 4.1), but no sensitivity analysis or validation procedure is described. A brief plot or table showing the impact of threshold variation on MAE would strengthen the paper.

3. **Non-monotonic trend in Table 4 not discussed.** With 128 prompts per iteration, MAE goes from 18.90 (8 iter) → 16.25 (16 iter) → 16.65 (32 iter). The increase at 32 iterations is not commented on. This may indicate over-counting or instability that the authors should explain.

4. **Figure 5 caption is confusing.** The caption describes a 2×2 grid layout but the text refers to "second column" and "third column" in a way that does not clearly match the described layout. This needs correction for clarity.
