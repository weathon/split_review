# Final Review

## Summary

This paper proposes a Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE) metric to quantify imbalance between trend and seasonal components in contrastive time-series representations, and uses it to adaptively weight the loss terms of a downstream model (CoST). The diagnostic is validated on TS2Vec with synthetic data (Table 1), and the asymmetric perceptual weighting (APW) is evaluated on forecasting benchmarks. The core idea — diagnosing and rebalancing semantic components via a directional recoverability measure — is conceptually interesting, but the experimental validation has significant gaps that prevent the paper from substantiating its claims.

---

## Strengths

1. **The SDE diagnostic on TS2Vec is well-motivated and informative.** Table 1 shows that when trend/seasonal amplitude ratios vary from 0.1 to 10, TS2Vec's embeddings exhibit highly asymmetric SDE values (Δ ranging from −1.29 to +1.11). This cleanly confirms that semantic imbalance exists in standard contrastive time-series models and grounds the motivation in measurable evidence.

2. **The ablation showing naive SDE regularization is ineffective (Table 2) is honest and instructive.** The paper tests direct SDE regularization and reports that it fails to improve performance (MSE on ETTm1, Electricity, Weather either matches or slightly worsens). This negative result strengthens the narrative: SDE is a diagnostic, not a training objective, motivating the more sophisticated APW approach.

3. **The plug-in design is lightweight.** APW adds only a small MLP fusion layer and a scalar reweighting on top of CoST's existing contrastive losses, requiring no architectural changes. This makes the mechanism easy to adopt if validated.

---

## Weaknesses

### Major

1. **SDE values are not reported for the main experiment, despite explicit claims to the contrary.**  
   Section 4.4.4 states: "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance on ETT, Electricity, and Weather datasets." Table 3 contains **only MSE and MAE** — no SDE values appear anywhere. The conclusion claims "consistently lower SDE values," yet zero SDE measurements are provided for the CoST+APW method on real benchmarks. This is not a minor omission; the paper's central diagnostic metric is never shown for the method that supposedly improves it.

2. **The MLP fusion layer changes what SDE measures, creating a confound.**  
   In the original SDE definition (Section 3.2), \(v(a+b) = f_\theta(a+b)\) — the encoder applied to the composite signal. In the CoST+APW pipeline (Section 4.4.2), \(v(a+b) = g_\phi([v(a)||v(b)])\) — a learned nonlinear MLP on concatenated component embeddings. These are fundamentally different objects. The SDE computed via the MLP could reflect the MLP's capacity to reconstruct components from concatenated features rather than any property of the encoder's representation. The method introduces the MLP, the SDE computation, and the weighting simultaneously with no ablation to isolate the contribution of each component.

3. **The paper never establishes that CoST suffers from the semantic imbalance it aims to fix.**  
   The SDE analysis (Table 1) is performed exclusively on TS2Vec with synthetic data. CoST is a multi-view model with explicit frequency-domain objectives, which already differs substantially from TS2Vec. The APW method is applied to CoST without ever measuring whether CoST's representations are imbalanced. Without this diagnostic, the motivation for the fix is unsubstantiated for the actual target model.

4. **Forecasting results are mixed and the claims of "consistent gains" and "superior accuracy" are overclaimed.**  
   Against CoST specifically, CoST+APW wins on roughly 15 of 25 horizon/dataset combinations but loses on several long-horizon settings. More critically, on Electricity (horizons 168–720) and Weather (most horizons), CoST+APW is often the *worst* method among the four compared, sometimes by a wide margin (e.g., Electricity 720: CoST+APW MSE=1.010 vs. TNC=0.859, a ≈18% relative degradation). The conclusion's phrasing "yielding consistently lower SDE values and improved forecasting accuracy across multiple benchmarks" is not supported by the data presented.

5. **Key hyperparameters \(\gamma, \gamma'\) are never reported.**  
   These control the sensitivity of the reweighting and directly determine how aggressively the method compensates for imbalance. Without their values, the experiments cannot be reproduced and it is unclear whether the method is robust to their choice.

### Minor

6. **Nomenclature inconsistency.** The metric is called "Semantic Separability Error" (Section 3.2), "Semantic Decomposition Error" (Section 4.1), and "Semantic Disentanglement Error" (title/abstract). While these refer to the same quantity, the shifting terminology is confusing.

7. **Inconsistency between Section 3.4 and Figure 1 caption.** Section 3.4 gives the trend weight as \(1 + \gamma' \cdot (-\Delta)\), while Figure 1 caption simplifies to \(1 - \gamma\Delta\). The discrepancy is minor but suggests a lack of care in presentation.

8. **Table formatting.** The dataset column lists "ETT1" twice (for ETTh1 and ETTm1) without distinguishing them, making the table harder to read.

9. **No standard deviations or significance tests.** Many claimed wins involve tiny margins (e.g., < 0.005 MSE), and without error bars it is impossible to assess whether these differences are meaningful.

---

## Nice-to-Haves

- Report SDE values for CoST vs. CoST+APW on real benchmarks, aggregated over multiple seeds.
- Ablate the MLP fusion layer to isolate the effect of the weighting from the nonlinear transformation.
- Show the evolution of Δ during training and its correlation with downstream performance.
- Either justify the linear-subtractability assumption underlying SDE or relax it in the metric definition.

---

## Removed Points

These points were considered and removed (with justifications):

- *"The paper wins on only 9 of 25 horizon/dataset combinations."* — The harsh critic's count (9/25) is factually incorrect. The actual count of global-best wins is about 11/25 against all methods, and about 15/25 against CoST alone. The underlying point (mixed results) is valid and retained as Major point 4; the incorrect number is removed.
- *"SDE definition assumes strict linear subtractability v(a+b) ≈ v(a) + v(b)."* — The cosine-based formulation only requires \(v(a+b) - v(b)\) to be directionally aligned with \(v(a)\), which is substantially weaker than linearity. This criticism overstates the assumption and is removed.
- *"Reproducibility concerns about missing code/appendix."* — The rules forbid reproducibility criticisms based on missing supplementary materials. Removed.

## Novel Insights

Beyond the paper's own contributions, the reviews surface a genuinely novel observation: the paper's narrative has a structural "model mismatch" between the diagnostic (TS2Vec) and the treatment (CoST) that is uncommon to see in the literature. Most methods diagnose and fix the same model; this paper diagnoses one model and fixes another without bridging the gap. This pattern — using an analysis on model A to motivate a modification on model B — is fragile and should be guarded against in future work. Additionally, the honest reporting of a negative result (Table 2, naive SDE regularization fails) is a useful methodological lesson: diagnostic metrics do not automatically make good training objectives, even when the diagnosis is correct.

---

## Suggestions

1. Report SDE values for CoST and CoST+APW on all benchmark datasets, with variance across seeds. This is the single most important addition: without it, the claimed mechanism is unvalidated.
2. Add an ablation removing the MLP and computing SDE directly from encoder outputs on decomposed signals (matching the TS2Vec setup), so the reader can isolate the weighting effect.
3. Apply the SDE diagnostic to CoST first (with synthetic or real data) to establish that the target model actually has the imbalance problem.
4. Tone down the claims in the conclusion. The results on Electricity and Weather do not support "consistently lower SDE values and improved forecasting accuracy across multiple benchmarks."
5. Report the values of \(\gamma, \gamma'\) and include a sensitivity analysis.
