## Summary

This paper presents *Calgacus*, a simple protocol that uses an LLM to hide an arbitrary text $e$ inside a generated text $s$ of the same token length. The method records the per-token probability ranks of $e$ under an LLM, then generates $s$ from a secret prompt $k$ by selecting the token at that same rank at each step rather than sampling. The authors argue this demonstrates a radical decoupling of text from authorial intent, and illustrate a concrete AI-safety scenario: encoding unfiltered LLM outputs inside compliant ones. The paper is a method/position hybrid: it proposes a technique but primarily explores its philosophical and safety implications.

## Strengths

- **Clever, simple method with full capacity**: The rank-copying protocol is elegant and achieves a 1:1 token ratio between hidden and cover text — a property the paper correctly identifies as distinguishing it from prior LLM-based steganography. The worked example (Figure 1) where a political critique is hidden inside a cooking recipe is striking and directly validates the core claim.

- **Quantitative evidence that stegotexts are plausible**: Using 1000 Reddit posts as a reference distribution, the log-probabilities of 300 stegotexts (3 source texts × 100 prompts each) generated by Llama 3 8B fall within the same range as real texts, while random ASCII or random-English-word strings do not (Figure 4). This provides meaningful, though narrow, empirical support.

- **Insightful mechanistic analysis of the probability gap**: The paper's explanation (Figure 5, "Low entropy token choices" paragraph) of why stegotexts are less probable than their originals — rank-1 tokens appear less frequently than their intrinsic probability would suggest — is a genuinely illuminating observation about the protocol's inner workings.

- **Concrete and thought-provoking AI-safety scenario**: The "Unaligned chatbots disguised as aligned chatbots" play (Section 4) is specific, well-structured, and grounded in a real example (gas-meter tampering query). It makes the safety implications tangible rather than abstract.

- **Well-written and accessible**: The paper is clear, engaging, and ties its technical contribution to broader philosophical questions about intentionality, hallucinations, and the nature of LLM knowledge — without losing the thread of the technical argument.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

- **The empirical evaluation is too narrow to support the paper's headline claims.** The quantitative evaluation (Section 3, Figure 4) rests on only three source texts, each exactly 85 tokens, encoded with 100 prompts, evaluated on a single LLM (Llama 3 8B). Log-probability under the same LLM is the sole quality metric. This is a pilot experiment, not a demonstration that the method reliably produces coherent, on-topic, steerable output for arbitrary source material or at the lengths the paper invokes (e.g., "an entire article can be encoded"). The abstract's claim of "high-quality results" is not adequately supported by the evidence presented. The paper acknowledges the method's limitations ("the protocol does not guarantee that every generated stegotext will be coherent or steered as intended"), but its headline framing overstates what has been demonstrated.

### Minor

- **No systematic analysis of steerability.** The paper claims the secret prompt $k$ steers the topic and style of $s$, and the qualitative examples are compelling, but the 100-prompt experiment never analyzes the relationship between prompt content and output quality. A reader cannot tell whether some prompts produce incoherent output while still having acceptable log-probability by repeating boilerplate.

- **The detectability question is incompletely addressed.** The paper shows that LLMs can distinguish original texts from stegotexts on average via log-probability, but it does not investigate whether the *presence of a hidden message* can be detected — e.g., by training a classifier on real vs. encoded texts. This is a natural question for a steganographic protocol and answering it (even with a negative result) would strengthen the paper's security and deniability discussion. The paper is explicitly modest about formal steganographic guarantees, so this is not a fatal gap, but it leaves a key practical question open.

- **Security analysis is informal.** The brute-force bound $O(d^{|k|})$ and the deniability argument (Section 3.1) are sensible sketches but not rigorous. The relationship between $k$ length/randomness and stegotext quality is unexplored; the deniability example (Figure 15) is a single anecdote rather than a systematic check. For a paper that draws strong implications about concealment, a more thorough treatment would be expected.

- **The $k'$ variation is mentioned but never evaluated.** The optional pre-prompt for $e$ could significantly affect output quality and key length, but its effect is left entirely to the reader's speculation.

### Trivial

- The deterministic-LM requirement (encoder and decoder must run identical LLM inference) is acknowledged only in passing and deserves a more prominent discussion given its practical significance.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Evaluate across a larger and more varied set of source texts (different lengths, domains) with a systematic variation of steering prompts. Include human judgments or an LLM-based judge for coherence and prompt fidelity alongside log-probability.
- Add a basic detectability experiment (e.g., a classifier trained to distinguish real from stegotexts) to put the security discussion on firmer ground.
- Demonstrate the safety scenario (Section 4) at fuller scale with a real aligned/unfiltered model pair and quantitative plausibility metrics.
- Provide a scatter plot or analysis relating properties of $e$ (e.g., average rank) to $s$ quality, which could help users predict when the protocol will succeed.

## Novel Insights

The paper's most novel insight is the connection it draws between its rank-copying protocol and the nature of standard LLM text generation: if stochastic sampling is itself an arbitrary constraint, then *all* LLM-generated text can be viewed through the lens of the protocol — as text produced under an external constraint rather than from authorial intention. This reframing of hallucinations as a "void of intention" rather than a failure of factuality is creative and, while essayistic, offers a genuinely fresh perspective on a well-worn problem. The observation that even a fully aligned model can serve as a conduit for knowledge it supposedly cannot express (the safety scenario) is also a concrete and provocative contribution to alignment discourse.

## Suggestions

- The paper would benefit from clearly separating empirical claims from philosophical/metaphorical ones. Adding a short subsection that states "what we have empirically demonstrated" vs. "what this suggests conceptually" would help readers navigate the hybrid genre.
- Consider a brief quantitative comparison with existing LLM-based steganography methods' capacity (even rough numbers) to ground the "full capacity" claim.
