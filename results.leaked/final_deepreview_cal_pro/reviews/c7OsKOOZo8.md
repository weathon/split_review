## Summary

This paper proposes an end-to-end framework for multi-view diabetic retinopathy (DR) grading that introduces two modules: GALP, which self-generates lesion proposals from stage-wise auxiliary classifiers via class activation maps, and LGRF, which performs cross-view fusion using a gated mixture-of-experts and Top-K-weighted cross-view attention over those proposals. The key motivation is to reduce dependency on costly external annotations (lesion maps, vessel masks, landmark coordinates) while preserving competitive grading accuracy. Results on MFIDDR (four-view) and DRTiD (two-view) show that the annotation-free variant matches or exceeds several externally informed baselines.

## Strengths

- **Annotation-free performance is genuinely competitive.** On MFIDDR, "Ours (w/o lesion)" achieves 83.9% accuracy, outperforming all end-to-end baselines and several externally informed methods including LFMVDR (with lesion, 82.2%) and CVSA (with vessel, 82.6%). On DRTiD, it reaches 76.0% accuracy, surpassing the externally informed CrossFiT (75.6%). (Tables 1 and 3)

- **The ablation study cleanly demonstrates that each proposed module matters.** Removing GALP drops accuracy from 83.9% to 82.7%; removing the expert pool drops it to 82.6%; removing LGRF entirely drops it to 82.3% (Table 4). These are consistent, non-trivial gaps that support the value of the architectural contributions.

- **The framework is architecturally flexible.** When external lesion annotations are available, the method seamlessly integrates them via SPADE and achieves a new SOTA of 84.6% accuracy on MFIDDR (Table 1), demonstrating that the architecture is not brittle to the presence or absence of side information.

- **Grade-wise evaluation is thorough.** Table 2 reports per-grade F1, precision, and specificity, revealing strong sensitivity on mild DR (Grade 1 F1 = 69.7% without lesion), which is clinically important for early screening where end-to-end models often underperform.

## Weaknesses

### Major

- **The central narrative—that the self-derived proposals capture *lesions* specifically—is not validated.** The paper's core story is that GALP generates "lesion proposals" that recover small, low-contrast lesions otherwise lost to striding/tokenization. However, no experiment tests whether the proposals actually correspond to lesions. The MFIDDR dataset includes lesion segmentation masks (line 243: "The provider also releases lesion segmentation masks generated by a segmentation model"), yet the paper never computes overlap (IoU, recall, precision) between GALP proposals and these masks, nor does it provide qualitative overlays. The auxiliary classifiers are supervised only by global grade labels (Eq. 2), so the resulting CAMs will highlight any grade-discriminative patterns—which could include vessels, the optic disc, or image-level artifacts. Consequently, the performance gains observed when GALP is ablated could arise from deeper supervision or stronger feature learning that has nothing to do with lesion localization *per se*. The ablation demonstrates that GALP helps, but not *why* it helps or whether the mechanism matches the claimed narrative. The authors should either (a) provide direct evidence linking proposals to lesions, or (b) temper the "lesion proposal" phrasing and acknowledge this as an assumption.

### Minor

- **SOTA comparisons are confounded by backbone variation, and no statistical measures are reported.** The method uses a Swin-B backbone, while the baselines it is compared against use RETFound's ViT, MVCINN's CNN, MVCNN's VGG/ResNet, etc. No baseline is re-implemented with the same Swin-B backbone and identical training recipe, making it impossible to isolate whether gains come from the proposed modules or from a stronger backbone. Additionally, no confidence intervals, error bars, or statistical tests accompany any result; on datasets of a few thousand eyes, accuracy differences as small as 0.4% (e.g., the margin over CrossFiT on DRTiD) may not be reliable.

- **The ablation conflates auxiliary supervision with proposal selection.** The "w/o GALP" condition (Table 4) removes the entire module, including both the auxiliary classification loss and the proposal mechanism. Without an additional condition that retains auxiliary classifiers but replaces learned proposals with uniform/random region tokens, we cannot separate the well-known benefits of deep supervision from the specific contribution of the self-derived proposal selection.

- **Interpretability claims are entirely unsubstantiated.** The paper lists "higher model interpretability" as an advantage of externally informed models in Figure 1, claims interpretability as a contribution (contribution #2, line 102: "superior robustness and interpretability"), yet provides zero attention maps, proposal overlays, or any qualitative analysis. This claim should either be supported with visualizations or removed.

- **No limitations section.** The paper does not acknowledge any failure modes, such as the unvalidated assumption that CAM activations correspond to lesions, the reliance on cyclic adjacent view pairing (j = i+1 mod N, with no justification or comparison to other configurations), or the additional computational cost of running multiple experts and auxiliary heads at every stage.

- **Hyperparameter study is limited to MFIDDR.** Figure 3 tunes α, K₂, and M only on the four-view dataset. There is no evidence these settings transfer to DRTiD, which uses different image resolutions and a different number of views.

- **Computational cost is not reported.** The method adds MoE routing, multi-stage auxiliary classifiers, and cross-view attention. No parameter count, FLOPs, or inference latency comparison against baselines is provided.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Overlay top-K proposals on fundus images alongside available lesion masks (from MFIDDR) to give readers a qualitative sense of what GALP is attending to.
- Add an ablation that retains auxiliary classifiers but replaces selected proposals with uniform grid tokens of the same number to isolate the proposal-selection mechanism from deep supervision.
- Perform subgroup analysis stratified by lesion size or type to test the specific claim that the method helps on small, subtle lesions.
- Discuss alternative cross-view pairing strategies (all-to-all, learned) beyond the cyclic adjacent default.

## Suggestions

- The single most impactful revision would be to validate the "lesion proposal" claim. Since MFIDDR provides lesion masks, compute the spatial overlap between top-K GALP proposals and those masks. Even a modest IoU, combined with qualitative overlays, would transform the story from "we assume these are lesions" to "we show these correspond to lesions." If the overlap is poor, the authors should recalibrate their narrative to "grade-relevant region proposals" and discuss what the model is actually attending to.
- Add standard deviations across at least 3 random seeds for the main results. This is a low-effort change that substantially increases confidence in the reported margins.
- Either remove the interpretability claim or add a figure with proposal overlays and attention maps for a few representative cases. An unsupported claim is worse than no claim.
