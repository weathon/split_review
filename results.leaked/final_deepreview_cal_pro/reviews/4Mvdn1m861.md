## Summary
This paper proposes TokenCount, a training-free object counting framework built entirely on SAM. It introduces two components: (1) a probabilistic prompt generation stage that iteratively samples point prompts from a similarity-based distribution, and (2) an output token-based verification stage that uses SAM's decoder output tokens and a TS-SS similarity metric to distinguish positive from negative instances. On FSC-147 (MAE 16.25) the method achieves the best performance among SAM-only training-free approaches, and on CARPK (MAE 4.68) it surpasses all supervised baselines.

## Strengths
- **Novel output-token verification idea:** The core insight — that SAM's decoder output tokens encode both semantic and positional information and can serve as a verification criterion — is genuinely novel and well-motivated. Table 5 confirms output-token features outperform image-embedding features (MAE 16.25 vs 16.94), and Figure 5 provides qualitative evidence via PCA that output tokens better separate positive and negative instances.

- **Strong CARPK results:** On CARPK the method achieves MAE 4.68 and RMSE 6.13, outperforming all supervised baselines listed in Table 1 (e.g., BMNet+ MAE 5.76, SAFECOUNT MAE 5.33, LOCA MAE 9.97). This is a notable result for a training-free approach.

- **Computational efficiency:** The method runs entirely on SAM without auxiliary models, averaging 1.69 seconds per image on FSC-147, making it practical for deployment.

- **Reasonable ablation coverage:** The paper ablates the verification metric (Table 3), iteration/prompt counts (Table 4), and output-token vs. image-embedding spaces (Table 5), providing evidence for key design choices.

## Weaknesses

### Major
- **Abstract claims are overstated.** The abstract states the method achieves "superior accuracy with an MAE of 16.25, outperforming existing training-based and training-free counting methods." This is incorrect: TFCAC, a training-free method, achieves MAE 12.26 on FSC-147 and 4.39 on CARPK — both better than the proposed method. The paper's own Section 4.2 acknowledges TFCAC's superiority, making the abstract's unqualified claim misleading. The claim should be narrowed to SAM-only methods or qualified with the efficiency–accuracy tradeoff.

- **The probabilistic prompt generation stage is not compared against simpler baselines.** The paper claims this stage "intelligently explores the image" and "reduces redundant background sampling," but no ablation compares it to a deterministic grid, superpixel, or uniform-random baseline. Table 4 only varies internal parameters (iterations, prompts-per-iteration). The implicit comparison via TFOC or raw SAM (which use simpler prompting) conflates the prompt-generation contribution with the verification-stage contribution. Isolating this would substantially strengthen the evidence.

### Minor
- **The improvement from TS-SS over cosine similarity is marginal and its significance is unverified.** Table 3 shows TS-SS (16.25) is only 0.23 MAE better than cosine similarity (16.48) and 0.28 better than L2 distance (16.53). No confidence intervals, standard deviations, or statistical tests are reported. On 1190 test images, this difference could easily be noise. The paper should either provide statistical evidence or acknowledge the gain is small. (The gain over dot product — 3.33 MAE — is substantial, so the direction is correct.)

- **The verification decision rule is underspecified.** The paper mentions a "token verification threshold to 300" (Section 4.1) but does not explain how TS-SS similarity scores are aggregated across multiple exemplar tokens to make a binary positive/negative decision. This hampers reproducibility.

- **The temperature parameter τ is unreported.** Equation 1 introduces temperature scaling with a parameter τ that controls the sharpness of the prompt-sampling distribution, but its value is never stated and no sensitivity analysis is provided.

- **No failure case analysis despite high RMSE.** The FSC-147 RMSE of 133.11 is substantially higher than training-based methods (LOCA: 56.97) and TFCAC (56.33). While the RMSE is comparable to other SAM-only training-free methods, the paper shows only two cherry-picked success cases (Figure 4) and does not discuss or visualize failure modes. Given that the Discussion mentions performance degradation with dense small instances, quantitative characterization of these failure cases would add valuable insight.

- **Inference-time comparison with TFCAC is unsubstantiated.** Section 4.2 claims the method is "more than twice as fast" as TFCAC, but TFCAC's actual inference time is never reported — only the proposed method's time (1.69s) is given.

### Trivial
- The claim in Section 3.1 that the method "starts from nearby positive objects and gradually expands the search range to more distant objects" describes an emergent behavior of the iterative sampling rather than an explicit mechanism (sampling is from a global multinomial distribution). The description could be clarified to avoid implying an explicit locality constraint.

## Nice-to-Haves
- Adding a grid-prompt or superpixel-prompt baseline (keeping the output-token verification identical) would cleanly isolate the contribution of the probabilistic prompt generation stage.
- Reporting per-image error distributions or confidence intervals for the main metrics, particularly to contextualize the high RMSE values.
- A runtime measurement for TFCAC to support the efficiency claim.

## Removed Points
These points are flagged to be removed, treat them with caution:

- **"High RMSE contradicts claimed accuracy" (Harsh Critic #1):** The harsh critic frames the RMSE as "contradicting" accuracy and implying "catastrophic failure." In fact, the paper's RMSE (133.11) is comparable to other SAM-only training-free methods (TFOC: 132.16, OmniCount: 112.98, raw SAM: 137.50). MAE and RMSE measure different things; a high RMSE with moderate MAE indicates a long-tailed error distribution, not invalid results. The paper's RMSE is not uniquely problematic in its category, though the lack of failure-case analysis is a valid concern (retained above as Minor).

- **"Motivation undercut by reliance on image-embedding similarity" (Harsh Critic #6):** The prompt generation uses image embeddings for coarse localization while verification uses output tokens for final decisions. This is a coherent two-stage design, not a contradiction. The image-embedding similarity is adequate for prompt placement; the verification stage corrects its ambiguities. Retained only as a potential clarification point, not a weakness.

- **"The verification mechanism's local-to-global claim is not realized" (Harsh Critic #5, first part):** The iterative algorithm naturally expands from high-similarity regions outward as verified positives are removed from the distribution. The paper's description of this as "starts from nearby... gradually expands" is a reasonable characterization of the emergent behavior, not a false claim about an explicit locality mechanism. Moved to Trivial as a clarity suggestion.

- **"Only two cherry-picked success cases shown" (Harsh Critic, Section-by-Section):** True but subsumed under the Minor weakness about no failure case analysis.

- **"PCA visualization is anecdotal" (Harsh Critic, Section-by-Section):** Figure 5 is illustrative and supports the claim that output tokens separate better; it's not presented as a rigorous quantitative evaluation. The quantitative support comes from Table 5.

- Strength Finder's claim about "Table 4 providing a systematic ablation showing increasing prompts improves MAE" is accurate but Table 4 does not prove the probabilistic design is better than deterministic alternatives — this gap is captured in the Major weakness above.

## Novel Insights
The most interesting finding is that SAM's output tokens — the representations produced by the decoder for mask prediction — carry enough discriminative information to serve as a verification signal for object counting, and they outperform image-embedding features for this purpose. The paper demonstrates this quantitatively (Table 5) and visually (Figure 5, PCA). This suggests that foundation model decoders produce condensed representations that are useful beyond their original purpose, which could generalize to other downstream tasks that currently rely on encoder features.

## Suggestions
- Narrow the abstract's performance claim to "best among SAM-only training-free methods" or explicitly acknowledge TFCAC's accuracy–efficiency tradeoff.
- Add a grid-prompt or superpixel-prompt baseline to Table 4 (or as a separate experiment) to isolate the contribution of probabilistic prompt generation.
- Report τ's value and provide a brief sensitivity analysis or justification.
- Specify the TS-SS score aggregation rule across multiple exemplar tokens for binary verification decisions.
- Include at least 2–3 failure cases with analysis, especially from the high-RMSE tail, to give a complete picture of the method's limitations.
