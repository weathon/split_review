## Summary

This paper revisits the trade-off between watermark strength and speculative sampling efficiency in language models. It introduces a quantitative measure of watermark strength (expected KL divergence between watermarked and original token distributions), proves this measure governs p-value decay rates, and fully characterizes the trade-off as a Pareto frontier via constrained optimization. The key contribution is a principled mechanism (Algorithm 1) that injects pseudorandomness into draft-token acceptance, which Theorem 4.1 proves simultaneously achieves maximal watermark strength and maximal sampling efficiency — breaking a previously stated impossibility result. Experiments on Gumbel-max and SynthID watermarks with Llama and Gemma model pairs confirm efficiency is maintained and detectability is improved through detection methods that leverage the pseudorandom acceptance variable.

## Strengths

- **Rigorous quantitative reframing of watermark strength.** The paper moves beyond the prior binary treatment of strength by defining it as expected KL divergence (Def. 3.1) and proving it directly governs the exponential decay rate of p-values under the uniformly most powerful test (Theorem 3.1). This provides a principled information-theoretic foundation for the entire analysis.

- **Complete and clean trade-off characterization.** The Pareto frontier formulation (Def. 3.2, Eq. 8) and the explicit convex reformulation for linearly watermarked classes (Eq. 10) give a precise, quantitative landscape that was absent in prior work. Figure 1 illustrates this for Gumbel-max, SynthID, and prior class constructions from Hu & Huang (2024) and Dathathri et al. (2024).

- **Elegant mechanism with strong theoretical backing.** Algorithm 1's core insight — making the acceptance decision pseudorandom so the entire generation becomes a deterministic function of pseudorandom variables — is clever and well-motivated. Theorem 4.1 formally proves it achieves maximal strength (Ent(P)) and maximal efficiency (1 − TV(Q, P)) simultaneously, directly overturning the impossibility claimed in Hu & Huang (2024).

- **Practical detection strategies that exploit the new signal.** The Ars-τ and Bayes-MLP detectors (Section 4.2) are concrete, implementable methods that use the pseudorandom acceptance variable to select the correct test statistic, replacing the heuristic averaging of prior work. Figure 2 confirms these improve true positive rates at fixed false positive rate.

- **Empirical validation of the core claims.** The left panel of Figure 2 confirms Algorithm 1 matches standard speculative sampling efficiency. The middle and right panels show improved detectability over prior-based detection methods, with performance approaching the oracle bound as token length increases. Results are replicated across two model pairs (Llama-68M/7B and Gemma-2B/7B) and two watermarking schemes.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal

None.

### Major

None.

### Minor

- **No end-to-end empirical comparison against prior combined generation+detection pipelines.** The experiments compare detection methods (Ars-τ vs. Ars-Prior, Bayes-MLP vs. Bayes-Prior) applied to texts generated by Algorithm 1. The efficiency comparison (left panel of Fig. 2) does verify that Algorithm 1 matches standard speculative sampling, and the detection comparisons demonstrate that knowing the pseudorandom acceptance variable improves detectability. However, the paper does not empirically show where prior full pipelines — e.g., the combined speculative-sampling-plus-watermarking approaches of Hu & Huang (2024) or Dathathri et al. (2024) — land on the efficiency–detectability plane relative to the proposed method. The theoretical curves in Figure 1 (simulation-based) already illustrate where these prior classes sit, but empirical confirmation with real LLM outputs would make the practical improvement claim more concrete. This does not undermine the theoretical contribution (Theorem 4.1 already establishes the result formally), but it limits the strength of the empirical validation.

### Trivial

- **Theorem 4.1's degeneracy assumption and SynthID experiments.** Theorem 4.1 assumes the watermark decoder is degenerate (i.e., achieves maximal strength). The Gumbel-max watermark satisfies this, so the theoretical results apply fully. For SynthID with m=30, the decoder is non-degenerate, and the paper is transparent about this — it notes the strength gap in the Figure 1 discussion and explicitly lists extension to non-degenerate watermarks as future work (line 286). The experiments with SynthID (m=30) are a practical test case, not claimed to achieve the theoretical optimum, and they still show improved detectability. A brief sentence in the experimental section explicitly connecting this gap would help, but it does not affect the paper's core validity.

- **Residual distribution normalization in Algorithm 1.** The algorithm passes the unnormalized residual $(P-Q)_+$ to the decoder $\mathcal{S}$. In standard speculative sampling (Eq. 5), this quantity is explicitly normalized by $\sum_z (P_z - Q_z)_+$. For the Gumbel-max decoder this is harmless (the argmax is scale-invariant), but for SynthID it matters. The paper relies on the decoder $\mathcal{S}$ to handle this implicitly, which is a minor ambiguity worth clarifying.

## Nice-to-Haves

- An empirical estimate of the actual watermark strength (e.g., estimated mutual information between generated tokens and pseudorandom variables) for both Gumbel-max and SynthID (m=30) cases would help connect the theory more tightly to the experiments and quantify how close the SynthID case comes to the theoretical maximum.

- A brief discussion of whether exposing the acceptance variable $u_t$ to the detector introduces any practical concerns (e.g., if the pseudorandom seed were compromised) would round out the practical considerations, though this is outside the paper's stated scope.

## Novel Insights

The paper's reframing of watermark strength as expected KL divergence and the demonstration that this exactly equals the p-value decay rate (Theorem 3.1) is a genuinely novel connection that bridges information theory and statistical hypothesis testing for watermarking. The insight that pseudorandomizing the acceptance decision — rather than just the token sampling — is what closes the gap to maximal strength is also novel and non-obvious, as prior work treated acceptance randomness as irreducible.

## Suggestions

- Add a brief paragraph or footnote in Section 5 explicitly stating that the end-to-end empirical comparison against prior combined pipelines is left to future work (or provide it if computationally feasible), to preempt the natural question readers will have.
- Clarify in Algorithm 1 or its description whether the decoder $\mathcal{S}$ is expected to normalize $(P-Q)_+$ before sampling, or whether this is handled implicitly by the specific decoder choice.
