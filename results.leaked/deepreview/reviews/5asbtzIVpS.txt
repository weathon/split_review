# Review

## Summary
The paper presents a novel approach to graph learning using a forest-based framework, which aims to balance the trade-off between cost-effectiveness and achieving a global receptive field in graph neural networks. The authors propose a method where message passing on a graph is reinterpreted as transportation over spanning trees, facilitating efficient long-range information propagation. The framework employs a homophily estimator-based tree sampler to generate homophilous trees with higher probability and a tree aggregator to propagate global messages with linear complexity. The authors provide theoretical insights into the relationship between the accuracy of the homophily estimator and the quality of the induced tree distribution. Empirical results on semi-supervised node classification tasks demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art methods while maintaining efficiency.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a novel paradigm for graph learning that leverages the concept of spanning trees to efficiently capture long-range dependencies on a graph. This approach is innovative and offers a new perspective on graph neural network architecture.
2. The authors provide a theoretical foundation for their proposed framework, demonstrating an asymptotic relationship between the accuracy of the homophily estimator and the quality of the induced tree distribution. This theoretical insight adds depth to the understanding of the proposed method.
3. The empirical results presented in the paper show that the proposed framework achieves comparable or even better performance than state-of-the-art methods on semi-supervised node classification tasks while maintaining efficiency. This indicates the practical utility of the proposed approach.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the sensitivity of the proposed method to various hyperparameters, such as the number of spanning trees sampled or the parameters of the homophily estimator. Understanding how these hyperparameters affect the performance of the method is crucial for practical implementation.
2. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the interpretability of the proposed method. Understanding how the tree sampler and aggregator work in practice and how they contribute to the overall performance of the method is crucial for building trust in the proposed approach.

## Questions
1. Can you provide a more detailed analysis of the sensitivity of your proposed method to various hyperparameters, such as the number of spanning trees sampled or the parameters of the homophily estimator? How do these hyperparameters affect the performance of your method?
2. Can you provide a more detailed analysis of the interpretability of your proposed method? How do the tree sampler and aggregator work in practice, and how do they contribute to the overall performance of your method?
3. How does your proposed method compare to other state-of-the-art methods in terms of computational efficiency? Can you provide a more detailed analysis of the computational complexity of your method and how it scales with the size of the graph?
