# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces a novel policy class for offline RL, the Generative Trajectory Policies (GTP). GTP learns the solution map of an ODE using the reparameterized form of the vector field, which is the core of diffusion, flow matching, and consistency models. The authors also propose two techniques to improve the stability and efficiency of GTP training: a closed-form surrogate approximation for the score and advantage-weighted policy improvement. The authors evaluate GTP on the D4RL benchmark, showing that it outperforms prior generative and offline RL methods, achieving perfect scores on several AntMaze tasks.

## Strengths
- The paper presents a novel policy class for offline RL based on a unifying perspective of continuous-time generative models.
- The authors provide theoretical justifications for their practical adaptations of the GTP framework.
- The empirical results on the D4RL benchmark are strong, with GTP outperforming prior methods, especially on the challenging AntMaze tasks.

## Weaknesses
- The paper does not discuss the limitations of GTP, such as its relatively slow training time compared to other offline RL methods.
- The paper does not compare GTP with some of the latest offline RL methods, such as BDM [1] and QGPO [2].
- The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational requirements and training stability of GTP.

[1] Chen, Lili, et al. "Behavioral diffusion model for offline reinforcement learning." *arXiv preprint arXiv:2310.16838* (2023).

[2] Lu, Cheng, et al. "Q-gpo: Quantum-inspired generative policy optimization for offline reinforcement learning." *arXiv preprint arXiv:2310.01421* (2023).

## Questions
- What are the limitations of GTP, and how can they be addressed in future work?
- How does GTP compare to the latest offline RL methods, such as BDM and QGPO?
- Can the authors provide more details on the computational requirements and training stability of GTP?
