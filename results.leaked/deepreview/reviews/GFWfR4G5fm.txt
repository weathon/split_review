# Review

## Summary
The paper presents a novel framework called Test-Time Training for Supervised Causal Learning (TTT-SCL), which aims to address the limitations of static supervised causal learning (SCL) paradigms. The authors identify three key challenges: fragility under distribution shifts, failure in compositional generalization, and poor performance on real-world data. To overcome these issues, the TTT-SCL framework dynamically generates training data aligned with each test instance, using an Alignment of Distribution (AD) metric and sparsity constraints to ensure both structural and mechanistic alignment while maintaining causal minimality. The proposed method, Test-time Aligned Causal Training with Informed Construction (TACTIC), is evaluated on synthetic, pseudo-real, and real-world datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing SCL and traditional causal discovery methods.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The paper proposes a novel framework that dynamically generates training data aligned with each test instance, addressing the limitations of static supervised causal learning (SCL) paradigms.
3. The experimental results on synthetic, pseudo-real, and real-world datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method.

## Weaknesses
1. The experimental results on real-world causal graphs are limited. The paper only provides results on one real-world causal graph (Sachs). It would be beneficial to include results on more real-world causal graphs, such as those available in the bnlearn repository (https://www.bnlearn.com/bnrepository/).
2. The paper does not provide a theoretical analysis of the proposed method.
3. The paper does not provide a sensitivity analysis of the hyperparameter $\lambda$.
4. The paper does not provide a comparison of the computational complexity of the proposed method with the baseline methods.

## Questions
1. How does the performance of TACTIC compare on other real-world causal graphs from the bnlearn repository (https://www.bnlearn.com/bnrepository/)?
2. Can you provide a theoretical analysis of the proposed method?
3. How sensitive is the performance of TACTIC to the choice of the hyperparameter $\lambda$? Can you provide a sensitivity analysis?
4. Can you provide a comparison of the computational complexity of TACTIC with the baseline methods?
