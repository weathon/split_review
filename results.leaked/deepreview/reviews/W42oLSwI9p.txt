# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes three one-step diffusion-based approaches to solve integer linear programming problems. The proposed methods handle non-binary integer problems using a newly proposed iterative integer projection layer, eliminating the need for the costly problem transformation. An objective-guided sampling with momentum scheme is also proposed to improve the solution quality. Experiments demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms existing learning-based methods on both binary and non-binary instances and shows strong scalability compared to traditional solvers.

## Strengths
1. The paper proposes three one-step diffusion-based approaches to solve integer linear programming problems, which can handle non-binary integer problems without the need for costly problem transformation.
2. Experiments demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms existing learning-based methods on both binary and non-binary instances and shows strong scalability compared to traditional solvers.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed methods lack novelty. The consistency model, shortcut model, and mean flow model are all existing methods. The proposed objective-guided sampling is also similar to existing work.
2. The proposed methods are not very effective in solving ILP problems. The optimality gap is much larger than that of traditional solvers, and the computational cost of gradient-based search increases substantially with dataset size.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed method handle cases where the ILP problem has multiple optimal solutions? How does it ensure diversity in the generated solutions?
2. How sensitive are the proposed methods to the choice of hyperparameters, such as the number of projection iterations and the penalty coefficient?
3. How does the proposed method scale to very large ILP problems, and what are the limits of its applicability?
