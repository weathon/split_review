# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a novel test-time scaling method for discrete diffusion models, which leverage the Multiple-Try Metropolis (MTM) framework to refine misaligned intermediate states. The proposed method, IterRef, explicitly refines each state in situ, progressively steering it toward the optimal intermediate distribution. The authors evaluate IterRef on diverse discrete diffusion models and observe consistent improvements in reward-guided generation quality.

## Strengths
1. The proposed method, IterRef, is novel and tailored to the discrete diffusion models. It explicitly refines each state in situ, progressively steering it toward the optimal intermediate distribution. 
2. The authors provide a theoretical guarantee that iterative refinement sampling converges to the target distribution.
3. The authors evaluate IterRef on diverse discrete diffusion models and observe consistent improvements in reward-guided generation quality.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks a clear explanation of the practical implementation of IterRef, including the choice of hyperparameters and the computational cost associated with the MTM framework.
2. The paper does not provide a detailed comparison of IterRef with other test-time scaling methods for continuous diffusion models, such as those based on Gaussian diffusion.
3. The paper lacks a thorough analysis of the limitations of IterRef, including its performance in scenarios with low NFE settings or high-dimensional data.

## Questions
1. How does the choice of hyperparameters, such as the number of iterations k and the number of particles N, affect the performance of IterRef? Are there any guidelines for selecting these hyperparameters?
2. What is the computational cost associated with IterRef, especially for large-scale applications? How does it compare to other test-time scaling methods?
3. How does IterRef perform in scenarios with low NFE settings or high-dimensional data? Are there any specific challenges or limitations in these scenarios?
4. How does IterRef compare to other test-time scaling methods for continuous diffusion models, such as those based on Gaussian diffusion? Can IterRef be adapted for use with continuous diffusion models?
5. Are there any specific use cases or domains where IterRef excels or falls short? Can you provide examples to illustrate these cases?
