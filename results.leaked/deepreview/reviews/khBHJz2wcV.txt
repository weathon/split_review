# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a framework for fine-tuning flow-matching models to enforce physical constraints and solve inverse problems. The key contributions include a post-training method that minimizes weak-form residuals of governing PDEs to promote physical consistency, and a joint optimization strategy for generating physically valid solutions and estimating hidden parameters. The approach is validated on canonical PDE problems and natural image models, demonstrating improved satisfaction of physical constraints and accurate recovery of latent coefficients.

## Strengths
The paper presents a novel framework for integrating physical constraints into generative modeling through post-training enforcement of PDE constraints. The combination of weak-form residuals and adjoint-matching is theoretically sound and demonstrates robust performance across various PDE problems. The method is thoroughly evaluated with both qualitative and quantitative results, showing its effectiveness in preserving generative diversity while enforcing physical constraints.

## Weaknesses
While the paper demonstrates robust performance, the evaluation could be more comprehensive. The authors could provide more details on the limitations of their approach, particularly regarding the trade-offs between constraint enforcement and generative diversity. Additionally, a more extensive discussion on the generalizability of the method to other types of physical systems would strengthen the paper.

## Questions
- How does the proposed method handle complex boundary conditions that are non-homogeneous or time-dependent?
- Can the approach be extended to systems with coupled PDEs or multiple phases?
- What are the computational costs associated with the fine-tuning process, and how do they scale with the size of the problem?
