# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces an online multimodal learning (OML) framework that dynamically adapts to learn new multimodal concepts without forgetting prior knowledge. Inspired by neural structures in the human brain, the model includes ascending, descending, and lateral pathways, enabling effective multimodal integration and conflict resolution through user interaction. Experimental results demonstrate the model's strong performance in continuous multimodal learning tasks.

## Strengths
1.	The paper proposes a hierarchical modular network structure with ascending, descending, and lateral pathways, along with a conflict detection mechanism that enables the model to handle multimodal learning tasks with high flexibility and adaptability.
2.	The reference extraction algorithm effectively identifies the precise features associated with a given word, enhancing the model's accuracy in multimodal tasks.
3.	Experimental results demonstrate the model's strong performance across various multimodal tasks, validating its capabilities in continuous learning environments.

## Weaknesses
1.	The paper lacks sufficient explanation of the hierarchical modular network structure, especially regarding the distinction between ascending, descending, and lateral pathways, which may lead to confusion.
2.	Although the paper includes a reference extraction algorithm, it lacks a detailed explanation of the feature selection criteria and processes, which may impact the model's accuracy.
3.	The paper does not provide a detailed description of the conflict detection mechanism, including how conflicts are identified and resolved, which may affect the model's stability in continuous learning tasks.
4.	The experimental section lacks ablation studies to verify the contribution of each component of the model to the overall performance, making it difficult to assess the independent impact of the proposed modules.
5.	The paper does not provide detailed experimental settings or parameter selection, which may affect the reproducibility of the experiments.
