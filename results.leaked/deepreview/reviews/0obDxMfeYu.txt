# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a novel OOD detection method that leverages unlabeled data. The proposed method first identifies potential outliers from the unlabeled data and then uses both labeled ID data and the identified outliers to train an OOD classifier. The authors also provide theoretical guarantees for the proposed method. Experimental results on multiple datasets show that the proposed method outperforms several existing methods.

## Strengths
1. The proposed method is well-motivated. Using the median operation to identify potential outliers from unlabeled data is reasonable.
2. The authors provide theoretical guarantees for the proposed method.
3. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed method is similar to existing PUL (positive-unlabeled learning) methods. The difference is that the proposed method further uses the identified outliers to train an OOD classifier. However, this paper does not discuss PUL methods and compare the proposed method with them.
2. The proposed method uses the median operation to identify potential outliers from the unlabeled data. However, the proposed method does not use the identified outliers directly but uses additional ID data to train an OOD classifier with the identified outliers. Therefore, it is not sure whether the ID data or the outliers have a greater impact on the performance of the OOD classifier. 
3. The proposed method uses the average gradient of ID data as a reference to identify outliers from the unlabeled data. However, it is not sure whether the average gradient can represent the distribution of ID data.
4. The proposed method uses a greedy method to identify outliers from the unlabeled data. The greedy method is slow and may not find the optimal solution. 
5. The proposed method uses the L2-norm deviation between the average gradient and the median gradient to identify outliers. However, the L2-norm deviation may be sensitive to extreme values. 
6. The proposed method only uses the penultimate layer weights to identify outliers. However, the proposed method may miss some potential outliers if it only uses the penultimate layer weights.
7. The proposed method uses a simple linear layer to perform binary classification on the penultimate-layer features to identify outliers. However, the simple linear layer may not be able to handle high-dimensional features effectively.
