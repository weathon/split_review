# Review

## Summary
This paper investigates the safety vulnerabilities of diffusion language models (DLMs), particularly focusing on the priming vulnerability during the denoising process. The authors propose a new safety alignment method called Recovery Alignment (RA), which trains models to recover from harmful intermediate states back to safe responses. The paper demonstrates that RA effectively mitigates the priming vulnerability and improves robustness against jailbreak attacks while maintaining task performance across various benchmarks.

## Strengths
1. The paper identifies a critical safety vulnerability in DLMs, the priming vulnerability, which is not well understood. This vulnerability arises because the model is not trained to handle affirmative tokens for harmful queries at intermediate steps of the denoising process. The paper provides a thorough analysis of this vulnerability, demonstrating how it can be exploited by both hypothetical attackers who can intervene in the denoising process and more realistic attackers using optimization-based jailbreak attacks like GCG.

2. The authors propose a novel safety alignment method, Recovery Alignment (RA), specifically tailored to address the priming vulnerability. RA trains the model to recover from contaminated intermediate states containing affirmative tokens back to safe responses. This approach is innovative in that it explicitly models and trains for these contaminated states, which previous safety alignment methods did not address.

3. The paper provides a theoretical analysis of the priming vulnerability, deriving a lower bound on the attack objective that exploits this vulnerability. This theoretical foundation supports the empirical results and demonstrates the severity of the issue.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational cost and training time required for the proposed Recovery Alignment (RA) method. While it mentions that RA can be implemented using existing datasets and pretrained models, a more thorough discussion of the practical feasibility and scalability of the approach would be beneficial. 

2. The paper does not extensively explore the potential for adaptive attacks that might specifically target the Recovery Alignment (RA) method. While the proposed approach is effective against existing jailbreak attacks, it would be valuable to investigate whether attackers could develop strategies to circumvent the recovery mechanism, especially by exploiting the increasingly complex decision boundaries introduced by RA.

## Questions
1. Can you provide more details on the computational cost and training time required for the Recovery Alignment (RA) method? How does it compare to the cost of training the base diffusion language models?

2. Have you considered the potential for adaptive attacks that specifically target the RA method? Are there any preliminary findings or insights on the robustness of RA against such targeted attacks?
