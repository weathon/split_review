# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a prompt-adaptive strategy for Best-of-N alignment that can be implemented efficiently at inference time. The method consists of two stages: an exploratory phase and an adaptive allocation phase. The exploratory phase estimates the reward distribution for each prompt using a small exploration budget. The adaptive allocation phase uses these estimates to determine the optimal allocation of the remaining budget. The authors evaluate their method on three different datasets and demonstrate that it outperforms uniform allocations with the same inference budget. The adaptive strategy also remains competitive against uniform allocations with larger inference budgets and improves in performance as the batch size grows.

## Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The proposed method is simple and easy to implement, and it can be applied to any LM-RM combination.
- The experiments are comprehensive, covering a variety of different datasets and language models. The results show that the adaptive strategy outperforms uniform allocations with the same inference budget and remains competitive with larger inference budgets.

## Weaknesses
- The main weakness of this paper is the lack of novelty. The idea of adaptive Best-of-N sampling has been proposed before, and the main contribution of this paper is to provide an efficient implementation of this idea. While the proposed implementation is simple and easy to use, it does not introduce any new concepts or techniques. Therefore, it is unclear how this paper makes a significant contribution to the field.
- The proposed method assumes that the reward distributions are smooth and can be estimated accurately using kernel density estimation. However, this may not always be the case, especially for more complex tasks or for reward models with more complex reward functions. The authors should discuss the limitations of this assumption and potential ways to address it.
- The experiments are conducted on a limited set of language models and reward models. It would be beneficial to evaluate the proposed method on a more diverse set of models to ensure that the results are generalizable.

## Questions
- How does the performance of the proposed method vary with the quality of the reward model? It would be useful to evaluate the method on reward models with different levels of accuracy to see how it impacts the results.
- How does the proposed method perform on longer and more complex prompts? The current experiments are conducted on relatively short prompts, and it would be interesting to see how the method handles longer and more complex prompts that may require more samples to generate high-quality responses.
