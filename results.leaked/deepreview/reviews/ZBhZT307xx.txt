# Review

## Summary
This paper investigates the reliability of current verifiers in the field of reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), particularly in the context of mathematical reasoning. The authors conduct a comprehensive analysis of various verifiers, including rule-based and model-based, in both static evaluation and RL training scenarios. They reveal critical limitations of existing verifiers: rule-based verifiers suffer from significant false negatives, especially as the capabilities of policy models increase, while model-based verifiers are vulnerable to reward hacking, leading to artificially inflated training rewards. The paper emphasizes the importance of developing more robust verification systems that maintain accuracy without sacrificing robustness to enhance the reliability and effectiveness of RL systems for complex reasoning tasks.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-structured, with a clear problem statement, methodology, and findings. The logical flow makes it easy to follow the authors' arguments and understand their conclusions.

2. The authors provide a thorough analysis of various verifiers, including rule-based and model-based, in both static and dynamic settings. The comprehensive evaluation demonstrates the limitations of current verifiers and highlights the need for more robust solutions.

3. The paper addresses a critical issue in the field of RLVR and has important implications for the development of more reliable and trustworthy AI systems.

## Weaknesses
1. While the paper provides a detailed analysis of the limitations of existing verifiers, it could benefit from a more in-depth discussion of potential solutions or mitigation strategies. The authors could explore and propose some potential ideas for developing more robust verifiers.

2. The paper focuses primarily on mathematical reasoning tasks. It would be valuable to explore how these findings generalize to other domains or types of reasoning tasks.

3. The paper could benefit from a more detailed comparison with related work in the field of RLVR. The authors could discuss how their findings relate to or differ from previous studies on verifier robustness.

## Questions
1. What specific strategies or techniques could be employed to improve the robustness of model-based verifiers against reward hacking?

2. How might the findings of this study generalize to other reasoning domains beyond mathematical reasoning?

3. What are the potential trade-offs between verifier accuracy and robustness? Is there a way to balance these two aspects?
