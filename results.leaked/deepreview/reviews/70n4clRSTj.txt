# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a new benchmark, SpookyBench, to evaluate the temporal reasoning abilities of video-language models. The key idea is to encode information solely in the temporal domain, such that the individual frames contain only structured noise and no meaningful information. This forces the models to rely on temporal cues. The authors find that humans can recognize shapes, text, and patterns in these sequences with over 98% accuracy, but state-of-the-art VLMs achieve 0% accuracy. They conclude that VLMs are "time blind" and rely too much on frame-level spatial features.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a novel and interesting idea to isolate temporal reasoning in VLMs. 
2. The paper is well-written and easy to follow. 
3. The paper provides a thorough analysis of the benchmark and the model results, including the impact of frame rates and fine-tuning.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide any concrete suggestions or proposals for how to address the temporal reasoning gap between humans and VLMs. This could be a limitation, as it leaves open the question of how to use the benchmark to improve models.

## Questions
1. How does the benchmark account for different frame rates? For example, if a model is presented with a video at 1 FPS, would it be expected to perform better than a model presented with the same video at 30 FPS? 

2. How does the benchmark handle the fact that some objects may be naturally occluded or invisible for certain frames? For example, in the "man playing basketball" example, if the man is behind the basket for a few frames, would that count against the model's performance?
