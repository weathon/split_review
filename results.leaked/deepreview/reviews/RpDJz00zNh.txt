# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes an in-reasoning intervention framework, ConciseHint, to boost the efficient reasoning of large reasoning models. Different from existing methods that try to enhance the efficiency before the actual reasoning, this paper highlights a promising paradigm of performing intervention during the generation of the reasoning to make it more concise. ConciseHint injects learnable hints (manually designed or learned on the concise data) into the reasoning process to encourage conciseness. To avoid accuracy degradation for complex queries due to excessive hints, ConciseHint adaptively controls the injection intensity according to the complexity of the query. Besides, it dynamically adjusts the injection position to achieve a good computing-accuracy balance.

## Strengths
1. This paper focuses on an important problem, the inefficiency problem in reasoning LLMs.
2. The proposed method is easy to understand.
3. The proposed method is effective and can be integrated with existing methods.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed method is not very novel, as it is quite similar to self-reflection. The difference is that the proposed method injects additional hints, while self-reflection is done by the model itself. Also, the proposed method is more like a prompt engineering technique.
2. The proposed method introduces additional hints, which will increase the inference cost. The authors should report the additional inference cost.
3. The authors should report the performance of more complex tasks, such as MMLU, to show that the proposed method can still perform well on complex tasks.
