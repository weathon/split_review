# Review

## Summary
The paper investigates the role of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) in prompt optimization for complex tasks, using event extraction as a case study. The authors compare two LRMs (DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI o1) and two general-purpose Large Language Models (LLMs) (GPT-4o and GPT-4.5) as task models and prompt optimizers within a Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) framework. Their findings indicate that LRMs not only benefit from prompt optimization but also excel at generating optimized prompts for other models, leading to improved performance and faster convergence.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a novel study on prompt optimization specifically for LRMs, addressing a critical gap in existing research.
2. The authors conduct thorough experiments on event extraction and two other tasks, providing robust evidence for their claims.
3. The paper is well-organized and clearly written, with detailed explanations of the methodology and results.

## Weaknesses
1. While the paper focuses on LRMs, it would be beneficial to include a comparison with traditional prompt optimization techniques to contextualize the findings.
2. The paper could provide more insight into how the optimized prompts differ between LRMs and LLMs, potentially offering a more detailed qualitative analysis.

## Questions
1. How do the optimized prompts generated by LRMs differ in terms of structure and content compared to those generated by LLMs?
2. Could the authors provide more details on the computational costs associated with using LRMs for prompt optimization, particularly in comparison to LLMs?
