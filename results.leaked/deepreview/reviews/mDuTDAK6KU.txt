# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces KOALA, a novel adversarial detection method that leverages disagreements between KL divergence and $L_0$ distance metrics to identify adversarial examples. The approach is theoretically grounded, providing formal guarantees of correctness under specific conditions. Empirically, KOALA demonstrates strong performance on ResNet-18 with CIFAR-10 and CLIP on Tiny-ImageNet, achieving high precision and recall on theorem-compliant samples.

## Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The proposed method is theoretically grounded, providing formal guarantees of correctness under specific conditions.
- Empirically, KOALA demonstrates strong performance on ResNet-18 with CIFAR-10 and CLIP on Tiny-ImageNet, achieving high precision and recall on theorem-compliant samples.

## Weaknesses
- The proposed method requires fine-tuning the backbone encoder using clean images only. However, adversarial training typically involves training on both clean and adversarial images. It would be beneficial to clarify why the method does not require adversarial images during training.
- The paper only considers a limited set of attacks, specifically PGD, CW, and AutoAttack. It would be more comprehensive to include evaluations against additional attacks, such as black-box attacks, to demonstrate the method's robustness under different adversarial scenarios.
- The proposed method requires the computation of class prototypes, which may not be feasible for large-scale datasets. It would be helpful to discuss potential strategies for handling such cases or to provide empirical results on larger datasets to demonstrate the method's scalability.

## Questions
- Why does the method not require adversarial images during training?
- How does the method perform against black-box attacks?
- How does the method scale to larger datasets?
