# Review

## Summary
The paper studies generalization of stochastic optimization algorithms by bounding the worst-case generalization error along the entire trajectory of the algorithm. The main contribution of the paper is a stability-based generalization bound for data-dependent random sets. The authors also apply their framework to obtain generalization bounds based on the fractal and topological complexity measures without any intractable mutual information terms.

## Strengths
The paper is well-written and the contributions are clear. The paper studies an important problem of bounding the worst-case generalization error along the entire trajectory of the algorithm, which can provide a more comprehensive understanding of the generalization behavior of modern machine learning models.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a comparison with existing bounds for the same setting. The authors should explicitly compare their bounds with existing bounds in terms of their computational tractability and tightness, highlighting the advantages and limitations of their approach. This would help to better position the paper within the existing literature and to understand the significance of the contributions.

2. The paper only provides an expected bound, while existing literature also includes high-probability bounds. The authors should explain the challenges in deriving high-probability bounds within their framework and discuss the potential implications of focusing solely on expected bounds.

3. The paper only considers Euclidean-based topological complexities and does not cover the case of the data-dependent pseudometric introduced by Dupuis et al. (2023). The authors should discuss the limitations of their approach in handling these complexities and pseudometrics, and explain why these cases are not covered. This would help to better understand the scope and limitations of the proposed framework.
