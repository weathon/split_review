# Review

## Summary
This paper focuses on the challenge of harmful content in the reasoning process of large reasoning models (LRMs). The authors propose a novel method, Intervened Preference Optimization (IPO), to enforce safe reasoning by substituting compliance steps with safety triggers and constructing pairs for preference learning with strong signals. Experimental results demonstrate that IPO significantly improves safety performance while maintaining strong reasoning capabilities.

## Strengths
1. The paper addresses a critical issue in the field of AI safety: the harmful content generated during the reasoning process of large reasoning models (LRMs). This is an important problem that requires urgent attention.
2. The authors propose a novel method, Intervened Preference Optimization (IPO), to enforce safe reasoning by substituting compliance steps with safety triggers and constructing pairs for preference learning with strong signals. This approach is innovative and shows promise in addressing the problem.
3. The experimental results demonstrate that IPO significantly improves safety performance while maintaining strong reasoning capabilities. This indicates that the proposed method is effective and can be practically applied to real-world scenarios.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks a detailed analysis of the limitations of the proposed method. It would be beneficial to discuss the potential drawbacks or scenarios where the method may not be as effective, providing a more comprehensive understanding of its applicability.
2. The paper does not provide sufficient details on the implementation of the proposed method. For example, the selection of safety triggers and the construction of preference pairs are not discussed in depth. More information on these aspects would help in reproducing the experiments and understanding the nuances of the proposed approach.

## Questions
1. How are safety triggers selected, and how do different selections impact the performance of the method?
2. Can the proposed method be extended to other LLMs beyond the ones tested in the paper? If so, what modifications would be necessary?
3. How robust is the proposed method to adversarial attacks or other attempts to circumvent the safety measures?
