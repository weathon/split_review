# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces VisFactor, a benchmark designed to evaluate the visual reasoning abilities of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) by adapting subtests from the Factor-Referenced Cognitive Test (FRCT), a well-established assessment in cognitive psychology. The benchmark covers four cognitive domains: Visualization and Spatial Processing, Perceptual and Closure, Memory, and Reasoning. To reduce the influence of random guessing, the authors generate multiple variants of each question, ensuring a more rigorous evaluation. The paper also includes a parametric generation method to create an unlimited supply of test cases with controlled difficulty. The authors evaluate 23 MLLMs, including both proprietary and open-source models, on this benchmark. The results reveal significant limitations in the visual reasoning abilities of these models, with the best-performing model achieving only 30.17% accuracy. The paper highlights consistent failures in tasks such as mental rotation, spatial relation inference, and figure-ground discrimination, regardless of model size or prompting strategies. This finding challenges the assumption that large-scale pretraining confers human-like visual reasoning capabilities.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The paper introduces a novel benchmark grounded in human cognitive factors, providing a more rigorous evaluation of MLLMs' visual reasoning abilities.
3. The paper adapts subtests from the Factor-Referenced Cognitive Test (FRCT) into an automated image-text format suitable for MLLMs.
4. The authors generate multiple rule-based variants of each question to reduce the impact of random guessing, ensuring that model performance reflects genuine visual reasoning.
5. The paper includes a parametric generation method to create an unlimited supply of test cases with controlled difficulty, supporting continuous evaluation and improvement of MLLMs.
6. The paper evaluates 23 MLLMs, providing a comprehensive assessment of current capabilities and identifying cognitive gaps in visual reasoning.

## Weaknesses
1. The benchmark is based on synthetic images, which may not fully reflect the model's performance on real-world tasks.
2. The benchmark is heavily focused on tasks that involve simple geometric shapes and basic visual concepts, which may not be sufficient to fully assess the model's visual reasoning abilities in more complex real-world scenarios.

## Questions
1. How do the authors ensure that the generated test cases maintain the same level of difficulty as the original FRCT subtests?
2. How do the authors verify that the generated test cases are diverse and representative of the original FRCT subtests?
3. How do the authors handle the potential overfitting of MLLMs to the specific style of the FRCT subtests?
4. How do the authors assess the generalizability of the findings from the VisFactor benchmark to other visual reasoning tasks in real-world applications?
