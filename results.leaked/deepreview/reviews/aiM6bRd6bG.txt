# Review

## Summary
The paper presents a novel approach to protein-protein interaction (PPI) candidate ranking by leveraging a combination of interpretability-guided retrieval and multi-source re-ranking. The authors introduce a method that significantly improves the prioritization of candidate proteins likely to interact with a target protein, addressing a critical bottleneck in PPI research— the empirical validation of predicted interactions.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces an innovative approach to PPI candidate ranking by integrating domain knowledge through interpretability-guided retrieval and multi-source re-ranking.
2. The authors provide a comprehensive evaluation across multiple metrics, demonstrating the effectiveness of their method in improving ranking accuracy and recall.
3. The paper includes a thorough literature review, contextualizing the research within the broader field of PPI prediction and highlighting the limitations of existing methods.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a clear and detailed description of the proposed method, making it difficult for readers to understand the technical aspects of the approach.
2. The authors do not adequately address the potential limitations and biases of their method, particularly in the context of underexplored proteins with limited known interactions.
3. The paper lacks a thorough discussion of the computational efficiency and scalability of the proposed method, which are critical considerations for practical applications.
4. The authors do not provide a detailed analysis of the sensitivity of their method to different hyperparameter settings, which could significantly impact its performance.
5. The paper does not include a comprehensive comparison with a wide range of state-of-the-art methods in PPI prediction, limiting the ability to assess the relative performance of the proposed approach.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed method handle proteins with very few or no known interacting partners, and what is the impact on ranking accuracy in such cases?
2. Can the authors provide more detailed information on the computational resources required for their method and how it scales with the size of the protein dataset?
3. How sensitive is the performance of the proposed method to different hyperparameter settings, and what guidelines can be provided for selecting optimal parameters?
4. How does the proposed method compare with other state-of-the-art PPI prediction methods in terms of accuracy, recall, and computational efficiency, and what are the key advantages of the proposed approach?
