# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a method called VeriFree, which extends the DeepSeek-R1-Zero-style RL training to general reasoning tasks. The authors argue that VeriFree has several benefits over traditional verifier-based methods, including reduced computational requirements, improved performance, and enhanced generalization capabilities. The paper provides a theoretical analysis of the VeriFree gradient estimator and demonstrates its equivalence to the verifier-based objective under certain conditions. The authors also present experimental results on multiple reasoning benchmarks, showing that VeriFree outperforms baseline methods and achieves state-of-the-art performance in some cases.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The authors provide clear explanations of the VeriFree method and its benefits. The experimental results are presented in a way that is easy to understand.
2. The paper addresses an important problem in the field of LLM reasoning, namely the difficulty of verifying answers in general reasoning tasks. The proposed VeriFree method offers a potential solution to this problem, which could have significant implications for the development of more advanced LLMs.
3. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of the VeriFree gradient estimator, showing its equivalence to the verifier-based objective under certain conditions. This analysis adds depth to the paper and helps to understand the underlying principles of the proposed method.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not compare VeriFree with other state-of-the-art methods for improving LLM reasoning, such as those based on advanced prompting techniques or other forms of RL. Without such comparisons, it is difficult to assess the relative advantages of VeriFree.
2. The paper focuses primarily on improvements in accuracy and response length, but does not extensively analyze other important aspects of LLM reasoning, such as the diversity of the generated reasoning traces or the explainability of the model's decision-making process.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational requirements of VeriFree, such as training time, inference time, or memory usage. This information would be valuable for assessing the practicality of the proposed method, especially for large-scale applications.

## Questions
1. How does VeriFree compare to other state-of-the-art methods for improving LLM reasoning, such as those based on advanced prompting techniques or other forms of RL?
2. Can you provide more details on the computational requirements of VeriFree, such as training time, inference time, or memory usage?
3. How does VeriFree handle cases where there are multiple valid answers to a question? Does it prioritize one answer over others, and if so, how is this prioritization done?
