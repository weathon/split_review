# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a benchmark for model merging in multimodal large language models (MLLMs), encompassing various tasks such as VQA, Geometry, Chart, OCR, and Grounding. The authors propose a novel method called OptMerge, which removes noise from task vectors and optimizes the merged vector based on interactions between task vectors, resulting in an average performance gain of 2.48%. The paper demonstrates the effectiveness of model merging in combining different modalities (e.g., vision-language, audio-language, video-language models) and highlights the complementary nature of multiple modalities over individual modalities.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a model merging benchmark specifically designed for MLLMs, covering a range of tasks and modalities. This benchmark provides a structured framework for evaluating and advancing MLLM capabilities.

2. The proposed OptMerge method effectively removes noise from task vectors and optimizes the merged vector based on task vector interactions, leading to performance improvements across various tasks.

3. The paper demonstrates that model merging can leverage the complementary strengths of multiple modalities, outperforming individual modalities and showcasing the potential for omni-language models.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed model merging method is only applied to the linear layers of the model. It is unclear whether this approach is effective for other types of layers, such as MLP layers. The paper does not provide evidence or analysis to support the generalizability of the method to different layers.

2. The proposed method is evaluated using task vectors computed from models fine-tuned on specific tasks. However, it is unclear whether this method is effective for task vectors computed from models fine-tuned on different domains. The paper lacks evidence or analysis to demonstrate the robustness of the method when dealing with task vectors from diverse domains.

3. The paper does not provide a comprehensive comparison with existing methods for merging models in the context of LLMs. It is unclear how the proposed method performs relative to other state-of-the-art techniques, such as those presented in references [1-5]. The absence of such comparisons makes it difficult to assess the relative advantages and disadvantages of the proposed approach.

[1] Yang, Z., Zhang, X., Li, J., Li, J., Wang, Z., Liu, Z., & Liu, Y. (2024). Mergezilla: All you need for merging LLMs are a few prompts and some data. arXiv preprint arXiv:2407.16216.

[2] Wang, J., Liu, X., Wang, Z., Guo, J., Jiang, M., Zhang, Y.,... & Ma, L. (2024). Merge and generalize: Enhancing large language models with domain-specific knowledge. arXiv preprint arXiv:2407.15558.

## Questions
1. Can the proposed model merging method be applied to other types of layers, such as MLP layers? What evidence or analysis supports the generalizability of the method to different layers?

2. How effective is the proposed method when dealing with task vectors computed from models fine-tuned on different domains? Is there any evidence or analysis to demonstrate the robustness of the method in such scenarios?

3. How does the proposed method compare to existing state-of-the-art techniques for merging models in the context of LLMs? Can you provide a comprehensive comparison to assess the relative advantages and disadvantages of the proposed approach?
