# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a novel adaptive discrete video tokenizer, INFOTOK, based on information theory. It addresses the suboptimality of fixed-rate compression in existing tokenizers and introduces an ELBO-based router that dynamically allocates token lengths according to video complexity. Additionally, an adaptive compressor is designed to manage variable-length representations efficiently. Experimental results demonstrate significant performance improvements in video reconstruction tasks.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-organized and easy to follow.
2. INFOTOK is the first adaptive discrete video tokenizer based on information theory, offering a theoretical guarantee of optimality.
3. Experimental results show that INFOTOK outperforms existing tokenizers in video reconstruction tasks.

## Weaknesses
1. The authors claim that the ELBO-based router can determine the optimal token length; however, the actual implementation uses a fixed compression rate (e.g., 0.81). How is the optimal token length defined in this context?
2. The paper lacks comparisons with state-of-the-art image tokenizers, such as VQGAN and VQVAE.
3. The authors do not address the impact of context on token length. For instance, in scenes with dynamic elements, the complexity may increase as the element moves closer to the camera, suggesting that context should influence token length allocation.
4. The authors do not provide a detailed analysis of the computational cost and runtime for INFOTOK.
