# Review

## Summary
This paper focuses on the issue of modality imbalance in multimodal learning. The authors propose a novel method called Classifier-Constrained Alternating Training (CCAT) to address this problem. The CCAT method first pre-trains an unbiased cross-modal classifier using bidirectional cross-attention and a regularization term that constrains modality contribution differences. This classifier is then frozen as a stable decision anchor during subsequent training, preventing bias toward any modality. To preserve modality-specific features while leveraging this anchor, the authors integrate modality-specific Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) modules into the classifier. During alternating training, CCAT updates only the encoder of the active modality and its corresponding LoRA parameters. The paper presents extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks that demonstrate the superiority of CCAT over state-of-the-art methods.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a novel method, CCAT, to address the issue of modality imbalance in multimodal learning. This method is innovative in its approach to pre-training an unbiased cross-modal classifier and freezing it during subsequent training to prevent bias toward any modality.
2. The paper provides a thorough analysis of the issue of modality imbalance in multimodal learning and its impact on model performance. The authors present a clear and concise explanation of the problem and its causes.
3. The paper presents extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks that demonstrate the superiority of CCAT over state-of-the-art methods. The experiments are well-designed and provide strong evidence of the effectiveness of the proposed method.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational complexity of the CCAT method. It would be beneficial to compare the computational complexity of CCAT to that of other state-of-the-art methods.
2. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the sensitivity of the CCAT method to its hyperparameters. It would be beneficial to perform a sensitivity analysis to understand how the performance of CCAT changes with different hyperparameters.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the interpretability of the CCAT method. It would be beneficial to provide an analysis of how the different components of the CCAT method contribute to the final predictions.

## Questions
1. How does the CCAT method compare to other state-of-the-art methods in terms of computational complexity?
2. How sensitive is the CCAT method to its hyperparameters? Have you performed a sensitivity analysis to understand how the performance of CCAT changes with different hyperparameters?
3. Can you provide an analysis of how the different components of the CCAT method contribute to the final predictions? How interpretable is the CCAT method?
