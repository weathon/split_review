## Summary:

This paper introduces FedMPDD, a novel federated learning algorithm designed to address the communication bottleneck and privacy concerns inherent in distributed learning. The core idea behind FedMPDD is to compress each client's high-dimensional gradient by projecting it onto multiple random vectors, effectively reducing the communication cost from $O(d)$ to $O(m)$, where $m$ is the number of random vectors used for projection and $m 	extless d$. This compression is achieved by computing directional derivatives along these random vectors, which are then transmitted to the server. The server, upon receiving these compressed gradients, reconstructs an approximation of the aggregated gradient by projecting the received information back onto the same random vectors. A key insight of this approach is that averaging multiple projections mitigates the dimension-dependent convergence limitations associated with single-projection methods. The authors provide a rigorous theoretical analysis demonstrating that FedMPDD converges at a rate of $O(1/	extsqrt{K})$, which is comparable to the convergence rate of FedSGD. Furthermore, the paper argues that FedMPDD offers inherent privacy against gradient inversion attacks due to the geometric properties of low-rank projections. The authors validate their theoretical findings through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, showing that FedMPDD significantly reduces network congestion while maintaining high model performance and providing strong privacy protection. The empirical results demonstrate that FedMPDD outperforms existing methods in resource-constrained scenarios, offering a practical solution for communication-efficient and privacy-preserving federated learning. The paper also explores the trade-off between privacy and utility, showing that the number of projections can be tuned to achieve the desired balance. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and technically sound approach to federated learning, with both theoretical and empirical evidence supporting its effectiveness.
