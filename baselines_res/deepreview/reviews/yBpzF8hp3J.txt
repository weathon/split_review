## Summary:

This paper tackles the challenging problem of differentially private domain discovery, focusing on scenarios where each user possesses a subset of items from a larger, unknown domain. The core objective is to output an informative subset of this domain while adhering to differential privacy constraints. The authors concentrate on three specific problems within this domain: set union, top-k selection, and k-hitting set. A central contribution of the paper is the analysis of the Weighted Gaussian Mechanism (WGM), a relatively simple yet scalable algorithm. The authors demonstrate that WGM achieves near-optimal ℓ1 missing mass guarantees on Zipfian data, a common distribution in real-world datasets, and also provides distribution-free ℓ∞ missing mass guarantees. This analysis is significant because it provides theoretical justification for the use of WGM in this context. Furthermore, the paper explores the use of WGM as a domain discovery precursor for existing known-domain algorithms. Specifically, they show that by first using WGM to identify a relevant domain, they can then apply existing differentially private algorithms for top-k selection and k-hitting set, obtaining new utility guarantees for these unknown domain variants. This approach allows them to leverage the strengths of existing algorithms while addressing the challenges of an unknown domain. The empirical evaluation of the proposed methods is conducted on real-world datasets, demonstrating that the WGM-based methods are competitive with or outperform existing baselines for all three problems. This is a crucial validation of the practical relevance of their approach. Overall, the paper makes a valuable contribution by providing a theoretical analysis of WGM in the context of domain discovery and by demonstrating its effectiveness in practice. However, as I will discuss in the weaknesses section, the paper has some limitations that need to be addressed.

## Strengths:

The paper presents several notable strengths. First, the paper is well-written and easy to follow, which makes the technical details accessible to a broad audience. The authors clearly articulate the problem of differentially private domain discovery and its importance in practical applications. The focus on the Weighted Gaussian Mechanism (WGM) is a strength, as it is a relatively simple and scalable algorithm, making it practical for real-world use. The theoretical analysis of WGM is a significant contribution. The authors provide rigorous proofs for the near-optimal ℓ1 missing mass guarantee on Zipfian data and the distribution-free ℓ∞ missing mass guarantee. These results provide a solid theoretical foundation for the use of WGM in domain discovery. The paper also makes a valuable contribution by applying WGM as a domain discovery precursor for existing known-domain algorithms. This approach allows the authors to leverage the strengths of existing algorithms while addressing the challenges of an unknown domain. The derivation of new utility guarantees for the top-k and k-hitting set problems in the unknown domain setting is a significant achievement. The empirical evaluation of the proposed methods is another strength. The authors conduct experiments on real-world datasets, demonstrating that the WGM-based methods are competitive with or outperform existing baselines for all three problems. This provides strong evidence for the practical relevance of their approach. The experiments are well-designed and the results are clearly presented. The paper also provides a clear and concise description of the experimental setup, which makes it easy to reproduce the results. Finally, the paper addresses an important and challenging problem in differential privacy, and the results are relevant to a wide range of applications.


## Weaknesses:

Despite its strengths, the paper has several weaknesses that need to be addressed. A primary concern, consistently raised by multiple reviewers, is the incremental nature of the contribution. The paper heavily relies on the Weighted Gaussian Mechanism (WGM), which was introduced in prior work by Gopi et al. (2020). While the authors provide new theoretical results for WGM, specifically the near-optimal ℓ1 missing mass guarantee on Zipfian data and the distribution-free ℓ∞ missing mass guarantee, the core mechanism itself is not novel. The analysis techniques used to derive these guarantees, while technically sound, are standard in the field of differential privacy. This reliance on existing techniques and the WGM itself makes the contribution feel somewhat incremental. The paper's approach to set union, top-k, and k-hitting set involves using WGM as a preprocessing step, followed by the application of known differentially private algorithms. While this approach is effective, it does not introduce a fundamentally new algorithmic framework. The improvements in utility guarantees, especially for top-k and k-hitting set, are marginal and do not represent a significant leap in the field. The experimental results, while showing competitive performance, do not demonstrate a substantial advantage over existing baselines. For example, in the set union experiments, the WGM achieves missing mass within 5% of the policy mechanisms, indicating competitive but not dominant performance. Similarly, in the top-k experiments, the proposed method consistently obtains smaller top-k missing mass than the limited-domain baselines, but the advantage is not substantial. In the k-hitting set experiments, the proposed method performs comparably with the baselines and only outperforms the known-domain private greedy algorithm in specific cases. This lack of a significant empirical advantage further reinforces the incremental nature of the contribution. Another significant weakness is the lack of comparison with a recent work that also studies differentially private domain discovery and improves the WGM. The paper does not compare its results with this relevant work, making it difficult to assess the relative performance of the proposed methods. This omission is a major oversight, as it prevents a thorough evaluation of the paper's contribution in the context of the current state-of-the-art. The absence of this comparison makes it difficult to determine whether the proposed methods offer a significant improvement over existing approaches. The paper's analysis also primarily focuses on refining the bounds for existing techniques rather than introducing a transformative approach. While the theoretical analysis is rigorous, it does not reveal any previously unknown properties of the WGM or provide any new insights into the problem of domain discovery. The analysis is limited to the ℓ1 and ℓ∞ missing mass guarantees, and the paper does not explore tighter bounds under specific data distributions or investigate the trade-offs between privacy and utility in a more nuanced manner. This lack of deeper analysis limits the impact of the paper. Finally, while the paper provides a theoretical analysis of the proposed methods, it does not fully explore the practical implications of these methods. The paper does not discuss the limitations of the WGM and how these limitations might impact the performance of the proposed algorithms in practice. The paper also does not provide guidelines for selecting the appropriate parameters for the algorithms based on the specific characteristics of the data and the desired level of privacy. This lack of practical guidance limits the usefulness of the paper for practitioners.


## Suggestions:

To strengthen the paper, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the authors should explore more novel algorithmic approaches or provide a more in-depth analysis that reveals previously unknown properties of the WGM in the context of domain discovery. Instead of solely focusing on the ℓ1 and ℓ∞ missing mass guarantees, they could investigate tighter bounds under specific data distributions or explore the trade-offs between privacy and utility in a more nuanced manner. This could involve developing new theoretical tools or adapting existing ones in a novel way. For example, they could investigate the impact of different data distributions on the performance of the WGM and derive tighter bounds for specific distributions. They could also explore the use of adaptive mechanisms that dynamically adjust the noise based on the observed data distribution. This could potentially lead to tighter bounds and better empirical performance. Second, the authors must include a comprehensive experimental comparison with the recent work that improves the WGM. This comparison should not only focus on the empirical performance but also on the theoretical guarantees and computational complexity. The authors should discuss the specific scenarios where their proposed methods outperform or underperform the methods in the other work, providing a clear understanding of the strengths and weaknesses of each approach. This would allow the reader to better understand the practical implications of the proposed methods and their applicability in different settings. The comparison should include a variety of datasets and parameter settings to ensure a thorough evaluation. Third, the authors should conduct experiments on real-world datasets to validate their theoretical findings and to demonstrate the practical utility of their proposed algorithms. This would involve not only comparing the performance of the proposed methods with existing baselines but also analyzing the sensitivity of the methods to different parameters and data characteristics. The authors should also provide guidelines for selecting the appropriate parameters for their algorithms based on the specific characteristics of the data and the desired level of privacy. This would make the paper more useful for practitioners. Fourth, the authors should provide a more detailed discussion of the limitations of the WGM and how these limitations might impact the performance of the proposed algorithms in practice. This discussion should include an analysis of the scenarios where the WGM is likely to perform poorly and how these limitations can be mitigated. The authors should also explore alternative mechanisms that might be more robust to these limitations. Finally, the authors should consider exploring the theoretical limits of their approach. While the paper provides utility guarantees, it does not delve into the fundamental limitations of using WGM for domain discovery. Understanding these limitations could help guide the development of more effective algorithms. For example, the authors could investigate the information-theoretic limits of domain discovery under differential privacy and compare their results against these limits. This would provide a more solid theoretical foundation for their work and highlight the potential for future advancements.
