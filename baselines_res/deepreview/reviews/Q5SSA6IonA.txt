## Summary:

This paper introduces Vision Filter (ViF), a novel neural network architecture for vision tasks, which builds upon the Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) framework. The core contribution lies in the development of the Fourier Neural Filter (FNF), a module designed to address the limitations of standard FNO in capturing local high-frequency patterns. FNF achieves this by incorporating input-dependent kernels and selective activation mechanisms, allowing for a more nuanced balance between local time-domain and global frequency-domain information. The authors argue that this approach enhances the model's ability to process visual information effectively. ViF is evaluated across a range of vision tasks, including image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation. The empirical results demonstrate that ViF achieves competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art Transformer and Mamba-based models. Specifically, the paper presents results on ImageNet-1K for image classification, COCO for object detection, and ADE20K for semantic segmentation, showing that ViF consistently outperforms or matches the performance of other leading models. The authors also provide a theoretical analysis of the limitations of FNO, such as over-smoothing and bandwidth bottlenecks, and explain how FNF addresses these issues. The paper claims that ViF offers a promising paradigm for visual representation learning by combining the strengths of FNO with adaptive modulation and selective activation. The authors emphasize that ViF achieves lower computational complexity than Transformer-based models and better structural modeling than Mamba-based models, which suffer from spatial disruption due to their directional scanning mechanism. The paper also includes an ablation study to demonstrate the effectiveness of the different components of the FNF module. Overall, the paper presents a novel approach to vision backbones by integrating FNO with adaptive mechanisms, achieving competitive performance across various vision tasks. However, as I will discuss in the weaknesses section, there are several areas where the paper could be improved to strengthen its claims and provide a more comprehensive analysis of the proposed method.
