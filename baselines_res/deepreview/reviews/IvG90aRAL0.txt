## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel approach to convex optimization by developing quantum algorithms for the Frank-Wolfe method, specifically targeting projection-free sparse optimization problems in both vector and matrix domains. The core contribution lies in the design of quantum subroutines to solve the linear subproblems inherent in the Frank-Wolfe algorithm, which leads to improved query and time complexities compared to classical methods. For the vector domain, the authors focus on constraints such as the $\ell_1$-norm ball and the simplex, achieving a query complexity of $O(\sqrt{d}/\epsilon)$, a significant improvement over the classical $O(d/\epsilon)$. They further show that if the objective function is Lipschitz continuous, this complexity can be reduced to $O(1/\epsilon)$ using a bounded-error Jordan quantum gradient estimation algorithm. In the matrix domain, the paper addresses nuclear norm constraints, proposing two algorithms tailored to high-rank and low-rank gradient matrices, respectively. These algorithms achieve time complexities of $\tilde{O}(rd/\epsilon^2)$ and $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{rd}/\epsilon^3)$ for computing the update step, which represents at least an $O(\sqrt{d})$ speedup over the best classical algorithms. The methodology involves the development of quantum gradient circuits, quantum maximum finding circuits, quantum top singular vector extraction (QTSVE), and quantum power methods (QPM). The paper provides a theoretical analysis of the query and time complexities of the proposed algorithms, demonstrating the potential of quantum computing to accelerate convex optimization. The authors also present a theoretical comparison of their quantum algorithms with classical counterparts, highlighting the potential speedups. However, the paper's primary focus is on theoretical analysis, and it lacks empirical validation on quantum hardware or simulations. The algorithms are presented at a high level, with limited discussion of the practical challenges of implementation, such as quantum circuit design, error correction, and the impact of noise. The paper's significance lies in its theoretical contribution to the field of quantum optimization, demonstrating the potential of quantum algorithms to outperform classical methods in specific optimization scenarios. However, the practical applicability of these algorithms remains to be fully explored, and the paper's theoretical focus leaves several open questions regarding their real-world performance.
