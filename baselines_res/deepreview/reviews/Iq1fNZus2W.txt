## Summary:

This paper introduces Patch-wise and Keyword-Aware Attention (PKA), a novel framework designed to enhance the efficiency of multi-condition control in Diffusion Transformers (DiTs). The core contribution of this work lies in addressing the computational and memory bottlenecks associated with the traditional 'concatenate-and-attend' strategy commonly used in multi-condition DiTs. The authors propose two specialized attention modules: Position-Aligned Attention (PAA) and Keyword-Scoped Attention (KSA). PAA confines spatial control to aligned patches, effectively reducing redundant computations in spatial conditioning. KSA, on the other hand, restricts subject-driven control to keyword-activated regions, thereby minimizing unnecessary attention computations related to subject conditions. These modules are motivated by empirical observations of attention patterns in DiTs, where spatial and subject conditions exhibit localized and sparse attention distributions. Furthermore, the authors introduce an early-timestep sampling strategy that accelerates training by prioritizing the most critical phases of the denoising process. This strategy involves skewing the sampling distribution towards earlier timesteps, which are found to have a more significant impact on the final image quality. The proposed PKA framework, combined with the early-timestep sampling strategy, achieves significant improvements in inference speed and memory usage. Specifically, the authors report up to a 10× inference speedup and a 5.12× reduction in attention module VRAM usage, while maintaining or even improving the generative quality of the images. The paper presents a thorough analysis of the computational redundancy in standard full-attention mechanisms of DiTs, which is a valuable contribution in itself. The proposed PKA framework, with its two specialized modules (PAA and KSA), is a well-thought-out solution to the identified problem. The design of each module is motivated by empirical observations of attention patterns. The early-timestep sampling strategy is a novel contribution that accelerates training without compromising performance. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear figures that effectively illustrate the proposed method and its advantages. Overall, this paper presents a practical and efficient solution for complex, fine-grained, and resource-friendly AI generation, making it a significant contribution to the field of generative models.

This limitation is evident in both the method description (Section 3.2) and the experimental setup (Section 4.2), which focus exclusively on spatial (Canny, Depth) and subject conditions. ## Suggestions:

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the authors should expand the scope of their experiments to include other types of conditions, such as style and texture. This would involve incorporating datasets and evaluation metrics that are relevant to these conditions. For example, they could explore the performance of PKA in style transfer tasks, using datasets like those used in style-based generative models. This would demonstrate the versatility of the proposed method and its applicability to a wider range of generative tasks. Second, the authors should conduct a more comprehensive analysis of the impact of different hyperparameters on the performance of PKA. This should include a systematic ablation study of the patch size in PAA and the keyword activation threshold in KSA. The study should investigate the sensitivity of the method to these parameters across different datasets and condition types. This would provide a more robust understanding of the method's behavior and allow for better tuning of the hyperparameters in different scenarios. Third, the authors should include a more detailed comparison with other efficient attention mechanisms, such as linear attention and low-rank approximations. This comparison should not only focus on computational efficiency but also on the quality of generated images. It would be useful to analyze the trade-offs between different methods in terms of both speed and image quality. The authors should also discuss the specific advantages and disadvantages of PKA compared to these methods, highlighting the unique contributions of the proposed approach. This would help to better position the paper within the existing literature and demonstrate its practical value. Fourth, the authors should include a more thorough discussion of the limitations of the proposed approach. This should include an analysis of how PKA performs in challenging scenarios, such as complex scenes with multiple interacting objects or scenes with significant occlusions. The paper should also discuss the potential failure cases of the method and the reasons behind these failures. Furthermore, the paper should address the potential for error propagation due to the early timestep sampling strategy. A more thorough analysis of these limitations would provide a more balanced view of the proposed method and help to identify areas for future research. Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed explanation of the early-timestep sampling strategy, particularly regarding the selection of the logit-normal distribution parameters. While the paper mentions using a shifted logit-normal distribution with parameters μ and δ, it does not provide a clear rationale for how these specific values are chosen or how they relate to the desired concentration of sampling in the early timesteps. A sensitivity analysis of these parameters would be valuable, demonstrating how different parameter values affect the training process and the final model performance. For instance, the authors could include experiments showing the impact of varying μ and δ on the convergence speed and the quality of generated images. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the sampling strategy and its influence on the overall effectiveness of the proposed method. Additionally, a theoretical justification for the choice of the logit-normal distribution would further strengthen the paper's claims.
