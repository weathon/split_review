## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel paradigm in time series forecasting, termed Influence-Aware Time Series Forecasting (IATSF), which challenges the traditional 'self-stimulation' assumption that relies solely on historical time series data. The authors argue that this assumption leads to inherent limitations in forecasting accuracy and propose a control-theoretic framework to formalize this issue. They demonstrate that incorporating external influences, particularly textual data, can significantly reduce forecasting errors by explicitly modeling these influences. To validate their approach, the authors introduce a new benchmark dataset, IATSF, which includes temporally aligned textual influences, and propose a lightweight model called FIATS (Forecaster for Influence-Aware Time Series). FIATS integrates textual influences through novel channel-aware mechanisms, including Channel-Aware Adaptive Sensitivity Modeling (CASM) and Channel-Aware Parameter Sharing (CAPS). The core argument is that by explicitly modeling external influences, rather than relying solely on historical data, forecasting accuracy can be significantly improved. The paper presents a theoretical analysis, supported by control theory, to demonstrate the limitations of self-stimulation and the benefits of influence-aware modeling. The experimental results, conducted across diverse datasets, including synthetic, physics-based, and market systems, show that FIATS outperforms state-of-the-art models, including large language model-based approaches, by effectively leveraging external influences. The authors emphasize the interpretability of FIATS through attention maps and provide an ablation study to verify the robustness of the architecture. The paper's main contribution lies in the introduction of the IATSF paradigm, the creation of a new benchmark dataset, and the development of the FIATS model, which together demonstrate the potential of influence-aware time series forecasting. The authors also provide a theoretical analysis that demonstrates the limitations of self-stimulation and the benefits of influence-aware modeling. Overall, the paper presents a compelling argument for the importance of external influences in time series forecasting and provides a practical framework for incorporating them into predictive models.
