## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel approach to detecting lead-lag relationships in financial markets by framing the problem as a temporal link prediction task on dynamic graphs. The authors propose a framework that leverages temporal graph neural networks (TGNNs) to model the complex, time-varying interactions between financial assets. They represent assets as nodes in a dynamic graph, where directed edges capture the predictive influence of one asset's price movement on another over time. The core contribution lies in the application of TGNNs to this financial problem, moving beyond traditional statistical methods. The authors construct a custom dataset of stocks and commodities, enriched with five years of daily pricing data, financial indicators, and sentiment features. This dataset serves as a valuable benchmark for evaluating the performance of different TGNN architectures. The study evaluates several state-of-the-art TGNN models, including JODIE, DySAT, TGAT, TGN, APAN, and GraphMixer, and demonstrates that these models can effectively capture complex temporal dependencies in financial networks. The empirical results show that the GraphMixer model achieves the best performance across most metrics, highlighting the potential of TGNNs for data-driven financial analysis. The paper's findings suggest that TGNNs can provide a more nuanced understanding of lead-lag relationships compared to traditional approaches, potentially leading to improved trading strategies and risk management. The authors also introduce a new threshold-based definition of lead-lag relationships, which they argue is more suitable for capturing the dynamic nature of financial markets. Overall, this work presents a significant advancement in the application of machine learning to financial time series analysis, offering a fresh perspective on a critical problem in finance.

This approach allows for the simultaneous modeling of multiple assets with interdependent time dynamics, extending beyond the limitations of traditional pairwise or static analysis methods. By representing financial assets as nodes in a dynamic graph, the authors can capture the evolving relationships between them over time, which is crucial for understanding the complex interactions in financial markets. Second, the paper's comprehensive evaluation of multiple state-of-the-art TGNN architectures is commendable.
