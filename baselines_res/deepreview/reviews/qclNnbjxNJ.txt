## Summary:

This paper tackles the challenging problem of causal discovery in the presence of latent confounders and post-treatment selection bias, a scenario often encountered in real-world datasets but not adequately addressed by existing causal inference methods. The authors introduce a novel causal formulation that explicitly models post-treatment selection, allowing for a more accurate identification of causal relationships by distinguishing them from selection-induced patterns. This formulation is grounded in the concept of Fine-grained Interventional Markov equivalence ($	ext{FI}$-Markov equivalence), which extends traditional Markov equivalence classes to account for interventions and selection bias. The authors represent this equivalence class using a new graphical representation called $	ext{F}$-PAG, which is an extension of the standard Partial Ancestral Graph (PAG). To learn causal structures under this framework, they propose a sound and complete algorithm, $	ext{F}$-FCI, which leverages both observational and interventional data. The algorithm is designed to identify causal relations, latent confounders, and post-treatment selection up to $	ext{FI}$-Markov equivalence. The core of the method lies in its ability to differentiate between causal effects and selection biases by analyzing how different variables react differentially to interventions. The authors validate their approach through experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in recovering causal relations despite the presence of selection and latent confounders. The empirical results show that the proposed method outperforms existing causal discovery algorithms that do not explicitly account for post-treatment selection bias. The paper's main contribution is the introduction of a novel causal framework that explicitly models post-treatment selection, along with a sound and complete algorithm for learning causal structures under this framework. The authors also provide a new graphical representation, $	ext{F}$-PAG, to represent the learned causal structures. The paper's significance lies in its ability to address a critical gap in causal discovery literature by providing a method that can handle both latent confounders and post-treatment selection bias, which are common in many real-world applications. The theoretical contributions are complemented by empirical validation, which demonstrates the practical utility of the proposed method. Overall, the paper presents a significant advancement in the field of causal inference by providing a more robust and accurate approach to causal discovery in complex scenarios.
