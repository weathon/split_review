## Summary:

This paper tackles the challenging problem of online inventory optimization (OIO) in non-stationary environments, a critical issue for supply chain management. The authors introduce a novel algorithm designed to achieve near-optimal dynamic regret guarantees, a significant improvement over existing methods that primarily focus on static regret. The core innovation lies in the algorithm's two-stage projection strategy, which effectively addresses the complexities introduced by the carryover stock constraint and the dynamic nature of demand. This strategy involves a base learner that operates independently of the carryover stock, followed by a projection onto the feasible region defined by the carryover constraint. The algorithm also incorporates a doubling trick to handle the unknown maximum sell-out period, $L_{max}$. The authors provide a rigorous theoretical analysis, establishing both upper and lower bounds for the dynamic regret, demonstrating the near-optimality of their proposed algorithm. The paper's main contribution is the development of the first algorithm with a dynamic regret bound for the OIO problem that matches the lower bound up to logarithmic factors. This result significantly improves upon existing static regret bounds and offers a more robust approach for managing inventory in fluctuating demand scenarios. The algorithm's design is rooted in connecting OIO with smoothed online convex optimization (SOCO), allowing the algorithm to adapt to demand fluctuations more effectively. The paper's analysis is comprehensive, including a lower bound for the OIO problem, which validates the near-optimality of the proposed algorithm. The authors also discuss the computational overhead of their algorithm, noting that it incurs a cost of $O(T 	ext{log} T)$, which is more expensive than Online Gradient Descent (OGD) by a factor of $	ext{log} T$. Despite the theoretical advancements, the paper lacks empirical validation, which is a notable limitation. The absence of experimental results makes it difficult to assess the practical performance of the proposed algorithm in real-world settings. The paper also assumes the observability of the full subgradient, which may not hold in practice, and focuses on a linear capacity constraint, which may not be applicable to all real-world scenarios. Overall, the paper makes a significant theoretical contribution to the field of online inventory optimization, but its practical applicability needs further investigation.
