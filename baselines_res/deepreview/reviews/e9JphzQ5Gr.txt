## Summary:

This paper introduces CLIP as a Prior Teacher (CaPT), a novel semi-supervised learning (SSL) framework designed to mitigate the label dependency issue inherent in traditional SSL methods. The core idea behind CaPT is to leverage the zero-shot capabilities of CLIP as a prior teacher, guiding the training of a unimodal network through the generation of co-pseudo labels. This approach employs an asymmetric multi-modal co-training strategy, combining a fully fine-tuned unimodal network with a parameter-efficiently fine-tuned CLIP model. The authors argue that this design facilitates richer cross-model information exchange and mitigates the pattern-homogeneity bottleneck often observed in co-training scenarios. The CaPT framework operates by generating pseudo-labels from both the unimodal and multimodal models, which are then fused using an entropy-based weighting scheme to provide a more robust supervision signal. The unimodal network is trained using a standard consistency regularization loss, while the CLIP model is trained using a feature-augmented consistency regularization loss. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of CaPT through extensive experiments on a variety of benchmarks, including the USB benchmark, ImageNet, and several fine-grained datasets. The results show that CaPT achieves state-of-the-art performance, particularly in low-label regimes, outperforming several existing SSL methods. The paper also includes a theoretical analysis of the label dependency in SSL, which provides a valuable contribution to the understanding of the field. The authors further conduct ablation studies to validate the effectiveness of the proposed components, such as the feature-augmented consistency regularization and the entropy-based weighting scheme. Overall, the paper presents a well-structured and clearly written account of a novel approach to SSL, with strong empirical results and a theoretical basis. The use of CLIP as a prior teacher and the asymmetric co-training framework are particularly noteworthy contributions. The paper's findings suggest that CaPT is a promising approach for addressing the challenges of limited labeled data in real-world applications.
