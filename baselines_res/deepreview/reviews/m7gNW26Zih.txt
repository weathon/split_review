## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel system for language-based audio retrieval, aiming to improve the alignment between audio recordings and text queries. The authors propose a dual-encoder architecture, where audio and text inputs are processed separately and then mapped into a shared embedding space. To enhance the model's performance, they employ three key techniques: soft-label distillation, LLM-driven caption augmentation, and cluster-guided auxiliary classification. Soft-label distillation leverages an ensemble of pre-trained models to generate soft correspondence probabilities, which are then used to refine the model's training. The LLM-driven caption augmentation uses GPT-4o to generate diverse captions through back-translation and caption mixing, enriching the training data. Finally, cluster-guided auxiliary classification is introduced to improve alignment in high-ambiguity scenarios by clustering captions and using the cluster assignments as auxiliary labels. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach on the CLOTHO dataset, achieving a maximum mAP@16 of 48.8 through a weighted ensemble of models. The paper presents a comprehensive approach, integrating multiple advanced techniques to address the challenges of audio-text retrieval. While the results on the CLOTHO dataset are promising, the paper's reliance on proprietary LLMs and the limited scope of the experimental evaluation raise some concerns about the generalizability and reproducibility of the proposed method. The authors also acknowledge the mixed results of cluster-guided classification across different models, indicating a need for further investigation into the underlying causes. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field, but it also highlights areas where further research and refinement are needed.

## Strengths:

I find several strengths in this paper, particularly in its comprehensive approach to language-based audio retrieval. The authors have effectively combined multiple advanced techniques to address the complexities of aligning audio and text modalities. The use of soft-label distillation, LLM-driven data augmentation, and cluster-guided auxiliary classification demonstrates a thoughtful and multi-faceted strategy. The soft-label distillation, which leverages an ensemble of pre-trained models, is a clever way to capture nuanced audio-text relationships, moving beyond simple binary correspondences. This approach allows the model to learn from the collective knowledge of multiple models, potentially leading to more robust and generalizable representations. The LLM-driven caption augmentation is another significant strength. By using GPT-4o to generate diverse captions through back-translation and caption mixing, the authors have effectively addressed the issue of limited training data. This technique not only increases the size of the training dataset but also introduces a wider range of linguistic variations, which can improve the model's ability to handle different types of text queries. The cluster-guided auxiliary classification is also a valuable contribution. By clustering captions and using the cluster assignments as auxiliary labels, the authors have introduced a way to guide the model's learning process, particularly in high-ambiguity scenarios. This approach encourages the model to learn more semantically meaningful representations, which can improve its ability to retrieve relevant audio recordings. Finally, the authors have demonstrated significant performance improvements on the CLOTHO dataset, achieving a maximum mAP@16 of 48.8 through a weighted ensemble of models. This result indicates the effectiveness of the proposed approach and its potential for real-world applications. The ablation studies, while not explicitly compared to external baselines, do show the impact of each component, demonstrating that the proposed enhancements contribute meaningfully to the overall system performance. The paper's detailed explanation of each technique, including the mathematical formulations and the rationale behind their choices, also contributes to its strengths, making the work reproducible and accessible to other researchers.

## Suggestions:

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the authors should provide a more detailed explanation of the proposed methods. For the distillation process, they should specify the architecture of the teacher models, how the ensemble is created (e.g., averaging, voting), and the exact loss function used for distillation, including any weighting factors. For the LLM-driven caption augmentation, they should name the specific LLM used (GPT-4o), provide the exact prompts used for augmentation, and describe how the quality of the augmented captions is assessed (e.g., manual evaluation, similarity metrics). For the cluster-guided auxiliary classification, they should specify the clustering algorithm used (e.g., K-means, hierarchical), the distance metric used for clustering, and the exact formulation of the auxiliary classification loss, as well as how it is combined with the main loss (e.g., weighted sum, multi-task learning). The authors should also provide a clear explanation of how the audio and text embeddings are generated for clustering. Second, the authors should improve the experimental evaluation by evaluating their method on additional datasets beyond CLOTHO, such as AudioCaps or MusicCaps, to demonstrate the generalizability of their approach. They should also compare their method against several strong baseline methods for language-based audio retrieval, including methods based on contrastive learning and cross-modal attention. The baselines should be implemented using the same datasets and evaluation metrics as the proposed method. Furthermore, the authors should conduct thorough ablation studies to demonstrate the contribution of each proposed technique. This should include experiments where each technique is added incrementally to the baseline model, and the performance is measured at each step. This will allow the reader to understand the individual contribution of each component. The authors should also report the statistical significance of the results to ensure that the observed improvements are not due to random chance. Third, the authors should discuss the limitations of their method in detail. This should include a discussion of the failure cases, such as when the audio contains background noise or the text query is ambiguous. The authors should also provide an analysis of the computational costs of their method, including the training time and inference time. The authors should also discuss the potential biases in the datasets and how these biases might affect the performance of their method. Fourth, the authors should investigate the impact of different open-source LLMs for data augmentation, comparing their performance against the proprietary models used in the current study. This would involve not only evaluating the retrieval performance but also analyzing the diversity and quality of the generated captions. Metrics such as BLEU, ROUGE, and BERTScore could be used to quantify the similarity between generated and original captions, and human evaluation could be used to assess the semantic correctness and naturalness of the augmented data. Furthermore, the authors should explore techniques to mitigate potential biases introduced by the LLMs during the augmentation process, such as using adversarial training or data filtering methods. Finally, to better understand the mixed results of cluster-guided auxiliary classification, the authors should conduct a more in-depth analysis of the feature spaces learned by different audio models. This could involve visualizing the cluster assignments for each model and examining how well the clusters align with the semantic content of the audio. Techniques such as t-SNE or UMAP could be used to project the high-dimensional feature vectors into a lower-dimensional space for visualization. Additionally, the authors should investigate the impact of different clustering algorithms and hyperparameters on the performance of the auxiliary classification task. A detailed analysis of the confusion matrices for the auxiliary classification task could also provide insights into the types of errors made by each model and guide further improvements. The authors should also provide a detailed breakdown of the computational resources required for each stage of the proposed approach, including pre-training, fine-tuning, and inference. This should include the number of GPUs, the type of GPUs, the training time, and the memory usage. Furthermore, the authors should report the inference time for a single retrieval query, which is crucial for assessing the real-time applicability of the proposed approach.
