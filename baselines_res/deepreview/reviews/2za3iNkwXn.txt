## Summary:

This paper presents a comprehensive investigation into the effects of compression techniques on Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), specifically focusing on quantization, distillation, and pruning. The authors' primary aim is to understand how these compression methods impact the reasoning capabilities of LRMs, a critical area given the increasing demand for efficient deployment of these models. The study centers its experimental analysis on the DeepSeek-R1 family of models, benchmarking various compressed versions against four reasoning datasets: AIME 2024, FOLIO, Temporal Sequences, and MuSiQue. These datasets are chosen to represent a range of reasoning tasks, including mathematical, logical, temporal, and multihop reasoning. The authors employ a combination of performance benchmarking and mechanistic interpretability techniques to achieve their goals. For performance benchmarking, they evaluate the accuracy of compressed models on the selected datasets, providing a quantitative measure of the impact of compression. For mechanistic interpretability, they utilize difference of means and attribution patching to identify which weights are most crucial for reasoning. This involves analyzing the activation of linear modules within each layer and computing the causal relationship between weight matrices and target reasoning behaviors. The core findings of the study reveal several key insights. First, the authors find that the weight count of a model significantly affects its knowledge retention capabilities more than its reasoning abilities. This suggests that the number of parameters plays a crucial role in storing knowledge, but may not be as critical for the reasoning process itself. Second, the study highlights the importance of specific components in the final layer of distilled models, particularly the MLP.up_proj, indicating that these components are vital for maintaining reasoning performance. Third, the authors demonstrate that selectively protecting certain weights in quantized models can lead to significant improvements in accuracy. They show that by identifying and preserving the most important weights, it is possible to mitigate the performance degradation typically associated with quantization. The authors also explore the impact of distillation, comparing the distilled DeepSeek-R1 models with their backbones. They analyze the change in relative importance scores to visualize the distillation effect, concluding that the important weights of the distilled model are primarily the result of distillation with SFT. Overall, this paper makes a valuable contribution to the field by providing a detailed analysis of the effects of compression on LRMs. The combination of performance benchmarking and mechanistic interpretability offers a deeper understanding of how compression techniques impact these models, and the findings provide practical insights for optimizing compression strategies. The study's focus on the DeepSeek-R1 family, while limiting generalizability, provides a detailed case study of compression effects on a specific LRM architecture. The paper's findings have important implications for the development of efficient and effective compression techniques for LRMs, paving the way for more practical deployment of these powerful models.

## Weaknesses:

Despite its strengths, this paper has several limitations that warrant careful consideration. The most significant weakness is the limited generalizability of the findings due to the study's primary focus on the DeepSeek-R1 model family and its distilled variants. While the authors acknowledge this limitation, the experimental design heavily relies on this specific model architecture, making it difficult to ascertain whether the observed trends are specific to DeepSeek-R1 or more broadly applicable to other LRMs. The paper's introduction, evaluation setup, and scope all explicitly state the focus on DeepSeek-R1, and the experimental results are primarily based on this model and its compressed variants. This narrow focus raises concerns about the applicability of the findings to models with different architectures, attention mechanisms, or pre-training objectives. For instance, models like LLaMA or Qwen, which have different architectural designs and training procedures, could exhibit different sensitivities to compression techniques. The lack of experiments on other model architectures makes it impossible to determine whether the observed trends are universal or specific to the DeepSeek-R1 family. This limitation significantly impacts the paper's overall conclusions and their broader relevance. Another significant weakness lies in the mechanistic interpretability analysis. The authors rely on difference of means and attribution patching, which, while useful, primarily focus on the contribution of individual weights or small groups of weights. These methods may not fully capture the complex interactions and dependencies within the network that are crucial for reasoning. The paper's methodology and visualizations support this, as they focus on individual linear modules within layers. The reviewer's concern about overlooking complex interactions is valid, as these methods might not fully capture emergent properties arising from the interplay of multiple layers and components. Furthermore, the paper does not discuss the choice of baseline for attribution patching or its potential impact on the results, which is a critical aspect of this method. The lack of discussion on the limitations of these methods and the choice of baseline weakens the confidence in the interpretation results. The paper also lacks a detailed analysis of the specific reasoning tasks used in the experiments. While the authors categorize the tasks as mathematical, logical, temporal, and multihop reasoning, they do not provide a deeper analysis of the specific characteristics or complexity of each task. The paper's results are presented by dataset name, without further categorization or analysis based on reasoning type. This lack of detailed analysis makes it difficult to understand how compression affects different types of reasoning. For example, it is possible that compression has a greater impact on tasks that require multi-step reasoning or those that rely on specific types of knowledge. The paper's conclusions are based on aggregate performance across these datasets, and a more granular analysis could reveal nuanced effects of compression on different types of reasoning. Finally, the paper's conclusion in Section 4.3, attributing the importance of the final-layer up projection to distillation, is not sufficiently supported by the experimental design. The conclusion is based solely on comparing the distilled model with its backbone, which does not account for other factors that could influence the model's performance, such as the additional training of the distilled model. The paper lacks any incremental analysis or gradient update examination, which would be necessary to isolate the effects of distillation. The reviewer's concern about other factors, such as the additional training during distillation, influencing the results is valid. The current method does not isolate the distillation effect from the effects of further training. The lack of a strong justification for choosing difference of means and attribution patching, the absence of a discussion on the limitations of these methods, and the lack of experiments involving perturbation or ablation of important weights further weaken the paper's conclusions. These limitations, taken together, significantly impact the paper's overall strength and the generalizability of its findings. I have high confidence in these identified weaknesses, as they are directly supported by the paper's content and methodology.


## Suggestions:

To address the identified weaknesses and enhance the overall quality of this research, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, to strengthen the generalizability of the findings, the authors should expand their experiments to include a more diverse set of LRM architectures. This could involve testing models with different attention mechanisms (e.g., sparse attention), varying numbers of layers, or different pre-training datasets. Specifically, including models like LLaMA or Qwen, which have different architectural designs and training procedures, would provide a more robust assessment of the impact of compression techniques on reasoning capabilities. Furthermore, the authors should consider exploring models with different pre-training objectives, as this could influence the sensitivity of the model to compression. Such an analysis would help determine whether the observed trends are specific to the DeepSeek-R1 family or more broadly applicable to LRMs in general. This would also help to identify any architectural biases that might influence the effectiveness of different compression techniques. The authors should also consider using a standardized benchmark suite for evaluating the performance of compressed models, which would allow for a more direct comparison with other studies. Second, to address the limitations of the mechanistic interpretability analysis, the authors should consider incorporating additional techniques that can capture more complex interactions within the network. For example, methods like Integrated Gradients or SHAP values could provide a more comprehensive view of the importance of different weights and their interactions. Furthermore, the authors should explore the sensitivity of their findings to different baselines used in attribution patching. This could involve testing different baseline inputs or using a distribution of baselines to assess the robustness of the results. Additionally, the authors could investigate the impact of compression on the activation patterns of different layers, which might provide insights into how compression affects the flow of information within the network. This could involve visualizing the activation patterns or using techniques like representational similarity analysis to compare the activations of compressed and uncompressed models. Third, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the specific reasoning tasks used in their experiments. It would be beneficial to categorize these tasks based on their complexity and the type of reasoning they require (e.g., logical reasoning, mathematical reasoning, common-sense reasoning). This would allow for a more nuanced understanding of how compression affects different types of reasoning. For example, it is possible that compression has a greater impact on tasks that require multi-step reasoning or those that rely on specific types of knowledge. By analyzing the performance of compressed models on different categories of reasoning tasks, the authors could provide more targeted insights into the strengths and weaknesses of different compression techniques. Fourth, to strengthen the conclusions drawn in Section 4.3, the authors should conduct a more rigorous analysis to isolate the effects of distillation on the final-layer up projection. Instead of simply comparing the distilled model with its backbone, they could perform an incremental comparison, such as analyzing the gradient updates during the distillation fine-tuning process. This would help to determine whether the increased importance of the up projection is indeed a result of distillation or simply a consequence of additional training. Furthermore, the authors could investigate the activation patterns of the up projection layer in both the backbone and distilled models to see if there are any qualitative differences that support their claim. This would provide a more robust foundation for their conclusions. Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed justification for choosing difference of means and attribution patching over other interpretation techniques. They should discuss the limitations of these methods and how they might affect the results. For example, they could acknowledge that difference of means might not capture non-linear relationships between weights and model behavior, and that attribution patching might be sensitive to the choice of baseline inputs. Furthermore, the authors should validate their interpretation results by demonstrating that perturbing or ablating the identified important weights leads to a significant drop in performance when perturbed or ablated, which is not shown in the current study. The authors could also consider using other interpretation methods, such as integrated gradients or SHAP values, to see if they arrive at similar conclusions, which would further strengthen their findings. By addressing these points, the authors can significantly improve the rigor and impact of their study.
