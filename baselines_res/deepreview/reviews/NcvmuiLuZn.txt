## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel paradigm for modeling stochastic processes, termed "noise-to-process" (N2P), which is particularly relevant in scenarios with limited data, such as when only a single trajectory is available. The core idea of N2P is to map a shared base-noise process to a full trajectory using a measurable generator, ensuring projective consistency by design. This approach aims to bridge the gap between prior-driven and data-driven methods, offering a flexible framework that can capture complex dependencies while maintaining theoretical rigor. The authors instantiate this paradigm with a deconvolution-based process transformation (DBPT), which utilizes deconvolution layers to capture long-range, inter-temporal dependencies within the trajectory. The DBPT model is trained by minimizing a masked mean squared error (MSE) loss on the observed subset of the trajectory. The authors provide a theoretical foundation for the N2P paradigm, including formal proofs of projective consistency and compatibility with the Kolmogorov extension theorem. Empirically, the proposed method is evaluated across a diverse set of tasks, including synthetic data generation, time series modeling, image completion, and black-box optimization. The results demonstrate that DBPT achieves competitive performance compared to prior-driven and data-driven baselines, particularly in scenarios with limited data. The paper's significance lies in its introduction of a new approach to stochastic process modeling that balances the benefits of prior-driven and data-driven methods, especially in data-scarce settings. The theoretical grounding of the method, combined with its empirical versatility, suggests a promising direction for future research in this area. However, as I will discuss in detail, there are several limitations that need to be addressed to fully realize the potential of this approach.

## Strengths:

The primary strength of this paper lies in its introduction of the noise-to-process (N2P) paradigm, which offers a fresh perspective on stochastic process modeling, particularly in scenarios with limited data. The N2P paradigm, with its core idea of mapping a shared base-noise process to a full trajectory via a measurable generator, presents a compelling approach that balances the benefits of prior-driven and data-driven methods. This is a significant contribution, as it addresses the challenge of modeling stochastic processes when only a single trajectory is available, a common problem in many real-world applications. The theoretical foundation provided for the N2P paradigm is another notable strength. The authors present formal proofs of projective consistency and compatibility with the Kolmogorov extension theorem, which lends credibility to the proposed method and demonstrates a rigorous understanding of the underlying mathematical principles. This theoretical grounding is crucial for establishing the validity and reliability of the N2P paradigm. Furthermore, the empirical evaluation of the proposed method is comprehensive, spanning diverse tasks such as synthetic data generation, time series modeling, image completion, and black-box optimization. This broad range of experiments demonstrates the versatility and effectiveness of the DBPT model, showing that it can be applied to various types of data and tasks. The fact that DBPT achieves competitive performance compared to prior-driven and data-driven baselines across these tasks is a testament to its potential. The authors also do a good job of explaining the intuition behind the method and the design choices, which makes the paper accessible and easy to follow. The use of deconvolution layers to capture long-range dependencies is a clever approach, and the authors provide a clear explanation of how this is achieved. Overall, the paper presents a novel and well-supported approach to stochastic process modeling, with a strong theoretical foundation and promising empirical results.


## Weaknesses:

Despite the strengths of the proposed N2P paradigm and DBPT model, several weaknesses need to be addressed. First, the paper lacks a detailed discussion of the computational complexity and scalability of the DBPT model. While the authors describe the deconvolution-based process decoder in Section 2.3.1, they do not provide a rigorous analysis of how the deconvolution operation scales with increasing trajectory length and input dimension. This is a significant oversight, as the deconvolution operation can be computationally expensive, and its scalability is crucial for practical applications. Furthermore, the paper does not address the memory requirements of the method, particularly for storing intermediate feature maps during the deconvolution process. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to assess the practical applicability of the method, especially when dealing with high-dimensional data or longer trajectories. The absence of a computational complexity analysis is a major limitation, as it prevents a clear understanding of the trade-offs involved in using the DBPT model compared to other methods. Second, the paper primarily focuses on the deconvolution-based instantiation of the N2P paradigm. While the authors present the N2P paradigm as a general framework, the paper's core contribution is the DBPT model, and there is no exploration of alternative instantiations or architectures. This limits the demonstration of the framework's full potential and flexibility. For example, the authors could have investigated alternative transformations, such as recurrent neural networks (RNNs) or transformers, to model the temporal dependencies in the stochastic process. These architectures might offer different trade-offs in terms of computational cost and modeling capacity. The lack of exploration of alternative instantiations limits the scope of the framework's applicability and leaves open questions about its versatility. Third, the paper does not provide sufficient insights into the limitations of the DBPT model, particularly in scenarios where the underlying stochastic process exhibits highly complex or non-smooth dynamics. The paper does not discuss how the model performs when the data contains abrupt changes or discontinuities, and whether the deconvolution-based approach is inherently limited in capturing such dynamics. This is a critical limitation, as many real-world stochastic processes exhibit non-smooth behavior. The absence of a detailed analysis of the model's failure modes makes it difficult to assess its robustness and reliability in challenging scenarios. The paper also lacks a theoretical analysis of the conditions under which the method can succeed with limited data, and how the choice of the base noise process affects the performance in low-data regimes. This is particularly important given that the paper focuses on scenarios with limited data, such as single-trajectory settings. The lack of such analysis makes it difficult to understand the limitations of the method in these critical scenarios. Finally, the empirical results are not consistently strong. As noted by Reviewer 2, the experimental results are quite mixed, and in several cases, the proposed method is outperformed by the baselines both in terms of predictive accuracy and uncertainty quantification. For example, in Table 1, WGP and MARKOV have lower MSE on average than DBPT, and in Table 2, CNP outperforms DBPT on CIFAR. This inconsistency in performance raises concerns about the robustness and generalizability of the proposed method. The lack of comparison to several important baselines, such as sequential neural processes, autoregressive conditional neural processes, and relational conditional neural processes, further weakens the empirical evaluation. These methods, being adaptations of conditional neural processes, have shown strong performance in similar scenarios and should have been included for a fair comparison. The absence of these comparisons makes it difficult to assess the true performance of the DBPT model relative to state-of-the-art techniques.


## Suggestions:

To address the identified weaknesses, several improvements can be made. First, the paper should include a detailed analysis of the computational complexity and scalability of the DBPT model. This analysis should include a breakdown of the time and space complexity of the deconvolution operation, considering the impact of trajectory length and input dimension. The authors should also discuss the number of parameters in the model and how it scales with the size of the input. Furthermore, it would be valuable to explore techniques for optimizing the implementation of the deconvolution operation, such as using FFT-based convolution or other efficient algorithms. The authors should also consider the practical implications of these computational costs, especially when dealing with large datasets or real-time applications. A comparison with the computational complexity of the baseline methods would also be beneficial to provide a more complete picture of the trade-offs involved. Second, the authors should explore alternative instantiations of the N2P paradigm beyond the deconvolution-based approach. For example, they could investigate the use of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) or transformers to model the temporal dependencies in the stochastic process. These architectures might offer different trade-offs in terms of computational cost and modeling capacity. Additionally, the authors could explore the use of attention mechanisms to allow the model to focus on the most relevant parts of the input trajectory. This would allow for a more flexible and adaptable framework that can be tailored to different types of stochastic processes. The exploration of these alternative architectures would demonstrate the versatility of the N2P framework and its potential for broader applicability. Third, the paper should include a more detailed analysis of the limitations of the DBPT model, particularly in scenarios with highly complex or non-smooth dynamics. The authors should investigate the model's performance on datasets that contain abrupt changes or discontinuities, and discuss the reasons for any observed limitations. This analysis should include a discussion of the inherent limitations of the deconvolution-based approach and whether alternative transformations might be better suited for capturing such dynamics. Furthermore, the authors should explore techniques for improving the robustness of the model, such as using adversarial training or other regularization methods. A more thorough analysis of the model's failure modes would provide valuable insights into its strengths and weaknesses and guide future research directions. Fourth, the paper should include a more thorough empirical evaluation, including comparisons to sequential neural processes, autoregressive conditional neural processes, and relational conditional neural processes, which are all relevant baselines for the one-trajectory setting. These methods, being adaptations of conditional neural processes, have shown strong performance in similar scenarios and should be included for a fair comparison. Furthermore, methods that learn stochastic differential equations from data, such as those based on neural SDEs or other likelihood-free inference techniques, should also be included as baselines. These methods represent a different approach to modeling stochastic processes and could provide valuable insights into the strengths and weaknesses of the proposed method. The authors should also provide a more detailed analysis of the performance of their method across different sample sizes, and discuss the limitations of their approach when the number of observations is very small. Finally, the authors should provide a theoretical analysis of the conditions under which their method can succeed with limited data, and discuss how the choice of the base noise process affects the performance in low-data regimes. This analysis should include a discussion of the inherent limitations of the deconvolution-based approach and whether alternative transformations might be better suited for capturing such dynamics. Furthermore, the authors should explore techniques for improving the robustness of the model, such as using adversarial training or other regularization methods.


## Questions:

Several questions arise from my analysis of this paper. First, how does the choice of the base-noise process affect the performance of the N2P paradigm? Are there specific types of base-noise processes that are better suited for certain types of stochastic processes? This is a crucial question, as the base-noise process is a key component of the N2P framework, and its choice could significantly impact the model's performance. Second, the paper focuses on a discrete-time setting. How could the N2P paradigm be extended to continuous-time stochastic processes? What challenges might arise in such an extension? This is an important question, as many real-world stochastic processes are continuous in time, and extending the N2P paradigm to this setting would significantly broaden its applicability. Third, in the black-box optimization experiments, how does the performance of DBPT compare to other surrogate-based optimization methods that are specifically designed for this task? What are the advantages and disadvantages of using DBPT in this context? This question is important for understanding the practical utility of the proposed method in optimization problems. Fourth, the paper mentions that the N2P paradigm ensures projective consistency by design. Could the authors provide more intuitive explanations or visualizations to illustrate this concept and its benefits compared to traditional approaches? This would help to clarify the theoretical advantages of the N2P paradigm and make it more accessible to a broader audience. Fifth, how does the DBPT model handle irregularly sampled data or trajectories with missing observations? Are there any modifications or extensions to the model that could address such scenarios? This is a critical question, as real-world data is often not uniformly sampled, and the ability to handle irregularly sampled data is essential for practical applications. Sixth, the paper focuses on single-trajectory modeling. How could the N2P paradigm and DBPT be extended or adapted to handle multi-trajectory data or meta-learning scenarios where information from multiple trajectories can be leveraged? This question is important for understanding the potential of the N2P paradigm in more complex learning scenarios. Seventh, in the image completion experiments, how does the performance of DBPT compare to specialized image completion models that are not based on stochastic process modeling? Could the N2P paradigm be combined with such models to further improve performance? This question is important for understanding the practical utility of the proposed method in image processing tasks. Finally, in Section 2.3.2, it is stated that the loss is a function of $\theta$ and not a random variable of $Z$, since the expectation over $Z$ is approximated using Monte Carlo. However, at test time, the loss will be a random variable, since it will depend on the particular draw of $Z$. How do you make predictions with a random loss? This is a crucial question that needs clarification.
