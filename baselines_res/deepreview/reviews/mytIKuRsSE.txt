## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel framework, termed RULE (dually Robust Learning), to address the problem of Dual-level Noisy Correspondence (DNC) in Multi-modal Entity Alignment (MMEA). The authors identify DNC as a significant challenge in MMEA, where noise exists both in intra-entity (entity-attribute) and inter-graph (entity-entity and attribute-attribute) correspondences. This is a critical issue because existing methods often overlook this dual-level noise, leading to suboptimal alignment results. The core idea behind RULE is to estimate the reliability of correspondences at both the intra-entity and inter-graph levels using a two-fold principle of uncertainty and consensus. This reliability estimation is then used to mitigate the negative impact of noise during attribute fusion and inter-graph discrepancy elimination. The framework incorporates a correspondence reasoning module to enhance test-time robustness, which is a significant contribution. The authors propose a novel approach to handle noisy correspondences by leveraging a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approach to guide a Multi-Layered Language Model (MLLM). This module aims to improve the reliability of the correspondence by reasoning about the relationships between entities and attributes. The paper presents extensive experimental results on five benchmark datasets, demonstrating the effectiveness of RULE compared to seven state-of-the-art methods. The results show that RULE consistently outperforms existing methods, highlighting its ability to handle DNC effectively. The paper's main contribution lies in identifying and addressing the overlooked problem of DNC in MMEA, proposing a robust framework to mitigate its impact, and demonstrating the effectiveness of this framework through comprehensive experiments. The authors' approach of using uncertainty and consensus to estimate reliability, combined with a correspondence reasoning module, offers a novel and effective way to tackle the challenges posed by noisy correspondences in multi-modal entity alignment. The paper's findings suggest that addressing DNC is crucial for achieving accurate and robust MMEA, and the proposed RULE framework provides a promising direction for future research in this area. The paper's focus on practical challenges and its empirical validation make it a valuable contribution to the field of knowledge graph alignment.

## Strengths:

The paper's primary strength lies in its identification and rigorous treatment of the Dual-level Noisy Correspondence (DNC) problem within Multi-modal Entity Alignment (MMEA). This is a novel and practical issue that has been largely overlooked in previous research, as the authors convincingly argue. The introduction of the RULE framework, designed specifically to address DNC, is a significant contribution. The two-fold principle of uncertainty and consensus for reliability estimation is both innovative and effective. By estimating the reliability of both intra-entity and inter-graph correspondences, the framework is able to mitigate the negative impact of noise in a more comprehensive manner than existing methods. The correspondence reasoning module, which leverages a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approach to guide a Multi-Layered Language Model (MLLM), is another notable strength. This module enhances test-time robustness, which is crucial for real-world applications where noise is often unavoidable. The experimental results presented in the paper are comprehensive and compelling. The authors conduct extensive experiments on five benchmark datasets, comparing RULE against seven state-of-the-art methods. The results consistently demonstrate the superiority of RULE, providing strong empirical evidence for its effectiveness. The paper's focus on a practical problem, coupled with its novel methodological approach and strong empirical validation, makes it a valuable contribution to the field of knowledge graph alignment. The authors have successfully identified a critical gap in existing research and have proposed a robust and effective solution. The clarity of the problem statement, the technical soundness of the proposed method, and the thoroughness of the experimental evaluation all contribute to the paper's overall strength. The paper's focus on practical challenges and its empirical validation make it a valuable contribution to the field of knowledge graph alignment.


## Weaknesses:

While the paper presents a compelling approach to addressing Dual-level Noisy Correspondence (DNC) in Multi-modal Entity Alignment (MMEA), several weaknesses warrant careful consideration. First, the paper lacks a comprehensive discussion of its limitations and potential directions for future research. Specifically, the authors do not address the scalability of the proposed method to larger and more complex datasets. While the experiments are conducted on benchmark datasets, there is no analysis of how the method would perform with significantly larger knowledge graphs, which is a crucial consideration for real-world applications. Furthermore, the paper does not delve into the computational cost associated with the proposed framework. This is a critical omission, as the computational complexity of the method, particularly the correspondence reasoning module, could limit its practical applicability. The paper also lacks a detailed analysis of the method's sensitivity to hyperparameter tuning. While some hyperparameters are mentioned, there is no discussion of how these parameters were chosen or how sensitive the method's performance is to variations in these values. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to assess the robustness of the method and to provide guidelines for its practical application. The paper also does not systematically explore the impact of varying levels of noise in the correspondence data. While the authors mention the DNC problem and conduct experiments with 20% and 50% noise levels, a more granular analysis of the method's performance under different noise levels is needed. This would provide a better understanding of the method's robustness and its limitations in highly noisy scenarios. The paper's description of the correspondence reasoning module, particularly how it leverages the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approach to guide the MLLM, lacks sufficient detail. The paper does not provide specifics on how the intermediate reasoning steps are generated, how the MLLM is prompted to perform reasoning, or how the outputs of the MLLM are processed to obtain the reliability weights. This lack of detail makes it difficult to understand and reproduce this crucial component of the framework. The experimental setup description also lacks crucial details for reproducibility. The paper does not provide specifics on the optimization algorithm, learning rate, batch size, and training epochs used in the experiments. Additionally, the paper does not discuss the specific hardware configurations used for the experiments. This lack of information makes it difficult for other researchers to replicate the results and build upon this work. Finally, the paper's analysis of the results lacks statistical rigor. The paper reports average performance metrics but does not provide insights into the variance of the results or conduct statistical significance tests. Furthermore, the paper does not compare the computational efficiency of the proposed method with existing approaches. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to assess the practical significance of the observed improvements and to evaluate the method's efficiency. These weaknesses, taken together, limit the paper's overall impact and raise questions about the practical applicability of the proposed method in real-world scenarios. The lack of detailed analysis and the absence of crucial implementation details make it difficult to fully assess the method's robustness and generalizability. The confidence level for each of these identified weaknesses is high, as they are directly supported by the absence of specific information or analysis within the paper.
