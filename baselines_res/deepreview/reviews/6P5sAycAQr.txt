## Summary:

This paper introduces Defined Taxonomic Stratification (DefNTaxS), a novel framework designed to enhance zero-shot image classification using vision-language models (VLMs) like CLIP. The core idea behind DefNTaxS is to leverage large language models (LLMs) to automatically discover and integrate taxonomic relationships into class representations. This is achieved by clustering semantically related classes into hierarchical subcategories, which are then used to augment the class labels with contextual information. For example, a class like 'boxer' might be augmented with the context 'commonly found among dog breeds' or 'used for combat sports', depending on its semantic grouping. This approach aims to disambiguate semantically similar classes, thereby improving classification accuracy without requiring model retraining or manual prompt engineering. The method operates in a fully automated, training-free manner, making it highly practical and scalable. The authors evaluate DefNTaxS on seven benchmark datasets, including ImageNet and EuroSAT, demonstrating significant improvements over existing zero-shot classification methods. Specifically, the method achieves an average accuracy gain of 5.5%, with up to 13% improvement on the EuroSAT dataset, which is known for its high inter-class similarity. The authors also show that the method maintains strong performance on ImageNetV2, indicating that the taxonomic context provides genuine disambiguation rather than dataset-specific overfitting. The paper presents a comprehensive solution that not only identifies taxonomic relationships but also integrates them into the classification process in an automated way. The experiments show that DefNTaxS consistently outperforms existing methods across multiple benchmarks, demonstrating the practical effectiveness of incorporating taxonomic context. The authors conduct a thorough evaluation of their method across seven standard benchmarks, including ImageNet and EuroSAT, providing a robust assessment of its performance. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to zero-shot image classification by leveraging the power of LLMs to enhance the semantic understanding of class labels.
