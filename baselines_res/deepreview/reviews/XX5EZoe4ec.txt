## Summary:

This paper introduces RetrievalFormer, a novel transformer-based dual-encoder architecture designed for scalable and efficient recommendation systems. The core contribution of RetrievalFormer lies in its ability to combine the strengths of transformer-based sequence modeling with the efficiency of Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) retrieval. The model employs a dual-encoder structure, where one tower processes user interaction sequences using a transformer, and the other encodes item features, including categorical, multi-valued categorical, and text-derived features, into dense embeddings. A key innovation is the attention-based heterogeneous feature fusion mechanism, which dynamically combines different feature modalities into a unified representation. The model is trained using contrastive learning with the InfoNCE loss, incorporating mixed negative sampling to address popularity bias. The authors introduce a Leave-One-Out Cold (LOOC) evaluation protocol to rigorously assess the model's ability to handle truly unseen items, a critical challenge in real-world recommendation systems. Empirically, RetrievalFormer demonstrates competitive accuracy compared to strong transformer-based sequential baselines on Amazon and MovieLens datasets, while achieving significant latency reductions, up to 288x, at a 10M-item scale due to the use of ANN search. The paper also shows the model's ability to recommend cold-start items, which is a significant advantage over traditional methods. The authors emphasize the practical applicability of their model, highlighting its potential for deployment in large-scale industrial settings. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and empirically validated approach to addressing the scalability and cold-start challenges in recommendation systems, making a valuable contribution to the field.
