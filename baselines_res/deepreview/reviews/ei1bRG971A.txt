## Summary:

This paper introduces Dynamic Nested Depth (DND), a novel post-training method designed to enhance the performance of large language models (LLMs) by selectively reprocessing critical tokens. The core idea behind DND is to identify tokens that require deeper processing and feed them back through an additional layer of the transformer, while leaving less critical tokens untouched. This approach aims to optimize computational resources by focusing on the most challenging parts of the input. The method employs a router, implemented as a simple linear layer, to select tokens for reprocessing based on their importance. The selection process is further refined by a router controlling loss, which enhances token selection distinguishability, and a threshold control scheme, which ensures selection stability. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of DND by integrating it into both dense and Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures, including Qwen3-1.7B, Llama3.2-1B, Gemma3-1B, and Qwen3-30B-A3B, through post-training. The empirical results show performance gains on diverse benchmarks, with minimal parameter and computational overhead. Specifically, the dense models showed improvements of 1.88%, 2.61%, and 2.50% respectively, while the MoE model demonstrated a 0.87% improvement. The paper also includes an analysis of the computational overhead, showing that DND introduces a relatively small increase in FLOPs and a slight decrease in throughput. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to improving LLM performance through dynamic computation, with a focus on efficiency and practical applicability. The method's ability to be integrated into existing models through post-training makes it a valuable contribution to the field, offering a way to enhance performance without requiring extensive retraining from scratch. The authors also provide an ablation study to demonstrate the impact of different components of the DND method, further solidifying the validity of their approach. The paper's clear presentation and thorough experimentation make it a significant contribution to the ongoing research on efficient and effective LLMs.

## Strengths:

DND introduces a compelling approach to enhancing LLM performance by dynamically allocating computational resources to the most critical tokens. This selective reprocessing mechanism, which feeds back important tokens for an additional round of processing, is a significant innovation. The method's ability to improve performance with minimal parameter and computational overhead is particularly noteworthy. The authors' decision to implement DND as a post-training method is also a significant advantage, as it allows for direct integration into existing dense and Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures without requiring extensive retraining from scratch. This makes DND a practical and readily applicable technique for improving the performance of a wide range of LLMs. The empirical results presented in the paper further support the effectiveness of DND.
