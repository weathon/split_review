## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel adversarial patch attack, termed Embedding Disruption Patch Attack (EDPA), specifically designed to target Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The core idea behind EDPA is to disrupt the alignment between visual and textual embeddings within the VLA model by maximizing the discrepancy between the latent representations of clean and adversarially perturbed visual inputs. This is achieved by crafting a patch that, when applied to the visual input, causes the model to misinterpret the scene and generate incorrect actions. The authors emphasize that EDPA is model-agnostic, requiring no prior knowledge of the model's architecture, action space, or the specific robotic manipulator being used, making it a versatile attack method. The paper also proposes a corresponding defense mechanism, which involves adversarial fine-tuning of the visual encoder. This fine-tuning process aims to enhance the robustness of the VLA model against EDPA by training the encoder to be less sensitive to adversarial perturbations in the visual input. The authors conduct extensive experiments on the LIBERO robotic simulation benchmark to evaluate both the effectiveness of the EDPA attack and the robustness of the proposed defense. The results demonstrate that EDPA significantly increases the task failure rates of VLA models, while the adversarial fine-tuning effectively mitigates this effect, improving the model's resilience to the attack. The paper's primary contribution lies in the introduction of a model-agnostic adversarial patch attack for VLA models and a corresponding defense strategy, along with empirical validation of these methods on a robotic simulation benchmark. The findings highlight the vulnerability of VLA models to adversarial attacks and underscore the importance of developing robust defense mechanisms to ensure their reliable operation in real-world scenarios. The authors also provide a detailed analysis of the generated adversarial patches, noting their structural patterns reminiscent of robotic arms, which offers insights into the nature of the adversarial perturbations that VLA models are susceptible to. Overall, this work makes a significant contribution to the field by addressing the critical issue of adversarial robustness in VLA models, which are increasingly being deployed in safety-critical applications.

## Strengths:

This paper presents several notable strengths that contribute to its overall significance. First and foremost, the introduction of the Embedding Disruption Patch Attack (EDPA) is a novel and valuable contribution. The model-agnostic nature of EDPA is particularly compelling, as it allows the attack to be applied across different VLA models without requiring specific knowledge of their internal architectures. This is a significant advancement over previous adversarial attacks that often rely on detailed model information. The authors' approach of targeting the embedding space, rather than the final action space, is also a clever and effective strategy. By disrupting the alignment between visual and textual embeddings, the attack effectively undermines the model's ability to interpret the scene and generate appropriate actions. The proposed adversarial fine-tuning defense mechanism is another strength of the paper. The authors demonstrate that this defense effectively mitigates the impact of EDPA, enhancing the robustness of VLA models against this specific type of attack. The fact that the defense is relatively straightforward to implement, involving fine-tuning of the visual encoder, makes it a practical solution for improving the security of VLA models. Furthermore, the paper's experimental evaluation is comprehensive, demonstrating the effectiveness of both the attack and defense mechanisms across multiple task suites within the LIBERO benchmark. The results clearly show that EDPA significantly increases task failure rates, while the adversarial fine-tuning effectively reduces these rates. The inclusion of comparisons with existing methods, such as UADA and UPA, further strengthens the paper's empirical findings. The authors also provide a detailed analysis of the generated adversarial patches, noting their structural patterns reminiscent of robotic arms. This observation offers valuable insights into the nature of the adversarial perturbations that VLA models are susceptible to and could potentially inform the development of more targeted defense strategies. Finally, the paper is well-written and easy to follow, making it accessible to a broad audience. The clear presentation of the methodology, experimental setup, and results contributes to the overall clarity and impact of the work. In summary, the paper's strengths lie in its novel attack method, effective defense mechanism, comprehensive evaluation, and clear presentation, making it a valuable contribution to the field of adversarial robustness in VLA models.


## Weaknesses:

While this paper presents a valuable contribution to the field, several weaknesses warrant careful consideration. A primary concern is the limited scope of the experimental evaluation. As explicitly stated in the '4.1 EXPERIMENT SETTINGS' section, the adversarial patch generation and evaluation are conducted solely on the LIBERO dataset, a simulation dataset designed for robotic manipulation. This reliance on a single dataset raises significant concerns about the generalizability of the findings. The paper lacks any evaluation on other robotic manipulation benchmarks, such as CALVIN or RoboMimic, which feature different types of tasks, environments, and object interactions. This limitation is further emphasized by the fact that the entire 'EXPERIMENT' section focuses exclusively on the LIBERO benchmark. The absence of evaluations on diverse datasets makes it difficult to assess whether the proposed attack and defense methods are effective in more realistic scenarios. This is a critical limitation, as the performance of adversarial attacks and defenses can vary significantly across different environments and task settings. The paper also suffers from a lack of comprehensive comparisons with existing adversarial patch attacks. While the authors justify their choice of baselines by stating the limited research in this domain, the comparison is primarily limited to UADA and UPA, as detailed in the '4.1 EXPERIMENT SETTINGS' section and demonstrated in Tables 2 and 3. This is insufficient to fully contextualize the performance of the proposed EDPA attack. The paper does not include comparisons with more recent and advanced adversarial patch attack methods, which could potentially offer stronger baselines or alternative approaches. This limited comparison makes it difficult to determine the relative strengths and weaknesses of EDPA compared to the broader landscape of adversarial attacks. Furthermore, the paper lacks a detailed ablation study to analyze the impact of different components of the proposed attack and defense methods. The 'EXPERIMENTS' section describes the setup and results for EDPA and the adversarial fine-tuning scheme but does not include any experiments that systematically vary components of these methods, such as patch size, shape, location, or fine-tuning parameters. This absence of ablation studies makes it difficult to understand the sensitivity of the attack and defense to different parameters and to identify the most critical factors that contribute to their effectiveness. For example, the paper does not explore how the size of the adversarial patch affects the performance of the attack or the computational cost. This lack of analysis limits the insights that can be gained from the paper and makes it difficult to optimize the proposed methods for different applications. Another significant weakness is the paper's primary focus on simulation environments. As stated in the '4.1 EXPERIMENT SETTINGS' section, the experiments are conducted on the LIBERO simulation benchmark. While the authors acknowledge the challenges of transferring these findings to real-world settings in the '6 LIMITATION' section, there is no in-depth discussion or experimental analysis of how factors such as sensor noise, lighting variations, and real-world object occlusions might affect the effectiveness of the proposed attack and defense mechanisms. The simulation environment, while useful, may not fully capture the complexities of real-world robotic manipulation tasks, potentially leading to an overestimation of the method's performance in practical scenarios. This lack of consideration for real-world factors limits the practical applicability of the findings. Additionally, the paper's evaluation is limited to a specific set of VLA models, namely OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, and $\pi_0$, as detailed in the '4.1 EXPERIMENT SETTINGS' section. While the paper does evaluate on multiple models, it does not explore how the attack and defense perform on models with different architectures or training methodologies. This limits the conclusions that can be drawn about the robustness of the proposed method across the diverse landscape of VLA models. The paper does not investigate how the attack and defense perform on models with different vision encoders, language models, or action decoders, which is a critical aspect of assessing the generalizability of the proposed methods. Finally, the paper lacks a detailed analysis of the failure modes of the proposed attack and defense methods. The 'RESULTS' sections of the experiments provide overall failure rates but do not delve into specific failure scenarios or task-specific vulnerabilities. The paper does not investigate which types of tasks or environments are more susceptible to the attack, nor does it analyze the cases where the defense fails to mitigate the attack. This lack of analysis limits the insights that can be gained from the paper and makes it difficult to identify potential weaknesses of the VLA models and guide the development of more robust architectures. In summary, the paper's weaknesses include the limited scope of evaluation, lack of comprehensive comparisons, absence of ablation studies, focus on simulation environments, limited model diversity, and lack of failure mode analysis. These limitations significantly impact the generalizability and practical applicability of the findings.


## Suggestions:

To address the identified weaknesses, several concrete improvements can be made. First and foremost, the paper should significantly expand the scope of its experimental evaluation. This includes incorporating multiple datasets beyond LIBERO, such as CALVIN or RoboMimic, which offer different task complexities, object interactions, and environmental conditions. This would provide a more robust assessment of the generalizability of the proposed attack and defense methods. The evaluation should also include a more diverse set of tasks, such as those involving more complex manipulation skills or multi-step procedures, to fully assess the robustness of the proposed attack and defense strategies. Furthermore, the paper should include a more comprehensive comparison with state-of-the-art adversarial patch attacks. This would involve including more recent and advanced adversarial patch attack methods, particularly those that have demonstrated strong performance in similar settings. This would provide a more rigorous evaluation of the proposed attack's effectiveness and its advantages over existing approaches. The comparison should not only focus on the attack success rate but also consider other relevant metrics, such as the computational cost and the transferability of the generated adversarial patches to different models or environments. In addition to expanding the evaluation, the paper should include a detailed ablation study to analyze the impact of different components of the proposed attack and defense methods. For the attack, this would involve investigating the effect of varying the size, shape, and location of the adversarial patch, as well as the optimization parameters used to generate the patch. This would provide insights into the sensitivity of the attack to different parameters and help to identify the most critical factors that contribute to its effectiveness. For the defense method, it would be important to analyze the impact of different fine-tuning parameters, such as the learning rate, the number of epochs, and the choice of adversarial examples used for training. This would help to understand the robustness of the defense method and its ability to generalize to different types of adversarial attacks. The ablation study should also include an analysis of the trade-off between robustness and performance on clean examples, which is a critical aspect of any defense strategy. To address the limitations of the simulation-based evaluation, the authors should consider incorporating more realistic simulation parameters, such as adding noise to sensor data, varying lighting conditions, and introducing partial occlusions of objects. This would provide a more robust assessment of the method's performance under conditions that more closely resemble real-world scenarios. Furthermore, the authors could explore the use of domain adaptation techniques to bridge the gap between simulation and real-world environments. This could involve training the model on a combination of simulated and real-world data, or using techniques such as adversarial domain adaptation to improve the model's generalization capabilities. A more detailed discussion of the potential challenges and limitations of transferring the proposed method to real-world settings would also be beneficial. To enhance the generalizability of the findings, the authors should evaluate the proposed attack and defense mechanisms on a wider range of VLA models, including those with different architectures and training methodologies. This could involve testing the method on models that use different vision encoders (e.g., ResNet, ViT), language models (e.g., BERT, GPT), and action decoders. Additionally, the authors could explore the impact of different training techniques, such as different loss functions or data augmentation strategies, on the robustness of the models. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the method's effectiveness across the diverse landscape of VLA models and help identify potential limitations or areas for improvement. The authors should also consider evaluating the method on models trained on different datasets to assess its performance across different task domains. Finally, the paper should provide a more detailed analysis of the failure modes of the proposed attack and defense mechanisms. This could involve examining the specific types of errors that occur when the attack is successful, as well as the conditions under which the defense is less effective. This analysis could provide valuable insights into the underlying vulnerabilities of VLA models and help guide the development of more robust and secure systems. For example, the authors could investigate whether the attack is more effective on certain types of objects or tasks, or whether the defense is less effective when the model is exposed to specific types of adversarial patches. This deeper analysis would provide a more nuanced understanding of the method's strengths and weaknesses. By addressing these points, the paper can significantly enhance its impact and contribute more effectively to the field of adversarial robustness in VLA models.

Specifically, what is the minimum patch size required to achieve a successful attack, and how does this relate to the computational cost of generating and applying the patch? Second, what is the computational cost of the proposed attack and defense methods? The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational resources required for generating adversarial patches and for fine-tuning the visual encoder. A comparison of the computational cost with existing methods would be beneficial for assessing the practicality of the proposed approach. This should include the time and memory requirements for both the attack and defense mechanisms. Third, how does the proposed defense method affect the performance of the VLA models on clean, non-adversarial inputs? It is important to ensure that the defense method does not degrade the performance of the models on clean inputs. The paper should provide a detailed analysis of the trade-off between robustness and performance on clean examples. Specifically, what is the impact of the adversarial fine-tuning on the model's accuracy and efficiency when processing clean data? Fourth, how does the proposed adversarial patch perform in real-world scenarios, where the environment is less controlled than in simulation? The paper focuses exclusively on simulation environments, and it would be valuable to have preliminary results or insights on how the attack and defense mechanisms would perform in real-world settings with factors such as sensor noise, lighting variations, and real-world object occlusions. What are the potential challenges and limitations of transferring the proposed method to real-world settings? Fifth, could the adversarial fine-tuning method potentially degrade the performance of the VLA model on clean, non-adversarial inputs? If so, how significant is this trade-off? The paper should provide a more detailed analysis of the impact of the adversarial fine-tuning on the model's performance on clean data. What are the potential negative consequences of the fine-tuning process, and how can these be mitigated? Finally, how does the method handle scenarios with multiple potential adversarial patches within the same scene? Is there a mechanism to prioritize or address multiple threats simultaneously? The paper does not discuss how the proposed method would handle situations where multiple adversarial patches are present in the visual input. What are the limitations of the current approach in such scenarios, and how can these limitations be addressed? These questions highlight key uncertainties and areas for further investigation that would significantly enhance the paper's contribution and practical applicability.
