## Summary:

This paper introduces Direct Optimal Action Learning (DOAL), a novel framework for policy extraction in offline reinforcement learning (RL). The core idea behind DOAL is to directly learn an optimized action based on Q-values, rather than relying on backpropagation through iterative sampling chains, which can be computationally expensive and require careful tuning. The authors achieve this by leveraging behavior cloning losses and introducing a Batch-Normalizing Optimizer, which reinterprets the traditional balancing factor between Q-loss and behavior-loss as a mechanism for selecting a trust region for optimal action. This optimizer simplifies hyperparameter tuning by making the trust region size shareable across policies, which is a valuable practical advantage. DOAL is designed to be versatile and can be integrated with various Q-value-based offline RL methods, and the authors demonstrate its compatibility with Gaussian, flow, and diffusion policies. The empirical results presented in the paper show that DOAL outperforms strong baseline models on several OGBench tasks, particularly when combined with regularized Q-learning. The authors also show that DOAL improves over baseline models on Adroit tasks from D4RL, but these improvements are more pronounced when regularized Q-learning is used. The paper also discusses the computational efficiency of DOAL, showing that it adds minimal overhead compared to baseline methods. Overall, the paper presents a promising approach to policy extraction in offline RL, with a focus on computational efficiency and simplified hyperparameter tuning. The core contribution lies in the novel approach to policy extraction, the introduction of the Batch-Normalizing Optimizer, and the empirical validation of DOAL's effectiveness across different policy models and Q-value functions. The paper's findings suggest that DOAL is a valuable addition to the offline RL toolkit, offering a computationally efficient and effective alternative to existing policy extraction methods.

## Strengths:

I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. First, the introduction of Direct Optimal Action Learning (DOAL) provides a novel approach to policy extraction in offline RL. By directly learning the optimized action based on Q-values, DOAL simplifies the training process and reduces the computational overhead associated with backpropagation through iterative sampling chains. This is a significant contribution, as it addresses a common bottleneck in offline RL. Second, the Batch-Normalizing Optimizer is a notable innovation. By reinterpreting the traditional balancing factor between Q-loss and behavior-loss as a mechanism for selecting a trust region for optimal action, the authors have simplified hyperparameter tuning and made it shareable across policies. This is a valuable practical advantage, as it reduces the need for extensive hyperparameter searches. The fact that DOAL can be easily integrated with existing Q-value-based offline RL methods is another strength. The authors demonstrate this versatility by testing DOAL with various policies, including Gaussian, flow, and diffusion models. This shows that the framework is not limited to a specific type of policy and can be adapted to different modeling approaches. The empirical results presented in the paper are compelling. The authors demonstrate that DOAL improves over strong baseline models on both OGBench and Adroit tasks. This suggests that the proposed method is effective in practice and can lead to better performance in offline RL tasks. The paper also provides a clear and well-structured presentation of the DOAL framework, making it easy to understand the core concepts and contributions. The inclusion of detailed experimental results and comparisons with baseline models further enhances the clarity and credibility of the work.
