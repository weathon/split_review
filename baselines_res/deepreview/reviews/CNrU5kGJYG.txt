## Summary:

This paper introduces TrojanTO, a novel post-training action-level backdoor attack framework specifically designed for Trajectory Optimization (TO) models in offline reinforcement learning. The core contribution of this work lies in its systematic investigation of action-level backdoors within the context of TO models, a domain that has been largely unexplored in the literature. TrojanTO leverages an alternating training strategy to establish a strong connection between a trigger and a target action, ensuring high attack effectiveness. To maintain stealth and preserve benign performance, the framework employs trajectory filtering and batch poisoning techniques. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of TrojanTO across diverse tasks and TO model architectures, including Decision Transformers (DT), Gated Decision Transformers (GDT), and Decision Convolutions (DC), using a low attack budget of only 0.3% of trajectories. The paper's methodology involves a comprehensive investigation into the factors influencing TO model security, revealing that both action and state are essential elements, which is a novel insight. The empirical findings show that TrojanTO achieves high attack success rates while maintaining a negligible impact on the agent's benign performance. The paper also includes a detailed analysis of the impact of trajectory filtering and trigger design on attack performance. The authors further evaluate the robustness of TrojanTO against various defense methods, providing a comprehensive understanding of the attack's capabilities and limitations. Overall, this paper makes a significant contribution by highlighting a critical security vulnerability in TO models and proposing an effective attack framework, thereby opening up a new avenue for research in the security of offline reinforcement learning.
