## Summary:

This paper introduces Calgacus, a novel steganographic method that leverages the capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) to conceal arbitrary text within other plausible text of the same length. The core idea behind Calgacus is to encode a secret message by manipulating the token ranks during the LLM's text generation process. Specifically, the method works by first encoding the original text's token ranks, and then using these ranks to guide the generation of a steganographic text that appears coherent and plausible. The authors demonstrate that this method allows for the recovery of the original message by those who possess the necessary key, which is used to steer the LLM's generation process. The paper explores the implications of this method for AI safety and the nature of text generation, raising important questions about the potential for misuse and the very definition of text generated by LLMs. The method is presented as a significant departure from traditional steganographic techniques, offering a new way to hide information in plain sight using the power of LLMs. The authors provide examples of the method in action, showing how a hash or a political critique can be hidden within seemingly innocuous text. The paper also discusses the challenges associated with distinguishing steganographic text from real text, highlighting the potential for this method to be used for malicious purposes. The authors also touch upon the implications of this method for AI safety, suggesting that it could be used to circumvent safety mechanisms and introduce unaligned content into LLM outputs. The paper concludes by emphasizing the need for further research into the detection and mitigation of such steganographic techniques. Overall, the paper presents a compelling and thought-provoking exploration of a novel application of LLMs, raising important questions about the future of text generation and AI safety. The method is shown to be efficient and can be implemented on consumer hardware, making it accessible to a wide range of users. The authors also discuss the limitations of the method, such as its dependence on the quality of the LLM and the need for a secret key. The paper's discussion of the implications of the method for AI safety and the nature of text generation is insightful and adds depth to the paper's contribution. The authors also provide a detailed analysis of the method's performance, including its ability to generate plausible steganographic texts and the challenges associated with distinguishing these texts from real texts. The paper's exploration of the potential for this method to be used for malicious purposes is also a valuable contribution, highlighting the need for further research into the detection and mitigation of such steganographic techniques. The paper's overall contribution lies in its introduction of a novel steganographic method that leverages the power of LLMs, and its exploration of the implications of this method for AI safety and the nature of text generation.

## Weaknesses:

My analysis of this paper has revealed several significant weaknesses that warrant careful consideration. First, the method's effectiveness is highly dependent on the quality of the underlying LLM. As the authors themselves acknowledge, the method requires a "good" LLM, meaning one that assigns high probabilities to plausible text (Appendix A.2). If the LLM is not sufficiently advanced, the generated steganographic texts may not be coherent or plausible, which could compromise the method's security. This dependence is evident in the paper's discussion of low-entropy token choices, where the authors note that the method may force the selection of less probable tokens, leading to less plausible stegotexts (Section 3, Low entropy token choices). The paper provides an example of this, showing that a hash encoded using the method produces a broken stegotext (Section 3, When the stegotext s sounds like a real text). This reliance on token rank preservation can lead to less probable word choices, making the steganographic text more susceptible to detection by frequency analysis or other statistical methods. The paper does not include a systematic analysis of how the method performs with LLMs of varying sizes and architectures, which is a significant oversight. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to assess the practical limitations of the method and identify the minimum LLM capabilities required for effective steganography. My confidence in this weakness is high, as it is directly supported by the paper's own discussion of the method's limitations and the lack of comparative experiments. Second, the security of the method relies heavily on the secrecy of the key, which is used to guide the LLM in generating the stegotext. The paper acknowledges that the method is vulnerable to brute-force attacks if the key is not properly managed (Section 3.1, Security). While the authors state that a naive brute-force attack is impractical due to the large key space, they also acknowledge that an attacker could reduce the search space by exploiting the fact that the key is expected to be a mostly sound instruction in natural language (Section 3.1, Security). This vulnerability is a significant concern, as it makes the method susceptible to targeted attacks. The paper does not sufficiently discuss the practical limitations of key management, especially in scenarios where the key might be compromised or exposed. This lack of discussion is a significant oversight, as key management is a critical aspect of any cryptographic or steganographic system. My confidence in this weakness is high, as it is directly supported by the paper's own discussion of the method's security and the lack of discussion on key management practices. Finally, the paper does not extensively discuss potential countermeasures to the proposed method, which is a significant gap in the analysis. The paper focuses on the method of hiding text and its implications, but it does not explore how such steganographic content could be detected or mitigated. The paper lacks any discussion of techniques like anomaly detection, statistical analysis of token distributions, or adversarial training, which could potentially be used to identify steganographic texts generated by Calgacus. This omission is a significant oversight, as the ability to detect and counteract steganographic techniques is crucial for maintaining the security and integrity of information systems. The lack of discussion on these countermeasures leaves a gap in the analysis of the method's practical implications and potential vulnerabilities. The paper's focus on the creation and properties of the stegotext, without addressing the possibility of detection, limits its practical relevance and raises concerns about its potential for misuse.


## Suggestions:

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the paper should include a more thorough investigation into the relationship between the quality of the LLM and the plausibility of the generated steganographic text. This should include a quantitative analysis of the perplexity or other relevant metrics of the generated text, comparing it to the original text and random text. The authors should also explore how the method performs with different LLMs, including smaller models and models trained on different datasets. This would help to establish the practical limitations of the method and identify the minimum LLM capabilities required for effective steganography. Furthermore, the paper should analyze the impact of the encoding process on the probability distribution of the generated text. Specifically, it should investigate how the forced ranking of tokens affects the overall probability of the steganographic text and whether this makes it more detectable than naturally occurring text. This analysis should include a comparison of the token frequency distributions in the steganographic text with those in the original text and in a large corpus of natural language. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the method's limitations and its applicability in different scenarios. Second, the paper should address the practical challenges of key management in more detail. The authors should discuss potential strategies for generating, distributing, and storing keys securely, and should consider the implications of key compromise. It would be useful to explore the use of key derivation functions or other cryptographic techniques to enhance the security of the key. Additionally, the paper should analyze the feasibility of brute-force attacks against the key, and should provide recommendations for key lengths and complexity that would make such attacks computationally infeasible. A more thorough discussion of these practical considerations would significantly strengthen the paper's contribution. Third, the paper should include a more comprehensive discussion of potential countermeasures to the proposed method. This could include an analysis of how statistical methods, machine learning techniques, or other forms of anomaly detection could be used to identify steganographic texts. The authors should also consider the possibility of adversarial attacks that could be used to disrupt the method's operation or to extract the hidden message without knowing the key. A more thorough analysis of these potential vulnerabilities and countermeasures would make the paper more relevant to the broader community and would help to ensure that the proposed method is used responsibly. This could involve analyzing the statistical properties of the stegotext, such as token frequencies, n-gram distributions, or semantic coherence. The paper could also investigate the use of anomaly detection techniques to identify text that deviates from the expected patterns of normal language. Furthermore, the authors should consider the possibility of using adversarial training to create models that are more robust to steganographic attacks. This would involve training a model to distinguish between normal text and stegotext, which could then be used to develop detection algorithms. By exploring these countermeasures, the paper would provide a more balanced perspective on the implications of the proposed method for AI safety. Finally, the paper should include a more detailed discussion of the practical applications and limitations of the method. This should include an analysis of the maximum size of the hidden message, the impact of the message length on the quality of the steganographic text, and the computational resources required for encoding and decoding. The authors should also discuss the potential for using the method in real-world scenarios, such as covert communication or data exfiltration, and the potential risks associated with these applications. This would help to contextualize the method's contribution and identify areas for future research.
