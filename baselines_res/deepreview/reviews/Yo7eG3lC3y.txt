## Summary:

This paper introduces LEGO-EVAL, a novel evaluation framework designed to assess the alignment between detailed textual instructions and generated 3D scenes. The authors convincingly argue that existing evaluation methods, such as CLIPScore and VLMs, often fall short in accurately evaluating fine-grained scene attributes and spatial relationships due to their limited understanding of 3D environments. To address this, LEGO-EVAL employs a tool-augmented approach, utilizing a diverse set of tools to explicitly ground scene components, enabling a more precise and interpretable assessment of instruction-scene alignment. The framework operates through a four-step process: constraint identification, tool execution planning, tool argument selection and execution, and constraint validation. The authors also present LEGO-BENCH, a benchmark dataset comprising detailed, fine-grained instructions designed to capture the complexity of real-world environments. This benchmark provides a standardized means of evaluating different scene generation methods, facilitating fair comparisons and progress tracking. The experimental results presented in the paper demonstrate that LEGO-EVAL significantly outperforms existing methods, particularly VLM-as-a-judge, in assessing scene-instruction alignment. The authors also use LEGO-EVAL to benchmark several existing text-to-3D scene synthesis methods, revealing their limitations in fully satisfying detailed instructions, with success rates as low as 10%. This highlights the challenges in current scene generation approaches and underscores the need for more robust evaluation frameworks like LEGO-EVAL. The paper's core contribution lies in its novel evaluation framework that addresses the limitations of existing methods by using tool-augmented VLMs for multi-hop grounding, and the introduction of a benchmark dataset that provides a standardized way to evaluate 3D scene synthesis methods. Overall, the paper makes a significant contribution to the field by providing a more reliable and interpretable method for evaluating 3D scene synthesis, which is crucial for advancing the development of more realistic and accurate scene generation models.

It would be beneficial to provide these metrics for different model sizes and configurations, allowing readers to understand the trade-offs between accuracy and computational cost. Furthermore, the authors should discuss the potential for optimizing the framework for resource-constrained environments, such as using model quantization or pruning techniques. This would make the framework more accessible and practical for a wider range of applications. Second, the authors should conduct a more thorough analysis of LEGO-EVAL's performance in scenes with varying degrees of occlusion and spatial complexity. This could involve creating a subset of LEGO-BENCH with scenes specifically designed to test these challenging scenarios. The analysis should include quantitative metrics that measure the accuracy of constraint satisfaction in these scenes, as well as a qualitative analysis of the types of errors that occur. The authors should also discuss how the tool-augmented approach can be further improved to handle these challenges, such as by incorporating more sophisticated reasoning mechanisms or by using additional sensors or data sources. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the framework's capabilities and limitations. While the current benchmark focuses on instructions with clear, definable constraints, real-world instructions often involve implicit constraints or require more abstract reasoning. This detailed comparison would provide a clearer understanding of the unique contributions of LEGO-EVAL. Finally, the paper should include more qualitative examples to illustrate the effectiveness of LEGO-EVAL. While the quantitative results demonstrate the framework's superiority, qualitative examples would provide a more intuitive understanding of its capabilities.
