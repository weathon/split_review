## Summary:

This paper presents a theoretical investigation into the in-context learning (ICL) capabilities of Mamba models, with a particular focus on their robustness to outliers. The authors delve into the training dynamics of a one-layer Mamba model, which is composed of a linear attention component followed by a nonlinear gating layer. They demonstrate that Mamba's gating mechanism plays a crucial role in suppressing the influence of outliers, allowing the model to maintain accurate predictions even when a significant portion of the context examples are corrupted. This is contrasted with linear Transformer models, which, while potentially converging faster, are shown to be more susceptible to performance degradation in the presence of outliers. The authors provide a rigorous theoretical framework, including convergence and sample complexity analysis, to support their claims. They also characterize the mechanism by which Mamba implements ICL, highlighting the roles of linear attention and nonlinear gating. The paper includes empirical experiments using synthetic data, which validate the theoretical findings and demonstrate Mamba's superior performance compared to linear Transformers when outliers are present. Specifically, the experiments show that Mamba can tolerate a higher proportion of outliers in the prompt without a significant drop in performance. The core contribution of this work lies in its theoretical analysis of Mamba's ICL capabilities, particularly in the context of outlier robustness, and in establishing a comparison with linear Transformers under the same conditions. This analysis provides valuable insights into the inner workings of Mamba models and their potential advantages in scenarios where data quality is a concern. The paper's findings suggest that Mamba's unique architecture, particularly its gating mechanism, makes it a promising alternative to Transformers in settings where robustness to noisy or corrupted data is crucial. The authors' work is a significant step towards understanding the theoretical underpinnings of Mamba's ICL abilities and provides a solid foundation for future research in this area. By offering a detailed comparison with linear Transformers, the paper also helps to clarify the relative strengths and weaknesses of these two architectures in the context of ICL and outlier robustness.

This simplification, while enabling a tractable theoretical analysis, may not fully capture the complexities of multi-layer Mamba models or other types of tasks, such as next-token prediction or natural language processing tasks. The single-layer architecture inherently limits the model's ability to capture hierarchical relationships in data, which are crucial for complex language understanding. The binary classification setup also restricts the analysis to a very specific type of learning problem, not reflecting the nuances of multi-class classification or regression tasks.
