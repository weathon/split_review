## Summary:

This paper introduces Stylus, a novel framework for 3D style transfer that leverages a single-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting representation. The core idea is to perform style transfer on unposed multi-view images of a scene, conditioned on a reference style image, without requiring per-scene optimization or precomputed poses. This is achieved through a carefully designed Transformer-based architecture that disentangles geometry and style information. The framework employs a shared backbone with two distinct pathways: one for geometry predictions, which retains self-attention to preserve geometric fidelity, and another for style injection, which uses cross-attention to enforce visual consistency across views. A key contribution is the introduction of a voxel-based 3D style loss, which aligns aggregated scene features to style statistics, ensuring view-consistent stylization while maintaining geometric coherence. The method is trained in two stages: first, a geometry pretraining stage, followed by a stylization fine-tuning stage. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach through extensive experiments on multiple datasets, showing that Stylus achieves high-quality zero-shot stylization, generalizing to unseen categories, scenes, and styles. The results highlight the effectiveness of the proposed style-content fusion block, the voxel-level style loss, and the scalability of the framework from single-view to large-scale multi-view settings. The paper presents a compelling approach to 3D style transfer, offering a single-forward pass solution that avoids the need for per-scene optimization, making it a practical and efficient method for stylizing 3D scenes.

## Strengths:

I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. The core idea of a single-forward 3D Gaussian framework for style transfer is compelling, as it avoids the need for per-scene optimization, a common bottleneck in many existing methods. The authors' approach to disentangling geometry and style using a shared Transformer backbone with two distinct pathways is both elegant and effective. The use of self-attention for geometry predictions and cross-attention for style injection allows the model to preserve geometric fidelity while ensuring visual consistency across views. The introduction of a voxel-based 3D style loss is a significant contribution, as it explicitly enforces view-consistent stylization by aligning aggregated scene features to style statistics. This loss function is a key factor in the method's ability to produce coherent stylized outputs from multiple viewpoints. The experimental results are also very promising, demonstrating the method's ability to achieve high-quality zero-shot stylization on unseen categories, scenes, and styles. The authors provide thorough ablation studies that analyze the impact of different design choices, further validating the effectiveness of their proposed components. The scalability of the framework, from single-view to large-scale multi-view settings, is another notable strength, highlighting its potential for practical applications. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, making the technical details accessible to a broad audience. The authors clearly explain the proposed method, including the architecture, training strategy, and style losses, which facilitates a thorough understanding of their approach. The use of a two-stage training process, with geometry pretraining followed by stylization fine-tuning, is a sensible approach that contributes to the overall performance of the model. The method's ability to generalize to unseen scenes and styles without per-scene optimization is a significant advantage, making it a practical solution for real-world applications. The authors have also provided sufficient experimental results to support their claims, which increases the credibility of their work.
