## Summary:

This paper introduces Prompt-Invariant Canonical Correlation Analysis Certificates (PI-CCA), a novel approach to continual learning in vision-language models (VLMs) that focuses on preserving the geometry of cross-modal alignment. The core idea is to summarize and maintain the essential alignment information without storing past data, addressing memory constraints in continual learning. PI-CCA achieves this by creating a compact 'CCA certificate' that captures the top-k canonical correlations and subspaces, which are then used to constrain alignment during training on new tasks. This method ensures that the model retains its zero-shot capabilities and prompt robustness. The authors also incorporate a prompt-robustness mechanism by averaging over prompt perturbations, enhancing the model's resilience to phrasing variations. The method is evaluated across multiple benchmarks, including MTIL, X-TAIL, VLCL, and ConStruct-VL, demonstrating its effectiveness in preserving zero-shot capabilities and resilience to prompt/style shifts. The paper presents a geometry-first approach to continual learning, which is a unique perspective compared to existing methods that rely on proxy signals or replay buffers. The empirical results show that PI-CCA achieves state-of-the-art performance among replay-free methods, highlighting its potential for practical applications. The authors also provide a detailed ablation study, analyzing the impact of different components of the method, and a scale and efficiency analysis, which explores the trade-offs between performance, memory, and computational cost. Overall, the paper makes a significant contribution to the field of continual learning in VLMs by introducing a novel and effective method for preserving cross-modal alignment geometry without relying on past data.

## Weaknesses:

While the paper presents a compelling approach, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant attention. First, the paper could benefit from a more thorough comparison with existing methods that also aim to preserve cross-modal alignment. While the authors mention related work, the comparison primarily focuses on the limitations of these methods in terms of regularizing proxy signals and relying on reference data. I found that a deeper dive into the specific mechanisms of cross-modal alignment preservation in those methods and a direct comparison of those mechanisms with PI-CCA's geometry-first approach is missing. For example, the paper could compare PI-CCA with methods that use replay buffers or knowledge distillation, analyzing how PI-CCA's approach of preserving alignment geometry compares to these alternatives. This lack of detailed mechanistic comparison makes it difficult to fully appreciate the unique advantages of PI-CCA. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper's focus on performance metrics rather than mechanistic differences is evident. Second, while the method is shown to be effective across several benchmarks, I believe it would be helpful to see an analysis of its performance on a wider range of tasks and datasets to assess its generalizability. The current evaluation focuses on standard benchmarks, but exploring performance on more diverse and challenging datasets could further validate the generalizability of the approach. For example, it would be useful to evaluate the method on datasets with different types of image and text modalities, as well as datasets with varying levels of noise and complexity.
