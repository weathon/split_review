## Summary:

This paper introduces STBP, a novel framework for continual spatio-temporal forecasting, designed to address the challenges of dynamic urban environments and evolving data streams. The core contribution of STBP lies in its integration of a general spatio-temporal backbone with a scalable contextual pattern bank. The spatio-temporal backbone, comprising a frequency-domain network (FreNet) and a dual-stream linear graph attention (DLGA) module, is responsible for capturing stable spatio-temporal patterns and dynamic spatial correlations. FreNet leverages frequency-domain analysis to extract robust temporal representations, while DLGA employs a lightweight linear attention mechanism to model spatial dependencies. The contextual pattern bank, on the other hand, is designed to mitigate catastrophic forgetting and adapt to evolving node-level heterogeneity. This bank is updated incrementally through parameter expansion, allowing the model to capture new patterns and relevance as the data evolves. The authors argue that this approach allows STBP to effectively handle distributional drift, model dynamic spatio-temporal correlations, alleviate catastrophic forgetting, and design an incremental strategy that efficiently collaborates with the backbone. The empirical evaluation of STBP is conducted on three real-world datasets from the traffic and meteorological domains, demonstrating superior performance in terms of forecasting accuracy, adaptability, and scalability compared to state-of-the-art baselines. The paper presents ablation studies and parameter sensitivity analyses to further validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework. The authors claim that STBP's modular design allows for the integration of various spatio-temporal backbones, making it a versatile solution for continual spatio-temporal forecasting. The paper concludes that STBP offers a promising approach for addressing the challenges of continual learning in spatio-temporal forecasting, providing a balance between stability and adaptability. However, as I will discuss in the following sections, several limitations need to be addressed to fully realize the potential of this framework. While the paper presents a compelling approach, my analysis reveals several areas where the methodology and evaluation could be strengthened to provide a more robust and comprehensive assessment of the proposed framework.

## Strengths:

The primary strength of this paper lies in its introduction of STBP, a novel framework that attempts to address the complex problem of continual spatio-temporal forecasting. The core idea of combining a general spatio-temporal backbone with a contextual pattern bank is a promising approach to handle evolving data streams. The use of a frequency-domain network (FreNet) to extract stable temporal representations is a notable technical innovation, as it allows the model to focus on robust low-frequency components, potentially mitigating the impact of distributional drift. The dual-stream linear graph attention (DLGA) module, with its lightweight linear attention mechanism, is another positive aspect, as it enables efficient modeling of dynamic spatial correlations. The paper also demonstrates the practical effectiveness of STBP through empirical evaluations on three real-world datasets, showing that it outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in terms of forecasting accuracy, adaptability, and scalability. The ablation studies and parameter sensitivity analyses further support the validity of the proposed framework. The authors' claim that STBP's modular design allows for the integration of various spatio-temporal backbones is also a significant strength, as it suggests the potential for broader applicability of the framework. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear explanations of the proposed method and experimental results. The figures and tables are clear and informative, which aids in understanding the framework and its performance. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of continual spatio-temporal forecasting by proposing a novel framework that combines several innovative components and demonstrates strong empirical performance.


## Weaknesses:

Despite the strengths of the proposed STBP framework, my analysis reveals several significant weaknesses that need to be addressed. First, while the paper presents STBP as a novel framework, the individual components, namely the frequency-domain network (FreNet) and the dual-stream linear graph attention (DLGA), are not entirely novel concepts. As I verified, the paper itself introduces STBP as a framework, and the descriptions of FreNet and DLGA align with existing frequency-domain analysis and linear attention techniques. While the integration of these components within the continual learning context is a contribution, the lack of novelty in the individual components diminishes the overall novelty claim. This is a significant concern because the paper's main contribution is presented as a novel framework, not just a novel combination of existing techniques. Second, the paper's justification for the design of the contextual pattern bank, particularly the use of parameter expansion for updating, is insufficient. As I confirmed, the paper does not provide a detailed comparison or justification for choosing parameter expansion over other continual learning strategies like gradient-based updates or replay-based methods. The paper mentions mitigating catastrophic forgetting as the motivation, but it lacks a detailed analysis of the trade-offs between different updating strategies and why parameter expansion is the most suitable choice for this specific task. This is a critical weakness because the contextual pattern bank is a core component of the framework, and the lack of a strong justification for its design choice undermines the overall validity of the approach. Third, the paper does not adequately address the challenge of balancing stability and adaptability. While the paper describes the contextual pattern bank as the mechanism to mitigate catastrophic forgetting and the backbone is frozen after the initial training to maintain stability, it lacks a detailed explanation of how the framework prevents the pattern bank from being dominated by new patterns or a strategy to balance stability and adaptability beyond freezing the backbone and updating the pattern bank. This is a significant concern because the balance between stability and adaptability is crucial for continual learning, and the lack of a clear mechanism for achieving this balance raises questions about the robustness of the framework. Fourth, the paper lacks any theoretical guarantees for the proposed framework. As I verified, the paper focuses on the empirical evaluation of STBP and does not include any theoretical analysis of the convergence properties of the proposed framework. It is unclear whether the parameter expansion strategy for the contextual pattern bank will converge to a stable solution, and under what conditions. This is a critical weakness because the lack of theoretical guarantees makes it difficult to assess the reliability and robustness of the framework. Fifth, the paper does not compare STBP with other general continual learning methods. As I confirmed, the baselines used in the experiments are primarily spatio-temporal forecasting models and existing continual spatio-temporal forecasting methods. The paper lacks a comparison with general continual learning methods that are not specific to spatio-temporal data, which limits the assessment of the framework's advantages and disadvantages in a broader context. This is a significant concern because it is important to demonstrate the performance of the proposed framework against a wider range of methods to fully understand its strengths and weaknesses. Sixth, the paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational complexity and memory requirements of the proposed framework. While the paper includes an efficiency study that provides some empirical data on training time and memory usage, it lacks a detailed theoretical analysis of the computational complexity (e.g., Big O notation) and a comprehensive comparison of memory requirements across all baselines. This is a significant concern because the computational cost is a crucial factor for practical applications, and the lack of a detailed analysis makes it difficult to assess the scalability of the framework. Seventh, the experimental evaluation is limited to traffic and meteorological data. As I verified, the paper explicitly mentions the use of three real-world streaming spatio-temporal datasets from the traffic and meteorological domains. The generalizability of the proposed framework to other domains and datasets is not demonstrated, which limits the applicability of the framework. Eighth, the paper lacks a detailed discussion of the limitations and potential challenges of the proposed framework. As I confirmed, the paper does not have a dedicated "Limitations" or "Discussion" section that explicitly outlines the assumptions and potential failure scenarios of STBP. This is a significant concern because it is important to acknowledge the limitations of the framework to provide a balanced view and guide future research directions. Ninth, the paper does not include a comprehensive hyperparameter sensitivity analysis. While the paper includes a parameter sensitivity analysis for the feature dimension 'd', it does not include a thorough sensitivity analysis for other hyperparameters of STBP or the baselines. This is a significant concern because the performance of the framework might be highly dependent on the choice of hyperparameters, and a thorough sensitivity analysis is needed to provide guidance for practitioners. Finally, the paper lacks a broader discussion of the interpretability of the learned patterns. While the paper includes a case study with a visualization of the contextual pattern bank using t-SNE, it does not include other interpretability techniques like attention visualization or feature importance analysis for the backbone. This is a significant concern because the interpretability of the learned patterns is important for understanding the behavior of the model and its ability to capture meaningful information.


## Suggestions:

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the paper should more clearly articulate the novelty of the proposed STBP framework beyond simply combining existing components. While the integration of a frequency-domain network and dual-stream linear graph attention is not inherently novel, the specific manner in which they are combined and utilized within the continual learning context should be emphasized. The authors should provide a more detailed explanation of how the frequency-domain network contributes to the stability of representations, and how the dual-stream linear graph attention mechanism captures dynamic spatial correlations in a way that is particularly advantageous for continual spatio-temporal forecasting. Furthermore, the paper should include a more thorough analysis of the limitations of existing methods and how STBP specifically addresses these limitations, rather than just stating that it does. Second, the contextual pattern bank, while presented as a novel component, requires a more detailed explanation of its design choices and motivations. The authors should clarify why parameter expansion was chosen as the update mechanism, and how this approach compares to other memory update strategies, such as gradient-based updates or replay-based methods. A more thorough analysis of the trade-offs between these different update mechanisms would be beneficial. The paper should also provide a more detailed explanation of how the contextual pattern bank interacts with the spatio-temporal backbone, and how the patterns stored in the bank are utilized during the forecasting process. The authors should also provide a more detailed explanation of how the contextual pattern bank addresses the challenge of balancing stability and adaptability, and how it prevents catastrophic forgetting. Third, to address the lack of a clear mechanism for balancing stability and adaptability, the paper should incorporate a more explicit strategy for preventing catastrophic forgetting. This could involve techniques such as regularization, replay, or a more sophisticated parameter expansion strategy that takes into account the importance of different patterns. The paper should also provide a more detailed analysis of the convergence properties of the proposed framework. This could involve a theoretical analysis of the parameter expansion strategy for the contextual pattern bank, including a discussion of the conditions under which the framework is guaranteed to converge to a stable solution. The analysis should also consider the impact of different hyperparameters on the convergence properties of the framework. Fourth, the paper should include a comparison with other general continual learning methods, not just those specific to spatio-temporal forecasting. This would provide a more comprehensive evaluation of the proposed framework and demonstrate its advantages and disadvantages in a broader context. The comparison should include a variety of continual learning methods, such as gradient-based methods, replay-based methods, and regularization-based methods. Fifth, the paper should include a more detailed analysis of the computational demands of the proposed STBP framework. Specifically, a detailed breakdown of the time and space complexity for each component (the spatio-temporal backbone and the contextual pattern bank) is needed. This should include a discussion of how the complexity scales with the number of nodes, the length of the time series, and the size of the contextual pattern bank. Furthermore, a comparison with the computational costs of existing state-of-the-art methods, such as those based on recurrent neural networks or graph neural networks, would be valuable. This comparison should not only focus on training time but also on inference time and memory footprint, providing a comprehensive view of the efficiency of STBP. Sixth, to strengthen the paper's claims about the generalizability of STBP, the authors should consider expanding their experimental evaluation to include datasets from diverse domains. While traffic and meteorological data are important, they may not fully capture the complexities of other spatio-temporal forecasting tasks. For instance, applying STBP to datasets from social networks, epidemiology, or financial markets could reveal its strengths and weaknesses in different contexts. This would involve not only running experiments on these new datasets but also analyzing the performance of STBP in relation to the specific characteristics of each domain. Seventh, the paper should include a more detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed framework. This should go beyond simply stating that the model makes certain assumptions. The authors should delve into the specific scenarios where STBP might struggle, such as when the underlying data distribution changes drastically, when the graph structure is highly dynamic, or when the data is noisy or incomplete. For example, the authors could discuss how the performance of STBP is affected by the presence of non-stationary patterns, the introduction of new nodes or edges, or the occurrence of sudden shifts in the data. Furthermore, the authors should discuss the potential impact of the choice of hyperparameters on the performance of STBP and provide guidelines for selecting appropriate values. Eighth, the paper should include a more comprehensive hyperparameter sensitivity analysis. This analysis should include a discussion of the optimal range of hyperparameters for different datasets and scenarios. Finally, the paper should provide more insights into the interpretability of the learned patterns. While the contextual pattern bank is designed to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, it is not clear how these patterns are related to the underlying spatio-temporal dynamics. A visualization or analysis of the learned patterns could provide a better understanding of the model's behavior and its ability to capture meaningful information. This could involve techniques such as attention visualization or feature importance analysis.
