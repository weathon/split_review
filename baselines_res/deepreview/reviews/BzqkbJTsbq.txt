## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel framework, termed Data and Process Guidance (DPG), designed to address imperfect-label guidance tasks within the context of diffusion models. The authors argue that current methods often fall short by either being task-specific, thus lacking generalizability, or by relying solely on loss-guided approaches that fail to fully leverage domain-specific knowledge. DPG aims to bridge this gap by integrating both data knowledge and process knowledge into the reverse diffusion process. The core idea is to incorporate task-specific data knowledge by diffusing imperfect-label data or its variants and injecting this information early in the reverse diffusion process. Simultaneously, process knowledge is utilized by ensuring that each step of the denoising process progressively aligns better with the label constraints, thereby refining optimization choices and improving guidance fidelity. This is achieved through a mechanism that ensures each denoising step better satisfies the label constraints than the previous one. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of DPG through extensive experiments on three tasks: text-to-image style transfer, image super-resolution, and image deblurring. The results, both quantitative and qualitative, suggest that DPG consistently generates high-quality outputs, outperforming existing methods in terms of metrics like PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. The paper provides a detailed explanation of the proposed framework, including mathematical formulations and algorithmic descriptions, and presents visual comparisons to support their claims. The authors position DPG as a unified approach that can handle both weak-label and degraded-label guidance tasks, offering a more generalizable solution compared to existing methods. Overall, the paper presents a promising approach to imperfect-label guidance in diffusion models, with a focus on integrating both data and process knowledge to improve the precision and efficiency of outputs. The experimental results, while promising, are limited to a specific set of tasks, and the paper lacks a detailed analysis of the computational overhead and sensitivity to hyperparameters, which are important considerations for practical applications. Despite these limitations, the paper makes a significant contribution by introducing a novel framework that addresses a critical challenge in the field of diffusion models.
