## Summary:

This paper introduces EmbodiedMAE, a novel 3D multi-modal representation learning framework designed for robot manipulation tasks. The authors address the limitations of existing vision foundation models (VFMs), which often struggle with domain gaps between training datasets and real-world robot manipulation scenarios, as well as the lack of effective architectures for incorporating 3D information. To overcome these challenges, the authors enhance the DROID dataset with high-quality depth maps and point clouds, creating the DROID-3D dataset. This dataset serves as a valuable resource for 3D embodied vision research. The EmbodiedMAE model is a multi-modal masked autoencoder that learns representations across RGB, depth, and point cloud modalities through stochastic masking and cross-modal fusion. The model is trained on DROID-3D and evaluated on a diverse set of simulation tasks from the LIBERO and MetaWorld benchmarks, as well as real-world robot manipulation tasks on two different robot platforms, the SO100 and the xArm. The experimental results demonstrate that EmbodiedMAE consistently outperforms various state-of-the-art VFMs in both training efficiency and final performance. The model exhibits strong scaling behavior with size and effectively promotes policy learning from 3D inputs. The authors show that EmbodiedMAE is a reliable unified 3D multi-modal vision foundation model for embodied AI systems, particularly in precise tabletop manipulation settings where spatial perception is critical. The core contribution of this work lies in the creation of the DROID-3D dataset and the development of the EmbodiedMAE model, which effectively leverages multi-modal data for robot manipulation. The paper's findings suggest that incorporating 3D information through a multi-modal masked autoencoder can significantly improve the performance of vision-based robot learning. The extensive evaluation across diverse settings, including multiple simulation benchmarks and real-world robot platforms, further strengthens the significance of the proposed approach. The authors also demonstrate the model's ability to scale with size, indicating its potential for further improvement with larger models and datasets. Overall, this paper presents a significant step forward in the development of robust and generalizable vision-based models for robot manipulation.

The use of the ZED SDK for depth estimation, along with temporal fusion and AI-augmented enhancement, ensures that the depth information is both accurate and consistent. This dataset will undoubtedly be a valuable resource for the research community. Second, the proposed EmbodiedMAE model is technically sound and innovative. Specifically, they should clarify the method used to filter out the robot arm from the depth maps, if any, and provide the original RGB images for direct comparison with the ones shown in Figure 2. This would allow for a more thorough assessment of the dataset's quality and reliability. Furthermore, the authors should provide specific details about the hardware and software used for data collection, including the specific models of cameras and robots, as well as the versions of the software libraries. They should also provide a step-by-step explanation of the data processing pipeline, including how the raw data is converted into the final dataset format. This should include details on any pre-processing steps, such as noise reduction or outlier removal, and how these steps affect the quality of the 3D data. The authors should also clarify how the data is synchronized across different modalities, and how they handle any potential misalignments. Second, to improve the transparency of the real-world experimental setup, the authors should provide more details about the specific robots used, the types of tasks performed, and the environmental conditions. They should also provide a description of the sensors used to collect the data, and how the data is processed and used for evaluation. It would be beneficial to include a detailed description of the evaluation metrics used, and how they are calculated. The authors should also discuss any potential limitations of the experimental setup, and how these limitations might affect the results. Third, to address the limitation of focusing primarily on tabletop manipulation tasks, the authors should explore the generalization capabilities of EmbodiedMAE in more diverse and challenging manipulation tasks. This could include scenarios with cluttered environments, deformable objects, or tasks requiring more complex reasoning about object affordances. Evaluating the model's ability to handle variations in object appearance, lighting conditions, and background clutter would provide a more comprehensive understanding of its robustness. Furthermore, the authors should consider incorporating techniques such as domain adaptation or meta-learning to improve the model's ability to generalize to new environments and tasks. Fourth, to provide a more comprehensive comparison with existing approaches, the authors should include a detailed analysis of EmbodiedMAE's performance against other 3D-aware models and methods specifically designed for robot manipulation. This should include methods that explicitly model 3D geometry, such as those using point clouds or meshes, and methods that leverage depth information for planning and control. A comparative analysis should focus on both performance metrics, such as task success rates and manipulation precision, as well as computational efficiency, including training time and inference speed. Additionally, the authors should investigate the impact of different depth estimation techniques on the model's performance, comparing the ZED SDK with other methods and analyzing the trade-offs between accuracy and computational cost. Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the computational cost and efficiency of EmbodiedMAE, particularly in real-world applications. This should include a breakdown of the model's inference time, memory footprint, and energy consumption, as well as an analysis of how these factors scale with the size of the input data and the complexity of the model.
