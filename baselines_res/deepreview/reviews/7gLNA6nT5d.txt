## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel approach to in-context reinforcement learning (ICRL) by integrating n-gram induction heads into transformer models. The core idea is to enhance the model's ability to learn from limited data by explicitly incorporating n-gram attention patterns, which are known to be effective in capturing sequential dependencies. The authors argue that this modification addresses the limitations of existing ICRL methods, such as Algorithm Distillation (AD), which often require large, curated datasets and can be unstable during training. The proposed method modifies the transformer's attention mechanism to include n-gram heads, which are designed to capture sequential dependencies within learning histories. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach through empirical evaluations in both grid-world and pixel-based environments. Their results indicate that the proposed method matches or surpasses the performance of AD, while requiring significantly less data for generalization and exhibiting more stable training dynamics. Specifically, the paper shows that the n-gram heads improve data efficiency and reduce hyperparameter sensitivity, making the model more robust and easier to train. The authors also extend their approach to visual observation spaces by employing vector quantization techniques, which allows the method to handle pixel-based inputs. This is a significant step towards applying ICRL to more complex, real-world scenarios. Overall, the paper presents a promising modification to transformer architectures for ICRL, with a focus on improving data efficiency and training stability. The empirical results, while promising, highlight the need for further investigation into the method's limitations and its applicability to more complex environments. The authors' work contributes to the growing body of research on ICRL and offers a valuable perspective on how to leverage sequential patterns for improved learning from limited data. The paper's focus on practical applications, such as visual observations, makes it a relevant contribution to the field of reinforcement learning.
