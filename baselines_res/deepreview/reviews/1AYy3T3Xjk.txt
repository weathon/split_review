## Summary:

This paper introduces the Creativity-Reality Evaluation with Decoupled Ontology (CREDO) framework, a novel approach designed to assess creativity in educational settings where large language models (LLMs) are integrated into the learning process. The core contribution of this work lies in its shift from evaluating the final product of a creative task to examining the process of creative thinking through multi-turn dialogues between students and LLMs. The CREDO framework is built upon four dimensions of creativity: Interdisciplinary Innovation, Problem Reframing, Risk-Driven Innovation, and Resource Integration Efficiency. To operationalize this framework, the authors developed the Innovation Tracing Atlas (ITA), a method for attributing contributions within dialogues to either the student or the LLM, and an instruction-tuned evaluator that scores these interactions based on the four creativity dimensions. The authors collected a dataset of 1,273 dialogues from 81 undergraduates across multiple STEM disciplines and used this data to fine-tune their evaluator model. The empirical evaluation demonstrates that the fine-tuned model outperforms baseline models, including a zero-shot GPT-4 model and a non-fine-tuned DeepSeek-32B model, in aligning with expert judgments. The paper's significance lies in its attempt to address the growing need for robust and interpretable methods to evaluate creativity in AI-assisted learning environments. By focusing on the process of creative thinking and attributing contributions between learners and LLMs, the CREDO framework offers a nuanced approach that could be more accurate and fair compared to traditional outcome-based assessments. The authors also provide a detailed methodology for data collection, preprocessing, and annotation, enhancing the reproducibility of the study. However, the paper also acknowledges limitations, such as the focus on STEM fields and the need for human-in-the-loop review for high-stakes evaluation. Despite these limitations, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of AI in education by providing a framework and a methodology for assessing creativity in human-LLM collaborative tasks.

## Strengths:

I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. First, the paper addresses a timely and significant challenge in AI-assisted learning: the need for a robust and interpretable method to evaluate creativity. As LLMs become increasingly integrated into educational environments, it is crucial to develop methods for assessing not just the final product of a creative task but also the process of creative thinking itself. The CREDO framework, with its focus on process-level evaluation and attribution of contributions between learners and LLMs, offers a nuanced approach that could be more accurate and fair compared to traditional outcome-based assessments. Second, the authors provide a comprehensive dataset and methodological details, which enhance the transparency and reproducibility of the study. The detailed description of the data collection and annotation process, including the multi-stage annotation process and the use of inter-rater reliability measures, is commendable. The release of the dataset and code is a valuable contribution to the research community, allowing others to build upon this work. Third, the proposed framework is theoretically grounded in cognitive science and educational theories, which adds to its credibility. The authors explicitly link the four dimensions of creativity to established theories such as Bloom's Taxonomy and the PISA 2022 creative thinking framework. This theoretical grounding provides a solid foundation for the framework and helps to justify its design choices. Finally, the empirical evaluation demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed model, showing that it outperforms baseline models in aligning with expert judgments. The use of expert annotations and the establishment of a "gold standard" dataset contribute to the validity of the evaluation framework. The authors also acknowledge the limitations of the study and suggest directions for future work, demonstrating a thoughtful and balanced approach. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated, well-executed, and significant contribution to the field of AI in education.


## Weaknesses:

Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant careful consideration. First, the paper lacks sufficient detail regarding the baseline models used in the experiments. Specifically, the authors do not mention which version of GPT-4 was used (chat or vision) and do not provide the precise prompt used for the zero-shot evaluation. This lack of information makes it difficult to interpret the results, as we cannot assess whether the baseline models were given a fair chance to perform well. The absence of prompt details for GPT-4 is particularly concerning, as the prompt could significantly influence the model's performance. Furthermore, the paper lacks specific details about the fine-tuning process for the proposed model, including the learning rate, batch size, and number of epochs. This lack of detail makes it hard to reproduce the results or understand the model's behavior. This is a high-confidence weakness, as the absence of these details is clearly evident in the paper's method and experimental sections (Sections 3.3 and 4.1). Second, the paper lacks a comprehensive description of the dataset used in the study. While the authors provide examples of initial student prompts, they do not provide a quantitative breakdown of the subjects covered in the dataset or the distribution of student and LLM contributions in the dialogues. This lack of information makes it difficult to assess the generalizability of the proposed method and the representativeness of the dataset. For example, if the dataset is heavily skewed towards a particular subject, the model might not perform well on other subjects. Similarly, if the LLM contributions dominate the dialogues, the model might not be learning to assess student creativity effectively. This is a high-confidence weakness, as the paper does not provide the necessary dataset statistics in Section 3.1. Third, the paper lacks a thorough ablation study to evaluate the contribution of each component of the proposed method. While the authors mention ablations, the results are not included in the main text, making it impossible to verify the effectiveness of individual components such as LoRA, KD, and rationales. Furthermore, the paper does not show how the model performs when predicting each of the four creativity axes separately. This makes it difficult to understand the contribution of each component and to identify potential areas for improvement. This is a medium-confidence weakness, as the paper mentions ablations in Section 3.3.3 but does not provide the results in the main text or appendix. Fourth, the paper's claims about the novelty and effectiveness of the CREDO framework are overstated. While the task of evaluating creativity in LLM-assisted learning is indeed novel, the connection to classical creativity theories is weak. The authors claim that their framework "builds upon classical theories to specifically address the new challenges of the LLM era," but the mapping of the four proposed creativity dimensions to established creativity theories is not convincingly demonstrated. The paper needs to provide a more rigorous theoretical grounding for these dimensions, demonstrating how they align with or extend existing frameworks in creativity research. This is a medium-confidence weakness, as the paper attempts to connect CREDO to existing theories in Section 3.2.1, but the connection is somewhat high-level and lacks depth. Fifth, the experimental validation is limited in scope and lacks a thorough analysis of the model's generalizability. The experiments were conducted on a specific dataset collected from a limited number of participants in STEM fields, raising concerns about the model's applicability to other domains, such as the arts or humanities. The paper does not provide sufficient evidence that the proposed framework can effectively assess creativity across diverse disciplines. Furthermore, the paper does not include any experiments or discussions on how the framework performs with different LLMs or in different cultural contexts. This is a high-confidence weakness, as the paper explicitly states the STEM focus in Section 3.1.1 and lacks any experiments on other domains, LLMs, or cultural contexts. Sixth, the evaluation methodology relies heavily on expert annotations, which, while valuable, introduce subjectivity and potential bias. While the paper reports inter-rater reliability using Cohen's Kappa, it lacks a detailed analysis of potential biases beyond this metric. The paper also does not provide sufficient details about the training of the annotators or the specific guidelines they followed. This is a medium-confidence weakness, as the paper reports Cohen's Kappa in Section 3.2.3 but lacks a deeper discussion of potential biases and annotation process details beyond calibration and arbitration in Section 3.2.2. Finally, the paper does not adequately address the potential biases in the training data of the LLMs used and their impact on the evaluation results. Given that LLMs are known to reflect biases present in their training data, the paper should include a thorough analysis of how these biases might affect the creativity assessment. This is a high-confidence weakness, as the paper does not discuss training data biases in Section 3.1. These weaknesses, taken together, highlight the need for further research and refinement of the proposed framework.


## Suggestions:

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the authors should provide a more detailed description of the baseline models used in the experiments. Specifically, they should specify the exact version of GPT-4 used (e.g., GPT-4 Turbo, GPT-4 Plus) and provide the precise prompt used for the zero-shot evaluation. This would allow for a more accurate assessment of the baseline performance and would help to determine whether the proposed method truly outperforms the state-of-the-art. Furthermore, the authors should provide details about the fine-tuning process of their proposed model, including the learning rate, batch size, and number of epochs. This would make the results more reproducible and would allow other researchers to build upon their work. The authors should also consider including a comparison with other state-of-the-art models, such as Llama-2 or Mistral, to further demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach. Second, the authors should provide a more comprehensive description of the dataset used in the study. This should include the subjects of the student prompts, the distribution of subjects across the dataset, and the average length and complexity of the prompts. Additionally, the authors should provide statistics on the distribution of student and LLM contributions in the dialogues, including the average number of turns and the average length of utterances. This would help to assess the representativeness of the dataset and the generalizability of the proposed method. The authors should also consider releasing a subset of the dataset to allow other researchers to validate their findings and to build upon their work. This would also help to address concerns about the dataset's diversity and potential biases. Third, the authors should conduct a more thorough ablation study to evaluate the contribution of each component of their proposed method. This should include experiments where each component (LoRA, KD, rationales) is removed individually, as well as experiments where different combinations of components are used. The authors should also report the performance of the model when predicting each of the four creativity axes separately, as well as the performance when predicting all four axes simultaneously. This would help to identify the most important components of the method and to understand the model's behavior on different creativity axes. The authors should also consider using other evaluation metrics, such as F1-score or AUC, to provide a more comprehensive assessment of the model's performance. Fourth, the authors should strengthen the theoretical foundation of the CREDO framework. This involves providing a more detailed and rigorous mapping of the four proposed creativity dimensions to established theories in creativity research. The authors should explicitly discuss how their dimensions align with or extend existing frameworks, such as those proposed by Amabile or Torrance. This would require a more in-depth literature review and a clearer articulation of the theoretical underpinnings of the CREDO framework. Fifth, the authors should conduct more comprehensive experimental validation to demonstrate the generalizability of their framework. This includes expanding the dataset to include participants from diverse disciplines, such as the arts and humanities, and different cultural backgrounds. The authors should also explore the performance of the framework with different LLMs to assess its robustness and adaptability. Sixth, the authors should include a more detailed analysis of the inter-rater reliability of the expert annotations. This involves reporting metrics such as Cohen's kappa or Krippendorff's alpha, and discussing the measures taken to ensure consistency and objectivity in the annotation process. The authors should also provide more details on the training of the annotators and the guidelines used for the annotation process. Finally, the authors should conduct a thorough analysis of the data used to train the LLMs and the potential impact of these biases on the creativity assessment. This includes examining the demographic and cultural representation in the training data and assessing how these factors might influence the evaluation results. The authors should also explore techniques for bias mitigation, such as data augmentation or adversarial training, to ensure that the evaluation framework is fair and unbiased. By addressing these weaknesses, the authors can significantly strengthen their work and make a more substantial contribution to the field of AI in education.
