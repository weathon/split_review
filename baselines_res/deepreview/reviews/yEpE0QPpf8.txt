## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel task, Grounding-IQA, which aims to enhance fine-grained image quality assessment (IQA) by integrating multimodal referring and grounding capabilities. The authors argue that existing MLLM-based IQA methods often lack the ability to provide precise, location-specific quality evaluations, thus motivating the need for a more nuanced approach. To address this, they propose a new paradigm that combines traditional IQA with visual grounding, enabling models to not only assess overall image quality but also to identify and describe the quality of specific regions or objects within an image. The core methodological approach involves constructing a large-scale dataset, GIQA-160K, using an automated annotation pipeline. This pipeline leverages advanced LLMs for object tag extraction, state-of-the-art object detection models like Grounding DINO for bounding box generation, and a box refinement process to improve the accuracy of the annotations. The dataset is then used to fine-tune existing MLLMs, and a benchmark dataset, GIQA-Bench, is created for evaluating model performance across three key dimensions: description quality, visual question answering (VQA) accuracy, and grounding precision. The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method, particularly when applied to fine-tuned MLLMs, outperforms existing models on the GIQA-Bench, suggesting the effectiveness of the grounding-IQA approach for fine-grained quality assessment. The paper's main contribution lies in the introduction of a new task and the creation of a corresponding dataset and benchmark, which together provide a framework for more detailed and spatially-aware image quality analysis. The authors also present a thorough evaluation of their method, comparing it against several baselines, including general MLLMs, multimodal referring and grounding models, and IQA models. The results indicate that while some models perform well on certain aspects, none of the existing models achieve strong performance across all metrics of the grounding-IQA task, highlighting the need for the proposed approach. Overall, this work presents a significant step towards more fine-grained and context-aware image quality assessment, with potential applications in various domains such as image processing, media transmission, and generative AI.
