## Summary:

This paper introduces DemoGrasp, a novel approach to learning universal dexterous grasping policies using reinforcement learning (RL) augmented with a single successful demonstration. The core idea is to adapt a single, successful grasp trajectory to novel objects and poses by editing the robot's actions within that demonstration. This editing process involves modifying the wrist pose (determining 'where' to grasp) and the hand joint angles (determining 'how' to grasp). The authors formulate this trajectory editing as a single-step Markov Decision Process (MDP) and use RL to optimize a policy that outputs the editing parameters. This approach simplifies the learning process by restricting the policy to a compact action space and a single-step decision horizon, thereby reducing the multi-task exploration burden. The method is trained in simulation and then transferred to real-world robots. The policy is trained using a simple reward function that combines binary success and a robot-table collision penalty. To enable real-world deployment, the authors collect rollouts using the trained RL policy with rendered images in simulation and apply imitation learning to obtain a closed-loop vision-based policy. The paper demonstrates state-of-the-art performance in both simulation and real-world experiments, outperforming previous methods. The authors conduct thorough evaluations, including ablation studies and comparisons to existing approaches, to validate the effectiveness of their design choices. The method's ability to learn from a single demonstration and its strong sim-to-real transfer capabilities are significant contributions. The paper also explores the method's extensibility to different robotic hand embodiments and camera configurations, further highlighting its versatility. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to dexterous grasping that is both effective and relatively simple to implement, making it a valuable contribution to the field of robotics.

Furthermore, the paper does not address how the method would handle partial or noisy point clouds, which are common in real-world settings.
