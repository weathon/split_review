## Summary:

This paper introduces MobileLLM-R1, a series of small language models designed to achieve strong reasoning capabilities with significantly less training data than typically required by larger models. The core contribution lies in a data-centric approach that emphasizes careful data curation and a novel data-model co-evolution strategy. The authors challenge the conventional wisdom that large-scale models necessitate massive datasets (over 10T tokens) for developing reasoning skills. Instead, they demonstrate that with meticulous data selection and a dynamic training process, strong reasoning abilities can emerge using only about 2T tokens of high-quality data. The methodology involves a benchmark-free, self-evolving data optimization method for pre-training data curation, which leverages cross-domain influences to dynamically tailor the data mixture. This approach allows for robust reasoning generalization without exposing the model to benchmark data during training. The data-model co-evolution strategy adapts to changes in model capacity during training, converging as most samples reach zero or negative influence, indicating that the dataset's information has been largely exhausted. The empirical results are compelling, showing that MobileLLM-R1 models outperform larger models trained on much more data across several reasoning benchmarks, including code generation, mathematical problem-solving, and general knowledge. Specifically, the MobileLLM-R1-950M model matches or surpasses Qwen3-0.6B across multiple reasoning benchmarks despite being trained on only 11.7% of its tokens. The authors have made their models, code, and training recipes publicly available, promoting reproducibility and further research in the field. This work contributes valuable insights into efficient data usage for training reasoning models, highlighting the pivotal role of data quality and principled data curation. The paper's findings suggest that the key to achieving strong reasoning in small language models lies not in simply scaling up the model size or the amount of data, but in carefully selecting and optimizing the training data to align with the model's evolving capabilities. This has significant implications for the development of more resource-efficient and accessible AI systems.

## Strengths:

The primary strength of this paper lies in its innovative data-centric approach to training small language models for reasoning. The authors introduce a benchmark-free, self-evolving data optimization method that is a significant departure from traditional data curation techniques. This method, which leverages cross-domain influences to dynamically tailor the data mixture, allows for robust reasoning generalization without exposing the model to benchmark data during training. This is a novel and principled dataset-level weighting method that focuses on data quality and token efficiency rather than sheer data quantity. The data-model co-evolution strategy is another key innovation. This strategy adapts to changes in model capacity during training, converging as most samples reach zero or negative influence. This dynamic approach ensures that the model is trained on the most relevant data at each stage of its development, maximizing the efficiency of the training process. The empirical results presented in the paper are compelling. The MobileLLM-R1 models achieve state-of-the-art performance among small models with fully open-sourced recipes. For example, the MobileLLM-R1-950M model matches or surpasses Qwen3-0.6B across multiple reasoning benchmarks despite being trained on only 11.7% of its tokens. This demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed data-centric approach and challenges the conventional wisdom that large models and massive datasets are necessary for strong reasoning performance. The paper provides a detailed explanation of the data curation process, including hierarchical rejection sampling and leave-one-out analysis. This transparency allows other researchers to understand and potentially replicate the methods used in the paper. Furthermore, the authors have made all models, code, and training recipes publicly available, which promotes reproducibility and fosters further research in the field of efficient reasoning model development. This commitment to open research is a significant contribution to the community. Overall, the paper presents a well-executed study with clear methodological innovations and strong empirical results, making it a valuable contribution to the field of efficient language model training.
