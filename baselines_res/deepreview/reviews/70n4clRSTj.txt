## Summary:

This paper introduces SpookyBench, a novel benchmark designed to evaluate the temporal reasoning capabilities of video-language models (VLMs) by isolating temporal information from spatial cues. The core idea is to create videos where information is encoded solely through temporal sequences of noise-like frames, effectively removing any spatial context. This approach is motivated by the observation that current VLMs often rely heavily on spatial features, potentially hindering their ability to process temporal information effectively. The authors demonstrate that while humans can recognize patterns in these noise-based temporal sequences with high accuracy, state-of-the-art VLMs, including large language models like GPT-4o and Gemini 2.0 Flash, fail completely, achieving 0% accuracy. This stark contrast highlights a significant limitation in current VLMs, which the authors term "time blindness." The benchmark consists of three types of tasks: temporal symbol recognition, temporal sequence understanding, and temporal pattern reasoning, all designed to test different aspects of temporal understanding. The paper provides a thorough analysis of the performance gap between humans and machines on SpookyBench, demonstrating that this issue persists across various model architectures, scales, and training strategies. The authors also explore the impact of fine-tuning on the benchmark, finding that it does not improve the models' performance. The paper concludes by emphasizing the need for novel architectures or training paradigms that can effectively process temporal information independently of spatial cues. Overall, the paper makes a significant contribution by identifying a critical limitation in current VLMs and providing a benchmark that can be used to evaluate progress in this area. The work underscores the importance of developing models that can truly understand temporal dynamics, rather than relying solely on spatial features.
