## Summary:

This paper introduces GAMA, a novel Learning-to-Improve (L2I) framework for the Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP). The core contribution of GAMA lies in its unique approach to operator selection, formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). Unlike traditional methods that rely on simplistic state representations and naive feature concatenation, GAMA employs a graph-aware multi-modal attention model to encode both the problem instance and the current solution. This model leverages graph convolutional networks (GCNs) to capture the structural information of the problem and solution, and then uses self- and cross-attention mechanisms to model the complex relationships between them. The encoded state is then used to select the most appropriate operator to improve the current solution. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of GAMA through extensive experiments on synthetic and benchmark datasets. The results show that GAMA consistently outperforms strong neural baselines in both optimization quality and stability. Furthermore, GAMA exhibits strong zero-shot generalization to out-of-distribution instances of significantly larger sizes and different spatial distributions, without retraining. This generalization capability is a particularly noteworthy achievement, suggesting that GAMA can adapt to new problem instances that are significantly different from those it was trained on. The paper's approach of combining graph neural networks with attention mechanisms for operator selection in local search is a significant departure from traditional methods, and the empirical results suggest that this approach is highly effective for solving CVRP. The authors have also made a significant effort to compare their method against a wide range of baselines, which further strengthens the validity of their claims. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated, technically sound, and empirically validated approach to solving CVRP, making a valuable contribution to the field of combinatorial optimization.
