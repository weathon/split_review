## Summary:

This paper introduces CoRAL, a novel modular framework designed for robotic manipulation, particularly in contact-rich scenarios. The core contribution of CoRAL lies in its integration of vision and language models with motion planning and reactive control, enabling zero-shot planning and adaptive manipulation without relying on extensive teleoperated datasets. The framework is structured into distinct modules, each with a specific role. A vision module, utilizing FoundationPose, is responsible for environmental parameter initialization and object pose tracking. A Large Language Model (LLM), specifically GPT-4o, generates initial contact strategies and cost function estimations. A memory unit leverages past manipulation experiences to facilitate generalization and efficient reuse of learned strategies. The system employs a Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controller for reactive control, using the cost function generated by the LLM to guide its actions. The paper presents a series of experiments in simulation, demonstrating CoRAL's performance on various contact-rich tasks, including pushing, picking, and flipping objects. The results indicate that CoRAL outperforms state-of-the-art baselines and human-designed cost functions in these tasks. The authors emphasize the modularity of their approach, which allows for clear separation of concerns and facilitates the integration of different components. The framework's ability to adapt to changing conditions through online parameter refinement is also highlighted as a key advantage. Overall, the paper presents a promising approach to robotic manipulation that leverages the power of foundation models while maintaining a structured and interpretable control architecture. However, the paper's reliance on simulation and the lack of detailed analysis of computational cost and limitations suggest areas for future work. The authors acknowledge the limitations of their approach, particularly the dependency on the fidelity of the vision-based world model and the computational latency of the sequential pipeline, and suggest future research directions to address these issues. The paper's focus on zero-shot learning and adaptive control positions it as a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, offering a pathway towards more flexible and robust robotic systems.

## Strengths:

I found several aspects of this paper to be particularly compelling. The most significant strength is the novel system design, which effectively integrates vision and language models with motion planning and reactive control. The clear division of labor between the vision models, the LLM, and the MPPI controller is a well-considered approach. The use of the LLM not only for task planning but also for adaptive refinement of cost functions and physical parameters is a particularly innovative aspect of the work. This allows the system to adapt to changing conditions and learn from past experiences, which is crucial for robust manipulation. The inclusion of a memory unit to leverage past manipulation experiences is another strength, enabling the system to generalize and efficiently reuse learned contact strategies and parameter adjustments across diverse manipulation scenarios. This is a key step towards creating more adaptable robotic systems. The experimental results, while conducted in simulation, are impressive. The system demonstrates robust performance on a variety of contact-rich tasks, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines and human-designed cost functions. The ablation studies effectively demonstrate the importance of each component in the proposed architecture, providing a clear understanding of the contribution of each module. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, with a clear explanation of each component and its role in the overall system. The authors provide a detailed description of the methodology, making it easy to understand the inner workings of CoRAL. The use of figures and tables effectively illustrates the system's architecture and experimental results. The authors also provide a thorough discussion of the results, highlighting the strengths and limitations of their approach. The paper's focus on zero-shot learning and adaptive control positions it as a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, offering a pathway towards more flexible and robust robotic systems. The modularity of the framework is also a strength, allowing for easy integration of different components and facilitating future research and development. Overall, the paper presents a well-designed and thoroughly evaluated system that makes a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation.


## Weaknesses:

While the paper presents a compelling framework, several weaknesses warrant careful consideration. A primary concern, independently validated by multiple reviewers, is the exclusive reliance on simulation experiments. The paper explicitly states that "All experiments were conducted in the evaluation world, implemented using ROBOSUITE library... which is based on the MUJOCo physics engine." This lack of real-world validation is a significant limitation, as the dynamics of object interactions, especially in contact-rich scenarios, are often difficult to accurately model. Subtle factors like surface roughness, material deformability, and even slight variations in object shape can significantly alter the outcomes of manipulations. Therefore, the performance of the proposed system in simulation may not directly translate to real-world scenarios. The paper acknowledges this limitation in the "Limitations & Conclusion" section, stating that "the framework’s performance is currently contingent on the fidelity of the vision-based world model." However, this acknowledgment does not mitigate the concern that the system's performance in the real world may be significantly different from what is reported in the paper. This is a high-confidence issue, as the paper itself confirms the simulation-only nature of the experiments. Another significant weakness, also independently validated, is the lack of a detailed analysis of the computational cost of the proposed framework. The paper does not provide a breakdown of the runtime for each component, such as the vision module, the LLM, the motion planner, and the reactive controller. The "Implementation Details" section mentions the hardware used but does not provide any timing information for individual components. The "Experiments" section reports the "Average Completion Time" for successful trials, but this is an end-to-end metric and does not offer insights into the computational bottlenecks. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to assess the real-time performance of the system and its suitability for practical applications. Furthermore, the paper does not discuss the scalability of the approach to more complex tasks or environments. It is unclear how the system would perform with a larger number of objects or more intricate manipulation sequences. This is a high-confidence issue, as the paper provides no evidence to support the computational efficiency or scalability of the framework. A third weakness, also independently validated, is the limited discussion of the system's limitations. While the paper includes an experiment on "Pick and Place in Clutter," it does not thoroughly explore the system's performance in highly cluttered environments or with objects that are not well-represented in the training data. The paper mentions randomizing object properties like mass and friction, but it does not explicitly analyze the system's behavior when encountering novel objects with significantly different shapes, sizes, or material properties. The paper also lacks a detailed analysis of potential failure modes and mitigation strategies. While the "Online Adaptation via LLM-Driven Refinement" section describes how the system adapts when the inner loop fails, it does not provide a comprehensive analysis of the types of failures that can occur and how the system handles them. This is a medium-confidence issue, as the paper does include some discussion of adaptation but lacks a thorough analysis of limitations and failure modes. Finally, the paper does not include a sensitivity analysis of the system's performance to the choice of hyperparameters, such as the feedback gain matrix K_f in the reactive control loop. The "Reactive Planning and Execution (The Inner Loop)" section mentions the feedback gain matrix K_f but does not discuss its tuning or sensitivity. The "Implementation Details" section provides the value of K_f but does not include a sensitivity analysis. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to assess the robustness of the system and how its performance might be affected by different parameter settings. This is a high-confidence issue, as the paper provides no evidence of a sensitivity analysis. In summary, the lack of real-world validation, detailed computational analysis, and thorough discussion of limitations are significant weaknesses that need to be addressed in future work.


## Suggestions:

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First and foremost, the authors should conduct experiments in real-world settings to validate the performance of CoRAL beyond simulation. This would involve deploying the system on a physical robot and testing it on a variety of manipulation tasks. The authors should consider using a more realistic simulation environment that includes more complex physics, such as friction variations, deformable objects, and more complex contact dynamics, to better bridge the gap between simulation and the real world. This would help to identify potential challenges in sim-to-real transfer and allow for the development of more robust control strategies. The authors should also include a more detailed discussion of the potential challenges in sim-to-real transfer, and consider including experiments in a more realistic simulation environment that includes more complex physics. Furthermore, the paper should discuss how the system would handle perception failures, which are more likely to occur in real-world settings due to lighting variations, occlusions, and sensor noise. The high dependency on accurate object pose tracking makes the system vulnerable to these issues, and a robust error-handling mechanism is needed to ensure reliable performance. To address the lack of computational analysis, the authors should provide a detailed breakdown of the runtime for each component of the framework, including the VLM, LLM, motion planner, and reactive controller. This analysis should include the average time taken for each component to execute, as well as the variance in execution time across different trials. The paper should also discuss the overall system latency and how it affects the real-time performance of the system. Furthermore, the paper should explore the scalability of the approach by evaluating its performance on more complex tasks with a larger number of objects and more intricate manipulation sequences. This could involve testing the system on tasks that require multi-step planning and coordination, and analyzing how the performance degrades as the complexity of the task increases. The paper should also discuss the computational resources required to run the system, such as the memory and processing power, and how these requirements scale with the complexity of the task. To address the limited discussion of limitations, the authors should include experiments with varying degrees of clutter, including scenarios with overlapping objects and occlusions. Furthermore, the system's ability to generalize to novel objects not seen during training should be rigorously evaluated. This could involve testing on objects with different shapes, sizes, and material properties. A detailed analysis of failure modes, such as the system's behavior when encountering unexpected object dynamics or collisions, would also be valuable. This analysis should include a discussion of how these failures can be mitigated, perhaps through improved perception or more robust planning strategies. Finally, to address the lack of sensitivity analysis, the authors should conduct a thorough sensitivity analysis of the system's performance to the choice of hyperparameters, particularly the feedback gain matrix K_f in the reactive control loop. This analysis should explore how different values of K_f affect the system's stability, responsiveness, and robustness to disturbances. The paper should also discuss how the optimal value of K_f may vary depending on the specific task and environment, and provide guidelines for selecting appropriate values. Additionally, the paper should investigate the sensitivity of the system to other hyperparameters, such as the parameters of the MPPI controller, and discuss how these parameters affect the overall performance and robustness of the system. This analysis should provide a more complete understanding of the system's behavior and help guide the selection of appropriate parameters for different applications. In summary, these suggestions aim to address the identified weaknesses and improve the robustness, real-world applicability, and overall understanding of the CoRAL framework.
