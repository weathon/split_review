## Summary:

This paper introduces DaVinci, a novel multimodal large language model (MLLM) specifically designed for parsing scientific diagrams into structured TiKZ code. The authors address the challenges posed by the diversity of visual primitives, complex layouts, and strict syntax requirements inherent in scientific diagrams. DaVinci employs a two-stage learning framework: first, it undergoes supervised learning to acquire visual primitives and grammatical rules using a newly curated dataset, TiKZ30K; second, it is refined through reinforcement learning (RL) to enhance the structural relationships and overall visual fidelity of the generated diagrams. The TiKZ30K dataset, consisting of high-quality diagram-TiKZ code pairs, is a significant contribution, featuring optimized drawing order and comment annotations. The reinforcement learning phase is guided by a hybrid reward function that combines visual fidelity, structural consistency, and code correctness. The experimental results demonstrate that DaVinci outperforms both open-source and proprietary models, including GPT-5 and Claude-Sonnet-4, in diagram parsing tasks. The authors also conduct ablation studies and human evaluations to validate the effectiveness of different components of their approach. The core contribution of this work lies in the novel two-stage training framework, the creation of the TiKZ30K dataset, and the hybrid reward function used in reinforcement learning. The model's ability to parse complex scientific diagrams into structured code has significant implications for researchers and practitioners who work with visual data. The paper's findings suggest that the proposed approach is a promising direction for advancing the field of multimodal diagram understanding and code generation. The combination of supervised learning for visual primitives and reinforcement learning for structural refinement is a well-reasoned approach that effectively addresses the complexities of scientific diagram parsing. The creation of the TiKZ30K dataset is a significant contribution, providing a valuable resource for training and evaluating MLLMs in this domain. The hybrid reward function in reinforcement learning is a creative solution to the challenges of evaluating diagram parsing, combining visual fidelity, structural consistency, and code correctness. The experimental results are compelling, demonstrating that DaVinci outperforms both open-source and proprietary models. The ablation studies and human evaluations further validate the effectiveness of the proposed approach. Overall, this paper presents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal diagram understanding and code generation, with potential applications in various scientific and engineering domains.
