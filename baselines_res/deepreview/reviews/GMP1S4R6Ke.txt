## Summary:

This paper introduces LoRA-Mixer, a novel framework designed to enhance the multi-task adaptation of Large Language Models (LLMs) by integrating Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) with a mixture-of-experts (MoE) architecture. Unlike traditional approaches that replace entire attention or feed-forward layers with switch experts, LoRA-Mixer innovatively routes task-specific LoRA experts into the core projection matrices of the attention module (input/output linear layers). This design enables fine-grained token-level specialization by leveraging the attention mechanism, while maintaining compatibility with both Transformers and state-space models (SSMs). To train robust routers from limited data, LoRA-Mixer employs an adaptive Routing Specialization Loss (RSL) that enforces global load balance and input-aware specialization through an entropy-shaping objective. The framework supports two regimes: joint optimization of adapters and router, and plug-and-play routing over frozen, pre-trained LoRA modules. The authors conduct extensive experiments across 15 benchmarks, demonstrating that RSL-optimized LoRA-Mixer outperforms state-of-the-art routing and LoRA-MoE baselines with significantly fewer trainable parameters. Cross-model transfer and adapter reuse experiments further highlight the approach's versatility and data efficiency. The core idea of using LoRA modules as experts and learning a routing function to dynamically fuse them is novel and interesting, allowing for more flexible and adaptive use of LoRA modules for different tasks and inputs. The introduction of the Routing Specialization Loss (RSL) is a valuable contribution that addresses the issue of overly uniform auxiliary loss and enables efficient routing training with minimal data. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear explanations of the proposed method and the experimental results. The empirical results demonstrate that RSL outperforms other routing mechanisms, especially in low-data scenarios, and the authors provide theoretical analysis of the proposed RSL loss, including convergence analysis and generalization bound proofs in the appendix. This strengthens the credibility of their approach and provides a solid foundation for its effectiveness. Overall, the paper presents a novel and effective approach to routing in mixture of experts models, with potential applications in various domains.

## Strengths:

I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. The core idea of integrating LoRA with a mixture-of-experts architecture is genuinely novel, offering a fresh perspective on how to adapt large language models for multi-task learning. By routing task-specific LoRA experts into the core projection matrices of the attention module, the authors achieve fine-grained token-level specialization, which is a significant departure from traditional approaches that replace entire layers. This design is both elegant and effective, allowing for a more nuanced and adaptive use of LoRA modules. The introduction of the Routing Specialization Loss (RSL) is another significant contribution. The authors convincingly argue that existing auxiliary loss functions lead to over-averaging, and they propose RSL as a solution to encourage high variance and peaked distributions that are consistent with the input semantics. This is a valuable innovation that addresses a critical challenge in training mixture-of-experts models. The theoretical analysis of RSL, including convergence analysis and generalization bound proofs in the appendix, further strengthens the credibility of their approach. The empirical results are also compelling. The authors conduct extensive experiments across 15 benchmarks, demonstrating that RSL-optimized LoRA-Mixer outperforms state-of-the-art routing and LoRA-MoE baselines with significantly fewer trainable parameters. The cross-model transfer and adapter reuse experiments further highlight the versatility and data efficiency of the proposed method. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, with clear explanations of the proposed method and the experimental results. The use of figures and tables effectively visualizes the results and facilitates comprehension. The authors clearly explain the motivation behind their work, the details of the proposed method, and the experimental setup, making it easy for the reader to understand the key contributions of the paper. The plug-and-play nature of the framework, allowing for the use of pre-trained LoRA modules, is also a significant strength, making it easier to apply the method in practice.
