## Summary:

This paper delves into the theoretical and practical limits of image watermarking capacity, particularly focusing on robust watermarking techniques. The authors present a comprehensive theoretical analysis, establishing upper bounds on the number of bits that can be embedded within an image while maintaining imperceptibility and robustness to various distortions. This analysis is grounded in PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio) and linear robustness constraints, providing a framework for understanding the potential capacity of watermarking systems. The core of the theoretical work involves modeling images as points in a high-dimensional space and analyzing the volume of the space that can be used for embedding messages without violating the PSNR constraint. The authors then introduce Chunky Seal, a novel watermarking model that builds upon the existing Video Seal architecture. Chunky Seal is designed to increase embedding capacity while preserving image quality and robustness. The key methodological approach in developing Chunky Seal involves scaling up the embedding dimension and the U-Net channel multipliers of the original Video Seal model, as well as enabling watermarking in all three color channels. This scaling is a significant departure from the original Video Seal, which primarily operated on the luma channel. The empirical findings of the paper are presented through a series of experiments that evaluate the performance of Chunky Seal in terms of capacity, image quality, and robustness. The experiments demonstrate that Chunky Seal can embed significantly more bits than previous models while maintaining comparable image quality, as measured by PSNR, SSIM, MS-SSIM, and LPIPS. The robustness of Chunky Seal is evaluated against a range of distortions, including flipping, rotation, resizing, cropping, brightness and contrast adjustments, JPEG compression, and Gaussian blur. The results show that Chunky Seal maintains a high level of bit accuracy even under these distortions. The overall significance of this work lies in its demonstration that current watermarking methods operate far below their theoretical capacity, suggesting significant room for improvement. By introducing Chunky Seal, the authors provide a practical example of how to increase embedding capacity while maintaining image quality and robustness. This work challenges the notion that current models are limited by theoretical constraints, instead highlighting the need for architectural innovation and improved training strategies. The paper's findings have implications for various applications, including copyright protection, content authentication, and data provenance. The authors' analysis and the development of Chunky Seal represent a notable step forward in the field of robust image watermarking, paving the way for future research aimed at further expanding capacity and improving robustness.
