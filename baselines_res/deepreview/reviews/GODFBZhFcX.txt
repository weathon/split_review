## Summary:

This paper introduces the Planner-Composer-Evaluator (PCE) framework, a novel approach designed to enhance the performance of embodied agents operating in multi-agent, partially observable, and decentralized environments. The core idea behind PCE is to transform the fragmented assumptions embedded within the reasoning traces of Large Language Models (LLMs) into a structured decision tree. This decision tree then guides action selection by evaluating potential paths based on scenario likelihood, goal-directed gain, and execution cost, effectively reducing the need for frequent inter-agent communication. The framework is composed of three key modules: a Planner that generates reasoning traces and identifies potential assumptions, a Composer that constructs a decision tree based on these assumptions, and an Evaluator that scores each path in the tree to determine the most promising course of action. The authors evaluate the PCE framework on two challenging multi-agent benchmarks, C-WAH and TDW-MAT, using three different LLM backbones. The results consistently demonstrate that PCE outperforms communication-centric baselines in terms of success rate, task efficiency, and token usage. Furthermore, ablation studies and a user study provide additional validation, highlighting the effectiveness of the proposed approach and its ability to generate communication patterns that human partners perceive as more efficient and trustworthy. The paper's contribution lies in its innovative method of leveraging latent assumptions in LLM reasoning to create a structured decision-making process, which reduces reliance on communication and improves overall performance in complex multi-agent scenarios. The framework's modular design, with distinct Planner, Composer, and Evaluator components, allows for a systematic approach to uncertainty management, making it a significant advancement in the field of embodied multi-agent systems.

As the paper states, "At each node, a local ranking policy selects the next assumption to branch on, prioritizing those that most reduce uncertainty and most strongly influence subsequent action choice." However, the specific features or criteria used to rank assumptions, as well as the algorithm used to compute these scores, are not detailed. This lack of clarity makes it difficult to understand how the decision tree is optimized for efficiency and effectiveness. Furthermore, the process of aggregating assumptions, especially how conflicting assumptions are resolved, is not clearly explained. The paper states, "Only assumptions not yet assigned on the path and consistent with current premises are considered, preventing incompatible scenarios from being generated." However, the mechanisms for resolving inconsistencies and ensuring that the decision tree remains consistent with the environment and other agents' behavior are not discussed.
