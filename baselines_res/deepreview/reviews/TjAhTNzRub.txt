## Summary:

This paper introduces MoRE (Mixture of Remapping Experts), a novel framework for feature-level machine unlearning, addressing the critical challenge of irreversibly removing specific knowledge from machine learning models. The authors identify key limitations in existing unlearning methods, such as utility degradation on remaining data, residual feature-level knowledge that allows for recovery of forgotten information, and high computational costs. MoRE tackles these issues through three core innovations: prototype-orthogonal projection, which decorrelates forget and remain prototypes to minimize interference during unlearning; remapping with mixture experts, which scatters forget features across the latent space, making them indistinguishable from remaining features and thus preventing recovery; and efficient activation-mean prototypes, which reduce unlearning to a single forward pass, achieving linear computational complexity and constant memory usage. The prototype-orthogonal projection ensures that the unlearning process targets only the unwanted information, while the remapping with mixture experts enhances irreversibility by dispersing the forget features. The use of activation-mean prototypes significantly reduces the computational overhead, making the method scalable to large models and datasets. The authors provide a theoretical foundation for their approach, including proofs of irreversibility and utility preservation. Empirically, MoRE demonstrates superior performance compared to existing unlearning methods across various metrics, including unlearning accuracy, remaining knowledge utility, and computational efficiency. The method is evaluated on several image classification datasets, including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, and ImageNet, as well as a concept unlearning task on a text-to-image diffusion model. The results show that MoRE effectively removes the target knowledge while preserving the utility of the remaining data, and it achieves this with significantly lower computational costs than previous methods. The paper's contribution lies in its novel approach to feature-level unlearning, which not only removes unwanted knowledge but also ensures that it is irrecoverable, addressing a critical gap in existing unlearning techniques. The authors' work opens up new avenues for research in this area and provides a valuable tool for practitioners seeking to develop more trustworthy and privacy-preserving machine learning systems. The combination of theoretical rigor, empirical validation, and practical efficiency makes this paper a significant contribution to the field of machine unlearning.

## Strengths:

This paper presents a compelling and innovative approach to machine unlearning, and I find several aspects of the work particularly strong. The core strength of MoRE lies in its novel combination of techniques to achieve irreversible feature-level unlearning. The introduction of prototype-orthogonal projection is a clever way to decorrelate forget and remain prototypes, ensuring that the unlearning process targets only the unwanted information. This is a significant improvement over existing methods that often leave residual knowledge vulnerable to recovery. The remapping with mixture experts further enhances the irreversibility by scattering the forget features across the latent space, making them indistinguishable from the remaining features. This approach effectively breaks the separable-cohesive structure of forget clusters, which is a key limitation of previous unlearning methods. The use of efficient activation-mean prototypes is another notable innovation, reducing the computational complexity of unlearning to a single forward pass. This makes MoRE highly scalable and practical for large models and datasets. The theoretical analysis provided in the paper is also a major strength. The authors provide rigorous mathematical proofs of irreversibility and utility preservation, which lends significant credibility to their approach. The empirical evaluation is comprehensive, covering various datasets and model architectures. The results demonstrate that MoRE achieves state-of-the-art performance in both unlearning effectiveness and efficiency. The paper is well-structured and clearly written, making it easy to follow the authors' arguments and understand the proposed method. The figures and tables are informative and effectively illustrate the key concepts and results. The authors also provide a thorough analysis of their findings, discussing the implications of their work and suggesting directions for future research. The combination of theoretical rigor, empirical validation, and practical efficiency makes this paper a significant contribution to the field of machine unlearning. The authors have successfully addressed the limitations of existing methods and have introduced a novel framework that has the potential to enable more trustworthy and privacy-preserving machine learning systems.
