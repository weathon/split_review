## Summary:

This paper introduces Adaptive Serial-Parallel Decoding (ASPD), a novel framework designed to accelerate the inference speed of large language models (LLMs) by leveraging intrinsic parallelism within their output sequences. The core idea behind ASPD is to identify and extract parallelizable segments from a given serial response and then process these segments concurrently. The authors propose a non-invasive pipeline to automatically transform serial data into a parallel format suitable for training. This pipeline involves prompting an LLM to rewrite serial responses into a parallel structure, verifying the independence of parallel branches, and ensuring the integrity of the transformed data. Once the parallel data is prepared, the authors modify the model architecture to enable efficient parallel decoding. This involves using branch-invisible attention masks, which prevent attention across different parallel branches, and shared positional embeddings, which allow parallel branches to be processed simultaneously without increasing the effective context length. The authors also implement a hybrid decoding engine that seamlessly switches between serial and parallel decoding modes, allowing for a combination of sequential and parallel processing within a single response. The experimental results demonstrate that ASPD achieves a significant speedup in inference time while maintaining comparable generation quality. The authors evaluate their method on various benchmarks, including dialogue, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and mathematical reasoning tasks, using both Vicuna and Qwen models. They report a 1.82x speedup on the Vicuna Bench while preserving response quality. The paper also includes ablation studies to analyze the impact of different components of the proposed method. Overall, this work presents a promising approach to address the inference latency challenges of LLMs by exploiting the inherent parallelism in their outputs, offering a practical method for accelerating LLM inference without significant compromises in quality.
