## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel brain-inspired neural network architecture, termed OML, designed for online multimodal learning (OML). The core contribution lies in its hierarchical and modular design, which incorporates ascending, descending, and lateral pathways, mirroring some aspects of biological neural networks. This architecture enables the model to learn new multimodal concepts and associations dynamically, without forgetting previously acquired knowledge. A key feature of the OML model is its ability to detect conflicts between new inputs and existing knowledge, engaging in a human-in-the-loop interaction to resolve these conflicts. This is achieved through a conflict detection mechanism that compares the current input with previously learned associations, and if a conflict is detected, the model poses questions to the user to clarify the discrepancy. The model then updates its knowledge based on the user's responses. The paper presents a detailed description of the model's architecture, including feature neurons, unimodal association neurons, and multimodal association neurons, along with their respective activation functions and pathways. The learning process is described for individual input pairs, where the model dynamically adapts its structure and parameters. The experimental evaluation of the OML model is conducted on several datasets, including Fruits, HomeF, E-Fruits, E-HomeF, VAT, and VAT-HomeF, primarily focusing on cross-modal retrieval and classification tasks. The experiments are designed to evaluate the model's performance in both closed and open environments, with the open environment specifically designed to test the model's ability to handle catastrophic forgetting. The results demonstrate the model's effectiveness in learning new concepts and associations without forgetting previous knowledge, and the human-in-the-loop interaction is shown to be crucial for resolving conflicts and ensuring the model's robustness. The paper also includes comparisons with several baseline methods, including DAE, DBM, DJSRH, NRCH, FUME, ART, and AEN, providing a quantitative assessment of the model's performance. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to online multimodal learning, with a novel architecture and an interactive learning mechanism that addresses some of the key challenges in this field. The model's ability to learn continuously and adapt to new information, while engaging with a human user to resolve conflicts, represents a significant step forward in the development of more robust and adaptable AI systems.

This design allows for dynamic adaptation of the model's structure and parameters, enabling it to learn new multimodal concepts and associations without forgetting previously learned information. This addresses a critical challenge in continual learning, where models often struggle to retain old knowledge while learning new things. The inclusion of a conflict detection mechanism is another significant strength. This interactive learning process is a practical and effective way to ensure the model's robustness and accuracy, as it allows the model to clarify ambiguities and adapt to new information that may not be readily available in the data.
