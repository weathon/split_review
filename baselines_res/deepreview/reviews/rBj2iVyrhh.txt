## Summary:

This paper introduces Classifier-Constrained Alternating Training (CCAT), a novel framework designed to address the persistent challenge of modality imbalance in multimodal learning. Modality imbalance arises from disparities in information quality and optimization dynamics across different modalities, leading to gradient conflicts and suboptimal model performance. The authors observe that existing alternating training methods often fail to prevent classifier bias towards dominant modalities, thereby suppressing the contributions of weaker modalities. To mitigate this, CCAT employs a two-stage training process. First, an unbiased cross-modal classifier is pre-trained using bidirectional cross-attention and a regularization term that constrains modality contribution differences. This pre-trained classifier is then frozen during subsequent alternating training, serving as a stable decision anchor and preventing bias towards any single modality. To preserve modality-specific features while leveraging the frozen classifier, the authors integrate modality-specific Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) modules. During alternating training, only the encoder of the active modality and its corresponding LoRA parameters are updated. Additionally, a sample-level imbalance detection mechanism is introduced to identify and optimize severely imbalanced samples, further enhancing the representation of weaker modalities. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of CCAT through extensive experiments across multiple benchmark datasets, including CREMA-D, Kinetic-Sound, and MVSA. The results show significant accuracy gains over state-of-the-art methods, validating the effectiveness of the proposed approach in learning robust, balanced multimodal representations. The core contribution of this work lies in its innovative approach to balancing modality contributions through a combination of a frozen, pre-trained classifier, modality-specific LoRA modules, and a sample-level re-optimization strategy. This framework effectively mitigates the problem of dominant modalities suppressing weaker ones, a limitation in existing methods. The paper also provides a thorough theoretical analysis of the similarities between class imbalance and modality imbalance, highlighting the gradient optimization dynamics that lead to bias in both scenarios. This analysis provides a strong foundation for the proposed CCAT framework and demonstrates a deep understanding of the underlying issues in multimodal learning. Overall, the paper presents a well-structured and clearly written approach to a significant problem in multimodal learning, with strong empirical evidence supporting its claims.

For instance, investigating techniques such as shared LoRA parameters across similar modalities or employing a hierarchical structure where modalities are grouped and processed at different levels could be beneficial. This would help to reduce the number of parameters and computational overhead associated with the LoRA modules. Furthermore, the authors should analyze the sensitivity of CCAT to different types of data imbalance, such as class imbalance within each modality, and propose strategies to mitigate these issues. This could involve incorporating techniques from the class imbalance literature, such as re-sampling or weighted loss functions, into the CCAT framework. A thorough analysis of these aspects would significantly enhance the practical applicability of the proposed method. Second, to address the computational overhead concerns, the authors should provide a detailed breakdown of the time and memory complexity of each component of CCAT, including the pre-training phase, the LoRA module adaptation, and the sample-level imbalance detection. This analysis should be presented in a tabular format, allowing for a clear comparison of the computational costs associated with each step. Furthermore, the authors should explore potential optimization strategies, such as pruning or quantization, to reduce the computational burden of the proposed method. For example, they could investigate the impact of pruning the LoRA modules after training or using low-precision arithmetic for the computations. A detailed analysis of these optimization techniques would make the method more practical for large-scale applications. The authors should also explore the impact of using different encoder architectures for different modalities.
