## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel inference-time sampling method, termed 'power sampling,' designed to enhance the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) without requiring additional training. The core idea revolves around sampling from a 'power distribution,' a sharpened version of the base model's distribution, which is achieved through an iterative process inspired by Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) techniques. Specifically, the authors propose an approximate sampling algorithm that iteratively resamples token subsequences based on their likelihoods, effectively biasing the generation towards more probable reasoning paths. This approach is motivated by the observation that base models may possess stronger reasoning capabilities than typically observed, and that appropriate sampling techniques can unlock these capabilities. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their method across several reasoning benchmarks, including MATH500, GPQA, and HumanEval, showing performance that matches or even surpasses that of reinforcement learning (RL)-based methods like GRPO. A key finding is that power sampling maintains generation diversity, unlike RL-posttrained models, which often suffer from a collapse in diversity over multiple samples. The method is training-free, dataset-free, and verifier-free, making it a computationally efficient and easily implementable approach. The paper provides a detailed explanation of the method, its theoretical underpinnings, and its empirical validation. The authors argue that their work reveals that base models possess more reasoning capabilities than previously thought, and that appropriate sampling techniques can unlock these capabilities. The core contribution lies in the introduction of the power distribution as a target for reasoning tasks, the development of an approximate sampling algorithm using MCMC, and the empirical validation of the algorithm across various models and reasoning tasks. The authors also demonstrate that the method maintains generation diversity, unlike RL-posttraining, which often suffers from a collapse in diversity over multiple samples. Overall, this work presents a compelling alternative to training-based methods for improving LLM reasoning, highlighting the potential of inference-time sampling techniques.
