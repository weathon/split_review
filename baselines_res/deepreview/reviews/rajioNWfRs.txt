## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel two-stage training paradigm, termed TNT, designed to enhance the training efficiency and inference performance of deep memory modules within recurrent neural networks (RNNs). The core contribution lies in addressing the inherent trade-off between training speed and model accuracy often encountered in RNNs with complex memory mechanisms. The authors propose a hierarchical memory architecture, comprising a global memory module for capturing long-range dependencies and multiple local memory modules for handling fine-grained details in parallel. This hierarchical structure is coupled with a two-stage training process. The first stage focuses on pre-training the model with an emphasis on efficiency, utilizing large chunk sizes for the global memory and smaller, parallelizable chunks for the local memories. The second stage involves fine-tuning the local memory modules with smaller chunk sizes to optimize performance during inference. This two-stage approach effectively decouples the pre-training phase, which prioritizes computational efficiency, from the fine-tuning phase, which aims to maximize accuracy. The key methodological innovation is the use of periodic resets of the local memory modules during pre-training, which enables parallel processing and significantly accelerates training. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach through comprehensive experiments on language modeling and common-sense reasoning tasks. They show that TNT achieves a substantial speedup in training time, up to 17.37x faster than baseline methods, while simultaneously improving model accuracy. The empirical findings are supported by thorough ablation studies that highlight the importance of both the global and local memory components, as well as the two-stage training process. The overall significance of this work lies in its potential to make deep memory modules more practical for real-world applications, particularly those involving long sequences, by addressing the critical bottleneck of training efficiency. By introducing a method that achieves both faster training and improved accuracy, the authors have made a valuable contribution to the field of efficient sequence modeling.
