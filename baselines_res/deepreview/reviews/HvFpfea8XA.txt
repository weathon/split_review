## Summary:

This paper introduces AMADEUS, a novel training-free framework designed to enhance persona consistency in role-playing agents (RPAs) using retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). The core idea behind AMADEUS is to improve how RPAs maintain a consistent persona, even when responding to queries that extend beyond the character's explicitly defined knowledge. The framework is composed of three main components: Adaptive Context-aware Text Splitter (ACTS), Guided Selection (GS), and Attribute Extractor (AE). ACTS is responsible for partitioning a character's persona into optimally sized, overlapping chunks, while also incorporating hierarchical contextual information. GS then selects the most relevant chunks based on the user's query, and AE extracts general attributes from these chunks to maintain persona consistency. To evaluate AMADEUS, the authors created CharacterRAG, a dataset consisting of persona documents for 15 distinct fictional characters and 450 question-answer pairs. The experimental results, which include evaluations using MBTI and BFI questionnaires, demonstrate that AMADEUS outperforms existing RAG methods in maintaining persona consistency and reducing hallucinations. The authors also compare their method to several off-the-shelf RAG baselines, showing improvements in metrics such as accuracy and hallucination score. The paper's contribution lies in its novel approach to persona consistency in RPAs, the introduction of the CharacterRAG dataset, and the empirical validation of the proposed framework. The framework's ability to maintain persona consistency even when addressing queries beyond a character's explicit knowledge is a significant advancement in the field of role-playing agents. The use of a retrieval-augmented approach allows the model to leverage external knowledge, which is a crucial aspect of maintaining a consistent persona. The paper's findings suggest that the proposed framework is a promising approach for developing more robust and reliable role-playing agents.
