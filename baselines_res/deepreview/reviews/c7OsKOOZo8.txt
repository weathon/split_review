## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel end-to-end framework for multi-view diabetic retinopathy (DR) grading, aiming to reduce the reliance on expensive expert annotations. The core of the proposed method lies in two key modules: the Grade-Activated Lesion Proposal (GALP) module and the Cross-View Lesion Expert Guided Regional Fusion (LGRF) module. The GALP module enhances intermediate feature representations through auxiliary classification, generating lesion proposals based on stage-wise class activation maps. These proposals are then used by the LGRF module, which employs a mixture-of-experts (MoE) framework and Top-K weighted cross-view attention to selectively fuse information from different views. This approach allows for a more targeted and precise integration of lesion-related information across multiple fundus images. The authors evaluate their method on two multi-view DR datasets, MFIDDR and DRTiD, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance without relying on external annotations. The experimental results show that the proposed method matches or surpasses strong baselines, highlighting the effectiveness of self-generated lesion proposals in reducing annotation needs. The authors emphasize that their end-to-end framework simplifies the training process and reduces the need for manual intervention, making it a practical solution for automated DR grading. The paper's main contribution is the introduction of a self-contained framework that can generate lesion proposals on the fly during training and inference, thereby reducing the dependence on costly external annotations. The method's ability to achieve state-of-the-art results without external annotations is a significant achievement, demonstrating the potential of self-derived cues for DR grading. The proposed GALP and LGRF modules are designed to work in tandem, with GALP providing lesion proposals and LGRF selectively fusing information based on these proposals. This approach allows for a more targeted and precise integration of lesion-related information across multiple views, leading to improved grading performance. The authors' focus on an end-to-end framework also simplifies the training process, making the method more practical for clinical deployment. Overall, the paper presents a promising approach to multi-view DR grading that addresses the challenge of annotation scarcity while achieving competitive performance.

These components, as described in the method section, introduce a significant number of parameters and computations.
