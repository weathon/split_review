## Summary:

This paper introduces ESS-Flow, a novel method for sampling from the posterior distribution of flow-based generative models without requiring gradient information. The core idea is to leverage the properties of elliptical slice sampling (ESS) in the latent space of these models, which are assumed to have a Gaussian source distribution. This approach is particularly valuable in scenarios where gradients are either unavailable or computationally expensive to obtain, such as in simulations with quantization or non-differentiable operations. The authors demonstrate that by performing Bayesian inference directly in the source space, they can circumvent the need for backpropagation through the generative model. The method is presented as a training-free approach, meaning it can be applied to pre-trained flow-based models without the need for further optimization. The paper also proposes a multi-fidelity extension of ESS-Flow, which aims to improve sampling efficiency by using coarser, computationally cheaper discretizations of the flow for most of the sampling process, and only using the high-fidelity, expensive discretization for a subset of samples. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of the convergence properties of ESS-Flow, showing that it converges geometrically fast to the target distribution under certain conditions. The method is evaluated on two challenging real-world problems: generating materials with desired properties and predicting protein structures from sparse distance measurements. The empirical results show that ESS-Flow can generate high-quality samples that match the desired properties, and that the multi-fidelity approach can lead to significant computational savings. The authors also demonstrate that ESS-Flow can handle non-differentiable settings, such as generating materials with specific space group symmetries, which is a significant advantage over gradient-based methods. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and technically sound approach to controlled generation with flow-based models, with a clear focus on practical applicability and computational efficiency. The method's ability to operate without gradients and its extension to multi-fidelity settings are key contributions that address important limitations of existing approaches.

As stated in the introduction, the authors leverage the fact that many pretrained diffusion and flow-based models can be recast as continuous normalizing flows with Gaussian source distributions. This is a critical assumption, and the paper does not explore the applicability of ESS-Flow to models with non-Gaussian source distributions. This limitation significantly restricts the scope of the method, as many real-world generative models may not satisfy this condition. My confidence in this limitation is high, as it is directly stated in the paper and is a core component of the method's design. While these methods typically require gradient information, they could potentially be adapted to the source space of the flow-based model, potentially mitigating the computational cost of gradient calculation in the high-dimensional data space. Furthermore, the authors should consider incorporating techniques to adapt the proposal distribution based on the target distribution, which could improve the efficiency of the sampling process, especially when the prior is not well-aligned with the target. This could involve learning a proposal distribution that is more concentrated around the regions of high posterior density, which could be achieved through techniques like normalizing flows or variational inference. Second, to address the limitations of the multi-fidelity sampling approach, the authors should explore more sophisticated importance weighting schemes. Techniques like adaptive importance sampling, which iteratively refines the proposal distribution based on the observed importance weights, could be beneficial. Additionally, the authors should investigate the use of variance reduction techniques, such as control variates, to reduce the variance of the importance weights, which could improve the stability and accuracy of the multi-fidelity sampling approach.
