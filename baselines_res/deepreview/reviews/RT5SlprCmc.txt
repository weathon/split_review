## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel framework for learning state representations in Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) by estimating the Minimum Action Distance (MAD), defined as the shortest path between states in terms of actions. The authors propose two algorithms, MADDist and TDMADDist, which learn state embeddings and quasimetric distance functions using only state trajectories, without requiring rewards or actions. The framework is evaluated on a diverse set of environments, including those with stochastic dynamics, noisy observations, and both discrete and continuous state spaces. The empirical results demonstrate that the proposed methods outperform existing state representation techniques in terms of MAD approximation accuracy and performance on downstream tasks, such as goal-conditioned reinforcement learning. The paper also introduces a novel quasimetric distance function that is computationally efficient and outperforms more elaborate quasimetrics in the existing literature. Overall, the paper makes a significant contribution to the field of reinforcement learning by providing a practical and effective method for learning state representations that can be applied in a wide range of scenarios, including those where rewards and actions are not available. The authors' focus on learning from state trajectories alone is a notable innovation, and the empirical results provide strong evidence for the effectiveness of their approach. However, the paper could benefit from a more detailed analysis of computational complexity, a thorough exploration of performance in highly stochastic and sparse reward environments, and a comprehensive comparison with other state-of-the-art representation learning methods.
