## Summary:

This paper introduces OMMP-Bench, a novel benchmark designed to evaluate the impact of online high-definition (HD) map estimation on motion prediction in autonomous driving. The authors identify several critical shortcomings in existing protocols, including inappropriate dataset splits, inconsistent ranges between online mapping and motion prediction, and non-discriminative evaluation metrics. To address these issues, they propose a new data partitioning strategy, refined evaluation metrics, and a boundary-free baseline that leverages image features to improve prediction for distant agents. The core contribution of this work lies in its systematic approach to evaluating online map-based motion prediction, highlighting the importance of accurate map information and its influence on the performance of motion prediction models. The authors' methodology involves a detailed analysis of the existing online map-based motion prediction protocol, followed by the development of a new benchmark that addresses the identified limitations. Specifically, they split the nuScenes dataset into three spatially disjoint sets to ensure a more realistic evaluation scenario. They also introduce a boundary-free baseline that uses deformable attention to integrate image features with the motion prediction model, aiming to mitigate the lack of map elements for distant agents. The main empirical findings demonstrate that the proposed benchmark and boundary-free baseline lead to improved performance, particularly for distant agents. The paper's significance lies in its contribution to the development of more robust and reliable autonomous driving systems by providing a more rigorous framework for evaluating online map-based motion prediction. By addressing the limitations of previous approaches, this work paves the way for more accurate and reliable motion prediction models that can better handle the complexities of real-world driving scenarios. The authors also explore the impact of different map element selections on motion prediction models, providing valuable insights for the design of both online mapping and motion prediction components. The paper's focus on the interplay between online mapping and motion prediction is a crucial contribution, as it highlights the need for a holistic approach to autonomous driving system development. Overall, this paper presents a well-structured and insightful analysis of the challenges in online map-based motion prediction, offering a valuable benchmark and a promising baseline for future research.

## Strengths:

Second, the proposed new data partition and evaluation metrics are both reasonable and well-justified. The decision to split the nuScenes dataset into three spatially disjoint sets is a crucial step towards ensuring a more realistic and rigorous evaluation of model performance. This approach helps to mitigate the issue of train-validation overlap, which can lead to inflated performance metrics. The refined evaluation metrics, which consider the performance of both close and distant agents, provide a more nuanced assessment of model capabilities. Third, the analysis of different map element selections is a valuable contribution. The authors' investigation into the impact of various map elements, such as lane boundaries and centerlines, provides crucial insights for the design of both online mapping and motion prediction models. This analysis helps to identify which map elements are most important for accurate motion prediction, which can guide future research in this area. Finally, the introduction of a boundary-free baseline that leverages image features is a notable technical innovation. This baseline addresses the issue of missing map elements for distant agents, which is a significant challenge in online map-based motion prediction. The use of deformable attention to integrate image features with the motion prediction model is a clever approach that demonstrates the potential of combining visual and map information. Overall, the paper's strengths lie in its rigorous analysis, well-justified methodological choices, and valuable technical contributions. The authors have made a significant step forward in the field of online map-based motion prediction, and their work will undoubtedly serve as a valuable resource for future research.


## Weaknesses:

While the paper presents several valuable contributions, I have identified some weaknesses that warrant further discussion. First, the boundary-free baseline, while innovative, lacks a thorough analysis of its scalability and robustness. The paper introduces this baseline as a method to address the lack of map elements for distant agents by leveraging image features and deformable attention. However, the paper does not provide any analysis of how this approach would scale in dense traffic scenarios, where the number of agents increases significantly. The computational cost of processing image features for numerous distant agents is not discussed, and it is unclear whether the proposed method would remain efficient under such conditions. Furthermore, the paper does not investigate the performance of the baseline under different weather conditions, such as rain or fog, which can significantly degrade image quality and impact the reliability of image-based features. This lack of analysis raises concerns about the practical applicability of the boundary-free baseline in real-world scenarios. The paper mentions the use of deformable attention to aggregate image features, but it lacks a detailed explanation of the specific parameters and configurations used. While the paper provides a mathematical formulation of the deformable attention mechanism, it does not specify the number of attention heads, the size of the attention window, or the specific feature maps used. This lack of detail makes it difficult to reproduce the results and hinders further research in this area. The paper also does not compare the performance of the deformable attention mechanism with other attention mechanisms, such as standard cross-attention, which would have provided a more complete understanding of its advantages. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper explicitly introduces the boundary-free baseline and its use of deformable attention but fails to provide the necessary details and analysis to support its robustness and scalability. The authors explicitly state that OMMP-Bench is constructed by splitting the nuScenes dataset into three spatially disjoint sets. While nuScenes is a widely used dataset, it has specific characteristics that may not be representative of all driving environments. The density of traffic, the complexity of road networks, and the types of map elements can vary significantly across different cities and countries. The paper does not discuss how the proposed data split and evaluation metrics might need to be adapted for datasets with different characteristics. Furthermore, there are no experiments conducted on other datasets to assess the generalizability of the benchmark. This lack of validation raises concerns about the applicability of the proposed benchmark to a wider range of autonomous driving scenarios. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the paper explicitly states its reliance on the nuScenes dataset and lacks any discussion or experiments on other datasets.
