## Summary:

This paper introduces PLAGUE, a novel framework for generating multi-turn jailbreak attacks on large language models (LLMs). The core contribution lies in its systematic approach to crafting adversarial conversations, which are designed to bypass safety mechanisms and elicit harmful responses. PLAGUE operates through a three-phase process: a Planner phase that generates a high-level plan for the attack, a Primer phase that establishes an adversarial context, and a Finisher phase that delivers the final attack prompt. Following these phases, a lifelong learning component allows the framework to adapt and improve over time by storing successful attack strategies. The paper evaluates PLAGUE on several state-of-the-art LLMs, including OpenAI's o3, Deepseek-R1, and Claude's Opus 4.1, demonstrating that it achieves higher attack success rates (ASR) and diversity compared to existing methods. The authors also present an analysis of the framework's efficiency, showing that it scales linearly with the number of conversation turns until it plateaus at six rounds. The empirical findings highlight the effectiveness of PLAGUE in generating multi-turn attacks, showcasing its potential for red-teaming and vulnerability assessment of LLMs. The framework's modular design and lifelong learning component make it a valuable tool for understanding and mitigating the risks associated with adversarial attacks on LLMs. Overall, the paper presents a significant advancement in the field of multi-turn jailbreak attacks, providing both a practical framework and a comprehensive evaluation of its performance.
