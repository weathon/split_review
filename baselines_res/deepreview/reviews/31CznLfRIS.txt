## Summary:

This paper introduces VideoJudge, a novel bootstrapping framework designed to train multimodal large language models (MLLMs) for evaluating video understanding tasks. The core idea revolves around a generator-evaluator pipeline that iteratively synthesizes high-quality training data without relying on human annotation. The process begins with a generator model producing candidate responses for a given video-instruction pair, which are then evaluated by an MLLM. This evaluation loop allows for the refinement of responses and the creation of a large dataset of video-instruction-response triplets with associated quality ratings. The authors leverage this bootstrapped data to fine-tune smaller MLLMs, demonstrating that these models can achieve performance comparable to or exceeding that of much larger models on various meta-evaluation benchmarks. A key innovation is the ability of these models to generate instance-specific rubrics at inference time, enhancing the interpretability of the evaluation process. The paper contributes a scalable and efficient method for training MLLM-based evaluators, along with a comprehensive suite of resources including trained models, meta-evaluation benchmarks, bootstrapped datasets, and detailed implementation details to support reproducible research in video understanding evaluation. The authors showcase the effectiveness of their approach through comprehensive experiments, demonstrating the potential of their framework to address the challenges of evaluating complex video understanding models. The paper's findings suggest that fine-tuned smaller models can be just as effective, if not more so, than larger models for video understanding evaluation, which has significant implications for resource-constrained environments. The introduction of instance-specific rubrics also provides a more transparent and explainable evaluation process, which is crucial for building trust in AI-driven evaluation systems. Overall, the paper presents a significant step forward in the field of video understanding evaluation, offering a practical and efficient approach to training MLLM-based evaluators.

This analysis would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the potential biases and their impact on model performance. The reliance on dense video descriptions as semantic context may not fully capture the nuances of raw video content, and the paper should explore the potential impact of this limitation on the performance of the framework.
