## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel active learning framework tailored for flow matching models, a class of generative models that have gained significant attention in recent years. The core contribution lies in the development of two distinct query strategies: one aimed at enhancing the diversity of the training data and the other at improving the accuracy of the model. The authors propose a hybrid approach that combines these two strategies, offering a flexible way to balance the trade-off between diversity and accuracy during the training process. The theoretical foundation of the proposed method rests on the assumption that flow matching models can be effectively represented as piecewise-linear neural networks (CPWL NNs). This assumption allows the authors to analyze how individual data points influence the model's diversity and accuracy. The diversity query strategy leverages a Radial Basis Function (RBF) neural network to predict labels for unlabeled data points, which are then used to select samples that maximize the entropy of the label distribution. The accuracy query strategy, on the other hand, focuses on selecting samples that minimize the distance between the predicted and true labels. The hybrid strategy combines these two approaches using a weighted average. The authors validate their proposed framework through a series of experiments on multiple datasets, demonstrating that their method outperforms traditional active learning techniques designed for discriminative models. The empirical findings suggest that the proposed active learning framework can effectively improve both the diversity and accuracy of flow matching models, offering a practical solution for training these models with limited labeled data. The paper's significance lies in its unique application of active learning to the domain of flow matching models, which are known for their high data labeling costs. By providing a theoretical framework and practical query strategies, the authors offer a valuable contribution to the field of generative modeling and active learning. The paper's approach of analyzing flow matching models through the lens of piecewise-linear neural networks provides a novel perspective and opens up new avenues for research in this area. The empirical results further underscore the practical utility of the proposed method, highlighting its potential for real-world applications where labeled data is scarce and expensive to obtain. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and rigorously evaluated approach to active learning for flow matching models, making a significant contribution to the field.

## Weaknesses:

Despite its strengths, the paper exhibits several weaknesses that warrant careful consideration. The most significant limitation is the core assumption that flow matching models can be effectively represented as piecewise-linear neural networks (CPWL NNs). While the authors acknowledge this assumption in Section 2.2, stating, "In this paper, we hypothesize that neural networks employed in flow matching also exhibit the property of piecewise-linear interpolation," the implications of this assumption are not fully explored. The paper does not provide a rigorous justification for why flow matching models, which involve complex, non-linear transformations, would adhere to this property. This is a critical concern because if the underlying model deviates significantly from piecewise-linearity, the derived generation law (Eq. 3) and the subsequent analysis of how data points influence diversity and accuracy may be inaccurate. This could undermine the entire theoretical framework and the validity of the proposed query strategies. The paper's reliance on this assumption without a thorough analysis of its limitations is a major weakness, and I have a high confidence in this assessment. The paper also lacks a detailed analysis of the computational complexity of the proposed query strategies. The diversity query strategy ($Q_D$) involves calculating distances in both data and label spaces, as well as computing the entropy of the label distribution, while the accuracy query strategy ($Q_A$) involves calculating distances in the label space. These operations, especially when performed over a large unlabeled pool, can be computationally intensive. The paper mentions using RBF networks for label prediction of unlabeled data in Section 2.3, which adds to the computational cost. However, there is no explicit discussion of the time complexity of these operations or how they scale with the size of the dataset. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to assess the practical scalability of the proposed methods, particularly for very large datasets or high-dimensional data. The absence of a computational complexity analysis is a significant oversight, and I have a high confidence in this assessment. Another notable weakness is the reliance of the diversity-based query strategy on the quality of the RBF network used for label prediction. As stated in Section 2.3, "For unlabeled data, we employ Radial Basis Function (RBF) Neural Networks for label prediction due to their favorable optimization properties." The diversity query strategy ($Q_D$) directly uses the predicted labels from the RBF network to calculate the distance to existing labels. The accuracy of these predictions directly influences the selection of new data points for enhancing diversity. If the RBF network performs poorly, it could misidentify the most informative points for diversity, leading to suboptimal data selection and hindering the effectiveness of the active learning process. The paper does not provide any details about the architecture, training procedure, or performance of the RBF network. Furthermore, there is no ablation study on the impact of the RBF network's accuracy on the overall performance of the diversity strategy. This dependence on an external component without a thorough analysis of its impact is a significant weakness, and I have a high confidence in this assessment. The paper also lacks a discussion of alternative methods for label prediction and does not justify the choice of RBF networks over other approaches. This omission further weakens the analysis of the diversity-based query strategy. Finally, while the paper presents empirical results on multiple datasets, it does not provide a detailed analysis of the sensitivity of the proposed methods to different hyperparameter settings. The choice of hyperparameters, such as the weighting factor in the hybrid strategy, could significantly impact the performance of the method. The absence of a sensitivity analysis makes it difficult to assess the robustness of the proposed approach and to provide practical guidance for its application. This lack of sensitivity analysis is a notable weakness, and I have a medium confidence in this assessment.
