## Summary:

This paper introduces ARSS, a novel framework for autoregressive novel view synthesis using a GPT-style decoder-only model. The core innovation lies in the integration of a video tokenizer, which converts continuous image sequences into discrete tokens, and a camera encoder, which transforms camera trajectories into 3D positional guidance. The method leverages a permuted spatial structure within the transformer to enhance the understanding of spatial relationships while maintaining temporal consistency. The authors demonstrate that ARSS achieves state-of-the-art performance on the RealEstate10K and ACID datasets, and also show promising results in zero-shot novel view synthesis on the DL3DV-10K benchmark. The paper provides extensive experimental results and ablation studies, but several key areas need further investigation and clarification to fully understand the strengths and limitations of the proposed method. The reliance on a pre-trained video tokenizer, the lack of detailed architectural specifications, and the absence of a comprehensive analysis of computational efficiency and scalability are notable concerns. Despite these limitations, the paper makes a significant contribution to the field of novel view synthesis by introducing a new autoregressive approach that leverages advanced tokenization and camera encoding techniques.

## Strengths:

The paper presents several compelling strengths that contribute to its overall significance. Firstly, the application of video finite scalar quantization (FSQ) to the novel view synthesis problem is innovative. By converting continuous image sequences into discrete tokens, the method effectively captures both spatial and temporal information, which is crucial for maintaining consistency across generated views. The use of a GPT-style decoder-only model for this task is a novel departure from traditional diffusion-based methods, addressing the challenge of imposing a causal structure in camera trajectories. This approach not only aligns well with the autoregressive nature of the problem but also demonstrates the potential of transformer architectures in generating high-quality, temporally consistent novel views. Secondly, the introduction of a camera encoder that converts camera trajectories into 3D positional guidance using Plücker raymaps is a creative solution. This component allows for precise camera control during the view synthesis process, which is essential for generating realistic and accurate views. The authors provide a clear explanation of how the Plücker raymaps are used to encode camera information, and the experimental results support the effectiveness of this approach. Thirdly, the paper includes extensive experimental results on public datasets, demonstrating that ARSS achieves state-of-the-art performance in novel view synthesis tasks. The qualitative and quantitative comparisons with other methods, such as SEVA and diffusion-based models, are thorough and provide strong evidence for the method's capabilities. The ablation studies, while limited, offer insights into the importance of the permuted spatial structure and the tokenization strategy. Overall, the paper is well-organized and easy to follow, with a clear presentation of the method and its components. The authors effectively communicate the significance of their contributions and the potential impact on the field of novel view synthesis.


## Weaknesses:

Despite the paper's strengths, several limitations and concerns need to be addressed to fully validate the proposed method. One of the primary concerns is the reliance on a pre-trained video tokenizer, which limits the novelty of the approach. The paper explicitly states the use of VidTok (Tang et al., 2024) for temporally causal modeling, but it does not provide ablation studies to isolate the contributions of the video tokenizer from other components. This reliance on a pre-trained model makes it difficult to assess the true value of the tokenizer in the context of novel view synthesis, as the main performance gains could be attributed to the pre-trained weights rather than the method itself. The fine-tuning of the tokenizer, while necessary for the specific task, does not represent a significant methodological advancement. Another critical limitation is the insufficient ablation studies to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method. The paper includes ablation studies on token order permutation and tokenization strategy, but it lacks a detailed comparison of the proposed camera encoder with simpler camera representations. This omission makes it challenging to determine whether the added complexity of the camera encoder is justified by performance gains. Additionally, the paper does not provide a comprehensive analysis of the computational efficiency and scalability of the autoregressive approach. While the authors mention the hardware used for training, they do not offer a detailed comparison of training and inference times, memory usage, or how the method scales with increasing sequence lengths or higher resolution images. This lack of computational analysis is a significant gap, especially for practical applications where efficiency is crucial. The paper also lacks a detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed method, particularly in scenarios with complex camera trajectories or highly dynamic scenes. The current evaluation does not thoroughly explore the boundaries of the method's applicability, and it is unclear how the method would handle rapid camera movements, significant occlusions, or large viewpoint changes. These scenarios are common in real-world applications and could potentially disrupt the temporal consistency that the video tokenizer aims to preserve. The paper could benefit from a more rigorous evaluation that specifically targets these challenging conditions, providing a balanced and comprehensive assessment of the method's strengths and weaknesses. Furthermore, the paper lacks a detailed explanation of the camera autoencoder, including the specific Plücker ray representation used and how it is converted into a latent space. The authors mention the use of Plücker raymaps but do not delve into the advantages of this representation over other camera encoding methods. A more detailed comparison of the training procedures, including the specific loss functions and optimization techniques, would also be beneficial. This would help to highlight the unique aspects of the proposed method and its advantages over existing approaches. Finally, the paper could include more qualitative results to support its claims. While quantitative metrics are provided, visual examples of the generated novel views are crucial for assessing the quality of the synthesis, especially in terms of texture, lighting, and overall realism. The current qualitative results are limited and do not showcase the full potential or limitations of the approach. A side-by-side comparison with ground truth images and results from other state-of-the-art methods would provide a more intuitive understanding of the method's performance.


## Suggestions:

To address the identified limitations, several key areas need further investigation and clarification. First, the authors should conduct a more thorough ablation study to isolate the impact of each component. This should include a comparison of the proposed method with a version that uses a standard image tokenizer instead of the video FSQ, keeping all other components the same. This would help to quantify the specific benefits of using video FSQ for novel view synthesis. Additionally, the ablation study should include a comparison of the proposed camera encoder with simpler camera control methods, such as directly embedding camera parameters into the transformer. This would help to determine whether the added complexity of the camera encoder is justified by performance gains. The contribution of the permuted spatial structure should also be evaluated by comparing the full model with a version that does not use this structure. This would help to isolate the impact of this specific design choice on the overall performance. Second, the authors should provide a more detailed analysis of the differences between their method and SEVA. While both methods use a GPT-style decoder, the specific ways in which they are applied to novel view synthesis differ significantly. The authors should clarify how their approach to using video FSQ and a camera encoder addresses the limitations of SEVA's anchor view approach. This should include a discussion of the specific challenges that arise in novel view synthesis, such as maintaining temporal consistency and handling large viewpoint changes, and how the proposed method addresses these challenges. A more detailed comparison of the training procedures, including the specific loss functions and optimization techniques, would also be beneficial. Third, the authors should include a more rigorous evaluation that specifically targets scenarios with complex camera trajectories and dynamic scenes. This could involve testing the method on datasets with more challenging camera paths, such as those involving rapid rotations or significant changes in viewpoint. The authors should also analyze the impact of occlusions and lighting variations on the quality of the generated views. A detailed analysis of failure cases would be beneficial, highlighting the specific conditions under which the method struggles to maintain temporal consistency or generate realistic views. Fourth, the authors should provide a detailed comparison of the computational efficiency and scalability of their method with those of state-of-the-art diffusion models. This comparison should include a breakdown of the computational cost associated with each component of the proposed method, such as the video tokenizer, camera encoder, and autoregressive transformer. The authors should also investigate how the method scales with increasing sequence lengths and image resolutions, and provide recommendations for optimizing the method for practical applications. Techniques for reducing the computational cost of the autoregressive transformer, such as using sparse attention mechanisms or model compression techniques, should be explored. A thorough analysis of the memory usage of the method would also be beneficial, particularly for large-scale datasets or high-resolution images. Finally, the authors should provide more qualitative results to support their claims. This should include a wider range of examples, showcasing both successful and challenging cases. The examples should highlight the method's ability to generate novel views with realistic textures, lighting, and geometric consistency. Side-by-side comparisons with ground truth images and results from other state-of-the-art methods would provide a more intuitive understanding of the method's performance. The authors should also consider including visualizations of the camera trajectories and the corresponding synthesized views to better illustrate the method's capabilities. A detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed method, including cases where the generated views exhibit artifacts or inconsistencies, would provide a more balanced and comprehensive evaluation.
