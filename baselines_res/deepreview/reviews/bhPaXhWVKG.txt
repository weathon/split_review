## Summary:

This paper introduces MermaidFlow, a novel framework for agentic workflow generation that leverages evolutionary programming and the Mermaid graph language. The core innovation lies in representing workflows as declarative graphs using Mermaid, which allows for static verification and structured evolution. Unlike existing methods that rely on brittle, low-level representations, MermaidFlow separates symbolic planning from execution, enabling the creation of robust, human-readable, and verifiable workflows. The framework employs domain-aware evolutionary operators, such as crossover, mutation, insertion, and deletion, to efficiently explore a high-quality workflow space. These operators are designed to maintain the validity of the workflows throughout the search process. The authors demonstrate MermaidFlow's effectiveness through comprehensive experiments on standard agentic reasoning benchmarks, including GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval, and MBPP. The results show that MermaidFlow consistently outperforms multiple baselines in terms of success rates and token efficiency, without requiring modifications to task settings or evaluation protocols. Specifically, MermaidFlow achieves superior performance across all benchmarks, demonstrating its ability to generate more effective workflows compared to existing methods. The paper also includes an ablation study that validates the effectiveness of the Mermaid representation and the evolutionary approach. The authors highlight that MermaidFlow offers a more interpretable framework for agentic workflow generation, addressing a key limitation of existing systems. The declarative representation and static verification make workflows easier to understand, debug, and adapt. Overall, MermaidFlow presents a significant advancement in the field of agentic workflow generation, providing a structured, verifiable, and efficient approach that advances workflow optimization through a statically verifiable, declarative graph representation. The framework's ability to guarantee static graph-level correctness across the entire generation process is a notable contribution, distinguishing it from prior methods. The paper's findings suggest that MermaidFlow has the potential to be a valuable tool for developing more robust and adaptable agentic systems.
