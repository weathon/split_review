## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel nonlinear, multimodal encoding model designed to predict brain responses to naturalistic speech. The core contribution lies in the integration of audio and semantic features extracted from pre-trained models, specifically Whisper and Llama, into a single predictive framework. The authors employ a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) to capture the complex, nonlinear interactions between these modalities, moving beyond traditional linear, unimodal approaches. Empirically, the proposed model demonstrates substantial improvements in prediction performance, achieving a 17.2% and 17.9% increase in unnormalized and normalized correlation, respectively, compared to traditional unimodal linear models. Furthermore, the model outperforms prior state-of-the-art models that rely on weighted averaging of linear unimodal predictions, showing a 7.7% and 14.4% improvement. These gains are not merely incremental; they suggest that current linear, unimodal practices may be missing a significant portion of structured, explainable variance, particularly in the realm of nonlinear multimodal interactions. The authors also employ variance partitioning and prediction accuracy analysis to demonstrate that multimodal integration is essential for speech encoding, with most brain regions relying on overlapping audio-semantic information. The unique contributions of each modality vary hierarchically from sensory to higher-order areas, extending existing neurolinguistic theories. The study's findings reveal distributed multimodal processing patterns across the cortex that align with key neurolinguistic theories, such as the Motor Theory of Speech Perception, the Convergence-Divergence Zone model, and embodied semantics. The authors further use a Relative Error Difference (RED)-based clustering analysis to show that their nonlinear model captures more structured spatiotemporal relationships in brain responses, consistent with established principles of cortical organization. Overall, this work underscores the transformative potential of nonlinear multimodal approaches in speech encoding, offering a more accurate and nuanced understanding of how the brain processes speech.
