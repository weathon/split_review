## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel method for real-time adaptive neural stimulation, aiming to perturb latent neural dynamics in desired directions. The core contribution lies in the integration of several existing techniques into a cohesive framework. This framework includes streaming latent space construction, adaptive nonparametric modeling of stimulation effects, and optimization of stimulation patterns under feasibility constraints. The authors propose a streaming version of jPCA (sjPCA) for real-time latent space construction, which is a key component of their approach. They then use kernel regression to model the relationship between stimulation parameters and changes in the latent space. Finally, they formulate an optimization problem to find stimulation patterns that maximize the projection of latent dynamics along a desired trajectory, subject to constraints on the stimulation amplitude. The method is evaluated on both simulated and real neural data, including calcium imaging and electrophysiological recordings. The authors demonstrate that their method can learn a stimulation-response mapping quickly and design effective stimuli to drive latent dynamics in desired directions. They also show that the algorithm achieves runtimes faster than real-time, making it potentially suitable for in vivo applications. The paper's significance lies in its potential to enable precise, real-time control of neural activity, which could have implications for both basic neuroscience research and clinical interventions. The ability to manipulate latent dynamics directly, rather than individual neurons, represents a significant step forward in the field. The authors' approach of integrating streaming algorithms, adaptive modeling, and optimization under constraints provides a comprehensive framework for addressing the challenges of real-time neural stimulation. The empirical results, while promising, also highlight areas where further investigation is needed, particularly in terms of robustness, computational complexity, and comparison to existing methods. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of neural engineering and stimulation, offering a novel approach to manipulating brain activity in real time.

## Strengths:

I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. The core idea of integrating streaming latent space construction, adaptive modeling of stimulation effects, and optimization of stimuli is a significant advancement. The authors have successfully combined several existing techniques in a creative and novel way to address the challenging problem of real-time neural stimulation. The proposed method, which includes sjPCA for streaming latent space construction, kernel regression for modeling stimulation effects, and a constrained optimization approach, is well-motivated and clearly explained. The use of sjPCA, a streaming version of jPCA, is a notable technical innovation that allows for real-time tracking of latent dynamics. The authors' approach to modeling the stimulation-response mapping using kernel regression is also well-chosen, as it allows for capturing non-linear relationships between stimulation parameters and latent space trajectories. The optimization framework, which incorporates feasibility constraints, is another strength of the paper. This ensures that the designed stimulation patterns are realistic and can be implemented in real-world experiments. The empirical evaluation of the method is also a strong point. The authors have demonstrated their approach on both simulated and real neural data, including calcium imaging and electrophysiological recordings. This comprehensive evaluation provides evidence for the effectiveness and robustness of the method across different data modalities. The fact that the method achieves runtimes faster than real-time is a significant achievement, making it potentially suitable for in vivo applications. The authors' claim of achieving runtimes under 10ms on average, and below 100ms, is impressive and suggests that the method could be used for real-time closed-loop experiments. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow. The authors have clearly explained the technical details of their method, making it accessible to a wide audience. The inclusion of illustrative figures and pseudocode in the appendix further enhances the clarity of the paper. Overall, the paper presents a novel and technically sound method for real-time neural stimulation, supported by strong empirical evidence and clear explanations.


## Weaknesses:

Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant further discussion. First, the paper lacks a detailed comparison of the proposed method with existing methods for designing neural stimulations. While the authors mention related work, they do not provide a quantitative comparison with other state-of-the-art techniques. For example, the paper does not compare its method to approaches that use closed-loop control or reinforcement learning to optimize stimulation parameters. This lack of comparison makes it difficult to assess the relative advantages and disadvantages of the proposed method. The paper only compares against a 'blind' version of itself, which is not sufficient to establish the method's superiority over existing approaches. Second, the paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational complexity of the proposed method. While the authors report overall runtimes, they do not provide a breakdown of the computational cost of each component of their algorithm. This makes it difficult to understand how the computational cost scales with the size of the neural data and the number of stimulation parameters. The paper mentions the use of specific algorithms like PCA, Kalman filter, and kernel regression, but it does not provide a computational complexity analysis in the context of its integrated framework. This lack of analysis is a significant limitation, as it makes it difficult to assess the scalability of the method for large-scale neural recordings. Third, the paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the robustness of the proposed method to noise and variability in the neural data. While the method incorporates noise terms in the dynamical model and uses kernel regression, which can be robust to noise, there is no explicit analysis of the method's performance under varying noise levels. The paper includes fixed noise levels in the toy model and uses real neural recordings, which inherently contain noise, but there is no systematic variation of noise levels to assess robustness. This lack of robustness analysis is a concern, as real-world neural data is often noisy and variable. Fourth, the paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the sensitivity of the proposed method to the choice of hyperparameters. The method uses several hyperparameters, such as the kernel scaling constants in the kernel regression and the regularization parameter in the optimization problem. While the paper mentions that kernel scaling constants are optionally tuned, it does not provide a systematic analysis of the impact of these hyperparameters on the method's performance. This lack of sensitivity analysis is a limitation, as it makes it difficult to understand the robustness of the results and provide guidance on parameter selection. Fifth, the paper lacks a comprehensive discussion of the limitations of the proposed method. While the authors briefly mention one specific limitation, they do not provide a broader discussion of potential challenges and limitations in real-world applications. This lack of discussion is a concern, as it does not provide a balanced view of the method's applicability and potential challenges. Finally, the paper's description of the sjPCA method lacks a detailed step-by-step derivation of the update rule and a more in-depth explanation of the Orthogonal Procrustes step. The mathematical details of sjPCA are not entirely clear, and the paper does not provide a rigorous argument for why the optimization problem in Equation 8 is non-convex. The implications of non-convexity for the optimization process are also not discussed. These weaknesses, which I have verified through direct examination of the paper, significantly impact my assessment of the paper's overall contribution. The lack of comparison to existing methods, the absence of a detailed computational complexity analysis, the lack of robustness analysis, the lack of hyperparameter sensitivity analysis, the limited discussion of limitations, and the lack of clarity in the sjPCA method all represent significant areas for improvement.
