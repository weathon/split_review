## Summary:

This paper introduces VisualPRM400K, a large-scale dataset comprising 400,000 multimodal process supervision examples, designed to facilitate the training of step-wise reward models (PRMs) for multimodal reasoning tasks. The authors leverage this dataset to train VisualPRM, a novel multimodal PRM capable of estimating the value score of each step in a reasoning process. This model aims to enhance the performance of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by providing a mechanism for self-correction through Best-of-N (BoN) evaluation. The paper also presents VisualProcessBench, a benchmark designed to evaluate the ability of PRMs and MLLMs to detect incorrect steps in multimodal reasoning. The core methodology involves automatically generating the VisualPRM400K dataset using a Monte Carlo sampling approach to annotate the correctness of each step in a reasoning process. VisualPRM is then trained on this dataset to predict step-wise correctness. The empirical findings demonstrate that VisualPRM significantly improves the reasoning performance of various MLLMs across different model scales and families when used in a Best-of-N evaluation setting. Specifically, the authors show that using VisualPRM as a critic model to select the best reasoning path from multiple samples leads to substantial performance gains on several multimodal reasoning benchmarks. The paper's significance lies in its contribution of a large-scale, automatically generated dataset for step-wise multimodal reasoning, a novel PRM model that leverages this dataset, and a benchmark for evaluating the step-wise reasoning capabilities of MLLMs. The results suggest that step-wise reward modeling is a promising approach for improving the reasoning abilities of MLLMs, particularly when combined with BoN evaluation strategies. The authors also highlight the efficiency of their model, noting that it achieves substantial performance gains with a relatively small number of parameters compared to larger proprietary models. Overall, this work provides valuable resources and insights for the community, advancing the field of multimodal reasoning and self-correction in large language models.

## Strengths:

This dataset, automatically generated using a Monte Carlo sampling approach, addresses a critical need for step-wise supervision data in the multimodal domain. The authors' approach to automatically annotate the correctness of each step in a reasoning process is both innovative and practical, allowing for the generation of a substantial dataset without relying on expensive human annotation. This is a significant contribution, as it provides the community with a valuable resource for training and evaluating step-wise reasoning models. Furthermore, the development of VisualPRM, a multimodal PRM trained on this dataset, is another notable achievement. The model demonstrates superior performance compared to existing methods, such as Outcome Reward Models and Self-Consistency, in Best-of-N evaluation settings. This is particularly impressive given the relatively small number of parameters in VisualPRM compared to larger proprietary models. The authors' empirical results clearly demonstrate that VisualPRM enhances the reasoning performance of various MLLMs across different model scales and families, showcasing the generalizability and effectiveness of their approach.
