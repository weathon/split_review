## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel approach to integrating score-based generative models into the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) framework for solving inverse problems. The core contribution is the AC-DC (Auto-Correction and Directional Correction) denoiser, a three-stage process designed to address the manifold mismatch issue that arises when using score-based denoisers within ADMM. The AC-DC denoiser first applies auto-correction via additive Gaussian noise, then performs directional correction using conditional Langevin dynamics, and finally employs score-based denoising. This approach aims to align the ADMM iterates with the data manifold, thereby improving the effectiveness of the score-based denoiser. The authors provide theoretical convergence guarantees for the proposed method under certain conditions, establishing that the ADMM-PnP algorithm with the AC-DC denoiser converges to a fixed point or a ball neighborhood. The theoretical analysis is supported by mathematical proofs, which are detailed in the appendices. The empirical evaluation of the method is conducted on various inverse problems, including inpainting, deblurring, and phase retrieval, using datasets such as FFHQ and ImageNet. The results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves improved solution quality compared to existing baselines, showcasing the potential of the AC-DC denoiser in addressing the challenges of integrating score-based models into ADMM. The paper is well-written and clearly explains the technical details of the proposed method, making it accessible to researchers in the field. The combination of theoretical analysis and empirical validation provides a strong foundation for the proposed approach. However, the paper also has some limitations, particularly in the assumptions made for the convergence analysis and the scope of the experimental evaluation. These limitations are discussed in detail in the 'Weakness' section. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of inverse problems by introducing a novel and effective method for integrating score-based generative models into ADMM, while also highlighting areas for future research and improvement.

## Strengths:

This three-stage denoiser, consisting of auto-correction, directional correction, and score-based denoising, effectively addresses the manifold mismatch issue that often plagues the use of score-based denoisers in ADMM. The auto-correction step, which adds Gaussian noise, helps to move the iterates closer to the data manifold, while the directional correction step, using conditional Langevin dynamics, refines the iterates in a direction that is more aligned with the data distribution. This combination is a creative solution that balances efficiency and accuracy, and it is a key technical innovation of this work. ## Weaknesses:

While the paper presents a compelling approach, several weaknesses warrant careful consideration. First, the convergence analysis relies on Assumptions 2 and 3, which may not always hold in practice, particularly for complex data distributions. Assumption 2 states that the log data density is M-smooth, meaning that the gradient of the log data density does not change too rapidly. Mathematically, this is expressed as $\|\nabla \log p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x}) - \nabla \log p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{y})\|_2 \leq M\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y}\|_2$ for all $\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y} \in \mathcal{X}$. This assumption is critical for the convergence theorems, but it may be restrictive for real-world data distributions, which can exhibit sharp boundaries or discontinuities. The paper does not provide a detailed discussion of the implications of violating this assumption, nor does it offer any empirical evidence to support its validity for the datasets used in the experiments (FFHQ and ImageNet). Similarly, Assumption 3 imposes a coercivity condition on the negative log data density, requiring that $\|\nabla \log p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x})\|_2^2 \geq -c_1 \log p_{\text{data}} - c_2$ and $\|\mathbf{x}\|_2 \leq -c_1 \log p_{\text{data}}(\mathbf{x}) + c_2$ for all $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X}$. This assumption ensures that the score function does not vanish at infinity, but it may be difficult to verify for real-world data manifolds. The paper lacks a discussion of the potential consequences of violating these assumptions and how it might affect the convergence of the algorithm. The absence of such a discussion is a significant limitation, as it leaves open the question of the robustness of the method in practical scenarios.
