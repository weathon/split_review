## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel dual ANN-to-SNN conversion framework specifically designed for large language models (LLMs), aiming to facilitate their efficient deployment on edge devices. The core contribution lies in a training-free approach that leverages quantized LLMs, thereby circumventing the need for a specialized, conversion-friendly ANN, which is often computationally expensive to train. The framework incorporates a parameter-efficient, layer-wise calibration technique to mitigate the unevenness error, a common challenge in spike arrival patterns that can degrade the performance of converted spiking neural networks (SNNs). The authors provide a theoretical analysis of the conversion errors, including clipping, quantization, and unevenness errors, and demonstrate the effectiveness of their calibration method in minimizing these errors. The proposed method is evaluated on the LLaMA-2-7B and LLaMA-3-8B models, achieving performance comparable to state-of-the-art quantization techniques such as PrefixQuant and DuQuant. The experimental results show that the proposed method can achieve average accuracies of 67.65% on LLaMA-2-7B and 69.03% on LLaMA-3-8B, with minimal learnable parameter size. The paper's significance lies in its potential to enable the deployment of LLMs on resource-constrained edge devices by providing a computationally efficient and effective conversion method. By eliminating the need for training a conversion-specific ANN and introducing a parameter-efficient calibration technique, the authors address key challenges in the ANN-to-SNN conversion process, making it more practical for real-world applications. The theoretical analysis and empirical validation provide a solid foundation for the proposed approach, highlighting its potential to advance the field of efficient LLM deployment. However, the paper's focus on the LLaMA family of models and the lack of empirical energy consumption measurements limit the generalizability and practical impact of the findings. Despite these limitations, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field, offering a promising direction for future research in efficient LLM deployment on edge devices.

## Strengths:

This is a crucial contribution, as it makes the conversion process more accessible and practical for researchers and practitioners working with limited resources. Furthermore, the proposed layer-wise calibration method is theory-backed and achieves significant conversion error reduction with minimal computational and memory overhead. This method effectively reduces unevenness error, which is a key challenge in ANN-to-SNN conversion.
