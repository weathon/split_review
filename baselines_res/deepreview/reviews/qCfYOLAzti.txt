## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel bootstrapping framework for large language model (LLM) unlearning, addressing the critical issue of spurious forgetting caused by the 'squeezing effect.' This effect occurs when traditional unlearning methods, which aim to reduce the likelihood of specific target responses, inadvertently cause the model to generate semantically similar, yet untargeted, outputs. The authors propose a method that incorporates the model's own high-confidence generations, or 'model beliefs,' into the unlearning process. This is achieved through two main approaches: Bootstrapping-Token (BS-T), which operates at the token level, and Bootstrapping-Sequence (BS-S), which operates at the sequence level. BS-T modifies the loss function by incorporating a weighted combination of the original target and the model's top-k predicted tokens, while BS-S samples high-confidence sequences from the model and adds them to the unlearning dataset. The core idea is to target not only the original target responses but also the model's own high-confidence rephrasings, thereby mitigating the squeezing effect. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of how BS-T reshapes gradient dynamics to effectively mitigate this effect. Empirically, the paper demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed framework across diverse benchmarks, including TOFU, MUSE, and WMDP, showing superior performance compared to state-of-the-art baselines. The results indicate that the bootstrapping approach achieves a more reliable balance between forgetting and retention. The paper also introduces a new evaluation metric, LLaJ, which aligns more closely with human judgment. Overall, this work makes a significant contribution to the field of LLM unlearning by identifying and addressing a key limitation of existing methods and proposing a novel framework that effectively mitigates this issue. The paper's findings highlight the importance of modeling internal beliefs for thorough unlearning and robust retention, and the empirical results demonstrate the practical effectiveness of the proposed approach.

## Strengths:

This paper presents several notable strengths that contribute to its overall significance and impact. First and foremost, the identification and characterization of the 'squeezing effect' is a crucial contribution. This effect, where traditional unlearning methods inadvertently cause the model to generate semantically related rephrasings of the target content, highlights a fundamental limitation in existing techniques. By recognizing and addressing this issue, the authors advance our understanding of the challenges in machine unlearning and pave the way for more robust solutions. The proposed bootstrapping framework is another significant strength. The innovative use of the model's own high-confidence generations to guide the unlearning process is a creative and effective approach to mitigating the squeezing effect. By targeting both the original target responses and the model's high-confidence rephrasings, the framework demonstrates a novel way to suppress unwanted knowledge. The instantiation of the framework at both the token level (BS-T) and the sequence level (BS-S) provides flexibility in addressing different aspects of the unlearning challenge. Furthermore, the paper provides a rigorous theoretical analysis of how BS-T mitigates the squeezing effect under the learning dynamics framework. This theoretical grounding strengthens the credibility of the proposed method and provides a deeper understanding of its underlying mechanisms. The extensive experiments conducted across multiple benchmarks and model families provide strong empirical evidence for the effectiveness and generalizability of the proposed approach. The results demonstrate that the bootstrapping framework outperforms state-of-the-art methods, achieving a more reliable balance between forgetting and retention. The inclusion of results on MUSE and WMDP, in addition to TOFU, further strengthens the empirical validation. Finally, the introduction of the LLaJ evaluation metric, which aligns more closely with human judgment, is a valuable contribution to the field. This metric provides a more accurate way to assess the effectiveness of unlearning methods, addressing the limitations of traditional metrics that may not capture the nuances of semantic rephrasing. In summary, the paper's strengths lie in its novel problem formulation, innovative methodology, theoretical grounding, and strong empirical validation, all of which contribute to a significant advancement in the field of LLM unlearning.


## Weaknesses:

As the paper describes, BS-S involves sampling and augmenting unlearning data, which inherently adds computational cost. However, the paper does not provide a breakdown of the time complexity associated with each step of the algorithm, such as the sampling process, the calculation of token-level and sequence-level probabilities, and the actual unlearning updates. While Appendix F.6 provides training time comparisons, this is insufficient to fully understand the computational burden. The absence of a detailed analysis makes it difficult to assess the scalability of the proposed method, especially when dealing with large-scale unlearning tasks. This lack of clarity is a significant limitation, as it hinders the practical application of the method in resource-constrained environments.
