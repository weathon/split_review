## Summary:

This paper introduces Spherical Watermark, a novel framework for lossless watermarking in diffusion models that aims to address the challenges of content provenance and authenticity in image synthesis. The core innovation lies in an encryption-free approach that integrates seamlessly with diffusion architectures, eliminating the need for per-image key storage and complex cryptographic operations. The method employs a spherical mapping module that transforms binary watermarks into Gaussian noise, ensuring that the watermarked images are statistically indistinguishable from unwatermarked ones while maintaining robust extraction capabilities. The framework consists of three main modules: a binary embedding module, a spherical mapping module, and a diffusion integration module. The spherical mapping module projects the watermark bits onto the unit sphere and applies an orthogonal rotation, which is designed to ensure that the watermarked noise distribution closely approximates a standard Gaussian distribution. The authors provide both theoretical analysis and empirical evidence to support the effectiveness of the proposed method. The theoretical analysis focuses on proving that the watermarked noise distribution is statistically indistinguishable from standard Gaussian noise, which is crucial for preserving the visual fidelity of the generated images. The empirical validation includes extensive experiments on Stable Diffusion models, demonstrating the superiority of Spherical Watermark over existing methods in terms of undetectability, traceability, and computational efficiency. The experiments cover various aspects, including visual fidelity, robustness under post-processing and adversarial attacks, and computational overhead. The results show that Spherical Watermark achieves high visual fidelity, strong robustness against various attacks, and efficient embedding and extraction processes. The paper also includes ablation studies to analyze the impact of different parameters on the performance of the method. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of watermarking for diffusion models, offering a practical and efficient solution for ensuring content provenance and authenticity. The encryption-free design and the lossless nature of the watermarking approach make it particularly appealing for real-world applications. The authors' rigorous theoretical analysis and extensive empirical validation provide strong evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed method, highlighting its potential to address the growing concerns about the misuse of diffusion models for generating malicious content.
