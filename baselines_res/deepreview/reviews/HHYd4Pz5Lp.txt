## Summary:

This paper introduces DelRec, a novel method for training synaptic delays within recurrent spiking neural networks (RSNNs) using surrogate gradient learning (SGL). The core innovation lies in the use of a differentiable interpolation technique to handle non-integer delays, enabling the optimization of these delays through backpropagation. The authors demonstrate that by making recurrent delays trainable, they can achieve state-of-the-art performance on two challenging temporal datasets, the Spiking Speech Command (SSC) and Permuted Sequential MNIST (PS-MNIST), while also matching state-of-the-art results on the Spiking Heidelberg Digit (SHD) dataset. The method is evaluated using simple Leaky-Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neurons, highlighting the effectiveness of the delay training approach. The paper provides a detailed explanation of the differentiable interpolation technique, which uses a triangle function to spread the spike signal across adjacent time steps, effectively approximating non-integer delays. The width of this triangle function is gradually reduced during training, leading to a more precise representation of the delays as integer values. The authors also conduct a functional study of delays, comparing the performance of feedforward and recurrent delays, and analyzing the method's robustness under low parameter constraints and sparsity. The results suggest that recurrent delays play a crucial role in temporal processing within SNNs and that the DelRec method provides an effective means of optimizing these delays. The authors propose that their method could be beneficial for deployment on neuromorphic hardware, which often features programmable synaptic delays. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of spiking neural networks by introducing a practical and effective method for training recurrent synaptic delays, which has been a challenging aspect in the past. The empirical results demonstrate the potential of this method for improving the performance of SNNs on temporal tasks, and the authors provide a clear and detailed explanation of their approach, making it accessible to a broad audience. The work also opens up new avenues for exploring the role of delays in neural computation and their potential for enhancing the capabilities of SNNs.
