## Summary:

This paper presents a comprehensive investigation into memory optimization strategies for large language models (LLMs), specifically focusing on the challenges posed by the Key-Value (KV) cache during long sequence generation in reasoning tasks. The authors address the critical issue of memory usage in LLMs, where the KV cache can quickly become a bottleneck, particularly during extended reasoning processes. The core contribution of this work lies in its systematic exploration of the trade-offs between model size, weight precision, token budget, parallel scaling, and KV cache compression. Through extensive empirical analysis, the authors demonstrate that there is no one-size-fits-all approach to memory optimization; instead, the optimal strategy is highly dependent on the model's effective size, defined as the product of the number of parameters and the bit precision of the weights, and the nature of the task. The paper's methodology involves a series of controlled experiments using various models from the Qwen3 family, along with additional evaluations on DeepSeek-R1-Distill and OpenReasoning-Nemotron, across diverse reasoning benchmarks, including AIME25, GPQA-Diamond, and LiveCodeBench. The authors employ a range of memory optimization techniques, such as weight quantization, KV cache eviction, and parallel scaling, to assess their impact on the memory-accuracy trade-off. The main empirical findings reveal that for smaller models, allocating memory to larger weights is more efficient, while larger models benefit from longer generations and parallel scaling. The paper also explores the effectiveness of KV cache eviction versus quantization, finding that eviction is generally more effective for smaller models, while quantization becomes more competitive for larger models. The authors provide general principles for memory-efficient reasoning models, emphasizing that memory optimization for reasoning models cannot be scale-agnostic. The overall significance of this work is in its practical guidance for deploying reasoning models in real-world applications, where memory constraints are often a limiting factor. By providing a nuanced understanding of the memory-accuracy trade-offs, this paper offers valuable insights for practitioners seeking to optimize the performance of their LLMs under limited memory budgets. The study highlights the importance of considering the interplay between model size, precision, and task characteristics when making memory optimization decisions, moving beyond a simplistic approach of simply reducing precision or compressing the KV cache. The paper's findings have important implications for the future development of reasoning models, suggesting that a more holistic approach to memory management is necessary to achieve both high accuracy and efficient resource utilization.
