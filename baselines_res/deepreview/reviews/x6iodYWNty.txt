## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel framework, Neural Predictor-Corrector (NPC), which leverages reinforcement learning to enhance the efficiency and adaptability of predictor-corrector (PC) methods across a diverse range of computational problems. The core idea is to unify problems such as robust optimization, global optimization, polynomial system root-finding, and sampling under the homotopy paradigm. The authors argue that traditional PC methods rely on fixed heuristics for step-size schedules and termination criteria, which are often suboptimal and lack adaptability to varying solution trajectories. To address this, they propose replacing these hand-crafted heuristics with learned policies using reinforcement learning. The NPC framework formulates the PC process as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), where the agent learns to select appropriate step sizes and termination criteria based on the current state of the solution trajectory. The agent is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a popular on-policy reinforcement learning algorithm. The reward function is designed to encourage both accuracy and efficiency, penalizing unnecessary corrector steps and rewarding accurate trajectory tracking. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of NPC through extensive experiments on four representative homotopy problems. In robust optimization, NPC is compared against classical and IRLS-based GNC methods. For global optimization, the comparisons include single-loop GH methods, Gaussian smoothing, and a learning-based approach. In polynomial root-finding, NPC is benchmarked against classic and simulator-based HC methods. Finally, for sampling, the authors compare NPC against classic ALD and iDEM methods. The results consistently show that NPC outperforms the baselines in terms of both computational efficiency and numerical stability, while also demonstrating the ability to generalize to unseen instances within the same problem class. The paper's significance lies in its unified approach to solving diverse problems using a single learning-based framework, offering a more flexible and efficient alternative to traditional methods that rely on problem-specific heuristics. By framing the PC process as a sequential decision-making problem, the authors open up new avenues for applying reinforcement learning to a wide range of computational challenges.
