## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel metric, High-Entropy Sum (HES), designed to evaluate the quality of reasoning in large language models (LLMs). The core idea behind HES is that high-quality reasoning paths contain critical decision points, which are reflected in tokens with high entropy. Instead of averaging entropy across all tokens, HES focuses on the top 0.5% of tokens with the highest entropy, summing their values to obtain a score. The authors argue that this approach better captures the complexity and diversity of reasoning patterns compared to traditional metrics like average entropy. The paper validates HES across three distinct training paradigms: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Rejection Fine-Tuning (RFT), and Reinforcement Learning (RL). In the SFT experiments, the authors demonstrate that training on a subset of data selected using HES can achieve comparable performance to training on the full dataset, while using low-HES data significantly degrades performance. In RFT, HES-based selection outperforms random baselines, indicating its effectiveness in identifying high-quality reasoning examples. Furthermore, in RL, combining HES-selected successful trajectories with random failed ones enables the model to learn both strong reasoning patterns and diverse failure modes, surpassing existing training-free selection methods. The authors present a comprehensive set of experiments across multiple datasets and tasks, providing strong empirical evidence for the effectiveness of HES as a robust, training-free metric for selecting high-quality reasoning data. The findings suggest that HES offers a computationally efficient and data-centric approach to developing advanced reasoning capabilities in LLMs, potentially reducing the need for extensive and costly training data. The paper's contribution lies in its introduction of a simple yet effective metric that can be applied across various training paradigms, offering a unified approach to data selection for reasoning tasks. The authors also provide a sensitivity analysis of the high-entropy token ratio, showing that the method is robust to variations in this parameter. Overall, the paper presents a compelling case for the use of HES as a valuable tool for improving the reasoning capabilities of LLMs.

## Strengths:

The primary strength of this paper lies in the introduction of the High-Entropy Sum (HES) metric, which offers a novel and effective approach to evaluating reasoning quality in LLMs. The core idea of focusing on the top 0.5% of high-entropy tokens, rather than averaging entropy across all tokens, is both intuitive and empirically validated. This approach addresses the limitations of traditional entropy-based metrics, which can be diluted by the presence of many predictable tokens in long reasoning chains. The paper's empirical achievements are also significant. The authors conduct extensive experiments across three different training paradigms—SFT, RFT, and RL—demonstrating the versatility and effectiveness of HES. In the SFT experiments, the finding that training on a small subset of HES-selected data can match the performance of training on the full dataset is particularly compelling, highlighting the potential for data efficiency. Furthermore, the RFT experiments show that HES-based selection outperforms random baselines, indicating its ability to identify high-quality reasoning examples. The RL experiments further demonstrate the utility of HES by showing that combining HES-selected successful trajectories with random failed ones leads to improved learning of both successful and unsuccessful reasoning patterns. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, making the core concepts and findings accessible to a broad audience. The inclusion of a sensitivity analysis for the high-entropy token ratio is another strength, showing that the method is robust to variations in this parameter. The authors also provide a clear rationale for the choice of the 0.5% threshold, although the sensitivity analysis shows that even smaller ratios can be effective. The paper's contribution is significant because it introduces a simple, computationally efficient, and training-free metric that can be applied across various training paradigms, offering a unified approach to data selection for reasoning tasks. This is a valuable contribution to the field, as it provides a practical tool for improving the reasoning capabilities of LLMs without requiring extensive computational resources or complex training procedures. The authors have successfully demonstrated the effectiveness of HES through rigorous experimentation and clear presentation, making this a strong and impactful contribution to the field.
