## Summary:

This paper introduces DeCodec, a novel neural audio codec designed to learn disentangled representations of speech and background sound, further decomposing speech into semantic and paralinguistic components. The core innovation lies in the use of a subspace orthogonal projection (SOP) module, which projects the input audio embedding into two orthogonal subspaces, one intended to capture speech-related information and the other background sound. This is further enhanced by a representation swap training (RST) procedure, which encourages the model to learn representations that are specific to either speech or background sound. Additionally, semantic guidance (SG) is applied to the speech branch to separate semantic and paralinguistic information. The authors evaluate DeCodec on several downstream tasks, including speech enhancement, one-shot voice conversion, and tasks related to speech recognition and synthesis. The experimental results demonstrate that DeCodec achieves promising performance, showing improvements over baseline models in speech enhancement and one-shot voice conversion. The paper argues that this approach offers a more efficient and effective way to handle complex audio scenarios compared to traditional cascaded pipelines that rely on separate speech separation and tokenization steps. The authors claim that DeCodec's ability to disentangle audio components at the representation level allows for more flexible feature selection and improved performance in downstream tasks. The proposed method is presented as a versatile front-end for various audio applications, offering a new perspective on audio codec design by focusing on disentangled representations rather than just signal reconstruction. The paper's main contribution is the introduction of a novel codec architecture that explicitly aims to disentangle audio components, which is a departure from traditional audio codecs that focus primarily on signal compression and reconstruction. The authors also introduce novel training techniques, such as representation swap training, to achieve this disentanglement. The experimental results, while promising, are limited in scope, and the paper lacks a detailed analysis of the computational complexity and real-time processing capabilities of DeCodec. Despite these limitations, the paper presents a significant step towards developing more versatile and efficient audio processing models.
