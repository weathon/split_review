## Summary:

This paper introduces Simulation-Calibrated Scientific Machine Learning (SCaSML), a novel framework designed to enhance the accuracy of pre-trained partial differential equation (PDE) solvers at inference time. The core idea behind SCaSML is to leverage a defect correction method that derives a new PDE, termed the Structural-preserving Law of Defect, which precisely describes the error of a given surrogate model. This defect PDE retains the structure of the original problem, allowing it to be solved efficiently using traditional stochastic simulation methods. The authors demonstrate that SCaSML achieves faster convergence rates and significantly reduces errors in high-dimensional PDEs, making it a promising approach for enhancing the reliability of AI in scientific discovery. The methodology involves first training a surrogate model, such as a neural network or Gaussian process, to approximate the solution of a target PDE. Then, at inference time, the framework derives the Structural-preserving Law of Defect, which governs the error of the surrogate model. This defect PDE is then solved using a Multilevel Picard (MLP) method, a stochastic simulation technique, to obtain a correction term that is added to the surrogate model's prediction. The paper presents a rigorous theoretical analysis of the convergence rate of SCaSML, demonstrating that it achieves a faster convergence rate compared to traditional methods. The empirical results, obtained from experiments on challenging high-dimensional PDEs, show that SCaSML significantly reduces approximation errors, often by 20-80%, compared to the surrogate models alone. The authors also provide a comparison with a naive Monte Carlo Langevin (MLP) solver, demonstrating the superiority of the proposed method. The significance of this work lies in its ability to systematically improve the accuracy of pre-trained PDE solvers without requiring retraining, offering a computationally efficient approach to enhance the reliability of AI in scientific applications. The framework's ability to handle high-dimensional problems and achieve significant error reduction makes it a valuable contribution to the field of scientific machine learning. The paper's focus on semi-linear parabolic PDEs, while a significant class, also highlights the need for further investigation into the applicability of SCaSML to other types of PDEs. Overall, the paper presents a well-structured and clearly written account of a novel and promising framework for improving the accuracy of PDE solvers, with strong theoretical backing and compelling empirical results.
