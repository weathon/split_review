## Summary:

This paper introduces EGG-SR, a novel framework designed to enhance symbolic regression by incorporating symbolic equivalence through equality graphs (e-graphs). The core idea is to represent mathematically equivalent expressions in a compact form using e-graphs, thereby reducing redundant computations and accelerating the search for optimal symbolic expressions. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of EGG-SR by integrating it with three distinct symbolic regression algorithms: Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL), and Large Language Models (LLMs). The methodology involves constructing e-graphs from generated expressions, applying rewrite rules to identify equivalent forms, and using these representations to guide the search process. For MCTS, the e-graph is used to prune redundant explorations of equivalent subtrees. In DRL, rewards are aggregated over equivalent generated sequences, and for LLMs, the feedback prompt is enriched with multiple equivalent expressions. The theoretical analysis provides strong justification for the proposed method, showing that EGG-MCTS achieves a tighter regret bound than standard MCTS, and EGG-DRL exhibits a lower variance in its gradient estimator. Empirically, the authors demonstrate that EGG-SR consistently outperforms baseline methods across multiple benchmarks, discovering more accurate expressions within the same time limits. The experiments show that EGG-MCTS, EGG-DRL, and EGG-LLM all achieve slight but consistent improvements over their respective baselines. The paper presents a unified approach to leveraging symbolic equivalence in symbolic regression, which is a significant contribution to the field. The use of e-graphs to represent and explore equivalent expressions is a clever and effective approach, and the theoretical analysis provides strong justification for the proposed method. The empirical results are comprehensive and convincing, demonstrating the practical effectiveness of the proposed framework. Overall, this paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of symbolic regression by introducing a novel and effective method for leveraging symbolic equivalence, which has the potential to significantly improve the efficiency and accuracy of symbolic regression algorithms.
