## Summary:

This paper introduces FEDSGM, a unified framework designed to tackle constrained federated learning (FL) problems. The core contribution of FEDSGM lies in its ability to simultaneously address four major challenges inherent in FL: functional constraints, communication bottlenecks, local updates, and partial client participation. The method builds upon the switching gradient method (SGM), extending its projection-free, primal-only approach to the federated setting. A key innovation is the incorporation of bidirectional error feedback to mitigate the bias introduced by compression, which is crucial for efficient communication. Furthermore, FEDSGM employs a soft switching mechanism, which stabilizes updates near the feasibility boundary, enhancing the robustness of the algorithm. The authors provide theoretical convergence guarantees for FEDSGM, demonstrating its ability to converge under convexity assumptions. Empirically, the framework is validated through experiments on Neyman-Pearson classification and constrained Markov decision process tasks, showcasing its effectiveness in practice. The paper's significance stems from its unified approach to constrained FL, offering a theoretically grounded and practically viable solution to a complex problem. By addressing multiple challenges simultaneously, FEDSGM represents a notable advancement in the field, providing a foundation for future research and development in constrained federated learning. The authors' approach of combining SGM with error feedback and soft switching is a novel contribution, offering a unique perspective on how to handle constrained optimization in federated settings. The theoretical analysis, while relying on convexity assumptions, provides a solid foundation for understanding the algorithm's behavior. The empirical results, though limited in scope, demonstrate the potential of FEDSGM in practical applications. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and technically sound approach to constrained federated learning, making a valuable contribution to the field.

While the authors present results on two tasks, the baselines are limited to a centralized version of the algorithm and variations of FEDSGM itself (hard vs. soft switching). Specifically, the paper lacks comparisons with other established constrained federated learning methods, such as projection-based approaches or those that incorporate dual variable updates. This omission makes it difficult to assess the relative performance of FEDSGM compared to the state-of-the-art. For example, the paper mentions related work in the introduction, including projection-based approaches and AL/ADMM-type methods, but does not provide any experimental comparisons with these methods. This is a significant weakness because it leaves the reader unsure of how FEDSGM performs in comparison to existing solutions. The experimental evaluation is also limited by the datasets used. The paper uses the breast cancer dataset for Neyman-Pearson classification and the continuous Cartpole environment for constrained Markov decision processes.
