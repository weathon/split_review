## Summary:

This paper introduces AUTO-RT, a novel reinforcement learning framework designed for automatic jailbreak strategy exploration in large language models (LLMs). The core contribution of this work lies in its approach to discovering diverse and effective prompts that can bypass the safety restrictions of LLMs. AUTO-RT frames jailbreak prompt construction as a sequential decision problem, enabling strategy-level exploration beyond static, handcrafted prompts. The framework incorporates two key techniques to enhance its performance: Dynamic Strategy Pruning (DSP) and Progressive Reward Tracking (PRT). DSP focuses exploration on high-potential strategies by eliminating redundant paths early, thereby improving efficiency. PRT, on the other hand, leverages intermediate downgrade models and a novel First Inverse Rate (FIR) metric to smooth sparse rewards and guide learning. The authors conducted extensive experiments across a variety of white-box and black-box LLM settings, demonstrating that AUTO-RT significantly improves success rates, expands vulnerability coverage, and accelerates discovery compared to existing methods. Specifically, the experiments involved 18 LLMs from different model families, including Llama, Mistral, Yi, Zephyr, Gemma, and Qwen. The evaluation was performed using a standard subset of the Harmbench dataset, with the toxicity intents partitioned into two halves for training and testing. Additionally, a subset of AdvBench was used to construct downgrade models. The results show that AUTO-RT outperforms several baselines, including few-shot learning, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning methods. The paper also includes experiments in a black-box setting, using in-context learning to obtain downgrade models when direct toxic fine-tuning is not feasible. The authors further evaluate the defense generalization diversity (DeD) of AUTO-RT, showing its ability to generate diverse attacks that can bypass different types of defenses. Overall, this paper presents a significant advancement in the field of automatic red-teaming, offering a more efficient and effective approach to identifying vulnerabilities in LLMs. The proposed framework and techniques have the potential to significantly impact the development of more robust and secure LLMs.

## Strengths:

This allows for a more dynamic and adaptive exploration of the attack space, leading to the discovery of more diverse and effective jailbreak strategies. The introduction of Dynamic Strategy Pruning (DSP) and Progressive Reward Tracking (PRT) are also notable technical innovations. DSP effectively improves the efficiency of the exploration process by eliminating redundant paths early on, focusing the search on high-potential strategies. PRT addresses the challenge of sparse rewards by leveraging intermediate downgrade models and the First Inverse Rate (FIR) metric, which guides the learning process and accelerates convergence. The experimental results presented in the paper are comprehensive and compelling. The authors demonstrate that AUTO-RT significantly outperforms existing methods across a wide range of LLMs, including both open-source and closed-source models. The experiments cover various settings, including white-box and black-box scenarios, and the results consistently show that AUTO-RT achieves higher success rates, expands vulnerability coverage, and accelerates discovery. The inclusion of experiments on R2D2, a model with targeted defenses, and the evaluation of defense generalization diversity (DeD) further strengthen the empirical findings, demonstrating the robustness of the proposed method.
