## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel approach to enhancing the safety alignment of large language models (LLMs) by incorporating reasoning into the alignment process. The authors begin by conducting a causal intervention, deactivating neurons critical for reasoning, and demonstrating that current alignment mechanisms operate independently of deep reasoning. This finding supports their hypothesis that existing safety alignments are superficial and do not rely on genuine reasoning. To address this, they propose a reasoning-aware post-training approach using a novel Chain-of-Thought (CoT) fine-tuning dataset. This dataset pairs harmful and safe prompts with detailed reasoning traces and corresponding responses, encouraging models to produce principled refusals grounded in reasoning. Furthermore, the authors introduce Alignment-Weighted Direct Preference Optimization (AW-DPO), an extension of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). AW-DPO decomposes each response into reasoning and response segments, assigning distinct preference weights to each based on their safety implications. This allows for finer-grained, targeted optimization compared to standard DPO. The paper presents extensive experiments across multiple safety and utility benchmarks, demonstrating that AW-DPO consistently improves safety alignment without significantly compromising model utility. The method outperforms strong baselines, including standard DPO and other alignment techniques. The authors argue that enhancing alignment through reasoning-aware post-training can lead to more robust safety mechanisms in LLMs, making them more resilient to jailbreak attacks and other adversarial prompts. The core contribution lies in the identification of the superficial nature of current alignment methods, the creation of a novel CoT dataset for safety, and the introduction of AW-DPO, which allows for more targeted and effective alignment by considering the reasoning process. The empirical results consistently demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach in improving safety alignment while maintaining model utility, highlighting the importance of reasoning in the alignment process.

## Strengths:

This paper presents several notable strengths that contribute to its overall significance. First, the authors' causal intervention analysis, using neuron deactivation to demonstrate the independence of current alignment from reasoning, is a novel approach that provides valuable insights into the limitations of existing methods. This finding is crucial as it highlights the superficial nature of current safety alignment techniques and motivates the need for reasoning-aware approaches. Second, the construction of a CoT dataset specifically for safety alignment is another significant contribution. This dataset addresses the need for models to ground their refusals in reasoning rather than superficial patterns, and it provides a valuable resource for future research in this area. The authors' approach to generating this dataset, combining safety-focused and general-purpose CoT data, is a practical and effective method. Third, the introduction of Alignment-Weighted DPO (AW-DPO) is an innovative extension of DPO that targets the reasoning and response segments separately, allowing for more fine-grained optimization. This method demonstrates a creative approach to addressing the problem of superficial alignment in LLMs. The experimental results are also a significant strength. The authors conduct extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks and compare their method with various baselines, consistently showing that their approach improves safety alignment without significantly compromising model utility. The paper is also well-structured and clearly explains the motivation, methodology, and experimental results. The use of figures and tables effectively illustrates the findings, making the paper easy to follow. Finally, the authors provide detailed appendices with additional information on the datasets, implementation details, and ablation studies, which further strengthens the quality of the research. The combination of a novel causal analysis, a valuable dataset, an innovative method, and thorough empirical validation makes this paper a strong contribution to the field of LLM safety alignment.
