## Summary:

This paper introduces GenCoGS, a novel few-shot novel view synthesis (NVS) method leveraging 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). The core contribution lies in its unified approach, integrating generative models for both scene initialization and optimization. The method proposes two main strategies: a generative point cloud completion-based Gaussian initialization (GCGI) and a generative pseudo-view completion-based Gaussian optimization (GCGO). The GCGI strategy employs a complementary point generation (CPG) module, followed by a filtering process using a point autoencoder, to enhance the initial sparse point cloud obtained from the input views. This enhanced point cloud is then used to initialize the 3D Gaussians. The GCGO strategy utilizes an image-to-video (I2V) diffusion model to synthesize pseudo-views from novel perspectives, which are then used to further optimize the 3DGS representation. This optimization is guided by a generative consistency loss, aiming to mitigate hallucinations and improve the quality of the synthesized views, particularly in unobserved regions. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their method through extensive experiments on three benchmark datasets: LLFF, DTU, and Shiny. The results show that GenCoGS achieves state-of-the-art performance compared to existing few-shot NVS methods, particularly in terms of scene completion and the reduction of artifacts. The paper emphasizes the unified nature of its approach, integrating generative models for both initialization and optimization, and highlights the effectiveness of the proposed generative consistency loss in mitigating hallucinations. The method aims to address the limitations of existing few-shot NVS methods by providing a more complete and coherent scene representation, leading to improved novel view synthesis. The authors also provide ablation studies to validate the effectiveness of the individual components of their method. Overall, the paper presents a promising approach to few-shot NVS, combining the strengths of 3DGS with generative models to achieve high-quality results.

By generating complementary points and filtering them using a point autoencoder, the method is able to create a more complete and accurate initial 3DGS representation. This is a significant improvement over methods that rely solely on the limited information from the input views.
