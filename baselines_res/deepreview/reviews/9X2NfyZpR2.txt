## Summary:

This paper introduces TBLTA, a novel weakly-supervised approach for Long-Term Action Anticipation (LTA) in videos, leveraging only video transcripts for training. The core idea is to use these transcripts to enhance video representations and provide a form of global supervision, circumventing the need for dense, frame-level annotations. The proposed model employs a modular architecture, including a transformer encoder, a temporal alignment module, a segmentation head, and an anticipation decoder. A key component is the cross-modal attention mechanism, which fuses transcript information with video features. The temporal alignment module generates pseudo-labels, which are then used to supervise both action segmentation and anticipation tasks. The method is evaluated on three benchmark datasets: Breakfast, 50Salads, and EGTEA Gaze+, demonstrating competitive performance compared to other weakly-supervised methods, though it generally underperforms relative to fully supervised approaches. The paper claims to be the first to address LTA under weak supervision using only video transcripts, which is a significant contribution to the field, even if the individual components of the model are not entirely novel. The authors present ablation studies to validate the effectiveness of the proposed components, but these studies are limited in scope, and the model's complexity raises concerns about its practical applicability. Overall, the paper presents a promising direction for weakly-supervised LTA, but there are several areas that require further investigation and improvement.
