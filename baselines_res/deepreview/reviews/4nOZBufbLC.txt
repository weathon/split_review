## Summary:

This paper introduces Count Bridges, a novel generative model for count data based on a stochastic bridge process on the integers. The core idea is to construct a tractable and exact analogue of diffusion-style models, which are typically applied to continuous data, for the discrete domain of count data. The authors achieve this by leveraging Poisson birth-death dynamics to define a bridge process with closed-form conditional distributions. This allows for efficient training and sampling, a significant advantage over many existing discrete generative models. The framework is further extended to handle aggregated measurements through an Expectation-Maximization (EM) approach, where unit-level counts are treated as latent variables. This is particularly relevant for applications where data is collected as sums or averages of individual counts. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of Count Bridges on several synthetic benchmarks, including a discrete version of the 8-Gaussians to 2-Moons problem and a low-rank Gaussian mixture scaling experiment. They also apply the method to two large-scale biological problems: deconvolving bulk RNA-seq data into single-cell gene expression profiles and resolving multicellular spatial transcriptomic spots into single-cell count profiles. The empirical results show that Count Bridges achieves state-of-the-art performance on these tasks, outperforming existing methods in terms of metrics such as Wasserstein-2 distance, Energy score, and MMD for synthetic data, and JSD, RMSE, and Spearman correlation for biological data. The paper's main contribution lies in the development of a theoretically sound and practically useful framework for modeling count data, offering a promising alternative to existing methods. The use of a stochastic bridge process with closed-form conditionals is a significant technical innovation, and the extension to aggregated measurements via EM further enhances the method's applicability. The empirical results, while promising, also highlight the need for further investigation into the method's robustness and limitations, particularly in scenarios where the underlying assumptions are violated. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of count data modeling, with potential impact in various domains, especially in biology.
