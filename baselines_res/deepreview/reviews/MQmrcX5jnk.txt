## Summary:

The paper introduces a novel variational framework called Constrained Mass Transport (CMT) for sampling from high-dimensional and multimodal unnormalized probability distributions, with a particular focus on Boltzmann generators (BGs) for physical systems. CMT addresses the limitations of traditional variational approaches, which often suffer from mode collapse, and annealing-based methods, which can experience mass teleportation and require extensive schedule tuning. The framework enhances distributional overlap and mitigates these issues by imposing constraints on both the KL divergence and entropy decay between successive steps. The authors demonstrate that CMT consistently outperforms state-of-the-art variational methods across standard BG benchmarks and a newly introduced benchmark, the ELIL tetrapeptide, achieving more than 2.5 times higher effective sample size without mode collapse. The paper is well-written and provides a rigorous mathematical formulation, but it could benefit from a more detailed analysis of computational costs, a broader exploration of applicability to other domains, and a more comprehensive comparison with existing methods, particularly diffusion-based models. Additionally, the paper lacks a thorough discussion of the method's limitations and the impact of hyperparameters, which would provide a more balanced view of its practical utility.

## Strengths:

I find the paper to be well-written and easy to follow, which is a significant strength. The authors clearly articulate the problem of sampling from high-dimensional and multimodal unnormalized probability distributions, a challenge that is central to many areas of science and machine learning. The introduction of Constrained Mass Transport (CMT) is a novel and original contribution, as it combines trust-region and entropy constraints to improve the sampling of Boltzmann generators (BGs). This approach is theoretically sound, and the paper provides a rigorous mathematical formulation, including the derivation of intermediate densities and the Lagrangian formalism used to solve the constrained optimization problems. The experimental setup is well-documented, and the results are presented in a clear and concise manner, supported by visualizations and detailed performance metrics. The proposed method has significant implications for molecular sampling, particularly in the context of BGs, by achieving higher effective sample sizes and avoiding mode collapse. The introduction of the ELIL tetrapeptide as a new benchmark is a valuable addition to the field, as it represents a larger and more complex system studied without access to samples from molecular dynamics. The ablation study on the effect of the constraints further strengthens the paper by verifying the importance of both the trust-region and entropy constraints in the performance of CMT.
