## Summary:

This paper introduces an information-theoretic framework designed to distinguish between two prominent hypotheses of probabilistic neural coding: likelihood coding, where sensory neurons encode the likelihood function of an external stimulus, and posterior coding, where neurons encode the posterior distribution, incorporating prior knowledge. The authors propose a novel approach based on evaluating the performance of decoding algorithms trained to reconstruct either the likelihood or the posterior from neural responses. The core idea is that the difference in performance between these two decoding approaches can be quantified by an "information gap," which serves as a metric to identify the correct neural coding hypothesis. This information gap is derived analytically, and the authors demonstrate how it can be used to optimize stimulus distributions to maximize the discriminability between the two coding hypotheses. The framework is validated through extensive simulations, where the information gap is shown to accurately predict decoder performance differences. Furthermore, the authors apply their framework to existing neurophysiology datasets, highlighting the importance of context-dependent prior manipulations in experiments aimed at distinguishing between these coding schemes. The paper's key contribution lies in providing a principled, quantitative method for addressing a fundamental question in neuroscience: how do sensory neurons represent uncertainty? By framing the problem within an information-theoretic context, the authors offer a powerful tool for designing experiments that can effectively differentiate between likelihood and posterior coding. The authors derive analytic expressions for the information gap, providing a theoretical foundation for their approach. This would allow the framework to be applied in situations where the true likelihood is unknown or difficult to estimate. Additionally, the framework could be extended to incorporate methods for learning the likelihood function from data, which would reduce the reliance on pre-specified models.
