## Summary:

This paper introduces CausalNovo, a model-agnostic framework designed to enhance de novo peptide sequencing by explicitly addressing the challenges posed by noisy spectral data. The core idea revolves around learning causal representations of mass spectra, moving beyond the superficial correlations that traditional deep learning models often capture. The authors propose a Causality Extraction Module (CEM) that employs information-theoretic objectives grounded in the principles of independence and sufficiency to disentangle causal factors from noise. This module is designed to be integrated into existing peptide sequencing models, aiming to improve their robustness and accuracy. The framework's methodology involves identifying and perturbing non-causal peaks in the mass spectra during training, thereby forcing the model to focus on the true signal fragment ions. The authors evaluate CausalNovo by integrating it with three established *de novo* sequencing models: CasaNovo, AdaNovo, and π-HelixNovo. They conduct comprehensive experiments on three public datasets: Nine-species, Seven-species, and HC-PT. The empirical results demonstrate that CausalNovo consistently improves performance across various metrics, including amino acid, peptide, and post-translational modification (PTM) levels. The framework is shown to be effective across different noise levels and datasets, indicating its robustness and generalizability. The authors also perform ablation studies and vulnerability analyses to further validate the claims of the paper. The paper's significance lies in its novel approach to *de novo* peptide sequencing by integrating causal inference principles into the model training process. This is a significant departure from traditional statistical methods that often rely on superficial correlations, making the work innovative and potentially impactful. By focusing on causal representations, the authors aim to address the limitations of existing deep learning models that are prone to spurious associations and poor generalization in noisy environments. The paper's contribution is not only in the proposed framework but also in the comprehensive experimental evaluation that demonstrates its effectiveness and robustness. The authors also provide a detailed explanation of the motivation, methodology, and results, making the technical contributions accessible to readers with a background in computational proteomics and machine learning. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and rigorously evaluated framework that has the potential to significantly advance the field of *de novo* peptide sequencing.
