## Summary:

This paper delves into the theoretical underpinnings of separable neural networks (SepNNs), a class of neural networks that factorize a multivariate function into a composition of univariate functions, offering potential computational advantages, particularly in high-dimensional settings. The core contributions of this work are threefold. First, the authors establish a universal approximation theorem for SepNNs, demonstrating their capacity to approximate any continuous multivariate function with arbitrary precision. This is a foundational result that validates the use of SepNNs as general function approximators. Second, the paper derives the neural tangent kernel (NTK) regimes for SepNNs under various asymptotic conditions, showing that the NTK converges to a deterministic kernel when both the network width and the decomposition rank tend to infinity, and to a random kernel when the width tends to infinity while the rank is fixed. These findings provide insights into the training dynamics of SepNNs in the infinite-width limit. Finally, the authors propose a novel optimization method called separable preconditioned gradient descent (SepPGD), which is designed to mitigate the spectral bias inherent in SepNNs by adjusting the eigenvalue distribution of the NTK matrix. This method leverages the separable structure of SepNNs to achieve computational efficiency. The paper also includes a series of experiments on various tasks, including kernel ridge regression, image and surface representation, and solving partial differential equations using physics-informed neural networks (PINNs). These experiments aim to validate the theoretical findings and demonstrate the practical effectiveness of the proposed SepPGD method. Overall, the paper makes a significant contribution to the theoretical understanding of SepNNs and provides a practical optimization technique that addresses some of their limitations. However, the work also has some limitations, particularly in the scope of its experimental validation and the depth of its discussion on practical implications.
