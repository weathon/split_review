## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel approach to differentially private (DP) data synthesis, termed SPS (Summarize-Privatize-Synthesize), which leverages dataset distillation techniques to generate compact, high-utility synthetic datasets. The core idea revolves around using a publicly pre-trained model to extract intermediate activation statistics from the original data, which are then privatized using the Gaussian mechanism. These privatized statistics serve as a guide for synthesizing new data that mimics the properties of the original dataset while adhering to DP constraints. The authors further enhance SPS with multitask clipping and grouped pseudo-classes, resulting in the SPS+ variant, which demonstrates improved performance, particularly in high-privacy regimes. The method is evaluated on image classification tasks using CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and CAMELYON17 datasets, showing that SPS+ can achieve comparable or even superior accuracy to traditional DP-SGD methods. Furthermore, the paper highlights the practical advantages of data-based privacy, demonstrating that the synthesized data can be used for downstream tasks like model ensembling, federated learning, and continual learning without incurring additional privacy costs. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of the privacy guarantees of their method, including detailed proofs for the RDP composition. The key innovation lies in adapting the D3S dataset-distillation framework to work with public pre-trained models while privatizing intermediate activation statistics. This approach allows for the generation of synthetic data that retains the utility of the original data while ensuring privacy, offering a promising alternative to traditional DP-SGD methods for training models on sensitive data. The paper's findings suggest that SPS+ can be a valuable tool for privacy-preserving machine learning, particularly in scenarios where data sharing is restricted or where multiple parties need to collaborate on model training. The authors also demonstrate the method's ability to handle domain shifts, showing its potential for real-world applications where the training and deployment data may differ. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of differentially private data synthesis, offering a novel and effective approach for generating high-quality synthetic data while preserving privacy.
