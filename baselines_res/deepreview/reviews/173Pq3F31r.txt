## Summary:

This paper introduces a unified framework for expressive performance rendering (EPR) and automatic piano transcription (APT), leveraging a transformer-based sequence-to-sequence (Seq2Seq) architecture. The core idea is to disentangle note-level score content from global performance style representations, enabling bidirectional modeling between symbolic and expressive musical forms. The authors propose a novel Performance Style Recommendation (PSR) module, which uses a diffusion-based model to generate style embeddings directly from score content. This module aims to mimic a pianist's ability to infer appropriate expressive styles from a written score, facilitating controllable rendering and style-aware performance. The framework is trained on both paired and unpaired data, using sequence-aligned data without requiring fine-grained note-level alignment, which enhances scalability and efficiency. The experimental results demonstrate competitive performance on both EPR and APT tasks, with the model achieving effective content-style disentanglement, reliable style transfer, and stylistically appropriate rendering. The authors evaluate their approach using the ASAP dataset for paired training and evaluation, and the ATEPP dataset for out-of-distribution (OOD) evaluation of disentangled representations. The paper presents a novel approach to jointly modeling EPR and APT, which have traditionally been addressed independently, and the results suggest that this joint approach allows for information sharing and mutual supervision between the two tasks, leading to improved performance. The PSR module is a key contribution, enabling automatic style inference and controllable rendering, making the system more flexible and user-friendly. The framework's ability to be trained using a combination of sequence-aligned and unpaired data, without the need for fine-grained note-level alignment, makes the training process more scalable and efficient. The authors also provide subjective evaluations to support the effectiveness of the proposed method, particularly in terms of the naturalness and stylistic appropriateness of the generated performances. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of music information retrieval, offering a promising approach for joint modeling of expressive performance and transcription.
