## Summary:

This paper introduces MolMiner, a novel fragment-based, geometry-aware, and order-agnostic autoregressive model for molecular design. The core contribution of MolMiner lies in its ability to generate molecules conditionally, based on a subset of target properties, while simultaneously sampling the remaining properties. This is achieved through a unique approach that combines symmetry-aware fragment attachments with dynamic 3D geometry updates using force fields. The model employs a probabilistic conditioning mechanism, allowing users to specify any combination of target properties, which are then used to guide the molecular generation process. The authors also propose improved benchmarking methods for both unconditional and conditional generation, including distributional comparisons via Wasserstein distance and calibration plots for property control. The model's architecture is based on a decoder-only transformer, which processes a sequence of fragment tokens, incorporating local chemical context and spatial information. The training process involves maximizing the log-likelihood of each molecule under an order-agnostic rollout factorization, conditioned on target properties. The authors demonstrate that MolMiner achieves calibrated conditional generation across most properties and offers competitive unconditional performance. The model is trained on a dataset of approximately 200,000 drug-like molecules from the ZINC database, with 12 properties computed using RDKit. The authors also conduct ablation studies to confirm the importance of various design choices, such as the geometry-aware attention mechanism and the order-agnostic rollout strategy. The paper presents a comprehensive approach to molecular generation, integrating several innovative techniques into a single framework. While the model shows promise, particularly in its ability to handle conditional generation, it also exhibits some limitations, especially in unconditional generation and control accuracy for certain properties. The authors acknowledge these limitations and suggest potential avenues for future research, highlighting the need for further improvements in the model's ability to generate molecules with specific properties and to address potential data imbalance issues. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of molecular design, offering a flexible and powerful tool for generating molecules with desired characteristics.

## Strengths:

I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. First, the introduction of MolMiner as a fragment-based, geometry-aware, and order-agnostic autoregressive model for molecular design is a significant contribution. The combination of these features distinguishes MolMiner from existing models and provides a novel approach to molecular generation. The use of symmetry-aware fragment attachments ensures that the generated molecules are chemically valid, while the dynamic 3D geometry updates using force fields allow the model to capture the spatial relationships between atoms. The order-agnostic rollout strategy, which allows the model to grow molecules from any starting fragment in any valid order, is another innovative aspect that enhances the model's flexibility and robustness. Second, the model's ability to support multi-property conditional generation is a major strength. The probabilistic conditioning mechanism, which allows users to specify any subset of target properties while sampling the rest, provides a high degree of flexibility and control. This is particularly valuable for practical molecular design tasks, where researchers often need to optimize multiple properties simultaneously. The authors demonstrate that MolMiner achieves calibrated conditional generation across most properties, indicating that the model can accurately generate molecules that meet the specified criteria. Third, the authors' proposal of improved benchmarking methods for both unconditional and conditional generation is a valuable contribution to the field. The use of distributional comparisons via Wasserstein distance and calibration plots for property control provides a more comprehensive and rigorous evaluation of generative models. These methods allow for a more nuanced understanding of the model's performance and its ability to generate molecules with desired properties. Finally, the paper is well-written and easy to follow, with a clear and detailed description of the proposed method. The figures and tables are informative and effectively illustrate the key concepts and results. The authors also provide a thorough discussion of the limitations of the proposed method and potential directions for future research, which demonstrates a balanced and critical approach to their work.


## Weaknesses:

Despite the strengths of the proposed model, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant further discussion. First, the paper's comparison to other state-of-the-art models is limited. While the authors justify their choice of HierVAE as a baseline for unconditional generation, the lack of a direct quantitative comparison with more recent models, such as MoLeR, is a significant limitation. Although the authors experimented with MoLeR, they excluded it from the main quantitative comparisons due to issues with sampling from its latent prior. This decision, while understandable, leaves a gap in the evaluation of MolMiner's relative performance. The absence of a comparison with models that utilize different generation strategies, such as diffusion models or graph-based methods, further limits the contextualization of MolMiner's performance. This lack of a comprehensive benchmark makes it difficult to fully assess the model's strengths and weaknesses relative to the broader landscape of molecular generation models. Second, MolMiner's performance in unconditional generation is not as strong as HierVAE, particularly for certain properties. The authors acknowledge that their model underperforms in unconditional generation for molecular weight, MR, and TPSA. This underperformance is a significant limitation, especially given the complexity introduced by the fragment-based approach and dynamic geometry updates. The authors suggest that this underperformance may be partly attributable to approximation error in GMM-based conditioning, but a more detailed analysis of the training data and the model's learning dynamics is needed to fully understand the reasons behind this issue. The lack of a clear advantage in unconditional generation raises questions about the model's overall effectiveness, especially given the complexity of the proposed approach. Third, the model's performance on QED during conditional generation is not as good as other models, and the authors note that the control accuracy degrades for this property. This suggests that the model may have limitations in its ability to accurately control certain molecular properties, particularly those that are highly sensitive to subtle structural changes. The authors do not provide a detailed investigation into the reasons behind this issue, which is a significant oversight. The lack of a thorough analysis of the model's predictions and the relationship between the input conditions and the generated molecules makes it difficult to pinpoint the specific factors that contribute to the degradation in control accuracy. Fourth, the paper lacks a detailed analysis of the computational cost of MolMiner. While the authors provide some information about training time, GPU usage, and batch size, a more detailed breakdown of time spent on different stages of the pipeline, such as fragment selection, geometry optimization, and property calculation, is missing. The absence of specific details regarding the hardware used for training and inference further hinders a comprehensive evaluation of the method's efficiency. This lack of information makes it difficult to assess the practicality of the method, especially for large-scale molecular design tasks. Fifth, the paper does not explore the potential of MolMiner for generating molecules with specific biological activities or binding affinities. The current evaluation focuses on physicochemical properties, but the ultimate goal of molecular design is often to find molecules with specific biological effects. The absence of an assessment of the model's ability to generate molecules with specific biological activities is a significant limitation. Finally, the paper does not discuss the potential ethical implications of using MolMiner for molecular design. The ability to generate novel molecules raises concerns about the potential misuse of this technology. The absence of a discussion about the safeguards that are in place to prevent the generation of harmful molecules and the potential risks associated with the technology is a significant oversight. These weaknesses, taken together, highlight the need for further research and development to fully realize the potential of MolMiner.
