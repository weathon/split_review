## Summary:

This paper presents a rigorous empirical investigation into the optimization dynamics of State-Space Models (SSMs), specifically Mamba, in comparison to Transformers, focusing on associative recall and copying tasks. The authors meticulously explore the performance of these models across a range of learning rates, model sizes, and architectural configurations, using over 3,000 experimental runs and 20,000 GPU hours. The core finding is that SSMs, unlike Transformers, exhibit a high sensitivity to learning rate selection, demonstrating significant performance variations across a narrow range of learning rates. This optimization instability is a key differentiator, suggesting that the performance gap between SSMs and Transformers on these tasks may stem from optimization challenges rather than inherent expressivity limitations. The study reveals that SSMs benefit from increased width, while Transformers struggle in single-layer configurations, further highlighting the distinct optimization behaviors of these architectures. Through ablation studies, the authors identify the convolutional component as crucial for Mamba's expressivity and show that newer architectures like DeltaNet can enhance stability. The paper underscores the importance of considering optimization dynamics when comparing sequence models and suggests that careful hyperparameter tuning and scaling strategies are essential for achieving optimal performance with SSMs. The authors challenge the notion that SSMs are inherently less capable than Transformers on associative tasks, demonstrating that with proper tuning, SSMs can achieve near-Transformer performance. This work contributes significantly to our understanding of the practical differences between SSMs and Transformers, emphasizing the need for careful hyperparameter tuning and scaling strategies. The study's findings have implications for both theoretical analysis and practical applications of sequence models, highlighting the importance of considering optimization dynamics when comparing different architectures. The authors' extensive experimentation and detailed analysis provide a valuable contribution to the field, offering insights into the optimization challenges and potential of SSMs. The paper's focus on synthetic benchmarks allows for controlled experimentation, but also raises questions about the generalizability of the findings to real-world language modeling tasks. Overall, this paper provides a comprehensive empirical comparison of SSMs and Transformers, revealing critical optimization instabilities in SSMs and highlighting the need for careful hyperparameter tuning and scaling strategies. The authors' work challenges existing assumptions about the capabilities of SSMs and provides valuable insights into their optimization dynamics, making a significant contribution to the field of sequence modeling.

## Strengths:

This paper's primary strength lies in its comprehensive empirical approach to comparing the optimization dynamics of SSMs and Transformers. The authors have undertaken a substantial experimental effort, involving over 3,000 runs and 20,000 GPU hours, to meticulously analyze the performance of these models on associative recall and copying tasks. This level of detail provides a robust foundation for their findings. The paper effectively challenges the prevailing notion that SSMs are inherently less capable than Transformers on associative tasks, demonstrating that with proper tuning, SSMs can achieve comparable performance. This is a significant contribution, as it suggests that the performance gap often observed may be more attributable to optimization challenges than to fundamental limitations in expressivity. The identification of critical optimization instabilities in SSMs, particularly their sensitivity to learning rate selection, is a key insight. The authors clearly demonstrate that SSMs require careful hyperparameter tuning to achieve optimal performance, unlike Transformers, which exhibit more robustness to variations in learning rates. This finding has important implications for the practical application of SSMs. Furthermore, the ablation studies conducted in the paper are well-designed and informative. The authors effectively identify the convolutional component as crucial for Mamba's expressivity and show that newer architectures like DeltaNet can enhance stability. These architectural insights are valuable for understanding the factors that contribute to the performance and stability of SSMs. The paper's focus on scaling behaviors also provides important insights, revealing that SSMs benefit from increased width, while Transformers struggle in single-layer configurations. This highlights the distinct scaling properties of these architectures and suggests that different strategies may be needed to optimize their performance. The paper's clear presentation of results and well-structured analysis make it easy to follow and understand. The authors effectively use figures and tables to illustrate their findings, enhancing the clarity and impact of their work. Overall, the paper's strengths lie in its rigorous empirical approach, its challenge to existing assumptions, its identification of key optimization challenges, and its valuable architectural insights. The extensive experimentation and clear presentation of results make this a significant contribution to the field of sequence modeling.


## Weaknesses:

While this paper presents a valuable empirical analysis, several limitations warrant careful consideration. A primary concern is the paper's reliance on synthetic benchmarks for evaluating the performance of SSMs and Transformers. As the authors themselves acknowledge in the 'Discussion and Conclusions' section, their analysis is conducted on synthetic benchmarks highly correlated with in-context learning, and validating these dynamics on downstream language modeling tasks is a critical next step. The paper's experiments are exclusively focused on associative recall (MQAR) and copying tasks, which, while useful for controlled analysis, may not fully capture the complexities of real-world language modeling. This raises questions about the generalizability of the findings to more complex, natural language data. Specifically, the associative recall and copying tasks may not fully reflect the nuances of long-range dependencies and semantic understanding required in natural language processing. The paper does not include experiments on standard language modeling datasets like the Penn Treebank or WikiText-103, which would provide a more robust assessment of the generalizability of their findings. This lack of validation on real-world tasks is a significant limitation, as it is unclear whether the observed optimization dynamics and scaling behaviors would persist in more complex settings. The paper's focus on empirical comparisons, while thorough, is not complemented by a strong theoretical framework. The authors identify critical optimization instabilities in SSMs, demonstrating their sensitivity to learning rate selection, but they do not provide a theoretical explanation for these observed phenomena. The lack of theoretical grounding makes it difficult to understand the underlying mechanisms causing this sensitivity and whether these instabilities are inherent to the SSM architecture or a result of specific implementation choices. A theoretical analysis could provide insights into how to mitigate these issues. For example, an analysis of the loss landscape or the gradient flow could be beneficial. The paper does not delve into the mathematical properties of the different architectures and how they influence optimization landscapes. This absence of theoretical analysis limits the paper's ability to provide a deeper understanding of the observed optimization disparities. Furthermore, while the paper identifies the convolutional component as crucial for Mamba's expressivity, it does not fully explore the role of other architectural differences between SSMs and Transformers. The 'Architectural Drivers of Performance and Stability' section includes ablations on the convolutional component and the gating mechanism, but the analysis could be expanded to include a more detailed investigation of the impact of gating mechanisms, normalization layers, and different types of recurrent connections on the observed optimization dynamics. For instance, the effect of different activation functions or the depth of the convolutional layers could be investigated. The paper's analysis of architectural differences is limited to a few key components, and a more comprehensive exploration of other architectural variations could provide a more complete picture of the factors contributing to the performance differences. The paper also does not propose novel optimization algorithms or training techniques specifically designed to address the instability. While the authors explore architectural modifications, these are presented as investigations into the drivers of performance rather than concrete solutions to the instability. The paper primarily identifies and analyzes the problem, and while it explores architectural variations, these are more about understanding the problem than providing definitive solutions to the optimization instability beyond tuning. The mention of DeltaNet achieving robustness points towards architectural solutions, but the paper doesn't delve into creating new optimization methods. The paper's focus on empirical results rather than theoretical explanations limits its ability to provide a deeper understanding of the observed optimization instabilities. The paper does not provide a theoretical framework for understanding the observed optimization instabilities. The paper's methodology is primarily empirical, lacking a formal theoretical analysis of optimization dynamics. In summary, the paper's weaknesses stem from its reliance on synthetic benchmarks, lack of theoretical analysis, limited exploration of architectural differences, and the absence of concrete solutions to the identified optimization challenges. These limitations impact the generalizability of the findings and the depth of understanding of the underlying mechanisms.


## Suggestions:

To strengthen this paper, several key areas warrant further investigation. First and foremost, the authors should extend their analysis to include real-world language modeling tasks. This would involve evaluating the models on standard benchmarks such as the Penn Treebank or WikiText-103, which would provide a more robust assessment of the generalizability of their findings. Such experiments would help determine if the optimization instabilities observed in the synthetic tasks also manifest in more complex, natural language settings. Furthermore, it would be beneficial to explore the impact of different data preprocessing techniques and data augmentation strategies on the training dynamics of SSMs and Transformers. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of how these models behave under different data conditions. Additionally, the authors could investigate the effect of different optimization algorithms beyond standard SGD or Adam, as these might interact differently with the architectures. This would help determine if the observed optimization instabilities are specific to the chosen optimizer or are a more general property of SSMs. Second, the authors should attempt to provide a theoretical explanation for the observed optimization instabilities in SSMs. This could involve analyzing the loss landscape of these models and investigating the properties of the gradients during training. For example, examining the condition number of the Hessian matrix could provide insights into the curvature of the loss landscape and the sensitivity to learning rate changes. Furthermore, exploring the impact of different initialization schemes on the training dynamics could shed light on the origin of these instabilities. A theoretical analysis could also help identify potential regularization techniques that could mitigate these issues and improve the robustness of SSMs. This could involve techniques such as weight normalization or spectral normalization. Such a theoretical analysis would provide a deeper understanding of the underlying mechanisms causing the observed optimization instabilities and suggest potential avenues for improvement. Third, the authors should conduct a more detailed ablation study to investigate the impact of various architectural components on the performance and stability of SSMs. This could include varying the size and number of convolutional filters, exploring different gating mechanisms, and analyzing the effect of normalization layers. For example, comparing the performance of Mamba with and without layer normalization or with different types of recurrent connections could provide valuable insights into the role of these components. Furthermore, it would be beneficial to explore the impact of different activation functions on the training dynamics. Such a detailed analysis would help identify the key architectural factors contributing to the observed optimization instabilities and provide guidance for designing more robust and efficient SSMs. This would also help to understand the role of different architectural choices in the optimization dynamics of SSMs. Finally, the authors should explore techniques for improving the optimization stability of SSMs. This could involve investigating adaptive learning rate methods specifically tailored for SSMs, or exploring regularization strategies that could improve the robustness of these models during training. For example, they could investigate the use of weight decay, dropout, or other regularization methods that might mitigate the sensitivity to learning rate changes. Additionally, exploring different initialization schemes for SSM parameters could potentially lead to more stable training dynamics. Another avenue to explore is the use of curriculum learning, where the model is initially trained on simpler tasks before being exposed to more complex ones. This could help the model learn more robust representations and improve its generalization capabilities. By addressing these limitations, the authors can significantly strengthen their work and provide a more comprehensive understanding of the optimization dynamics of SSMs and Transformers.
