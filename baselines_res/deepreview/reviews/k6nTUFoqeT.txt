## Summary:

This paper introduces FASTer, a novel framework designed to enhance the efficiency and performance of vision-language-action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. The core of FASTer lies in its two main components: FASTerVQ, a learnable action tokenizer, and FASTerVLA, an autoregressive VLA model built upon FASTerVQ. FASTerVQ employs a unique approach to action tokenization, encoding action sequences as single-channel images to capture both local dynamics and global trends. This is achieved through an action patchifier that groups action sequences into semantically meaningful patches, followed by a transformer-based residual vector quantization (RVQ) tokenizer. This design allows for a high compression ratio while preserving fine-grained motion details. The RVQ process involves a series of codebooks that progressively refine the action representation, capturing both low-frequency and high-frequency components of the action sequence. FASTerVLA, on the other hand, leverages the tokenized action sequences generated by FASTerVQ. It employs block-wise autoregressive decoding, which allows for faster inference by generating multiple tokens in parallel within each block. Additionally, a lightweight action expert is incorporated to bridge the modality gap between linguistic reasoning and continuous control. This expert is a small neural network that processes the tokenized action sequences and generates the parameters for the action distribution. The paper presents extensive experimental results across a variety of simulated and real-world benchmarks. These experiments demonstrate that FASTerVQ achieves superior reconstruction quality, high token utilization, and strong generalization across different tasks and robot embodiments. Furthermore, FASTerVLA surpasses previous state-of-the-art models in both inference speed and task performance. The authors also establish a comprehensive benchmark covering four real robots and four simulated environments, providing a systematic analysis of action tokenization for VLAs. The paper's main contribution is the introduction of a novel and effective action tokenization method, FASTerVQ, that addresses the limitations of existing tokenization techniques in autoregressive VLA models. The combination of an action patchifier and RVQ achieves a superior balance between reconstruction fidelity and compression ratio. The authors also propose block-wise autoregressive decoding and a lightweight action expert, which significantly improve the inference speed and task performance of FASTerVLA. The paper is well-written and clearly explains the proposed method and its advantages. The authors provide detailed experimental setups and results, making it easy to understand and reproduce the findings. The paper addresses a critical challenge in autoregressive VLA models-the trade-off between reconstruction fidelity and inference efficiency. The proposed solution has practical implications for real-world robotic applications.
