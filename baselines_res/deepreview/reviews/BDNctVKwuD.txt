## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel approach to improve the interpretability of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) by addressing a key limitation in the widely used HiResCAM technique. The authors identify that HiResCAM explanations are not unique, allowing for arbitrary shifts that do not affect the model's predictions. To rectify this, they propose ContrastiveCAMs, which provide more consistent and class-specific explanations by contrasting the target class with other classes. Furthermore, they introduce Core-Focused Cross-Entropy (CFCE), a modified loss function that encourages the model to focus on core image regions, thereby reducing reliance on spurious features. The core idea behind CFCE is to penalize the model for attending to non-core regions, thus promoting feature alignment with the core object of interest. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their proposed methods through experiments on several datasets, including Hard-ImageNet, Oxford-IIIT Pets, and the Semantic Boundaries Dataset. The results show that models trained with CFCE and guided by ContrastiveCAMs exhibit improved interpretability, as evidenced by more focused attention maps, and enhanced generalization, as indicated by better performance under core region ablation. The paper's main contribution lies in its identification of a critical flaw in HiResCAM, the proposal of a more robust alternative in ContrastiveCAMs, and the development of a novel loss function, CFCE, that leverages these insights to improve model training. The empirical findings suggest that the proposed approach effectively enhances the interpretability of CNNs and promotes more robust feature learning by focusing on core image regions. The authors also demonstrate the method's applicability to multilabel classification tasks, further highlighting its versatility. Overall, this paper presents a significant contribution to the field of explainable AI by providing a practical and effective method for improving the interpretability and robustness of CNNs.
