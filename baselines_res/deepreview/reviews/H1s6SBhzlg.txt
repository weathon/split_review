## Summary:

This paper introduces two novel algorithms, Optimal Weight (OW) and Inverse Surprising Popularity (ISP), designed to enhance the aggregation of answers from multiple Large Language Models (LLMs). The core motivation stems from the limitations of traditional majority voting, which treats all LLM outputs equally, disregarding potential heterogeneity and correlations among models. OW leverages first-order information, specifically the accuracies of individual LLMs, to assign optimal weights to each model's prediction. ISP, on the other hand, utilizes second-order information, focusing on the correlations between different LLMs' answers, to identify the most reliable response. The authors provide a rigorous theoretical analysis demonstrating that these methods can outperform majority voting under mild assumptions. They further validate their approach through empirical evaluations on both synthetic datasets and real-world benchmarks, including UltraFeedback, MMLU, and ARMMAN. The empirical results consistently show that OW and ISP achieve superior performance compared to majority voting. The paper's key contributions lie in the development of these novel aggregation algorithms, their theoretical justification, and their empirical validation across diverse datasets. The authors also explore practical adaptations of OW, such as OW-L and OW-I, which estimate model accuracies without relying on ground truth labels. This work is significant because it addresses a critical challenge in multi-agent LLM reasoning, offering a more sophisticated approach to combining LLM outputs than simple majority voting. By leveraging higher-order information, the proposed methods offer a pathway to more robust and accurate multi-agent LLM systems. The paper's exploration of both theoretical underpinnings and practical applications makes it a valuable contribution to the field of multi-agent learning and LLM aggregation.
