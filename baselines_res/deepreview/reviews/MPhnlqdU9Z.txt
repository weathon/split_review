## Summary:

This paper introduces the concept of 'monitorability' as a fundamental property of neural networks, defining it as the intrinsic ability of a model to detect potential inference errors through its internal activations. This is a critical area of research, as it addresses the need for more reliable and trustworthy AI systems, particularly in safety-critical applications. The authors propose a novel metric, the Monitorability via Input perturbation (MIRA) score, to quantify this property. The MIRA score is computed by applying norm-bounded input perturbations to in-distribution data and then measuring the separability of the resulting internal representations using Mahalanobis distance. The core idea is that a model with high monitorability will exhibit significant changes in its internal activations when presented with perturbed inputs, making these changes detectable. The authors validate their proposed metric by comparing it against the best achievable out-of-distribution (OoD) detection performance across three representative methods. The empirical results, spanning multiple architectures, datasets, and data modalities, demonstrate a strong correlation between the MIRA score and the actual OoD detection performance. This correlation suggests that the MIRA score can serve as a reliable tool for evaluating and comparing the monitorability of different models. The paper's main contribution lies in formalizing the concept of monitorability and providing a practical, efficient metric that can be computed pre-deployment using only in-distribution data. This is particularly valuable because it avoids the need for external OoD data or detector-specific tuning, making it applicable in real-world scenarios where such resources might be limited. The authors also show that the MIRA score can be used to identify models that are more likely to detect potential inference errors, which is a significant step towards building more robust and reliable AI systems. The paper's approach of using internal activations to assess model reliability is a promising direction, and the empirical results provide a solid foundation for further research in this area. Overall, the paper makes a significant contribution to the field of neural network monitoring and reliability by introducing a novel concept, a practical metric, and empirical evidence of its effectiveness.
