## Summary:

This paper introduces UniHM, a novel framework designed for generating dexterous hand manipulation trajectories directly from open-vocabulary language instructions. The core contribution of this work lies in its unified approach, integrating three key components: a Unified Hand-Dexterous Tokenizer, a vision language action model, and a physics-guided dynamic refinement module. The tokenizer effectively maps diverse hand morphologies into a shared codebook, enabling the model to learn a common representation across different hand types. The vision language action model, trained on human-object interaction data, generates manipulation sequences conditioned on the provided language instructions. Finally, the physics-guided dynamic refinement module ensures that the generated trajectories are physically realistic and feasible by optimizing for contact and temporal consistency. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach through extensive experiments on two datasets, DexYCB and OakInk, showing that UniHM achieves state-of-the-art performance and exhibits strong generalization capabilities to unseen objects and tasks. Notably, the framework is trained without relying on expensive teleoperated data, instead learning from readily available human-object interaction videos. The real-world evaluations further validate the practical applicability of the framework, showcasing its ability to produce physically consistent and executable manipulations. This work represents a significant step towards enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks based on natural language commands, moving beyond static grasp generation to dynamic, language-conditioned manipulation sequences. The methodology is innovative, combining a morphology-agnostic codebook with a physics-based refinement process, addressing key challenges in dexterous hand manipulation. The empirical results are compelling, demonstrating strong generalization to unseen objects and tasks, outperforming existing methods on multiple datasets. The real-world evaluations provide further evidence of the framework's practical potential, showing that it can produce physically consistent and executable manipulations. Overall, this paper presents a significant advancement in the field of dexterous manipulation, offering a promising approach for enabling robots to interact with the world in a more intuitive and flexible way.

## Suggestions:

To address the identified weaknesses, I propose several concrete and actionable improvements. First, to enhance the evaluation of complex, long-horizon tasks, the authors should include a more comprehensive evaluation protocol that specifically targets multi-step manipulation scenarios. This could involve tasks that require the hand to perform a sequence of actions, such as opening a lid, picking up an object, and placing it in a specific location. The evaluation should not only focus on the accuracy of the hand poses but also on the success rate of completing the entire task. Furthermore, the authors should provide a detailed analysis of how the model handles changes in contact points and object transformations throughout the task. This could include visualizations of the hand-object interactions and a quantitative analysis of the contact forces and object trajectories. Such an analysis would provide a more thorough understanding of the model's capabilities in complex manipulation scenarios. Additionally, the authors should consider using metrics that directly measure task success, such as the percentage of tasks completed successfully, rather than relying solely on pose accuracy metrics. Second, to mitigate the limitations of relying on RGB-D data and object point clouds, the authors should explore the robustness of the system to various sensor noise and occlusions. This could involve adding synthetic noise to the input data and evaluating the model's performance under different levels of noise. The authors should also investigate the use of more robust perception techniques that are less sensitive to variations in lighting conditions and occlusions.
