## Summary:

This paper introduces BadDet+, a novel framework for backdoor attacks on object detection models, aiming to unify and improve upon existing methods for both region misclassification (RMA) and object disappearance (ODA) attacks. The core contribution lies in the introduction of a log-barrier penalty term that is integrated into the training loss function. This penalty is designed to suppress the true-class predictions for objects bearing the backdoor trigger, thereby forcing the model to either misclassify them or fail to detect them altogether. The authors argue that this unified approach addresses limitations in prior work, which often treated RMA and ODA as separate problems. To better evaluate the effectiveness of these attacks, the paper also introduces new metrics: the True Detection Rate (TDR) for RMA, which accounts for the possibility of multiple detections of the same object, and an instance-level Attack Success Rate (ASR) for ODA, which focuses on the disappearance of specific objects. The empirical evaluation of BadDet+ is conducted across multiple datasets, including COCO and MTSD, and various object detection architectures, such as FCOS, Faster R-CNN, and DINO. The results demonstrate that BadDet+ achieves superior performance compared to baseline methods, particularly in terms of robustness to physical triggers and maintaining clean-task performance. The paper also includes a theoretical analysis of the proposed penalty term, providing insights into its effectiveness and impact on the model's learning process. Furthermore, the authors explore the performance of BadDet+ under fine-tuning based defenses, such as standard fine-tuning and fine-tuning with sharpness-aware minimization. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of backdoor attacks on object detection, offering a unified and effective framework along with new evaluation metrics that provide a more accurate assessment of attack success. However, as I will discuss, there are several limitations that warrant further investigation.

## Strengths:

I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. First, the introduction of a unified framework for both RMA and ODA attacks is a significant contribution. By addressing these attack types with a single mechanism, the authors simplify the attack process and increase its applicability. The log-barrier penalty term, which is at the heart of BadDet+, is a technically sound approach that effectively suppresses true-class predictions for trigger-bearing objects. This is well-supported by the theoretical analysis provided in the paper, which gives me confidence in the underlying mechanism. Second, the introduction of new evaluation metrics, TDR for RMA and instance-level ASR for ODA, is a crucial step forward. I appreciate that the authors have explicitly addressed the limitations of existing metrics. The TDR metric, in particular, provides a more accurate assessment of attack success in RMA by accounting for the possibility of multiple detections of the same object. Similarly, the instance-level ASR for ODA focuses on the disappearance of specific objects, which is more relevant than relying solely on mAP. These metrics provide a more nuanced understanding of the attack's effectiveness. Third, the empirical results presented in the paper are compelling. The authors have conducted a comprehensive evaluation of BadDet+ across multiple datasets, model architectures, and trigger placements. The results consistently demonstrate that BadDet+ outperforms baseline methods, particularly in terms of robustness to physical triggers and maintaining clean-task performance. The inclusion of both COCO and MTSD datasets, along with various object detection architectures, provides a strong basis for the claims made in the paper. Finally, I commend the authors for their efforts in evaluating the performance of BadDet+ under fine-tuning based defenses. While the scope of the defense evaluation is limited, it is a valuable step in understanding the robustness of the proposed attack. The inclusion of fine-tuning with sharpness-aware minimization is also a positive aspect, as it explores a more advanced defense strategy. These strengths, taken together, make a compelling case for the effectiveness and novelty of BadDet+.


## Weaknesses:

Despite the strengths of this paper, I have identified several weaknesses that warrant careful consideration. First, while the paper introduces a unified framework for RMA and ODA attacks, it does not sufficiently explore the impact of different trigger types on the effectiveness of BadDet+. The primary evaluation uses a single, artificial trigger—a blue square—and while Appendix A.4 explores some variations of this square, it does not include natural triggers or a systematic analysis of different trigger characteristics such as size, shape, and texture. This is a significant limitation, as real-world triggers could be more subtle or integrated into the scene. The lack of analysis on how different trigger characteristics affect the attack's success rate makes it difficult to assess the robustness and generalizability of the proposed method. For example, the paper does not investigate the use of adversarial patches or subtle image manipulations as triggers, which could reveal important insights into the attack's vulnerabilities. This is a high-confidence weakness, as the paper explicitly states the use of a blue square trigger and the limited exploration of variations in the appendix, without any analysis of natural triggers or a systematic variation of trigger characteristics. While the paper acknowledges this underperformance, it does not provide a detailed analysis of the architectural differences between the object detectors and how these differences interact with the attack mechanism. This is a high-confidence weakness, as the paper explicitly acknowledges the underperformance in the results section and lacks a detailed explanation of the architectural reasons for this. The paper explicitly states this assumption in the threat model section, and does not explore scenarios with limited attacker control. This is a high-confidence weakness, as the paper explicitly states the assumption of a strong adversarial setting.
