## Summary:

This paper introduces QUOKA, a novel sparse attention method designed to accelerate the prefill stage of large language model (LLM) inference. The core idea behind QUOKA stems from the observation that queries exhibiting low cosine similarity to the mean query tend to attend to a broader range of keys, making them more influential in the attention computation. Building upon this observation, QUOKA employs a two-step process: first, it selects a subset of representative queries based on their cosine dissimilarity from the mean query; second, it selects the most relevant key-value pairs for each selected query based on their cosine similarity. This approach aims to reduce the computational cost of attention while preserving accuracy. The method is training-free and hardware-agnostic, relying on standard linear algebra operations, which makes it easily deployable across various hardware platforms. The authors demonstrate QUOKA's effectiveness through extensive experiments on a range of benchmarks, including Needle-In-A-Haystack, LongBench, RULER, and Math500. Their results show that QUOKA achieves significant speedups, up to 7x on CPUs and 5-6x on GPUs, while maintaining near-baseline accuracy. The paper also includes thorough ablation studies and hyperparameter analysis, providing insights into the method's behavior and robustness. The authors emphasize that QUOKA is particularly effective during the prefill stage of LLM inference, where the computational cost of attention can be a significant bottleneck. While the paper does present some results on generation, the primary focus is clearly on prefill optimization. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to sparse attention that has the potential to significantly improve the efficiency of LLM inference, particularly in resource-constrained environments. The method's simplicity, hardware agnosticism, and training-free nature make it a practical solution for accelerating LLMs. However, as I will discuss in detail, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened through more rigorous analysis and experimentation.

## Suggestions:

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, to strengthen the theoretical foundation of the paper, the authors should provide a more formal definition of query importance. This definition should be grounded in the mathematical framework of attention mechanisms and should clearly articulate how the proposed metric relates to the actual contribution of a query to the final output. The authors could explore concepts like the Shapley value or other attribution methods to quantify the importance of each query. Furthermore, the paper should include a more rigorous analysis of the proposed method, demonstrating how it effectively captures the defined notion of importance. This analysis should include mathematical proofs or derivations that justify the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The authors should also investigate the sensitivity of the method to different choices of similarity metrics and subselection strategies. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the method's behavior and limitations. Second, to address the limited analysis of the generation phase, the authors should conduct more extensive experiments on long-generation tasks. This should include a comparison of QUOKA with other sparse attention methods, as well as a baseline dense attention model. The evaluation should not only focus on accuracy but also on the quality of generated text, using metrics such as BLEU, ROUGE, or human evaluation. The authors should also investigate the impact of the proposed method on the diversity of generated text and the ability to maintain coherence over long sequences. It would be beneficial to analyze the attention patterns during generation and understand how QUOKA affects the model's ability to capture long-range dependencies. The authors should also explore the potential of combining QUOKA with other techniques, such as caching or speculative decoding, to further improve generation performance. Third, to better understand the impact of QUOKA on attention patterns, the authors should conduct a more detailed analysis of the attention weights and their distribution. This could involve visualizing the attention maps and analyzing the patterns of attention across different layers and heads. The authors should also investigate whether QUOKA introduces any biases or alters the model's ability to capture long-range dependencies. For example, they could analyze the attention patterns on tasks that require reasoning over long sequences and compare them to the patterns observed with dense attention. Furthermore, the authors should explore the possibility of using different aggregation strategies for the attention weights, such as averaging or max-pooling, and analyze their impact on the performance of QUOKA. Fourth, the paper should include a more detailed comparison with other dynamic sparse attention methods, including those based on learned sparsity patterns or reinforcement learning. This comparison should include a discussion of the computational overhead of each method, as some methods may achieve higher accuracy at the cost of increased computational complexity. The authors should also consider comparing QUOKA with methods that use different sparsity patterns, such as block sparsity or strided sparsity. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the strengths and weaknesses of QUOKA compared to other approaches. The authors should also discuss the potential for combining QUOKA with other techniques, such as quantization or pruning, to further improve the efficiency of LLM inference. Finally, to improve the analysis of the accuracy-efficiency trade-off, the authors should conduct a more granular study of how accuracy changes with varying numbers of selected KVs. This should include a layer-wise analysis, showing how different layers of the model are affected by the KV selection process. For example, the authors could plot the accuracy of each layer as a function of the sparsity level, which would reveal which layers are more sensitive to KV selection. Furthermore, the authors should explore the impact of different sparsity levels on various tasks, such as question answering, text summarization, and code generation. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the method's robustness and limitations. The authors could also consider using adaptive sparsity levels, where the number of selected KVs is adjusted based on the specific task or input sequence. This could potentially improve the overall performance of the method.
