## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel approach to address the 'copy-paste' artifact in identity-preserving image generation, a phenomenon where models overly adhere to the reference image, failing to capture natural variations in pose, expression, and lighting. The authors define this problem as a scenario where the similarity between the generated image and the reference image is higher than the similarity between the generated image and the ground truth. To tackle this, the paper makes several key contributions. First, it presents MultiID-2M, a large-scale paired dataset of multi-person images, each with diverse references for each identity. This dataset is designed to facilitate the training of models that can generate images with consistent identities while allowing for variations in other attributes. Second, the paper introduces MultiID-Bench, a benchmark that quantifies both copy-paste artifacts and the trade-off between identity fidelity and variation. This benchmark provides a standardized way to evaluate the performance of multi-identity generation models. Finally, the paper proposes a novel training paradigm for a diffusion-based model, named WithAnyone, which leverages a contrastive identity loss with extended negatives to balance fidelity with diversity. This training approach aims to mitigate copy-paste artifacts while preserving high identity similarity. The core of the method involves a carefully designed training process that includes a ground-truth aligned identity loss and a contrastive loss that uses a large number of negative samples to improve identity representation. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach through extensive experiments, showing that WithAnyone substantially reduces copy-paste artifacts, improves controllability over pose and expression, and maintains strong perceptual quality. The paper's main empirical findings highlight the success of the proposed method in mitigating the copy-paste issue, achieving a better balance between identity preservation and image variation, and demonstrating state-of-the-art performance on the proposed benchmark. The overall significance of this work lies in its comprehensive approach to addressing a critical limitation in identity-preserving image generation, providing a valuable dataset, benchmark, and a novel training method that can be used as a foundation for future research in this area.

In these cases, a higher similarity between the generated image and the reference image might be desirable, as the goal is to generate an image that closely resembles the reference. ## Suggestions:

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the authors should refine their definition of the 'copy-paste' problem to account for the semantic content and context of the images. Instead of relying solely on a comparison of similarity scores, the method should consider the semantic content and context of the images, perhaps by incorporating a measure of feature alignment or object correspondence. For example, the method could focus on the similarity of specific facial features or regions of interest, allowing for a more fine-grained analysis of copy-paste artifacts. This would also enable the method to better handle cases where the reference image contains unique attributes that should be preserved in the generated image. The authors should explore alternative metrics that can capture the nuances of identity preservation and style transfer, rather than relying on a single similarity score. Second, to address the lack of analysis on the trade-off between copy-paste mitigation and image quality, the paper should include a more comprehensive evaluation using a range of quantitative metrics. The current analysis focuses primarily on the proposed copy-paste metric, but it does not provide sufficient insight into how the method affects other aspects of image quality, such as sharpness, color accuracy, and overall realism. The authors should consider using metrics such as PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS to quantify the impact of their method on image quality. These examples should highlight cases where the method successfully mitigates copy-paste artifacts while maintaining high image quality, as well as cases where the method may compromise image quality to achieve better copy-paste mitigation. A more detailed analysis of these trade-offs would provide a more complete understanding of the strengths and limitations of the proposed method. Third, the paper should provide a detailed analysis of the computational cost of the proposed method, including both training and inference time. The current analysis lacks sufficient detail, making it difficult to assess the practicality of the proposed method. The authors should provide a breakdown of the computational cost of each component of their method, as well as a comparison with the computational cost of existing methods. Fourth, the authors should include a dedicated section discussing the limitations of the proposed method. This section should analyze the method's sensitivity to variations in input image quality, pose, and lighting conditions.
