## Summary:

This paper introduces INFO-SEDD, a novel approach for estimating information-theoretic measures, specifically mutual information (MI) and entropy, for high-dimensional discrete data. The core innovation lies in leveraging continuous-time Markov chains (CTMCs) and score functions derived from discrete diffusion models. Unlike many existing methods that rely on embedding discrete data into continuous spaces, INFO-SEDD operates directly on the discrete data, offering a potentially more accurate and scalable solution. The method constructs a perturbation process that gradually transforms the data into noise, and then analyzes the reverse process using score functions to estimate the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, from which MI and entropy are derived. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of INFO-SEDD through a series of experiments on synthetic data, text summarization, and genomics. In these experiments, INFO-SEDD consistently outperforms existing methods, particularly in high-dimensional settings, showcasing its robustness and accuracy. The paper provides a theoretical foundation for the method, including an analysis of error bounds and consistency properties. The authors also highlight the potential for extending INFO-SEDD to mixed continuous and discrete data, further broadening its applicability. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of information-theoretic estimation, offering a novel and effective approach for handling high-dimensional discrete data, a challenge that has long plagued traditional methods. The use of CTMCs and diffusion models in this context is a notable innovation, and the empirical results provide strong evidence for the method's practical utility. The paper's findings suggest that INFO-SEDD could be a valuable tool for researchers working with complex discrete datasets across various domains.
