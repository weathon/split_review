## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel batch acquisition function for multi-objective Bayesian optimization (MOBO), termed Probability of Matching, which aims to balance solution quality and diversity. The core idea is to estimate the probability that a batch of candidate solutions matches the true Pareto optimal set, which is decomposed into two factors: the probability that all batch points are Pareto optimal and the probability that the batch collectively covers the full Pareto set. The first factor is estimated using the parallel Expected Hypervolume Improvement (qEHVI), while the second factor is promoted through a space-filling design, resulting in the proposed qEHVI-SF algorithm. The method is evaluated on two synthetic problems and a real-world alloy design task, demonstrating superior performance in terms of both quality and diversity metrics, such as hypervolume and Expected Minimum Distance (EMD). The paper provides a thorough complexity analysis, showing that the proposed method does not introduce significant computational overhead. However, several aspects of the paper, including the motivation for the Probability of Matching, the novelty of the approach, and the robustness of the method, require further clarification and analysis.

## Strengths:

The paper makes several valuable contributions to the field of multi-objective Bayesian optimization (MOBO). The introduction of the Probability of Matching acquisition function is a novel and interesting approach that explicitly captures the likelihood of a batch of points matching the true Pareto optimal set. This is a significant departure from traditional methods that often focus on scalarized metrics or additive combinations of quality and diversity. The factorization of the Probability of Matching into two components—quality and coverage—provides a clear and interpretable framework for balancing these two objectives. The use of qEHVI for estimating the quality component and space-filling designs for promoting diversity is well-motivated and grounded in established techniques. The empirical results are promising, showing that the proposed qEHVI-SF method consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on both synthetic and real-world benchmarks. The introduction of the Expected Minimum Distance (EMD) metric is a valuable addition, as it provides a new way to evaluate the coverage of the Pareto front in the design space, complementing existing metrics like hypervolume and IGD. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with a clear structure that guides the reader through the problem setting, proposed method, and experimental results. The complexity analysis is thorough and demonstrates that the proposed method does not introduce significant computational overhead, which is crucial for practical applications.


## Weaknesses:

Despite the paper's promising contributions, several aspects require further clarification and analysis. First, the motivation for the Probability of Matching is not entirely clear. The paper states that the goal of MOBO is to sample the entire Pareto front, but this is not always the case in practice. In many scenarios, the objective is to find the Pareto front, not to sample it extensively. The paper needs to better justify why covering the entire Pareto front is a desirable goal, especially when compared to methods that focus on finding the true Pareto front. This lack of clarity in the motivation weakens the theoretical foundation of the proposed method. Second, the proposed method is essentially a combination of qEHVI and a space-filling design, which is not a novel approach. The paper does not adequately address the existing literature on quality-diversity optimization, which also aims to balance quality and diversity. The novelty of the proposed method is questionable given the existing work in this area, and a more detailed comparison to these approaches is necessary to establish the true contribution of the work. Third, the paper lacks a thorough discussion of related work. There is a substantial body of literature on quality-diversity optimization that is not mentioned, and the paper does not adequately compare the proposed method to these existing approaches. This omission makes it difficult to assess the true contribution of the work. Fourth, the paper does not provide a rigorous analysis of the convergence properties of the proposed method. While the empirical results are promising, a theoretical understanding of how the method converges to the true Pareto set would be valuable. This theoretical gap limits the confidence in the method's long-term performance and robustness. Fifth, the paper does not explore the sensitivity of the method to different choices of the space-filling design parameters. It would be useful to investigate how the performance of qEHVI-SF varies with different space-filling designs and to provide guidelines for selecting appropriate parameters. This sensitivity analysis is crucial for practitioners who may need to adapt the method to different problem settings. Sixth, the paper does not compare the proposed method to other state-of-the-art MOBO algorithms, such as those based on determinantal point processes or other diversity metrics. A more comprehensive comparison would provide a better understanding of the strengths and weaknesses of qEHVI-SF compared to existing approaches. Finally, the paper does not explore the potential limitations of the proposed method in scenarios with noisy objective function evaluations or with non-convex Pareto fronts. It would be useful to investigate how the method performs in these challenging scenarios and to provide guidelines for addressing potential issues. These limitations, if not addressed, could significantly impact the practical applicability of the proposed method.


## Suggestions:

To strengthen the paper, several improvements could be made. First, the motivation for the Probability of Matching should be clarified. The paper should provide a more detailed discussion of the trade-offs between sampling the entire Pareto front and finding the true Pareto front. This discussion should include specific scenarios where covering the entire Pareto front is more beneficial than finding the true Pareto front, and vice versa. For example, in material design, while identifying diverse materials is important, the ultimate goal is often to find the best material for a specific application, which might not require extensive sampling. Second, the paper should address the existing literature on quality-diversity optimization more thoroughly. The current approach of combining qEHVI with a space-filling design is not novel, and the paper needs to clearly differentiate the proposed method from existing quality-diversity optimization techniques. This could involve a detailed comparison of the computational complexity, convergence properties, and performance on different types of problems. Third, the paper should include a more comprehensive experimental evaluation. The current evaluation is limited to a few synthetic and real-world benchmarks, and the paper should include a more diverse set of problems to assess the robustness and generalizability of the proposed method. This could involve more complex synthetic problems with non-convex Pareto fronts and additional real-world problems from different domains. Fourth, the paper should provide a more detailed analysis of the computational cost of the proposed method, particularly in high-dimensional spaces or with large batch sizes. While the complexity analysis is helpful, a more detailed breakdown of the computational bottlenecks would be valuable for practitioners. This analysis should consider the impact of different implementation choices, such as the use of parallel computing or optimized algorithms for hypervolume calculation. Fifth, the paper should explore the sensitivity of the method to different choices of the space-filling design parameters. A sensitivity analysis could involve varying the number of points in the space-filling design, the type of design (e.g., Latin hypercube, Sobol sequence), and the distance metric used to define the space-filling property. This analysis should provide practical guidelines for selecting appropriate parameters for different problem settings. Finally, the paper should investigate the performance of the proposed method in scenarios with noisy objective function evaluations and non-convex Pareto fronts. This could involve using robust optimization techniques or adaptive sampling strategies to mitigate the impact of noise and non-convexity. A detailed discussion of these potential limitations and how to address them would enhance the practical value of the proposed method.
