## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel framework for evaluating heterogeneous treatment effect (HTE) estimators, focusing on the concept of relative error. The core contribution lies in relaxing the stringent requirement of consistent outcome regression models, a limitation often encountered in existing evaluation methods. Instead, the authors propose a method that relies on a correctly specified propensity score model, which is a less restrictive assumption. This is achieved through the development of new loss functions and a neural network architecture designed to estimate nuisance parameters, specifically the propensity score and outcome models. The proposed method leverages a weighted least squares approach for the outcome model, where the weights are derived from the estimated propensity score, and introduces balance regularizers to encourage the estimated propensity scores to satisfy the balance property. The theoretical analysis provides conditions for robust relative error estimation and demonstrates the asymptotic properties of the proposed estimator. The authors also present a new learning algorithm for HTE that leverages both previously estimated HTE estimators and the nuisance parameters learned through their neural network architecture. The empirical evaluation of the framework is conducted through extensive experiments on semi-synthetic and real-world datasets, including the IHDP and Twins datasets. These experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in evaluating HTE estimators and show that the proposed learning algorithm for HTE exhibits desirable performance. The paper's significance lies in its ability to provide a more robust and reliable evaluation of HTE estimators, particularly in scenarios where outcome models are difficult to specify accurately. By shifting the focus to relative error and relying on a correctly specified propensity score model, the authors offer a practical approach to HTE evaluation that is less sensitive to model misspecification. The paper also contributes a new HTE learning algorithm that leverages the proposed framework. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of causal inference by addressing a critical challenge in the evaluation of HTE estimators and providing a practical and robust solution.
