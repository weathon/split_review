## Summary:

This paper introduces BuilderBench, a novel benchmark designed to evaluate the exploration, reasoning, and generalization capabilities of AI agents in a block-building environment. The core idea is to create a setting where agents learn through open-ended interaction, developing skills that can then be transferred to specific tasks. The benchmark consists of a simulated environment where a robotic agent interacts with blocks, and a task suite of 42 diverse structures that require different skills to build. The authors emphasize the importance of block-building as a fundamental activity that fosters spatial and arithmetic skills in children, and they argue that it provides a rich mathematical basis for studying AI agent learning. The environment is designed to be hardware-accelerated, allowing for faster training compared to CPU-based benchmarks. The authors provide single-file implementations of several reinforcement learning algorithms, including model-free (PPO, SAC), model-based (MEGA), and intrinsic motivation-based methods (SFL, RND), to serve as reference points for researchers. The paper presents experimental results demonstrating that some algorithms, like PPO, achieve higher success rates and returns compared to others, particularly as the complexity of the tasks increases. The authors also evaluate the performance of these algorithms under both sparse and dense reward settings. The paper argues that BuilderBench can accelerate research towards agents that learn to explore and generalize through interaction with the environment, moving beyond the limitations of current benchmarks that often focus on narrow, hand-designed tasks. The authors highlight that current language models struggle to solve the tasks, suggesting that the benchmark poses a significant challenge for existing AI systems. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and well-executed benchmark that has the potential to contribute significantly to the field of AI research by providing a platform for studying open-ended learning and generalization in a controlled and accessible environment.
