## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel framework, D²GS, designed to enhance the performance of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) in sparse-view settings. The core contribution lies in addressing the critical issues of overfitting in near-field regions and underfitting in far-field regions, which are common challenges when training 3DGS with limited viewpoints. To combat overfitting, the authors propose a Depth-and-Density Guided Dropout (DD-Drop) module. This module adaptively masks redundant Gaussian primitives based on their depth and local density, effectively regularizing the near-field areas where overfitting is most prevalent. The DD-Drop module calculates a dropout score for each Gaussian primitive, considering both its depth and the local density of primitives. This score is then used to probabilistically drop redundant primitives, thereby mitigating overfitting. To tackle underfitting in the far-field, the paper introduces a Distance-Aware Fidelity Enhancement (DAFE) module. This module leverages depth priors obtained from a monocular depth estimation model to enhance loss supervision in distant regions. By focusing on areas that are typically under-represented in sparse-view settings, the DAFE module improves the fidelity of the reconstructed scene in the far-field. The paper also introduces a new evaluation metric, Inter-Model Robustness (IMR), which quantifies the stability of learned Gaussian distributions. The IMR metric is based on the 2-Wasserstein Distance and Optimal Transport, providing a measure of the consistency of independently trained models. This metric aims to capture the robustness of the model to initialization and training noise, which is crucial for evaluating the reliability of 3DGS models. The authors conduct extensive experiments on the LLFF and Mip-NeRF360 datasets, demonstrating that D²GS significantly improves both visual quality and robustness under sparse-view conditions. The experimental results show that D²GS outperforms existing methods, including both 3DGS-based and NeRF-based approaches, in terms of quantitative metrics such as PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. The ablation studies further validate the effectiveness of the proposed DD-Drop and DAFE modules, as well as the impact of different monocular depth estimators on the performance of DAFE. Overall, this paper presents a well-motivated and effective framework for improving 3DGS in sparse-view settings, with a novel evaluation metric that provides valuable insights into model robustness. The combination of DD-Drop and DAFE, along with the introduction of the IMR metric, represents a significant contribution to the field of 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis.

The use of both depth and density to guide the dropout process is a clever way to target redundant primitives in the near-field. The DAFE module, which leverages depth priors to enhance supervision in distant regions, is also a significant contribution. By focusing on the under-represented far-field areas, the DAFE module effectively addresses the underfitting problem. ## Weaknesses:

While the paper presents several strengths, I have identified some weaknesses that warrant further discussion. First, the paper's reliance on depth priors for the Depth-and-Density Guided Dropout (DD-Drop) module introduces a potential source of error. The method description clearly states that the dropout score is based on depth, and the DAFE module also uses a monocular depth estimation model. However, the paper does not explicitly discuss the potential errors introduced by the depth estimation process in the context of DD-Drop. The accuracy of the depth estimation directly impacts the effectiveness of the dropout strategy. If the depth estimation is inaccurate, the dropout may not target the correct Gaussian primitives, potentially leading to suboptimal results. This is a significant concern because the paper does not explore the sensitivity of the proposed method to the accuracy of the depth priors. For instance, the authors could have investigated how the performance of the method varies when using depth maps generated by different depth estimation algorithms or by introducing controlled noise to the depth maps. A more detailed analysis of the impact of depth estimation errors on the final reconstruction quality would strengthen the paper's claims. My confidence in this weakness is high, as the method description clearly states the use of depth information for the dropout strategy and the use of a monocular depth estimator, and the paper lacks any discussion of the potential errors introduced by this process. The paper acknowledges the impact of the depth estimator but lacks a detailed analysis of *why* certain estimators perform better. ## Suggestions:

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, to mitigate the potential errors introduced by the depth estimation process in the DD-Drop module, the authors should conduct a sensitivity analysis of the proposed method to the accuracy of the depth priors. This could involve investigating how the performance of the method varies when using depth maps generated by different depth estimation algorithms. The authors could also explore the impact of introducing controlled noise to the depth maps. This analysis would provide a better understanding of the robustness of the method to errors in depth estimation. Furthermore, a more detailed analysis of the impact of depth estimation errors on the final reconstruction quality would strengthen the paper's claims. Second, to address the lack of in-depth analysis of different monocular depth estimators, the authors should analyze the characteristics of the depth maps generated by each estimator, such as their accuracy, noise levels, and handling of occlusions, and correlate these characteristics with the final reconstruction quality. For example, they could analyze how the performance of DAFE changes when using depth estimators with varying levels of accuracy or when using depth estimators that are more robust to occlusions. This analysis should also include a discussion on the potential limitations of using monocular depth estimators, such as their inherent ambiguity and potential for errors, and how these limitations might affect the performance of DAFE. The authors could also explore the possibility of using multi-view depth estimation techniques to improve the robustness of the DAFE module. Third, to address the lack of computational cost analysis, the authors should provide a detailed breakdown of the time spent on each stage of their proposed method, including the depth-and-density guided dropout and distance-aware fidelity enhancement modules. This analysis should be performed on a standard hardware setup and should include both training and inference times. Furthermore, the authors should compare the computational cost of their method with existing state-of-the-art methods to provide a clear understanding of the trade-offs. The memory footprint of the proposed method should also be analyzed and discussed, as this is a critical factor for real-time applications. This analysis should include the memory usage of the Gaussian primitives, the depth maps, and any other intermediate data structures. The authors should also investigate the scalability of their method with respect to the number of Gaussian primitives and the size of the input images. This would provide a more complete picture of the computational cost of the proposed method. Finally, the authors should consider providing a more detailed explanation of how the IMR metric relates to the visual quality of the results. While the paper does provide a detailed explanation of how the IMR metric is calculated, it does not explicitly demonstrate a direct correlation between the IMR value and the perceived visual quality. The authors could show that a lower value of the proposed metric corresponds to a better visual quality. Furthermore, it would be useful to compare the proposed metric with other existing metrics for evaluating the robustness of 3D reconstruction methods. This would help to establish the validity and usefulness of the proposed metric.
