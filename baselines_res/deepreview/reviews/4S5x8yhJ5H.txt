## Summary:

This paper introduces VIBEFACE, a new dataset designed for evaluating face verification systems, particularly in the context of electronic Know Your Customer (eKYC) scenarios. The authors aim to address the limitations of existing datasets by creating a resource that simulates real-world conditions more closely. The dataset comprises 2,250 high-quality facial images and 1,550 short videos, collected from 50 diverse subjects. The data includes variations in pose, lighting, and the presence of eyeglasses, aiming to provide a comprehensive benchmark for testing face verification algorithms. The authors collected data in a controlled studio environment, simulating different lighting conditions such as artificial light, flash, natural daylight, and weak natural light. They also included scenarios with and without eyeglasses to account for common real-world variations. The dataset includes both image and video modalities, with the video sequences mimicking eKYC workflows. The authors emphasize the ethical considerations of their work, stating that informed consent was obtained from all participants and that the dataset is intended for non-commercial research use only. To evaluate the dataset's utility, the authors conducted experiments using three face detection algorithms (MTCNN, RetinaFace, and MediaPipe) and two face recognition models (ArcFace and MagFace). The results of these experiments provide a baseline for future research using the VIBEFACE dataset. The paper highlights the balanced distribution of demographic attributes, including gender, race, and age, as a key strength of the dataset. The authors argue that this balance helps to mitigate potential biases in face verification systems. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field by providing a new, ethically sourced dataset that addresses some of the limitations of existing resources. However, as I will discuss in the weaknesses section, there are some limitations that need to be considered when using this dataset.
