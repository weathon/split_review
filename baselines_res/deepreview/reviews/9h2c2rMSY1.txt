## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel weighted conformal prediction (WCP) method tailored for uncertainty quantification in surrogate models of time-dependent partial differential equations (PDEs). The authors address a critical limitation of standard conformal prediction (CP), which assumes exchangeability between calibration and test samples—an assumption often violated in time-dependent settings due to non-stationary data distributions. They demonstrate that for linear PDEs with Gaussian initial conditions, the distribution of the solution at different time points can be non-exchangeable, leading to under-coverage of standard CP methods. To overcome this, the authors derive explicit densities for the discretized solutions of linear PDEs over time, enabling the calculation of density ratios between calibration and test distributions. These ratios are then used to re-weight the calibration samples in a conformal prediction framework, resulting in the proposed WCP method. This approach provides marginal coverage guarantees, ensuring that the overall error rate is controlled, even when the exchangeability assumption is violated. The authors validate their method through experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets. The synthetic experiments focus on a second-order PDE framework, while the real-world application involves a 2D dataset of pulsed-thermography measurements for detecting subsurface defects. The results demonstrate that the WCP method achieves the desired coverage guarantees, outperforming baseline methods that do not account for distribution shifts. The core contribution of this work lies in its rigorous theoretical analysis of distribution shifts in function spaces and the development of a practical, weighted conformal prediction approach that addresses the non-exchangeability issue in time-dependent PDEs. By deriving explicit densities for discretized PDE solutions, the authors provide a principled way to handle the temporal distribution shifts, offering a significant advancement in uncertainty quantification for surrogate models in scientific computing. The paper's focus on linear PDEs with Gaussian initial conditions provides a solid foundation for future research, and the empirical results, while limited in scope, demonstrate the potential of the proposed method for real-world applications. The authors' approach of using density ratios to re-weight calibration samples is a clever way to adapt the conformal prediction framework to non-exchangeable data, and the theoretical analysis provides a strong justification for this approach. Overall, this paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of uncertainty quantification for time-dependent PDEs, offering a practical and theoretically sound method for handling non-exchangeability.
