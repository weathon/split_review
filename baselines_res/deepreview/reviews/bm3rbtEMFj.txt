## Summary:

This paper introduces ELMUR, a novel transformer architecture designed to address the challenges of long-horizon decision-making in partially observable environments, a critical issue in reinforcement learning. The core innovation of ELMUR lies in its integration of layer-local external memory, bidirectional token-memory cross-attention, and a Least Recently Used (LRU) update mechanism. This architecture allows the model to retain and utilize information over extended time horizons, significantly surpassing the limitations of traditional attention mechanisms. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of ELMUR through a series of experiments across diverse environments, including synthetic T-Maze tasks, robotic manipulation challenges (MIKASA-Robo), and the POPGym benchmark. The empirical results show that ELMUR achieves substantial improvements over baseline methods, particularly in tasks requiring long-term memory and planning. The paper also includes a theoretical analysis of the LRU-based memory dynamics, providing insights into the model's forgetting and retention properties. The authors present a clear and well-structured approach, detailing the architecture and its components, and providing a comprehensive evaluation of its performance. The results highlight the potential of ELMUR for handling complex, long-horizon tasks in partially observable settings, which is a significant contribution to the field of reinforcement learning. The paper's focus on memory management and its impact on long-horizon performance makes it a valuable contribution to the ongoing research in this area. The authors also provide an ablation study to analyze the impact of different components of ELMUR, which further strengthens the paper's findings. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated, technically sound, and empirically validated approach to addressing long-horizon decision-making in reinforcement learning, making it a significant contribution to the field.
