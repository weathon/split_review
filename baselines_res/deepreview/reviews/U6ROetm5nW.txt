## Summary:

In this paper, I explore the kernel density estimation (KDE) problem, which involves creating a data structure that can efficiently approximate the density of a dataset at any given query point using a kernel function. The primary contribution of this work is the development of a new data structure that significantly improves the query time for KDE, achieving a query time of $O_\epsilon(1/\mu^{0.05})$ at the cost of increased space complexity, $O_\epsilon(1/\mu^{4.15})$. More generally, the paper introduces the first known trade-offs between query time and space for KDE, demonstrating that for any $\delta \geq 0$, a data structure with space $O_\epsilon(1/\mu^{1+\delta})$ can achieve a query time of $O_\epsilon(1/\mu^{\xi(\delta)})$, where $\xi(\delta)$ is a non-increasing function of $\delta$. Notably, for the linear space regime ($\delta = 0$), the paper achieves a query time of $O_\epsilon(1/\mu^{0.1865})$, which is a notable improvement over the previous bound of $O_\epsilon(1/\mu^{0.25})$ from Charikar et al. (2020). The paper leverages asymmetric locality-sensitive hashing (LSH) to achieve these results, providing a simpler and more flexible approach compared to prior work. However, the paper's focus on the Gaussian kernel and the lack of empirical evaluation and detailed comparison with existing methods are significant limitations that affect the overall impact and practical relevance of the results.

## Strengths:

One of the key strengths of this paper is its novel approach to kernel density estimation (KDE) using asymmetric locality-sensitive hashing (LSH). This method allows for a trade-off between query time and space complexity, which is a significant advancement in the field. The paper achieves a query time of $O_\epsilon(1/\mu^{0.05})$ with a space complexity of $O_\epsilon(1/\mu^{4.15})$, and more generally, it provides the first known query time vs. space trade-offs for KDE. For the linear space regime, the paper improves the query time to $O_\epsilon(1/\mu^{0.1865})$, which is better than the previous non-adaptive bound of $O_\epsilon(1/\mu^{0.25})$ from Charikar et al. (2020). The techniques used in this paper are interesting and non-trivial, particularly the use of asymmetric LSH, which has not been extensively explored in the context of KDE. The paper also simplifies the analysis compared to previous work, making the results more accessible and easier to implement. The problem of KDE is fundamental and widely studied in statistical learning theory and AI, and the improvements in query time and space trade-offs are valuable contributions to the field. The paper's theoretical results are well-supported, and the authors provide a clear and concise explanation of the mathematical foundations and the implications of their findings.


## Weaknesses:

Despite the significant theoretical contributions, the paper has several limitations that affect its clarity, generality, and practical relevance. First, the presentation of the paper is not clear enough, and it is challenging to fully understand the techniques used. The paper dives directly into technical details without providing a high-level, intuitive explanation of the problem or the core idea behind the proposed solution. For instance, the introduction to the KDE problem is formal but lacks a simple, relatable explanation. The explanation of Charikar et al. (2020)'s work is concise but assumes prior knowledge, making it difficult for readers to grasp the context and significance of the improvements. The intuition behind using asymmetric LSH is mentioned but not fully elaborated upon, which could leave readers wondering why this approach is beneficial compared to symmetric LSH. This lack of clarity is a significant issue, as it hinders the accessibility of the paper to a broader audience and makes it difficult to appreciate the technical contributions without a deep background in the field (Confidence Level: High, Key Evidence: Direct quotes from the paper and the reviewer's comments).

Second, the paper's focus on the Gaussian kernel is a significant limitation. While the Gaussian kernel is widely used, the analysis and results might not directly extend to other common kernels, such as the Laplace kernel. The paper explicitly states that the Gaussian kernel will be the main focus, and the entire technical development and analysis are centered around the properties of this kernel. The authors do not discuss the potential challenges and modifications required to adapt their techniques to other kernels, which limits the generality of the approach. This is particularly important because the KDE problem is often encountered with various kernel functions, and the ability to handle different kernels would significantly broaden the impact of the paper (Confidence Level: High, Key Evidence: Explicit statements in the paper and the reviewer's comments).

Third, the improvement in query time for the linear space regime is not as significant as it could be. The paper achieves a query time of $O_\epsilon(1/\mu^{0.1865})$ for the linear space regime, which is an improvement over the previous bound of $O_\epsilon(1/\mu^{0.25})$. However, the exponent difference is relatively small (0.25 - 0.1865 = 0.0635), and the paper does not provide a thorough discussion of the practical implications of this improvement. ## Suggestions:

To address the limitations identified in the paper, several concrete and actionable improvements can be made. First, the paper would benefit significantly from a more detailed and intuitive explanation of the techniques used. The authors should provide a step-by-step walkthrough of how the data structure is constructed and how queries are processed. This should include a clear explanation of the specific LSH families used, the choice of parameters, and the resulting trade-offs between query time and space. A concrete example illustrating the query process would be beneficial, as it would help readers better understand the core ideas behind the result and appreciate the technical contribution. The authors should also discuss the limitations of their approach and how it compares to other existing methods in terms of both theoretical guarantees and practical performance. This would help readers understand the significance of the results and the potential for future improvements (Confidence Level: High, Key Evidence: Reviewer's comments and the paper's content).

Second, the authors should extend their analysis to other common kernels, such as the Laplace kernel. This would involve adapting the existing techniques to handle the different properties of these kernels. The authors should discuss the challenges involved in extending the analysis and provide a detailed explanation of how these challenges are addressed. For instance, the Laplace kernel has a slower decay rate compared to the Gaussian kernel, which could significantly impact the performance of the proposed data structure. The authors should explore whether the same performance guarantees can be achieved for the Laplace kernel and, if not, what modifications are necessary. This would demonstrate the generality of the approach and make the results more applicable to a wider range of problems (Confidence Level: High, Key Evidence: Reviewer's comments and the paper's content).

Third, the authors should provide a more thorough discussion of the practical implications of the achieved query time improvements, especially for the linear space regime. The paper should explore the trade-offs between query time and space in real-world scenarios and whether the achieved trade-off is meaningful. The authors should consider providing empirical evidence to support their theoretical findings, which would further strengthen the paper. Experiments comparing the query time and space usage of the proposed data structure with existing methods, especially in the linear space regime, would be valuable. The experiments should explore the impact of different parameter settings on the performance of the data structure and include a variety of datasets with different characteristics, such as varying dimensionality and data distributions, to assess the robustness of the proposed approach (Confidence Level: High, Key Evidence: Reviewer's comments and the paper's content).

This comparison should not only focus on the asymptotic bounds but also discuss the practical implications of these bounds, such as the constant factors involved and the dependence on the dimensionality of the data. A table summarizing the performance of different methods, including their query time, space complexity, and any other relevant parameters, would be beneficial. This would help readers understand the advantages and limitations of the proposed approach in the context of existing work and provide a clearer picture of the state-of-the-art in KDE data structures (Confidence Level: High, Key Evidence: Reviewer's comments and the paper's content).
