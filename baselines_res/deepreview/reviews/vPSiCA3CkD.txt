## Summary:

This paper introduces an adaptive accelerated gradient method, termed Accelerated GRAAL, designed for convex optimization problems. The core contribution lies in combining Nesterov's acceleration techniques with an adaptive stepsize adjustment mechanism that leverages local curvature estimation. This approach aims to achieve near-optimal convergence rates for both $L$-smooth and $(L_0, L_1)$-smooth functions, without requiring prior knowledge of the smoothness parameters or extensive hyperparameter tuning. The method's adaptivity stems from its ability to adjust the stepsize based on local curvature information, allowing it to potentially converge faster than standard gradient descent methods. The theoretical analysis provided in the paper demonstrates that the proposed method achieves near-optimal iteration complexities under the specified smoothness conditions. Specifically, the algorithm estimates the local Lipschitz constant of the gradient at each iteration using two gradient evaluations and uses this estimate to adapt the step size. This adaptive step size is then used within an accelerated framework, which is based on a specific choice of parameters that allow for a geometric rate of convergence. The paper provides a rigorous theoretical analysis, including proofs of the convergence rates. However, the paper lacks experimental results to validate the performance of the proposed method in practice. The absence of empirical evidence makes it difficult to assess the practical effectiveness of the algorithm and compare it with existing optimization algorithms. The paper's primary focus is on the theoretical development and analysis of the algorithm, with a detailed explanation of the adaptive stepsize rule and the theoretical guarantees. The method's novelty lies in the specific combination of Nesterov's acceleration with the adaptive stepsize mechanism based on local curvature estimation, which allows for a parameter-free approach to convex optimization. The paper's theoretical results are significant, demonstrating that the proposed method achieves near-optimal convergence rates for a broad class of convex functions. However, the lack of experimental validation leaves open questions about the practical applicability and performance of the algorithm in real-world scenarios. The paper's contribution is primarily theoretical, providing a new adaptive accelerated gradient method with strong convergence guarantees, but its practical impact remains to be fully explored.
