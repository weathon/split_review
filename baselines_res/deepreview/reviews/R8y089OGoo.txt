## Summary:

This paper introduces DIPOLE (Dichotomous diffusion Policy improvement), a novel reinforcement learning framework designed to optimize diffusion policies in a stable and controllable manner. The core innovation of DIPOLE lies in its decomposition of the optimal policy into two dichotomous policies: one that maximizes rewards and another that minimizes them. This decomposition is achieved through a greedified KL-regularized objective, which allows for the separate learning of these two policies. During inference, the final policy is generated by linearly combining the scores of these dichotomous policies, enabling fine-grained control over the level of greediness. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of DIPOLE through extensive evaluations on offline and offline-to-online RL benchmarks, specifically ExORL and OGBench, showing superior performance compared to several baselines. Furthermore, the paper showcases the practical applicability of DIPOLE by applying it to train a large vision-language-action (VLA) model for end-to-end autonomous driving on the NAVSIM benchmark, highlighting its potential for complex real-world applications. The method leverages the classifier-free guidance (CFG) mechanism to combine the scores of the dichotomous policies, drawing a clear connection between the proposed approach and existing techniques in diffusion model research. The theoretical analysis provides a solid foundation for the proposed method, deriving a closed-form solution for the greedified KL-regularized objective and demonstrating its connection to CFG. The empirical results, particularly on the autonomous driving task, underscore the practical significance of the proposed method. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to diffusion policy optimization, addressing key challenges in stability and controllability, and demonstrating its effectiveness in both simulated and real-world scenarios. The decomposition of the policy into dichotomous components and the use of a greedified KL-regularized objective are significant contributions to the field of reinforcement learning with diffusion models.

## Strengths:

I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. The most compelling is the novel approach to training diffusion policies by decomposing the optimal policy into two dichotomous components, one for reward maximization and the other for reward minimization. This is a significant departure from traditional methods and effectively addresses the instability issues often encountered in KL-regularized reinforcement learning. The theoretical analysis is also a notable strength, providing a solid foundation for the proposed method. The authors derive the closed-form solution for the greedified KL-regularized objective and clearly demonstrate its connection to the classifier-free guidance mechanism, which is a well-established technique in diffusion model research. This connection not only provides a clear interpretation of the method but also enhances its credibility. Furthermore, the empirical evaluation is comprehensive, covering both simulated benchmarks and a real-world autonomous driving task. The results on the offline and offline-to-online RL benchmarks, ExORL and OGBench, demonstrate the effectiveness of DIPOLE in various settings. The application of DIPOLE to train a large vision-language-action (VLA) model for end-to-end autonomous driving on the NAVSIM benchmark is particularly impressive, showcasing the practical applicability and potential of the method for complex real-world tasks. The fact that the method scales to a large VLA model with 1 billion parameters is a testament to its robustness. The paper is also well-written and clearly explains the technical details of the proposed method, making it accessible to a broad audience. The authors provide a thorough explanation of the method, the theoretical underpinnings, and the experimental setup, which allows for a clear understanding of the contributions and findings. The inclusion of a baseline method (DIPOLE w/o rs) that removes the dichotomous policy component provides a clear ablation study, further strengthening the paper's claims. In summary, the paper's strengths lie in its novel approach, solid theoretical foundation, comprehensive empirical evaluation, and clear presentation.
