## Summary:

This paper introduces ASPEC, a novel framework for automating the design of adaptive, stateful multi-agent systems. The core idea is to create teams of specialist agents, each with persistent memory, that accumulate expertise over time. The framework operates in two main stages: first, it discovers specialist agent archetypes through an evolutionary search process, and second, it cultivates their expertise through experience. A key innovation is the "retain-then-escalate" control policy, which balances efficiency and adaptability by deciding whether to retain the current agent architecture or resample a new one. The framework is evaluated on several benchmarks, including MMLU, GPQA, MATH, HumanEval, and SciCode, demonstrating significant performance gains and cost efficiency compared to existing methods. The paper's approach addresses a critical gap in the literature by combining the benefits of task-level robustness with per-query adaptability. The experimental results show that ASPEC achieves state-of-the-art performance on several benchmarks, highlighting the potential of this approach for creating more flexible and efficient multi-agent systems. The authors present a comprehensive experimental evaluation, including ablation studies and sensitivity analyses, which provide valuable insights into the framework's behavior. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of automated agent design, offering a promising direction for future research.
