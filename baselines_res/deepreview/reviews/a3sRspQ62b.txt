## Summary:

This paper introduces FourierFlow, a novel generative framework designed to address the challenges of spectral bias and common-mode noise in turbulence modeling. The authors propose a dual-branch architecture that combines a salient flow attention module with a frequency-guided Fourier mixing module, further enhanced by a pre-trained masked autoencoder for high-frequency feature representation. The core idea is to explicitly mitigate spectral bias by incorporating frequency-aware learning mechanisms, while also addressing common-mode noise through a differential attention approach. The proposed method is evaluated on two turbulent flow datasets from PDEBench: the Compressible Navier-Stokes and Shear Flow datasets. The empirical results demonstrate that FourierFlow achieves superior performance compared to existing methods, particularly in long sequence generation and generalization to out-of-distribution initial conditions. The authors also provide a theoretical analysis of spectral bias in generative models, which serves as a foundation for their proposed approach. The paper's main contribution lies in its novel architecture and the explicit attempt to address spectral bias and common-mode noise, which are significant challenges in the field of turbulence modeling. The authors claim that their method achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple turbulence scenarios, and they provide ablation studies to validate the effectiveness of each component. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, providing a clear motivation for the work and a thorough explanation of the proposed method. However, as I will discuss in detail, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened, particularly in terms of providing more comprehensive empirical evidence and detailed architectural specifications. Despite these limitations, the paper presents a promising approach to generative modeling for fluid dynamics, and the results suggest that the proposed method has the potential to advance the field.
