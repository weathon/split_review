## Summary:

This paper introduces TSPulse, a novel family of compact, pre-trained time-series models designed for diagnostic tasks. The core contribution lies in its ability to learn disentangled representations across time, frequency, and semantic spaces, enabling the model to capture complementary cues from different perspectives. TSPulse employs a TSMixer backbone and multiple heads for distinct objectives, such as reconstruction and semantic signature prediction. The pre-training framework combines masked reconstruction with explicit disentanglement objectives, and lightweight post-hoc fusers are introduced to selectively combine the disentangled views based on the task type. The authors demonstrate that TSPulse achieves strong zero-shot and fine-tuned performance across various time-series diagnostic tasks, including anomaly detection, imputation, classification, and similarity search, outperforming larger models while maintaining efficiency and supporting GPU-free deployment. The model's compact size, with only 1 million parameters, makes it suitable for real-time, low-latency applications, especially in resource-constrained environments. The authors emphasize the model's ability to enable zero-shot transfer, which is a significant advantage for practical applications where labeled data is scarce. The paper presents a well-motivated approach to time-series representation learning, focusing on the specific needs of diagnostic tasks, and provides empirical evidence of its effectiveness across a range of benchmarks. The use of disentangled representations and task-specific fusers is a key methodological innovation, allowing the model to adapt to different task requirements. The authors also introduce a hybrid masking strategy during pre-training, which randomizes both masking types and span lengths, enhancing pre-training robustness and mitigating mask-induced bias. The paper's findings suggest that compact, task-specialized models can achieve competitive performance with larger models, offering a more efficient and practical solution for time-series diagnostic tasks. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of time-series analysis by introducing a compact, efficient, and effective pre-trained model for diagnostic tasks, with a focus on disentangled representations and zero-shot transfer capabilities.

While the authors demonstrate strong performance on anomaly detection, classification, imputation, and similarity search, they do not explore the model's performance on other crucial time-series tasks, such as forecasting. This is a significant limitation, as forecasting is a fundamental application of time-series analysis, and the paper does not provide any evidence that TSPulse can handle it effectively. The model's architecture, particularly the decoder and task-specific heads, seems tailored for diagnostic tasks, and it is unclear how these components would adapt to forecasting, which requires different output structures and loss functions. Furthermore, the paper lacks a discussion on how the model handles long-term dependencies, which are crucial for many real-world time-series applications. The absence of experiments with longer, more complex time series data makes it difficult to assess the model's applicability in such scenarios.
