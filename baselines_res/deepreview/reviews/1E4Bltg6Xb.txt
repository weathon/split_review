## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel Dynamics Feature Representation (DFR) framework designed to enhance reinforcement learning (RL) for dynamic path planning (DPP) in urban road networks. The core challenge addressed is the trade-off between information completeness and computational efficiency when representing dynamic traffic conditions. The DFR framework tackles this by progressively refining high-dimensional global traffic dynamics into compact, decision-relevant features. This is achieved through a two-pronged approach: first, a policy attention mechanism identifies a core subset of dynamics based on a distance-oriented policy, effectively highlighting the most relevant information for path planning. Second, an n-hop neighborhood method further decouples this subset into node-related local feature sequences, providing a localized context for decision-making. The framework leverages a pre-trained, distance-based policy to extract a subgraph of relevant edges, which is then used to compute the dynamics features. The authors evaluate the DFR framework using standard RL algorithms, including Deep Q-Networks (DQN), Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and a Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) enhanced DQN, on real-world urban road networks from Nanjing, Chaoyang (Beijing), and Pudong (Shanghai). The experimental results demonstrate that the DFR framework significantly improves the performance of these RL algorithms in terms of both convergence speed and solution quality, as measured by metrics such as Mean GAP, Success Rate (SR), and Compactness Rate (CR). Specifically, the DFR-enhanced models show faster convergence and lower Mean GAP values compared to their counterparts without DFR. The paper also includes an ablation study to analyze the impact of key hyperparameters, such as the number of shortest paths (k) and the size of the n-hop neighborhood (n), on the overall performance. The authors claim that the DFR framework incurs negligible additional computational overhead due to the offline computation of the policy attention and n-hop neighborhoods, which depend solely on the fixed road network topology. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to addressing the challenges of dynamic path planning using RL, with a focus on efficient state representation and practical applicability to real-world scenarios. The core contribution lies in the novel combination of policy attention and n-hop neighborhoods to create a compact yet informative state representation for RL-based DPP.
