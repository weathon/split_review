## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel framework, Physics-Aware Tensor Field Neural PDE (PA-TFNP), for weather and climate prediction, aiming to bridge the gap between purely data-driven models and physics-based simulations. The core idea is to integrate physical principles into a deep learning architecture, specifically by employing rotation-equivariant tensor-field neural operators on the sphere, a numerically rigorous spherical-transform-based gradient operator with physically consistent boundary conditions, and diffusion terms derived from the atmospheric primitive equations. The authors argue that this approach enhances both the accuracy and stability of predictions, particularly for long-term forecasting. The PA-TFNP model is evaluated against several baselines, including ClimODE, a state-of-the-art data-driven neural ODE model, as well as ClimaX and NODE. The empirical results, presented across various weather and climate prediction tasks, demonstrate that PA-TFNP achieves superior performance compared to these baselines. The paper emphasizes the importance of incorporating physical constraints into deep learning models for improved predictive capabilities in geophysical forecasting. The authors highlight the model's ability to maintain physical fidelity while achieving efficient learning, suggesting a promising direction for future research in this domain. The methodology involves a detailed description of the model architecture, including the tensor-field neural network, the spherical gradient operator, and the diffusion dynamics. The experimental section presents results for global weather forecasting, short-term regional weather forecasting, and monthly averaged weather forecasting, showcasing the model's performance across different spatial and temporal scales. The authors also conduct ablation studies to analyze the impact of different components of the model, such as the physics-aware modifications and the rotation-equivariant architecture. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and technically sound approach to incorporating physics into deep learning models for weather and climate prediction, with empirical results supporting the effectiveness of the proposed framework. However, as I will detail in the following sections, there are several areas where the paper could be improved to further strengthen its contributions and impact.

This approach allows the model to better capture the dynamics of atmospheric processes on a spherical domain, which is a notable improvement over traditional Euclidean-based methods. The authors' decision to incorporate a numerically rigorous spherical-transform-based gradient operator with physically consistent boundary conditions further enhances the model's physical fidelity. This ensures that the model's computations are consistent with the underlying physics of the atmosphere, leading to more reliable predictions. Furthermore, the inclusion of diffusion terms derived from the atmospheric primitive equations is a valuable contribution, as it allows the model to account for subgrid-scale processes that are crucial for accurate long-term forecasting.
