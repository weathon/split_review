## Summary:

This paper introduces an innovative automated pipeline for extracting, monitoring, and controlling 'persona vectors' within large language models (LLMs). These vectors, representing specific personality traits in the model's activation space, are derived from contrastive prompts designed to elicit target behaviors. The core methodology involves generating these prompts, computing activation differences, and then using the resulting vectors to monitor and manipulate model behavior. The authors demonstrate the utility of these persona vectors in several key areas. First, they show that these vectors can effectively track shifts in model persona during deployment and fine-tuning. Second, they propose a preventative steering method that limits undesirable persona drift during fine-tuning, preserving the model's general capabilities. Finally, they demonstrate that fine-tuning-induced persona shifts can be predicted by analyzing training data projections onto these persona vectors, enabling proactive mitigation strategies. The empirical findings are compelling, showcasing the effectiveness of the proposed approach across various traits, including 'evil,' 'sycophancy,' and 'hallucination,' using models like Qwen 2.5 7B and Llama 3.1 8B. The paper's significance lies in its automated approach to understanding and controlling LLM behavior, offering a practical framework for mitigating undesirable traits and enhancing model reliability. The authors present a comprehensive approach, covering the extraction of persona vectors, their application in monitoring and control, and the development of a preventative steering method. The ability to predict persona shifts before fine-tuning is a particularly valuable contribution, opening up possibilities for proactive model management. Overall, the paper presents a significant step forward in our ability to understand and control the behavior of large language models, with potential implications for a wide range of applications.

## Strengths:

I found several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. The most compelling is the automated pipeline for extracting persona vectors from natural language trait descriptions. This approach is both novel and generalizable, allowing for the analysis of multiple traits without manual intervention. The use of contrastive prompts to elicit target behaviors and then compute activation differences is a clever and effective technique. The paper also provides extensive empirical evidence supporting the effectiveness of persona vectors across multiple applications. The authors demonstrate that these vectors can be used to monitor shifts in model persona during deployment and fine-tuning, to mitigate undesirable shifts during fine-tuning, and to flag problematic training data. The preventative steering method, which limits unwanted persona drift during fine-tuning while preserving general capabilities, is another significant contribution. This method outperforms prompt-based baselines, highlighting its practical value. The ability to predict fine-tuning-induced persona shifts before training begins is a particularly innovative aspect, opening up possibilities for proactive mitigation strategies. ## Suggestions:

To address the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements. First, the paper should incorporate more human evaluations, particularly for traits that are challenging to assess automatically, such as sycophancy. This could involve recruiting human annotators to assess the trait expression of model responses, using a detailed rubric to ensure consistency. The authors should also explore different evaluation metrics that are less susceptible to bias, such as those that focus on the semantic content of the responses rather than just the stylistic choices. Furthermore, they should investigate the correlation between LLM-based evaluations and human evaluations to better understand the limitations of the automated approach. This would provide a more robust and reliable assessment of the method's performance. Second, the paper should include a more detailed discussion of the multi-layer steering results in the main text, comparing its performance with the single-layer approach. This should include an analysis of the performance of multi-layer steering across different layers and a discussion of the trade-offs between effectiveness and computational cost. The paper should also compare the performance of multi-layer steering with other baselines, such as regularization techniques, to determine the most effective approach for mitigating undesirable traits. Third, the paper should provide a more detailed comparison with previous work on activation steering, particularly concerning the specific techniques used for identifying and manipulating directions in activation space. The paper should clarify how the proposed method for extracting persona vectors differs from existing approaches and discuss the advantages and disadvantages of each method. This could involve comparing the proposed automated pipeline for generating contrastive prompts with the manual approach used in other studies and discussing the impact of these differences on the quality of the extracted vectors. Fourth, the paper should acknowledge the limitations of using activation steering for complex traits like hallucination, where the issue is not just a persona shift but also a lack of grounding in knowledge. The paper should discuss the root causes of hallucination and acknowledge that activation steering might not be sufficient to address these causes. The authors should also explore other techniques, such as retrieval augmentation or knowledge editing, that might be necessary to mitigate hallucination. Fifth, the paper should conduct a more systematic exploration of the trait space, including both positive and negative attributes, as well as traits that are more nuanced and context-dependent. This could involve selecting a diverse set of traits and evaluating the effectiveness of the steering and monitoring methods across this expanded trait set. The authors should also provide a more detailed analysis of how the quality of the generated contrastive prompts impacts the resulting persona vectors. Sixth, the paper should provide a more detailed analysis of the computational cost of the proposed methods, particularly the preventative steering approach, and compare it to the computational cost of other methods. This would help to assess the practical feasibility of the approach for large-scale applications. Finally, the paper should explore the generalizability of the findings to different model architectures and sizes. This could involve conducting experiments on a wider range of models and discussing the potential impact of different model architectures or sizes on the effectiveness of the proposed methods. These changes would significantly strengthen the paper and provide a more complete and robust analysis of the proposed approach.
