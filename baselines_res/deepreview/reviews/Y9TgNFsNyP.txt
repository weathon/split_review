## Summary:

This paper introduces FF-Erase, a novel machine unlearning framework specifically designed for Forward-Forward (FF) models, which are emerging as biologically plausible alternatives to backpropagation-based models. The core contribution of this work lies in addressing the unique challenges of applying machine unlearning to FF models, which are trained layer-wise using goodness scores rather than gradients. The authors identify that existing unlearning methods, particularly those based on gradient ascent, are not directly applicable to FF models due to their sensitivity to parameter tuning and layer-wise independent training. To overcome these challenges, FF-Erase employs a goodness-guided approach, utilizing a guidance model to generate target goodness distributions that steer the original model to unlearn specific data points by shifting its layer-wise goodness scores. This approach aims to remove the influence of forgetting data while preserving the model's utility on the remaining data. The paper also introduces a goodness-based membership inference attack (G-MIA) as a verification method for FF unlearning. The experimental evaluation of FF-Erase is conducted on four standard image benchmarks: CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, MNIST, and Fashion-MNIST. The results demonstrate that FF-Erase effectively removes the influence of target forgetting data on FF models while preserving model utility on the remaining data, achieving significant speedups compared to retraining from scratch. Specifically, the experiments show that FF-Erase achieves comparable unlearning effectiveness to retraining while being 1.9-3.1× faster. The authors also explore the impact of different hyperparameters on the performance of FF-Erase, particularly the learning rate and the number of unlearning epochs. The paper concludes that FF-Erase provides an efficient and effective solution for machine unlearning in FF models, addressing a previously unexplored problem in this domain. The work is significant because it tackles the practical need for data removal in machine learning models, particularly in the context of the emerging Forward-Forward algorithm, and provides a practical solution that is faster than retraining from scratch.
