## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel data-driven approach to solving combinatorial optimization (CO) problems, specifically focusing on the NP-hard Max-Cut/Ising problem. The core idea revolves around learning the parameters of an iterative dynamical system, which is then used to efficiently sample high-quality solutions. The authors propose a neural network parameterization of this dynamical system, where the network's weights are optimized using a zeroth-order method. This approach, termed Neural Network Ising Machines (NPIM), aims to learn effective dynamics for navigating the complex solution spaces inherent in NP-hard problems. The paper explores both continuous and discrete versions of NPIM, finding that the discrete variant often achieves superior performance and generalization capabilities. The authors benchmark their method against both state-of-the-art neural CO approaches and classical Max-Cut/Ising algorithms, demonstrating competitive performance. The empirical results highlight the potential of the proposed method to learn efficient and scalable algorithms for solving combinatorial optimization problems. The paper also delves into the impact of various architectural choices and training strategies, providing insights into the factors that influence the performance of the proposed method. The authors acknowledge the limitations of their approach, particularly in terms of generalization to other CO problems beyond the Max-Cut/Ising problem and the computational cost associated with training. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of neural combinatorial optimization by introducing a novel approach that combines algorithm unrolling with neural networks and zeroth-order optimization. The work opens up new avenues for exploring the potential of data-driven methods for solving complex optimization problems, while also highlighting the challenges and limitations that need to be addressed in future research.

## Strengths:

I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly compelling. The core strength lies in the innovative approach of applying algorithm unrolling to the Max-Cut problem, which is a novel contribution to the field of neural combinatorial optimization. The authors' decision to parameterize the iterative dynamical system with a neural network, and then train this network using a zeroth-order optimization method, is a significant technical innovation. This approach allows the method to learn complex dynamics without relying on backpropagation, which can be challenging for non-differentiable problems. The empirical results presented in the paper are also a strong point. The authors demonstrate that their proposed method, particularly the discrete version (dNPIM), achieves competitive performance against both state-of-the-art neural CO methods and classical algorithms. This is a notable achievement, as it suggests that the learned dynamics can effectively navigate the complex solution spaces of NP-hard problems. Furthermore, the paper provides a thorough experimental evaluation, comparing the proposed method against a range of benchmarks from both the machine learning and dynamical Ising machine communities. The authors also explore the impact of different architectural choices and training strategies, providing valuable insights into the factors that influence the performance of their method. The analysis of the differences between the continuous and discrete versions of NPIM is also insightful, as it highlights the importance of the discrete formulation for achieving better generalization. The paper is well-written and clearly explains the proposed method and the experimental results. The authors also acknowledge the limitations of their approach and suggest directions for future research, which demonstrates a balanced and thoughtful approach to the problem. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of neural combinatorial optimization, and I believe that the proposed method has the potential to be applied to a wide range of CO problems.


## Weaknesses:

The authors themselves acknowledge this limitation, stating, 'Although we have tested our approach on a variety of benchmarks, these problem instances are synthetic and are constrained to the class of quadratic optimization over binary variables.' (Section 6). This narrow focus raises concerns about the generalizability of the proposed method to other, more complex combinatorial optimization problems, such as the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP) or the Quadratic Assignment Problem (QAP). The paper lacks any empirical evidence or detailed discussion on how the method could be adapted to problems with different structures or constraints, such as those involving non-binary variables or specific constraint satisfaction requirements. This limitation is significant, as it restricts the applicability of the proposed method and makes it difficult to assess its potential as a general-purpose algorithm for combinatorial optimization.
