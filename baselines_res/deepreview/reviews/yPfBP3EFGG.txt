## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel stochastic optimization algorithm, termed Stochastic Two-track Nesterov-accelerated Adaptive Momentum Estimation (STNAdam), designed to tackle nonconvex composite optimization problems. The core contribution lies in the development of a two-track iteration framework, which maintains both an extrapolation trajectory and a regular update trajectory, aiming to explore a larger update neighborhood and achieve better convergence properties. The algorithm leverages Nesterov momentum and adaptive learning rates, drawing inspiration from existing methods like Adam and NAdam, but combines them in a unique manner. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of the convergence properties of STNAdam under the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KL) property, a condition that encompasses a wide range of nonconvex functions. Specifically, they demonstrate that the sequence generated by STNAdam converges to a stationary point of the original problem at an explicit rate, depending on the KL exponent. The paper also presents empirical results on low-light image enhancement tasks, showcasing the superior performance of STNAdam compared to several baseline algorithms, including SGD, Adam, SAdam, SNAdam, and several specialized low-light image enhancement methods. The experiments include comparisons of STNAdam with different variance reduction techniques, such as SAGA and SARAH, further highlighting the algorithm's adaptability. The authors also demonstrate the joint denoising performance of STNAdam-SARAH. Overall, the paper presents a well-motivated and theoretically grounded algorithm with promising empirical results, contributing to the field of stochastic optimization for nonconvex composite problems. However, as I will discuss in the weaknesses section, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened through more comprehensive comparisons, analysis, and discussion.
