## Summary:

This paper introduces ConciseHint, a novel framework designed to enhance the efficiency of large reasoning models (LRMs) by reducing the length of their reasoning processes. Unlike existing methods that primarily focus on pre-reasoning optimization, ConciseHint intervenes during the reasoning process itself by injecting learnable hints, encouraging the model to generate more concise reasoning steps. The core idea is to dynamically adjust the intensity and position of these hint injections based on the complexity of the input query, aiming to maintain accuracy while reducing token usage. The framework operates by first identifying the complexity of the query and then adaptively controlling the injection interval, which is the number of tokens between two adjacent hint injections. The injection position is also dynamically selected to avoid accuracy degradation and minimize computational overhead. The authors propose two variants of the framework: ConciseHint, which uses manually designed hints, and ConciseHint-T, which uses learned hint embeddings trained on a concise reasoning dataset. The learned embeddings are then interpolated with the original hint embeddings to control the degree of conciseness. The empirical evaluation of ConciseHint is conducted on several benchmarks, including GSM8K (math), AIME24 (math), and GPQA-Diamond (science reasoning), using state-of-the-art LRMs such as Qwen3 and DeepSeek-R1. The results demonstrate that ConciseHint effectively reduces token usage while maintaining accuracy, and that ConciseHint-T further improves performance by learning task-specific hint embeddings. The paper also includes ablation studies that analyze the impact of different injection intervals and positions, as well as the effect of the interpolation parameter gamma in ConciseHint-T. The authors further demonstrate the generalizability of the approach by including results on CommonsenseQA and HumanEval in the appendix. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to improving the efficiency of LRMs, offering a practical method for reducing token usage without compromising accuracy. The adaptive nature of the framework, along with the use of learned hint embeddings, makes it a valuable contribution to the field of efficient reasoning with large language models.

## Strengths:

The primary strength of this paper lies in its innovative approach to improving the efficiency of large reasoning models (LRMs) by directly intervening during the reasoning process. Unlike many existing methods that focus on pre-reasoning optimization, ConciseHint introduces a novel mechanism of injecting learnable hints to encourage conciseness. This is a significant contribution because it addresses a critical limitation of current approaches, which often fail to control the verbosity of the reasoning process itself. The adaptive adjustment of hint intensity and position based on query complexity is another notable strength. This demonstrates the authors' attention to the balance between efficiency and accuracy, ensuring that the model does not sacrifice performance in its pursuit of conciseness. The dynamic control of the injection interval, which is the number of tokens between two adjacent hint injections, is a clever way to tailor the intervention to the specific needs of each query. The paper also presents a thorough empirical evaluation of the proposed framework. The experiments are conducted on multiple benchmarks and models, demonstrating the effectiveness of ConciseHint across different reasoning tasks and model architectures. The results consistently show a reduction in token usage while maintaining accuracy, which is a compelling demonstration of the framework's practical value. The inclusion of ablation studies further strengthens the paper by providing insights into the impact of different design choices, such as the injection interval, injection position, and the interpolation parameter gamma. These studies help to validate the effectiveness of the adaptive strategy and the learned hint embeddings. The introduction of ConciseHint-T, which uses learned hint embeddings, is another significant contribution. This approach allows the framework to adapt to specific tasks and datasets, potentially leading to further improvements in efficiency and accuracy. The generalizability of the approach is also demonstrated by the inclusion of results on CommonsenseQA and HumanEval in the appendix, showing that the framework is not limited to math and science reasoning. Finally, the paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear explanations of the methodology and experimental setup. This makes the proposed method and its advantages accessible to a wide audience.
