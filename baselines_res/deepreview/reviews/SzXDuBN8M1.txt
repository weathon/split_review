## Summary:

This paper introduces TD-JEPA, a novel approach to unsupervised reinforcement learning that leverages temporal difference (TD) learning to learn representations predictive of long-term latent dynamics across multiple policies and tasks from offline, reward-free transitions. The core idea is to train explicit state and task encoders, a policy-conditioned multi-step predictor, and a set of parameterized policies directly in the latent space. This enables zero-shot optimization of any reward function at test time. The method is grounded in the idea of learning successor features in the latent space, which allows for efficient policy optimization for a wide range of tasks. The authors provide a theoretical analysis demonstrating that an idealized variant of TD-JEPA avoids collapse with proper initialization and learns encoders that capture a low-rank factorization of long-term policy dynamics. Empirically, TD-JEPA is evaluated on a diverse set of tasks, including locomotion, navigation, and manipulation, across 13 datasets in ExoRL and OGBench. The results show that TD-JEPA matches or outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, particularly in the challenging setting of zero-shot RL from pixels. The paper also includes detailed ablation studies and visualizations, providing further insights into the behavior and performance of the proposed method. The authors emphasize the ability of their method to learn effective representations for zero-shot RL, which is a significant advancement over existing methods that are often limited to single-task learning, one-step prediction, or on-policy trajectory data. The combination of TD learning with latent-predictive representations allows for learning representations that are predictive of long-term latent dynamics across multiple policies and tasks from offline, reward-free transitions. This is a significant advancement over existing methods that are typically limited to single-task learning, one-step prediction, or on-policy trajectory data. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear explanations of the motivation, methodology, and results. The authors clearly explain the motivation, methodology, and results of their work, making it accessible to a broad audience. The paper is also well-organized, with clear sections and subsections, and provides sufficient background information to understand the context of the work. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to unsupervised RL with strong empirical results and a solid theoretical foundation, making a significant contribution to the field.
