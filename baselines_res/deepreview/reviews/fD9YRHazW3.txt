## Summary:

This paper introduces In-Context Watermarking (ICW), a novel approach for embedding watermarks into text generated by Large Language Models (LLMs) using prompt engineering alone, without requiring access to the model's decoding process. The core idea behind ICW is to leverage the in-context learning and instruction-following capabilities of LLMs to embed watermarks directly through carefully crafted prompts. The authors propose four distinct ICW strategies—Unicode, Initials, Lexical, and Acrostics—each operating at different levels of granularity and paired with tailored detection methods. These strategies range from inserting invisible Unicode characters to structuring text based on predefined secret keys. The paper explores the effectiveness of ICW in two primary settings: Direct Text Stamping (DTS), where watermarks are embedded directly through prompts, and Indirect Prompt Injection (IPI), where watermarks are covertly triggered by modifying input documents. The IPI setting is motivated by the scenario of detecting AI-generated academic reviews, where conference organizers might embed watermarking instructions in submitted papers, which are then picked up by LLMs used by reviewers. The authors evaluate the proposed ICW methods across various criteria, including detection accuracy, robustness against text transformations, and the impact on text quality. The experiments demonstrate promising performance, particularly as LLM capabilities improve, suggesting that ICW is a viable, model-agnostic approach for watermarking LLM-generated text. The paper also discusses the limitations of current ICW methods and highlights future research directions, such as improving watermarking instructions and treating ICW as a new alignment task. Overall, the paper presents a compelling and practical method for watermarking LLM outputs, addressing a critical need for provenance and accountability in the age of powerful language models.

## Strengths:

I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. The most significant is the introduction of In-Context Watermarking (ICW) itself, which offers a novel and practical approach to embedding watermarks into LLM-generated text without requiring access to the model's decoding process. This model-agnostic nature is a major advantage, making ICW broadly applicable across different LLMs, including closed-source models where direct access is not feasible. The exploration of four distinct ICW strategies—Unicode, Initials, Lexical, and Acrostics—is another strength. Each strategy operates at a different level of granularity and is paired with a tailored detection method, providing a comprehensive analysis of the trade-offs between different approaches. The paper's evaluation of these strategies in both Direct Text Stamping (DTS) and Indirect Prompt Injection (IPI) settings is also commendable. The IPI setting, motivated by the practical scenario of detecting AI-generated academic reviews, highlights the real-world relevance of ICW. Furthermore, the experiments demonstrate promising performance in terms of detection accuracy, robustness, and text quality, particularly as LLM capabilities improve. The authors also acknowledge the limitations of current ICW methods and highlight future research directions, which shows a balanced and thoughtful approach. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, making the complex technical details accessible to a broad audience. The inclusion of an ethics statement, although brief, is also a positive aspect, demonstrating an awareness of the potential ethical implications of this technology. Finally, the authors' commitment to releasing their code and data further enhances the value of their work, promoting reproducibility and future research in this area.


## Weaknesses:

Despite its strengths, I have identified several weaknesses in this paper that warrant careful consideration. First, the paper lacks a detailed analysis of the computational overhead associated with the proposed ICW methods. While the paper focuses on the effectiveness of the watermarking methods in terms of detectability, robustness, and text quality, it does not delve into the time and memory requirements for both embedding and detecting the watermarks. This is a significant omission, especially for practical applications involving large-scale text generation tasks. The absence of a computational cost analysis makes it difficult to assess the practical feasibility of ICW in real-world scenarios. Second, the evaluation of ICW methods is limited in terms of the range of applications considered. While the paper evaluates ICW in the context of question answering (through the DTS setting) and academic peer review (through the IPI setting), it does not explore other potential applications, such as code generation or creative writing. This limited scope makes it difficult to assess the generalizability of the proposed approach. Furthermore, while the paper evaluates robustness against paraphrasing, the analysis could be more thorough. The paper does not explore the robustness of the watermarks against more sophisticated adversarial attacks, such as sentence reordering or more complex semantic transformations. This lack of a more thorough robustness analysis raises concerns about the practical utility of the proposed approach in adversarial settings. Third, the paper does not adequately address the potential for ICW methods to be used for malicious purposes. While the paper includes a brief ethics statement, it does not delve into specific malicious uses, such as embedding hidden messages or tracking user behavior without their consent. The paper also lacks a detailed discussion of potential safeguards to prevent such misuse. The absence of a more thorough discussion of the ethical implications of ICW is a significant weakness. Fourth, the paper acknowledges that the clarity of the watermarked text is generally lower than the unwatermarked text, but it does not provide a sufficient analysis of the impact of this on the overall quality of the text. The decrease in clarity is a significant concern, as it suggests that the watermarking process is not entirely transparent and may introduce unwanted artifacts into the generated text. The paper needs to provide a more detailed analysis of the trade-off between watermark detectability and text quality. Fifth, the paper's motivation and the IPI setting are not entirely clear. While the paper explains the limitations of existing methods and the need for a model-agnostic approach, the specific advantage of watermarking the *review* rather than the *paper* could be more explicitly justified. The IPI setting, while explained, might seem convoluted without a clear understanding of the threat model (dishonest reviewers using external LLMs). Finally, the detection performance of some ICW methods, particularly Acrostics ICW with `gpt-4o-mini`, is worse than the baselines in certain settings. The paper acknowledges this but could provide a more in-depth analysis of the reasons behind this underperformance and potential mitigation strategies. The lack of a detailed theoretical analysis of the proposed ICW methods is also a weakness. The paper does not provide a formal mathematical analysis, proofs, or discussion of information-theoretic limits, which would be valuable for understanding the fundamental properties of the proposed approach.


## Suggestions:

Based on the identified weaknesses, I recommend several concrete improvements for this paper. First, the authors should conduct a thorough analysis of the computational overhead associated with the proposed ICW methods. This analysis should include a breakdown of the time and memory requirements for both embedding and detecting the watermarks, as well as how these scale with the length of the input text and the complexity of the watermark. A comparison with existing watermarking methods in terms of computational efficiency would also be valuable. Second, the authors should expand the evaluation of ICW methods to include a wider range of applications, such as code generation and creative writing. This would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the generalizability of the proposed approach. Furthermore, the authors should evaluate the robustness of the watermarks against more sophisticated adversarial attacks, such as sentence reordering, semantic transformations, and adversarial training to remove the watermark. This would provide a more realistic assessment of the method's resilience. Third, the authors should include a more detailed discussion of the ethical implications of ICW methods, including potential malicious uses and safeguards to prevent misuse. This discussion should include specific examples of how ICW could be used for harmful purposes and propose concrete measures to mitigate these risks. Fourth, the authors should conduct a more detailed analysis of the impact of watermarking on text quality, including metrics such as coherence, fluency, and semantic similarity to the original text. The authors should also explore methods for improving the text quality of the watermarked text, such as using different watermarking parameters or post-processing techniques. Fifth, the authors should clarify the motivation for the IPI setting and provide a more explicit justification for watermarking the review rather than the paper. This could involve a more detailed explanation of the threat model and the specific advantages of the proposed approach in this context. Sixth, the authors should provide a more in-depth analysis of why some ICW methods underperform in certain scenarios and explore potential mitigation strategies. This could involve investigating the specific factors that contribute to the lower detection performance of Acrostics ICW with `gpt-4o-mini` and exploring alternative watermarking strategies. Finally, the authors should provide a more rigorous theoretical analysis of the proposed ICW methods, including mathematical proofs and bounds on the detectability and robustness of the watermark. This would provide a more solid foundation for the proposed method and would help to identify its limitations and potential areas for improvement.
