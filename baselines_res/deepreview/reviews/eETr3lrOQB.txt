## Summary:

This paper introduces VQ-Transplant, a novel framework designed to facilitate the efficient integration of new Vector Quantization (VQ) modules into pre-trained visual tokenizers. The core idea revolves around replacing the VQ module of a frozen pre-trained tokenizer while preserving the encoder and decoder parameters, thereby circumventing the need for computationally expensive end-to-end retraining. To address potential mismatches between the new VQ module and the existing decoder, the authors propose a lightweight decoder adaptation strategy. This strategy involves fine-tuning the decoder using a combination of reconstruction, perceptual, and adversarial losses. Furthermore, the paper introduces MMD-VQ, a new VQ method that leverages maximum mean discrepancy to align the distributions of features and codebook vectors, aiming to enhance compatibility with the VQ-Transplant framework. The authors demonstrate through empirical evaluations that VQ-Transplant achieves near state-of-the-art reconstruction fidelity while significantly reducing training costs. The experimental results, primarily conducted on the VAR tokenizer, show that the proposed method can effectively integrate various VQ modules, including Vanilla VQ, EMA VQ, Online VQ, Wasserstein VQ, and the proposed MMD-VQ. The paper's main contribution lies in its ability to enable rapid experimentation with novel quantization techniques by decoupling the VQ module from the rest of the tokenizer, thus reducing the computational burden associated with training such models from scratch. The authors also show that MMD-VQ, when used in conjunction with VQ-Transplant, achieves superior reconstruction fidelity compared to other VQ methods. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of visual tokenization by providing a practical and efficient approach for integrating new VQ modules into existing pre-trained models, thereby accelerating the development and deployment of advanced quantization techniques.

## Strengths:

The primary strength of this paper lies in the introduction of the VQ-Transplant framework, which offers a computationally efficient method for integrating new VQ modules into pre-trained visual tokenizers. This approach is particularly valuable because it eliminates the need for costly end-to-end retraining, a significant hurdle in the development of novel quantization techniques. By freezing the encoder and decoder parameters of a pre-trained tokenizer and only training the new VQ module, the authors have effectively decoupled the VQ module from the rest of the tokenizer, allowing for rapid experimentation. This is a significant contribution, as it reduces the computational resources needed for developing and testing new VQ techniques. The introduction of MMD-VQ, a novel VQ method that leverages maximum mean discrepancy to align feature and codebook distributions, is another notable strength. This method not only improves compatibility with the VQ-Transplant framework but also achieves superior reconstruction fidelity compared to other VQ methods, as demonstrated in the experimental results. The empirical evaluation of VQ-Transplant is comprehensive, showing its effectiveness across various VQ algorithms and datasets. The results consistently demonstrate that the framework achieves near state-of-the-art reconstruction fidelity while significantly reducing training costs. The paper also provides a detailed analysis of the decoder adaptation strategy, which is crucial for addressing the mismatch between the new VQ module and the existing decoder. The authors have shown that fine-tuning the decoder with a combination of reconstruction, perceptual, and adversarial losses can effectively align the decoder with the new quantized latent space. The paper is also well-written and easy to follow, making it accessible to a broad audience. The authors clearly articulate the problem, the proposed solution, and the experimental results. The figures and tables are well-organized and provide a clear visualization of the findings. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of visual tokenization by providing a practical and efficient approach for integrating new VQ modules into existing pre-trained models, thereby accelerating the development and deployment of advanced quantization techniques.


## Weaknesses:

While the paper presents a compelling framework, several weaknesses warrant careful consideration. First, the method's reliance on a pre-trained tokenizer is a significant limitation. As the paper explicitly states in Section 4.1, the VQ-Transplant framework begins with a "pretrained discrete visual tokenizer." This dependence restricts the method's flexibility and increases the overall complexity of the workflow. The paper does not explore the performance of the method when starting from a randomly initialized tokenizer, which would be a valuable ablation study to understand the true impact of the proposed approach. This lack of investigation introduces a potential bias, as the performance of the method is inherently tied to the quality of the pre-trained model. The paper also lacks a detailed analysis of how the proposed method can be applied to VQ modules that do not have a specific uniqueness loss. As shown in Equation (3), the training objective for the new VQ module includes a uniqueness loss, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{unique}}$. While the paper provides examples of what this loss could be for specific VQ methods like Wasserstein VQ, it does not explicitly state how the uniqueness loss is applied or if it's even used when substituting with Vanilla VQ. The fact that "Vanilla VAR" is listed as a method tested in Table 3 suggests it was implemented, but the details of its training objective in the substitution phase are missing. This lack of clarity limits the applicability of the method to VQ modules that have a defined uniqueness loss, which is a significant constraint. The experimental results are also limited to the VAR tokenizer. As stated in the "Experiment Setup" section in Section 5, "This work investigates the VQ-Transplant framework using a pre-trained VAR tokenizer throughout all experiments." All tables in the Experiments section present results obtained using the VAR tokenizer or by transplanting into the VAR tokenizer. This narrow focus raises concerns about the generalizability of the proposed method to other types of visual tokenizers. The performance of the method might be highly dependent on the specific architecture and training procedure of the VAR tokenizer, and it is unclear how well it would perform with other tokenizers that have different characteristics, such as different encoder/decoder depths or attention mechanisms. The decoder adaptation strategy, while effective, is relatively simple. As described in Section 4.1, the decoder adaptation involves fine-tuning the decoder with a combination of reconstruction, perceptual, and adversarial losses. While the paper shows results for up to 20 epochs of adaptation, it does not explore more advanced techniques, such as knowledge distillation or more sophisticated fine-tuning methods. This lack of exploration limits the potential for further improvement in the decoder's performance. Finally, the paper lacks a detailed discussion of the limitations of VQ-Transplant. The paper primarily focuses on the strengths and benefits of the framework, without thoroughly exploring potential failure cases or scenarios where the method might struggle. For example, it is unclear how the method would perform with VQ modules that have significantly different codebook sizes or quantization strategies. This lack of discussion provides an incomplete picture of the method's applicability and potential drawbacks.


## Suggestions:

To address the identified weaknesses, several concrete improvements can be made. First, the authors should conduct experiments with randomly initialized tokenizers to evaluate the performance of VQ-Transplant in the absence of pre-training. This would provide a clearer understanding of the true impact of the proposed approach and help to isolate the benefits of the VQ-transplant method from the benefits of pre-training. It would also be beneficial to explore the possibility of using the proposed method to fine-tune a pre-trained tokenizer, rather than relying on a completely frozen encoder-decoder. This could potentially lead to better performance and reduce the dependence on the pre-trained model. Second, the paper should provide a more detailed analysis of how the proposed method can be applied to VQ modules that do not have a specific uniqueness loss. It would be beneficial to explore alternative loss functions or strategies that can be used in such cases. For example, the authors could investigate the use of a reconstruction loss or a perceptual loss to guide the training of the new VQ module when a uniqueness loss is not available. Additionally, the paper should discuss the limitations of the method when applied to different types of quantization methods, such as vector quantization with a continuous codebook, and provide insights into how the method could be adapted to handle such cases. This would significantly broaden the applicability of the proposed method and make it more robust. Third, the experimental evaluation should be expanded to include a wider range of tokenizers, including both discrete and continuous tokenizers. This would help to demonstrate the generalizability of the proposed method and provide a more comprehensive understanding of its performance. The paper should also compare the performance of the proposed method with other state-of-the-art methods for visual tokenization, including those that do not rely on VQ modules. This would help to establish the superiority of the proposed method and highlight its unique advantages. Fourth, the authors should explore more sophisticated decoder adaptation techniques to improve the performance of VQ-Transplant. Instead of simply fine-tuning the decoder for a few epochs, they could investigate methods like knowledge distillation, where the pre-trained decoder is used as a teacher to guide the adaptation of the new decoder. They could also explore more advanced fine-tuning techniques, such as adaptive learning rate schedules or regularization methods, to prevent overfitting and improve the stability of the adaptation process. A detailed analysis of the decoder's learning dynamics, including the evolution of its weights and activations during adaptation, would also provide valuable insights into the effectiveness of different adaptation strategies. Finally, the authors should provide a more detailed discussion of the limitations of VQ-Transplant, including specific scenarios where the framework might not perform well. This should include an analysis of how the method performs with VQ modules that have significantly different codebook sizes or quantization strategies. The authors should also investigate the impact of the quality of the pre-trained tokenizer on the performance of VQ-Transplant. A thorough discussion of these limitations would also guide future research in this area and help to improve the robustness and generalizability of the proposed framework.


## Questions:

Several questions arise from my analysis of this paper. First, how does the quality of the pre-trained tokenizer affect the performance of VQ-Transplant? Is there a threshold below which the framework's benefits diminish? This is important to understand the robustness of the method and its applicability to different pre-trained models. Second, can VQ-Transplant be applied to other modalities beyond vision, such as natural language processing or audio processing? If so, what modifications would be necessary? This question explores the potential for broader applicability of the proposed framework. Third, the paper mentions that adversarial training is inherently unstable. How does VQ-Transplant mitigate this instability when integrating new VQ modules that rely on adversarial training? This is crucial to understand the reliability of the method when used with adversarial training techniques. Fourth, what is the computational cost of the decoder adaptation process, especially when using more sophisticated techniques? This is important to assess the practical feasibility of the method and identify potential bottlenecks. Finally, how does the choice of the uniqueness loss affect the performance of the VQ module during the substitution phase, particularly when using Vanilla VQ? This question aims to clarify the impact of the uniqueness loss on the overall performance of the framework.
