## Summary:

This paper delves into the convergence properties of adaptive optimizers, particularly in non-convex settings, introducing the concept of adaptive smoothness to characterize their convergence behavior. The authors present a unified framework for analyzing a broad class of adaptive optimizers, including AdaGrad, AdaGrad-Norm, and one-sided Shampoo. A key technical contribution is a novel matrix inequality that relates the difference between two positive definite matrices to the difference between their logarithms. The paper establishes that adaptive smoothness enables an accelerated rate for adaptive optimizers with Nesterov momentum in the convex setting, and that adaptive variance can provide a dimension-free convergence rate for NSD with momentum in the non-convex setting. The analysis is rigorous and well-supported by empirical evidence, with experiments conducted on both deterministic and stochastic non-convex functions. The paper's core contributions lie in its theoretical advancements and the practical implications of these findings, which are demonstrated through detailed experimental setups and results. However, the paper could benefit from a more explicit discussion of its limitations and future research directions, as well as a clearer presentation of the experimental results to better highlight the practical significance of the theoretical findings.

While the paper provides a comprehensive theoretical framework and empirical validation, it does not explicitly address the assumptions under which the results hold or the potential challenges in applying these methods to more complex or high-dimensional problems.
