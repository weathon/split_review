## Summary:

This paper introduces Critique-RL, a novel two-stage reinforcement learning (RL) framework designed to train critique models that can assess and provide feedback on the outputs of language models, specifically focusing on mathematical reasoning tasks. The core contribution lies in the two-stage approach, which aims to enhance both the discriminability and helpfulness of the critique model without relying on stronger supervision signals. In the first stage, the critique model is trained to accurately judge the correctness of responses generated by an actor model. This is achieved through a reinforcement learning process that optimizes the critique model's ability to discern correct from incorrect solutions. The second stage focuses on improving the helpfulness of the critique by training it to provide feedback that guides the actor model towards better performance. This is accomplished by optimizing the critique model to suggest refinements that lead to improved solutions. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of Critique-RL through extensive experiments on a variety of mathematical reasoning datasets, including MATH, GSM8K, AQUA, SVAMP, and TheoremQA. These experiments show that the proposed approach leads to substantial performance improvements over baseline methods, indicating that the trained critique model can effectively guide the actor model towards more accurate solutions. The paper also explores the impact of an oracle verifier on the helpfulness of the critique model, showing that access to an oracle verifier can further enhance the performance of the actor model. The authors provide a thorough analysis of the limitations of existing RL methods for training critique models, highlighting the challenges of optimizing for both discriminability and helpfulness simultaneously. The proposed two-stage approach is presented as a solution to these challenges, offering a more effective way to train critique models for complex reasoning tasks. Overall, this paper presents a significant contribution to the field of language model training, offering a novel approach to developing critique models that can provide effective feedback for complex reasoning tasks. The two-stage RL framework is a notable innovation, and the empirical results demonstrate the potential of this approach to improve the performance of language models on challenging problems. However, as I will discuss in detail, the paper's evaluation is limited in scope, and there are several areas where further investigation is needed to fully understand the capabilities and limitations of the proposed method.

The two-stage approach, which separates the optimization of discriminability and helpfulness, is a clever innovation that allows the model to effectively learn both aspects of the critique process. The authors provide a clear and well-motivated explanation of the method, making it easy to understand the underlying principles and the rationale behind the design choices. ## Weaknesses:

Despite the strengths of this paper, there are several significant weaknesses that limit the scope and generalizability of the findings. The most prominent weakness is the limited diversity of tasks used in the experimental evaluation. As I have verified, the paper focuses almost exclusively on mathematical reasoning tasks, using datasets such as MATH, GSM8K, AQUA, SVAMP, and TheoremQA. While these datasets are challenging and widely used in the field, they do not represent the full spectrum of complex reasoning tasks that language models might encounter. The absence of experiments on tasks that require different types of reasoning, such as symbolic manipulation in coding or long-term planning in decision-making scenarios, is a major limitation. This narrow focus makes it difficult to assess the true scalability and generalizability of the proposed Critique-RL method. The paper claims that the method is designed to improve the performance of actor models on sequential decision-making tasks, but this claim is not supported by empirical evidence from tasks outside of mathematical reasoning. This lack of task diversity is a critical weakness, as it limits the applicability of the findings and raises questions about the method's effectiveness in other domains. The second major weakness is the insufficient evaluation of the proposed method on state-of-the-art models. While the paper includes experiments on models like Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen2.5-72B, Llama3.2, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, it notably lacks evaluations on highly optimized models such as GPT-4o. The absence of a comparison with GPT-4o makes it difficult to benchmark the proposed method against the current state-of-the-art performance. It is crucial to understand how Critique-RL performs relative to these highly optimized models, especially given their widespread use and established benchmarks. The paper's claims of effectiveness would be much stronger if the method were shown to improve performance on models like GPT-4o, or at least to be competitive with them. The lack of such a comparison leaves a significant gap in the evaluation and raises questions about the practical relevance of the proposed method. Finally, the paper does not provide a comprehensive discussion of the limitations of the proposed method. While the authors acknowledge the reliance on an oracle verifier during training, they do not delve into other potential limitations, such as the sensitivity of the method to the quality of the initial actor model, the computational cost associated with training and deploying the critique model, or the potential for the critique model to overfit to the specific characteristics of the training data. The paper also lacks a detailed analysis of the critique model's behavior, such as what aspects of the actor's output the critique model focuses on and how it learns to identify errors or areas for improvement. This lack of a thorough discussion of limitations makes it difficult to fully understand the applicability and potential drawbacks of the proposed method. The paper would benefit from a dedicated section that explicitly addresses these limitations and discusses potential avenues for future research to address them. These weaknesses, which I have verified through a close reading of the paper, significantly limit the impact and generalizability of the findings. The lack of task diversity, the insufficient evaluation on state-of-the-art models, and the limited discussion of limitations are all critical issues that need to be addressed in future work.

The inclusion of these diverse tasks would provide a more comprehensive assessment of the method's generalization capabilities and would help to identify potential limitations or areas where the method might need adaptation to be effective. For example, in coding tasks, the critique model would need to evaluate the correctness and efficiency of the generated code, while in planning tasks, it would need to assess the feasibility and optimality of the planned actions. These diverse evaluations would provide a more robust understanding of the method's strengths and weaknesses. Second, the authors should conduct a more thorough evaluation of the proposed method on state-of-the-art models, such as GPT-4o and Llama3.1. This would involve fine-tuning and evaluating Critique-RL on these models and comparing their performance with the results obtained on the current models. This comparison would provide valuable insights into the effectiveness of the proposed approach relative to the current state-of-the-art and would help to identify any potential limitations or areas for improvement. The evaluation should include a range of metrics, such as accuracy, convergence speed, and robustness, to provide a comprehensive comparison. Furthermore, the authors should consider reporting the computational resources required for training and evaluation on these models, as this is an important factor for practical applications. Third, the authors should include a dedicated section in the paper that explicitly discusses the limitations of the proposed method. This section should address potential drawbacks beyond the reliance on an oracle verifier, such as the sensitivity of the method to the quality of the initial actor model, the computational cost associated with training and deploying the critique model, and the potential for the critique model to overfit to the specific characteristics of the training data. The authors should also explore the sensitivity of the method to different hyperparameter settings, such as the learning rate and the strength of the regularization. This would help to ensure the robustness of the method and provide guidance for its application to other tasks and models. Furthermore, the authors should investigate the behavior of the critique model in more detail, analyzing what aspects of the actor's output the critique model focuses on and how it learns to identify errors or areas for improvement. This could involve analyzing the attention patterns of the critique model or visualizing the features it uses to make its decisions. Understanding the inner workings of the critique model would provide valuable insights into its strengths and weaknesses and could guide future research in this area. Finally, the authors should explore the potential for using the critique model to provide more informative feedback to the actor model, such as highlighting specific errors or suggesting concrete improvements. This could lead to more effective and efficient learning of the actor model. By addressing these limitations and incorporating these suggestions, the authors can significantly strengthen their work and make a more substantial contribution to the field.
