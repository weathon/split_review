## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel benchmark for evaluating model merging techniques in the context of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), addressing a critical gap in the field. The authors propose a new method, OptMerge, designed to enhance the merging process by reducing noise in task vectors and optimizing the merged vector through a carefully defined loss function. The core idea behind OptMerge is to improve the task vector optimization by applying low-rank approximations, which reduces redundant noise and leads to better performance. The paper presents a comprehensive set of experiments demonstrating that OptMerge outperforms existing merging methods across a diverse range of multimodal tasks, including VQA, Geometry, Chart analysis, OCR, and Grounding. These tasks are evaluated using both InternVL2.5 and Qwen2-VL models, with both full fine-tuning and LoRA fine-tuning approaches. The authors also explore the potential of model merging to combine different modalities, such as vision-language, audio-language, and video-language models, moving towards the concept of an Omni-language model. The empirical results suggest that model merging can outperform mixture training, offering a scalable approach to developing MLLMs with reduced computational cost and training time. The authors make their code and checkpoints publicly available, which promotes reproducibility and further research in this area. Overall, this work makes a significant contribution by providing a standardized benchmark for evaluating model merging techniques in MLLMs and introducing a novel method that demonstrates superior performance compared to existing approaches. The paper's focus on data-free merging and the potential for combining different modalities highlights the practical relevance and future potential of this research direction. The authors' efforts to provide a comprehensive evaluation across multiple tasks and modalities further strengthen the significance of their findings, positioning this work as a valuable contribution to the field of multimodal machine learning.
