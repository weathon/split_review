## Summary:

This paper introduces Guided Hybrid Policy Optimization (GHPO), a novel reinforcement learning framework designed to enhance the training of large language models (LLMs) for complex reasoning tasks, particularly in mathematics. The core motivation stems from the observed instability and inefficiency in training LLMs using traditional on-policy reinforcement learning methods, which the authors attribute to a capacity-difficulty mismatch and resulting reward sparsity. GHPO addresses this challenge by dynamically adjusting the difficulty of training examples through adaptive prompt refinement. Specifically, the method combines on-policy reinforcement learning for problems the model can handle with imitation learning, using partial ground-truth solution traces, for more challenging problems. This hybrid approach aims to provide a smoother and more effective learning process by balancing exploration and guided learning. The method employs an automated difficulty detection module that analyzes the sparsity of group-level rewards to identify challenging problems, triggering the integration of ground-truth hints into the prompt. The hint ratio, which determines the proportion of the ground-truth solution included, is dynamically adjusted based on the model's performance and the difficulty of the problems. The authors conducted experiments on six challenging mathematical benchmarks, demonstrating that GHPO achieves a consistent performance gain of approximately 5% over strong on-policy reinforcement learning and curriculum learning baselines. The paper also provides a detailed analysis of training dynamics, including metrics such as format reward, accuracy reward, mean response length, and gradient norm, offering valuable insights into the behavior of GHPO compared to the GRPO baseline. The authors claim that GHPO significantly improves both training stability and final reasoning performance, providing a more adaptive and efficient RL framework for LLMs. The method is presented as relatively simple to implement and not requiring significant computational overhead compared to GRPO, making it a practical approach for enhancing LLM reasoning capabilities. Overall, the paper presents a compelling approach to addressing the challenges of training LLMs for complex reasoning tasks, with a focus on dynamic difficulty adjustment and hybrid learning strategies.

This reliance on ground-truth data introduces a potential source of bias or variability, as the quality and consistency of these traces can significantly impact the learning process. This dependence on human-annotated or externally-sourced data makes the method less applicable in scenarios where such resources are scarce.
