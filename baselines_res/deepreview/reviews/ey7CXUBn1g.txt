## Summary:

This paper introduces AdaSVD, an adaptive SVD-based compression technique designed to mitigate the memory footprint of large language models (LLMs). The core contribution lies in two key components: adaComp, an adaptive compensation method for SVD truncation errors, and adaCR, an adaptive compression ratio assignment based on layer importance. The authors argue that traditional SVD-based compression methods, which often apply uniform compression ratios across all layers, are suboptimal. AdaSVD addresses this by iteratively refining the truncated SVD components using a calibration dataset and assigning layer-specific compression ratios based on the similarity between input and output activations. The method aims to improve the trade-off between compression ratio and model performance. The empirical evaluation demonstrates that AdaSVD achieves superior performance compared to existing SVD-based compression techniques across various LLMs and tasks, particularly at high compression ratios. The authors showcase results on language modeling datasets, common-sense reasoning tasks, and image captioning, indicating the method's versatility. The paper's significance lies in its attempt to make SVD-based compression more practical for LLMs by addressing the limitations of uniform compression and truncation errors. However, the paper's focus on performance gains is not fully balanced by a thorough analysis of the computational costs and practical implications of the proposed adaptive methods. While the results are promising, a deeper investigation into the computational overhead, the sensitivity to calibration data, and the impact on model parallelism is needed to fully assess the method's real-world applicability. The paper also lacks a detailed comparison with non-SVD-based compression techniques, which would provide a broader context for its performance. Despite these limitations, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of LLM compression by introducing a novel adaptive approach to SVD-based methods.

## Strengths:

I found several aspects of this paper to be commendable. The core idea of adaptively compensating for SVD truncation errors (adaComp) and assigning layer-specific compression ratios (adaCR) is both novel and well-motivated. The authors correctly identify the limitations of uniform compression ratios in traditional SVD-based methods and propose a more nuanced approach. The iterative refinement of the truncated SVD components using a calibration dataset, as implemented in adaComp, is a technically sound method for reducing compression errors. The use of layer importance, measured by the similarity between input and output activations, to guide the compression ratio assignment in adaCR is also a valuable contribution. The experimental results, which demonstrate the effectiveness of AdaSVD across multiple LLMs and tasks, are compelling. The method consistently outperforms existing SVD-based approaches, particularly at high compression ratios, indicating its potential for practical applications. The inclusion of an experiment integrating AdaSVD with the quantization method GPTQ is a positive step towards demonstrating the method's compatibility with other compression techniques. The paper is generally well-written and organized, with clear explanations of the methodology and results. The ablation study, which shows the impact of adaComp and adaCR, further supports the effectiveness of the proposed components. The introduction of the stack-of-batch strategy to handle limited GPU memory during the adaComp process is a practical consideration that enhances the method's usability. Overall, the paper presents a valuable contribution to the field of LLM compression by introducing a novel adaptive approach to SVD-based methods, and the empirical results demonstrate the potential of this approach.
