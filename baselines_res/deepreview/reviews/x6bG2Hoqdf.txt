## Summary:

This paper introduces CALM, a novel framework for automatic heuristic design (AHD) that combines the power of large language models (LLMs) with reinforcement learning (RL). The core idea behind CALM is to leverage both verbal and numerical guidance to fine-tune an LLM, enabling it to generate increasingly effective heuristics for combinatorial optimization problems. The framework maintains a population of heuristics that evolve over time through mutation and crossover operators, guided by a reward signal derived from the performance of the generated heuristics. A key component of CALM is the use of a memory-efficient RL algorithm called GRPO to fine-tune the LLM, allowing it to adapt and improve its heuristic generation capabilities. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of CALM on four combinatorial optimization problems: Online Bin Packing (OBP), Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP), Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP), and Orienteering Problem (OP). The empirical results show that CALM outperforms existing LLM-based AHD methods across these tasks, highlighting the potential of combining LLMs and RL for automated algorithm design. The paper also includes ablation studies to analyze the impact of different components of the framework, such as the reward function and the collapse mechanism, providing insights into the design choices and their effects on performance. The authors emphasize the resource efficiency of their approach, noting that CALM can be run on a single 24GB GPU with a quantized 7B model, making it accessible to a wider range of researchers. Overall, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of AHD by demonstrating a novel and effective approach that leverages the strengths of both LLMs and RL, and provides a thorough empirical evaluation of the proposed method.
