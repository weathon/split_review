## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel calibration procedure for constructing a lower prediction bound (LPB) for survival time under different treatments, specifically addressing the challenges posed by right-censored data. The core innovation lies in a reweighting scheme that transforms the problem into a weighted conformal inference problem, enabling the derivation of an LPB with exact marginal coverage guarantees, a significant improvement over the probably approximately correct (PAC) guarantees offered by previous methods. This approach is doubly robust, meaning that the LPB remains valid if either the weight function or the counterfactual quantile regression model is correctly specified, enhancing the reliability of the method. The authors achieve this by leveraging a potential outcome framework, incorporating a reweighting scheme that effectively addresses the complexities of right-censored data. The method's validity is supported by rigorous theoretical proofs, including the exact marginal coverage and the doubly robust property. The empirical evaluation of the proposed method is conducted using both synthetic and real-world clinical datasets. The results from these evaluations demonstrate the validity and informativeness of the constructed LPBs, showing that they are less conservative than existing methods while maintaining the desired coverage. Specifically, the authors show that the LPB under different treatments varies across patient populations, providing valuable insights for personalized treatment strategies. The paper's contribution is significant in the field of causal inference and survival analysis, as it provides a more robust and reliable method for predicting survival times under different treatment regimes, which is crucial for personalized medicine and clinical decision-making. The authors have successfully combined theoretical rigor with practical application, making a valuable contribution to the field. The paper is well-organized and clearly written, making the technical details accessible to readers with a background in causal inference and survival analysis. The combination of theoretical guarantees, practical applicability, and empirical validation makes this paper a valuable contribution to the field.
