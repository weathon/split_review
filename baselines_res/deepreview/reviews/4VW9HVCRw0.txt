## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel task called Free-Form Hand-Object Interaction (HOI) generation, which aims to generate diverse and physically plausible hand-object interactions based on fine-grained textual descriptions, moving beyond traditional grasp-centric approaches. To support this task, the authors have created WildO2, a large-scale dataset of 3D HOIs extracted from in-the-wild videos, encompassing a variety of objects, actions, and contact details. The core of their method, named TOUCH, is a three-stage framework. First, a two-stage conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE) is employed to predict contact maps for both the hand and the object. These contact maps, along with object point clouds and text embeddings, are then fed into a multi-level conditioned diffusion model to generate hand meshes parameterized by the MANO model. Finally, a refiner network, also based on a transformer architecture, is used to enforce physical constraints and refine the generated hand poses. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach through quantitative and qualitative experiments, comparing TOUCH against two baseline methods, ContactGen and Text2HOI, on the proposed WildO2 dataset. The results indicate that TOUCH achieves superior performance in generating realistic and semantically consistent HOIs. The paper's main contributions lie in the introduction of the free-form HOI generation task, the creation of the WildO2 dataset, and the development of the TOUCH framework, which leverages a multi-level diffusion model and explicit contact modeling to generate diverse and physically plausible HOIs. The authors also provide an ablation study to analyze the impact of different components of their framework. Overall, this work presents a significant advancement in the field of HOI generation by expanding the scope beyond grasping and introducing a large-scale dataset and a novel framework for generating diverse and realistic interactions.

## Strengths:

I find several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. First and foremost, the introduction of the Free-Form HOI generation task is a significant contribution. This task expands the scope of HOI generation beyond traditional grasping, opening up possibilities for more realistic and varied interactions in applications like VR/AR and robotics. This is a clear advancement over existing methods that primarily focus on grasp-centric interactions. Second, the creation of the WildO2 dataset is a major achievement. This large-scale, in-the-wild 3D HOI dataset, with its diverse range of objects, actions, and contact details, provides a valuable resource for the research community. The dataset's scale and diversity are crucial for training and evaluating models for the proposed free-form HOI generation task. The authors have clearly put in considerable effort to construct this dataset, and it will undoubtedly be a valuable asset for future research. Third, the proposed TOUCH framework is technically sound and well-designed. The three-stage process, which combines contact modeling, diffusion-based generation, and physical constraints, is a novel approach that effectively addresses the challenges of generating diverse and physically plausible HOIs. The use of a multi-level diffusion model, conditioned on text embeddings and contact maps, allows for fine-grained control over the generated interactions. The inclusion of a refiner network to enforce physical constraints further enhances the realism of the generated hand poses. Finally, the paper is well-structured and clearly explains the methodology, experiments, and results. The authors have done a good job of presenting their work in a clear and accessible manner, making it easy for readers with a background in the field to understand the key contributions and findings. The inclusion of both quantitative and qualitative results provides a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed method. These strengths collectively make this paper a valuable contribution to the field of HOI generation.
