## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel approach to the co-design of soft robot morphologies and controllers by integrating Graph Attention Networks (GATs) with Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL). The core idea revolves around representing robot morphologies as graphs, where nodes correspond to actuators and edges represent spatial relationships. This graph-based representation is then fed into a GAT, which learns a policy that is inherently aware of the robot's structure. The authors propose a morphology-aware controller inheritance mechanism, which allows for the transfer of learned policies between robots with different morphologies. This is achieved by mapping the weights of the GATs based on the correspondence between the nodes of the parent and child robot graphs. The proposed method is evaluated on the EvoGym benchmark, demonstrating improved performance compared to Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP)-based approaches. Specifically, the GAT-based policies exhibit greater adaptability to morphological changes, achieving higher final fitness scores in various evolutionary tasks. The authors argue that their approach addresses the limitations of traditional MLP-based methods, which struggle to adapt to changes in input dimensionality caused by variations in robot morphology. The paper's main contribution lies in the specific combination of GATs and DRL for soft robot co-design, along with the proposed morphology-aware inheritance mechanism. While the individual components are not entirely novel, their integration and application to this specific problem domain represent a significant step forward. The empirical results, although limited to a single simulation environment, provide compelling evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The paper is generally well-written and easy to follow, making it accessible to a broad audience. However, several limitations, particularly regarding the lack of comparisons with other state-of-the-art methods and the limited exploration of the GAT architecture, need to be addressed to further strengthen the contribution.

## Strengths:

I found several aspects of this paper to be particularly strong. First, the core idea of using a graph-based representation for robot morphologies and employing GATs for policy learning is both intuitive and effective. By representing robots as graphs, the authors create a flexible framework that can naturally handle variations in morphology, which is a significant challenge in traditional MLP-based approaches. This approach allows the controller to be inherently aware of the robot's structure, enabling more efficient adaptation to morphological changes. The proposed morphology-aware controller inheritance mechanism is another notable strength. By mapping weights between GATs based on the correspondence between parent and child robot graphs, the authors enable the transfer of learned knowledge across generations, which accelerates the learning process. This is a significant improvement over methods that rely on retraining controllers from scratch for each new morphology. The empirical results presented in the paper are also compelling. The GAT-based policies consistently outperform MLP-based baselines in terms of final fitness and adaptability to morphological changes. This provides strong evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The paper is generally well-written and easy to understand, which makes it accessible to a broad audience. The authors clearly explain their methodology and provide sufficient details for others to reproduce their results. The use of the EvoGym benchmark is also a strength, as it provides a standardized platform for evaluating the performance of the proposed method. Finally, the paper's focus on soft robot co-design is highly relevant, as it addresses a critical challenge in the field of robotics. The ability to simultaneously optimize both the morphology and the controller of soft robots has the potential to significantly advance the field.
