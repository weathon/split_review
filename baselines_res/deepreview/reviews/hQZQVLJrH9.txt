## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel theoretical framework that unifies two previously disparate approaches in neural network interpretability and control: activation steering and influence functions. The core contribution lies in demonstrating that, to the first order, these techniques are equivalent. Specifically, the authors prove that any steering vector, which is a perturbation applied to the intermediate activations of a neural network, can be represented as an influence weighting over the training data, and conversely, that any influence function can be represented as a steering vector. This equivalence is not merely a theoretical curiosity; it yields several practical benefits. First, it provides a method for mapping undesirable model behaviors back to specific training examples, allowing for targeted interventions. Second, it offers an optimal-control perspective on steering, revealing its inherent regularization properties. Finally, it provides generalization bounds for low-rank steering interventions, which are crucial for understanding the behavior of these interventions on unseen data. The authors achieve this unification through a rigorous mathematical framework, including proofs and theoretical guarantees, and introduce a practical diagnostic tool, the alignment measure γ, which helps determine the feasibility of steering. The method, termed Influence-Aligned Steering (IAS), leverages Jacobian-vector products and pseudo-inverses, making it scalable to large models. Empirically, the paper validates the proposed method on GPT-2 Medium for language tasks and ResNet-50 for vision tasks, demonstrating the practical applicability of the framework. The paper's significance lies in its ability to bridge the gap between two previously disconnected areas of interpretability research, offering a unified and efficient workflow for model controllability and data provenance. By establishing this duality, the authors provide a powerful tool for understanding and controlling the behavior of complex neural networks, with implications for both model debugging and the development of more robust and reliable AI systems. The paper's theoretical contributions are substantial, providing a solid foundation for future research in this area, while the practical tools and diagnostics offer immediate benefits for practitioners working with large-scale models.

## Weaknesses:

The authors explicitly state that their analysis is valid 'to first order,' and while they acknowledge the presence of higher-order terms, they do not delve into the limitations of this approximation. Specifically, the paper does not adequately address how the error accumulates with larger steering magnitudes or when the influence function is evaluated far from the initial parameter point. The practical implications of this approximation, such as the range of steering magnitudes for which the equivalence remains valid, are not clearly defined. For instance, Theorem 4.2 includes an $O(\alpha^2)$ term, indicating awareness of higher-order effects, but the paper lacks a detailed analysis of how these terms affect the accuracy of the equivalence. This is a critical point because the practical utility of the method depends on the range of perturbations for which the linear approximation is accurate. This analysis should provide a clear understanding of the limitations of the linear approximation and define the range of perturbations for which the proposed equivalence is valid. It would be beneficial to include empirical studies that demonstrate the accuracy of the first-order approximation for different perturbation sizes and model architectures. Furthermore, the authors should discuss how the choice of the perturbation magnitude affects the trade-off between the accuracy of the approximation and the effectiveness of the steering intervention. This discussion should include practical guidelines for selecting appropriate perturbation sizes in different scenarios. For example, the authors could explore the use of adaptive step sizes based on the magnitude of the influence weights, or provide a rule of thumb for selecting a maximum perturbation size based on the norm of the input or the sensitivity of the model. This would provide a more robust and practical framework for applying the proposed method. Second, to address the lack of empirical validation, the authors should conduct experiments on a variety of datasets and model architectures to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method for activation steering and influence function analysis. These experiments should include a comparison with existing methods and a detailed analysis of the results. The authors should also investigate the sensitivity of the method to different hyperparameters and provide practical guidelines for selecting appropriate values. For example, the authors could explore the impact of different choices of the damping parameter in the influence function calculation, or the effect of using different types of perturbations. The experiments should also include a quantitative evaluation of the alignment measure γ, and how it correlates with the effectiveness of the steering intervention. This would provide a more complete picture of the strengths and limitations of the proposed method and increase its impact on the field. The authors should also consider including experiments on more complex tasks, such as image classification or natural language processing, to demonstrate the generalizability of their approach. This would provide a more comprehensive evaluation of the method's performance across different domains. Furthermore, the authors should discuss potential strategies for mitigating these computational challenges, such as using approximations or distributed computing techniques. For example, the authors could explore the use of stochastic approximations of the Jacobian, or the use of low-rank approximations to reduce the memory footprint. The authors should also discuss the trade-offs between computational efficiency and accuracy when using these approximation techniques. A more detailed discussion of these practical considerations would make the paper more useful for practitioners.
