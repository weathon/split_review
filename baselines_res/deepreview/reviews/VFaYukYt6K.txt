## Summary:

This paper introduces a novel framework for motion planning that bridges the gap between deep learning and traditional model-based planning. The core idea revolves around learning a highly compressed, discrete, and causally-ordered latent space representation of trajectories using a conditional autoencoder. This latent space, composed of tokens, allows for efficient search and optimization of user-defined objectives without the need for retraining the model. The authors train this autoencoder on the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD), demonstrating its effectiveness in motion prediction, behavior generation, and multi-agent interaction modeling. The method leverages a greedy search strategy within the latent space to find trajectories that minimize a given objective function, which can be tailored to specific planning goals. The paper's key contribution lies in its ability to unify deep learning-based priors with model-based planning, offering a flexible and efficient approach to motion planning. The authors present a detailed explanation of the autoencoder architecture, the adaptive noise injection technique, and the greedy search strategy, supported by ablation studies and visualizations. The experimental results on the WOMD dataset show the method's ability to generate diverse behaviors and adapt to different objectives. The paper's significance lies in its potential to provide a general framework for motion planning that can be applied to various robotic tasks, combining the strengths of both deep learning and classical planning techniques. However, the paper's claims of generality are not fully supported by the experimental evidence, which is limited to autonomous driving scenarios. The paper also lacks a detailed comparison with state-of-the-art motion prediction and planning methods, particularly those that also leverage deep learning. Despite these limitations, the paper presents a promising approach to motion planning that warrants further investigation and development.
