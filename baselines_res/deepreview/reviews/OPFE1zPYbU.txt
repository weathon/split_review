## Summary:

This paper presents a novel perspective on diffusion models, challenging the conventional assumption that these models learn the underlying probability distribution of data. The authors propose that in high-dimensional and sparse scenarios, the objective function of diffusion models degrades, preventing them from effectively learning essential statistical quantities. Instead, they argue that diffusion models operate through a different mechanism, which they explore through a new inference framework called 'Natural Inference.' This framework unifies various existing inference methods and offers an intuitive, non-statistical understanding of the inference process. The core of their argument revolves around the phenomenon of 'weighted sum degradation,' where the fitting target of the diffusion model's objective function degrades from a weighted sum of multiple samples to a single sample due to data sparsity. This degradation, they contend, hinders the model's ability to learn the true data distribution. To support this claim, the authors analyze the posterior probability distribution and its degradation under high-dimensional sparse conditions, using ImageNet-256 and ImageNet-512 as examples. They introduce the concept of 'Natural Inference' as a framework that aligns with this degraded objective function, providing a unified and intuitive way to understand various existing inference methods. The paper's significance lies in its thought-provoking re-evaluation of established assumptions about diffusion models, potentially opening up new avenues for research and development in this field. By questioning the traditional view that diffusion models learn the underlying probability distribution, the authors encourage a deeper investigation into the actual mechanisms that enable these models to generate high-quality samples. The paper's analysis of the objective function in high-dimensional sparse scenarios and the introduction of the 'Natural Inference' framework are key contributions that add depth to the understanding of diffusion models. However, the paper also has limitations, particularly in the lack of empirical validation of the proposed framework and the absence of a rigorous definition of sparsity, which will be discussed in detail in the 'Weaknesses' section.

## Strengths:

This paper's primary strength lies in its novel perspective on diffusion models, which challenges the traditional understanding of their operational principles. The authors' argument that diffusion models do not learn the underlying probability distribution, particularly in high-dimensional sparse scenarios, is a significant contribution that encourages a re-evaluation of established assumptions. This is a valuable contribution to the field, as it opens up new avenues for research and development. The identification of the 'weighted sum degradation' phenomenon is a key insight that adds depth to the understanding of diffusion models. The authors provide a detailed analysis of the objective function in high-dimensional sparse scenarios, demonstrating how the fitting target degrades due to data sparsity. This analysis is supported by empirical evidence from ImageNet-256 and ImageNet-512 datasets, which demonstrates the prevalence of this phenomenon in practical applications. The proposed 'Natural Inference' framework is another significant contribution. It provides a unified and intuitive way to understand various existing inference methods, such as DDPM, DDIM, and others. This framework has the potential to simplify the development and optimization of diffusion models by offering a non-statistical perspective on the inference process. The paper is well-structured and clearly written, making it accessible to a broad audience. The authors provide detailed explanations of their analysis and framework, and the use of figures and tables enhances the clarity of the presentation. The paper's findings have important implications for the development and application of diffusion models. By recognizing the limitations of these models in high-dimensional sparse scenarios and providing a new perspective on their operational principles, the paper opens up new avenues for research and development in this field. The paper's ability to unify various existing inference methods under a single framework is a notable achievement that could lead to more efficient and effective development of diffusion models in the future. The paper's focus on the practical implications of the degradation phenomenon, particularly in the context of high-dimensional image data, makes it relevant to a wide range of applications.


## Weaknesses:

While the paper presents a compelling new perspective on diffusion models, several weaknesses limit its impact and generalizability. A primary concern is the paper's heavy reliance on the concept of 'sparsity' without providing a rigorous, quantitative definition. The authors use the term 'sparse data' extensively, particularly in the abstract and Section 3.2, to explain the 'weighted sum degradation' phenomenon. For instance, the abstract states, 'In high-dimensional sparse scenarios, the fitting target of the diffusion model's objective function degrades from a weighted sum of multiple samples to a single sample...' (Abstract). However, the paper lacks a formal, mathematical definition of sparsity. While the implicit definition through the discrete posterior (equation 14) is provided, a more explicit and formal definition is missing. This lack of a precise definition makes it difficult to assess the scope of the 'weighted sum degradation' phenomenon and to determine when it becomes dominant. The paper's analysis primarily focuses on high-dimensional sparse scenarios, which may limit the generalizability of its findings to other types of data and models. It is unclear how the proposed perspective and framework would apply to low-dimensional or dense data scenarios. The experiments in Section 3.2 are conducted on ImageNet-256 and ImageNet-512, which are high-dimensional datasets. There is no experimental validation on low-dimensional or dense datasets. This limitation is significant because it restricts the applicability of the paper's conclusions. The paper's central claim that diffusion models do not learn essential statistical quantities in high-dimensional spaces is presented without sufficient rigorous mathematical or experimental support. The authors argue that the 'weighted sum degradation' prevents the model from learning the true data distribution. While the paper provides a mathematical formulation of the posterior and the mean, a formal mathematical proof demonstrating that this degradation *prevents* learning of statistical quantities is not explicitly provided. The argument relies on the intuitive understanding of the degradation phenomenon and its implications, which is supported by empirical evidence but lacks a rigorous mathematical demonstration. The paper's analysis of the degradation phenomenon is primarily theoretical, and it would benefit from more extensive experimental validation. While the experiments in Section 3.2 provide empirical evidence for the degradation phenomenon, they do not directly compare performance on sparse vs. dense datasets or analyze behavior under varying levels of sparsity. The impact on the model's ability to learn the true data distribution is inferred but not directly measured through standard generative model evaluation metrics. The methodology for quantifying degradation is described, but its robustness and generalizability could be questioned. The paper also lacks a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed 'Natural Inference' framework. While the paper introduces this framework and explains its components, there is no quantitative comparison with existing inference methods in terms of performance metrics such as sample quality, diversity, or computational efficiency. There are no ablation studies to understand the impact of different components of the proposed framework. This lack of empirical validation makes it difficult to assess the practical significance of the proposed framework. The paper does not provide a detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed perspective and framework. For example, it is unclear how the 'Natural Inference' framework would handle complex data distributions or tasks that require more sophisticated statistical modeling. The framework's reliance on a degraded objective function might limit its ability to capture the full complexity of the data distribution, especially in cases where the underlying data structure is not well-represented by a single sample. The paper's focus on the degradation phenomenon might lead to a somewhat negative view of diffusion models, emphasizing their limitations rather than their strengths. While it is important to acknowledge the limitations, the paper could also explore potential ways to mitigate the degradation phenomenon or develop new methods that can effectively learn the true data distribution even in high-dimensional sparse scenarios. The paper does not discuss the potential trade-offs between the proposed framework and existing methods, especially in terms of computational cost and sample quality. Finally, the paper's presentation requires some improvement. The title 'Rethinking Diffusion Model in High Dimension' is too broad, given that the analysis primarily focuses on sparse data scenarios rather than high-dimensional spaces in general. Additionally, the paper suffers from instances of grammatical errors and areas where clarity could be improved. For example, in the introduction, the sentence 'This discrepancy prompts a fundamental inquiry: **This discrepancy raises a fundamental question: Do diffusion models truly learn these complex distributions and their statistical quantities as theoretically assumed? If not, why are they still able to generate high-quality samples? Could it be that diffusion models operate via a different underlying mechanism?**' is repetitive. The paper also lacks a well-structured discussion of its contributions. The authors should clearly articulate the significance of their findings and their impact on the field. This could include a discussion of potential applications, limitations, and directions for future research. The current discussion is too brief and does not fully explore the implications of the proposed framework. I have high confidence in these identified weaknesses, as they are directly supported by the paper's content and the lack of specific evidence or analysis.
