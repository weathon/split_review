## Summary:

This paper introduces a hybrid neural-physical system for real-time, interactive fluid simulations, aiming to bridge the gap between the high fidelity of numerical methods and the low latency of neural approaches. The core idea is to combine the strengths of both paradigms by using a graph neural network (GNN) to accelerate fluid simulations at low spatiotemporal resolutions, while employing a fallback mechanism to the Material Point Method (MPM) to ensure high fidelity when complex fluid phenomena arise. This hybrid approach is further enhanced by a diffusion-based generative controller, which allows for interactive fluid manipulation through user-friendly freehand sketches. The authors demonstrate that their system achieves significant latency reduction (11-29%) while maintaining low errors across diverse 2D/3D scenarios, material types, and obstacle interactions. The methodology involves training a GNN to predict fluid dynamics at a lower resolution, which is then used to accelerate the MPM simulation. When the predicted fluid behavior deviates significantly from the MPM simulation, the system falls back to the full MPM solver to maintain accuracy. The diffusion-based controller is trained using a reverse modeling strategy, where the goal is to generate external force fields that achieve a desired fluid manipulation based on user input. The authors evaluate their approach through a series of experiments, comparing the hybrid solver against both the original neural physics model and the MPM solver. The results show that the hybrid approach achieves a better balance between speed and accuracy than either method alone. The generative controller is evaluated based on its ability to achieve the desired fluid manipulation, with metrics focusing on the final state of the fluid. Overall, the paper presents a novel and effective approach to real-time fluid simulation, combining the strengths of neural and numerical methods to achieve both speed and accuracy. The integration of a generative controller further enhances the system's interactivity and usability. The authors demonstrate the robustness of their approach across a range of scenarios, highlighting its potential for various applications in computer graphics and beyond. However, as I will discuss in the weaknesses section, there are several areas where the paper could be improved to further strengthen its claims and impact.

## Strengths:

The primary strength of this paper lies in its innovative hybrid approach to fluid simulation, which effectively combines the advantages of neural physics and classical numerical solvers. By using a graph neural network (GNN) to accelerate fluid simulations at low spatiotemporal resolutions and a fallback mechanism to the Material Point Method (MPM) for complex scenarios, the authors have achieved a significant reduction in latency while maintaining high fidelity. This is a notable achievement, as it addresses a key challenge in real-time fluid simulation: balancing speed and accuracy. The paper also introduces a diffusion-based generative controller that enables interactive fluid control through user-friendly freehand sketches. This is a significant advancement, as it makes fluid simulations more accessible and controllable for users. The ability to manipulate fluids with simple sketches opens up new possibilities for interactive applications and creative tools. The system demonstrates robust performance across diverse 2D/3D scenarios, material types, and obstacle interactions, achieving real-time simulations at high frame rates with significant latency reduction (11-29%) while maintaining low errors. This is a testament to the effectiveness of the hybrid approach and the generative controller. The authors provide a thorough explanation of the hybrid simulation pipeline, the neural physics model, and the generative control mechanism. The paper is well-written and clearly explains the technical details of the proposed method, making it easy to follow and understand. The experimental results, presented in both quantitative and qualitative forms, effectively support the claims made by the authors. The inclusion of visualizations further enhances the understanding of the system's capabilities. The paper's focus on real-time, interactive fluid simulations is also a significant strength. This is a challenging problem with broad applications in computer graphics, game development, and other fields. The authors have made a valuable contribution by developing a system that can achieve both high fidelity and low latency, while also providing interactive control. The use of a diffusion-based generative model for fluid control is another notable contribution. This approach allows for a more intuitive and flexible way of interacting with fluid simulations compared to traditional methods. The reverse modeling strategy used to train the controller is also a clever innovation, as it allows the system to learn how to generate force fields that achieve a desired fluid manipulation. Overall, the paper presents a well-designed and effective system for real-time, interactive fluid simulations, with several novel contributions that advance the state of the art.


## Weaknesses:

While the paper presents a compelling approach to real-time fluid simulation, several weaknesses need to be addressed to further strengthen its claims and impact. Firstly, the paper lacks a detailed comparison with a broader range of state-of-the-art neural fluid simulation methods. While the authors compare their hybrid approach against a baseline "Original Neural Physics" method and MPM, a more comprehensive quantitative comparison against other established neural fluid simulation methods is needed. This is crucial to assess the true advancement of the proposed hybrid approach and to determine if the performance gains are significant compared to existing neural-based methods. The absence of such comparisons makes it difficult to determine if the hybrid approach offers a substantial improvement over existing methods or if the performance gains are marginal. This limitation is evident in the experimental results, where the focus is primarily on comparing the hybrid solver with its components, rather than with other state-of-the-art neural fluid simulation techniques. The paper also lacks a detailed analysis of the diffusion-based controller's performance, particularly regarding its robustness and generalization capabilities. While the authors provide quantitative results using Grid RMSE between ground truth and predictions at the last time step, and visualizations of the controller's performance, a more rigorous evaluation of the controller's ability to handle complex fluid manipulation scenarios is needed. The paper does not include a clear metric for evaluating the quality of the generated force fields beyond their effect on the final particle positions. Furthermore, the paper does not explicitly test the robustness of the controller to variations in input sketches, such as differences in stroke thickness, curvature, or noise. The generalization capabilities of the controller are also not thoroughly investigated, such as its ability to handle unseen control tasks or fluid properties. This lack of analysis makes it difficult to assess the practical utility of the controller in diverse and challenging scenarios. The paper also lacks a detailed analysis of the limitations of the proposed method, including potential failure cases and areas for future improvement. While the authors describe a fallback mechanism to MPM as a way to handle complex fluid dynamics, they do not explicitly test the sensitivity of the hybrid simulator to changes in simulation parameters, such as the time step size or the resolution of the simulation grid. The robustness of the diffusion-based controller to variations in input sketches is also not explicitly tested. The absence of such analysis limits the understanding of the method's applicability and potential drawbacks. The paper also lacks a detailed breakdown of the computational cost of the method, including the time spent in the neural network and numerical method stages. While the paper reports "Time per step (ms)" as a performance metric, it does not provide a detailed breakdown of the computational overhead, such as the time spent on data transfer between the neural network and the numerical solver, and the time spent on the numerical integration steps. This lack of granularity makes it difficult to identify the bottlenecks in the simulation and suggest potential areas for optimization. The paper also trains a separate model for each simulation scenario, which is not suitable for real-world applications. The authors do not provide a discussion on the limitations of their approach in terms of generalization and how it can be extended to handle more complex and varied scenarios. The current approach of training a separate model for each scenario is not practical for real-world applications where the simulation conditions can vary significantly. The authors should discuss the potential for a more generalizable model that can handle a wider range of scenarios without requiring retraining. Finally, the paper does not provide a detailed comparison with other control methods, such as reinforcement learning or model predictive control, to justify the choice of a diffusion model for fluid control. While the authors provide some justification for using diffusion models based on their properties, a comparison with other control methods would further strengthen the choice. The paper also lacks a detailed discussion of the system's performance in highly turbulent flows or complex interactions between multiple fluids. While the "Water-Sand (2D)" scenario involves interactions between two different fluid types, the paper does not deeply explore the boundaries of the method's applicability in these areas. The paper also lacks specific details on GPU memory usage, training time, and detailed inference time for different scenarios, which would help readers understand the practical feasibility of the proposed method. These limitations, which I have verified through a close examination of the paper, highlight areas where further research and analysis are needed to fully realize the potential of the proposed approach.
