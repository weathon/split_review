## Summary:

This paper introduces a semi-supervised learning (SSL) framework designed to enhance the detection of illicit cryptocurrency transactions, specifically focusing on Bitcoin's Shared Send Mixers (SSMs). The authors argue that the quality of data and features is more critical than the sheer quantity of data when applying SSL in this domain. Their approach centers on the development of high-fidelity features, including KeyLinker address clustering, which leverages cryptographic key reuse patterns, and Shared Send Untangling (SSU) complexity metrics, which categorize transactions based on their structural complexity. These features are intended to capture the intricate mixing behaviors associated with illicit activities. The study also contributes a substantial historical dataset of 163 million Bitcoin transactions, classified with SSM labels, which serves as a valuable resource for the blockchain forensics community. The authors demonstrate that their SSL model, guided by these high-fidelity features, achieves a strong F1-score of 0.84, outperforming traditional heuristics and supervised learning methods. The core methodological approach involves a selective pseudo-labeling process, where the model is trained on a small set of labeled data and then used to predict labels for unlabeled data. The most confident predictions are then added to the training set, iteratively improving the model's performance. The authors emphasize that the selection of pseudo-labels is not based on arbitrary thresholds but rather on the inherent quality of the features used to generate them, prioritizing transactions with simpler structures and those associated with KeyLinker clusters. The main empirical finding is that the SSL model, when guided by high-fidelity features, achieves superior performance compared to models trained on lower-quality features or using traditional heuristics. The paper's overall significance lies in its emphasis on feature engineering and data quality in the context of blockchain forensics, highlighting the limitations of relying solely on large datasets with potentially noisy or unreliable features. By introducing novel features and demonstrating their effectiveness within an SSL framework, the authors provide a valuable contribution to the field, suggesting that a focus on data quality is essential for improving the detection of illicit activities on blockchain networks.
