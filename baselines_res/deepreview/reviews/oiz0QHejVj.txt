## Summary:

This paper introduces CLIP-Map, a novel mapping-based compression framework designed for CLIP-like multimodal models. Unlike traditional pruning methods that select and remove less important weights, CLIP-Map employs learnable transformation matrices to map and combine pretrained weights, aiming to preserve more information from the original model. The core of this approach lies in the use of Kronecker Factorization, which reduces the complexity of the learnable mappings, making the framework more practical for real-world applications. The authors propose a Diagonal Inheritance Initialization strategy to address optimization challenges associated with learnable mapping, contributing to the stability and efficiency of the training process. This initialization method leverages the diagonal structure of the mapping matrices to reduce distribution shifting and enable efficient learning. The framework is trained in two stages: first, the mapping matrices are learned to compress the model, and second, knowledge distillation is used to transfer knowledge from the original model to the compressed one. The experimental results presented in the paper demonstrate that CLIP-Map outperforms select-based frameworks across various compression ratios, particularly under high compression settings, while also reducing training overhead. The authors evaluate their method on various zero-shot retrieval and classification benchmarks, showing that CLIP-Map maintains model performance while significantly reducing model size. The paper's main contribution is the introduction of a novel mapping-based compression approach that differs from traditional pruning methods, leveraging learnable matrices and Kronecker Factorization to achieve efficient and effective model compression. The use of Diagonal Inheritance Initialization is also a significant contribution, addressing the optimization challenges associated with learnable mapping. The experimental results provide strong evidence for the effectiveness of the proposed method, particularly under high compression settings. Overall, this paper presents a promising approach to compressing large multimodal models, with potential implications for deploying these models in resource-constrained environments. However, as I will discuss, there are several areas where the paper could be strengthened to provide a more comprehensive and robust evaluation of the proposed method.
