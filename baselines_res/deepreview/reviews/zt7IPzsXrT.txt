## Summary:

This paper introduces ScaPre, a novel framework for large-scale concept unlearning in text-to-image diffusion models, addressing critical challenges such as conflicting weight updates, imprecise unlearning, and reliance on additional data. The core contribution of ScaPre lies in its ability to efficiently and precisely remove specific concepts from pre-trained diffusion models without requiring auxiliary sub-models or fine-tuning. The method employs a conflict-aware stable design, which combines a spectral trace regularizer with geometry alignment to manage inter-concept interactions and preserve the pre-trained global structure. This design aims to mitigate the issue of conflicting weight updates that often occur during multi-concept unlearning, where updates for one concept can negatively impact the representation of others. Additionally, ScaPre incorporates an Informax Decoupler, which measures the coupling between parameters and target concepts, adaptively scaling updates to focus unlearning on target subspaces while protecting similar, non-targeted concepts. This component is designed to enhance the precision of unlearning by isolating the parameters most relevant to the concepts being removed. The method leverages a closed-form solution, which significantly enhances its efficiency and scalability, allowing it to handle a large number of concepts without substantial computational overhead. The experimental results presented in the paper demonstrate that ScaPre outperforms existing methods in both unlearning efficacy and generative quality across various benchmarks. The authors show that their method can effectively remove target concepts while maintaining the overall quality of generated images, a crucial aspect for practical applications. The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of ScaPre, comparing it against several baselines and demonstrating its superiority in terms of both unlearning performance and the preservation of generative quality. The authors also provide ablation studies to analyze the impact of different components of their method, further supporting their claims. Overall, ScaPre represents a significant advancement in the field of concept unlearning for diffusion models, offering a scalable, precise, and efficient solution to a challenging problem. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, making the proposed method accessible to a wide audience. The authors clearly articulate the limitations of existing methods and provide a coherent rationale for their approach, making a strong case for the significance of their work.
