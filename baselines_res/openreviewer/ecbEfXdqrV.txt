# Review

## Summary
The paper explores the counterintuitive phenomenon in anomaly detection using deep generative models, particularly normalizing flows, focusing on tabular data. The authors define this phenomenon and demonstrate its rarity in tabular datasets through extensive experiments. The study investigates the roles of data dimensionality and feature correlation, offering theoretical and empirical analyses to explain why this phenomenon does not occur as frequently in tabular data as it does in image data. The paper concludes that flow-based likelihood tests are effective for anomaly detection in tabular domains, outperforming traditional models.

## Strengths
The paper introduces a novel definition of the counterintuitive phenomenon in anomaly detection and provides a thorough investigation into its occurrence in tabular data, addressing a gap in the existing literature.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper's structure and writing could be improved for clarity and flow. Currently, the introduction lacks a clear problem statement and motivation, which makes it difficult to understand the significance of the research. Additionally, the paper lacks a conclusion section, which is essential for summarizing key findings and implications. The presentation of the results is also somewhat confusing, with an excessive focus on detailed experimental settings and proofs, making it challenging for readers to grasp the main outcomes and takeaways.

2. The paper's motivation and problem statement are not clearly articulated. The introduction does not sufficiently establish the importance of addressing the counterintuitive phenomenon in anomaly detection or why it is crucial to investigate this issue in the context of tabular data. A stronger motivation and clearer problem definition are necessary to justify the relevance and significance of the research.

3. The paper lacks a comprehensive comparison with existing methods for anomaly detection in tabular data. While it focuses on the counterintuitive phenomenon with normalizing flows, it does not provide a thorough comparison with other state-of-the-art techniques, such as XGBoost, Isolation Forest, or KNN, which are commonly used in tabular anomaly detection. Including such comparisons would enhance the paper's credibility and allow for a better assessment of the proposed method's performance relative to established approaches.

4. The paper's experimental design and results presentation could be more focused on the main findings. Currently, the paper includes extensive details about the experimental setup and proofs, which detract from the main outcomes. A more streamlined approach that highlights the key results and implications would improve the paper's readability and impact.

## Questions
1. The paper mentions the use of 10 repeated experiments for each model and dataset. Could you clarify the purpose of these repetitions? Are they intended to account for different initializations of the models, or are they used to compute confidence intervals or standard deviations for the reported results?

2. The paper introduces the concept of intrinsic dimension (ID) and uses it as a measure of feature correlation and heterogeneity. Could you provide a more detailed explanation of how ID is calculated and why it is considered a suitable measure for these properties?
