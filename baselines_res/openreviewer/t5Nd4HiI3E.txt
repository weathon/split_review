# Review

## Summary
The authors propose a new method for preference optimization, called Bias-Variance Optimized Preference Optimization (BVPO). The method is designed to address the high variance in gradient estimations that arises from sampling reasoning traces during training. BVPO combines a high-variance trace-based gradient estimator with a low-variance empty-trace gradient estimator, allowing for a bias-variance trade-off. The authors provide theoretical guarantees that the combined estimator reduces variance and tightens convergence bounds for stochastic gradient descent (SGD). Empirically, BVPO demonstrates improvements in alignment on benchmarks such as AlpacaEval 2 and Arena-Hard, and enhances reasoning performance on math reasoning tasks.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a novel approach to preference optimization for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), focusing on the bias-variance trade-off to address gradient variance issues.
2. The authors provide a strong theoretical analysis, including proofs that BVPO reduces variance and tightens convergence bounds.
3. Empirical results show consistent improvements over baselines on multiple benchmarks, demonstrating the practical effectiveness of the proposed method.

## Weaknesses
1. The experimental setup lacks clarity, particularly regarding the evaluation of the base model. It is unclear whether the base model is evaluated with or without reasoning traces. This ambiguity is significant because the primary motivation for using an empty-trace gradient is to reduce the high variance introduced by reasoning trace sampling. If the base model is evaluated with reasoning traces, the reported performance improvements might be misleading, as these gains could simply result from the use of an additional ground-truth answer in the gradient computation. This would mean that the improvements are not due to the reduced variance from the empty-trace gradient but rather from using a more direct and accurate training signal.

2. The paper lacks a detailed analysis of the optimal mixing coefficient, $\alpha$. The authors mention that $\alpha$ is chosen based on the MSE-optimal estimator, but they do not provide sufficient details on how this optimal value is determined in practice. A more thorough discussion of the $\alpha$ selection process, including any empirical or theoretical considerations, would strengthen the paper.

3. The paper does not sufficiently address the potential impact of using an empty-trace gradient on the overall reasoning capabilities of the model. While the authors demonstrate that BVPO improves alignment and reasoning performance on specific benchmarks, a more detailed analysis of how the method affects the general reasoning abilities of the model is needed. This could include a comparison of the model's performance on a wider range of tasks, including those that require different types of reasoning beyond math problems.
