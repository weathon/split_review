# Review

## Summary
The authors present HighClass, a novel framework for metagenomic classification that improves accuracy and computational efficiency by using three key innovations: variable-length token indexing, quality-aware scoring, and learned sparsification. By replacing alignment operations with hash-based token mapping, HighClass achieves O(|T|) complexity, providing a significant speedup and memory reduction compared to existing methods while maintaining competitive accuracy. The framework also incorporates quality-aware scoring to optimize the contribution of sequencing evidence and gradient-based sparsification to reduce the index size while preserving accuracy. The authors evaluate HighClass on the CAMI II dataset, demonstrating its effectiveness and efficiency compared to state-of-the-art methods. Additionally, they provide a theoretical analysis of token dependencies and the framework's convergence properties.

## Strengths
The paper introduces HighClass, a novel framework that combines three key innovations—variable-length token indexing, quality-aware scoring, and learned sparsification—to improve metagenomic classification. This approach represents a significant advancement in the field, offering a new computational paradigm that balances accuracy and efficiency. The authors provide a rigorous theoretical analysis of the token dependency graph and the framework's convergence properties, establishing a solid foundation for their method. The paper is well-structured, with a clear and logical flow that makes it easy to follow. The authors present their results in a clear and concise manner, using appropriate visualizations to illustrate their findings. The evaluation of HighClass on the CAMI II dataset is comprehensive, and the comparison with state-of-the-art methods demonstrates the framework's effectiveness and efficiency. The authors also conduct a thorough ablation study to isolate the contributions of each component of their method, providing a clear understanding of the impact of each innovation on the overall performance.

## Weaknesses
The paper lacks a detailed description of the implementation of the framework, including the specific algorithms and data structures used. This makes it difficult for other researchers to replicate the results or integrate the framework into their own work. While the authors provide a complexity analysis of their method, they do not include a detailed analysis of the time and space complexity of each component of the framework. This information would be valuable for understanding the practical performance of the method. The evaluation of HighClass is limited to a single dataset (CAMI II), which may not fully represent the diversity of real-world metagenomic data. It would be beneficial to evaluate the framework on additional datasets to demonstrate its generalizability. The authors do not provide a detailed analysis of the sensitivity of the framework to various hyperparameters, such as the vocabulary size or the sensitivity parameter for quality-aware scoring. Understanding the impact of these hyperparameters on the performance of the framework would be valuable for practical implementation.

## Questions
The authors mention that they will release the source code and pre-computed indices, but it would be helpful to have a more detailed discussion of the reproducibility of the results, including any challenges that other researchers may face in replicating the experiments. The authors provide a complexity analysis of their method, but it would be beneficial to include a more detailed analysis of the time and space complexity of each component of the framework. This would provide a better understanding of the practical performance of the method. The authors evaluate HighClass on the CAMI II dataset, which is a relatively small dataset. It would be valuable to see an evaluation of the framework on larger and more diverse datasets to demonstrate its generalizability.
