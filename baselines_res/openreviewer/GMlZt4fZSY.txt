# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces MobileLLM-R1, a series of sub-billion parameter language models that demonstrate strong reasoning capabilities despite being trained on relatively small datasets. The authors challenge the prevailing assumptions that large model sizes and extensive training data are necessary for reasoning abilities. They show that by carefully curating and resampling open-source datasets, it is possible to achieve reasoning emergence with only ~2T tokens of high-quality data. The MobileLLM-R1 models, trained with a novel pre-training, mid-training, and post-training pipeline, outperform prior fully open-source models on reasoning benchmarks, matching or surpassing larger models like Qwen3-0.6B while using only 11.7% of its training data. The paper provides a detailed explanation of the training process, the data selection criteria, and the evaluation results, and offers a comprehensive comparison with other models in the field.

## Strengths
- The paper demonstrates that strong reasoning abilities can emerge in models trained on a small fraction of the data typically used for large language models, challenging the conventional wisdom that massive datasets are required for complex reasoning.
- The authors provide a detailed and transparent description of their data curation process, including the metrics used, the datasets selected, and the reasoning behind the choices. This level of detail allows for reproducibility and further exploration by other researchers.
- The paper not only introduces a new model but also offers insights into the mechanisms behind the development of reasoning capabilities in language models, even at small scales.

## Weaknesses
- The paper primarily focuses on reasoning abilities as measured by benchmarks such as HumanEval and GSM8K. However, it does not explore the generalization capabilities of the MobileLLM-R1 models to real-world tasks or domains that may not be covered by the training data.
- While the authors compare their models with Qwen3-0.6B and other partially open-source models, the comparison is not exhaustive. There are other models that may have similar or better performance on reasoning tasks, and the paper does not include a comprehensive comparison with the entire landscape of available models.
- The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational resources required for training the MobileLLM-R1 models. Information on training time, hardware used, and scalability would be valuable for other researchers looking to replicate or build upon this work.
- The paper does not explicitly address the potential for overfitting or the robustness of the MobileLLM-R1 models to various types of noise or adversarial attacks. Evaluating the models on out-of-distribution data or in the presence of noise could provide a more comprehensive understanding of their capabilities.

## Questions
1. Can you provide more details on the computational resources required for training the MobileLLM-R1 models? Specifically, how long did the training process take, and what kind of hardware was used?
2. How did you ensure that the data curation process did not inadvertently introduce biases or limitations that could affect the model's performance on certain tasks or domains?
3. Have you evaluated the MobileLLM-R1 models on any real-world tasks or domains that are not directly related to the benchmarks used in the paper? If so, what were the results?
4. How did you handle potential conflicts between different types of knowledge or reasoning skills during the training process? Was there any mechanism in place to resolve these conflicts?
5. Can you provide more insights into the decision-making process behind the selection of the specific datasets used for training? How did you determine that these particular datasets would be most beneficial for developing strong reasoning capabilities in the models?
6. How did you ensure that the models were not overfitting to the training data, especially given the relatively small size of the datasets used?
7. Have you considered the potential impact of the MobileLLM-R1 models on the broader field of natural language processing? How do you see these models being used in practical applications?
