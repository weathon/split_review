# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a new benchmark for evaluating LLMs in a dynamic text-to-SQL environment. The authors propose two evaluation settings, c-Interact and a-Interact, to assess LLMs' abilities in handling ambiguous queries and evolving user requirements through multi-turn interactions. The benchmark includes a comprehensive task suite covering various CRUD operations and features a function-driven user simulator for end-to-end evaluation without human intervention. The experimental results show that current state-of-the-art models face significant challenges in this benchmark, with GPT-5 achieving only 8.67% success in c-Interact and 17% in a-Interact. The authors also discuss the importance of effective interaction strategies and the need for more sophisticated exploration patterns in handling complex database reasoning tasks.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a new benchmark that more accurately simulates real-world database applications by incorporating multi-turn interactions, ambiguity, and follow-up sub-tasks. This benchmark addresses the limitations of existing benchmarks that rely on static context or narrow, read-only operations.
2. The authors provide a detailed description of the benchmark construction process, including the selection of databases, ambiguity injection, and follow-up sub-task generation. They also propose a function-driven user simulator to enable end-to-end evaluation without human intervention.
3. The paper includes comprehensive experimental results that demonstrate the difficulty of the proposed benchmark. The authors analyze the performance of several state-of-the-art models in different interaction settings and discuss the impact of various factors on task success.
4. The authors provide a thorough comparison of the proposed benchmark with existing ones, highlighting its unique features and challenges. They also discuss the potential applications of the benchmark in evaluating LLMs for database applications and the broader implications of their findings for the field of natural language processing.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the limitations of the proposed benchmark or potential directions for future research. While the authors mention some future work, a more thorough discussion of the benchmark's limitations and potential improvements would be beneficial.
2. The paper focuses on evaluating LLMs in a specific context (database applications). It's not clear how well the findings generalize to other natural language processing tasks or applications.

## Questions
1. How well do the findings generalize to other natural language processing tasks or applications outside of database management?
2. What are the limitations of the proposed benchmark, and how could it be improved in future work?
