# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces a real-time method for designing neural stimulations that perturb latent dynamics in arbitrary directions. The method uses kernel regression to nonparametrically regress changes in dynamics based on both the delivered stimulation and the neural latent state at the time of stimulation. The authors do not assume that responses to stimulations are robust, involve the neurons that the stimulation intended to target, or are static over time. They evaluate their method across multiple latent space representations and multiple models of dynamics in parallel, and additionally provide a streaming estimator to determine which representation is most predictive of ongoing neural dynamics at any timepoint.

## Strengths
The paper is well-written and clearly organized. The authors provide a thorough explanation of their method, including the mathematical formulations and algorithmic details. The experimental evaluation is comprehensive, covering both simulated and real neural data from different modalities. The comparison with existing methods is particularly valuable, as it provides a clear picture of the advantages and limitations of the proposed approach.

## Weaknesses
The paper lacks a clear comparison with existing methods for neural stimulation and adaptive experimentation. While the authors mention some related work, they do not provide a comprehensive comparison of their method's performance against state-of-the-art approaches. This makes it difficult to assess the true novelty and impact of the proposed method. The authors should include a thorough comparison with existing methods, highlighting the specific advantages and disadvantages of their approach in terms of performance, flexibility, and computational efficiency.

The authors should provide a more detailed discussion of the limitations of their method, including the assumptions underlying the proposed approach and potential scenarios where it may not perform well. This would help readers understand the practical constraints and potential pitfalls of using the method in different experimental contexts.
