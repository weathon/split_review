# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a theoretical framework to prove that nonlinear neural policy ensembles are sub-optimal compared to linear policy ensembles. The authors provide empirical validation through experiments comparing linear and nonlinear policy ensembles, showing that well-tuned neural policy ensembles can underperform linear ensembles by two orders of magnitude. The paper highlights significant instability in neural policy ensembles under identical operating conditions, whereas linear policy ensembles remain stable.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a novel theoretical framework to demonstrate the sub-optimality of nonlinear neural policy ensembles compared to linear policy ensembles, which contributes to the understanding of ensemble methods in reinforcement learning and beyond.

2. The paper is well-organized, with a clear presentation of definitions, theoretical results, and empirical validation. The authors effectively communicate complex ideas, making the paper accessible to readers with a background in machine learning and control theory.

3. The theoretical claims are rigorously proven, and the empirical results are presented with appropriate statistical analysis, enhancing the credibility of the findings.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion on the practical implications of the findings, particularly for real-world applications in reinforcement learning and agentic AI.

2. While the empirical validation is robust, the experiments are limited to specific environments. Broader experiments across different domains would strengthen the generalizability of the findings.

3. The theoretical framework assumes certain conditions, such as bounded disturbances in the dynamical system. These assumptions might limit the applicability of the results to more complex or noisy environments.

4. The paper does not thoroughly address potential methods for mitigating the sub-optimality of neural policy ensembles or suggest directions for future research in this area.

5. The paper could provide more insight into the sensitivity of the results to different hyperparameters and training configurations of the neural networks.

## Questions
1. How do the results generalize to other types of nonlinear systems or more complex environments beyond the ones tested in the experiments?

2. Are there any potential methods or modifications to neural policy ensembles that could reduce the sub-optimality gap compared to linear ensembles?

3. How sensitive are the results to the hyperparameters and training configurations of the neural networks used in the experiments?

4. What are the implications of the findings for real-world applications in reinforcement learning and agentic AI, particularly in safety-critical systems?
