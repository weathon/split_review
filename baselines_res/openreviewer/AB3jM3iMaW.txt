# Review

## Summary
This paper presents an innovative framework, ReaL-TG, designed to enhance the explainability and generalization capabilities of large language models (LLMs) in the context of temporal graph (TG) reasoning. By leveraging reinforcement learning (RL), ReaL-TG fine-tunes LLMs to generate human-readable explanations for their link forecasting predictions on real-world TGs. The framework employs an outcome-based reward mechanism that encourages models to self-explore and improve their reasoning strategies, thereby enhancing predictive accuracy without the need for retraining on unseen graphs. Additionally, the paper introduces a novel evaluation protocol that combines traditional ranking metrics with an LLM-as-a-Judge system to comprehensively assess the quality of LLM-generated reasoning traces. Experimental results demonstrate that ReaL-TG outperforms larger frontier LLMs, including GPT-5 mini, in both prediction accuracy and reasoning quality, as confirmed by both LLM and human evaluations.

## Strengths
1.	The paper is well-organized and clearly written, with a logical flow that makes the complex concepts accessible and easy to understand.
2.	The introduction of an LLM-as-a-Judge system for evaluating reasoning quality is a significant contribution, providing a scalable and reliable method for assessing the reasoning outputs of LLMs in TG link forecasting.
3.	The proposed ReaL-TG framework is a novel approach that enhances the explainability and generalization capabilities of LLMs in the context of temporal graph reasoning.

## Weaknesses
1.	The paper does not provide a comprehensive comparison with existing state-of-the-art methods in temporal graph reasoning, particularly those that focus on explainability and generalization. It would be beneficial to include comparisons with other approaches that also aim to address these challenges in TGs.
2.	The paper lacks a detailed discussion on the limitations of the proposed ReaL-TG framework. It would be valuable to address potential constraints or scenarios where the framework may not perform optimally, providing insights for future research and potential improvements.
3.	The paper does not thoroughly explore the potential biases and fairness issues that may arise in the LLM-as-a-Judge system. It would be important to discuss how the system handles bias and ensures fair evaluations, as this is crucial for the reliability of the evaluation process.
4.	The paper could benefit from a more detailed analysis of the computational efficiency and scalability of the proposed framework. It would be helpful to provide insights into the computational resources required and how the framework performs with larger datasets.

## Questions
1.	How does the ReaL-TG framework compare to other state-of-the-art methods in temporal graph reasoning that focus on explainability and generalization? It would be valuable to include comparative experiments or discussions to highlight the advantages and limitations of ReaL-TG relative to existing approaches.
2.	What are the limitations of the ReaL-TG framework, and how might these impact its applicability in real-world scenarios? A thorough discussion of the limitations would provide a more balanced perspective and guide future research directions.
3.	Can the authors elaborate on the potential biases and fairness issues that may be present in the LLM-as-a-Judge system, and how these are addressed or mitigated?
4.	How does the computational efficiency of the ReaL-TG framework scale with larger datasets, and what are the resource requirements for training and evaluating the model?
