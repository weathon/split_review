# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a training-free object counting framework that integrates probabilistic prompt generation with output token-based verification, utilizing the SAM model’s original structure. The framework demonstrates competitive performance across object counting benchmarks, with notable improvements over training-based methods on the CARPK dataset. Experimental results confirm the effectiveness of this approach, often surpassing existing methods while maintaining the advantages of training-free deployment.

## Strengths
1. The proposed method is training-free, which is advantageous in terms of computational efficiency.

2. The proposed method outperforms existing training-based and training-free counting methods.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed method is built upon SAM, which can already perform object counting tasks to some extent. This raises questions about the extent of the novel contributions presented in this work. The authors are encouraged to conduct a thorough analysis comparing the performance of the proposed method to SAM's counting capabilities, highlighting the specific improvements and unique aspects of their approach.

2. The proposed method relies on a similarity score between the exemplar and the query image. However, as noted in the OmniCount paper, this similarity score can be influenced by various factors, such as scale and background. The authors should address how these potential influences are mitigated in their approach.

3. The proposed method appears to be a hybrid of TFOC and OmniCount, with the addition of a new similarity metric. The authors should provide a more detailed explanation of the motivation behind this combination and the specific advantages of the new similarity metric.

4. The proposed method shows inferior performance on the CARPK dataset compared to TFCAC. The authors should offer a more in-depth analysis of the factors contributing to this performance gap.
