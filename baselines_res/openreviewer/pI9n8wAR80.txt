# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces Co-Calibrated Logit Adjustment (CoLA), a framework designed to address the challenges of long-tailed semi-supervised learning (LTSSL). The authors identify two key limitations in existing Logit Adjustment (LA) methods: overestimation of head class prevalence due to sample redundancy and the oversight of the interplay between class-wise and overall adjustment. CoLA addresses these issues by introducing De-Duplicated Distribution Estimation (DDDE) for more accurate class distribution estimation and Logit Meta-Calibration (LMC) to dynamically determine the optimal overall adjustment strength. The framework is supported by theoretical analysis and extensive empirical validation, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-structured, with clear explanations of the proposed methods and thorough experimental descriptions.
2. The authors provide a rigorous theoretical analysis, including a generalization bound, which strengthens the credibility of their approach.
3. CoLA demonstrates superior performance across various datasets and distributions, highlighting its robustness and effectiveness.
4. The paper includes comprehensive ablation studies and visualizations, effectively illustrating the impact of each component of the proposed method.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper could benefit from a more detailed comparison with a broader range of existing methods, particularly those that do not rely on Logit Adjustment.
2. The method involves several steps, which might increase the complexity of implementation. A more streamlined approach could be beneficial.
3. The effectiveness of CoLA is heavily dependent on the quality of the initial labeled data. Performance may degrade in scenarios with poorly annotated datasets.

## Questions
1. How does CoLA perform on datasets with significant label noise? Is there a mechanism to mitigate the impact of noisy labels?
2. Could the authors elaborate on the computational cost associated with the two-stage optimization process in LMC?
3. How does the framework handle scenarios where the unlabeled data distribution is highly diverse or contains outliers?
4. Are there specific types of datasets or tasks where CoLA might underperform? If so, what are the authors' hypotheses for these cases?
