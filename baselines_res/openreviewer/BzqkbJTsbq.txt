# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a framework that leverages both data and process knowledge to tackle imperfect-label guidance tasks. The proposed method, DPG, unifies weak-label and degraded-label guidance tasks by integrating task-specific data knowledge with diffusion-model process knowledge. Extensive experiments on style transfer, image super-resolution, and image deblurring demonstrate the effectiveness of DPG.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow. 
2. The authors provide a comprehensive analysis of the rationale behind task unification, its benefits, and the challenges that hinder its practical implementation.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks clear motivation for the task unification. Although the authors claim that this paper is the first study to analyze the gap between weak-label and degraded-label guidance tasks, several previous works have already explored the connection between these tasks, such as [1, 2]. These works should be discussed in detail.

2. The proposed method appears to be a simple combination of data and process knowledge. While the authors claim that the injection of data knowledge can help the model select the most relevant information for the task, the data knowledge injection seems to function more as a style injection, which has been extensively explored in the style transfer literature. Furthermore, the process knowledge integration seems to be a variant of the widely used diffusion guidance.

3. The experimental results are not convincing. First, the authors should clarify why the results of TFG and FreeDom differ from the official results. Additionally, the authors should explain why the results of style transfer in Figure 4 do not match the results in Table 1. It is also unclear why the quantitative results of style transfer in Table 1 are much lower than those in previous works, such as [3]. Finally, the authors should include more qualitative results to demonstrate the effectiveness of DPG.

[1] Kim, J., Kim, B. S., & Ye, J. C. (2022). Diffusion posterior sampling for general noisy inverse problems: Beyond analytical methods. arXiv preprint arXiv:2209.14687.

[2] Chung, H., Kim, J., Mccann, M. T., Klasky, M. L., & Ye, J. C. (2023). Diffusion posterior sampling for general noisy inverse problems. Advances in neural information processing systems, 36, 50824-50837.

[3] Yu, F., Vahdat, A., Kreis, K., & Kautz, J. (2023). Freedom: Training-free energy-guided conditional diffusion model. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 36, 61124-61137.
