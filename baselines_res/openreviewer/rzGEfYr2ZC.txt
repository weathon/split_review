# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a new method to prune LLMs. The method is based on Frank-Wolfe algorithm, which solves a convex relaxation of the original problem. The authors also provide theoretical analysis of the proposed method.

## Strengths
The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The proposed method is novel and interesting.

## Weaknesses
1. The experiments are not convincing enough. First, the authors only compare the proposed method with Wanda and RIA, while SparseGPT is also a widely used method as introduced in the paper. Second, the authors only report the performance under high sparsity (50%, 60% and 2:4). I think the performance under low sparsity (e.g., 5%, 10%) is more important since under such sparsity, the performance loss is acceptable and the computation cost of pruning is affordable. Moreover, the authors should also report the performance of the original LLMs.

2. The theoretical analysis is not rigorous enough. The authors only analyze the difference between the optimal solution of the relaxed problem and the original problem, while the difference between the solution returned by the Frank-Wolfe algorithm and the optimal solution of the relaxed problem is not analyzed. In other words, the error of the Frank-Wolfe algorithm is not bounded.

## Questions
1. Why not compare the proposed method with SparseGPT?
2. What is the performance of the proposed method under low sparsity?
3. What is the difference between the solution returned by the Frank-Wolfe algorithm and the optimal solution of the relaxed problem?
