# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces EmbodiedMAE, a 3D multi-modal representation learning framework for robot manipulation that integrates RGB, depth, and point cloud data. The authors enhance the DROID dataset with high-quality depth maps and point clouds, creating DROID-3D, and develop a multi-modal masked autoencoder that outperforms state-of-the-art vision foundation models in both training efficiency and final performance across various simulation and real-world robot manipulation tasks.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The authors provide a high-quality dataset, DROID-3D, which contributes to the field of 3D robot learning.
3. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method.

## Weaknesses
1. The technical contribution of this paper is limited. The proposed EmbodiedMAE is a simple combination of DINOv2 and MAE, lacking innovation.
2. The authors do not provide a detailed analysis of the experimental results, which limits the understanding of the model's behavior and the reasons behind its success.
3. The authors do not discuss the limitations of the proposed method, which could provide valuable insights for future research and practical applications.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed EmbodiedMAE handle potential noise and inaccuracies in the depth maps and point clouds?
2. Can the authors provide a more detailed analysis of the model's performance across different types of manipulation tasks?
3. How does the model's performance vary with different sensor configurations or environmental conditions?
