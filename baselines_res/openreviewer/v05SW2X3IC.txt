# Review

## Summary
This paper revisits the Gray-Wyner Network, a classical concept in information theory, and proposes a learnable three-channel codec that disentangles shared information from task-specific details across multiple vision tasks. The authors demonstrate that their approach outperforms independent coding through comparisons of three codec architectures on two-task scenarios spanning six vision benchmarks.

## Strengths
1. The paper provides a theoretical framework for understanding lossy common information and presents bounds for lossy common information, extending previous work by Wyner (1975) from lossless to lossy settings.
2. The authors propose a novel optimization objective that can explore the transmit-receive tradeoff, allowing for more flexible and efficient representation learning.
3. The paper demonstrates the practical value of revisiting Gray-Wyner theory in modern machine learning contexts through comprehensive experiments on various vision tasks.

## Weaknesses
1. While the paper presents a theoretical framework, it could benefit from more intuitive explanations and examples to make the concepts more accessible to a broader audience.
2. The experimental evaluation is primarily focused on vision tasks. It would be interesting to see how the proposed approach applies to other domains, such as natural language processing or speech recognition.
3. The paper could provide more details on the implementation and computational complexity of the proposed method, which would help readers better understand the practical implications of adopting this approach.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed method handle scenarios where the common information is not easily separable or is distributed unevenly across tasks? Are there any limitations or assumptions that need to be aware of?
2. Can the proposed approach be extended to more than three tasks? If so, how would the complexity and performance scale with the number of tasks?
3. How does the proposed method compare with other state-of-the-art methods in terms of computational efficiency and resource requirements?
