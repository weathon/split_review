# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a new framework for single-trajectory stochastic process modeling, addressing limitations in prior-driven and data-driven methods. The authors introduce a noise-to-process (N2P) paradigm, where a shared base-noise process is transformed through a single generator to produce a full trajectory, ensuring projective consistency. They further develop the Deconvolution-Based Process Transformation (DBPT) model, which captures long-range temporal dependencies and provides uncertainty estimates. Experiments on synthetic and real-world tasks show that DBPT outperforms prior- and data-driven baselines in single-trajectory settings, offering flexible uncertainty modeling without relying on strong priors or multi-trajectory data.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a novel N2P paradigm that effectively combines the strengths of prior-driven and data-driven methods, addressing their individual limitations. This approach is innovative as it leverages a shared base-noise process and a single generator to ensure projective consistency, which is a unique solution for single-trajectory stochastic process modeling.
2. The paper provides a rigorous theoretical foundation for the proposed N2P paradigm and DBPT model, including formal proofs of well-defined processes and intrinsic projective consistency. 
3. The paper is well-structured and clearly written, with detailed explanations of the N2P paradigm and DBPT model. The use of diagrams and flowcharts, such as the DBPT flowchart in Figure 1, effectively illustrates the model's architecture and processes.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational complexity of the DBPT model, particularly in comparison to other baseline methods. Given that the model involves a deconvolution-based process transformation, there may be concerns about its scalability to larger datasets or more complex tasks. Including an analysis of training and inference times, as well as memory usage, would provide valuable insights into the model's practical applicability.
2. The paper could benefit from a more comprehensive discussion of the limitations of the proposed method. While the authors mention that the model's tendency to provide overly confident predictions can lead to worse performance in certain scenarios, a more detailed analysis of the conditions under which this occurs would be valuable. Additionally, discussing potential limitations in the model's ability to capture certain types of dependencies or handle extreme noise conditions would provide a more balanced view of its capabilities.

## Questions
1. The paper mentions that the DBPT model can capture long-range temporal dependencies. However, it would be helpful to provide more concrete examples or case studies demonstrating this capability in real-world scenarios. Additionally, how does the model's ability to capture long-range dependencies compare to that of other baseline methods?
2. The paper discusses the trade-off between MSE and NLL performance in the DBPT model. However, it would be helpful to provide a more detailed analysis of this trade-off, including examples or scenarios where one metric is more important than the other. Additionally, are there any strategies that can be employed to better balance this trade-off?
