# Review

## Summary
The paper studies adaptive optimization methods such as Adam and compares them to normalized steepest descent (NSD) methods such as SignGD. It is known that these methods are closely related, with NSD being a special case of adaptive optimizers when the exponential moving average (EMA) of past gradients is disabled. The key difference between them is the underlying geometry and smoothness assumptions: adaptive optimizers rely on adaptive smoothness, while NSD relies on standard smoothness. The paper extends the theory of adaptive smoothness to the nonconvex setting and shows that it precisely characterizes the convergence of adaptive optimizers. It also demonstrates that adaptive smoothness enables acceleration with Nesterov momentum in the convex setting, a guarantee unattainable under standard smoothness for certain non-Euclidean geometry. The paper develops an analogous comparison for stochastic optimization by introducing adaptive gradient variance, which parallels adaptive smoothness and leads to dimension-free convergence guarantees that cannot be achieved under standard gradient variance for certain non-Euclidean geometry.

## Strengths
The paper provides a rigorous theoretical framework for analyzing adaptive optimizers and their relationship to NSD. It extends the theory of adaptive smoothness to the nonconvex setting, which is a novel contribution. The paper also demonstrates the practical benefits of adaptive smoothness, such as enabling acceleration with Nesterov momentum in the convex setting. The introduction of adaptive gradient variance as an analogue of adaptive smoothness for gradient noise is an interesting concept that could lead to further research in stochastic optimization. The paper is well-written and clearly explains the key concepts and results.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper focuses primarily on theoretical analysis and may lack empirical validation through experiments. It would be beneficial to include some experimental results to demonstrate the practical performance of adaptive optimizers and compare them to NSD methods.

2. The paper assumes a certain level of mathematical sophistication from the reader and may not be accessible to a broader audience. It would be helpful to provide more intuitive explanations and examples to make the concepts more accessible.

3. The paper does not provide any practical guidance on how to choose hyperparameters for adaptive optimizers. It would be beneficial to include some practical tips and recommendations based on the theoretical analysis.

## Questions
1. Can you provide any empirical results to support your theoretical analysis? It would be helpful to see how adaptive optimizers perform in practice compared to NSD methods.

2. Can you provide more intuitive explanations and examples to make the key concepts more accessible to a broader audience?

3. Can you provide any practical tips or recommendations on how to choose hyperparameters for adaptive optimizers based on your analysis?
