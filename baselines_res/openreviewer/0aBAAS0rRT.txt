# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a new method for localization using wireless signals. The main contributions are twofold: first, the paper proposes a cycle-adaptive masking strategy that masks out periodic patterns in the channel state information (CSI) data, preventing the model from learning shortcuts. Second, the paper introduces a map-as-prompt approach, where a graph neural network is used to encode 3D map information and generate soft prompts for fine-tuning the model. The paper evaluates the proposed method on two datasets and demonstrates that it outperforms existing methods.

## Strengths
1. The paper proposes a novel cycle-adaptive masking strategy that effectively prevents the model from learning periodic shortcuts in the CSI data. This approach is original and has not been explored in previous work on wireless localization.
2. The paper presents a novel map-as-prompt approach that incorporates 3D map information into the model through soft prompts. This approach is original and has not been explored in previous work on wireless localization.
3. The paper demonstrates that the proposed method outperforms existing methods on two datasets, achieving better accuracy and robustness. The results are presented in a clear and concise manner.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational complexity of the proposed method. It would be helpful to report the training and inference time of the method compared to existing methods.
2. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the robustness of the proposed method to noise and other forms of uncertainty in the CSI data. It would be helpful to evaluate the method on noisy CSI data and compare its performance to existing methods.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed method perform in terms of computational complexity compared to existing methods?
2. How does the proposed method perform in terms of robustness to noise and other forms of uncertainty in the CSI data?
