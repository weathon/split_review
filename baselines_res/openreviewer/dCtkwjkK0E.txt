# Review

## Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of selecting the most informative samples for training flow matching models, particularly in fields where high-quality labeled datasets are costly to obtain. The authors propose two query strategies: one aimed at enhancing model diversity and the other at improving model accuracy. These strategies are shown to be conflicting, as increasing diversity often reduces accuracy and vice versa. A mixed strategy is introduced that combines both approaches through a weighted mechanism, allowing adjustable control over the diversity-accuracy trade-off. The proposed methods are validated through extensive experiments, demonstrating their effectiveness in outperforming traditional active learning strategies designed for discriminative models.

## Strengths
The paper introduces a novel analytical framework for flow matching models using piecewise-linear neural networks, enabling a deeper understanding of how individual data points influence model diversity and accuracy. This framework provides valuable insights into the inherent trade-off between diversity and accuracy in flow matching models.

## Weaknesses
1. The authors' assumption that neural networks used in flow matching exhibit piecewise-linear interpolation behavior is questionable. The paper lacks sufficient evidence or references to support this claim. While the authors cite works like Gu et al. (2023), they do not provide a comprehensive analysis or empirical evidence demonstrating that flow matching models consistently exhibit piecewise-linear behavior across various conditions and datasets.

2. The paper lacks a thorough discussion on the limitations of using piecewise-linear neural networks to analyze flow matching models. It is unclear whether this assumption holds true for all types of flow matching architectures, especially those with different noise schedules or more complex conditions. The authors should address potential limitations and discuss the generalizability of their approach to different flow matching models.

3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational complexity of the proposed query strategies. It is unclear how the strategies perform with large-scale datasets or in resource-constrained environments. The authors should discuss the scalability of their approach and provide empirical results on computational efficiency.

4. The paper does not provide a detailed comparison with other active learning strategies specifically designed for generative models. While the authors mention existing methods like VAAL and TAVAAL, they do not provide a comprehensive analysis or empirical comparison with these approaches. A more thorough comparison would strengthen the paper's contribution.

5. The paper does not discuss the interpretability of the generated samples. It is unclear how well the proposed methods can generate samples that are not only diverse and accurate but also interpretable. The authors should address the interpretability of their approach and provide examples or case studies demonstrating the practical utility of the generated samples.

## Questions
1. Can the authors provide empirical evidence or additional references to support the assumption that neural networks used in flow matching exhibit piecewise-linear interpolation behavior?

2. How does the proposed approach perform with large-scale datasets or in resource-constrained environments? Can the authors provide empirical results on computational efficiency and scalability?

3. Can the authors provide a more comprehensive comparison with other active learning strategies specifically designed for generative models? How does the proposed approach perform against state-of-the-art methods in terms of diversity, accuracy, and computational efficiency?

4. How does the proposed approach ensure the interpretability of the generated samples? Can the authors provide examples or case studies demonstrating the practical utility and interpretability of the generated samples?
