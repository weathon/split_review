# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces PLAGUE, a modular and adaptable framework for creating multi-turn jailbreak attacks on LLMs. PLAGUE comprises three main phases: Planner, Primer, and Finisher, which work together to systematically design and execute attacks. The framework also incorporates lifelong learning, allowing it to improve over time by retaining successful strategies in a memory bank. PLAGUE achieves a notable improvement in attack success rates, surpassing existing methods by more than 30% on leading models like OpenAI’s o3 and Claude’s Opus 4.1. The authors provide a comprehensive evaluation, using metrics such as ASR and StrongReject to assess the effectiveness of their approach.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-structured and easy to follow, with a clear explanation of the PLAGUE framework and its components.
2. The framework is modular, allowing for flexibility and adaptability by easily incorporating existing attacks like GOAT, Crescendo, and ActorBreaker.
3. PLAGUE demonstrates high attack success rates across multiple models, outperforming existing methods by over 30%.
4. The paper provides a detailed analysis of the framework's efficiency, showing that it achieves strong results with a comparable number of queries to existing methods.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not sufficiently compare PLAGUE with recent state-of-the-art methods like X-Teaming and FITD, which could provide a more comprehensive evaluation of its effectiveness.
2. While PLAGUE is effective, the paper could benefit from a deeper exploration of its limitations and potential vulnerabilities, which would strengthen the understanding of the framework's applicability.
3. The paper could provide more detailed ablation studies to analyze the contribution of each component within the PLAGUE framework, which would help clarify their importance and optimize the framework's performance.

## Questions
1. How does PLAGUE compare to recent state-of-the-art jailbreak methods like X-Teaming and FITD in terms of attack success rates and efficiency?
2. Could the authors provide more details on the potential limitations of PLAGUE and how it might perform against models with stronger defensive mechanisms?
3. Can the authors conduct more ablation studies to analyze the contribution of each component within PLAGUE and provide insights into which aspects are most crucial for its success?
