# Review

## Summary
The paper studies sparse recovery in a setting where the measurements are split into two sets: a small set of high-quality measurements with low noise variance and a larger set of low-quality measurements with higher variance. The authors provide sufficient conditions for support recovery in the agnostic setting and for signed support recovery in the informed setting. The paper also analyzes the LASSO algorithm in the agnostic setting and shows that the recovery threshold matches the homogeneous-noise case, revealing a robustness to data heterogeneity.

## Strengths
The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The authors provide a clear motivation for the problem and a thorough discussion of related work. The technical results are presented in a clear and concise manner, and the authors provide detailed explanations of the proof techniques used. The paper also discusses the implications of the results and their relevance to real-world applications.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks novelty and originality. It does not introduce any new techniques or methodologies but rather extends existing results to a slightly different setting. The authors should clarify the technical challenges and novel contributions of their work.
2. The paper does not provide any empirical results or simulations to validate their theoretical findings. The authors should consider conducting experiments to demonstrate the practical applicability of their results and to compare the performance of different algorithms in the mixed-quality setting.
3. The paper does not provide any discussion on the computational complexity of the LASSO algorithm in the mixed-quality setting. The authors should analyze the computational cost of the algorithm and compare it to the homogeneous-noise setting.

## Questions
1. What are the technical challenges and novel contributions of your work?
2. Can you provide any empirical results or simulations to validate your theoretical findings?
3. What is the computational complexity of the LASSO algorithm in the mixed-quality setting? How does it compare to the homogeneous-noise setting?
