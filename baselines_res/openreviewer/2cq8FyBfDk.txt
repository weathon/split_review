# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces ProteinVista, a compute-efficient 3D convolutional neural network (3D CNN) designed to process full-atom protein structures. ProteinVista voxelizes every heavy atom in a protein and uses 3D data augmentation to learn rotation-robust representations. The model is pre-trained on over 500,000 AlphaFold-2 structures. The authors demonstrate that ProteinVista outperforms sequence-based transformers on three benchmarks: enzyme-substrate classification, transporter-substrate classification, and drug-target inhibition prediction. The paper also shows that a simple ensemble of ProteinVista and ESM-2 can further improve accuracy, indicating that sequence and structure signals are partly complementary. The authors provide an open-source Python implementation of ProteinVista, making it easily accessible for further research and application.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a novel approach by introducing a compute-efficient full-atom 3D CNN, ProteinVista, for processing protein structures. This method fills a gap in the field, as most existing models rely on sequence-based embeddings or simplified protein graphs that do not capture atom-level details.

2. The authors provide a comprehensive evaluation of ProteinVista on three different benchmarks, demonstrating its superior performance compared to sequence transformers. The results show that ProteinVista can outperform state-of-the-art models on tasks that require fine structural resolution.

3. The paper provides an open-source implementation of ProteinVista, making it easily accessible for further research and application. This contributes to the transparency and reproducibility of the work.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed comparison with other 3D CNN approaches for protein modeling. It would be beneficial to include a discussion of how ProteinVista compares to other 3D CNN methods in terms of performance and efficiency.

2. The paper could benefit from a more in-depth analysis of the limitations of ProteinVista. For example, it would be helpful to discuss the potential impact of the model's reliance on AlphaFold-2 predicted structures on its performance.

3. The paper could provide more details on the computational resources required for training and inference with ProteinVista. This would help readers better understand the practical implications of using this model.

## Questions
1. How does ProteinVista compare to other 3D CNN approaches for protein modeling in terms of performance and efficiency? Can the authors provide a detailed comparison with existing methods?

2. What are the potential limitations of ProteinVista, particularly given its reliance on AlphaFold-2 predicted structures? How might these limitations affect its performance in different scenarios?

3. Can the authors provide more details on the computational resources required for training and inference with ProteinVista? How does the model's efficiency compare to other state-of-the-art approaches?
