# Review

## Summary
This paper investigates online inventory optimization (OIO) in non-stationary environments and proposes an algorithm with near-optimal dynamic regret guarantee. The regret bound is $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{L_{\max}T(1+P_T)})$, where $L_{\max}$ is the maximum sell-out period, $T$ is the time horizon, and $P_T$ is the total path-length of the comparator. The algorithm employs a simple two-stage projection strategy and connects OIO to the smoothed online convex optimization (SOCO). The paper also provides a $\Omega(\sqrt{L_{\max}T})$ lower bound for the OIO setting, which establishes the optimality of the upper bound.

## Strengths
1. The paper proposes an algorithm with near-optimal dynamic regret guarantee for OIO in non-stationary environments. The regret bound is $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{L_{\max}T(1+P_T)})$, which matches the lower bound up to a logarithmic factor. The algorithm employs a simple two-stage projection strategy and connects OIO to the smoothed online convex optimization (SOCO).

2. The paper provides a $\Omega(\sqrt{L_{\max}T})$ lower bound for the OIO setting, which establishes the optimality of the upper bound.

3. The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The theoretical analysis is sound and the results are well-explained.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide any empirical results to verify the theoretical findings. It would be beneficial to conduct experiments to evaluate the performance of the proposed algorithm and compare it to existing methods.

2. The paper does not provide any insights or guidelines on how to choose the parameters of the proposed algorithm in practice. It would be helpful to provide some practical guidance on parameter selection.

## Questions
1. Can the results be extended to other types of capacity constraints, such as convex constraints or interval constraints?

2. Can the results be extended to other types of loss functions, such as convex functions or strongly convex functions?
