# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes Thoughtbubbles, a transformer variant that performs parallel adaptive computation in latent space by learning to fork or delete residual streams. The method outperforms standard decoder LMs and non-adaptive parallel computation approaches on OpenWebText and pe2o perplexity and in zero-shot evaluations such as HellaSwag and LAMBADA.

## Strengths
1. The method enables unsupervised dynamic allocation of latent parallel computation, which can be trained as a regular decoder LM without additional signals beyond language modeling loss.

2. The method is evaluated on various datasets and tasks, showing improved performance compared to baselines.

3. The method provides interpretable regions of extra computation, allocating more computation at regions of higher uncertainty.

## Weaknesses
1. The performance improvement on downstream tasks is limited compared to the baseline model.

2. The method is not well-suited for GPU implementation, limiting its practical applicability and requiring specialized hardware for efficient computation.

3. The evaluation is primarily focused on zero-shot tasks, and it is unclear how the method would perform on fine-tuning tasks.
