# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces Distributed Neural Architectures (DNAs), a novel approach to neural network architecture that allows for dynamic routing of tokens through a collection of modules, enabling conditional computation. The authors demonstrate the feasibility of DNAs in both vision and language domains, showing competitive performance with dense baselines. They also explore the emergent properties of DNAs, including the distribution of token paths, specialization of modules, and interpretable routing decisions.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a novel approach to neural architecture that generalizes several existing techniques and opens up new research directions.
2. The authors provide a thorough empirical analysis of the properties of DNAs, including interpretability and efficiency aspects.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a comprehensive comparison with existing sparse and efficient architectures, making it difficult to assess the relative advantages of DNAs.
2. The authors do not provide a clear framework for designing DNAs, making it unclear how to effectively construct these architectures for specific tasks.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational and memory requirements of DNAs compared to traditional architectures.
4. The authors do not explore the scalability of DNAs to larger models and datasets, leaving questions about their applicability to state-of-the-art tasks.

## Questions
1. How do DNAs compare to other conditional computation methods in terms of performance and efficiency?
2. What are the key factors in designing effective DNAs for a given task?
3. How do the interpretability and efficiency properties of DNAs change as the complexity of the task increases?
4. Can the authors provide a more detailed analysis of the computational and memory requirements of DNAs?
5. How do DNAs scale to larger models and datasets, and what are the potential challenges?
