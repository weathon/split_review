# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to offline safe reinforcement learning (OSRL) called Constraint-aware Reward Relabeling (CARL). CARL addresses the challenge of learning reward-maximizing policies while satisfying safety constraints in offline RL. The method involves two key steps: updating cost evaluation functions using off-policy evaluation and relabeling rewards for unsafe state-action pairs to enforce safety constraints. The paper demonstrates that CARL effectively ensures safety and achieves high rewards in DSRL benchmark tasks, particularly under strict cost budgets. The approach is designed to be minimalist, introducing no additional hyperparameters and leveraging existing offline RL algorithms.

## Strengths
The paper is well-written and easy to understand, with a clear presentation of the problem, methodology, and results. The proposed CARL method is innovative and practical, addressing key limitations in existing OSRL approaches by eliminating the need for complex hyperparameter tuning and providing a straightforward integration with existing offline RL methods. The experimental evaluation is thorough, covering a range of tasks and comparing CARL against multiple state-of-the-art baselines. The results convincingly demonstrate CARL's ability to achieve high rewards while satisfying safety constraints, particularly under challenging conditions. The availability of code and detailed implementation information enhances reproducibility and allows for community validation and extension of the work.

## Weaknesses
The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion of the limitations of CARL, particularly in terms of scenarios where the method might not perform as well. Additionally, a deeper exploration of the trade-offs between safety and reward performance in different tasks would provide valuable insights. It would be interesting to see how CARL performs when trained on datasets with varying levels of safety violations. The paper could also benefit from a more detailed comparison with other OSRL methods, particularly those that also aim to learn safe policies from unsafe datasets. For example, it would be interesting to compare CARL with methods that use diffusion models or other generative models to sample safe actions. Finally, the paper could benefit from a more detailed analysis of the computational requirements and runtime performance of CARL compared to other methods. This would be particularly useful for applications where computational resources are limited.

## Questions
1. How does CARL handle highly non-linear or non-smooth cost constraints? Does the method maintain stability and effectiveness in such cases?
2. How does CARL perform when applied to high-dimensional state and action spaces, such as in robotics or healthcare applications?
3. How does CARL handle scenarios where the dataset contains a significant amount of noisy or suboptimal data?
4. How does CARL compare to other OSRL methods in terms of computational efficiency and scalability?
