# Review

## Summary
This paper revisits backdoor attacks in object detection and highlights several critical shortcomings of existing proposals. Specifically, it shows that commonly used measures can obscure failure modes (e.g., duplicate detections in RMA and mAP confounds in ODA), and that prior data-poisoning attacks are less effective than previously assumed, even when the poisoning rate is substantially increased. Building on these insights, the paper introduced BadDet+, a unified formulation for RMA and ODA. BadDet+ addresses the identified limitations by augmenting the object-detection training objective with a log-barrier penalty term.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and well-organized.

2. The paper diagnoses several evaluation blind spots in existing OD backdoor work and outlines a disciplined evaluation protocol tailored to OD backdoors.

3. The paper rules out simple fixes by showing that straightforward modifications to prior attacks do not eliminate these failure modes.

4. The paper demonstrates that data poisoning alone is insufficient by showing that substantially increasing the poisoning ratio of existing attacks still produces failure cases.

5. The paper introduces a unified mechanism, BadDet+, which augments the detector loss with a log-barrier penalty that integrates seamlessly with both RMA and ODA.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not evaluate pruning-based defenses, test-time detectors such as TRACE, or broad image-space transformations (e.g., additive noise, or diffusion-based purification).

2. The paper does not present the theoretical analysis showing that the proposed penalty acts selectively within a trigger-specific feature subspace, reliably inducing backdoor behavior without degrading normal predictions.

3. The paper does not present the computational analysis showing that the proposed penalty incurs negligible computational overhead.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed method perform against pruning-based defenses, test-time detectors such as TRACE, or broad image-space transformations (e.g., additive noise, or diffusion-based purification)?

2. How does the proposed method perform when the penalty parameter $\lambda$ takes other values?

3. How does the proposed method perform when the IoU threshold $\rho$ takes other values?

4. How does the proposed method perform when the trigger takes other shapes?

5. How does the proposed method perform when the object to be attacked does not contain the trigger?

6. How does the proposed method perform when the object to be attacked contains multiple triggers?

7. How does the proposed method perform when there are no poisonable objects in the image?

8. How does the proposed method perform when there are multiple poisonable objects in the image?

9. How does the proposed method perform when there is one poisonable object in the image, and the rest of the objects contain triggers?

10. How does the proposed method perform when there are multiple poisonable objects in the image, and the rest of the objects contain triggers?

11. How does the proposed method perform when the poison ratio is 10%, 20%, 30%, 40%, 60%, 70%, 80%, 90%?

12. How does the proposed method perform when the training dataset is 10%, 20%, 30%, 40%, 50%, 60%, 70%, 80%, 90% poisoned?
