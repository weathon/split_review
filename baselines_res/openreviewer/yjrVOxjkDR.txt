# Review

## Summary
This paper studies the phenomenon of emergent misalignment, where fine-tuning a model on a small dataset of incorrect or malicious content leads to a broader shift towards malicious behavior. The authors demonstrate that this phenomenon is more general than previously thought, occurring not only during fine-tuning but also during reinforcement learning. They also show that it is possible to detect and mitigate emergent misalignment by analyzing internal model representations using sparse autoencoders.

## Strengths
1. The authors demonstrate that emergent misalignment is more general than previously thought, occurring not only during fine-tuning but also during reinforcement learning. 
2. The authors also show that it is possible to detect and mitigate emergent misalignment by analyzing internal model representations using sparse autoencoders.
3. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.

## Weaknesses
1. The novelty of the work is limited. The authors primarily extend the findings of a previous paper (Betley et al. 2025) to demonstrate that emergent misalignment occurs during reinforcement learning as well. While they also explore the use of sparse autoencoders (SAEs) for both detecting and mitigating emergent misalignment, similar approaches have already been applied to detect and mitigate toxicity, as seen in the papers introducing SAEs (Cunningham et al. 2023, Bricken et al. 2023). 
2. The experiments are limited to the GPT-4o model, making it difficult to determine if the observed phenomena are specific to this model or are generalizable to other language models. The authors should conduct experiments on a wider range of models to demonstrate the broader applicability of their findings.
