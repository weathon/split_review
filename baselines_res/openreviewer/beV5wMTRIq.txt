# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces the Physics-Aware Tensor Field Neural PDE (PA-TFNP), a framework designed for climate and weather forecasting. The proposed method addresses the limitations of existing data-driven models by integrating physical principles through a rotation-equivariant tensor-field neural operator, a numerically rigorous spherical-transform gradient, and diffusion terms derived from atmospheric primitive equations. The authors demonstrate that PA-TFNP achieves significant improvements over the baseline ClimODE model.

## Strengths
- The paper is well-structured and clearly written.
- The proposed method effectively incorporates physical constraints, enhancing the model's interpretability and robustness.

## Weaknesses
- The methodological contribution of the paper is limited. The core framework is primarily based on ClimODE and the Method of Lines (MOL), with the main modifications being the integration of a diffusion term and the adoption of a tensor-field neural network (TFN) from existing work.
- The paper does not sufficiently discuss or compare with relevant related work, such as Neural GCM [1] and Aurora [2], which also propose physics-informed methods for weather and climate forecasting.
- The experimental results lack uncertainty estimates (e.g., standard deviation), making it difficult to assess the statistical significance of the reported improvements over the baseline.
- The ablation study is limited. It would be valuable to explore the impact of different padding strategies on the model's performance, as these strategies significantly affect the results (as shown in Figure 2c).
- The paper does not provide a comprehensive analysis of the computational efficiency of PA-TFNP, which is crucial for practical applications in weather and climate forecasting.

[1] Neural general circulation models for weather and climate. Nature, 632(8027):1060-1066, 2024.

[2] Aurora: A foundation model of the atmosphere. arXiv preprint arXiv:2405.13063, 2024.

## Questions
- Could the authors provide more details on the computational efficiency of PA-TFNP, including training time, inference time, and memory requirements? How does it compare to the baseline and other relevant models?
- Could the authors include uncertainty estimates (e.g., standard deviation) in the experimental results to assess the statistical significance of the improvements over the baseline?
- Could the authors provide a more detailed discussion of the related work, particularly the similarities and differences with Neural GCM and Aurora? How does PA-TFNP compare to these methods in terms of methodology and performance?
