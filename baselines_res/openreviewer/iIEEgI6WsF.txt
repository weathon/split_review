# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes On-Demand Communication (ODC) to improve the efficiency of distributed training of LLMs. ODC replaces the collective all-gather and reduce-scatter operations in FSDP with direct point-to-point communication. This allows devices to fetch or push data independently, reducing synchronization barriers from once per layer to once per minibatch. ODC also enables more effective load balancing at the minibatch level, improving device utilization and training throughput.

## Strengths
1. The paper addresses a significant issue in distributed training of LLMs, namely the inefficiency caused by synchronization barriers and workload imbalance. The proposed ODC solution provides a novel approach to mitigate these problems.

2. The authors provide a thorough analysis of the inefficiency caused by synchronization barriers and workload imbalance in FSDP. They also clearly explain how ODC addresses these issues.

3. The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of ODC on two LLM post-training tasks: SFT and RL. The experiments demonstrate consistent improvements in device utilization and training throughput, with ODC achieving up to 36% speedup over standard FSDP.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed method is only evaluated on FSDP. It is not clear whether it can be applied to other parallel training methods, such as tensor parallelism and pipeline parallelism. 

2. The authors did not provide a detailed analysis of the overhead of ODC. It would be helpful to understand the impact of ODC on communication overhead and whether it introduces any new synchronization points in the training process.

## Questions
1. Can the proposed method be applied to other parallel training methods, such as tensor parallelism and pipeline parallelism?

2. What is the overhead of ODC? Does it introduce any new synchronization points in the training process?
