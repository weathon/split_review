# Review

## Summary
This paper analyzes the challenges of integrating SSA into SNNs, focusing on two key limitations: the distinct attention distribution form compared to VSA, which limits further performance improvements, and the substantial memory overhead caused by the self-attention mechanism, which requires storing large attention weight matrices. To address these challenges, the authors propose LRF-Dyn, achieving an effective balance between performance and memory efficiency.

## Strengths
1. This paper provides a detailed theoretical analysis of the shortcomings of the SSA mechanism, which is convincing.
2. The proposed LRF-Dyn method is novel and interesting, and it effectively addresses the shortcomings of SSA.
3. The paper is well-structured and clearly written.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks a comparison of the computational complexity of the different methods.
2. The performance of LRF-Dyn on ImageNet is not as good as that of LRF-SSA, and the authors do not explain the reason for this in the paper.
3. The authors should provide a comparison of the energy consumption of the different methods to further validate the advantages of LRF-Dyn.
