# Review

## Summary
This paper shows that activation steering and influence functions, two popular but disjoint techniques, are equivalent to first order. This reveals a unifying framework for analyzing these two techniques. The paper also provides various theoretical guarantees and practical guidance on when steering is sufficient and when it is not. Experiments on language models and vision models validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-motivated and well-written. It addresses an important problem of unifying activation steering and influence functions.
2. The theoretical results are novel and comprehensive, covering the equivalence between activation steering and influence functions, practical guidance on when steering is sufficient, and the generalization bound of low-rank steering. The theoretical results are clearly presented with good intuitions and implications.
3. The experiments are solid, covering both language models and vision models.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not discuss the computational complexity of the proposed framework. It would be good to provide a comparison between the computational complexity of the proposed framework and that of activation steering and influence functions.
2. The paper does not discuss the limitations of the proposed framework. It would be good to discuss the potential limitations and future directions.

## Questions
1. What is the computational complexity of the proposed framework? How does it compare to the computational complexity of activation steering and influence functions?
2. What are the limitations of the proposed framework? What are some future directions based on the proposed framework?
