# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a new scoring function for causal discovery in discrete Bayesian networks. The scoring function is an enhanced version of BIC, which softly penalizes the complexity term based on the Shapley evidence of each edge. The empirical results show that the proposed method outperforms several baseline methods in various settings.

## Strengths
- The idea of incorporating the Shapley value into the scoring function for causal discovery is novel.
- The proposed method is a drop-in upgrade of BIC, which is easy to adopt.
- The empirical results are promising.

## Weaknesses
- The proposed method heavily relies on the Shapley value, which is known to be inconsistent in general [1]. It would be better if the authors could provide some theoretical guarantees on the consistency of the proposed method.

[1] Kumar, I. Elizabeth, and Jingrui He. "Inconsistency of Shapley values." Advances in neural information processing systems 33 (2020): 8967-8978.

- The proposed method requires training a classifier for each variable, which is computationally expensive. The runtime of the proposed method is significantly higher than that of the baseline methods, as shown in Table 5. It would be better if the authors could provide a more detailed analysis of the computational complexity of the proposed method.

- The proposed method is a local search method, while GES is an equivalence-class search method. It seems unfair to compare the proposed method with GES. It would be better if the authors could provide a comparison with an equivalence-class search version of the proposed method.

## Questions
- The proposed method uses the mean attributions of the Shapley values as the measure of the strength of an edge. However, the Shapley value depends on the choice of the reference set. Different choices of the reference set may lead to significantly different Shapley values [2]. It would be better if the authors could provide a discussion on how to choose the reference set in the proposed method.

[2] Lundberg, Scott M., and Su-In Lee. "A unified approach to interpreting model predictions." Advances in neural information processing systems 30 (2017).
