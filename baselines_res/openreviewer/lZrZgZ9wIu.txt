# Review

## Summary
This paper investigates the trade-off between accuracy and theoretical energy efficiency in the conversion of pre-trained ANNs to sparse SNNs. By adopting Cannistraci-Hebb Training (CHT), a state-of-the-art dynamic sparse training method, the authors explore how connectivity sparsity impacts SNN performance across different conversion approaches. The study reveals that sparse SNNs can achieve comparable or even superior accuracy to dense SNNs while reducing theoretical energy consumption by up to 99% compared to dense counterparts. Additionally, the research provides insights into the temporal relationship between firing rate saturation and accuracy in SNNs, showing a significant time lag where firing rate saturation precedes accuracy saturation, with sparse networks exhibiting a longer average time lag than dense ones. The findings demonstrate the effectiveness of integrating CHT into ANN-to-SNN conversion pipelines to obtain SNNs with a competitive balance between accuracy and theoretical energy efficiency.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces an interesting approach by combining dynamic sparse training (DST) with ANN-to-SNN conversion, particularly using Cannistraci-Hebb Training (CHT). This approach addresses the challenge of reducing both memory and energy consumption in ANNs.
2. The paper provides a thorough analysis of the trade-off between accuracy and theoretical energy efficiency in sparse SNNs. The authors compare the performance of sparse SNNs with dense SNNs across different conversion methods and architectures, offering valuable insights into the impact of connectivity sparsity on SNN accuracy and energy consumption.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks a detailed explanation of the Cannistraci-Hebb Training (CHT) method, which is a crucial aspect of the proposed approach. Including a more comprehensive description of CHT in the main text would enhance the reader's understanding of the methodology and its implications for the results.
2. The paper does not provide a thorough discussion of the limitations of the proposed method. It would be beneficial to address potential challenges or drawbacks of the approach, such as scalability or adaptability to different types of neural networks or datasets, to offer a more balanced perspective.
3. The paper could benefit from a more detailed comparison with existing methods. While the authors compare their approach with dense SNNs, a broader comparison with other sparse SNN methods or alternative approaches to reducing energy consumption in ANNs would provide a more comprehensive evaluation of the proposed method's effectiveness.
4. The paper does not provide any experimental results or data to support the claims made. Including empirical evidence or showcasing examples of the proposed method's application would strengthen the paper's credibility and provide a clearer understanding of its practical implications.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed approach using CHT compare with other state-of-the-art methods for reducing energy consumption in ANNs or SNNs? Including a more comprehensive comparison with existing approaches would provide a clearer understanding of the novelty and effectiveness of the proposed method.
2. What are the limitations of the proposed approach? It would be beneficial to address any potential challenges or drawbacks of the method, such as scalability or adaptability to different types of neural networks or datasets, to offer a more balanced perspective.
3. The paper mentions a significant time lag between firing rate saturation and accuracy saturation in SNNs. How does this time lag impact the practicality and real-time applicability of the proposed method? Addressing the implications of this time lag would provide a more comprehensive evaluation of the method's effectiveness.
