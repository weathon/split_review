# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a method for learning a dexterous grasping policy for tabletop settings using a single demonstration. The authors propose to use the demonstration as an initial guess and then modify it based on the object's geometry and the agent's configuration. The resulting demonstration is used to train a RL policy, which is further transferred to the real world using a flow-matching policy. The experiments show that the method can generalize to unseen objects and different embodiments.

## Strengths
- The method is novel and interesting. I believe it can be a valuable contribution to the community.
- The method is simple, and the paper is easy to follow.
- The experiments show that the method is effective in simulation and the real world, generalizing to unseen objects and different embodiments.

## Weaknesses
- The method relies on a single demonstration. Although the authors show in Table 9 that it doesn't matter much what the demonstration is, it still feels like a weakness to me. Imagine that the demonstration is not good (for example, it's grasping from the top, but most objects are easier to grasp from the side). Wouldn't it be better to have a few demonstrations, each with different preferences?
- The experiments in the real world are not very comprehensive. It would be nice to have some experiments on how the method performs with different camera extrinsics, backgrounds, and lighting conditions. The authors mention in the introduction that the method is more robust than previous methods, but this is not supported by the real-world experiments.

## Questions
- Why is the success rate for small objects higher than the success rate for flat objects? Intuitively, I would expect the opposite.
- How does the method perform with different camera extrinsics, backgrounds, and lighting conditions?
- How much does the demonstration matter? If the demonstration is not good (for example, it's grasping from the top, but most objects are easier to grasp from the side), can the method still perform well?
