# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes GoalRank, a generator-only ranking framework for recommender systems. The authors theoretically prove that for any finite Multi-generator-Evaluator model, there exists a generator-only model that achieves strictly smaller approximation error to the optimal ranking policy. They derive an evidence upper bound of the one-stage optimization objective and introduce the group-relative optimization principle, which leverages a reward model trained on real user feedback to construct a reference policy and provides a practical training objective for large generator-only rankers. GoalRank consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods in offline experiments and shows clear scaling behavior as model size increases.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a novel generator-only ranking framework, GoalRank, that challenges the prevailing Multi-generator-Evaluator paradigm in recommender systems. The theoretical proof that a generator-only model can achieve strictly smaller approximation error to the optimal ranking policy is a significant contribution.
2. The group-relative optimization principle leverages a reward model trained on real user feedback to construct a reference policy, providing a practical training objective for large generator-only rankers. This method is innovative and has shown to be effective in improving ranking performance.
3. The paper presents a solid theoretical foundation for the proposed GoalRank framework, including proofs of the existence of a generator-only model that can achieve strictly smaller approximation error and the introduction of the group-relative optimization principle. The empirical studies, including offline experiments and large-scale online A/B tests, are well-designed and provide strong evidence of the effectiveness of GoalRank.

## Weaknesses
1. While the paper provides a solid theoretical foundation for the GoalRank framework, the theoretical analysis is based on certain assumptions, such as the existence of an optimal ranking policy and the specific form of the approximation error. These assumptions may not hold in all practical scenarios, limiting the generalizability of the results.
2. The paper introduces the group-relative optimization principle, which relies on a reward model trained on real user feedback to construct a reference policy. However, the quality of the ranking depends heavily on the quality of this reward model. If the reward model is biased or inaccurate, it could negatively impact the performance of the GoalRank framework.
3. The paper focuses on the performance of the GoalRank framework in terms of ranking quality, but does not provide a detailed analysis of its computational efficiency and scalability. As the size of the dataset increases, the computational cost of training and inference may become prohibitively expensive, especially for large-scale industrial applications.

## Questions
1. The paper assumes the existence of an optimal ranking policy. How can we ensure that the reward model trained on real user feedback is unbiased and accurate enough to approximate this optimal policy?
2. How does the GoalRank framework perform in scenarios where the reward model is biased or inaccurate?
3. The paper focuses on the ranking performance of GoalRank. How does its computational efficiency and scalability compare to existing Multi-generator-Evaluator models, especially as the size of the dataset increases?
