# Review

## Summary
This paper rethinks the benign relearning in machine unlearning and proposes that the syntactic similarity is the key driver of relearning, rather than the topical relevance as previously thought. The authors conduct controlled experiments and find that fine-tuning on data syntactically similar to the forgotten content can cause the model to recover the forgotten information, even if there is no topical overlap. They further propose a simple but effective strategy to mitigate benign relearning: syntactic diversification, which paraphrases the forget set into diverse forms before applying unlearning. The experiments demonstrate that this approach can effectively suppress benign relearning, accelerate forgetting, and alleviate the trade-off between unlearning efficacy and model utility.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a novel perspective on the underlying mechanisms of benign relearning in machine unlearning, challenging the prevailing assumption that topical relevance is the primary driver. By identifying syntactic similarity as the key factor, the authors provide a new lens through which to understand unlearning failures, potentially opening new avenues for research in this area.

2. The proposed syntactic diversification method is simple but effective. By paraphrasing the forget set into diverse structures, the method breaks the structural rigidity that leads to benign relearning. This approach not only improves the robustness of unlearning but also accelerates the forgetting process, addressing a common trade-off between unlearning efficacy and model utility.

3. The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The authors present their findings in a clear and organized manner, making it accessible to readers who may not be familiar with the technical details of machine unlearning.

## Weaknesses
1. The experiments are primarily conducted on the TOFU dataset, which is a synthetic dataset consisting of QA pairs generated from biographies of fictitious authors. While this dataset is useful for controlled experiments, it may not fully represent the complexity and diversity of real-world data. The authors should consider conducting experiments on more diverse and realistic datasets to validate their findings.

2. The paper focuses on the phenomenon of benign relearning, where forgotten information reemerges after fine-tuning on seemingly unrelated data. However, it does not explore other potential forms of relearning or unlearning failures, such as the relearning of harmful or sensitive content. The authors should consider discussing how their findings might generalize to these other scenarios.

3. The authors propose the syntactic diversification method to mitigate benign relearning, but they do not provide a comprehensive comparison with existing unlearning methods. The authors should consider comparing their method with other state-of-the-art unlearning techniques to demonstrate its effectiveness and advantages.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed syntactic diversification method perform on more diverse and realistic datasets beyond the TOFU dataset? Are there any limitations or challenges in applying this method to real-world scenarios?

2. The paper focuses on the phenomenon of benign relearning. How might the findings generalize to other forms of relearning or unlearning failures, such as the relearning of harmful or sensitive content?

3. How does the proposed syntactic diversification method compare with existing unlearning methods in terms of effectiveness and efficiency? Are there any scenarios where other methods might be more appropriate or effective?
