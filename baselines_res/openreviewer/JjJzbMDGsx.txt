# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces the Language Confusion Gate (LCG), a lightweight, plug-in solution that filters tokens during decoding without altering the base LLM. The LCG is trained using norm-adjusted self-distillation to predict appropriate language families and apply masking only when needed. When evaluated across various models, including Qwen3, GPT-OSS, Gemma3, Llama3.1, LCG decreases language confusion significantly, often by an order of magnitude, without negatively impacting task performance.

## Strengths
- The proposed method is well motivated, based on the observation that language confusion is infrequent, correct-language tokens are usually among the top predictions, and output token embedding norms are larger for high-resource languages, which biases sampling. 
- The proposed method is simple and effective, achieving significant improvement in language confusion while maintaining task performance.

## Weaknesses
- The proposed method is only evaluated on LLMs with a size of 8-30B. It is unclear whether the proposed method can still be effective on larger LLMs (e.g., 70B, 175B).

## Questions
- How does the proposed method perform on larger LLMs (e.g., 70B, 175B)?
