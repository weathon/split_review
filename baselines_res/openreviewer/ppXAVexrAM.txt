# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a novel framework, ARSS, which leverages a GPT-style decoder-only autoregressive model to generate novel views from a single image, conditioned on a predefined camera trajectory. The framework includes a video tokenizer to map continuous image sequences into discrete tokens and a camera encoder to convert camera trajectories into 3D positional guidance. Additionally, an autoregressive transformer module is proposed to randomly permute the spatial order of tokens while maintaining their temporal order. Qualitative and quantitative experiments on public datasets demonstrate that ARSS achieves comparable performance to state-of-the-art view synthesis methods based on diffusion models.

## Strengths
1. This paper is well-written and easy to follow.

2. The motivation is clear, and the proposed method is technically sound.

3. The results are promising, as the method achieves comparable performance to state-of-the-art view synthesis methods based on diffusion models.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed method lacks significant novelty, as it primarily combines existing modules, such as the video tokenizer and the autoregressive transformer, to achieve camera control. While the camera autoencoder is a new addition, it is not sufficiently highlighted as a key contribution.

2. The paper lacks an analysis of the camera autoencoder. The authors claim that the camera autoencoder can extract camera features containing the information of the original camera trajectory, but this is not clearly demonstrated in the experiments. Additionally, it is unclear whether the camera autoencoder is trained end-to-end with the autoregressive transformer or separately.

3. The paper does not include an analysis of the autoregressive transformer module, particularly regarding the random permutation of spatial orders. It is unclear whether this random permutation is truly beneficial or if it might introduce additional complexity without significant gain.

4. The paper does not provide an analysis of the computational complexity or runtime of the proposed method compared to other state-of-the-art approaches. This information would be valuable for assessing the practicality of the method.
