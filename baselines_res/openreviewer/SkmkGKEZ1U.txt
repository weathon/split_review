# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a framework, LLM+CAS, and a tool, O-Forge, that combines LLMs with CAS to generate proofs of asymptotic inequalities. The framework consists of several steps: latex input, decomposition proposal by LLM, regime-wise simplification, and symbolic verification by CAS. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of this framework on two examples proposed by Terry Tao.

## Strengths
The paper is well-motivated and addresses an important problem. It is also well-written and easy to follow. The authors provide a clear explanation of the framework and its components.

## Weaknesses
The main weakness of this paper is its lack of novelty and contribution. The proposed framework is a straightforward application of LLMs combined with CAS, which is not a new idea. The two examples presented in the paper are not significant mathematical problems. The framework only demonstrates trivial asymptotic inequalities that can be easily verified by mathematica. 

The authors claim that their framework can help professional mathematicians, but they do not provide any evidence or examples of how it can assist with real research-level math problems. The empirical evaluation is limited to a small number of problems, and there is no comparison with other methods or baselines.

## Questions
1. Can you provide more examples of real research-level math problems that can be solved or assisted by your framework?
2. How does your framework compare with other methods or baselines in terms of performance and accuracy?
3. Have you conducted a more extensive empirical evaluation of your framework, including a wider range of problems and datasets?
