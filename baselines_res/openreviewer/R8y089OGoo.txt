# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a new RL algorithm DIPOLE for stable and controllable diffusion policy optimization. The authors decompose the optimal policy into a pair of stably learned dichotomous policies, one for reward maximization and the other for reward minimization. This design enables flexible control over the level of greediness during inference. The effectiveness of DIPOLE is demonstrated through evaluations in offline and offline-to-online RL settings, as well as training a large vision-language-action model for autonomous driving.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a novel decomposition of the optimal policy into dichotomous policies, which addresses the instability issues associated with traditional weighted regression schemes.
2. The method allows for flexible control over the level of greediness in the final policy, which is a significant advantage in many decision-making tasks.
3. The paper provides a thorough evaluation of DIPOLE across various benchmarks, including ExORL, OGBench, and NAVSIM, demonstrating its effectiveness in different settings.

## Weaknesses
1. The dichotomous policy decomposition and the linear combination for inference may introduce additional computational overhead compared to simpler methods.
2. The performance of DIPOLE heavily depends on the quality of the reference policy, which may affect its robustness in different scenarios.
3. The method involves several hyperparameters, such as the greediness factor, that require careful tuning, which could be a limitation for practical applications.

## Questions
1. How does the computational overhead of DIPOLE compare to other state-of-the-art methods, especially during inference?
2. Can the authors provide more insights into the robustness of DIPOLE with respect to the quality of the reference policy?
3. How sensitive is DIPOLE to the choice of hyperparameters, and are there any guidelines for tuning these parameters?
