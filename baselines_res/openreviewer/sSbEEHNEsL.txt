# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a new training method for USR, which addresses two key limitations of USR that prevent scaling to larger models and diverse unlabelled data: the computational cost of autoregressive pseudo-labelling and the decoupled design that reduces robustness to out-of-distribution inputs. By introducing CTC-driven teacher forcing and mixed sampling, USR 2.0 combines the efficiency and robustness of CTC with the modelling flexibility of attention-based decoding. This design improves generalisation to long utterances, noisy conditions, and unseen datasets, while reducing training time by nearly half. These robustness gains also translate to improved in-distribution performance, since unlabelled data often comes from out-of-distribution sources. Scaling both model and corpus, USR 2.0 achieves state-of-the-art performance across ASR, VSR, and AVSR with a single model.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper should conduct experiments on more OOD datasets. For example, the paper could evaluate the model's performance on the LibriSpeech test-other set for ASR, which is a more challenging dataset than the test-clean set. Additionally, for AVSR, the paper could evaluate the model's performance on the LRS2 dataset, which is different from LRS3 and contains more variations.

2. The paper should conduct experiments on more model sizes. The current experiments only include Base, Base+, and Large models, which are relatively small in size. It is recommended to conduct experiments on larger models, such as Huge and Giant models, to demonstrate the scalability of the method.
