# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces an array of benchmarks for LLM agents to measure their capabilities in economic settings, specifically in the tasks of procurement, scheduling, and pricing. The authors construct the benchmark environments in a way that allows for difficulty scaling to arbitrarily high difficulty levels, and experimentally validate this technique. The authors evaluate the performance of a diverse array of LLM agents and demonstrate that analyzing the way LLM agents tackle our benchmarks can give rise to economically meaningful insights regarding mechanisms underlying observed differences in benchmark scores.

## Strengths
1. The authors introduce an array of benchmarks for LLM agents in economic settings, specifically in the tasks of procurement, scheduling, and pricing. The benchmarks are designed to measure the capabilities of LLM agents in tackling complex economic problems in these areas.
2. The benchmark environments are constructed in a way that allows for difficulty scaling to arbitrarily high difficulty levels. This is achieved by using synthetic environments and scalable difficulty levels, which can forestall saturation and enable the benchmarks to be used for a long time.
3. The authors evaluate the performance of a diverse array of LLM agents, including LLM agents based on cutting-edge models such as GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro. The evaluation shows that the benchmarks can provide economically meaningful insights regarding mechanisms underlying observed differences in benchmark scores.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a comprehensive evaluation of the benchmarks, including the reliability and validity of the evaluation results, the comparison with other benchmarks, and the limitations of the benchmarks.
2. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the performance of LLM agents on the benchmarks, including the strengths and weaknesses of the agents, the impact of different factors on the performance, and the potential biases and fairness issues in the evaluation.
3. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the economically meaningful insights provided by the benchmarks, including the accuracy and reliability of the insights, the potential biases and limitations of the insights, and the impact of the insights on real-world economic decisions.

## Questions
1. How reliable and valid are the evaluation results of the benchmarks? How do they compare with other existing benchmarks in the field?
2. What are the strengths and weaknesses of the LLM agents evaluated on the benchmarks? How do different factors, such as the architecture, size, and training data of the models, impact their performance?
3. What are the economically meaningful insights provided by the benchmarks? How accurate and reliable are the insights? What are the potential biases and limitations of the insights? How can the insights be used to inform real-world economic decisions?
