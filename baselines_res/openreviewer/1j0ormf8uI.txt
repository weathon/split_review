# Review

## Summary
This paper develops a lower prediction bound (LPB) for survival time across different treatments in the general right-censored setting. The authors propose a new calibration procedure under the potential outcome framework. Under the strong ignorability assumption, they propose a reweighting scheme that can transform the problem into a weighted conformal inference problem, allowing an LPB to be obtained via quantile regression with an exact miscoverage guarantee. Furthermore, the procedure is doubly robust against model misspecification. Empirical evaluations on synthetic and real-world clinical data demonstrate the validity and informativeness of the constructed LPBs, which indicate the potential of the analytical benchmark for comparing and selecting personalized treatments.

## Strengths
- The authors propose a new procedure for quantifying the uncertainty of counterfactual survival time predictions under different treatments in general right-censored data. The procedure establishes an upper bound for the miscoverage rate that can be reliably identified and further calibrated through weighted conformal prediction.
- The authors provide a distribution-free exact guarantee for the counterfactual prediction set and quantify the error from weight estimation. They also provide the doubly robustness property.
- The authors validate the procedure using both synthetic and real clinical data. On the synthetic data, they show that the calibration process yields less conservative LPBs while maintaining the desired coverage guarantee. On the clinical data, they demonstrate its effectiveness in identifying optimal treatments across diverse populations.

## Weaknesses
- The authors only compare their method to other baselines in the synthetic data experiment. It would be helpful to also include a comparison on real data.
- The authors could consider comparing their method to other existing methods, such as the one proposed by Gui et al. (2024), which offers more informative LPBs for Type-I right-censored data by employing the adaptive cutoff method.
- The authors should provide a more detailed discussion of the limitations of their proposed method, including the strong ignorability assumption and the potential impact of data imbalance on the estimation of the weight function.

## Questions
- How does the proposed method perform compared to other existing methods, such as the one proposed by Gui et al. (2024), on real-world clinical data?
- Can the authors provide a more detailed discussion of the limitations of their proposed method, including the strong ignorability assumption and the potential impact of data imbalance on the estimation of the weight function?
- How does the proposed method handle extreme scenarios, such as imbalanced treatment usage proportions and high censoring rates, in real-world clinical data?
- What are the potential implications of inaccurate estimation of the weight function on the overall performance of the proposed method in counterfactual outcome predictions under such treatments?
- How does the proposed method compare to other state-of-the-art methods in terms of computational efficiency and scalability to large datasets?
- Are there any specific hyperparameter settings or tuning procedures required for the proposed method to achieve optimal performance?
