# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a new backpropagation method based on the inverse sigmoid function. The method is claimed to be more precise than the traditional derivative-based backpropagation and to have the potential to overcome the gradient vanishing problem. The method is illustrated with some basic examples.

## Strengths
The paper is clear and well written. The proposed method is simple and easy to understand.

## Weaknesses
The paper is clearly not ready for publication. The proposed method is not properly analysed or investigated. The experimental evaluation is very limited and inconclusive. 

The proposed method is a minor variation of the traditional backpropagation, which is a special case of the more general BPTT (Backpropagation Through Time) method. The authors seem to be unaware of the existence of BPTT, which is a pity since their method can actually be seen as a variant of BPTT where the weight updates are calculated using a finite difference method on the activations. 

The authors also seem to be unaware of the existence of the "local representation alignment" (LRA) principle (see, for example, the paper "Local representation alignment in neural networks" by Krähenbühl et al., 2019). The LRA principle states that the activation function should be applied before the weights in the backward pass, which is exactly what the authors propose. The LRA principle has been shown to significantly improve the performance of neural networks. 

The experimental evaluation is very limited and inconclusive. The authors use a very small dataset consisting of only 100 random points with a scaled cosine function. They also use a very small neural network with only one hidden layer and two neurons. It is not clear why the authors did not use a more standard benchmark dataset and a more standard neural network architecture. 

The authors also claim that their method can overcome the gradient vanishing problem. However, there is no experimental evidence to support this claim. The authors should have compared their method with traditional backpropagation and other methods that claim to overcome the gradient vanishing problem on a variety of tasks and neural network architectures. 

Overall, the paper does not present a significant contribution to the field and is not ready for publication.

## Questions
1) Have you compared your method with BPTT and other methods that use a local representation alignment principle?
2) Why did you use such a small dataset and neural network for the experiments?
3) Is there any experimental evidence that your method can overcome the gradient vanishing problem?
