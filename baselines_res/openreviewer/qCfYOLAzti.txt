# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a novel approach to unlearning in large language models (LLMs) by introducing a bootstrapping (BS) framework that addresses the "squeezing effect." This effect is identified as a common issue in existing unlearning methods, where probability mass is redistributed into high-likelihood regions, often resulting in semantically related rephrasing of the target content. The authors demonstrate that this redistribution can obscure true unlearning, as models may appear to generate different words but still convey the same harmful or sensitive information. To counteract this, the BS framework incorporates the model's own high-confidence predictions (model beliefs) into the unlearning objective. This approach aims to suppress not only the target responses but also these high-likelihood alternatives, thereby achieving more effective unlearning. The paper presents two implementations: BS-T (token) and BS-S (sequence), which target specific tokens and entire sequences, respectively. Extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks show that the proposed method outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in both unlearning and retention of desired knowledge, with theoretical analysis supporting the effectiveness of the approach in reshaping gradient dynamics and mitigating the squeezing effect.

## Strengths
- The paper introduces a novel method to address unlearning in LLMs by tackling the often-overlooked "squeezing effect," where models redistribute probability mass into semantically related rephrasing of target content. This approach is innovative in incorporating the model's own high-confidence predictions into the unlearning objective, which is a fresh perspective compared to traditional gradient ascent methods.
- The authors provide a solid theoretical foundation for their proposed bootstrapping framework. They draw on existing learning dynamics frameworks and extend them to explain how their method mitigates the squeezing effect. This theoretical grounding not only strengthens the credibility of the approach but also provides valuable insights into the underlying mechanisms of unlearning in LLMs.
- The proposed method is practical and can be easily integrated into existing unlearning frameworks. The authors provide clear pseudocode for their algorithms (BS-T and BS-S) and demonstrate compatibility with other unlearning objectives and regularizations. This makes the approach adaptable to various use cases and accessible for implementation.
- The paper conducts extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks, including TOFU, MUSE, and WMDP, using various LLM families such as Llama and Zephyr. This comprehensive evaluation demonstrates the robustness and effectiveness of the proposed method, showing superior performance in unlearning while preserving retention of desired knowledge.

## Weaknesses
- The paper does not provide a thorough analysis of the computational cost associated with the proposed methods, particularly for the BS-S (sequence) approach. Sampling and processing high-confidence sequences could potentially be resource-intensive, especially for larger models or in resource-constrained environments. Including an analysis of training time, memory usage, or computational efficiency would provide a more complete picture of the practicality of the approach.
- The paper primarily focuses on unlearning harmful or sensitive content. However, it does not extensively explore how the proposed methods might affect other aspects of model performance, such as general knowledge or task-specific capabilities. Evaluating the potential impact on these areas could strengthen the assessment of the method's broader applicability and potential trade-offs.
- While the paper includes comparisons with state-of-the-art baselines, it does not provide a detailed analysis of how sensitive the proposed methods are to hyperparameters, such as the temperature used in sampling for BS-S or the lambda values in the loss functions. Understanding the impact of these hyperparameters on the effectiveness of the unlearning process would be valuable for practical implementation.

## Questions
- Can you provide more details on the computational cost of the BS-S method, especially in comparison to other baselines? How does the sampling process affect training time and memory usage?
- How does the proposed method affect other aspects of model performance beyond unlearning? Have you evaluated potential impacts on general knowledge or task-specific capabilities?
- How sensitive are the proposed methods to hyperparameters like temperature and lambda? Could you provide guidelines or best practices for setting these values?
