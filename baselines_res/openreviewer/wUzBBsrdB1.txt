# Review

## Summary
The paper discusses the importance of correctly setting the $L_0$ parameter in Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) to ensure they effectively disentangle features in Large Language Models (LLMs). It highlights that using an incorrect $L_0$, whether too high or too low, can lead to feature mixing, reducing the monosemanticity of the SAE's latent representations. The authors propose a new metric to help identify the optimal $L_0$ and demonstrate its effectiveness on synthetic data and LLMs. They also suggest that many commonly used SAEs have suboptimal $L_0$ values, potentially affecting their interpretability and performance.

## Strengths
- The paper addresses a critical parameter in SAE training, showing that $L_0$ significantly impacts feature disentanglement. This is particularly relevant for LLM interpretability, where monosemanticity is key.
- The authors provide a new metric to guide $L_0$ selection, which is validated on synthetic data and LLMs. This metric offers a practical way to assess SAE quality and ensure correct feature disentanglement.
- By revealing that many SAEs use inappropriate $L_0$ values, the paper highlights a common issue and provides guidance on how to improve SAE training, benefiting the broader field of LLM interpretability.

## Weaknesses
- The paper primarily focuses on $L_0$ without discussing other important SAE hyperparameters, such as the activation function, number of hidden neurons, or learning rates. These parameters also impact feature disentanglement and model performance, so a more comprehensive analysis that includes their effects would strengthen the findings.
- While the proposed metric is helpful, it still requires manual sweeping over different $L_0$ values to find the optimal one. This approach can be labor-intensive and impractical for large-scale applications. The paper would benefit from discussing potential automated methods to determine $L_0$ during training, which would make the metric more practical for real-world use.
- The paper could benefit from a more detailed comparison with other methods for choosing SAE hyperparameters, such as the method proposed in [1]. This would provide a clearer context for the advantages and limitations of the proposed metric.
- The paper lacks a theoretical framework for why the proposed metric works. Providing a more rigorous theoretical justification would help readers understand the underlying principles and potentially extend the approach to other SAE hyperparameters.
- The experiments are conducted on a limited number of models and datasets. Expanding the analysis to include a wider range of LLMs and SAE architectures would provide more generalizable insights and increase the robustness of the findings.

[1] Lee, J., Pearce, M. T., & Sharkey, L. (2025). Adaptive feature activation sparse autoencoders. arXiv preprint arXiv:2507.04516.

## Questions
- How does the proposed metric for selecting $L_0$ compare to other methods for choosing SAE hyperparameters, such as the method proposed in [1]?
- Are there any plans to automate the selection of $L_0$ during training? How might this be achieved?
- Can the insights gained from this study be extended to other SAE hyperparameters, such as activation functions or learning rates?
- How do the findings generalize across different LLM architectures, such as GPT models or other non-transformer-based models?
