# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a large-scale empirical study of the scaling properties of different techniques used in RL for LLMs. The authors derive a method for fitting predictive scaling fits for accuracy on the validation set that allows us to quantify the asymptotic performance and compute efficiency of an RL method. They conduct a careful series of ablations of several algorithmic options that go into the RL recipe. Combining these choices yields the SCALERL recipe which scales better than all existing recipes in our experiments.

## Strengths
The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The empirical study is extensive, and the conclusions are convincing. The fitted curve is a valuable tool for researchers and practitioners in the field.

## Weaknesses
The paper focuses on the scaling properties of different techniques used in RL for LLMs, and it would be beneficial to explore the applicability of the proposed framework to other domains or types of RL tasks.

## Questions
1. How do you think of the applicability of the fitted curve beyond LLMs?
2. The paper presents extensive empirical results, but it would be valuable to provide a more in-depth theoretical analysis of the scaling phenomena. Can you offer any theoretical insights or hypotheses to complement the empirical findings?
