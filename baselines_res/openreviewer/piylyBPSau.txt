# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes GenCoGS, a novel 3DGS-based method for few-shot novel view synthesis. GenCoGS aims to enhance the scene completion capability of existing 3DGS-based few-shot methods. The method incorporates two generative completion-based strategies: a generative point cloud completion-based Gaussian initialization strategy and a generative pseudo view completion-based Gaussian optimization strategy. The generative point cloud completion-based strategy generates and filters complementary points to create a complete point cloud with refined structural and appearance information for Gaussian initialization. The generative pseudo view completion-based strategy uses an image-to-video diffusion model to synthesize complete pseudo views, which benefits Gaussian optimization especially within unobserved scene regions and mitigates hallucination for less appearance distortion. The method is evaluated on three benchmark datasets and shows significant improvements over baseline methods.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a novel approach to few-shot novel view synthesis by incorporating generative completion-based strategies. This approach addresses the limitations of existing 3DGS-based few-shot methods and enhances scene completion capability.
2. The paper provides a thorough evaluation of the proposed method on three benchmark datasets and demonstrates its superiority over baseline methods. The quantitative results show significant improvements in common metrics such as PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational complexity of the proposed method. Given that the method involves generative models and multiple strategies, it would be beneficial to discuss the computational requirements and compare them with baseline methods.
2. The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion of the limitations of the proposed method. It would be helpful to address any potential drawbacks or scenarios where the method may not perform as well, providing insights for future research and potential improvements.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed method handle scenes with significant occlusions or complex structures that may not be fully captured by the initial point cloud?
2. Can the method be extended to handle dynamic scenes or moving objects, and if so, how would this affect the performance and initialization strategy?
3. How sensitive is the method to the choice of hyperparameters, such as the number of points in the initial point cloud or the perturbation amplitude in the camera trajectory?
