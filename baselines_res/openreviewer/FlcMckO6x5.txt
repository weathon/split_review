# Review

## Summary
This paper provides a theoretical understanding of the separable neural networks (SepNNs), which factorize a multivariate function into linear combinations of univariate functions. Specifically, the authors prove that SepNN can approximate any multivariate function with arbitrary precision, confirming its representation completeness. Then, they derive the neural tangent kernel (NTK) regimes for SepNN, showing that the NTK of infinite-width SepNN converges to a deterministic (or random) kernel under infinite (or fixed) decomposition rank. Finally, they propose an efficient separable preconditioned gradient descent (SepPGD) for optimizing SepNN to alleviate the spectral bias of SepNN by provably adjusting its NTK spectrum. The experiments validate the efficiency of SepNN and the effectiveness of SepPGD for alleviating spectral bias.

## Strengths
1. This paper is well-written and easy to follow.

2. The authors provide a comprehensive theoretical analysis of SepNN, including approximation theory and NTK regimes. 

3. SepNN can approximate any multivariate function with arbitrary precision, confirming its representation completeness.

4. The proposed SepPGD enjoys an efficient complexity for training samples, which is much more efficient than previous neural network PGD methods.

## Weaknesses
1. The authors focus on three types of SepNNs: CP, TT, and Tucker. It would be better to provide a brief discussion on the differences between these three types and when to use them. 

2. The experiments are conducted on synthetic datasets. How about the performance on real-world datasets?

## Questions
1. How does the decomposition rank affect the performance of SepNN?

2. In Theorem 1, the authors prove that SepNNs possess the capacity to approximate any continuous multivariate function with arbitrary precision. How about the convergence rate?

3. How does the proposed SepPGD affect the convergence rate?

4. In Table 1, what is the meaning of r for the Hessian-based methods?
