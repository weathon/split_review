# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces a novel approach for modeling multivariate time series by treating them as videos. The proposed Spectro-Temporal Diffusion (ST-Diff) framework leverages the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) to convert time series data into a time-frequency video tensor, enabling the application of video diffusion models. This representation preserves the temporal evolution of frequency components, which is crucial for capturing complex temporal dynamics. The framework is evaluated extensively on standard benchmarks, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance in unconditional time series generation.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a novel paradigm by treating time series as videos, which allows for the application of spatiotemporal models and better captures the temporal and spectral dynamics of the data.
2. The ST-Diff framework leverages the STFT to convert time series into a time-frequency video tensor, preserving the temporal evolution of frequency components and enhancing the model's ability to learn complex temporal dependencies.
3. The paper provides a thorough empirical evaluation of the ST-Diff framework, demonstrating its superiority over existing time-domain and image-based diffusion models in unconditional time series generation tasks.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks a detailed discussion on the computational complexity of the proposed method, which is an important consideration for practical applications.
2. The evaluation is limited to unconditional generation tasks, and the framework's performance on conditional tasks, such as forecasting and imputation, is not explored.
3. The paper does not thoroughly discuss the potential limitations of the proposed method, such as its sensitivity to hyperparameters or its performance on non-stationary data.

## Questions
1. How does the computational complexity of the Spectro-Temporal Diffusion (ST-Diff) framework compare to existing diffusion models for time series, and what are the trade-offs between performance and efficiency?
2. Can the ST-Diff framework be extended to conditional generation tasks, such as forecasting and imputation, and if so, how would this affect the model's design and performance?
3. How does the framework handle non-stationary data, and what are the limitations of using the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) for such cases?
4. Can the authors provide more insights into the choice of evaluation metrics and how they relate to the quality of the generated time series?
5. How does the framework perform on longer sequences, and what are the potential challenges in scaling the model to larger datasets?
