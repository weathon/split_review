# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a neural audio codec that decouples speech and background sound representations. This disentanglement enables several downstream tasks such as speech enhancement, one-shot voice conversion, and automatic speech recognition.

## Soundness
3

## Presentation
3

## Contribution
3

## Strengths
The proposed method is simple and effective, and the paper is well-written and easy to follow.

## Weaknesses
- The authors should clarify why DAC and HiFi-Codec are not included in the comparison for speech enhancement and one-shot voice conversion.
- For one-shot voice conversion, the authors should also report the speaker similarity and WER for the reference speech. Additionally, the authors should explain why the SIM score for SpeechTokenizer is lower than that of DeCodec, even though the WER for DeCodec is higher.
- In the ablation study, the authors should include results for SG only and SG+RST to better demonstrate the effectiveness of SOP.
- The authors should also conduct a subjective quality assessment for the reconstructed speech.

## Questions
- The authors state that "the performance of these methods in real noisy environments remains limited." Could you clarify what is meant by "real noisy environments"?
- The authors state that "the performance of these methods in real noisy environments remains limited. Synergistic optimization with background sound decoupling mechanisms offers the potential to enhance their performance in real applications." However, it appears that the proposed method is not optimized for specific downstream tasks. Could you clarify this?
- For one-shot voice conversion, what is the difference between DeCodec and DeCodec+SE?
- In Table 4, what does SDR-B represent? It seems that SDR-S is the most relevant to the proposed method, so the authors should emphasize this metric.
