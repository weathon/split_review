# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a cross-validation method for selecting tuning parameters for graphon models. The method is based on randomly imputing missing values in the folds. The authors show that the method is theoretically sound and has computational efficiency. The method is validated using synthetic and real data.

## Strengths
The paper proposes a novel method for cross-validation on graphs. The method is simple and theoretically sound. The paper is generally well-written and the method is well-validated using synthetic and real data. The method is novel and potentially impactful as it can be used for tuning parameters in a wide range of graph-based machine learning methods.

## Weaknesses
- The method is not compared to other model selection methods, such as the one based on the out-of-bootstrap (see "Efficient model selection using the out-of-bootstrap", T. Jiang and Z. Shen, 2021). It would be good to add a comparison to this method and other model selection methods.
- The theoretical results rely on the assumption that the number of folds $K$ grows as fast as the number of nodes $n$ goes to infinity. However, in practice, a finite value of $K$ is used, and it is unclear how large $K$ should be in practice. It would be good to investigate the performance of the method for different values of $K$ and $n$ to provide practical guidance on how to choose $K$ in practice.
- The method is only applicable to graph-based models where the edges are assumed to be independent. This limits the applicability of the method to a narrower range of models compared to other model selection methods that can be used for a wider range of machine learning models.

## Questions
- How does the method compare to other model selection methods, such as the one based on the out-of-bootstrap?
- How should the value of $K$ be chosen in practice, and how does the performance of the method vary with different values of $K$?
- Are there any other limitations or assumptions of the method that I may have missed?
