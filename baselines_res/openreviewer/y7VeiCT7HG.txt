# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a new batch acquisition function for multi-objective Bayesian optimization based on the probability of matching, which is the likelihood that a batch matches the true Pareto set. The proposed acquisition function is a product of the expected hypervolume improvement and the minimum distance between points in the batch. The authors claim that the proposed acquisition function balances the exploitation and exploration better than the existing methods.

## Strengths
The authors propose a new batch acquisition function for multi-objective Bayesian optimization based on the probability of matching. They claim that the proposed acquisition function balances the exploitation and exploration better than the existing methods.

## Weaknesses
The proposed acquisition function is a simple modification of qEHVI with an additional term that encourages the points in the batch to be spread out. It is not clear why this simple modification can improve the performance of multi-objective Bayesian optimization significantly. The authors did not provide any theoretical justification for the proposed acquisition function. The empirical results are not convincing either. More details are provided in the questions below.

## Questions
1. Why does the proposed acquisition function balance the exploitation and exploration better than the existing methods? It is not clear to me why adding an additional term that encourages the points in the batch to be spread out can help with the balance of exploitation and exploration. 

2. The authors claim that the proposed method can improve coverage performance. However, there is no theoretical justification for this claim. 

3. In Equation (8), the authors propose to use the minimum distance between the selected batch and the previous observations to encourage the new batch not to overlap with the previously queried regions. However, the current Pareto optimal set $\mathcal{X}_n^*$ may not be the true Pareto optimal set $\mathcal{X}^*$. Using $\mathcal{X}_n^*$ here may lead to oversampling in the local region around $\mathcal{X}_n^*$ and undermine the goal of achieving comprehensive coverage over the true Pareto optimal set $\mathcal{X}^*$. 

4. In Section 3.3, the authors claim that the proposed method has the same complexity as qEHVI for estimating the probability of being Pareto optimal. However, the proposed method has an additional term that estimates the coverage probability. The complexity of this term should also be taken into account. 

5. The empirical results are not convincing. The proposed method is only compared against qEHVI and QSVGD. There are many other batch acquisition functions for multi-objective Bayesian optimization, such as the ones mentioned in the related work section. The authors should compare the proposed method against these baselines too. 

6. The performance of the proposed method is not stable. In Figure 2, the performance of the proposed method with batch size 10 is worse than the performance with batch size 5 for most of the cases. 

7. The proposed method has a large standard deviation for some of the cases, such as Bi-2, Bi-3, and Tri-2 in Figure 2. The proposed method may not be robust.
