# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a new method to improve the safety alignment of LLMs, called Alignment-Weighted DPO (AW-DPO). The authors first conduct a probing experiment to show that the alignment of current LLMs is largely independent of deep reasoning, supporting the view that existing safety alignment is often superficial. Then the authors construct a new CoT safety fine-tuning dataset and propose the AW-DPO method, which assigns separate weights to reasoning and response components to enable more fine-grained and targeted preference optimization. Extensive experiments show that AW-DPO outperforms several baselines in safety while maintaining overall utility.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The probing experiment is interesting and provides valuable insights into the mechanism of current LLMs.
3. The proposed method is well-motivated and novel.
4. The experiments are comprehensive and solid.

## Weaknesses
1. The authors propose to deactivate the reasoning-critical neurons to validate the hypothesis that the alignment of current LLMs is largely independent of deep reasoning. However, deactivating these neurons may also affect the model's reasoning ability, which in turn could affect the model's performance on reasoning tasks. The authors should discuss this potential influence and rule out the effect of weakened reasoning ability on the alignment performance when drawing the conclusion that the alignment of current LLMs is largely independent of deep reasoning.
2. The authors should explain why they use linear probing to locate the reasoning-critical neurons instead of other methods, such as the method proposed in [1].
3. The authors should explain why they use the difference in full harmfulness scores to construct preference pairs instead of the difference in reasoning scores, as the former may overlook some reasoning-related mis-alignments.

[1] Finding Safety Neurons in Large Language Models. https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14144

## Questions
1. Why do the authors use linear probing to locate the reasoning-critical neurons instead of other methods, such as the method proposed in [1]?
2. Why do the authors use the difference in full harmfulness scores to construct preference pairs instead of the difference in reasoning scores?
3. The authors should discuss the potential influence of weakened reasoning ability on the alignment performance when drawing the conclusion that the alignment of current LLMs is largely independent of deep reasoning.
