# Review

## Summary
This paper investigates the theoretical limits of image watermarking capacity and demonstrates that current methods are far from reaching these limits. The authors present a series of bounds on the capacity for different conditions and show that, even under simple constraints, the theoretical capacity far exceeds current practical capabilities. To support their findings, they train a new watermarking model called Chunky Seal, which significantly increases the message capacity while maintaining image quality and robustness. The paper argues that there is substantial room for improvement in watermarking capacity, which could enable new applications and improve existing ones.

## Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The authors present a clear and compelling motivation for their work, and the theoretical framework is well-explained.
- The theoretical analysis is thorough and well-structured, with clear explanations of the different capacity bounds and the conditions under which they apply.
- The experimental results are impressive, showing that even a simple linear embedder and extractor can achieve higher capacity than current state-of-the-art models like Video Seal.
- The introduction of Chunky Seal, a watermarking model that significantly increases capacity while maintaining quality and robustness, demonstrates the potential for improving watermarking performance.

## Weaknesses
- The paper focuses primarily on image watermarking, and it is unclear how well the theoretical insights would generalize to other domains such as video or multi-modal content.
- The robustness bounds are heuristic rather than formal, which could limit the reliability of the theoretical analysis in certain scenarios.
- The paper does not provide a comprehensive comparison with a wide range of existing watermarking methods, which could help to contextualize the results and highlight the advantages of the proposed approach.
- The Chunky Seal model, while showing promising results, is relatively simple and may not represent the best possible performance achievable with more complex architectures and training strategies.

## Questions
- How would the theoretical capacity bounds change when considering more complex and realistic image distributions, such as datasets like ImageNet?
- Can the authors provide more details on the handcrafted embedder and extractor used in the experiments? How does it compare to state-of-the-art watermarking models in terms of computational efficiency and robustness?
- How does the performance of Chunky Seal compare to other recent advances in watermarking, such as TrustMark [1] or RoSteALS [2]?
- What are the potential trade-offs between capacity, quality, and robustness in the Chunky Seal model? How does increasing capacity affect the robustness and visual quality of the watermarked images?
- How does the computational complexity and runtime of Chunky Seal compare to other watermarking methods, especially for large-scale applications?

[1] Bui, T., Agarwal, S., & Collomosse, J. (2023). Trustmark: Universal watermarking for arbitrary resolution images. arXiv preprint arXiv:2311.18297.

[2] Bui, T., Agarwal, S., Yu, N., & Collomosse, J. (2023). RoSteALS: Robust steganography using autoencoder latent space. In Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR) Workshops.
