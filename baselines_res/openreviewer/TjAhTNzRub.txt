# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes MoRE (Mixture of Remapping Experts), a novel framework for achieving irreversible feature-level unlearning. The method addresses the limitations of existing approaches by introducing three key innovations: prototype-orthogonal projection for preserving utility, remapping with mixture experts for ensuring irreversibility, and efficient activation-mean prototypes for unlearning efficiency.

## Strengths
1. The proposed method addresses key challenges in machine unlearning, such as irreversibility and scalability, which are critical for real-world applications.
2. The paper provides extensive experiments across multiple datasets and models, demonstrating the effectiveness of MoRE in comparison to existing methods.
3. MoRE achieves linear computational complexity and constant memory, making it highly scalable to large models and datasets.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper could provide more insight into the selection of hyperparameters, particularly the number of experts, and how this affects unlearning performance.
2. The paper could include a more detailed discussion on the potential for adaptive attacks, where attackers modify their behavior to bypass the unlearning mechanisms.
3. The paper could provide more details on the implementation of the stochastic router and its impact on the overall unlearning performance.

## Questions
1. How does the choice of hyperparameters, such as the number of experts, affect the unlearning performance? Is there an upper limit to the number of experts that can be used before performance degrades?
2. What are the potential vulnerabilities in the proposed method, especially concerning adaptive attacks where attackers modify their behavior to bypass the unlearning mechanisms?
3. Could the authors provide more details on the implementation of the stochastic router and how it contributes to the overall unlearning performance?
