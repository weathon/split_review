# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a method to find the best order of arithmetic operations for a given arithmetic expression. The method is based on the observation that neural networks tend to learn easy samples first. The method has two stages: global stage that finds block-level orders and local stage that refines the order for each block.

## Strengths
The paper is well motivated. Finding the best order of operations is a challenging optimization problem. The proposed method is based on the observation that neural networks tend to learn easy samples first. This is a good observation.

## Weaknesses
The proposed method is based on the observation that neural networks tend to learn easy samples first. This is a good observation. However, the proposed method does not seem to be scalable. The method requires training a neural network for each candidate permutation. As the number of terms in the expression grows, the number of candidates grow factorially and it becomes impractical to train a neural network for each candidate permutation. This is the reason why the authors had to limit their experiments to expressions with 13 terms.

## Questions
1. How does the method work for expressions with more than 13 terms?
2. Why did you use GPT-2 architecture? Transformer architecture has evolved since GPT-2. Current transformer architectures are more efficient and performant.
3. Why did you use only 1000 samples for evaluation? It is not clear how reliable are the results obtained with such a small evaluation set.
4. How do you define the success rate?
