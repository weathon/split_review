# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a new concept called "monitorability" for DNNs, which refers to the model's ability to highlight potential inference errors through internal activations. The authors propose a metric called MIRA score to quantify this property without requiring external OoD data. The MIRA score is based on the Mahalanobis distance between the perturbed and unperturbed features. The authors validate MIRA by comparing it against the best achievable OoD detection performance across three representative methods. The experiments are conducted across multiple architectures and domain applications.

## Strengths
The paper introduces a new concept of monitorability and provides a formal definition for it. The MIRA score is a novel metric that can quantify the monitorability of a model without requiring external OoD data. The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The experiments are conducted across multiple architectures and domain applications, which demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed MIRA score.

## Weaknesses
The paper does not provide a clear explanation of how the MIRA score is computed. It would be helpful to provide a step-by-step description of the computation process. The paper could benefit from a more thorough discussion of the limitations of the proposed approach. For example, it would be helpful to discuss potential scenarios where the MIRA score may not accurately reflect the monitorability of a model. The paper could also benefit from a more detailed analysis of the computational cost of computing the MIRA score. It would be helpful to provide information on the computational resources required to compute the MIRA score for different models and datasets.
