# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces SigmaDock, an SE(3) Riemannian diffusion model that generates poses by learning to reassemble these rigid bodies within the binding pocket. The method is evaluated on the PoseBusters and Astex datasets, and the results show that it achieves state-of-the-art performance, reaching Top-1 success rates (RMSD < 2 Å PB-valid) above 79.9% on the PoseBusters set, compared to 12.7-32.8% reported by recent deep learning approaches.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The authors provide a clear explanation of the problem and the proposed solution.
2. The authors evaluate their method on two different datasets and compare it to several state-of-the-art methods, demonstrating its effectiveness.
3. The proposed method is novel and has the potential to improve the accuracy of molecular docking, which is an important problem in drug discovery.

## Weaknesses
1. The method is only evaluated on two datasets, PoseBusters and Astex. It would be beneficial to evaluate the method on more datasets to demonstrate its generalizability.
2. The method is only compared to a few state-of-the-art methods. It would be beneficial to compare the method to more methods to demonstrate its superiority.
3. The method is only evaluated on rigid receptor scenarios. It would be beneficial to evaluate the method on more complex scenarios, such as flexible receptor docking and protein-ligand co-folding.

## Questions
1. Can you provide more details on the fragmentation scheme and how it leverages inductive biases from structural chemistry?
2. Can you provide more details on the SE(3) Riemannian diffusion model and how it generates poses by learning to reassemble rigid bodies within the binding pocket?
3. Can you provide more details on the evaluation datasets and the state-of-the-art methods that you compare your method to?
4. Can you provide more details on the limitations of your method and potential areas for future research?
