# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a method to incorporate equality graphs (EGGs) into symbolic regression (SR) methods that use search algorithms. SR is the task of discovering mathematical expressions that fit given data. In SR, there are many equivalent ways of expressing the same function, but previous SR methods have not typically accounted for this, leading to redundant exploration. The authors propose to use EGGs to guide search algorithms to avoid redundant exploration. They incorporate EGGs into Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) and deep reinforcement learning (DRL) SR methods and show that doing so leads to better performance on datasets with trigonometric functions. They also show that using EGGs leads to better memory and time performance than not using them.

## Strengths
- The paper is clearly written and easy to follow.
- The authors provide a good introduction to symbolic regression and related work.
- The authors provide theoretical justification for their method.
- The authors show that their method leads to better performance than not using it.
- The authors provide detailed experimental settings.

## Weaknesses
- The authors only show results on datasets with trigonometric functions. It would be good to see results on other types of datasets.
- The authors only incorporate EGGs into two SR methods, MCTS and DRL. It would be good to see EGG incorporated into other SR methods, such as genetic programming or a combination of genetic programming and neural networks (e.g. GP-SRNN).
- The authors do not compare their method to other methods that incorporate symbolic equivalence, such as SymNet.

## Questions
- How does EGG-SR compare to other methods that incorporate symbolic equivalence, such as SymNet?
- How does EGG-SR perform on datasets without trigonometric functions?
