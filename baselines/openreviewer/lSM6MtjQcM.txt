# Review

## Summary
The paper presents AetherCode, a benchmark designed to evaluate the coding and reasoning abilities of LLMs by drawing from premier programming competitions like IOI and ICPC. AetherCode addresses two main limitations of existing benchmarks: their insufficient difficulty and scope, and evaluation biases from low-quality test cases. The benchmark includes problems curated from top-tier competitions, annotated with classification tags by experts, and test cases constructed using a hybrid approach combining automated generation and human annotation. The paper evaluates several LLMs on AetherCode, identifying a significant performance gap between reasoning and non-reasoning models, and highlighting areas where current LLMs struggle, such as handling abstract problems and corner cases.

## Strengths
1. The paper presents a novel benchmark that addresses the limitations of existing code reasoning benchmarks by incorporating more challenging problems from premier programming competitions and employing a rigorous test case generation process.
2. The paper is well-organized, with clear sections detailing the benchmark curation process, evaluation methodology, and results.
3. The significance of this work lies in its potential to set a new standard for evaluating LLMs' coding and reasoning abilities, providing a more accurate reflection of model capabilities and identifying areas for improvement.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not include a detailed comparison with other recent code reasoning benchmarks, such as CodeELO or LiveCodeBench Pro, which could provide additional context for AetherCode's contributions.
2. The paper does not discuss the scalability of the AetherCode benchmark for future expansions or its adaptability for evaluating emerging LLMs.

## Questions
1. How does AetherCode compare to other recent code reasoning benchmarks in terms of difficulty and scope?
2. What steps have you taken to ensure the long-term relevance and scalability of AetherCode?
3. How do you plan to update AetherCode in the future to reflect changes in LLM capabilities and competition landscapes?
