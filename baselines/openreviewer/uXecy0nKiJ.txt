# Review

## Summary
This paper studies the safety risks of activation steering, a technique for controlling LLMs by injecting interpretable vectors into the model’s hidden states during inference. The authors show that activation steering, far from being a safe and precise alternative to fine-tuning, actually significantly increases the likelihood of harmful compliance with requests, even when the vectors are sourced from benign features. Their experiments demonstrate that random steering vectors can increase the harmful compliance rate by up to 27 percentage points, and that combining just 20 random vectors that jailbreak a single prompt creates a “universal attack” that generalizes to unseen requests. The authors conclude that activation steering, by compromising the model’s safety mechanisms, poses a substantial risk to reliable control over LLM behavior.

## Strengths
1. The paper provides extensive experimental evidence across multiple model families and scales, demonstrating the consistent failure of activation steering to uphold safety guardrails. The use of both random vectors and SAE-based vectors, as well as the creation of universal attack vectors, strengthens the argument about the broad applicability of the problem.

2. The paper challenges the assumption that interpretability implies safety, showing that even benign-looking steering vectors can bypass the model’s refusal mechanisms. This is an important finding for the field of AI safety, as it highlights a previously overlooked vulnerability in a technique that is being promoted as a safe alternative to fine-tuning.

3. The paper is clearly written and well-structured, with each finding supported by concrete experimental results. The case study using the Goodfire API demonstrates the practical implications of the findings, showing how a seemingly benign feature can be used to jailbreak a production model.

## Weaknesses
1. While the paper shows that activation steering can compromise safety mechanisms, it doesn’t provide a deep understanding of why this happens. The authors suggest that activation steering interferes with the model’s refusal circuits, but a more thorough analysis of the underlying mechanisms would be valuable. For example, the authors could have analyzed the activation patterns in the model’s layers before and after steering to better understand how steering affects the model’s internal computations. This could also help to better understand the relationship between activation steering and the refusal mechanism.

2. The paper doesn’t provide a detailed analysis of the semantic properties of the most effective steering vectors. While it shows that many vectors representing benign concepts can jailbreak the model, it would be interesting to explore whether there are common semantic themes among these vectors. This could provide additional insights into the nature of the vulnerability.

3. The paper doesn’t provide a comparison of the effectiveness of activation steering in bypassing safety mechanisms compared to other well-known jailbreak techniques, such as GCG or Many Shot Jailbreaking. This would help to better understand the relative importance of the vulnerability discovered in this work.

4. The paper focuses primarily on the empirical vulnerability of activation steering, but doesn’t propose any concrete mitigation strategies. The authors suggest the possibility of adversarial training or automated audits, but don’t explore these ideas in depth. This limits the practical impact of the work, as it doesn’t provide a clear path forward for addressing the identified vulnerability.

## Questions
1. Could the authors provide a more detailed analysis of the activation patterns in the model’s layers before and after steering? This could help to better understand how steering affects the model’s internal computations and refusal mechanisms.

2. Could the authors explore the semantic properties of the most effective steering vectors in more detail? For example, are there common themes or concepts among the vectors that jailbreak the model?

3. Could the authors compare the effectiveness of activation steering in bypassing safety mechanisms with other well-known jailbreak techniques, such as GCG or Many Shot Jailbreaking? This would help to better understand the relative importance of the vulnerability discovered in this work.

4. Could the authors elaborate on potential mitigation strategies for the vulnerability identified in this work? For example, how might adversarial training or automated audits be used to address this problem?

5. Could the authors discuss the potential trade-offs between interpretability and safety in activation steering? While the technique is valued for its interpretability, the paper shows that this can come at the cost of safety mechanisms. How might these trade-offs be better balanced?
