# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a novel framework for weakly supervised long-term action anticipation (LTA). The proposed method relies solely on video transcripts as supervision, without requiring frame-level annotations. The key innovation is an encoder-decoder architecture that uses temporal alignment to generate pseudo-labels for training, while cross-modal attention mechanisms integrate transcript information for better video feature representation. The method achieves competitive performance on benchmark datasets (Breakfast, 50Salads, and EGTEA) compared to fully supervised approaches.

## Strengths
1. The paper addresses an important problem in action anticipation by exploring weakly supervised methods, which can potentially reduce annotation costs and improve scalability.

2. The proposed method creatively combines video transcripts with frame-level features through temporal alignment and cross-modal attention, providing a novel approach to LTA.

3. The method achieves competitive performance on multiple benchmark datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in real-world applications.

4. The paper provides detailed ablation studies that validate the contribution of each component in the proposed method.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper could provide more detailed analysis on how the quality of pseudo-labels affects the final performance. This would be particularly useful in understanding the limitations of the method.

2. While the method performs well on the evaluated datasets, it is unclear how well it generalizes to other domains or different types of action sequences.

3. The paper could benefit from a more detailed comparison with other weakly supervised methods in the literature to highlight the specific advantages of the proposed approach.

## Questions
1. How sensitive is the performance of the proposed method to the quality of video transcripts? Is there a significant degradation in performance if the transcripts contain errors or are incomplete?

2. The paper mentions the use of a temporal alignment module to generate pseudo-labels. Can you provide more details on how this module works and how it handles the uncertainty in aligning transcripts with frame-level features?

3. How does the proposed method handle ambiguous or context-dependent actions where the boundaries or duration might not be clear-cut? Can you provide examples to illustrate this?

4. The paper mentions that the method uses a cross-modal attention layer to integrate transcript information with video features. Can you provide more details on how this layer is trained and how it affects the final performance?
