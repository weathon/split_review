# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a method for co-designing the morphology and control of soft robots. The method is based on Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) and uses a genetic algorithm to evolve both the robot's morphology and the parameters of a Graph Attention Network (GAT) used for control. The authors evaluate their method on four tasks from the EvoGym benchmark and compare it to an MLP-based baseline. The results show that the GAT-based approach outperforms the MLP-based approach in terms of final fitness and adaptability to morphological variations.

## Strengths
- The paper addresses an important problem in the field of soft robot design, namely the co-design of morphology and control.

- The paper proposes a novel approach based on GNNs, which has not been explored much in the context of soft robot co-design.

- The paper provides a detailed description of the proposed method and evaluates it on a standard benchmark.

## Weaknesses
- The paper does not provide a comprehensive comparison to other state-of-the-art methods for soft robot co-design, such as HyperNEAT or CPPN.

- The paper does not provide an analysis of the computational complexity of the proposed method, which could be an important factor to consider in practical applications.

- The paper does not provide a clear explanation of how the proposed method handles the trade-off between the complexity of the morphology and the complexity of the control policy.

- The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the sensitivity of the proposed method to different hyperparameters, such as the number of hidden layers or the number of attention heads.

## Questions
- How does the proposed method compare to other state-of-the-art methods for soft robot co-design, such as HyperNEAT or CPPN?

- What is the computational complexity of the proposed method, and how does it scale with the size of the robot's morphology?

- How does the proposed method handle the trade-off between the complexity of the morphology and the complexity of the control policy?

- How sensitive is the proposed method to different hyperparameters, such as the number of hidden layers or the number of attention heads?

- How does the proposed method compare to a baseline that uses a more traditional control method, such as a PID controller?

- How does the proposed method compare to a baseline that uses a different graph neural network architecture, such as a Graph Convolutional Network (GCN)?
