# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a new benchmark, GeoGramBench, which evaluates LLMs' ability to convert programmatic drawing code into accurate geometric reasoning. It finds that even advanced models struggle with this task, achieving less than 50% accuracy on complex problems. The study highlights significant limitations in LLMs' spatial reasoning and offers GeoGramBench as a resource for future research in symbolic-to-spatial geometric reasoning.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The proposed benchmark is novel and fills a gap in evaluating LLMs' spatial reasoning abilities.
3. The paper provides a detailed analysis of model behaviors and identifies key limitations in program-driven spatial reasoning.
4. The taxonomy used for categorizing problems is well-justified and provides a meaningful way to assess model performance.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper lacks a detailed analysis of the reasons behind the observed accuracy drops when models encounter programmatic codes.
2. The paper could benefit from a more in-depth exploration of how different types of programmatic expressions (e.g., different levels of complexity, different structural patterns) impact model performance.
3. The paper does not discuss the potential impact of different prompt engineering strategies on model performance.

## Questions
1. Can the authors provide more insights into the specific errors made by LLMs when solving problems at different levels of complexity?
2. How do different types of programmatic expressions (e.g., different levels of complexity, different structural patterns) affect model performance?
3. Have the authors experimented with different prompt engineering strategies, and if so, how did they impact model performance?
