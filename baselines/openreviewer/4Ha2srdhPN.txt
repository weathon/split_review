# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces GRAID, a novel framework for generating high-quality visual question-answer (VQA) datasets that enhance the spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision Language Models (VLMs). GRAID operates exclusively on 2D bounding boxes from standard object detectors, avoiding the errors and hallucinations associated with 3D reconstruction and generative models. The framework generates over 8.5 million VQA pairs across multiple spatial relations and question types, achieving a human-validated accuracy of 91.16%. Models trained on GRAID data demonstrate significant improvements in spatial reasoning, generalizing well to held-out question types and external benchmarks.

## Strengths
1. GRAID introduces a novel approach to generating spatial reasoning VQA data by leveraging only 2D bounding boxes, which avoids the errors and hallucinations associated with 3D reconstruction and generative models.
2. The framework generates a large-scale dataset of over 8.5 million VQA pairs with a high human-validated accuracy of 91.16%, making it one of the largest high-quality spatial VQA resources available.
3. GRAID demonstrates the ability of models to generalize learned spatial reasoning concepts to new question types and datasets, showcasing its potential for broader application.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper does not provide a detailed comparison of GRAID with other state-of-the-art methods for generating spatial reasoning VQA data, which could highlight its relative strengths and weaknesses.
2. The paper could benefit from a more in-depth analysis of the limitations of GRAID, such as the types of spatial reasoning tasks where it may not perform as well.
3. The paper does not discuss the potential biases in the generated dataset, such as the distribution of objects, scenes, or question types, which could affect the generalizability of the models trained on it.

## Questions
1. How does GRAID compare to other state-of-the-art methods in terms of the size and quality of the generated VQA datasets?
2. What are the specific limitations of GRAID in generating spatial reasoning VQA data, and how might these be addressed in future work?
3. What measures were taken to ensure the diversity and representativeness of the objects, scenes, and question types in the generated dataset?
