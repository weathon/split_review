# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces R-Horizon, a method for creating long-horizon reasoning tasks by composing simpler problems into complex, interdependent sequences. The authors use R-Horizon to construct a benchmark and training dataset, revealing that even advanced LLMs struggle with multi-problem reasoning. Through reinforcement learning with R-Horizon data, the authors show improvements in both single and multi-problem performance, positioning R-Horizon as a scalable and low-cost solution for enhancing LLM reasoning capabilities.

## Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow. 
- The proposed method is simple and effective, and can be applied to various tasks and models.
- The paper provides a comprehensive evaluation of 25 models on the R-Horizon benchmark, revealing significant performance degradation as reasoning length increases.
- The paper also shows that using R-Horizon data in RL can improve performance on both single and multi-problem tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness in enhancing long-horizon reasoning capabilities.

## Weaknesses
- The paper primarily focuses on mathematical reasoning tasks. Including other types of tasks, such as logical or commonsense reasoning, could provide a more comprehensive evaluation of LLMs' long-horizon reasoning abilities.
- The paper shows that larger models experience less degradation than smaller models, but it does not provide a detailed analysis of why this occurs. A deeper exploration of the factors contributing to this difference could provide valuable insights.
- The paper shows that R-Horizon data can improve performance on single-problem tasks, but it does not provide a detailed analysis of why this occurs. A deeper exploration of the factors contributing to this improvement could provide valuable insights.

## Questions
- Could you provide more details on the types of errors made by the models? Are there specific patterns or types of mistakes that become more frequent as the reasoning horizon increases?
- Have you analyzed the impact of different prompt strategies on the models' performance in long-horizon reasoning tasks? Could specific prompt templates or examples improve their reasoning capabilities?
