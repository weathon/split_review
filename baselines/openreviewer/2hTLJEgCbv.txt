# Review

## Summary
The paper empirically investigates the impact of different encoder and decoder architectures in Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) on the learned latent representations and generative quality. The authors conduct experiments using different architectures and latent space sizes on the MNIST dataset. They find that small dense networks are effective for encoding, while decoding benefits from architectures with structural processing capabilities. Dimensionally bigger latent space compression levels degrade representation quality but maintain separability at moderate compression levels. Models with non-zero Kullback-Leibler Divergence (KLD) loss outperform collapsed latent space models, emphasizing the importance of balancing reconstruction and generative regularization.

## Strengths
The paper is well-written and easy to understand.

## Weaknesses
The paper's contributions are not significant enough for ICLR. The authors only provide empirical results on the MNIST dataset, which is not convincing enough. The findings are also not surprising, e.g., small dense networks are effective for encoding, while decoding benefits from architectures with structural processing capabilities. I would expect more comprehensive experiments on different datasets and architectures. The authors should also provide more insights and explanations on why certain architectures work better for encoding or decoding.
