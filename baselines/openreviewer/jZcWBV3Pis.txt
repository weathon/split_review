# Review

## Summary
This paper investigates the robustness of the Chinchilla scaling law. First, the authors show that the Chinchilla model parameters are not clearly defined, and the parameters used in the original Chinchilla paper may be ambiguous. The authors show that the scaling law fits are robust to different definitions of model parameters. Then, the authors investigate how distorted the model parameters could have been without affecting the Chinchilla results. The authors perturb the model parameters in four different ways and show that Chinchilla results are sensitive to additive or systematic errors.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The authors provide a detailed analysis of the robustness of the Chinchilla scaling law. The results are interesting and could be useful for the community.

## Weaknesses
1. The main contribution of this paper is to investigate the robustness of the Chinchilla scaling law. Although the results are interesting, the contribution is incremental, and the results are not surprising.
2. There are some concerns about the results and analysis. Please see the questions below.

## Questions
1. In Section 2, the authors show that the Chinchilla results are robust to different definitions of model parameters. The authors use the best-fit parameters to refit the Chinchilla scaling law. However, the best-fit parameters are derived from the reported model parameters. It is not clear why using the best-fit parameters would make the Chinchilla results more robust.
2. In Figure 2 (bottom), the authors show that the Chinchilla results are robust to different definitions of model parameters. However, the confidence intervals of the results from the standard formula are much larger than the other two methods. Does it mean the results from the standard formula are less reliable?
3. In Section 3.1, the authors show that the Chinchilla results are robust to multiplicative constant perturbations. However, the results of the multiplicative constant perturbation are very different from the other three perturbations. The differences in log FLOPs are much larger than the other three methods. Does it mean the results from the multiplicative constant perturbation are less reliable?
4. The authors show that the Chinchilla results are sensitive to additive or systematic errors. Does it mean the Chinchilla results are not robust to random errors?
