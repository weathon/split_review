# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a new defense mechanism against prompt injection attacks for LLMs. The authors identify a limitation in existing defenses, which rely on injecting instruction hierarchy (IH) signals only at the input layer, limiting the signal's effectiveness as it propagates through the model's layers. To address this, they introduce Augmented Intermediate Representations (AIR), which injects IH signals across all layers of the LLM. The paper shows that AIR significantly reduces the success rate of gradient-based prompt injection attacks while maintaining the model's utility.

## Strengths
1. The paper identifies a key limitation in existing prompt injection defenses and proposes a novel solution (AIR) to address it.
2. The method is evaluated across multiple models and training setups, showing consistent improvements in robustness against prompt injection attacks.
3. The paper is well-organized and clearly written.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper primarily focuses on gradient-based attacks. It would be valuable to see how AIR performs against other types of attacks, especially those that don't rely on gradient information.
2. The paper mentions that AIR introduces a small increase in the number of parameters. It would be helpful to see a more detailed analysis of the computational overhead this incurs during training and inference.

## Questions
1. How does AIR perform against attacks that don't rely on gradient information? For example, how effective is AIR against jailbreaking attacks or other types of prompt injection attacks?
2. Can you provide more details on the computational overhead introduced by AIR during training and inference? How does this compare to the overhead of existing defense mechanisms?
