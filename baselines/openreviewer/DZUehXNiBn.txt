# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a novel framework for causal discovery from observational data, called VISTA (Voting-based Integration of Subgraph Topologies for Acyclicity). VISTA addresses the challenge of high computational complexity in causal structure learning by decomposing the global causal structure into local subgraphs based on Markov Blankets. It integrates these subgraphs through a weighted voting mechanism and ensures acyclicity using a Feedback Arc Set (FAS) algorithm. The framework is model-agnostic and compatible with various data settings and base learners, and it supports parallelization. The paper also provides theoretical guarantees on error bounds and asymptotic consistency. Experimental results on synthetic and real datasets demonstrate VISTA's improvements in accuracy and efficiency over existing methods.

## Strengths
1. VISTA presents a novel approach to causal discovery by introducing a modular framework that decomposes the global causal structure into local subgraphs based on Markov Blankets. This method offers a fresh perspective on addressing the challenges of high computational complexity in causal structure learning.
2. The authors provide a theoretical analysis, establishing finite-sample error bounds and asymptotic consistency for the VISTA framework. This analysis adds rigor to the proposed method and demonstrates its reliability under mild conditions.
3. VISTA is designed to be model-agnostic, meaning it can be integrated with various base learners and is compatible with different data settings without imposing specific structural assumptions. This versatility enhances its applicability across a wide range of scenarios.

## Weaknesses
1. While the paper presents a novel framework, the core idea of decomposing the global causal structure into local subgraphs is not entirely new. Similar approaches have been explored in previous work, such as the SADA framework [1]. The paper could benefit from a more detailed comparison with these existing methods to highlight the unique contributions and advantages of VISTA.
2. The paper lacks a thorough discussion of the assumptions and limitations of the VISTA framework. For example, it does not explicitly address the potential for latent confounding when aggregating local graphs, which could lead to the inclusion of redundant edges. A more transparent discussion of these assumptions and limitations would provide a clearer understanding of the framework's applicability and potential pitfalls.
3. The theoretical guarantees provided in the paper are based on certain assumptions, such as the independence of votes from different local subgraphs. In practice, these assumptions may not hold, and the correlation among votes could affect the validity of the guarantees. The paper could benefit from a more thorough exploration of the robustness of these theoretical results under various conditions.
4. While the paper presents experimental results on both synthetic and real datasets, the evaluation could be more comprehensive. For instance, the authors could consider comparing VISTA with a broader range of baseline methods, including more recent and advanced causal discovery techniques. Additionally, a more detailed analysis of the performance across different graph structures and sizes would provide a deeper understanding of the framework's strengths and limitations.

[1] Cai, Ruichu, et al. "SADA: A general framework to support robust causation discovery." International conference on machine learning. PMLR, 2013.

## Questions
1. How does VISTA compare to other existing methods that decompose the global causal structure into local subgraphs, such as the SADA framework [1]? A detailed comparison could highlight the unique contributions and advantages of VISTA.
2. What are the assumptions and limitations of the VISTA framework, particularly regarding the aggregation of local graphs and the potential for latent confounding? A more transparent discussion of these factors would provide a clearer understanding of the framework's applicability and potential pitfalls.
3. How robust are the theoretical guarantees provided in the paper under various conditions, particularly when the votes from different local subgraphs are not independent? A more thorough exploration of the robustness of these results would strengthen the theoretical foundation of VISTA.
4. Can the authors provide more detailed experimental results, including comparisons with a broader range of baseline methods and performance analysis across different graph structures and sizes? This would provide a deeper understanding of the framework's strengths and limitations.
