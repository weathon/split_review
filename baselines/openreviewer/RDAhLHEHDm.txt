# Review

## Summary
The paper proposes a context-driven approach to improve Sci-LLMs' ability to understand biomolecular sequences. The authors demonstrate that providing high-level context derived from bioinformatics tools is more effective than relying solely on raw sequence data. The paper presents a systematic comparison of three input modes - sequence-only, context-only, and combined - showing that the context-only approach consistently outperforms others. The findings suggest that Sci-LLMs are better suited for reasoning over structured knowledge rather than directly interpreting biomolecular sequences. The paper argues for reframing Sci-LLMs as reasoning engines over expert knowledge rather than sequence decoders.

## Strengths
- The paper presents a novel approach to using LLMs in biological sequence analysis. The context-driven method avoids the tokenization dilemma by utilizing high-level biological context rather than relying on raw sequence data.

- The paper provides a systematic empirical study comparing different input modes (sequence-only, context-only, combined) across state-of-the-art Sci-LLMs. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of the context-only approach, offering valuable insights into how LLMs can be best utilized for biological sequence analysis.

- The paper is well-written and clearly presents the motivation, methodology, and results. The figures and tables effectively support the analysis.

- The findings have significant implications for the development of Sci-LLMs. By demonstrating that reasoning over high-level context is more effective than sequence interpretation, the paper suggests a new direction for designing more effective Sci-LLMs.

## Weaknesses
- The paper primarily focuses on proteins. It would be interesting to see how the approach performs with other biomolecular sequences like RNA or DNA.

- The paper could provide more details on the computational requirements and efficiency of the context-driven approach compared to other methods.

- The paper could benefit from a more detailed discussion of the limitations of the context-driven approach, particularly for novel or orphan sequences where less contextual information may be available.

## Questions
- How does the context-driven approach perform with other types of biomolecular sequences, such as RNA or DNA?

- What are the computational requirements and efficiency of the context-driven approach compared to other methods?

- What are the limitations of the context-driven approach, particularly for novel or orphan sequences where less contextual information may be available?
