# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces a novel framework called Dimension Domain Co-Decomposition (3D) that combines dimension decomposition with Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) domain decomposition to address challenges in solving high-dimensional partial differential equations (PDEs). The 3D framework aims to improve computational efficiency, accuracy, and interpretability in PDE solutions. Key contributions include a shared MLP architecture for dimension decomposition, a Variable Interpretability (VI) metric for assessing dimension-wise interpretability, and an MoE-driven domain decomposition that adapts automatically to the solution space without predefined subdomains.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a unified framework that combines dimension decomposition and domain decomposition, which is a creative approach to addressing the challenges of high-dimensional PDEs.

2. The introduction of the Variable Interpretability (VI) metric provides a quantitative measure of interpretability, which is a novel contribution to the field.

3. The use of MoE to automate domain decomposition eliminates the need for manually predefined subdomains, which is a significant practical advantage.

## Weaknesses
1. While the paper claims improved computational efficiency, it does not provide a detailed analysis of the computational complexity or runtime comparisons with existing methods.

2. The paper primarily focuses on academic examples and does not discuss the framework's applicability or performance in real-world industrial settings or more complex boundary condition scenarios.

3. The paper lacks a detailed comparison with other state-of-the-art methods in terms of accuracy and efficiency, which makes it difficult to assess the relative performance of the proposed method.

4. The paper does not provide a sensitivity analysis of the model's performance with respect to changes in hyperparameters, such as the number of experts or the decomposition rank.

## Questions
1. Can you provide more details on the computational complexity of your method compared to traditional approaches?

2. How does your method perform on more complex real-world PDEs with irregular boundaries and heterogeneous coefficients?

3. Can you provide a more comprehensive comparison with other state-of-the-art methods in terms of both accuracy and efficiency?

4. How sensitive is your model's performance to changes in hyperparameters, and how did you select the values used in your experiments?
