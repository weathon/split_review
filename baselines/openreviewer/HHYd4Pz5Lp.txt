# Review

## Summary
This paper proposes a method for training recurrent spiking neural networks with synaptic delays. The authors employ a triangular function to distribute the spike in a short time window after the spike is generated, allowing the spike to be treated as a current. This approach effectively transforms the spike into a current that can be handled by the neuron's dynamics. The proposed method demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across multiple datasets.

## Strengths
1. This method is simple and easy to implement. It has the potential to be applied to a wide range of tasks.
2. The performance of this method is impressive, surpassing previous state-of-the-art results.

## Weaknesses
1. The presentation of the paper is poor, with many typos, missing punctuation, and some sentences that are difficult to understand.
2. The paper lacks an ablation study to verify the effectiveness of the proposed method.
3. The authors do not compare their method with other state-of-the-art methods on the SHD dataset.

## Questions
1. Could the authors explain the sentence, "The optimization of a single delay in a recurrent connection can transform two recurrently connected neurons in a pattern generator." in Fig.1's caption?
2. Could the authors conduct an ablation study to verify the effectiveness of the proposed method?
3. Could the authors provide more details on the computational cost of the proposed method and compare it with other methods?
4. Could the authors explain the meaning of $w_{ij}^{rec}$ in Eq.6? Is it the weight of the recurrent connection from neuron $j$ to neuron $i$? If so, the subscript $i$ in $w_{ij}^{rec}$ seems unnecessary.
5. Could the authors explain why the performance of the proposed method is better than that of the ASRC-SNN method on the SSC dataset but worse on the PS-MNIST dataset?
