# Review

## Summary
The paper analyzes the online mapping-based motion prediction problem and identifies several challenges and misunderstandings in the current paradigm. The paper then proposes a benchmark to study the problem, along with a new data split, new evaluation metrics, and a new baseline method.

## Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The paper analyzes the online mapping-based motion prediction problem and identifies several challenges and misunderstandings in the current paradigm.
- The paper proposes a benchmark to study the problem, along with a new data split, new evaluation metrics, and a new baseline method.

## Weaknesses
- The paper's contribution is limited. The main contribution of the paper is the proposal of a new benchmark for online mapping-based motion prediction. While the benchmark is indeed new, it is simply a new split of the existing nuScenes dataset. The new evaluation metrics and the new baseline method are also straightforward and not technically novel.
- The paper does not provide a comprehensive analysis of the proposed benchmark. While the paper identifies several challenges and misunderstandings in the current paradigm, it does not provide a comprehensive analysis of the proposed benchmark. For example, the paper does not provide an analysis of the train-val gap and the spatial overlap between the training and validation sets.
- The paper does not provide a comparison of the proposed benchmark with existing benchmarks. For example, the paper does not provide a comparison of the proposed benchmark with the existing nuScenes dataset, which also contains a split for motion prediction.

## Questions
- The paper should provide a comprehensive analysis of the proposed benchmark, including an analysis of the train-val gap and the spatial overlap between the training and validation sets.
- The paper should provide a comparison of the proposed benchmark with existing benchmarks, such as the nuScenes dataset.
