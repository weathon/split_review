# Review

## Summary
This paper studies the complexity of finding an $\epsilon$-stationary point for stochastic bilevel optimization when the upper-level problem is nonconvex and the lower-level problem is strongly convex. Based on the $F^2SA$ method, this paper proposes a class of methods $F^2SA-p$ that uses $p$th-order finite difference for hyper-gradient approximation and improves the upper bound to $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(p\epsilon^{-4-2/p})$ for $p$ th-order smooth problems. Finally, this paper demonstrates that the $\Omega(\epsilon^{-4})$ lower bound also holds for stochastic bilevel problems when the high-order smoothness holds for the lower-level variable, indicating that the upper bound of $F^2SA-p$ is nearly optimal in the region $p = \Omega(\log \epsilon^{-1} / \log \log \epsilon^{-1})$.

## Strengths
This paper proposes a class of fully first-order method $F^2SA-p$ that achieves the $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(p\epsilon^{-4-2/p})$ SFO complexity for $p$ th-order smooth bilevel problems. This paper also generalizes the best-known $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-6})$ result from $p = 1$ to any $p \in \mathbb{N}_+$. Moreover, this paper also provides an $\Omega(\epsilon^{-4})$ lower bound to show that the proposed method is near-optimal when $p = \Omega(\log \epsilon^{-1} / \log \log \epsilon^{-1})$.

## Weaknesses
1. The novelty of this paper is limited. This paper only proposes a class of methods $F^2SA-p$ that uses $p$th-order finite difference for hyper-gradient approximation based on the $F^2SA$ method. The contribution of this paper is not enough for ICLR.

2. This paper only provides the convergence results of the proposed methods. The convergence rates of the proposed methods did not show a significant improvement compared to existing methods. The contribution of this paper is not enough for ICLR.
