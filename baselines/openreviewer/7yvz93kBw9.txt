# Review

## Summary
This paper presents a method for sparse-view reconstruction using 3D Gaussian Splatting. The key idea is to dynamically adjust the degree of reconstruction based on depth and density information. The authors also introduce a new evaluation metric to quantify the stability of learned Gaussian distributions. Experiments on multiple datasets demonstrate that the proposed method improves both visual quality and robustness under sparse view conditions.

## Strengths
1. The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
2. The proposed method is well-motivated and addresses an important problem in 3D reconstruction.
3. The introduction of a new evaluation metric is a valuable contribution to the field.

## Weaknesses
1. The proposed method is built upon DropGaussian, but the comparison with DropGaussian in Tables 1 and 2 is not clear. It would be helpful to have a direct comparison with DropGaussian using the same number of views.
2. The performance of the proposed method is not state-of-the-art. For example, the results on the MipNeRF360 dataset are not as good as those of LoopSparseGS.
3. The proposed method is only evaluated on the LLFF and MipNeRF360 datasets. It would be beneficial to evaluate the method on more challenging datasets, such as the DTU dataset.

## Questions
1. How does the proposed method compare with DropGaussian using the same number of views?
2. Why is the performance of the proposed method not state-of-the-art on the MipNeRF360 dataset?
3. How does the proposed method perform on more challenging datasets, such as the DTU dataset?
