# Review

## Summary
This paper studies the effect of adding reasoning data during pretraining and SFT on the reasoning ability of LLMs. It studies how the scale, diversity, and quality of reasoning data affect the performance of LLMs.

## Strengths
- The paper is clearly written and easy to follow.
- The paper conducts comprehensive experiments to study the effect of adding reasoning data during pretraining and SFT on the reasoning ability of LLMs. 
- The paper uncovers an asymmetric allocation principle—diversity drives pretraining effectiveness, while quality governs SFT—providing a clear, actionable blueprint for data strategy.

## Weaknesses
- The paper does not provide enough details about the training process, making it difficult to reproduce the results. For example, the paper does not mention what optimizer and scheduler are used, and what the learning rate and other hyperparameters are for pretraining and SFT.
- The paper does not provide enough details about the evaluation process, making it difficult to evaluate the quality of the experiments. For example, the paper does not mention how the evaluation data is selected and how the evaluation is conducted (e.g., whether it is zero-shot or few-shot).

## Questions
- What optimizer and scheduler are used for pretraining and SFT? What are the learning rates and other hyperparameters?
- How is the evaluation data selected?
- How is the evaluation conducted (e.g., is it zero-shot or few-shot)?
