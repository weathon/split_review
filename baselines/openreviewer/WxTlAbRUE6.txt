# Review

## Summary
The paper introduces GMD-25, a benchmark for evaluating the compositional generalization capabilities of machine learning force fields (MLFFs). It consists of four tasks that test different aspects of compositional generalization. The training and test sets contain different molecules, but the training data is chosen such that generalization to the test examples should be feasible for models that learn the physical principles. The empirical analysis shows that the considered tasks are highly challenging for state-of-the-art models, with errors for out-of-distribution examples often being orders of magnitude higher than for in-distribution examples.

## Strengths
1. The paper introduces a new benchmark that focuses on compositional generalization, which is an important aspect of MLFFs that has not been extensively studied before.
2. The tasks are well-designed and cover different aspects of compositional generalization, allowing for a comprehensive evaluation of MLFFs.
3. The empirical analysis reveals significant limitations in current approaches, highlighting the challenges in learning transferable representations of inter-atomic interactions.
4. The paper provides a detailed analysis of the performance of different MLFFs on the benchmark, which can guide the development of more robust and physically-informed models.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper focuses on a specific class of molecules (substituted linear (alkyl) carbon chains) and tasks, and it is unclear how well the findings generalize to other molecular systems and tasks.
2. The paper does not include foundation models in the analysis, which could provide insights into the comparison between pretrained models and models trained from scratch on specific tasks.
3. The paper could benefit from a more detailed analysis of the sensitivity of the models to hyperparameter selection and architectural choices.
4. The paper could provide more insights into why certain models perform better or worse on different tasks and how the findings could guide the development of new MLFFs.
