# Review

## Summary
The paper studies the problem of learning causal graphs in the presence of latent confounders and post-treatment selection from interventional data. The authors propose a new graphical representation called F-PAG and a corresponding algorithm called F-FCI to identify causal relations, latent confounders, and post-treatment selection. They also provide theoretical results on the soundness and completeness of the algorithm. The paper also includes experimental results on synthetic and real-world datasets to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed methods.

## Strengths
- The paper addresses an important and challenging problem in causal discovery, which has not been fully explored in existing literature.
- The proposed F-PAG representation and F-FCI algorithm are novel and well-motivated, with solid theoretical guarantees.
- The experimental results show promising performance compared to baseline methods.

## Weaknesses
- The paper could benefit from more detailed discussions on the assumptions made and their implications for the proposed methods.
- While the synthetic experiments use various graph distributions, more real-world datasets could be evaluated to demonstrate the practical utility of the proposed methods.

## Questions
- Can you elaborate on the assumptions made for the theoretical results, such as the faithfulness assumption? How do these assumptions limit the applicability of the proposed methods?
- Have you considered evaluating the proposed methods on more diverse real-world datasets beyond the gene expression analysis? What challenges might arise in such evaluations?
