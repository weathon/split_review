# Review

## Summary
This paper investigates the capabilities of a one-layer transformer in addressing the 0-1 knapsack problem. The authors conduct experiments to evaluate its performance and explore the reasons behind its limitations through visualizations and interpretability techniques.

## Strengths
The authors aim to examine the generalization capabilities of transformers on NP-complete tasks and discuss the broader implications of their findings.

## Weaknesses
1. The paper is not well written and the logic is not clear. For example, in the introduction, the authors claim that existing mechanistic interpretability research focuses on toy problems and P problems, and position their work on NP-complete problems. However, in the experiments, the authors only use a single-layer transformer, which is also a toy setup. Additionally, there is no clear definition of what constitutes a P problem versus an NP-complete problem in this context.
2. The experimental setup is not clear. The authors mention training the transformers for 100k epochs, but it is unclear what the input and output of the transformer are, what the training objective is, and how the data is formulated.
3. The authors claim that the transformer is unable to grok, but it is not clear what grok means in this context. The paper does not provide a clear definition or explanation of this term.
4. The figures presented in the paper are unclear and poorly labeled. For instance, in Figure 3, the lines for train and test log loss are indistinguishable, making it impossible to draw conclusions about the training process. Similarly, in Figure 4, the attention patterns are not well-explained, leaving the reader uncertain about how to interpret the values or what conclusions to draw.
