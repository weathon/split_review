# Review

## Summary
This paper introduces a new CLIP compression method that uses learnable matrices to map and combine pretrained weights. The method includes a two-stage training process: mapping learning and retraining. The approach is novel, and the experimental results are comprehensive. However, the paper could benefit from clearer explanations of the method and more detailed discussions of the results.

## Strengths
The CLIP-MAP method is novel and shows strong performance in experiments. The approach of using learnable matrices to map and combine pretrained weights offers a new perspective on CLIP compression.

The paper provides a thorough evaluation of the method on multiple benchmarks, including zero-shot image-text retrieval and zero-shot classification tasks.

## Weaknesses
The method section could be clearer. It is not easy to understand how the learnable matrices are trained and how they map the pretrained weights.

The paper does not sufficiently discuss the limitations of the proposed method or potential areas for future work.

## Questions
How does the proposed method compare to other state-of-the-art CLIP compression techniques in terms of computational efficiency?

What are the limitations of the proposed method, and how might they be addressed in future research?
