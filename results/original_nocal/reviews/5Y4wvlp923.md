## Summary

This paper identifies a genuine problem in contrastive time-series representation learning: dominant signal components (e.g., trend) can suppress weaker but semantically meaningful ones (e.g., seasonality). It introduces **Semantic Disentanglement Error (SDE)**, a cosine-based metric to quantify this imbalance, and proposes **Asymmetric Perceptual Weighting (APW)**, which uses SDE-derived △ to dynamically reweight trend and seasonal contrastive losses within the CoST framework. The method is evaluated on ETT, Electricity, and Weather datasets for forecasting.

## Strengths

- **SDE provides a clean, interpretable diagnostic and is validated on synthetic data.** Table 1 demonstrates that when the amplitude ratio of trend to periodicity varies, the SDE values become highly asymmetric (△ ranging from –1.29 to +1.11), confirming that TS2Vec systematically underrepresents the weaker component. This is direct empirical evidence of the semantic imbalance problem.

- **CoST+APW yields consistent gains on ETT datasets.** On ETT1 (first block, 3 of 5 horizons), ETT2 (4 of 5 horizons), and ETTm1 (all 5 horizons; labeled "ETT1" in the third block), CoST+APW achieves the lowest MSE among all baselines. The improvement is especially noticeable at mid-range horizons (e.g., ETT1-168: 0.101 vs. 0.107 CoST; ETTm1-288: 0.056 vs. 0.067 CoST).

- **Direct SDE regularization fails while the proposed weighting succeeds, validating the design choice.** Table 2 shows that adding SDE as a direct regularizer to TS2Vec yields no improvement (often degrades MSE). In contrast, Table 3 shows that the asymmetric weighting approach built on CoST improves over vanilla CoST in most ETT settings. This contrast supports the paper's claim that SDE works better as a diagnostic signal for reweighting than as a direct gradient target.

## Weaknesses

### Fatal
None.

### Major

1. **SDE values are not reported in Table 3, despite the paper explicitly claiming they are.** Line 266 states: "Table 3 reports the SDE metrics and forecasting performance on ETT, Electricity, and Weather datasets." The actual table (lines 274–301) contains only MSE and MAE columns. Since SDE is the core diagnostic that motivates the entire method, its absence from the main experimental comparison is a critical omission — there is no direct evidence that CoST+APW actually reduces semantic imbalance on real-world benchmarks relative to CoST or TS2Vec. Table 1 only shows SDE for TS2Vec on synthetic data; this does not substitute.

2. **The central claim of "consistently lower SDE values and superior forecasting accuracy" (line 303) is contradicted by the paper's own results.** On Electricity at horizons 168, 336, and 720, CoST+APW achieves higher MSE than *all* baselines (CoST, TS2Vec, TNC). On Weather at horizons 336 and 720, it is again the worst method. The method actively degrades forecasting accuracy on these settings. The paper selectively highlights wins on ETT while ignoring these systematic failures, which misrepresents the empirical picture.

3. **No controlled ablations isolate the contributions of the proposed components.** The method introduces two new elements on top of CoST: an MLP fusion layer (to compute composite embeddings for SDE) and the asymmetric weighting based on △. Without ablations — (a) CoST + MLP only (no weighting), (b) CoST + fixed weighting, (c) CoST + weighting without MLP — it is impossible to determine which component drives the observed improvements (or whether the improvements on ETT come from the MLP adding capacity rather than from the reweighting). The "Direct SDE Regularization" experiment (Section 4.3) is on TS2Vec, not an ablation of the proposed method.

### Minor

4. **No error bars, confidence intervals, or variance statistics are reported for any of the forecasting results (Table 2, Table 3).** Given the mixed performance — where the method sometimes improves over CoST and sometimes underperforms it by a wide margin (e.g., Elec-168: 0.566 vs. 0.425) — it is impossible to assess whether the differences are statistically meaningful or within noise.

5. **Duplicate and confusing dataset labels in Table 3.** The table lists "ETT1" twice: once with horizons {24, 48, 168, 336, 720} and again with horizons {24, 48, 96, 288, 672}. The paper's experimental setup mentions four subsets (ETT1h, ETT2h, ETTm1, ETTm2), so the second "ETT1" block is presumably ETTm1, but the labeling is inconsistent and unprofessional.

6. **The feedback loop in the asymmetric weighting mechanism is not analyzed.** △ is computed from SDE, which depends on the same encoders being trained. As the encoder updates, △ changes, which reweights the losses, which further updates the encoder, etc. The paper provides no analysis of training dynamics (△ over epochs), no discussion of stability or convergence, and no check for degenerate solutions (e.g., collapse to trivial embeddings that minimize SDE artificially). This is a plausible concern that the paper does not address.

### Trivial

7. The paper's self-declared "systematic ablations" (line 35) are misleading — the experiments compare high-level strategies (spectral regularization vs. frequency-aware contrast vs. weighting), not ablations of the method's internal components.

## Nice-to-Haves

- Report SDE values for CoST and CoST+APW on all real datasets to directly support the claim of reduced semantic imbalance.
- Analyze the correlation between SDE and downstream forecasting performance to validate that the diagnostic is practically relevant.
- Test asymmetric weighting on other contrastive frameworks (e.g., TS2Vec with manual signal decomposition) to demonstrate generality beyond CoST.
