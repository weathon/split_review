## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes DeepDIVE, a VAE-based framework for time-series forecasting that disentangles the latent space into marginal dimensions `b` (capturing shared patterns) and conditional dimensions `a` (task-specific, conditioned on `b`). The derivation extends the ELBO for forecasting, uses Naive Bayes for disentanglement, and employs RBF and cross-entropy with interleaving training. Experiments are conducted on two time-series datasets (including NEMS electricity data). Reviewer scores are 3, 3, 6, 3 — one reviewer (R3) is substantially more positive (score 6; confidence 4), while the other three rate the paper low (score 3; confidence 2–4). R3 finds the paper clearly written and the contributions novel; R1, R2, and R4 find the presentation inconsistent and the positioning unclear.

### Strengths

- **Theoretical grounding** (R1, R2, R3): The derivations of the variational ELBO for time-series forecasting are solid and mathematically well‑explained. The loss function is novel and theoretically grounded (R2). The decomposition of the latent space into marginal and conditional dimensions (b and a) is a principled design to address conflicting objectives (R3).

- **Reporting of variance** (R1): The authors report standard deviations of model performance, enabling proper evaluation of results.

- **Empirical performance** (R3, R4): On one dataset, DeepDIVE achieves higher forecasting accuracy than VAE and beta‑VAE. The proposed framework outperforms several baselines (R3).

- **Clear assumptions** (R4): The mathematical assumptions needed for the derivations are clearly stated, making the expansion of equations easy to follow.

- **Minority positive view (R3)**: R3 finds the paper “clearly written, easy to follow and understand,” praises the novelty of the b/a decomposition, and considers that the design effectively captures shared patterns and task‑specific features to avoid conflicts.

### Weaknesses

- **Inconsistent and unclear presentation** (R1, R2, R4; minority R3 disagrees on clarity):  
  - Text, figures, and equations contradict each other (R1). For example, the inference of `q(a|x,b)` is not described; Fig. 1 suggests `a` is computed directly from `x`, conflicting with the equation (R1). The relationship between frozen weights during backpropagation and the conditioning of `a` on `b` is not articulated (R1).  
  - The cross-attention mechanism and fusion stage are mentioned in the abstract/introduction but never explained in the body (R1). It is unclear how cross-attention is applied in the decoder, and line 518 further confuses the role of attention (R1).  
  - Fig. 3’s purpose is not described in the main text or caption; its connection to Fig. 2 is missing (R1).  
  - Abstract lacks coherence: concepts like multi-task learning, disentanglement, Naive Bayes, cross-entropy, RBF are presented without a clear relationship (R2). The abstract does not mention explainability, yet line 46 of the introduction claims explainability as a key contribution (R2).  
  - Conflicting objectives (line 50) are not elaborated; examples of how objectives conflict in this context would clarify the motivation (R2).

- **Missing comparisons and rationale for baselines** (R2, R4):  
  - Baselines in Table 2 (AUTOCTS-KDF/KDP, DsaNet, MtGnn) are not discussed or referenced in the related work or the experiment section (R2).  
  - Why are recent baselines like DeepGLO, TCN, TLAE (discussed in Section 2.1) not included in the comparison? (R2).  
  - Baselines differ between Table 1 (VAE, beta‑VAE) and Table 2 (AUTOCTS, DsaNet, MtGnn) without justification. Using VAE and beta‑VAE as baselines for time-series forecasting is questionable if the paper is about forecasting (R4).  
  - No baselines from Tables 1–3 are discussed in the “Related works” section (R2).

- **Insufficient evaluation of the latent space** (R1, R4):  
  - The roles of `a` and `b` are not evaluated separately (R1). t-SNE plots and traversal results for each dimension individually are missing, making it hard to assess whether the disentanglement is meaningful (R1).  
  - No comparison of latent variable visualization with existing disentanglement methods (R4). Prior work on disentanglement in time-series (e.g., [a] cited by R4) should be compared.

- **Unclear positioning and motivation** (R4; partially R2, R3):  
  - The paper does not clearly state whether it is a fundamental method or an application to a specific problem (R4). The abstract claims to solve general multi-task learning, but the introduction focuses on time-series (or ASC) forecasting; this inconsistency makes the motivation unclear (R4).  
  - The design principles of the proposed method are unclear: the assumptions leading to the theorem are given, but the practical validity and intuition behind them are not explained (R4, also R3 for Assumption 2). A graphical model of `a, b, x, y` and how they relate to practical tasks would help readers understand the assumptions (R4).  
  - Although Assumption 2 (`q(b_i,b_j|x)=q(b_i|x)q(b_j|x)`) is noted as a common simplification, no intuition is provided for why it often works in practice (R3).  
  - The introduction criticizes deep learning approaches as black-box, but does not adequately justify why the proposed framework is helpful for time-series forecasting (R3).

- **Missing details and unresolved questions** (R1, R2, R3, R4):  
  - The paper mentions “total correlation” as an alternative for disentanglement; the authors are asked whether they explored it and how it compares to Naive Bayes (R1; question that overlaps with a missing analysis).  
  - In Table 2, what does “std” mean? (R3; unclear abbreviation).  
  - The paper referenced in Section 2 (Wong et al.) is not linked or specified as published (R2; missing reference).  
  - The interleaving training scheme is mentioned but lacks a detailed explanation of how it achieves decoupling of `a` and `b` (R1).
