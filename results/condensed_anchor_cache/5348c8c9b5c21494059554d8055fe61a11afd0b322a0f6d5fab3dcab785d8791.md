## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents Reasoning on Graphs (RoG), a planning-retrieval-reasoning framework that integrates LLMs with Knowledge Graphs (KGs) for faithful and interpretable reasoning. RoG generates relation paths grounded by KGs as faithful plans, retrieves valid reasoning paths from the KG, and feeds them to the LLM for final answer prediction. The model is trained with two objectives: planning optimization (distilling KG structure into the LLM) and retrieval-reasoning optimization. Experiments on WebQSP and CWQ datasets with multiple LLMs (GPT-3.5-turbo? T5, LLaMA, Alpaca) show significant improvements over a variety of baselines and prior SOTA methods. Reviewers broadly find the approach novel, well-designed, and impactful, with strong empirical results. However, one reviewer (score 6) raises concerns about mathematical rigor, limited datasets, and the limited usefulness of the integrability feature. Others note clarity issues about training details, missing supervision explanations, and potential over-claiming of hallucination/lack-of-knowledge mitigation.

### Strengths
* **Novel and interesting approach** that synergizes LLMs with the structural information of KGs for reasoning (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). The planning-retrieval-reasoning framework is sensible, well-described, and makes reasoning interpretable and controllable via explicit KG grounding.
* **Strong empirical performance** with significant improvements over SOTA on two KGQA benchmarks (WebQSP and CWQ) across multiple LLM backbones (GPT, T5, LLaMA, Alpaca) (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). The breadth of comparison techniques is appreciated.
* **Thorough experimental evaluation** (Reviewer 2) and ablation studies confirming the importance of both planning and retrieval objectives (Reviewer 3).
* **Actionable approach** that addresses practical shortcomings of LLMs (hallucination, lack of up-to-date knowledge) and is likely to be impactful for future research and implementations (Reviewer 2).
* **Good integration** of LLM reasoning and KG information, with the fine-tuned LLM also serving as a planning module for other LLMs (Reviewer 3 – though usefulness contested; see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
* **Clarity and presentation issues**:
  * The approach section is hard to follow; the flow of information and the order of explanation (before versus after optimization) needs improvement. The use of ground-truth supervision (retrieved paths) should be made explicit earlier (Reviewer 1).
  * It is not clearly stated which version of ChatGPT (3.5-turbo?) was used and the evaluation dates – ChatGPT changes over time (Reviewer 2).
* **Mathematical non-rigor**:
  * Equation 4 mixes expectation and sum incorrectly: should be either \(\mathbb{E}_Q \log P\) or \(\sum_z Q \log P\). The notation \(z \in Q\) is nonsensical since Q is a distribution. The equality is missing a constant term (Reviewers 3, 4).
  * Equation 6 and Equation 10 have similar issues; the marginalization over conditions in Eq. 10 is mathematically incorrect. The correctness of the final training objective needs re-checking (Reviewer 3).
  * Equation 3 assumes a single faithful relation path, but there could be multiple valid solutions/paths; the authors should provide justification for this assumption (Reviewer 4).
* **Training cost and generalization concerns**:
  * The approach requires training/instruction-tuning LLMs to generate relation paths specific to a KG. It is unclear whether this helps LLM QA capability in general beyond the specific KG used (Reviewer 1).
  * Only two datasets are evaluated; the authors should test generalization to other QA datasets (Reviewer 3). Moreover, Reviewer 3 asked whether RoG is trained on both WebQSP and CWQ simultaneously – if so, comparison with baselines trained on only one dataset each would be unfair. The paper should clarify training data and ensure fair comparison.
* **Insufficient detail on fine-tuning and module evaluation**:
  * The base LLM used for the planning module in the RoG results is not clearly identified (Reviewer 1).
  * The training objectives (Equation 7) are given, but the planning and retrieval modules are not evaluated individually; no validation loss is provided, making it hard to understand why they work (Reviewer 4).
  * The comparison in Table 2 pits the fine-tuned RoG against LLMs under zero/few-shot settings – is this a fair comparison? (Reviewer 4).
* **Limited usefulness of integrability with other LLMs**:
  * Table 4 shows that combining RoG with a stronger LLM (e.g., ChatGPT) does not improve over the original fine-tuned RoG alone. This undermines the claimed integrability benefit (Reviewer 3).
* **Over-claiming of addressing hallucination and lack of knowledge**:
  * The paper asserts that RoG addresses hallucination and knowledge limitations, but only a few case studies are provided. More comprehensive evidence is needed (Reviewer 4).
* **Missing details**:
  * How is the KG constructed? Is it provided with the dataset or built by the authors? (Reviewer 4).
  * Reviewer 3 notes a missing constant in Equation 4; Reviewer 4 also flags the equality issue.
