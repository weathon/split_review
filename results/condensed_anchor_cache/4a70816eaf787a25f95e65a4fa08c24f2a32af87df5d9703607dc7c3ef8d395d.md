## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces LLaVA-Surg, a multimodal conversational assistant for surgical videos, and the Surg-QA dataset (102,000 surgical video-instruction pairs), generated via a structured two-stage question-answer pipeline that reduces hallucinations and cost. The model is based on LLaVA, uses CLIP for visual encoding, and is fine-tuned on Surg-QA. Evaluations show LLaVA-Surg outperforms general-domain models in zero-shot surgical video question-answering. The dataset, model, and code are to be released. The work is well-written and makes a significant contribution to surgical AI, but there are concerns about data quality, limited comparisons, and missing evaluations on downstream tasks and other surgical datasets.

### Strengths
- **Surg-QA Dataset**: Largest surgical video-instruction dataset (102k pairs) covering diverse procedures (Reviewers 1, 4, 5). It is a significant resource for training multimodal surgical models (R1, R2, R3). The dataset can be valuable for surgical education (R4).
- **Two-Stage QA Pipeline**: Mitigates LLM hallucinations and reduces task complexity, allowing use of an affordable open-source LLM (R1, R4). Compared to Quilt-1M’s approach, this pipeline enhances data quality and reliability (R5).
- **LLaVA-Surg Performance**: Demonstrates notable improvements over general-domain models in zero-shot surgical video QA (R1, R2, R3). Its ability to process surgical video content (not just static images) is a key differentiator (R3). The model and dataset are expected to be valuable resources (R2).
- **Evaluation Approach**: Using Spearman rank correlation between expert and GPT scores validates large-scale GPT evaluation reliability (R5, positive minority).
- **Clarity**: Paper is well-written, precise, and easy to follow (R2, R3).
- **Open Release**: Releasing dataset, model, and code fosters further research (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited Model Comparisons**: The paper misses comparisons with recent multimodal LLMs (e.g., ReAct, Yao et al., 2023) and two-stage VQA methods (e.g., MedThink, Gai et al., 2024) (R1, R4). All comparison methods in Table 3 are from 2023; more recent SOTA video MLLM architectures should be included (R4). Comparisons with specific surgical-domain models (e.g., trained on other surgical datasets) are absent, making the current comparison unfair (R3). A zero-shot comparison with the same VLM trained on other surgical datasets (e.g., EndoVis-18-VQA, Cholec80-VQA, SSG-VQA) is needed to demonstrate generalizability (R4, R5).
- **Missing Evaluations on Surgical Downstream Tasks**: The paper lacks rigorous validation on essential surgical downstream tasks such as phase recognition, action/instrument classification, and other surgical VQA datasets (R4). Evaluation on existing surgical-domain datasets (EndoVis-18-VQA, Cholec80-VQA, SSG-VQA) is needed to demonstrate Surg-QA’s potential as a foundational dataset (R5). Extension to other medical fields (RAD, SLAKE, PathVQA) is unclear (R1).
- **Temporal Modeling Deficiency**: Using CLIP with frame-by-frame encoding loses spatiotemporal information and increases computational cost/redundancy. Video encoders (e.g., Video Swin Transformer) would better capture temporal dynamics (R1, R5). Reviewer 5 notes that other video VLM models offer more sophisticated temporal fusion than simple average pooling (R5).
- **Data Quality and Hallucination Concerns**: The dataset is automatically generated; although the paper reports ChatGPT and human evaluations, these are insufficient to guarantee quality (R2). There is no confirmation that clinicians or domain experts reviewed raw data (R4). More detailed analysis on types and examples of hallucinations is needed (R2). The claim of reduced hallucinations is challenged by potential inaccuracies in complex surgical scenarios (R1).
- **Data Privacy and Legal Risks**: The surgical videos come from WebSurg, not the authors, so dataset availability may be subject to license changes (R2). Privacy and permission risks for sensitive surgical content are not adequately addressed; IRB approval may be required (R3, R4).
- **Methodological Novelty**: The approach is very similar to LLaVA-Med, with only simple fine-tuning (SFT). The authors could propose improvements beyond this pipeline (R3).
- **Error Analysis**: In-depth error analysis is missing, especially regarding potential hallucinations or misunderstandings in complex surgical scenarios (R1).
- **Visual Quality**: The paper does not evaluate the visual quality of the surgical videos obtained from the web (R4).
- **Reproducibility**: The model is not yet available, though enough details are provided (R2).
