## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a detection method for “safety neurons” in LLMs (neurons consistently crucial for handling harmful queries), finding they constitute <1% of parameters, are language-specific, and concentrate in self-attention layers of early blocks. It introduces SN-Tune (fine-tuning only safety neurons) and RSN-Tune (also excluding foundation neurons). SN-Tune significantly reduces harmful scores (e.g., Llama3-8B-Instruct from 65.5 to 2.0, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 from 70.8 to 4.5, Vicuna-13B-1.5 from 93.5 to 3.0) and also works on base models. RSN-Tune aims to preserve downstream capabilities. Reviewers generally found the work interesting and well-written (R1, R2, R3, R4), with strong experimental results and practical relevance. However, one reviewer (R5) rated the paper 3, citing major concerns about novelty overlap with prior work (Zhao et al. 2024b) and insufficient credit, as well as questionable interpretations of results. All reviewers noted some theoretical, notational, and experimental detail gaps.

### Strengths
* **Novel and simple concept of safety neurons**: Provides a clear, empirically identifiable definition (deactivation changes next-token embedding by a Euclidean distance threshold) that departs from traditional safety alignment approaches (R2, R4). The detection method is conceptually simple and parallelizable (R4).
* **Well-written and easy to follow**: The paper is clear, logically organized, and the findings are presented coherently (R1, R3, R4).
* **Extensive experiments with strong defense performance**: SN-Tune achieves large reductions in harmful scores across multiple open-weight models (instruction-tuned and base), often outperforming existing SOTA defenses (R3). Key numeric results: Llama3-8B-Instruct harmful score reduction 65.5→2.0; Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 70.8→4.5; Vicuna-13B-1.5 93.5→3.0; base models ~100→5.3, 13.5, 13.8 (R1, R2, R3, R4). Evaluated on downstream fine-tuning tasks (GSM8K, etc.) with RSN-Tune (R1).
* **Empirical insights into safety mechanisms**: Safety neurons are very concentrated (<1% of parameters), predominantly in self-attention modules, and in earlier layers (first 10–20 layers most sensitive). They are mostly not shared across languages (only ~30% overlap). These findings are relevant for understanding safety mechanisms and multilingual defenses (R1, R2, R4).
* **Practical impact**: SN-Tune enhances safety without notable performance degradation on general capabilities, making it efficient and applicable for practitioners without requiring manual analysis or large corpora (R2, R4). The ablation studies (e.g., grid search over learning rates, number of safety documents) add rigor (R1).

### Weaknesses
* **Theoretical foundation lacking**: The detection method is empirical without theoretical justification for why identified neurons have such a significant impact on safety (R1, R2). The “back-to-front deactivation” pattern (safety breaks only after almost all safety neurons deactivated) is unexplained – is it a fundamental property or experimental artifact? Later-layer safety neurons may merely relay information from earlier layers; their mechanistic role is unclear (R2). The 30% overlap across languages is noted but not analyzed functionally (do shared neurons encode universal safety concepts or coincidental overlap? Are they adapted to language-specific mechanisms?) (R2). Minority reviewer (R5) adds that the formulations in Section 2.1 are essentially identical to Zhao et al. (2024b) except for feeding harmful queries; the only difference is a minor tweak, making the distinctive contribution unclear. The paper fails to cite the original source for equations (1) and (2) and provides insufficient novelty distinction from “Assessing the Brittleness of Safety Alignment via Pruning and Low-Rank Modifications” (Wei et al., 2024) (R5).
* **Notation and presentation issues**: In the main paper, the RHS of equations (4)(5) appears independent of l (neuron index), implying the same value for different l – needs clarification (R1). In the appendix, the form of \(h_{ffn}\) as a vector is unclear; derivation of (8) from (7) is confusing because \(x\) in (8) is an input for \(W_{down}\) but not in (7); dimensions and definitions need specification (R1). Section 2.1 describes “Mask” as an identity matrix, but it is actually a binary matrix (0/1); “parallel computations” is vague – it should clarify whether masking is element-wise or via matrix multiplication and how parallelization (e.g., batching) works (R4). R5 also notes broken sentence at lines 124–125, making the actual implementation impossible to verify.
* **Insufficient experimental detail and missing analyses**:  
  * The dataset used for safety neuron detection is not specified; parameter details and time costs are absent. The generalizability depends on whether neurons identified with violent-instruction datasets also defend against other safety categories (e.g., pornography, jailbreaks) (R3).
  * **RSN-Tune foundation neuron detection**: No details on dataset, threshold, or metric used to identify foundation neurons, harming reproducibility (R4).
  * **Support for claim “safety neurons in self-attention layers”**: The paper reports the split of safety neurons between self-attention and feed-forward layers but does not normalize by the total number of neurons in each layer type. The observed distribution may simply reflect the overall neuron count, not a higher concentration (R5).
  * **Impact on general capability**: Table 1 shows Deact_SN’s Avg. Capability is consistently lower than Deact_R (random neuron deactivation), contradicting the claim of minimal impact. This suggests safety neurons also contribute to general capability (R5).
  * **Unfair cost comparison for SN-Tune**: The paper compares training cost with Circ-Break without including the cost of identifying safety neurons (data collection, processing, detection). A detailed breakdown is needed (R5).
  * Missing results: Llama2 instruction-tuned models not reported in Table 2 (though Llama2 is more resilient) (R5); helpfulness scores (not just harmfulness) not reported (R5); language names X and Y in Fig. 5 not specified; results for all five languages not shown (R5); Section 2.3.2 (cross-lingual transferability) appears tangential to the main theme (R5); “Language-specific neurons” (line 291) is not defined beforehand (R5).
  * Limited downstream fine-tuning datasets: only GSM8K used; consider Alpaca, Dolly (R1). In Table 4, inconsistency: Llama2-7B-chat (safety-aligned) and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 (not safety-aligned) are used; why not use a Mistral model safety-aligned by SN-Tune? (R1). R5 also questions the impact of RSN-Tune vs SN-Tune on GSM8K results.
* **Missing related work citations**:  
  * Kotha et al. (2023) on multilingual catastrophic forgetting and attacks (complementary to cross-lingual safety neuron findings) (R4).  
  * Jain et al. (2024) on mechanistic effects of fine-tuning (finding that pruning few neurons can neutralize fine-tuning effects) (R4).  
  * Molchanov et al. (2019) on importance estimation for pruning (R5).  
  * Other safety neuron/mechanism/backdoor papers: Wei et al. (2024), Chen et al. (2024), Hsu et al. (2024), Zhang et al. (2022) (R1).  
* **Questions overlapping with criticisms/missing experiments**:  
  * How does position of jailbreaking prompts affect activation? How does context window length affect stability? (R2)  
  * Can non-safety neurons transform into safety neurons during full fine-tuning? How does SN-Tune handle emerging safety neurons? (R2)  
  * From which figure/table can we infer “1%” safety neurons? (R5)  
  * Do base models already contain safety neurons? (R4) – Would disabling safety neurons in base models worsen harmfulness? A study of safety neuron distribution in base models is needed (R4).  
  * Are the claimed insights about safety neuron location consistent for base models? (R4)  

**Minority Reviewer Note**: Reviewer 5 (score 3, confidence 5) is substantially more negative than the others, raising the above points about novelty credit, data/implementation ambiguities, and misinterpretation of Table 1 and cost comparison. The other four reviewers (scores 6,8,6,8) were positive about the work’s significance but still noted the theoretical and experimental gaps.
