## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes InstructBrush, a diffusion-based method that extracts editing effects from exemplar {before, after} image pairs using Attention-based Instruction Optimization and Transformation-oriented Instruction Initialization, and introduces TOP-Bench for evaluation. The approach aims to handle fine-grained edits that are difficult to describe in text. Quantitative and qualitative results show competitive performance.

### Strengths
- Among the first works to focus on fine-grained details in visual prompting for image editing (e.g., editing only the color of water in a cup) rather than global style edits (Reviewer 1, high confidence).
- The use of attention manipulation for visual prompting is novel and interesting (Reviewer 1).
- The feature similarity-based unique phrase extraction is a simple yet novel approach (Reviewer 3).
- The paper identifies limitations in conventional image editing evaluation and proposes a new benchmark (TOP-Bench) to address them (Reviewers 2 and 3).
- Quantitative results indicate strong performance and produce effectively edited images (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 4 notes the method yields more satisfactory results compared to other approaches on the given task.
- The writing is clear and easy to understand (Reviewer 4).
- Reviewer 1 was notably more positive (rating 6 vs. others at 5) and considered the experiments extensive and convincing.

### Weaknesses
- **Attribution and overclaiming**: Some formulas (e.g., line 760) are not correctly attributed to original papers (e.g., [1,2,3]), making the paper appear to overclaim novelty (Reviewer 1, moderate concern).
- **Reliance on language template**: Despite aiming to address edits hard to describe, the method still converts image pairs into a descriptive template with a fixed vocabulary. The paper does not explore directly encoding editing directions from image features, and if language is needed, existing text-based methods seem sufficient (Reviewer 2, primary concern). Reviewer 4 echoes this: Unique Phrase Extraction derives textual features from pairs, raising redundancy—if textual descriptions were provided directly, similar results could be achieved via GPT-4v + IP2P, questioning the necessity of exemplar image pairs.
- **Input constraint and practicality**: The method requires reference image pairs with small pixel-level differences (Eq.6, Eq.7), which are hard to acquire in practice. Obtaining such pairs may itself require text-based editing or prompt-to-prompt, making input conditions more complex than providing text and reducing practicality (Reviewer 4, major concern).
- **Qualitative suboptimality vs. IP2P+GPT-4o**: In some cases, IP2P+GPT-4o delivers superior results compared to the proposed method (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). The paper does not adequately explain why the approach improves over this strong baseline, especially given the simpler pipeline (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- **Limited novelty of core modules**: The attention-based instruction module is a relatively simple technique, and the concept of optimizing features in cross-attention space has been previously introduced. The time-aware instruction strategy is too common and resembles recaptioning and null-text inversion (Reviewers 3 and 4). The paper lacks a clear articulation of specific advantages over existing methods (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient analysis of time-aware instruction**: The explanation of when time-aware optimization is effective or ineffective is lacking. No detailed analysis of how it impacts different types of edits, and under what conditions it provides significant improvement versus being unnecessary or detrimental. In the Peach-Apple example (Figure 5), it does not appear effective (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). An ablation study on this component is requested (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing ablations and experiments**: 
  - Ablation on the degree of truncation is needed (Reviewer 3).
  - Ablation comparing optimization of initial m tokens vs. full set of tokens is needed (Reviewer 2).
  - More real-world image tests and comparative experiments with text-guided editing methods are needed to demonstrate applicability to diverse scenarios (Reviewer 3).
  - If unique phrase extraction is effective, the authors should show whether sampling images from those conditions matches real concepts, to strengthen the claim (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation issue**: Notation `CAP_x` in line 281 is undefined—likely intended to be `P_x` (Reviewer 2).
