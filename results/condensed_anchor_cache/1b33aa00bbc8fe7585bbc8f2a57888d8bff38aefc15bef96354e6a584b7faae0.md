## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a generator-unlearner framework (MixUnlearn) that uses adversarial mixup samples to improve machine unlearning. A generator is trained with a novel contrastive objective to produce challenging mixup examples that reveal forgotten information while disrupting retention of remaining data. The unlearner then processes synthesized and real samples jointly, guided by contrastive losses, to forget target data without catastrophic unlearning. Experiments on benchmark datasets (CIFAR-10, SVHN) under both label-aware and label-agnostic settings show the method outperforms prior approaches with relatively small time cost.

### Strengths

1. **Novel adversarial process** (R1, R3): The generator-unlearner adversarial framework is unique and effectively forces the unlearner to forget target data while retaining remaining knowledge. The toy example motivating the approach is well-designed and clear (R3).
2. **Effectiveness across settings** (R1, R2): The method achieves strong performance in both label-agnostic and label-aware unlearning scenarios, demonstrating broad applicability.
3. **Simple yet effective scheme** (R2, R3): The design is relatively simple but highly effective, with particularly well-crafted contrastive loss terms. The paper is well-organized, easy to follow, with helpful figures and tables (R2, R3; minority disagreement: R1 rates presentation 2/4, describing the methods section as needing improvement).
4. **Good visualization** (R2): Visual experiments provide rich evidence supporting the method’s effectiveness.
5. **Adequate comparison** (R3): Experimental results include sufficient comparisons to existing methods, supporting the method’s effectiveness.

### Weaknesses

**Conceptual concerns** (minority reviewer R2, score 3, confidence 4; versus R1 and R3 scores 6):
- The method incorporates information from forgotten data (via generated mixups), which intuitively undermines complete unlearning – retraining never touches forgotten data, so any generalization from those data should be lost. The design does not convincingly demonstrate actual unlearning improvement (R2).
- One reviewer (R1) specifically questions: Why does MixUnlearn sometimes outperform retrain (e.g., on CIFAR-10)? This needs explanation (R1).

**Insufficient experiments and scalability** (R1, R2, R3):
- All datasets used are small (CIFAR-10, SVHN). Experiments on larger datasets like ImageNet are expected (R1, R2, R3).
- Only older architectures (e.g., ResNet) are tested; newer models like Vision Transformers (ViT) or Swin Transformers should be evaluated (R3). Computational challenges for scaling up and needed adaptations should be discussed (R3).
- Additional deletion results at varying forgetting ratios are needed (R2).
- No epoch description or convergence criteria: how many training rounds are needed for satisfactory results? The termination criterion is crucial for approximate forgetting (R2).
- Inconsistency: the retrained model uses a different number of epochs than the initial model. Testing accuracy is higher than training accuracy on CIFAR-10 and SVHN, suggesting the original model may not fully converge. Full convergence should be ensured before drawing unlearning conclusions (R2).

**Presentation and reproducibility** (R1, R2):
- The methods section description needs improvement; it is currently hard to follow (R1).
- Comparative experiments lack sufficient hyperparameter explanations. Some state-of-the-art baseline results appear too low (e.g., TS). The authors state they copy results from [1] when they match, or provide their own otherwise – but copying most experimental data from [1] is unacceptable; baseline results should be actual experimental findings (R2).
- Regarding TS: the implementation appears to use further training of the teacher model, but the original TS uses the original and initialized models as teachers. The authors should review the original control scheme rather than relying on [1]’s introduction. This raises doubts about experimental workload authenticity (R2).

**Missing ablation** (R3):
- An ablation replacing the adversarial mixup generator with traditional mixup at fixed λ values (e.g., 0.9, 0.95, 0.99) should be included. This would clarify whether simpler mixup strategies can achieve similar effects.
