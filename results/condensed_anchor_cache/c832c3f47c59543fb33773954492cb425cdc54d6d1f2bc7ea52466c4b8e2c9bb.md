## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FedPeWS, a personalized warmup via subnetworks for federated learning under extreme data heterogeneity. The method splits training into a warmup phase where each client learns a personalized binary mask and updates only a subnetwork, followed by standard FedAvg. Reviewers find the problem important and the idea of subnet training novel, but raise significant concerns about unproven hypotheses, lack of theoretical justification, insufficient comparison with state-of-the-art methods, overly simple experimental setups (datasets, models, number of clients, heterogeneity level), the fact that the simpler FedPeWS-Fixed variant sometimes outperforms FedPeWS, unclear motivation, inadequate hyperparameter guidance, unconvincing convergence comparison based on rounds alone, and potential overlap with existing techniques like FedPM. Reviewer 3 is notably more critical (score 1), emphasizing that core assumptions are not justified and that the method may not bring real benefits.

### Strengths
- Studies the problem of personalized federated learning from the perspective of subnetworks. (R1, R4)
- The idea of using independent subnet training in federated learning is novel and reasonable. (R4)
- Addresses the important problem of convergence and performance under extreme data heterogeneity. (R3)
- The paper is well written and easy to follow. (R1, R4)
- Conducts extensive experiments under various datasets and provides detailed ablation studies around relevant parameters. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Unproven core hypothesis and lack of theoretical justification.** The paper builds on the hypothesis that convergence issues in extreme heterogeneity stem from conflicting updates in initial rounds (abstract lines 15–17). This premise is misleading because conflicts and objective misalignment persist throughout FL, not only in early rounds. No empirical or theoretical proof is provided for the hypothesis. The claim that a personalized binary mask can map to each client’s data distribution is not explained; there is no theoretical basis for how a subnetwork structure directly represents a data distribution in a nonlinear parameter space. The lack of convergence proofs and theoretical analysis weakens the paper’s foundation. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Insufficient comparison with state-of-the-art methods.** Comparisons are limited to FedAvg and FedProx. Missing SOTA methods such as MOON (Li et al., 2021), PFL (Arivazhagan et al., 2019), FedOpt, and FedUV (R2, R4). The paper does not adequately discuss or compare with recent FL initialization techniques using pre-trained models, which may make warmup unnecessary. The method’s novelty relative to these works is not established. (R2, R3, R4)
- **Experimental setup is too simple and not robust.**   
  *Datasets*: Too simple; harder datasets like CIFAR-100, (Tiny) ImageNet are needed. (R3, R4)   
  *Models*: Too simple; deeper architectures such as ResNet and ViT should be used. (R3, R4)   
  *Number of clients*: Often small (e.g., N=2 for synthetic datasets when comparing with FedProx). In the appendix, N=200 shows much less improvement. Larger N settings are required. (R2, R4)   
  *Data heterogeneity*: α=0.1 is not extreme; try α=0.01. (R4)   
  *Missing ablations*: Replace the warmup with random neuron sampling or other iterative synaptic pruning (IST) approaches; compare with FedPM (Isik et al., 2023), which also uses mask training. (R4)
- **FedPeWS-Fixed sometimes outperforms FedPeWS.** FedPeWS-Fixed (a static, even division of subnetworks) performs comparably and in some stages better than the dynamic mask optimization of FedPeWS (Appendix B.2). This contradicts the intuition that dynamic optimization should be more effective and raises concerns about the actual benefit of the FedPeWS method. The paper does not adequately analyze or explain this. (R2)
- **Lack of guidance for hyperparameter selection.** The paper does not analyze the relationship between hyperparameters λ, τ and convergence, nor does it provide theoretical ranges. For new scenarios, multiple experiments (e.g., grid search) are likely needed, limiting practicality. The balance between τ (warmup rounds) and actual convergence speed in terms of wall-clock time is important but not discussed. (Note: typo in Appendix C.4: τ=25 should be W=25.) (R2)
- **Unclear motivation and intuition.** The reason for splitting learning into two phases (warmup + standard FL) is not clearly explained. The intuition of why a personalized warmup helps beyond vague statements is not convincing. The paper does not clarify how the algorithm specifically addresses the challenges of data heterogeneity. (R1, R3)
- **Overhead comparison using rounds is insufficient.** Comparing convergence by number of rounds is not convincing because FedPeWS has higher computational overhead per round during warmup (masks require extra optimization), although communication is lower due to subnetwork transmission. Wall-clock time experiments in the appendix show FedPeWS takes more time in the warmup phase, so the practical trade-off needs better analysis. (R2)
- **Potential lack of novelty.** The method extends existing techniques without substantial innovation. The paper does not adequately distinguish itself from FedPM (R4) and does not discuss why a personalized warmup is necessary when pre-trained or meta-learned initialization often yields better results without warmup (R3).
