## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper extends Diffusion Schrödinger Bridges (DSB) to the unbalanced setting, allowing endpoints with arbitrary finite mass by deriving time reversal of SDEs with killing and birth terms. Two algorithmic schemes are presented, with theoretical analysis and experiments on synthetic data and a single-cell drug response dataset.

### Strengths
- The problem of generalizing DSB to unbalanced measures is important and naturally extends the Schrödinger Bridge framework to settings with mass change, such as cell birth/death.
- The authors derive a fundamental theoretical result on the time-reversal of diffusions with killing/birth terms, and extend the Iterative Proportional Fitting (IPF) algorithm accordingly.
- The theory is mathematically sound, with rigorous conditions and statements for all theorems; the logical flow between sections is clear for readers familiar with the area.
- The introduction and motivations are clear, and the paper provides a complete theoretical framework: optimization problem, optimality conditions, algorithm, and sampling scheme.
- The method addresses a difficult and practically important problem in cell dynamics modeling.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental validation**: Results are presented on a single small single-cell dataset (one baseline, Ours, and Ours-no death/births). Even accounting for standard deviation, the differences (e.g., slightly lower MMD) are marginal, and it is unclear whether this improvement translates to practically meaningful gains such as better prediction of cancer drug responses. The dimensionality of the data is low, and it is not shown whether the method scales to higher-dimensional settings.
- **Lack of comparisons**: Only one competitor (a basic DSB variant) is compared. Many specialized methods for cell dynamics with branching SDEs exist and are not benchmarked (e.g., Lavenant et al. 2021, Chizat et al. 2022, Ventre et al. 2023 for trajectory inference; balanced SB approaches such as De Bortoli et al., Shi et al., Tong et al.; and other single-cell datasets such as TrajectoryNet [Tong et al.] and the Root dataset). Comparisons with non-SB/DSB methods would help demonstrate the advantage of the proposed UDSB.
- **Clarity issues for non-experts**: The paper is mathematically heavy. Several important quantities (e.g., the generator in Eq. 3, the decomposition of generator K, and the connection to the unbalanced SB problem) are not derived or explained in a self-contained way. The description of DSB in the related work is too brief, making it hard for readers unfamiliar with DSB to follow the unbalanced extension. One reviewer found the practical implementation very difficult to understand and reproduce, while another (more familiar with the area) noted that the exposition is fairly clear despite heavy notation. The description of the algorithm steps in the appendix is also difficult to follow.
- **Missing ablations and analysis**: The necessity of the TD loss (Sec. 5) is not justified; no ablation study shows how it affects final quality. The computational complexity and the influence of neural network capacity on results are not discussed. The requirement that the killing rate k is non-negative is not standard in unbalanced dynamic optimal transport and is not explained in the main text.
- **Known birth/killing rates required**: UDSB assumes the killing and/or birth rates are known, which may limit practical applicability. It is unclear how to tune these rates in the absence of prior knowledge.
- **Unclear experimental setup**: The reconstruction quality at the end timepoint is evaluated, but it is unclear why it should be better than a simple IID generative model – is the baseline DSB underfit? The data partitioning (80/20 split) and how the observed mass changes (increase 35% then decrease to -25%) are obtained when the middle timepoint is left out are not clarified. The “simulating virus spread” experiment appears only in the introduction and supplement; it should either be part of the main experiments or removed.
- **Missed related work on Trajectory Inference** (Lavenant et al. 2021, Chizat et al. 2022, Ventre et al. 2023) that also model cell growth and death in an SDE framework.
- **Open questions that are also criticisms**: Does the method recover the original SB when both endpoint measures have equal mass? Can it be applied to other single-cell datasets (e.g., TrajectoryNet dataset), and how would it compare to established methods? The overall limited scope of experiments and lack of broader validation leave unclear when and how UDSB should be applied in practice.
