## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes KDGCN, a semi-supervised graph classification method with two GCN modules. The first GCN learns graph-level embeddings via readout; the second GCN operates on a supergraph whose nodes are all dataset graphs and edges are determined by a graph kernel (e.g., WL subtree kernel). The two GCNs are jointly optimized. The method aims to address label scarcity without the complexity of contrastive learning. Experiments on seven benchmark datasets compare against supervised GCNs, label propagation, graph contrastive learning (MVGRL, SimGRACE, GLA), and graph kernels. Note: Reviewer 3 is the most positive (score 8, confidence 4) and finds the idea novel, while Reviewer 4 is the most negative (score 3, confidence 5) and questions novelty and missing comparisons.

### Strengths
- The problem of semi-supervised graph classification with scarce labels is important and challenging (Reviewer 3).
- The double-level GCN design – first on individual graphs, second on a supergraph constructed via graph kernel – is novel and appealing (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The use of a supergraph eliminates the need to construct negative samples, simplifying the framework compared to contrastive learning (Reviewer 1).
- The method is simple, easy to implement, and has fewer hyperparameters than contrastive-learning alternatives (Reviewers 1, 3).
- On smaller datasets the method achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA results; improvements are also seen when labels are extremely scarce (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The paper is overall easy to understand and well-organized (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Competitors include SOTA methods such as SimGRACE and GLA; performance is competitive (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Scalability and dataset size**: The experiments are conducted on relatively small datasets; advantages are less pronounced on larger datasets. The COLLAB dataset is mentioned as large but the paper does not clarify its specific challenges (e.g., variance in graph size, structural complexity) or how KDGCN addresses them. Scalability to large-scale datasets is questionable (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
   - Reviewer 1 asks whether training requires all graphs to reside in hardware simultaneously, limiting scalability, and whether inference is transductive (entire dataset required) – budget details (platform, memory, inference time) are missing.
   - Reviewer 2 questions whether the method scales to large datasets in terms of complexity.
   - Reviewer 4 notes the model is evaluated only on small datasets; scalability unknown.

2. **Missing comparisons and relations to prior work**:
   - Reviewer 1 identifies two papers: **Few-Shot Learning on Graphs via Super-Classes based on Graph Spectral Measures** (supergraph for few-shot graph classification) and **PRODIGY: Enabling In-context Learning Over Graphs** (supergraph for few-shot node/edge classification). The paper does not clearly articulate differences in supergraph construction and usage beyond stating different tasks.
   - Reviewer 4 states that introducing graph kernels in semi-supervised graph classification is not novel and has been done in previous works [1-3] not referenced; also missing highly relevant works [4-6]. The paper needs supplementary comparisons and detailed discussion.
   - The paper does not compare with these baselines; their absence undermines claims of novelty and superiority (Reviewers 1, 4).

3. **Supergraph construction details**:
   - **Threshold τ selection**: The method uses a predefined similarity threshold τ to binarize the kernel matrix, but how to choose τ is not explained. The impact of τ on supergraph sparsity/density and on information propagation is unclear (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 adds that the sparsification and binarization may significantly affect performance.
   - **Two post-processing methods**: The paper presents two ways to turn the kernel matrix into edges (τ-based and c-based), but it is unclear which one is better and which was used in the main experiments. Figure 2 suggests c-based is better, yet Tables 2–4 do not specify (Reviewer 3). 
   - The graph kernel is not learnable; quality of the supergraph depends entirely on learned node representations and the chosen threshold. A learnable kernel could be better (Reviewer 4).

4. **Hyperparameter claim not substantiated**: The paper claims fewer hyperparameters than contrastive-learning methods like GLA, but does not provide a specific comparison (e.g., count of hyperparameters for each method). The proposed method itself requires determining c or τ, and dimensions d, d′, d′. The impact of these hyperparameters on accuracy is not ablated systematically (Reviewer 3).

5. **Kernel choice justification**: The WL subtree kernel is used but the paper does not sufficiently justify its choice over alternatives (e.g., graphlet kernels, random walk kernels). A more detailed analysis of kernel properties is needed (Reviewers 2, 3). Reviewer 3 also notes that the graphlet sampling kernel is always worst in Table 4, but the discussion on why is missing.

6. **Experimental setup gaps**:
   - Larger labeling rates (e.g., 30%, 50%) are not included – only low rates are reported (Reviewer 3).
   - No time cost comparison with baselines (Reviewer 3).
   - The number of layers in the two GNNs is not stated; does depth matter? (Reviewer 3).
   - The best settings for d and d′ are not analyzed; only d=d′=64 is reported (Reviewer 3).

7. **Transductive vs. inductive setting**: The paper does not clarify whether KDGCN supports inductive inference (like MVGRL, SimGRACE, GLA) or only transductive. If transductive, the entire dataset must be inferred together, which has implications for deployment (Reviewer 1).

8. **Novelty concerns**: While Reviewer 3 considers the double-level GCN idea novel, Reviewer 4 strongly doubts novelty because graph kernels have been used for semi-supervised graph classification before and the proposed supergraph construction has similarities to existing work. Reviewer 1 also asks whether this is the first such approach and requests re-emphasis of core innovation. The paper does not adequately distinguish its contributions from prior supergraph-based methods (Reviewers 1, 4).
