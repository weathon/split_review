## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes CirT (Circular Transformer) for Subseasonal-to-Seasonal (S2S) climate forecasting, with two key designs: (1) decomposing weather data by latitude into circular patches as input tokens, and (2) using Fourier transform in self-attention to capture global information and model spatial periodicity. Experiments on the ERA5 reanalysis dataset show improvements over PanguWeather, GraphCast, and ECMWF systems. Reviewers find the work well-motivated and experimentally solid, but raise concerns about missing complexity comparisons, unclear resolution details for baseline Pangu, omitted wind variables, limited novelty of the Fourier approach given prior work (AFNO, FourCastNet), insufficient discussion with GraphCast, and lack of ablation on autoregressive vs. direct prediction.

### Strengths
- Experiments are extensive and solid, comparing against both numerical (ECMWF) and data-driven (PanguWeather, GraphCast) models, with strong results on latitude-weighted RMSE and anomaly correlation metrics. Visualizations effectively show method impact across dimensions.
- The method well utilizes input data modality: circular patching provides an effective inductive bias, and the discrete Fourier transform (DFT) captures latitudinal spatial periodicity.
- The paper is well-written with intuitive motivation; Figure 1 clearly highlights limitations of prior methods and provides a strong rationale.
- Innovations include partitioning graticule uniformly by latitude and leveraging Fourier transform to capture global features for self-attention.
- The method achieves excellent results particularly in hard-to-predict high-latitude regions (e.g., polar areas).

### Weaknesses
- Missing comparison of computation complexity and model size. Without this, it is unclear whether the performance gain comes from architectural design or simply larger model capacity, weakening assessment of practical applicability.
- No ablation study comparing autoregressive prediction versus direct prediction of all future values. Autoregressive error accumulation is a known issue, and this omission masks potential benefits of a direct formulation.
- Insufficient discussion contrasting with GraphCast, which shares the motivation of reducing inappropriate geometric inductive biases. The paper should clarify architectural distinctions and highlight unique advantages of CirT.
- Unclear how the 1.5° results for PanguWeather were obtained (whether downsampled from the existing 0.25° model or a separate 1.5° model). This resolution difference directly impacts comparability and validity of conclusions.
- Missing experimental results for critical variables such as wind u and wind v. These are essential for a comprehensive evaluation given their complex spatial and temporal dynamics.
- The use of Fourier transform in self-attention is not sufficiently novel: related structures appear in AFNO and FourCastNet. The paper should clearly articulate unique implementation aspects and how it differs from existing methods.
- The model was not fine-tuned after training, which could further improve performance.
- The design choice of patching all longitudes into the same patch deserves more justification; an alternative such as 2D axial transformer (interleaving attention along latitude and longitude axes) with Fourier transform could be compared.
