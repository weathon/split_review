## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MG-LLaVA, a multimodal large language model that enhances visual processing by fusing low-resolution, high-resolution, and object-centric features. High-resolution visual features are incorporated via an additional encoder and fused with base features using a Conv-Gate network. Object-level features from an offline detector are added to improve recognition. The model is evaluated at scales from 3.8B to 34B on general VQA, video, and perception benchmarks. Two reviewers rated the paper 5, two rated it 3, and one rated it 5 (overall 3 positive, 2 negative). All agree the work is well-structured and the experiments are extensive, but most find the technical contribution incremental and the performance gains limited relative to the added complexity.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized, clearly presented, and easy to follow. (R1, R2, R3, R5)
- The goal of improving fine-grained visual understanding via multi-granularity features (low-res, high-res, object-level) is reasonable, and the Conv-Gate fusion module is demonstrated through complete ablation studies. (R2, R4, R5)
- The model series (3.8B to 34B based on Phi, Vicuna, LLaMA3, Yi1.5) is comprehensive, and evaluation spans multiple benchmarks (general VQA, video, perception). (R1, R2, R4)
- The integration of object-level features from a detector is a new element for MLLMs and the first explicit introduction of such features in this context. (R3, R4)
- On MMBench and SEEDBench, the proposed model outperforms even private models like GPT-4V and GeminiPro-V. (R4)
- Ablation and runtime evaluations are provided. (R1)

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental novelty.** The core idea of fusing low- and high-resolution images directly follows prior works (Mini-Gemini, LLaVA-NeXt, LLaVA-HR, LLaVA-UHD). The main addition—object-level features from a detector—is considered a trick rather than a fundamental advance. (R1, R2, R4, R5) Two reviewers (score 3) emphasize that migrating multi-granularity from CNNs to MLLMs is hardly innovative. (R2, R4)
- **Modest performance gains.** For Vicuna-7B, MG-LLaVA either does not surpass or shows only marginal improvements over comparable baselines on several benchmarks:
  - SEED: 69.4 vs. 68.9 for Mini-Gemini-7B (R1)
  - MMStar: 35.1 vs. 37.6 for Mini-Gemini-7B (R1)
  - Not surpassing baselines on SQA, TextVQA, MMStar (R2)
  - Gains on large benchmarks (Tables 2, 3) are generally modest. (R3, R5)
- **Missing comparisons to recent strong MLLMs.** The evaluations lack comparisons to 2024 models with similar ideas or stronger performance:
  - Qwen2-VL, LLaVA-Onevision, Pixtral (R1)
  - Cambrian-1 (R1)
  - Honeybee, AllSeeing-v2 on MMBench (R4)
  - Flash-VStream, Monkey on MSRVTT-QA (R4)
  - Monkey, which uses a similar resolution enhancement approach (R4)
- **Unnecessary complexity from detector reliance.** Introducing an external detection model adds computational cost, biases, and makes the pipeline non-end-to-end. (R1, R2, R4, R5) The performance gains do not justify the extra expense; one reviewer (R5) notes that MMStar performance drop (35.1 vs. 37.6) may indicate data leakage or detector bias.
- **Unfair training data comparisons.** The model uses 2.6M training samples, much heavier than many baselines, making evaluation comparisons unfair. (R2)
- **Lack of fine-grained perception evaluation.** The paper uses general VQA but does not assess fine-grained perception on datasets like RefCOCO with region-level methods (e.g., Next-Chat, Osprey). (R5) Also, no dedicated small-object or counting benchmarks are shown. (R4)
- **Insufficient ablation and analysis.** The ablation study lacks visual comparisons (e.g., case studies, feature maps) to illustrate the impact of each granularity component. (R2, R3) Failure cases are not shown. (R3) It is unclear if the method scales to higher resolutions like 1024p or 2k. (R3)
- **Efficiency concerns.** No efficiency comparison (inference cost vs. competitors) is provided. The added detector cost may outweigh benefits from data or parameter scaling. (R5) External dependencies also make inference cumbersome. (R5)
- **SEEDBench version ambiguity.** The paper may use SEEDBench-Image while comparing to methods that use SEEDBench-Avg (including video), making comparisons unreliable. (R4)
- **Detector design question.** An open-vocabulary detector is used, but a tagger determines bounding boxes rather than generating ROIs directly from text embeddings. (R4)
