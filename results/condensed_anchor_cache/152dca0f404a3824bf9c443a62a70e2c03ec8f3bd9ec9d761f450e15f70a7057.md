## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviewers collectively describe the paper as proposing a self-distillation-based label rectification method (Annealing Self-Distillation Rectification, ADR) for adversarial training that is simple, plug-and-play, and shows robustness gains with reduced overfitting. Experimental results across multiple datasets and baselines are presented. However, concerns are raised about the theoretical motivation, performance limitations on larger architectures (WRN), practical memory overhead, and whether the experiments are sufficiently extensive for the claimed simplicity.

### Strengths
- **Simplicity and ease of integration**: ADR is intuitive, easy to implement, and can be seamlessly plugged into existing adversarial training methods (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Interesting idea**: The combination of self-distillation with exponential moving average (EMA) teacher, annealed interpolation, and softmax temperature is a novel modification (Reviewer 2).
- **Clear writing and reproducibility**: The paper is well‑organized, easy to understand, contains abundant experimental details, and provides the source code (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). Presentation quality is rated good to excellent.
- **Low computational overhead**: The method adds only a small additional training time compared to vanilla adversarial training (Reviewer 3).
- **Performance improvements over baselines**: ADR achieves significant robust accuracy gains over vanilla adversarial training and several baselines (Reviewer 1). On ResNet‑18, it reaches state‑of‑the‑art robust performance (Reviewer 4). (Note: the same reviewer also points out poor performance on WRN; see Weaknesses.)
- **Inspiration from empirical findings**: The motivation is supported by experimental observations, and the idea is consistent with the intuition that easier (less adversarial/weaker signal) training leads to better results (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of strong theoretical motivation**: The connection between the empirical observations in Section 3.2 and the design choices (EMA teacher, interpolation factor) is unclear. No direct evidence (analytical or empirical) is provided that ADR helps achieve the desired properties of robust models (e.g., high uncertainty on OOD data). The motivation appears to rely on prior work on label rectification rather than a novel theoretical insight (Reviewer 1). A more rigorous analysis of how rectified labels affect the loss landscape and optimization dynamics is missing (Reviewer 4). Additionally, the finding that robust models are better calibrated is not new, yet the paper presents it as a major contribution (Reviewer 3).
- **Performance concerns on larger architectures**: In Table 2, AT+ADR underperforms AT+WA (weight averaging) on WRN models across all three datasets, and AT+WA+ADR underperforms AT+WA+AWP (adversarial weight perturbation). This suggests ADR may not be effective when combined with weight‑smoothing techniques, especially on high‑capacity models. ADR shows strong robustness on ResNet‑18 but poor performance on WRN, raising questions about architecture scalability (Reviewer 4). Furthermore, in Table 3, using DDPM data with WRN‑34‑10 improves robust accuracy but significantly decreases standard accuracy, contrary to Rebuffi et al. (2021a) where DDPM data improves both. This trade‑off is concerning and needs explanation (Reviewer 1).
- **Sufficiency and fairness of experiments**: Some reviewers find the experiments insufficient given the simplicity of the method. Additional datasets (e.g., ImageNet), stronger attacks, and more baseline adversarial training methods should be tested (Reviewer 3). The comparison with RobustBench is not entirely fair because ADR is presented in combination with WA and AWP rather than in isolation; an ablation showing ADR alone on RobustBench would clarify its individual contribution (Reviewer 4). (Contrast: Reviewers 2 and 4 consider the experiments extensive, while Reviewer 3 and 4 also note this limitation.)
- **High memory cost**: The self‑distillation teacher requires storing an additional copy of the model, which is a practical limitation for deployment (Reviewer 3).
- **Significance of hyperparameter studies questioned**: The ablation for annealing and temperature (Figure 5) may not show statistically significant differences to support the conclusion that annealing both parameters is beneficial (Reviewer 2). The weight loss landscape analysis also lacks clarity on significance (Reviewer 2).
- **Unaddressed questions that overlap with missing analyses/ablations**:
  - Are robust models trained via ADR more conformed to the empirical findings in Section 3.2 compared to vanilla adversarial training? (Reviewer 1)
  - Should rectified labels be assigned to adversarial images only, or both clean and adversarial images? This is important for applying ADR to methods like PGD‑AT and TRADES (Reviewer 1)
  - Are ADR, WA, and AWP complementary? The results in Table 2 show similar gain magnitudes, but no analysis on their interaction or complementarity is provided (Reviewer 2)
  - Why does the teacher network (EMA of student) reflect the “true distribution”? (Reviewer 2)
  - How does ADR perform when using pretrained models for initialization? (Reviewer 3)
