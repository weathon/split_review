## Merged Review

### Summary  
The paper studies the security of vision-language models (VLMs) and introduces “image hijacks” – adversarial images that control VLM behavior at inference time. The authors propose a Behavior Matching algorithm and derive Prompt Matching to craft hijacks matching arbitrary text prompts. They demonstrate three attack types (specific string, leak context, jailbreak) and test them on LLaVA, reporting over 80 % success (often above 90 %). The work is well-organized and clearly presented, with visualizations and real-world experiments (drawing, taping). However, reviewers identify major concerns: limited technical novelty relative to prior works, insufficient exploration of attack transferability to closed-source models, and a narrow experimental scope (only white‑box settings with a single attack objective).

### Strengths  
- The problem motivation is timely and relevant, highlighting real security vulnerabilities in VLMs that are increasingly deployed.  
- The Behavior Matching methodology is sound and intuitive, and the three proposed attack types provide a broad view of potential threats.  
- Evaluation results are striking: all attack types achieve ≥ 80 % success (often > 90 %) on LLaVA, underscoring the practical risk.  
- Real‑world experiments with human studies (drawing and pasting tapes) add credibility and practical insight.  
- The paper is well written, with sufficient example visualizations, clear technical descriptions, and a simple yet effective core idea (using an image to replace prompt templates).

### Weaknesses  
- **Limited technical novelty.** The attack process closely resembles prior works (e.g., Qi et al., Carlini et al., GCG). The paper does not clearly differentiate its contributions from these existing studies; without a transferability study it appears to be a standard white‑box attack, and reviewers note that DNNs are already known to be vulnerable to adversarial examples.  
- **No transferability study to closed‑source VLMs.** This is the most critical weakness. The attacks are demonstrated only in a white‑box setting on LLaVA. There is no evaluation on black‑box or API‑accessible models (e.g., GPT‑4V), and no query‑based or transfer‑based attack experiments. Without this, the practical impact and significance of the work are dramatically limited.  
- **Narrow attack diversity.** The three attack types all pursue the same underlying goal – making the model output a pre‑defined text – rather than covering more diverse undesired behaviors.  
- **Missing baseline comparison.** The paper does not compare against the “Visual Adversarial Examples Jailbreak Aligned Large Language Models” baseline (Qi et al.). Also, while GCG is used as a baseline for the jailbreak attack, it is not compared on the specific string and leak‑context attacks, even though GCG also targets exact‑match outputs.  
- **Unclear problem formulation for closed‑source models.** The approach assumes direct access to the VLM’s image channel and hidden space; its applicability to models that do not expose these interfaces (e.g., many closed‑source VLMs) is unclear, limiting broader relevance.  
- **No discussion of defenses or mitigation strategies.** The paper omits any mention of potential countermeasures, which would strengthen its practical relevance.  
- **Questionable relevance of perturbation‑type experiments.** One reviewer argues that testing different perturbation constraints (Lp norm, stationary patch, moving patch) is not especially meaningful – what matters is the effectiveness of the mapping between the adversarial image and the hijack behavior.  
- **Missing exploration of universal vs. image‑specific perturbations.** The paper splits data into train/val/test sets, but it is unclear whether the perturbation is universal (trained once and applied to any test image) or image‑specific; this should be explicitly stated.  
- **Figure 4 is excessively large** (occupies one page) for showing three well‑known constraint types.  
- **Suggestion for future work that is also a missing experiment:** The paper does not investigate using a single adversarial image to match a series of jailbreak prompt templates (e.g., the DAN series).
