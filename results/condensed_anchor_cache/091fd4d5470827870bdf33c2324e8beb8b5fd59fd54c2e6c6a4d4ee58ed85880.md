## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes FineXtract, a framework to extract fine-tuning data from fine-tuned diffusion models by approximating the distribution shift from the pretrained model and guiding generation toward high-probability regions of fine-tuned data, followed by clustering. Experiments on WikiArt, DreamBooth, real-world checkpoints from HuggingFace, and various diffusion models report roughly 20% extraction accuracy, outperforming two baselines (Direct Text2img+Clustering and Classifier-Free Guidance+Clustering). The method extends to conditional DMs and includes defense analysis.

### Strengths

- Well-written with clear presentation; method sections, figures, and tables are easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Novel focus on extracting fine-tuning data from diffusion model checkpoints, revealing an underexplored privacy concern and offering a new perspective on data leakage and copyright infringement (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- Experiments are comprehensive: multiple datasets (WikiArt, DreamBooth), varying diffusion models, ablation studies on guidance scale \(w'\), correction term \(k\), number of training images \(N_0\), generated images \(N\), and defenses (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Framework applies to both unconditional and conditional DMs, with validation on real-world HuggingFace checkpoints demonstrating practical applicability (Reviewers 3, 4).
- Code is publicly available (Reviewer 2).
- Results highlight significant data leakage risks, important for security and privacy (Reviewers 1, 4).

### Weaknesses

- Task setup is overly clean: assumes pretrained model is always available and fine-tuning data comes from a single distribution. Real-world settings may involve noisy labels, unavailable pretrained models, or mixtures of multiple fine-tuned data distributions; applicability to realistic scenarios is unclear (Reviewer 1).
- Main experiments use a small test set: 20 artist models (10 images each) and 30 object models (4-6 images each), limiting result significance (Reviewer 1).
- Performance drops substantially when fine-tuning image count \(N_0\) increases (Figure 4a) (Reviewer 1). Mitigation for large-scale industry fine-tuning is not addressed (Reviewers 1, 3). The effect of increasing class number (versus per-class images) on performance is also missing (Reviewer 3).
- Effectiveness under LoRA fine-tuning is noticeably worse, suggesting limited applicability for parameter-efficient tuning (Reviewer 3).
- Baseline comparison is insufficient. Only two baselines are used (Direct Text2img+Clustering and CFG+Clustering), which are essentially ablation counterparts. Other existing approaches for extracting training/fine-tuning data should be compared (Reviewer 2). Further discussion of baseline performance under varying \(N\) and \(N_0\) in Sec. 5.2 is needed (Reviewer 4).
- Method is sensitive to hyperparameters \(w'\) and \(k\); deciding these in practice is challenging (Reviewer 2). The reason why \(k\) performs better in the negative case—inconsistent with its motivation—requires further analysis (Reviewer 4).
- Necessity of a dedicated method is questioned: simply applying existing extraction methods on the fine-tuned checkpoint and filtering out results overlapping with the pretrained model may achieve similar outcomes (Reviewer 2). *Minority skeptical view: Reviewer 2 gives score 5 and soundness 2, contrasting with Reviewers 3 and 4 who give score 6.*
- Under adversarial defenses (e.g., transformations), effectiveness diminishes significantly. The paper states images become “largely unusable” but provides no statistics on the degree of unusability or quantification of attacker utility loss to achieve the reported attack performance in Table 3 (Reviewer 3).
- Using PCA to extract important signals from multiple-word prompts may be infeasible when number of words \(W\) is large (Reviewer 4).
- Symbol error: in Appendix A.3, the second \(\epsilon_{0,77}\) should be \(\epsilon_{1,77}\) (Reviewer 4).
- Additional analyses needed: intuition behind Eq. (3) and how \(\lambda\) is affected by fine-tuning data size and similarity (Reviewer 1); performance differences across artist-style vs. object datasets (Reviewer 1).
