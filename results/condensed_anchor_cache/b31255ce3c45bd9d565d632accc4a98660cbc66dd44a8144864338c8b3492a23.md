## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Sparling, a technique for learning extremely sparse intermediate representations without any supervision on those representations. It employs a spatial sparsity layer (a parametric ReLU with an annealed threshold) and adaptive sparse training to enforce high sparsity during training. The method is evaluated on three datasets – DigitCircle, LaTeXOCR, and AudioMNISTSequence – where it achieves high localization accuracy (>90%) and extreme activation sparsity.

### Strengths
- The problem of effectively learning sparse representations is important, and the proposed approach is effective and novel to my knowledge. *(Reviewer 2)*
- Sparling achieves extreme activation sparsity and promotes interpretable representations. *(Reviewers 1,4)*
- The method yields good localization performance on the provided evaluation datasets without requiring additional supervision on the representation. *(Reviewers 1,2)*
- The proposed method is simple, and the spatial sparsify layer together with the adaptive sparsifying method seems to induce a very high sparsity ratio in activations. The spatial sparsify layer appears to be novel. *(Reviewer 4)*
- The evaluation spans multiple modalities (synthetic, visual, audio), providing some evidence of general applicability. *(Reviewer 3)*
- The idea of using activation sparsity to maintain representation capacity while allowing for sparse interpretable representations is an interesting direction for future study. *(Reviewer 1)*

### Weaknesses
- **Strong, restrictive assumptions.** The requirement that the intermediate representation \(g^*\) be a sparse set of localized features that are individually predictive is a harsh constraint. This limits applicability to tasks where such a structure is naturally present (e.g., OCR) and is problematic for settings with highly distributed or entangled features (e.g., natural images, complex audio). *(Reviewers 1,2)* It is unclear whether Sparling works for non-convolutional architectures (e.g., MLP) or for standard image classification tasks. *(Reviewer 2)*
- **Limited scale and scope of evaluation.** The datasets are small-scale and artificial; the paper does not demonstrate that the method generalizes to larger, more complex data. *(Reviewer 4)* Missing comparisons with other OCR methods (e.g., Deng 2016, which inspired several design choices) and end-to-end evaluations on downstream tasks (im2Latex, neural attribution) make it difficult to assess practical utility. The paper needs to show that the method does not sacrifice performance for sparsity. *(Reviewer 1)*
- **Unfair baselines.** The Sparling coefficient \(t\) is annealed during training, but the L1 coefficient for the baseline is set only once, likely preventing the L1 baseline from learning sparse representations due to the same optimization challenges. An ablation where the L1 coefficient is also annealed is needed to isolate whether the improved sparsity comes from the spatial sparsity layer or just the annealing scheme. *(Reviewer 2)*
- **Clarity and readability issues.** The paper is meandering and hard to read, especially Sections 3 and 4. Quantities like the functions \(g\) and \(h\) are poorly motivated and not properly formalized. The connection between the information bound and Sparling (Section 3.3) is unclear. Figure captions (e.g., Fig 2) are unclear. The paper would benefit from a clearer problem statement, step-by-step method development, and proofreading. *(Reviewers 1,3)*
- **Lack of theoretical guarantees on identifiability.** The paper does not address when the learned sparse representations actually correspond to the true underlying latent factors. Without identifiability guarantees, the method could learn arbitrary sparse representations that do not capture the actual structure of the data. There is no discussion of data requirements or conditions (e.g., disentanglement conditions from [2]) under which recovery is possible. *(Reviewer 3)* This concern is critical: while some reviewers found the empirical results impressive *(Reviewer 2)*, others consider the lack of theoretical grounding a major weakness that undermines confidence in the method’s reliability. *(Reviewer 3)*
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity.** Many hyperparameters (e.g., \(M\), \(d_T\), \(\delta_{\text{update}}\)) are introduced without any explanation of how they were selected, the search space, or their sensitivity. *(Reviewer 4)*
- **Novelty clarification needed.** The adaptive sparsity training appears similar to the iterative pruning from the lottery ticket hypothesis, applied to activations via a parameter in the spatial sparsity layer. The paper should clearly differentiate the novelty. *(Reviewer 4)*
- **Unresolved empirical observation.** In Figure 6, the retrained \(\hat{h}\) outperforms the end-to-end non-sparse network. It is unclear why this happens, and the difference from the Sparling network (which trains both motif model and prediction head end-to-end) needs explanation. *(Reviewer 2)*
- **Motivation for design choice.** The motivation for computing the spatial sparsity layer channelwise is not provided. *(Reviewer 2)*
- **Minor issues:** Typos and unclear examples (e.g., do the letters in Fig 2 represent specific shapes or the digits themselves?). *(Reviewers 1,3)*
