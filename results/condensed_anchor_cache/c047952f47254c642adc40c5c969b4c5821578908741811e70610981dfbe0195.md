## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper challenges the Linear Representation Hypothesis (LRH) by formalizing irreducible multi-dimensional features and extending the superposition hypothesis. The authors use sparse autoencoders (SAEs) to discover such features in GPT-2, Mistral 7B, and Llama 3 8B. They identify circular representations for periodic concepts (days of the week, months) and provide causal intervention experiments showing these features are used in modular arithmetic tasks. The work aims to show that some language model features are inherently multi-dimensional, not one-dimensional.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles a timely and important question in mechanistic interpretability by formalizing multi-dimensional features. The high-level idea of defining irreducible multi-dimensional features is sensible and useful.
- The updated superposition hypothesis (Hypothesis 2) is sound and may lead to discovery of other interesting structures.
- Clear, compelling visual examples of circular representations (e.g., Figure 1 showing days of the week in PCA 2/3 space) strongly illustrate the phenomenon.
- Experiments are comprehensive, with concrete results on non-toy LLMs (GPT-2, Mistral 7B, Llama 3 8B), demonstrating that circular features exist “in the wild.” Causal intervention experiments back up the findings.
- The discovered representations and the framework connect clearly to existing interpretability methods, making replication straightforward.
- Provides good empirical evidence that multi-dimensional features exist, with particular interest in circular representations for modular arithmetic.

### Weaknesses
- **Conceptual formalization issues:** The definition of irreducible features (Definition 1, 2) is incomplete. It is not clear whether the two types of reducibility (separability, mixture/disjointness) are exhaustive; there could be a third category. Equating separability with statistical independence (Definition 2) may be limiting, as correlated but separable concepts could still be disentangled. The source of randomness for the distribution $\mathbb{P}$ (over input tokens) is not formally defined upfront (e.g., restricted to $n$-length sequences). The term “point cloud” in Definition 1 is ambiguous—does it imply the range of $f$ is discrete? The notation $d_f$-dimensional point cloud in $\mathbb{R}^{d_f}$ seems redundant, and the “dimension” of a discrete set of points is unclear.
- **Ambiguity about the proposed hypothesis:** The paper’s main motivation is to challenge the LRH, but the alternative hypothesis (p. 5, lines 239–240) appears to be a stricter version rather than a contradiction, making the message confusing. Reviewer 1 noted that the title similarly conflates “multi-dimensional” with “non-linear”; a clearer title might be “Not All Language Model Features are One-dimensionally Linear” (R3).
- **Subjectivity of irreducibility tests:** The “tests” (separability and mixture indices) rely on arbitrary thresholds (e.g., $\epsilon$) rather than formal statistical significance, making irreducibility a user judgment call. The computation of the mixture index uses approximate optimization (SGD), and the separability index is computed only in 2-d, limiting formal rigor. This limitation should be explicitly acknowledged.
- **Unclear exposition and dense organization (especially Reviewer 1):** Section 3 is hard to follow. Figure 2 is not self-contained; it lacks intuitive explanation (e.g., why PCA 2/3 for Figure 1? what do the histogram and angle subfigure represent? what are “typical clusters” and their indices?). The text often references Appendix figures while main-text figures (2,4) are not referenced. Experimental sections are dense and unorganized; clearer motivation, key results, and supplementary references are needed.
- **Figure presentation issues:**
  - Figure 2: color choice (black scatter) is heavy; using a softer color (e.g., green like in the angle subfigure) would help.
  - Figure 3: scatter icons for “other cluster” points should differ from those in Figure 1 (e.g., “x”) for visual clarity.
  - Captions for Figures 5 and 6 are lacking sufficient explanation.
- **Missing analysis on feature interpretability and distribution:** The proportion of extracted multi-dimensional features that are clearly interpretable (vs. noisy/unstructured) is not reported. A more detailed analysis of the distribution of feature dimensionalities is needed (R2). The paper focuses on circular features, but variables with intrinsic dimension >1 (e.g., spherical variables) would better demonstrate the scope of Hypothesis 2. Currently, the shown circles still represent one-dimensional variables, which may only violate the LRH in name, not in spirit (R4). Are there other multi-dimensional feature shapes besides circles (R1 question)?
- **Ablation and threshold sensitivity missing:** There is no systematic exploration of how the threshold parameter $T$ affects cluster extraction and circular feature emergence. Understanding critical $T$ values and how they impact cluster count and size would provide useful insights (R2).
- **Specific concerns about claims and experiments:**
  - In Figure 2, the histogram suggests a mixture, yet the $\epsilon$-mixture index of 0.4750 is said to be “low.” Figure 10 states 0.3621 indicates a mixture, which seems contradictory (R3). What baseline is being used?
  - Line 269 states dictionary elements spanning a feature will have high cosine similarity, but this is not necessarily true; they could have low cosine similarity even if many elements span the feature (R3).
  - Figure 1 labeling: what does “Monday” indicate—that it is the next token in the corresponding texts? This should be clarified (R3).
  - In Section 5, why are new probes trained on specific texts instead of using the circular representations identified by SAE clustering? This decision is not justified (R3).
  - The causal intervention experiments (Section 5) may not conclusively demonstrate “causal implication” of the circular feature, as alternative pathways could affect both the feature and the output, washing away causation (R1 second-to-last question).
  - The claim that “these dictionary elements will have high cosine similarity” (line 269) seems to depend on the SAE being large enough and the feature being active in the model (R1).
- **Clustering method hard to follow:** The clustering algorithm could benefit from pseudocode to improve clarity (R4).
