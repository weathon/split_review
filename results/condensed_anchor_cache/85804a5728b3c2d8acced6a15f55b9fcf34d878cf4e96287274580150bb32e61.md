## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes INS, a data synthesis method for offline multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) using diffusion models with sparse attention for inter-agent interactions, a bit action module for discrete/continuous action spaces, and a value-based selection mechanism to prioritize high-value transitions. Experiments are conducted on MPE and SMAC environments. Reviewers generally agree that the paper is clearly written, addresses an important problem (data scarcity in MARL), and that the experiments are thorough. However, there are substantial concerns about novelty, limited comparisons, overclaiming of being the "first", unclear handling of global states, and insufficient analysis of computational efficiency, dataset validity, and significance of results. One reviewer (score 8) is notably more positive about the high-level contribution, while the other three (score 6) consider the novelty low and the results only incremental.

### Strengths

- Well-written, easy to follow, with clear motivation and contributions (R1, R3, R4).
- Experiments are complete and promising (R1); carefully designed and provide strong support (R3).
- Addresses a key challenge: data scarcity in offline MARL and difficulty of modeling multi-agent interactions (R1).
- Considers both dataset diversity and high-value transitions, which is valuable (R2).
- First attempt to apply data synthesis to MARL (R3 – positive perspective; however, see Weaknesses for counterclaim by R2).
- Incremental improvements (Bit Action, Sparse Attention, Value-Based Selection) are well-motivated and predictably effective; ablation studies are detailed with figures and tables that explain the reasons behind gains (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty:** R1 states the solution "simply applies a diffusion model with additional attention mechanisms across agents", lacking novelty. R4 agrees that INS is essentially an improved version of SynthER and that sparse attention and value-based selection are "neither difficult to conceive nor novel". R2 adds that sparsemax (used for sparse attention) has already been applied in MARL [3,4] and that the formulation (Equations 3–6) overlaps with [4]; the authors should acknowledge prior use and clarify differences.
- **Overclaim of being first:** R2 strongly disagrees with the claim on Line 087 that "INS is the first data synthesis approach for offline MARL", because the authors themselves mention prior work [1] on Line 118, and R2 identifies an additional prior paper [2]. R2 requests an accurate representation of contribution relative to prior art. (R3 conversely calls it the _first attempt_ as a strength; this discrepancy should be addressed.)
- **No implicit guidance for trajectory quality:** R1 notes that the diffusion model objective only ensures trajectories are realistic; there is no mechanism to guide generation toward high accumulated reward.
- **Scalability:** R1 asks whether the approach scales to a large number of agents.
- **Bit Action orthogonality:** R2 questions whether non-orthogonal bit actions can cause ambiguity in the action space.
- **Selection proportion parameter:** R2 states the parameter lacks flexibility and requires tuning per task.
- **Limited synthetic method comparison:** R2 criticizes that only SynthER is compared; should include both single-agent methods from related work and multi-agent methods [1,2].
- **Motivation clarity (partial observability, non-stationarity):** R3 finds the motivation somewhat unclear – while agent interactions are highlighted, other challenges like partial observability and environmental non-stationarity are not explicitly addressed.
- **Modeling agent interactions simplistic:** R3 suggests that relying solely on an attention mechanism may be overly simplistic; more sophisticated agent relationship modeling might improve effectiveness.
- **Generation of global states:** R3 points out that the generated data includes joint actions and observations but not the global state, and asks how this data can be applied to value decomposition methods (e.g., QMIX) that require global state information.
- **Consistency with environment dynamics:** R3 questions how the method ensures generated data conforms to the environment's dynamics and whether invalid transitions could harm pre-training; recommends theoretical analysis or experimental results.
- **Computational efficiency:** R3 raises concerns about efficiency of using diffusion models in MARL and requests experimental data or analysis.
- **Significance of experimental results:** R4 finds some results unconvincing – e.g., in SMAC-8m-good the mean improvement is only +0.1 with a standard deviation of ~1.0. R4 also suggests including online RL efficiency experiments (as done for SynthER) to better showcase INS's capabilities.
