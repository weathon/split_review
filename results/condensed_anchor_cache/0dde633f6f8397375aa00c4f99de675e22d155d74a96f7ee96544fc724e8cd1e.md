## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces ImageNet-OOD, a curated OOD evaluation dataset for ImageNet-1K that minimizes covariate shift in order to isolate semantic shift. Starting from ImageNet-21K, the authors apply four filtering steps (removing hypernyms/hyponyms, hyponyms of “Organism,” semantically-grounded covariate shifts, and a final manual visual/semantic ambiguity check) to obtain 31,807 images from 637 classes. They evaluate seven post-hoc OOD detectors across 13 architectures and report that (1) modern detectors are significantly more sensitive to covariate shift than to semantic shift, (2) on ImageNet-OOD they provide <1% AUROC improvement over the MSP baseline for new-class detection, and (3) MSP still outperforms them for failure detection. The paper concludes that the benefits of recent algorithms largely stem from separating incorrect ID examples rather than improved semantic shift detection.

### Strengths

- **Dataset contribution.** ImageNet-OOD is a carefully curated, large-scale benchmark for semantic-shift OOD detection that minimizes covariate shift. The multi-step filtering process (including manual human inspection) is rigorous and addressed limitations of prior datasets such as ImageNet-C (covariate shift only), Species, OpenImage-O, SSB, and NINCO. [R1, R3, R4]
- **Comprehensive experimental analysis.** The study includes 7 OOD detection algorithms and 13 network architectures, covering both new-class detection and failure detection scenarios. The evaluations provide a unified assessment. [R1, R3, R4]
- **Important negative result.** The finding that modern OOD detectors offer little practical benefit over the simple maximum softmax probability (MSP) baseline on a clean semantic-shift dataset is valuable for calibrating community expectations. [R2, R3]
- **Insight into covariate shift sensitivity.** Demonstrating quantitatively that detectors are much more sensitive to covariate than semantic shift, and that this bias is already present in randomly-initialized networks (Sec. 4.2), is an insightful analysis. [R1, R4]

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of method contribution.** The paper focuses solely on dataset and analysis; it does not propose any new OOD detection algorithm or improvement. While the dataset is useful, the lack of a method limits the depth and impact. [R2]
- **Missing comparisons with latest methods and hyperparameter discussion.** The evaluation does not include the most recent approaches from CVPR 2023 and beyond. Additionally, many detectors are sensitive to hyperparameters; the paper does not discuss how hyperparameters were tuned or adapted for ImageNet-OOD. [R2]  
- **Observations are not entirely novel.** The conclusions that OOD detectors are more sensitive to covariate shift and that gains on semantic shift are limited have already been reported in concurrent work (e.g., OpenOOD v1.5, which evaluates both semantic- and covariate-shifted samples together). This prior work should be cited and discussed. [R4]  
- **Missing analysis of dataset distances.** The claim that covariate shift is more easily detected than semantic shift remains unfounded without a direct analysis of dataset-level distances between ID and OOD sets (e.g., using optimal transport distances as in Alvarez-Melis & Fusi 2020). Such an analysis could separate model/method bias from inherent statistical differences. [R1 (Q1)]  
- **Curation steps need further justification and automation potential.** Several curation choices are insufficiently validated:  
  - Step 1 (“All ImageNet-1K classes, their hypernyms, and hyponyms”) may be redundant after Step 3 (“semantically-grounded covariate shifts”). A rationale for the ordering is missing. [R1 (Q2)]  
  - The example of ‘viola’/‘violin’ ambiguity (Figure 1) should not survive Step 3; an explanation is needed. [R1 (Q3)]  
  - The final manual check could be automated using CLIP embeddings to detect visual/semantic confusions, making the curation pipeline reproducible and scalable. [R1 (Q4)]  
  - The threshold for removing visually similar classes (Figure 2) is chosen manually; an automatic selection method would improve objectivity. [R1 (Q5)]  
  - The number of human inspectors and time required for the final review of 31,807 images are not reported, raising questions about thoroughness and scalability. [R4]  
- **Randomized model analysis is limited.** Sec. 4.2 tests only a restricted set of covariate shifts (image blur, noise, etc.). Covariate shifts can also arise from background changes (e.g., city vs. forest), which likely interact differently with the inductive bias of ResNet-50. The conclusion that untrained models are biased toward covariate shift may not generalize. [R1 (Q6.1)] Moreover, the same analysis was not performed for semantic shifts, so the claim of greater sensitivity to covariate shift in random models is incompletely supported. [R1 (Q6.2)]  
- **Heavy focus on covariate shift may undervalue semantic shift detection.** While the paper criticises methods for being sensitive to covariate shift, many applications (e.g., open-set recognition) require detecting semantic shift even in the presence of covariate shift. The framing implicitly de-emphasises this goal. [R3]  
- **Missing explicit discussion of subpopulation shift.** The “animal/vehicle” example in Figure 1 is a case of subpopulation shift (a specific form of covariate shift). Relating OOD detection to this widely-studied concept would improve clarity. [R4]  
- **Writing and organization.** The high-level conclusions could be stated more clearly in the introduction and abstract. The paper would benefit from additional analysis on what characteristics of examples lead detectors to confuse covariate and semantic shifts. [R3]
