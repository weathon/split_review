## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes that qualitative changes in LLM outputs with sampling temperature can be analyzed as phase transitions drawn from statistical physics. Using GPT-2 small, the authors map generated texts (conditioned only on BOS token) into POS tag sequences, focusing primarily on the PROPN tag. They study three features: correlation between PROPN tags at a given interval, power spectra of POS sequences, and time evolution of the PROPN tag probability. They conclude a phase transition occurs around T=1.0, with low temperatures producing repetitive structures and high temperatures producing incomprehensible text, and they claim critical behavior at T≈1.0 is similar to natural languages.

### Strengths
* The motivation of applying a well-studied concept from physics (phase transitions) to understand LLMs is nice and fosters an interesting angle of investigation.
* The work provides a quantitative comparison of human- and machine-generated language, which is more objective than typical qualitative comparisons.
* The statistics introduced (correlation, power spectra, time evolution) may have value for future work studying structural features of LLM generations.
* The paper identifies a phase-like change in multiple metrics (correlation between tags and power spectra), and the experiments are well designed and clearly documented according to some reviewers.
* Writing and organization are clear, and figures are clearly explained (Reviewer 2; contrast with Reviewer 1 who found the paper difficult to follow).

### Weaknesses
* **Narrow scope and small model:** The paper only tests GPT-2 small (~100M parameters), not modern LLMs, which limits relevance for current models (Reviewers 1, 3). All main analyses focus only on the PROPN tag, with other POS tags and models relegated to the appendix (Reviewer 2). The work only varies the externally set temperature parameter, not internal model attributes, making the exploration narrow and somewhat contrived (Reviewer 1).
* **Lack of clear singularity at T=1.0:** Despite claiming a phase transition, the empirical evidence does not convincingly show singular behavior at T=1.0 (Reviewer 2). In Figure 3(A), the peak in integrated correlation occurs at T=1.1, not T=1.0, and values neighboring T=1.0 (from 0.9 to 1.2) all behave qualitatively similarly. This raises concern that the paper merely shows structurally different generations for small vs. large T, which is almost trivially true because temperature directly controls prediction peakiness (low T gives redundant sequences, high T gives degenerate outputs, especially for GPT-2 era models) (Reviewer 2). In Figure 5, v(t) appears to reach its limiting value immediately for T=1.0, contradicting the paper's claim of slow convergence (Reviewer 2).
* **Unsurprising/tautological findings:** The result that low temperature leads to repetitive loops and high temperature to random text is well known (e.g., https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.09751) (Reviewer 4). The finding that GPT-2 is closest to natural text at T=1.0 is unsurprising because the model is explicitly trained to minimize loss at temperature 1.0; the paper does not address this tautology or how the observed phase transition relates to the training objective (Reviewer 4).
* **Unclear implications and novelty:** It is not clear how important the findings are for the community. The claimed “meaningful analogy between LLMs and natural phenomena” is not well explained, and practical insights for understanding or building LLMs are missing (Reviewers 1, 2). Related work from 2023 (Wolfram community post) also studies phase transition at T=1.0, lessening novelty—the paper does not clearly distinguish its contributions (Reviewer 2).
* **Presentation and methodological issues:** The paper is generally difficult to follow (Reviewer 1). Terminology is used without definition or context (e.g., “long-range correlation”, “changes with a singularity”, “critical phenomenon”), and the implications of critical properties are unclear. The motivation for studying POS tag correlation is not intuitive—why should a PROPN tag at time t be highly correlated with one 100 tokens later? The meaning of such correlations for actual generated text is not explained (Reviewer 4). Several specific claims are questionable or unclear: the plateau value in Figure 2 does not increase monotonically with position t (Reviewer 2, L.240); the prefactor decay trend is unreliable given large error bars for t=0, t=63, t=225 but not for t=3, t=15 (Reviewer 2); the interpretation of Equation (1) needs clarification, especially the second term's role and why only 18×17/2 tag pairs are considered (Reviewer 2); what it means for a spectrum to be “featureless” is not defined (Reviewer 2).
* **Potential validity concern:** Causality appears to be broken in the analysis, and it is unclear whether this invalidates the results. The paper's justification that causality violation “shouldn't matter” is unconvincing, and the analogy to next-character prediction is inaccurate because character-level LM can succeed (Reviewer 1).
* **Unaddressed questions:** Several reviewer questions overlap with missing experiments or explanations, including: what concrete correlations in natural language follow a power law (Reviewer 1); what a concrete example of a critical phenomenon in language would be (Reviewer 1); what the high-temperature phase looks like qualitatively and what it tells us about human language (Reviewer 1); and how this understanding of phase transition could impact future LLM work (Reviewer 3).
