## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces the MM-R³ benchmark to evaluate accuracy and consistency of MLLMs under three tasks: question rephrasing, image restyling, and context reasoning. The benchmark uses over 13.3K testing examples from multiple source datasets. The authors analyze several open and proprietary MLLMs (including GPT-4, Gemini) and find that consistency does not always correlate with accuracy. They also propose an adapter module to improve consistency, showing absolute improvements of 5.7% and 12.5% on BLIP-2 and LLaVa 1.5M. Reviewers agree the topic is interesting and the experiments are intensive, but concerns about novelty, practicality of tasks, fairness of adapter evaluation, missing analyses (pretraining, error types, overhead, downstream impact), and dataset quality control are raised. One reviewer (score 3) is strongly negative, citing lack of novelty, impractical tasks, and flawed comparisons; the other three (scores 6) are positive but identify multiple weaknesses.

### Strengths
- The problem of consistency in MLLMs is well-motivated and underexplored in existing benchmarks (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- The MM-R³ benchmark is a valuable contribution that provides a systematic evaluation framework (Reviewer 4).
- The paper is well-organized, clearly written, and easy to follow (Reviewers 2,3,4).
- Experiments are intensive, covering a variety of open and proprietary MLLMs such as GPT-4, Gemini, BLIP-2, LLaVa 1.5M (Reviewers 2,3).
- The finding that consistency does not necessarily align with accuracy is interesting and non-trivial (Reviewer 3).
- The proposed adapter module shows clear improvements in consistency (and slight accuracy gains) for BLIP-2 and LLaVa 1.5M (Reviewers 2,3,4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: The paper largely confirms already known behavior that small input changes can alter output and that training on shifted data reduces variability. Extensive prior work on robustness to input shifts in CNNs/Transformers (e.g., [A][B][C][E]), language sensitivity [F][G][H], and joint models [I] is not sufficiently differentiated. The proposed adapter is not novel compared to existing methods like adversarial training, data augmentation, or ensemble methods; a thorough comparison is missing (Reviewers 1,4; minority Reviewer 1 is especially critical, stating “yet another empirical evaluation” and recommending rejection).
- **Impracticality of image restyling and context reasoning tasks**: The image restyling task uses exotic styles (Candy Mosaic, Udnie) that are unrealistic; only grayscale is plausible in real-world conditions. The context reasoning task (guessing occluded objects) is ill-posed with many plausible answers, and existing benchmarks [J][K] already assess context reasoning. The practical utility of these tasks for real-world MLLM applications is unclear (Reviewer 1, minority).
- **Flawed adapter evaluation**: Table 6 compares the fine-tuned adapter (Adapt.) against the untrained baseline (Ori.) in a zero-shot manner, which is unfair. A fair comparison would retrain the original model without the adapter, test different adapter architectures, and verify performance on standard benchmarks (e.g., VQAv2). Without this, the adapter cannot be presented as a contribution (Reviewers 1,3,4; minority Reviewer 1 calls the empirical validation “flawed”).
- **Limited adapter evaluation**: Only two models (BLIP-2 and LLaVa 1.5M) were used to evaluate the adapter; its effectiveness on other models is unclear. Even on these models, improved consistency for image restyling remains low compared to other models (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing analysis of pretraining and architecture**: The paper does not analyze how different pretraining objectives (e.g., masked language modeling, contrastive learning) or architectural choices (depth/width of encoders) affect consistency and why certain models are more prone to inconsistency (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing error analysis**: No detailed categorization of inconsistency types (semantic, numerical, contextual) or analysis of specific failure modes. It is unclear how the adapter addresses such failures (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing analysis of overhead and downstream impact**: No quantification of adapter overhead (parameters, memory, inference time) or assessment of whether the adapter degrades performance on standard benchmarks beyond MM-R³ (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- **Dataset quality control insufficient**: Only 100 random question-rephrasing pairs and 100 images (≈0.002% of 87K examples) were manually inspected. While 92% and 86% were semantically equivalent, the sample is too small to guarantee dataset quality (Reviewer 1).
- **Results overwhelming and lack of summary metric**: The three tasks and four metrics are presented piecemeal; a single proxy or average metric would help interpret overall performance (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing discussion of stochasticity**: The evaluation measures inconsistency due to input variations, but the role of model stochasticity (randomness in generation) is not separated or addressed by the adapter (Reviewer 2).
- **Presentation issues**: Figures 2, 3, and embedded figures are too small to view clearly (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of comparison with prior consistency works**: The paper does not sufficiently discuss or compare with existing works studying consistency in VQA/MLLMs (Reviewer 3).
- **Questions about dataset construction and evaluation**: Why different source datasets were chosen instead of, e.g., applying image re-styling to MSCOCO or InfographicsVQA? What exactly is the evaluation procedure in line 246 — is correctness based on substring matching of ground truth? (Reviewer 1)
