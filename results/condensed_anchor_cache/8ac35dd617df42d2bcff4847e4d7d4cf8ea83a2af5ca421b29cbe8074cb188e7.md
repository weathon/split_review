## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes HCTrack, a hybrid contrastive transformer for visual tracking that combines contrastive learning with a transformer architecture to improve target-background discrimination. It includes a hybrid feature interaction module (semantic self-association and cross-layer semantic association) for multi-level template–search region exchange, a redundant information pruning (RIP) module to eliminate background noise, and an improved InfoNCE loss. Experiments are conducted on GOT-10k and TrackingNet, with ablation studies on the proposed components. One reviewer (Rating 6) was significantly more positive than the other three (Ratings 3,3,3).

### Strengths
- The motivation to apply contrastive learning to transformer-based trackers for better target–background differentiation is sound and relevant.
- The proposed hybrid feature interaction (self- and cross-association) is validated by ablation studies; the cross-layer attention architecture is well documented in the appendix.
- A new improved InfoNCE contrastive loss is introduced.
- HCTrack achieves high inference speed compared to most baselines, demonstrating efficiency.
- The paper is well-structured, with clear figures and a well-written related works section.
- Comprehensive evaluations include parameter, architecture, and strategy analysis.
- The work is early in exploring contrastive learning for transformer trackers, and any findings could drive future advancements.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty is limited**: contrastive learning has already been used in vision transformers and in single-object tracking separately; the cross-attention module appears straightforward (concatenating features and applying attention) without deep insight into why it works specifically for tracking. The overall method feels like a reconstruction of attention layers for feature fusion.
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation**:
  - Only two datasets are tested (GOT-10k and TrackingNet); important benchmarks like LaSOT, UAV, or challenge-focused evaluations (occlusion, fast motion) are missing. Notably, the LaSOT training split is used, but no test results are reported.
  - Performance on GOT-10k is not state-of-the-art according to the official leaderboard; improvements over selected baselines are marginal. On TrackingNet, results are poor compared to many other trackers.
  - Only a few recent trackers are compared; reviewers explicitly asked for comparisons with MixFormer, ARTrack, SeqTrack, and temporal trackers like TrDimp and TCTrack (the latter are also missing from the related work).
  - No ablation is provided for the improved InfoNCE against the original InfoNCE (Oord et al., 2018), nor is there an ablation over different transformer architectures to show generalizability.
  - The ablation study (Table 2) shows that both the CSA and RIP modules yield only slight gains in AO and SR0.5, and actually decrease SR0.75; the paper does not explain this drop.
  - No qualitative comparisons are given alongside quantitative results.
- **Missing analysis**:
  - There is no exploration of how the method scales with larger models or higher resolution inputs; the argument that CL improves speed does not compensate for lower accuracy on TrackingNet.
  - The choice to build a new tracker (HCTrack) rather than integrating the contrastive module into an existing tracker is not justified; this remains a reviewer question.
- **Presentation issues**:
  - Variable names in figures are not explained (e.g., “S” used both for “search frame image” and “feature separation operation”), causing confusion.
  - Hyper-parameter settings in Section 4.1 are not described.
  - The paper does not meet academic presentation standards in places (e.g., inconsistent notation).
