## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Sparse Training, a technique to reduce the quadratic complexity of Transformer training for long sequences. It splits input into a memory part (distant tokens) and a target part (recent tokens), samples memory tokens with a decaying probability while preserving positions, and performs next-token prediction only on the target part. Reviews acknowledge the interesting and intuitive approach to an important problem (long-context extension), but raise major concerns about insufficient and potentially inappropriate evaluation, lack of comparison to existing methods (e.g., YaRN, NTK, window attention, POSÉ, randomized PEs), limited metrics (perplexity and next-word prediction accuracy are considered unreliable), unclear motivation for sparsity, and training efficiency claims that are not clearly superior. One reviewer (score 5) is more positive, highlighting the architecture-invariant plug-and-play nature and code release, but still notes several of the same weaknesses.

### Strengths
*   Addresses a key challenge: extending the context window of LLMs for long-context modeling. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
*   The proposed method is intuitive and clearly presented, with both textual and figure explanations. (R1, R2, R3)
*   Original approach: moving sparsity from the architecture to the input, making it a plug-and-play training technique that is architecture-invariant and can be easily integrated into standard LLM training pipelines. (R1, R4)
*   Experiments demonstrate that the method can extend a model (e.g., LLaMA-2-7B) from a 4k context length to support longer contexts (up to 64k) and that training time complexity remains constant as context length increases. (R2, R4)
*   The method roughly maintains pre-trained LLM performance on shorter sequences (perplexity) and is computationally cheap during training. (R2, R4)
*   Code is provided, aiding reproducibility. (R4)

### Weaknesses
*   **Insufficient baseline comparisons:** All reviewers note the lack of comparison to existing long-context extension methods. The paper only compares to the vanilla pre-trained LLaMA (which cannot extrapolate). Critical missing baselines include: YaRN, LongQLoRA, NTK-aware scaling, window attention, randomized positional encodings [Ruoss et al.], positional skip-wise training [Zhu et al.], and other context pruning methods (e.g., Anagnostidis et al.). (R1, R2, R3, R4)  
    *   Some baselines (e.g., NTK scaling) can be applied without training, offering stronger baselines than the vanilla approach. (R2)  
    *   The paper also claims orthogonality to previous methods but provides no experiments to support joint utilization. (R1)
*   **Limited and potentially flawed evaluation metrics:**
    *   The paper primarily reports perplexity and “LongBench accuracy” (next-word prediction accuracy on LongBench datasets) rather than standard LongBench task scores. Recent work shows perplexity is unreliable for long-context modeling. (R1, R3)
    *   The reported PassageCount score of 0.5 vs. a prior SOTA of 0.05 seems implausibly high, suggesting metric misinterpretation. (R1)
    *   No results on standard LM benchmarks (MMLU, HellaSwag, ARC-Easy/Hard, Winograd) to verify that pre-trained performance is maintained. (R2)
    *   No evaluation on long-context generation (only comprehension). (R2)
    *   Missing evaluations on needle-in-a-haystack or passkey retrieval tasks. (R4)
*   **Confusing motivation for sparsity:** The paper claims that distant tokens contribute little (low attention scores) and that the method samples them uniformly with a distance-decaying probability. However, modeling long-range dependencies is crucial, and the sparse sampling strategy may overlook important tokens. (R1, R3)  
    *   It is unclear how the method guarantees that important tokens are sampled if attention is sparse. (R3)  
    *   This makes the technique similar to existing random-sampling approaches [Ruoss, Zhu] without clear novelty. (R3)  
    *   The claim that sparsity always holds (even for code domains) is questionable. (R2)
*   **Training efficiency claims not clearly superior:**
    *   Sparse training requires 30k fine-tuning steps (16h on 8 H100 GPUs), while YaRN uses only ~0.4k steps (~20h on 8 A100 GPUs). The efficiency advantage is therefore not obvious. (R1, R3)
    *   The method uses PEFT (LoRA on K and Q), but no ablation is provided to assess its importance. (R3)
    *   No comparison of training time to other long-context fine-tuning methods. (R1)
*   **Inference still quadratic:** The proposed training-time technique does not reduce inference complexity. No results are provided when similar sampling is applied during inference. (R2, R4)  
    *   The method may also be inherently limited to the maximum input length seen during fine-tuning (unlike positional interpolation methods like YaRN that can extrapolate further). (R4)
*   **Unclear and incomplete ablation study (mixed training):**
    *   The ablation on mixed training (to prevent forgetting on regular sequences) only reports long-context results (16k–64k) and omits results for short sequences (4k and below). (R2)
    *   Why omitting mixed training significantly impacts long-context performance is not explained; the effect should mostly appear on short sequences. (R2)
    *   Results for the “without mixed training” setting are only in the Appendix; they should be in the same table. (R2)
    *   Missing ablation on the hyperparameter β in Equation (3). (R1)
*   **Unnecessary lemmas:** Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2 are trivial, lack mathematical contribution, and are not used in any subsequent theorem or proof. (R1, R3, R4)
*   **Limited model scope:** Experiments are only on LLaMA-2-7B. The method’s motivation uses GPT-2 and Mistral-7B attention patterns, but these models are not fine-tuned, raising concerns about generalizability. (R4)  
    *   No results from training from scratch (even on a smaller dataset). (R4)
*   **Minor issues:** Inconsistent formatting (e.g., \(L_{sample}\) vs \(X_{target}\)). (R1)  
    *   Typo on line 429. (R2)  
    *   Table 4 is redundant and could be merged into Table 2. (R2)  
    *   Explanation of \(\alpha_{(i)}\) and \(S_k\) (lines 146-148) is vague. (R2)  
    *   Figure 4 has unexplained diagonal artifacts; white strips in Figures 2 and 4 are not sufficiently explained (should diminish over thousands of samples). (R2, R3)
