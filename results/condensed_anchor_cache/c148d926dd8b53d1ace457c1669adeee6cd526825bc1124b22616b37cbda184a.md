## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MOKD (Maximizing Optimized Kernel Dependence) for cross-domain few-shot classification. It replaces the NCC loss with a bi-level optimization based on Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC): first optimize kernel parameters via test-power maximization (TPM) to obtain opt-HSIC, then maximize within-class dependence and minimize between-class dependence. Experiments on Meta-Dataset show improved performance over state-of-the-art methods, especially on unseen domains, with better clustered prototypes and no significant increase in running time. One reviewer (score 5) argues the method is a near-identical extension of SSL-HSIC (Li et al., 2021b) with only the test-power addition, limiting novelty, and identifies technical errors. Another reviewer (score 8) finds the work well-motivated, theoretically grounded, and empirically solid, but requests clarifications on several details. Two reviewers (score 6) note the method's sensitivity to parameter γ and the small gain on the “Trained on All Datasets” setting.

### Strengths
- Built on solid theoretical foundations: HSIC and kernel dependence, with a theoretical connection to NCC derived via Theorem 1, 3, 4 and an upper-bound analysis of the NCC loss (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Introduction of test-power maximization (TPM) for kernel parameter optimization is novel, and ablation studies confirm its effectiveness (R1, R3).
- Achieves state-of-the-art results on Meta-Dataset, with particularly impressive gains on unseen domains (R1, R3, R4).
- Produces better clustered prototypes (R3) and is well-motivated by empirical observations of high inter-class similarities in NCC (R4).
- Running time does not increase significantly compared to the URL baseline (R3).
- Cross-domain few-shot classification is an important and timely problem, especially in the context of foundation models (R4).
- HSIC is rarely used in few-shot learning, making this an interesting exploration (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concern**: The method is very similar to SSL-HSIC (Li et al., 2021b); the only difference is the test-power concept. This limits novelty, and the reviewer suspects shared authorship (R1).
- **Technical errors**: In Eq. (1), the likelihood function should use `exp(-d(z,c_c))`, not the stated kernel form; Li et al. (2021a) use Mahalanobis distance, not cosine. The term resembles a Gaussian kernel without a width parameter. These need correction and would affect the proofs (R1).
- **Not-new theoretical claims**: The arguments under Theorem 1 are not new—they exist in the original NCC paper and in Li et al. (2021b) (R1).
- **Missing optimization details**: The constrained optimization problem (5) is not fully explained. The first constraint is handled by width selection; the second constraint’s enforcement is unclear. More information is needed (R1).
- **Missing comparisons**: Why is the method compared to URL but not directly to SSL-HSIC and NCC? (R1)
- **Hypersphere feature space**: Using cosine similarity in the likelihood reduces the method to bias-less cross-entropy on normalized features, constraining features to a hypersphere. This is problematic for open-set recognition (R1).
- **Sensitive to γ parameter**: MOKD is very sensitive to the hyperparameter γ (Figure 8 in appendix); this sensitivity is not discussed in the main paper—only benefits of the HSIC term are mentioned. The reviewers request elaboration on how γ is set for different datasets (R2).
- **Small gain on one setting**: Performance improvement on the “Trained on All Datasets” setting is small (R3).
- **Relevance to cross-domain not clear**: The connection of learning NCC via HSIC to the cross-domain problem is not directly explained (R3).
- **Questions about test power**: HSIC is always a valid statistic; what exactly does maximizing test power add? What is the meaning of test power in this context? (R4)
- **Questions about assumptions**: What implicit assumptions (e.g., meta-distribution or distribution-closeness) does the paper adopt for few-shot learning? (R4)
- **Figure 1 observation**: Intra-class similarity appears smaller in the MOKD case than in NCC; please explain (R4).
- **Confidence intervals**: How are the 95% confidence intervals in the tables obtained? Details are needed (R4).
- **Figure formatting**: In Figure 4, the number 4.3 surpasses the figure boundary and should be revised (R4).
- **Classification details**: How is classification performed on the test dataset? Is it the same as NCC? Is TPM also applied on the test dataset? (R3)
