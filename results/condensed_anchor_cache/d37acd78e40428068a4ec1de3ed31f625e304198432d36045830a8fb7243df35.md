## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Refine-PPI, a framework for predicting binding free energy change upon mutation (ΔΔG). It introduces two main contributions: (1) a masked mutation modeling (MMM) task that hallucinates mutant protein structures from wild-type structures, and (2) a probabilistic density cloud (PDC) representation with a new message-passing network (PDC-Net) that models uncertainty in atomic positions. Evaluated on the SKEMPI benchmark, Refine-PPI shows slight improvement over prior methods. Reviewer scores are 3, 6, and 5, indicating sharp disagreement: one reviewer found the framework interesting but performance too marginal and reproducibility lacking (score 3); a second found the contributions novel and evaluation comprehensive (score 6); a third found the ideas interesting but mathematically flawed and over-claimed (score 5). All three express moderate confidence (4/5).

### Strengths
- The general framework of Refine-PPI is considered interesting, though one reviewer noted that performance improvements are only marginal.
- The PDC representation and the new message-passing network (PDC-Net) are appreciated as a novel attempt to introduce physical inductive biases (uncertainty modeling) into point cloud networks. The use of both mean and variance in messages is highlighted.
- The MMM task is seen as sensible and well-suited for the data-poor task of mutation stability prediction.
- The evaluation setup is comprehensive, with relevant baselines (e.g., RDE-Net, ESM-1v, B-factors) and the standard SKEMPI benchmark.
- The clarity of exposition is above average for ICLR submissions; each contribution is well-motivated and contextualized.

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal performance improvement**: The model outperforms previous state-of-the-art (RDE-Net) only on a subset of metrics, and the overall improvement is slight. No variance or confidence intervals are reported. Time complexity is not studied. The statistical significance per complex is not assessed.
- **Missing structure prediction evaluation**: The first stage (mutant structure hallucination) is not quantitatively evaluated. The paper does not report the RMSD between predicted and ground-truth mutant structures, nor visualizations of the hallucinated structures to support the method’s validity.
- **Mathematical issues with the PDC module** (raised by one reviewer):
  - The assumption that all atom positions are independent is questionable, as thermal fluctuations and epistemic uncertainty of neighboring atoms are certainly correlated (e.g., covalent bonds and non-covalent interactions).
  - The variance update in Eq. 7 is not correctly derived. Even under independence, the correct update should include the mean update term and use the same function φ_μ, not a separate φ_σ. The current formulation does not propagate variance correctly through message passing.
  - Consequently, the learned σ² cannot be claimed to model atomic uncertainty; it is more accurately described as a latent variable “loosely inspired” by uncertainty modeling. The paper should either provide a rigorous proof or revise the claim.
- **Equivariance claim unproven**: The statement that PDC-Net maintains equivariance is neither proven nor argued in sufficient detail. One reviewer specifically requested a proof.
- **Over-claiming and misleading terminology**:
  - Eq. 1 claims to “recover the structural distribution” of masked residues, but the training objective is MSE (Eq. 3), which does not imply distributional learning.
  - The claim that MMM “encourages graph manifold learning with the denoising objective” is not elaborated or demonstrated.
  - The term “thermodynamics” is used repeatedly to mean “uncertainty”; both reviewers 1 and 3 found this misuse confusing. The former should be reserved for physically meaningful quantities (energy, entropy, free energy).
  - The claim that “particles in the interface have smaller variation” is not backed quantitatively; visual inspection is insufficient.
  - The statement that “a small error of wide-type structure reconstruction leads to a more accurate ΔΔG prediction” (Figure 4B) is not supported by the figure as presented.
- **Reproducibility and missing details**:
  - The precise architectures of modules h_ρ, f_θ, g_τ, and the φ functions in Eq. 5–7 are not specified.
  - The initialization and modeling of σ (or Σ) are unclear. In Eq. 4 it is a matrix; in Eq. 7 it appears as a vector. If initialized as zero, it may remain zero. The method’s SE(3) invariance is compromised if σ is a vector (implied ellipsoid with standard axis).
  - Data splits and the exact training/evaluation procedure are insufficiently described. The paper states a 3-fold cross-validation but does not specify which complexes are in each split. Since all benchmark performances are “directly copied” from a preprint, the equivalence of evaluation schemes must be justified.
  - Baseline setups for ESM-1v, B-factors, etc., are not spelled out, leaving room for ambiguity.
- **Methodological concerns about the MMM task**:
  - Because there are no gradients from the ΔΔG task to the structure refinement module f_θ, MMM is effectively pretraining, not an auxiliary training task. The paper does not justify why MMM is not trained on the entire PDB.
  - The same encoding module h_ρ is shared between the ΔΔG predictor and the structure refinement module; it is not explained why f_θ cannot carry its own encoder.
  - In the ablation study, Model 1 uses the RDE-Net backbone instead of the Refine-PPI modules (h_ρ, etc.), making the ablation confounded.
- **Lack of deep component analysis**: Aside from a general ablation, the paper lacks detailed analysis of each component’s contribution, such as the effect of the PDC representation vs. standard EGNN, or the impact of the MMM pretraining on different complex types.
- **Minor issues**: Broken link to Figure 3.2 where PDC visualization is promised; inconsistent use of Σ vs. σ; several typos (“Wide type”, “paradiagm”, “disucssion”, “intergrate”, “envision”); the term “probability density cloud” suggests a more expressive parameterization than Gaussian uncertainty (one reviewer suggested renaming); Table 1’s classification of “pretraining” is unclear (Refine-PPI and ESM are labeled no-pretraining, but B-factors are labeled pretraining).
