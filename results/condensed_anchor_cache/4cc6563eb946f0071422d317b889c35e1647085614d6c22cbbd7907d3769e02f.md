## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Geo-3DGS to address multi-view geometry inconsistency in 3D Gaussian Splatting for both rendering and surface reconstruction. It introduces multi-view photometric consistency regularization to constrain rendered depth and uses this to guide densification of Gaussians. A neural SDF field is added to represent continuous surfaces, with a geometry consistency-based SDF learning strategy leveraging multi-view cues from 3DGS. The method is evaluated on DTU, Tanks and Temples, and other datasets, showing improved surface reconstruction while preserving rendering quality and efficiency.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and Reproducibility**: The paper is well-written, and the math is easy to follow. Code will be released upon acceptance, supporting reproducibility.
- **Significance of Problem**: Extracting high-quality 3D surfaces from 3DGS without harming rendering quality is a relevant and timely problem.
- **Investigates Inconsistency**: The work reveals depth inconsistencies in 3DGS across multiple views and introduces regularization to enforce multi-view consistency, which is a clear analysis.
- **Tailored Contributions**: The regularizations (photometric consistency and SDF consistency) are adapted for the 3DGS pipeline—e.g., photometric loss used not just as regularization but for densification, and SDF used to promote geometric consistency rather than as the primary representation.
- **Good Experimental Coverage**: Evaluated on multiple standard benchmarks (DTU, Tanks and Temples) against existing state-of-the-art methods.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty Concerns**: The core ideas—multi-view photometric and geometric consistency—are well-established in MVS and NeRF literature (e.g., COLMAP MVS, GeoNeus). Applying them to 3DGS is seen as incremental. Similar combinations of 3DGS and neural SDF have already been proposed by GSDF, NeuSG, and 3DGSR; the differences are minor (e.g., using GeoNeus-style constraints). The paper lacks a detailed comparison and discussion of advantages over these concurrent works, especially GSDF.
- **Limited Improvements Over Baselines**: On DTU (Table 1), the proposed method does not compare to GeoNeus directly. On Tanks and Temples (Table 2), reconstruction quality (F1) is notably lower than Neuralangelo (e.g., 34.08 vs. 53.15 for the full set). Even in rendering (Table 3), Geo-3DGS underperforms 3DGS and Scaffold-3DGS on PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS. The ablation study (Table 4) shows that the most novel component—multi-view photometric consistency regularization—improves the F1 score by only 0.02, casting doubt on its importance. The method appears to trade rendering quality for geometric accuracy, which is hard to justify as a major contribution.
- **Missing Comparisons and Analyses**: 
  - No direct comparison with GeoNeus on DTU, despite sharing a similar regularization approach.
  - Neuralangelo results on DTU are not reported; this would clarify the performance gap.
  - No analysis of why Neuralangelo (which also uses hash grids) achieves much better surface reconstruction despite similar architecture components—e.g., why convergence time is 24h for Neuralangelo vs. 48m for Geo-3DGS. A time vs. chamfer distance plot is needed.
  - The purpose and effect of Equation 11 (presumably a regularization term) are not ablated, and its impact on reconstruction accuracy is unclear.
- **Insufficient Ablation and Parameter Analysis**:
  - The densification strategy uses downsampling to control the number of Gaussians, but the effect of the downsampling rate on performance and computational cost is not analyzed. 
  - The paper does not discuss how the balance between new Gaussian primitives derived from depth maps and existing Gaussians is achieved.
  - Reviewer 4 notes that since SDF depth maps are more accurate, using them for densification (as in GSDF) might be better—the authors should justify their design.
- **Typography and Presentation Issues**: 
  - In Tables 1 and 2, the timing numbers for baselines are completely incorrect (e.g., unrealistic values).
  - Figure 5 is cluttered with too many lines, making it unclear.
  - Typos exist (e.g., Line 261 mislabels Tab. 1 and Tab. 2).
- **Missing Demo**: No supplementary demos or videos were submitted.
- **Technical Questions Raised as Weaknesses**:
  - **Multi-view photometric consistency**: How is the base pixel chosen? Why use pixel/patch-based projection instead of the entire rendered depth map? What is the performance difference between these strategies?
  - **SDF architecture and initialization**: What type of SDF network is used (e.g., hash-encoding NeuS)? Has different initialization been tried (e.g., solving Poisson from Gaussians)? What are the training iterations for the SDF branch?
  - **Training time discrepancy**: Why is training time 48 minutes on Tanks and Temples (larger scenes) vs. 45 minutes on DTU (smaller scenes)? The reviewer expected Tanks and Temples to be more challenging.
- **Textureless & Small-Overlap Scenarios**: The multi-view projection still cannot mitigate issues in textureless or small-overlap regions. The example in Figure 1 is not a truly textureless scene (e.g., white background), so the claim is not well-supported.
