## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CORESET-PFEDBAYES, a personalized federated learning method that uses Bayesian coresets to select representative data points from each client’s dataset, aiming to reduce training burden while maintaining accuracy. The authors modify the coreset objective for the personalized FL setting (eq.9), provide theoretical convergence guarantees (minimax optimal generalization error with bounds \(\mathcal{O}(n_k^{-\frac{2\beta}{2\beta+\Lambda}} \log^{2\delta'}(n_k))\) and closed-form deviation from vanilla FL), and conduct experiments on benchmark and medical datasets comparing with various FL algorithms and sampling strategies.

### Strengths
- **Innovation and motivation**: Incorporating Bayesian coreset optimization into federated learning is new and well-motivated (Reviewer1, Reviewer2, Reviewer3). There is limited prior work on coresets for FL, making the paper of interest to the community (Reviewer1).
- **Novel objective for personalized FL**: The paper presents new considerations in defining the coreset objective (eq.9) specifically for personalized federated learning, differing from common coreset definitions (Reviewer2).
- **Theoretical guarantees**: The paper provides solid theoretical results including minimax convergence rates and generalization error bounds (Reviewer1, Reviewer3). Reviewer1 did not check proofs but statements appear sound; Reviewer3 finds them solid. *Note: Reviewer2 notes that the overall architecture and theoretical analysis methods are largely derived from previous work, but still acknowledges the theoretical results.*
- **Empirical evaluation**: Experiments on multiple benchmark and medical datasets show significant gains over random sampling and compare against diverse baselines including other submodular optimization based subset selection methods (Reviewer1, Reviewer3). The method performs well in most cases (Reviewer1).
- **Writing (mixed views)**: Reviewer1 finds the paper well written overall. However, Reviewer2 considers the content bloated and notation messy (see Weaknesses). This disagreement on presentation is noted.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and contribution depth**:
  - The challenge of incorporating Bayesian coresets into FL is not clearly articulated; the algorithm and proof techniques are heavily inspired from Zhang 2022b (Reviewer1).
  - The overall architecture and theoretical analysis largely derive from prior work (Reviewer2).
  - The paper introduces a modified objective (eq.9) but provides insufficient new optimization techniques; most techniques are borrowed (Reviewer2).
  - The advantages of the modified objective (eq.9) are not adequately explained—specifically, the intuition behind adding the distance term between \(\hat{q}^i(\theta,w)\) and \(\hat{q}^i(\theta)\) is not persuasive: if a small-loss coreset exists, the term seems unnecessary; if no such coreset exists, the method may not work well (Reviewer2). Experiments demonstrating the effectiveness of the new objective would strengthen the argument (Reviewer2).
- **Experiments: missing convergence and communication cost analysis**:
  - The empirical part lacks comparison of convergence speed (number of communication rounds). Communication cost is a major FL concern; only final accuracy with 50% subsample size is reported (Reviewer3). (Reviewer2 also asks for theoretical and practical quantification of communication complexity reduction.)
  - The subsample size is 50%, which is quite large; computational time benefits over full data are not demonstrated except in Figure 3 (Reviewer1).
  - There is no empirical runtime result for coreset weight calculation (Reviewer3).
- **Presentation, notation, and typos**:
  - The paper has many minor grammatical errors and typos (Reviewer1, Reviewer2, Reviewer3).
  - Notation is messy: confusion of \(n\) and \(N\) (e.g., \(n\) in Fig1 should be \(N\)); bold subscript variables incorrectly typed; missing equation references; incomplete first sentence in Section 3.2; inconsistent \(\hat{}\) notation on \(q^i(\theta,w)\) (Reviewer2, Reviewer3).
  - Section 3.2 and Section 4 could be condensed to simplify formulaic aspects (Reviewer2).
- **Clarity of algorithm and definitions**:
  - Algorithm 1 is hard to follow; a high-level description of how the coreset is constructed would help (Reviewer1).
  - The definition of \(\hat{\pi}\) in Eq. (3) and (4) is unclear (Reviewer3).
  - The replacement of prior \(\pi\) by \(\mathbf{z}\) from eq.1 to eq.6 needs clarification (Reviewer1).
  - It is unclear whether the modifications of eq.6 consist of using weighted likelihood and replacing prior with global distribution (Reviewer2).
  - In section 3.2, it should be \( g_j = \mathcal{P}_\theta(\mathcal{D}_j^i) = E_{\theta\sim \hat{\pi}} P_\theta(\mathcal{D}_j^i) \) (Reviewer2).
- **Missing practical guidelines**:
  - No strategy is provided for choosing the coreset size \(k\) (the budget) in practice (Reviewer2).
  - The performance under different sample complexities (not only 50%) is not explored (Reviewer3).
  - The question of how expensive coreset sample/weight calculation is remains unanswered (Reviewer3).
- **Reviewer confidence and disagreement**: Reviewer1 and Reviewer2 are fairly confident (3), while Reviewer3 is less confident (2) yet gave a higher score. This disagreement is noted. The paper's overall assessment ranges from weak accept to strong accept (ratings 5,6,8). Reviewer1 would raise score after doubts are cleared.
