## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces Neuro-Symbolic Predicates (NSPs), a first-order abstraction language combining symbolic and neural representations for high-level planning in robotics. The authors propose an online algorithm that invents predicates from environment interactions without demonstrations, leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to ground predicates in perceptual data and integrating them into a framework that interleaves predicate proposal, validation, and goal-driven exploration. The approach is evaluated across five simulated robotic domains, comparing against hierarchical RL (e.g., MAPLE), VLM planning, and symbolic predicate invention methods. Results show better sample efficiency, stronger out-of-distribution generalization, and improved interpretability. Two reviewers give scores of 8 (one with confidence 5, one with confidence 3), while a third gives a score of 6 (confidence 2), raising concerns about clarity, VLM robustness, and time complexity.

### Strengths

- **Novel approach**: Effectively integrates symbolic reasoning with neural perceptual representations via NSPs, using VLMs to invent predicates in a task-relevant manner—a clear advantage over static symbolic approaches.
- **Online predicate invention**: Invention from environment interactions without demonstrations, valuable for real-world applications where demonstrations are expensive or infeasible.
- **Integration of VLMs**: Enables handling rich visual and logical representations, grounding predicates in perceptual data for more flexible and stronger abstractions.
- **Strong empirical results**: Outperforms baselines across five domains in terms of sample efficiency, out-of-distribution generalization, and planning efficiency (Figures 4, 5). Comprehensive evaluation includes comparisons with multiple baselines and ablation studies.
- **Interpretability**: NSPs provide interpretable outputs, aiding understanding and debugging, crucial for safety-critical applications.
- **Well-structured presentation**: Clear explanation with good coverage of literature; methodology incrementally builds task-relevant abstractions, improving sample efficiency.
- **Fundamental problem**: Learning symbolic predicates is a foundational issue for high-level planning; the proposed flexible approach grounds predicates on image observations and natural language.

### Weaknesses

- **Reliance on VLMs**: Scalability to complex/noisy real-world settings is questionable. VLM accuracy can degrade with domain shifts or challenging visual inputs, and the paper does not discuss how VLM misinterpretations (e.g., misidentifying an object or its state) are handled, potentially causing cascading errors in planning (R1, R3; both reviewers 1 and 3).
- **Sensitivity to VLM inaccuracies**: The VLM-based predicate functions can produce wrong grounding even after the techniques described (line 178–189). How do such errors affect downstream operator learning and planning? (R2; Reviewer 2). The approach is sensitive to inaccuracies or biases in VLM outputs, and no mitigation strategy is discussed (R1).
- **Assumption of pre-existing skills**: The method assumes access to well-defined low-level motor skills and object-centric state representations, which are not always available in practice. Could the approach be extended to learn or adapt these skills jointly with high-level predicates? (R1). Additionally, the paper lacks details on how low-level skills are realized in experiments (e.g., via motion planning?) and how MAPLE baseline learns them (R2).
- **Clarity of explanation**: The process of conditioning on the previous action (line 178) is unclear: how is the action encoded and used to influence predicate proposal/validation? The validation process for proposed predicates also lacks detail—criteria for accepting/rejecting a predicate (thresholds, metrics) need explicit definition (R3).
- **Oracle baseline anomaly**: In Figure 4, the oracle baseline fails to achieve 100% success rate in Blocks and Coffee domains, and is slightly worse than the proposed method in Blocks. This is surprising since Blocks is a well-known classical domain solvable with few predicates and operators (R2; Reviewer 2, minority critical observation).
- **Time complexity and scalability**: As an online method, computational efficiency is critical for real-time robotics. The paper lacks a detailed analysis of how computational cost scales with number of objects, predicates, or environment complexity. The supplementary results do not provide a clear breakdown of time spent on VLM queries vs. symbolic reasoning, and do not convincingly demonstrate scalability in time-sensitive or resource-constrained settings (R3; Reviewer 3, more critical).
- **Ensuring robust predicate set**: How does the framework ensure a robust and diverse set of predicates while maintaining task relevance? Is there a systematic mechanism to leverage failures and refine future planning decisions, rather than simply discarding infeasible options? (R3; questions overlapping with missing analysis).
