## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SSR, a framework that uses only 16 navigation-guided sparse tokens to represent the scene for end-to-end trajectory prediction, eliminating all supervised perception sub-tasks (detection, mapping, motion). It applies a TokenLearner for BEV feature compression, injects the navigation command via cross-attention, and introduces a BEV world model (BWM) that self-supervises by predicting future BEV features. On nuScenes, SSR achieves a 27.2% relative L2 error reduction and 51.6% collision rate reduction over UniAD with 10.9× faster inference and 13× faster training. It also outperforms VAD-Base on the CARLA Town05 Long benchmark. The authors argue that discarding explicit supervision lets computational resources focus on navigation-relevant scene content.

### Strengths
- Novel use of the TokenLearner to compress dense BEV features into a sparse set of learned queries, achieving SOTA trajectory prediction without any intermediate perception signals.
- Comprehensive ablations show the individual contributions of the navigation command cross-attention and the future prediction auxiliary task; visualizations confirm that attention focuses on relevant agents/objects.
- Well-written with a clear, motivating hypothesis that questions the necessity of multi-head designs in end-to-end autonomous driving.
- The navigation-guided concept is human-inspired and provides a solid theoretical motivation; the ablation study and analysis encourage deeper thinking about model design.
- Significant experimental gains over strong baselines on nuScenes, coupled with very fast inference and training speed on 3090 GPUs.
- Eliminating intermediate tasks directly reduces latency and annotation requirements, as noted by multiple reviewers.

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of closed-loop evaluation and nuScenes simplicity** – A major concern (especially Reviewer 5) that the nuScenes open-loop benchmark is easy to fit: AD-MLP (no sensor inputs) and BEVPlanner (no scene queries) can already perform well. The paper cites BEVPlanner but does not compare against it in Table 1. Reviewer 5 states the open-loop results are insufficient to support the claim that no middle supervision is needed and gives a reject rating, but would increase if closed-loop results (e.g., CARLA) and ablations were provided. Reviewer 2 also references “Rethinking the Open-Loop Evaluation” and notes that Table 2(b) shows more scene queries leads to worse performance, raising concerns about overfitting when trajectories are simple.
2. **Missing theoretical analysis** – Reviewer 2 notes the paper lacks analysis of convergence properties or an information bottleneck explanation for why the sparse queries improve performance. The behavior in Table 2(b) (more tokens → worse results) demands explanation.
3. **Insufficient architectural and preprocessing details** – Reviewer 3 points out missing details: the specific layers, activation functions, and parameter counts for the image backbone, BEV encoder, TokenLearner adaptation, planning decoder, and BWM module. The encoding of high-level navigation commands (open-set or closed-set, number of commands, preprocessing) and the transformation of ground-truth trajectories into commands are not described, hindering reproducibility. Reviewer 1 also asks for the channel dimension of the scene queries.
4. **Questionable naming and novelty of the “world model” (BWM)** – Reviewer 2 argues that the BWM predicts only the next BEV feature with no reconstruction loss or other tasks, so calling it a "world model" is misleading; the representation may be specialized for trajectory prediction and not generalize. Reviewer 4 adds that the STL and BWM modules lack novelty, and the core contribution is the introduction of navigation information – a common industry practice – which may not be sufficiently novel for ICLR.
5. **Fixed token count and scalability concerns** – Reviewer 1 questions whether 16 scene queries can handle extremely dense scenes; the number seems surprisingly small and results may be skewed by the average difficulty of nuScenes. Reviewer 2 further notes that increasing queries hurts performance (Table 2b), indicating a potential overfitting or limitation.
6. **Missing perception task verification in Table 4** – Reviewer 1 notes that when a perception module is added, no results for the perception tasks themselves are provided, so it is impossible to verify whether those tasks were trained correctly or diverged.
7. **No analysis of failure cases** – Reviewer 3 requests failure cases, especially those where the predicted trajectory is opposite to the navigation command, and questions how the model handles unseen or ambiguous commands. This makes it hard to understand the method’s limitations.
8. **Figure presentation issues** – Reviewer 1 points out that Figures 7 and 8 lack an explanation of the colormap. Reviewer 4 finds the figures visually unappealing (white-heavy).
