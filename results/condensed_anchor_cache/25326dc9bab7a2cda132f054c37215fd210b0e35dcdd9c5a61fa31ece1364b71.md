## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the metro network expansion problem, formulated as a Markov decision process (MDP) to select regions for new stations, maximizing total origin-destination (OD) flow satisfaction under budget and geometric constraints (e.g., spacing between stations, line straightness). The proposed MetroGNN framework uses a graph neural network (GNN) to encode spatial features and OD flows, an action mask to enforce feasibility, and an attentive policy network to select nodes. Experiments on real-world urban data from Beijing and Changsha claim over 30% improvement in satisfied transportation demands compared to state-of-the-art methods, including traditional optimization and RL baselines. Reviewer assessments vary (scores: 3, 6, 5, 6); the paper is seen as tackling a significant problem but with limited technical novelty and insufficient clarity and rigor in the evaluation.

### Strengths
- The problem of metro network expansion is significant, and the use of an RL+GNN framework is a reasonable approach (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- The proposed method achieves over 30% improvement in OD flow satisfaction compared to SOTA methods, demonstrated on two real-world datasets from Beijing and Changsha (Reviewers 1,2).
- The notion of regions as nodes in a heterogeneous multi-graph overcomes fixed-size grids and captures both spatial proximity and traffic flow patterns (Reviewer 4).
- The paper includes a comprehensive set of baselines (traditional and RL-based) and detailed comparisons; the case study provides convincing explanations (Reviewer 4).
- The ablation study thoroughly examines design choices, strengthening the framework (Reviewer 4).
- The methodology is considered moderately original by applying RL+GNN to a new combinatorial optimization problem; it is of interest to the ICLR community (Reviewer 3).
- While some reviewers criticized the presentation, Reviewer 2 found the paper overall well-written and easy to follow, with clear illustrations (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty**: Multiple reviewers (1, 2, 3) note that the combination of RL and GNN for combinatorial optimization is a common "recipe"; the paper lacks substantial technical innovations beyond applying existing techniques (RL for large search spaces known since 1996; GNN state representations standard; action masks common). Reviewer 4 assessed contribution as 3 (good), indicating disagreement, but the majority see insufficient novelty for a top ML venue (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- **Lack of mathematical precision and clarity**:  
  - The MDP components (state space, action space, transition function) are not formalized mathematically; only described in plain English in Appendix A (Reviewers 1,3,4).  
  - Key equations have unclear or conflicting notations: Eq. 1 does not specify \(i \neq j\); Eq. 3 uses \(\mathcal{N}\) for both the entire node set and the neighborhood; Eq. 6 leaves \(\alpha_{i,j}\) (relevance measure) and indices undefined; Eq. 5 has missing context for \(n_{+-1}\) and \(n_{+-2}\) (which metro line they refer to) (Reviewers 3,4).  
  - The set of input features to the GNN is not fully described in the Method section (only in the ablation section 4.4) (Reviewer 3).  
  - The RL algorithm used, GNN integration, policy network architecture, and training procedure lack sufficient detail (Reviewers 1,4).  
- **Missing experimental rigor and reproducibility**:  
  - Results are reported without aggregated statistics or confidence measures (error bars) across multiple runs; single-run results undermine reliability (Reviewer 3).  
  - Dataset statistics, analysis, and how OD trips are precisely defined and obtained are not provided (Reviewers 2,4).  
  - Implementation and hyper-parameter details of baselines are missing (Reviewer 2).  
  - Scalability is not studied: experiments only on networks up to 60 nodes, while the real Beijing network has ~500 nodes; no runtime comparisons with baselines are given (Reviewer 3).  
  - Construction of the action mask (static vs. dynamic) is not discussed (Reviewer 1).  
  - The analysis in Section 4.5 is tenuous without multiple runs; conclusions could change if stopping at step 60 (Reviewer 3).  
- **Limited realism and scope**:  
  - Only total satisfied OD flow is optimized; other critical factors (social equity, environmental impact, revenues) are ignored, making real-world applicability unclear (Reviewer 2).  
  - The model does not handle dynamic city expansion (new regions, changes in graph structure) and is a simplification (≤60 nodes) (Reviewers 1,3).  
- **Writing quality disagreements**: While Reviewer 2 found the paper well-written and clear, Reviewers 1, 3, and 4 noted inaccuracies, typos, and lack of clarity. Examples include: typos (“generic” → “genetic”, “maksed” → “masked”, “donate” → “denote”), and imprecise phrasing (e.g., “search of NP-hard problem” should be “search of solution space”; “model reduces solution space” mischaracterization) (Reviewers 1,3). Reviewer 1 rated presentation as 1 (poor).  
- **Missing citations**: A related paper on planning spatial networks with Monte Carlo tree search (Darvariu et al., 2023, Proceedings of the Royal Society A) is not cited (Reviewer 3).  
- **Unresolved questions that highlight missing details**:  
  - How is the OD flow for a changed metro network obtained? (Reviewer 2)  
  - How does the agent determine whether a selected region should be an extension of an existing metro line (and which line) or the start of a new line? (Reviewer 4)  
  - In Equation (5), which metro line do \(n_{+-1}\) and \(n_{+-2}\) refer to? (Reviewer 4)
