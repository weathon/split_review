## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes NFTS (Neural Fine-Tuning Search), a hierarchical NAS method for few-shot image classification. Given a pre-trained network, NFTS searches over adapter placements, frozen/fine-tuned layers, and adaptation parameters to find the optimal adaptation strategy for a support set. It limits the number of search paths to control computational cost and prevent overfitting. Experiments on Meta-Dataset and Meta-Album with ResNet and ViT backbones achieve state-of-the-art results. Ablations justify key design choices.

### Strengths
- The motivation for introducing NAS into few-shot learning is strong: instead of manual tuning of freezing/fine-tuning, automatic search is sensible. (Reviewer 2)
- NFTS is empirically effective, achieving a good balance between adaptation and overfitting prevention, with clear evidence that it selects a more optimal architectural combination than previous baselines. (Reviewer 1)
- Large-scale experiments demonstrate strong efficacy across both ResNet and ViT backbones, with significant performance gains on Meta-Dataset. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- Ablation studies justify the total number of search paths and the granularity of options; the analysis shows that the best-searched configuration also performs best on unseen downstream tasks. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- The method discovers interpretable trends in layer-wise adaptation—e.g., consistent adaptation of block 14 and lack of adaptation for block 9 in ResNet, and similar patterns in ViT early/late layers. (Reviewers 2, 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing confidence intervals**: All empirical results lack confidence intervals, making statistical significance unverifiable. This is a major weakness; if any results are not statistically significant, tables and claims must be adjusted. (Reviewer 1)
- **Ablation gap for number of search paths**: The study shows N=3 prevents overfitting, but does not compare with N=2 or N=4, nor does it analyze how performance varies with N across ViT and ResNet. Further insight on setting this hyperparameter is needed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing recent baselines**: Comparisons on Meta-Dataset omit several recent methods (e.g., [1,2,3] cited by Reviewer 1); inclusion would strengthen completeness. (Reviewer 1)
- **Computational cost vs. marginal gain**: The method requires training a supernet and evaluating to find the best subnet, yet achieves less than 1% accuracy gain on Meta-Dataset in the multi-domain setting. The overhead is excessively high relative to the gain, and the paper does not provide a detailed breakdown of computational costs for each stage, making it hard to assess the cost-benefit ratio. (Reviewer 2)
- **Insufficient explanation of discovered architectures**: The consistent adaptation of block 14 and lack of adaptation for block 9 are identified but not explained; deeper analysis of why these patterns emerge is missing. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing parameter counts**: The paper does not specify the structure and number of parameters for NFTS compared to baselines. A table comparing NFTS (e.g., with ResNet-18) on parameter counts would be helpful. (Reviewer 3) – *Note: this overlaps with Reviewer 2's request for architectural detail.*
- **Lack of ablation on two-stage search (Sec. 2.4)**: The method has three options: best path during training, search at test time, and the proposed hybrid. No ablation is provided comparing these variants. (Reviewer 3)
- **Differentiation from prior NAS structures**: What is the most differentiating point from existing NAS approaches? This is not clearly articulated. (Reviewer 3) – *We include this as a weakness because it reflects a missing comparison/explanation.*
