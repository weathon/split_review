## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the Cross-Prompt Attack (CroPA), which generates a single adversarial image that can mislead Vision-Language Models (VLMs) across many different textual prompts. CroPA uses a minimax optimization that jointly learns an image perturbation (in pixel space) and learnable textual prompt perturbations (in embedding space) with opposing objectives: the image perturbation aims to fool the VLM, while the prompt perturbations are optimized to counteract the image perturbation's effect, thereby improving cross-prompt transferability. The method is evaluated on OpenFlamingo-9B, BLIP-2, and InstructBLIP across VQA, classification, and captioning tasks using targeted and non-targeted settings. Reviews are split: two reviewers rate the paper as 8 (high novelty, strong validation), while three rate it as 6 (borderline acceptance, citing limited technical novelty and missing practical evaluations).

### Strengths
- **Novel problem and method:** The study of cross-prompt adversarial transferability is a new perspective not emphasized before. The idea of jointly optimizing pixel-space and text-space perturbations (with opposing objectives) is novel and intuitively sound. (Reviewers 1,2,4; scores 6,8,8; confidence high)
- **Comprehensive experiments:** The evaluation covers multiple VLMs (OpenFlamingo-9B, BLIP-2, InstructBLIP), different tasks (VQA, classification, captioning), and both targeted and non-targeted attacks. Baseline comparisons are strong. (Reviewers 1,4; scores 6,8)
- **Consistent outperformance:** CroPA consistently outperforms baseline attack schemes across all settings. (Reviewers 3,5; scores 6,6)
- **Insightful analysis:** Visualizations of sentence embeddings explain why CroPA achieves better coverage of the prompt embedding space, even when using fewer prompts than baselines. Ablations on update strategies and in-context learning examples are also included. (Reviewer 5; score 6)
- **Well-written and accessible:** The paper is well-structured and easy to follow. (Reviewers 3,4; scores 6,8)
- **Practical significance highlighted:** The paper demonstrates that cross-prompt transferability with a single prompt is very limited, and that multi-prompt baselines converge quickly. CroPA offers a clear improvement. (Reviewer 5; score 6)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty (minority view):** While some reviewers praise the problem’s novelty, others argue the optimization method is general gradient-based updating (similar to adversarial training) and that perturbing prompts to maximize loss then training image perturbations on the worst-case prompts is a standard adversarial-training trick. The technical contribution may be limited. (Reviewer 5, score 6; contrast with Reviewers 2 and 4 who found it novel)
- **Missing black-box evaluation:** The paper assumes white-box access to VLMs. For practical security applications (e.g., preventing unauthorized extraction of personal images) or for assessing generalizability, black-box attacks must be evaluated. No comparison with query-based black-box attacks (e.g., [1]) is provided, making it hard to assess CroPA’s practical advantage over existing baselines. (Reviewers 3,4; scores 6,8)
- **No cross-model or cross-image transferability:** CroPA only demonstrates cross-prompt transferability; it does not transfer across different VLMs or across different clean images. This limits practical applicability and generalization. (Reviewers 1,5; scores 6,6; also noted by Reviewer 1’s questions about ASR of Multi-P across models)
- **Undefined experimental details:** The paper does not explicitly state the optimization algorithm (appears to be projected gradient descent), the norm (presumably L∞), or the perturbation size (ε). Performance with different ε values is not reported. For non-targeted attacks, the optimization in Equation (2) is described as “maximize over token and minimize over image,” which appears contradictory—maximizing the image perturbation to induce misclassification would be expected. More details are needed. (Reviewers 1,5)
- **Unclear evaluation protocol for prompt perturbations:** It is not specified whether the learned prompt perturbations are used only during training or also during evaluation. (Reviewer 1)
- **Semantic quality of learned prompts not analyzed:** The paper only mentions optimizing prompt embeddings but does not provide a qualitative analysis of the resulting textual prompts (e.g., semantic coherence, syntactic structure). Without such analysis, it is unclear whether the learned prompts are meaningful or merely exploiting embedding-space noise. (Reviewer 4)
- **Real-world security impact unclear:** Sample target prompts in Table 1 can disrupt VLM functionality, but it is unclear how they would pose security risks in practice (e.g., whether they can trick the model into giving harmful instructions or contradictory predictions). The attack may be more of a robustness exploration than a credible threat. (Reviewer 3)
- **Scaling and computational concerns:** White-box access required for backpropagation becomes prohibitively expensive for large-scale VLMs (e.g., those with LLaMA-65B or black-box APIs such as GPT4-V). The paper does not address scalability. (Reviewer 4)
- **Potentially redundant improvement mechanism:** One reviewer questions whether CroPA’s improvement over Multi-P stems solely from the increased number of effective prompts via minimax optimization, which could be replicated by simply rephrasing prompts with a language model as data augmentation. (Reviewer 3)
- **Need for defense analysis:** No experiments with straightforward defenses (e.g., input data augmentation) are provided to test whether the adversarial perturbation converges to a stable local minimum and can be easily defeated. (Reviewer 3)
- **Effect of prompt length:** It is unclear how the method performs with longer prompts—whether transferability still holds. (Reviewer 2)
- **Terminology confusion:** The term “prompt perturbation” may be misleading, as it suggests an adversarial attack on prompts, whereas in this work it is used in the opposite direction to encourage better transferability. A more fitting name is recommended. (Reviewer 2)
- **Minor errors:** Typos exist, e.g., “archive” should be “achieve” (conclusion) and “leanable” should be “learnable” (introduction). (Reviewers 1,4)
