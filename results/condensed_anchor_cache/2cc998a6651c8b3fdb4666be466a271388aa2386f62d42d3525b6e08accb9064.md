## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes BAQ (Behavior Adaption Q-Learning), a framework for offline-to-online RL that uses an implicit behavioral model to address distribution shift. BAQ adds two components to existing offline RL algorithms (CQL, IQL): (1) a weighted Q-learning mechanism that gives higher weight to transitions where the action matches a BC policy trained on the offline data, and (2) a priority sampling scheme that replays out-of-distribution transitions more frequently from a combined offline+online replay buffer. Evaluated on D4RL MuJoCo tasks for 30,000 online fine-tuning steps, BAQ shows improvements over some baselines (SO2, SUF) but is compared against a limited set of methods.

### Strengths
- The method is simple, clear, and well described (Reviewers 1,2,3,4). The integration of behavior cloning with dynamic Q-value adjustments is straightforward yet effective (Reviewer 3). The pseudocode and source code are provided (Reviewer 5).
- The approach is a general plug-in that can be combined with various Q-value based offline RL methods (Reviewer 2). Practical implementations for both CQL and IQL are given (Reviewer 2).
- The idea is novel to the best of the reviewers’ knowledge and placed well within existing literature (Reviewer 1).
- Ablation studies help show that both priority sampling and weighted Q-learning are important for BAQ (Reviewer 1).

**Minority positive view:** One reviewer (Reviewer 3, score 5) found the framework clear and simple, and considered the evaluations to show outperformance, though this view is countered by other reviewers’ detailed criticisms.

### Weaknesses
**Experimental scope and completeness**
- Evaluations are limited to MuJoCo environments from D4RL. Harder domains such as AntMaze, Franka-Kitchen, and Adroit are missing (Reviewers 1,5).
- Reported results are only after 30,000 online fine-tuning steps, not after running to completion. This makes direct comparison with methods like Cal-QL (which reaches ~100 on medium tasks) difficult (Reviewer 1,2). Numbers appear significantly lower than established fine-tuning methods run to convergence (Reviewer 1).
- Missing important baselines: Cal-QL, RLPD, BOORL, ReBRAC, Actor-Critic Alignment (Yu & Zhang), and FamO2O are not compared (Reviewers 1,3,4,5). The claim that “advanced methods such as FamO2O are excluded” due to inaccessibility is not a valid excuse given that all methods have access to the offline training dataset (Reviewer 5).
- Confidence intervals are missing from Table 2 and Figures 2 and 3; results are only averaged over 4 seeds, making significance unclear (Reviewers 4,5). All reported figures should include error bars (Reviewer 5).
- BAQ combined with CQL or IQL does not outperform the original CQL in 7 out of 9 tasks (Reviewer 2). Emphasizing the total normalized score is less meaningful when individual task performance is worse (Reviewer 2). The improvement from BC-divergence priority sampling appears limited (only 1–2 points) (Reviewer 5).
- In Figure 2, all methods (including BAQ) suffer a big performance drop during online fine-tuning, contradicting the expectation that fine-tuning should improve asymptotic performance (Reviewer 5). The figure also does not include CQL itself, and the backbone (CQL or IQL) is unclear (Reviewer 2).
- Missing evaluations on harder domains and larger-scale or continuous control tasks (Reviewers 1,5).

**Logic and justification issues**
- The logic of the paper is difficult to follow; it is unclear how BAQ specifically addresses the offline-to-online distribution shift problem (Reviewer 2). The claim in Line 375 (“outperforms all baselines”) and Line 398 (“robust performance from the beginning without additional mechanisms”) appear overstated or incorrect based on results (Reviewer 2).
- Figure 1 is used to claim “stabilizing the learning process,” but it only shows that a BC model (which is directly optimized to minimize prediction error on offline data) achieves lower MSE prediction error on offline data; this is trivial and provides no evidence of stabilization (Reviewer 4).
- No theoretical evidence is provided to support claims of reduced Q-value estimation errors or improved learning efficiency (Reviewer 5). The paper lacks explanation of why the proposed components work (Reviewer 5). The simultaneous use of weighting (which reduces the contribution of OOD updates) and priority sampling (which increases OOD sampling frequency) seems contradictory; the authors should explain how both help together (Reviewer 1).
- The OOD nature discussion in Section 4.1 presents known ideas that are not novel (Reviewer 5). Terminology such as “bootstrap error” is used imprecisely (it refers to error accumulation in the Bellman backup, not just inaccurate Q-value estimates) (Reviewer 5).

**Method and implementation details missing**
- The value of α in Eq. (10) is not specified in text or appendix (appears to be 0.6 from code) (Reviewer 2). hyperparameters k_q and k_ρ are manually set per task without sensitivity analysis or discussion of impact (Reviewer 2).
- Details on how CQL and IQL are fine-tuned online are missing (Reviewer 2). The reviewer asks: does online fine-tuning of CQL/IQL reduce to w(s,a)=1 and ρ_i=1? (Reviewer 2).
- It is unclear how BAQ applies to general offline RL methods beyond CQL and IQL (Reviewer 3).

**Writing and formatting errors**
- Repetition: Line 252 repeats “both offline and online interactions” (Reviewer 2).
- Misuse of \citet vs \citep starting from Line 348 (Reviewer 3).
- Citation errors: Lines 32-35 cite papers that are not about pure offline RL or not the original benchmark sources; some citations (Zhang & Zanette, 2024; Xie et al., 2021) are misattributed to offline RL discussions (Reviewer 4). Two of the three papers cited for benchmarks (Zhao et al., 2023; Rafailov et al., 2023) are actually about offline-to-online RL, not benchmarks (Reviewer 4).

**Additional questions and requests**
- Why is the approach called “implicit behavioral models”? On CQL variants, how is the behavioral model implicit? (Reviewer 5).
- In Figure 1, what is the difference between the “offline model” and the “BC model”? Is the offline model trained with offline RL? (Reviewer 4).
- Does parameter sweeping for baselines exist? (Reviewer 5).
- Would adding priority sampling and weighted Q-learning enhance other methods (e.g., Cal-QL)? (Reviewer 1).
