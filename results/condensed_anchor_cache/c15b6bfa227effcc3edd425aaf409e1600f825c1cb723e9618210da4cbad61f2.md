## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Frequency Information Aggregation Net (FIA-Net), a time-series forecasting model that applies the Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) to input sequences and then aggregates information across STFT windows using two optional backbones: a Window-Mixing MLP (WM-MLP) that processes adjacent windows in the frequency domain, and a Hyper-Complex MLP (HC-MLP) that treats the set of windows as a hyper-complex vector and uses hyper-complex algebra to combine them. The model also uses a top-M frequency selection to reduce dimensionality. Experiments are reported on several benchmarks with MAE and RMSE. The paper claims improved accuracy and efficiency, but the reviewers raise serious concerns about novelty, experimental validation, theoretical grounding, clarity, and reproducibility.

### Strengths
1. The core idea of integrating information across STFT windows (via both adjacent-window mixing and global hyper-complex aggregation) is innovative and a plausible direction for capturing temporal dependencies in the frequency domain. (R1, R4)
2. The HC-MLP employs hyper-complex number operations that can reduce the number of parameters compared to a standard MLP aggregator (up to three times fewer), which is noted as a potential efficiency benefit. (R1)
3. The paper is generally well-structured, with clear descriptions of the methodology, figures/diagrams illustrating architecture and operations, and an appendix with extensive experimental details and ablation studies. (R1)
4. The investigation of deep neural network architectures for frequency-domain time-series processing is a relevant and important research topic. (R2)
5. The selection of the most relevant frequency components (top-M) is a reasonable technique for reducing dimensionality while potentially preserving important spectral information. (R4)
6. The code is made publicly available via anonymous GitHub, aiding reproducibility. (R1)
7. The concept of hyper-complex numbers for this task is novel and theoretically sound. (R3)

### Weaknesses
1. **Marginal performance improvements over simple baselines:** The reported MSE differences between FIA-Net and FreTS (which shares a similar overall structure) are frequently 0.001 or even equal, making the practical significance of the window-mixing contribution questionable. (R1) This small difference is echoed by R3, who asks whether it would pass statistical tests. (R3)
2. **Theoretical contradiction in HC-MLP’s design and results:** The paper claims that HC-MLP aggregates all windows to better handle long-term sequences, but experiments show that HC-MLP is better for shorter prediction horizons, while WM-MLP (which only mixes adjacent windows) is better for longer horizons. This contradiction undermines the theoretical justification for HC-MLP and is not explained. (R1, R4)
3. **Inadequate comparison to simpler alternatives:** The authors do not compare HC-MLP against a straightforward global MLP aggregator that does not use hyper-complex algebra. Without such a baseline, it is unclear whether hyper-complex numbers provide any advantage or are an unnecessary complication. (R4) Similarly, the finding that the real part alone might be sufficient is noted but not explored, which could suggest a simpler architecture and further undermines the need for the hyper-complex formulation. (R4)
4. **Incomplete literature review and missing recent baselines:** The related work does not cover several recent (2024) time-series forecasting methods that employ similar frequency-domain or window-aggregation ideas. (R3, R4) Specific papers mentioned as missing: arXiv 2407.21275v1, 2403.11047v1, and a ScienceDirect paper (2024) that also uses temporal correlations between STFT windows; these omissions undermine the claimed novelty. (R4)
5. **No rigorous analysis of efficiency (model complexity and runtime):** Despite the title claiming “Efficient” forecasting, the paper does not provide wall-clock time, FLOPs, or any experimental efficiency comparison. The only complexity discussion is a short parameter count comparison. (R2, R3)
6. **Lack of statistical rigor:** Results are reported without error bars, confidence intervals, or statistical significance tests, making it impossible to assess whether the (very small) performance differences are robust. (R3, R4)
7. **Insufficient motivation for using STFT over wavelets:** The paper uses STFT without justifying its choice compared to wavelet transforms, which offer better time-frequency localization and have been widely used in similar tasks. (R2) The statement “a recent line of work applied STFT” is inaccurate, as STFT has been used for decades. (R2)
8. **Theoretical foundation is weak:** The paper lacks a deeper explanation of why the proposed methods outperform existing ones, relying heavily on empirical results without providing intuition or algebraic insights that justify the hyper-complex approach specifically for time-series forecasting. (R4)
9. **Numerous presentation and reproducibility issues:**
   - Many grammatical errors, typos, and inconsistencies in terminology: “FeeqShiftNet” appears in the introduction; “novel complex-valued MLP” vs. “two novel MLP architectures”; Table 2 confusingly labels one row “FIA-Net” and the other “OctontionMLP” rather than clarifying which backbone is used; inconsistent hyphenation of “long-term.” (R2, R4)
   - Equations in Section 3.1 have notation problems: variable definitions unclear (e.g., D dimension, t vs. L for length); N_FFT not defined; iSTFT equation unnumbered with undefined variables c and F. (R2)
   - Appendix is referenced but not included in the manuscript file (according to R2). (R2) Later R1 refers to the appendix, so this may be a submission format issue, but it harms readability.
   - Figure 4 (HC-MLP architecture) is hard to interpret because the symbols for different operations are too similar, making it less informative than the corresponding equation. (R1)
   - Figure and table captions are incomplete (e.g., Table 1 lacks details about the evaluation metric; Figure 5 has no punctuation). (R2)
   - Citations appear incorrectly placed (likely LaTeX command issue at end of paragraphs). (R2)
   - The claim of “8% improvement” in Section 5.1 is not substantiated by the reported results, where FIA-Net does not consistently outperform FreTS. (R1)
10. **Missing applications of model components:** It is unclear whether the proposal is designed for consecutive windows, how many consecutive windows are used, and how long-term dependencies are assessed. (R2) Also, the variable “p” for window size is not clearly defined. (R2)
11. **Only MAE and RMSE used for evaluation:** Other metrics (e.g., sMAPE, MASE) that could provide additional insight are not reported. (R2)
12. **Statement about GNNs in Section 2 is incorrect:** GNNs are not an architecture proposed to improve RNNs. (R2)
13. **No discussion of limitations in the conclusion.** (R3)
