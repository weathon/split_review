## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes DIAR, an offline reinforcement learning method that combines a latent diffusion skill model for temporal abstraction with Implicit Q-learning (IQL). It introduces an Adaptive Revaluation (AR) mechanism that compares current and future state values to decide when to re-plan during execution. DIAR is evaluated on three D4RL sparse-reward environments (Maze2D, AntMaze, Kitchen) and, according to the results, outperforms several prior offline RL and diffusion-based RL methods. The reviewers agree that the empirical performance is strong, but they raise significant concerns about limited novelty, insufficient motivation for design choices, writing quality, and the generalizability of the AR assumption. The four reviewers gave scores of 3, 3, 5, and 3, indicating that one reviewer was notably more positive (rating 5) while the majority rated the paper as a weak reject (3).

### Strengths

- DIAR consistently outperforms prior offline RL methods on sparse-reward D4RL tasks, achieving better results in 7 out of 9 tasks (Table 1). This is highlighted by multiple reviewers as a clear empirical contribution.  
- The Adaptive Revaluation mechanism is considered a simple and novel idea by one reviewer (rating 5); it dynamically adjusts decision lengths by comparing current and future learned value estimates, which prevents the policy from moving to worse states and provides flexibility. Another reviewer (rating 3) also praised its simplicity and validity under the goal-conditioned RL assumption.  
- The problem of offline RL in long-horizon, sparse-reward tasks is significant, and DIAR shows superior performance compared to existing approaches on such tasks (reviewer 2).  
- One reviewer (rating 5) found the paper well‑organized and logically structured, breaking down the methodology and implementation clearly. (Other reviewers disagreed on writing quality, which is reflected in Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses

- **Writing and presentation:** The paper is poorly written; the logic is not coherent, many sentences are hard to understand, and the text includes repetitive padding (e.g., Section 4.3 "Theoretical Analysis" is elementary and adds no value). Reviewers 1 and 2 both criticized the clarity and structure.  
- **Limited novelty and unclear contribution:** The method is essentially a combination of existing components (LDCQ and IQL) with the addition of the Adaptive Revaluation mechanism, which is only a simple iterative improvement. The selection of components is not properly justified, and no coherent story links them. One reviewer (rating 5) considered the Adaptive Revaluation novel, but the other three reviewers judged the overall novelty as low and noted that DIAR appears to be the IQL version of LDCQ plus re‑evaluation.  
- **Poorly motivated use of diffusion model:** It is not explained why the latent representation \(z\) from the diffusion model needs to be input during the IQL stage; the motivation for this design choice is lacking (reviewer 2).  
- **Weak and non‑general AR assumption:** The adaptive revaluation relies on the assumption \(V(s_{t+1}) > V(s_t)\), which does not hold in many common settings (e.g., finite-horizon tasks with positive rewards). The theoretical analysis uses this strong assumption without justification, making the method’s theoretical grounding questionable. Consequently, DIAR is restricted to goal‑conditioned tasks with sparse rewards and does not readily generalize to dense‑reward tasks (reviewers 2 and 4).  
- **Mixed empirical effectiveness of AR:** According to Table 2, applying the AR mechanism improves performance in only 5 out of 9 tasks and degrades it in the other 4. No analysis of these failure cases is provided (reviewer 4).  
- **Loose theoretical bound for AR:** The inequality \(V(s_{t+H}) \geq V(s_t)\) used to derive AR is loose; a tighter bound would be \(V(s_{t+H}) \geq \gamma^{-H} V(s_t)\). The choice of bound is not justified (reviewer 4).  
- **Missing ablation studies:** The contributions of the mixed training of Q and V, as well as the weighted training techniques, are not evaluated through ablation. Their effect remains unclear (reviewer 3).  
- **No hyperparameter sensitivity analysis:** Sensitivity to the key hyperparameters \(\tau\) and \(\beta\) is not analyzed (reviewer 3).  
- **Limited evaluation scope:** Experiments are restricted to three D4RL environments (9 datasets). Standard locomotion tasks (HalfCheetah, Walker2d, Hopper) are omitted, and more diverse environments are needed to assess generalizability. Moreover, no comparisons are made with offline goal‑conditioned RL methods (e.g., HIQL, GoFar, SMORe), which are directly relevant given the goal‑conditioned assumption of AR (reviewers 1, 3, and 4).  
- **Questionable justification for overestimation reduction:** The claim that mixed training of Q and V reduces Q‑value overestimation is not convincingly supported, because the latent states generated by the diffusion model are themselves out‑of‑distribution, and no pessimistic mechanism is applied to the value function (reviewer 3).  
- **Minor concern:** The D4RL benchmark is heavily over‑optimized, so small engineering improvements do not provide strong evidence of algorithmic value (reviewer 1).
