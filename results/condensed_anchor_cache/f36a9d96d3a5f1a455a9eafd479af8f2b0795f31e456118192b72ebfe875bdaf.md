## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces StepProof, a method for sentence-level autoformalization of natural-language mathematical proofs by decomposing them into independently verifiable subproofs. Ratings range from 1 to 6 (scores: 3, 3, 1, 6), indicating strong disagreement. One reviewer (Score 6) finds the compositional approach sensible, showing core utility and a promising direction. Another reviewer (Score 1) considers the paper nearly incomprehensible and suspects substantial LLM generation. The remaining reviewers (both Score 3) acknowledge the step-by-step idea but question its novelty, evaluation methodology, and experimental validity.

### Strengths
- **Granular verification approach**: Breaking proofs into smaller subproofs for sentence-level verification is a notable advancement over complete-proof verification, enabling targeted error correction (Reviewer 1, high confidence).
- **Improved success rates and efficiency**: Experimental results (e.g., 6.1% vs 5.3% one-attempt proof pass rate; 27.9% vs 25.3% over DTV baseline; reduction from 64 to 10 attempts) indicate benefits. (Reviewer 1, high confidence; Reviewer 4, moderate confidence notes the improvement is marginal.)
- **Foundation for interactive user experience**: Compositional verification allows step-level feedback and selective retrying, creating a basis for user-assisted proof development. (Reviewer 4)
- **Use of open-source models and baselines**: Experiments with Llama and implementation of baseline systems help accessibility. (Reviewer 4)
- **Application of step-by-step reasoning to autoformalization**: Leveraging the well-known effectiveness of chain-of-thought reasoning for LLMs (Wei et al., 2022) is a sensible extension. (Reviewer 2, notes the idea is known but still positive.)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty and prior work**:
  - Stepwise translation in autoformalization is not new; the DSP approach and LEGO-Prover (Wang et al., ICLR 2024) already decompose and prove sub-goals step-by-step. The paper does not theoretically, empirically, or qualitatively compare with such methods. (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 2; Reviewer 4 similarly notes similar ideas in other domains.)
  - The claimed advantage of selective error correction (retracting only erroneous steps) is not unique; ITPs already support stepwise correction, making the benefit overstated. (Reviewer 1)
- **Overly restrictive decomposition assumption**: Assuming each sentence is an independent, verifiable sub-proposition is limiting. Many proofs have complex dependencies (nested assumptions, indirect reasoning, multi-sentence propositions). StepProof may require significant manual adjustments for compatibility. (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 4 elaborates: the paper admits “simple manual modifications” to tailor proofs, yet provides no details or examples of required changes, making it unclear how “simple” they are.)
- **Ambiguous evaluation methodology**:
  - Incremental verification may count intermediary, potentially misleading subproofs as correct, inflating success rates. (Reviewer 1)
  - Only one dataset (GSM8K) is used; it is too simple and lacks complex logical structures. Datasets like ProofNet or MiniF2F would better test real-world applicability. (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 4 also calls for more datasets.)
  - Reported improvements are marginal (e.g., 6.1% vs 5.3% on one-attempt proof pass rate; 27.9% vs 25.3% over DTV) and may be within experimental variation. (Reviewer 4)
  - The comparison of attempts (10 for StepProof vs 64 for baselines) lacks clarity: Did baselines actually need 64 attempts, or was performance saturated earlier? A plot of accuracy vs. number of attempts would help. (Reviewer 4)
- **Serious presentation and clarity issues**:
  - The paper is nearly impossible to comprehend; one reviewer suspects major LLM generation, and finds the full-proof strategy baffling (Reviewer 3).
  - Many errors: Figure 1 caption says “left” but workflow is on the right; Table 4.2 references should be Table 1 and Table 2; missing conference venues in citations; poor section organization. (Reviewer 1: insufficient methodological details on prompting and output syntax; Reviewer 2: spacing before citations, unexplained abbreviations like “bert”→BERT, “DTV” not defined, missing citation link for “Qinghua et al.”; Reviewer 4: inconsistent definitions of “strategy performance tests” vs “baseline tests”, unclear that Table 1 shows one-attempt rates while Table 2 shows multiple attempts, “comments rate” in Table 2 undefined (100% for StepProof vs 31.3% for DTV).)
- **Missing details and experimental gaps**:
  - No evaluation of interactive features with real users, even though the approach is motivated by user interaction. (Reviewer 4)
  - No comparison of StepProof to Whole-Proof when given the same line-by-line proof states (could Whole-Proof also benefit?). (Reviewer 1)
  - It is unclear if the evaluations are fully automated or involve user interaction. (Reviewer 4)
  - StepProof could perform worse than Full-Proof when decomposition fails, but this is not investigated. (Reviewer 4)
- **Other reviewer questions reflecting weaknesses**:
  - How does StepProof handle non-linear proof structures (nested assumptions, indirect reasoning, reordering)? (Reviewer 1)
  - What is the purpose of “simple fitting” (l351) – does it mean proof tuning is needed? (Reviewer 2)
  - Provide concrete examples of issues in the FULL-PROOF strategy to make the motivation clearer. (Reviewer 2)
