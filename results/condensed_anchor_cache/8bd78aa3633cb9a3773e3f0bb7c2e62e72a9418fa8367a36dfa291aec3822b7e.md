## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a lightweight unsupervised federated learning approach (FSTCBDG) that leverages a pretrained vision-language model (CLIP). Clients train only a linear classifier on a fixed image encoder using self-training with pseudo-labels, and the server generates synthetic features via class-balanced text feature sampling to handle data heterogeneity. Experiments show improvements over CLIP zero-shot and some supervised FL benchmarks with limited overhead. Reviewer 2 (rating 5) was more positive, praising the lightweight design and extensive experiments, while Reviewers 1 and 3 (ratings 3) raised concerns about novelty, insufficient evidence for lightweight claims, and weak baselines.

### Strengths
- Unsupervised federated learning is a relatively unexplored domain (R1).
- The idea is interesting; Figure 1 illustrates it well; the paper is generally well-written and easy to follow (R3).
- Lightweight: only a single linear layer is trained on top of a fixed CLIP encoder (R2, R3).
- A self-training objective is designed for that linear classifier (R2).
- Ablation study shows the two proposed approaches (self-training with pseudo-labels and class-balanced text feature sampling) effectively improve zero-shot accuracy and mitigate class imbalance (R1).
- The proposed method achieves significantly better performance than compared counterparts, especially under heterogeneous data distribution, and requires only a few communication rounds (R3).
- Extensive experiments were conducted to show good performance over baselines (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: Pseudo-label refinement has been explored in self-supervised learning; class-balanced data generation is similar to SMOTE (R1). The Gaussian-based class prototype augmentation was used in prior work PASS (Zhu CVPR 2021), and follow-up work (Zhu CVPR 2022) points out that synthetic data from such augmentation can cause similar classes to overlap (R2). Overall, the two main methods lack novelty (R1).
- **Insufficient evidence for lightweight claim**:
  - The only indication of lightness is the use of a linear classifier, but baseline methods also use the same classifier; a comprehensive analysis of computation and communication costs is missing (R1).
  - The paper mentions savings from reduced communication rounds but does not analyze memory footprint and computational cost on the client side, especially considering the CLIP model size even for its smallest variant (R3).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**:
  - Important unsupervised FL baselines are not discussed or compared: Collaborative Unsupervised Visual Representation Learning from Decentralized Data (ICCV’21), Divergence-aware Federated Self-Supervised Learning (ICLR’22), Orchestra (ICML’22), MoCoSFL (ICLR’23) (R3).
  - Works integrating CLIP into FL are not discussed: FedCLIP, When Foundation Model Meets Federated Learning (R3).
  - The method is not compared with FedUL baseline (R2).
- **Missing experimental details and analysis**:
  - It is unclear whether baselines use the same fixed CLIP encoder or are trained from scratch; model architecture specifics for other methods are not provided (R3).
  - The impact of using different backbone types is not explored (R3).
  - The impact of training for more local epochs per round is not studied (R3).
  - No experiments on large-scale datasets like ImageNet (R1).
  - The paper does not explain why the testing accuracy of baselines drops as communication rounds increase (R2).
- **Minor issues**: typos such as “FedBR (Guo et al., 2023b) FedBR (Guo et al., 2023b)” and “tranfer” (R1).
