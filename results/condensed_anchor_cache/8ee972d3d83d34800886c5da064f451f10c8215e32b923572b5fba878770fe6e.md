## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces IHDiff, the first generative model for learning prior distributions of interacting 3D hands using a denoising diffusion model. The reverse process employs a Transformer network with self- and cross-attention to model inter-hand joint correlations. The method supports three applications: random sampling, conditional random sampling, and fitting to observations (e.g., single-image hand reconstruction). Evaluations on InterHand2.6M and HIC datasets show improved physical plausibility (contact accuracy, collision reduction) over VAE baselines, though vertex error does not reach SOTA.

### Strengths
- First generative model (prior) for interacting hands using a diffusion process (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- Enables multiple applications: unconditional sampling, conditional generation with partial conditions, and post-hoc fitting to image observations (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- Produces physically plausible interactions with significantly reduced collisions and better contact compared to VAE baselines (Reviewers 1,2).
- Collision loss prevents both self- and inter-hand collisions, whereas prior SDF-based losses only handle inter-hand collisions (Reviewer 2).
- Impressive qualitative results and clear quantitative gains over InterWild on the HIC dataset for single-image reconstruction (Reviewer 1).
- Manuscript is exceptionally clear and accessible (Reviewers 2,3).
- Thorough experiments across three settings, including user studies, detailed appendix with problem settings, network configurations, dataset specifics, and baseline comparisons (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- Limited novelty: The method is considered a straightforward application of diffusion with a Transformer architecture that is already common in hand mesh reconstruction (Reviewers 1,2,4). Predicting the original clean sample instead of noise is standard practice (Reviewer 4). The cross-attention between hands is widely used (Reviewers 2,4).
- Insufficient evaluation for single-image two-hand recovery:
  * Why not evaluate on the InterHand2.6M dataset for this task? (Reviewer 1)
  * Missing comparison with Zuo et al. (ICCV 2023), which also proposes an interaction prior for monocular reconstruction (Reviewer 1).
  * Missing comparison with IntagHand (Li et al. CVPR 2022), a strong baseline specifically designed to capture inter-hand interactions via attention (Reviewer 1).
  * Among diffusion-based methods, only the VAE component of MLD is compared; more diffusion baselines are needed (Reviewer 4).
- The method achieves SOTA on contact accuracy but fails to achieve SOTA on vertex error (Reviewer 2; note: this reviewer’s summary claims SOTA vertex error, but the weakness explicitly states otherwise).
- Simple nearest neighbor search outperforms the method in collision ratio, making the reported improvements unconvincing (Reviewer 2).
- DDIM sampling uses 1000 steps, making the fitting process extremely slow while providing only marginal improvement in vertex error (Reviewer 4).
- The methodology for integrating the generative prior with the regressor in an end-to-end manner is ambiguous (Reviewer 3).
- Generalization to varying hand sizes/shapes and unseen data is not explicitly addressed (Reviewers 2,3).
- The practical usefulness of the three applications beyond existing methods is not clearly justified (Reviewer 4: “What makes these three applications useful?”).
- The representation uses a right-hand-relative space; it is unclear whether a left-hand-relative space would also be viable (Reviewer 2).
