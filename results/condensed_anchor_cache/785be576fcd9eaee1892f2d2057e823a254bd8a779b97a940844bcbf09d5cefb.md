## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a topological framework for understanding mode connectivity via parameter space symmetries. For deep linear networks with Euclidean loss, the number of connected components of the minimum set is \(2^{l-1}\) for \(l\) hidden layers (invertible weight matrices). Skip connections reduce this number. Permutations can map minima from different components to each other (demonstrated for two layers). Layer-wise scale symmetries cause linear mode connectivity to fail, but controlling weight norms and curvature yields approximate linear mode connectivity. General symmetry-induced curves are characterized, and curvature bounds give sufficient conditions for approximate linear connectivity.

### Strengths
- Well-written and self-contained; includes sufficient background for non-experts in topology.
- Novel analysis of mode connectivity in deep linear networks; mathematically elementary but insightful.
- Demonstrates that skip connections reduce the number of connected components, aligning with practical ease of optimization.
- Shows that permutations connect components (for two layers), offering a fresh perspective on empirical observations.
- The homeomorphism between parameter space symmetry and the set of zero-loss parameters is innovative and intriguing.
- Provides a clear rationale for residual connections via connected components.
- The framework is powerful and helps understand the loss surface; proofs are clear and concise.
- Contributions on necessary conditions for mode connectivity and approximate linear mode connectivity are valuable.

### Weaknesses
- The number of connected components does not depend on network width, yet permutation symmetries grow exponentially with width. The authors should discuss this limitation (Reviewer 1).
- Section 5.1 would benefit from an explicit example/figure illustrating how permutations lead to mode connectivity (Reviewer 1).
- Scale symmetries causing LMC failure is known; the interesting new point about controlling norms and curvature lacks connection to empirical solutions found by SGD. Specifically, weight decay appears necessary for LMC under permutations — how does this relate to SGD’s implicit bias? (Reviewer 1, question overlapping with missing empirical analysis).
- The meaning of being in the same “connected component” vs. “path-connected” is not intuitive. How pathological are counter‑examples of components that are connected but not path‑connected? Are they measure zero in parameter space? (Reviewer 2)
- The results are global in nature and do not account for the types of minima SGD actually finds. The constructed paths (e.g., Proposition 5.3 and 5.4) may come from degenerate solutions that SGD never reaches due to its implicit bias. The paper should discuss how these global results inform practical optimization (Reviewer 2).
- The term “connected” is overloaded (topologically connected, linearly connected, path connected). Distinct terminology (e.g., “topologically connected” vs. “linearly connected”) would avoid confusion (Reviewer 2).
- One reviewer (score 3) finds that many results are standard mathematical facts restated, do not provide substantial new insights for nonlinear neural networks, and lack practical relevance. The application of group actions beyond trivially obvious cases is not convincingly justified, adding abstraction without clear benefits.
- The paper does not clearly state its own limitations: the homeomorphism technique is tailored to linear regression with invertible weight matrices. This limitation should be announced in the introduction (Reviewer 4).
- Practical implications remain unclear. How does knowing the number of connected components inform architecture design or optimization algorithm choice beyond linear regression? (Reviewer 4)
- Experimental validation is missing: can the valleys predicted by Eq. 7 be confirmed as actual valleys in the 2D loss landscape heatmap? (Reviewer 4, question requesting experiment)
- Can the analysis be extended to realistic models (e.g., ResNet with softmax) using parameter symmetry and homeomorphic mapping? (Reviewer 4, missing extension)
- The paper does not fully clarify how topological concepts (connected components) translate to the loss landscape of neural networks; necessary assumptions (e.g., invertibility of all layers) and their impact on expressiveness are not discussed (Reviewer 5).
- Little exploration of how the number of connected components changes with depth and what elements correspond to orbits/groups; examples would help link the framework to neural network applications (Reviewer 5).
- The concept of curvature is used without a clear definition in this context; the final section could demonstrate empirical curves connecting modes that align with derived formulas (e.g., loss growth) (Reviewer 5).
- Section 5 on symmetries lacks concrete examples of how permutation and scaling symmetries manifest in practical architectures and affect connectivity (Reviewer 5).
- Minor issues: reference to the first connectivity algorithm should be [1] not [2]; “Param” is referenced in Section 3.3 before being formally defined; \citep should be used where appropriate (Reviewer 5).
- Questions that overlap with missing experimental/comparison work: (a) How strictly is the invertibility condition required? Would a one-dimensional output immediately yield negative results as in Section 5.2? (b) Does Proposition 5.3 contradict the layer-wise mode connectivity result of Adilova et al.? (c) Is the proposition about residual connections restricted to one dimension? (Reviewer 5)
