## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes using Sobol's total indices for feature selection instead of Shapley values, claiming advantages in capturing feature interactions, computational efficiency (linear scaling with features), and model-agnosticism. The paper includes theoretical arguments and empirical comparisons on synthetic and real datasets. Reviewers overall find the paper well-written and the topic relevant, but there is significant disagreement. Two reviewers (R1, R3) are positive, highlighting thoroughness and runtime benefits, while three reviewers (R2, R4, R5) are critical, citing lack of novelty, flawed comparisons, missing baselines, and insufficient experimental rigor. The most serious concerns involve the fairness of the Shapley vs Sobol comparison, the omission of efficient Shapley approximations and other feature selection methods, and theoretical issues with equations and claims.

### Strengths
- **Clear exposition**: The diagram (Fig1) together with Sections 1 and 2 provides an intuitive understanding of how the two metrics differ. The paper is well-written with clear explanations and a bolded logical structure that aids readability. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Comprehensive empirical comparison**: The paper provides a number of experiments comparing Sobol indices to Shapley values across synthetic datasets with known correlation structures and real datasets, including runtime studies. This comparison has not been empirically studied in much detail before. (R2, R5)
- The code is annotated and easy to understand. (R1)
- Visualizations (runtime comparisons, accuracy trends) are clear and effective. (R2)
- **Running time advantage** is significant, especially for high-dimensional data. (R3, R4)
- The paper raises a valid point that Sobol indices more accurately capture variance reduction when features are removed compared to Shapley values. (R3)
- Demonstrates the potential effectiveness of Sobol's total index as a feature attribution method, particularly due to its simplicity and computational efficiency. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty and insufficient context**: The paper does not adequately distinguish its contributions from prior work. It is well known that Shapley values can be derived from Sobol indices and that Sobol bounds Shapley; the comparison has been studied theoretically before (e.g., Iooss & Prieur 2019, Vuillod et al 2023). The paper should clearly state what new insights it provides beyond these known relationships. (R2, R5)
- **Unfair and narrow comparison**: The paper compares Shapley values only with Sobol total indices, ignoring many other feature attribution and selection methods. Each method defines importance differently, and usefulness depends on application. A thorough evaluation should include comparisons with other major methods (e.g., AIC, BIC for linear regression, Mean Decrease in Impurity for trees, SAGE, etc.). (R1, R4) Additionally, the chosen Shapley implementation is extremely slow (9111 seconds), while efficient approximations like SAGE and Kernel SHAP exist and are commonly used for high-dimensional data; these should have been included. (R3) The claim that Kernel SHAP and Tree SHAP are model-dependent is incorrect (Kernel SHAP is model-agnostic). (R2)
- **Motivation is unclear**: The paper's stated motivation is high-dimensional feature selection, but Shapley values are rarely used in high dimensions due to computational cost. For low-dimensional data, exact computation (Equation 1) is feasible, making Sobol's advantage less clear. The paper should better justify the scenario where this comparison is meaningful. (R5)
- **Theoretical issues with equations and definitions**:
  - Equation (8) is misleading; it suggests δ = R² or δ = accuracy, but the text seems to refer to differences. (R1, R3)
  - The proof for Inequality (9) is missing. Moreover, the inequality does not hold in practice: from Table 3 (Synthetic Correlated Dataset), Δ(x_i)=0, φ_i=0.25, and S_Ti=0, which violates the claimed inequality. (R5)
  - Equation (10) does not seem to hold based on the definition in Equation (3); the authors may have intended to use S_{rT3} as defined in line 264. (R3)
  - The paragraph on scaling performance loss for regression vs. classification is insufficiently justified and its utility in the paper is unclear. (R2)
- **Experimental design flaws**:
  - The paper should provide a detailed algorithm for how experiments are conducted and how synthetic datasets are generated. (R1)
  - The synthetic datasets (correlation, XOR) only test basic interactions; experiments with higher-order or more complex interactions are needed to support claims about Sobol's interaction-handling capabilities. (R2)
  - None of the real datasets used are high-dimensional, which contradicts the paper's emphasis on high-dimensional feature selection. (R1) For example, the Concrete dataset is known for nonlinear derived features; interaction features should be generated to test the methods properly. (R1)
  - Results on real datasets are not convincing: Figure 3 shows overlapping confidence intervals, and Tables 5–7 lack confidence intervals entirely, making it impossible to assess statistical significance. (R5) Notably, in Table 7, Shapley values begin to outperform Sobol when the number of features reaches 9, suggesting Shapley may have better selection ability in that data. (R3)
  - The discussion on Shapley values on synthetic datasets (lines 351–387) is inappropriate because Shapley values are not designed to estimate performance loss; the obtained value of 0.25 is perfectly fine from that perspective. (R5)
- **Disagreement on the role of efficiency**: The authors argue that the efficiency axiom is not needed for feature selection, but this is highly problem-dependent (e.g., correlated features vs. p>n scenarios). The paper's dismissal of efficiency is not well-justified. (R2)
- **Presentation and writing issues**:
  - The related work section (Section 5) is confusing and deviates from the central discussion; better organization would improve flow. (R3)
  - The comparison table (Table 2) oversimplifies the limitations of Shapley-based methods: it omits that TreeSHAP avoids factorial time complexity and that SAGE is model-agnostic. A more balanced presentation is needed. (R3)
  - Minor notation: use \widehat or \widebar for variables (eq7, line 157–158). (R1)
  - Editing: broken lines at line 378 and line 432; Fig. 4 touches the left border. (R3)
  - The grouping of y by unique x values in the Sobol implementation may be problematic for continuous features; the paper should discuss this issue. (R1)
