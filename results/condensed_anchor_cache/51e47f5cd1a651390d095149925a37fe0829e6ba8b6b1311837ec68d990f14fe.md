## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the Image-Guided Code Patch Framework (IGCP) for patch representation learning in software development, aiming to bridge code analysis and image processing. It presents a measure-theoretic foundation, optimization analysis via Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics, and empirical evaluations on patch description generation. Reviewers largely find the paper to be poorly written, incoherent, and likely generated by an LLM. The methodology is unclear, mathematical definitions are disconnected, experiments are incompletely described, and formatting/technical errors abound. One reviewer (Reviewer 2, score 3) is more positive but has limited domain experience and notes only minor formatting/metric issues, while the three other reviewers (scores 1) strongly criticize the work’s substance and presentation.

### Strengths
- Patch analysis is an important topic where machine learning techniques can be explored (Reviewer 3).
- The authors attempt to propose a method from a very theoretical point of view (Reviewer 3).
- (Reviewer 2, minority more positive) Acknowledges the paper addresses a relevant gap (existing methods specialize in either predictive or generative tasks) and attempts to integrate code analysis with image processing using measure theory.

### Weaknesses
- The writing is very unnatural and appears to be generated with LLMs; the entire paper seems nonsensical in places (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The methodology is ambiguous and lacks coherence. Section 3 reads as a collection of complex mathematical definitions that are disconnected, with no clear explanation of how they contribute to the overall approach (Reviewers 1, 3).
- No clear definition or source of “image” is provided, nor how it relates to software patches; “patch” itself is not well-defined (Reviewer 1).
- The theoretical analysis is weak. For example, Theorem 3.1 claims “If \(k\) is characteristic, then the mapping \(Z \to \mu_Z\) is injective,” which is essentially the definition of a kernel being characteristic, making the assumption too strong to yield a non-trivial result. Similar issues occur elsewhere (Reviewer 3).
- Several passages in the paper are clearly incomplete or missing: lines 351, 355, 365, and 368 have equations or text after colons that are missing (Reviewer 3).
- After Section 3.5, all text (including the entire experimental section) is italicized, making it very difficult to read (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Line 428 states “to evaluate the performance of IPPMF” but “IPPMF” is never defined anywhere in the paper (Reviewer 3).
- The experimental setup is poorly described; metric explanations are difficult to follow (Reviewer 2).
- Figure 1’s purpose and its connection to the core content of the paper are unclear (Reviewer 2).
- No connection is shown between the problem and the mathematical frameworks introduced, nor how metrics like BLEU, ROUGE emerge from the theory (Reviewer 1).
