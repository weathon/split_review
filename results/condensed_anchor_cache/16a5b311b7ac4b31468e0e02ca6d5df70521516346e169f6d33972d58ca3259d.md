## Merged Review

### Summary
FRAPPE is a task-agnostic prompt compression method that removes redundant and non-salient phrases via a multi-stage pipeline (rule-based filtering, embedding-based ranking, TextRank). It is evaluated on summarization, QA, in-context learning, and code completion tasks. Reviews are mixed: two reviewers (score 5) acknowledge the approach is simple, efficient, and connects to unsupervised summarization (e.g. PacSum), while three reviewers (score 3) raise significant concerns about experimental setup, missing baselines, ablation, clarity, and the practical value of the compression rate. There is consensus that the method is promising but insufficiently validated.

### Strengths
- The method is simple, efficient, and unsupervised, providing speed gains over baselines.
- Evaluated across multiple tasks and LLMs with decent performance; compared against several recent approaches.
- The phrase-removal pipeline uses four categories (phatic expressions, filler utterances, connectives, stop words) with neural-network-based similarity, and phrase ranking via a graph-based algorithm (TextRank).
- The context-aware extension addresses information loss relative to the query, adding flexibility.
- Performance on QA tasks is solid at a high compression rate.
- The collected expressions, utterances, and GPT-4 resources could benefit the community if published.
- The method connects to prior unsupervised summarization work (e.g. PacSum), which is interesting.

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and clarity issues**: Variables not introduced properly (e.g., line 218: $\bar{\tau_r}$); toxicity discussion appears abruptly in the introduction; motivation for detoxification experiments is unclear (toxicity is evaluated on compressed context, not on model outputs); the context-aware FRAPPE algorithm is not described in enough detail (lacks equations/pseudocode).
- **Incomplete baselines**: Missing comparisons with ICAE, RECOMP, an LSTM-based simple compression method, selective-Context, and a random-token-removal baseline. The choice of included baselines is not justified.
- **Fixed compression rate**: All experiments use a single compression rate of 70% (i.e., keeping 30% of tokens). No results are reported for other compression rates, making it unclear whether FRAPPE consistently outperforms alternatives across the trade-off spectrum.
- **Low effective compression**: Because FRAPPE only removes phrases (no merging or shortening), its theoretical compression is bounded; removing only 30% of tokens may not compensate for performance loss or the extra overhead of the compression step. The major LLM overhead is generation, not pre-filling, limiting practical gains.
- **Missing ablation studies**: No ablation of the three pipeline steps (3.1, 3.2, 3.3); it is unclear how much each stage contributes to performance/efficiency. The first rule-based step (removing filler words, articles, etc.) could be merged with the centrality step, and its arbitrariness (e.g., removing "but" affects sentiment analysis) is not justified. Removing articles may break math reasoning (e.g., GSM8K: "If each child receives a cookie…").
- **Robustness concerns**: The chunking and removal strategies can break semantically related phrases and alter meaning. The paper lacks a systematic breakdown of performance across question types that are sensitive to articles/prepositions vs. those that are not. Reliance on a fixed set of filler words may not generalize across domains/languages.
- **Toxicity section is problematic**: It is hard to follow, and the claimed toxicity reduction (50%) is likely an artifact of removing extraneous vitriolic phrases; toxicity reduction in generated text depends on LLM safety training, not compression. Selective-Context is missing as a baseline here. (One reviewer does not regard toxicity reduction as a plus.)
- **Time measurement and reporting**: How "Time" is measured is not explained; uncompressed latency is not reported for comparison (e.g., ShareGPT in Table 1). Latency measured via LLM APIs is less informative than runtime on local open-source models; authors should consider local inference.
- **Missing rationale for unexpected results**: In some cases (e.g., Arxiv Articles in Table 1), compressed text outperforms the full context; no explanation is given. (One reviewer questions how compression can improve performance if information is removed.)
- **Hyperparameter reporting**: Values of $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$ per experiment are not given.
- **Minor issues**: Citation formatting needs correction (e.g., line 133: “Pan…” should be \citet); typo in line 92 (“ATo” → “To”); Figure 4 in appendix is low resolution.
- **Missing related work**: RECOMP and arXiv:2310.03025 are relevant and should be discussed.
