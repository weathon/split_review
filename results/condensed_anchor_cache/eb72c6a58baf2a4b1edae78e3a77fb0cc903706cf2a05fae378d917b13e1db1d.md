- Decision: Reject

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a Feature-Aware Federated Learning (FAFL) framework for unsupervised anomaly detection in 5G networks, designed to address data privacy and scalability challenges inherent in distributed 5G infrastructures. The framework uses integrated gradients to compute per-client feature importance, aggregates these importance vectors with differential privacy (DP) noise, and weights model parameters accordingly during aggregation. A Dynamic Feature Importance Adaptation (DFIA) mechanism periodically recalculates importance to adapt to evolving data distributions. Experiments on a 5G testbed (two 5G cores) compare FAFL against FedAvg and FedProx. Reviewers acknowledge the relevance of the 5G scenario and the interesting use of integrated gradients, but raise major concerns about insufficient novelty, overly narrow baselines, missing experimental details, lack of ablation and sensitivity analysis, unclear technical details (especially for feature importance calculation and the aggregation step in Eq.10), improper DP justification (one reviewer argues DP does not protect raw data), weak motivation for feature weighting over learned representations, and writing/structural issues that give the paper a “report-like” appearance.

### Strengths
- The FAFL framework addresses data privacy, scalability, heterogeneity, and dynamic environments in 5G network anomaly detection.
- Applying differential privacy when aggregating cross-client feature importance is a step toward privacy preservation (reviewer 2).
- The paper uses a real 5G testbed with two 5G cores, lending authenticity to the dataset and experiments (reviewer 2).
- Modeling of the 5G scenario is commendable and the attempt to handle heterogeneous features via feature importance is interesting (reviewer 3).
- The use of integrated gradients to estimate client-level feature importance is noted as an interesting starting point (reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty**: The proposed method is a straightforward combination of existing techniques (feature-aware aggregation, integrated gradients, DP, periodic updates) without a clear distinction from prior work. It appears more as a fusion of known components than an original contribution (reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Insufficient baselines**: Only FedAvg and FedProx are compared. The paper omits state-of-the-art FL-based anomaly detection methods and advanced approaches for heterogeneous features in FL. This limits the ability to demonstrate superiority (reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Missing experimental details and analyses**:
  - Experimental settings such as learning rate, dataset size, training rounds, and hyperparameters are not provided (reviewer 1).
  - No ablation study to isolate the contribution of each component (feature importance aggregation, DFIA, DP) (reviewer 1, 3).
  - No parameter sensitivity analysis (reviewer 1).
  - The advantages of the DFIA mechanism (recalculating every T rounds) are not investigated experimentally; one reviewer finds this step trivial and its benefits unexplored (reviewer 4).
  - Privacy attack experiments are missing; the paper claims privacy benefits but does not test whether DP actually protects client raw data (reviewers 2, 4).
- **Contradictions and inconsistencies**:
  - Introducing DP typically reduces accuracy, but the reported results exceed FedAvg without explanation (reviewer 1).
  - The paper assumes non-i.i.d. data (heterogeneity) yet averages feature importance vectors from different clients in Eq.5, which seems counterintuitive – the rationale is unclear (reviewer 3).
  - Equation 10 appears incorrect: since \(\theta_k\) is a vector of model parameters, \(w_k\) should also be a vector; the notation \(\Sigma a_i\) does not make sense in context and makes the method difficult to understand (reviewer 3).
- **Incorrect or insufficient handling of DP**:
  - One reviewer (reviewer 4) argues that adding random noise to feature importance does **not** protect the raw data of clients, and the related experiments are confusing and lack details. The paper does not clarify what DP guarantees are achieved.
- **Lack of motivation**: The paper does not adequately justify why a feature weighting method is needed instead of letting the model learn feature relevance end-to-end. The necessity in specific 5G scenarios is not clarified (reviewer 3).
- **Missing technical details on key steps**:
  - How each client calculates its local feature importance using integrated gradients is not fully explained; in particular, integrated gradients requires a baseline which is not specified – does it come from the global model or other clients? (reviewer 4)
  - Why must each client independently calculate its own importance, and what happens if a client is malicious or dishonest? (reviewer 4)
  - The purpose and benefits of adding noise to feature importance (beyond trivial privacy claims) are not explained (reviewer 4).
- **Writing and presentation quality**:
  - The writing style appears informal and contains expressions suggestive of LLM generation; the language lacks academic polish (reviewer 1, 2).
  - The paper resembles a scientific report rather than a publication; it lacks standardized language, contains writing errors (e.g., line 126), and fails to provide a detailed description of the proposed framework (reviewer 2).
  - The paper does not align well with the conference’s themes of representation learning and deep learning (reviewer 2).
