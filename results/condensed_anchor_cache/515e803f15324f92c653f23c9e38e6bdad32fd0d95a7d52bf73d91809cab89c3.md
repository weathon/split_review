- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary

Reviewers collectively found that the paper proposes replacing the CLIP text encoder with a decoder-only LLM (Llama 3, Mistral-Nemo) by following the LLM2Vec approach (bidirectional attention, masked next-token prediction, contrastive fine-tuning) and then performing CLIP-style training with a frozen LLM and trainable adaptor/projector. The method achieves strong image-text retrieval results, especially on dense captions and Chinese data, and shows potential for improving downstream LMMs (e.g., LLaVA 1.5). However, all four reviewers had major concerns about overclaimed findings, missing evaluations (especially ImageNet zero-shot and pure visual tasks), lack of comparison to LLM2Vec and other embedding baselines, underspecified implementation details, and a writing style that uses marketing language and contains multiple errors. The overall confidence is moderate (scores: 5, 4, 3, 4) and the consensus is that the paper is not yet ready for acceptance.

### Strengths

- **Clear motivation and insight:** The paper convincingly argues that CLIP’s text encoder is a limiting factor, and shows that off-the-shelf LLMs produce poorly discriminative caption embeddings (lack of discriminability) – an insight that motivates the proposed fine‑tuning (Reviewer 1, confidence 5).
- **Novel idea and recipe:** Replacing the CLIP text encoder with a decoder‑only LLM is a novel and interesting direction, and the paper provides a full recipe to achieve strong retrieval results (Reviewer 3, confidence 3; Reviewer 4, confidence 4).
- **Strong retrieval results across benchmarks:** The method achieves top results on multiple English and Chinese image‑text/text‑image retrieval datasets, particularly on tasks with dense captions (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). The cross‑lingual transfer to Chinese, beating Chinese‑specific models, is a highlight (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- **Model‑size analysis:** The paper compares Llama 3 1B, 8B, and Mistral‑Nemo 12B, showing the impact of LLM size (Reviewer 2).
- **Public data and models:** All training uses publicly available data and model weights (Reviewer 2).
- **Downstream integration:** The method improves LLaVA 1.5 on several benchmarks, demonstrating that a better text encoder can benefit other multimodal models (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Main claim not convincingly demonstrated:** The paper claims that LLM2CLIP takes full advantage of an LLM without losing CLIP’s original capabilities. The only downstream evaluation (Table 7) is mixed – only marginal improvements (e.g., VQAv2 79.04 vs 79.68) and the model often does not surpass the baseline (Reviewer 1, confidence 5). This is a critical weakness given that CLIP’s main strengths (zero‑shot classification on ImageNet, visual representation quality) are not tested.
- **Missing ImageNet zero‑shot evaluation:** One of the biggest challenges of replacing the text encoder is preserving zero‑shot top‑1 accuracy on ImageNet. The paper does not report this, though CLIP ViT‑L/14 achieves 75.3% (original paper). This is a severe omission (Reviewer 1, confidence 5).
- **Lack of purely visual representation evaluation:** The claim that LLM knowledge unlocks “richer visual representations” is only tested on retrieval tasks. The paper lacks evaluations on classification and other pure vision benchmarks (e.g., linear probing, fine‑grained recognition) that would validate this claim – especially critical because LLM2CLIP uses a much larger text encoder than competitors (Reviewer 4, confidence 4).
- **No comparison to LLM2Vec or existing embedding models:** The paper does not compare against LLM2Vec‑transformed models (which the method essentially replicates) or off‑the‑shelf embedding models (e.g., from HuggingFace) that could serve as a cheap baseline. Since LLM2Vec is cited, the failure to use it as a baseline weakens the novelty claims (Reviewer 4, confidence 4; also Reviewer 2, confidence 4).
- **First two contributions lack novelty:** Section 3.1 (poor raw LLM retrieval performance is well known) and Section 3.2 (replication of LLM2Vec with different data) do not represent new insights and could be moved to related work (Reviewer 4, confidence 4). The CRA metric is not novel (Reviewer 4).
- **Justification for freezing the LLM is weak:** The claim that large batch sizes are required for effective LLM training is not well‑supported; SigCLIP and others succeed without large batches. LoRA could have been used to fine‑tune the LLM without catastrophic forgetting (Reviewer 4, confidence 4).
- **Overclaimed and marketing language:** The paper uses phrases like “super text encoder”, “transformative leap”, “magical power”, “miracle”, “audacious step”. These are inappropriate for a scientific paper and overstate the results (Reviewer 1, confidence 5; Reviewer 2, confidence 4; Reviewer 3, confidence 3). The claim that Chinese is a “completely unfamiliar language” is misleading because Llama 3 was pretrained with multilingual data (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- **Missing implementation details hinder reproducibility:**
    - The LLM adaptor is described only as “several linear layers” – no depth, width, or activation function (Reviewer 3, confidence 3; Reviewer 4, confidence 4).
    - Training hyperparameters (optimizer, learning rate, schedule, weight decay) are not reported for the CLIP‑style training stage (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
    - LoRA parameters are claimed to follow LLM2Vec, but that paper does not cover Mistral‑Nemo; it is unclear whether parameters for Mistral‑7B were reused (Reviewer 3).
    - Details on how text embeddings are computed before fine‑tuning (last token vs pooled) are missing (Reviewer 4, question 1).
    - The “caption contractive” fine‑tuning step is unclear – “fine‑tuning only the output tokens” needs clarification (Reviewer 4, question 2).
- **Missing ablations:**
    - No ablation of the two‑stage text‑encoder training (masked next‑token prediction vs contrastive text‑only stage) to show the necessity of each stage (Reviewer 2, question 3).
    - No ablation of the MLM objective – would a simple adaptor with next‑token prediction be enough to get good embeddings when fine‑tuned on captions? (Reviewer 2, confidence 4).
    - No ablation to justify the design choices (e.g., why freeze LLM, why use LoRA for initial stages but not final stage) (Reviewer 4).
    - The optimal ratio of standard vs dense captions (50% vs 75%) is not clearly justified; the text says 50–75% is best, but training uses 50% (Reviewer 2, question 1).
- **Presentation and writing quality is poor:**
    - Repeated sentences (e.g., “CLIP is one of the most important…” appears multiple times) (Reviewer 2).
    - Sections are not well‑organized; the paper reads like a chronologic log of discoveries rather than a concise explanation (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
    - Tables are out of order, Table 7 (key results) uses tiny font, and table references are missing (“Table X” on page 9) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
    - Multiple typos: “Mistral Namo” → Mistral‑NeMo; “T5‑V” → E5‑V; “MS‑COCO” not cited correctly; “line 232 sentence unclear” (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
    - Bolding errors in Table 2 (value 9.2 highlighted as highest but appears to be a typo), Table 3 (metrics “@X” unclear – presumably Recall@X), Table 7 (bolding in Seed‑Benchmarks Video) (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
    - Numbers in text (line 387: 51.7/50.6) do not match Table 4 (32.3/39.3) (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
    - Abstract is too long and reads like a generated summary of the full paper (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
    - Figure 1 is hard to interpret due to multiple sub‑images and misaligned caption (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing references and discussion of related work:** Should cite and discuss work on extracting embeddings from decoder‑only LLMs (e.g., “Fine‑Tuning LLaMA for Multi‑Stage Text Retrieval” (SIGIR 2024), “Improving Text Embeddings with Large Language Models” (ACL 2024)) (Reviewer 3). Also missing citation for claim that bidirectional attention improves contextual capture (Reviewer 2).
