## Merged Review

### Summary
This work studies optimization in deep linear networks, specifically the effect of depth on convergence, and proposes a novel algorithm, aligned gradient descent (AGD), that achieves fast convergence even as depth increases. The authors argue that prior negative results on depth are due to specific pitfalls and that depth can be used to speed up convergence in the scalar regression (width-1) setting with un-whitened data.

### Strengths
- The results appear correct (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is clearly written and well organized, and the problem is well introduced (Reviewer 2).
- The authors construct a concrete and solvable example where a specific gradient method (AGD) achieves fast convergence in a deep linear network, notably demonstrating that depth can be an advantage even with un-whitened data (Reviewer 3).
- The intuition behind the speed-up of convergence is well explained, including examples from previous works on acceleration (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Narrow scope and unrealistic assumptions**: The work considers a very narrow problem (deep linear networks, width 1) that Reviewer 1 finds of very little interest, and Reviewer 2 states it does not meet ICLR standards. Section 3.1 and 3.2 describe trivial special-case initializations (near saddle points) where depth plays no role; any initialization sufficiently far from those points would not suffer the described problems (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 notes that the analysis relies on a single, highly contrived example where all layers but one are initialized at 1 and the others at 0, reducing the model to standard linear regression – a measure-zero scenario from which the authors claim depth is not detrimental. This does not imply deeper networks are trainable in practice. Even in this case GD is unstable, so the algorithm is changed (Reviewer 2).
- **Misleading conclusions**: Reviewer 2 states the conclusions are not supported by the toy models provided, and the paper fails to address its central thesis. One reviewer (2) is far more negative, viewing the work as “cooking up an example in which they kill the effect of depth to say that depth there has no effect” and that everywhere else in parameter space depth has an effect.
- **Limitation to width 1**: All three reviewers note the restriction to width 1 (hidden layers). Reviewer 3 explicitly asks: “Why do the authors not generalize the results to networks with general widths? A width of 1 is very restrictive.” It is unclear whether convergence properties extend to wider networks, and practical relevance is limited given modern over-parameterized networks. The width used in the experiments of Section 4.5 is not stated (Reviewer 3). Even the claim that computational increase is limited to 5L results from choosing network width of one, not from the algorithm itself (Reviewer 2).
- **Necessity of AGD not justified**: It is unclear why AGD’s specific adaptive learning rate scaling is crucial. Why wouldn’t Adam or Newton’s method work? (Reviewer 3). More analysis comparing AGD to other methods is needed. The scaling uses $(\Theta_t^{(2:L)})^2$; Reviewer 3 asks whether $p$>0 could be used instead.
- **Terminology issue**: Reviewer 2 points out that describing shallow networks as “standard linear regression” contradicts the literature, where shallow networks are generally recognized as having a single hidden layer.
- **Text redundancy**: The last paragraph of page 1 is repeated with identical wording at line 168 and again in section 5 (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing generalization to non-linear case**: Reviewer 1 notes it remains unclear why AGD (limited to deep linear networks) would be useful or interesting in any non-linear setting. This overlaps with the criticism of limited scope.
