## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a refined exponential solver (RES) for diffusion model sampling, revisiting the probability flow ODE parameterization and deriving order conditions for exponential integrators. All reviewers note that the paper is well-written and shows clear empirical improvements over DPM-Solver++ on ImageNet (e.g., FID improvement from 16.77 to 12.51 at NFE=9, 25.2% reduction in numerical defects). However, one reviewer (confidence 4) argues that the theoretical contribution largely reproduces known results from Hochbruck and Ostermann (2005) without proper attribution, severely limiting novelty. Additional weaknesses include missing comparisons, unclear experiments, stochastic performance not convincing, and various presentation issues.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and well-organized (R1, R3).
- It addresses a significant problem: the choice of numerical solver in diffusion models directly impacts sample efficiency (R3).
- The proposed RES consistently outperforms DPM-Solver++ and Heun on ImageNet unconditional generation, with clear convergence improvements in FID and smoother generated images (R1, R2, R3).
- The scheme is extensible to higher-order integration methods (R2).
- A clear intuition for the improvement is given: existing solvers like DPM-Solver do not satisfy the correct order conditions (R2).
- The empirical margin over benchmarks is clear, and the simple switch from DPM-Solver++ to RES yields 25.4% FID improvement (16.77 vs 12.51) and 25.2% reduction in numerical defects at NFE=9 (R2, abstract).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons and applicability:** No comparison with step-distillation methods or consistency models at the same NFE (R1). The paper does not discuss whether RES can be applied to conditional generation with classifier-free guidance (R1).
- **Novelty concern:** One reviewer (confidence 4) identifies that the main contribution, the order condition analysis and the resulting RES solver, already exists in Hochbruck and Ostermann (2005). Specifically:
  - Lemma 1 is equivalent to Equations 4.9–4.12 of that work; the proof in Appendix B.1 is a near-verbatim reproduction of Section 4.1 without attribution.
  - Theorem 1 is a version of Theorem 4.2, Theorem 2 of Theorem 4.3, and Proposition 1 paraphrases Theorem 4.7 from the same source.
  - The second- and third-order Butcher tableaus in Table 3 are Tables 5.3, 5.4, and 5.9 from Hochbruck and Ostermann (2005).
  - The observation that the RES-type solver outperforms DPM-Solver++ is also mathematically underpinned by that work (Figure 6.3).
  - Although the paper cites Hochbruck and Ostermann (2005) on page 18, the analysis is presented as new. This reviewer considers that the strengths are dominated by this weakness, leading to a low contribution score (R3).
- **Ambiguous experiments and figures:** Secondary experiments in Figure 4 lack a clear message and could be relegated to the appendix (R2). The left part of Figure 1(b) is unclear and may be clutter (R2). The dashed line in Figure 3 is not explained (R2).
- **Stochastic sampling setting is not clearly presented:** Performance in the stochastic setting is not conclusive, so benefits may be limited to the deterministic setting (R2). More details are needed on what problem is considered and how to interpret those results (R2).
- **Assumptions and theoretical rigor:** The uniform Lipschitz assumption, though used in prior work, is not satisfactory for realistic diffusion processes. It is unclear if this assumption can be removed or is fundamentally required (R2). One reviewer also notes a mild disagreement with Table 1: EDM/DEIS is also semilinear; the claimed distinction (time-varying vs. time-invariant linear part) does not capture the actual difference (R3).
- **Presentation errors and typos (R2):** Missing period in Conclusion; “upsampling” misspelled as “up sampling” in Appendix A; definition of \(\phi_{k+1}\) should be \(\phi_k\) to match Eq. 2.11 of Hochbruck; “satisfy” misspelled in multiple places in the appendix; “Butcher” tableau erroneously called “Buther” multiple times; in Equation (43) the product in the exponent should be a sum.
- **Unanswered question about figure:** In Figure 2, why is the multistep setting even worse than the single step? (R1)
