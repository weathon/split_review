## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes ArchLock, a NAS-based method to reduce DNN transferability at the architecture level using zero-cost proxies and a binary predictor. Reviewers generally found the idea interesting and novel, but expressed concerns about motivation, evaluation scale, predictor details, and potential overfitting. There is strong disagreement: two reviewers (scores 6 and 8) were positive, while one reviewer (score 3, confidence 5) was very negative, questioning the fundamental motivation and finding the evaluation insufficient.

### Strengths
- Addresses security at the architecture level, filling a gap left by parameter-level defenses (R1). The formulation of reducing transferability via architecture search is solid (R2).
- Uses efficient zero-cost proxies and architecture rankings to reduce search cost, scalable to large architecture pools (R1). The binary predictor-based NAS is efficient and effective (R2). Leverages zero-cost proxies in a new way (R3).
- Demonstrates effectiveness on NAS-Bench-201 and TransNAS-Bench-101, degrading target task performance by up to 30% and 50% respectively with <2% source task degradation (R1, R3 acknowledges results on TransNAS-Bench).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and threat model**: Not fully convincing (R3). If preventing fine-tuning, why release model parameters at all? The definition of "task" is too broad: does adding more CIFAR-10 data count as a new task? Is it only when the classification head is changed? (R3) The paper does not discuss what architectures lead to poor transferability or whether there are general patterns; visualizations and discussions of un-transferable architectures would be beneficial (R1).
- **Limited evaluation**: Only on NAS benchmarks (CIFAR-level), limiting appreciation of real-world motivation (R3). Need evaluation on unseen large-scale datasets like ImageNet (subset with full 224×224 resolution) or Miniplaces (R1). The numbers in Tables 1 and 2 may be directly taken from benchmarks; zero-cost proxies/predictors are trained on the same datasets, risking overfitting (R1). Zero-cost proxies have not been verified for the task of minimizing out-of-distribution accuracy (R3).
- **2% source task degradation**: Paper treats <2% as negligible, but R3 (score 3) argues it is actually quite large for CIFAR-level NNs. (Explicit contrast: one reviewer considers it significant, while others accept it as minor.)
- **Missing details and overhead**: Scenarios S/TU/TK are unclear; Algorithm 1 only covers the known-target case (R1). GraphEncoder execution and task embedding extraction are unclear; overhead for new tasks not reported (R1). Training details of the binary predictor are missing: what is the training cost? Is one pre-trained predictor suitable for architectures from different search spaces? (R2) Paper should cite predictor-based NAS works such as PINAT (AAAI 2023) and TNASP (NeurIPS 2021) (R2).
