## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the novel task of Visual Data-Type Identification – recognizing whether an image has been altered and how (e.g., rotation, noise, cartoon, sketch). Two datasets (TypeIdent and a natural version) cover 27 data-types under four categories (geometric, pixel, semantic, style). 39 VLMs (100M–80B parameters) are evaluated zero-shot. Findings: VLMs handle stylistic types (cartoons, sketches) reasonably but struggle with simpler manipulations (rotations, additive noise). Scaling yields marginal gains for contrastively-trained models (C-VLMs) and auto-regressive VLMs (LMMs) like OpenFlamingo show pronounced drops. Incorporating data-type information into captions during fine-tuning (TeDaTy dataset) significantly improves performance. The work is praised for its dataset, extensive evaluation, and practical relevance for data curation and autonomous vision.

### Strengths
- **Novel problem formulation**: Visual data-type identification is relatively unexplored and practically useful for data curation, noisy-data removal, domain-specific retrieval, and distinguishing weather changes from lens staining.
- **Dataset contributions**: Two datasets with animal images altered across 27 data-types (geometric, pixel, semantic, style). One synthetic (generated via manipulations and generative models), one natural (web-crawled). Provides a framework for creating more data-type datasets.
- **Extensive evaluation**: 39 VLMs from 13 model families, ranging from 100M to 80B parameters, covering C-VLMs (CLIP variants) and LMMs (OpenFlamingo, LLaVA, etc.). Evaluation includes zero-shot classification, error analysis via embeddings, pre-training distribution analysis, in-context learning (ICL), and fine-tuning with data-type supervision.
- **Key negative findings**: Model scaling alone yields marginal gains for CLIP; in-context learning with 7B models does not improve performance much; the largest auto-regressive VLMs perform worse than smaller C-VLMs, indicating a blind spot in current frontier VLMs.
- **Promising improvement direction**: Fine-tuning with data-type information in captions (TeDaTy dataset) gives substantial gains, suggesting a path to equip VLMs with data-type understanding.
- **Clear writing and well-structured** (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Prompt sensitivity and synonym ambiguity** (R3, R1): The evaluation uses a single short description per data-type (e.g., “patch&reshuffle”). VLMs are known to be sensitive to prompting, and many data-types have multiple valid descriptions (e.g., “pencil sketch” could be “pencil drawing”, “black and white drawing”; “Origami style” could be “low-poly style”). This may underestimate true model abilities. For example, a quick test with 10 images and the online LLaVA API on patch&reshuffle produced outputs like “collage, mosaic, collage art style” – terms semantically related to the augmentation – yet Figure 4 reports 0 informedness for LMMs on that type. This suggests either the metric or the evaluation protocol does not fully capture the model’s awareness.
- **Limited ICL model size** (R1): In-context learning experiments use the 7B OpenFlamingo model. This may be too small to test ICL capability; larger models might show improvement. The authors should clarify whether larger LMMs could benefit from ICL.
- **LMM evaluation details missing** (R3): The paper states LMMs are evaluated “by log-likelihood scoring” but provides no explanation in main text or appendix (and references lead to further redirections). This evaluation is crucial for trust in all reported LMM results and must be clearly described.
- **Unpacking LMM underperformance** (R2): The paper attributes LMMs’ consistent underperformance vs. C-VLMs to “weak alignment” and a “discriminative–generative gap,” but these are offered as observations rather than thoroughly investigated causes. Further analysis is needed to understand why.
- **Feature space analysis could be strengthened** (R3): The t-SNE visualization (Figure 5) is visually appealing but can be misleading. A more rigorous analysis using KNN evaluation and linear probing on the C-VLM features would clarify whether data-type information is linearly separable from visual features alone. If separable, the issue is primarily a text–vision alignment problem; if not, the problem may be deeper.
- **Pre-training dataset retrieval could be improved** (R3): The text-based search for data-type terms in VLM pre-training corpora is reasonable, but using semantic similarity metrics (e.g., word vectors, METEOR) to build a more comprehensive vocabulary (e.g., “collage, mosaic” for patch&reshuffle) would yield a more accurate picture of pre-training exposure.
- **Language–image mismatch hypothesis needs direct test** (R3): Given that simple data-type supervision yields large gains, the weakness may stem from a language–image mismatch (model rarely sees these terms in captions). The authors could perform a simple experiment to verify or refute this hypothesis (e.g., measuring co-occurrence statistics or probing caption distributions).
