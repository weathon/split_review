## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper extends prior work on invariance manifolds by introducing an affine transformation that aligns the continuous manifold of maximally exciting inputs from one neuron to another, enabling systematic comparison of invariances across neurons. The method combines implicit neural representations, contrastive learning, and affine fitting, and is validated on synthetic neurons and digital twin models of macaque V1 neurons. The authors demonstrate that the approach identifies known cell types (simple and complex cells) and reveals novel functional clusters.

### Strengths
- The problem of characterizing neuronal invariances in a modern, systematic way is important and under-explored.
- The method is principled, innovative, and elegantly combines INR, contrastive learning, and affine fitting; similar ideas could impact other fields.
- The paper is well written and easy to read; Figure 1 is very illustrative and of high quality; result figures are clean and convincing.
- The method is thoroughly evaluated on synthetic and real neural data, including consistency across noise seeds and robustness across encoding models.
- It captures previously identified V1 receptive field types (simple/complex cells) and suggests new cell types; the general procedure is promising for systematic classification.
- The focus on continuous transformations (via implicit representations) rather than pointwise estimates is a clear strength.

### Weaknesses
- **Misleading wording about biological data.** The paper claims to test on “biological neurons from macaque V1” (line 72), but the data come from *models* (digital twins) trained to predict V1 responses. This must be corrected; findings may be only properties of the models, not of actual biological neurons. (Reviewer 1, high severity)
- **Inappropriate use of “trivial.”** Referring to receptive field properties like position, size, and orientation as “trivial” is unfounded; these are fundamental to V1 organization. Rephrase (e.g., “allow flexibility to account for known variability”). (Reviewer 1)
- **INR methods are insufficiently explained.** The description of INRs (Lines 136-158, Figure 1) is hard to follow and not self-contained. Missing details: whether the response-predicting model parameters are fixed, what images train the INRs (train/test set, how they were chosen), and how modifications allow direct spatial frequency control. These should be clarified in the main text or supplement. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Contrastive objective parameters (Z+, Z-) not justified.** How are “near” and “distant” points defined? Sensitivity to this choice should be discussed. The claim that the formulation encourages smooth and diverse manifolds (218-219) should be quantified (e.g., plotting latent parameter traversal akin to Figure 1 bottom right). (Reviewer 1, also mentioned by Reviewer 3)
- **Missing details on simulations and alignment metric.** Provide more on model neurons in Section 4.1 (main text). Clarify how “Manifold similarity” (right bars in Fig 2E,F; Fig 3C) is measured and how it differs from “Activation similarity”. (Reviewer 1)
- **Assumption of continuous invariance manifold not discussed.** The method assumes the invariance manifold is continuous in pixel space, but counterexamples exist (e.g., y=min(x²,1) producing two disconnected maxima). This limitation should be acknowledged. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing comparison to prior work on idiosyncratic invariances.** Feather et al. (NeurIPS 2019, Nature Neuro 2023) showed deep nets often have idiosyncratic invariances. Do the regularizations (smoothness, etc.) in the initial MEI generation promote shared invariances? The use of an ensemble of CNNs versus a single model may also reduce idiosyncrasies. This work should be discussed. (Reviewer 1)
- **No target activation level in the objective.** The method optimizes activation toward 1, but does not allow setting a specific fraction of the MEI response (e.g., |αᵢ/α_MEI − λ|), which could lead to the manifold being too close to the MEI with limited variability. Compare to Wang & Ponce (PMLR 2022). (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing comparison/discussion of Wang & Ponce (Cell Rep 2022).** That work also finds invariance manifolds using a deep generative model latent space around an MEI, with multiple target levels. It is a related, comparable method and should be cited. (Reviewer 2)
- **Contrastive loss redundancy.** The positive pair part may be unnecessary given smooth INR parameterization; negative pairs alone might suffice. Justification is needed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Necessity of invariance alignment matrix.** How much of the alignment in Fig 2C,D and 3B comes from MEI similarity versus the invariance manifold itself? Since manifold images are close to the MEI, a comparison between MEI-based and manifold-based matrices would clarify the additional information. (Reviewer 2)
- **What else beyond cell type clusters?** The method is impressive, but its contribution to visual neuroscience beyond defining functional clusters should be articulated. (Reviewer 2)
- **Only 1D manifold.** The method finds a 1D invariance manifold, but neurons may be invariant to much higher-dimensional subspaces. Can the approach be extended to determine the true dimensionality? (Reviewer 2; also related to the continuity assumption from Reviewer 1)
- **Early ambiguity of “invariance manifold.”** The introduction introduces this concept without a clear definition; walking through a concrete example (e.g., with simple cells) would help. (Reviewer 3)
- **Nullspace concept and potential to game objective.** Discuss the nullspace of the neural response; what prevents the method from making large changes outside the receptive field (e.g., background) while keeping the MEI unchanged? (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing comparisons to prior functional clustering methods.** Klindt et al. (2017) handles RF position shifts; Ustyuzhaninov et al. (2019) handles both shifts and rotations. The affine transformations in this paper add scaling, shearing, and reflections, but comparisons to these methods are needed to show added value. (Reviewer 4; also implied by Reviewer 1’s question about casting affine transformations as a similarity metric, e.g., Williams et al. 2021)
- **Invariance only studied at the MEI activation level.** Invariance can occur at any activation level; the paper should clarify this choice and explain why only the MEI level is considered. (Reviewer 4)
- **Periodic 1D latent variable justification.** Explain why a periodic latent is used. (Reviewer 4)
- **Implementation details.** Why was batchnorm removed? (line 152) Are the Fourier features new compared to Baroni? Why do they allow better spatial frequency control? (line 155–157) Cosine similarity as image metric: discuss shortcomings. (Reviewer 4)
- **Clustering preprocessing.** Instead of using the activation matrix directly as a similarity matrix for agglomerative clustering, the authors compute cosine similarity between rows. This extra step should be justified. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minor presentation issues.** Fix typo “identifying” → “identify” (line 53), “steps” typo (line 153), “neuron + s” (line 253). Note whether Figure 1 bottom right is real or synthetic data. Explain how noise is handled for neural data. Rephrase strong wording: the Gabor pattern of the simple cell will not always maximize complex cell activity (lines 269-272); the statement that Gabor patterns have “no invariances” is too strong (line 429). (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4)
