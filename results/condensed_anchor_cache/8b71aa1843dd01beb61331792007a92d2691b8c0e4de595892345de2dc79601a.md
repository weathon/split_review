## Merged Review

### Summary
Video2Policy proposes to reconstruct object meshes and 6D poses from internet videos (e.g., Something-Something-v2) and then train RL policies in simulation using LLM-generated reward functions with iterative reflection. The pipeline uses off-the-shelf components (InstantMesh, FoundationPose, GPT-4o, DINO, SAM, UniDepth) and assumes a flat tabletop environment. Experiments cover 60 videos across 9 tasks in IsaacGym, plus 3 self-collected tasks. The generalist BC policy trained on generated expert data is shown to improve with more videos. Reviewers are split: one (score 3) finds the contribution minimal and real‑world relevance missing, while two (scores 6, 8) find the direction promising and experiments thorough; a fourth (score 5) agrees on potential but wants stronger robustness analysis.

### Strengths
- Leveraging in‑the‑wild video for robotic manipulation is an important direction (reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- Reconstructed scenes are consistent with original video under the flat‑table assumption (reviewer 1).
- The generalist policy approach is a promising step toward behavior cloning without human‑collected data (reviewer 2).
- Evaluation covers several tasks (9 from SSv2 + 3 self‑collected) and includes real‑world video input (reviewer 2).
- Ablations and experiments showing scalability (more videos improve performance) are provided (reviewer 2).
- Using human videos overcomes LLM hallucination in task generation (reviewer 4); the 6D relative poses provide critical spatial information not easily extracted by LLMs alone (reviewer 4).
- The pipeline uses well‑established components, making it replicable and extensible to more diverse videos (reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing physical properties**: No handling of mass, density, center of mass, surface roughness, or deformation. This makes real‑world transfer unrealistic (reviewer 1).
- **Simplistic environment**: Assumes a flat tabletop surface; no mechanism to handle non‑planar or cluttered scenes. Severely limits complexity (reviewers 1, 3).
- **No real‑world evaluation**: All experiments are in simulation; claiming a “generalist policy” is an overclaim without real‑world validation (reviewers 1, 4).
- **Novelty concerns**: The framework chains off‑the‑shelf methods (InstantMesh, FoundationPose, GPT‑4o) without novel technical innovations (reviewers 1, 4). The title “Video2Policy” is misleading—core contribution is reconstruction, not a new policy learning method (reviewer 1).
- **Limited scale**: Only 60 of the many available Something‑Something‑v2 videos are used. The claim of favorable scaling with number of videos is weakly supported (Figure 5 may conflate “video” with “task”) (reviewers 1, 3). More videos from a larger dataset would strengthen the demonstration (reviewer 3).
- **Task code examples missing**: The 6‑component structure (JSON, reset, success, reward, observations, code) is described only abstractly. Concrete examples and the exact format of each component are needed (reviewer 2). The term “task code” should be defined early (reviewer 2). Figure 3 should include task descriptions (reviewer 2).
- **Reward reflection unclear**: No graph of iteration vs. success rate to show that iterative refinement improves the reward function. Criteria for “better” reward and how the reward is initialized are not given (reviewer 2). Efficiency of multi‑round reflection vs. baselines without reflection is not compared (reviewers 2, 4).
- **Additional observation generation vague**: When and how GPT‑4o generates extra observations (e.g., distance between objects) is not described—are observations generated once or re‑generated each iteration? (reviewer 2).
- **Reset cheating concern**: Generated reset functions may place the robot closer to success than hard‑coded resets used by baselines (reviewer 2).
- **Tracking and reconstruction robustness**: No analysis of tracking precision, failure rates, or how segmentation/reconstruction quality affects downstream policy (reviewer 2). Only one image is used for mesh reconstruction; more images could improve quality (reviewer 3). InstantMesh struggles with masked, low‑resolution images; this limitation is not addressed (reviewer 4).
- **6D pose usage**: The need for 6D pose tracking is unclear since the policy does not imitate the trajectory (reviewer 3). How relative 6D poses (camera‑frame) are used by GPT in reward generation requires clarification (reviewer 4).
- **Size estimation noise**: UniDepth adds noise; no study on how this noise impacts final performance (reviewer 3).
- **Task scenario scope**: Evaluated tasks are simple tabletop scenarios; long‑horizon or more complex tasks would increase impact (reviewers 1, 4). Generalization to complex scenes is not shown (reviewer 3).
- **Hallucination in task generation**: No measurement of how often GPT‑4o generates invalid success/reset/reward functions (reviewer 2).
