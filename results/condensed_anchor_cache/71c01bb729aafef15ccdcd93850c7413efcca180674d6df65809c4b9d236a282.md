## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper presents SDXL, a latent diffusion model for text-to-image synthesis that uses a three times larger UNet, a second text encoder, novel conditioning on image size and cropping, multi-aspect-ratio training, an improved autoencoder, and a refinement model for high-resolution details. SDXL achieves impressive results competitive with state-of-the-art models like Midjourney and is open-sourced. Reviewers describe it as well-written with clear intuitions, thorough analysis, and effective solutions to practical problems.

### Strengths

- Impressive text-to-image generation results; serves as a strong base model for downstream tasks (R1, R4).
- Ablation experiments (e.g., Table 2) convincingly support some architectural and training choices (R1).
- The refinement model is an additional contribution for improving visual fidelity of real/generated images via post-hoc image-to-image (R1).
- Innovative training methods (conditioning on size/crop, multi-aspect) are presented with clear intuition and thorough analysis, supported by numerical metrics and visual comparisons (R2).
- The paper is well-written, coherent, and easy to follow for audiences with basic knowledge of diffusion models (R2, R4).
- Proposed solutions are simple and effective for realistic problems: size conditioning uses all resolution-varied data, crop conditioning prevents cut-out artifacts, refinement model adds high-resolution details, and pooled text embedding conditioning enables multimodal control (R4).
- The model, code, and implementation details (including pseudo code) are promised open-source, benefiting the community (R3, R4).
- The overall method is sound and the improvements are reasonable and effective (R3).

### Weaknesses

- Insufficient ablation for the novel conditioning techniques (cropping and size conditioning). The paper does not provide a clear quantitative comparison of image quality or text alignment when training with and without these mechanisms, making it unclear if gains are from increased model size or these specific conditioning methods. Multiple reviewers request a more rigorous ablation to isolate individual contributions (R1, R4).
- Missing comparison with state-of-the-art pixel-space diffusion models such as Simple Diffusion from Google (R3).
- SDXL still relies on two-stage training (separate autoencoder and diffusion). It is unclear if combining into a single-stage could further boost performance (R3).
- The improvements in the autoencoder are insufficiently explained. The paper does not detail which factors contribute to better reconstruction (EMA vs larger batch size) and lacks a clear description of how the autoencoder was advanced over previous versions (R1, R2).
- The refinement model lacks evaluation on other real images or images generated by previous Stable Diffusion versions; its training details (only 200 discrete noise scales?) are not fully described (R1, R2).
- No clear description of the user study methodology, raising questions about how it was conducted (R2).
- The training dataset is not specified. If it differs from LAION (used in Stable Diffusion), comparisons may not isolate the effectiveness of the proposed techniques (R4).
- The handling of multiple aspect ratios is unclear: the paper combines cropping conditioning with data bucketing from NovelAI without separate evaluation. It is also ambiguous whether cropping conditioning is applied during multi-aspect training or only at fixed resolution (R4).
- The rightmost examples in Fig. 5 are confusing: the (512,512) label is unexplained, and the behavior under out-of-distribution conditions is not properly analyzed (R4).
- Minor mistakes: Fig. 3 incorrectly labels image size as (256,236) instead of (256,256); quotation marks are incorrectly used in prompts in Figures 3 and 4 (R1).
- The combination of two language models (CLIP ViT-L and OpenCLIP ViT-bigG) lacks intuition and an ablation study to show whether both are necessary (R2).
- The claim of "no immediate benefit" from using Transformer architecture for diffusion models needs more detailed explanation (R2).
