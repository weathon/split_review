## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a multi-task transformer framework for 3D face alignment that jointly predicts 2D landmarks and 3DMM parameters. Two transformer-based branches with self- and cross-attention are used to enhance information exchange. A query-aware memory (QAMem) module is designed to maintain accuracy on low-resolution feature maps, removing the need for high-resolution maps. A multi-layer additive residual regression (MARR) module predicts offsets from an average face model, and a multi-information loss with Euler Angles Loss supervises the network. Experiments on AFLW2000-3D and other benchmarks show competitive results.

### Strengths
- The presentation is clear and easy to follow (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- Experiments are intensive and reasonable results are achieved (Reviewer 1).
- The use of query embeddings and cross attention for information communication among all estimated parameters is interesting and novel (Reviewer 2).
- Visual demos show effectiveness of the proposed method in 3D pose and landmark estimation (Reviewer 2).
- The QAMem module is sound and well presented in Figure 2 (Reviewer 4).
- MARR and Euler Angles Loss appear effective for the targeted problem (Reviewer 4).
- The overall framework (multi-task transformer, cross-attention, Euler Angles Loss) is a notable contribution (Reviewer 3: “The main contributions are…”).

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental novelty**: The contributions are a combination of existing techniques—multi-task structure, QAMem (a variation of memory-based attention), MARR (residual from average face), and Euler Angles Loss—none of which is fundamentally new in 3D face alignment. The combination does not constitute a significant advance (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 explicitly finds the query/cross-attention novel, but the overall novelty is debated; Reviewer 1 and 3 consider contributions incremental.
- **Not state-of-the-art on key metric**: On AFLW2000-3D (Table 2) the proposed method underperforms SynergyNet (3DV 2021) in MAE, pitch, and roll (Reviewer 1). Table 5 also shows the method is not quantitatively better than SynergyNet (Reviewer 2). SynergyNet is the main competitor that outperforms the proposed method, yet it is not included in qualitative comparisons (Reviewer 2).
- **Ablation study weaknesses**:
  - Table 3: Chamfer distance for the Trans3DHead variant is worse than the baseline; adding modules does not show consistent improvement (Reviewer 1). The QA module alone does not improve accuracy over the baseline (Reviewer 2). When comparing Euler+QA and MARR+QA, the accuracy improvement is quite limited (Reviewer 4). It is unclear whether improvements come from the components or not (Reviewer 4). Partial combinations are not effective, making the ablation unconvincing (Reviewer 4).
  - The impact of the specific 3DMM choice on results is not examined; no experiments with different 3DMMs are presented (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing computational cost analysis**: The paper claims efficiency but provides no runtime or complexity comparison (Reviewers 3, 4). Real-time performance is important for landmark detection (Reviewer 3).
- **QAMem module description is unclear**:
  - It is not clear whether more feature maps are used to trade for lower resolution, or what the exact benefit is (Reviewer 2).
  - The mechanism by which 1×1 convolutions with N groups provide discriminative values for different queries is not explained (Reviewer 2).
  - The term “memory” is introduced in section 3.1 without context or explanation (Reviewer 2).
- **Average-face residual regression concern**: Using the average face as a reference and predicting residuals may limit generalization to unseen face shapes and poses that deviate significantly from the average (Reviewer 2).
- **Euler Angles Loss issues**:
  - It requires estimating Euler angles from predicted 3DMM parameters, potentially introducing an additional module and error propagation (Reviewer 2).
  - The exact method for extracting Euler angles is not specified; it is unclear if a neural network is used (Reviewer 2, Question 4).
- **Inappropriate title**: The title emphasizes “face alignment” while the paper focuses on facial landmark detection (Reviewer 3).
- **Multiple decoders not explained**: Section 3.3 uses multiple decoders without clarifying their architecture, purpose, or computational efficiency (Reviewer 2, Question 3).
- **Qualitative results limited**: Visual demos use only one dataset and compare only with DAD-3DNet, omitting stronger baselines like SynergyNet (Reviewer 2). More datasets should be used (Reviewer 4).
- **Equation explanations blurry**: The derivation from eq. 2 to eq. 3 is insufficiently explained (Reviewer 4).
