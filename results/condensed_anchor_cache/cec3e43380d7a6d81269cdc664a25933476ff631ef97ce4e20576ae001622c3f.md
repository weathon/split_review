## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a transfer learning approach for quantile regression via adapter-tuning, introducing a quantile regression adapter with lightweight, sparse parameters. It provides theoretical analysis with error bounds and a transferability measure, and conducts numerical simulations to demonstrate effectiveness over vanilla methods.

### Strengths
- Well-written and easy to follow (reviewers 1, 2).
- Novel adaptation of transfer learning to the quantile regression objective (reviewer 1).
- Thorough theoretical analysis of the proposed method (reviewer 1).
- Quantile objective is well-motivated and theoretical concepts are well explained (reviewer 2).
- The research direction—designing transfer learning for risk-sensitive tasks—is important (reviewer 3).
- Both empirical and theoretical analyses demonstrate effectiveness in sample-efficient transfer learning (reviewer 3).
- One reviewer (score 5) found the setting (regularized quantile regression) interesting and results supported by rigorous proof (reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited theoretical novelty**: The analysis directly applies existing high-dimensional statistics and sparse quantile regression tools; the setup is detached from the adapter fine-tuning context because assumptions and results focus on \(\delta^*\) rather than the transfer mechanism (reviewer 2). Even the more positive reviewer (score 5) noted that proving techniques are standard and the contribution may not meet top conference bars (reviewer 4).
- **Restricted to linear models**: The method is limited to a linear adapter and linear structural model; it is unclear how to extend to nonlinear applications (e.g., neural networks, LLMs) (reviewers 1, 3). The presentation is misleading because the introduction extensively discusses adapter-tuning in LLMs, yet the proposed approach cannot be applied to such models (reviewer 3).
- **Weak empirical evaluation**: Numerical experiments are limited to synthetic data with small sample sizes and low dimensions, which do not reflect typical adapter fine-tuning scenarios (reviewers 2, 3, 4). No real-world transfer learning tasks are included; baselines used are limited (reviewer 3).
- **Shallow transfer learning aspect**: The transfer learning component is reduced to the assumption that \(\theta - \theta_s\) is small; there is no explicit algorithmic design to address or leverage distribution shift between source and target (reviewer 4).
- **Technical errors in presentation**: Equation (4) is incorrect—the left side should be \(\rho_\tau(y - f(x;\theta))\) instead of \(\rho_\tau(x)\) (reviewers 1, 2). Multiple other corrections are needed: the sum over \(i\) should start at 1 (line 145); \(j\) should be in set \([d]\) (line 143); only positive semi-definite matrices have matrix square roots (line 147); inequality (30)→(31) is invalid (e.g., \(a=1,b=-1\)); missing assumption in line 954; unclear definition of \(q\) in (39) and why (39) implies (48); in line 1031 \(\mathbb{E}[-v(\tau - \mathbf{1}\{w\leq0\})]=0\) seems to assume unbiasedness; line 1142/ (65) missing terms; line 365 should be order \(O(d^{1/2}/n_s^{1/2})\); line 836 grammar; line 887 \(|ab|_1\) should be \(|a^T b|\); formula (25) should be equality then inequality; line 1030 \(v = x'(\hat\delta - \tilde\delta)\); etc. (reviewer 2). These issues undermine the rigor of the proofs.
- **Missing experiments and justification**: Testing on real-world nonlinear pretrained models (e.g., Mistral-7B, Llama3-8B) is needed (reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 asked for an explanation of the nontriviality in incorporating source estimation error into the target analysis (Line 271), which remains unaddressed.
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 5) was overall more positive, praising the interesting problem and rigorous proofs, but still acknowledged the limited scope and standard techniques. The other three reviewers (score 3) were more critical of the technical contribution and applicability.
