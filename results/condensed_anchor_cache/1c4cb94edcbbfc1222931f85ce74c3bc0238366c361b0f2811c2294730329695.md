## Merged Review

### Summary
DeepGate4 is a scalable graph transformer for large-scale circuit representation learning. It introduces (1) a partitioning method and update strategy reducing memory complexity to sub-linear, (2) a GAT-based sparse transformer exploiting AIG sparsity, (3) global/local structural encodings, and (4) a dynamic loss balancer. Experiments on ITC99 and EPFL benchmarks show significant improvements over SOTA (15.5% and 31.1% gains), and a Fused variant reduces runtime by 35.1% and memory by 46.8%.

### Strengths
- **Strong empirical results**: All reviewers agree that DeepGate4 significantly outperforms previous SOTA baselines across multiple metrics and circuit scales (Reviewers 1,2,3). Reviewers 1 and 2 specifically call the evaluation “comprehensive and compelling”.
- **Addresses a fundamental problem**: Circuit representation learning for EDA is an important research topic (Reviewers 1,2,3). The work targets scalability and over-squashing challenges.
- **Technical contributions**: Reviewer 1 highlights three innovations: novel graph partitioning strategy, efficient sparse transformer, adaptive loss balancing. Reviewer 2 finds the modules “interesting and effective”. Reviewer 4 acknowledges the memory reduction achievement: “can reduce GPU memory usage while ensuring convergence”.
- **Thorough literature review** (Reviewer 1): effectively traces GNN application evolution in EDA and critically analyzes transformer limitations, establishing theoretical foundations.
- **Minority disagreement**: Reviewer 2 is the most positive (score 8), emphasizing the modules’ effectiveness and the significant improvement. Other reviewers (scores 6,6,5) are more reserved about novelty but still praise results.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty** (Reviewers 3,4; partly Reviewer 2). Reviewer 2 notes the graph partition and loss balancer are “commonly-used” or “widely investigated” (e.g., in computer vision [1]). Reviewer 3 states “the novelty and contribution of these modules seems limited”. Reviewer 4 points out that DeepGate3 already proposed a partitioning strategy, weakening the partitioning contribution.
- **Missing comparison with HOGA [2]** (Reviewers 2,3). HOGA is a recent SOTA that also addresses large-scale circuit scaling; the paper does not compare against it.
- **No downstream task validation** (Reviewers 3,4). The experiments use only custom representation learning metrics (e.g., \(L_{gate}^{prob}\), \(L_{gate}^{con}\)), not real-world EDA tasks (e.g., testability, power estimation, SAT solving). Reviewer 4 states: “does not provide any experimental validation on specific EDA downstream tasks” and “does not elaborate on how much benefit such methods could bring to actual EDA tasks”.
- **Unclear evaluation metrics** (Reviewers 2,3). The metrics in Table 1 and others are not defined (e.g., \(L_{gate}^{prob}\), \(L_{gate}^{con}\)). Reviewer 2 asks to explain the metrics used in Table 1.
- **Insufficient datasets and data scale** (Reviewer 4). The paper uses two old benchmarks (ITC99, EPFL); data is “insufficient for such a large-scale model”. Reviewer 4 recommends evaluating on modern, larger-scale datasets/netlists (e.g., CircuitNet [1], MacroPlacement [2]). Reviewer 2 also suggests the OpenABC-D benchmark.
- **Unclear method description** (Reviewers 1,3,4). Reviewer 3 requests a summary of contributions; the graph partition module and multi-task training objective are unclear. Reviewer 1 finds Section 3.3 (receptive fields in inter-class and intra-class) not clearly elaborated. Reviewer 4 asks: “What’s the difference between the two partitioning strategies used in DeepGate3 and DeepGate4?”
- **Missing hyperparameter sensitivity analysis** (Reviewers 1,3). Reviewer 1 asks why k=8 in ablation; Reviewer 3 asks for sensitivity analysis on \(k\) and \(\delta\) and whether the same hyperparameters were used across all experiments.
- **Loss balancer explanation** (Reviewer 1). The description is insufficient; the paper says it is very effective but lacks theoretical analysis or explanation of why it must be applied to the last layer.
- **Figure clarity** (Reviewer 1). Figure 2 arrows (red/black) and other figures need clearer explanation.
- **No application to sequential circuits** (Reviewer 2). The method’s applicability to sequential circuit representation learning is not addressed.
- **Unseen vs training data** (Reviewer 2). It is unclear whether evaluation is on unseen testing sets or training datasets.
- **Receptive field computation details** (Reviewer 1). The effects of parameters \(k\) and \(d\) on computing receptive fields and subsequent cone calculations are not explained.
- **Minority reviewer contrast**: Reviewer 2 (score 8) found the modules “interesting and effective” and did not raise novelty concerns as strongly, while Reviewers 3 and 4 (scores 6 and 5) explicitly questioned novelty and sufficiency of experiments. This disagreement should be noted.
