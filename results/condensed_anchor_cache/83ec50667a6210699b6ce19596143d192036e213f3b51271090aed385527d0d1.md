## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper tackles mono-to-binaural speech synthesis in arbitrary environments. It introduces a new synthetic benchmark, TUT Mono-to-Binaural, and proposes BinauralZero, which combines two parameter-free transforms (geometric time warping, amplitude scaling) with a pre-trained diffusion-based vocoder (WaveFit, trained from scratch on 60k hours of LibriLight). Evaluations on TUT and the existing Binaural Speech dataset show that BinauralZero can match or outperform supervised neural baselines subjectively despite never training on binaural data. Both objective and subjective metrics are reported, with the authors highlighting a decorrelation between automated metrics and human ratings.

### Strengths
- **New benchmark dataset (TUT Mono-to-Binaural):** Provides a diverse, challenging evaluation testbed for mono-to-binaural methods. All four reviewers acknowledge this as a useful contribution.
- **Simple and effective design:** BinauralZero is parameter-free after pre-training the vocoder, easy to follow, and can serve as a plug-in module. The integration of simple transforms with a denoising vocoder is reasonable. Ablation studies confirm the contribution of each component. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4)
- **Highlighting limitations of objective metrics:** The paper demonstrates and analyzes the discrepancy between automated metrics and perceptual quality, supported by theoretical induction of phase and amplitude errors. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3)
- **Generalization potential:** BinauralZero works across acoustic environments without binaural training data, a practical strength. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Evaluation concerns (sample length, anchors, sampling rate, orientation):**
    - TUT samples are 2 seconds long, which is too short to demonstrate changing speaker position; Binaural Speech test samples (6 seconds) are also shorter than prior work (e.g., BinauralGrad). (Reviewer 1)
    - DSP baselines (e.g., Resonance Audio) are not compared in the subjective evaluation (Table 1). Prior works (WarpNet, BinauralGrad) showed strong subjective quality for DSP methods. (Reviewer 1)
    - MUSHRA testing details are incomplete: what anchors were used and how were they scored? (Reviewer 3)
    - Sampling rate inconsistencies: baseline models use 48 kHz, WaveFit uses 24 kHz, TUT appears 44.1 kHz. No conversion details are given. (Reviewer 3)
    - Baseline methods require orientation input; how were orientations synthesized for TUT, and how does the distribution of orientation/location differ from Binaural Speech? (Reviewer 3)
    - The 95% confidence intervals for MOS scores are very high despite over 30 raters scoring 50 samples per method. (Reviewer 1)

- **Dataset description and implications:**
    - The paper describes TUT as “anechoic,” but the provided GT samples are clearly echoic. This must be clarified. (Reviewer 3)
    - If anechoic: baselines were trained on reverberant rooms, so BinauralZero’s advantage on TUT may come from its anechoic output (matching the test set). This weakens the claim that neural models “overfit to non-spatial acoustic features.” Provide specific examples of those features. (Reviewer 3)
    - If echoic: it is contradictory that anechoic BinauralZero receives high MUSHRA scores on reverberant TUT. Additionally, BinauralZero outputs show audible out-of-phase artifacts (especially sibilants) on Binaural Speech but not on TUT; NFS outputs on TUT are slightly detuned (contradicting NFS’s linear-phase design). (Reviewer 3)

- **Over-claims and limited novelty:**
    - The method essentially applies DSP transforms followed by vocoder refinement. Claiming that the vocoder step models RIR/HRTF (L262) or implicitly conditions on position (L252) is hard to justify: the vocoder takes mono input, so it acts only as a waveform refiner. (Reviewer 4)
    - GTW, AS, and diffusion vocoders are all existing components. The key advantage over prior work is unclear, and the paper does not clearly explain what is novel. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3)

- **Discrepancy analysis is insufficient:**
    - The paper does not deeply explain why objective metrics (e.g., energy-based) fail to correlate with human ratings, beyond a high-level error analysis. Reporting additional metrics (SDR, SAQAM, NORD, AODG) could strengthen the claim. (Reviewer 3)
    - The subjective-objective discrepancy for the proposed method on Binaural Speech is noted but not thoroughly analyzed. (Reviewer 2)

- **Ablation and architectural choices need more justification:**
    - Why is linear interpolation chosen for GTW? A linear-phase filter (like NFS) could handle non-integer delays. (Reviewer 3)
    - Why is WaveFit selected? Comparisons with other vocoders (Griffin-Lim, BigVGAN) are missing. (Reviewer 3)
    - How does BinauralZero preserve ITD (e.g., 1 ms) when converting a delay-encoded waveform to Mel spectrogram (hop size 12.5 ms)? Mel-filterbank preprocessing likely discards fine timing. Provide Mel conversion configuration. (Reviewer 3)
    - Removing AS causes a surprisingly large drop in N-MOS (Table 4); explanation needed. (Reviewer 4)
    - Why not use a more complete DSP baseline (e.g., Resonance Audio package) with WaveFit? (Reviewer 3)

- **Generalization and fair comparison:**
    - Baseline methods were trained on different data distributions (microphone array, room conditions). Comparisons on TUT may be unfair; few-shot fine-tuning of baselines on TUT would be more equitable. (Reviewer 2)
    - The method should be compared with more DSP-based models that use general RIR/HRTF to demonstrate generalization advantage. (Reviewer 4)
    - Generalization to moving sound sources and real spatial recordings (e.g., DCASE 2022 Task 3) is not tested. (Reviewer 2)

- **Efficiency and training cost:**
    - Pre-training the vocoder from scratch on 60k-hour LibriLight is expensive; GPU hours are not reported. Inference is slow due to diffusion steps in waveform space. (Reviewer 1)
    - Computational complexity comparison with baselines is missing. (Reviewer 3)

- **Minority reviewer contrast:** Two reviewers (R2, R4) gave score 5, emphasizing the benchmark and practical simplicity; two (R1, R3) gave score 3, questioning the evaluation rigor, novelty, and methodological details. The negative reviewers are particularly critical of the dataset description, ablation completeness, and the subjective evaluation procedure.
