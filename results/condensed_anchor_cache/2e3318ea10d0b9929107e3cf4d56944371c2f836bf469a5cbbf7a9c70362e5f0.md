## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents a systematic evaluation of incorporating explicit 2D and 3D geometric information into RNA property prediction, introducing a curated set of RNA datasets with enhanced structural annotations. The main findings are that geometry-aware models outperform sequence-based models (average RMSE reduction ~12%) but are less robust to sequencing noise and often require 2–5× more training data to match performance. The study covers real-world challenges (limited data, partial labeling, noise, OOD scenarios). Reviewers acknowledge the comprehensive experiments and dataset contribution, but raise significant concerns about methodological novelty, model selection (only EGNN, SchNet for 3D, both early and not RNA-specific), reliance on predicted structures (especially 3D), and insufficient analytical depth. Reviewer 3 (rating 8) was notably more positive about the dataset’s value for AI for Science, while Reviewers 1 and 2 (ratings 6 and 5) were more critical of innovation and model choices.

### Strengths
- First systematic evaluation of 2D and 3D geometric context in RNA property prediction, covering performance, noise, limited data, partial labeling, and computational efficiency.
- Introduction of newly curated RNA datasets with enhanced 2D and 3D annotations, providing a reproducible resource for the community.
- Extensive experiments across multiple models (1D, 2D, 3D) and diverse conditions (low-data, partial labels, sequencing noise, OOD), with thorough coverage of nucleotide length intervals.
- Commitment to public release of methodologies, datasets, and code, promoting transparency and reproducibility.
- One reviewer (rating 8) particularly emphasized the high-quality dataset integration and its importance as an assistant for AI for Science.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of methodological innovation.** No new model is proposed; the pooling strategy is simple and the paper only compares existing models. The work is described as merely evaluating metrics without deep improvements or novel algorithm designs.
- **Outdated and insufficient 3D models.** Only EGNN and SchNet are used for 3D geometry, both early models not designed for RNA. Their limited capacity (e.g., EGNN’s form capacity limitations) makes conclusions potentially unreliable. Reviewers strongly recommend adding RNA-specific 3D models (ARES, PaxNet, MEAN, dyMEAN) and more recent general 3D GNNs (GVP, GearNet, SaVeNet, LEFTNet, FAENet, TFN, NequIP, MACE, EquiformerV2, EPT).
- **All-atom modeling limitations.** SchNet and EGNN rely on a limited local neighborhood of adjacent atoms, restricting receptive fields and missing long-range dependencies. The computational burden is high. Alternatives: treating each nucleotide as a node (as in MEAN) or using two-branch long/short-range modeling (PaxNet).
- **Reliance on predicted structures introduces significant uncertainty.** Both secondary and tertiary structures are predicted by software (especially 3D, which is inaccurate). Noise in predicted structures propagates into property predictions, explaining why 1D models are more robust under noise. One reviewer suggested using direct 3D datasets (e.g., RNAsolo) to avoid this.
- **Analysis lacks depth and insight.** Section 4.2 conclusions (e.g., more training data improves performance, especially in transformers) are obvious. The inferior performance of 3D models vs. 2D is attributed to poor 3D structure prediction, but this is not deeply discussed. Section 4.3 similarly offers predictable explanations (noise accumulation in 2D/3D due to predictive tools). The key points are not sufficiently emphasized; the paper is described as merely a comparison of metrics.
- **Missing justification for experimental design.** The motivation for the five task settings is unclear, making the paper appear cumbersome. Criteria for selecting secondary structure prediction tools are not detailed. No comparison with RNA language models (e.g., recent RNA-specific LM) is provided, leaving a gap in the 1D baseline landscape.
- **Lack of explanation for RNA’s uniqueness.** The methodology appears generic for any sequence molecule (DNA, protein). It is not clarified whether there are fundamental differences in the approach beyond molecular composition.
- **Specific property definitions unclear.** The paper does not clearly state what RNA properties are being predicted (e.g., stability, interactions) or the units of prediction values (e.g., kcal/mol).
