## Merged Review

### Summary
Three reviewers rated the paper 6, 5, and 5. One reviewer (score 6) was more positive, highlighting the simple yet effective approach and clear improvements over DetectGPT. The other two (score 5) acknowledged the novelty and intuitive design but raised significant concerns about limited evaluation, missing analyses, and methodological issues. All reviewers agreed the idea is relevant and the presentation is generally clear.

### Strengths
- Simple yet effective approach: using a Gaussian Process surrogate model to select typical perturbations based on Bayesian uncertainty and interpolate scores, improving query efficiency over DetectGPT.
- Clear performance improvements (AUROC) over DetectGPT across three datasets, including in the realistic mismatch setting between source LLM and detection LLM.
- The approach is intuitive and a relevant step in improving DetectGPT.
- Detailed description of the approach and all design choices.
- Originality: constructing a surrogate to replace the true score function for LLM is novel.
- Significance: reducing query expense while maintaining high detection performance is important.

### Weaknesses
- **Evaluation limited to only two LLMs**: only GPT-2 and Llama-1 (65B) are used; no experiments on more recent open-source models (e.g., Llama-2, Guanaco, Vicuna, Falcon, MPT, ChatGLM) or across different parameter size classes. No analysis of performance trends with increasing model size. For mismatch experiments (Section 4.3), only smaller models (GPT-J, GPT-Neo) are used.
- **Lack of black-box LLM evaluation**: no results for state-of-the-art black-box models such as GPT-3.5, GPT-4, PaLM-2, or Claude, which are the most commonly used for text generation and are easiest to misuse.
- **Insufficient experimental rigor**: some claims are not adequately supported across all settings. For example, the statement that the method remains effective under high query budgets (page 7, end of Section 4.1) is demonstrated only on WritingPrompts with GPT-2, not across all datasets and LLMs.
- **Missing baselines**: experiments do not include comparisons with other detection methods beyond DetectGPT. The performance numbers for DetectGPT are noted to possibly not match those in the original DetectGPT paper; this discrepancy is not addressed.
- **Cost of kernel construction with BertScore**: constructing the kernel function using BertScore may itself require extensive LLM queries, undermining the claimed efficiency gain; this is not discussed.
- **No analysis of bias-variance trade-off**: replacing the true log-probability \( \log p_\theta(\tilde{\mathbf{X}}) \) with the surrogate function \( f \) introduces bias and reduces variance. The impact of this trade-off on detection performance is not analyzed in either the methodology or experiments.
- **Unaddressed questions**: could a non-GP surrogate model perform differently? Could an encoder-based classifier trained on generated data (easy to obtain) be used as an alternative? These are mentioned but left unanswered.
- **Minor presentation issues**: some figure labels are unclear; the paper contains typos and grammatical errors (e.g., "bedget" for "budget" on page 7, misuse of "typicity" on page 4).
