## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CLeVER, an equivariant contrastive learning (ECL) framework that disentangles representations into invariant and equivariant parts, applies contrastive learning on the invariant part and orthogonality on the equivariant part, and adds a regularization term (L_PReg) to prevent trivial solutions. The method builds on DDCL and is evaluated mainly on DINO and DDCL across multiple backbones, showing improvements in downstream tasks.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-motivated and addresses the important problem of trivial solutions in equivariant contrastive learning (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). The main motivation is clear and the solution is straightforward (Reviewer 4).
- CLeVER is concise and intuitive (Reviewer 3).
- The regularization improves DINO across multiple backbones (Reviewer 1).
- Analytical results and visualizations are helpful; the experimental section is thoughtful and covers various aspects (Reviewer 1). Experiments are comprehensive and show benefits in different scenarios (Reviewer 4).
- The paper is easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty is limited**: the primary difference from DDCL is the incorporation of L_PReg, which is applicable only to DDCL (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Without the regularization, CLeVER is very similar to DDCL, only changing how L_CL and L_Orth are computed (Reviewer 3). This raises concerns about contribution to the general contrastive learning literature (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of theoretical analysis**: there is no discussion of theoretical insights or a thorough examination of why the proposed regularization is the most effective solution for collapse in DDCL (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). For example, when minimizing ||h_V|| - ||h_I||, why does h_V increase toward h_I while h_I does not decrease much? (Reviewer 1). No evidence is provided that DDCL actually obtains trivial solutions in practice (Reviewer 4). A corresponding table for CLeVER (like Table 1) is missing to verify improvements (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient generalization and comparisons**:
  - Effectiveness has not been validated on alternative frameworks such as SimCLR or Barlow Twins (Reviewers 1, 2). The method appears to rely heavily on DDCL (Reviewers 1, 2).
  - Only DINO and DDCL are tested; more recent methods should be considered (Reviewer 1).
  - Comparisons with other equivariant contrastive learning methods (e.g., Dangovski et al., Xie et al., Gupta et al.) are missing (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Empirical improvements are marginal**: the 0.7 improvement on DDCL for one setting is not convincing, and the paper lacks results across various backbones and scales for DDCL as done for DINO (Reviewer 1). The advantages over DDCL need more discussion (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing ablation studies**: the balance of the three terms in the pretraining objective should be analyzed (Reviewer 4). The advantages and disadvantages of invariant vs. equivariant features require further investigation (Reviewer 4).
- **Issues with attention visualizations**: lack of quantitative results; CLeVER shows larger values and wider coverage on objects but also spills attention to unrelated pixels (especially for independent attention heads in the supplementary) (Reviewer 1).
- **Presentation and clarity issues**:
  - Section 3.3 (backbone description) is irrelevant to the method and should be moved to experimental settings (Reviewer 3).
  - Table 2 conveys too much information; it should be broken into smaller tables based on the discussions. Redundant information (SimCLR, Debiased, BYOL, MoCo, MoCo V2, RefosNet) is not discussed in the main text (Reviewer 3).
  - In Tables 3 and 4, the purpose of gray shading is unclear and best results in shaded columns are not bolded (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of dedicated related work section**: there is no comprehensive review of studies on preventing representation collapse in contrastive learning, nor a clear explanation of how CLeVER distinguishes itself (Reviewer 2).
- **Downstream task selection of factors**: without knowledge of downstream task properties, how should one decide whether to rely on invariant or equivariant factors? (Reviewer 4).
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - In Section 3.2, does satisfying Eq. 6 constitute T-equivariance? Possibly a typo (Reviewer 3).
  - Why is DDCL used to analyze SimSiam while CLeVER is used to analyze DINO in Tables 3 and 4? Should use the same method for fair comparison (Reviewer 3).
  - How does addressing collapse in DDCL contribute to improving equivariance? (Reviewer 2).

While Reviewers 1 and 4 found the method well-motivated and the experiments comprehensive, Reviewers 2 and 3 argued that the novelty is limited and improvements are marginal.
