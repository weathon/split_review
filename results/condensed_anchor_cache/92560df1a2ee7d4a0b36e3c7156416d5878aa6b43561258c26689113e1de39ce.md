## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper aims to improve classifier-guided score-based generation under limited labeled data by regularizing the classifier during training. The key idea is to reinterpret the classifier as a time-dependent energy-based model (EBM) and apply a self-calibration (SC) loss derived from denoising score matching (DSM), enabling regularization on both labeled and unlabeled data without an external SGM. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 with few labels show improved intra-FID and accuracy over several baselines.

### Strengths
- Clear motivation and well-written paper with a logical structure and comprehensive review of related work (Section 2).
- Extensive experiments comparing with all existing conditional score-based generative models (CG, CG-DLSM, CG-LS, CG-JR, CFG) and demonstrating superiority in semi-supervised settings, with results in Table 1 and Appendix G.
- The proposed SC loss is computationally more appealing than JEM’s maximum likelihood objective, and the illustrative example in Fig. 3 empirically supports its effectiveness.
- Theoretical analysis is sound (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 5).
- Intra-FID scoring is crucial for class-wise interpretation in partially labeled scenarios, and the semi-supervised conditional generation direction is not commonly explored (Reviewer 2).
- The method enables effective use of unlabeled data through self-calibration, improving generalization.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The SC loss heavily depends on two prior works (EBMs and JEM). It essentially applies the JEM objective within a score-based generative model framework, introducing marginal innovation (Reviewer 1, 2, 4). The methodology appears incremental, primarily applying standard DSM to train the internal score function (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing comparison with JEM** on real datasets. Given the resemblance to JEM, a direct comparison (e.g., using Gao et al. ICLR 2021 to incorporate diffusion into JEM) is needed to assess the true advantage (Reviewer 5).
- **Lack of stronger baseline experiments**: The distribution-alignment claim is justified only against adversarial training. Stronger regularizers (RandAugment, CutMix) and semi-supervised classifier training (e.g., [1] in Reviewer 3) should be compared (Reviewer 3).
- **Incomplete reporting**: Table 1 for CIFAR-100 does not include accuracy metrics (Reviewer 3).
- **Fair comparison concerns**: Baseline CG-DLSM regularizes the classifier over labeled data only, but it could also use unlabeled data; experiments with that variant would be a fairer comparison (Reviewer 3). Hyperparameters for baselines (balancing factors in CG-DLSM/CG-JR, smoothing factor in CG-LS, guidance scales λ_CG and λ_CFG) are not transparently chosen; tuning these could affect results (Reviewer 4, Reviewer 5).
- **Redundancy in formulation**: The paper uses both a learned score function s(x,t;φ) and the gradient of log sum over classifier outputs ∇_x log Σ_y f(x,y,t;φ) as parameterized scores. The necessity of this redundancy, especially in the classifier-guided generation context, is not justified (Reviewer 5).
- **Limited generalizability**: Experiments are confined to CIFAR-10/100 (32×32, 100 classes). It is unclear if the method scales to larger, more realistic benchmarks like ImageNet (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 5).
- **Classification ability not tested**: Despite involving a classifier, the paper does not evaluate its classification performance after training; the reviewer requests this analysis (Reviewer 1).
- **CFG outperforms when more labels are available**: When labeled data exceeds 50%, CFG gives better conditional generation than the proposed CG-SC and traditional CG (Sec 4.2 and Appendix A). CFG is more prevalent and easier to apply (Reviewer 4).
- **Inconsistency between conditional and unconditional metrics**: Intra-FID (conditional) improves but unconditional FID does not, raising questions about metric consistency (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing theoretical justification**: An analytical explanation for why SC loss should outperform DLSM (Fisher divergence) and JEM (KL divergence) is lacking, and theoretical grounding for the semi-supervised setup is absent (Reviewer 5).
- **Minor weaknesses**: Figure 1 is too naïve and space in the main paper could be used more wisely (Reviewer 2). The paper does not discuss relationship with a highly relevant NeurIPS 2023 paper on diffusion models and semi-supervised learners (Reviewer 1).
- **Reviewer questions (converted to weaknesses)**: 
  - Why not test classification ability of the trained classifier? (Reviewer 1)
  - Does the argument suggest classifier architectures are more prone to overfitting? (Reviewer 2)
  - Why are FID scores for CFG-all or CFG-labeled notably poor? (Reviewer 2)
  - What happens if CG-DLSM’s classifier is also regularized over unlabeled data? (Reviewer 3)
  - How were hyperparameters for baselines chosen to ensure fair comparison? (Reviewer 4, 5)
  - Why is there inconsistency between conditional and unconditional FID? (Reviewer 4)
  - Can the authors theoretically justify SC loss’s effectiveness in a semi-supervised setup (e.g., via optimal transport)? (Reviewer 5)
