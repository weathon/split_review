## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Tree of Attributes Prompt Learning (TAP) for vision-language models. TAP first instructs LLMs to generate a hierarchical “concept – attribute – description” knowledge graph for each category. It then introduces learnable vision and text prompt tokens that act as “domain experts” to capture fine-grained visual attributes, and a vision-conditional pooling (VCP) module to align general descriptions with instance-specific image content. Experiments on 11 datasets show state-of-the-art performance on zero-shot base-to-novel generalization, cross-dataset transfer, and few-shot classification.

### Strengths
- Strong motivation: a structured tree of attributes is a logical way to organize class information and provides richer supervisory signal than unstructured descriptions.
- The method is novel in distilling structured knowledge graphs from LLMs and reimagining prompt tokens as domain experts, combined with VCP for instance-specific alignment.
- Achieves state-of-the-art results across multiple challenging scenarios (zero-shot base-to-novel, cross-dataset, few-shot) on 11 diverse benchmarks.
- Experiments and ablations are helpful in understanding the effect of each component; the approach captures both high-level and fine-grained details, enhancing interpretability.
- The VCP module has a clear motivation (avoiding misalignment with absent attributes) and technically appears sound (Reviewer 5, score 8, notes no major issues). Extensive experimental validation supports the approach.

### Weaknesses
- **Statistical significance**: No standard deviations are reported; many improvements appear minor, so significance is unclear. Standard deviations at minimum would help.
- **LLM sensitivity**: The method’s dependence on a single LLM (e.g., GPT-3.5) is not studied. The authors should evaluate with alternative LLMs (GPT-4, PaLM, Claude) to assess robustness and report consistency.
- **Unfair comparison / training details**: Different learning rates are used per dataset, while prior methods typically use the same LR for all datasets, making the comparison somewhat unfair. Also, the use of dynamic attribute generation (vs. fixed number in baselines) gives an uneven advantage; a controlled experiment isolating this effect is missing.
- **Human review requirement**: The method requires human review to “ensure the quality of the example” (L175), contradicting prompt tuning’s goal of quick adaptation. The extent of human effort and potential bias are not addressed.
- **Missing references / related work**: The claim of being the first to use a tree of attributes is challenged by “Enhancing CLIP with GPT-4: Harnessing Visual Descriptions as Prompts” (ICCV2023W) and “Democratizing Fine-grained Visual Recognition with Large Language Models” (ICLR24). Additional missing comparisons: [a] Graph-Based Captioning…, [b] Learning Hierarchical Prompt… . These should be discussed.
- **Insufficient details on key components**:
  - The edges in the attribute tree are not explained (do they represent semantic relations like similarity/hierarchy?). No details on how edges improve alignment.
  - The VCP layer’s operation is not clearly described (step-by-step; how does it pool attributes based on visual inputs? Does it use specific pooling operations or weighted aggregation?). It pools descriptions but does not filter out entire absent attributes, only the most applicable ones.
  - The combination of VPT and CoOP (Sec 3.4) is not clarified: are there new mechanisms or innovations beyond combining prompts?
  - The set elements in L245 (e.g., examples) are not shown; the entire attribute set should be in the appendix.
- **Missing ablations**: No ablation on the effect of learnable context tokens (e.g., number, impact). Also, Table 4: it is unclear whether prior works used the “example generation” strategy; the CLIP model choice and its match to prior work should be described to interpret differences.
- **Domain generalization concern**: The tree is built in a dataset-aware manner using specific categorical priors; its domain generalization performance is not studied.
- **Presentation issues**:
  - Figure 2 lacks clarity (input/output streams unclear) and needs redrawing.
  - Typos exist: e.g., “A\{CLS}” in Eq.(7) and Fig. 1(b).
  - Table/figure captions could be more detailed.
- **Minor**: In Figure 4, it is unclear if every image is described with exactly two sentences; the meaning of “contextually relevant” is ambiguous.

**Positive minority view**: Reviewer 5 (score 8) states no major issues and considers the limitations minor and easy to correct, but the above weaknesses are retained from all reviewers.
