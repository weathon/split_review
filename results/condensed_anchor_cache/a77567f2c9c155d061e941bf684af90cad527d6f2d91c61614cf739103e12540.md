## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper makes three contributions: (1) It identifies a trade-off—decreasing the Lipschitz constant improves individual model robustness but increases adversarial transferability across models in an ensemble, undermining ensemble robustness. (2) It introduces LOTOS (Layer-wise Orthogonalization for Training rObust enSembles), a training method that promotes subspace orthogonality between corresponding affine layers in ensemble models, with negligible computational overhead. (3) On CIFAR-10/100, LOTOS improves ensemble robust accuracy against black-box attacks by 6 percentage points (p.p.) for ResNet-18 ensembles and, when combined with existing robust-ensemble methods (e.g., TRS), boosts their performance by up to 10.7 p.p.

### Strengths
- The observation that lowering Lipschitz continuity simultaneously strengthens individual models and weakens ensemble diversity is a curious and previously overlooked empirical phenomenon (R1, R2, R3, R4).  
- LOTOS is novel in its focus on orthogonalizing affine-layer subspaces, setting it apart from existing diversity-promoting methods (R1, R4).  
- The empirical evaluation is generally clear and demonstrates that LOTOS improves ensemble robustness (R1, R4).  
- While R2 acknowledged the interesting trade-off, they also raised substantial concerns about method clarity, baseline choices, and attack evaluation (see Weaknesses), rating the paper lower (5 vs. 6).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient attack evaluation**: Only PGD-50 (L∞) is tested. Reviewers ask for:  
  - More attack types, including black-box attacks, white-box attacks, AutoAttack [Croce et al., ICML 2020], and MORA [Yu et al., NeurIPS 2022] (R1, R2).  
  - Evaluation on standard suites like RobustBench (R1).  
  - Testing under different attack norms (L₂ vs L∞) and attack sizes (e.g., 8/255) (R1).  
  - White-box scenario analysis to separate robustness from attack difficulty (R2, R3).  
  - Testing when the source model in a black-box transfer attack is itself a LOTOS model (R3).  

- **Missing baseline comparisons**: DVERGE, a known robust-ensemble defense, is not included (R2, R3). Only TRS is compared; the paper would benefit from comparisons with more methods to show consistency of the claim (R4).  

- **Method clarity (Section 4)**:  
  - Equation (3) is unclear: where is \(w_i\) introduced? How does the sum of norms measure similarity? Are \(A, B\) weight matrices? Is SVD backpropagated, and if so, how efficiently? (R2).  
  - Equation (4) lacks \(k\) on the left-hand side; the role of hyperparameter \(k\) is not clearly defined (R2, R3).  
  - Theorem 4.1: the proof for \(k=1\) and its connection to making remaining singular vectors orthogonal is not explained; why is circular padding used? The link between Theorem 4.1 and Equation (4) is not well-established (R2).  
  - In Theorem 4, Equation (5): which term is dominant—\(\epsilon^2\) or the others? (R3).  
  - It is not explained why the LOTOS loss improves upon TRS, especially given that TRS has been shown to overestimate robustness [Yu et al., 2022] (R2).  

- **Soundness of the central claim**: The trade-off may be influenced by other factors (e.g., model generalization or optimization difficulty), not solely Lipschitz continuity vs. diversity (R4). The paper lacks deeper discussion of these confounding aspects and does not cite relevant theory (e.g., vulnerability-diversity decomposition, smoothness bounds on transferability) (R4).  

- **Generalizability concerns**:  
  - All experiments use ResNet-18 or DLA; it is unclear whether LOTOS extends to other architectures such as ViT (R3).  
  - The baseline models are themselves not very robust (R1). Results may not generalize to models with higher base robustness; addressing this is considered crucial by R1 for raising the score.  

- **Other issues**:  
  - Clarify whether ensemble models are trained simultaneously or sequentially (R3).  
  - The approximation of Equation (1) by population-loss differences (Line 215) is not evaluated; its accuracy is unclear (R3).  
  - For Equation (3), how is \(w_i\) determined, and why is it needed instead of directly using singular values? (R3).  
  - Potential scenario where the first singular vector of one model is orthogonal to the first of another but similar to the second – would transferability remain low? (R3).  
  - Typos: line 333 “ensebmels” (R3, R4).  
  - Definition of \(\Re\) in the appendix is missing (R4).
