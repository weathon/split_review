## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Cross-Resolution Encoding-Decoding (CRED) mechanism for Detection Transformers (DETR) that uses a low-resolution encoder and high-resolution decoder to reduce computational cost while maintaining accuracy. CRED consists of two modules: Cross-Resolution Attention Module (CRAM) transfers knowledge from low-resolution encoder outputs to high-resolution features, and One-Step Multiscale Attention (OSMA) fuses multiscale features in a single step. When applied to DN-DETR, CRED achieves similar accuracy with roughly 50% fewer FLOPs and 76% faster inference (202G FLOPs baseline) on MS-COCO 2017. Experiments also cover Deformable DETR and IMFA.

### Strengths
1. **Effective speed-accuracy tradeoff**: The low-resolution encoder and high-resolution decoder design delivers high-resolution detection accuracy at low-resolution computational cost, achieving significant FLOPs reduction and speedup (all reviewers).
2. **Well-designed modules**: CRAM transfers knowledge between resolutions, and OSMA fuses multiscale features in a single step (R1, R2).
3. **Comprehensive ablation studies**: The paper thoroughly analyzes the individual contributions of CRAM and OSMA, providing insight into design choices (R2, R3).
4. **Scalability**: CRED is effective across different DETR variants (DN-DETR, Deformable DETR, IMFA) and backbone architectures (R3).
5. **Well-founded motivation**: Reducing the computational burden of high-resolution detection by combining low- and high-level features is a reasonable approach (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and theoretical depth**: The design lacks technical novelty; similar ideas combining low- and high-resolution or multiscale features have been explored in prior work (e.g., Zhang et al. 2023a, Zhao et al. 2024b, Li et al. 2023). CRAM is structurally similar to FPN, appearing as an adaptation rather than a breakthrough. The paper would benefit from a deeper theoretical analysis of why transferring low-resolution encoder information to high-resolution decoder inputs improves performance (R1, R3, R4).
- **Limited evaluation and comparisons**: The method is evaluated only on MS-COCO 2017. On this benchmark, CRED achieves only competitive results (Table 2) with modest improvement over the base DN-DETR. No comparisons are made with recent efficient DETR variants such as RT-DETR (CVPR 2024), sparse transformers, or vision transformers, which are designed for speed and efficiency. This weakens the assessment of CRED’s advantages (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Small object detection**: Improvements in average precision for small objects (APS) are modest in some configurations. The paper lacks qualitative visual comparisons with state-of-the-art methods for small objects to demonstrate CRED’s claimed advantage (R3, R4).
- **Limited qualitative analysis**: Detection visualizations (Figures 7–10) do not include detailed analysis of challenging cases (e.g., cluttered backgrounds, occlusions) or side-by-side comparisons with other methods (R3, R4).
- **Missing implementation details and potential efficiency bottlenecks**: (a) OSMA aggregates multiscale features along the channel dimension, but different scales typically have different channel counts (high-resolution features have fewer channels); the paper does not clarify how this discrepancy is handled (e.g., whether channels for low-resolution features are reduced). (b) OSMA’s local aggregation may involve many memory-discontinuous operations, which could limit actual speed gains; hardware efficiency of this module needs analysis. (c) In Figure 1, CRAM takes input from Stage-4 or Stage-3; it is unclear why Stage-2 or a combination of Stage-4 and Stage-3 is not used—additional ablation on the encoder stage input is needed (R1, R4).
- **Lack of in-depth analysis of tradeoffs**: The paper provides FLOPs and FPS metrics but lacks a deeper discussion of the tradeoffs between accuracy, computational cost, and inference speed. It also does not justify why augmenting global information to high-resolution features via CRAM is preferable to sparse sampling methods like IFMA (R2).
- **Generalizability**: The method is only evaluated on DETR-based detectors; its applicability to other advanced detection architectures is not demonstrated (R2).
