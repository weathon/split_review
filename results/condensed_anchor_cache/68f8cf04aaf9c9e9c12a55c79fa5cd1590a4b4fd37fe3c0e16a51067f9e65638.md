## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Dual Caption Preference Optimization (DCPO) to align text-to-image diffusion models with human preferences. DCPO addresses two issues in existing preference datasets: conflict distribution (overlap between preferred and less-preferred image distributions) and irrelevant prompts (using the same prompt for both images). The method introduces separate captions for each image via three strategies—captioning, perturbation, and a hybrid method—and creates the Pick-Double Caption dataset from Pick-a-Pic v2. Experiments on SD 2.1 show DCPO outperforms SD 2.1, SFT-Chosen, Diffusion-DPO, and MaPO across Pickscore, HPSv2.1, GenEval, CLIPscore, and ImageReward, with additional side-by-side GPT-4o evaluation.

### Strengths
- Well‑organized paper, easy to follow, with clear figures (e.g., Figure 2). The story is complete: hypotheses are proposed and validated with ablation studies in Sec 3.3.
- Clear problem setup and sufficient reproducibility details.
- The dual‑caption framework is reasonable: it effectively addresses overlapping distributions in existing datasets.
- Demonstrated improvements across multiple automatic metrics (Pickscore, CLIPscore) and benchmarks (GenEval), plus side‑by‑side evaluation using GPT‑4o as judge.
- Extensive quantitative and qualitative analysis with comprehensive metrics and several baselines.
- Sufficient theoretical support for the motivation; the identified issues are well‑motivated.
- The method is relatively straightforward to implement.
- Experiments are conducted on a popular open‑source model (SD 2.1) with a wide set of evaluation metrics.

### Weaknesses
- **Generalization concerns:** The current positive/negative samples only address alignment; it is unclear how the approach generalizes when the model is already well‑aligned and finer‑grained preferences are needed. Moreover, the paper does not evaluate on other diffusion models (e.g., SD XL) or widely‑used benchmarks like LAION‑2B and MSCOCO, raising questions about general applicability.
- **Motivation and theoretical grounding:**  
  – The “conflict distribution” issue is argued to be a natural consequence of improving model quality—overlap increases as both preferred and less‑preferred images become closer to real human preference. It is not clear why this is a problem that needs solving.  
  – The “irrelevant prompt” issue is described as a problem, but in the original DPO objective (Eqn 1), decreasing log p_θ(x^l | z^l) is a desired behavior (making the model less likely to generate the less‑preferred image), not an issue.  
  – By changing the captions, the pair becomes two separate samples, losing the original DPO formulation. No theoretical connection (e.g., upper bound, minimization guarantee) is provided to justify why solving the new problem yields better results than original DPO. The connection and differences to KTO should also be explicitly discussed.
- **Caption dependency and quality:** Performance heavily depends on the quality of generated captions, especially for less‑preferred images, where creating effective captions without straying out‑of‑distribution remains challenging.
- **Incremental visual impact:** The visual differences between images generated by DCPO and baseline methods are often subtle (e.g., Figure 1), making it difficult to assess practical impact despite quantitative gains.
- **Unclear implementation details:**  
  – How exactly are the preferred and less‑preferred images obtained? Are human annotations used?  
  – The distinction between medium and strong perturbation is not clearly quantified. Is there a controlled generation process? Why is a medium level needed if weak and strong exist?
- **Evaluation with GPT‑4o:** It is not specified whether images are shown together or separately, and whether the order of presentation matters for the evaluation.
- **Reorganization needed:** Explanations of conflict distribution and irrelevant prompts in the abstract and introduction are obscure and hard to grasp without reading the methods section. Concrete examples would improve clarity.
- **Consolidation of issues:** The two issues (conflict distribution and irrelevant prompts) could be considered two aspects of the same problem—a single prompt corresponding to two images leads to ambiguous optimization—and could be consolidated.
- **Duplicate references:** Several papers appear multiple times in the reference list; deduplication is needed.
