## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the Vision-guided Expression Diffusion Model (VIE-DM) for referring expression generation (REG), aiming to produce diverse, high-quality synonymous expressions that can be used to augment referring expression comprehension (REC) datasets. VIE-DM consists of a vision-text condition (VTC) module and a transformer decoder to address feature discrepancies between visual and textual domains. Experiments on five datasets (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg, Flickr30k, Refclef) demonstrate improved REG quality and diversity, and augmented data boosts REC performance. Reviewers generally find the idea of applying diffusion models to REG innovative, but raise concerns about novelty, missing comparisons, lacking failure analysis, and insufficient justification of key design choices.

### Strengths
- First study to introduce language models using diffusion models into REG and REC tasks; the approach is innovative and explores an underexplored area.
- The vision-text condition (VTC) module and token selection strategy effectively enhance alignment between visual and textual information.
- Extensive experiments and ablation studies on five datasets validate generalization and effectiveness, achieving state-of-the-art results.
- The method is described in detail with well-founded motivations.
- The paper is clearly written and easy to follow (one reviewer).  
*Note: One reviewer (score 8) was notably more positive, stating no obvious disadvantages, while the other three reviewers (score 6) identified several weaknesses.*

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and contributions**: The proposed method is a straightforward combination of existing techniques (Cross-Attention, Token Selection, Minimum Bayes Risk, classifier-free guidance). The specific effect of the diffusion model is not isolated; existing REG baselines use transformer-LSTM or CNN-LSTM frameworks, but VIE-DM uses a transformer-based decoder plus diffusion. Without comparing against a pure transformer decoder without diffusion, the contribution of diffusion alone is unclear.
- **Missing comparisons**:  
  - Recent work CCL (Cycle-Consistency Learning for Captioning and Grounding, AAAI 2024) uses a similar idea of enhancing REC through REG but is not compared.  
  - The idea of amplifying REC data using REG methods dates back to Mao+ (CVPR 2016). The paper does not show how much REC accuracy improves when augmenting with other REG methods, so the contribution to REC is unclear.  
  - Augmentation using VIE-DM leads only to limited improvement (Table 5); a direct comparison with other REG-based augmentation is needed.
- **Lack of failure analysis and instability**: Diffusion data generation is typically unstable, but the paper does not discuss failure cases. In Figure 3, some generated responses are erroneous (e.g., “recover” in first and last samples of the third row). The authors need to analyze how they ensure quality.
- **Missing statistics**: Model parameters, training time, and inference time are not reported. The number of samples in each augmented dataset is not provided.
- **Insufficient explanation of ablation results**:  
  - In Table 1, removing CFG (classifier-free guidance) improves performance on some metrics, but this is not explained.  
  - The definition of CFG is missing in Table 1 (only guessed by reviewers).  
  - The claim of “robust data diversity” based on Table 1 (Meteor, CIDEr similarity) is misleading; the evidence from Table 3 is more convincing.
- **Writing and presentation issues**:  
  - The paper lacks in-depth analysis of the reasons behind observed patterns in results.  
  - Some sections are verbose (e.g., Subsection 3.6, second sentence redundant).  
  - Equations 9 and 10 differ only by one symbol and could be improved.  
  - Typos: missing period on line 82, incorrect number on line 360.  
  - A limitations section is missing; including one would provide a more balanced perspective.
