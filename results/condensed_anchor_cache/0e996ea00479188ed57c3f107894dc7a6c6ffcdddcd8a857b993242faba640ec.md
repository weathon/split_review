## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes ParetoPrompt, a reinforcement learning method for multi-objective prompt optimization. It leverages dominance relationships between prompts to learn a policy model using preference-based losses, avoiding predefined scalarization. Experiments show that ParetoPrompt outperforms existing algorithms on multiple objectives. Reviewers agree the problem is important and the approach is novel, but note limitations in experimental scope, missing comparisons, and need for clarity on several design choices. Reviewer 4 was notably more positive (score 8 vs 6), highlighting the practical importance and novel loss design, but also raised the most detailed criticisms.

### Strengths
- Introducing RLHF (or preference-based learning) into Pareto optimization for prompts is novel and inspiring (R1, R3).
- The special treatment for non-dominated prompts contributes to novel design of the overall loss function (R4).
- Clear visual illustration of the results lying on the Pareto front (R4).
- Reported experiments show the proposed algorithm outperforms baseline methods under a variety of metrics (R2, R3).
- The Pareto prompt optimization problem is practical and well-motivated (R3, R4).
- The presentation of the algorithm design is clear and great (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing baselines**: More recent baseline methods should be compared (e.g., arXiv:2406.12845) (R2). Also, no comparison with existing prompt optimization works like [1,2,3,4] (R3). The approach should be compared to Lin et al. (2024, "Prompt Optimization with Human Feedback", ICML 2024 Workshop) since it also considers human preference in prompt optimization (R3).
- **Theoretical justification**: Lack of theoretical justification that the training process (end of Section 3) yields Pareto-optimal prompts (R2).
- **Motivation for RLHF**: The motivation for using RLHF in Pareto optimization, as opposed to standard Pareto algorithms, could be further elaborated (R1).
- **Optimization efficiency**: The paper lacks a detailed report on querying budgets or optimization efficiency, critical for assessing practical performance (R1).
- **Complexity**: The learning procedure for the reward model appears complex and may be challenging to implement or adapt across diverse applications (R1).
- **Limited experiments**: Experiments are not extensive enough – no experiment with more than 3 objectives, and only models like BERT and GPT-2 are used (R4). Include results with a greater number of objective functions to demonstrate broader applicability (R1).
- **Choice of objectives**: The experiment design on the choice of objectives can be improved. Using prompt fluency as an objective is counterintuitive; output fluency, conciseness, informativeness, style alignment would be more meaningful. This makes the experiment results less convincing (R4).
- **Non-dominated prompts dominating loss**: Since non-dominated prompts consist of the majority of sampled prompts, will they dominate the loss function? If most prompts are non-dominated, optimizing gives similar rewards for almost all prompts. Please clarify (R4).
- **Diversifying non-dominated prompts**: Clarify how the loss in equation (4) encourages “diversifying non-dominated prompts” as stated in line 257 (R4).
- **Choosing a single prompt**: From Figure 3, ParetoPrompt produces many prompts on the Pareto front. How should one choose a single prompt to deploy in the system? (R4).
- **Counterintuitive loss behavior**: It seems strange that the percentage of non-dominated samples increases with training but the loss also keeps increasing (R4).
- **Scalability with objectives**: When the number of objectives increases, more conflicts (non-dominant pairs) are expected. This is discussed as a limitation in the last section but should be raised earlier. What is the rough maximum number of objectives ParetoPrompt can handle in practice? (R4).
- **Missing related work on preference optimization for prompt optimization**: A missing related work [1] on using preference optimization for prompt optimization (R4). Also insufficient discussion of related works on prompt optimization in general (R3).
- **Training process details**: In the proposed training process, how to estimate the objectives of the corresponding outputs y1 and y2? (R2).
- **Typo**: Line 404, “ouput” → “output” (R4).
