## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes OC-CLIP, a method to improve compositional understanding of CLIP-like models by integrating a binding module that connects a scene graph from text with a slot-structured image representation. A structured similarity score (object scoring + relationship scoring) is used. The approach uses an LLM to extract object and relation tags from captions, then fine-tunes CLIP with additional supervision. Evaluated on ARO, SugarCrepe, GQA, Winoground. Reviewer scores are mixed (5,6,5,5); one reviewer (R2, confidence 5) found the method simple and effective, while others (R1,R3,R4, confidences 3–5) raised significant concerns about missing baselines, ablations, zero-shot evaluation, and methodology.

### Strengths
- The binding module and structured similarity score (object and relationship scoring functions) are well-motivated, technically sound, and a reasonable way to model scene graph and visual slots relationships (R1, R4).
- The method is simple and intuitive (R2).
- Using an LLM to generate object/relation tags for scene graph understanding is an interesting idea (R2).
- Performance improvements are reported on multiple compositional benchmarks (R2, R3, R4).
- The paper is clearly organized with an explicit problem definition (R4).
- Compositional reasoning remains an important problem, and the explicit alignment modules for objects and relations are interesting (R3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient experimental comparisons**.  
   - Missing baselines: DAC, SVLC, sDCI, Tagalign (R2, R3, R4).  
   - Missing benchmark datasets: VL-Checklist, Flickr/COCO subsets of ARO, sDCI dataset (R4).  
   - The real data experiments fine-tune on supervised human-annotated data from the same datasets used for evaluation (ARO, SugarCrepe, COCO, GQA), unlike hard-negative methods that train on unconstrained internet data (R3).  
   - No zero-shot evaluation (e.g., ELEVATER) to assess degradation of general applicability after fine-tuning (R3, R4).  
   - No downstream tasks: zero-shot classification, video action recognition, geo-localization, text-to-image retrieval, VQA (R1).  
   - Synthetic data experiments are limited and less convincing (R3).

2. **Ablation studies completely missing**.  
   - No ablation of the proposed components (binding module, object/relation losses, noise register tokens) (R3, R4).  
   - No analysis of potential collapse in Eq.3 (whether f_s and f_o just extract the “r” part from the concatenation) (R3).  
   - No ablation of the LLM parsing quality or comparison of different LLMs/cues (R2, R4).  
   - No ablation of object/relation extraction models (R4).  
   - No analysis of multiple hyperparameters in the loss function (R4).

3. **Methodology concerns**.  
   - Using an LLM for parsing and training with object+relation classification loss is considered too straightforward to be a contribution (R2).  
   - The “rel” loss is very similar to hard-negative losses used in other works, contradicting the (unjustified) reasoning that hard-negative approaches are detrimental; also the rel loss is not ablated (R3).  
   - Reliance on LLM parsing is not ablated; parsing real captions (e.g., from unconstrained data like LAION/CC) is much harder than negative augmentation, contrary to the authors’ reasoning (R3).  
   - No method is provided to ensure scene graph accuracy; models like Llama3 themselves have misalignment in compositional reasoning (R4).  
   - How would MLLMs (e.g., InternVL, LLaVA) perform as text parsers vs. text-only LLMs? (R2).

4. **Missing computational overhead analysis**.  
   - No breakdown of FLOPs introduced by the binding module and how it scales with input resolution and batch size (R1).  
   - No memory footprint analysis (R1).  
   - No estimate of the computational cost of running an LLM to process Visual Genome data (R2).

5. **No analysis of CLIP’s original capabilities after fine-tuning**.  
   - The paper does not examine whether CLIP retains its original image understanding, zero-shot performance, or general applicability after being fine-tuned with scene graph supervision (R4).

One reviewer (R2, score 6, confidence 5) was notably more positive, highlighting the method’s simplicity and large accuracy gains, while others (R1,R3,R4, scores 5, confidences 3–5) expressed stronger criticisms regarding missing evaluations, ablations, and methodological justification.
