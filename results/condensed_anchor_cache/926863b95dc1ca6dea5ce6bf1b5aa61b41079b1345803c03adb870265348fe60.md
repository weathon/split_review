## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper investigates Learning Rate Rewinding (LRR) as a variant of Iterative Magnitude Pruning (IMP) for finding lottery tickets. The authors disentangle mask learning and parameter optimization, presenting theoretical evidence in a single-hidden-neuron setting that LRR succeeds more often than IMP because it can escape problematic initial sign configurations by inheriting sign information from a well-trained overparameterized model. Empirical experiments on CIFAR10, CIFAR100, and Tiny ImageNet with ResNet18/ResNet50 validate that sign flipping is a key characteristic of LRR, making it more effective for both mask identification and sparse network optimization. Overall, the paper is clearly written, offers new insights into sign flipping, and provides valuable ablation studies, though performance differences between LRR and IMP are often small and the evaluation is limited in architectural and domain scope.

### Strengths

- The paper offers a clear rationale for LRR’s effectiveness through the lens of weight sign flipping—a pioneering exploration of sign flipping in sparse neural networks. The theoretical evidence (single hidden neuron) shows LRR advantages from flexible mask sign configurations compared to IMP.
- Ablation studies are carefully designed to decouple two roles of LRR: (i) sparse network optimization and (ii) discovering a good sparse mask.
- The research addresses an important, timely problem with high impact on improving efficiency and decreasing cost of sparse training.
- Experimental validation spans multiple benchmarks (CIFAR10, CIFAR100, Tiny ImageNet) and architectures (ResNet18, ResNet50), consistently supporting LRR’s superiority.
- The writing is well-structured and clear; the paper covers a fair amount of relevant prior work.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited architecture/dataset scope:** Empirical evaluation is mainly on ResNet architectures with CIFAR10/100. It remains unclear whether the trends hold for other architectures (e.g., VGG, Transformers) or domains (e.g., natural language processing, object detection). Reviewers request results on larger-scale datasets like ImageNet and more diverse architectures to strengthen generalizability.
- **Convergence and computational cost of IMP cycles:** One reviewer questions whether both WR (weight rewinding) and LRR are fully converged in the pruning cycles (Figure 3). If the optimal mask is only attained at the end of training epochs, reducing the computational cost of IMP (WR & LRR) may be challenging.
- **Figure interpretation concerns:**
  - In Figure 4, the “LRR with IMP mask” (blue) shows no significant improvement over WR (orange) except for CIFAR10 at moderate sparsity; the implication of “LRR w/ BN rewind” (yellow) in the context of flexible LRR training is unclear.
  - In Figures 3 and 6, results are limited to CIFAR10 and CIFAR100; the authors note CIFAR100 may not fully meet expectations due to complexity, but no additional experiment supports or rejects the hypothesis.
  - The meaning of the light blue line in Figure 11 is not explained.
  - It is unclear how Figure 12 supports the claim that “LRR enables considerably more sign flips than IMP and thus improving mask identification and parameter optimization.”
- **Lack of exploration beyond magnitude pruning:** Only magnitude pruning is studied. It is an open question whether LRR’s advantages generalize to other pruning strategies (e.g., based on gradient information or activation patterns).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:**
  - Sensitivity to pruning ratio is not examined; this could impact the observed performance gains.
  - The influence of the learning rate schedule on LRR’s performance is not investigated. One reviewer asks whether LRR’s benefits hold across different learning rate regimes or require specific schedules, and whether hyperparameter sensitivity hinders practical application.
  - The paper shows LRR benefits from overparameterization; one reviewer asks whether excessive overparameterization could negatively impact LRR’s performance.
- **Minor issues:** Missing reference on p.3; typo on p.7 (“LRR is improves parameter optimization”).
- **Disagreement among reviewers:** While one reviewer found no significant shortcomings, other reviewers raised the concerns above, with some questioning whether the figures fully support the claims. The performance differences between LRR and IMP are acknowledged as small in some cases (reviewer 3).
