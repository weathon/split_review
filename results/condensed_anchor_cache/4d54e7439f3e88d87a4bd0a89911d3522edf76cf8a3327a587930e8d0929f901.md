## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces RL4CO, a unified Reinforcement Learning library for combinatorial optimization (CO) that modularly implements several auto-regressive (AR) methods (e.g., AM, POMO) for routing problems (TSP, CVRP). It aims to improve reproducibility and fair comparison, and benchmarks these methods on zero-shot performance, generalization, and adaptability. The paper reports interesting findings: some recent methods underperform on out-of-distribution data, and policy gradient methods outperform value-based methods. However, multiple reviewers note that the library is narrow in scope (only AR methods, only routing problems) and overclaims the “RL4CO” name. One reviewer (score 3) considers the contribution insufficient for a research track due to lack of novel algorithms, while others (scores 5) find it a useful benchmark/tool but with limited coverage and missing comparisons.

### Strengths
- Unified toolbox for implementing and evaluating RL methods for CO, addressing reproducibility and fair comparison (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Modular, easy-to-use interface built with state-of-the-art software practices (R2, R3).
- Comprehensive empirical comparison across different models, problems, and search algorithms (R2, R4).
- Interesting results: some SOTA methods (e.g., those based on AM/POMO) underperform under out-of-distribution settings; policy gradient methods generally outperform value-based methods (R1, R4).
- Well-written, well-motivated, and easy to follow (R1, R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty for research track**: One reviewer (score 3) noted the paper does not introduce fundamentally new algorithms, methodologies, or problem formulations, and aligns more with a benchmark/tool track than a research track (R1).
- **Narrow RL formulation**: The library covers only auto-regressive methods (Equation 1–3) and excludes other RL paradigms for CO, such as neighborhood search (e.g., local‑rewrite [1,2]), one‑step RL (e.g., DIMES), or solution improvement methods. Reviewers questioned whether such methods can be integrated (R2, R3).
- **Limited problem scope**: Only routing problems (TSP, CVRP) are addressed; other classical CO problems (set covering, maximum independent set, bin‑packing, job scheduling, mixed integer programming) are not covered. Reviewers suggested “RL4Routing” would be more appropriate than “RL4CO” (R2, R3, R4).
- **Limited baselines and instance sizes**: Only three RL methods are compared, and experiments are limited to TSP/CVRP50, which is considered small. Larger instances (e.g., TSP1000, TSP10000) and non‑autoregressive / non‑RL methods (which reportedly achieve faster inference and lower optimal gap) are missing (R3, R4).
- **Method classification issues**: The footnote misclassifies DIMES as a supervised method, indicating inconsistency in how methods are categorized (R2).
- **Missing analysis**: Reviewers noted a lack of insights explaining why policy gradient methods outperform value-based methods in neural CO, and a general lack of organizing principles to unify the implemented methods effectively (R1, R4).
- **Overclaiming**: The title “RL4CO” implies broad coverage, but the library in practice covers only a small subset of RL methods and CO problems (R2, R3, R4).
