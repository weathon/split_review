## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a zero-shot and few-shot detection method for AI-generated images by analyzing biases inherent in the manifold of a pre-trained diffusion model. It uses score-function, curvature, and gradient approximations to derive a scalar criterion for classification, and extends to few-shot via mixture-of-experts. Experiments across 20 generative models show improved performance over existing approaches. One reviewer was especially positive about the contribution (rating 8), while others were more cautiously positive (rating 6). All reviewers agree the idea is novel, the theoretical grounding is interesting, and empirical results are strong, but raise concerns about theoretical assumptions, approximation reliability, robustness, and presentation.

### Strengths
- The zero-shot and few-shot capability makes the method practical and reduces reliance on large labeled datasets.
- The theoretical perspective—leveraging manifold-induced biases from pre-trained diffusion models—is novel and interesting, and may inspire further research.
- The methodology is well-articulated; equations and definitions support the approach, and Figures 1 and 2 are intuitive and easy to understand.
- Empirical results are promising: the method outperforms current state-of-the-art approaches in both zero-shot and few-shot settings across a diverse set of generative models, including GANs, diffusion models, and commercial tools.
- The work provides a sound theoretical foundation by integrating manifold analysis with diffusion models, advancing the field of generated image detection.

### Weaknesses
- The assumption that generated samples are more likely to be stable local maxima on the learned manifold is not fully justified. The paper lacks a rigorous definition of “stable local maxima” in the high-dimensional latent space of diffusion models, and does not analyze the conditions under which this assumption holds or explore potential counterexamples. A more detailed mathematical or empirical rationale is needed.
- The method relies heavily on approximations in score-function and curvature estimations (e.g., Eq.5, 16-18). There is limited discussion of the tightness of these approximations, no error bounds, and no analysis of how approximation error scales with step size and dimensionality. The sensitivity of detection performance to the choice of approximation parameters should be investigated.
- The use of a pre-trained SD1.4 introduces potential bias towards its training distribution. It is unclear whether the method generalizes to images generated by models with significantly different latent space characteristics or trained on different datasets. The authors should also test more recent diffusion models (e.g., SDXL, SD3) to see if this improves performance.
- The presentation of mathematical derivations is dense and difficult to follow. Illustrative examples (e.g., a simple 2D visualization) and more intuitive explanations would help; detailed proofs could be moved to the appendix.
- Implementation details in Section 4.3 need elaboration to enhance readability and reproducibility.
- The method depends on hyperparameters such as perturbation strength and number of spherical noises. Robustness to these parameters across different models should be analyzed, and guidelines for selection provided.
- The impact of image post-processing other than JPEG compression (e.g., augmentation, denoising, flipping) on detection performance is not evaluated.
- It is unclear why the curvature/gradient metric works well for models where score functions are not directly applicable (e.g., CycleGAN). The connection should be clarified.
- The need for the few-shot setting is not fully justified. Since zero-shot scenarios are more challenging and often more realistic, the authors should explain why few-shot is necessary and why zero-shot alone would not suffice as a compelling approach.
- Typographical errors: inconsistent use of \(\mathcal{N}\) and \(N\) (L149, L153); inconsistent use of \(\mathbb{R}\) and \(R\) (L146, L170); missing LaTeX for \(\log\) (L157); missing backticks for upper quotes (L191); inconsistent use of “Sec.” and “Section”; unexpected equation at L298–299.
- Citation issues on page 7: footnotes and page numbers are not correctly referenced.
- Reviewer 1 also raises a concern about the potential computational cost of high-dimensional manifold analysis; an analysis of inference time and memory requirements is requested.
