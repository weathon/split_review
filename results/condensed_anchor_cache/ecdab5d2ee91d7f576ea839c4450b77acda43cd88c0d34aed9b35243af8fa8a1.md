## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper advocates reinstating ReLU activation in LLMs for inference efficiency. ReLU induces high activation sparsity in FFN layers, which can be exploited to reduce computation. Authors show that training from scratch with ReLU yields performance comparable to non-ReLU activations (GELU, SiLU). They also propose "relufication": replacing activations in pretrained non-ReLU LLMs with ReLU and fine-tuning to recover performance. Analysis reveals aggregated sparsity—consecutive tokens reuse a subset of neurons—which enables optimizations like faster speculative decoding. The paper claims up to 3× inference computation reduction with minimal performance trade-offs.

### Strengths
- Tackles the important, under-explored issue of activation function choice from an inference efficiency perspective.
- The solution is simple (ReLU) and practical, including relufication to avoid costly retraining from scratch.
- The idea of similar sparsity patterns among consecutive tokens (aggregated sparsity) is novel.
- Evaluation on realistic benchmarks (HEML) with representative datasets; shows performance depends on compute/data scaling, not activation function, supported by scaling laws (Kaplan et al.; Hoffmann et al.).
- Thorough evaluation of activation impact under both pretraining and finetuning; exploration of ReLU in attention layers (Stage 2) is interesting, even if it generally hurts ICL performance.

### Weaknesses
- The observation that replacing GELU/SiLU with ReLU only marginally influences performance is not new and has been discussed in prior work (as cited by Reviewer 3 [1][2]).
- The argument that longer training can compensate for any performance gap is not well supported: Figure 2 (bottom row) only uses three downstream datasets; accuracy is still growing, making it unclear whether longer training converges and how much is needed. Perplexity might be a more indicative metric.
- Evaluation focuses primarily on zero-shot and ICL scenarios; missing generation tasks that could further demonstrate how activations affect performance.
- No empirical comparisons to other size-reduction methods (e.g., pruning techniques as referenced in the reviews) are provided, which could be seen as competing approaches.
- The sparsification mechanism requires underlying hardware/software support for sparse BLAS operations, which is not universally available. This shortcoming is not discussed in detail, and latency measurements are absent from the main text.
- No link to implementation is provided, making it difficult to reproduce or apply the approach.
- In Section 5.1, the concept of “loading new weights” is unclear—standard inference frameworks like vLLM pre-load all weights to GPU HBM; further clarification is needed.
- One reviewer (score 6) notes that the accuracy drop after replacing activations may not be as marginal as claimed, though it could represent a practical trade-off. This contrasts with the majority view (scores 8,8) that the impact is negligible.
- Questions that overlap with missing analyses: (a) Different layers show different aggregated sparsity ratios (Figure 7(a)) – did the authors use the same γ for all layers? Could per-layer γ recover more performance? (b) How could relufication finetuning be further optimized to fully recover performance? (c) What are the actual latency speedups on the tested hardware, and what prerequisites are needed (e.g., custom NN implementations) to realize the full benefits?
