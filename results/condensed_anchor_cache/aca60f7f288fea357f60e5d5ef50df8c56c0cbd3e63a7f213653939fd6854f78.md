## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a pretraining method that leverages coarse-grained set-level labels to improve instance-level classification, then fine-tunes on fine-grained labels. Theoretical analysis derives conditions for fast excess risk rate. Experiments on natural and histopathology datasets show gains, including 13% accuracy improvement on histopathology benchmarks. One reviewer (R2, score 5) had lower enthusiasm about novelty and contribution (rated fair), while R1 and R3 (scores 6) rated contribution as good.

### Strengths
- Novel idea: using set-level coarse-grained labels as a pretraining task for instance-level classification, bridging the gap between instance-level and set-level classification. (R1, R2, R3)
- Strong empirical performance: e.g., 13% improvement over strongest baseline on histopathology benchmarks. (R1)
- Theoretical analysis provides conditions for fast excess risk rate, adding robustness and credibility. (R1, R2, R3)
- Clear articulation of method and results on both natural and histopathology datasets. (R2)
- Potential for real-world scenarios where set-level labels offer richer information than single-instance labels. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- Missing baselines against more recent self-supervised learning (SSL) methods such as BYOL, DINO, or DINOv2; current comparisons are limited to SimCLR and SimSiam. (R1, R2)
- Need ablation on how similarity between pretraining dataset/coarse labels and target dataset/fine labels affects effectiveness. Example: CIFAR-100 pretrain → medical target; how does proposed method compare to supervised ImageNet pretrain or SSL? (R1)
- Need ablation on scenario where the same related dataset provides both fine and coarse labels: which pretraining strategy (supervised on fine labels vs. set-level coarse pretraining) is preferable? (R1)
- Choice of coarse labels (TCGA, NCT) is insufficiently justified. Why these umbrella terms, and what advantages do they offer over other labelings? (R2)
- Novelty insufficiently articulated: what specifically sets this application/implementation apart from standard pretrain-then-finetune pipelines? (R2)
- Missing evaluation on standardized few-shot learning benchmarks (e.g., Mini-ImageNet 5-way 1-shot) to validate generalizability. (R2)
- Missing ablation without coarse labels or with other pretrained models to isolate the benefit of coarse-label pretraining. (R2)
- Insufficient training details: image augmentations, learning schedules, optimizer settings; specifically, how SupCon is trained (supervised contrastive learning performing worse than CE) needs explanation. (R2, R3)
- Confusion in figures: Fig. 2(a) and Fig. 1(a) are unclear about whether input samples all belong to the same set-level label; Fig. 3 is referenced before Fig. 2. (R3)
- Most hyperlinks (other than page 1) are not working. (R3)
- Rationale for choosing SupCon and FSP as pretraining methods is missing. (R2)
- Method needs a clearer diagram to illustrate whether it is simply pretrain on coarse labels then fine-tune, or involves additional components. (R2)
- Disagreement among reviewers: R2 rated contribution as fair (score 5) and questioned novelty, while R1 and R3 rated contribution as good (scores 6).
