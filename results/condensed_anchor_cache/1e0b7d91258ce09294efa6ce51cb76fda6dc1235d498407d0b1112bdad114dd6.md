## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies improvements to the ReFlow procedure for flow-matching generative models, organizing the design space into training dynamics, learning, and inference. Through thorough ablations on CIFAR-10, AFHQv2, FFHQ (all 64×64), and ImageNet-64, the authors propose seven modifications (e.g., initialization from EDM, loss reweighting, a new high-pass loss, refinements on noise-image pairs, ODE discretization) and achieve state-of-the-art FID scores using only 9 neural function evaluations. The paper generalizes the ReFlow training loss and aims to stay within the theoretical framework of rectified flows.

### Strengths
- Well-presented and easy to follow; the design space is clearly communicated and the ablations are convincing (R1, R3).
- Provides a useful aggregation of design choices from the diffusion model literature applied to flow matching, with all knobs considered in one place (R2).
- The inclusion of a new high-pass filter metric (PIPs) is a nice contribution, potentially well-motivated for domains where specific frequency regimes matter (e.g., scientific generative models) (R2).
- State-of-the-art results for fast neural ODE generation, with lower FID and lower straightness compared to other diffusion- and flow-based methods (R3, R4).
- The paper can be very helpful for practitioners facing similar choices when training their models (R4).
- The extent of the ablations is thorough; the reviewer acknowledges the size of this experimental undertaking (R2).

### Weaknesses
- The contributions are largely incremental; many innovations are directly inspired by prior work (e.g., loss normalization from Karras et al., 2023b). Each individual improvement may be labeled as incremental, though the paper as a whole is valuable to the community (R2, R4).
- The paper lacks a deeper theoretical investigation into *why* the proposed tricks help. For example, loss normalization is shown effective but no theoretical justification is given for why normalizing w.r.t. both $x_t$ and $t$ is superior or why the specific form of time-dependent scaling is optimal (R2).
- The paper is limited to the image domain and does not discuss generalizability to other data modalities (e.g., time-series, point clouds) (R1, R2).
- **Terminology issue**: The paper frequently uses “OT ODE” and “OT map”, but iterative rectified flow does not generally converge to optimal transport in dimensions ≥ 2. The algorithm corresponds to 2-rectified flow, not an OT solution. The paper should be more precise and avoid misleading theoretical claims (R2, R3). Reviewer 2 also notes that the argument about OT in §3.3.1 (harder task) actually holds for *any* Monge coupling, not just OT.
- **Proposition 2 is confusing**: The first line appears to have a typo—it likely should refer to the DM loss, not the GFM loss. The lack of clarity makes it difficult to assess the validity of the proposition (R2).
- **False theoretical claim**: The statement “This again ensures that the loss is minimized when $D_{\theta}$ outputs the posterior mean” (line 184) is incorrect when $\phi$ is far from identity. Actually $D^* = \phi^{-1}(\mathbb{E}[\phi(x_0) | x_t])$, which may be far from the desired mean. This does not invalidate the empirical findings but does undermine the theoretical justification offered (R4).
- **Projected pairs**: This step adds significant complexity (GAN training + Sinkhorn algorithm) while yielding only marginal FID improvements (max 0.02, not statistically significant in Table 6). The paper does not discuss sensitivity to hyperparameters in the GAN or Sinkhorn, which further limits practical utility (R3). Reviewer 4 also asks about simpler alternatives and requests more details on solving the projection problem (Subsec. D.3).
- Reviewer 4's questions: (1) How many ReFlow iterations are used in Simple ReFlow (§3.7)? (2) How are backward pairs, forward pairs, and projected pairs combined (e.g., for Table 8)? (3) Which improvement is most responsible for the performance boost? The authors should comment on this.
- Reviewer 2 asks whether the bias in rectification under different procedures can be measured (difficult but would be informative). Also, the remark in Appendix C about rectification vs. distillation is flawed because rectification also fits an imperfect teacher model; this remark does not contribute much.
- The paper misses a citation to stochastic interpolants, which are equivalent to FM and rectified flow (R2).
- Minor: typo “we  we” at top of page 5 (R2).
