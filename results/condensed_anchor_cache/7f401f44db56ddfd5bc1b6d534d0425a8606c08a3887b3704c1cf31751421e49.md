## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces CoELA, a modular framework integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) for decentralized multi-agent cooperation in embodied environments (C-WAH, TDW-MAT). CoELA uses five modules (perception, three memory, planning, execution) and two separate LLMs for communication and planning. Experiments show GPT-4-driven CoELA outperforms planning-based baselines; a fine-tuned open LM (CoLLAMA) also shows promise. A human-agent study indicates natural-language communication improves trust and cooperation. Reviewers generally find the approach promising but raise significant concerns. One reviewer was more positive (rating 8), highlighting the strong motivation, careful design, and thorough analysis. Three other reviewers (ratings 6) while acknowledging the potential, point to major weaknesses in generality, missing ablations, unclear module contributions, insufficient baselines, limited task diversity, incomplete details on execution/perception modules, problematic manual skill library design, and communication limitations not fully addressed.

### Strengths
- **Strong motivation and problem framing**: The paper addresses important real-world challenges in multi-agent cooperation—decentralized control, raw sensory observations, costly communication, and multi-objective tasks—establishing a clear rationale for the approach.
- **Innovative modular integration of LLMs**: CoELA uniquely incorporates separate LLMs for communication and planning within a cognitive-inspired framework, combining perception, memory, and execution modules. This design is considered thoughtful and well-reasoned by multiple reviewers.
- **Comprehensive experimental and user study**: Evaluation covers both AI (C-WAH, TDW-MAT) and human-agent cooperation, demonstrating that GPT-4-driven CoELA surpasses strong planning-based methods. The human study shows natural-language communication builds trust and improves collaboration.
- **Thorough analysis and discussion**: The paper includes in-depth analysis of results, failure cases, and limitations (e.g., bottleneck of textual descriptions, communication ablated performance). One reviewer specifically praised the discussion of failure cases and limitations as valuable for future research.
- **Exploration of open LMs**: Fine-tuning CoLLAMA from LLAMA-2 with agent-collected data and showing promising performance is seen as an impressive and practical contribution.
- **Clear presentation** (noted by two reviewers), making the paper accessible and well-structured.

### Weaknesses
- **Complex system design with limited module justification**: CoELA requires each agent to manage five modules and two distinct LLMs. Reviewers question whether all components (especially three separate memory modules) are necessary; an ablation study is missing. The pipeline’s intricacy causes instability when processing lengthy textual prompts describing complex scenarios, limiting scalability as objects and details increase.
- **Manual skill library and high-level plan definition**: The execution module relies on a manually predefined skill library (e.g., motion planning with A*) for a specific domain. Reviewers note that this is a tedious, environment-specific process that restricts generality and scalability. Listing possible high-level plans per state also increases prompt length, potentially degrading LLM performance. No automated or semi-automated method is provided to adapt to novel environments.
- **Missing details on perception, execution, and memory modules**: Concrete implementation choices are unclear. For perception, how Mask-RCNN is integrated with depth to produce a 3D voxel semantic map (including parameters/training data) is not described. For execution, the form of "procedures" (pre-defined scripts, learned policies?) and how high-level plans are translated to low-level actions remain unspecified. The roles and information flow among the three memory modules are not clearly explained, hindering reproducibility and assessment of necessity.
- **Insufficient baselines and limited evaluation scope**: Experiments only test one rearrangement scenario. Reviewers recommend comparisons with state-of-the-art embodied-agent methods rather than only simple/heuristic baselines. Additional environments with varied object types, agent configurations, and task goals are needed to demonstrate robustness.
- **Missing results for CoLLAMA in human-agent study**: Section 5.3.1 omits the performance of CoELA driven by CoLLAMA, making it impossible to evaluate how different LLM backbones affect human-agent cooperation.
- **Communication protocol limitations and lack of consensus handling**: The ablation study (Figure 5a) shows communication effectiveness is suboptimal (e.g., Alice misinterprets Bob’s actions). The protocol is predominantly one-way broadcast, lacking bidirectional dialogue for clarification. The paper does not discuss failure modes from communication breakdowns—e.g., conflicting goals (Alice wants Bob to go to A while Bob wants Alice to go to B) or misinterpretations. How agents reach or fail to reach consensus is not addressed.
- **Spatial reasoning bottleneck**: Reliance on textual descriptions of the environment (instead of direct spatial reasoning) introduces a bottleneck for precise manipulation or navigation in cluttered scenes. The paper does not clarify how visual-related textual input is organized (e.g., for objects behind the agent) or whether oracle spatial information (full environment objects and relations) is used.
- **Lack of communication cost evaluation**: No tradeoff study between communication cost and effectiveness is provided, leaving an open question about efficiency.
- **Unclear cooperation mechanisms between different agent types**: Sections 4 and 5 do not explain how a traditional MHP agent cooperates with CoELA or another MHP—whether internal states are shared or agents simply react to the environment. The information exchange protocol among heterogeneous agents is not detailed.
