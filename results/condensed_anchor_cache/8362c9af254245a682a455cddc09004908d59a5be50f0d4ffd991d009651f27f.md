## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes PatchMixer, a CNN-based model for long-term time series forecasting using patchification and depthwise separable convolutions to replace self-attention. It claims 3.9% improvement over SOTA (relative) and 2–3× faster training/inference on seven benchmarks. Reviewers are divided: one finds the submission very strong with little flaws (R2, score 8), while others (R1, R3, R4; scores 5,6,5) view the novelty as limited, the improvements marginal, and several experimental details unclear or insufficient.

### Strengths

- **Simple and intuitive architecture** (R3): relies on depthwise/pointwise separable convolutions with patch (dis)aggregation and dual forecasting heads.
- **Strong performance and efficiency** (R1,R2,R4): outperforms SOTA Transformer, MLP, and CNN baselines; reported speedups of 2–3× in training and inference. R2 emphasizes the results are “very good” across multiple datasets.
- **Comprehensive evaluations and ablation studies** (R2,R3): including diverse datasets, model variants, and ablation tables (Tables 1,2; Figure 4) that help understand the approach. R2 describes it as “a very pleasant read with interesting insights, ablations and evaluations.”
- **Clarity of exposition** (R1,R4): “well written and easy to understand” (R4); “detailed overview of the proposed method” (R1).  
*Contrast:* R3 and R4 consider the novelty incremental (simply replacing self-attention with convolutions; dual heads already in TiDE) and the performance improvements marginal (especially on datasets other than ETTh1/ETTh2). R2, however, sees little flaws and rates contribution as excellent.

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty and contribution are weak / marginal** (R3,R4). The idea of channel independence and separately modeling inter- and intra-patch correlations already appears in PatchTST; the proposed model “simply replaces self-attention with convolutions” and the dual heads resemble skip connections in TiDE (R4). R3 adds that the benefit of depthwise separable convolution and dual heads is not clearly supported by the ablations (Table 2, Figure 4).
- **Incremental accuracy gains** (R3,R4). Improvements over PatchTST are negligible on most datasets; only ETTh1/ETTh2 show some gain, but not enough to justify the added complexity (R4). In Table 2, standard convolution, separable convolution, and attention produce comparable performance (R4). R3 questions whether the error gap is statistically significant and whether all methods have comparable training setups (parameters, number of runs).
- **Unconvincing experimental evidence and missing details**:
  - Table 2 is very confusing (R2,R3): the “minus” notation makes it appear as separate rows, not as ablations of components. Bold/underline formatting is unclear (R2).
  - Figure 3 shows speed-ups but does not explain the source (model size, computational cost, parallelization) – “I try to understand where the efficiency comes from” (R3). Lacks comparison of model parameters and FLOPs.
  - No comparison of the two patch representation types (PatchTST vs. TimesNet) mentioned in the paper (R4).
  - Missing long-range analysis: errors at different prediction lengths versus transformer- and conv-based methods (R3).
  - R1 asks for results of PatchTST with MSE+MAE and SmoothL1Loss (Table 4) to verify if the improvements are only due to the dual head and loss change – a missing ablation.
  - R4 similarly questions whether performance gains are solely due to dual heads and MSE+MAE loss, which other baselines could also incorporate.
- **Motivation and writing clarity** (R1): the paper elaborates on *how* the module is designed but not *why*; the motivation should be better highlighted. R4 notes redundant paragraphs (e.g., patch representation description could be condensed).
- **Minor errors/lapses** (R2,R4): citation error in the depthwise separable convolution section of related work (R2); typo in equation reference (R4: “equations 8” should be “equations 10”).
- **Missing discussion of alternative long-convolution models** (R2): no comparison or discussion with S4, CKConv, Hyena etc., which can model long sequences without patching.
