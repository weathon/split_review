## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper derives the full Hessian matrix of a single self-attention layer (Theorems 3.1 and 3.2), expressing it in terms of data, weight, and attention moment dependencies, and highlights structural differences from MLP/CNN Hessians. The analysis aims to explain why Transformers require adaptive optimizers, layer normalization, and learning rate warmup. Empirical validation uses a digit addition task framed as next-token prediction with cross-entropy loss. The Hessian is decomposed into outer-product and F‑functional (δ_XY) blocks.

### Strengths
- First full derivation of the Hessian for a single self-attention layer (Theorems 3.1, 3.2). This is novel and provides a new framework for analyzing Transformer dynamics. [R1, R2, R4, R5]
- Categorization of Hessian dependencies offers a structured understanding of complex interactions within the architecture. [R1]
- Discovery of data dependencies among key, query, and value matrices is fundamental for future theoretical and practical work. [R4]
- Empirical evidence on the digit addition task (next-token prediction, CE loss) validates theoretical scaling observations for diagonal Hessian blocks. [R2, R5]
- Paper is generally well written, with color-coded equations that aid readability. [R1, R3, R5] (Note: R2 finds it poorly written – see Weaknesses.)
- Derivation quality appears sound; sufficient detail provided for Hessian expressions. [R1]
- The exploration of how different components (softmax, query-key parameterization) impact the Hessian adds depth. [R1]
- Significance: can serve as foundation for understanding self-attention’s singular behavior versus CNNs/MLPs. [R5]

### Weaknesses
- **Single-layer focus, limited to practical architectures.** [R1, R3, R4] The paper analyzes only one self-attention layer and does not discuss how properties propagate through multiple layers or interact with residual connections, multi-head attention, or deeper stacks. [R1, R4] It is unclear how conclusions extend to deeper models. [R3] No discussion of the role of residual connections in the Hessian. [R1]
- **Missing empirical comparison with MLPs/CNNs.** [R4] Without direct empirical comparison, it is difficult to assess whether observed data dependencies are unique to Transformers. [R4] This weakens the claim that the Hessian explains differences between architectures.
- **Omission of δ_XY (F‑functional Hessian blocks) weakens results.** [R4] The paper omits these blocks from the overall data-dependence analysis (Equation 5). It does not explore their magnitude relative to other terms, nor discuss whether they contain significant data dependencies. If δ_XY is large, the dependency on X becomes trivial. [R4] No justification for this omission is provided. [R4]
- **Poor clarity in experimental setups and figures.** [R4, R5] Experimental settings (architecture details, number of layers, hidden dimensions, optimization algorithm, loss function, dataset characteristics) are missing or insufficiently described. [R5] Plots lack context: Figure 1 histograms (x‑axis, relation to theory), Figure 1b color scale and matrix block labels. [R5] Figure 3 shows a mismatch between predicted and observed Hessian scaling; the top‑right plot of Figure 3a has no prediction line. [R1] Figure 4 is not clearly described – the claim that softmax reduces query Hessian block magnitude is not apparent. [R4] The experiments behind dashed lines in Figure 3 are not explained (how “trend estimated from data points by linear regression coefficient” is obtained). [R5] The range of σ in Figure 3 appears too small to verify theoretical scaling orders; a larger range is needed. [R5] The overall narrative feels like a “bag of tricks” rather than a cohesive story. [R1]
- **Lack of deep connection to real Transformer behaviors.** [R1, R3] The paper claims to explain why Transformers require Adam(W) over SGD (lines 516–519), but this explanation is not explicitly provided or linked to the derived Hessian structure. [R1, R3] It would benefit from detailed discussion showing how the derived Hessian connects to practical optimization choices. [R3] Similarly, consequences for interpretability, sparse attention, or stable training are not given. [R2]
- **Analysis could start with gradient before Hessian.** [R1] Starting with gradient analysis (simpler) and then comparing gradient and Hessian properties would strengthen the narrative. [R1]
- **Minority weak review (R3, score 5):** This reviewer found “no deep and detailed analysis” beyond the Hessian expression, and considers the paper lacking substantive discussion linking structure to behavior. [R3] In contrast, other reviewers (R1, R2, R4, R5, scores 6,8,8,8) found valuable insights; this contrast should be noted.
- **Section 4.2 alternative query-key parameterization (W_QK vs W_Q W_K^T) not discussed for performance or interpretability impact.** [R2] The paper does not explain whether this change affects overall performance or interpretability of self-attention. [R2]
- **Implications of low-rank W_V (rank d_k) not addressed.** [R1]
