## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ContextDiff, a conditional diffusion model that incorporates cross-modal context (text–visual interactions) into both the forward and reverse processes, using a context-aware adapter to optimize diffusion trajectories. It generalizes the approach to DDPMs and DDIMs and evaluates on text-to-image generation and text-to-video editing, reporting improved semantic alignment. Reviewers generally find the idea novel and interesting, but raise concerns about insufficient analysis, limited comparisons, missing evaluations, and computational cost.

### Strengths
- The idea of modeling cross-modal context in the forward process is novel and differs from prior works that only consider conditional modeling in the backward process (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The method is supported by sound theoretical foundations, including mathematical derivations for both DDPMs and DDIMs (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). The generalization to DDIMs that allows fast sampling is a clear strength (Reviewer 3).
- The writing of the method section is clear, and the adaptation of the diffusion process via a bias term is straightforward to understand (Reviewer 3).
- Experimental results show promising performance compared to existing models. Quantitative metrics (automated evaluation and user study) and visualizations demonstrate improved semantic alignment between text conditions and generated images/videos (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and comparison to prior work**: A minority reviewer (Reviewer 2) notes that existing methods also use cross-modal attention in the forward process to control generated content, making the novelty claim less distinctive. Additionally, Reviewer 2 finds that the improvements over existing methods (e.g., Imagen) are limited in image generation tasks.
- **Insufficient analysis of why forward-process context helps**: Multiple reviewers (2, 4) state that the paper provides no clear explanation—only intuitive reasoning—for why adding textual information during the forward process enhances multimodal semantic relevance. A more rigorous analysis (e.g., information-theoretic) is requested (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 also asks for a theoretical and intuitive analysis of the necessity of adding cross-modal context to the forward process.
- **Missing ELBO/NLL evaluation for likelihood claim**: Reviewer 4 points out that the paper claims “ContextDiff is theoretically capable of achieving better likelihood compared to original DDPMs” but does not provide empirical quantitative results on ELBO or negative log-likelihood to support this claim.
- **Lack of experiments on latent diffusion (e.g., Stable Diffusion)**: The method is built on an Imagen-based framework. Reviewer 3 notes that the method is not evaluated on latent diffusion architecture; the ablation in Sec 5.3 mentions LDM but lacks detail on the specific model, training procedure, evaluation metrics beyond FID, and comparisons with other works. It is unclear if the same data and hyperparameters were used as the baseline LDM. Reviewer 4 also suggests that T2V editing experiments based on pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.4 are insufficient to prove the method’s editing ability; a comparison with/without the proposed method on the same base model is needed.
- **Missing inference latency evaluation**: Reviewer 3 stresses that no inference latency comparison with baseline methods is provided, making it difficult to assess whether the added computational cost of the context-aware adapter negates the benefits.
- **Limited scope of conditional tasks**: Reviewer 4 argues that since the paper presents a general improvement for conditional diffusion models, experiments on other tasks (e.g., class-to-image, layout-to-image) should be included to demonstrate versatility.
- **Incomplete figure and analysis**: Reviewer 2 observes that Figure 6 claims an ablation on the trade-off between CLIP score and FID across a range of guidance weights, but only FID scores are provided. The corresponding CLIP scores are missing.
- **No discussion of limitations**: Reviewer 2 notes that the paper does not analyze the limitations of the proposed method. Reviewer 1 also asks for discussion of whether the method remains effective for fine-grained context-awareness, e.g., generating precise text within an image or modifying specific parts of an image.
- **Missing cost analysis**: Reviewer 1 requests additional parameters, training time, and testing time to help understand the method’s overhead.
- **Lack of human evaluation for fine-grained alignment**: Reviewer 1 suggests that for fine-grained, context-aware generation, existing automated metrics may be insufficient, and recommends incorporating human evaluation.
- **Missing discussion on incremental training on pretrained models**: Reviewer 2 asks whether the method can be incrementally trained on other pretrained generative models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) and whether that would improve performance and convergence.
- **T2V editing evaluation concerns**: Reviewer 4 finds the T2V editing experiments confusing because they rely on a pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.4 model without isolating the contributions of ContextDiff. A more thorough evaluation comparing editing performance with and without the method, along with clarification on which aspects of editing are improved, is requested. Reviewer 1 also asks whether the method can be applied to text-to-image editing based on the well-trained T2I generation model.
- **Typo**: Reviewer 3 notes a minor typo: “A red ross” should be “A red rose”.
