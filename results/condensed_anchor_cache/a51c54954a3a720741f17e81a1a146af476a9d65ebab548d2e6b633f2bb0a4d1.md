## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a channel-dimension Fourier transform (CFTL) mechanism for image enhancement. The design consists of three steps: applying Fourier transform to the channel dimension to obtain channel-wise Fourier domain features, performing a channel-wise transformation on both amplitude and phase components, and then reverting back to the spatial domain. Based on this core mechanism, three alternative implementation formats are introduced (operations in global vector with higher orders, global vector with channel groups, and Fourier features from spatial-based Fourier transform). The modules are plug-and-play with existing networks and are evaluated on multiple tasks including low-light image enhancement, exposure correction, SDR2HDR translation, and underwater image enhancement. Reviewers’ ratings range from 1 to 8, indicating strong disagreement. One reviewer (score 1) was highly critical, while another (score 8) found the work very promising.

### Strengths
- The idea of applying Fourier transform along the channel dimension is novel and provides a fresh perspective on global information representation for image enhancement. The mechanism is clearly illustrated with figures and descriptions.
- The core module design is simple, easy to implement, and introduces negligible computational overhead.
- Extensive experiments are conducted across multiple image enhancement tasks and datasets, demonstrating consistent performance gains over baselines. Additional analysis in the appendix further validates the method’s mechanism.
- The writing and organization are clear and easy to follow.
- The method is plug-and-play and compatible with various existing architectures.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient motivation and comparison with related work.** The paper does not clearly differentiate its approach from prior work [1] that also applies frequency-domain processing to channel attention. A detailed comparison of mathematical formulations and practical implications is missing. Moreover, the rationale for using channel-dimension Fourier transform—specifically what limitations of existing methods motivated this design—is not adequately discussed.
- **Unclear core claim and mechanism.** The claim that CFTL captures “global discriminative representations by modeling channel-dimension discrepancy” is not well-defined. It is unclear how modeling this discrepancy leads to improved image enhancement, and the underlying mechanism requires a more rigorous explanation.
- **Questionable necessity and justification of design choices.**
  - The decision to apply the Fourier transform in the global pooling space is not thoroughly justified. The paper should compare this space with other global-information spaces (e.g., self-attention features or alternative pooling strategies).
  - The use of an attention operation in Eq. (6) is introduced without sufficient explanation or justification of how it relates to the channel-dimension Fourier transform.
  - The paper presents multiple implementation formats, but does not explain how they are derived from the original format, the relationships among them, or whether all are necessary given that they achieve comparable results.
- **Limited ablation and generalization analysis.**
  - Ablation studies (Table 5) are mainly conducted on exposure correction. Ablation on other core tasks (e.g., low-light image enhancement) and on additional datasets is needed to comprehensively validate each component.
  - The generalization ability of the method is questioned. One reviewer suspects that the improvements may come primarily from global information modeling, and that applying simple operations (e.g., channel/spatial attention) on the baselines might yield similar gains. Another reviewer doubts whether the method would work when baselines already have large receptive fields, and whether the improved version might suffer from weaker generalization than the baseline networks.
- **Lack of visual results.** Despite being an image processing paper, the number of qualitative comparisons is limited. More visual results (similar to Fig. 6) should be included, especially in the appendix, to better demonstrate the method’s effectiveness.
- **Outdated baselines.** Several baseline architectures used in experiments are somewhat dated; the reviewer suggests including more contemporary methods.
- **Training details.** It is unclear whether the baseline and baseline+CFTL models are trained with the same number of iterations or until convergence.
- **Minority reviewer concerns.** One reviewer (score 1) expressed strong reservations, particularly about the necessity of multiple formats and the significance of improvements over baselines. Another reviewer (score 8) was highly positive but still noted the lack of visual results and missing relationship between formats.
