## Merged Review

### Summary
Four reviewers assessed “Neural Topic Modeling with Large Language Models in the Loop”. Reviewer 1 rated the paper 5 (confidence 5), while Reviewers 2, 3, and 4 each rated it 3 (confidences 5, 4, 5). The paper proposes LLM-ITL, an LLM-in-the-loop framework that refines topics from existing Neural Topic Models (NTMs) using an LLM guided by a confidence-weighted Optimal Transport (OT) alignment objective. Experiments are conducted on four datasets using metrics such as NPMI, topic diversity, document clustering (Purity, NMI), and topic alignment. Reviewer 1 found the approach promising and the evaluation robust, but the other three reviewers identified substantial methodological and evidential shortcomings that weaken the core claims.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a timely and relatively unexplored direction: incorporating LLMs into topic modeling to improve topic quality. (Reviewer 1, 2, 3)
- Writing is clear and easy to follow, aided by figures, a formal algorithm, and detailed experimental setup. (Reviewer 1, 3, 4)
- The word-level use of LLMs for topic refinement is a novel distinction from existing document-level LLM approaches. (Reviewer 4; also implicitly noted by Reviewer 1 as “progress”)
- Experiments are comprehensive: four datasets, multiple evaluation tasks (topic coherence, diversity, alignment, document clustering), and an ablation study. (Reviewer 1, 3)
- Reviewer 1 highlighted the qualitative evaluation and thorough ablation as strengths, and noted the approach is “robust” in terms of experimental breadth (4 datasets, 2 evaluation metrics).

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental novelty and limited advancement:** The approach extends prior NTM+external knowledge work; previous studies already improved NTMs for topic extraction and document representation. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing critical baselines:** Several strong, recent neural topic models are absent, including DocNADE (AISTATS 2011; AAAI 2019), DVAE, NSTM (ICLR 2021), WeTe (ICLR 2022), ECRTM (ICML 2023), and OT-LDA (NeurIPS 2020). NSTM is particularly relevant as it also uses OT distance. This omission weakens the comparative claims. (Reviewer 1, 2, 4)
- **Inadequate evaluation metrics:**
    - Document representation quality is claimed but not directly measured (e.g., no document retrieval task). The loss mainly optimizes topic coherence; better coherence does not guarantee better representations. (Reviewer 1)
    - No perplexity (document completion) experiment, a standard metric for topic models (e.g., LDA, ProdLDA). (Reviewer 3)
    - NPMI is reported as a percentage rather than standardized coherence metrics (C\_NPMI, C\_V from Röder et al., 2015), which address known incoherence in automatic evaluation. (Reviewer 2, 4)
    - Standard deviation is missing from most figures, reducing rigor. (Reviewer 3)
- **Methodological concerns with LLM refinement:**
    - It is unclear how the LLM generates a new topic distribution from NTM topics (given as distributions) and produces refined topics using only topic labels without document context. (Reviewer 1)
    - The probability vector *u* and how the LLM is constrained to suggest words from the model’s vocabulary (V) are not explained. (Reviewer 2)
    - The LLM can suggest out-of-vocabulary words, risking topics that are only superficially coherent but not representative of the corpus. (Reviewer 2)
    - Given known LLM biases (e.g., missing global coverage), aligning NTM topics with LLM outputs may inject these biases rather than correct them. (Reviewer 4; also implicit in Reviewer 1’s question about suboptimal LLM quality)
    - If the initial NTM output is incoherent, it is unclear how the LLM can generate accurate topic labels and perform meaningful refinement. (Reviewer 1)
    - The motivation for using OT over alternatives like KL-divergence is not justified. (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient support for central claims:**
    - The claim that LLM-ITL shows “comparable performance” (L1101-1102) is misleading; Figure E3 reveals a considerable difference in topic diversity between LLM-ITL and the best baselines. (Reviewer 2)
    - In clustering tasks (Figure E4), the proposed model does not outperform baselines on Purity/NMI, and no explanation is given. (Reviewer 3)
- **Scalability claims are under-supported:** Only empirical runtime and parameter counts are given; no theoretical computational complexity analysis is provided. Datasets are small, and no million-scale corpus is tested to validate scalability. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing related work:** Papers on OT for topic modeling (Zhang & Lauw 2023; Huynh et al. 2020; Xu et al. 2018) are not cited or discussed. (Reviewer 3)
- **Minor procedural issues:** In Step 1 of Figure A1, it is unclear why proper nouns are avoided and topics are fixed to 2 words. (Reviewer 4)

Despite these weaknesses, Reviewer 1 was more positive overall (score 5, confidence 5), acknowledging the approach as progress while still noting the incremental nature and missing evaluations. The other three reviewers (scores 3) saw the direction as worthwhile but found the evidence and methodology insufficient to support the paper’s claims.
