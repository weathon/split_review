## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a Real SR approach using LR RAW data as a detail supplement to RGB inputs, contributing a new dataset (RealSR-RAW, over 10,000 pairs of LR RGB, HR RGB, and LR RAW from smartphones at varying focal lengths), and a RAW adapter to integrate RAW data into existing CNN/Transformer/Diffusion-based Real SR models. Experiments show that incorporating RAW data improves performance across ten fidelity and perception metrics. Reviewers acknowledge extensive experiments and clear motivation, but raise concerns about novelty, dataset construction, methodological details, and experimental validation. Two reviewers gave a score of 5 (with moderate to low confidence), and two gave a score of 3 (high confidence), indicating a split in overall assessment.

### Strengths
- **Detailed experimental data**: The paper provides thorough quantitative and qualitative evaluations, showing significant metric improvements when RAW data is used (R1, R3).
- **Clear motivation**: The rationale for using RAW data to compensate for detail loss in ISP is well articulated (R2).
- **Extensive analyzing experiments**: Real-world test images, model complexity, and cross-lens generalization ability are evaluated (R2).
- **Well-written and easy to read**: The paper is clearly organized and the analysis of detail loss during ISP is comprehensive (R4).
- **Significant metric improvements**: Multiple fidelity and perception-oriented metrics demonstrate the effectiveness of RAW data (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty**: The concept of leveraging RAW data in super-resolution is not new; prior works (e.g., “Towards Real Scene Super-Resolution with RAW Images” and the NTIRE 2024 Challenge on Deep RAW Image Super-Resolution) have explored similar ideas and datasets (R3). The method of combining RAW and RGB through concatenation and denoising is straightforward and lacks substantial innovation (R3).  
- **Dataset construction issues**:
  - HR RGB images collected from Unsplash inherently suffer from unknown degradations (e.g., JPEG compression), making them unsuitable as ground truth (R4).
  - The process of capturing LR RGB and LR RAW by photographing HR images displayed on a UHD monitor is described, but key steps such as focal length selection, diverse scene collection, alignment procedures (LR RGB ↔ LR RAW, LR RGB ↔ HR RGB), and color correction are either missing or insufficiently detailed in the main text and supplementary (R2, R4). The alignment challenge is noted but not explained (R4).
  - Why not capture HR-LR pairs directly using different resolution settings on the smartphone? (R4, question)
- **Missing critical methodological details in RAW adapter design**: The feature extractor and `k_learn` are not specified; Figure 4(b) lacks proper letter annotations and does not meet academic illustration standards (R2).
- **Experimental analysis shortcomings**:
  - The claim that the RAW adapter suppresses noise needs validation via comparative experiments (e.g., estimated noise levels before/after applying the adapter) (R2).
  - Visual results are unconvincing: in Figure 12’s third column, SwinIR’s result appears more blurry than the LR input, which contradicts intuition. Visual comparisons are limited to dated methods (RRDB, SwinIR) and omit current state-of-the-art methods such as ResShift (R2, R1 question: “Have comparative experiments been conducted with SOTA work?”).
  - Lack of comparisons with existing synthetic degradation pipelines that simulate ISP-like degradation, e.g., RealESRGAN (R4).
  - Lack of quantitative comparisons with raw image enhancement methods mentioned in the related works (R2).
  - All experiments are evaluated solely on the proposed benchmark; no validation of the dataset’s effectiveness is performed on existing public benchmarks (R4).
  - Objective metrics (PSNR, SSIM) are relatively low, and no explanation is provided for this (R1, question).
  - The user study uses only 10 randomly selected images, which is too small a sample to ensure reliability (R2).
- **Limited theoretical analysis**: The paper provides limited theoretical justification beyond empirical results (R1).
- **Writing issues**:
  - Abstract states “over 10,000 pairs” rather than the specific “11,726 pairs” (R2).
  - Captions of Figures 7–9 in the appendix incorrectly reverse “right” and “left” (R2).
  - Possible typo in line 317: “elevation” should likely be something else (R2).
  - The calculation of residual images in Figure 9 is unclear—demosaicing changes image dimensions from H/2×W/2×4 (RGGB) to H×W×3 (RGB); how are pixel-level differences computed? (R2, question)
