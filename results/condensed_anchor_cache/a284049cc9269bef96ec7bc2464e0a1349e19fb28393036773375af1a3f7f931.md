## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Prompt-OIRL, an offline inverse reinforcement learning approach to achieve query-dependent prompt optimization for improving LLM arithmetic reasoning. The method addresses two key challenges: the inability to evaluate prompts without ground-truth answers at inference time, and the high cost of online LLM interactions. Prompt-OIRL learns an offline reward model (without LLM calls) from existing prompting benchmark data, then uses a best-of-N strategy to recommend prompts. Experiments across multiple arithmetic reasoning datasets and three LLM scales (LLaMA, TigerBot, etc.) demonstrate effectiveness and cost savings compared to several baselines.

### Strengths
- The query-dependent prompt optimization setting is novel and important, as no single prompt works for all queries and online evaluation of prompts is expensive.
- The method is technically robust and well articulated; the offline RL framework is promising and reduces dependence on costly LLM interactions.
- Prompt-OIRL achieves higher accuracy and precision in assessing prompt success than LMSC for both held-out and seen prompts.
- Evaluation covers three different arithmetic reasoning tasks and three models of varying sizes, and comparison with multiple SOTA baselines (ORPO, APE, APO) supports the claims.
- The paper is well-structured and easy to follow.
- In the offline setting, relatively thorough experiments and ablations are conducted to find optimal methods.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing baseline comparisons:**
  - No comparison with “online” prompt optimization methods (e.g., iterative LLM-based search). Showing that Prompt-OIRL achieves similar performance at significantly lower cost would strengthen the practical argument.
  - Missing oracle baseline: “always choose the best prompt for every query” (perfect reward). Without this, the potential benefit of the method is hard to quantify.
  - For the scarce demonstration setting (Figure 5), it is unclear which single prompt was used. Baseline methods were limited to that single prompt while Prompt-OIRL could choose any of the 6 considered prompts, making the comparison unfair.
  - A simpler nearest-neighbor baseline (find the most similar training query with a correct prompt and reuse that prompt) is not included.
  - Comparison to always choosing each prompt individually and always choosing the on-average best prompt (across test set) is missing; the paper should report which prompt that is per dataset/model.

- **Unclear or missing technical details:**
  - The modelling of the proxy reward is not discussed in the main text; the appendix mentions an XGBT on top of LLM embeddings of prompt and query, but the specific architecture, hyperparameters, and how embeddings are combined are not detailed.
  - No ablation of different reward model design choices (e.g., MLP vs. XGBT). The paper notes that MLP is less effective but provides no analysis of why or how to choose the right architecture.
  - The curated offline dataset is not described (size, composition, how prompts were chosen).
  - The values of K, M, and P in Appendix C.1 are not defined.

- **Generalization and scalability concerns:**
  - Dataset generation requires many model interactions for every new task and model; no cross-task or cross-model generalization is demonstrated.
  - The scope is limited to arithmetic reasoning, but the title suggests a generic framework. The bottleneck for applying the method to other instruction prompt optimization tasks (e.g., ORPO, APE, APO) is not discussed.
  - The computational cost of embedding prompts (using LLMs) is not thoroughly addressed. For large prompt sets, this cost might become prohibitive; the paper should clearly state that this cost is incurred once per prompt set, and show whether it is included in Figure 7’s cost analysis.
  - Figure 7 would benefit from a relative scale for cost, especially for “Cost with Commercial APIs”; reporting cost improvements using 100 prompts (while the rest of the paper uses 6 prompts) is misleading.

- **Missing experiments and analyses:**
  - How does performance depend on the size of the offline prompt-alignment dataset? The paper only tests 10 and 100 held-out prompts; real-world tasks may have fewer prompts.
  - Without query dependence, what is the performance drop? The necessity of query-dependent selection is not proven.
  - Failures of Prompt-OIRL (e.g., GSM8K with LLaMA or TigerBot, where it performs worse than all baselines by a large margin) are only shown in the appendix (Figures 20, 21). This limitation should be discussed in the main text.
  - No comparison with GPT-4 or PaLM 2 (though they may be too costly for online search).
  - Limited discussion of potential limitations (e.g., stability of inverse RL).

- **Other issues:**
  - The paper could benefit from careful copyediting (typos, grammatical errors, inconsistent writing style).
  - In the scarce demonstration setting, how does Prompt-OIRL differ from BoTr (Eq. 2) when trained on all considered prompts? The description suggests similar best-of-N selection but Figure 6 shows worse performance for BoTr.
