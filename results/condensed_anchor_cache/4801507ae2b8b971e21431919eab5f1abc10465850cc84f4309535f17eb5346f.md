## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces cascade routing, a framework that unifies and generalizes routing and cascading for LLM model selection. The authors derive optimal strategies for both routing and cascading theoretically, then combine them into cascade routing to dynamically select and sequence models. Experiments on RouterBench show 1–4% AUC improvement over baselines. Reviewers acknowledge the theoretical contribution but are split on practical impact: some find the framework well-motivated and the results significant, while others argue the gains are modest relative to the added complexity, latency, and estimation sensitivity.

### Strengths
- Strong theoretical foundation: the paper provides formal proofs and derives optimal strategies for routing and cascading as linear optimization problems, establishing provable optimality under certain conditions (R1, R2, R3, R5).
- The framework effectively combines routing and cascading, unifying two previously separate approaches into a single coherent strategy (R1, R2, R4).
- Experiments on RouterBench demonstrate consistent performance gains (1–4% AUC) across various noise levels and model set sizes, with especially strong results under low-noise settings (R1, R4).
- The paper is well-written, clearly presented, and easy to follow (R1, R3, R5).
- The research topic (efficient model selection for LLMs) is timely and valuable (R5).

### Weaknesses
- The core idea is an incremental combination of two existing methods, which limits novelty; the method does not change model order, cost, or quality estimation approaches—it only expands the search space (R1, R3).
- The reported 1–4% accuracy improvement is modest and may not justify the significant complexity, computation, and latency overhead of running multiple models sequentially; reviewers question practical value and whether gains could be due to experimental uncertainty (R2, R3, R5). More rigorous statistical significance testing is needed (R5).
- End-to-end inference latency is completely ignored; the paper only analyzes API costs, not total response time, which is critical for real-world applications (R2).
- Quality estimation relies on perplexity and uncertainty features, which reviewers consider insufficient; more sophisticated methods (e.g., LLM-as-Judge) should be considered (R2).
- Missing comparisons to other routing/cascading methods from related work (e.g., [1]) and Chain-of-thought baselines (R2, R3). No comparison to ensemble methods (R2).
- The method’s effectiveness degrades when quality and cost estimates are inaccurate; this is acknowledged but not thoroughly addressed, raising concerns about practical deployment (R1, R3, R4).
- Cascade routing requires tuning multiple hyperparameters (e.g., per-model cost-quality tradeoffs), which can be time-consuming and computationally expensive; training data requirements versus existing routing/cascading approaches are not provided (R3, R4).
- Detailed runtime comparisons are missing: the authors should report runtime for all methods in Figure 2 and Table 2, and include both runtime and AUC for Linear Interpolation, Routing, and Cascade (Baseline) in Table 3 (R3).
- No error analysis across different benchmarks to show where cascade routing excels or underperforms (R1).
- Criteria for assigning models to the same cascade level and the optimal number of models per level are not discussed (R5).
- It remains unclear whether combining cascading and routing is the key contribution, or whether improving quality estimation approaches would be more promising (R5).
