## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper benchmarks the representational alignment between 99 deep learning models (image and video) and human brain activity (fMRI) from 10 subjects watching videos. Using Representational Similarity Analysis, the authors disentangle four factors: temporal modeling, classification task, architecture (CNN vs. Transformer), and training dataset. Key findings: temporal modeling is key for alignment to early visual areas (V1–V4), while a relevant classification task (action recognition) is key for higher-level regions (e.g., LOC, FBA). Transformers align with early areas at shallower layers due to global attention, contrasting with CNNs’ more hierarchical pattern. Computationally efficient models align better with higher-level brain regions. Large-scale comparisons and statistical tests validate trends. Limitations include reliance on the fMRI dataset (low temporal resolution, only 102 video clips used), narrow focus on visual cortex, use of only models from a single library, and insufficient motivation for neuroscience/ML benefits.

### Strengths
- Novel and important question: large-scale systematic comparison of diverse video/image models to brain responses during video viewing, bridging ML and neuroscience. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- Methodology is well-thought-out with multiple differentiating criteria (temporal modeling vs. static, task type, architecture, dataset, compute), and statistical tests carefully validate results. [R2, R3]
- Provides nuanced insights: temporal modeling aids early visual alignment; action recognition task aids high-level semantic alignment; CNN models exhibit a hierarchical structure more similar to the biological visual system, while Transformers show similar representations across depth (confirming Raghu et al. 2021). [R1, R3]
- Finding that computationally efficient models (lower FLOPs) align better with high-level brain areas suggests human-like abstract semantics do not require heavy compute, guiding efficient model development. [R1]
- Detailed analysis of layer-wise alignment (including mid-layer responses in V1/V2) and discussion of architecture differences (self-attention vs. convolution) is more thorough than prior work. [R2, R3]
- The paper is well-written, clear, and well-organized; figures and tables effectively communicate results. [R1, R3, R4]

### Weaknesses
- **Limited brain coverage**: Only visual regions are analyzed, neglecting networks for emotion, memory, and multisensory processing. The entire brain could yield more comprehensive insights and improve alignment. [R1] (also raised as a question by R1)
- **fMRI temporal resolution**: Low TR is insufficient to capture real-time temporal processing; the study should consider higher-temporal-resolution modalities like MEG. [R4] R2 also notes temporal lag is not accounted for.
- **Dataset limitations**: Of the 1102 total video clips, only 102 were used; with each clip only 3 seconds and limited scene transitions, it is hard to draw general conclusions. [R4] (R4 question: why not use all?) R2 additionally notes missing acquisition parameters (resolution, TR) and preprocessing details (anatomical vs. functional alignment, ROI definition, surface vs. voxel modeling). This undermines reliability. [R2]
- **Model library restriction**: Only models from a specific library are benchmarked, limiting diversity across training paradigms (e.g., supervised, contrastive, self-supervised). [R1]
- **Limited novelty/concrete guidance**: Similar comparative studies on natural scenes exist; the paper’s method and conclusions offer relatively limited novelty. [R4] Also, how exactly do these comparisons motivate efficient video model design? Concrete suggestions are missing. [R4] (R4 question)
- **Clarity and motivation**: Abstract and introduction lack clarity (e.g., “brain representational alignment” is confusing). The neuroscience motivation for comparing brain activations to model features and the expected benefits for both fields are insufficiently explained. [R2]
- **Missing methodology details**: How feature extraction works for Transformers (line 270)? PCA impact on alignment? For image models on video, how is temporal input handled (line 275)? [R2, R3]
- **Unexplored dimensions**: No comparison with recent neural decoding papers (e.g., MindEye, MindVis) or with NLP brain-alignment methods. [R1, R2] No examination of hybrid architectures (e.g., CvT) that combine convolution and self-attention. [R3] No analysis of whether model task performance (accuracy) correlates with alignment. [R3]
- **Visualization issues**: Figure 2a brackets and asterisks are crammed; readability is difficult. [R3]
- **Reproducibility**: No project code (e.g., GitHub link) provided. [R4]
