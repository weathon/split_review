## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Polynomial Attention Drop-in Replacement (PADRe), a unifying framework that replaces standard self-attention with polynomial approximants using hardware-friendly operations (e.g., Hadamard products) to achieve linear complexity. The framework subsumes several recent efficient attention mechanisms (Hyena, Mamba, SimA, Conv2Former, Castling-ViT) as special cases. Experiments on image classification, 2D detection, and 3D point cloud detection show 11×–43× speedup on server GPU and mobile NPU while maintaining accuracy. Reviewers acknowledge the theoretical contribution and practical potential but raise concerns about confounded experimental design, missing baselines, limited scope (no NLP/LLM tasks), numerical stability, and unclear guidance for practitioners.

### Strengths
- The unified polynomial formulation is intriguing and mathematically establishes connections among diverse attention variants, providing a foundation for future efficient attention design. (R1, R2, R3)
- The paper provides solid theoretical proofs showing PADRe covers previous methods (Hyena, Mamba, SimA, etc.) as special cases. (R2, R3)
- The framework uses straightforward, mobile-friendly operations (Hadamard products) and avoids Softmax, yielding significant FLOPs reduction (demonstrated in Table 3 and Table 4) and speedups of 11×–43× over conventional self-attention. (R2, R4)
- PADRe is advantageous over Flash attention, especially on devices. (R4)
- The paper addresses an important problem in vision transformer design and is well-structured with clear figures. (R2, R3)

### Weaknesses
1. **Missing strong baseline for polynomial approximation**: Figure 1 illustrates a specific PADRe design, but a straightforward baseline—an ensemble of $d$ MLP-Mixers for degree-$d$ approximation—is omitted. The paper should compare computational cost (training/inference time, memory) and the trade-off between polynomial degree $d$ and model performance against this baseline. (R1)

2. **Unclear guidance on instantiating the framework**: The coefficients $\pi_k$ depend complexly on transformation matrices $A_i$, $B_i$, $C_i$, $D_i$ (Eq. 8). The paper lacks explicit heuristics or a practical approximation for the softmax operation (e.g., from Eq. 82). Without clear guidance, it is difficult for practitioners to leverage the framework effectively. (R1)

3. **Confound between polynomial approximation and added locality**: The experiments use pointwise and 2D convolutions to mix channels and tokens, introducing locality inductive biases. Performance gains may stem from these locality enhancements rather than the polynomial approximation itself. A controlled experiment on NLP benchmarks (where no locality bias exists) would isolate the effect of PADRe. (R1)

4. **Numerical stability with higher-degree polynomials**: The paper notes that model performance saturates when polynomial degree exceeds 3 (Lines 462–463), suggesting potential numerical instability. Investigating more stable polynomial bases, such as orthogonal Chebyshev or Legendre polynomials, could permit higher degrees without saturation and improve performance. (R2)

5. **Lack of LLM/long-sequence experiments**: The paper only evaluates on vision tasks. To demonstrate PADRe’s relevance to sequence modeling (where attention is most critical), experiments on LLMs (or at least a small-scale language model) are needed, especially given the method’s framing as a general attention replacement. (R2, R3) The method’s performance on much longer sequence lengths remains unclear. (R3)

6. **Minor improvement on DeiT-Base and limited model size range**: In Table 1, the improvement over DeiT-Base is minor. It is unclear whether PADRe works more effectively for smaller transformers. Testing on a wider range of model sizes and architectures (e.g., ViT from Dosovitskiy et al.) is needed to understand its effectiveness and limitations. (R2, R4)

7. **Missing device-level metrics**: Since the paper evaluates on mobile NPU, it should report peak memory consumption and energy efficiency improvements when running PADRe compared to standard self-attention. (R4)

8. **Lack of analysis on parameter count**: Replacing self-attention with PADRe may change the number of model parameters. This information is essential for understanding the impact on mobile devices (storage, DRAM usage). (R4)

9. **No guidance on selecting optimal polynomial degree**: The paper does not discuss how to determine the optimal degree for PADRe. (R3)

10. **Cross-attention and multi-modal attention not explored**: The current framework does not address cross-attention or multi-modal setups, although extension in principle may be possible. (R4)
