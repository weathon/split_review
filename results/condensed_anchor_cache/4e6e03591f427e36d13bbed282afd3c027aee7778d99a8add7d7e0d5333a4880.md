## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper revisits the online Sinkhorn algorithm of Mensch & Peyre (2020) for entropic-regularized OT on continuous distributions. Two contributions: (1) an improved convergence rate that corrects an inaccuracy in the previous bound, with experiments suggesting tightness; (2) a compressed online Sinkhorn that combines measure compression (Gaussian quadrature, Fourier moments) with the online method, providing theoretical guarantees and numerical evidence of runtime gains while maintaining comparable error.

### Strengths
- The problem of estimating dual potentials for OT on continuous distributions is difficult; despite being incremental, the result may be important (Reviewer 1).
- The idea of compressing measures is interesting algorithmically (Reviewer 2).
- The previous bound for online Sinkhorn indeed seems incorrect; fixing this is a contribution (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Clear theoretical analysis of the issues in Mensch & Peyre and of the new method (Reviewer 4).
- Presentation is clear and the paper is well written (Reviewers 3, 4).
- The analysis is quite simple (Reviewer 2).
- Two concrete compression settings are provided: Gaussian quadrature and Fourier moments compression (Reviewer 3).
- Numerical evidence is presented (Reviewers 1, 3).

### Weaknesses
- The five assumptions are hard to appreciate; the necessity of the specific step-size choices (Assumption 2), batch size criteria (Assumption 3), and the interplay of Assumptions 4–5 on the error term \(O(t^{b-a})\) is not clearly justified. A more intuitive explanation would be beneficial (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is accessible mainly to those familiar with prior work; it lacks a broader comparison of time, space, and sample complexities with existing algorithms for continuous OT (Reviewer 1).
- The bounds depend on a constant \(\kappa\) that can be quite small (Reviewer 2).
- Proof writing has issues: the constant \(\kappa\) is explained late; the Lipschitz constant in Lemma 4 should be \(L\) or \(T_\beta\) is missing a \(1/\epsilon\) factor; the last equality in the first math display on page 13 should be an upper bound (Reviewer 2).
- Experiments are done in very low dimensionality; for \(d=5\) the uncompressed method starts to look quite better (Reviewer 3). This raises the question whether the uncompressed method works better with increasing dimension as a general trend (Reviewer 3).
- High-dimensional performance is not discussed. In Section A.5.2 the compression error is \(O(|\log m|^d / m)\), which may be too large in high dimensions (Assumption 4 with a large coefficient for \(O(m_t^{-\zeta})\)). In Figure 2(c), for \(d=5\) the online Sinkhorn already has lower error than the compressed algorithm (Reviewer 4).
- Algorithm 2 trades off accuracy and speed, but there is no detailed discussion on how to choose an appropriate batch size \(m_t\) when solving actual OT problems (Reviewer 4).
- Experimental sections lack application on real-world data and more complex distributions (Reviewer 4).
- The new convergence rate is better when \(a > -b\), and Algorithm 2 is more efficient when \(\zeta > \frac{3(a-b)}{4a+1}\), but the paper lacks explanation on how to choose specific values for \(a, b, \zeta\), making the experiments less persuasive (Reviewer 4).
