## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes MERGEPRINT, a fingerprinting method for LLMs that embeds robust fingerprints designed to survive model merging. It optimizes fingerprint inputs and model parameters against a pseudo-merged model that simulates post-merge weights. A two-step optimization (input then parameter) minimizes performance loss. Experiments on LLaMA models show high fingerprint retention after merging using Task-Arithmetic, TIES, and DARE, with verification via VSR. Reviewers generally agree the problem is relevant, but opinions on execution diverge: two reviewers (scores 5) praise the novelty and empirical results, while two others (scores 3) find the method overly simplistic, the evaluation limited, and the robustness insufficiently validated.

### Strengths
- **Novel focus**: Targets fingerprinting specifically against model merging, a growing but underexplored challenge. (All reviewers)
- **Two-step optimization**: Ensures harmlessness (low performance degradation) and reliability of fingerprints. (Reviewer 3)
- **Robustness results**: Demonstrates fingerprint verification for up to seven merged models and across various merging ratios. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Practicality**: Minimizes impact on model performance, making it feasible for real-world use. (Reviewer 2)
- **Clear verification**: Uses VSR metric for straightforward ownership proof. (Reviewer 2)
- **Well-structured paper**: Methodology and experimental setup are described in detail, easy to follow. (Reviewers 1, 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Method simplicity & lack of theory**: The core approach – simulating merging via weighted coefficient addition – is seen as simplistic and lacks theoretical justification or proof of effectiveness. (Reviewer 1, high confidence 3)
- **Insufficient related work**: Ignores weight-space fingerprinting, federated learning aggregation parallels, backdoor attacks against model merge (e.g., [1]), and non-weight-based LLM watermarking [2,3,4]. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Limited experimental scope**:  
  - Only tested on three merging techniques (Task-Arithmetic, TIES, DARE) and same-source models; generalizability to other methods and cross-origin models is unproven. (Reviewer 1)  
  - Too few baseline methods for comparison. (Reviewer 1)  
  - No evaluation across diverse model architectures or merging strategies. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing hyperparameter and ablation studies**:  
  - No experimental analysis of regularization coefficient λ and merging coefficients. (Reviewer 1)  
  - No study of hyperparameters α_x and α_w. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Ablation lacks parameter optimization step and study of the pseudo-merged model’s effect (e.g., optimization on the fine-tuned model alone). (Reviewer 4)  
  - Tests only one watermark in a single model; multi-model, multi-fingerprint scenario unexplored. (Reviewer 1)
- **Robustness gaps**:  
  - No experiments against basic adversarial attacks like overwriting. (Reviewer 1)  
  - Fingerprints vulnerable to membership inference; highly memorized low-loss fingerprints could be reverse-engineered. (Reviewer 2, confidence 4)  
  - No tests against fine-tuning, pruning, or distillation – authors claim these are out of scope, but they are common in real-world usage. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Efficiency trade-offs not analyzed: low learning rate (1e-6) and early stopping may yield suboptimal robustness. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of formal validation**: No cryptographic or formal methods to strengthen ownership claims; statistical significance alone may not be legally defensible. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing optimization and efficiency details**:  
  - Two-step optimization procedure unclear (alternating or sequential); hyperparameters and hardware not specified. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Time complexity or runtime not reported. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Optimized fingerprint input x and the impact of fingerprint output y (e.g., “transformers”) are not disclosed. (Reviewer 3)
- **Ethical considerations superficial**: Potential for malicious embedding of harmful/bias information not deeply addressed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Typos and grammatical errors** (e.g., lines 223-224). (Reviewer 4)
- **Presentation quality**: One reviewer gave a score of 1 for presentation, citing poor clarity or organization. (Reviewer 2, confidence 4)
