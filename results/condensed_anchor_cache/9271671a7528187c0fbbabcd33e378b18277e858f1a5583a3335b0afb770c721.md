## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Neuroplastic Expansion (NE), a method to mitigate plasticity loss in deep RL by dynamically growing the network from a small initial size. NE has three components: elastic neuron generation based on gradient potential, dormant neuron pruning, and neuron consolidation via experience review. Experiments in MuJoCo (with TD3) and DeepMind Control Suite (with DrQ) show NE outperforms baselines like LayerNorm, ReDo, and Plasticity Injection. Reviewers rate the paper 6–8 (average ~6.5). Reviewer 2 gives a score of 8 and is positive; Reviewers 1, 3, 4 rate 6 and raise concerns about writing clarity, missing comparisons, experimental rigor, and unsubstantiated claims.

### Strengths

- Novel, biologically motivated approach (inspired by cortical cortex expansion) to address plasticity loss. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Well-written and well-motivated. (R1)
- NE is effective and outperforms existing methods such as LayerNorm, ReDo, and Plasticity Injection across MuJoCo and DMC environments. (R2, R3)
- Extensive experimental study across different environments (MuJoCo, DMC) and algorithms (TD3, DrQ). (R2)
- The necessity of each component was well explained using dedicated figures (Fig. 2,3,4), including a proof of catastrophic forgetting in a MuJoCo task. (R3)
- Meticulous architectural design that balances network growth with resource efficiency by recycling dormant neurons. (R4)

### Weaknesses

- **Writing and mathematical clarity**: The paper lacks mathematical rigor with inaccuracies, e.g., set notation misuse (should be \(\breve{\theta} \subset \theta\) not \(\breve{\theta} \in \theta\); \(\mathbb{I_{grow}}\) should be a set union, not addition) and \(\mathbb{I_{prune}}\) improperly defined (missing set notation for index of weights with \(f(\theta_i)\leq0\)). (R2) The algorithm (Alg. 1) works at weight level but description mentions neuron-level regeneration and pruning; missing cosine annealing scheduler and experience review details. (R2) No definition of "elastic neuron" is given; "plasticity" is used loosely as activated neuron ratio without clear definition, and the approach does not actually prevent decrease in activated neuron ratio (all methods show declines). (R2) Terms like \(Clip(a,b,c)\) used without definition. (R3) The dynamic threshold \(\nabla f(\theta)\) for neuron consolidation is confusingly defined and not clearly linked to \(\epsilon\). (R3) Many figures lack axis labels (Fig 2 x-axis, Fig 4 y-axis, Fig 5 x-axis, Fig 6 y-axis), making interpretation difficult. (R2) Line 301 mentions "dormant ratio of negative", which is impossible since ratio must be in [0,1]. (R2)
- **Unsupported claims**: The paper overclaims, stating NE mitigates loss of plasticity, primacy bias, and catastrophic forgetting, but experiments only demonstrate reduction in dormant neuron ratio. (R2) Claim that "topology growth alleviates primacy bias" is not supported. (R2) The neuron consolidation component is meant to prevent catastrophic forgetting, yet its dynamic threshold plateaus to strongest consolidation before halfway through training, questioning necessity over a simple time-dependent schedule. (R3)
- **Incomplete ablations**: Missing ablation of the dormant neuron pruning component (only neuron generation and experience review are ablated). (R2) Need baselines like only network growth without pruning to isolate component effects. (R4) Design choices such as forcing pruning amount < growth amount (guaranteeing increase) lack justification; could be achieved with ReDo plus small growth. (R3) The use of cosine annealing schedule from RigL is not designed for continual learning and needs validation. (R3) Hyperparameter sensitivity (starting sparsity, growth/prune rates) not analyzed. (R4)
- **Missing comparisons and baselines**: Not compared to Plasticity Injection in sufficient detail. (R1) Missing key baselines: NaP (Lyle et al.), CReLU/LayerNorm synergy, and other plasticity-related works cited in reviews (Ash et al., Berariu et al., Lee et al. [PLASTIC, Hare and Tortoise], Lyle et al.). (R3, R4) Also missing foundational cortical expansion citations. (R4) Need comparison with reset-based methods under identical configurations as primacy bias paper using state-based DMC. (R4)
- **Experimental setup issues**: Low number of independent runs (7) with overlapping error bars; suggests need for 30 runs for statistical significance. (R2) Fixed number of episodes should use fixed number of steps for consistent experience. (R2) Cycling experiment (Fig 9) plotted on episodes – problematic because different episode lengths give different numbers of update steps. (R3) Only tested with TD3, which is outdated; should evaluate with TD7, TD-MPC2, or BRO. (R4) Missing evaluations in state-based DMC and more challenging environments. (R3) Figures show unexplained spikes (Fig 8) and Plasticity Injection failing before injection in Walker2d and HalfCheetah (Fig 6), requiring explanation. (R3)
- **Specific technical concerns**: Policy stability during network expansion needs analysis and ablation; authors claim stability but only show Figure 6. (R1) Impact of dead neurons (positive/negative) needs analysis and visualization. (R1) The gradient approximation for elastic generation is inaccurate: it uses top-k gradients over all weights of fully expanded network, but accurate method would require adding one weight at a time with additional forward/backward passes; this approximation is not clearly justified. (R2) Reinitialized random weights may be immediately pruned if large feature values dominate; unclear how this is avoided. (R4) Experience Review’s effectiveness is not validated across all tasks; its dynamic threshold schedule matches strongest consolidation early, making its necessity unclear. (R3, R4) Dynamic actor shows similar plasticity rate to static but performs better; reason unclear. (R4) No justification for starting sparsity rate. (R4) The claim that resetting is the most effective approach (line 467) lacks a reference. (R2)
