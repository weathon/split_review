## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper analyzes the correlation between example learning speed and catastrophic forgetting, finding that examples learned early are rarely forgotten while those learned later are more susceptible. Based on this, the authors propose Goldilocks, a replay buffer sampling method that filters out examples learned too quickly or too slowly, retaining those learned at intermediate speed. They evaluate on low-to-mid complexity image classification tasks (CIFAR10, CIFAR100, TinyImageNet) using various buffer sizes, task orderings, and continual learning methods, reporting consistent but modest improvements over uniform sampling and other baselines.

### Strengths
- The analysis of learning speed and catastrophic forgetting is novel and could impact understanding of neural network learning and replay-based continual learning methods.
- The idea is clearly presented with useful illustrations and figures (e.g., binary classification matrix plots, Figure 2).
- Demonstrates simplicity bias in neural networks.
- Proposes Goldilocks, an effective replay buffer sampling method that outperforms uniform sampling and other subsampling algorithms in many cases.
- Strong empirical evidence on CIFAR100: training multiple networks and consistent observation that learning speed strongly correlates with forgetting rate.
- Consistent improvements when applying Goldilocks on top of existing methods across datasets (CIFAR10, CIFAR100, TinyImageNet).
- Results clearly presented with comprehensive ablation of quickly/slowly learned samples, easy to read.
- Experimentation covers a range of datasets and buffer sizes, two task orderings, and includes standard error and class-incremental (CIL) results in the appendix.
- Motivation is evident, and the analysis helps understanding before presenting the method.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical analysis**: The observed correlation between learning speed and forgetting is purely empirical, with no theoretical justification, limiting significance. (Reviewer 2 is particularly critical of this.)
- **Insufficient exploration of learning dynamics**: The analysis does not explore how factors like learning rate (for different tasks), optimizers (SGD, Adam, AdaGrad), network architecture (only ResNet and its variants; transformers, VGG not tested), and regularization affect the correlation.
- **No rationale for medium-speed examples**: No explanation or intuition is provided for why medium learning speed items are most useful for replay; rationale beyond empirical results is missing.
- **Limited evaluation scope**:
  - Only low-to-mid complexity image classification tasks (CIFAR10, CIFAR100, TinyImageNet); evaluation on higher complexity tasks (e.g., ImageNet) is missing.
  - Most experiments use only 2-task sequences; performance may change with more tasks.
  - Task-Incremental (TIL) setting is thoroughly analyzed, but class-incremental (CIL) results are less furnished: only on CIFAR100-2 with buffer size 500, not comparable to state-of-the-art. One reviewer (R5) suggests CIFAR100-10 with buffer sizes 1k/2k and anti-task-recency bias; another (R4) states CIL results were not found in the appendix.
  - Method only applies to offline CL scenarios (not online/stream learning) and only to classification tasks.
- **Marginal improvement**: The benefit of Goldilocks is often marginal. One reviewer (R5) considers improvements consistent, but others note that changes are often ≤2% between removal combinations and frequently within standard deviation (R3, R4).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity without guidance**: The parameters s and q (percentages of slowest/fastest examples to remove) vary across datasets and buffer sizes; no analysis or guidance is provided for setting them in new settings. If extensive tuning is required, it defeats the premise of continual learning (R1). The paper does not demonstrate how to deploy the method in a new setting (R3).
- **Dependence on training time**: The conclusion may depend on the number of epochs per task—with few epochs, hardest examples may not be learned, requiring different replay strategy; guidance on required epochs is missing (R1).
- **Missing analysis of example types**: No analysis of what characterizes too-easy and too-hard examples (e.g., relation to c-score, Jiang et al. 2021). (R4)
- **Incomplete experimental details**:
  - Table 1 should include results on CIFAR-100-5, CIFAR-100-20, and TinyImageNet for completeness. (R1)
  - The same class order is always used; seeds should introduce new class orders to avoid bias. (R4)
  - The replay strategy alternates batches instead of standard 50-50 mixing; reason not explained. (R4)
  - For TinyImageNet with buffer size 500, there are less than 3 examples per class; experiments with larger buffer sizes are needed. (R4)
  - Need a reference for the “original work” in line 466. (R4)
- **Claims not fully supported**: The assertion that optimal replay buffer composition is largely independent of task similarity (line 397) is based only on CIFAR100 experiments and is not clearly demonstrated. (R4)
- **Minor**: The title does not fit the primary message of the paper. (R3)
