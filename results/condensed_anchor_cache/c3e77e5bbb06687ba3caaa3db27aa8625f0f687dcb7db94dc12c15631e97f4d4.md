## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a post-training sublayer pruning method for LLMs, using a novel normalized relative impact factor (NRI) metric based on perplexity to progressively prune MHA and FFN sublayers. The method achieves 20% parameter reduction and 37% inference speedup with minimal performance loss on tasks such as PIQA, WinoGrande, HellaSwag, ARC-e, ARC-c. Experiments span models from 6.7B to 70B. Reviewers disagree on novelty and evaluation comprehensiveness: R1 and R3 (score 5) find the analysis insightful and method effective and well-presented; R2 (score 3) considers the contribution incremental; R4 (score 1) strongly criticizes similarity to prior work and insufficient evaluation.

### Strengths
- Detailed analysis of sublayer activations (output magnitude, input-output similarity, importance) and feature L2 norm for residual connections and MHA/FFN, providing intriguing insights into layer redundancy (R1, R3).
- Approach is intuitive and clearly presented, with helpful design guidelines for the community (R1, R2, R3).
- Pruning method is neat and clean: removes sublayers directly without additional hardware support, and shows relatively good performance compared to baselines (R3).
- Real-device acceleration is profiled on GPU; experiments conducted on multiple model scales (6.7B to 70B) (R1, R2).
- Visualizations are well-done, aiding understanding (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and similarity to prior work**: Multiple reviewers note strong resemblance to existing depth pruning methods (ShortGPT [3], Shortened LLaMA [2], and He et al., June 2024 [1]), with similar conclusions about intermediate layer redundancy and scaling behaviour (R1, R2, R4). R2 specifically notes that Guidelines 1 and 2 have been established in prior work. R4 asks for an explicit explanation of essential innovation over [1].
- **Insufficient evaluation on challenging benchmarks**: Evaluation limited to PIQA, WinoGrande, HellaSwag, ARC-e, ARC-c. Reviewers request evaluation on MMLU, GSM8K, AGIEval, HumanEval, MT-Bench, Alpaca-Eval, and recall-intensive tasks to comprehensively assess pruned model quality (R1, R2, R4).
- **Missing comparisons with competitive baselines**: Paper does not compare against coarse-grained pruning methods such as ShortGPT, Shortened LLaMA, EDGE-LLM, and others (e.g., [1], [5], [6] in R2, [2], [3], [1] in R4) (R2, R4).
- **Ablation study gaps**:
  - No ablation comparing MHA-only vs. FFN-only pruning under the same sparsity to support claim that MHA is more redundant (R3).
  - No ablation justifying the progressive search strategy over a naive one-shot identification of all prune indices (R3, R4). R4 notes prior work [4] removes layers all at once.
- **Dependence on perplexity is questionable**: Relative impact factor relies solely on perplexity, which prior work [3,4] shows is not always reliable. Authors should discuss rationale and limitations (R2).
- **Progressive search is computationally inefficient**: Iterative process requires multiple passes and grows with model depth (Table 5); scalability concerns for larger models (R3, R4). R4 questions whether iterative removal is better than all-at-once decisions.
- **Calibration data sensitivity unexamined**: No analysis of how number of calibration samples or data domain affects pruning decisions (R4).
- **Speedup discrepancy with Wanda**: Paper reports 1.10× speedup on LLaMA-7B with 2:4 sparsity, while Wanda reports 1.24×; using 2 GPUs for LLaMA-2-70B benchmarking introduces communication overhead that may affect accuracy (R4).
- **Model choice and currency**: Experiments are mainly on OPT and LLaMA-2; reviewers recommend including newer models like Mistral and LLaMA-3 for generality (R4).
- **Minor issues**: Footer/header misalignment on pages 5-6 (R4). In Line 796, PIQA accuracy at 10% pruning is worse than at 20% pruning, which may indicate a data recording error (R4). Tables should highlight best results for quick reading (R4).
