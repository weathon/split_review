## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Video-STaR, a self-training method for video instruction tuning that uses any labeled video dataset. The method cycles between answer generation, label rationalization, and filtering with original labels as weak supervision. Experiments show improvements on TempCompass (+10%), Kinetics700-QA (+20%), FineDiving (+15%), and release the VSTAR-1M dataset. Reviewers note that the approach addresses a significant problem and is clearly written, but raise concerns about computational cost, limited technical novelty relative to STaR, weak baselines, insufficient analysis of hallucination and verification, marginal cross-dataset generalization, and heuristic label verification.

### Strengths
- **Originality**: First self-training pipeline for video instruction tuning that enables use of any labeled video dataset (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Significance**: Addresses a critical problem in video QA and LMM adaptation; releases large VSTAR-1M dataset (1M videos) with rich CoT reasoning (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Clarity**: Well-written, methodology clearly articulated with effective visual aids (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Effectiveness**: Demonstrates large improvements (TempCompass +10%, Kinetics700-QA +20%, FineDiving +15%) and reduces hallucinations in answers (Reviewer 1).
- **Ablation study**: Comprehensive ablation experiments validate contributions of different components (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty**: The approach closely follows STaR with no special video-specific considerations; all modules (generation, rationalization, verification) are text-based, making transfer straightforward (Reviewer 2). (Minority: Reviewer 4 calls it elegant, but Reviewer 2 emphasizes lack of video novelty.)
- **Weak baselines**: Video-LLaVA Gemini is trained on only 2K samples compared to 550K for Video-STaR; the paper does not show how performance scales with labeled data quantity (Reviewers 3, 4). Video-LLaVA+ sample construction is not explained (Reviewer 4). Missing baseline comparison with VideoInstruct, a standard video instruction dataset creation method (Reviewer 4).
- **Hallucination and error analysis insufficient**: The paper claims rationalization may cause hallucinations but provides no quantitative analysis (frequency, types); the example in Figure 3 shows a repetition error, not hallucination, and incomplete generation (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). No significance testing or partial sample analysis (Reviewer 2).
- **Label verification is heuristic and inflexible**: Requires custom parsers (e.g., regex for timestamps) and manual matching of label variants (e.g., "chopping wood" vs "smashing"), limiting scalability (Reviewer 3). No precision/recall analysis of the parser-verifier (Reviewer 3). Including GT labels in answers is not justified and may introduce bias (Reviewer 3). Verifier type assignment (manual or rule-based) is not clarified (Reviewer 2).
- **Computational overhead**: Iterative self-training cycles are computationally intensive, especially for long/high-res videos; no per-cycle cost analysis (Reviewer 1). No mechanism to skip rationalization for simple labels (Reviewer 1).
- **Marginal cross-dataset generalization**: Improvement in Table 4 and Table 7 is small; authors attribute to benchmark noise but do not test on alternative high-quality datasets (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited dataset variety**: Evaluations focus on FineDiving, STAR, Kinetics700; broader tasks and annotation styles needed (Reviewer 1).
- **Self-training stability not addressed**: No discussion of potential catastrophic degradation from self-generated data; no experiment on alternating training between two models (Reviewer 3).
- **Undefined stopping criterion**: No threshold or metric to determine when self-training converges (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing justification for model initialization**: In each cycle the model is initialized from the same non-instruction-tuned checkpoint instead of reusing the previous cycle model (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear handling of numerical labels**: No details on verifier for temporal localization, bounding boxes, performance scores (e.g., L1 distance) or model accuracy in predicting numerical values (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Incomplete ablation details**: The last row of Tables 6 and 7 (“- Generation”) is ambiguous – it is unclear whether both generation and rationalization are removed (Reviewer 4). The effect of using the full VSTAR-1M (1M) vs 550K is not tested (Reviewer 2).
- **Typographical errors**: Table indices are incorrect (e.g., Table 2.3, 3.2) (Reviewer 3).
