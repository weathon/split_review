## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces a three-stage framework for latent 3D reconstruction: (1) correspondence-aware autoencoding to enforce 3D consistency in the VAE latent space, (2) a latent radiance field (LRF) built with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) operating on latent features, and (3) a VAE-Radiance Field (VAE-RF) alignment strategy to improve decoding. The method is evaluated on novel view synthesis (MVImgNet, NeRF-LLFF, MipNeRF360, DL3DV-10K) and text-to-3D generation, claiming to achieve photorealistic 3D reconstruction from latent representations without per-scene refinement. Multiple reviewers note strong engineering and success in injecting 3D awareness into the latent space, but raise significant concerns about motivation, experimental fairness, and technical soundness.

### Strengths
- The overarching innovations integrate 3D awareness into 2D latent representations, with the correspondence-aware autoencoding being the key enabler (R1).
- Using 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to model the latent space is a novel and intriguing idea (R2).
- The paper demonstrates competent engineering in combining multiple components into a working system that functions without per-scene refinement modules (R3).
- Evaluation covers multiple datasets (MVImgNet, NeRF-LLFF, MipNeRF360, DL3DV-10K) and tasks (novel view synthesis, text-to-3D generation), providing some evidence of cross-dataset generalizability (R3).
- The proposed framework effectively enhances the 3D consistency of 2D latent representations and mitigates the gap between 2D latent space and natural 3D space (R1, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear motivation / insufficient justification for operating in latent space** (R2, R3): The paper fails to show clear benefits over image-space methods in terms of reconstruction accuracy, rendering speed, storage reduction, or other applications. Modest PSNR/SSIM gains do not justify the added complexity. A thorough efficiency analysis (training/inference speed, memory usage) against image-space baselines is missing (R3).
- **Correspondence mechanism has fundamental technical gaps** (R3):
  - Scale mismatch: COLMAP provides pixel-level correspondences while the VAE operates at a lower resolution (likely 8× or 16× downsampled) with large receptive fields. The paper does not explain how pixel-to-pixel correspondences are meaningfully mapped to latent cells, how multiple pixel correspondences within one latent cell are handled, or how the receptive field size affects correspondence accuracy.
  - Details of the correspondence filtering pipeline are absent: thresholds for COLMAP matching, additional filtering criteria in latent space, and handling of outlier correspondences.
  - Justification for using L1 distance on latent features (Eq. 6) is not provided given the coarse nature of latent correspondences.
- **Unfair experimental setup handicaps comparison methods** (R2, R3, R4):
  - Image-space baselines (e.g., 3DGS, Mip-Splatting) are trained at the latent-space resolution (8× lower than image resolution), which yields very poor visual results. Meanwhile the proposed method has access to full-resolution data during VAE training. A fair comparison requires either training at matched resolutions or demonstrating a specific advantage under computational constraints (R3). Reviewer 4 suggests training competing methods at full resolution, and notes that the proposed method might outperform Mip-Splatting in sparse-view settings due to the generation capability of Stable Diffusion VAE.
- **Incomplete evaluation of speed, memory, and practical aspects** (R2, R3):
  - Training and inference times for Stage 2 (LRF construction) and the overall pipeline are not reported; only Stage 1 and Stage 3 training times are given (Section 5.1) (R2).
  - Stage 2 is scene-specific (requiring per-scene optimization of 3DGS in latent space), which does not save storage compared to 3DGS (R2).
  - Failure cases are not shown; the method’s performance under varying illumination, complex geometry, or challenging scenes is not assessed (R3).
- **View-consistency issues due to standard 2D VAE decoder** (R4):
  - The correspondence-aware constraint encourages the encoder to produce consistent latent codes, but the decoder is a standard 2D VAE decoder not trained for multi-view consistency. This leads to flickering effects and detail losses when decoding different views of the same 3D point, making strict multi-view consistency unattainable.
- **Object appearance change in rendering** (R4):
  - In Fig 1, the building color changes between rendering (red) and ground truth (white), raising concerns about identity preservation and reliability of generated novel views.
- **Spherical harmonics (SH) in latent space is puzzling** (R3): Given the features are already in a learned latent space, adding SH basis functions (Eq. 8) seems unnecessary; a simpler view-dependent representation might suffice.
- **VAE-RF alignment stage appears unnecessarily complex** (R3): The authors should quantify the alleged distribution shift and explore simpler alternatives. Details on balancing training/novel view losses and preventing overfitting during decoder fine-tuning are missing.
- **Ablation study is insufficient** (R3): Missing ablations on correspondence quality (e.g., with/without correspondence filtering), loss function components, and architectural variations.
- **Text-to-3D generation comparisons are outdated** (R2): The two methods used for comparison are relatively old; recent approaches like IPDreamer should be considered.
- **Missing related work citation** (R1): CaesarNeRF (Calibrated Semantic Representation for Few-shot Generalizable Neural Rendering) uses calibrated image features in each 2D latent and should be cited and compared.
- **Experiment settings are not detailed** (R4): The paper lacks information about view sampling strategy during training, number of input vs. evaluation views, camera pose distribution, and rendering camera parameters. This hinders reproducibility and assessment of generalizability, particularly for sparse-view settings.
- **Claims of “photorealistic reconstruction” lack validation** (R3): Proper user studies or established perceptual metrics are needed to substantiate this claim (R3).
