## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Restricted Adaptive Feedback (RAF), a learning rule based on Feedback Alignment that uses low-rank matrices for error feedback, enabling control over error signal dimensionality. A theoretical derivation for linear networks is provided and extended to nonlinear and convolutional architectures. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and analysis of receptive fields show that low-dimensional error (matching task dimensionality) can achieve performance comparable to backpropagation, and that error dimensionality shapes learned features.

### Strengths
- Theoretical derivation for linear networks is clear, well-structured, and validated with simulations (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Experiments on CIFAR-10 with varying numbers of classes demonstrate a match between error rank and task dimensionality, yielding competitive performance (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Analysis of receptive fields shows that error dimensionality influences feature emergence (center-surround vs. orientation selective), providing insights into representation learning (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The method achieves performance comparable to backpropagation in several settings, and the link between minimal error rank and task dimensionality offers useful intuition for biologically plausible learning (Reviewers 3, 4).
- The paper is well-structured and correct (Reviewer 4; Reviewer 2 disagrees on writing quality, see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and presentation issues**: The paper uses figurative language and redundancy, making it less rigorous; notations are repeated; there is a placeholder caption in Appendix Figure 5 (“Enter Caption”); and a typo in line 215 (“necessary”) (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 3 also notes small fonts in figures).  
- **Limited experimental scope**: The evaluation is on simple settings (linear/shallow networks, CIFAR-10). No experiments on ImageNet or other datasets (e.g., SVHN) are provided, which would be necessary to demonstrate scalability and fair comparison with prior work (Reviewers 1, 2). Reviewer 2 emphasizes that Akrout et al. (2019) matched backprop on ResNet-50/ImageNet, and Sanfiz and Akrout (2021) and Launay et al. (2020) (scaling DFA to transformers) are not cited.  
- **Novelty and significance concerns**:
  - The low-dimensionality of the error signal is trivial: for any network, the gradient is \( J^T err \), where \( err \) is d-dimensional (d = output dimension) (Reviewer 4).  
  - Implicit regularization literature (Ma & Ying 2021, Feng & Tu 2021) suggests SGD already operates in a low-dimensional space; these references should be discussed (Reviewer 1).  
  - The improvement over Akrout (2019) is small; the core contribution (low-rank feedback) is a minor extension, and adding an extra feedback layer reduces biological plausibility (Reviewer 3).  
- **Biological plausibility issues**:
  - The Oja’s rule implementation for updating \( P \) is not local because the matrix version uses \( (P\delta)(P\delta)^T P \), which requires multiplying by the transpose of weights, contradicting locality claims (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 notes the 1D Oja’s rule is local but only finds the top principal component.  
  - The use of batch normalization, max-pooling, and dropout lacks biological interpretation; it is unclear how batch norm is trained (via RAF? affine=false?) (Reviewer 2).  
  - Reviewer 4 argues that “biologically plausible” is a meaningless, controversial term that adds little value to the paper.  
- **Missing baselines in Section 5**: The analysis of receptive fields does not include baselines (e.g., same architecture trained with backprop with/without a feedforward bottleneck), making the qualitative results difficult to assess (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 1 also asks about the relationship with Lindsay et al. 2019). A quantitative plot of center-surround to orientation shift as a function of bottleneck width is requested (Reviewer 3).  
- **Misrepresentation of prior work**:
  - The paper states that Akrout et al. (2019) “struggles to match BP performance in complex CNNs,” but that work matches backprop on ResNet-50/ImageNet (Reviewer 3).  
  - The difficulty of training CNNs with feedback alignment is overstated; Liao et al. (2016) (sign-symmetry) and Akrout et al. (2019) should be cited in lines 416–420 (Reviewer 3).  
  - Equation 10 is described as “superficially similar” to feedback alignment, but it is essentially the Kolen-Pollack rule with one extra step; the similarity should be acknowledged (Reviewer 3).  
- **Questions about generalizability and resource costs**:
  - Can the findings be extended to NLP or self-supervised learning? Would task dimensionality still determine minimal rank? (Reviewer 2)  
  - The algorithm may reduce reverse-pass compute but requires storing additional feedback weights and activations, increasing memory costs; these trade-offs are not studied (Reviewer 4).  
- **Minor issues**: In Equation 10 (left), \( h_l \) should be \( h_{l-1} \) (Reviewer 3).
