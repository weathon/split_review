- Scores: 3, 3, 5, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces CAT Pruning (Cluster-Aware Token Pruning), a training-free acceleration method for text-to-image diffusion models. It selectively updates tokens in attention layers based on relative noise magnitude, spatial clustering, and balanced selection frequencies (staleness), combined with a caching mechanism. Experiments on Stable Diffusion 3 and Pixart‑Σ (28 and 50 steps) on PartiPrompts and COCO2017 show up to 2× speedup and 50–60% reduction in MACs while maintaining CLIP Score. The paper is well-received by two reviewers (scores 5) but seen as lacking rigor by two others (scores 3).

### Strengths
- **Important topic** – Computational efficiency is a major limitation of diffusion models; work addressing it is of high value to the field.
- **Notable speedup** – Up to 2× inference speedup with 50–60% reduction in MACs while preserving CLIP Score (all reviewers agree).
- **No additional training** – The method applies to pre-trained models without extra training costs.
- **Novel combination** – CAT Pruning uniquely combines token-level pruning with caching, introduces “Relative Noise Magnitude” (difference between current predicted noise and noise at a reference step \(t_0\)), and incorporates spatial clustering with distributional balance to improve token selection (Reviewer 4).
- **Comprehensive analysis of token selection** – The analysis of noise magnitude, spatial clustering, and balancing is thorough and well motivated (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Reasonable experimental scope** – Evaluation on standard datasets (MS‑COCO 2017, PartiPrompts) and latest models (SD3, Pixart‑Σ), with comparisons to AT‑EDM and visualizations at different sparsity levels (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

**Presentation and clarity (Reviewers 2, 3; Reviewer 3 considers minor)**  
- The paper never clearly states that tokens are pruned in the attention layers of the diffusion model; multiple researchers mistakenly assumed tokens were pruned in the text embeddings (Reviewer 2).
- MACs are used as a key performance metric but never defined (Reviewer 2).
- It is not specified between which samples the CLIP Score is calculated (Reviewer 2).
- Diffusion notations are inconsistent with common conventions: noise estimates are usually denoted \(\epsilon\); \(t\) should decrease during reverse diffusion; the claim that diffusion involves solving a reverse‑time SDE is incompatible with the rectified flow model SD3 used in experiments (Reviewer 2).
- Layout problems: a third of page 7 is empty, while page 8 is almost exclusively figures (Reviewer 2).
- Tables 2 and 3 lack bold numbers to highlight best results (Reviewer 2).
- A brief overview of the diffusion model architecture (e.g., SD3‑like) would substantially improve readability (Reviewer 3).

**Insufficient comparison with baselines (all reviewers)**  
- Only one token‑pruning baseline (AT‑EDM) is compared; missing comparisons with other training‑free methods: \(\Delta\)-DiT, Faster Diffusion, TGATE, KV caching, and DeepCache (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- No qualitative comparison with competing methods (Reviewer 2).
- Missing comparison with distillation‑based and consistency‑model approaches to assess whether CAT Pruning offers advantages when applied to those models (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The paper does not acknowledge or compare with VQVAE/VQGAN‑based methods that achieve speedups via compressed latent spaces (e.g., VQ‑Diffusion, LDM with VQ‑regularization); direct empirical comparison on efficiency (inference time, memory, FLOPs) is needed (Reviewer 4).

**Limited evaluation metrics (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)**  
- CLIP Score alone is used to measure image quality; it captures text‑image alignment but not visual fidelity. FID and LPIPS should be added (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The main claim – that outputs after pruning are perceptually similar to the unpruned baseline – is never empirically evaluated; LPIPS distance would be the obvious metric (Reviewer 2).
- Testing on compositionality and long‑caption generation would provide a more holistic generation quality assessment (Reviewer 3).

**Missing ablations and empirical rigor (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)**  
- The impact of spatial clustering is only shown with subtle qualitative examples (Figure 6); a thorough quantitative ablation is needed (Reviewer 1).
- The trade‑off between compute reduction and pruning percentage \(\alpha\) is demonstrated with only four examples, not ablated empirically (Reviewer 2).
- Many design choices are showcased with one or two qualitative examples but lack systematic empirical ablation (Reviewer 2).
- Results in Tables 2 and 3 report no confidence intervals or standard deviations (Reviewer 2).
- Limited theoretical justification: the method relies on empirical observations; Proposition 1 (with a simplified proof in the appendix) would benefit from error bounds on pruning, convergence properties, and deeper analysis of the relationship between noise magnitude, staleness, and clustering (Reviewer 4).

**Applicability and architecture**  
- It is unclear whether CAT Pruning is restricted to DiT architectures or also applies to UNet‑based diffusion models with attention (Reviewer 2, Q1).
- The paper does not test on any architecture other than DiTs (Reviewer 2).

**Unclear method components and missing explanations**  
- How exactly is the cache optimization realized? The paper mentions combining token‑level pruning with a cache mechanism but never specifies which part of the method performs cache optimization; is it the frequency monitoring over timesteps? (Reviewer 2, Q2).
- What is \(t_0\) used in the experiments? (Reviewer 1, Q2).
- Differences in inference speed between CAT Pruning and existing token pruning methods are not explained (Reviewer 1, Q1).
- Differences in speed and CLIP Score between CAT Pruning and the AT‑EDM baseline are not explained (Reviewer 1, Q3).

**Additional suggestions that reflect missing analysis**  
- No discussion of how the method relates to temporal redundancy observed in caching works like DeepCache; the “staleness” metric may capture similar consistency (Reviewer 4).
- No comparison of pruning schedules with AT‑EDM’s Denoising‑Steps‑Aware Pruning (DSAP) that adjusts ratios across timesteps; CAT Pruning also uses a “prune‑less” early schedule (Reviewer 4).
- No discussion of token recovery strategies, e.g., the similarity‑based copy used in AT‑EDM, which could improve generalizability (Reviewer 4).
