## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an adaptive hierarchical certification method for semantic segmentation. It builds a class hierarchy and adaptively relaxes certification to coarser levels for uncertain pixels, reducing the abstain rate compared to the flat baseline SegCertify (Fischer et al., 2021). A new evaluation metric, Certified Information Gain (CIG), is introduced to account for information loss at coarser levels. The paper claims experiments on Cityscapes, PASCAL-Context, ACDC, and COCO-Stuff, but several reviewers noted that the main paper appears to present results only for Cityscapes and ACDC. One reviewer (R2, score 6) found the method well-organized and technically sound but admitted lack of expertise; the other three reviewers (scores 3, 3, 3) raised concerns about novelty, experimental sufficiency, and clarity.

### Strengths
- The research problem (certification for semantic segmentation with lower abstain rates) is important and timely (R3, R4).
- The paper is well-structured and easy to follow, building clearly on prior work (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The proposed method shows better results than the single baseline SegCertify (R1, R2, R4). Reviewer R4 specifically highlights good results in terms of the proposed CIG metric.
- Introducing hierarchical certification for segmentation is a clear novelty, being more suitable than flat certification for per-pixel prediction (R4). Reviewer R4 rated contribution and presentation as good (3/4 each) in contrast to the other reviewers who rated contribution lower (2 or 1).
- One reviewer (R2, score 6) considered the method technically sound and verified by experiments, though this reviewer also stated low confidence and inability to assess the area professionally.

### Weaknesses
**Limited novelty and theoretical contribution**  
- The method is largely based on SegCertify (Fischer et al., 2021); differences are only the hierarchical structure and the CIG metric, which reviewers view as a simple modification without essential theoretical novelty (R1, R3).  
- Theorem 1 is taken directly from Fischer et al. and placed in the main paper without clear justification (R4).  
- The adaptation mechanism is not clearly delineated from the hierarchical certification itself; it is unclear how the adaptive component contributes independently (R3).  
- The CIG metric lacks insight into why that specific form was chosen; the motivation is insufficiently explained (R1).

**Insufficient experiments**  
- Only one baseline (SegCertify) is compared; more baselines are needed, including methods for hierarchical classification or robust segmentation (R1, R3).  
- Reported performance gaps are very small (<2%), which is not convincing (R1).  
- The main paper uses only two datasets (Cityscapes and ACDC), both with 19 classes; the abstract mentions four but experiments on PASCAL-Context and COCO-Stuff are not sufficiently evaluated in the main paper (R1, R4). Reviewers recommend testing on datasets with more classes (e.g., ADE20K with >100 categories) to verify the method’s effectiveness and universality (R4).  
- Results are presented for only 100 images (unless misunderstood) (R1); full dataset results should be provided.  
- No visual examples of failure cases or cases where adaptive certification helps; Figure 1 is insufficient (R1, R4).  
- Less than 2/9 of the main paper space is used for experimental evaluation; the supplementary material is not adequate to compensate (R4).

**Unclear details and notation**  
- The meaning of “IP” in Eq. (1) is not explained (R1, R2).  
- The notation for probability (\(\mathbb{P}\)) is not immediately clear; more explicit definition and motivation for its use in the certification framework are needed (R2).  
- Type I and Type II errors are not defined (R1).  
- The operations SAMPLEPOSTERIORS and HSAMPLE are not explained (R1).  
- The effect of using different hierarchical structures for the class graph is not studied (R1).  
- The method requires a pre-defined class hierarchy graph for each new dataset, and it is unclear how to make the approach universal when applying to datasets like ADE20K (R4).  
- Equations in the preliminaries are difficult to follow for readers not deeply familiar with certification literature; more detailed explanations of theorems are needed (R2).
