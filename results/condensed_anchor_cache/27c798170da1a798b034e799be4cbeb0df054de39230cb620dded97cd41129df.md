## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Flow Graph Neural Networks (FlowGNNs) and their DAG extension (DAFlowGNN) to enforce conservation laws (Kirchhoff’s first law) in flow graphs. The key idea is to normalize attention over outgoing edges instead of incoming ones, preventing information duplication. DAFlowGNN adds both forward and backward passes on DAGs to consider all descendants. Experiments on power grid classification and electronic circuit regression compare with GCN, GAT, GIN, GT, DAGNN, PACE, showing mixed improvements.

### Strengths
- **Simple and intuitive idea** – normalizing outgoing messages is a clear architectural bias for flow conservation; the motivation captures reader’s interest quickly (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 5).
- **Sensible methodological propositions** – both the outgoing normalization and the forward+backward pass on DAGs are well-motivated (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- **Extension to DAGs enables discrimination** – DAFlowGNN can distinguish some non-isomorphic flow graphs that standard DAG-specific GNNs cannot (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 5, partially Reviewer 4).
- **Practical performance** – FlowGNN-based architectures outperform several baselines on the majority of studied datasets (especially Ckt-Bench101) with marginal gains in some cases (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- **Self-contained and clearly written** – paper is well organized with neat figures, easy to reproduce experimental details (Reviewer 4; Reviewer 5 finds introduction too long – see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty** – the core change is simply normalizing attention over outgoing instead of incoming neighbors; the significance of this adjustment is not well-illustrated for non-experts (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 5). One reviewer notes it introduces no new architectures or theoretical innovations beyond existing methods (Reviewer 5).
- **Insufficient theoretical analysis** – the discussion of expressiveness (Section 3.3) is informal and incomplete; a formal theorem is missing. The paper only partially shows that DAFlowGNN distinguishes one pair of DAGs, but claims about discriminating “all” non-isomorphic DAGs are misleading (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- **Mixed empirical evidence** – FlowGNN is sometimes outperformed by GIN (Table 1, PowerGraph), and improvements on the PowerGraph dataset are not exciting; benefits are not directly realized consistently (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2). In binary classification tasks FlowGNN does not achieve SOTA while it does on multiclass (Reviewer 5).
- **Missing methodological details** – notation is unclear (e.g., what \(h_i\) refers to in Eqs. 9‑11, how \(h^{rv}, h^{fw}\) are initialized); the attention scheme differs from standard GAT (no LeakyReLU, one less weight matrix) without motivation; the use of a GRU over a sequence of length 2 (vs. MLP concatenation) is not justified; the FlowGT model is never defined, leaving its forward/backward pass unclear (Reviewer 1).
- **Flawed model selection procedure** – results are reported for all layer depths (e.g., DAGNN-2/4, DAFlowGNN-1/2) instead of using the best configuration per model found on the validation set; this invalidates fair risk estimation and fair comparison across model classes (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing computational efficiency analysis** – time and space complexity of FlowGNN and DAFlowGNN are not provided; DAFlowGNN is admitted to be twice as expensive as DAGNN, but no efficiency experiments or mitigation techniques are discussed (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 5).
- **Conservation law claim may be misleading** – the paper invokes Kirchhoff’s law, but there is no guarantee that the total “flow” is conserved (nodes may receive more or less information than a single message, and always output a single message). If flow is not conserved, the impact is reduced and the “flow” naming should be reconsidered (Reviewer 4).
- **Narrow application domain** – experiments only cover electronic circuits and power grids (both electrical), raising doubts about generalizability to other resource flows (e.g., traffic); more diverse benchmarks are needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Baseline methods are not the latest/strongest** – comparisons do not include recent DAG-specific methods such as GraphNOTEARS, ContextualDAG, or DAG Transformers (Reviewer 5).
- **Missing related work** – the paper fails to acknowledge earlier recursive neural network architectures for DAGs (Sperduti 1997, Frasconi 1998, Micheli 2009) that incorporate similar contextual bias and whose expressive power is fully characterized (Reviewer 4).
- **Notation convention confusion** – reviewer points out that the notation \((u,v)\) for a directed edge is assumed opposite to the usual convention (Reviewer 2). 
- **Intuition for reverse pass insufficient** – it is not completely clear why the backward propagation (reverse pass) is needed for flow dependency on all descendants; an ablation or formal proof is missing (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 1’s Q5).
- **Poor justification for global max pooling** – the use of global max pooling for graph-level readout is unusual; sum pooling is often preferred; the choice is not motivated (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of experiments with deeper models** – maximum number of layers appears optimal; it is unclear whether deeper models would yield further improvements; a grid search over layers is recommended (Reviewer 1).
