## Merged Review

### Summary

Three reviewers gave a score of 6 and one gave a score of 3. The paper uses path patching (causal intervention) and mean ablation to identify attention heads responsible for one-digit integer addition in LLaMA2-7B, Qwen-7B, and ChatGLM2-6B. Using 10,000 samples across 20 question formats, the authors find that only ~0.5% of attention heads significantly impact addition performance. Ablating those heads degrades performance, and the same heads also affect subtraction. The work is presented as a preliminary exploration into LLM arithmetic mechanisms.

### Strengths

- **Timely and important problem**: Understanding inner mechanisms of LLMs, especially mathematical reasoning, is crucial given rapid progress.
- **Clear and well‑organized presentation**: The paper is concisely written, with many figures making analysis concrete; the task is cleanly defined and hypothesis testing is systematic.
- **Thorough and rigorous analysis**: The use of three models (LLaMA2‑7B, Qwen‑7B, ChatGLM2‑6B), 20 question formats, and 10,000 samples provides breadth and depth. The key finding (a small, consistent set of attention heads is used for addition across formats and models) is confirmed via detailed ablation and counterfactual experiments.
- **Validation across models and tasks**: Findings are replicated on several LLMs, and preliminary results show the same heads influence subtraction—a non‑obvious result suggesting possible duality discovery or number‑focused attention.
- **Methodological value**: The introduced approach (path patching for sensitivity analysis) could be valuable for model sparsification or analyzing other capabilities.
- **Attempts dependency analysis**: The paper recognizes that later heads depend on earlier ones and tries to analyze these dependencies (though none were found).

*Minority reviewer (score 3) agrees that the analysis is thorough and clearly written, but stresses that the work focuses on localization rather than true interpretation.*

### Weaknesses

- **Scope is very restrictive**: The paper is limited to **one‑digit addition and subtraction without “carry over” or “borrow one”** examples. The abstract should explicitly state this scope. No experiments on multi‑digit numbers, more than two integers, rational numbers, or repeated additions are provided.
- **No mechanistic explanation**: The paper identifies *which* attention heads are involved but does not explain *how* they perform the calculation—i.e., no analysis of what each head computes (e.g., numerical representation manipulation vs. intermediate processing). The work is localization, not interpretation.
- **Missing comparisons/ablation requests**:
  - No analogous experiments on multiplication and division were conducted; these would strengthen the claim that a select group of heads consistently influences all arithmetic.
  - No analysis of alternative number representations (e.g., “two” vs. “2”, Roman numerals, other languages). This leaves open whether heads merely attend to numeric tokens or perform higher‑order arithmetic reasoning.
  - The paper does not discuss differences between the three models (e.g., number of heads used, architectural differences). Even a statement that no differences were found would be valuable.
  - The subtraction analysis is preliminary; a detailed comparison (including whether different algorithms are used for A > B vs. A < B) is left for future work.
- **Methodological concerns**:
  - Attention patterns in Fig. 4 are unclear: attention values appear negative (impossible for standard attention) and the heads do not attend to all relevant numbers (e.g., head attends to 3 but not 5). This undermines the claim about how these heads participate in summation.
  - The analysis focuses almost exclusively on attention heads, ignoring MLPs, which are known to perform complex computations. A complete picture of arithmetic mechanisms requires including MLP contributions.
  - Decoupling effects of individual heads is problematic because in LLaMA (and likely others) a single output projection matrix is applied to the concatenation of all heads, mixing information across heads. The paper does not resolve this.
- **Overclaiming and lack of controls**:
  - The claim “only the absence of these heads leads to a notable decline” is too strong; perturbing embeddings or output projections would also cause decline.
  - It is not surprising that only a few heads are involved in summation (attention heads typically have diverse functions). The paper should contextualize this expectation.
  - It is unclear whether the identified heads are specialized for arithmetic or are polysemantic (important for other tasks too). A control task (e.g., a non‑numeric sentence) is missing.
- **Missing related work**: The paper does not cite some relevant publications on mathematical reasoning in LLMs.
- **Contribution is limited**: The small scope (only one‑digit addition/subtraction) and lack of mechanistic interpretation constrain reusability and impact, as noted by the minority reviewer (score 3, contribution 2) and two other reviewers (contribution 2).
