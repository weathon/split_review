## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces JOWA (Jointly-Optimized World-Action model), an offline model-based RL agent pretrained on multiple Atari games (15 games, 6 billion tokens) using a shared transformer backbone that jointly optimizes world dynamics and Q‑value functions. A parallelizable beam‑search planning algorithm is used at inference to compensate for Q‑value estimation errors. Empirical results show that JOWA (150 M parameters) achieves 78.9 % human‑level performance on pretrained games using only 10 % subsampled data, outperforming prior large‑scale offline RL baselines by 31.6 % on average. The model also transfers to novel games with only 5 k offline fine‑tuning data (~4 trajectories) per game.

### Strengths
- **Strong performance and scalability.** JOWA significantly outperforms existing multi‑task offline RL methods on Atari (Table 2: Mean 0.456, IQM 0.789; Table 3: Mean 0.647, IQM 0.647). Performance improves with model size (40 M → 70 M → 150 M) without instability, demonstrating favorable scaling.
- **Joint optimization stabilizes large‑scale training.** The shared backbone with world‑modeling loss regularizes temporal‑difference learning, enabling stable training of large transformers across multiple tasks.
- **Comprehensive ablation studies.** The paper examines the impact of task embeddings, training losses, synthetic data usage, planning, and policy‑world model co‑training, providing useful insights.
- **Open‑source code and checkpoints** are released, benefiting the community.
- **Clear writing and sufficient experimental validation** are noted by several reviewers.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty.** The core contributions are an incremental combination of existing techniques: transformer, VQ‑VAE tokenization, CQL, beam‑search planning, and offline RL. The planning algorithm does not fundamentally differ from existing lookahead methods. (Noted by Reviewers 2, 4; Reviewer 1 adds that missing comparison with model‑based RL baselines like Dreamer or MCTS weakens the novelty claim.)
- **Only evaluated on Atari.** All experiments are on Atari image‑based tasks. It is unproven whether JOWA extends to continuous control (e.g., DMControl, Kitchen, Meta‑World) or high‑dimensional robotic tasks. Some reviewers consider this overclaiming of generalization ability. (Reviewers 1, 3, 5)
- **Missing comparison with related work.** The paper does not discuss or compare with Forward‑Inverse Cycle Consistency (FICC) (Ye et al., 2022), which uses VQ‑VAE on offline Atari for representation and action learning. The architectural similarity is not addressed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Inconsistent scaling across tasks.** In Table 2, the 150 M variant does not consistently outperform 40 M and 70 M on all games (e.g., Centipede best with 40 M, NameThisGame, SpaceInvaders, StarGunner best with 70 M). The cause (training instability, data sufficiency) and whether further scaling (e.g., 1 B) would resolve this are unexplained. (Reviewer 2)
- **Discrepancy between theory and planning.** Theorem 1 assumes the critic estimates the optimal Q‑function, but JOWA uses CQL, which intentionally underestimates Q‑values. Thus the error bound in Theorem 1 may not apply to the planning algorithm. (Reviewer 5)
- **Reliance on expert data for fine‑tuning.** The fine‑tuning experiments use expert‑level trajectories for new games. The paper does not explore performance with suboptimal data, limiting practical applicability. (Reviewer 4)
- **Excessive reward discretization.** Rewards are binned into {‑1, 0, 1}, losing granularity needed for tasks with nuanced reward signals, and making it hard to evaluate the model’s ability to capture subtle reward differences. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing analysis of emergent behaviors.** For tasks where 150 M JOWA substantially outperforms smaller models (e.g., Zaxxon), no explanation of emergent behaviors is provided. (Reviewer 2)
- **Short sequence length.** The context length is only 8 frames, which may limit temporal reasoning in more complex domains. (Reviewer 4)
- **No single‑task or model‑based RL comparisons.** The paper does not compare JOWA to a single‑task model‑based method (e.g., training from scratch) in a low‑data regime, nor to MCTS or Dreamer. (Reviewer 1)
- **Citation omissions.** The paper misses citations for [1, 2] as noted by Reviewer 2.
- **Discrepancy with prior MGDT numbers.** The reported MGDT numbers differ from the original paper (e.g., MGDT achieved 93 % IQM HNS on 40 M model); the cause is not explained. (Reviewer 4)
- **No fine‑tuning scaling results for smaller models.** The fine‑tuning results (Table 3) are only reported for the 150 M variant; scaling effects in fine‑tuning are not shown. (Reviewer 5; also raised by Reviewer 2)
- **CQL tuning difficulty.** The action‑part module (CQL) may be hard to tune in this framework. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minority reviewer disagreement.** One reviewer (Rating 5) is more critical of the generalization claims and novelty, while others (Ratings 8, 8) are strongly positive. This contrast is preserved above (e.g., the overclaim concern comes from a low‑rating reviewer).
