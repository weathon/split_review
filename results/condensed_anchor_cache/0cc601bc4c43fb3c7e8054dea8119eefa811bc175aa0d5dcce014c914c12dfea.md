## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CloneBO, a Bayesian optimization procedure for antibody sequence optimization. It trains a language model (CloneLM) on computationally inferred clonal families—groups of evolutionary-related antibody sequences—to capture how the immune system diversifies antibodies. Optimization is guided by a twisted sequential Monte Carlo scheme that biases generation toward previously measured sequences. The method is validated both in silico (using oracles for binding affinity, humanness, thermal stability) and in vitro (wet lab), outperforming existing methods like Lambo and DiffAb. Reviewers highlight the novel use of clonal family evolution and martingale posteriors, the importance of the problem, and the thorough validation. One reviewer is markedly more critical (score 5), while three are very positive (score 8).

### Strengths
- **Novelty:** The idea of applying martingale posteriors and modeling clonal family evolution for antibody optimization is original and well-matched to the biological process (R1, R2, R3, R4). The combination of simulating clonal evolution, martingale posteriors, and twisted Monte Carlo with LLMs is innovative (R3).
- **In vitro validation:** Wet lab experiments are a highlight and rare in ML papers; they significantly strengthen the contribution (R1, R2, R3). Extensive validation both in silico and in vitro, including ablation studies showing each component helps (R3).
- **Practical relevance:** The approach addresses an important multi-objective drug discovery problem with potential to streamline development and reduce costs (R2, R3).
- **Benchmarking:** Multiple relevant design approaches are compared (Lambo, DiffAb, etc.), and the method performs well (R3). Results significantly outperform state-of-the-art on binding affinity, humanness, and thermal stability (R2). KD results are convincing (R4).
- **Code and exposition:** Code is shared and well-organized. The paper is lucid about the problem context and path to impact (R3).
- **Strong baseline ablation:** Each component of the sampling scheme is shown to contribute to performance (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **One reviewer (R1, score 5) finds the experiments weak**, citing missing comparisons with influential recent works such as [1] and [2] (not specified). The same reviewer suspects that dismissing structure-based de novo design (e.g., DiffAb) is unjustified; the authors should demonstrate that using only sequence data leads to higher performance (R1). Other reviewers (score 8) consider the benchmarking thorough and results convincing, creating a clear disagreement.
- **Representation is vague:** The paper lacks a specific table, making direct comprehension difficult (R1). Other reviewers also note the paper is hard to read because too many ideas are covered in the main text; moving some mathematical exposition to the SI and clarifying intuition is suggested (R3).
- **Method requires an initial viable sequence**, limiting applicability in scenarios where such a starting point is unknown (R2).
- **Experimental data are not shared** (R3).
- **Unclear claims:** The exchangeability assumption for evolutionary processes (which have an arrow of time) is not clearly justified, especially for immune populations (R3). The reason for modeling light and heavy chains separately is not explained (R3).
- **Misleading wording:** Line 39 “up to thousands of previous iterations” is misleading because real measurements are done in batches, rarely exceeding 10 iterations (R3).
- **Model behavior** – CloneLM makes more Fv mutations than expected (R4). The appendix shows the clonal family does not fix the missing starting gap ‘-’ in some cases, which is not ideal (R4).
- **Experiment design issues:** The melting temperature and binding oracle experiment uses VHHs, which are not ideal since they differ from human VH chains (e.g., longer CDR H3); this may not be entirely fair to baselines developed for normal antibodies (R4).
- **Figure 4b (second plot)** – only 1 point beats Lambo’s best point; Lambo’s average looks better, so the method’s advantage in stability is not fully convincing despite strong KD results (R4).
- **Incorrect claim:** DiffAb is described as a de novo design method, but it requires a co-crystal structure as a starting point (R4).
- **Missing ablations/experiments (from reviewer questions):**
  - The impact of different preprocessing tools/hyperparameters for clonal family inference (FastBCR) on downstream performance is not examined (R2).
  - When training CloneLM, are clonal families re-mixed across epochs? If not, how is ordering chosen? (R4)
  - In vitro designs are constrained to maximum edit distance 3 (“up to 3 substitutions”); were baselines constrained to the same edit distance? (R4)
