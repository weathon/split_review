## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes RMB, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating reward models (RMs) in LLM alignment. It covers 49 real-world scenarios categorized into helpfulness and harmlessness, using both pairwise and Best-of-N (BoN) evaluations. The benchmark shows positive correlation with downstream alignment performance. Experiments reveal generalization issues in current RMs and explore factors affecting generative RM effectiveness. Generative models often outperform discriminative models.

### Strengths

- The studied problem is significant and the paper is well-written, clearly structured, and easy to understand.  
- RMB is much larger than any similar dataset (notably RewardBench), with thorough categorization and subcategorization for broad coverage.  
- The paper provides a thorough investigation of BoN evaluation, which prior work had proposed as a future direction.  
- Correlation evaluations show that RM performance on RMB can more accurately reflect downstream aligned model performance, enabling efficient iteration before computationally expensive alignment training.  
- Solid empirical evidence: experiments over pairwise and BoN accuracy cover different generative and discriminative RMs, providing good insight into their usefulness as judges.  
- The results appear to outperform SOTA RM evaluation datasets.

### Weaknesses

- The novelty relative to existing benchmarks (especially RewardBench) is not explicitly highlighted early in the paper; a feature comparison would better differentiate the approach rather than relying solely on experimental results.  
- The human annotator sample size is small relative to the dataset size.  
- Scoring was done only by GPT-4, which may introduce bias. Alternatives (e.g., other models, ensembles, or human annotators) were not considered.  
- The correlation analysis focuses mainly on BoN sampling rather than full RLHF training, which is acknowledged but nevertheless a limitation. It would be useful to gather RM and aligned-model pairs that use the RM during training to see correlation there.  
- The dependence on LLM-generated responses for dataset curation may create a circular dependency: using responses from current LLMs to evaluate RMs may not differentiate RMs for LLMs beyond current capabilities. Incorporating human-generated responses in future iterations is recommended.  
- The paper does not deeply explore the trade-offs between helpfulness and harmlessness, nor the underlying reasons for findings such as “it is hard for an RM to be both competitive” and “Generative models show great promise.” Adding such explanations would inspire further community engagement.  
- There is no analysis of patterns when BoN evaluation diverges significantly from pairwise evaluation.  
- Practical recommendations for effectively using RMB (e.g., as a validation set) are missing.  
- Potential negative impacts or possible misuses of the benchmark are not discussed.  
- Styling errors and typos exist; e.g., Mu et al. citation is missing a year (L229).
