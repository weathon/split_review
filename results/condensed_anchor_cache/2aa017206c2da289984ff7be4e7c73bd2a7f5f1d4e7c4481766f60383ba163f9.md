## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents a biologically constrained spiking network model of the mouse barrel cortex (4,218 neurons, 13 subtypes, connection probabilities from anatomical data), a coefficient-of-variation (CV) based initialization technique, and a conversion of an existing whisker sweep dataset to spiking format via Bernoulli sampling. The model is trained with backpropagation through time on a classification task and achieves accuracy exceeding CNNs/RNNs/LSTMs by an average of 8.6%, on par with recent SNNs, and shows superior robustness in whisker deprivation experiments. Post-training analysis reveals firing selectivity, shifts in timescale parameters, neuron-type-dependent firing rates, and a long-tailed weight degree distribution, which the authors argue resemble biological barrel cortex statistics. Most reviewers (3/4) rated the paper 8/10 and found the approach promising, while one reviewer (rating 3/10) considered the technical novelty insufficient for the venue.

### Strengths
- Develops an efficient training framework specifically designed for a biologically constrained barrel cortex model, with realistic sensory-motor integration as a goal.
- Constructs a biologically realistic network of 4,218 neurons across 13 neuronal subtypes, with connection strengths constrained by anatomical experimental findings.
- Introduces an initialization technique based on the coefficient of variation (CV) of network activity to identify desirable initial dynamical regimes.
- Converts a simulated whisker sweep dataset into a spiking-based format using a sigmoid mapping and Bernoulli sampling, enabling training on more realistic neural signals.
- The training approach that synchronizes neuronal electrical activity is novel and effective, enhancing both stability and biological realism.
- The model achieves classification accuracy exceeding classical ANNs (CNNs, RNNs, LSTMs) by an average of 8.6%, is competitive with recent SNNs, and outperforms SNNs in whisker deprivation experiments.
- Post-training analysis reveals biologically plausible features: neural selectivity, shifts in timescale parameters, firing rate profiles similar to real barrel cortex data (Fig. 5C–E), and a long-tailed weight degree distribution (Fig. 6A–C). These align with known barrel cortex phenomenology.
- The quantitative measures used for characterization (e.g., selectivity index, firing rate profiles, degree distribution) are well motivated and creatively connected to neuroscience literature.
- The work tackles a challenging problem – effective training of biologically constrained networks – and achieves competitive performance given the biological constraints; the number of dimensions along which the network resembles biology after training is impressive.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty (minority reviewer perspective):** Training aLIF networks with backpropagation has been demonstrated previously (Chen et al., 2022), and constraining network topology with connection probabilities is standard. One reviewer found the contributions incremental and insufficient for the venue, stressing that the CV initialization, dataset conversion, and training framework offer limited novelty over prior work.
- **Reliability and generalizability of the biological model:** The model contains many free parameters that are difficult to constrain (e.g., noise shape, exact synaptic efficacy). Small changes in these parameters can lead to completely different dynamical states and computational properties. The CV measure alone may be insufficient to assess the plausibility of the network activity, and it is unclear how generalizable the findings are across different parameter regimes.
- **Effectiveness and efficiency of the CV initialization are unconvincingly demonstrated:** (i) The CV analysis is shown for only three selected configurations, with no averaging over random seeds. (ii) In Fig. 2C, all three initializations perform similarly during the first 10 training epochs, which is inconsistent with the claim that they correspond to bifurcating dynamical regimes. (iii) The network in Fig. 2D displays pathological global synchrony even after training, which does not match the decorrelated activity typical of biological barrel cortex. (iv) The best CV-identified initialization still shows two major performance drops during training (Fig. 2C), possibly indicating gradient issues rather than initialization benefits. (v) No comparison is made with other initialization techniques used in spiking literature (e.g., weight scaling by in-degree, Rossbroich et al. 2022). (vi) No quantitative efficiency metrics (e.g., Performance/FLOPs, Performance/Samples) are provided to support the claim of an “efficient” training framework.
- **Confound between architecture and data type in comparisons with ANNs:** The comparison with CNNs, RNNs, and LSTMs is performed on the spiking dataset, which may inherently disadvantage these non-spiking models. It is unclear whether the performance gain is due to the network architecture or the switch to spiking data. The authors should also compare with ANNs on the original continuous dataset and with SNNs on both datasets to disentangle effects. Relatedly, it is unclear why continuous signals cannot be directly fed into the spiking network for training.
- **Post-training biological interpretability claims lack appropriate controls:** (i) Neural selectivity: symmetrical breaking after training is expected; comparison with selectivity in other SNN models (or CNNs/LSTMs) on the same task is needed to claim uniqueness. (ii) Shift in timescale parameters: it would be more significant if the shifted distribution quantitatively matches biological measurements; without such a direct comparison it is unclear whether the shift is a specific property or a general artifact of gradient descent with heterogeneous connectivity. (iii) Firing rate profile by subtype: it is unclear whether the profile is computed by applying identical current to each subpopulation or to the whole network. The profiles should be compared not only on firing rate but also on specific spike patterns (tonic, bursting, on/off/on-off) seen in biological recordings. (iv) Long-tailed degree distribution: one-sided metrics on low-variance variables naturally produce longer tails after training in directed graphs; a control (e.g., from an LSTM) would clarify whether this is a specific consequence of the biological constraints. (v) Connectivity probability analysis is more convincing only if the network was properly re-initialized using the CV technique for each connectivity variant.
- **Dataset conversion limitations:** The conversion of the existing whisker sweep dataset to spiking format via Bernoulli sampling is presented as a contribution, but it is not clear that the resulting spiking data faithfully mimic the input that barrel cortex would receive from real whiskers. No validation against biological recordings or alternative encoding schemes is provided. Furthermore, the task description and dataset characteristics would benefit from an additional supplementary figure (e.g., Figure 3) to improve clarity.
- **Missing ablation studies:** The importance of neuronal subtype heterogeneity is not tested – a comparison between the full 13-subtype model and a homogeneous (single-type) model would clarify the contribution. The whisker deprivation experiments are shown qualitatively, but a quantitative panel with performance decay as a function of occlusion level across models, and a comparison to corresponding biological experiments, is missing.
- **Paper structure, clarity, and missing details:** (i) The paper jumps between sections; information in Figure 2 would be better placed after the methodology explanation. (ii) Figure 1A labels (1,2,3) are unclear (region vs. pathway). (iii) Figure 2A does not clearly distinguish connection probability from the excitatory/inhibitory type of each connection. (iv) Equation 3’s beta parameter is not elaborated. (v) Tables 1 and 2 do not explicitly state that the dataset used is the spiking whisker sweep dataset, requiring inference from the main text. (vi) No mention of normalization, regularization, or gradient clipping techniques used during training, which is relevant given the training instability seen in Fig. 2C.
- **Code not provided:** The code is not made available for review, making it difficult to assess the reproducibility and solidness of the results. The authors state it will be released upon acceptance, but for a full evaluation it should be available during review.
