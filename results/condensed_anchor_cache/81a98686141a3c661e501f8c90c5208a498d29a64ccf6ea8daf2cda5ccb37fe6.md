## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes IMAGE (Interpretative MAsking with Gaussian Radiation ModEling), an adaptive masking strategy for low-shot visual grounding. It generates masks by thresholding an importance prior computed from an attention module and Gaussian radiance fields. The approach aims to improve generalization in zero-shot and few-shot scenarios without enlarging datasets. Reviewers note that the method shows empirical gains over random masking on COCO and ODinW, but raise major concerns about positioning, insufficient baselines, limited comparisons, unclear theoretical grounding, missing implementation details, and weak novelty. One reviewer is more positive (score 6 vs 3,3) and finds the theoretical analysis convincing; the other two find it underdeveloped or disconnected.

### Strengths

- Novel combination of attention-based importance prior and Gaussian radiance fields for adaptive masking in low-shot visual grounding.
- Empirical improvement over random masking in closed-set, zero-shot, and few-shot settings (COCO, ODinW) with minimal computational overhead.
- Motivation of masking only key details is intuitive and may enforce the network to capture generalizable information.
- Theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) is considered convincing by one reviewer (Reviewer 3); the same reviewer finds the quantitative analysis extensive and convincing.
- Demonstrates potential of adaptive feature manipulation via attention and Gaussian modeling as an alternative to dataset scaling for low-shot learning.
- Fast adaptation improvement shown in 10-epoch training on COCO2017.

### Weaknesses

- **Major – positioning and baselines:**
  - Broader impact is unclear: given large-scale pre-trained VLMs with strong generalization, does training a VLM from scratch on limited data offer practical advantages? Everyday objects like COCO can be easily scaled via web crawling (Reviewer 1).
  - Experiments primarily compare against random masking; comparisons with related adaptive masking works are missing (e.g., Attentive Mask CLIP [1], Cluster Masking [2], Centered Masking [3]) (Reviewer 1). Also missing comparison with Random Erase and latest SOTA methods from 2024 (Reviewer 2).
  - No comparison with state-of-the-art approaches from 2024 (Reviewer 2).

- **Major – theoretical analysis:**
  - Most reviewers (1 and 2) find the theoretical analysis unconvincing or insufficiently connected. Section 3.3 is underdeveloped: does Theorem 1 claim data-efficiency over random masking? How does it align with empirical findings? (Reviewer 1). The derivation from Generalization Bound needs clarification, and the proposed method does not seem connected to the mathematical derivations (Reviewer 2).
  - One reviewer (Reviewer 3) considers the theoretical analysis convincing, creating a clear disagreement.

- **Major – insufficient novelty and methodological issues:**
  - Approach is largely excluding the most recognizable regions, common in detection/segmentation; novelty is insufficient (Reviewer 2).
  - Method of finding Top‑k patches is questionable: detected patches may correspond to background rather than foreground (e.g., dog in a large field yielding a field patch, not the dog) (Reviewer 2).
  - The method requires experimental validation of the effectiveness of the variance of radiation points (Reviewer 2).

- **Major – missing experiments:**
  - 10-epoch training on COCO2017 is insufficient for Grounding DINO fine-tuning; results may only show improvement in fast adaptation. Should verify with original fine-tuning setting (e.g., 50 epochs) as in Grounding DINO (ECCV 2024) (Reviewer 3).
  - Hyperparameter analysis missing: impact of δ and k not reported (Reviewer 2).
  - Qualitative results needed: heatmaps of patch-level importance and masked results with different masking strategies would improve readability (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 did not request this, but it is a distinct point.

- **Minor – presentation and clarity:**
  - Loss terms unclear and inconsistently named: L_contrastive, L_localization not defined; L_mask and L_IMAGE appear in Section 3.3 without explanation (Reviewer 1). Localization loss lacks detail (Reviewer 2).
  - Symbols in Section 3.1 (Importance Prior) not explicitly defined (Reviewer 2).
  - Methodology lacks a clear overview of the entire process, including key details of training and inference stages (Reviewer 2). Is the RF-GAM module still used during inference? Should be model-agnostic and removable; clarify (Reviewer 3).
  - Implementation details insufficient: baselines used, type of GPUs, learning rate, etc. not provided (Reviewer 2).
  - Related work section reviews few works from the past few years; most citations pre-2021 (Reviewer 2).
  - Term “few-shot” misleading: 5–30% of data is larger than typical few-shot (usually a few dozen examples) (Reviewer 1).
  - Figure 5 uses “Occlusion Ratios” without definition or explanation (Reviewer 2).
