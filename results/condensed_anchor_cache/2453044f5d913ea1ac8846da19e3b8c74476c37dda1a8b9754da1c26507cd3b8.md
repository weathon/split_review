## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes STRAP, a method for robot policy learning that retrieves relevant sub-trajectories from large offline datasets at test time using pre-trained vision foundation models (DINO, MVP) and subsequence dynamic time warping (S-DTW). The retrieved data is combined with few-shot target demonstrations to train a behavioral cloning policy. STRAP is evaluated on LIBERO-10 simulation tasks and real-world pick-and-place tasks, outperforming prior retrieval methods (e.g., Behavior Retrieval) and multi-task policies that are not fine-tuned. Reviewers generally find the motivation strong and the sub-trajectory retrieval novel, but raise significant concerns about computational cost, scalability, weak baselines (no fine-tuning of multi-task policies), limited real-world task diversity, and missing ablation on the retrieval parameter K. One reviewer is notably more positive (score 8) about clarity and robustness, while another is more negative (score 5) citing presentation issues and incomplete project materials.

### Strengths

- **Well-motivated novel idea**: Retrieving sub-trajectories rather than full trajectories or single state-action pairs is a compelling and novel contribution for few-shot behavioral cloning. [R1, R2, R3]
- **Use of Dynamic Time Warping (DTW)**: Subsequence DTW on vision foundation model features is robust to variations in trajectory length and captures task semantics effectively, enabling variable-length sub-trajectory matching. [R1, R2, R3]
- **Comprehensive evaluation against recent retrieval baselines**: STRAP is compared to Behavior Retrieval (Du et al., 2023) and other baselines, showing clear improvements. [R1, R3]
- **Thorough ablation studies**: Experiments investigate different pretrained visual encoders (DINO, MVP, R3M), justifying architecture choices. [R1]
- **Strong simulated and real-world results**: STRAP outperforms both prior retrieval methods and pure behavioral cloning on LIBERO-10 and real pick-and-place tasks. [R1, R2]
- **Method is clear and straightforward to implement**. [R2]
- **Efficient data usage**: The method leverages sub-trajectory retrieval to improve data utilization compared to full-trajectory retrieval. [R4]

### Weaknesses

- **Weak multi-task policy baseline**: The multi-task policy comparison uses only pretrained weights without fine-tuning, which is an artificially weak baseline; standard practice includes fine-tuning on the target task. [R1] Similarly, a behavioral cloning baseline using all available data (not just retrieved) is missing. [R2]
- **Computational cost and scalability at test time not adequately addressed**: STRAP requires pairwise similarity computation between prior dataset sub-trajectories and target demos, plus model training for each new task. This raises concerns about real-world scalability, especially on large datasets like Droid, and no analysis of memory/computation requirements is provided. [R1, R3] The paper lacks discussion of how computational cost scales with dataset size. [R3]
- **Missing ablation on retrieval parameter K**: The choice of K (number of top-k sub-trajectories) for constructing the retrieval dataset lacks justification and ablation. The effect of K on retrieval quality, computational overhead, and policy performance is not explored, despite likely presenting a trade-off. [R1, R3]
- **Limited real-world evaluation**: Only a small number of real-world tasks are tested, and baselines such as a fine-tuned multi-task policy are missing from real-world experiments. [R1] More simulation and real environments would be helpful. [R2]
- **Cross-embodiment generalization not tested**: Real-world retrieval uses the same robot embodiment and gripper; the method’s ability to retrieve useful data from other datasets (e.g., BridgeV2) with different robots and scenes is not evaluated. [R1]
- **Comparison to imitation learning methods showing stronger generalization**: Other methods (e.g., Sparse Diffusion Policy, HPT, RDT-Robotics, Humanoid Manipulation) appear to generalize more broadly in similar settings; experiments comparing STRAP to these are missing. [R4]
- **Writing quality, readability, and formatting issues**: The paper’s writing needs improvement in clarity, images are difficult to interpret, and formatting problems hinder readability. [R4] Additionally, a project website or supplementary material is not accessible, suggesting incompleteness. [R4]
- **Need for comparison to pretrain-then-finetune setup**: How does STRAP compare to standard fine-tuning of a multi-task policy on the prior dataset then fine-tuning on the few-shot target demos, controlling for total data used? [R1, R2]
- **No analysis of which component contributes most to performance**: The method differs from Behavior Retrieval in three ways (non-parametric retrieval vs. VAE, sub-trajectory vs. single state-action, DTW); an ablation isolating each component is requested. [R3]
- **Average LIBERO-10 performance**: The main table should include the average performance across tasks. [R1]
