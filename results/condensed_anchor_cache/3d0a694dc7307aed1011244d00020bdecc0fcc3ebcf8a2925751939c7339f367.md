## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses three key reliability bottlenecks in test-time adaptation (TTA): evaluating OOD performance, miscalibration after adaptation, and hyperparameter selection without labeled data. It empirically observes that TTAed models exhibit strong agreement-on-the-line (AGL) and accuracy-on-the-line (ACL) phenomena across diverse distribution shifts and hyperparameters, even when vanilla models do not. Leveraging these linear trends, the authors propose methods to estimate OOD accuracy using only in-distribution (ID) data, calibrate TTAed models via temperature scaling, and select hyperparameters that maximize ID performance as a proxy for OOD performance. Experiments on multiple TTA methods and datasets (e.g., CIFAR100-C, ImageNet-C) demonstrate that these unsupervised approaches achieve results close to those using ground-truth labels both in accuracy and calibration error.

### Strengths
- The problem of TTA reliability is important and often overlooked; the paper makes a timely and relevant contribution.
- The observation that AGL and ACL persist in TTAed models (even where they fail for vanilla models) is novel and interesting, offering a new angle for hyperparameter tuning and evaluation without OOD labels.
- The proposed methods for unsupervised calibration and hyperparameter selection are simple yet effective—e.g., reducing ECE from 13.40 to 2.10 using TENT on CIFAR100-C.
- Experiments are extensive, covering multiple TTA methods (TENT, TTT, etc.), datasets, and architectures, with convincing results.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty is limited**: The work is largely an application of Baek et al. (2022) to the TTA setting, and the claim about Baek et al. in the introduction is imprecise—they studied disagreement-error correlation, not agreement-on-the-line. This needs correction.
- **Assumption of full ID data and labels**: The proposed evaluation and calibration methods require access to ID data with labels, which violates standard TTA settings. In many real-world scenarios (e.g., data privacy, compute constraints) such access is unavailable. The authors do not discuss how to adapt the method when only a trained model is given.
- **Offline nature limits practical deployment**: Temperature scaling and OOD accuracy estimation require processing the entire test set before predictions can be made, precluding online/streaming use. For latency-sensitive applications, this delay is problematic, and the computational overhead of temperature scaling relative to inference time is not quantified.
- **Multiple-model overhead**: Algorithm 1 requires maintaining \(n\) models during TTA, which is memory-consuming and inefficient. It also raises questions about how predictions are made on a data stream (does one use the ID model, one of the TTA models, or wait until AGL is computed?). No sensitivity analysis of \(n\) is provided.
- **Insufficient empirical evidence for AGL/ACL generality**:
  - Only CNN-based models are tested. No experiments on Vision Transformers (ViT, Swin Transformer, PoolFormer) or more complex tasks (object detection, segmentation) are reported, leaving the generality of the phenomenon unestablished.
  - On ImageNet-C, only a few corruption types are shown in Table 2; all 15 corruptions should be reported.
  - A negative correlation between ID and OOD accuracy is observed for TTT with varying learning rate (Figure 4). This needs further investigation—are other TTT methods (TTT++, TTT-MAE) also affected? An explanation for this negative correlation is missing.
- **Ablation studies are incomplete**: The hyperparameter-selection analysis only covers different architectures/checkpoints; varying TTA hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size) in the ablation is needed to verify robustness. The number of ID samples used and its impact on performance is also not studied.
- **Missing comparisons**: The proposed methods are not evaluated under wild test settings (SAR, ICLR 2023). The ID-OOD correlation is not shown for MEMO or Contrastive Test-Time Adaptation.
- **Presentation issues**: Figures 1–3 are difficult to interpret—the axes (accuracy vs. agreement), the meaning of plotted points (are they different checkpoints, architectures, or hyperparameters?), and the specific TTA methods/datasets used are not clearly labeled or explained.
- **Minor error**: On page 7, “let X the random variable” should be “let X be the random variable”.
- **No explanation of the linear relationship**: The paper does not discuss why AGL/ACL emerges after TTA or under what conditions it might break, which limits theoretical insight.
