## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents DeepEMD, a transformer-based model to approximate the Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) between 3D point clouds. By predicting the matching matrix directly, the method achieves wall-clock speed-ups of two orders of magnitude over the exact Hungarian algorithm and one order over the approximate Sinkhorn algorithm. Experiments on ShapeNet and ModelNet40 show accurate EMD and gradient estimation, good out-of-distribution behaviour, and the ability to scale to larger point clouds during inference. The approach is demonstrated as a training loss for a point cloud VAE. Reviewers were divided: two rated the paper 5 (positive), praising the speed and accuracy; two rated it 3, citing insufficient novelty, missing comparisons, and limited evaluation.

### Strengths
- **Speed**: DeepEMD is significantly faster than the Hungarian method (O(N³)) and the standard Sinkhorn algorithm, providing a practical alternative for scenarios where EMD computation is a bottleneck (all reviewers).
- **Accuracy**: Figure 5 shows that predicted distances have small errors relative to ground-truth EMD, and the model provides effective gradient approximations. Strong correlation between predicted and true distances is demonstrated (Reviewer 1, 3).
- **Out-of-distribution generalization**: The model generalizes well to larger point clouds during inference (e.g., up to 4096 points) and exhibits promising behaviour on out-of-distribution data (Reviewer 4; Table 2).
- **Clear baseline comparison**: The paper provides explicit runtime and accuracy comparisons against Chamfer distance, Hungarian algorithm, and Sinkhorn (Figure 8), giving a clear view of the method’s performance (Reviewer 4).
- **Sound framing**: Casting point-cloud matching as learning an attention matrix approximating the optimal transport matching is an interesting approach that could be useful wherever EMD is currently used (Reviewer 4).
- **Potential impact**: The method could serve as a fast differentiable loss for training generative point cloud models, which was demonstrated on a VAE (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty**: The core idea of using transformers to compare point clouds and predict a matching matrix/score is not new. Prior works (e.g., [A], [B]; see also the point-cloud registration survey at https://github.com/XuyangBai/awesome-point-cloud-registration) already use attention for correspondence/registration. Those methods handle partial matching and outliers, whereas DeepEMD does not (Reviewer 2, 4).
- **Missing comparisons**:
  - No comparison to state-of-the-art point-cloud registration methods, which are strong baselines for matching (Reviewer 4).
  - No comparison to faster EMD variants such as improved Sinkhorn convergence methods [C] or learning-free fast EMD algorithms [D, E] (Reviewer 2).
  - No comparison to the linear-time EMD of Shirdhonkar and Jacobs (2008); the claim that it is “limited to low-dimensional histograms” is insufficiently justified – 3D point clouds could be represented as coarser histograms and compared that way (Reviewer 2).
  - The paper mentions CD and DPDist in related work but does not include them in the experimental comparison, even though they are faster (Reviewer 3).
  - No speed / accuracy trade-off analysis: Figure 8 shows DeepEMD is ~100× faster than Hungarian but has almost half the accuracy. A detailed trade-off (e.g., over iterations or hyperparameters) is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited evaluation scope**:
  - All experiments are on object-level datasets (ShapeNet, ModelNet40) with 1024 points. No experiments on real-world scenes, LiDAR scans, depth images, or partial point clouds, which are more challenging and realistic (Reviewer 1, 2, 4).
  - No 3D visualizations of reconstruction or generation results, making it difficult to assess the practical impact qualitatively (Reviewer 1).
  - Only tested as loss for a VAE; not evaluated on more complex generative tasks like single-view point cloud generation or point cloud completion, where EMD is more commonly used (Reviewer 1).
- **Scalability and memory**: Attention mechanisms can be memory-intensive; scaling to millions of points (common in LiDAR) may be problematic. The paper does not report memory usage or discuss scalability in large-scale settings (Reviewer 4; also asked by Reviewer 1: “Will DeepEMD still be efficient with a large point number?”).
- **Missing technical details**:
  - Equation (10) uses an argmax; how is the non-differentiability handled? (Reviewer 1)
  - Training time and GPU resources not reported (Reviewer 1).
  - The description of synthetic perturbations used to augment train/test splits is vague (Reviewer 2).
  - The discussion of Amos et al. (2022) is unclear; the distinction/similarity with DeepEMD needs better explanation (Reviewer 2).
  - Comparisons in Figure 8 use only 100 iterations for all methods; a graph showing performance over iterations would be more informative (Reviewer 3).
- **Title and abstract overclaim**: The title suggests a generic method for computing EMD between arbitrary distributions, but the work is limited to 3D point clouds. The approximation factor is ad-hoc and not theoretically analysed, unlike existing methods (Reviewer 2).
- **Unjustified claims**: The statement “quite remarkable behaviour … as shown in Table 2” is not supported by technical insight; further analysis of why the model generalises well is needed (Reviewer 2).
