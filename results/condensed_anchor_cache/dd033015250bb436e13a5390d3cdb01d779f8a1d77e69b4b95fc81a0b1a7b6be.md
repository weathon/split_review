## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces LoCAL (LoRA-Contextualizing Adaptation of Large Multimodal Models), a framework that extends LMMs to multi-page visually-rich document understanding by using the LMM itself as an efficient evidence page retriever before performing question answering. Dual LoRA adapters allow a single LMM to serve both retrieval and QA roles, leveraging intermediate hidden embeddings for retrieval. Experiments on SlideVQA, MMLongBench-Doc, DocVQA, and a newly collected LoCAL-bench dataset (226 documents, 471 QA pairs across nine domains) show state-of-the-art performance. The approach achieves comparable results to proprietary models like Claude-3 Opus and Gemini-1.5-Pro.

### Strengths
- **Novel framework and insight**: Using LMMs themselves as multimodal retrievers via intermediate hidden embeddings for question-based evidence page retrieval is a significant advancement (R1, R3, R4). A minority reviewer (R2) notes that the idea is not entirely novel—citing ColBERT (Khattab & Zaharia, 2020) for contextualized late interaction and widespread use of LoRA for task adaptation—but still acknowledges the contribution is interesting and creative.
- **Well-designed architecture**: Dual LoRA adapters efficiently enable parameter sharing so a single LMM handles both retrieval and QA, enhancing modularity and memory efficiency (R1, R2, R4). The structure is compatible with multiple existing LLM bases (R4).
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Extensive evaluation across multiple benchmarks (SlideVQA, MMLongBench-Doc, DocVQA, LoCAL-bench) with comparisons to both LLM-based and non-LLM-based baselines; results are sound and convincing (R2, R4).
- **New dataset**: LoCAL-bench covers nine domains and provides a valuable resource for future research (R1, R3).
- **Clear motivation and writing**: The paper is well-motivated, well-organized, fluent, and highly readable; the solution is simple, effective, and insightful (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty positioning and missing comparisons** (R1, R2, R3): The paper’s contribution could be better delineated against prior work. One reviewer (R1) requests comparison with additional retrieval methods: *bge-large, NV-Embed-v2, SigLIP, ColPali*. Another (R2) notes that the idea of using contextualized late interaction for retrieval is pre-dated by ColBERT, and LoRA-based task adaptation is widely used; the paper should show more clearly how LoCAL surpasses these baselines. The paper also lacks a direct comparison to RAG methods that use document parsers (R3).
- **Dual-LoRA decoupling and interference** (R2): Using a single LMM for both retrieval and QA may be suboptimal if the representations needed for each task diverge. The dual LoRA adapters attempt to mitigate this, but the paper does not sufficiently analyze how well they truly decouple the tasks or avoid interference.
- **LoCAL-bench dataset concerns** (R3, R4): 
  - Quality: The dataset is entirely GPT-4o-generated (with human verification). Its quality compared to human-labeled datasets is questionable (R3); the authors should detail their evaluation of dataset quality.
  - Length: The dataset does not feature long document lengths (few pages), making it hard to assess the method’s real performance on multi-page evidence retrieval. The authors should clarify whether LoCAL-bench is necessary and how it demonstrates effectiveness over longer documents (R4).
- **Generalization limitations** (R4): The method shows strong results on document QA benchmarks but is not tested on other downstream tasks. Given that comparable proprietary models (Claude-3 Opus, Gemini-1.5-Pro) exhibit broad abilities, the paper should test generalization (e.g., following Textmonkey’s multi-task evaluation) or explain why such testing is unnecessary.
- **Limited evidence page capacity** (R4): The retrieval module is limited to five evidence pages. When relevant pages exceed five, performance may degrade, reducing general effectiveness.
- **Data bias discussion** (R2): The use of web-crawled documents introduces potential biases that could affect generalization; the paper lacks a thorough discussion of this issue.
- **Ablation/fine-tuning details** (R2): How does the retrieval module’s performance change when trained on the original ColPali data alone versus supplemented with DocMatix-IR and PFLDocVQA? The paper should report results on the ViDoRe benchmark and the Table 1 benchmarks. Additionally, would a more recent text-based baseline like BGE-M3 significantly outperform the SBERT baseline used?
