## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ALD-VAE, a heuristic method that automatically determines the optimal latent space dimensionality in VAEs by gradually decreasing the latent size during training. The stopping decision is based on the slopes of three metrics: Silhouette score, reconstruction loss, and FID (for reconstructed and generated data). Experiments are performed on MNIST, FashionMNIST, SPRITES, and EuroSAT. The method is compared to grid search and claims faster convergence with comparable final performance.

### Strengths
- Proposes a heuristic approach to find the optimal latent dimension for VAEs by gradually decreasing latent size and evaluating FID, Silhouette score, and reconstruction error (Reviewer 1).
- Empirical results in Fig. 2 show the method finds latent sizes yielding near-optimal FID, Silhouette, and reconstruction error on four datasets (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is clearly written and easy to understand (Reviewers 2, 4).
- ALD-VAE is claimed to be faster compared to grid search for optimal latent dimension (Reviewer 2).
- Training on MNIST and FashionMNIST is robust to different initial latent sizes and data seeds (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and theoretical justification**: The algorithm is heuristic and lacks theoretical justification (Reviewer 1). One reviewer (Rating 1) found the method trivial—essentially a loop of “train, evaluate with Eq. 4, reduce dimension if not converged, repeat”—akin to a simple hyperparameter search, and considered the paper below publication standards (Reviewer 3). Evaluation methods (FID, reconstruction loss, K-means) are well-established; simply aggregating them yields limited novelty (Reviewer 3).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Many hyperparameters (p=5, n=5, window w, number of clusters, latent_decrease, etc.). Need sensitivity analysis of how different values affect final performance (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited experimental scope**: Datasets are small in image count and resolution (MNIST, FashionMNIST, SPRITES, EuroSAT). Experiments on large-scale high-resolution datasets (e.g., CelebA-HQ, ImageNet) are missing (Reviewers 1, 2). This also hinders evaluation of hyperparameter sensitivity (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**: No comparison with other state-of-the-art methods such as MaskAAE, GECO (Reviewer 4). Even a simple baseline like PCA is not included (Reviewer 3). The paper’s focus on dimensionality alone may not be impactful; a more relevant evaluation would consider bits per dimension, ELBO, or rate-distortion (Reviewer 3 cites Alemi et al., Higgins et al., Balle et al., Yang et al.).
- **Lack of ablation study**: Unclear whether all three metrics (Silhouette, reconstruction loss, FID) are necessary. An ablation e.g., removing Silhouette would be valuable (Reviewer 4).
- **No clear quantitative results**: Comparisons in Figure 3 are difficult to interpret (e.g., FID_gen in MNIST, FashionMNIST, reconstruction loss in EuroSAT). A table would be more informative (Reviewer 4).
- **Speed claim unsubstantiated**: Paper claims significantly faster than grid search but provides no experimental evidence or time analysis (Reviewers 2, 4). Grid search can be parallelized while ALD-VAE runs sequentially and includes clustering, making speed comparisons non-trivial (Reviewer 4).
- **Robustness concerns**: Optimal latent dimensions on SPRITES and EuroSAT are not robust to different data seeds (Reviewer 2). The stopping criteria (slopes positive over last k=20 epochs) may not be robust; metrics could later decrease/increase again (Reviewer 1).
- **Multi-objective optimization**: The stopping rule (Eq. 2) aggregates three objectives (FID, reconstruction loss, Silhouette) without clear balancing (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear definitions and notation**: FID'_r and FID'_g are not defined. “Polynomial scores” are unclear. Variable p is used both as scalar (p=5) and as a function (Eq. 5) (Reviewer 1). Minor typo: wrong sign ‘¿’ in line 23 of Algorithm 1 (Reviewer 2).
- **Neuron pruning strategy unclear**: How are the n neurons selected during pruning? If random, more important neurons may be removed (Reviewer 1).
- **Number of clusters for Silhouette**: The number of clusters is a hyperparameter that needs justification or optimization (Reviewer 1).
