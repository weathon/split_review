## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a causality-inspired generative model (DyGNNExplainer) based on Structural Causal Model (SCM) to interpret Dynamic Graph Neural Network (DyGNN) predictions by disentangling trivial, static, and dynamic causal relationships. It employs a contrastive learning module to separate trivial and causal relationships, a dynamic correlating module to separate static and dynamic causal relationships, and a VGAE-based framework to generate causal-and-dynamic masks for spatial interpretability and recognize dynamic relationships for temporal interpretability. The paper constructs a new synthetic benchmark dataset for dynamic graph interpretability. Experiments are conducted on synthetic and real-world datasets. The work is noted as the first to address interpretability in dynamic GNNs.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized and logically coherent; the causality-driven approach for out-of-distribution generalization is intriguing (R1).  
- Introduces research on temporal distribution shift in sequential processes, providing valuable insights for subsequent studies (R1).  
- This is the first work for interpretability in dynamic graph neural networks, a significant contribution. The research motivation is clear, and bridging this gap is meaningful (R2, R4).  
- The method is technically sound, with comprehensive experiments showing effectiveness. The new constructed synthetic benchmark dataset is a good addition to the research domain (R2).  
- Originality: addressing dynamic graphs by disentangling spatial and temporal relationships and explicitly constructing an SCM with trivial, static, and dynamic causal relationships is interesting (R3).  
- The description of existing challenges for DyGNN interpretability is interesting (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation issues**:  
  * The derivation of the backdoor adjustment is absent from the main text and should be placed in the appendix (R1).  
  * Computational complexity is not discussed; VGAE is known to be computationally demanding, and contrastive learning further increases cost (R1).  
  * Figure 1 text is too small (R2, minor).  
  * The paper is hard to follow; illustrations for spatial and temporal interpretability would help, and the challenges in implementing the SCM (third paragraph of Introduction) and how the proposed approach addresses them (fourth paragraph) are not clearly explained (R3).  
  * The sentence “Hence, our ultimate objective is to define a generative model” (Section 2.1) is confusing because DyGNNExplainer is a representational model (R4).  
  * Equation 1’s logical derivation is unclear; the relationship between causal (C), dynamic (D), and static (S) relationships (P(S)=P(C)−P(D)) is not justified (R4).  
  * In Equation 7, variable \(e\) and function \(s(\cdot,\cdot)\) are not defined (R4).  
  * Equation 14’s sparsity formulation may be incorrect: to satisfy sparsity requirements, it should be \(\frac{||A_t^C||_1 + ||A_t^S||_1}{||A_t||_1}\) rather than the current form (R4).  
  * In the last line of Section 2, \(\Theta\) should be replaced by \(\Psi\) (R4).  
- **Training and hyperparameter concerns**:  
  * Using 4 loss functions makes training unfriendly; improper tuning of even one parameter could cause instability or failure. Systematic parameter selection guidelines are needed (R1).  
  * Sensitivity to hyperparameters in Equation 15 is not addressed, and the computational complexity of solving Equation 15 is not provided (R3).  
- **Experimental limitations**:  
  * Lack of standard benchmarks; recommend adding Ogbn-Arxiv dataset (R1).  
  * An ablation study replacing VGAE-like models is crucial to understand each component’s contribution (R1).  
  * All baseline methods are for static graphs, making it hard to judge the significance of performance improvements for dynamic graph interpretability. Comparison on static graphs with state-of-the-art explainers is needed (R3, R4).  
  * Interpretability results only show static interpretability (e.g., ‘house’ motif in BA-Shapes); no specific causal relationships in dynamic graphs are observed from the provided results. Datasets are not classic dynamic graph datasets (e.g., traffic, citation), so the proposal does not fully satisfy the expectation of exploring dynamic graph interpretability (R4).  
  * It is unclear how ‘dynamic’ is interpreted in DyGNNExplainer; the datasets may not support the claim of dynamic graphs in experiments (R4).  
  * In Table 2, on the DTree-Grid dataset for node classification, OrphicX achieves 96.1 while DyGNNExplainer achieves 94.2, yet DyGNNExplainer is bolded. Similarly, for the node classification task on the same dataset, OrphicX performs better than DyGNNExplainer but is not bolded. This inconsistency needs clarification or correction (R2, R3, R4).  
- **Missing related work**:  
  * Missing references to spatio-temporal related work [5], generalization/extrapolation on graphs, and causality learning [2–4] (R1).  
  * Important literature on causal spatio-temporal models, such as CauSTG (capturing invariant relations for temporal shifts in ST graphs) and CaST (discovering via SCM with back-door and front-door adjustment), is not cited. The distinctions between DyGNNExplainer and CaST need to be clearly articulated (R4).

**Reviewer disagreement**: While Reviewer 2 finds the paper well-organized and technically sound, Reviewers 3 and 4 find the presentation hard to follow and the significance unclear due to all baselines being static graph explainers and the lack of truly dynamic interpretability evidence. Reviewers 1, 3, and 4 raise multiple concerns about missing details, training stability, benchmark selection, and incomplete related work.
