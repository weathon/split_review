## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes two causal models (FICIM and PICIM) for data generation in Open Set Domain Adaptation and derives theoretical bounds on target risk under ERM, with experiments on CMNIST, synthetic data, and restaurant review text. Reviewers disagree: one (score 5) finds the problem important, the approach novel, and the experimental verification strong; two others (score 3) argue the core assumption (P(X|C) invariant across domains) is unrealistic or insufficiently justified, the concepts echo existing invariant-learning work (Ahuja et al. 2021), notation is confusing, and no practical OSDA method is developed.

### Strengths

- The problem is important and directly relevant to Open Set Domain Adaptation. (R1, R3)
- The proposed causal framework (FICIM/PICIM) and theoretical results are novel. (R1)
- Experimental results strongly verify the theoretical claims. (R1)
- The authors include proofs and a comprehensive appendix. (R2)
- The paper combines theoretical and empirical results on an interesting and meaningful problem. (R3)

### Weaknesses

- **Assumption 1 is too strong/unrealistic.**  
  – Requiring both P(Y|C) and P(X|C) to be invariant across domains is unrealistic. Example: images of dogs on different backgrounds; P(X|C) changes as the background changes. (R3)  
  – In Figure 2, both C and V are parents of X, so P(X|C) generally depends on V and should not be invariant. (R3)  
  – The bidirectional arrow between C and V in FICIM further implies P(X|C) need not remain invariant. (R1)  
  – Adding a domain variable D that points to V creates the chain X←V←D, meaning X is related to D and thus P^{d_i}(X|C) ≠ P^{d_j}(X|C). (R2, citing Chen et al. 2022)  
  – This assumption is used repeatedly in derivations; no practical example is given to justify it. (R3)  
  – One reviewer would re-evaluate if such examples were provided. (R3)

- **PICIM/FICIM are not sufficiently novel.**  
  – The concepts mirror the FIIF and PIIF models from Ahuja et al. 2021, widely cited in invariant learning (Liu et al. 2021, Chen et al. 2022). (R2)  
  – No meaningful OSDA adaptation method is derived from the framework, limiting the contribution. (R2)

- **Notation and presentation are confusing.**  
  – Notation deviates from common usage, making reading difficult. (R2)  
  – Using P_X and P_{X|Y} seems meaningless. (R2)  
  – Line 218: the expression p_{Y|X^C}^{d_i}(y|x) = p^{d_j}_{Y|X^C}(y|x) is unclear; a clearer form would be P^{d_i}(Y|X = X^C) = P^{d_j}(Y|X = X^C). (R2)  
  – Definitions 5–6 are redundant, reiterating well-known concepts. (R2)  
  – Line 282–283: subscript in sup_{C∈C_S} is not displayed correctly; p^TC(c) and p^SC(c) are miswritten (same error in line 300). (R2)  
  – In Definition 6, f maps to the invariant element X^C, but the loss ℓ(f(x),y) requires f to map to the label—an inconsistency. (R2)

- **Variable Z in Figure 2 is undefined, and arrow semantics are unclear.**  
  – Z appears in the causal graph but is never defined. (R2)  
  – Bidirectional arrows between Y and V are not clearly explained; the authors should add an environment variable E to represent changes across domains (as described in Pearl, Causality 2009). (R2)

- **Theorem 3 issues.**  
  – Under FICIM, ERM cannot disentangle invariant (C) and spurious (V) features; it only obtains R_S(Y|X), not the stable expected risk R_S(Y|X^C) claimed. This appears to contradict Theorems 3 and 4 of Ahuja et al. 2021. (R2)  
  – How the “risk of unknown target classes” is calculated in Theorem 3 is unclear. (R2)

- **Theorems 2 and 4: constant β may be large.**  
  – While these theorems prove the target risk is bounded, a large β weakens the explanation of why ERM performs so well or why other DA algorithms might not be better. (R1)

- **Proposition 1: X is not domain‑invariant given C.**  
  – With a domain variable D pointing to V, the chain X←V←D implies P(X|C) depends on the domain, contradicting the proposed invariance. (R2)
