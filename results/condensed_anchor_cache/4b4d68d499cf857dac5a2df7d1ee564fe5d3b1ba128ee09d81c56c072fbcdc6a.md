## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes DSparsE, a pure MLP-based knowledge graph completion model. It uses a shallow encoder with a dynamic layer and a relation-aware layer, followed by a deep decoder with residual connections. Sparse connection layers replace dense layers to mitigate overfitting. Evaluated on FB15k-237 and WN18RR, the ablation study examines the dynamic and relation-aware layers. Reviewers agree the motivation is clear and the model is technically reasonable but differ on clarity: Reviewer 3 finds it clearly written and easy to follow, while Reviewer 4 finds it not easy to follow, with uncommon words (e.g., "reminiscent") and insufficient citation and analysis of previous models. Scores are mixed: two reviewers give 3 (low) and two give 5 (moderate), indicating substantial disagreement in overall assessment.

### Strengths

- The proposed architecture uses sparse layers, residual connections, and a dynamic layer to address overfitting, which is important for knowledge graph completion. (Reviewer 1)
- Ablation studies demonstrate the benefit of the proposed modules (dynamic layer and relation-aware layer). (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 2 partially acknowledges ablation exists but finds it insufficient)
- The paper investigates how powerful a pure MLP-based model can be for KGC, which is significantly different from existing methods and interesting. (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 2 adds that results show DSparsE is effective, though not state-of-the-art)
- The model combines a dynamic layer and residual structure to enable better information fusion. (Reviewer 3)
- The motivation is easy to understand. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses

- Better explanations are needed for key terms: "expert kernels" is not explained (Reviewer 1); the term "reminiscent" is used without explanation (Reviewer 4). 
- Figures and captions are poor: architecture figures are too small and captions too limited (Reviewer 1); in Figure 6, both the blue line and orange dashed line are marked "with residual structure," causing confusion, and no specific depth values are given (Reviewers 2, 3). Figure 4 compares DSparsE to only one baseline on one dataset (Reviewer 3). Figure 5 shows only three expert quantities, making it difficult to discern a clear trend (Reviewer 3).
- The key advantage of a pure MLP-based model over existing methods (tensor decomposition, translational models, etc.) is unclear. The model uses 15 (FB15k-237) or 35 (WN18RR) experts, each an MLP, introducing many extra parameters without a clear demonstration of substantial performance gain over simpler models (e.g., RESCAL, ComDensE). Efficiency is not improved because parameters increase; computational cost is a concern. (Reviewer 2; also echoed by Reviewer 1 questioning computational complexity)
- The claim in the Contribution section of significant parameter reduction and efficiency improvement is not substantiated. No runtime or exact parameter counts are provided. (Reviewer 3; Reviewers 1 and 2 also request parameter and complexity comparisons)
- Ablation study is insufficient: it does not quantify the contribution of each module (dynamic MLP vs relation-aware layer vs concatenation) individually. It is unclear which output vector (dynamic or relation-aware) affects results more. (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 3 agrees supplementary experiments are insufficient overall)
- Only two datasets (FB15k-237, WN18RR) are used; more datasets are needed to verify effectiveness and generalizability. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- No statistical significance tests or critical plots are provided for comparing methods; the paper should show how statistically different the top competing models are. (Reviewer 1; implicitly echoed by Reviewers 2 and 3 requesting more baselines/comparisons)
- The difference and superiority of DSparsE over baselines is unclear; contribution is limited as it combines existing techniques (dynamic layer, residual structure, sparsity). (Reviewer 4)
- Scalability results are missing. (Reviewer 4)
- Minor: In Table 2, the best MRR on FB15k-237 should be 0.396 from ConvKB (Reviewer 2); the distinction between deep learning models and translation models is unclear (Reviewer 2); it is unclear how imposing sparse structure differs from drop-connect (Reviewer 1); how the dynamic layer enhances robustness (by ensembling?) is not explained (Reviewer 1).
