## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes NeurFlow, a framework that interprets neural networks by focusing on groups of critical neurons rather than individual neurons. It identifies critical neurons via an importance score (using Integrated Gradients on top-\(k\) image patches), clusters them into semantic groups, and constructs hierarchical circuits (hypertrees) representing interactions across layers. A multimodal LLM labels the groups. Evaluation validates critical neuron impact against random baselines, assesses optimality, and demonstrates applications (image debugging, bias detection). Reviewers found the motivation compelling but raised significant concerns about evaluation rigor, positioning against prior work, terminology clarity, and architecture generality.

### Strengths
- **Tackling polysemantic behavior**: Shifting focus from individual neurons to groups helps address the challenge of neurons encoding multiple features (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Novel use of MLLMs**: Automatically annotating group circuits with multimodal large language models is original and aids interpretability (Reviewer 1).
- **Pragmatic balance**: The approach pragmatically balances single‑neuron analysis and the combinatorial explosion of full circuit enumeration (Reviewer 2).
- **Rigorous analysis of correctness and usefulness**: Includes optimality experiments (though confidence intervals are missing), masking/retaining tests, and explicit application demonstrations (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Clever handling of polysemy**: Step (3) allows a neuron to be active in multiple clusters, and connections between semantic groups are a clever design choice (Reviewer 3).
- **Fresh unified perspective**: Integrates existing research directions into a single coherent framework (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 additionally finds the paper *very complete and self‑contained*, whereas other reviewers note gaps.
- **Novel semantic clustering**: Clustering neuron responses into semantic groups yields interpretable, concept‑specific units (Reviewer 4).
- **Well‑structured and generally clear**: Most reviewers found the paper well written and easy to follow (Reviewers 3, 4; Reviewer 2 had clarity concerns – see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Terminology confusion**: Many similar terms (“critical neuron group”, “neuron concept”, “neuron circuit”, “visual feature”) are used without a glossary figure, making the paper hard to follow. “Neuron concept” implies a single concept but actually refers to a set. “Visual features” and “patches” are used interchangeably when they should be distinguished (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Visual feature construction is vague**: It is unclear whether visual features are individual images, images of a class, or some subset, and how crops relate to the original data (Reviewer 2).
- **Formal definition of critical neuron group is not intuitive**: The definition focuses on change in the neuron’s concept representation rather than direct impact on model output, which is counter‑intuitive (Reviewer 1).
- **Mismatch between strong claims and evidence**: The claim that “only a subset of neurons are critical” is not proven – experiments only show average greater impact than random, not that no other neurons matter. The claim should be lowered or additional experiments provided (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of comparison with prior work**:
  - Step 1 (critical neurons): prior work (e.g., [1], Vu et al., 2022) identifies class‑specific critical neurons; overlap and differences are not discussed (Reviewers 3, 4).
  - Step 2 (groups of neurons): collaborative neurons ([2]) and other methods that identify concept‑related groups are not compared (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 4 mentions [1*], [2*]).
  - Extracted circuits are not compared with existing circuit‑based approaches (e.g., whether they share the same neurons) (Reviewer 3).
- **Polysemantic behavior beyond highest activations is ignored**: The framework only uses top‑\(k\) patches; neurons may also recognize concepts in low activations. This limitation should be acknowledged or the formulation generalized (Reviewer 3).
- **Evaluation limited to CNNs**: Only ResNet and GoogLeNet are tested; applicability to ViTs or other architectures is not shown (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing evaluation details and confidence intervals**:
  - **Optimality experiment (Figure 4)**: No confidence intervals reported, so significance is unclear. It is not specified whether 50 target neurons from 10 classes means 500 total or 5 per class (Reviewer 1).
  - **Masking/retaining experiment**: It is unclear whether random neurons are selected per layer matching the number of critical neurons at that layer, or only the total count is matched (Reviewer 1).
  - **Multi‑layer retaining experiment**: Performance decreases or fluctuates with added layers, which does **not** support the interpretation of critical nodes (Reviewer 1).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity not analyzed**: Dependence on the choice of \(k\) (in the neuron concept) is not investigated (Reviewer 1).
- **Normalization issue**: The weight \(W(G_i,G_j)\) in Equation 5 is unnormalized by group size, making cross‑group comparison difficult (Reviewer 1).
- **Clustering independence question**: In Algorithm 3, the concept of child neuron \(s_i\) appears to be computed independently of the parent neuron’s concept; it is unclear whether it is guaranteed to be a subset (Reviewer 1).
- **Stability of random crop patches**: The number of patches needed for stable performance is not addressed (Reviewer 4).
- **Importance score approximations**: Only one method (Integrated Gradients) is used; alternatives (e.g., Shapley value approximations) should be evaluated (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing reference**: Work [3*] that identifies neuron responses with highly activating crops and groups is not cited where relevant (Reviewer 4).
- **Figure 6 anomaly**: Low correlation in layers 2,3,4 (ResNet) and 3b,4b,4d (GoogLeNet) is not explained; insights are needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Typographical errors**: Line 056: “there” → “their”; Line 181: “our” → “out” (also “knocking out these neurons” form); Line 250: “its all” → “all its”; Line 311: missing word (Reviewers 1, 4).
