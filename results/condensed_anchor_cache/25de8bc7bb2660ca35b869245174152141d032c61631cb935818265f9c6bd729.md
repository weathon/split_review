## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces TRACE, a task-interleaved video LLM for Video Temporal Grounding (VTG). TRACE represents videos as causal sequences of events (timestamps, salient scores, captions), uses separate encoders/heads for each modality, and interleaves task tokens. It achieves SOTA zero-shot performance on Charades-STA, QVHighlights, and YouCook2 compared to prior video LLMs. Reviewers praise the causal event modeling framework but raise concerns about limited comparisons, compression choices, tokenization clarity, autoregressive direction, and generality.

### Strengths
1. **Causal event modeling as a novel framework** – First work to employ causal modeling in VLLM grounding (R4, confidence 5). It fits naturally with next-token prediction (R3) and addresses structural gaps in video data by decomposing videos into events with timestamps, salient scores, and captions (R1, R3). This granularity is crucial for precise video event parsing (R1).
2. **Strong zero-shot and overall performance** – Outperforms previous video LLMs consistently on Charades-STA, QVHighlights, and YouCook2 (R1, R2, R3). Two reviewers (R1, score 8; R4, score 8) highlight the method’s efficacy across benchmarks, and R2 (score 6) notes superior zero-shot results.
3. **Clear motivation and intuitive design** – Presentation is clear and easy to follow (R2). The decomposition into events and using causal modeling is straightforward yet effective (R2).
4. **Maximizes pre-trained LLMs** – Leverages existing LLMs via causal event modeling, aligning video frames with textual prompts (R1).
5. **Inspiration for future video understanding** – The approach will inspire future work on video LLMs (R4, confidence 5). Rest of the reviewers (R1, R2, R3) agree that improving VTG in LLMs is valuable.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited comparison to non-generative/task-specific models** – The paper primarily compares with other video LLMs, not traditional non-generative methods (R2, R3). R3 (score 5, most critical) argues that for VTG, lightweight task-specific models may be more suitable and that TRACE’s multiple task-specific heads may limit the versatility that makes video LLMs appealing. R2 also notes that the design’s applicability to other video tasks beyond VTG is unclear and should be demonstrated.
2. **Compression to 8 visual tokens per frame insufficiently justified** – No analysis or experiments on the trade-off between token count and performance (R1, R3). R3 adds that slot-based compression is not explained; no comparison to established methods like Q-Former or 2D average pooling. The choice of 8 tokens appears arbitrary, and may cause significant information loss (R1, R3).
3. **Tokenization and separation of timestamps vs. scores** – It is unclear whether the same set of number tokens is used for both timestamps and salient scores. If so, this could blend the two types of information, contradicting the claim that the model preserves distinct event structure (R3). The tokenization process needs clarification (R3).
4. **Autoregressive modeling only uses past events, not future events** – The model conditions only on earlier events \(e_{1:k-1}\), ignoring known later events \(e_{k+1:K}\). This may be unreasonable because identical past events can lead to different current events; modeling simultaneous alternatives could avoid this issue. Cited works (Liang et al. CVPR22, Lam et al. NeurIPS24, Chen et al. NeurIPS24, Du et al. arXiv24) use all contextual events. Authors need to justify this design choice (R4, confidence 5). (R4 is otherwise positive, score 8.)
5. **Inference speed** – Like autoregressive models, TRACE may incur time overhead. Reviewer would like to see inference speed comparison with mainstream LLMs (R4).
6. **Confound in LLM backbone** – TRACE uses Mistral-7B while comparison methods use LLaMA-2 (Timechat) or Vicuna (HawkEye, Momentor, VTimeLLM). Experiments with LLaMA-2 or Vicuna are needed to ensure that performance gains come from causal modeling, not a stronger backbone (R4).
7. **Missing related work on causal event modeling** – The related work section does not discuss prior approaches that model causal event structures in video understanding, leaving novelty unclarified (R1).
8. **Grammatical and spelling errors** – e.g., Line 22 “processes” should be “process”, Line 44–45 punctuation/lacks issues (R1). Affects readability.
