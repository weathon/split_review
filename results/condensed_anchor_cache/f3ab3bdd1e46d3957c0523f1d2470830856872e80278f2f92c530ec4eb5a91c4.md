## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces SpeechFake, a large-scale dataset for speech deepfake detection. It contains over 3 million deepfake samples (3,000+ hours) generated by 40 different speech generation tools (TTS, VC, NV) across 46 languages. Baseline detection results and analyses of performance across languages, speakers, and generation methods are provided. The dataset will be publicly released.

### Strengths
- **Scale and diversity**: The dataset covers 46 languages and 40 speech generation tools, making it the largest and most diverse public dataset for speech deepfake detection (3M utterances, 3,000 hours). (R1, R2)
- **Rich metadata**: Includes metadata enabling in-depth analysis of anti-spoofing performance and targeted optimizations. (R1)
- **Informative analysis**: The exploration of generator types, language diversity, and speaker variability effects on detection performance is valuable. (R1, R3)
- **Potential for other uses**: The dataset may be useful beyond deepfake detection, e.g., for data augmentation. (R3)
- **Addresses an important problem**: The paper tackles a timely and socially relevant issue. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental contribution**: The dataset scope is closely aligned with previous work (acknowledged in Table 1). The paper offers a solid but incremental addition rather than a groundbreaking advance for the anti-spoofing community. (R1)
- **Not enough empirical comparisons**: The evaluation only compares training on SpeechFake vs. training on ASV19. Comparisons against other existing datasets or combinations of datasets are missing, which would better demonstrate the dataset’s added value. (R3)
- **Narrow contribution for the venue**: A dataset + baseline paper is better suited for a speech-focused conference or workshop; the contribution is narrow for ICLR. (R3) *[Minority reviewer R3 is more negative; R1 and R2 rated 6, R3 rated 5.]*
- **Multilingual imbalance**: Although the dataset spans 46 languages, it is heavily skewed toward English and Chinese. For 37 languages, only 5,000 test utterances are provided and no training data, limiting the utility for truly multilingual deepfake detection research. (R2)
- **Coverage of generation tools is incomplete and not clearly justified**: The selection criteria for the 40 systems are not explained; it is unclear if they represent the full spectrum of generation techniques or are biased. The dataset also cannot account for future techniques, so it may become outdated. (R2)
- **Missing dataset construction details**: There is no clear algorithm for how multiple speaker IDs were sampled and generated for each model, nor how the number of utterances per generation tool per original utterance was determined. (R2, R3)
- **Quality filtering needs clarification**: How was “noticeable distortions, excessive noise, or unnatural artifacts” discarded? Was this automatic or manual? No human verification of generated samples is reported (e.g., for silences, truncated words). (R2, R3)
- **Inconsistent and suspiciously low detection results**: The EERs in Tables 5 and 6 are much better than those in Tables 3 and 4 (e.g., English and Chinese results in Table 4 vs. BD-EN/BD-CN in Table 3). This discrepancy is unexplained. (R3)
- **Extremely low EERs suggest the multilingual dataset may be too easy**: In Table 5, very low EERs indicate that the detection task might be trivial, undermining the dataset’s value for deepfake detection research. (R3)
- **Lack of confidence intervals/significance estimates**: For settings with very low EERs, it is hard to tell whether differences (e.g., between speaker settings in Table 6) are meaningful without statistical estimates. (R3)
- **Missing analysis on why BD-UT is excluded or separated** from the result discussion table. (R1)
- **Missing version/access dates for API services** used to generate data; these may change over time. (R1)
- **Unclear why TTS-Tortoise and VC-OpenVoiceTTS contain significantly more data** than other systems. (R1)
- **Choice of test sets (Table 3)**: Why were test sets from 2019 selected rather than more recent benchmarks? Evaluating on all available benchmarks would be more comprehensive. (R2)
- **Inconsistency in “Other” dataset performance (Table 3)**: Why does the BD (combined set) not consistently outperform its individual subsets on the “Other” datasets? (R2)
- **Minor presentation issues**: Title wording (“toward” is unclear) (R3); third contribution bullet seems like an elaboration (R3); Table 1 missing explanation of “-” (R3); Related Work incorrectly implies TTS and VC are the only speech generation tasks (R3); Related Work subsection on existing datasets does not explicitly state what is new/different (R3); Appendix “speaker information” description is vague (R3); waveform/mel-spectrogram comparisons in the appendix have unclear and questionable statements (e.g., measuring timbre from waveforms, “overall structure”, “energy distribution”) and axes are not labeled (R3).
