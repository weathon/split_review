## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes RedCO, a framework that reduces multiple combinatorial optimization (CO) problems (ATSP, HCP, SAT) into a general TSP form with distance matrices, and introduces two neural solvers (MatPOENet, MatDIFFNet) for matrix-encoded TSPs. MatPOENet is a Transformer-based RL model with pseudo one-hot embedding; MatDIFFNet is a diffusion-based generative model. Experiments cover ATSP, 2DTSP, HCP-distributed, and 3SAT-distributed instances up to 100 nodes. The goal is to train a single neural TSP solver on diverse CO instances, optionally finetuning per problem type.

### Strengths
- RedCO unifies multiple CO problems via reduction to a general TSP, expanding the scope of neural solvers to handle non-metric, asymmetric, and discrete TSP instances (not just Euclidean) [R1, R2].
- Two novel solvers, MatPOENet and MatDIFFNet, leverage Transformer and diffusion architectures for matrix-encoded TSP problems [R1].
- The framework is modular, allowing incorporation of existing solvers like DIMES [R1].
- The paper is well-written and easy to understand [R2, R3].
- First known attempt at a general framework for learning various COPs via reduction [R2].
- Experiments are thorough and effectively showcase capability with arbitrary matrix-encoded TSPs [R2, R3].
- Problem reduction has theoretical support [R4].
- Proposed methods show advantages in effectiveness on tested problems [R4].

### Weaknesses
- **Scalability and large-scale feasibility**: The paper does not thoroughly test or analyze performance on large-scale instances (e.g., tens of thousands of nodes). Experiments are limited to ≤100 nodes, raising doubts about scalability. The complexity scaling with node count, edge density, time, and memory is not analyzed. The claim that MatDIFFNet has potential for larger instances is unsupported; no evidence is provided [R1, R3, R4].
- **Limited applicability and reduction concerns**: The reduction of multi-CO problems to general TSP is not intuitively applicable to a wide range of CO problems. Some reductions (e.g., CVRP via clustering) require solving a TSP per cluster, which can be an NP-hard subproblem and may harm the found rate. The transformation of NP problems to SAT (as in Fig. 1) is not proven, and the time complexity of reductions may increase dramatically. The paper does not discuss which types of COPs (or properties) can be reduced or the limitations of the reduction process [R1, R3, R4].
- **Missing baseline comparisons**: Results for DIFUSCO and T2T (MatDIFFNet builds on them) are not included, making it hard to assess MatDIFFNet’s contribution. Comparisons to Gurobi for efficiency are missing. Additional baselines such as GOAL, MVMOE, GLOP, and large-scale methods are recommended by reviewers. A time comparison in Table 1 is also suggested [R2, R3, R4].
- **Poor experimental organization and clarity**: The seven research questions are not clearly linked to specific experiments, making navigation difficult. The settings for “* version”, “single”, and “mixed” in Table 2 are not well described. The N and d settings for variants in Table 4 lack explanation. Synthetic data generation is not fully detailed, raising concerns about generalizability to real-world distributions [R2, R3, R4].
- **Lack of interpretability and robustness analysis**: The paper provides no insight into which features of the input matrix influence decisions, limiting trust and diagnosis of biases. No sensitivity analysis to noise, missing entries, or corrupted edge weights is performed. This is critical for real-world applicability [R1].
- **Computational cost and resource demands**: MatDIFFNet is computationally intensive (trained on 8 H800 80GB GPUs with 16-core CPU), and no ablation on computational resources or breakdown of diffusion-step cost is provided. Inference times are longer, and the paper does not discuss optimizations [R1, R3, R4].
- **Questionable contribution and novelty**: Translating CO problems to TSP is not a new idea, and TSP solvers are well developed. The added value of RedCO for multi-task learning is questionable given limited scope of tested problems (only ATSP, HCP, 3SAT). MatDIFFNet lags in performance and efficiency compared to baselines, and its significance is unclear; some reviewers suggest moving it to the appendix [R4].
- **Specific performance anomalies**: In Table 2, MatPOENet and MatDIFFNet outperform LKH on 3SAT but are worse elsewhere. The explanation is missing, and a visual example is requested. The average L across problems may be skewed by the 3SAT result [R2, R4].
- **Missing evaluation on standard symmetric TSP** and on more diverse CO problems (e.g., VC, Clique, VRPs, FFSP, MIS) that the paper claims applicability to [R2, R3].
