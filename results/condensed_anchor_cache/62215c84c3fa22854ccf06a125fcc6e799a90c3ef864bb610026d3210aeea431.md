## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers found MMCAT’s goal of using multimodal data (LiDAR, images, 2D boxes) for 3D auto-annotation useful and acknowledged improvements on hard samples (occlusion, truncation). However, they raised several limitations: failure to outperform prior SoTA on easy KITTI cases, reliance on manually annotated 2D boxes (and some 3D annotations), evaluation only on the car/vehicle category, missing comparisons with DetZero and ViT-WSS3D, limited architectural novelty, and dependence on 2D boxes being available. One reviewer (score 6) was more positive about the impact and multi-modal direction; the other two (score 5) found the technical contribution limited and the annotation cost still significant.

### Strengths
- Impact: leveraging abundant multimodal data (LiDAR, images, 2D boxes) to train better 3D detectors cheaply is valuable.
- Leveraging multi-modal data: aligning 3D point cloud with 2D image and bounding-box data is a useful research direction given the maturity of image encoders.
- MMCAT’s multimodal context-aware transformer with four encoders makes comprehensive use of multimodal information.
- Using 2D bounding boxes to optimize 3D bounding-box regression can reduce labeling cost compared to full 3D annotation.
- 2D visual cues from images and boxes effectively address limitations of sparse point clouds, leading to accuracy improvements on hard samples (e.g., truncation, occlusion, distance) on KITTI and Waymo.
- Qualitative results demonstrate MMCAT’s effectiveness.

### Weaknesses
- Table 1 shows MMCAT fails to beat previous SoTA on easier cases within KITTI; the paper does not analyze failure modes to explain this behavior.
- The method still uses manually annotated 2D boxes and some 3D annotation information (Section 3.4), incurring a non-negligible labeling cost.
- Experiments are limited to the car/vehicle category on KITTI and Waymo; no results are provided for other categories (e.g., pedestrian, cyclist).
- Missing comparison with state-of-the-art automatic annotation algorithms, particularly DetZero (ICCV 2023) and ViT-WSS3D (ICCV 2023). The proposed design is similar to ViT-WSS3D but requires higher annotation cost, making the contribution limited.
- The paper does not report whether the KITTI test-set accuracy has been verified on the official KITTI benchmark; the corresponding results were not found there.
- The architectural contributions to the multimodal transformer itself are limited; the design largely follows existing encoder patterns.
- 2D bounding boxes are not always available, preventing MMCAT from being applied to raw point clouds without 2D annotations.
- Several open questions overlap with weaknesses:
    - What is the rationale for a uniform architecture design across modalities (Section 3.2)? Would modality-specific designs benefit?
    - Why does the MMCAT column in Tables 2 and 4 list “2D Box” as supervision, when MMCAT is also trained with 3D bounding boxes (Section 3.4)?
    - What information do images and 2D boxes actually provide for 3D bounding-box regression? How effective is using only a Point+2D encoder (ablation needed)?
    - Would using existing pre-trained encoders (e.g., instead of MMCAT’s custom encoders) affect performance?
    - What is the effect of inaccurate 2D bounding boxes as inputs (e.g., from an off-the-shelf 2D detector)? Robustness to noisy 2D cues is not studied.
    - What are the concrete advantages of MMCAT over ViT-WSS3D beyond using an additional 2D box encoder?
