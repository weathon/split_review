## Merged Review

### Summary
Four reviewers evaluate “Sparkle”, a framework that enhances 2D spatial reasoning in Vision Language Models (VLMs) by fine-tuning on three basic spatial capabilities – direction comprehension, distance estimation, and localization – using synthetic data. Experiments on InternVL2‑8B show large gains on synthetic composite tasks (shortest path problem improves from 13.5% to 40.0%) and generalization to real‑world benchmarks (What’s Up, COCO‑spatial, GQA‑spatial). Reviewers score the paper 5,5,3,5 (one minority score of 3). All reviewers note the paper is well motivated but raise concerns about limited model evaluation, missing comparisons, under‑explained out‑of‑domain results, and insufficient ablation.

### Strengths
1. **Decomposition of spatial reasoning** – The paper identifies and focuses on three fundamental components (direction, distance, localization) as the building blocks of 2D spatial reasoning, providing a structured approach.
2. **Performance gains on composite and out‑of‑distribution tasks** – Fine‑tuning on basic capabilities yields substantial improvements on synthetic composite tasks (e.g., shortest path problem from 13.5% to 40.0%) and generalizes to real‑world spatial reasoning benchmarks such as What’s Up, COCO‑spatial, and GQA‑spatial.
3. **Demonstration of generalization beyond training distribution** – The improvements on real‑world benchmarks (COCO‑spatial, GQA‑spatial) show that training on artificial basic spatial tasks can transfer, reinforcing the central hypothesis.
4. **Identification of a challenging benchmark** – The proposed synthetic 2D space dataset captures tasks where current state‑of‑the‑art VLMs fail, providing a useful evaluation resource.
5. **Clear motivation** – The paper is well‑motivated, breaking down spatial reasoning into foundational elements and underscoring the necessity of core spatial capabilities in VLMs.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited model evaluation** – The main experiments only use InternVL2‑8B. Generalizability to other open‑source VLMs is uncertain; results on Qwen‑VL‑7B are relegated to the appendix. One reviewer questions why InternVL2‑8B outperforms the 26B variant without fine‑tuning (Table 3), suggesting a need for explanation.
2. **Missing comparison to prompting/in‑context learning** – No baseline uses simple prompting or chain‑of‑thought (e.g., inserting three basic spatial reasoning questions before generating the final answer), so it is unclear whether fine‑tuning is necessary.
3. **Under‑developed out‑of‑domain analysis** – The OOD generalization section is only three short paragraphs, despite its critical importance for showing that training on basic tasks genuinely improves real VLM spatial reasoning. Reviewers request a deeper explanation of why performance transfers from artificial data to COCO‑spatial and GQA‑spatial, including qualitative examples and a mechanistic analysis. The limited dataset size (2000 images) further makes this transfer counter‑intuitive.
4. **Unclear choice of OOD benchmarks** – The paper selects What’s Up, COCO‑spatial, and GQA‑spatial for OOD evaluation but does not test on datasets that explicitly require all three trained abilities (e.g., SpatialRGPT, QualSR, listed in Table 1). Evaluating on those would better validate the framework.
5. **Missing explanation for omitting two of five basic abilities** – Table 1 identifies five basic spatial abilities, but the paper only creates synthetic data and trains on three (direction, distance, localization). The rationale for excluding the other two is not given, breaking the narrative consistency.
6. **Insufficient ablation of component contributions** – Figure 6 does not include experiments with every combination of two basic tasks. It therefore does not answer why two tasks are insufficient or which pair is most important.
7. **Lack of evaluation on non‑spatial tasks** – The paper does not test Sparkle on general visual tasks (e.g., object recognition, image captioning), so it is unclear whether fine‑tuning causes overfitting to the synthetic spatial data.
8. **Concern about real‑world perspective variations** – Training images may all be from straight‑on views, limiting generalization to real applications with varying perspectives. The paper does not address this.
9. **Novelty and dataset positioning** – The framework combines abilities that are partially covered by existing benchmarks, and the proposed Sparkle dataset is missing from Table 1, making its contribution unclear. One reviewer questions the novelty of the framework.
10. **Toy experimental setting** – The synthetic tasks (SPP, TSP) are artificial; the paper lacks a concrete application scenario that justifies the benchmark set.
11. **No comparison with explicit solvers** – SPP and TSP have known algorithmic solvers; it would be interesting to see whether the model can use external tools as solvers, but this is not explored.
12. **Minority reviewer contrast** – One reviewer (score 3) finds the main finding unsurprising (basic tasks are subtasks of SPP) and criticizes the under‑emphasis of OOD results; the other three reviewers (scores 5) are more positive overall.
