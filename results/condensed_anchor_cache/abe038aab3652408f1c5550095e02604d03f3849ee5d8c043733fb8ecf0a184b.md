## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Momentum-SAM (MSAM), which replaces SAM’s inner gradient ascent step with the negative momentum direction to halve the per‑iteration backward pass cost. Scores range from 3 to 6 (R1:6, R2:6, R3:5, R4:3), reflecting a split between appreciation of the practical motivation and doubts about novelty, theoretical grounding, and empirical performance relative to SAM. R4 is the most critical; R1 and R2 see the method as promising but with clear gaps.

### Strengths
- Well‑written, easy to follow, and clearly motivated (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- No extra hyperparameter beyond the momentum coefficient (R2).
- The observation that the negative momentum direction is a (partial) surrogate for the SAM ascent direction is interesting (R1).
- Empirically effective on CIFAR‑10 and ImageNet (R1).
- Provides experimental results from several perspectives (R2) and a detailed study of properties on conventional benchmarks (R3).
- Reduces the computational overhead of SAM from two backward passes to one; Figure 1 gives a clear explanation (R4).
- The phenomenon of negative correlation between momentum and current‑batch gradient is highlighted as a potentially important finding (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty / relation to prior work.** The core idea resembles LookSAM and several other efficient SAM variants; the improvement over them is marginal (R3, R4). The paper should discuss [1] Bahri et al. (arXiv:2110.08529) on language model generalization and [2] K‑SAM (Ni et al., arXiv:2210.12864) (R3). As a speed‑up method, it should be compared with random‑perturbation based methods that offer a similar computational advantage (R4). R4 also notes that the current reported performance for baseline SAM and other methods is not very good.
- **Theoretical justification.** The rationale for using negative momentum as a stand‑in for the SAM ascent direction is not convincingly established. The paper provides no convergence or sharpness‑minimisation guarantee, which is a minimum expectation for ICLR (see Andriushchenko & Flammarion, ICML 2022) (R3). The empirical evidence is limited to cosine similarity (Section 4.1), which does not directly relate to loss‑value changes in a non‑linear DNN; R4 requests direct reporting of loss changes and tests with different learning rates. The claim that the method leads to flat minima is unsupported (R3).
- **Mechanism of momentum.** The connection between momentum and sharpness minimisation is unclearly explained (R1, R4). R4 points out that using momentum in the next iteration is roughly equivalent to using a larger learning rate in the previous iteration, but this relies on a “well‑chosen” learning rate – not necessarily optimal in practice – so increasing it may not always increase the loss as required for the SAM attack. R1 asks whether the effect is dataset‑ or task‑specific (vision only? classification only?). R2 finds the claims in Chapter 4 unconvincing, especially why the positive momentum direction does not improve performance and why \(\rho_0\) is close to \(\rho^{\text{opt}}\).
- **Performance relative to SAM.** In Tables 1,2,3, MSAM generally achieves lower accuracy than SAM except in a few cases (R2). Doubling the number of epochs to match the total computational budget (Appendix A.9) might change the comparison; more results (e.g., ESAM, other models/datasets) should be added to Figure A.8 (R2). R4 states the overall performance is not competitive with recent papers on this topic.
- **Missing experiments and comparisons.**
  - Finetuning experiments (R1).
  - Language model experiments (R1, R3).
  - ImageNet NAG results (R1).
  - Comparison with other SAM speed‑up methods (R4).
  - Clarity on whether momentum is applied to SAM itself (SAM has no momentum term) (R2).
  - Report whether MSAM can surpass SAM’s final performance when given the same computational budget (R2).
  - Link to a NeurIPS 2023 paper (arxiv 2309.15639) that argues for *adding momentum* to the SAM attack; even without a backward pass, one may need to increase (not decrease) the loss (R4).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity.** The choice of \(\rho\) and sensitivity to it are not investigated (R1).
- **Section 4.3 clarity.** The relation of the analysis to m‑sharpness needs to be made explicit (R1).
- **Implementation description.** The “efficient implementation” on page 4 is straightforward and could be omitted (R2).
