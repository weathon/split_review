## Merged Review

### Summary

The reviewers disagree significantly: one rates the paper 8 for originality, quality, and significance; three rate it 3 and one rates it 5. Most reviewers acknowledge the method is feasible and performs adequately, but raise concerns about novelty (the distance already defined for positive definite matrices), incomplete theoretical validation, ad hoc choices, limited experimental scope, and overclaimed generality. The minority positive reviewer finds the approach original and well presented, though notes missing generalisation of theoretical results and an overly GNN-focused framing.

### Strengths

- The problem of graph metric development is significant and the proposed approach is original to the best of the positive reviewer’s knowledge (R4).
- Quality of exposition is good; mathematical derivations are reasonable and sound; motivation is provided; ample experiments and comparisons are made; theoretical statements are relevant to the bigger picture (R4).
- The idea is not too complex, the method is feasible, and the three steps are well documented with sufficient level of explanations and details (R2).
- Performance is correct and computing times are slightly reduced compared to state-of-the-art; scaling is O(n³) while previous OT-based approaches have an additional multiplicative log n term (R2).
- The topic is useful for understanding GNN behavior; GGD behaves similarly to the GNN called GIN, aiding graph comparison (R2).
- GGD empirically outperforms existing measures on classification tasks, as demonstrated in Fig. 1 and Tab. 1 (R3, R5).
- Relates geodesic distances to GNN outputs and explains structural mismatches (R1).

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of originality**: The distance is essentially the geodesic distance between positive definite matrices introduced in Lim et al. (2019); the only modification is making Laplacians positive definite by adding ε I. Contribution is insufficient for a top venue, though some practical tricks are useful (R1, R5).
- **Incomplete metric proof**: It is unclear whether GGD gives zero for the same graph under two different adjacency matrices, because the alignment step might not yield the same modified matrix. More careful theoretical analysis with concrete examples is needed (R3).
- **Weak connection to cuts (Sec. 4.3)**: The bound on the cut ratio does not clearly relate to the quality of the graph distance measure; how does the bound support GGD? (R3). This analysis is further limited to same node set with known correspondence; no analogous result for different dimensions or node features, despite abstract/intro overclaiming generality (R4).
- **Strong assumption in Lemma 4.2**: The requirement null(L₂) ⊆ null(L₁) is very strong beyond the trivial connected-graph case; examples of common graph types satisfying it are needed to understand practical restrictiveness (R4).
- **Ad hoc methodological choices** (R2):
  - Why use a Riemannian distance only in the third step, and never consider this structure for coarsening or alignment?
  - Why use GRAMPA alignment instead of alternatives?
  - How are parameters like η chosen? The choice η = 0.5 does not seem coherent with Lemma 4.1 (1/log n ≈ 1/3 for MUTAG).
  - Is the proposed coarsening method optimal? Why not use existing coarsening methods?
  - The combination of effective resistance and features (eq. 15) is purely empirical with no justification.
- **Missing algorithmic pipeline**: No detailed algorithm or summary of all steps is provided (R2, R3).
- **Need to justify Riemannian metric**: The paper does not show whether using a proper Riemannian metric after coarsening and alignment is important; what would change with a simpler spectral distance? (R2).
- **Unclear role of modified Laplacian**: Why not use the pseudoinverse of the Laplacian instead of adding ε I? Please explain the advantage (R3).
- **Limited experimental scope**: Only four datasets tested, all small molecules (avg nodes 18–47); results are specific and narrow (R2). Benchmarks in Tab. 2 are not recent (R5).
- **Incomplete validation setup**: Only train/test split used on small datasets; a train/validation/test split is expected (R2). Standard deviations are only reported for MUTAG in Tab. 3, not for other datasets; significant digits need checking (R2).
- **Overly inflated appendix**: Sections A.1, A.3, A.8 cover common knowledge or unnecessary comments; A.2 is too short to introduce Riemannian geometry usefully; A.4 follows directly from AIRM; A.7 could be simplified. The paper is inflated with known material (R2).
- **Redundant presentation**: Section 3 repeats content from the introduction and later sections (R2).
- **Framing vs. content**: The title and abstract overemphasize GNNs, even though GNN applications appear only in later sections; the general graph comparison framework should be better reflected (R4).
- **Additional technical questions left unresolved**:
  - If resistances are identical, are two graphs guaranteed to be identical? If not, the resistance-based method may have a disadvantage (R3).
  - Why use an approximated method for effective resistance when the cost of obtaining full eigenvectors for the same-size case is almost the same? (R3).
  - Why are features used in eq. (14) (should they only be in eq. 15)? (R2).
  - Can a GGD-based SVC classifier be incorporated into a GNN model? (R5).
