## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes two active evaluation algorithms (UCB-E and its variant UCB-E-LRF) based on upper confidence bounds and low-rank matrix factorization. The goal is to identify the best-performing method (among LLMs, prompts, hyperparameters) across validation examples while using only 5–15% of the resources. The work is motivated by the combinatorial explosion of method choices and the cost of evaluating every method on every sample.

### Strengths
- **Novel application and solid idea:** This is the first paper to apply multi-armed bandits to LLM evaluation (Reviewer 3). The approach can accelerate selection of the best model/setup for a custom task, substantially reducing resource usage (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **General applicability:** The algorithms are not limited to LLMs and can be used for any evaluation use case (Reviewer 1).
- **Clear algorithmic description:** Algorithms are clearly outlined, making understanding and reproduction easy (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Thorough empirical evaluation:** Experiments span multiple datasets (GSM8K, PIQA, etc.), setups (methods sets: models, prompts), and metrics (Top-1 precision, NDCG@10) with repeated random seeds for stability (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Ablations cover ensemble size, uncertainty scaling factor, warm-up budget, and rank (Reviewer 3).
- **Demonstrated cost savings:** The approach achieves 85–95% LLM cost savings while successfully identifying top methods (Reviewer 1). Evaluated on a variety of tasks and setups (Reviewer 1).
- **Theoretical support:** Paper provides theoretical proof and discussion (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Figures and tables lack clarity:** 
  - Figure 3, which presents key results, has no legend, making it difficult to interpret (Reviewers 2, 3). The caption of Figure 4 states UCB-E and UCB-E-LRF are blue and red, but other curves are not identified (Reviewer 3).
  - Table 2 columns (“Size m x n”, “Method Set”, “Dataset Name”) are ordered confusingly; size is given as m×n but the natural reading order is unclear (Reviewer 3). The H1 hardness value is stated but not explained near the table; readers must refer to Corollary 1 or section 4.4 (Reviewer 3).
  - Table 3 is described as difficult to understand (Reviewer 4).
- **Baseline descriptions are incomplete:** Only one baseline (uniformly sample and evaluate T examples) is described in the text, but three baselines are mentioned and appear in figures. The other two baselines are never explicitly defined or given attention (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Unclear experimental results and contradictory conclusions:**
  - Figure 3 shows inconsistent performance of UCB-E and UCB-E-LRF across datasets. The paper attributes this to varying dataset difficulty (H1 hardness). However, comparing dataset pairs like (GSM8K Models, PIQA Models) and (GSM8K Prompts, PIQA Prompts) yields contradictory conclusions that require a more detailed explanation (Reviewer 2).
  - The rank r is set to 1 for UCB-E-LRF. Although the ablation study shows r=1 performs best, this choice is unusually low compared to related research. The authors should provide more evidence that this is not due to sampling error or an inadequately small dataset (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing or weak citations:** Section 3.2 on low-rank factorization states “if the method-examples are very correlated, there should exist…” – this intuition should be cited (e.g., Chen et al. 2020, Cai et al. 2019, already in appendix) (Reviewer 3). The related work section is weak and needs more discussions on evaluation of LLMs (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation issues:**
  - The paper appears recycled from a longer submission; many figures/tables have tiny fonts, making them hard to read (Reviewer 4). Proofreading is needed to fix typos (e.g., “by adaptive selecting” → “by adaptively selecting”) (Reviewer 1).
  - The variable \(a\) in the equation on line 190 is not defined (Reviewer 1). A table/mapping to keep track of notations would be helpful (Reviewer 1).
  - Figure 3 has datasets ordered from highest H1 to lowest, but no mention that higher H1 means harder and lower H1 means easier; the ordering and its meaning are not immediately obvious (Reviewer 3).
- **Broader evaluation concerns:** The paper does not address potential biases in test datasets or how the comparison results align with human evaluation, since automatic evaluation may not reflect real LLM capacities (Reviewer 4).

**Note:** Reviewer 1 reports “Nothing major to report” in weaknesses, contrasting with the more critical assessments of Reviewers 2–4. This minority positive view is preserved.
