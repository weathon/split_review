## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes CARD, a Transformer for time series forecasting that introduces channel-aligned attention to capture cross-channel dependencies, a token blend module for multi-scale tokens, and a robust loss weighting predictions by uncertainty. Evaluations on long-term and short-term datasets show improvements over state-of-the-art methods. Reviewers generally found the paper well-written with extensive experiments, but raised concerns about novelty, experimental consistency, baseline comparisons, and specific analytical results.

### Strengths

- Architecture design is novel and interesting, particularly the channel-aligned attention structure and token blend module that address limitations of channel-independent (CI) Transformers (Reviewers 1,3,4). However, Reviewer 2 noted many components (channel independence, patchify, EMA, dynamic projection) are from prior work, making overall novelty unclear.

- Comprehensive experiments: extensive ablation studies, hyperparameter sensitivity, loss function analysis, and results on multiple long-term and short-term forecasting and anomaly detection tasks (Reviewers 1,3,4). Reviewers appreciated the level of detail in the main paper and appendices.

- The paper is well written and easy to follow, with clear figures (Figures 1,2) and a well-organized structure that aids understanding of the proposed method (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).

- The robust loss function that weights predictions based on uncertainty to mitigate overfitting is a thoughtful contribution (Reviewer 3). However, its advantage over SOTA without robust loss is not always significant (Reviewer 4).

- The paper includes comparisons against basic baselines like Zeng et al. (AAAI'23), which is seen as a positive step (Reviewer 1). However, other reviewers noted that the experimental setup needs more consistency with prior work for fair comparison (Reviewer 2,3).

### Weaknesses

1. **Missing or inconsistent baseline comparisons**:
   - The paper does not include results for Zeng et al. (AAAI'23) in Table 1, which Reviewer 1 considers the most important baseline. Reviewer 1 suggests removing some other entries to include it.
   - Table 2 (M4 short-term) results are not impressive: the model does not perform well compared to the first entry in the M4 competition and barely beats the second entry. Reviewer 1 recommends using the well-accepted protocol for M4 evaluation, including N-BEATS ensemble results (ref. arXiv:1905.10437), or replacing Table 2 with ablation studies.
   - The comparison with PatchTST uses different input lengths (336 vs 512) and unofficial code, making it difficult to assess true performance gains (Reviewer 2). The authors should use official PatchTST code with the same input length (336 or 512) for a fair comparison.

2. **Lack of novelty and reliance on existing techniques**:
   Reviewer 2 finds the method combines existing ideas (channel independence, patchify, EMA, dynamic projection) without clear justification for a significant advancement. The specific combination's novelty is not well articulated.

3. **Insufficient validation of dynamic projection on large-channel datasets**:
   Reviewer 2 notes that the ablation for dynamic projection uses only ETT datasets (7 channels); it is unclear how it performs on datasets with many variables like Traffic (862 channels) or ECL (321 channels). More thorough validation is needed.

4. **Ablation studies need more structural comparison**:
   - Reviewer 2 notes that ablation experiments focus on parameter changes (e.g., projection dimensions) rather than structural differences between CARD and PatchTST. To evaluate the method's rationality, ablations should isolate the effect of key design choices (e.g., channel-aligned attention vs. standard channel-independent).
   - Reviewer 3 requests an ablation study comparing fixed-parameter EMA vs. learnable-parameter EMA to understand whether a fixed decay rate limits adaptability to varying temporal dynamics, especially on non-stationary datasets. The paper should discuss trade-offs and include such analysis.
   - Reviewer 4 observes that in the ablation of the robust loss function (Tables 12,13), CARD does not show a significant advantage over the SOTA baseline without the robust loss. Additional test results on more datasets would be beneficial.

5. **Confusing or inconsistent experimental results**:
   - Reviewer 4 points out that in Table 18, the average performance of CARD and "wo. hidden" are identical but the diffs column is not zero, which is confusing and needs clarification.
   - Reviewer 3 notes a discrepancy between visual results and tabular data in short-term forecasting (M4 figures vs. tables). Also, short-term evaluation is limited to only the M4 dataset, which may not be sufficient to demonstrate general performance.

6. **Missing complexity and efficiency comparison**:
   Reviewer 4 notes that the paper provides complexity analysis for CARD but does not compare running time or efficiency with baseline models. Such comparisons would strengthen the paper.

7. **Missing datasets**:
   Reviewer 1 requests adding results on ILI and Exchange datasets to Table 1.

8. **Overfitting mitigation needs theoretical support**:
   Reviewer 4 states that while empirical analysis is provided, the claim of effectively mitigating overfitting would be more convincing with appropriate theoretical analysis.

9. **Insufficient exploration of lookback window**:
   Reviewer 3 notes the paper does not compare different lookback values on the M4 dataset to analyze sensitivity to input window size and impact on short-term vs. long-term dependencies.

10. **Limited data regime experiment**:
    Reviewer 2 questions the experiment using only 70% of training data for long-term forecasting, stating it may not be sufficient to draw strong conclusions about model behavior with less data.
