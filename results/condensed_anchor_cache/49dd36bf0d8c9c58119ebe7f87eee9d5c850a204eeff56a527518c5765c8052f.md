## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Attention-based Graph Coreset Labeling (AGCL) for active learning in GNNs. AGCL uses an attention mechanism to model correlations between labeled and unlabeled nodes, selecting nodes that maximize representation diversity from the current labeled pool. The authors provide theoretical analysis bounding covering radius, and extensive experiments on homophilic and heterophilic graph datasets, across multiple GNN backbones (GCN, GAT, APPNP), as well as on image classification. Results show improved label efficiency and generalization over existing methods.

### Strengths

- Novel attention-based approach for active learning in graphs, replacing heuristic selection (e.g., entropy or graph-based scores) with a learned correlation measure. (R1, R3)
- Comprehensive experimental evaluation across datasets of varying scale and homophily levels, multiple GNN architectures (GCN, GAT, APPNP), and even image classification, demonstrating broad applicability and superior performance over existing methods. (R1, R3, R4)
- Attempts to provide theoretical foundations for the coreset selection strategy via covering radius bounds. (R1, R3) *Note: there is disagreement among reviewers on the quality of this theory (see Weaknesses).*
- Framework is flexible, applicable to both homophilic and heterophilic graphs, and can be extended to non-graph tasks like image classification. (R3)
- The paper addresses an important problem (label efficiency for GNNs) and is clearly structured and easy to understand. (R2)

### Weaknesses

- **Theoretical analysis issues.** Reviewer 1 finds the proof of Proposition 4.1 unconvincing: the coreset analysis assumes i.i.d. data, but graph data is non-i.i.d. and this is not addressed; the derivation using d(h,hv) < d(h',hv) < rv does not lead to Eq 11 and relies on a trivial step after Assumption 1 (dL/dh=0). Notation is confusing (d used for distance, derivative, and variable). Reviewer 3 notes that the theory does not explain how the attention mechanism’s architecture (number of heads, activation functions) affects the guarantees or how attention weights relate to coreset selection. Reviewer 4 adds that the paper lacks a clear definition of “informative” data point, which undercuts the theoretical motivation. In contrast, Reviewer 3 describes the theory as “solid,” indicating disagreement on this point.

- **Unfair advantage from hyperparameter search for initial set S0.** Reviewer 1 observes that the method searches for an initial labeled set S0, which appears to use additional budget beyond the given B. Since baselines do not have this search, experimental gains may partly come from exceeding the budget rather than the algorithm itself. Clarification is needed on whether S0 is always within total budget.

- **Reliability of attention coefficients with limited labels.** Reviewer 4 questions how attention weights can be learned accurately when only a small number of initial labels are available. The paper does not discuss this cold-start problem or provide justification for the coefficients’ reliability.

- **Insufficient scalability and real-world testing.** Reviewer 2 notes that datasets are limited in size (largest ~160k nodes); the paper does not discuss challenges or benefits on larger graphs. Reviewer 3 points out that the evaluation does not consider dynamic (time-varying) graphs, adversarial attacks, or label noise, which limits validation of robustness in real-world scenarios.

- **Outdated baselines and missing justification for backbone models.** Reviewer 4 states that the comparison methods are somewhat outdated and suggests incorporating more recent state-of-the-art active learning methods. Reviewer 1 notes that the choice of GCN, GAT, APPNP as backbones is not justified; common alternatives like GraphSage and GIN are not tested or discussed.

- **Presentation and clarity issues.** Reviewer 1 reports multiple typos (e.g., “assumpation,” “proposation”; double equation at line 772). Reviewer 2 finds the abstract lacking sufficient background for outsiders and Figure 1 unclear in conveying model structure. Computational complexity is discussed only briefly; detailed time/memory analysis is missing. (R1, R2)

Note: Reviewer opinions vary in overall positivity (scores 5,6,6,5), with two reviewers more favorable (R2, R3) and two more critical (R1, R4).
