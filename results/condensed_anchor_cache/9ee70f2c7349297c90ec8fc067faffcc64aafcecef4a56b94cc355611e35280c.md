## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces a “strategy-centric synthesis” paradigm to automatically generate high-quality visual instruction data from large-scale image-text pairs. It uses heuristic filtering of DataComp-1B, strategy mining with VLMs, and retrieval to produce diverse, complex instructions. Fine-tuning LLAVA-1.5-7B with LoRA on only 3,000 synthesized samples improves performance across multiple benchmarks. Two reviewers rated the paper 5 (positive), citing its novel paradigm and automated pipeline. Three reviewers rated the paper 3 (negative), raising substantial concerns about completeness, scalability, experimental rigor, and lack of comparisons. Overall, the method shows promise but the evaluation is considered insufficient and the motivation partially unsubstantiated.

### Strengths

1.  **Novel paradigm**: The strategy-centric synthesis method introduces automated strategy mining and strategy-guided multimodal instruction generation, moving beyond traditional task-based instruction tuning.
2.  **Automated pipeline**: The framework leverages large-scale image-text pairs (DataComp-1B) and VLMs to automatically extract query strategies and generate corresponding instructions, including a diverse seed image library.
3.  **Performance improvement with few samples**: Fine-tuning LLAVA-1.5 with only 3,000 synthesized samples (0.45% of the original SFT data) yields significant gains across benchmarks (MMBench, MMMU, POPE, VizWiz), demonstrating effectiveness.
4.  **Self-reflection for response quality**: The inclusion of a self-reflection step to evaluate and filter response quality (noted by one reviewer) adds a reliability mechanism.
5.  **Logical organization and clear presentation** (noted by one reviewer).

### Weaknesses

1.  **Insufficient experimental setup for rigorous evaluation**: The core experiment uses LoRA fine-tuning on only 3,000 samples, which is too small to attribute gains confidently to synthetic data quality rather than training variability. Multiple runs with averaged results and variance reporting are needed. The pipeline should be evaluated at a larger scale (at least 50,000 samples) to demonstrate robustness and reduce random fluctuations. [Reviewers 4, 1, 2]
2.  **Unfair or incomplete comparisons**: 
    - Table 2 compares fine-tuning with additional synthesized data against the original LLAVA-1.5 baseline, which is not a direct quality comparison. The correct control is to replace the original LLAVA-558k SFT data (or a portion) with the synthetic data and retrain from scratch. [Reviewers 5, 4]
    - Missing comparisons with other synthesis methods and datasets such as LVIS-Instruct4v, ShareGPT4v, Allava, and MMInstruct. Simply adding heterogeneous data could yield similar improvements. [Reviewers 5, 2, 4]
3.  **Lack of demonstration of completeness and scalability**: 
    - Completeness: The paper only validates on a fixed set of benchmarks (MMBench, MMMU, POPE, VizWiz) without showing that the method covers new tasks or open-domain instructions. Arguments about diversity and complexity are not backed by specific statistics [Reviewer 1, 3].
    - Scalability: The method’s ability to scale up data sources or algorithmic steps is not discussed. A universal strategy may produce similar data and reduce diversity when applied at scale [Reviewer 1, 2].
4.  **Motivation and claim justification are weak**: The paper asserts that existing multimodal instruction data lacks diversity and complexity, yet current benchmarks like MMMU and MMBench already include diverse domains and complex tasks (e.g., scientific reasoning, fine-grained perception). No specific statistical analysis is provided to support this gap [Reviewer 3].
5.  **Missing handling of noisy or bad instruction samples**: The paper does not detail a strategy for detecting or filtering generated low-quality instructions, which is critical for automated pipelines [Reviewer 3].
6.  **Missing analyses and ablations**: 
    - No ablation studying performance trend with increasing data size [Reviewer 2].
    - No experiments starting SFT from scratch (instead of continued fine-tuning) to isolate the effect of synthetic data [Reviewer 2].
    - Method section lacks specific details on how prompts are designed to address question variety, image diversity, and task complexity [Reviewer 3].
7.  **Need for human evaluation**: Relying solely on automatic benchmarks, which are not entirely reliable, is insufficient. Human studies on generated instruction quality and model outputs would strengthen the claims [Reviewer 1].

*Note: Reviewers 4 and 5 (score 5) are more positive overall, but they still raise the same core weaknesses about experimental setup and missing comparisons.*
