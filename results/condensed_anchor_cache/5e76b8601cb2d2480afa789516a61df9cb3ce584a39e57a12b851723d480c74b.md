## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies shortcomings in the uniformity metric from Wang & Isola (2020) – specifically its failure to detect dimensional collapse – and proposes five principled properties (invariance to instance permutations and sample replications, accurate capture of feature redundancy and dimensional collapse) that an effective metric should satisfy. The authors introduce a new uniformity metric based on the quadratic Wasserstein distance (W₂) between learned representations and a zero-mean isotropic Gaussian. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 show that adding the proposed loss improves downstream performance across several frameworks (MoCo, BarlowTwins, etc.) and mitigates dimensional collapse.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, with a clear motivation and intuitive justification/visualization for the five criteria.  
- The criteria are logically and mathematically sensible, and the analysis convincingly shows how the previous metric (Wang & Isola, 2020) fails to meet most of them.  
- Extensive empirical analysis demonstrates the proposed metric’s effectiveness across a variety of self-supervised learning frameworks (MoCo, BarlowTwins, BYOL, SimSiam, etc.), suggesting robustness when incorporated into different models.  
- One reviewer was notably more positive, praising the paper’s clarity, theoretical grounding, and the combination of empirical evidence. Other reviewers were more critical (see Weaknesses) but acknowledged the good motivation and the plausibility of the approach.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited datasets and generality:** Experiments are restricted to CIFAR-10/100. It is unclear whether the observed improvements generalize to other complexities and modalities (e.g., ImageNet, NLP, time series) or whether dimensional collapse is a fundamental issue versus an artifact of these datasets. No experiments on datasets where dimensional collapse is known to be particularly pronounced.  
- **Sensitivity to representation dimension not explored:** The paper does not vary the representation dimension (e.g., doubling or tripling it). The proposed loss penalizes constant dimensions, which could make the dimension a critical hyperparameter. Robustness across different representation sizes and interaction with the data’s inherent dimensionality remain unexamined.  
- **Unexpected accuracy drops:** On CIFAR-10, the proposed loss increases top-1 accuracy but decreases top-5 accuracy for MoCo and BarlowTwins. No explanation is provided.  
- **Small improvement on zero-CL:** The performance gain on the zero-CL framework is relatively small; justification is needed.  
- **Necessary but not sufficient criteria:** The five properties are not assessed for sufficiency – there is no proof or guarantee that satisfying them yields an ideal uniformity metric.  
- **Gaussian assumption unexamined:** The new metric assumes representations follow a Gaussian distribution. This assumption is particularly concerning because representation collapse (the very problem being addressed) can easily lead to non-Gaussian distributions. The authors provide no justification or robustness check for this assumption.  
- **Lack of comparison against the original uniformity loss:** The paper only compares the proposed metric to the Wang & Isola metric in the context of collapse analysis (e.g., singular values), but does not show that using the proposed loss outperforms using the original uniformity loss when integrated into existing frameworks. Therefore, it is not convincing that the new loss is superior to the existing one for downstream performance.  
- **Clarity and justification gaps:**  
  - The abbreviations ICC, FCC, and FBC need more elaboration to clarify the difference from previous metrics.  
  - The connection between the five properties and dimensional collapse, and why the original metric fails them, should be explained more explicitly.  
  - The reason why dimensional collapse is undesirable in self-supervised learning requires clearer justification.  
  - The relationship between the W₂ metric and existing methods such as Barlow Twins and VICReg (which also involve covariance matrices) is not discussed.  
  - KL divergence is not presented in Figure 3 (which shows singular values) – why was this comparison omitted?  
  - Figure 8 shows collapsed singular values of the representation despite findings in [2] that with a projector, the representation space does not collapse – this contradiction is not resolved.  
  - Font sizes in figures are too small.  

- **Reviewer disagreement in confidence and emphasis:** One highly confident reviewer (R3) was particularly critical of the clarity and experimental gaps, while another reviewer (R1, higher initial enthusiasm) still noted the dataset and dimension limitations.
