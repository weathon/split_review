## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes COND P-DIFF, a method to generate LoRA parameters for fine-tuning using a conditional latent diffusion model. An autoencoder reduces LoRA parameter dimensionality, and a diffusion model conditioned on task descriptions (text for NLP, image features for vision) synthesizes new parameters. The method is evaluated on GLUE tasks, style transfer (SemArt, WikiArt), and other datasets. Reviewers disagree strongly: one rates the work very poorly (score 1) citing incompleteness and limited novelty, while two rate it highly (score 5) praising originality and practical value; two rate it 3. All note missing figures and incomplete presentation.

### Strengths
- The idea of using conditional diffusion models to synthesize LoRA parameters for fine-tuning is novel, bridging parameter-efficient tuning and conditional parameter generation (first paper to do so, to the best of our knowledge).
- The method goes beyond shallow architectures and presents results with widely-used pretrained networks (BERT, RoBERTa, GPT-2, ResNet), making it practically compelling.
- The overall methodology is simple and the components (autoencoder, latent diffusion) are well-studied in the literature.
- Experiments span two modalities (language understanding and image stylization), demonstrating cross-domain applicability.
- The presented qualitative results are on par or slightly better than baselines.
- An ablation study explores the impact of training dataset size, normalization techniques, and LoRA layers on performance.
- The paper gathers a dataset of LoRA weights tailored to specific models, used to train the latent diffusion model.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing figures:** Several key figures (Section 4.4 Analysis, result comparisons, t-SNE projections, appendix) are referenced but absent, severely limiting evaluation of quality and generalization claims. Multiple reviewers note this.
- **Limited novelty:** The approach builds heavily on P-Diff and adds only a task conditioning mechanism; the core contribution is incremental.
- **Editorial errors:** Numerous formatting issues (missing spaces lines 061, 222, 292; incorrect quotation marks in Table 1; wrong formatting of eq. 6b, 7; grammatical errors L270-271; “xw” typo at L420; figure citation mistakes; inconsistent “Fig”/“Figure” usage).
- **Dataset concerns:** 
  * The dataset is small (150 samples per task × 6 tasks = 900 total), risking memorization rather than generation. The diffusion process may only add Gaussian noise to training data, and averaging synthesized weights could mask this.
  * Missing dataset citations and full descriptions for GLUE, SemArt, WikiArt, making reproduction difficult.
- **Generalization claim overstated:** The claim is demonstrated using images that were used to collect the parameter training dataset, not new unseen images. Generalization to out-of-distribution tasks (e.g., a task not in the 6 NLP tasks) is not evaluated.
- **Missing baselines:** A useful baseline—randomly selecting one training checkpoint LoRA and testing on a different task (e.g., using a style transfer LoRA for classification)—is absent. Performance drop would be expected but not shown.
- **Ensemble/use of multiple parameters:** The paper generates 20 LoRA parameters and selects the best, but other ensemble uses (e.g., averaging all, using diversity for robustness) are not explored, despite the motivation from parameter-space generation for ensembles (Wang et al., 2024).
- **Autoencoder complexity:** Training the autoencoder adds pipeline complexity. Efficiency gains or justification over simpler dimensionality reduction are not provided. Also, the autoencoder training pipeline (end-to-end or separate, training time) is unclear.
- **Limited image tasks:** For the vision domain, only image stylization is studied; more practical tasks like image classification (which may require more conditioning samples) are omitted.
- **No time/performance trade-off:** Comparison between LoRA fine-tuning time and COND P-DIFF training+generation time is missing, which would strengthen the practical argument.
- **Memory cost:** Flattening low-rank weight matrices for the dataset can become memory-expensive for very large parameter spaces.
- **Style transfer results are weak** (as noted by one reviewer).
- **Conditioning details unclear:** Explanations of how conditioning works for both NLP and image tasks are missing; the effect of the number of in-context examples in NLP is not ablated.
- **Missing details:** 
  * Autoencoder design is not fully elaborated; why Gaussian noise is included; whether VAE was tried.
  * The number of generated parameters (20) is not ablated for latency or quality.
  * The role of LoRA scale during inference and behavior with COND P-DIFF parameters is not discussed.
  * A more robust representation comparison (e.g., CKA) than L2 distance is suggested but not used.
- **Presentation issues:** Related work is split between main paper and supplementary; figures cited before their location.

### Reviewer Disagreement
One reviewer (score 1, confidence 5) judges the paper as very incomplete and lacking novelty, with poor presentation. Two reviewers (scores 5, confidence 3 and 4) find the approach original, well-defined, and significant for real-world applications. Two reviewers (scores 3) are neutral, noting interesting ideas but major weaknesses in missing figures, dataset size, and generalization evidence.
