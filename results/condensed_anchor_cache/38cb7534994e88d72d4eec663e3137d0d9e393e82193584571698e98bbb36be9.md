## Merged Review

### Summary
Two reviewers (scores 5) find the paper clearly defined, with numerous experiments and promising results for dimensionality reduction and intrinsic dimensionality estimation; they highlight the adaptive compression, unified framework, and interpretable latent ordering. Two reviewers (scores 3) are less positive, citing unclear novelty, insufficient theoretical support, missing comparisons, and confusing experimental presentation. All reviewers agree the topic is interesting and the method simple, but they raise overlapping concerns about algorithmic originality, theoretical depth, experimental rigor, and clarity.

### Strengths
- The research topic—learning high-quality compressed representations—is interesting and important in the deep learning community.
- IOB provides an adaptive data compression mechanism that dynamically adjusts bottleneck width at inference while ensuring likelihood maximization, capturing the most crucial information in the first latent variables.
- IOB unifies existing approaches (Nested Dropout, Triangular Dropout, PCAAE) into a single framework, and demonstrates broad applicability across diverse synthetic and real datasets.
- The method yields semantically meaningful latent ordering and allows intrinsic dimensionality estimation, adding interpretability to the learned latent space.
- The scope is clearly defined, and the method is well suited for dimensionality reduction, showing promising results for intrinsic dimensionality estimation.
- Numerous experiments on synthetic (S-curves, n-disk, dSprite) and real datasets (image and text embeddings) verify the effectiveness of the IOB layer.
- The method is simple and intuitive; the paper is overall easy to read.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and theoretical contribution**: The algorithmic novelty is unclear. The core formulation and training rely on Nested Dropout and unit‑sweeping, and the paper does not sufficiently articulate how combining these existing techniques yields a unique contribution beyond a straightforward application. Several reviewers note that simply masking top‑k elements of latent variables lacks analytic evidence for how compression avoids data distortion. The claim in the abstract of a “novel theory for estimating global intrinsic dimensionality” is not supported by any theoretical analysis. The method also depends on static hyperparameters (the weight model with only two options—geometric and linear—and the threshold \(k_{\text{max}}\)), which are set empirically, contradicting the paper’s own critique of needing manual dimension parameters.
- **Related work and baselines**: Key approaches on ordered dimension reduction are not discussed, e.g., [1] where a stochastic latent dimension and ordered sparsity are applied via a Bayesian prior; IOB appears to be a special case with fixed linear/geometric weighting. The paper does not compare with model compression methods such as Distillation, Quantization, or Pruning. In many experiments, the baseline PCA is too simple; more complex baselines like Kernel PCA and PCAAE are missing. In Fig. 2(c) and (d), PCA performs similarly to IOB. In Fig. 3, Separate Autoencoders are comparable to IOB—does this mean they can perform the same function of delivering ordered latent embeddings?
- **Experimental evaluation**: The organization into several sections is confusing, and there is a lack of numerical evaluation (particularly in Section 4), making it difficult to compare or assess performance. Many of the datasets used are toy (S-curves, n-disk, dSprite). There are only limited experiments demonstrating plugging IOB into different pre‑trained models, despite claiming such flexibility. In Fig. 2(a), the left and right panels appear identical.
- **Clarity and missing explanations**: The paper fails to explain well why the method delivers ordered latent variables; it lacks theoretical guidance and intuitive explanation behind heuristic experiment settings. Specific technical points are unclear: regarding Eq. 2, (i) whether \(b_i\) is shared by all \(f_{\theta}^i\) for \(i \ge k\), and (ii) whether all \(b_i\) are updated simultaneously. Additional open questions remain: could the hyperparameters (weights, \(k_{\text{max}}\)) be learned or adjusted automatically? Could the IOB layer be modified to provide a precise information‑theoretic measure?
