## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes ReAttention, a training-free approach to extend the context window of LLMs by performing a position-agnostic top-k attention before the ordinary position-aware self-attention. It splits tokens into initial, middle, and local parts, maintains initial and local KV caches, selects a subset of middle tokens using attention scores without positional embeddings (with a similar idea to token eviction methods like H2O and StreamingLLM), then recalibrates positional indices and computes self-attention. The method is motivated by the insight that attention scores without positional embeddings can identify salient tokens. Main claims: (1) ReAttention matches or outperforms traditional self-attention and other training-free methods (e.g., InfLLM) on long-context benchmarks (LongBench, L-Eval, InfiniteBench); (2) it extends context length of LLaMA3.2-3B-chat by 128× to 4M tokens, and LLaMA3.1-8B and Mistral-v0.3-7B to at least 1M tokens in Needle-In-A-Haystack tests; (3) it adds no computational overhead and uses less memory than regular self-attention (though only after ~100k context length). The method is optimized with custom Triton kernels to improve efficiency.

### Strengths

- The method is simple, intuitive, and training-free, making it practical for immediate deployment on pre-trained models.
- The problem is highly relevant and timely; the paper clearly articulates key conditions for infinite context extension.
- The empirical results are impressive: ReAttention shows comparable or better performance vs full attention across multiple benchmarks (LongBench, L-Eval, InfiniteBench) and multiple model architectures (LLaMA, Mistral, Qwen series).
- It successfully extends context length of LLaMA3.2-3B-chat by 128× to 4M tokens, and other models to at least 1M tokens without additional training.
- The evaluation is comprehensive, including detailed ablation studies on hyperparameters (Table 2) and thorough efficiency analysis with Triton optimization (Section 3.4).
- The insight that attention scores without positional embeddings can effectively identify salient tokens is interesting and, to one reviewer's knowledge, novel.
- The Triton kernel optimization reduces computational overhead and memory usage, enabling efficient extrapolation.
- The performance improvement is notable compared to InfLLM and full attention (Figure 2, Table 1).

### Weaknesses

*Reviewer opinions varied: one reviewer (score 8) was more positive, praising the method's simplicity and comprehensive evaluation, while noting missing related work. The other three reviewers (scores 5-6) raised more critical points regarding novelty, missing baselines, efficiency concerns, and lack of theoretical justification.*

- **Novelty concerns**: The top-K selection over the KV cache has been studied in H2O and FastGen. Several reviewers felt ReAttention is essentially a combination of StreamingLLM and H2O, with the only difference being that position embeddings are not applied during the lookup step. The paper does not clearly articulate what distinguishes its approach from these existing methods. More specific details on how the position-agnostic top-k selection interacts with the attention mechanism compared to position-aware selection in H2O are needed. One reviewer also noted that the Triton optimization is more of an engineering contribution.
- **Missing baselines**: Several important baselines are omitted in comparisons.
    - In long-context evals (e.g., InfiniteBench), StreamingLLM is absent; in LongBench, InfLLM is absent. The paper should include consistent baselines across all benchmarks.
    - For million-scale Needle-In-A-Haystack experiments, other long-context methods like InfLLM are not compared. Without these, it's hard to assess relative advantages at extreme context lengths.
    - The paper lacks comparisons to other KV-cache optimization methods such as SnapKV, PyramidKV, LongGen, InfiniPot, DuoAttention, and GemFilter. A more comprehensive set of baselines is needed to contextualize performance gains.
- **Efficiency and latency concerns**:
    - The two-round attention operation introduces non-trivial overhead, potentially resulting in latency similar to full attention. The first (position-agnostic) round still requires full computation.
    - Efficiency in long-sequence tasks remains a concern: the method requires sufficient memory resources where full attention is also feasible; the advantage over full attention lies mainly in evicting irrelevant tokens.
    - Only prefill time (time to first token) is compared; token throughput during the decoding phase is not reported. A comparison of decoding-phase efficiency is needed.
- **Memory usage tradeoff**: The claim that ReAttention uses less memory than self-attention is not consistent across all context lengths. Based on Figure 7, memory benefits only appear after ~100k context length. The paper should highlight this crossover point and provide a more detailed analysis.
- **Method description lacks detail**: The description is unclear on key aspects:
    - What does "adopts chunked streaming input during the prefilling stage" mean? How does prefill with ReAttention differ from decoding?
    - How is the middle-cache selection handled during long-sequence generation when query tokens change dynamically? Does it re-select middle caches periodically? (Reviewer 1's question that overlaps with a missing detail.)
    - The paper needs a precise description of the chunking mechanism during prefill and how recalled tokens are integrated into attention during both prefill and decoding. A pseudocode description would improve clarity.
- **"Infinite" context is misleading**: The method does not truly achieve infinite context; it is still limited by available memory. The paper should be more precise about what "infinite" means and acknowledge real-world constraints.
- **Missing explanation for performance gains**: There is no intuitive or formal explanation for why the method achieves good results beyond empirical evidence. Theoretical analysis or visualizations (e.g., showing how the top-k selection interacts with attention) are lacking.
- **Missing evaluation on structured tasks**: How does ReAttention perform on tasks requiring precise positional understanding, such as code completion or mathematical reasoning? (Reviewer 2's question.)
- **Minor issues**: Writing issues and typos (e.g., Llama vs LLaMA, wrong quotation in line 067). In Figures 3 and 4, axes and color bars are too small to be legible. The paper should clarify whether the FullAttn* implementation in Figure 7 is FlashAttention. The paper also needs to explain how ReAttention differs from InfLLM in more detail.
