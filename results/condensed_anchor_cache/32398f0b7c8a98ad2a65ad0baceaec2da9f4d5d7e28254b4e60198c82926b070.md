## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces H2DiLR, a framework that disentangles homogeneous and heterogeneous neural representations from intracranial recordings across multiple subjects for lexical tone decoding. Using a vector-quantized autoencoder with shared and private codebooks, the method first learns discrete neural features via reconstruction, then uses a frozen encoder with a temporal Transformer for 4-way tone classification. The authors collected a new sEEG dataset from multiple Mandarin-speaking participants reading 407 syllables (3 trials per syllable, ~1221 trials per subject, total ~20 hours). Experiments claim H2DiLR outperforms prior baselines by 12% in Top-1 accuracy. Three reviewers are more positive (scores 8, 8, 5), while one reviewer is negative (score 3), questioning whether the performance improvement arises from model design versus hyperparameter selection and noting insufficient validation. All reviewers agree on the novelty of the approach but raise concerns about reproducibility, missing ablations, and lack of neuroscience analyses.

### Strengths
- Novel H2DiLR approach that explicitly addresses data heterogeneity by disentangling shared (homogeneous) and subject-specific (heterogeneous) neural features.
- Clear motivation and good presentation; well-structured text and informative figures.
- Significant improvement in tone decoding accuracy: H2DiLR outperforms previous baseline methods by 12% in Top-1 accuracy (claim).
- The idea of separating homogeneous and heterogeneous representations is interesting and generalizable beyond tone decoding, e.g., to semantic neural decoding.
- The authors built a new sEEG dataset (promise to release upon acceptance), which lowers the entry barrier for future research in tonal language decoding.
- Ablation studies on partition ratio, number of subjects, and comparison with prior pre-training methods (BIOT, NeuroBERT) confirm the model design’s effectiveness.
- SSL-based pre-training demonstrates improved performance over supervised-only training, though one reviewer doubts whether this results from codebook hyperparameter tuning rather than the method itself.

### Weaknesses
**Reproducibility and Data**
- No code or dataset provided; without these, it is difficult to verify claims. The promise to release upon acceptance is noted but not yet fulfilled. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- Preprocessing details are missing: no description of referencing scheme (bipolar/Laplacian) or steps from raw 2kHz sEEG to 1kHz epoched trials. The absence of such steps could significantly impact signal quality and neuroscientific conclusions. (Reviewer 3)
- Normalization method unclear: whether each channel is normalized independently or across all channels. (Reviewer 3)

**Dataset and Task Scope**
- The claim that 407 syllables “cover nearly all Mandarin characters” needs clarification: a specific vocabulary list should be provided in rebuttal to support this. (Reviewer 1)
- The claim of being “the first” to work with 407 syllables is inaccurate: the 2023 study “A high-performance brain-to-sentence decoder for logosyllabic language” also uses a dataset with 407 syllables. The related work section lacks a detailed discussion and differentiation. (Reviewer 1)
- The tone decoding task itself is not fully introduced/defined in the methodology section, which is problematic given it is not a common task in brain decoding. (Reviewer 4)
- The study focuses only on tone decoding; a deeper exploration of disentangling homogeneous representations for semantic decoding or neural encoding models is missing. (Reviewer 2)
- An evaluation on other syllable classification tasks (e.g., initial syllable classification) would further highlight H2DiLR’s effectiveness. (Reviewer 3)

**Methodology and Validation**
- The evidence for disentangling homogeneity and heterogeneity is weak: it relies on feature visualization of specific samples only. A statistically robust analysis is needed. (Reviewer 1)
- The advantage of VQ code over the original signal is not clearly demonstrated via ablation studies. Additionally, the goal of disentangling different subjects (brain desynchronization) does not appear well-aligned with the current model architecture. (Reviewer 3)
- Performance improvement might be attributed to careful hyperparameter selection in the codebook rather than the model’s core idea. (Reviewer 3, minority negative view)
- Separate encoders for each subject significantly increase computational cost; this overhead should be addressed and optimized. (Reviewer 1)
- No data augmentation (temporal jittering, additive noise) is used to avoid overfitting, which is a common strategy in sEEG speech decoding. With only ~1221 trials per subject, it is unclear whether the improvement comes from pre-training or the codebook design. (Reviewer 3)
- In the cited baseline [1] (Du-IN), a CNN achieves ~40% and NAR model ~45.75% accuracy, all under a supervised heterogeneous paradigm; these moderate baselines make it hard to assess the true contribution of H2DiLR. (Reviewer 3)

**Missing Experiments and Analyses**
- No generalization analysis of the shared codebook: how well does a codebook learned from three subjects generalize to a held-out fourth subject? Do any three subjects produce a similar shared codebook? The dataset contains four subjects, which is sufficient for such leave-one-subject-out experiments. (Reviewer 4)
- No location-based ablation showing which brain regions are most important for tone decoding, nor analysis of region-specific representational homogeneity versus idiosyncrasy. sEEG coverage differences make this challenging but not impossible. (Reviewer 2, also questioned by reviewer 1)
- The preprocessing approach may bias any neuroscience results derived from the model. (Reviewer 3)
- Missing performance comparison on non-invasive EEG recordings; the applicability to EEG is an open question. (Reviewer 4)
- No exploration of whether the method benefits neural encoding models that predict neural responses from feature input. (Reviewer 2)

**Presentation and Minor Issues**
- Figure 1 may mislead readers into thinking H2DiLR uses Product Quantization, whereas the actual method quantizes each embedding item with different codebooks. (Reviewer 3)
- Typographical errors: Line 155 “LaBraM Anonymous (2024)”; Line 841 “the learning of 5e-5” should be “the learning rate of 5e-5”. Clarify if learning rate is the only training difference between pre-trained and non-pretrained baselines. (Reviewer 3)
- Citation style should use `\citet` or `\citep` instead of `\cite`. (Reviewer 3)
- Unclear method aspects: how and why private and shared codebooks are defined and utilized needs a more comprehensive rationale. (Reviewer 1) Why codebook size and dimension are set the same. (Reviewer 4) What the codebook size is for the heterogeneous H2DiLR variant (ν=0), and whether signal length differs between pre-training and downstream tasks. (Reviewer 3)
- Total duration of the sEEG dataset is requested but not provided. (Reviewer 1)
