## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces visual trace prompting (TraceVLA), which overlays historical state-action trajectories as visual traces on the current RGB image to enhance spatial-temporal awareness in VLA models. The method uses CoTracker to generate traces from selected active points and is applied to fine-tune OpenVLA on a curated 150K trajectory dataset. Evaluations in SimplerEnv (137 configurations) and 4 real-robot tasks show improvements over OpenVLA (10% and 3.5x respectively) and robust generalization. A compact 4B Phi-3-Vision model rivals 7B OpenVLA with better inference efficiency. The approach is neat and effective, but multiple reviewers note limitations in generalization assessment, spatial reasoning validation, and missing experimental details.

### Strengths
- Novel application of visual traces to encode historical state-action information for VLA models, enhancing spatial-temporal reasoning in manipulation tasks (reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- Clear method description with comprehensive ablations on prompt settings, trace length, and memory/speed analysis (reviewer 1).
- Curated dataset of 150K manipulation trajectories; fine-tuned 7B and 4B VLA models achieve state-of-the-art performance (reviewer 2).
- Rigorous validation across diverse simulated and real-world tasks, with real-robot experiments confirming real-world applicability (reviewers 1,2,3).
- Visual traces avoid redundancy compared to appending historical image observations, as shown in ablation studies (reviewer 3).
- Real-time inference optimization considered (reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Spatial reasoning validation missing**: Reviewer 2 notes no explicit results demonstrating spatial reasoning capabilities. A potential baseline would be connecting a VLM fine-tuned for line-drawing to low-level policy (e.g., RoboPoint framework). (reviewer 2)
- **Generalization scope limited**: Reviewer 3 points out that real-world generalization assessment only includes one task; more novel backgrounds and tasks are needed. Reviewer 2 suggests testing on simulation benchmarks like Colosseum (robustness), and reviewer 1 asks about generalization under camera orientation changes (2D tracking limits, degree of variation). (reviewers 1,2,3)
- **Comparison with smaller model unclear**: Reviewer 3 observes that RT1-X (much fewer parameters) outperforms TraceVLA-Phi3 (3B) in SimplerEnv, and the 7B advantage is not substantial. RT1-X should be included in real-robot experiments for generalization comparison. (reviewer 3)
- **Active point selection and threshold**: Reviewer 4 asks how the threshold for selecting active points is determined, if it requires tuning per robot or dataset, and whether a single value is universally applicable or an adaptive approach is needed. Reviewer 1 queries if κ is chosen so that M=5 takes a reasonable ratio of active points, and whether this selection generalizes across visually different robots. (reviewers 1,4)
- **Trace length vs. dual input contradiction**: Reviewer 4 points out a contradiction: the paper states longer traces can obscure key information, yet claims dual input (original + overlay) mitigates this. Clarification needed on how dual input truly prevents information loss from visual clutter. (reviewer 4)
- **Lack of ablation on history frames for image input**: Reviewer 4 notes that when finetuning OpenVLA with raw history images, the optimal number of frames (e.g., 3 vs. 6) is not ablated; redundancy across frames needs exploration. (reviewer 4)
- **Failure cases from tracking**: Reviewer 3 asks about failure cases due to inaccurate CoTracker tracking and robustness. Also, active points may be selected from moving background objects – method performance under such cases is unclear. (reviewer 3)
- **Reproducibility and experiment details missing**: Reviewer 1 notes lack of success definition for real-robot tasks, limits on noise parameters in environment variations, and absence of case studies showing advantage of visual trace prompting. Reviewer 3 asks how many fine-tuning iterations were used and whether pre-training data was included. Reviewer 4 asks if the fine-tuning dataset contains scenes similar to SimplerEnv (i.e., are testing scenes unseen). (reviewers 1,3,4)
- **Synthetic trace generation not explored**: Reviewer 2 suggests generating traces in simulation (e.g., RLBench) to reduce variability from lighting and ease evaluation with ground truth; relying solely on real-world data may introduce environmental factors. (reviewer 2)
- **Prompt visual properties**: Reviewer 1 questions whether color, thickness, or transparency of visual traces affect performance. (reviewer 1)
- **Edge cases in action movement**: Reviewer 1 asks about performance drop when actions move into/out of camera frame or rotate. (reviewer 1)

*Minority contrast*: Two reviewers (scores 6) are less positive, raising substantial concerns about limited generalization evaluation, unclear advantage over smaller baselines (RT1-X), lack of spatial reasoning validation, and contradictory explanation on trace length. Two reviewers (scores 8) are more positive, emphasizing novelty, comprehensive evaluation, and practical improvements.
