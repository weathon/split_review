## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes selective recalibration, which jointly trains a selection function and a recalibration model (e.g., Platt scaling, temperature scaling) to minimize calibration error on the accepted subset of data. It builds on prior work by Fisch et al. (2022) that only optimized the selection part given a fixed model, and introduces a simplified loss (S-TLBCE). The method is evaluated on medical imaging and zero-shot classification tasks. A theoretical analysis for a synthetic domain motivates the joint approach.

One reviewer (R2, score 8, confidence 5) is highly positive and confident, stating the work is well-written, well-motivated, and provides useful empirical and theoretical justification for joint training. The other three reviewers are moderately critical (R1: 3, R3: 5, R4: 5) and raise concerns about limited novelty, missing baselines, insufficient experimental rigor, and lack of significance testing. R1 and R3 have lower confidence (3 and 2); R4 is confident (4).

### Strengths

- The proposed approach is sensible and the joint optimization of selection and recalibration is a natural and well-motivated extension of prior work (R1, R2, R4).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with adequate detail for most experiments and theory (R2, R4).
- The simplified loss S-TLBCE appears to work better in practice than alternatives, and the empirical and theoretical comparisons of joint vs. sequential training are useful (R2).
- The idea of combining selective prediction with post-hoc recalibration is important for safety-aware tasks, and the work includes theoretical results for a simple data generation model (R3).
- The objective S-TLBCE (Equation 8) is new in this context and is supported by a theoretical analysis showing joint optimization can outperform separate optimization (R4).
- The problem of selective calibration is clearly described and the experiments are designed appropriately to evaluate the method (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty**: The approach is a conceptually straightforward extension of Fisch et al. (2022) and Geifman & El-Yaniv (2019). S-TLBCE does not consistently outperform the jointly optimized version of S-MMCE from Fisch et al. (2022); it performs worse on ImageNet and only shows advantage on CIFAR-100-C OOD tests (R1, R3, R4).
- **Missing baseline: more complex calibrators**: The paper argues that simple calibrators (e.g., Platt/temperature scaling) struggle to fit the entire data, but a more direct solution is to use a more expressive calibrator (e.g., MLP with non-linearities). This baseline is not compared (R1, R2).
- **Weak sequential baseline**: The sequential baseline only uses one epoch of calibration before training the selection model; recalibrating after selection would trivially improve performance, and iterative recalibration would resemble joint optimization. A more thorough investigation is needed (R1).
- **No significance testing or confidence intervals**: Statistics for the reported improvements are absent, making it hard to assess robustness and practical reliability (R1).
- **Accuracy degradation with decreased coverage**: In Section C.2, classification accuracy drops as coverage decreases under the proposed method, raising concerns that the selection mechanism does not reliably identify high-accuracy instances (R1).
- **Lack of nuanced calibration objectives**: The work only evaluates calibration via ECE-1/ECE-2. Not all calibration errors are equally important (e.g., rejecting overconfident wrong predictions should be preferred over rejecting underconfident correct ones). Considering objectives like Decision Calibration (Zhao et al., 2021) could increase impact (R2).
- **Data requirement ambiguity**: It is unclear whether the method can produce reliable rejections when labeled examples from the target domain are unavailable, and whether recalibration on the training domain alone helps (R2).
- **Confusing Figure 1**: The figure is difficult to interpret; it is not clear what subsets the blue/green curves correspond to or what coverage level is used. A selective-only approach is expected to reject mid-confidence examples more, but the figure does not convey this clearly (R2, R4).
- **Table 1 inconsistency**: The caption claims to report AUC over coverage levels, but the table only shows ECE-1 and ECE-2 (R4).
- **Missing analysis of confidence-based rejection with recalibration**: The naive confidence-based rejection strategy works well on ImageNet/Camelyon17. The authors say it fails in OOD settings, but it is not tested with recalibration applied in sequence (R4).
- **Insufficient empirical evaluation**: Common calibration benchmarks (SVHN, CIFAR-10) are omitted. Details about dataset splits, model architectures, and training/validation division are lacking (R3).
- **Writing/organization issues**: Notations are often confusing or undefined before first use (e.g., \(\tilde f\) in Section B.2.1, lost equations, missing equation numbers). Typos: “correctly classifier”, “outputs, We”, Section B.4.2 does not mention validation data (R1, R3).
- **Definition of R-ECE is questionable**: Linearly rescaling predicted probabilities by \(T\) is not standard temperature scaling, and the ECE calculations in the appendix are difficult to follow due to poor spacing and presentation (R4).
