## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper addresses embedding collapse when jointly training an embedding reconstruction module and a latent diffusion model for categorical data. The authors propose CATDM, which combines a consistency-matching (CM) regularizer, a shifted cosine noise schedule, and a random dropping strategy. They claim that CATDM mitigates embedding collapse and achieves superior performance on FFHQ, LSUN Churches, LSUN Bedrooms, and ImageNet 256×256 (FID 6.81 with 50 steps). The method is also applied to text generation (character-level text8) and machine translation (WMT14 EN-DE, IWSLT14 DE-EN). Reviewers question the theoretical correctness of a key proposition, the necessity of the proposed objectives over a simpler cross-entropy loss, the appropriateness of comparing mainly on quantized continuous data (images) while claiming to target categorical data, the use of outdated baselines, missing comparisons with recent diffusion and non-autoregressive models, and the overall performance relative to state-of-the-art.

### Strengths

- The issue of embedding collapse in diffusion models for categorical data is interesting and important.
- The authors provide an intuitive understanding of the collapse problem, supported by experimental results (e.g., Figure 1 analysis).
- Applying a consistency loss after softmax (CM regularizer) is a novel idea, and the argument in Section 4.3 behind it is reasonable.
- CATDM is demonstrated on both image and text modalities, covering multiple data types.
- The paper includes theoretical support (Theorem 1 and Proposition 2) toward optimality, albeit for idealized cases.
- A re-weighting of the objectives combined with an improved noise schedule and random dropping leads to improved results.
- The algorithm is tested across multiple settings (image, text, translation) showing comparatively good performance against the selected baselines.
- The paper is clearly written and well organized (though notation for sequence positions could be improved).

### Weaknesses

1. **Theoretical gap in Proposition 2 / Theorem 1.**  
   Reviewer 1 argues that the conclusion in line 922 (appendix) is incorrect: if at perturbed $z_t$ the model predicts a one-hot encoding for token $x_i$ at position $i$, this prediction will be wrong for other sequences $y$ that also produce $z_t$ and differ only at position $i$. The true optimal model is $p_{0|t}(x^i|z_t)$, and $L_{\text{CE}} = \sum_i \mathbb{E}_{p(z_t)} H(p_{0|t}(x_i|z_t)) > 0$. This threatens the proof of Theorem 1. The authors are asked to discuss this issue.

2. **Unclear necessity of the proposed objectives vs. pure denoising cross-entropy.**  
   Reviewer 1 notes that if Proposition 2 is correct, the other direction also holds: perfect denoising cross-entropy loss ($L_{\text{CE}}$) would imply perfect reconstruction at $t=0$ and consistent predictions, thus mitigating embedding collapse. It is puzzling why one should use the more convoluted $L_{\text{CM}} + L_0$ instead of $L_{\text{CE}}$ alone (as in Dieleman et al. 2022). Furthermore, $L_{\text{DM}}$ (score matching) is theoretically unnecessary according to Theorem 1 and its $L_2$ loss may reintroduce embedding collapse. **Missing an ablation:** the paper should include a row in Table 1 using only $L_{\text{CM}} + L_0$, and compare against a baseline with only denoising cross-entropy loss.

3. **Mismatch between premise (categorical data) and experimental focus.**  
   The method is mostly applied to quantized continuous data (images) rather than discrete categorical data (text, proteins). Experiments on text are limited to character-level text8 (underperforming categorical diffusion models) and machine translation. For translation, results on WMT14 EN-DE (14.37 BLEU) are lower than reported in the original SeqDiffuSeq paper (19.76 SacreBLEU). **Missing comparisons:** no results for SEDD (Lou et al., 2023) on machine translation; no comparison with Dieleman et al. 2022 on text data. The authors should clarify the BLEU type (tokenized vs. SacreBLEU), decoding parameters (beam size, minimum Bayes risk), and how parameters were chosen for baselines.

4. **Outdated baselines and missing recent comparisons.**  
   Many baselines are several years old (Table 3 baselines at least three years old). Recent continuous diffusion models achieve FIDs under 2 on ImageNet 256×256, whereas CATDM achieves 6.81. Comparisons with LDM, DiT, and other modern models are missing for image generation. For translation, recent diffusion-based models (e.g., DINOISER with 24.62 SacreBLEU on WMT14 EN-DE and 31.44 on IWSLT14 DE-EN without knowledge distillation) are not included. The claim of “best results among non-autoregressive models” is inaccurate given models like CMLM and others.

5. **Weak overall performance relative to state-of-the-art.**  
   Across tasks, CATDM’s performance is often outperformed by existing baselines. Image generation FID (6.81 on ImageNet) lags far behind recent models. Text generation (text8) underperforms categorical diffusion models. Machine translation results are lower than those reported in the original papers for several baselines, raising questions about fairness of comparison.

6. **Unclear motivation for improving non-autoregressive methods.**  
   The paper does not justify why researchers should pursue improvements in non-autoregressive generation (given its weak results) instead of switching to autoregressive models, which typically achieve better performance.

7. **Insufficient analysis of Figure 1 and the embedding collapse phenomenon.**  
   - Reviewer 3 finds the analysis unclear. For CSDM, the VQGAN embedding variance is small, but small variance does not imply meaningless embeddings – semantic meaning could reside in a small region.  
   - If using a KL-regularized autoencoder (which has high variance), reconstruction is poor. Why is high variance important?  
   - The statement “cross-entropy loss for CSDM is low around t=0 but increases rapidly as t arises” is presented as a problem, but it could be mitigated by an appropriate noise schedule (e.g., cosine). Visualization of the generative process would help.

8. **Poor writing and presentation quality.**  
   - The method section is poorly structured; domain-specific concepts (PFODE, variational diffusion formulation, FFHQ) are introduced without proper explanation of their relevance to the paper’s contribution.  
   - Many claims are presented as personal insights or intuition without supporting literature or explicit experimental evidence (e.g., lines 35, 247, 300).  
   - The paper lacks a general overview showing how the different subsections connect.  
   - The notation for “element i” in line 124 is confusing (position in sequence vs. dimension of vector).  
   - Line 108 should read “backward” process.  
   - The expression in line 240 does not match the continuous-time consistency model of Song et al. (2023); their loss only uses neighbor timesteps, not non-neighbor times.  
   - Typos exist around lines 457, 478, and 933.  
   - The presentation of DE-EN and EN-DE translation results could be clearer to avoid reader confusion about directionality.

9. **Request for implementation details and ablation.**  
   - How is $L_{\text{CM}}$ implemented in practice? What is the training speed? A detailed algorithm would be helpful.  
   - The authors are asked to provide a row in Table 1 with only $L_{\text{CM}} + L_0$ (without $L_{\text{DM}}$).  
   - Specify the source of performance data for CATDM (which table/section) and for baselines, to allow verification.
