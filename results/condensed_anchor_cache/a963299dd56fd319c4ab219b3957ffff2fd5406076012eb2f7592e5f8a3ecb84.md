## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces TDDBench, a benchmark for Training Data Detection (TDD) covering 13 datasets across image, tabular, and text modalities, benchmarking 21 methods across four detection paradigms (including large models like Pythia-12B). Reviewers generally agree the benchmark is comprehensive and the problem is important. However, they identify significant limitations: insufficient in-depth analysis of results (e.g., unexplained high performance on CIFAR-100 in Table 4, CatBoost outperformance in Table 5), unclear or inconsistent explanations (overfitting in Figure 2, residual connections in Figure 3(a), reference model architecture claim in Table 6), missing protocol explanations (training-test protocol for reference models, query model operation), and omission of Transformer-based image models and LLaMa. Additional concerns include: the benchmark not being truly large-scale (only regular-sized datasets), limited novelty over existing benchmarks, a controversial claim about computational expense trade-off (Figure 2(a) shows learning-based methods achieve best performance with less computation), lack of discussion on TDD generalizability across training methods (supervised, self-supervised, unsupervised), absence of defense analysis, and limited future directions. One reviewer (Reviewer 2) was more critical than others, questioning the scale, novelty, and the computational expense claim. Overall, the benchmark is seen as a valuable reference but lacking depth and coverage of modern architectures.

### Strengths

- Comprehensive evaluation spanning multiple data modalities (image, tabular, text) with 13 datasets, 21 TDD methods, and 4 detection paradigms, making it the largest TDD benchmark to date. Includes large language models like Pythia-12B. (Reviewers 1,3,4)
- Careful analysis of latest methods’ limitations and strengths, particularly the interesting correlation between train-test performance gap and TDD performance. (Reviewer 2)
- The TDD problem is very important for privacy, copyright, and unlearning verification. (Reviewer 4)
- The paper is well-written, decently formatted, and easy to understand. (Reviewers 3,4)

### Weaknesses

- Limited in-depth analysis of results. No explanation why CIFAR-100 performance is noticeably higher than other datasets (Table 4), or why CatBoost outperforms other tabular models (Table 5). (Reviewer 1)
- Several explanations are unclear or inconsistent:
  - Attributing high TDD performance to overfitting when train-test gap is large (Figure 2) is confusing because overfitting typically degrades test-set performance. (Reviewer 1)
  - Stating residual connections in ResNet alleviate memorization thus decreasing detection performance (Figure 3(a)) is unclear. (Reviewer 1)
  - Claiming detection performance declines when reference model has limited knowledge of target architecture, but for learn-top3 all reference models show similar performance without decline (Table 6); explanation appears inconsistent. (Reviewer 1)
- Missing necessary explanations of TDD protocols, such as the training-test protocol when using a reference model or how the query model operates. (Reviewer 1)
- Evaluation omits Transformer-based models, which are widely used for image tasks; for LLMs, evaluating LLaMa (most widely used public LLM) would be more relevant than Pythia for real-world TDD scenarios. (Reviewer 1)
- The benchmark is not truly large-scale as it only considers regular-sized datasets; also missing evaluation on the size of data points under TDD. (Reviewer 2) *Minority reviewer more negative: Reviewer 2 found the benchmark less novel and the scale insufficient.*
- The benchmark is not significantly different from existing ones except for covering multiple modalities; following existing data split rules limits novelty. (Reviewer 2) Discussion of differences with existing benchmarks is also missing. (Reviewer 4)
- The claim that TDD outperformance comes at a cost of computational expense is controversial because Figure 2(a) shows learning-based methods achieve best performance with significantly less computation than model-based ones. (Reviewer 2)
- Lacks discussion about TDD generalizability across different training methods (supervised, self-supervised, unsupervised). (Reviewer 3) Additionally, if a model is initialized with ImageNet pretrain weights and then fine-tuned on dataset A, it is unclear whether an ImageNet example should be considered a member or non-member. (Reviewer 3, question)
- Does not include discussion or experimental results on defenses against TDD. (Reviewer 4)
- Takeaway messages and future directions are limited; the paper could better elaborate on limitations of detection algorithms and possible improvements. (Reviewer 4) One reviewer suggests the authors could propose a novel method based on their benchmark analysis, indicating a gap in contributions beyond evaluation. (Reviewer 3)
