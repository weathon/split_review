## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes an uncertainty-aware fine-tuning (UA-CLM) loss for causal LLMs, designed to increase token entropy on incorrectly predicted tokens and decrease entropy on correctly predicted tokens, grounded in decision theory. Experiments on CoQA, TriviaQA, OK-VQA (short-form QA and VQA), and BioASK (OOD) evaluate hallucination detection (AUROC), selective generation (AUARC), OOD detection, and calibration, using Llama-2, Gemma, and LLaVA. Compared to standard CLM fine-tuning, UA-CLM improves several uncertainty metrics while maintaining QA accuracy. However, all reviewers note significant limitations: the evaluation is restricted to short-form, single-task settings; no comparisons with alternative baselines or related work are made; key training details (definition of incorrect tokens, loss weighting, hyperparameters) are unclear; and critical analyses (latency, longer-form performance, generalization to other capabilities) are missing. The proposed loss function is simple but lacks thorough validation beyond the narrow experimental scope.

### Strengths
- **Well-written and easy to follow** (Reviewers 1, 5).
- **Proposed loss is straightforward and simple to implement** (Reviewer 2).
- **Grounded in decision theory; loss can theoretically converge to zero** (Reviewer 4).
- **Numerous experiments across multiple datasets (CoQA, TriviaQA, OK-VQA), models (Llama, Gemma, LLaVA), and uncertainty estimators (token entropy, perplexity, predictive entropy, semantic entropy)** (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Improves hallucination detection, selective generation, and OOD detection metrics compared to standard CLM fine-tuning** (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **No degradation in generation quality/accuracy** (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Shows generality to vision-language models** (Reviewer 2).
- **Meaningful to study uncertainty on the model’s own responses** (Reviewer 1).
- **Metrics chosen are comprehensive and gaps between UA-CLM and CLM are notable** (Reviewer 3).
- **Thorough literature review** (Reviewer 5; note: Reviewer 3 disagrees—see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental scope**:
  - Only single-task fine-tuning with sufficient data; no low-resource or multi-task scenarios (Reviewer 1).
  - All experiments are short-form QA; longer-form generation is not evaluated despite the paper’s stated goal of “open-ended and free-form natural language generation” (Reviewers 2, 3). Reviewer 2 explicitly flags that uncertainty from paraphrases in longer settings could degrade performance.
  - Missing evaluations on reasoning, creative writing, and coding capabilities to verify no collateral damage (Reviewer 3).
- **No comparisons with alternative baselines or related work**:
  - Only standard CLM fine-tuning as baseline; no comparison with pre-fine-tuning (vanilla) model (Reviewer 3).
  - No comparison with post-hoc rescaling methods, probes, unlikelihood training, or fine-tuning LMs to abstain/calibrated confidence statements (Reviewers 2, 4, 5). Specific missing references: [2], [4], [5], [6] in Reviewer 2; probes [2,3] in Reviewer 2.
  - Related work comparison needed for overlapping QA benchmarks (Reviewer 5).
- **Unclear technical details of proposed loss**:
  - **Definition of “incorrect token prediction”**: Not specified whether based on argmax or other criteria; also unclear which tokens are included in $\widetilde{C}$ and how many (Reviewers 1, 2).
  - **Loss weighting**: No hyperparameter balancing uncertainty loss vs. standard language modeling loss; lack of ablation on this leads to concerns about inadvertent accuracy improvements (Reviewer 4).
  - **Choice of tanh**: No motivation over other functions mapping to [0,1] (Reviewer 2).
  - **Token-level label assignment for longer answers**: For datasets like TriviaQA, unclear how raw dataset answers are adapted for LM-style fine-tuning, especially when models generate lengthy answers (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing evaluation and analysis**:
  - **Expected Calibration Error (ECE) not reported** (Reviewer 5). Reference [1] given.
  - **Latency/training time**: No analysis of computational overhead introduced by the proposed loss (Reviewers 1, 4). Reviewer 4 asks about training time or faster convergence.
  - **Evaluation metrics not defined**: How uncertainty is solicited from the model (e.g., computing H) and metrics should be self-contained (Reviewer 5).
  - **Out-of-domain detection**: BioASK dataset not verified to be truly out-of-domain for the pre-trained models; no discussion of potential contamination effects (Reviewer 3).
  - **Accuracy improvement unexplained**: In Table 2, UA-CLM sometimes achieves higher accuracy than CLM (except for Gemma), contradicting the claimed trade-off and using brittle ROUGE-L (score > 0.3) metric (Reviewer 4).
  - **No analysis of the loss’s effect on longer texts**: Uncertainty can arise from semantic equivalence of paraphrases; the loss treats all incorrect predictions identically, potentially harming longer-form quality (Reviewer 2).
  - **No ablation studies on the loss hyperparameter or weighting** (Reviewer 4).
- **Literature review concerns**:
  - Multiple unsupported or inaccurate claims: e.g., confabulation as subcategory of hallucination without proper definition, assertion about LLM calibration contrasted with larger models’ improved calibration (Ref: 2207.05221), SFT exacerbating hallucinations via overfitting without acknowledging data conflicts (Reviewer 3).
  - Missing citations for calibration in long-form generation (e.g., arxiv 2310.19208, 2404.00474) (Reviewer 3). Note: one reviewer (5) called the review thorough, indicating a need for clarification.
- **Contrast in reviewer assessments**: While most reviewers find the paper clearly written and the proposed loss interesting, the experimental validation is considered insufficient by multiple reviewers, with scores of 3, 5, 5 vs. 6, 6. The primary critiques center on lack of baselines, narrow task scope, and missing technical details.
