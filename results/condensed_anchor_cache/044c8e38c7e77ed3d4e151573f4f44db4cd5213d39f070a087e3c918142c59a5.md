## Merged Review

### Summary

KV-Distill trains a token importance scorer (FFN) and LoRA adapters to compress KV caches by keeping only the top‑k tokens, using a KL divergence distillation objective between compressed and uncompressed next‑token distributions. Evaluated on SQuAD and three long‑context tasks, it claims near‑lossless compression (up to 1000×, 95% reduction) for models like Llama2, Llama3. The method outperforms training‑free baselines (e.g., H₂O) on some extractive tasks but shows inconsistent gains (e.g., GovReport). Reviewers disagreed sharply: Reviewer 2 (score 6, confidence 5) found it original and empirically strong; Reviewers 1 and 3 (scores 3, confidences 4 and 3) saw limited novelty, missing comparisons, and insufficient detail.

### Strengths

- Introducing distillation into KV‑cache compression improves effectiveness (Reviewer 1).
- First work to distill KV‑caches across general‑purpose tasks with flexible compression rates; originality noted (Reviewer 2).
- Strong experimental results: performs on‑par with or slightly above previous state‑of‑the‑art (H₂O, ICAE) and consistently well on a series of downstream tasks (Reviewer 2).
- Good coverage of related work (Reviewer 3).
- Method tested across multiple model families (Reviewer 3).
- Works favorably to baselines in most evaluated cases (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty (disagreement):** Reviewer 2 considers KV‑Distill the first general‑purpose KV‑cache distillation; Reviewers 1 and 3 note that context compression via distillation is not new (e.g., prompt compression, GIST tokens) and that token‑eviction through importance scoring closely resembles prior work like SnapKV, NACL, H₂O, and “Keep the Cost Down” – the paper fails to clearly differentiate from these (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Missing comparisons:** No comparison with SnapKV (Reviewer 1), no complete LongBench evaluation (Reviewer 1), no Needle‑In‑A‑Haystack test (Reviewer 1), no comparison with GIST tokens in their setting (Reviewer 3). NACL and the survey “Keep the Cost Down” are also not compared despite being closely related (Reviewer 2).
- **Unfair baseline selection:** Most compared methods are training‑free; the advantage may come from training rather than the distillation approach (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 adds that H₂O outperforms KV‑Distill on GovReport, and no explanation is given.
- **Missing instruction‑following experiments:** The abstract claims preserved instruction‑following ability, yet no related experiments appear in the results (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing training/inference costs:** No information on GPU memory cost, GPU time cost, total training parameters (Reviewers 1, 2). Inference speedup (e.g., tokens per second) is not reported, which is critical for a compression method (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of reproducibility details:** No algorithmic pipeline or pseudo‑code; insufficient description of how to train/deploy KV‑Distill for an off‑the‑shelf model (Reviewer 2). Method description is unclear, and many design choices are unexplained (Reviewer 3).
- **Inconsistent performance:** Why does H₂O outperform KV‑Distill on GovReport? (Reviewer 3). Why do Llama2 models degrade far more than Llama3? (Reviewer 3). Performance varies wildly with the α parameter of the KL divergence objective – could baselines be improved with similar tuning? (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient ablations:** Layer 6 was chosen for the scoring function without justification; can different token selections per layer improve performance? (Reviewer 3). Only Top‑p routing (similar to MoE) is tested; other pruning/routing strategies are not explored (Reviewer 2 question – not affecting overall rating, but a missing ablation). The authors mention an ablation on line 225 but do not report results (Reviewer 3 question).
- **Table design:** Table 2 should include a zero‑shot column similar to Table 3 to show the advantage brought by distillation (Reviewer 1).
- **Unaddressed limitations of prior work:** The paper lists limitations of prior approaches (lines 071‑076), but it is unclear how KV‑Distill actually addresses them (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear problem setting:** The use case (fixed reusable context vs. streaming, training‑allowed vs. training‑free) is not crisply described (Reviewer 3).
- **Routing of discarded tokens:** During training, why are discarded tokens routed separately if the scoring function is meant to gate them? (Reviewer 3 question).
