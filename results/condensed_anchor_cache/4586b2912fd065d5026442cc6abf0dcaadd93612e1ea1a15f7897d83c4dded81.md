## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces ADOPD, a large-scale dataset for document page decomposition supporting four tasks: Doc2Mask (entity segmentation), Doc2Box (text detection), Doc2Tag (tagging), and Doc2Seq (captioning). The dataset contains 120k high-resolution document images sourced from LAION, with dense annotations. A key methodological contribution is a data-driven document taxonomy discovery pipeline that uses a human-in-the-loop process integrating five pretrained models (ViT, CLIP, BLIP-2, OCR, image tagger) orchestrated by an LLM, combined with iterative k-means clustering and outlier detection. This pipeline ensures diversity and balance in the collected data. The paper provides quantitative analysis of the taxonomy distribution, and evaluates several baseline models on the four tasks.

### Strengths
1. **Large-scale, high-quality resource**: The dataset (120k images) is one of the largest and most comprehensive for document segmentation, detection, tagging, and captioning. It offers segment and caption annotations rare in existing document datasets. [Reviewers 1,2,4]
2. **Novel hybrid annotation pipeline**: The orchestration of multiple pretrained models (ViT, CLIP, BLIP-2, OCR, tagging model) within a human-in-the-loop iterative clustering is highly innovative and rarely attempted. This reduces human annotation cost while maintaining diversity and balance. [Reviewers 1,2,3] One reviewer emphasizes this as “very interesting and rarely presented.” [Reviewer 3]
3. **Data-driven taxonomy**: The systematic discovery of document taxonomy via machine learning and human feedback is a distinctive contribution. Qualitative and quantitative analysis confirms the taxonomy improves dataset balance. [Reviewers 1,2,4]
4. **Thorough evaluation of diversity**: The paper quantifies data distribution diversity, providing empirical evidence that the dataset is balanced and comprehensive. [Reviewer 2]
5. **Potential for future research**: The dataset is clearly positioned to fill a gap in document understanding, with potential applications in digital archiving and automated content extraction. [Reviewer 4 – the most positive reviewer (score 8) particularly emphasizes the significance and quality of the dataset.]

### Weaknesses
1. **Clarity and organization**: The paper is very hard to follow. It is overfilled with unnecessary commentary and lacks clear background setup. The term “Document Page Decomposition Dataset” is not explained until late in the introduction. This affects every section. [Reviewer 1, also noted by Reviewer 3 regarding reproducibility of section 4.1]
2. **Unclear contribution relative to LAION**: It is ambiguous which annotations come from existing LAION data (e.g., tags, captions) and which were manually labeled in this work. The description of Doc2Seq annotation is specifically confusing: the paper states 5,000 samples are manually annotated, but later uses 80k training, 20k validation, 20k test – it is not explained how the 5k manual annotations are used or why validation/test sets are larger. [Reviewers 1,2]
3. **Annotation quality not sufficiently validated**:
   - *Segmentation and box annotations*: These come from pretrained models (e.g., DINO) and are corrected by human annotators, but the correction procedure is not detailed. In comparison, DocBank box annotations extracted from PDF are likely more precise. [Reviewer 2]
   - *Caption annotations*: These are generated by BLIP-2 without human correction. BLIP-2 is trained for general visual-language tasks, not document-specific captioning, raising concerns about quality and usefulness. [Reviewer 2] One reviewer questions whether such model-annotated captions are “meaningless” for the field. [Reviewer 2 – explicit question merged here as overlap with criticism.]
4. **Insufficient evaluation of the dataset’s impact**:
   - The paper claims training on ADOPD confirms dataset effectiveness, but it does not compare models *with and without* fine-tuning on ADOPD. Showing absolute performance on ADOPD alone does not prove the dataset’s contribution. [Reviewer 3]
   - No cross-dataset evaluation is provided. Showing performance after pre-training on ADOPD vs. pre-training on ImageNet (or other datasets) on standard document benchmarks (e.g., DocBank, RVL-CDIP) would demonstrate generalization benefits. [Reviewer 3]
   - The caption evaluation metrics are insufficiently justified; the rationale for choosing them and their limitations for document captioning should be discussed. [Reviewer 3]
   - A deeper comparative analysis with existing datasets (e.g., DocBank) using task-specific metrics (mAP, F1) across document categories is lacking. [Reviewer 4 – more positive reviewer still identifies this weakness]
5. **Missing reproducibility details**:
   - Section 4.1 (image selection process) lacks specific thresholds used for filtering outputs from the various pretrained models, and exact criteria for final image selection. A step-by-step reproducible description is needed. [Reviewer 3]
   - Computational cost (GPU hours, memory, inference time per image and for the full dataset) is not reported. This is crucial for assessing feasibility for researchers with limited resources. [Reviewer 3]
6. **Human-in-the-loop methodology justification**: The choice of human-in-the-loop for outlier detection is novel but not adequately compared with purely automated methods. The paper should discuss trade-offs between human annotation cost and potential bias vs. automated error rates, and describe how annotator standardization was achieved. [Reviewer 4, supported by Reviewer 2’s concern about correction procedure.]
7. **Dataset composition detail**: A more detailed breakdown of document types, sources, geographic and linguistic diversity would help assess applicability. [Reviewer 4 – question, but also a missing detail]
8. **Open-source status**: Multiple reviewers ask whether the dataset will be released and how. [Reviewers 2,3] This is an open question, but also an implicit missing commitment.
