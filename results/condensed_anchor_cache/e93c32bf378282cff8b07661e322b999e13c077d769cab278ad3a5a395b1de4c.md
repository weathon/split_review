## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates natural, robust, and catastrophic overfitting from a unified perspective by focusing on a shared phenomenon called “over-memorization” – where a DNN suddenly becomes high-confidence on certain training patterns and retains persistent memory for them. The authors observe that when a model over-memorizes an adversarial pattern, it also shows high confidence on the corresponding natural pattern. Based on this, they propose a general framework, Distraction Over-Memorization (DOM), that explicitly prevents over-memorization by either removing or augmenting high-confidence natural patterns. Extensive experiments on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 with ResNet architectures demonstrate improved generalization across different training paradigms. The paper is well-structured and easy to follow, and the identification of over-memorization as a common thread linking different overfitting types is a novel contribution.

### Strengths
- **Unified view of overfitting:** The paper discovers that “over-memorization” is a shared behavior underlying natural, robust, and catastrophic overfitting, providing a new perspective for analyzing and mitigating overfitting.  
- **Clarity and structure:** The paper is generally well-written with a clear motivation and thorough background/review of related work.  
- **Robust experimental design:** The empirical evaluation covers several training paradigms, baselines, datasets (CIFAR-10/100), and network architectures (ResNet), with results reported with standard deviations.  
- **Significant improvements:** The proposed DOM framework demonstrates superior performance in reducing the generalization gap compared to baseline models.  
- **Novel insight into overfitting:** The identification of over-memorization as a unifying factor adds depth to our understanding of how DNNs lose generalization ability.  
- **Practical mitigation approach:** The paper shows that natural overfitting can be alleviated by removing “transformed” data (those with a rapid decrease in training loss), and this property extends to robust and catastrophic overfitting, enabling a universal mitigation method.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited scope of evaluation:** Experiments are restricted to ResNet architectures on CIFAR-10/100. The paper lacks results on larger-scale datasets (e.g., Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet) and diverse architectures (e.g., Transformers, other CNN backbones). This raises concerns about the generality and scalability of the observed over-memorization phenomenon and the effectiveness of DOM.  
- **Threshold sensitivity without ablation:** The loss threshold in DOM is fixed, but the optimal value likely varies across datasets, loss functions, and architectures. The paper does not provide a sensitivity analysis or a strategy for choosing the threshold, nor does it explore adaptive thresholding. This makes the method difficult to apply in practice.  
- **Missing comparisons with other overfitting mitigation methods:** The paper does not compare DOM against established regularization techniques (dropout, ensemble, label smoothing, weight decay) or against methods designed for individual overfitting types (natural, robust, catastrophic). Without such comparisons, it is unclear whether DOM offers advantages over existing approaches.  
- **Limited improvement on natural and robust overfitting:** According to Tables 2–4, the proposed strategies predominantly improve class overfitting (CO), with only marginal gains for natural overfitting (NO) and random overfitting (RO). The paper does not explore why this discrepancy occurs, limiting mechanistic understanding.  
- **Incomplete experimental details (reproducibility):**  
  - Section 3.1 does not specify the architecture or training procedure of the auxiliary model used to categorize original vs. transformed high-confidence patterns.  
  - Section 3.2 does not clearly state the criteria for grouping adversarial patterns based on their corresponding natural training loss, nor the specific loss threshold used.  
  - Table 4 contains many “0.00” values without explanation; the authors should clarify what these represent (e.g., whether they are negligible or exactly zero).  
- **Unresolved claim about AT and natural patterns:** The paper states “the AT-trained model never actually encounters natural patterns,” but methods like TRADES do. The authors should clarify whether the over-memorization phenomenon also occurs when natural patterns are present during AT, and provide experimental evidence or discussion.  
- **Novelty concerns relative to prior work (MLCAT [1]):** One reviewer noted that the analysis of “transformed data” was already introduced in MLCAT, which studied robust overfitting. Extending the same analysis to three overfitting types may be expected given the algorithmic similarity across settings, and the current analysis does not offer substantial new insight beyond that extension. The authors should better differentiate their contribution.  
- **Ambiguous terminology:** The term “pattern” is used loosely; it is unclear whether it refers to individual training samples, specific features, or something else. A rigorous definition is needed to understand how the method identifies and manipulates these patterns.  
- **Computational cost analysis missing:** The proposed method may involve repeated gradient computation and extra operations (identification of high-confidence patterns, iterative augmentation/removal). The paper does not quantify the overhead in terms of training time or memory compared to baseline training.  
- **Learning rate scheduling not explored:** All experiments use step decay; the paper does not investigate whether similar over-memorization phenomena occur with smoother schedules (e.g., cosine annealing).  
- **Iterative augmentation rationale unclear:** It is not explained why data augmentation is applied iteratively rather than in a straightforward, one-step manner in the DOM framework.  
- **One reviewer (score 8) was particularly positive, emphasizing the unifying discovery of over-memorization and the promise of DOM, while another reviewer (score 5) was more critical, questioning the novelty beyond MLCAT and demanding stronger comparisons with individual overfitting methods.**
