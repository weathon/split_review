## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces CtrlRegen, a watermark removal method that starts from clean Gaussian noise and uses a controllable diffusion model. Semantic and spatial control modules (semantic control adapter and spatial control network) guide denoising to preserve image content while removing watermarks. CtrlRegen+ adds adjustable noise steps to the latent representation before denoising, enabling a trade-off between watermark removal and image quality. Experiments across multiple watermarking techniques (including high-perturbation ones like StegaStamp and TreeRing) compare only against Regen and Rinse. Reviewers note the approach outperforms these baselines in visual consistency and removal performance. Four reviewers score 6, one reviewer scores 8 (minority, more positive) praising novelty and experimental capability but also raising concerns about ablation and image distortion.

### Strengths
- **Novel idea**: Starting regeneration from clean Gaussian noise is a novel approach, enabling removal of high-perturbation watermarks that prior methods (e.g., Regen) could not handle. (R1, R3, R4, R5)
- **Adjustable regeneration**: CtrlRegen+ allows varying noise steps to trade off watermark removal and image quality, considering different use cases and watermark strengths. (R1, R3)
- **Effective combination of controls**: Semantic control adapter (via cross-attention on watermarked image features) and spatial control network (ControlNet-like for edge features) guide denoising to maintain consistency. (R1, R5)
- **Strong empirical performance**: Outperforms Regen and Rinse on high-perturbation watermarks (e.g., StegaStamp, TreeRing) while maintaining better visual quality. Demonstrates quantitative results with multiple metrics: CLIP-FID, PSNR, Q-Align, LIQE. (R1, R3, R5)
- **No-box attack**: Requires only the watermarked image, no knowledge of the watermarking scheme. (R3)
- **Ablation confirms importance**: Ablation studies show both semantic and spatial controls are important for image consistency. (R3)
- **Clear presentation**: Paper is well-organized and easy to follow. (R1, R5)
- **Minority reviewer (R4, score 8)** finds the approach especially novel and the experiments effectively demonstrate capability across varying watermarking techniques.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited baselines**: Only Regen and Rinse are compared; missing other watermark removal methods like adversarial attacks, editing attacks, or general-purpose methods (e.g., Unmarker). (R2)
- **Missing CtrlRegen+ in Table 1**: Table 1 does not include CtrlRegen+ results; it is unclear how it compares to other methods across all watermarking techniques. (R2)
- **Figure 5 excludes base CtrlRegen**: Figure 5 shows results for CtrlRegen+ but not the base version; why is it omitted? (R2)
- **No theoretical analysis**: The paper lacks a theoretical explanation for why regeneration from clean noise removes watermarks (contrast Regen which provides theoretical justification). (R2, R3)
- **No analysis of why high-perturbation watermarks are harder**: The paper notes StegaStamp and TreeRing are more challenging but provides no in-depth analysis or experimental explanation. (R2)
- **Failure cases and limitations not discussed**: No examples of when the method fails or its inherent limitations are provided. (R1)
- **Insufficient qualitative comparisons**: Diverse image types (e.g., text, complex scenes) are not shown; visual comparisons are limited. (R1, R5)
- **Low PSNR values indicate significant alteration**: Reference-based metrics (PSNR) are low, suggesting the method alters images substantially; detailed analysis of the types of distortions introduced is needed. (R4)
- **Lacks module ablation**: The individual contributions of semantic control and spatial control are not disentangled—e.g., results with only semantic, only spatial, and both are missing. (R4; also R2 mentions insufficient analysis)
- **Watermark information may persist**: Through the image encoder and cross-attention, high-frequency watermark signals could be reintroduced into the output; the paper does not fully argue why this is unlikely. (R5)
- **Why not a unified attention mechanism?**: The paper uses different mechanisms for semantic (cross-attention) and spatial (ControlNet) features. It is unclear why a single attention mechanism cannot handle both. (R5)
- **VAE can also remove watermarks**: The difficulty of watermark removal is reduced because simple attacks like VAE encoding/decoding also remove them; the main contribution is in preserving semantic consistency, not the removal itself. (R3)
- **Computational cost for high-resolution images**: No discussion of model size, inference time (requested by R3), or how the method scales to 2K/4K images. (R3)
- **Problematic motivation**: The paper focuses on breaking watermarks without addressing societal benefit; it should discuss broader impact, potential misuse, and provide a defense scheme. (R5)
- **Missing details on watermark bits**: How many bits are used in the watermarking experiments? Could watermark algorithms use more bits to increase protection? (R5)
- **Influence of watermark constraints**: Do constraints like L2 norm or L∞ norm in watermark algorithm development affect removal capacity? (R5)
- **Model size and inference time not reported**: R3 requests these details to assess practicality.
