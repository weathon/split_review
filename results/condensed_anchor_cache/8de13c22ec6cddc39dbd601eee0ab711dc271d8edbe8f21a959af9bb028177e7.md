## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes VQScore, a self-supervised speech quality metric that uses the quantization error of a VQ-VAE trained on clean speech, and a speech enhancement model trained via self-distillation with adversarial training—also using only clean speech. Reviewers unanimously find the core idea novel and the approach technically sound, with experimental results supporting the hypotheses. However, multiple reviewers flag significant gaps in evaluation (no downstream tasks, missing intelligibility metrics, insufficient ablation) and note that the proposed metric performs poorly on highly degraded speech and the enhancement lags supervised baselines.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and neatness of the core idea**: Using VQ-VAE quantization error as a quality metric is novel (Reviewers 1, 4). The combination of VQ-VAE with adversarial training for speech enhancement is also a novel contribution (Reviewer 1).
- **Self-supervised paradigm**: The method only requires clean speech for training, reducing dependence on noisy/clean pairs (Reviewer 2). This is an interesting alternative to supervised approaches (Reviewer 3).
- **Clear presentation and well-designed experiments**: The paper clearly describes the approach and its experiments are well designed (Reviewer 2). Experimental results support the hypotheses (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Baseline selection**: Choice of compared models is good (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **No downstream evaluation**: Speech enhancement is not evaluated on downstream tasks (ASR, speaker recognition, diarization), which is expected for an ICLR publication (Reviewer 1). A new enhancement solution needs such evaluation to demonstrate practical utility (Reviewer 1, question also presents this as a criticism).
- **Poor metric performance on degraded speech**: VQScore shows low correlation with human scores when speech quality is poor, a significant limitation for a reliable quality metric (Reviewer 3).
- **Enhancement results lag supervised baselines**: The proposed enhancement model falls behind supervised models, especially in matched conditions; this raises concerns about practical applicability (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing intelligibility and standard metrics**: Tables 4 and 5 lack STOI and PESQ numbers, which are standard for speech enhancement (Reviewer 1). PESQ is downplayed without explanation or citation support (Reviewer 1’s question). The Weiner filter being best on SIG (real subset) in Table 4 is suspicious and unexplained; DNS1 dataset details are also missing (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of ablation and thorough analysis of the technical contribution**: The paper’s main technical method is not robustly evaluated with ablation studies (Reviewer 1). Hyperparameters such as codebook size and commitment weight β (Equation 3) are critical but their values are not empirically or theoretically justified (Reviewers 2, 4). How β=3 was decided is unexplained (Reviewer 4’s question).
- **Notation inconsistency**: Equations 5 and 6 are inconsistent (Lce is introduced with two arguments but not developed correctly in Equation 6); notation should be reviewed (Reviewer 3).
- **Risk of token collapse**: No evidence of training stability during adversarial training; VQ-VAE risks collapsing to select the same token (a known issue similar to mode collapse in GANs) (Reviewer 3).
- **Computational cost omitted**: The computational load and scalability of the proposed adversarial training are not analyzed; the method does not differ from other expensive adversarial approaches (Reviewer 3). Model complexity comparison with baselines is missing (Reviewer 2).
- **No generalization analysis**: Experiments supporting claims of better generalization to new domains (e.g., telephony speech, artificially generated speech) are absent (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing statistical significance**: Standard deviations or significance tests are not reported for correlations or enhancement metrics; results may not be statistically significant (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Alternative noise corpora not used**: Available external noise corpora are not utilized; doing so could improve performance, and adversarial noise is not the only option (Reviewer 1). Adversarial training is noted as slow.
- **Mismatch in baseline**: TorchaudioSquim could be retrained to address mismatch issues, making comparison fairer (Reviewer 1).
- **No theoretical analysis**: Little theoretical analysis about when the method would work or fail (Reviewer 4). Distributional properties of quantization errors are not explored (Reviewer 4’s question).
