## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Scalable Modular Network (SMN) with an agreement router that iteratively selects and composes specialist modules via message passing. Reviewers find the agreement router novel and effective, with strong results on a toy min-max game (adaptation to a parity code task) and few-shot image classification using a ConvNet backbone. The method also allows post-training addition of new modules. However, reviewers consistently identify insufficient experimentation (only two tasks, small/low-dimensional datasets) and lack of critical analyses: computational cost (FLOPs, training/inference time), scalability to multiple modules/layers and weight adaptation when modules are added, performance inconsistency on ViT vs ConvNet, missing comparison with Mixture of Experts (MoE), need for degeneracy-prevention losses, and no evaluation in continual learning or other domains (RL, NLP, LLMs). Several clarifying questions remain about the router mechanics, the fine-tuning process, and pre-training integration.

### Strengths
* Novel and effective agreement router design that dynamically selects modules using iterative message passing (all reviewers).
* Strong empirical results on the toy min-max game (adaptation to parity code task) and few-shot image classification with ConvNet (Reviewer 1).
* Well-structured presentation of motivation, problem statement, and comprehensive review of existing modular network methods (Reviewer 2).
* Clear rationale and technical details; implementation and experimental settings ensure fairness of comparisons (Reviewer 2).
* Potential value for large-scale modular networks that can integrate new modules post-training (Reviewers 2 and 3).
* Advantages in meta-learning and continual learning settings (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
* Insufficient experimentation: evaluation limited to a toy min-max game and few-shot classification on small datasets (e.g., miniImageNet); need for more diverse and complex tasks (higher-dimensional data, reinforcement learning, natural language processing, time series) and evaluation on large-scale benchmarks to demonstrate scalability and generalization (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
* Missing computational cost analysis: no FLOPs, training/inference time, or memory complexity reported; the iterative agreement router likely adds significant overhead, and the trade-off between accuracy and compute is not discussed (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
* Scalability and adaptation mechanism unclear: how are weights \(W_a\) (agreement router) and \(W_c\) (module parameters) adapted when new modules are added? Is \(W_c\) frozen or trainable (Equations 4 and 12)? The method is only demonstrated on a single layer of modules; translating to multiple layers is not explained (Reviewer 1). Adding modules still requires additional fine-tuning with no analysis of sample or computational cost, contradicting the claim of seamless integration (Reviewer 3).
* Performance inconsistency on the ViT backbone vs ConvNet: the improvements seen on ConvNet for few-shot classification do not carry over to ViT; authors should investigate and discuss this discrepancy (Reviewer 1).
* No comparison with Mixture of Experts (MoE) architectures, limiting the assessment of novelty and relative performance of SMN (Reviewer 3).
* Method needs additional loss terms to prevent degeneracy (similar to Top-K approaches) to ensure diverse module utilization; otherwise the model may converge to using only a few modules (Reviewer 1).
* No evaluation under a continual learning setting despite being a stated advantage: e.g., adding modules for new tasks without forgetting old tasks (Reviewer 1, Question 8; Reviewer 4).
* Pre-training paradigm not adequately explored: limited experimental evidence or discussion of how SMN performs in pre-training contexts; no comparison with contrastive learning or masked language model losses (Reviewer 3, Questions 1 and 2).
* Missing clarification on several design details: the relationship between \(y\) from Equation 4 and module outputs (Reviewer 1, Question 2); the value of \(K\) in Top‑K for the two-module case in Table 1 and comparison with an Ensemble baseline (Reviewer 1, Question 5); a detailed explanation of what happens in each iteration and how “agreement” is computed (Reviewer 1, Question 6).
* Minor presentation issues: typographical errors (e.g., “impose constrains” – Reviewer 4) should be fixed.
