## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Dyn-VQA, a new multimodal VQA dataset (1.5k samples, 9 domains, 2 languages, 3 categories: rapidly changing answers, multi-modal knowledge, multi-hop questions) to fill a gap in existing mRAG benchmarks where most knowledge is obtainable via standard two-step retrieval. The authors also propose OmniSearch, a self-adaptive planning agent that decomposes complex questions into sub-question chains with retrieval actions. Experiments show OmniSearch improves over heuristic mRAGs, but reviewers note limitations in dataset scale, sustainability, and method novelty.

### Strengths
- **Novel and practical dataset**: Dyn-VQA focuses on “dynamic” visual questions with answers that change over time, multi-modal knowledge, and multi-hop reasoning—real-world complexities underrepresented in existing VQA datasets. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Manual curation and detailed analysis**: The dataset is manually curated with extensive descriptions of construction, statistical information, and diversity analysis. (R1, R4)
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Extensive ablations and comparisons with multiple MLLMs and mRAG baselines demonstrate OmniSearch’s effectiveness. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Clear motivation and presentation**: The paper is clearly written, well motivated, and easy to follow. (R1, R2, R3)
- **OmniSearch as a plug-and-play module**: The self-adaptive retrieval agent is designed to be incorporated into various MLLMs, showing broad applicability. (R3)
- **Strong performance**: OmniSearch outperforms heuristic mRAGs on the challenging Dyn-VQA tasks. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Small dataset scale**: Only 1,500 questions (with only 178 covering all three categories) raises concerns about sufficient complexity and diversity for broad research community benefit. (R1, R4) *Reviewer R4 notes it is significantly smaller than existing knowledge-seeking VQA datasets.*
- **Language and cultural bias**: Dataset only includes English and Chinese; intractable instances that do not translate well are filtered, potentially introducing bias. More languages and examples of human correction of Google Translate would improve generalizability. (R1)
- **Sustainability of ground-truth accuracy**: Answers to “dynamic” questions (e.g., “What is his latest film?”) may become outdated after release. The paper does not address how to ensure reliable, up-to-date evaluation over time. (R3)
- **Unclear definition of “hops” in multi-hop questions**: The paper does not specify how a single reasoning step is defined, leading to possible varying interpretations. (R3)
- **Missing related work**: Does not cite recent multi-hop visual question answering work such as Kil et al., II-MMR, ACL’24. (R3)
- **Limited innovation of OmniSearch**: While effective, the proposed method’s novelty is considered limited by one reviewer. (R4)
- **OmniSearch struggles with long reasoning chains and fine-grained questions**: As mentioned in the case study, the method has difficulty with questions requiring long-term reasoning chains. (R4)
- **No discussion of latency or computational overhead**: The multi-step retrieval process of OmniSearch lacks analysis of its real-world applicability (e.g., time cost, scalability). (R1, R2)
- **Missing ablation on feedback generation**: The paper does not ablate the effectiveness of feedback generation in OmniSearch or its application to other baselines. (R1)
- **No reasoning-based commercial search engine baseline**: Experiments omit comparison with GPT-4o or similar models. (R1)
- **Unclear explanation of OmniSearch’s decomposition and termination**: It is not explained how OmniSearch dynamically decomposes sub-questions or determines when a question is resolved; also the difference from chain-of-thought methods is not clarified. (R4)
- **Abstract and introduction clarity**: The concepts of “Non-adaptive Retrieval Queries” and “Overloaded Retrieval Queries” could be more clearly explained up front to better position the work. (R2)
- **Typo in Figure 3**: Question says “what's the price on this car” instead of “what’s the price of this car”. (R1)
- **Minority reviewer contrast**: Reviewer R2 (score 8) finds the dataset “strong, novel” and the experimental section strong, while Reviewer R4 (score 5) considers the dataset scale small and the innovation of OmniSearch limited. Reviewer R3 (score 6) raises sustainability and missing citations.
