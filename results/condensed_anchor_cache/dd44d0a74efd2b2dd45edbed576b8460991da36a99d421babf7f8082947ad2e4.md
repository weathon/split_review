## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DRIVE, a model-based distributional RL method that uses variational inference to incorporate aleatoric uncertainty for risk-aware policy search. It introduces a learning proxy via the log-likelihood of an optimality variable, decoupled from traditional value functions. Empirical results on vision-based DMControl tasks are compared against Dreamer, D4PG, and SAC.

### Strengths
- Novel integration of variational inference into distributional RL, offering a unique perspective and a principled way to perform policy search (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Reduces effect of aleatoric uncertainty by assessing return distribution quality through imagination, preventing overfitting to maximum value (Reviewer 1).
- Well-motivated idea with reasonable empirical performance (Reviewer 2).
- Theoretical framework provides convergence conditions (Reviewer 3) and completes the distributional approach end-to-end (Reviewer 4).
- Writing and derivations are largely clear and logically structured (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The perspective may be helpful for future work combining RL, distributional RL, and probabilistic inference (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and clarity issues**: Notation is inconsistent—e.g., multiple q functions in Eq 9, Eq 16, and Algorithm 1 without proper subscripts (Reviewer 1). Some derivations are confusing, particularly the introduction of distribution q in Eq 30 and the variational lower bound (Reviewer 2). The source of Eq (13) and the transition in Eq (9) are unclear (Reviewer 4). Use of "\citep" and "\citet" is inconsistent (Reviewer 2).
- **Theoretical connection to contribution is weak**: Theorem 5.1 appears standard policy improvement; Theorem 5.2 connects to standard RL, but neither directly addresses how the method handles aleatoric uncertainty for risk-awareness (Reviewer 1). The analysis is dense and would benefit from more intuitive explanation bridging theory and practice (Reviewer 3). Assumption 3.1 (defining U_max) is not clearly motivated and its impact is not discussed (Reviewer 4). The variational approximation q seems to be used as the "true" return distribution, yet it does not need to appear in the lower bound, which seems conceptually off (Reviewer 2).
- **Methodological concerns**: It is unclear which distributional RL method (e.g., C51, QRDQN) is used to estimate the return distribution U, and whether U is treated as a continuous or categorical variable (Reviewer 1). The effect of taking more samples from q (Figure 4b) actually worsens performance, which is counterintuitive and lacks sufficient explanation (Reviewer 2). The method is computationally expensive due to marginalization over returns and inclusion of Dreamer; the impact of approximate transition model and horizon H is not discussed or investigated (Reviewer 4). The multi-term objective and complex posterior approximation may lead to overfitting, especially in limited-data scenarios; scalability to high-dimensional and large action spaces is not addressed (Reviewer 3). No evaluation on discrete-action games (e.g., Atari) (Reviewer 3). Risk-aware exploration is emphasized qualitatively but lacks quantitative metrics (e.g., state coverage) and illustrative examples (Reviewers 2, 3). The ablation study in Figure 4a shows overlapping 95% confidence intervals, suggesting no statistically significant difference between conditions (Reviewer 2).
- **Experimental limitations**: Experiments are conducted only on vision-based DMControl tasks (Reviewer 1). The paper evaluates on 12 environments, whereas the Dreamer baseline was tested on 22, raising concerns about generalizability (Reviewer 1). Per-game results for SAC and DreamerV3 are missing from Table 1 and Figure 2; only aggregated performance across 11 tasks is shown, losing insight into when the method excels or fails (Reviewers 2, 4). SAC is excluded from Table 1 without explanation (Reviewer 4). The paper does not specify the policy (critic) used for continuous control, making implementation details hard to follow (Reviewer 1). No comparison to bisimulation-based model-based approaches [1,2,3] which also address exploration and representation learning (Reviewer 3). The relation to existing work beyond D4PG is not motivated (Reviewer 4).

*Minority contrast*: One reviewer (score 3, high confidence) found the paper poorly presented and experimentally insufficient, while another (score 8) considered it well-written and theoretically sound but acknowledged missing empirical depth. Reviewers 2 and 4 (scores 6) saw potential but criticized incomplete evaluations and unclear derivations.
