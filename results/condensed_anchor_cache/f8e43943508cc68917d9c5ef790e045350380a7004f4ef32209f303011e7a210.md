## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Multi-Scale Latent Points Consistency Models (MLPCM) for 3D point cloud generation. It builds a latent diffusion model with hierarchical latent representations (point-level to super-point levels), uses a multi-scale latent integration (MLI) module and 3D spatial attention (3DSA) in the denoiser, and distills the diffusion model into a few-step consistency model. The method achieves significant sampling speed-up with marginal quality loss, outperforming baselines on ShapeNet and ShapeNet-Vol on metrics like 1-NNA. Reviewers find the quantitative results and acceleration impressive but criticize limited novelty, missing qualitative comparisons, insufficient ablation details, and unclear presentation.

### Strengths
- Extends consistency models to point cloud generation for the first time, demonstrating automatic, few-step generation (1–4 steps) with a 100× speed-up over the teacher model. [R1, R3, R4, R5]
- The multi-scale hierarchical representation (point-level and super-point levels) is a reasonable choice for capturing both details and overall structure; ablation (Table 5) confirms its benefit over a single-level variant. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- The 3D spatial attention mechanism (prioritizing nearby points with bias term B) is shown to be effective; design adapts Point Transformer ideas to the latent diffusion setting. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- Quantitative results on single-category and all-category generation are strong, surpassing several baselines (e.g., DPM, MeshDiffusion, LION, DPC, etc.) in 1-NNA. [R2, R3, R5]
- The distillation stage yields minimal performance degradation while enabling rapid sampling, making the model potentially useful for interactive applications. [R1, R2, R4]
- The paper is generally clear and easy to follow, with well-structured sections and an ablation study. [R2]

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and incremental contributions.** The core contribution is a straightforward application of the existing consistency model formulation to 3D point clouds without any specific modification to the distillation process or latent diffusion framework. [R1, R2, R3, R4] Many reviewers note that previous works like LION could also be accelerated with consistency distillation, making the comparison to LION (which only uses DDIM sampling) somewhat unfair. [R1, R2] The hierarchical latent representation and spatial attention are also known techniques (e.g., XCube, Point Transformers, StyleGAN's AdaIn), and the paper does not introduce conceptually new architectural ideas. [R1, R2]
- **Insufficient qualitative results and missing visual comparisons.** The paper contains very few qualitative examples (only one visual in Fig. 3), and its caption does not specify whether the shapes are generated by the one-step consistency model or the teacher model. [R2, R4, R5] No visual comparison with baselines is provided, making it hard to judge shape quality and diversity from the paper. [R2, R4, R5] The supplementary material, referenced in the text (e.g., line 237), was missing from the submission. [R2, R4]
- **Incomplete ablation and missing experiments.** Table 5 only reports ablations for the "car" category; results for "airplane" and "chair" (or a joint 13-class average) are needed for convincing conclusions. [R5] The claim that single-level representation is insufficient (line 45) is not backed by experimental evidence or proper citations. [R5] An ablation comparing only the point-level (PL) latent without the multi-scale components would help assess how much the additional hierarchies contribute. [R4] The number of sampling steps used for the consistency model in Tables 1 and 2 is not reported; authors should provide results for 1, 2, 3, and 4 steps if applicable. [R4]
- **Unclear presentation and figure quality.** Figure 1 (overview) and Figure 2 (architecture) lack detailed captions and do not explain the components clearly. [R4] Figure 2 does not show where the MLI layer integrates, making the architecture flow hard to follow. [R3] The VAE latent variable structure is confusing: the text states \(N_0 = N\) and \(Z^0\) as the point-level latent, but Equation (1) suggests \(X\) is encoded to \(Z^L\); the figure implies latents come from the bottleneck, contradicting the text. [R3] The bias term \(B\) in the spatial attention is described as a distance matrix; adding it as a dense map seems to emphasize distant points rather than close ones—clarification is needed. [R3] There are several typos (e.g., "were were" in line 98, "exiting" in line 71, "consistency training." in line 286, "we aims" in line 305). [R3, R4]
- **Missing discussion of related work and limitations.** Recent relevant works such as SDFusion, 3DQD, and 3DILG are not discussed in the main text. [R5] The paper does not clearly state limitations of the approach (e.g., output sparsity of 2048 points, lack of a mesh conversion pipeline like LION's). [R2, R4] The model as presented does not support interactive editing or conditional generation (e.g., voxel- or text-based conditioning), which would be desirable for the claimed fast feedback applications. [R2, R3]
- **Technical questions and missing details.** Motivation for the MLI layer (why latent modulation of \(F_s\) and the role of two-dimensional scales) is not explained. [R3] The notation in Equation (4) is unclear: the superscript on \(Z\) is dropped in \(q(Z_t|Z^{0}_{t-1})\), and the forward process appears independent of \(\mathcal{Z}^{\backslash0}\) despite being written. [R2] It is not clear why \(Z^0 \sim q_{\phi}(Z^1|X)\) in line 207. [R2] The paper mentions "skip connection" in line 370 but does not explain it. [R5] Training and inference costs (time, memory) are not reported. [R5] The low performance of DPM and MeshDiffusion in Table 1 raises possible unfairness in comparison. [R5] Reviewer 3 questions whether the distillation process would hold up under conditioning or guidance. [R3] The paper should discuss whether the 3D spatial attention or multi-scale latent integration offer significant benefit beyond the point-level latent; Reviewer 4 notes that the gains from these components appear marginal. [R4] (Minority: R3 finds the first demonstration of CM on 3D point clouds a contribution, but still criticizes the lack of modification.)

**Note:** Several reviewers (R1, R2, R3, R4) agree that the paper's contributions are incremental; however, they diverge on the significance of the acceleration: R4 is slightly more positive ("right direction"), while R1 and R2 emphasize the lack of novelty. R3 explicitly praises the first demonstration of distillation for 3D point cloud generation. R5 finds the quantitative evidence for acceleration clear but wants more experimental thoroughness.
