## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PSformer, a Transformer for multivariate time series forecasting with two main components: parameter sharing (PS) across encoder blocks, and Spatial-Temporal Segment Attention (SegAtt), which concatenates sequence patches from the same positions across variables into segments before applying attention. Experiments on standard benchmarks show competitive or improved performance compared to several baselines. Reviewers agree the ideas are clearly presented and motivated, but note several limitations: marginal accuracy gains without variance reporting, insufficient comparisons with state-of-the-art baselines, missing experimental details (platform, hyperparameter tuning procedure), lack of analysis on failure cases and component interplay, and writing errors.

### Strengths
1. **Innovative design and clear motivation.** Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R3, R5) find the parameter‑sharing and SegAtt ideas interesting and well‑motivated. R4 notes that parameter sharing demonstrates effectiveness empirically. R6, however, finds the paper lacks innovation, describing parameter sharing as “simple module parameter‑sharing” and SegAtt as “merely merging channel and patch dimensions” – this minority view should be noted.
2. **Clear writing.** R1, R2, R3 praise the clarity and ease of following the paper. R4 and R6, however, report writing errors (see Weaknesses).
3. **Thorough experimental evaluation.** R3 and R6 note that experiments cover a wide range of datasets and include comparisons of training parameters and running time. R4 states that PSformer achieves state‑of‑the‑art performance on most datasets.
4. **Efficiency gains.** R5 and R6 highlight that PSformer shows shorter running time and smaller model size due to parameter sharing, which also enhances scalability and may mitigate overfitting (R5).

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient comparisons with recent state‑of‑the‑art.** R1 notes missing comparisons with TimeMixer, FITS, CrossGNN, FourierGNN, MICN, and TimesNet. R6 additionally requests PDF (ICLR2024) and Time‑LLM (ICLR2024). R1 further points out that the paper does not compare with GNN‑based methods (CrossGNN, FourierGNN, NeurIPS 2023). Overall, it cannot be concluded that PSformer achieves SOTA without these baselines.
2. **Discrepancy in PatchTST results.** R6 observes a significant difference between the PatchTST results in Table 1 and the original PatchTST paper; an explanation is requested.
3. **Marginal improvement and missing variance/statistical significance.** R2 states that accuracy improvement is relatively marginal, especially in ablation studies, and variances should be reported to support significance. R3 and R5 also request results with multiple random seeds for robustness.
4. **Missing experimental details and fairness concerns.** R1 notes that the experimental platform details and parameter search space for the compared methods are not provided. It is unclear whether best model parameters were selected on a validation set; if not, the comparison may be unfair. R5 asks for a detailed description of the hyperparameter tuning process (e.g., grid search, random search, Bayesian optimization).
5. **Lack of analysis on component interplay and failure cases.** 
   - R2 asks why PSformer does not work well on the Exchange dataset. R5 similarly notes weaker performance on Electricity and Exchange and asks about specific settings where PSformer performs well.
   - R1, R4, R5, R6 request deeper analysis of the parameter‑sharing mechanism: e.g., convergence rates with/without sharing (R4), effect on model capacity (R4), impact on temporal pattern capture across layers (R5), and why the PS Block is placed inside SegAtt rather than after attention (R6: “what is the purpose of including the PS Block in the Segment Attention? why not just add the PS Block after the output of the Attention?”).
   - R5 also asks for an ablation study comparing SAM with other optimizers (Adam, SGD).
   - R6 questions why “two SegAttn can be viewed as one FFN layer” and whether the integration of temporal and channel information may lead to incomplete capture of both – requests visualization and comparison with separated‑modeling approaches like Crossformer and iTransformer.
6. **Segmentation number selection.** R3 notes that segmentation number sensitivity is evaluated only on ETTh1 and ETTm1; more diverse datasets and practical guidance for selection are needed.
7. **Channel‑mixing and positional encoding analysis.** R3 requests a comparative discussion of channel independence vs. mixing (similar to PatchTST A.7). R5 requests additional experiments on positional encoding’s effect on generalization across seasonal/non‑seasonal and stationary/non‑stationary series.
8. **Writing and presentation errors.** R1 points out that Table 27 appears to be missing. R4 mentions several writing errors throughout the article. R6 lists specific errors: “Moment()” on line 125, “GPT4TS uses BERT as the backbone” on line 127, among others.
