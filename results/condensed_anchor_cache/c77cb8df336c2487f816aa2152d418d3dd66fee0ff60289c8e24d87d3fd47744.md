## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a federated learning algorithm (FedHBM) based on a generalized heavy-ball momentum that computes local momentum at the client level without increasing communication cost. Experiments on vision and language tasks (CIFAR-10/100, Shakespeare) in both cross-silo and cross-device settings compare favourably against several baselines. Reviewer 1 was highly positive (score 8), praising the novelty and clarity. Reviewers 2–4 were less convinced (scores 6, 3, 3), raising concerns about limited novelty, lack of theoretical analysis, missing baselines, and problematic experimental choices (especially no learning rate decay). The code was not released, and reproducibility is questioned.

### Strengths
- **Well-written and clear**: The paper is dynamic, easy to follow, and provides intuitive explanations alongside mathematical statements (R1, R2; R3 noted writing needs improvement).
- **Important and well-motivated problem**: Tackles statistical and system heterogeneity in FL (R1, R2).
- **Novel and clever methodology** (R1): Efficiently uses client participation over multiple rounds to estimate local momentum without extra communication. Other reviewers (R2, R3, R4) considered the novelty limited—the algorithm is a simple generalization of heavy-ball momentum combined with variance reduction, and similar updates have appeared in prior work (e.g., multi-step inertial momentum [2]).
- **Clear relation to prior work**: Mathematical formulations and empirical comparisons with FedCM, FedADC, MimeMom, etc., are thoroughly discussed (R1, R4).
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Both academic and real-world datasets, cross-silo and cross-device settings, and an ablation study on the window parameter τ are included (R1, R2, R4).
- **Practical variation without extra communication**: A variant of FedHBM that avoids additional communication requirements shows strong performance (R1, R2).
- **Shift window reduces communication** (R4).
- **Limitations discussed**: The paper addresses issues in high cross-device settings and proposes alternatives (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The method additively combines two existing techniques (heavy-ball momentum and momentum-based variance reduction [Cutkosky & Orabona, 2019]) with a parameterized time step τᵢ. The claimed “generalized heavy-ball momentum” is a far stretch (R3). Reviewer 4 notes that the same updates (global GHB) have been studied in [2] (multi-step inertial momentum), and this paper merely extends it to the local level without optimization or generalization analysis, greatly reducing novelty (R3, R4). Reviewer 2 also considers the novelty as limited.
- **No theoretical analysis**: The paper provides no convergence or generalization guarantees. It is not proven why the “window” (gap between current and past iterate) is beneficial in federated settings; motivation and intuition are missing (R2, R3, R4).
- **Hyperparameter setting unclear**: No guidance is given on how to set step sizes η, β, or the window parameter τᵢ (R1). For the experiments, the choice of these hyperparameters (and those of competitors) is not reported, making comparisons hard to interpret. Reviewer 3 points out that setting τᵢ = τ = 1/C (from Appendix A) makes the term negligible in large-scale settings (R3).
- **Missing baselines / incomplete results**: Table 2 (cross‑silo) and Table 3, 4 lack results for FedCM and FedADC, which are key momentum-based baselines. In Figure 3, FedCM appears only as an ablation; its omission from the main comparison tables weakens the evaluation (R4). Reviewer 2 notes that in Table 2 FEDDYN achieves a higher target accuracy (90%) in the cross-device setting, which is not adequately explained.
- **No learning rate decay**: All experiments use a fixed learning rate (no scheduler). This significantly disadvantages several baselines (e.g., FedDyn, SCAFFOLD, FedCM) that require careful local optimization. This is considered a major weakness; the results reflect only a fixed‑LR scenario, whereas modern ML practice often relies on learning rate schedules (R4).
- **Reproducibility concerns**: Code was not provided for the review process, and the paper states it “will be released upon acceptance”. The description of the non‑IID data split using a Dirichlet distribution (α→0) is inconsistent with the appendix (α=0) and unclear about how heterogeneity is actually created (R1, R4). Labels are missing on some graphs (R3).
- **Plot readability**: In Figures 3 and 4(a), curves overlap and are hard to distinguish (R2).
- **Missing related work**: References [Xin & Khan, 2018], [Das et al., 2022], [Kim et al., 2022] are not cited (R3).
- **Initial model dependency**: In the cross-device setting, the algorithm requires a “proper” initial model to achieve improvement (R2, also noted by R1 as a discussed limitation).
- **MVR term in Eq.(5) not explained**: The variance‑reduction term is defined as a difference of sums over local steps, but its connection to existing variance‑reduction techniques (e.g., [Cutkosky & Orabona, 2019]) is not demonstrated in the main text (R4).
- **Minor errors**: Typo on page 3 (“express as special…“ should be “expressed”); page 6, α=10k needs definition of k; Table 5 shows FEDAVG’s performance in bold for C≈0.5, which appears to be a typo (R2).
