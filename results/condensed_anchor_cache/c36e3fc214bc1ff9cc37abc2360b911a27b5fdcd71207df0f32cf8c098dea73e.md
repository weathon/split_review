## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Adversarial Self Flow Matching (ASFM), which combines Online Self Training (an online, on-the-fly version of rectified flow reflow) with Adversarial Training (GAN loss) to straighten flow trajectories for few-step image generation in ODE-based generative models. ASFM is validated on CIFAR-10, CelebA-HQ (256), and AFHQ-Cat (256), achieving competitive FID scores with NFE=1 to 6 (e.g., 8.15 on CelebA-HQ, 14.9 on AFHQ-Cat with NFE=6). The method also demonstrates zero-shot image editing.

### Strengths
- **Few-step generation and efficiency:** ASFM enables high-quality generation in very few steps (NFE=1–6) through straight flows, significantly reducing computational cost compared to traditional flow-based models. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Simple and intuitive idea:** The integration of an adversarial objective with flow rectification is conceptually straightforward and shows strong empirical results on multiple datasets. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Clear presentation:** The paper is well-structured, with detailed explanations of the two training components, loss functions, and Algorithm 1, aiding reproducibility. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Preserves zero-shot capabilities:** The method retains the ability to perform downstream tasks like image editing without paired data, leveraging the ODE formulation. (Reviewer 1)
- **Extensive experimental validation:** Experiments cover CIFAR-10, CelebA-HQ, and AFHQ-Cat, with competitive quantitative scores and ablation studies (e.g., Table 3 on queued samples). (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty:** The use of adversarial training for one-step generation in diffusion/flow models has been introduced in prior works (ASM, CTM, ADD). ASFM is essentially a combination of existing rectified-flow reflow and GAN loss with limited conceptual advance. (Reviewer 3)  *(R3 is noticeably more negative on this point; R1 finds the method novel in contrast.)*
2. **Insufficient validation of one-step generation claim:** No high-resolution samples are shown with NFE<6, making it unclear if the method truly achieves high-quality one-step generation on challenging datasets. The paper’s abstract claims one-step capability, but this is not convincingly demonstrated. (Reviewer 4)
3. **Incomplete baselines and unfair reporting:**
   - Table 1 omits results for 2-rectified-flow and 3-rectified-flow and their distilled versions (which achieve better FID under few steps: e.g., 1-rectified-flow 378→distilled 6.18, 2-rectified-flow 12.21→distilled 4.85, 3-rectified-flow 8.15→distilled 5.21). (Reviewer 3)
   - Table 2 is missing comparisons with few-step methods like CTM, CD, and the original flow matching model that ASFM builds upon (pretrained 1-rectified-flow). (Reviewer 3)
   - The FID and NFE of the original flow model (before rectification) should be reported; the method should also be tested starting from the original flow, not just 1-rectified-flow. (Reviewer 3)
4. **Unclear training dynamics and failure modes:**
   - L238–239 claims that generating new samples every batch (Model A) causes training to fail to converge. The paper does not explain why this happens despite the GAN loss aiming to align distributions. A deeper analysis (training dynamics visualization, theoretical justification) is needed. (Reviewer 2)
   - The interplay between self-training and adversarial components needs clarification; the method may be sensitive to hyperparameters and prone to instability from GAN loss. (Reviewer 1)
5. **Missing ablations and parameter sensitivity:**
   - No ablation on the critical `freq` parameter that controls how often new generated data are added to the queue. (Reviewer 2)
   - No ablation of λ₁ and λ₂ in Eq. 13. (Reviewer 3)
   - The ablation on the number of generated samples (Table 3) should include a comparison with “2-rectified-flow-online” using the same data size as ASFM. (Reviewer 3)
   - The model requires pretrained initialization (1-rectified-flow); no study on random initialization or robustness to initialization. (Reviewer 2)
6. **Incomplete evaluation of distribution quality and mode collapse:**
   - GAN loss and reflow both risk mode collapse. The paper should report recall (from https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06991) in addition to FID. (Reviewer 2)
   - On CIFAR-10 (Table 2), FID for NFE=1 and 2 remains high (e.g., >10), and the method still needs >1 step for high-resolution datasets to get FID under 10 (e.g., CelebA-HQ NFE=6). (Reviewer 3)
7. **Visual quality concerns:** Generated images in Fig.4 appear to lack fine details and show artifacts; more qualitative comparisons with baseline methods are needed. (Reviewer 4)
8. **Lack of straight-flow validation:** The paper claims straight flows but does not visualize intermediate steps or show that the trajectory is linear over the entire time span (e.g., same noise with different sampling steps). (Reviewer 4)
9. **Missing results for NFE=6 on AFHQ-Cat and CelebA-HQ:** It is unclear why these are not reported. (Reviewer 2)  *(Reviewer 1’s question about balancing quality vs NFE overlaps with this.)*
10. **Generalization and scalability concerns:**
    - Not extended to conditional tasks (e.g., text-to-image) or large-scale datasets. (Reviewer 1)
    - Computational cost of online sampling (two separate forward passes per batch, additional forward for adversarial loss) is not discussed, nor is the memory cost (only tested with batch size 128). (Reviewer 3)
11. **Clarity issues:**
    - Fig.2’s transparent red dashed line is confusing and needs better labeling. (Reviewer 4)
    - The “image mixture” description in Appendix C.1 is unclear, and Fig.7–8 are not convincing. (Reviewer 4)
    - L213–215 uses the triangle inequality as a justification, but the data are in high-dimensional space, not 2D. (Reviewer 2)
12. **Missing zero-shot editing results in main paper:** The editing application should be moved from supplementary to the main manuscript. (Reviewer 3)
