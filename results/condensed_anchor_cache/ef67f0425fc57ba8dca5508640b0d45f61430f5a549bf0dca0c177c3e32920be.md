## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes CTRL, a reinforcement learning (RL) based method for adding conditional control to pre-trained diffusion models. CTRL frames conditional generation as an RL problem with a reward derived from the conditional likelihood of labels given data, regularized by KL divergence against the pre-trained model (using Girsanov’s theorem). Compared to classifier guidance and classifier-free guidance, CTRL can use more flexible offline datasets (pairs instead of triplets) and avoids training predictors from noisy intermediate states. Experiments on compressibility and aesthetic pleasingness demonstrate superior controllability in single- and multi-task settings. Reviewer opinions range from moderately positive to strong (scores 6,6,8,6). One reviewer (R3) is notably more positive (score 8) and finds the comparison comprehensive and results strong, while others (R1,R2,R4) raise substantial concerns about limited evaluation, missing convergence guarantees, computational cost, and insufficient baselines.

### Strengths
- The problem of adding conditional control to pre-trained diffusion models is highly valuable, and the use of RL for this purpose is novel. The derivation using KL-regularized RL with Girsanov’s theorem is rigorous and reasonable (R1, R3, R4).
- The paper is well-organized and clearly presented (R1). It includes a comprehensive discussion of the relationship between CTRL, classifier guidance, and classifier-free guidance (R1, R3).
- Compared to existing methods, CTRL offers practical advantages: (a) more flexible offline dataset construction by leveraging conditional independence, allowing use of pairs instead of triplets; (b) avoids the need to train a classifier on noisy intermediate states, reducing accumulated inaccuracies; (c) improved sample efficiency and control precision (R2, R3).
- Experimental results show CTRL meets target conditions accurately in single-task and multi-task settings, with fewer data dependencies (R3). Source code is provided for reproducibility (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient evaluation metrics**: The paper only reports controllability metrics; commonly used image quality metrics such as FID or Inception Score are not provided. It is unclear whether the improved controllability comes at the cost of image fidelity (R2). (One reviewer (R3) did not raise this issue and praised the results, but this concern is strongly expressed by R2.)
- **Limited experimental tasks and conditions**: Only compressibility and aesthetic pleasingness are used. More complex conditions (e.g., sketch, normal map) should be tested to demonstrate generalizability. Also, the paper lacks explanation of how these conditions are encoded and incorporated into the model (R2, R4).
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons**: The paper only compares with classifier guidance and classifier-free guidance (both from 2022). It lacks comparisons with more recent guided diffusion variants, alternative conditional generation approaches, and other RL-based methods for conditional generation (R3, R4). While one reviewer (R3) found the comparison comprehensive, others (R2, R4) consider it incomplete. R4 further questions whether comparing with classifier-free guidance is unfair given CTRL uses a classifier, and requests an explanation of the experimental design considerations.
- **Missing theoretical convergence guarantees**: The paper does not provide a rigorous analysis of convergence of the RL training under the non-convex reward and stochastic diffusion process. Discussion on conditions for convergence, convergence rate, and the impact of hyperparameters is needed (R3).
- **Potential deviation from the forward process**: After RL fine-tuning, the forward process may no longer correspond to the original SDE because the optimization lacks explicit constraints. This could impact performance or generalization. The authors are asked to discuss this concern (R1; also raised as a question).
- **Computational and practical concerns**: (a) Algorithm 1 backpropagates through the entire diffusion path, requiring high GPU memory and computation, which limits scalability (R1). (b) CTRL requires training both a classifier (reward model) and fine-tuning the diffusion model, complicating the procedure relative to classifier guidance (trains only a classifier) or classifier-free guidance (fine-tunes only the model) (R1). (c) A computational cost analysis compared to baselines is needed (R3).
- **Unclear RL formulation**: The paper introduces only the reward function but does not formalize the MDP (state, action, transition function), making the specific RL method unclear (R4).
- **Claim about off-the-shelf RL algorithms unsubstantiated**: The claim that CTRL can be adapted to any off-the-shelf RL algorithm is not justified; only model-free on-policy examples (e.g., PPO) were considered, not offline or model-based RL. The authors should explain or provide experiments (R4). Additionally, a citation to the PPO paper is recommended (R4).
- **No ablation analysis**: The paper lacks ablation studies of the proposed method (R4).
- **Error sources not quantified**: The paper mentions three sources of error but does not precisely quantify them (R3, as a question).
