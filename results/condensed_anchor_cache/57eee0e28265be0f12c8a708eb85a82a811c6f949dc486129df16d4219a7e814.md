## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Hebbian Architecture Generation (HAG), a method that uses Pearson correlation to dynamically form excitatory connections in reservoir computing networks, starting from a blank connectivity matrix. Two variants are presented: mean-HAG and variance-HAG, which adjust weights based on mean activity and standard error. The goal is to decorrelate features for better linear separability (Cover’s theorem). Experiments on classification and prediction tasks (e.g., audio classification, complex-system time-series) show consistent improvements over standard Echo State Networks (ESNs). Four reviewers rated the paper 5, 5, 5, and 6; the latter (Reviewer 4) found more potential but also more fundamental concerns.

### Strengths
- The use of Pearson correlation to form connections is novel to the knowledge of Reviewer 1.
- The bio-plausible approach combining homeostasis with reservoir learning is interesting (Reviewer 2).
- Results are promising and impressive given the simplicity of the homeostatic constraint (maintaining Δz between -1 and 1) (Reviewer 2).
- Thorough evaluation spanning both classification and regression benchmarks (Reviewer 2).
- Original combination of known methods (hebbian/homeostatic plasticity and growth/pruning) in the context of reservoir computers (Reviewer 4).
- Good selection of diverse and difficult tasks, with appropriate cross-validation (Reviewer 4).
- Goals are well motivated: reservoirs are sensitive to initialization, so adaptive methods are a reasonable direction (Reviewer 4).
- Potential significance for self-organized/adaptive unsupervised networks, an area where efficient online architectural modifications are important (Reviewer 4).
- Empirical results on the tested datasets seem reasonable (Reviewer 1).
- HAG achieves improved performance over the original ESN method (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Methodology clarity and missing details**  
   - The exact training algorithm is not presented; an explicit algorithm listing is needed (Reviewer 1).  
   - Calculation of Pearson correlation: unclear if over entire time series or a sliding window, and whether raw activations or transformed versions are used (Reviewer 2).  
   - How the mean and standard deviation (used in computing Δz) are calculated is not specified (Reviewer 1).  
   - Choice of correlation bounds -1 and 1 appears arbitrary and lacks justification; robustness to different bounds is not explored (Reviewer 2).  
   - The threshold for “highly correlated neuron pairs” is unspecified (fixed number, percentage, or dynamic) (Reviewer 2).  
   - The weight decrement Δw (or δw) is not clearly defined; how is δw calculated? (Reviewer 2).  
   - Random pruning is not justified; why not prune based on contribution to performance? (Reviewer 2).  
   - Target activity ρ is not explained (how is it determined?) (Reviewer 2).  
   - The term “Suitability Characterization” is mentioned but not clarified (Reviewer 2).  
   - The variables Δw and δw appear to be different but the distinction is unclear (Reviewer 4).  
   - Typos and unclear sentences need editing: line 165 “we then identifying highly correlated neuron pairs we establishes a connction”, line 166 “if Δz_i > +1 connections weight”, line 172 “maximum number of synaptic partners, γ” (degree), line 174 “which might limits the”, line 297 “actual targetst” (Reviewer 4).

2. **Comparison and baseline issues**  
   - No comparisons with other state-of-the-art methods (e.g., NG-RC) beyond standard ESN (Reviewers 1, 3).  
   - E-ESN is mentioned in results but not explained (Reviewer 1).  
   - The baseline ESNs: which spectral radii were used? Were “best practices” for initialization employed, or were the ESNs handicapped by random initializations? (Reviewer 4).  
   - Only improvement over ESN is shown; no analysis on computational cost, parameter sensitivity, or convergence (Reviewer 3).

3. **Theoretical framing issues**  
   - Section on Cover’s theorem adds little; could be summarized in a sentence (Reviewer 1). Cover’s theorem is not mentioned again (Reviewer 2).  
   - The plasticity rule is described as Hebbian but actually is a homeostatic rule (matching a target per-neuron rate, not product of activations). This mischaracterization is flagged by Reviewer 4, who notes it is neither exactly Hebbian nor local. The use of correlations for all neurons makes the rule global in space and time. The description should be more accurate (Reviewer 4).  

4. **Motivation and necessity**  
   - The motivation for HAG is unclear: what specific representational deficiencies in standard ESNs does it address? No discussion of limitations of existing ESNs that HAG overcomes (Reviewer 3).  
   - Novelty and necessity of HAG are difficult to assess without a clear problem statement (Reviewer 3).

5. **Analysis of the novel contribution (growth/pruning)**  
   - The effect of the growth/pruning mechanism on the architecture is not studied: e.g., resulting connectivity, clusters, spectral radius changes before/after (Reviewer 4).  
   - Only Figures 5 and 6 relate to this; Figure 6’s interpretation is questionable: evolution of cumulative explained variance starts high (22-25) and decreases, but if growth/pruning happen during training, shouldn’t it start low? The timing of the process is unclear (Reviewer 4).  
   - No ablation analysis: removing growth/pruning (keeping only the hebbian/homeostatic rule) would quantify its contribution (Reviewer 4).  
   - The mechanism is likely responsible for the largest performance gain on SPEECHCOMMANDS, but this is not quantified (Reviewer 4).  
   - No hyperparameter sensitivity heatmaps or ranges of successful values are provided (Reviewer 4).

6. **Figures and presentation**  
   - Figure 2 appears arbitrary: not referenced in text, inadequately explained, colors/legends illegible, text at 0 mark illegible; Reviewer 2 and 3 recommend removal or moving to appendix; Reviewer 4 suggests it takes space better used for explaining the growth/pruning mechanism.  
   - Figures 3 and 4 have uninformative y-axis labels (“Value”), missing units, and unclear subplot meanings (Reviewers 3, 4).  
   - Figure captions lack detail on variables, axes, implications of trends (Reviewer 3).  
   - Overall figure sizes and font sizes need standardization for legibility (Reviewer 4).  
   - Section 5.1 implications are unclear (Reviewer 1).  
   - Minor: “First” and “Third” but no “Second” on pg. 132-139 (Reviewer 1).

7. **Missing references**  
   - Relevant works not cited: “Homeostatic Plasticity and External Input Shape Neural Network Dynamics” (PhysRevX 2018) and “SORN: a self-organizing recurrent neural network” (Frontiers 2009) (Reviewer 4).

8. **Additional reviewer questions**  
   - How does performance scale with model size? Does the advantage hold across smaller/larger networks? (Reviewer 4).  
   - For Figure 6: why does the moving cumulative explained variance start high for HAG and then decrease? Shouldn’t panel (a) start low if growth/pruning is happening during training? (Reviewer 4).  
   - Clarify whether the rule is local as claimed (line 102) given the global nature of the correlation computation and architecture (Reviewer 4).
