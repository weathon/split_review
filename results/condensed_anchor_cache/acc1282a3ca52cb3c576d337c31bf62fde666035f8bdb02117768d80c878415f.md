## Merged Review

### Summary
InstaRevive proposes a one-step image enhancement framework using score-based diffusion distillation. It employs dynamic noise control (adjusting the maximum noise level based on the LQ–GT difference) and dynamic loss control (balancing fidelity and distribution matching) to refine training trajectories. Textual prompts from image captioning are incorporated as auxiliary conditions. The method is validated on tasks including blind face restoration, blind super-resolution, style transfer, low-light enhancement, and inpainting, achieving strong results with significantly reduced sampling steps.

### Strengths
- Strong empirical performance across diverse image enhancement tasks, convincingly demonstrated on challenging datasets.
- The one-step restoration via diffusion distillation is a compelling direction for reducing computational and time costs.
- Dynamic score matching (noise/loss control) effectively guides the distillation process and addresses inaccuracies at large timesteps, a valuable contribution for distillation studies.
- Clear and well-structured presentation; the paper is easy to follow.
- Incorporation of textual prompts via image captioning provides additional guidance, leveraging the pre-trained diffusion model’s capabilities.

### Weaknesses
- The paper omits important prior works (e.g., img2img-turbo, OSEDiff) that also use distillation for image restoration, potentially overstating novelty. The core innovation is the dynamic control, not the distillation idea itself.
- Comparisons lack recent state-of-the-art methods such as OSEDiff and SUPIR, making the evaluation less comprehensive.
- The ablation in Figure 7 couples dynamic noise and dynamic loss controls; separate ablation lines for each component are needed to isolate individual contributions.
- The method relies on an initial restorer (e.g., SwinIR-GAN) that is degradation-specific, limiting flexibility and introducing a bottleneck. This supervised paired training also hinders generalization to unseen degradations and zero-shot scenarios. One reviewer questions the necessity of this restorer-fused approach over zero-shot diffusion-based restoration methods (e.g., DPS, PSLD, GDP).
- Insufficient comparison with the initial restorer using standard metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS). The initial restorer may recover most information; without such comparison, the added value of the distillation stage is unclear.
- The scope of demonstrated degradations is limited (BFR, BSR, cartoonization, low-light). Exploration of tasks like deraining, dehazing, deblurring, and salt-and-pepper noise is missing.
- For face cartoonization, the approach struggles to preserve facial identity, a significant limitation for practical applications. Extension to real-image style transfer (e.g., day-to-night conversion, season transfer) is suggested but not addressed.
