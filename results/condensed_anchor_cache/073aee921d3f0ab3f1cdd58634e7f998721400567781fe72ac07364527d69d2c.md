## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces CALICO, a self-supervised contrastive pre-training framework for multimodal (camera-LiDAR) BEV perception. It uses two stages: point-region contrast (PRC) and region-aware distillation (RAD). PRC applies both point- and region-level contrastive learning on point clouds via point-wise positive/negative pairs. RAD aligns camera and LiDAR feature maps via distillation from a self-trained teacher. The method is evaluated on nuScene and Waymo datasets for 3D object detection and BEV map segmentation, showing substantial improvements over baselines. Robustness tests against adversarial attacks and corruption are also conducted. Reviewers noted the novelty, strong empirical results, and generalizability, but raised concerns about the empirical focus, missing qualitative analysis, limited backbone evaluation, and unclear design choices.

### Strengths
- Novel contrastive pre-training for multimodal BEV perception: utilizes both point- and region-level contrastive learning on point clouds (point-wise positive/negative pairs to balance region- and scene-level representations). This combination is less studied in BEV compared to prior works. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- Generalizable and easily reproducible; can be applied to any BEV perception setup. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- Strong empirical results on nuScene and Waymo for 3D detection and BEV map segmentation, with substantial improvements over baselines. Ablation studies and robustness tests (adversarial attacks, corruptions) are thorough and demonstrate effectiveness. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- Saliency maps with vs. without pre-training are particularly impressive, showing clear feature learning benefits. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- The paper is entirely empirical and application-based; it is heavy on tables but light on qualitative analysis of where improvements come from. More failure case analysis and carefully designed experiments to understand the source of gains would increase insight. (Reviewer 1 – high confidence)
- The identification of semantic-less vs. semantic-rich points (used for clustering/pooling, e.g., Fig. 5) is not clearly explained. Further details are needed on how the 4th dimension of points is calculated and its range, and on how these clustering heuristics perform in different scenarios. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- The relevance of the adversarial robustness experiment to the proposed method is not clearly motivated. (Reviewer 1)
- No analysis of robustness to calibration error, which is a common issue in autonomous driving systems. (Reviewer 1)
- Superfluous lines appear in Appendix C (e.g., “As we mentioned in the rebuttal…”), indicating leftover text from a prior submission. (Reviewer 1)
- Evaluation is limited to a single architecture (BEVFusion with PointPillars and Swin-T). It is unclear how much performance gain is attributable to the proposed method versus the specific backbone choices. Using stronger backbones (e.g., VoxelNet, transformer-based image backbone) could lead to different conclusions. Interaction with different backbones is not explored. (Reviewer 2 – score 8, but with this concern)
- The necessity of negative samples in P_PLRC and P_RAPC is supported by ablation, but more detailed analysis is needed: the impact of the number of negative samples, how they are selected, and whether they are truly semantically disjoint from the positives (i.e., no shared semantic information). (Reviewer 2)
- The transformation T₂ in Fig. 1 is not clearly described. If it is not an identity transform, how are the corresponding images generated from the original? Are any augmentations applied to images when LiDAR points are changed? (Reviewer 3)
