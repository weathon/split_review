## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Adaptive Projected Guidance (APG) to address oversaturation and artifacts from high guidance scales in classifier-free guidance (CFG) for diffusion models. The authors decompose the CFG update into parallel and orthogonal components relative to the conditional prediction, down-weight the parallel component, and introduce rescaling and reverse momentum. Experiments across models and samplers show APG improves FID, recall, and saturation while maintaining precision, with no additional computational overhead.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a significant, well-known problem in diffusion models (oversaturation and artifacts at high guidance scales) with an innovative approach.
- The intuition and theory are well-balanced; the decomposition of the CFG update into parallel and orthogonal components and the analysis of their roles is novel and interesting.
- APG is presented as a plug-and-play method with virtually no computational overhead, making it attractive for practical use.
- Extensive experimental validation across various conditional diffusion models and samplers (including robustness across different model types) demonstrates effectiveness.
- Clear qualitative and quantitative comparisons between APG and CFG are provided; visual results are particularly compelling in showing reduced oversaturation without drastically altering image content.
- APG improves FID, recall, and saturation scores while maintaining precision comparable to CFG.

### Weaknesses
- **Discrepancy in Figure 8 vs. Table 1:** Reviewer 1 notes a discrepancy between the minimum point in Figure 8 and the values in Table 1, and asks whether different configurations were used. This needs clarification.
- **Model for Figure 8:** The specific model used in Figure 8 is not stated; this should be provided.
- **Low guidance scale performance:** Reviewer 3 points out that in Figure 8, when the guidance scale \( w \) is below certain values (e.g., \( w = 2.5 \)), APG shows lower precision and higher FID compared to CFG. The authors should explain this behavior.
- **SDXL guidance scale choice:** Reviewer 2 questions why Stable Diffusion XL uses such a large guidance scale (\( w = 15 \)) in Table 1, and asks whether the improvements are limited when the scale is reduced. More quantitative results for lower guidance scales should be provided.
- **Missing metrics:** Reviewer 2 suggests adding HPSv2 and ImageReward metrics to Table 1 for evaluating generation performance.
- **Logical error in Figure 10 (APG example):** Reviewer 2 observes that the final APG-generated example in Figure 10 contains a logical error (the cat has only one paw).
- **Evidence supporting parallel/orthogonal component claims:** Reviewer 2 asks how many examples were observed to support the claim that the orthogonal component chiefly improves image quality while the parallel component increases saturation, and whether any theory supports this. More evidence (e.g., number of examples or theoretical justification) is needed.
- **Analysis of rescale weight over timestep:** Reviewer 1 requests further analysis on the rescale weight – does it show any trends with respect to \( t \)?
- **Intuition behind reverse momentum:** Reviewer 3 notes that the intuition behind why reverse momentum improves generation quality could be expanded; more detailed theoretical or experimental exploration of this component would clarify its importance.
- **Clarity of contribution relative to CFG:** Reviewer 1 comments that the method is not viewed as a straightforward rescaling of CFG, and would like the authors’ perspective on this. The relationship to standard CFG and the nature of the modification should be clarified.
- **EDM2 guidance value:** Reviewer 1 asks about EDM2 – does the original paper suggest an ideal guidance value? Values used in the paper (e.g., \( w = 2, 4 \)) are higher than typical EDM2 values (\(\leq 2\)); was APG what made these higher values possible? This should be addressed.
- **Request for geometric illustrations:** Reviewer 1 suggests including illustrations of the geometry (decomposition, component directions) along with related intuitions to enhance clarity.
