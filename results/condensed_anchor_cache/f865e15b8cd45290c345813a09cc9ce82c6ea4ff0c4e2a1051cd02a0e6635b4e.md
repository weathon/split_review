## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper develops a solvable two-layer linear network model (trained with projected gradient descent on online SGD) to analyze scaling laws beyond the kernel regime. It identifies three scaling regimes (hard, easy, super-easy) based on task source exponent. The main result is that feature learning nearly doubles the scaling exponent for hard tasks (source < 1) in terms of training time and compute, while leaving easy and super-easy exponents unchanged. Scaling laws are expressed as power laws in training time \(t\), number of parameters \(N\), and batch size \(B\) (Eqs. 8–9). Theoretical predictions are validated on synthetic circle experiments (nonlinear MLP) and real datasets (MNIST, CIFAR). The paper is well written, clar about limitations, and provides a comprehensive framework.

### Strengths

- **Advanced theoretical tools and complete phase diagram**: A mean-field analysis of feature learning is used to derive a complete phase diagram showing when feature learning provides a significant advantage (Reviewer 1).
- **First theoretical explanation of compute-optimal scaling in nonlinear feature learning networks**: This is the first paper to theoretically explain compute-optimal scaling laws beyond the lazy regime. The separation of four bottleneck mechanisms in Eq. (9) and the improved scaling exponent through feature learning are particularly interesting (Reviewer 5).
- **Comprehensive scaling law framework**: The paper reveals power-law relationships between loss and parameters, training time, and batch size, providing a theoretical basis for understanding neural network performance (Reviewer 3).
- **Empirical validation**: Theoretical scaling laws are checked empirically and appear to extend to some degree to deep nonlinear networks (Reviewer 1). Experiments on synthetic tasks, MNIST, and CIFAR make the conclusions more convincing (Reviewer 3).
- **Tractable theoretical setting**: The two-layer linear model extends prior one-layer linear models, allowing clearer and rigorous exploration beyond the kernel regime (Reviewer 2).
- **Well written and honest about limitations**: The paper is very pleasant to read, easy to follow, and clearly states its limitations (e.g., the CIFAR experiment failure). Derivations are explained both intuitively and formally, with technical details properly deferred (Reviewer 4).
- **Valuable contribution**: Despite studying a toy algorithm, the paper makes a valuable contribution towards a theory of scaling laws for finite, feature learning neural networks (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses

- **Unclear definition of feature learning**: The paper defines feature learning as when the empirical NTK changes, but this is insufficient (random parameter changes also alter the NTK without producing useful features). The statement that feature learning means the network operates outside the lazy training regime is also inadequate. The authors do not explain why the NTK's evolution represents a *good* evolution that leads to meaningful feature learning (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 also notes a lack of intuition for why the improvement in scaling occurs—the authors merely mention a self-consistent dynamic derived in the appendix.

- **Artificial and limited learning setup**: The analysis uses projected gradient descent with frozen first-layer weights, which is a constructed training procedure far from practical deep learning (SGD/Adam on all weights). It is unclear whether the observed scaling laws transfer to more realistic settings. The paper does not adequately justify this choice or explore the impact of training all layers (Reviewer 5). Relatedly, the model scope is very limited: allowing only \(A(t)\) to change is reasonable for the chosen target function \(W^*\psi_{inf}(x)\), but why this mimics real-world feature learning remains vague (Reviewer 2).

- **Oversimplifications and heuristic approach**: The paper uses asymptotic notation without rigorously defining the limits being taken. For example, “\(N,B\gg 1\)” lacks a precise mathematical definition. The limit \(M\to\infty\) omits many technicalities, and it is unclear why the claimed limiting properties are well-defined (Reviewer 5).

- **Lack of connection to related literature**: The paper does not relate to existing work on the dynamics of linear networks (e.g., arXiv:2211.16980, 2406.06158, 2405.17580). It would be interesting to know whether the observed dynamics is similar (Reviewer 1). The effect described here differs from other feature learning results that assume sparse structure (low-index models or low-rank matrices); here feature learning helps when the signal is nearly uniform across all directions. Reviewer 1 raises the question: does this effect disappear when switching from online learning to GD on a fixed dataset, because training speed might improve but the final solution (and test error) might be the same?

- **Missing analysis of the gamma parameter and critical regime**: The paper ignores the impact of \(\gamma\) on the feature learning scaling law and lacks an analysis of the critical state between the two regimes (Reviewer 3). The experimental results (Figures 9, 12) show that the scaling law does not change suddenly at \(\gamma=0\), but gradually at very small gamma; the paper does not cover this aspect (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 5 asks: which \(\gamma_0\) is optimal, and is it another hyperparameter requiring tuning? In experiments larger \(\gamma_0\) seems better but values considered are still small; larger \(\gamma_0\) might give better-than-predicted scaling even earlier in training (Figure 6b), which should be acknowledged.

- **Limited justification for transferability to deep nonlinear models**: It is not clear why results from the proposed linear model are expected to transfer to deep, nonlinear models (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 5 also questions whether the experiments in Section 5 train all weights (not explicitly stated). The long training regime in Figure 6b is not well covered, and Reviewer 5 asks: what is happening there? Are structures learned that are not covered by the simple distributional assumption?

- **Online setting and finite dataset extension**: A major assumption is the online setting; the extension to finite datasets (Appendix D) is limited. While this is expected from a theoretical paper, a more general analysis would be preferable (Reviewer 4).

- **Dynamics Mean Field Theory (DMFT) not explained**: The DMFT derivation is not explained in the main paper. The informal attempt in lines 202–211 introduces an equation (line 206) that is out of context with undefined notation, making it practically useless for the reader (Reviewer 4).

- **Importance of omitted constants and approximation error**: The paper reports pure scalings but omits constants that could arbitrarily change the global optimum given finite resources. To assess practical usefulness, Reviewer 5 suggests an experiment comparing predicted compute-optimal scaling to empirical loss measurements for the more practical datasets in Figure 4 (like CIFAR). Additionally, the error of the approximation in Eq. (9) is not characterized—in which cases does it fail? (Reviewer 5)

- **Scattered presentation**: The paper is difficult to follow due to heavy reliance on prior work, making it challenging for readers not deeply familiar with the foundational literature (Reviewer 2). Some minor typos appear (e.g., page 5 incomplete sentence; lines 128, 201, 202, 476). Figure colors are insufficiently differentiated (Reviewer 3). In Eq. (6), “\(\lambda_k^{-\beta}\sim k^{-\alpha\beta}\)” may be a typo (Reviewer 5). The sentence “we then diagonalize…” (line 133) could be misinterpreted as discarding non-diagonal elements (Reviewer 4).

- **Unaddressed questions**: 
  - How to choose the feature learning dynamics? The choice “allow” (line 160) appears out of nowhere. The assumption that SGD is sufficient (but not necessary) to obtain such dynamics means the derived scaling law is at least an upper bound—is this correct? (Reviewer 4)
  - A short paragraph clarifying the common points with Bordelon et al. (2024a) and the novel contribution over that paper would be helpful (Reviewer 4).
  - How would label noise affect the results? (Reviewer 5)
  - In line 192, “the components of \(\omega^*, v^0(t)\) in the k-th eigenspace of \(\Lambda\)” should refer to the k-th eigenspace of \(K_\infty\), not \(\Lambda\) (Reviewer 5).
