## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses over-smoothing in deep GNNs from a geometric perspective using Dirichlet energy. The authors propose Charge Dirichlet Energy (CDE-GNN), a method that adds a residual term weighted by the initial Dirichlet energy in the layer-wise propagation (Eq. 8). They provide theoretical analysis relating Dirichlet energy decay to edge space collapse and show that maintaining a healthy geometric structure improves trainability. Experiments on small benchmark datasets demonstrate improved performance with increasing depth, but reviewers found the evaluation limited and the novelty insufficient.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles the important problem of over-smoothing from an interesting geometric angle (R2, R3, R4).
- The presentation is well-structured, progressing from motivation to theory to solution (R1, R2, R4). R1 and R2 found it easy to follow, though R3 noted wordiness.
- The method is intuitive and generally applicable (R4).
- Empirical validation across multiple datasets shows consistent performance gains over baseline GCNs (R1, R3, R5). R5 specifically noted “extensive benchmark performance comparisons.”
- The authors revealed that Dirichlet energy decay is linearly proportional to the edge space sum with a constant \(c\), and Proposition 3.2 explains the importance of activation functions satisfying \(\phi(0)=0\) for preserving Dirichlet energy (R5). This indirectly explains why SReLU is effective.
- The minimum Dirichlet energy constrained message updating scheme showed good performance (R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty / Overlap with prior work**: Multiple reviewers noted that using Dirichlet energy or residual connections to combat over-smoothing is well studied. R1 called novelty limited; R2 pointed to GCNII (ICML 2020) and PPRGo (ICLR 2019). R5 provided a detailed comparison showing Eq. (8) is “remarkably similar” to EGNN (Zhou et al., NeurIPS 2021) when \(\beta=0\), differing only by placement of a weight matrix. R5 concluded the method is a specific instance of EGNN’s framework, with the main contribution being using initial Dirichlet energy as the lower bound. The paper must clarify how it distinguishes from or enhances EGNN, and justify omitting the weight matrix and selecting the initial Dirichlet energy among possible lower bounds (R5). (R3 also questioned how the approach compares to GCNII in preserving Dirichlet energy.)
- **Insufficient theoretical contribution and clarity**:
  - Lemma 1 is not new; it has been proved in prior works (R2 cites [3,4]; R3 and R5 also note familiarity).
  - The analysis only recapitulates the well-known fact that over-smoothing occurs with increasing layers, without explaining why or how CDE-GNN addresses it (R2).
  - The link between Proposition 3.2, edge space (Definition 3.2), Corollary 3.1, and the proposed Eq. (8) is unclear (R3). For example, how Proposition 3.2 relates to Eq. (8) and how Eq. (8) preserves Dirichlet energy is not justified (R3). R5 similarly asks for a rationale for the edge space concept and its role in justifying the method.
  - Some theoretical terms are insufficiently introduced (R4). R4 also noted that Eq. (5) appears to sum over edges twice, indicating a mistake.
- **Incomplete and questionable experimental evaluation**:
  - Experiments are limited to small datasets; missing evaluation on large graphs like ogbn-products and ogbn-proteins (R2). R2 and R3 recommend these.
  - Performance on ogbn-arxiv is much lower than the official leaderboard (R3: “remarkably lower”). The source of baseline numbers is unclear (R5 asks if results were taken from other papers or run by authors).
  - Missing comparison with closely related methods: EGNN is absent from Table 1 (R1, R5); GCNII is not compared in Table 1 either (R2). R3 asks for a comparison with EGNN, GCNII, and others on preserving Dirichlet energy.
  - No visualization of layer-wise Dirichlet energy for CDE-GNN or baselines (e.g., GCNII) to demonstrate that the proposed method prevents energy decay (R1, R3, R5). R5 explicitly asks for a plot showing Dirichlet energy converging to \(E_{\text{init}}\) as claimed.
  - No experimental validation that over-smoothing is actually addressed (e.g., t-SNE visualization or color propagation test) (R5). R2 also asks whether the problem is addressed in practice.
  - Hyperparameter analysis is not insightful: activation functions, hidden dimensions, dropout studies are generic and not unique to the method (R4). The analysis of \(\alpha\) is missing; it is not stated whether \(\alpha\) is learnable or fixed, exact values per dataset are not given, and no sensitivity study is provided (R5).
  - Several results are inconsistent: Physics dataset with 32 layers shows 94.2 in one table and 94.4 in another (R4). Table 1 has two bolded results for Citeseer and Film without explanation (R4). Semi-supervised vs. fully-supervised settings are conflated (R4).
  - Standard deviations are not reported despite results being based on 10 random splits (R5).
  - Data splits are inconsistently described: Cora/Citeseer/PubMed are said to follow Yang splits (48/32/20) but then citations and split ratios conflict (R5). R4 also notes missing marks for settings in Table 1.
  - No computational overhead analysis compared to baseline methods (R1).
- **Presentation and reproducibility issues**:
  - Wordy and repetitive: Section 4.1 and parts of Section 5.2 repeat earlier content (R3, R4). R3 also notes misuse of \citet vs. \citep.
  - Notation inconsistency: In the Introduction, “minimum Dirichlet energy \(\omega\)” is used, but thereafter replaced by \(E_{\text{init}}\) (R5).
  - Equation (8) and text contradict: Line 304 says \(E_{\text{init}}\) is multiplied by initial embeddings \(X^{(0)}\), but Eq. (8) multiplies it by \(X^{(l)}\) (R4, R5). Also, Line 080 claims the lower bound is learnable, but Section 4.2.1 fixes it as the initial energy (R4).
  - Tables 1, 2, 3 are out of page width (R5).
  - Public code is not provided, hindering reproducibility (R5).
