## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces NeuSymEA, a neuro-symbolic framework for entity alignment (EA) that integrates symbolic and neural models via variational EM optimization. It models joint alignment probabilities in a Markov random field with weighted rules, uses neural embeddings to parameterize truth score distributions, and updates rule weights in alternating steps. A path-ranking-based explainer generates supporting rules for inferred alignments. Experiments on the DBP15K benchmark show significant performance gains over baselines, with strong robustness in low-resource settings. The framework is praised for combining precision of symbolic reasoning with high recall of neural embeddings, and for providing interpretable results. Reviewer ratings range from 5 to 6 (confidences 2–4), with one reviewer (score 5) more critical of methodological exposition and validation depth, and another (score 6) questioning dataset choice and missing comparisons with newer symbolic methods.

### Strengths

- **State-of-the-art performance** on DBP15K benchmarks, significantly improving alignment effectiveness and validating the neuro-symbolic fusion approach (reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Interpretability via a path-ranking-based explainer** that provides rule-based justifications (e.g., supporting paths and confidence scores), enhancing transparency for applications requiring accountability (e.g., medical, legal) (reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Logical decomposition strategy** reduces the computational complexity of rule-based inference, enabling scaling to larger knowledge graphs and effective handling of long-tail entities (reviewer 1).
- **Novel integration** of symbolic and neural models within a unified variational EM framework that alternatively learns embeddings and path scorer, balancing interpretability and recall (reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Good overall writing** and clear presentation of the framework’s components (reviewer 4).
- **Robust performance in low-resource settings**, outperforming existing methods (reviewers 1, 2).

### Weaknesses

- **Complexity analysis missing**: The paper lacks a thorough analysis of time and space complexity, including the impact of rule length, dataset size, and the rule mining/reasoning processes on runtime and memory usage. This is needed to assess scalability to extremely large KGs (reviewers 1, 3).
- **Unclear rule definition**: The rules used (Eq. 1) are not horn rules but path pairs derived from anchor pairs. The paper does not clearly specify how these path pairs are constructed, what constraints are imposed on their structure, or how they contribute to entity alignment (reviewer 3).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Performance is sensitive to rule threshold δ and number of EM iterations; a comprehensive analysis of their influence on results is missing (reviewer 1).
- **Lack of failure case analysis**: The paper does not explore scenarios where NeuSymEA may underperform, such as extremely sparse or highly heterogeneous knowledge graphs (reviewer 1). One reviewer also notes that trade-offs between symbolic and neural components in the EM optimization are not examined in depth, especially on high-dimensional datasets where symbolic reasoning may face efficiency issues and the neural model may depend heavily on sparse data (reviewer 2).
- **Outdated dataset**: DBP15K constructs entailment links from popular entities, but the paper highlights strengths on long-tail entities without evaluating on datasets that better represent such scenarios (e.g., OpenEA). The authors are advised to conduct experiments on more recent benchmarks (reviewer 4).
- **Missing comparisons**:  
  - The paper should compare against recent methods (e.g., [1]) that also possess symbolic reasoning properties but do not require two-step optimization and an additional explainer, potentially being more effective (reviewer 4).  
  - The related work omits a relevant paper: "Xiaobin Tian, Zequn Sun, Wei Hu: Generating Explanations to Understand and Repair Embedding-Based Entity Alignment. ICDE 2024: 2205-2217" (reviewer 3).
- **PARIS performance discrepancy**: PARIS, which has strong performance on the OpenEA dataset, performs poorly in the experiments. The authors should explain this phenomenon (reviewer 4).
- **Interpretability validation lacking**: The utility of the rule-based explanations is not validated; user studies or qualitative assessments would help confirm their practical usefulness (reviewer 1).
- **Methodological clarity**: The description of the variational EM algorithm and inference steps is dense and complex for readers unfamiliar with probabilistic modeling. More illustrative diagrams or simplified explanations are needed (reviewer 1). Additionally, the rationale for using EM in this context and its connection to the problem could be better motivated at the beginning of the methodology section (reviewer 4).
- **Scalability concerns**: The framework may still face challenges with extremely large KGs due to exponential growth of the search space for rules, and no mitigation strategies are discussed (reviewer 3). One reviewer also questions whether performance will improve with more data and whether that improvement significantly differs from existing baselines (reviewer 2).
- **Rule mining complexity**: How rules are mined (e.g., using the algorithm in Appendix A.2?) and the complexity of generating supporting rules are not clarified (reviewer 3).
- **Minority reviewer contrast**: While most reviewers rated 6, one reviewer (score 5) critically noted the lack of depth in analyzing underperformance scenarios, hyperparameter sensitivity, and interpretability utility, as well as soundness concerns (soundness 2).
