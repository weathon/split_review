## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies instabilities in FP8 training of LLMs when scaled to 2 trillion tokens (20× previous limit), links the root cause to weight alignment in the SwiGLU activation function causing outlier amplification over prolonged training, and proposes Smooth‑SwiGLU to mitigate outliers. It also introduces, for the first time, FP8 quantization of both Adam optimizer moments. Experiments on a 7B LLaMA2 model (256 Intel Gaudi2 accelerators) show convergence on par with BF16 and throughput improvements of up to ~34% (5.1% over the variant that keeps SwiGLU output in BF16). All reviewers acknowledge the large‑scale empirical findings and the practical contribution of stable FP8/FP8‑optimizer training; one reviewer (score 6, conf 3) raises concerns about novelty and missing comparisons, while the other three (scores 8, conf 4/3/2) are very positive. Reviewer confidence: 4, 3, 3, 2.

### Strengths
- **Identifying the root cause of FP8 instability at trillion‑token scale.** The paper demonstrates that SwiGLU’s two weight matrices (w₁, w₂) undergo an alignment process over long training, producing extreme outliers in the activation fed to the last linear layer – a phenomenon not observable in shorter runs. This is supported by analysis of norm correlations (Figure 2b) and per‑layer outlier measurements. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- **Proposing Smooth‑SwiGLU** – a per‑channel scaling that prevents outlier overflow in FP8 quantization without altering the function’s mathematical behaviour. The design is efficiently implemented as a fused kernel. [R1, R4]
- **First demonstration of FP8 quantization for both Adam optimizer moments** (moment 1: E4M3, moment 2: E4M3). Figure 5 shows that a 100M LLaMA2 model trained with this configuration matches the BF16 loss curve. [R1, R2, R3]
- **Solid large‑scale empirical validation**: training on a 2‑trillion‑token dataset with a 7B LLaMA2 model, achieving perplexity/accuracy equal to BF16 on downstream tasks (Table 2). [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- **Concrete throughput gains**: up to ~34% improvement over BF16 (abstract/R1) and a 5.1% improvement over “FP8 + SwiGLU output BF16” on 256 Intel Gaudi2 accelerators, despite the overhead of Smooth‑SwiGLU. [R1, R4]
- **Clear writing and well‑structured presentation** of the bottleneck, analysis, and solution. [R1, R4]
- **Successful FP8 training sets a new benchmark** for scaling LLMs in FP8, addressing reproducibility and practicality. [R2]

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns.** Reviewer 3 (score 6) states that “adding a scaling factor on top of SwiGLU may not be a big novelty” because previous work has already studied smooth activation functions (e.g., Swish [170.05941], SMU [2111.04682]). The FP8 optimizer contribution is seen as “extending previous 1‑momentum FP8 to both momenta” – an incremental step. [R3]
- **Missing comparison with NVIDIA Transformer Engine FP8.** No experimental or methodological comparison is provided, making it difficult to assess how Smooth‑SwiGLU relates to existing industrial FP8 training pipelines. [R3]
- **No ablation isolating contributions of Smooth‑SwiGLU vs. FP8 optimizer.** Table 2 only compares the final combined method against BF16. Missing variants such as “FP8 + Smooth‑SwiGLU”, “FP8 + SwiGLU output BF16”, and “FP8 + SwiGLU output BF16 + FP8 Optimizer” are needed to quantify each component’s effect. [R4]
- **Marginal throughput gain vs. simpler baseline.** “FP8 + Smooth‑SwiGLU” is only 5.1% faster than keeping SwiGLU output in BF16. This small advantage may not justify the added complexity, and the experiments are limited to Intel Gaudi2 – no results on NVIDIA/AMD GPUs to check generalizability. [R4]
- **Lack of analysis for other model components.** The paper focuses on SwiGLU but does not discuss whether RMSNorm, MHA, or GQA affect FP8 training stability. Quantitative breakdown (e.g., disabling FP8 quantization per component) would help isolate root causes. [R1]
- **Sporadic nature of outliers not fully explained.** While weight alignment is identified as a cause, it is unclear why outliers appear sporadically. Do they correlate with specific input sequences or training stages? How does the alignment evolve over time? [R1]
- **No study of sensitivity to training hyperparameters.** The dynamics of w₁/w₂ norm correlation (Figure 2b) are shown for LLaMA2 defaults; additional results with different learning rates, weight decay, etc., are missing. [R1]
- **Questionable experimental choices.** Table 3 uses micro‑batch size 1, which hinders throughput and contradicts the usual goal of FP8 (maximizing batch size). Why use ZeRO‑1 instead of ZeRO‑2/3? (Table 4). In Figure 6, the BF16 loss curve shows more spikes than the FP8 curve – this is counterintuitive and needs explanation. [R3]
- **Limited applicability of trillion‑token training.** Reviewer 3 notes that billion‑token datasets are sufficient for most LLM downstream tasks (citing Apple’s paper [2407.21075]), so the trillion‑token scale may not be broadly needed. Further discussion of scenarios where this scale is beneficial would strengthen the paper. [R3]
- **Generalization beyond LLaMA2 not explored.** The method is only tested on the LLaMA2 architecture with SwiGLU. How it transfers to other architectures, other activations (e.g., GELU, ReLU), or larger model sizes (e.g., 70B) remains open. [R2, R3] (Reviewer 2 notes this; Reviewer 3 asks about second‑momentum format choice for bigger models – whether E5M2 is always needed for dynamic range.)
- **When do similar issues arise for larger models?** Would a 70B model exhibit outlier problems earlier (smaller token budget) or later? No scaling analysis is provided. [R4]
