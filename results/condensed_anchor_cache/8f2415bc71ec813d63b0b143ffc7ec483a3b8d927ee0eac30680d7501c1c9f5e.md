## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes SparseCL, a method for contradiction retrieval using sparse-aware sentence embeddings trained with a new contrastive loss based on the Hoyer sparsity measure combined with cosine similarity. The loss encourages sparsity in the difference between contradicting embeddings, motivated by the observation that contradiction pairs can be superficially similar. Experiments on the Arguana dataset and synthetic contradictions from MSMARCO and HotpotQA (generated via GPT-4) show consistent improvements over conventional contrastively trained sentence embeddings across three LLM-based retrievers (UAE-Large-V1, bge-base-en-v1.5, gte-large-en-v1.5). The method is also applied to cleaning corrupted corpora for QA retrieval. Reviewer 3 (score 5, confidence 4) is more critical than the other reviewers (scores 6), raising concerns about dataset scale, generalization, and data contamination. Reviewer 4 (score 6, confidence 5) acknowledges the interesting idea but argues several major components need addressing before the work reaches publication quality.

### Strengths
- The problem of contradiction retrieval is important and practically valuable (e.g., fact checking, data cleaning, scientific claim verification). The paper clearly points out limitations of existing bi-encoder and cross-encoder approaches. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- A new contrastive loss is proposed for training sentence embeddings specifically for contradiction retrieval, motivated by the failure of traditional contrastive learning (which favors similarity). (R1, R4)
- The idea of using sparse-aware embeddings with the Hoyer sparsity measure is intuitive, well-motivated, and novel. The Hoyer measure is non-transitive, which helps distinguish indirectly related but contradictory sentences. This is the first method for contradiction retrieval that relies solely on sentence embeddings. (R2, R3, R4)
- The method is empirically effective, outperforming baselines by large margins (more than 30% accuracy improvements on MSMARCO and HotpotQA synthesized splits). (R1, R2)
- Experiments are comprehensive: SparseCL is plugged into various LLM-based retrievers to validate generalizability, and ablation studies are conducted to verify design choices. (R2)
- SparseCL maintains high performance compared with cross-encoders while using simple vector calculations, making it computationally efficient. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Need for more explanation and analysis on why sparsity-based distance works better for contradiction retrieval.** The motivation is mentioned but not deeply supported with analysis or examples. (R1)
- **Incorrect statement:** The Introduction claims that the cosine similarity metric is transitive. This is incorrect, especially in high-dimensional vector spaces. (R1)
- **Experiments lack quantitative analysis or qualitative examples on the role of the sparsity loss.** (R1)
- **Paper needs polishing for formal writing; there are typos (e.g., Line 225 "can can").** (R1)
- **Statistical significance tests are not conducted.** In Table 1, the improvement of X+ZeroShot+SparseCL over X+ZeroShot is small in some columns, and it is unclear whether gaps are significant. A two-tailed t-test should be reported. (R2)
- **Only one real dataset (Arguana) is used; the other two are synthesized from MSMARCO and HotpotQA using GPT-4.** Arguana is small (<7k argument-counterargument pairs). Additional real datasets, such as SciFact (scientific claim refutation), should be explored to validate the approach. (R2, R3)
- **The scalar α in the scoring function F(p1,p2) = α·cos_sim + (1–α)·Sparsity requires tuning on the target dataset**, which limits generalization and suggests the method cannot automatically adapt to unseen data (domain shift). (R3)
- **Potential data contamination between MSMARCO and HotpotQA**: HotpotQA uses Wikipedia, and MSMARCO is derived from Bing search logs that likely include some Wikipedia documents. The paper must address whether the model is tested on truly unseen data before claiming generalization of the embeddings. (R3)
- **Missing baseline: appending an instruction prefix (e.g., "Contradicted to: [INPUT]") to adapt contradiction to similarity** (Instructor-style approach). Outperforming this baseline would establish the necessity of learning a sparse metric; otherwise, context switching merged with general similarity learning may be preferred. (R4)
- **Unstable selection of α and insufficient justification for why semantic similarity should play a weighted role in contradiction retrieval.** For example, a direct contradiction (e.g., "Dinosaurs live on earth today" vs. "Dinosaurs don't live on earth today") and a less similar contradiction (e.g., "A huge asteroid hit the earth 65 million years ago and caused the extinction of dinosaurs") show that contradiction can be independent of textual similarity. Semantic similarity should perhaps be treated as a threshold, not a weighted term; real-world distribution analysis would help. (R4)
- **Claim that the Hoyer function has an advantage due to its intransitive nature is not supported.** There is no baseline trained on reversed similarity labels (i.e., contradicting pairs pushed apart, similar pairs pulled together under cosine). Such a baseline would be needed to show that sparsity, not just the intransitive metric, is beneficial. (R4)
- **The synthesized datasets (MSMARCO, HotpotQA) likely favor the proposed method** because GPT-4 generates contradictions that are similar texts with sparse differences (e.g., negations, small edits). In reality, contradictions can occur between texts with very different length, style, and format. The paper should generate more diverse contradictory pairs (e.g., with different attributes) to avoid oversimplifying the problem. (R4)
- **Lack of baselines:** The main comparison only includes a contrastively learned encoder unintended for contradiction retrieval. Simple baselines (e.g., instruction-prefix version of Instructor) and proper baselines for the new task should be added. (R4)
- **Lack of task significance justification:** The paper introduces a narrow-scope task without fully demonstrating its importance or impact on downstream applications (e.g., fact checking, claim verification) via concrete improvement. (R4)
- **Why not include NLI datasets or fact-checking datasets?** They directly contain contradicted text pairs and could serve as natural benchmarks. (R3, R4)
- **Questions from reviewers that overlap with missing experiments or ablations:**
  - For the synthetic MSMARCO and HotpotQA tasks, do the train/dev/test splits stem from different sets of original passages, or can there be overlap? (R1)
  - Can a statistical significance test (e.g., two-tailed t-test) be conducted to compare models and report p-values? (R2)
  - Can SparseCL be evaluated on the SciFact benchmark dataset (original or BEIR version) to retrieve paper abstracts that refute a given query claim? (R2)
  - How would the method define contradiction for non-statements like questions? (R4)
  - Check reference format (e.g., line 237–238). (R3)
