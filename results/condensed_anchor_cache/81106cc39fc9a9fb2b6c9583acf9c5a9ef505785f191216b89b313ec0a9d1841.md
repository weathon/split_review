## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Implicit Neural Clustering, which equates clustering with disentangled representation learning by manipulating factors of variation in a latent space to generate implicit clusters. The method is evaluated on synthetic datasets: dSprites, 3D Shapes, MPI3D. One set of reviewers (ratings 3,3,3,3) finds the theoretical insights novel but the practical applicability limited; two reviewers (ratings 5,5) find the perspective fresh and the experiments adequate for synthetic benchmarks, but still note limitations in real-world validation.

### Strengths
- **Novel theoretical perspective**: Equating clustering with disentanglement is a fresh angle (Reviewers 1,3,4,5).
- **Important problem**: Learning from limited combinations of factors and generating novel unseen combinations is challenging and relevant (Reviewer 2,3,4,5).
- **Qualitative and quantitative experiments**: Comprehensive evaluation on common synthetic disentanglement benchmarks (dSprites, 3D Shapes, MPI3D) and multiple ablation studies (Reviewers 1,3,4). The experiments effectively demonstrate the ability to form multi-partitioning clusters without assuming the number of clusters a priori (Reviewer 4).
- **Clear method details**: Technical details, pseudo-code, and illustrative figures are provided, aiding reproducibility (Reviewer 3).
- **Clustering without predefined similarity**: Valuable for datasets where similarity is difficult to define (Reviewer 6).
- **Potential applications**: Data augmentation, interpretability, data-efficient ML, especially in cost-limited domains like medical imaging (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
- **No experiments on real-world datasets**: All experiments are on synthetic datasets (dSprites, 3D Shapes, MPI3D). Real-world validation (e.g., CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, or medical data) is missing. Reviewers 1,3,4,5,6 explicitly call for experiments on non-synthetic data to verify practical scalability.
- **Strong assumptions**: The method relies on a disentangled latent space and identifiable encoder, which are strong and often unrealistic (Reviewers 5,6). The assumption is not fully resolved for self-supervised methods (Reviewer 4). One reviewer asks: “Would this hold in practice?” (Reviewer 5).
- **Lack of baselines**: No comparisons against existing clustering or generative methods (e.g., deep clustering, stable diffusion, or simple latent space traversal). Reviewer 6 notes missing baselines for both clustering performance and generative capabilities. Reviewer 4 asks for comparison to naive latent traversal sampling.
- **Combinatorial generalization not experimentally validated**: The paper claims combinatorial generalization but provides limited analysis. Reviewers 5 and 2 ask for experiments showing how well the method handles unseen factor combinations.
- **Unclear notation and presentation**: Many terms are undefined or confusing: “lower bound” with operator $<\approx$, “atomic group actions”, “combinatorial generalization”, “explicit vs implicit clusters”. Reviewers 1,2,5,6 note that Sections 2.1 and 2.3 are hard to follow. Notation is overloaded (e.g., $G$ for group action and generator, $\mathcal{D}_i’$ used for two different sets). Reviewer 2 states the symbol $<\approx$ is not explained.
- **Qualitative measure for disentanglement lacks justification**: The proposed measure (“more informative than Hinton Matrices”) is introduced without evidence of effectiveness or quantitative validation (Reviewers 2,4). Reviewers 5 and 6 say the atomic group action plots are not explained.
- **Missing proofreading and incomplete citations**: In-line citations like “eastwood2018framework, montero2022lost” appear incomplete (Reviewers 2,3). Appendix looks unfinished (Reviewer 2). Missing reference to original VAE paper (Kingma & Welling 2014) (Reviewer 6).
- **Limited scope of method and experiments**:
  - Only tested within VAE latent spaces; not extended to modern generative models (e.g., diffusion models) (Reviewer 4).
  - No runtime analysis or complexity evaluation (Reviewer 3).
  - Related work on alternative clustering / non-redundant clustering is missing (Reviewer 3).
- **Overclaimed advantages**: Claims of benefits for SSL, classification, data storage, etc. are not supported by experiments on small toy datasets (Reviewer 1). The paper references the impossibility result for unsupervised disentanglement (Locatello et al., 2018) but does not address it (Reviewer 1).
- **Minor issues**:
  - Code not available (only upon acceptance); claims cannot be checked (Reviewer 2).
  - Title not explained (“The Sky Is The Limit” never used in text) (Reviewer 2).
  - Table 2 unclear: what is being clustered? Are results on train or test split? Are clustering metrics (ACC, NMI) computed with ground-truth labels? How many clusters does KDE find? Does it discover the true number? (Reviewers 3,6).
- **Reviewer concern about self-consuming models**: Reviewer 4 asks if using generated samples to train generative models could lead to degenerate results (citing Alemohammad, ICLR 2023).
- **Missing definition**: Function $\cdot(\cdot,\cdot)$ and the term “combinatorial generalization” are not defined (Reviewer 6).
- **Unclear lower bound**: The notion of lower bound (Equation 3) is mathematically vague; not explained how one set is a “lower bound” of another (Reviewers 2,6).
