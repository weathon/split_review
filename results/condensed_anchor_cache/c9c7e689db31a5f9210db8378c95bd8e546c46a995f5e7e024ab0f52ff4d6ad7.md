## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes LSTR (Long-short Range Aggregation) for vehicle trajectory prediction at intersections, combining local interaction modeling with global intention inference and evaluating on inD, rounD, and a WOMD subset. The four reviewers gave scores 6, 3, 3, 6, revealing strong disagreement. R1 and R4 (score 6) found the approach novel and well-presented with promising benchmark results. R2 and R3 (score 3) raised significant concerns about novelty, motivation clarity, lack of implementation details, insufficient baselines, and inconsistent use of map information.

### Strengths

- **Focus on intersection-specific challenges**: The paper highlights the two core challenges of intersection trajectory prediction – local interaction and global intention – addressing a relatively less explored problem (R2, R4). R4 specifically values modeling global/local intents under no or sparsely annotated lane information.
- **Novel architecture design**: R1 praises the unique methodology of focusing on entry/exit points in the Macroscopic Traffic Flow Generation module, with sequential refinement (local path planning then global exit point tuning), viewing it as a creative synthesis of existing concepts. R3 acknowledges that integrating local behavior decision-making with global intention inference effectively captures both short-range and long-range patterns.
- **Strong experimental benchmarking**: The method is evaluated on multiple real-world intersection datasets (inD, rounD, WOMD) and demonstrates performance improvements over baselines (R1, R3, R4). R1 notes a 4.5 improvement in b-minFDE6 on inD and a 4.2 improvement in minADE6 on rounD over the next best method. R2 also notes that ablations show the effectiveness of the major modules (LBDM, GIIM, decoder).
- **Presentation quality**: R2 and R4 find the paper well-written and easy to follow, with clear problem definition, equations, and figures (R2, R4). R2 specifically mentions that the paper enhances understanding.

### Weaknesses

1. **Insufficient novelty and unclear contributions relative to existing methods**:
   - R1 (score 6, confidence 3) notes that the COP head with self-attention in LBDM is not a significant innovation; it relies on historical data to directly predict future states. R1 also asks how LBDM deviates from existing prediction methods (Q1).
   - R2 (score 3, confidence 4) states that LSTR integrates several existing techniques (MTR encoder [1], HiVT encoder [2], QCNet offset predictor [3]) except pattern re-featuring and map filtering in GIIM, which are described only briefly. R2 questions why intersection scenarios need a custom framework and how the differences contribute.
   - R3 (score 3, confidence 5) finds that the method seems a combination of HiVT (CVPR ‘22) and QCNet (CVPR ’24) with no substantial distinction; the author may not fully understand terms like "anchors." R3 also notes limited 2024 references and that related work should include map-free approaches. R3 states the paper appears to "fit the solution to the problem," giving an impression of a model assembled without genuine innovation. Additionally, the claim of interpretable predictions lacks evidence or explanation (R3, weakness 12).

2. **Unclear motivation, ambiguous terminology, and lack of definitions**:
   - R2 (score 3) finds the motivation and contributions unclear.
   - R3 (score 3, confidence 5) says the challenges in the Introduction are inadequately supported; the paper fails to discuss map-free methods. Core challenges lack references; terms like local interaction, global interaction, and route choice pattern lack standardized definitions. Terms such as short-range motion patterns, long-term dynamics, and anchors global intentions are unclear and unusual in autonomous trajectory prediction, making the paper hard to interpret.
   - R4 (score 6, confidence 4) points out that the motivation claims existing methods fail in intersections without detailed annotations, but experiments use intersections with detailed map annotations (Fig. 3) and the proposed method consumes lane graphs (Fig. 2). It is not fully justified whether LSTR outperforms under no detailed map annotations.

3. **Missing implementation details and computational efficiency**:
   - R1 (score 6) notes that the paper lacks the model’s parameter count and would benefit from a comparative analysis of parameter volume with other models.
   - R2 (score 3) states that hyperparameters (hidden size, number of layers, radius for collecting agent/map information) are missing. R2 also asks for parameter size and inference latency compared to baselines.
   - R4 (score 6) notes minor typos (e.g., "flows Zhao et al. (2023).Microscopic" missing space) and asks for the criteria used for selecting the WOMD subset.

4. **Inconsistent use of map information and reliance on maps despite criticism**:
   - R3 (score 3, confidence 5) observes that the approach section does not specify which map information is used; the paper initially criticizes other methods for over-relying on map information, yet LSTR uses map information, leading to inconsistency. R3 also asks whether the rounD dataset includes detailed map information (given drone collection). R1’s question Q2 (why disable map features in backbone for fairness; how does that affect reliance on environmental information) also points to this issue.
   - R4 (score 6) raises the same map-annotation inconsistency as in weakness 2 above.

5. **Experimental flaws: insufficient baselines, metrics, ablation, and validity concerns**:
   - R4 (score 6) complains that on WOMD, only MTRv3 (based on reimplementation) is compared; more SOTA comparisons (especially open-sourced methods) are needed.
   - R3 (score 3, confidence 5) states that baselines are relatively old; more recent baselines should be included. The paper also lacks ablation studies and a discussion of limitations/future work. R3 questions the use of “Argoverse competition metrics” for inD, rounD, and WOMD, asking why not use suitable metrics for those datasets. R3 is skeptical of the results’ validity and suggests experiments on benchmark datasets like Argoverse or NuScenes. Qualitative analysis figures (Fig. 3) are unclear, making it difficult to distinguish ground truth from predictions; using WOMD’s official API could provide clearer insights. R1’s question Q3 (how the model adapts to scenarios with high VRUs) indicates missing analysis for an important use case.
   - R2 (score 3) also requests inference latency comparison.

6. **Writing, formatting, and presentation issues**:
   - R1 (score 6) notes that Table 3 is misplaced in the Conclusion section and that referencing style is inconsistent.
   - R2 (score 3) reports missing legend caption in Fig. 1 and typos on lines 71 and 282. R2 also questions whether MmTransformer [4] belongs to the MTR [1] series.
   - R3 (score 3, confidence 5) points out misplaced quotation marks (lines 41,52,80,89), missing spaces after numbers (lines 85,89,92), undefined acronyms (e.g., VRUs line 304), and formatting artefacts (bolding of “b-minFDEk” line 320). R3 also notes heavy reliance on LLMs for drafting, resulting in vague, verbose writing lacking manual refinement.
   - R4 (score 6) mentions minor typos.
