## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces DS-Prover, a dynamic sampling method for neural theorem proving in Lean that adjusts the number of tactics explored per goal based on remaining time, and a data augmentation that decomposes compound tactics (e.g., `simp` and `rewrite` with multiple premises) into sequences of single-premise tactics. Experiments are conducted on Mathlib, MiniF2F, and ProofNet using a fine-tuned ByT5 model. Reviewers are divided: one (score 6) found the approach well-explained, reproducible, and yielding a new SOTA; three others (scores 3,3,3) deemed the improvements incremental, the SOTA claim misleading, the ablations insufficient, and the writing/presentation poor.

### Strengths

- The idea of incorporating remaining time into the explore-exploit tradeoff for tactic search is novel and of practical significance, especially for low-budget neural theorem proving that could benefit daily interactive theorem prover (ITP) users (R1, R2, R3).
- The methods are straightforward, easy to implement, and bring measurable performance gains over the Fixed Sampling baseline (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The paper provides Lean code examples, which help readers unfamiliar with Lean understand the approach (R3).
- The model and training data are open-source; a public website with the trained model was promised (though not provided at review time) (R2, R4).
- The experiments show improved results on MiniF2F (30.6%) and ProofNet (13.65%), with small models (R2).

### Weaknesses

- **SOTA claim is misleading.** The paper claims “a new state-of-the-art performance of 30.6% on MiniF2F using Lean,” but the HyperTree Proof Search (HTPS) paper (Lample et al., 2022) reported >40% success rate on the same dataset at pass@64, versus the paper’s pass@1. Without controlling for compute or evaluation protocol, the claim is inaccurate. Experiments at higher pass@k are needed to substantiate it (R1, R4). Reviewer 4 calls this “critical” (R4).
- **Unfair or incomplete comparisons in Table 1.** The time budgets used by baselines (LeanDojo, its “optimized” version) versus DS-Prover are not reported, making it impossible to assess if gains are due to more time rather than the method (R2). “Original data” columns show only a 0.4-point improvement over the optimized LeanDojo (R3). Adding time per problem to Table 1 is requested (R2).
- **Data augmentation’s effect is unclear and seemingly not beneficial across domains.** No experiment isolates the augmentation alone (without standardization), so its independent contribution cannot be judged (R3). Moreover, the augmentation helps on MiniF2F but is detrimental on ProofNet (harder problems) and on Mathlib (wider domains), suggesting it is not a generally positive technique (R4). The revised paper adds standardization (tactic conversion) that confounds the augmentation effect; the original paper showed no improvement on ProofNet/Mathlib, raising doubt that the augmentation itself matters (R3).
- **Missing ablations and analysis.** The paper lacks detailed analysis of the time tradeoff in Figure 2; the difference between dynamic and fixed sampling is nearly constant across time budgets, which suggests dynamic sampling works only for short proofs (R3). Yet the updated paper claims it is especially effective for long proofs, creating an unexplained inconsistency (R3). No proof-length distribution comparison or qualitative examples contrasting dynamic and fixed sampling are provided (R1, R2). The number of tactics sampled in fixed sampling is not stated, nor is an ablation of varying that number presented (R3). The tradeoff between exploration and exploitation (e.g., which proof sizes benefit) is not examined (R2).
- **Insufficient and poorly structured related work.** Background on tactic prediction in other ITPs (Coq, HOL4) is omitted, especially discussions of atomic vs. compound tactics (e.g., arXiv:1905.09381, Proverbot9001) (R1). The related works section is a stack of papers without comparison or contrast (R4). Highly influential non-Lean research is missing (R2).
- **Writing and presentation issues.** Citations are glued to text without proper spacing or use of \citep/\citet (R1, R3, R4). Several sentences need polishing (e.g., “where anyone can put the formal statement in Lean for their mathematical theorem”) (R1). The description of the `assumption` tactic is inaccurate: “one never assumes the goal to be true” (R4). The claim of releasing a public theorem-prover website is not verifiable because the website is not provided (R4).
- **Missing experimental details.** The number of training steps, training/validation metrics, and wallclock time limits per problem for MiniF2F, ProofNet, and Mathlib are not given (R4). Whether code and model weights will be open-sourced is unclear (R2).
- **Potential misleading of prior work.** Reference to Polu & Sutskever (2020) as an “alternative attempt” is odd, as that work is arguably the first to use generative transformers with ITPs (R4).
- **Disagreement among reviewers.** While one reviewer (R2, score 6) found the contribution straightforward and providing SOTA, three others (R1,R3,R4, scores 3) consider the improvements incremental and the contributions minor. No consensus on significance.
