- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 3, 1, 1

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper explores using large language models (LLMs) to automatically extract parameters (neuron morphology, synapse dynamics, receptor properties) from scientific literature for building biologically realistic neural models, particularly spiking neural networks (SNNs). The authors evaluate different prompting strategies (general extraction, in-context hints, masked prompting) and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) via LangChain to construct knowledge graphs (KGs), with SNNBuilder as a validation example. Most reviewers found the topic promising and relevant (bridging AI and neuroscience, automating KG construction), but nearly all agreed that the work is a preliminary project report rather than a mature research paper, with severe shortcomings in experimental validation, clarity, and rigor relative to ICLR standards.

### Strengths
- **Interesting topic and good intuition**: Using LLMs to extract parameters for building SNNs is a timely and relevant problem. The core idea of automating parameter extraction from literature to bridge AI and neuroscience has practical value. (Reviewers 1,2,4)
- **Relevant research questions**: The paper tackles the challenge of automatically building and updating knowledge graphs from unstructured data, and converting complex neuroscience data into computable models—both important and underexplored areas. (Reviewer 3,4)
- **Use of open-source LLMs** ensures wider applicability and reproducibility. (Reviewer 3)
- **Acknowledgment of limitations** in the conclusions. (Reviewer 3)
- **Potential synergy between LLMs and KGs** is correctly identified: LLMs can help parse natural language and KGs can alleviate hallucinations. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Formatting and presentation issues are severe**  
  - The paper does not follow the ICLR template; the main text has incorrect/narrow margins. (Reviewers 1,2,4)  
  - Many formulations are imprecise or contain errors: “promoting strategies” (presumably “prompting”), “one-shot promoting”, spelling errors (“nda”, “sparge”), unclear phrases like “parameterization of brain models”, “agumenting external data”, “brain data”, “Structurally, this loss is a critical limitation…” (Reviewer 3)  
  - The paper reads as a preliminary project report rather than a research paper; it is too short, with insufficient depth and rigor. (Reviewers 2,4)  
  - Figure 3 is mostly unreadable and contains non-neuroscience terms like “Keep”, “To”, “It”, “Assuming”, “Based”, “Name:”, “Not”, “\”, “don’t” – indicating many extracted entities are meaningless. (Reviewers 3,4)  
  - The structuring is confusing: “Preliminaries” section describes experiments/methods unrelated to the main approach; some results and additional experiments are introduced only in the Discussion. (Reviewer 3)

- **Lack of novelty and insufficient methodological detail**  
  - The only methodological contribution is proposing three prompting strategies; no further technical advances beyond this. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Many details are missing: How was the hand-authored ontology derived (section 3.1)? How are Figure 1 and the ontology related? What are the exact equations for “Leiden Modularity”? What is the corpus of papers? (Reviewer 3)  
  - It is unclear how the proposed methods (Section 4) alleviate the hierarchy-maintenance problems identified in Section 3 (e.g., “LLM with Graph RAG KG generation can struggle to maintain hierarchy”). (Reviewer 4)  
  - The characterization of KGs using Leiden Modularity and Average Degree Centrality is not explained in relation to the paper’s claims. (Reviewer 3)  
  - Entity disambiguation, a key problem in KG construction, is not addressed despite using existing tools (GraphRAG/Neo4j) that are insufficient; no further contribution is made. (Reviewer 4)

- **Insufficient experiments and missing evaluation of claims**  
  - The accuracy of parameter extraction was not evaluated. (Reviewers 1,3)  
  - The “data-to-model” process was not evaluated. (Reviewer 3)  
  - SNNBuilder was not actually used in any evaluation—it is unclear how/where it was employed. (Reviewers 3,4)  
  - The suitability of the generated KGs for computational neuroscience model generation/configuration is neither qualitatively nor quantitatively shown. (Reviewer 3)  
  - There is no measurement of whether the extracted parameters are biologically correct or meaningful for the ML community (e.g., could they lead to better models?). (Reviewer 4)  
  - The paper lacks any comprehensive experimental analysis or validation; only minimal experiments are presented. (Reviewers 1,2)  
  - Abstract claims about “improving accuracy of parameter extraction” and “enhancing the data-to-model process” are not supported by the reported results. (Reviewer 3)  
  - The analysis of the generated graphs (Leiden Modularity, etc.) does not connect to any claim about model constructability. (Reviewer 3)

- **Missing comparisons with closely related work**  
  - The related literature section omits methodologically closer papers. (Reviewer 3)

- **Unanswered questions that expose missing experiments/analyses** (from reviewers)  
  - Which paper was the source for Table 1? How was the ontology in 3.1 derived? What exactly is the corpus of papers? (Reviewer 3)  
  - Were the “different parts of the ontology from 3.1” actually used (line 286)? (Reviewer 3)  
  - Does the extracted SNN triple (entity, relation, entity) strictly follow the prompt? How does the method alleviate the hierarchy issue? (Reviewer 4)  
  - How to measure the quality of the LLM-extracted KG given the many meaningless entities in Figure 3? (Reviewer 4)  
  - Are the SNN models generated by LLM biologically correct and usable? (Reviewer 4)

- **Minority contrast**: Although a few reviewers (scores 5 and 3) found the topic interesting and the intuition good, they also noted the paper lacks experimental results and needs extensive restructuring. The two low-scoring reviewers (1,1) are much more critical, pointing out severe failures in evaluation, clarity, and scientific contribution. (Reviewers 1,2 vs. 3,4)
