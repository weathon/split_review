## Merged Review

### Summary

Video Active Perception (VAP) is a training-free, VLM-agnostic method for long-form video QA that uses a text-conditioned video generation model as prior world knowledge to select key frames. By uniformly sampling a small number of frames and then using a diffusion model to interpolate/generate intermediate latents, VAP identifies frames that most diverge from the expected dynamics (surprising frames) and feeds only those to a VLM for inference. It achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot results on EgoSchema, NExT-QA, ActivityNet-QA, and CLEVRER with up to 5.6× reduction in frames per question compared to standard GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, and LLaVA-OV.

### Strengths

- **Novel approach inspired by active perception theory** – using a lightweight, text-conditioned video generation model as a prior to select key frames that deviate from expected dynamics. This contrasts with traditional key-frame extraction or captioning-based methods. (R1, R2, R3, R5)
- **Training-free and VLM-agnostic** – works with both open-source and proprietary VLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, LLaVA-OV) without requiring fine-tuning or additional training. (R1)
- **Extensive evaluation on multiple long-form video QA benchmarks** – EgoSchema, NExT-QA, ActivityNet-QA, and CLEVRER – with strong results. (R1, R3, R5)
- **Demonstrated efficiency improvement** – reduces the number of frames required for VLM inference by up to 5.6×, lowering prompting cost. (R1, R4)
- **Shows that video diffusion models can be used to predict/anticipate frames** for the purpose of key-frame selection. (R5)
- **Paper is well-structured and clearly written**, with a clear motivation and systematic presentation of technical details. (R1, R2, R3)

### Weaknesses

1. **Incomplete efficiency analysis** – The claimed efficiency gain (fewer frames) is not accompanied by a full accounting of the computational overhead of the additional components: 3D Causal VAE, video diffusion model, and interpolation model. Metrics such as wall-clock time, FLOPs, memory usage, and total runtime are absent. Several reviewers noted that the method may be more computationally expensive than existing approaches when considering the full pipeline. (R1, R3, R4, R5)  
   - R1 asks for a detailed breakdown of the costs of the extra models and a comparison to the savings from reduced VLM inference.  
   - R3 questions whether the claimed efficiency translates to practical runtime or FLOP/memory savings.  
   - R4 suggests including number of inserted captions and inference speed in Table 1.  
   - R5 points out that Figure 3 compares average number of frames instead of latency/FLOPs.

2. **Unfair comparisons with prior methods** – Several baselines (VideoAgent, VideoTree) are evaluated with older GPT-4, while VAP uses GPT-4o. This confounds whether performance gains come from VAP or the stronger VLM. (R1, R3, R4)  
   - R1 and R3 request experiments with GPT-4 to ensure a fair comparison.  
   - R4 also notes that comparison with LVNet uses GPT-4o vs. GPT-4o mini, and recommends directly comparing VAP to LVNet using the same number of frames and the same VLM.

3. **Modest or inconsistent performance gains** – On EgoSchema, VAP improves only 0.2% over the GPT-4o baseline (R1). On CLEVRER, VAP underperforms some baselines (R1). R3 notes that gains in Table 1 are generally minimal. R4 specifically contrasts: LVNet achieves 61.1% accuracy using 12 frames with GPT-4o on EgoSchema, whereas VAP attains 58.6% with 16 frames and GPT-4o mini – lower accuracy despite more frames, suggesting VAP’s key-frame selection may be less effective. (R4)

4. **Missing evaluations and concerns about generalizability**  
   - VAP has not been tested on popular long-video benchmarks such as VideoMME, MLVU, and LongVideoBench. (R2, R5)  
   - Underperformance on CLEVRER is not explained; specific aspects of CLEVRER (counterfactual or explanatory tasks) may make VAP less effective. (R1)  
   - Performance on NExT-QA is reported lower than in the VideoTree paper; the discrepancy is not explained. (R2)  
   - Comparison with LVNet on IntentQA is missing, which would help test generalizability on diverse very long-form datasets. (R4)

5. **Potential loss of critical information from initial random sampling** – The method first uniformly samples a small number of frames; those frames are then excluded from being selected as key frames. If any randomly sampled frame contains critical information, it may be lost. (R2)

6. **Lack of details and doubt about fidelity of the diffusion prior** – Missing details about the generation process: which frames are generated (intermediate/subsequent), whether any masking is used, exact conditioning, number of denoising steps, model scale, and whether transformer layers in L146 are pretrained. (R3)  
   - R3 also questions the fidelity/usefulness of generated frames and suggests converting latents back to pixel space for visualization to compare with actual frames.

7. **Motivation concerns for generic questions** – The method always selects frames that deviate from the expected dynamics (surprising frames). For questions about normal or vanilla dynamics (e.g., asking about the generic/prominent activity), sampling surprising frames may not be beneficial. The authors should explain how VAP generalizes to such scenarios. (R3)

8. **Scalability to extremely long videos** – The reliance on a video generation model may limit the method when videos are extremely long, as it is hard for such models to capture long-term temporal dependencies (e.g., continuous surveillance footage). (R1)
