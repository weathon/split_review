## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses concept drift in Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) from long-tailed data (gradual drift) and Out-Of-Distribution (OOD) data (sudden drift) during pre-training. It proposes a unified framework extending concept drift theory to the multi-modal domain, with a T-distribution based drift adapter to mitigate gradual-drift bias and distinguish sudden changes. Experiments show improved image-text alignment and downstream task performance (long-tailed classification, OOD detection). A new multi-modal dataset, OpenMMlo, is created for long-tailed open world settings. Reviewers acknowledge the novelty, clear motivation, good performance, and theoretical grounding, but raise significant concerns about experimental setup fairness, clarity of method comparisons (e.g., to CLIP), missing ablations, lack of VL-model downstream evaluations, and dataset definition.

### Strengths
- **Novel contribution**: Extends concept drift theory to the multi-modal domain, providing a new perspective on adapting VL models to distribution changes (Reviewers 2, 3).  
- **Effective mitigation**: The T-distribution based drift adapter addresses gradual drift biases and distinguishes sudden distribution changes; properly theoretically motivated with correct math (Reviewers 2, 3). Shows substantial improvements over SOTA across most settings (Reviewer 4).  
- **Empirical validation**: Extensive experiments on long-tailed classification and OOD detection; new OpenMMlo dataset tailored for long-tailed open world settings (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Good performance on both long-tail and OOD datasets (Reviewer 1). Results show substantial improvements over SOTA baselines (Reviewer 4). Clear illustration of tailed drift and OOD drift issues (Reviewer 3).  
- **Presentation**: Well-structured and easy to follow (Reviewer 4). Clear motivation with detailed experiments and figures (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Experimental setup and unfair comparisons** (Reviewers 1, 2, 4): Only training results on OpenMMlo are shown, making comparisons with other methods unfair; training strategy should be aligned with remaining methods. The introduction of additional data (OpenMMlo), larger resolution (336×336 vs. standard 224), and extra modules (image-grounded decoder) makes it impossible to evaluate the method alone. LIVT should be compared at corresponding resolution. In Fig. 1a, it is unclear whether test sets for BL and LT trained models are the same and whether they are balanced. It is also unclear whether OOD samples are included in pre-training or only for evaluation, and if they come from single or multiple distributions. In Fig. 1b, it is unclear whether the VL model was fine-tuned on a downstream class-imbalanced dataset; does fine-tuning on balanced data mitigate pre-training imbalance? All downstream tasks only test on vision models; experiments on VL models (long-tailed classification, OOD detection) are needed (Reviewer 4).  
- **Missing introduction/evaluation of the image-grounded decoder and text modeling loss** (Reviewer 1): The motivation and function of this module are not introduced or evaluated in methods or experiments. How can the method only fine-tune using a pretrained CLIP model if the text decoder is trained from the pretrain stage?  
- **Lack of comparison to CLIP and clarity on architectural differences** (Reviewer 3): The manuscript lacks clarity on how the proposed method compares to CLIP; related work fails to draw a clear comparison. It is unclear how the T-distribution adapter and training regime differ from CLIP’s contrastive learning, and whether improvements are due to the adapter or other architectural differences. Quantitative results on the same datasets are needed.  
- **Insufficient motivation and details of the method** (Reviewers 3, 4): Section 2 defines concept shift and the T-distribution adapter but does not address how the architecture works. More details needed on how each loss (e.g., CE loss) is calculated and on which labels. It is unclear whether the T-distribution adapter interacts with CE loss and is applied to the image encoder, text encoder, or both. Reviewers ask for more ablation studies on the T-distributed spherical adapter to identify which parts adapt to concept drift and benefit downstream tasks (Reviewer 4).  
- **Similarity to existing work** (Reviewer 2): The intra-class compactness and T-distribution are somewhat similar to those in [a] (Structurally Regularized Deep Clustering, TPAMI 2022). A detailed discussion of differences in objective function, optimization process, and how intra-class compactness is achieved is needed.  
- **Dataset issues and definition** (Reviewers 1, 3): OpenMMlo alone is insufficient for evaluation; experiments must be comparable to other methods. What advantage does OpenMMlo have over WIT? Are there other benefits besides supporting long-tailed study? The paper should discuss trade-offs of using a long-tailed dataset for pre-training when balanced datasets are generally preferred; is the long-tail deliberate or a limitation? Additionally, how is long-tailed data defined in image-caption pairs without class labels—does it refer to image categories, word frequency, or other metrics?  
- **Clarification of concept drift in the pre-training context** (Reviewers 2, 3, 4): Why are long-tail and OOD issues particularly relevant during pre-training—how do they emerge, affect feature representation and generalization, and does the model overfit to head classes? Should sub-distributions (image styles, lighting, text styles) be considered? Does increasing dataset size amplify head bias or offer inherent mitigation? Would pre-training on WIT mostly alleviate the issues? What does “also encounter concept drift” mean in the related work—does WIT itself have drift, or does it arise when transferring? Are pre-training experiments conducted in different stages due to evolving distributions? Can the T-distribution-based drift adapter handle domain shift (e.g., ImageNet-Sketch) as well? (Reviewer 4)  
- **Typos and writing quality** (Reviewers 1, 2, 3): Fig. 1a has “LL” which should be “LT” (noted by multiple reviewers). Abstract: “distribution unpredictable changes” should be “unpredictable distribution changes”. Writing and flow need improvement for clarity and coherence.
