## Merged Review

### Summary
The proposed PIG integrates imputation and prediction by learning feature relationships via a Graph Learning Layer (GLL), a residual graph layer (GRIL), and a prediction layer (PL). Pre-training uses only non-missing values; fine-tuning uses missing inputs initially imputed with Soft-Impute. Two reviewers are very positive (score 5), one is negative (score 3) citing strong similarity to MIRACLE and limited novelty. All three agree the model shows strong empirical results but question certain design choices and fairness of comparisons.

### Strengths
- Integrates imputation with downstream analysis in a unified framework, addressing both numerical and categorical variables and supporting regression and classification.
- Captures feature interdependence via a graph-based multi-task loss; modules (GLL, GRIL) explicitly learn and use these relationships.
- Novel use of prior knowledge from feature importance derived from a decision tree to guide adjacency learning.
- Extensive experiments across nine benchmark datasets (three regression, six classification) versus appropriate baselines; achieves average ranks of 1.33 (imputation/regression), 1.83/1.17 (imputation/classification), demonstrating state-of-the-art performance.
- Robustness shown in sensitivity analysis w.r.t. missing rates.
- Paper is clearly written and easy to understand.

### Weaknesses
- Limited technical novelty: reviewer 2 notes algorithm design is highly similar to MIRACLE; reviewer 1 notes components (GLL, GRIL) derive from general techniques. No theoretical explanation supports the claimed advantages.
- Model has a complex two-phase training (pre-training + fine-tuning) plus initial Soft-Impute, likely resulting in higher overall complexity than existing methods.
- Ablation studies are insufficient:
  - Missing evaluation of impact of each loss term (imputation, downstream task, prior adjacency) and synergy between them (reviewer 3).
  - No separate ablation showing effect of pre-training for GLL vs. PL; reviewer 1 asks whether pre-training for GLL is necessary, since the ablation only shows downstream task effect.
  - Missing analysis of contribution of the prior adjacency: does performance come from the beneficial prior or the relational learning capability of GLL? Compare using only prior-derived relationships (reviewer 3).
- Fairness of comparison concerns:
  - Soft-Impute initial imputation could create an ensemble effect; baseline methods should also get this starting point for fair comparison (reviewer 3).
  - MTL versions of baselines: the exact training scheme (two-step vs. simultaneous) needs clarification; if not identical to PIG, comparisons may be unfair (reviewer 3).
- Missing experiments:
  - Need qualitative demonstration that learned feature relationships are meaningful beyond quantitative improvement (reviewer 1).
  - Should analyze overall training complexity (reviewer 1).
  - Missing full bar-plot visualization (reviewer 2).
  - Experiments focus on low-dimensional datasets; how does the model perform on higher-dimensional datasets? (reviewer 2)
  - How does the approach handle different missing mechanisms (MCAR, MAR, MNAR)? (reviewer 2)
