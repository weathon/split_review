## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers found the paper well-organized and easy to follow, with an interesting study on attention redundancy and a simple, easy-to-replicate pruning method. The method shows practical speedup with minor performance drop across Llama and Mistral models. However, reviewers raised significant concerns about limited novelty, insufficient comparison with prior pruning methods (e.g., ShortenedLLaMA, LLMPruner, SliceGPT, ShearedLLaMA, Minitron), lack of analysis on why redundancy occurs, and limited evaluation on diverse or long-context tasks. The use of cosine similarity as importance measure may miss magnitude changes. There is also no study of post-pruning fine-tuning or finer-grained head pruning. Two reviewers rated 6, two rated 5, with differing emphasis on the method's practical value versus its incremental contribution.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized, clearly written, and easy to follow.
- The study on attention layer redundancy is interesting, and the proposed pruning approach is reasonable and simple, making it easy to implement and replicate.
- Provides useful insights: FFNs appear more important than Attention layers; deeper layers are less important than shallow ones, suggesting the model obtains answers in early layers.
- Experiments are extensive across tasks and models (Llama-2, Mistral) and include latency measurements and KV-cache reductions (e.g., Llama-2-70B achieves 48.4% speedup with 2.4% performance drop by pruning half the attention layers; Llama-2-13B KV-cache reduced from 52GB to 26GB).
- The findings have practical implications for efficient LLM deployment.
- The paper proposes a Speedup Degradation Ratio metric to assess speed-accuracy trade-offs.
- Visual insights and empirical results back the claims.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and incremental contribution:** The main findings (attention is more easily pruned than MLP, shallow layers more important than first and last) are known from prior work, e.g., "A deeper look at depth pruning of LLMs" (arXiv:2407.16286). Reviewer 2 notes many previous similar layer pruning methods [1][2][3] and this paper is a simple extension to MLP and attention layers.
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons:** No comparisons against other pruning methods in the main paper. The appendix only compares with ShortenedLLaMA and Wanda. Missing comparisons with LLMPruner, SliceGPT, ShearedLLaMA, Minitron (or others) that are standard in structured pruning.
- **Lack of analysis on redundancy origin:** The paper does not explain why normally trained Transformers exhibit layer-level redundancy when each layer likely learns distinct information. (Reviewer 2: "Why a normally trained Transformer model exhibits redundancy in layer levels?…it is more likely that each layer learns distinct information… Why pruning several layers do not affect the performance?")
- **Limited evaluation scope:** Experiments are conducted only on Llama and Mistral; findings may not generalize to other LLMs. The datasets used are limited and may not represent real-world applications; long-context tasks (e.g., LongBench, needle-in-a-haystack) could be seriously affected by attention dropping. (Reviewer 3: "if the input sequence is not short, early layer attention modules can model the token-wise relationships… but long sequence tasks… might be seriously affected.")
- **Calibration dataset sensitivity not explored:** Importance scores rely on calibration datasets, but the paper does not study how variations in these datasets affect pruning results or why performance degradation is extensive on some datasets but negligible on others.
- **Cosine similarity measure limitations:** The scale-invariant cosine similarity fails to capture magnitude information. A block that changes the magnitude but not the orientation of its input could be deemed unimportant, potentially misidentifying blocks that could be replaced by a scaling factor.
- **No post-pruning fine-tuning study:** The paper does not investigate whether further fine-tuning after pruning could recover accuracy loss.
- **No finer-grained pruning:** The paper only prunes whole attention layers, not individual attention heads. (Reviewer 4 asks about pruning individual heads.)
- **KV-cache reduction numbers may be reversed:** In Section 5, "Llama-2-13B, the KV-cache is reduced from 52GB to 26GB" and "Llama-2-70B… decreases from 20GB to 10GB" seems reversed; 13B would have smaller cache than 70B. (Reviewer 4 points this out.)
- **Selection of dropped layers unclear:** It is not clarified how the layers are selected when a specific number of layers are to be dropped (e.g., testing attention layers one by one to find the 4 with lowest importance score). (Reviewer 1 question.)
