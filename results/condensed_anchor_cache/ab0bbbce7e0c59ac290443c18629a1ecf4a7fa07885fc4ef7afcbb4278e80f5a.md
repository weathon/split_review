## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies separate optimization and noisy semantics as key issues within semantic drift for class-incremental semantic segmentation (CISS). It proposes IPSeg, comprising image posterior probabilities (to guide pixel predictions and mitigate separate optimization) and semantics decoupling (to split noisy semantics into permanent and temporary branches). Experiments on PASCAL VOC 2012 and ADE20K demonstrate competitive results, especially in long-term scenarios, with extensive ablation studies.

### Strengths
- **Well-motivated problem analysis** – The identification and analysis of separate optimization and noisy semantics are thorough, with compelling visual support (Figs. 1, 5, 6) and comprehensive ablation.  
- **Innovative design** – The image posterior guidance (using global image‑level predictions to calibrate pixel‑level outputs) and the decoupling of stable (permanent) vs. dynamic (temporary) semantics are well‑conceived and together enhance pixel‑level accuracy while balancing plasticity and stability.  
- **State‑of‑the‑art performance** – IPSeg outperforms previous methods on both VOC2012 and ADE20K, particularly under long‑term incremental settings, and its effectiveness is supported by extensive ablations.  
- **Clear overall presentation** – The paper is structured clearly; the motivation, proposed components, and experimental setup are well explained (though a few sections could be simplified, see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and completeness of explanations**  
  - Sections 3.3 and 3.4 (especially Equation 5) need simplification; the targets of the permanent and temporary branches in Fig. 2 are unclear.  
  - The implementation of background decoupling (pure background vs. unknown foreground via a saliency model) is complicated and not clearly distinguished from prior works (e.g., SSUL).  
  - The construction of the memory buffer with image‑level samples is not sufficiently explained, despite its importance for obtaining good guidance.  
  - It is unclear how final prediction maps are generated from the two branches.  
  - For the temporary branch, it is not specified whether its predictions (pure background and other foreground regions) contribute to the final output or are only used to multiply confidence scores from the image posterior branch.  
- **Missing comparisons and unfair advantage**  
  - Several recent SOTA methods are omitted (e.g., Incrementer [CVPR 2023], LGKD+PLOP).  
  - The use of a salient object detector (to obtain foreground regions) incurs additional computational cost and provides information not available to competing methods, making the comparison unfair.  
  - On ADE20K, many semantic regions (e.g., grassland) are not well captured by the saliency model (see supplementary Fig. 9); the impact of saliency map quality should be studied (e.g., by replacing with SAM).  
- **Efficiency concerns**  
  - The additional components (image posterior branch, decoupled semantics) increase model complexity. Computational cost (137.1G vs. SSUL’s 94.9G, a 44.5% increase) is a concern, yet no training/inference time or convergence iterations are reported.  
  - The memory buffer raises scalability and privacy concerns for larger datasets.  
- **Claim accuracy and experimental contradictions**  
  - The claim that “separate optimization does not attract any attention” is inaccurate; prior work [2] already addressed scale inconsistency via logit manipulation.  
  - In Table 2, the replay‑based version of IPSeg (33.6) is worse than the data‑free method PLOP+NeST (34.8), which is not explained.  
  - The data‑free version of IPSeg is inferior to LGKD+PLOP on three ADE20K settings (100‑10, 100‑50, 50‑50), raising doubts about the claim that “image‑level classification (IP) suffers less forgetting than the segmentation.”  
- **Technical questions that overlap with criticisms**  
  - The image‑level classification subnetwork is trained across steps; why does it not suffer catastrophic forgetting? Its performance over steps should be evaluated.  
  - How is the image posterior supervised when previous‑class image labels are unavailable?  
  - The feature extractor is frozen – is it pre‑trained from the first step or from ImageNet?  
  - The concept of “permanent semantics” is questionable: “unknown” objects change across steps, so only truly constant classes should be called permanent.  
- **Figure quality** – Most figures (2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8) appear blurry; they should be provided in higher resolution (e.g., PDF format).  

*Note: One reviewer (R3) found no specific weaknesses beyond those already addressed in the appendix, while the other three reviewers raised the concerns listed above, indicating a split in overall assessment.*
