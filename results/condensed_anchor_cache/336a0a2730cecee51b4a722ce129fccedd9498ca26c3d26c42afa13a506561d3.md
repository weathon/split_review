## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces FinRipple, a framework that aligns large language models (LLMs) with financial market dynamics to predict the ripple effects of sudden events. It combines a time-varying financial knowledge graph (KG) capturing inter-company relationships (supply chains, leadership networks), injects the KG into LLMs via adapters as memory modules, and uses reinforcement learning (PPO) with asset pricing theories (CAPM) to align predictions with actual market behavior. Experiments show FinRipple outperforms baseline LLM methods in event impact prediction and portfolio management, supported by ablation and case studies. The task is interesting and practically useful, and the overall framework is novel. Scores range from 3 to 6: one reviewer (R1, score 3) is substantially more critical of the problem formulation, literature review, and writing clarity, while the other three (score 6) view the work as solid with minor concerns.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an interesting and important real-world problem: predicting ripple effects of financial events across companies (R1,R3,R4). The motivation and potential for practical applications (e.g., portfolio management) are clear.
- The framework is novel and well-conceived: it integrates knowledge graphs, LLMs, and financial theory (CAPM) in a structured way, using adapters for parameter-efficient injection without full retraining, and aligning market behavior via reinforcement learning (R2,R3,R4).
- Comprehensive experimental evaluation with multiple LLM baselines, detailed ablation studies that isolate each component’s contribution, and a case study illustrating logical consistency (R2,R3,R4). The experiments demonstrate practical applicability in financial market analysis (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Problem formulation and temporal ambiguity (R1,R3):**  
  The prediction task is not clearly defined. It is unclear whether the model predicts present or future influence, how time lags between events and ripple effects are accounted for, and what the temporal resolution (interval between t and t+1) is. The equation (line 231-234) only includes the graph \(G^t\), but the textual description (line 227-228) also mentions news data as input, creating inconsistency (R3). One reviewer (R3) notes the task appears to be interpolation (predicting concurrent effects) rather than extrapolation (predicting future effects), which may risk data leakage if news already contains event-company relations. Addressing the extrapolation setting (e.g., using \(G^{<t}\) to predict future returns) is suggested as more meaningful.

- **Knowledge graph quality and construction details (R2,R3,R4):**  
  There is insufficient discussion of KG quality (e.g., how missing or incorrect relationships are handled) and no direct evaluation of the KG-injection module (e.g., via temporal knowledge graph prediction benchmarks) (R2,R3). The KG–LLM integration is described as shallow – the KG is used primarily for dataset construction rather than deeply incorporated into the model’s reasoning, risking information loss (R4). The frequency of KG updates, inference time, and memory requirements are not reported (R2).

- **Choice of asset pricing model and reward design (R2):**  
  The use of CAPM residuals to measure event impact is not adequately justified over other asset pricing models (e.g., Fama-French). The reward function (lines 292-296) is unclear, and the choice of \(\lambda = 0.1\) (in appendix) lacks explanation.

- **Baselines limited to LLM methods (R3):**  
  All baselines are LLM-based; no comparison with conventional methods (e.g., GNN-based temporal models) is provided, which would better assess the contribution of the LLM components.

- **Event representation and cost (R3):**  
  Events are represented only as raw-text news articles (avg. 5443 words), leading to high LLM cost and stricter long-context requirements. Noise and misinformation in raw text are concerns. A structured event representation (following a predefined schema) might be beneficial.

- **Overclaiming “ripple effect” (R4):**  
  The introduction frames motivation around ripple effects, but the task setup and experiments focus on isolated events; the specific effectiveness for ripple-effect analysis is not clearly demonstrated.

- **Writing and literature completeness (R1):**  
  The writing is vague in key sections (problem formulation, data pipeline). The literature review misses relevant works (e.g., FinMem, FinCon; also recent event-level financial text analysis). One reviewer (R1) rates the paper much lower (score 3) due to these weaknesses, while others (score 6) find the paper solid despite minor issues.

- **Minor concerns (R2,R3,R4):**  
  - High refusal-to-answer rates may be caused by overly complex formatting requirements; simplification could reduce refusals (R4).  
  - The fine-grained scoring (-10 to 10) may lead models to favor neutral scores (R4).  
  - Typo: line 773 states “March 2019 to December 2021 ≠ 226 months” (R3).
