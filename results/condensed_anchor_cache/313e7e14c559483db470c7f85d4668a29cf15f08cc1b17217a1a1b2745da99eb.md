## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates Mamba’s length generalization limitations, identifies a restricted effective receptive field (ERF) due to fast decay of discretization step sums, and proposes DeciMamba — a token‑filtering mechanism that discards less‑important tokens based on delta t values. Experiments on long‑range NLP tasks show DeciMamba can extrapolate to 25× training length without extra compute.

### Strengths
- **Useful phenomenon:** The identification of Mamba’s limited effective receptive field and the Mamba Mean Distance metric are valuable contributions for future research on SSMs (reviewers 1, 4).
- **Well‑motivated design:** The proposed method is directly motivated by the ERF analysis (reviewer 4).
- **Consistent improvements:** DeciMamba shows consistent gains over Mamba across diverse long‑context benchmarks (SQuAD, Passkey retrieval, etc.) (reviewers 1, 4).
- **Simplicity:** The approach is straightforward, easily combined with Mamba, and yields notable improvements at no additional inference cost (reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Effective visualizations:** Especially Figure 2 is praised (reviewer 4).
- **Scientific approach:** The paper isolates the problematic component before proposing a solution (reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Hyperparameter choices and ablations:** The method introduces several new hyperparameters (decay factor, maximal length after decimation, number of layers to decimate) without clear guidance for selection; ablations on these (e.g., number of layers, decay rate) are missing (reviewer 1).
- **Limited experimental scope and unsatisfactory results:**
  - DeciMamba is only evaluated on Mamba itself; validation on other SSMs or linear‑attention models is lacking (reviewer 3).
  - Absolute performance is low: on LongBench, DeciMamba scores below 10% on most datasets; by comparison, Llama‑2‑7B‑chat with only a 4k context averages 31% on English tasks (reviewer 3).
  - For simple tasks like Passkey retrieval, DeciMamba still requires fine‑tuning (reviewer 3).
  - Minor: Table 1 is missing the LongBench score for the LCC dataset (reviewer 3).
- **Method restricts sequential processing:** DeciMamba must sort delta values and retain top‑k tokens, requiring all tokens to be seen simultaneously. This undermines Mamba’s core RNN‑like sequential processing advantage and limits the method to the pre‑filling stage only; it cannot be applied during decoding (reviewers 2, 3). One reviewer (2) notes this significantly limits practical impact for long‑text generation.
- **Potential information loss:** Discarding tokens may hurt tasks requiring comprehensive context (e.g., document QA) because important tokens might be removed during pre‑filling and unavailable for generation (reviewer 2).
- **Incomplete fundamental solution:** The method is a practical workaround rather than addressing the root limitation of Mamba’s small state space. A deeper analysis of the Mamba Mean Distance metric could lead to more fundamental improvements (reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 suggests that the bounded hidden state size is a key factor and that the authors should highlight it.
- **Missing analyses and comparisons:**
  - How does DeciMamba perform on short‑context tasks? Is there a trade‑off between short and long context performance? (reviewer 4)
  - Comparison with Transformers on the same benchmarks is missing (reviewer 1).
  - Impact of hidden state size on length generalization: have models with larger state spaces been studied? (reviewer 1)
  - The OOM in Figure 6 is due to training complexity O(L); this should be clarified since inference complexity is constant (reviewer 1).
- **Confusing or incomplete observations:**
  - Figure 1 shows performance degrading at middle lengths rather than a clear diagonal boundary; this is counterintuitive and suggests a more complex mechanism than simple distance (reviewer 2).
  - Figure 4’s Mamba Mean Distance results do not definitively prove decreasing long‑text capability because the metric may not scale linearly with length. Are similar studies available for attention‑based architectures? (reviewer 2)
  - Tasks where DeciMamba yields much larger gains (e.g., SQuAD, Passkey) vs. smaller gains (e.g., Multi‑Document QA) lack explanation (reviewer 1).
- **Presentation issues:**
  - Decimation strategy (Section 4) is hard to follow (reviewer 1).
  - Introduce Decimamba better in the abstract/introduction; possibly move Figure 5 earlier (reviewer 1).
  - Proper citation formats (\citep, \citet) should be used; table captions must be above tables (reviewer 3).
  - Typo in Eq. (8): “j” and “L” swapped, should be d(L,j) (reviewer 1).
  - Model selection varies across figures (Fig. 3 vs. Fig. 4); justification and impact on generalizability should be discussed (reviewer 2).
