## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes IV-Mixed Sampler, a training-free algorithm that combines image diffusion models (IDMs) and video diffusion models (VDMs) during sampling to improve video generation quality. IDMs are used to enhance per-frame fidelity and VDMs to preserve temporal coherence, alternating between their score functions via DDIM inversion and DDIM steps. The method achieves state-of-the-art FVD scores on UCF-101, MSR-VTT, and Chronomagic benchmarks. For example, Animatediff with IV-Mixed Sampler reduces UMT-FVD from 275.2 to 228.6, approaching Pika-2.0’s 223.1. The paper includes theoretical analysis connecting the method to a standard inverse ODE process and explores hyperparameter design space.

### Strengths
- **Novel and interesting idea**: Leveraging pre-trained IDMs (which have higher visual fidelity than current open-source VDMs) to boost video quality is innovative (R1, R3).  
- **Clear and well-written overall**: The problem statement, methodology, and experimental validation are structured; complex concepts are explained concisely (R1, R3). Reviewer 2 found the writing confusing in parts, but the majority found it clear.  
- **Thorough experiments and ablations**: Main experiments demonstrate good improvement in generation quality while retaining motion; ablations show trade-offs of different instantiations (R1).  
- **Theoretical grounding**: The paper combines theoretical proof with practical implementation, providing a solid foundation (R2, R3).  
- **Performance gains**: Quantitative results are strong, especially for Animatediff; visual quality improvement is evident (R1). FreeInit outperforms IV-Mixed Sampler on ModelScope-T2V in UMT-FVD (R2), indicating method is not universally dominant.

### Weaknesses
- **Restrictive compatibility**: The method requires IDM and VDM to share the same latent space (e.g., same VAE). This limits applicability to many state-of-the-art video models that use temporally downsampled or specialized VAEs (R1, R3). No experiments are provided for cases where VAEs differ (R3).  
- **Potential short-term relevance**: The main motivation—lack of good open-source VDMs—may diminish as better models (e.g., Mochi-1, OpenVid10M) become available in 6+ months (R1).  
- **Lack of statistical significance**: Variance of metrics (e.g., FVD in Tables 1 and 2) is not reported; values are close, making it unclear if improvements are significant (R1).  
- **Insufficient video demos**: Only one set of videos in supplementary; motion is hard to see in paper appendix. More samples online would help validate temporal quality (R1).  
- **Unclear performance on distilled/models with few steps**: Whether the method works for distilled 1-step or 2-step generators is not addressed (R1).  
- **Writing clarity issues (minority view)**: Reviewer 2 found the paper confusing. Specific problems:  
  - Abstract/Introduction’s repeated reference to “OpenAI’s Strawberry” is irrelevant and unmotivated for the paper’s contribution.  
  - Figure 2 (in Introduction) is confusing and discusses “R-” variants without prior explanation; “I-” and “V-” notations appear abruptly (line 107).  
  - The use of “go”, “back”, “begin”, “end” in equations is unclear.  
  - Figure 6 is hard to read.  
- **Demonstration examples are not compelling**: Visual differences between IV-Mixed Sampler and baselines are subtle in demos; more striking examples (e.g., complex motions) are needed to prove superiority (R3).  
- **Illustration clarity of Figure 3**: The figure fails to clearly convey quality or temporal coherence differences between samplers; zoomed-in sections or side-by-side comparisons would help (R3).  
- **Evaluation metrics are suboptimal for text-to-video**: Benchmarks like UCF-FVD and MSR-VTT-FVD focus on frame quality and temporal consistency but ignore semantic alignment between text prompt and video content. More suitable benchmarks (text-video correspondence) are needed for comprehensive evaluation (R3).  
- **Scalability and edge-case concerns**: It is unclear how performance degrades with longer/complex videos or in scenarios where the method might underperform (e.g., specific content types) (R3).
