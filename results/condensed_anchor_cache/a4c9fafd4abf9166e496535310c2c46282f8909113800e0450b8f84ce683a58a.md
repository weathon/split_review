## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces two memory-efficient training methods for discrete diffusion samplers to sample from complex unnormalized distributions over discrete domains. The first method uses policy gradient (RL) to minimize reverse KL divergence, and the second uses Self-Normalized Neural Importance Sampling (SN-NIS) to estimate the forward KL divergence. These approaches reduce memory scaling with number of diffusion steps and enable unbiased sampling, validated on Ising model benchmarks and combinatorial optimization problems.

### Strengths

- The two novel training objectives (RL-based reverse KL and SN-NIS-based forward KL) directly address memory scaling issues, enabling more diffusion steps within fixed memory constraints. [R1, R2]
- The paper enables unbiased sampling—critical for scientific applications—by adapting importance sampling and Neural MCMC to discrete diffusion models. [R2]
- The paper is clearly written and results are well presented, with a reasonable list of optimization benchmarks. [R1, R3]

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient empirical validation against standard diffusion-based samplers.** Comparisons are only against autoregressive baselines; no direct comparison to conventional diffusion samplers under the same memory/computational budget. This makes it hard to assess the practical advantage of the proposed methods. [R1, R2]
- **Experiments not sufficiently challenging.** The Ising model benchmarks may not fully stress memory bottlenecks; more difficult cases (e.g., 2D spin glass with J=±1, phi-4 model, or image generation like CIFAR-10) would strengthen the evaluation. For the spin glass case, the authors can refer to arXiv:2407.19483 for a different approach. [R1, R3]
- **Missing computational cost analysis.** Only inference time is reported; training time, wall-clock time, GPU hours, and memory usage per experiment are needed to assess practical resource requirements. [R1, R2]
- **Lack of ablation studies.** Effects of individual components (e.g., mini-batch size in PPO, number of hidden layers, number of diffusion steps) are not systematically evaluated. [R2]
- **Insufficient comparisons.** The paper primarily compares with DiffUCO; other state-of-the-art methods such as GFlowNets (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2023) should be included, especially since MDS results may not be state-of-the-art. [R2]
- **Lack of theoretical convergence analysis.** For the RL-based approach, conditions for convergence and stability across problem instances are not provided; hyperparameter sensitivity is unclear. [R2]
- **Clarity issues in several places.** (i) Why the entire trajectory is needed for optimizing eq. 2 (reverse KL) is not explicitly stated; the gradient affects the expectation over all steps, which should be clarified after eq. 2. (ii) The distribution 𝒟(Q) in Sec. 2.2 is not specified; a reference to Appendix Sec. 4 with details per problem is needed. (iii) The difference between IS and SN-NIS is not explained. (iv) The distinction between neural MCMC and classical MCMC is not defined. (v) Sec. 2.3 is misleading—it describes classical sampling methods without clarifying how they will be used, and much of it is repeated in Sec. 3.2. (vi) Minor: eq. line 114, variable p should be p_B. [R3, R2]
