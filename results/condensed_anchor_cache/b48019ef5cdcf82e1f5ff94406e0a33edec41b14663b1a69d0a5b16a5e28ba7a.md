## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a Noisy Multi-view Contrastive Learning framework for Knowledge-aware recommendation (NMCLK). It generates three views (global structural, local user-item, item-item semantic), adds a noise module, and uses alignment/uniformity losses. Experiments on ML-100K and ML-1M show performance gains over baselines. Two reviewers gave score 5, two gave score 3, reflecting disagreement over novelty and experimental depth.

### Strengths
- Well-written, clearly structured, and easy to follow (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- The proposed method outperforms several baselines on two movie recommendation datasets (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- Addresses an important research direction: self-supervised learning for knowledge-aware recommendation (Reviewer 4).
- Technically sound multi-view contrastive design with alignment and uniformity constraints (Reviewers 1,4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty.** Several existing contrastive learning methods for KG-based recommendation exist (e.g., KGCL, KGIC, KACL), and NMCLK does not make sufficiently innovative contributions in view generation or contrastive constraints (Reviewers 2,3,4). Reviewer 3 notes that introducing self-supervised learning from CV/NLP is not enough for an ICLR paper. Reviewer 2 states the authors should derive more insights in alignment/uniformity constraints.
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation.**  
  - Only two small movie datasets (ML-100K, ML-1M) are used; generalization to other domains is questionable (Reviewers 1,2,4).  
  - No ablation study is conducted to verify the effectiveness of each proposed module: noise module, contrastive learning module, alignment/uniformity losses (Reviewers 1,2,3).  
  - Missing comparisons with existing contrastive learning methods for KG recommendation (Reviewer 4).  
  - Lacks hyperparameter sensitivity and anti-noise robustness experiments (Reviewer 4).  
  - Section 5.1.1 mentions three datasets while only two are used (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of theoretical analysis and unclear explanations.**  
  - No theoretical justification for how each contrastive module benefits the CTR task (Reviewer 1).  
  - Design choices of the contrastive module and multi-view framework are not explained; the reader cannot see the performance improvement brought by the multi-view framework (Reviewer 2).  
  - Specific definitions for contrastive loss terms are missing (e.g., $\mathcal{L}_u^g$, $\mathcal{L}_i^g$, $\mathcal{L}_u^l$, $\mathcal{L}_i^l$, $L_{local}$) (Reviewer 4).  
  - Reviewer 3 questions whether the contrastive assumption (similar for same field, distant for disparate) always holds in recommendation.
- **Writing and presentation issues.**  
  - Citation error below Equation (2): "Inspired by SimGCL (simgcl) and Noisytune wu2022noisytune papers' additions" (Reviewer 1).  
  - wu2022noisytune and simgcl are not properly cited (Reviewer 2).  
  - Inconsistent notation: both $\mathcal{L}$ and $L$ used for loss terms (Reviewer 4).  
  - Typo: "This results ..." on page 5 (Reviewer 4).  
  - In task formulation, braces missing in set definitions (Reviewer 4).  
  - Using "Light-GCN" instead of the official "LightGCN" may cause confusion (Reviewer 4).  
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms/missing details:**  
  - What are the major improvements over existing contrastive learning methods for KG recommendation? (Reviewer 4)  
  - How are the contrastive loss terms calculated in detail? (Reviewer 4)  
  - Why does the multi-view framework work? (Reviewer 2)  
  - Does contrastive learning always benefit recommendation? (Reviewer 3)
