## Merged Review

### Summary
DA-Bench is a benchmark framework for evaluating unsupervised domain adaptation (DA) methods across diverse data modalities – computer vision, NLP, tabular data, and biosignals. It includes 20 shallow and 3 deep DA methods, and uses nested cross-validation with unsupervised model selection scores (IW, CircV) to address realistic hyperparameter selection when target labels are absent. The benchmark is open-source, reproducible, and claims easy extensibility.

### Strengths
- Comprehensive benchmark covering diverse modalities (CV, NLP, tabular, biosignals), multiple DA methods (20 shallow, 3 deep), and multiple model selection strategies.
- Realistic evaluation protocol using nested CV and unsupervised scorers that do not rely on target labels, addressing a key practical challenge.
- Open-source, reproducible, and designed to be easily extended with new methods, datasets, and selection criteria.
- Well-organized presentation with clear explanations and effective tables/visuals.
- Motivation for validating parameters with unsupervised scorers is commendable.
- Extensive experiments across multiple datasets are adequate.
- Provides practical insights for real-life applications.

### Weaknesses
1. **Outdated and missing recent deep DA methods**: The deep methods evaluated (e.g., DeepJDOT) are outdated, lacking state-of-the-art approaches from the past three years. Crucial methods such as pseudo-labeling techniques (e.g., MIC – Masked Image Consistency) are absent. The minority reviewer (score 5) is especially critical of this gap, noting the absence of work from the past three years. Several other reviewers also raise this concern.

2. **Figure 1 is unclear and not self-contained**: The figure fails to explain what colors represent and why multiple clusters appear per class. Subspace shift is mischaracterized as a standalone shift type rather than a transformation for aligning distributions. The figure appears to depict a binary classification task, leaving unclear whether the four shifts generalize to other tasks.

3. **Lack of theoretical analysis**: The paper focuses solely on empirical benchmarking without providing deep theoretical insights into why certain methods perform better or worse under different shifts. The minority reviewer (score 5) notes that the paper does not propose any new theories. Another reviewer also calls for more theoretical analysis.

4. **Missing analysis of shift types in common benchmarks**: The paper does not characterize the dominant shifts (e.g., covariate, target, conditional) in standard datasets like Office-31, Office-Home, MNIST/USPS. It also lacks analysis of how specific shift types influence method performance, making it harder to guide method selection.

5. **Limited evaluation metrics**: Only accuracy is used as the primary metric. Additional metrics such as ECE, Brier Score, F1-score, or domain-specific metrics are needed for a more comprehensive evaluation.

6. **Incomplete deep method results**: Deep method accuracy scores (Table 3) are provided only for selected real-life datasets; results on simulated datasets are missing, preventing full comparison with shallow methods.

7. **Confusing comparison in Table 2**: Different DA methods with different model selection techniques are compared simultaneously, making it difficult to isolate the impact of the scorer. A clearer comparison would keep the DA method constant while varying the scorer.

8. **Weak correlation between modalities**: The connection across diverse modalities (e.g., images vs. tabular) is weak, raising doubt about whether insights or transferability can be achieved across these domains. This concern is raised specifically by the minority reviewer (score 5).

9. **Unclear practical evaluation without target labels**: The paper states (line 217) that supervised scorers cannot be used in practice. This raises the question of how to evaluate DA methods when target labels are completely absent, and whether this makes the supervised scorer meaningless for deployment. This overlaps with a criticism about the practical utility of the provided guidance.
