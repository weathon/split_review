## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GenFlowNet, a method to generate GFlowNet parameters without training. It first uses an autoencoder (VAE) to learn a latent space of GFlowNet parameters, then trains a conditional latent diffusion model conditioned on an encoding of the environment structure (e.g., state dimensions, reward function specifications) to sample new parameter vectors. The authors claim this is the first exploration of parameter generation for GFlowNets, aiming to reduce trial-and-error cost during development. Experiments evaluate on synthetic hypergrid tasks and molecule generation against trained baselines (FM, DB, TB, LS). Two reviewers (R1, R3, R4) give score 3, one reviewer (R2) gives score 5, calling the idea “very novel” but raising major concerns about motivation, scalability, and experimental validity.

### Strengths
- **First exploration** of generating GFlowNet parameters (R1, R4). R4 notes this can be naturally extended to reinforcement learning applications.
- **Novel and unconventional idea** (R2: “crazy idea”). R3 agrees it *could* help improve training in scenarios where GFlowNet struggles, if proven effective in real-world settings.
- **Demonstrates that generated parameters can achieve similar or superior performance** compared to conventionally trained GFlowNets (R4). R4 states this shows adaptability to unseen environmental structures.

### Weaknesses
- **Weak and unclear motivation** (R2, R3, R4). R4 questions why generating parameters is better than simply training a forward policy conditioned on environment structure (e.g., \(P_F(s'|s; \text{Structure})\)). The paper does not justify why parameter generation is more efficient given the overhead of training a diffusion model. R2 and R3 also say the motivation is unclear and the paper does not clarify whether the method successfully addresses known GFlowNet training difficulties (e.g., increasing trajectory length).
- **Limited and poorly described experiments** (all reviewers).  
  - The hypergrid task uses only eight different structures (R4) and does not vary reward functions (R1: “Are different rewards used to train the autoencoder? Or do you fix the same \(R_0, R_1, R_2, H\) for all GFlowNets? Is the aim solely to create new architectures for the same reward that has been learned before?”).  
  - The molecular generation task uses an unspecified condition \(I\) (R4: “What is \(I\) in Table 3?”).  
  - Only synthetic data is used (R3: “does not support most claims, e.g., Figure 1”); no real-world dataset beyond a simple molecule task (R3, R4).  
  - No error bars or standard deviations (R1).  
  - R1 notes the experimental suit is “slim” compared to recent GFlowNet literature; R4 calls for more diverse and challenging tasks (e.g., RNA sequence generation) and complex environmental variations beyond dimension changes.  
  - R2 states “empirical results do not reflect real-world performance.”
- **Unaddressed limitations and extensibility concerns** (R1, R4).  
  - How does GenFlowNet behave for varying rewards? For non-MLP forward policies? (R1).  
  - It is unclear how to incorporate diverse reward functions (e.g., different molecular properties) or action spaces (fragment-based vs. reaction-based transitions) (R4).  
  - R3 asks: “What are the specific distinctions that make adapting existing parameter generation methods challenging for GFlowNet parameters?”  
  - R2: “Scalability may be limited” – especially to large-scale tasks requiring complex parameterizations (LLMs, text-to-image models).
- **Minor or questionable performance improvements** (R1, R4).  
  - In Table 4, \(N=2\) and \(N=5\) yield similar generalization performance – R4 asks for clarification.  
  - Table 1 shows very small gaps between GenFlowNet and trained baselines; R1 calls the claim of “superior sampling distribution accuracy” an overstatement given the lack of uncertainty.  
  - R1 questions: “It seems odd that a training-free GFlowNet would perform better than a trained one. Could you share a rationale?”
- **Unclear experimental details and methodology** (R1, R3, R4).  
  - Unit for “Time usage” in Table 1? (R1).  
  - Figure 4 suggests generated parameters are used as *initialization*, not training-free – R1 asks for elaboration.  
  - For Section 3.3: Are the GFlowNets drawn from the GenFlowNet trained for the hypergrid task? Provide more details (R1).  
  - Dataset preparation for the autoencoder is unclear – how many models, samples, which datasets? (R3).  
  - R2 asks: “Can this method be used to measure uncertainty from a Bayesian perspective? I'm asking because this work seems connected with AutoML and hypernetworks.”
- **Limited novelty** (R3): the method relies on an existing latent diffusion model without modifications.
