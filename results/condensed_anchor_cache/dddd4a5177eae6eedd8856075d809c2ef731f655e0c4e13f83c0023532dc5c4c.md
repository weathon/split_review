## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Pessimistic Nonlinear Least-Square Value Iteration (PNLSVI) for offline RL with nonlinear function approximation. The algorithm combines variance-weighted least-squares regression (with pessimistic variance estimation from a data fold) and pessimistic value iteration. It introduces a \(D^2\)-divergence to quantify dataset uncertainty and assumes Bellman completeness (first- and second-order), uniform coverage of the hypothesis class by the offline dataset, and access to a nonlinear least-squares oracle. The main result is an instance-dependent regret bound scaling with the square root of the logarithm of the class cardinality (or covering number) and inverse data coverage as measured by the \(D^2\)-divergence. The bound recovers minimax optimal instance-dependent regret when specialized to linear function approximation. The algorithm is oracle-efficient.

### Strengths
- The paper is well written and clear; theoretical claims are supported by detailed, well-commented proofs; main technical tools are highlighted and explained; assumptions are clear; the summary of the state of the art is complete and detailed; strengths and limitations of the work are properly discussed. [Reviewer 1]
- The algorithm is oracle-efficient and strictly generalizes existing pessimism-based algorithms for linear and differentiable function approximation [Xiong et al., 2023; Yin et al., 2022b]. [Reviewer 2]
- It introduces a new type of \(D^2\)-divergence to quantify uncertainty of offline datasets, extending the role of the elliptical norm in the linear setting and the \(D^2\)-divergence from online RL to offline RL. [Reviewers 2, 3]
- The work fills a gap by designing an efficient offline RL algorithm with general function approximation that has instance-dependent regret guarantees, which is new in the literature. [Reviewers 3, 4]
- It generalizes reference-advantage decomposition and variance-weighted ridge regression from linear MDPs to nonlinear function classes. [Reviewers 3, 4]

### Weaknesses
- **Notation and presentation issues (minor):**  
  - In Section 3, the abuse of notation when defining Bellman operators is confusing (e.g., \(f(s) = \max_a f(s,a)\) without explicit definition; the Bellman optimality operator should relate optimal Q to optimal V, not a fixed policy’s Q and V). [Reviewer 1]  
  - Assumption 3.2 is followed by in-line assumptions (realizability, completeness) that should be stated separately for clarity. [Reviewer 1]  
  - The paper defines \(\mathcal{N}\) as the cardinality of the hypothesis class but refers to it as the “covering number”; the two are distinct, and the only covering argument appears only in Remark 5.3 for the linear case. This blurs the distinction, especially for infinite classes. [Reviewer 1]  
  - Typos: page 4 “closed to the optimal value function”; page 6 “construct this variance estimator with…”. [Reviewer 1]

- **Conceptual question about pessimism:** The underestimation of variance inflates value estimates via inverse variance weights, which seems optimistic rather than pessimistic. The paper should justify why underestimation is needed and whether “pessimistic” is the correct term. [Reviewer 1]

- **Insufficient computational details for the bonus:**  
  - Appendix C addresses only the first bullet point in Section 4.3; it does not explain how to efficiently compute or approximate the \(D^2\)-divergence (the second bullet point). Details on solving the optimization problem, existence of closed-form solutions, iterative methods, convergence properties, and computational costs are missing. [Reviewer 2]

- **Lack of clear comparison with prior work on differentiable parametric models:** The paper claims to generalize [Yin et al., 2022b], but Theorem 5.1’s improvements over that work are not explicitly stated. A precise comparison highlighting any trade-offs (e.g., improved function class complexity dependency at the cost of worse dependence on dataset size or horizon) is needed. [Reviewer 2]

- **Insufficient discussion of relation to [Alekh et al., 2023]:** The paper mentions inspiration from that work regarding \(D^2\)-divergence but does not clarify algorithmic differences (e.g., whether the proposed algorithm is an offline version of [Alekh et al., 2023], and whether techniques can be transferred between settings). [Reviewer 2]

- **Overall incremental nature:** Key techniques (variance-weighted regression, reference-advantage decomposition, \(D^2\)-divergence) are largely known from online RL or offline RL; the extensions to general function approximation are not particularly difficult. The regret improvement of a square-root of \(d\) from reference-advantage decomposition is standard, representing a refinement of a previous paper rather than a truly novel method/analysis. [Reviewer 3]

- **Practical interpretation and applicability of the regret bound:**  
  - It is unclear how to interpret Theorem 5.1 and when it yields a better bound than instance-independent guarantees. [Reviewer 3]  
  - The uniform coverage assumption (Assumption 3.5) is very strong and unrealistic; most pessimism-based offline RL papers adopt weaker partial coverage assumptions. The value of an instance-dependent bound under such a strong condition is questionable. [Reviewer 4] (Also echoed by Reviewer 3: “Does it really matter in practice?”)  
  - The lower bound cited only applies to linear cases; no matching lower bound is provided for general function approximation, making it difficult to assess the tightness of the upper bound or the algorithm’s optimality. [Reviewer 4]  
  - No numerical experiments are provided to demonstrate practical performance or compare with existing offline RL algorithms for general function approximation. [Reviewer 4]

- **Unaddressed practical concerns:**  
  - Which part of the proof requires uniform coverage and why partial coverage fails? [Reviewer 4]  
  - What concrete examples of function classes (beyond linear MDPs) satisfy the conditions? [Reviewer 4]  
  - The dataset is assumed to come from a single behavior policy; what is the impact of multiple behavior policies? [Reviewer 3]  
  - Can a lower bound be provided for general function approximations? [Reviewer 4]

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* The reviewer who assigned a score of 8 (Reviewer 1) considered the weaknesses minor, while the reviewer who assigned a score of 5 (Reviewer 4) raised the most fundamental concerns (strong coverage assumption, lack of lower bound, no experiments). Reviewers 2 and 3 (scores 6) were moderately critical, highlighting incremental nature and missing computational/comparison details. All weaknesses are retained as distinct points.
