## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Safe Skill Planning (SSkP) for safe RL with offline demonstration data. SSkP first learns a skill prior and a skill risk predictor via PU learning from offline data, then uses a cross-entropy-method (CEM) risk planner during online RL to select low-risk skills, while a separate skill policy is trained with SAC to maximize reward. Experiments on four MuJoCo locomotion tasks (Point, Ant, HalfCheetah, Walker) compare SSkP against Recovery RL, CPQ, and SMBPO. The paper claims consistent improvement over prior safe RL methods.

### Strengths
- Investigates an important and relatively unexplored combination: using skills (high-level actions) for safe RL with offline data.
- Clear algorithm description and high-quality writing (pseudocode, logical flow).
- Novel algorithm SSkP that combines skill learning with risk planning, making mild assumptions (only some unsafe labels needed).
- Two-stage process is efficient and reduces unsafe online exploration by first learning from offline data.
- Risk planning process is simple yet effective, generating safer skill decisions; the risk predictor is adapted online.
- (Minority positive view: Reviewer 3 gave score 6, praising the method as novel and effective, while other reviewers rated 3 and raised significant concerns.)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited contributions and novelty**  
  - Each module (skills from [Pertsch et al. 2021], PU learning from [Xu & Denil 2021], planning) is borrowed; no new training techniques are highlighted.  
  - Motivation is ambiguous; the paper does not clearly distinguish its contributions from prior work.  
  - No theoretical analysis is provided.  
  - The related work section lacks a clear differentiation of how SSkP differs from each prior approach.

- **Insufficient experimental validation**  
  - Only four locomotion tasks (all MuJoCo) are used; Safety Gymnasium or other safe RL benchmarks with diverse constraints are missing.  
  - Only three random seeds per task, too few for robust conclusions.  
  - Baseline methods are from 2021-2022; more recent state-of-the-art methods should be included.  
  - Unfair comparison: SMBPO should be pretrained like SSkP; Recovery RL’s epsilon parameter (triggers recovery policy) was not tuned to this benchmark. Even so, Recovery RL matches SSkP in 2 environments.  
  - Claim in line 415 that “SMBPO demonstrates a similar inability as CPQ” is inaccurate: Table 1 shows SMBPO’s final reward on Ant is close to SSkP’s.

- **Ablation studies are insufficient**  
  - No comparison of skill-based policy vs. direct (low-level) action policy; it is unclear whether performance gains come from skills or from the CEM planner applied every step.  
  - Ablation focuses on removing risk planning, which is obvious – the method would likely be unsafe without any cost consideration.  
  - No ablation varying the proportion of cost-violating examples or the diversity of offline demonstrations.  
  - No ablation of the risk predictor learning method (PU vs. a safety critic trained with temporal difference learning).

- **Lack of offline data details**  
  - Number of samples, ratio of constraint-violating samples, collection procedure are not reported; this harms reproducibility.

- **Concerns about the method’s safety mechanisms**  
  - The policy is trained only to maximize reward, not safety; the paper repeatedly claims “learning a safe policy,” but safety comes entirely from the risk planner.  
  - Risk planning (Algorithm 2) selects the safest skill without considering task progress, potentially choosing safe but task-irrelevant skills (e.g., a vehicle stopping to avoid collisions).  
  - Why not use a safety critic (via dynamic programming) instead of PU learning for the risk predictor? PU learning only captures immediate risk, not cumulative cost.  
  - The action policy (skill-conditioned low-level policy) is not updated online; only the skill policy is trained. This restricts the agent’s ability to refine low-level control from new experiences.

- **Method complexity and clarity issues**  
  - Integration of multiple components (skill model, risk predictor, risk planning) makes the approach complex; hyperparameter tuning is challenging and error propagation between components is not discussed.  
  - Preliminary section is missing; it is hard to separate novel contributions from prior work.  
  - Abbreviation “PU” is used in the abstract without definition.  
  - “Reinforcement Learning from Demonstration” is incorrectly equated to Imitation Learning (line 96).  
  - In Algorithm 1, line 218 contradicts Section 4.2.1 regarding the sampling from \(q_\psi\).  
  - Equation 6 uses only diagonal covariance for CEM; allowing full covariance could improve optimization.  
  - \(\lambda\) for PU learning is not justified; its determination is essential.  
  - Section 4.1 lacks loss functions / algorithms for training the skill encoder and decoder.

- **Practical limitations**  
  - Heavy reliance on offline data quality and quantity: performance degrades with smaller datasets (sensitivity analysis shown).  
  - Computational cost of risk planning is not reported; may be high in large state/action spaces and limit real-time use.  
  - Only simulated robotic environments are tested; real-world generalization (sensor noise, disturbances) is unknown.

- **Reviewer questions that reinforce weaknesses**  
  - How would the method perform if skills were replaced with low-level actions?  
  - Why is the policy called “safe” if it only maximizes reward?  
  - How are execution times with different planning parameters?  
  - Does the method improve skills online? If not, initial skills bound performance.  
  - How does the penalty (lines 334–336) ensure actions stay close to the prior and resolve unsafe behavior during planning?  
  - How does the method handle partially incorrect or noisy demonstrations?  
  - What are the computational requirements compared to other safe RL methods?
