## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes C-PSRL, a hierarchical Bayesian posterior sampling algorithm for reinforcement learning that uses a (partial) causal graph over environment variables as a prior. It simultaneously learns the full causal graph at the higher level and the parameters of the resulting factored dynamics at the lower level. The paper provides a Bayesian regret analysis showing a rate of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{K/2^\eta})$ where $\eta$ measures the degree of prior knowledge, and demonstrates through experiments that C-PSRL improves upon uninformative PSRL while performing close to PSRL with the full causal graph. A byproduct of the algorithm is a weak causal discovery result: it recovers a $Z$-sparse super-graph of the ground-truth causal graph.

### Strengths
- The idea of replacing an uninformative prior with a (partial) causal graph is attractive and compelling for improving the practicality of posterior sampling in RL.
- The problem setting where only a partial causal graph is known is interesting, with a clear motivation from real-world scenarios (e.g., known causal dependencies in medical treatments).
- The proposed algorithm is intuitive and well-structured, extending the PSRL framework of Osband & Van Roy (2014) with a hierarchical Bayesian treatment (building on Hong et al, 2022b).
- The Bayesian regret analysis is provided and explicitly connects the regret rate with the degree of prior knowledge, which is interesting.
- The paper presents a weak causal discovery result as a byproduct, revealing a potential bridge between RL and causal discovery.
- The empirical evaluation demonstrates effectiveness compared to naive PSRL.
- The paper is well-written and relatively easy to follow even for non-experts in PSRL.
- The statement of new contributions is clear.

### Weaknesses
- **Focus on parents of actions/states vs. reward (R1).** The motivation for exploiting causal prior knowledge would suggest focusing on the parents of the reward, as the value of a variable depends only on its causal parents (line 122). The paper instead focuses on parents of actions and states; it’s unclear why this is more efficient or simpler than directly learning from the reward’s parents.
- **DAG representation and interpretability (R1, R3).** 
  - Line 92: The division of the DAG into $X$ and $Y$ parts is confusing. Where is the reward in the DAG? In common DAGs each node is a random variable, but here nodes seem to represent realizations of $(s,a)$ or just $s$, which casts doubt on interpretability.
  - The paper is not written in causality notation; it is unclear what the “interventions” are. Definitions in Section 2.1 are wrong or incomplete: the Markov factorization does not make a CBN – a CBN requires that *all* interventional distributions satisfy the Markov factorization and other constraints (see Pearl, 2009, Definition 1.3.1).
- **Figure 1 (right) and set $Z$ (R1).** The set $Z$ includes 9 different DAGs, but the set of factorizations consistent with $G_0$ should contain $2 \times 2 \times 3 = 12$ possible FMDPs. Are there missing selection criteria? This needs clarification.
- **Algorithm 1 details (R1, R3, R4).**
  - Line 5 to line 6: More details are needed on how to obtain $F_*$ from $F_k$.
  - Step 2: What is the computational complexity added relative to standard PSRL? (e.g., complexity of step 2 in Algorithm 1).
  - Step 7: Are the posteriors in closed form? If not, what approximations are used and how do they affect the regret?
  - Line 180: “line 9” does not exist in Algorithm 1 (R4, minor).
- **Unclear implications of weak causal discovery (R2, minority critical).** The weak causal discovery result states that a $Z$-sparse super-graph of the ground-truth causal graph can be recovered, but its significance is not self-explanatory.
  - Is it fundamentally impossible to recover the ground-truth causal graph? If so, can C-PSRL still converge to the optimal policy?
  - Is the degree of difference between the true causal graph and the super-graph associated with convergence rate or optimality?
  - How can the byproduct (super-graph) be utilized? Is there a way to prune it to obtain the true graph?
- **Robustness to misspecified prior (R2, minority critical).** The paper assumes the (correct) partial causal graph is given a priori but does not discuss what happens if this assumption is violated (e.g., spurious causal edges). Incorporating incorrect causal relationships could lead to suboptimal exploration or biased posterior estimates. This is a significant gap for real-world applicability where causal knowledge is often incomplete or noisy.
- **Missing experimental details and comparisons (R2, R4).**
  - The experiment section is very brief. Construction of priors is not discussed in detail: how is the support of the distribution over factorizations chosen (given sparseness $Z$)? How are prior probabilities assigned to each factorization? What are the priors over transition probabilities and rewards? Are conjugate priors used to allow closed-form posterior updates? This is a crucial component left unelaborated.
  - Is the assumption in Lemma D.1 satisfied by the baseline with uninformative priors in the experiments?
  - It would be beneficial to include an experiment with F-PSRL using the partial causal graph as the causal graph to see if C-PSRL still adds value.
  - The paper only shows the case $\eta=2$. It would be interesting to see performance with low prior knowledge (e.g., $\eta=0,1$).
- **Assumption in Definition 2 (R4).** The assumption that any misspecification in $\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F}_k}$ negatively affects the value function of $\pi_k$ seems strong. Some causal relationships may be irrelevant to the optimal policy, so misspecifying them might not affect the value. The authors should justify this assumption or discuss conditions under which it holds.
- **Missing comparison with causal bandits literature (R3).** The paper should compare with Lattimore et al (2016) on causal bandits with known structure and Lu et al (2021) on causal bandits with unknown graph, and discuss possible connections and differences in regret bounds.
