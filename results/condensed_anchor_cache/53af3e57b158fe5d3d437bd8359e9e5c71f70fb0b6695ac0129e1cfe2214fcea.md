## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces SyCO, a framework that maps 2D molecular graphs to latent 3D point clouds via synthetic ETKDG conformers and an EGNN encoder, then applies an E(3)-equivariant diffusion model (EDM) for generation, followed by MLP decoders for atom/bond prediction. Reviewers find the idea interesting and intuitive, with clear writing and creative conditional generation experiments. However, concerns are raised about whether the 3D latent inductive bias is conclusively beneficial, experimental gaps (e.g., EGNN-decoder ablations, bond-prediction comparisons), scalability to larger molecules, heavy reliance on prior diffusion methodology, and insufficient depth in geometric GNN exploration. One reviewer (R2, score 8) is notably more positive after author response, while the others (score 6) see remaining issues.

### Strengths
- The model and experiments are clearly described, with details often missing in other papers (R1, R2).
- The overall idea is novel and matches geometric intuition that molecules naturally live in 3D space (R2, R3).
- The reconstruction ablation study on the autoencoder justifies the use of the EGNN architecture (R3).
- SyCO achieves state-of-the-art results for non-autoregressive models on ZINC250K, outperforming existing all-at-once 2D graph generative methods (R2).
- The framework enables diverse conditional generation/optimization settings: scaffold-constrained generation, similarity-constrained optimization, inpainting, and property-guided generation (R2, R3).
- For guided generation, comparable results with “Translation” methods are impressive (R1).
- The authors’ classifier guidance is well explained and motivated (R4).
- The case study on adding atoms to a molecule during diffusion is well motivated (R4).
- Code is well documented (R4).
- SyCO’s simplicity and versatility should encourage further development at the intersection of 2D and 3D generation (R4).

### Weaknesses
- The experiments are ambiguous on whether the 3D-latent inductive bias is indeed helpful; none of the experiments conclusively prove or disprove the hypothesis (R1).
- Table 3 results on ZINC250K appear saturated, making gains difficult to interpret (R1).
- FCD and KL values in Table 4 are presented without enough interpretation; without prior expertise it is hard to evaluate their significance (R1).
- The authors should clarify what the error bars in Table 3 represent (e.g., standard deviations across 3 runs as in Table 4?) (R4).
- The most compelling experiment, Similarity-Constrained Optimization, is presented with only scant data; more detail is needed, especially as this is the most real-world useful application and most likely to show signal (R1).
- De novo generation is not a terribly useful task in practice; more emphasis on guided tasks would strengthen the paper (R1).
- A comparison to a decoder-only EGNN setup (encoder outputs ETKDG coordinates, EGNN + MLP predicts atom/bond types) is missing; this would show whether the EGNN encoder latent space is necessary (R2).
- In Section 6.1, GeoLDM should be paired with a more sophisticated bond-determining algorithm (e.g., RDKit’s rdDetermineBonds or OpenBabel) to prove the utility of the EGNN latent space for the diffusion model to target (R2).
- Training time (~10 days on a single A100 GPU) is high relative to other methods; this likely prevented hyperparameter tuning on GuacaMol (R2).
- Scalability is a concern: latent point clouds likely require far higher computational cost and memory for larger molecules; learning 3D information for large structures may be challenging compared to learning 2D graph distributions (R3).
- SyCO borrows most of its diffusion methodology from prior work (EDM); the only notably new component is adding nodes during inpainting (R4).
- EDM is no longer state-of-the-art for 3D molecule generation; the authors could have investigated which geometric GNNs best encode 2D graphs as latent 3D point clouds, rather than taking a wide benchmarking approach (R4).
- Conditional generation results for LogP (Table 5) and diversity (Table 6) are underwhelming; the authors should discuss how these could be improved, not merely attribute it to molecule denoising networks (R4).
- The authors should discuss pros and cons of relying on RDKit for conformer generation, including inherent biases in its distance-based coordinate estimation that might impact the generative model (R4).
- It is unclear how the model handles chirality, since the diffusion model is E(3)-equivariant; this could motivate using SE(3)-equivariant GNNs (R2).
- The KL divergence reference at the end of page 8 (“a variety of physiochemical descriptors”) is too vague; a precise definition is needed (R1).
- The novelty metric on GuacaMol is surprisingly high (100%), indicating no training molecules are sampled; this is very different from typical 3D generation behavior and requires explanation (R2).
- Experiments on generalization (train on smaller graphs, generate larger ones) would be valuable (R2).
- Analysis of invalid molecules would help motivate future work (R2).
- Comparison of training and sampling time to other models would clarify the tradeoff between compute and performance (R2).
- Line 503 has a typo: “... factors 2.7 ad 15.6” (R2).
