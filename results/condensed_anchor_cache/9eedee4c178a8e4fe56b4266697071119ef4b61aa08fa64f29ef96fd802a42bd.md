## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces "neuron regeneration" to maintain plasticity in deep RL, combining cycle reset (periodic neuron reinitialization) with inner distillation (knowledge recovery via KD). The Sustainable Back Propagation (SBP) framework is integrated with PPO to create P3O. Experiments are conducted on MuJoCo and DMC (including non-stationary tasks). One reviewer (score 8) finds the approach clear and effective; the other three (scores 3, 5, 5) raise major concerns about novelty, evaluation, and generalizability.

### Strengths
- **Knowledge distillation for reset**: Introducing KD into the resetting process helps alleviate performance drops after each reset cycle (R1). This is considered a promising solution to preserve critical knowledge during reset (R3).
- **Clear framing and presentation**: The paper provides good framing of plasticity issues, clear and concise descriptions, and helpful visuals (R2, R4). The method is introduced in a reasonable and theoretical way (R4).
- **Empirical performance**: P3O improves on baseline PPO in all tested environments, beats other methods in most experiments, and greatly surpasses them in several cases (R2). Experimental validation is comprehensive, covering standard and non-stationary scenarios (R3, R4).
- **Novel cyclic reset perspective**: The neuron-level cyclic reset (refreshing different neurons on a cyclical basis, rather than specifically identifying neurons for reset) offers a fresh approach (R3). The use of weight norm and gradient norm as analytical metrics provides insights into network behavior (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical contribution**: The cycle reset is no different from widely-used periodic reset mechanisms (R1). The distillation module uses an established KD method; the only novelty is applying KD to resetting (R1). The "neuron regeneration" concept heavily overlaps with the plasticity-stability trade-off and is not justified (R3). Terminology issues: "Sustainable Back Propagation" is misleading (no backprop modification); "Stay Hungry, Keep Learning" and inconsistent use of "Sustainable Brain Plasticity" in the abstract reduce precision (R3).
- **Insufficient experimental validation**:
  - Only PPO is tested. Reviewer argues critic suffers more plasticity loss, so DQN/SAC/DrQ are more suited for studying plasticity (R1). The framework must be validated on DQN-based and off-policy actor-critic methods to demonstrate generality (R3, R4).
  - PPO results appear sub-optimal and potentially unfair: e.g., SAC+ReDo achieves >10,000 return on HalfCheetah (MuJoCo) vs. much lower reported values (R1); adaptive replay ratio achieves ~800 on Quadruped Run (DMC) vs. lower results in Fig. 9 (R1). PPO+Cycle shows no benefit over PPO in Fig. 4, suggesting cycle reset does not help plasticity loss in this setting (R1).
  - On DMC, using original PPO is rare because it struggles to learn effective policies; examples Dog Walk and Hopper Hop show no method achieves effective learning (highest score <0.1), undermining claims (R4).
  - Number of seeds not specified (R4). Variance shading in Fig. 5 shows 0 variance for PPO+CBP, raising concerns about experimental fairness (R4). Large variance differences across curves question the design.
- **Missing ablation and comparisons**: 
  - Alpha=0.5 (symmetrical KL divergence) not tested in alpha-DKL ablation – if optimal, explanation would be unnecessary (R2).
  - Insufficient comparison with targeted reset approaches (ReDo). Need controlled experiment with aligned reset frequency/magnitude to isolate effect of cyclic nature vs. increased reset frequency (R3).
  - No comparison of extra computational cost: training after reset to recover performance vs. using knowledge distillation (R1).
- **Plasticity metrics**:
  - Weight norm and gradient norm are indirect and less convincing than direct metrics like fraction of active units, dormant ratio, or covariance metric (R3, R4). Lower magnitude weights do not necessarily capture meaningful plasticity (R2).
  - Fig. 14 shows P3O performs significantly worse than other methods on mainstream plasticity metrics; the explanation is confusing and reinforces doubt about mismatch between method and motivation (R4). The approach may only work with tanh activation (R4 suggests retesting with ReLU).
- **Minor issues**: Incorrect explanation of PPO clipping (R2); duplicate reference (Schulman et al. 2017) (R2); missing details on image encoder for DMC (R4).
