## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Wasserstein Flow Matching (WFM), a generative model that lifts Riemannian Flow Matching to the space of probability measures (the Wasserstein manifold). WFM is instantiated for two settings: (i) families of Gaussian distributions, using closed-form geodesics on the Bures–Wasserstein space, and (ii) point-cloud data (empirical measures), where McCann’s interpolant is approximated via entropic optimal transport. The method is tested on single-cell genomics data, 2D/3D point-cloud generation, and spatial transcriptomics. The paper is well-structured and clearly written.

### Strengths
- The problem is novel and important: generative modeling over families of distributions (distributions of distributions) with applications in single-cell genomics and point-cloud generation. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- The methodology is sound for the two considered settings. (Reviewer 2) Gaussian cases are handled effectively via McCann’s (displacement) interpolant on the Bures–Wasserstein space. (Reviewer 3)
- The method is the first (to the reviewers’ knowledge) to tractably generate point-clouds of non-uniform size. (Reviewer 2)
- The algorithm is tested on a variety of data types: Gaussian-based synthetic data, single-cell genomics, and point-cloud data. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3) Results are competitive and show improved scalability. (Reviewer 2)
- In Gaussian-distribution experiments, WFM outperforms naive baselines. (Reviewer 1)
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow. (Reviewers 2, 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited theoretical contribution.** The method is built on existing theoretical results from Ambrosio et al. (2008) and Lambert et al. (2022); the paper’s main contribution is algorithmic. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3) Reviewers 1 and 3 consider this incremental.
- **Approximation issues for point-cloud data.** The theoretical framework requires absolutely continuous measures, but point-clouds (discrete measures) violate this condition. (Reviewer 1) Moreover, entropic OT is used to approximate the true OT map needed for McCann’s interpolant; this deviation is not rigorously analyzed, and the approximation error’s effect on the learned vector field is unclear. (Reviewer 3)
- **Inconclusive performance in point-cloud generation.** Table 3 shows WFM does not outperform existing competitors on point-cloud tasks; the authors acknowledge this (lines 95–96). (Reviewer 1) Reviewer 3 finds the performance results unconvincing and lacking a clear advantage over existing methods.
- **Missing baselines and comparisons.** The experimental section could be strengthened by including additional baselines, especially from recent single-cell flow-matching methods (Klein et al., 2024; Eyring et al., 2024). (Reviewer 1) A comparison with diffusion-based point-cloud generation methods is also requested. (Reviewer 3)
- **Computational cost and scalability concerns.** Solving entropic OT during training (e.g., Sinkhorn iterations) imposes a significant computational burden that scales poorly with the number of particles. The paper does not report training times, nor does it analyze scalability beyond ~2000 particles. (Reviewer 3)
- **Unclear motivation for mean-field objective.** The objective of learning a mean field on the Wasserstein space may not capture meaningful structures like Wasserstein geodesics; a clear theoretical justification is missing. (Reviewer 3)
- **Theoretical guarantee question.** Reviewer 3 asks whether the optimal velocity field \(v_t\) that minimizes the WFM objective guarantees transporting one marginal (\(\mathfrak{p}_0\)) to the other (\(\mathfrak{p}_1\)), and requests theoretical proofs.
- **Clarity of notation.** The notation \(\overline{(\cdot)}^{L^2(\mu)}\) (lines 182–183) is unclear; it should be written rigorously and moved to Section 2. (Reviewer 1)
- **Lack of discussion of other applications.** The paper could have discussed other potential uses of generative modeling on the Wasserstein manifold, such as amortized Bayesian inference, beyond the two considered cases. (Reviewer 2)

**Reviewer disagreement.** Reviewer 2 (score 8) considers the methodology sound, the experimental validation convincing, and the problem novel. Reviewers 1 and 3 (scores 5 and 6, respectively) are more critical: they emphasize the lack of theoretical novelty, the inaccuracy of entropic OT approximations, and the unconvincing performance on point-cloud generation, along with missing baselines and scalability issues. Reviewer 3, after the rebuttal, raised their score to 6 but maintained concerns about theoretical grounding and approximation.
