## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CDAM (Class Distribution-induced Attention Map), a training-free method that uses Jensen-Shannon divergence between class distributions of patches from the same object to enhance localization in CLIP-based open-vocabulary semantic segmentation. It integrates into CLIP’s final layer and supports multi-scale patches, augmented text prompts, and entropy-based background thresholding. All five reviewers acknowledge the clear motivation and consistent performance gains across multiple datasets when CDAM is combined with several existing methods, but raise concerns about the insufficient validation of core claims, limited analyses on newer baselines, missing complexity and runtime evaluation, and heuristic choices in background thresholding. Reviewers 1 and 2 (score 8) are more positive about novelty and writing; Reviewers 3–5 (score 6) are more critical about justification and generalizability.

### Strengths
- Clear, well-articulated motivation: Addressing noisy patch-wise CLIP predictions by exploiting the observation that patches from the same object have highly correlated class distributions. This leads to a novel, intuitive use of class distribution similarities for attention refinement (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5).
- Well-written paper with clear figures; methodology, experiments, and results are easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Training-free: CDAM requires no additional training, making it easy to integrate (Reviewer 2).
- Comprehensive experiments demonstrate consistent performance improvements when CDAM is added to several SOTA methods across multiple public datasets (VOC21, etc.) (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Compatibility with existing zero-shot segmentation approaches – CDAM is complementary and can compound improvements without redundancy (Reviewer 4).
- Multi-scale patch processing and augmented text prompts enhance the versatility and precision of attention maps (Reviewers 4, 5).
- Entropy-based background thresholding dynamically adapts per image, aiding foreground-background separation (Reviewer 5; note reviewer 4 sees this as a weakness due to heuristics).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient validation of core claim.** The claim that CLIP-based prior works yield “patch-wise noisy class predictions while having highly correlated class distributions for each object” lacks rigorous statistical evidence; the analysis is limited to CLIP and MaskCLIP (an earlier method), and may not generalize to newer approaches that already address noisy predictions. This weakens the novelty argument (Reviewers 2, 3; also reviewed 4 notes dependency on CLIP limitations).
- **Limited justification for Jensen-Shannon divergence.** The paper does not adequately explain why JS divergence is specifically suitable for semantic segmentation, nor does it compare with alternatives like KL divergence or other distribution distance measures (Reviewers 3, 5).
- **Missing computational complexity and runtime analysis.** There is no breakdown of the computational cost from JS divergence calculation, multi-scale patch processing, and augmented text prompts. Scaling behavior with number of patches/scales/prompts is absent. The feasibility for real-time or resource-constrained applications is unclear (Reviewers 2, 4, 5). Several reviewers request explicit inference time measurements (Rev5) and runtime details (Rev2, Rev4).
- **Incomplete evaluation on state-of-the-art methods.** CDAM is not integrated with all strong baselines: CaR (computationally heavy) and CLIP-DIY (uses extra background extractor) are not tested. On VOC21, the best overall performance is not achieved. Most detailed analyses are done on older MaskCLIP, not on newer more performant approaches. An ablation study on CDAM components with a second baseline (e.g., other than MaskCLIP) is missing (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Performance discrepancy needs explanation.** In Table 2, ClearCLIP originally outperforms SCLIP significantly, but after adding CDAM, SCLIP outperforms ClearCLIP. The reason for this reversal is not discussed (Reviewer 3).
- **Entropy-based background thresholding is heuristic and potentially fragile.** The method uses two hyper-parameters (α, Thr_default) without clear justification; its robustness in complex, cluttered scenes with ambiguous background is not analyzed. A comparison with data-driven alternatives is missing (Reviewers 4, 5). Reviewer 4 also questions how it handles scenes where background and foreground have similar entropy values.
- **Dependency on CLIP’s inherent limitations.** CDAM inherits CLIP’s biases regarding class diversity and image-text representation. There is no analysis of performance on rare, fine-grained, or underrepresented classes, nor discussion on how the approach handles categories with complex visual attributes (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing ablation on patch size impact.** The effect of different patch sizes on CDAM’s attention quality and segmentation performance is not studied (Reviewer 2).
- **Practical integration challenges unaddressed.** While compatibility is claimed, there is no discussion of potential issues: additional tuning required, overhead variations across architectures, or maintenance difficulties when combining CDAM with different zero-shot methods (Reviewer 4).
- **Related work omission.** Methods that reuse the CLIP [CLS] token and optimize feature space (e.g., [1],[2],[3]) are not discussed; it remains unclear whether they already mitigate the noisy predictions that CDAM targets (Reviewer 3).
- **Writing and presentation issues.** Figure order: Figure 2 is referenced before Figure 1 (lines 125–126) – order should be swapped (Reviewer 2). Symbol confusion: the final similarity map S (line 251) and class distribution S (line 266) are ambiguous; S_p1 in Figure 1 is not introduced (Reviewer 5). Typos: "food" appears twice in Supercategory in Table 4; extra '()' in line 330 (Reviewer 5). More detail on how and why CDAM is constructed (Section 1, lines 51-60) is needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing mIoU breakdown for seen vs. unseen classes** (Reviewer 1).
