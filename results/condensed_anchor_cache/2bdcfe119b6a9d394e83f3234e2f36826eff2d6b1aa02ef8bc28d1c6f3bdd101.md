## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MG-TSD, a multi-granularity diffusion model for probabilistic time series forecasting. It leverages coarse-grained data at various granularities to guide the diffusion learning process, motivated by an intuitive alignment between the forward diffusion (noising) process and data smoothing. The model comprises a Multi-granularity Data Generator, a Temporal Process Module (RNN), and a Guided Diffusion Process Module. The multi-granularity guidance loss is derived without requiring external data. Experiments on real-world datasets claim state-of-the-art performance. All three reviewers rated the paper 6/10 and gave confidence 4/5, with overall positive reception but several substantive concerns.

### Strengths
- The paper introduces a novel connection between data granularity and the forward diffusion process, using coarse-grained temporal signals to stabilize the diffusion model for time series forecasting. (R1, R2, R3)
- The derivation of the multi-granularity guidance loss function is solid, and the overall learning procedure is well designed and applicable. (R3)
- The paper is clearly written and provides a well-structured presentation of the model architecture and methodology. (R1, R2)
- The ablation experiments on share ratio and number of granularities offer useful insights into model behavior. (R2)
- The problem of probabilistic time series forecasting with diffusion models is significant and practically relevant. (R1)

### Weaknesses
- The assumption that the forward diffusion process aligns with smoothing to coarser granularities is not rigorously justified; theoretical or empirical validation (e.g., spectral analysis) is needed. (R3)
- The related work discussion is insufficient: the paper should compare with existing diffusion-based time series methods (e.g., TimeDiff, D3VAE) and justify technical advantages over them. (R1)
- The experimental evaluation uses only a single metric, which is insufficient for probabilistic forecasts; additional metrics such as sharpness and calibration should be reported. (R1)
- Performance improvements over strong baselines are modest, and statistical significance analysis is lacking. (R1)
- The use of RNN in the Temporal Process Module is not adequately justified; the paper should discuss relative merits compared to Transformer-based alternatives such as PatchTST and Autoformer. (R1)
- The framework’s differentiation from existing diffusion models is unclear: does MG-TSD differ only in the Guided Diffusion Process Module? (R2)
- Missing evaluation of time and memory complexity. (R2)
- The ablation study does not assess the contribution of each module individually, particularly the Multi-granularity Data Generator. (R2)
- Experimental settings are inadequately described: details on how context and prediction intervals are constructed (sliding window, overlap, train/validation/test split) are missing. (R3)
- The method is only evaluated under one prediction horizon per dataset (context‑24‑predict‑24 in Solar, Electricity, Traffic, Taxi, KDD‑Cup); experiments with varying horizons (e.g., 48, 96) are needed to demonstrate robustness for long-term forecasting. (R3)
- The paper’s reproducibility is a concern due to missing experimental details. (R3)
- The inference procedure predicts one future step at a time; how it is applied for long-term forecasting with multiple future steps is not clearly addressed. (R3)
- Equation 7 lacks clarity: the distribution and meaning of ϵ, and its consistency with x_N, are not specified. (R2)
