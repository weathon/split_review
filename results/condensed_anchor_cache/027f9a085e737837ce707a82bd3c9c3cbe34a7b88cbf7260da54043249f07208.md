## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Extended Implicit Q-Learning (EIQL), which modifies Implicit Q-Learning by stochastically trading off (via a Bernoulli parameter) between in-sample value loss and using the learned policy to sample potentially unseen actions. The goal is to extend the Bellman update’s maximization capability to out-of-sample actions while mitigating error extrapolation. Experiments are conducted on D4RL offline RL benchmarks. Reviewers agree the problem is important and the idea is simple, but disagree on execution: some find the paper well-structured and easy to implement (reviewers 2,4), while others see fatal clarity, theoretical, and experimental flaws (reviewers 1,3). Scores range from 1 to 3 (mean 2).

### Strengths
- The problem being worked on is well-motivated and important (reviewer 2).
- There are a lot of experiments with potentially significant results (reviewer 2).
- The paper is well structured; the proposed idea is simple and easy to implement (reviewer 4).
- Empirical results are provided on various robotics benchmarks, showcasing the effectiveness of the approach (reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- Lack of rigor and completeness: related work section is underdeveloped, hampering understanding of the contribution and missing critical references, including Sparse Q-learning (SQL) and Exponential Q-learning (EQL) [Xu et al., 2023] which have outperformed IQL on suboptimal trajectories (reviewers 1,4).
- Notation is inconsistent and ambiguous: undefined terms (e.g., \(L_2^\tau\) is never defined), unclear derivations, notation for the \(\tau\)-th expectile changes between L163 and L167–170, and \(V_\psi\) in Eq (2) has not rendered correctly (reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- The paper does not explain Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) in the preliminaries, making it difficult to follow Equation 1; a short summary or setup of IQL is needed to clarify what is being added (reviewers 2,4).
- Theorem 4.1 is poorly presented: lacks formal results, coherence, and has inconsistent notation (e.g., \(B\) in Eq1 vs \(p\) in the theorem; \(\beta\) is used but not defined – one reviewer notes \(\beta\) is meant to denote the behavior policy, not a parameter). The proof claims \(\beta\) controls deviation from optimality without justification (reviewers 1,2,4).
- Section 4.3 appears disconnected; its purpose is unclear until the end; it should be stated clearly at the beginning (reviewers 1,2).
- Experimental evaluation issues:
  - Only 3 seeds are used; even for comparison to IQL, at least 10 seeds per experiment are recommended (reviewer 2).
  - Figures 1 and 2 appear to use only one seed each, making it hard to draw conclusions with confidence; conclusions in L358–359 are not supported by Figure 2 (methods look similar) (reviewer 2).
  - The experiments exclude state-of-the-art offline RL algorithms (reviewers 3,4). The paper could also benefit from discussion of model-based approaches (e.g., MOReL, MOPO) that handle OOD actions differently (reviewer 4).
  - No hyperparameters are provided in the appendix, challenging reproducibility (reviewer 4).
- Theoretical analysis is misleading: a loss function cannot be regarded as a random variable (reviewer 3).
- Formatting errors: unformatted algorithm environments, incorrect citation formats (citations not in parentheses when not used as subject), typo in line 321, figures have fonts too small to read on paper and become blurry when zoomed in (reviewers 1,2,4).
- Reviewer questions overlapping with criticisms (missing definitions, inconsistent notation, unclear selection of actions): “Where does the action in the second part of Equation 3 come from?” “Why is the Bernoulli parameter required during policy extraction in Eq 3?” “Is \(\tau'\) related to \(\tau\) or independent?” “What is \(\beta\) in Theorem 4.1?” “What was the choice of \(B\) for each environment and why does EIQL have lower performance than some baselines in Table 1?” (reviewers 2,3,4).

Note: Reviewer 1 provided no strengths; reviewer 3 provided “No” strengths. Reviewers 1 and 3 are strongly negative (scores 1), while reviewers 2 and 4 are moderately positive (scores 3).
