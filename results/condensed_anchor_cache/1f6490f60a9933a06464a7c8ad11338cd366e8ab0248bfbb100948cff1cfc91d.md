## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Consistent4D, a framework for generating 360° 4D dynamic objects from a single uncalibrated monocular video captured by a static camera. It formulates the problem as a 4D generation task supervised by a 2D diffusion model via score distillation sampling (SDS), using a Cascade DyNeRF (based on K-planes) and an interpolation-driven consistency loss (ICL) enforced by a pre-trained video interpolation model. A video enhancer (GAN + super-resolution) is applied as a post-processing step. The method is evaluated on synthetic and real datasets, and ablation studies are provided.

### Strengths
- **Novel task formulation and pipeline.** The paper breaks from traditional multi-camera 4D reconstruction by framing the problem as 4D generation, uniquely leveraging generative priors (diffusion model, SDS) to address the ill-posed setting. The combination of DyNeRF, K-planes, and Dreamfusion-style SDS is a first in this space. (R1, R2, R3, R5)
- **Interpolation-driven consistency loss (ICL).** The use of a pre-trained video interpolation model to enforce spatiotemporal consistency is an interesting and effective contribution, with potential to improve other generative 3D tasks. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Cascade DyNeRF architecture.** The cascaded K-planes representation with coarse-to-fine training helps stabilize convergence and maintain temporal continuity. (R1, R5)
- **Strong qualitative results and ablation studies.** The method shows superior results over baseline DyNeRF methods (D-NeRF, K-planes) on both synthetic and real videos, with dedicated ablation on ICL. (R1, R2, R5) Some reviewers find results impressive enough for publication. (R1)
- **Clarity of presentation.** Several reviewers find the paper well-structured and easy to follow, with clear figures and tables. (R2, R3, R4) The prior work section is sufficient. (R5)

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and readability issues (multiple reviewers).** The paper suffers from informal writing, obscure terminology, grammatical errors, and misplaced notations. Crucial details are omitted, making the methodology hard to follow in places. (R1)  
  *Examples:*  
  - The term “temporal coherence” is not formally defined; the motivation for low-rank DyNeRF is unclear. (R1)  
  - Eq. (6) has a typo: likely should use \(x_J\) instead of \(x_j\). (R1, R4)  
  - Training details (e.g., “resolution 256”) are ambiguous. (R1)  
  - Figure 2 is confusing regarding inputs, outputs, and loss propagation. (R3)
- **Insufficient quantitative evaluation and missing baselines.**  
  - Comparisons are only made with regression-based methods (D-NeRF, K-planes) that require multi-view input or sufficient camera motion. A generative baseline (e.g., per-frame single-view reconstruction with Zero-1-to-3 or threestudio) is missing. (R4)  
  - The only ablation on ICL is qualitative (user study). Missing ablations with quantitative numbers for: each loss term (foreground mask loss, normal orientation loss, 3D smoothness loss), the video enhancer, the 25% probability for ICL, and the large weight difference between reconstruction loss (500) and SDS loss (0.01). (R1, R4)  
  - The inferior results of D-NeRF and K-planes in Fig. 3 are surprising and unexplained. It is unclear if the same reconstruction losses were applied to those baselines. (R4)  
  - Lack of quantitative evaluation on standard Dynamic NeRF datasets (with camera motion) and no keypoint tracking or structure evaluation for 4D consistency. (R1)  
  - No comparison of training time, inference time, or computational cost with other methods. (R2, R3)
- **Reproducibility concerns.** The pipeline involves multiple pre-trained networks (diffusion model, video interpolator, video enhancer, GAN) with many implementation details omitted even from the appendix. Reconstruction times and compute resources are not reported. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Unclear role and justification of the video enhancer.** It is not explained how “real” images for the GAN are generated, whether the super-resolution network can be applied directly, and what the GAN adds beyond a distillation step. (R5) The benefit of this component is not convincingly demonstrated. (R1)
- **Heavy reliance on pre-trained models.** This may limit flexibility to different scenarios and generalization. (R2)
- **Artifacts and limited quality.** The supplementary results show noticeable artifacts, especially considering the cascaded approach. Only two cascade stages (\(s=2\)) are used; impact of more layers is not explored. (R5) Some generated views lack texture consistency with input (e.g., T-rex roar, seagull turning). (R1)
- **Missing discussion of limitations and failure cases.** Technical limitations, scenarios where the method fails, and practical deployment issues are not addressed. (R2)  
  - **Minority reviewer (R5, score 1) is significantly more negative**, expressing disappointment with the quality and unclear video enhancer justification, and questioning the design choice of \(s=2\).
