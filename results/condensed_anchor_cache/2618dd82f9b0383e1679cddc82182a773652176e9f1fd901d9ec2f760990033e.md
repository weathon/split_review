## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GPT-Driver, which uses GPT-3.5 for motion planning by converting perception and prediction results (e.g., object types, coordinates, predicted future coordinates) into language tokens and fine-tuning the LLM to output a planned trajectory alongside a natural-language reasoning process. The approach shows promising open-loop results on the nuScenes dataset, outperforming some learned planners after fine-tuning, and the authors will open-source code. However, reviewers raise significant concerns about novelty, experimental methodology, missing analyses, and real-world applicability.

### Strengths
- **Relevance and timeliness:** The problem of using LLMs for autonomous driving motion planning is interesting and timely, and the paper provides informative insights on the feasibility of steering LLMs into motion planners that produce numerical waypoints.
- **Simplicity and effectiveness:** The approach is straightforward, well-performing, and shows that fine-tuning GPT-3.5 can transform a poor planner into one that outperforms existing learning-based methods (e.g., significant improvement from worst to best results after few-shot fine-tuning).
- **Promising results and reproducibility:** Impressive open-loop performance on the large-scale nuScenes dataset is demonstrated. The authors will open-source code, and the experiments are simple enough to be reproducible with high confidence.
- **Clarity:** The overall text flows smoothly and is easy to understand.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient novelty for a top-tier venue:** Some reviewers consider the work primarily an application of the ChatGPT API without introducing new modules or methodologies, questioning its contribution to the field (e.g., “just an application experiment report”). Other reviewers found the approach novel, but this disagreement is noted.
- **No closed-loop evaluation:** Motion planning requires closed-loop validation; open-loop results may not correlate with closed-loop performance. The authors should test on available closed-loop benchmarks (e.g., nuPlan, Waymo Motion Dataset, MetaDrive) or at least analyze online feasibility and latency.
- **Dependence on external perception/prediction modules:** The method assumes detections and predicted trajectories from existing SOTA methods (e.g., UniAD) as input, adding significant complexity and making comparisons to end-to-end approaches unfair. No ablation is performed to evaluate robustness to errors in this upstream module.
- **Missing essential ablation studies:** The paper lacks analysis of (a) the contribution of chain-of-thought reasoning to trajectory generation, (b) the quality and impact of auto-generated reasoning labels (e.g., compared to human-annotated labels), (c) performance breakdown across different driving scenarios (e.g., lane changes, intersections), and (d) the effect of perception/prediction quality on final planning performance. Average L2 errors and collision rates can be misleading.
- **Weak and inaccurate related work:** The related work section omits recent motion planning papers from robotics/AI venues (e.g., Teng et al. 2023 survey) and misclassifies Dauner et al. (2023) as a rule-based method.
- **Clarity and presentation issues:** Several issues hamper understanding: incorrect reference formatting (e.g., “P3 Sadat et al.” instead of “P3 (Sadat et al.)”); Figure 1 not cited in the text; Equation (2) claims map input but maps are not used in practice and it is unclear how a map could be represented as text; Section 4.3 does not specify whether UniAD is fully trained or partially trained; the three-stage prompting/fine-tuning process is incompletely described (Figure 3 only shows the final stage); and the baseline prompt includes future positions of obstacles without velocities, which may be acausal and incomplete.
- **Missing latency, reliability, and multi-modality analysis:** The paper provides only a hand-wavy discussion of online latency, without quantitative analysis. The stability of the LLM’s output (e.g., multi-modality, sensitivity to prompt variation, reliability across runs) is not evaluated.
- **Questions about data and evaluation:** It is unclear what split of nuScenes data is used for fine-tuning vs. evaluation. Achieving centimeter-level precision on trajectory prediction is surprising given the ambiguity of optimal driving behavior.
- **Unclear auto-labeling process:** The generation of hypothetical ego trajectories for auto-labeling reasoning is not sufficiently explained; details on the algorithm and hyperparameter choices are needed.
