## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes HiLoRA, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method that augments low-rank adaptation (LoRA) by learning high-frequency components (small singular values) while constraining larger singular values to mitigate catastrophic forgetting. The method is validated on NLU and QA tasks. Reviewers agree the topic is important and the paper contains extensive experiments, but raise major concerns about novelty, clarity of the “frequency” concept, small performance gains, missing comparisons, and lack of analysis on forgetting behavior in related methods.

### Strengths

- The motivation – mitigating catastrophic forgetting in LoRA by controlling singular values – is technically sound and addresses an important problem (R1, R4).
- Extensive experiments and ablation studies are conducted across multiple datasets, with sensitivity analysis (R1).
- The method reduces accuracy loss on pre-trained tasks (i.e., forgets less than LoRA and some variants) (R3, R4).
- The writing is clear and easy to follow (R1).
- The description of the problem and method is detailed (R2).

### Weaknesses

- **Performance improvement is marginal**; Tables 1 and 2 show very small gains and sometimes worse performance than baselines (e.g., AdaLoRA outperforms HiLoRA on Table 2) (R1, R2). Reviewer 2 notes this is a critical issue.
- **Novelty is limited.** The implementation (Figure 7, Equation 8) closely resembles prior work, with the only difference being a learnable and clipped Σ matrix (R2). Reviewer 3 echoes the lack of novelty. However, Reviewer 1 finds the formulation sound – this disagreement should be noted: one reviewer sees contribution, two others see little novelty.
- **Definition of “frequency” is confusing.** The paper equates low/high frequency with eigenvalue magnitude, but the claim that increasing largest singular values corresponds to forgetting is not supported by Figure 1(a), which shows a negative or no correlation (R2).
- **Missing comparisons.** Table 2 does not include PiSSA, MiLoRA, DoRA, rsLoRA, or LoRA+ (R2). Reviewer 3 asks for scaling experiments on LLaMA-2-7B with Meta-Math/GSM8K, and effectiveness when rank increases (R3). Reviewer 4 notes that Table 1 (on RoBERTa) shows AdaLoRA worse than LoRA, contradicting the original AdaLoRA paper and others; authors should clarify if results are reproduced or cited (R4).
- **Hyper-parameter setting lacks principles.** No rule of thumb for setting thresholds/ranks across tasks or models (R1). Which initialization strategy was used (random r singular vectors vs. zero U and random Gaussian V)? (R3).
- **Motivation for catastrophic forgetting in LoRA is insufficient.** For single-task fine-tuning, one may prefer performance on the downstream task; for multi-task, MultiLoRA or general LLMs are alternatives (R2).
- **Architecture diagrams unclear** (Figure 2). Implementation details should be moved from appendix to main paper (R3). Equations (6) and (7) should be introduced within Algorithm 1 (R3).
- **Specific corrections and clarifications:**
  - Equation (6): shapes should be \(d_1 \times r\), \(r \times r\), \(r \times d_2\) (R4).
  - Equation (4): index \(i\) undefined (instead of \(n\)) (R4).
  - Line 190: explain why depth ≥ 2 causes separation of values (R4).
  - Tables 1 and 2 should include model names in table descriptions (they use different models) (R4).
  - Figure 4: only 4 variants shown (missing AdaLoRA), but tables include 5; readers are interested in AdaLoRA’s forgetting property (R4). Also, figure title says MRPC but line 464 mentions STS-B (R4).
  - Line 354: capitalization error (“Indicating” should be lowercase) (R4).
- **Deeper analysis needed:** Relationship between pre-trained and fine-tuning task similarity and forgetting (if tasks are similar, does forgetting still occur?) (R3). The paper does not address why AdaLoRA may also suffer from forgetting (R2).
