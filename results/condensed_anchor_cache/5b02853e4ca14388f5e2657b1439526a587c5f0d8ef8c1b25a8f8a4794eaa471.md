## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes L-DRO, a method to mitigate sub-population shifts in vision-language models (CLIP) by using language prompts to debias image features. A vision feature adapter is trained to maximize entropy over sub-population text embeddings (e.g., “man”/“woman”), making the adapted features indistinguishable across subgroups, while a consistency loss keeps them close to original CLIP features. Experiments on Waterbirds and CelebA show worst-group accuracy improvements over zero-shot CLIP and some DRO baselines. However, the method’s performance, novelty, and practicality are debated. One reviewer (Score 6) finds it effective and comprehensive; another (Score 3) considers it underperforming and flawed; two others (Scores 5) see promise but raise significant concerns about applicability and assumptions.

### Strengths
- The core idea is simple and appealing: using language descriptions to directly remove spurious features from vision embeddings.
- The method does not require instance-level sub-population labels, and can handle multiple spurious attributes simultaneously.
- Training is stable across epochs (Reviewer 1), and the approach can be combined with other debiasing methods (e.g., CVaR-DRO, χ²-DRO).
- Evaluation is comprehensive: multiple datasets (Waterbirds, CelebA), metrics (worst-group, average accuracy), and comparisons with several baselines (ERM, CVaR DRO, JTT, χ²-DRO).
- Table 4’s analysis of (dis)alignment between source and target sub-populations provides innovative insights (Reviewer 2).
- Performance improvements are consistent and impressive according to Reviewer 3 (e.g., worst-case gains of ~10% on Waterbirds and ~30% on CelebA vs. zero-shot CLIP). Reviewer 4 also notes encouraging results. However, Reviewer 1 contests this: comparing with recent methods (e.g., [1] achieving 92.9% vs. L-DRO’s ~82% on Waterbirds), the gains appear modest.

### Weaknesses
- **Limitation of entropy maximization (Reviewer 1, high confidence):** Maximizing entropy only forces adapted features to be orthogonal to the *difference* of spurious embeddings, not to each individual embedding. Thus spurious correlations may persist. This is empirically suggested by Table 5, where penalizing semantically correlated sources can even improve worst-case accuracy in zero-shot classification.
- **Baseline implementation concerns (Reviewer 1):** Reported JTT worst-group accuracy (e.g., 86.7% on Waterbirds, 81.1% on CelebA in [1]) is much higher than the results presented here, indicating possible mis-tuning or unfair comparison.
- **Modest performance relative to recent SOTA (Reviewer 1, minority negative):** The method underperforms recently published approaches by 10–30% in worst-group accuracy. This may be due to weak entropy regularization or reliance on a single feature adapter, which is insufficient to disentangle complex spurious correlations.
- **Novelty concerns (Reviewer 2):** Using an auxiliary modality to guide debiasing is not new; the method is a straightforward application of existing contrastive loss ideas (maximizing entropy over prompts while preserving task features). The paper does not clearly differentiate its contribution from prior work.
- **Restrictive to vision-language paradigm (Reviewers 2 and 4):** The method fundamentally requires language modality to supply debiasing prompts. Generalizing to other modality pairs (e.g., vision-audio) is not discussed, and the debiasing prompt concept may not transfer.
- **Practical issues with prompt engineering (Reviewer 4):** The method assumes no sub-population labels for validation, yet performance is highly sensitive to prompt choice (Table 1 shows large variance). Without labels, practitioners cannot validate prompts. The paper does not address how to select or combine multiple debiasing prompts when ground truth is unavailable.
- **Consistency loss contradiction (Reviewer 3):** The debiasing loss (maximizing entropy) and the consistency loss (keeping adapted features close to original) may be contradictory—especially since original features may contain spurious information. Ablation on the trade-off coefficient is missing.
- **Data size sensitivity (Reviewer 4; also Table 3):** With small datasets (<100 examples per group) L-DRO harms worst-case accuracy compared to zero-shot CLIP. The paper lacks analysis of when the method becomes beneficial and how practitioners can assess whether they have enough data.
- **Failure on ViT-L/14 (Reviewer 4):** The method fails on the largest model tested (ViT-L/14 on CelebA). No explanation or practical guidance is provided.
- **Missing ablation:** No results for training with only the debiasing loss (Eq. 1 without consistency) are reported (Reviewer 1).
- **Table 4 interpretation gap (Reviewers 1 and 2):** When source and target sub-populations are misaligned, average accuracy stays similar to zero-shot but worst-case accuracy diverges. The paper does not explain whether other subgroups are disproportionately affected or whether the algorithm still works on attributes not directly related to the target domain.
- **Minor issues:** Parentheses in prompts (Table 1) should be made distinguishable; repeated "Eq." and "equation" in text (Reviewer 1); notation inconsistency (y_p vs y_b) in Sec. 4 (Reviewer 3).
