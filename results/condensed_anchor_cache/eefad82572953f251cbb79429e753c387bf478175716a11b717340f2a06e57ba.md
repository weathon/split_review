## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents the first theoretical convergence analysis of Adafactor (a memory-efficient variant of Adam) under non-convex smooth stochastic optimization. Analytic results are given for full-batch and stochastic settings, with and without update clipping. All three reviewers acknowledge the novelty of the work and that the proofs appear correct. However, they collectively raise concerns about restrictive assumptions (bounded iterates, bounded gradients, bounded noise), poor dependence of the bounds on the epsilon₁ parameter, weak experimental validation (missing comparisons with other optimizers, insufficient reporting of accuracy/test loss, lack of visible differences in plots), and various writing/presentation issues. Reviewers question whether the analysis can be extended to more realistic settings and whether the clipping variant adds theoretical value.

### Strengths
- First theoretical convergence analysis of Adafactor, addressing a practically relevant and popular optimizer. (R1, R2, R3)
- Proofs appear correct and are accompanied by a sketch that clarifies the key steps. (R1, R2)
- The paper clearly lists the differences between Adafactor and Adam, discusses advantages of the presented results, and explains the proof challenges in detail. (R3)

### Weaknesses
**Assumptions**
- Assumes boundedness of the ℓ∞-norms of iterates both from below (Θ_min > 0) and above (Θ_max). This assumption is not stated in the assumptions section; the lower bound is particularly restrictive and not required for comparable Adam analyses; it is unclear how Θ_max can be bounded in practice or how Θ_min > 0 is guaranteed. (R1 W1; R1 Q1)
- Assumes almost sure boundedness of stochastic gradients and bounded noise (A.4). This is a strong assumption: even Gaussian noise violates it, and in practice the bound can be huge, especially for LLMs. Existing work on AdaGrad obtains convergence under sub-Gaussian noise only. (R1 W2; R2 W1a; R1 Q2)
- The authors are asked whether the bounded gradient assumption can be removed for the deterministic case. (R1 Q3; R2 Q)

**Theory Limitations**
- The derived bounds depend at least on ε₁⁻¹/² and up to ε₁⁻⁵/², whereas the best-known Adam results have only logarithmic dependence on the analogous parameter. (R1 W3; R1 Q4)
- The deterministic convergence rate is either O(mn log T / T) (dimension-dependent) or O(ε₁⁻² log T / √T) (where ε₁⁻² can be ∼10⁶⁰ for the default ε₁), raising concerns about practical applicability. (R1 W4; R1 Q5)
- The analysis of Adafactor with clipping yields the same rate as without clipping; no theoretical or practical benefit is demonstrated, and the clipping is not the variant used to handle heavy-tailed noise. (R2 W1b; R1 Q6)
- The results are not as advanced as those available for other adaptive methods; the theoretical contribution is considered rough. (R2 W1)
- **Inconsistencies and errors:**
  - Abstract states full-batch rate is Õ(1/T) but the contribution section (lines 67–70) says Õ(1/√T). (R3 W2; R2 W3a)
  - In Equation (13), the second result should use Δ₀² instead of Δ̃₀². (R3 W3)
  - Line 1430: C₀ should be C′₀. (R3 W4)
  - The term “estimation” appears once and is not explained. (R3 W5)
  - The derivation of the free-dimension bound relies on whether d√(mn)/‖Ū_k‖_F > 1, which is not guaranteed. (R3 Q2)
  - Theorem B.1 does not show dimension dependence, yet the result is claimed to be free-dimension. (R3 Q3)
  - Why is Õ(1/√T) described as the “best” rate when Õ(1/T) is faster? (R3 Q1)

**Experiments**
- For BERT-base, only training loss is reported; no accuracy or test loss. (R1 W5)
- For ResNet, accuracy largely depends on the parameter c, but the training loss plots do not make this clear. (R1 W5)
- Sections D.2 and D.3 do not report accuracy or test loss, so the influence of ε₁ and α remains unclear. (R1 W5)
- Figures 4, 5, and 6 show almost no visible difference between lines; the plots should be zoomed to show the final stages of training. (R1 W5)
- No comparisons with other optimizers (Adam, RMSProp, Lion, etc.) are provided to demonstrate practical feasibility. (R2 W2)

**Writing and Presentation**
- Numerous English inaccuracies and structural flaws: title (“under” → “for”), lines 48, 54, 92–94, 145, missing periods on page 4, line 182 (“maintain” → “maintains”), redundant paragraphs in Section 6.1, and a technical epsilon₁/epsilon₂ discussion that is not self-contained. (R1 W6)
- Many formula numbers (4, 5, 11, 19, 21, 23, 37, 39, 41, 64, 69, 76, 86, 101, 102) are never referenced and should be removed for readability. (R2 W3b)
- The abstract does not fully present the contributions, which may harm reader perception. (R3 W1)
- The paper requires thorough proofreading. (R1 W6; R2 W3)
