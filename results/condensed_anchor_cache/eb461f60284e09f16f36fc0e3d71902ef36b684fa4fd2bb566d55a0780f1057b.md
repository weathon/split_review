## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces GDL-DS, a benchmark for evaluating geometric deep learning models under distribution shifts. It covers three scientific domains (particle physics, materials science, biochemistry), categorizes shifts as conditional, covariate, and concept shifts, explores three levels of OOD information access, and evaluates 3 backbones with 11 learning algorithms across 30 settings. Two reviewers (1 and 3) regard the benchmark as comprehensive, well-motivated, and a valuable contribution. One reviewer (2, confidence 5) is strongly critical, asserting that the causal framework is fundamentally flawed, key terms are misused, claims about novelty are overblown, the experimental setup is unfair, baselines are outdated, and the dataset scope is limited.

### Strengths
- Introduction of a new benchmark that spans multiple scientific domains and distribution shift types, filling a gap in the GDL literature. (R1, R3)
- Use of causality to classify shifts (conditional, covariate, concept) offers a clearer understanding of the challenges. (R3)
- Exploration of three levels of OOD information access (no OOD info, only features, few labels) provides a nuanced view of model behavior. (R3)
- Extensive experimentation with 30 settings, 3 GDL backbones, and 11 learning algorithms; the evaluation appears correct and analyses are sufficient. (R1, R2, R3)
- Resulting key takeaways can guide practitioners in selecting solutions based on available OOD data. (R3)
- The paper is clearly written and technically sound (R1; though R2 disagrees on the causal section).
- The topic (OOD in geometric deep learning for science) is compelling and innovative. (R2)
- Presentation is rich, with well-organized tables and figures. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- Detailed dataset characteristics (size, other properties) for both new and existing datasets are missing. (R1)
- **Critical: Misuse of established terms.** Concept drift and covariate shift should follow conventional definitions (e.g., concept drift as changes in \(p(y|X)\)). The paper’s modifications (e.g., \(P(Y|X_c)\)) risk confusion and should be corrected. (R2, confidence 5)
- **Critical: Causal statements in Section 3.1 are logically unclear.** The claim that \(X\) consists of two disjoint parts with \(X_i \perp Y | X_c\) is violated by a simple chain \(X_c \rightarrow Y \rightarrow X_i\). Furthermore, for \(X \rightarrow Y\), \(P(Y|X)P(X)=P(Y|X_c)P(X)\) does not hold; changes in \(P(Y|X_i)\) also produce conditional shift. Constraining \(Y \rightarrow X\) is not necessary. The framework needs major revision. (R2, confidence 5)
- **Overclaiming novelty and misrepresenting related work.** Table 1 states that no existing OOD benchmark considers conditional shift, but OoD-Bench, GDS, and GOOD include conditional shift (e.g., CMNIST). Also, WILDS makes OOD information available, contradicting the table’s claim. Existing work is not properly treated. (R2)
- **Unfair experimental setting.** Different methods are trained on different amounts of data: some on Train-ID only, domain adaptation methods on Train-ID + OOD inputs, transfer learning on labeled Train-OOD. Comparing all methods together under these disparities is not valid. (R2)
- **Outdated baselines.** Many recent OOD methods (e.g., from the WILDS leaderboard, graph OOD methods that outperform DIR, geometric methods tailored to scientific tasks) are missing. The benchmark should include more recent state-of-the-art. (R2)
- **Limited dataset scope.** Only three (not newly collected) datasets are used, which is few for a benchmark. More discussion on contributions like curating 3D coordinates would help. (R2)
- **No comparison with distribution shift research in CV and NLP.** The study would benefit from a holistic perspective relating its findings to prior work in other domains. (R3)
- **Rationale for choosing the three GDL backbones is not given.** It is unclear whether adding more diverse backbones or algorithms would alter the conclusions. (R3)

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* Reviewers 1 and 3 are largely positive about the benchmark’s value, whereas Reviewer 2 (confidence 5) finds the causal foundation and experimental evaluation deeply problematic.
