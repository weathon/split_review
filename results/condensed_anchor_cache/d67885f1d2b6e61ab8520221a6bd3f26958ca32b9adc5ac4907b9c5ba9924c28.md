## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents a synthetic theorem generation framework for Lean that uses forward reasoning with a limited set of tactics and premise selection to produce new theorems and proofs from existing libraries (Mathlib, Lean Workbook). The generated data is used to fine-tune Falcon2-11B, yielding modest improvements on miniF2F (e.g., 38.1% to 39.3% on the mixed-dataset setting). All four reviewers gave a score of 5 and recommended reject, citing marginal results, data quality concerns, and limited novelty.

### Strengths
- **Well-motivated**: Addressing the data scarcity in formal theorem proving is of clear interest and importance. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Scalable generation**: The framework can produce millions of new theorems and proofs, significantly expanding existing corpora (2B tokens vs 208M in Mathlib). (R1, R4)
- **Methodology clarity**: The paper is logically structured and provides good implementation details in the appendix, making the approach reproducible. (R1, R3) – however, some reviewers (R2, R4) note that the writing and figures (e.g., Figure 1) could be more illustrative and that adding concrete examples would improve understandability.
- **Novelty divergence**: R2 considers this the first work to introduce forward reasoning in tactic mode for theorem generation, while R4 views the pipeline as lacking significant technical innovation, building on well-established ideas. The remaining reviewers (R1, R3) highlight useful components (premise selection, search for diversity, proof minimization) without explicit novelty claims.
- **Premise selection with LeanDojo ReProver** ensures the generated theorems remain mathematically relevant and grounded. (R3)
- **Systematic search and minimization** are designed to enhance diversity and eliminate irrelevant proof steps. (R2, R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal benchmark improvement**: Despite generating ~2B tokens of synthetic data (about 10× the Mathlib size), the gain on miniF2F is only ~1% (3 additional problems) in both evaluated settings (from 37.3% to 38.5% and from 38.1% to 39.3%). This small improvement raises concerns about the practical utility and significance of the synthetic data. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Limited tactic set**: Only five tactics (rewrite, simp_arith, omega, etc.) are used for generation, applied linearly. This strongly restricts the diversity, complexity, and mathematical breadth of the generated theorems. R2 argues this leads to a biased dataset that cannot leverage the variety of backward reasoning tactics. R4 notes that the absence of concrete generated examples makes it impossible to assess theorem difficulty or diversity. (R2, R4)
- **Lack of quality metrics**: The paper provides no metrics to evaluate the novelty, difficulty, semantic richness, or diversity of the synthetic dataset (e.g., average proof length, dependency depth, number of unique tactics). Without these, it is unclear whether the theorems are merely variations of existing ones or introduce genuinely new insights. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Missing examples and analysis**: No concrete examples of generated theorems or their proofs are shown. It is not reported which specific miniF2F problems were newly solved or whether these overlap with those solved by the baseline model. (R2, R4) R1 also requests a qualitative analysis of the additional theorems proven.
- **Missing ablations**: No ablation study isolates the effect of the search strategy, diversity mechanisms, proof minimization, or tactic selection on the final performance. R1 asks for comparisons with autoformalization as an alternative augmentation method. R4 suggests fine-tuning solely on synthetic data or on equivalent-sized real data to gauge data quality.
- **Computational cost**: Generation requires 60 × 36 vCPU for 24 hours, even with a search depth of only 10. This high resource demand may limit accessibility and practical adoption. (R3)
- **Implementation details lacking**: The implementation of the TacticsFor function (Algorithm A) is not described; it may be time-consuming to iterate over all theorems and variable assignments. (R3) R1 also asks for elaboration on the 'have' tactic’s requirement of a library lemma.
- **Concerns about statistical significance**: The small performance gain (3 additional solved problems) may not be statistically significant, especially given the randomness in training and evaluation. (R4)
- **Disagreement on novelty**: While R2 claims the tactic-mode forward reasoning is novel, R4 considers the overall pipeline standard and the technical contribution incremental. (R2, R4)
