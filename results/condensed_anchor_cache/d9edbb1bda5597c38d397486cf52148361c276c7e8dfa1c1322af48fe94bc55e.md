## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes PerSAM, a training-free approach to personalize the Segment Anything Model (SAM) for one-shot object segmentation given a single image with a reference mask. PerSAM uses two techniques: target-guided attention and target-semantic prompting. To handle varying object scales, a fine-tuned variant PerSAM-F is introduced that freezes SAM and tunes only 2 parameters within 10 seconds. The authors also construct a new evaluation dataset, PerSeg, and demonstrate results on one-shot image and video segmentation benchmarks. Additionally, they apply PerSAM to improve DreamBooth for personalized text-to-image synthesis. All reviewers agree the paper is well-written, the method is simple and effective, and the evaluation is comprehensive. One reviewer (score 8) was particularly positive, rating the contribution and soundness as excellent; the other two (scores 6) found the contribution good or fair and noted some limitations in quantitative evaluation and comparisons.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important problem: single-shot personalization of segmentation models, with real-world applications (e.g., design, healthcare).
- Proposed method (PerSAM) is training-free, computationally efficient, and easy to implement.
- Two core techniques are clearly motivated: target-guided attention and target-semantic prompting.
- PerSAM-F efficiently alleviates scale ambiguity by tuning only 2 parameters within 10 seconds.
- A new dataset, PerSeg, is constructed and made available for personalized segmentation evaluation.
- Extensive ablation studies (Tables 4, 5, 6) and good performance across multiple image/video segmentation benchmarks.
- The method demonstrates utility beyond segmentation: improving DreamBooth for text-to-image synthesis.
- Paper is well-organized with clear motivation and high-quality visualizations.
- All three reviewers note the writing is easy to follow and the idea is simple yet effective.

### Weaknesses
- Quantitative evaluation is limited. Reviewer 1 suggests testing on more challenging datasets like MS-COCO, ADE20K, and CityScapes, and comparing against zero-shot or text-guided methods such as DenseCLIP.
- Missing comparison to SAM-PT (a related SAM adaptation for video object segmentation) in Table 2 (requested by Reviewer 2).
- Reviewer 2 asks for running speed/memory consumption of PerSAM relative to plain SAM.
- The method’s reliance on SAM’s class-agnostic feature semantics limits its ability to distinguish objects with similar appearances but different semantic categories (e.g., multiple chairs vs. tables in a scene). Reviewer 3 suggests incorporating stronger semantics like CLIP or additional training could help, but the current design may struggle in complex scenes.
- Reviewer 2 asks for more failure case visualizations.
- Reviewer 3 raises open questions: (1) Can PerSAM be extended to few-shot (5/10-shot) for better performance? (2) Is PerSAM-F generalized only to one specific object, or can it generalize across different objects with the same parameters? Could LoRA be used for multi-object generalization? (3) Can PerSAM help DreamBooth achieve more complex multi-object customization?
- Reviewer 1 notes the comparison is lacking – it would be useful to see performance against DenseCLIP for zero-shot/text-guided segmentation.
- In the appendix, DINOv2 features are used; Reviewer 2 requests results in Tables 2 and 3 using SAM’s default image encoder features to assess the necessity of DINOv2.
- Reviewer 2 asks how PerSAM differentiates objects with similar appearances in video segmentation (e.g., self-driving scenario with many similar cars/dense traffic).
