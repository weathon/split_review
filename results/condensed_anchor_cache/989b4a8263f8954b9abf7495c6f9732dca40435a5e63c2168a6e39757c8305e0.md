## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes *Shallow Diffuse*, a training-free watermarking technique for diffusion models that decouples watermark embedding from the generation process by leveraging the low-dimensional subspace of the PMP's Jacobian. Watermark lies in the null space of this subspace to maintain consistency and robustness. The method supports both server-side (protect generated image) and user-side (protect existing image) scenarios. It is compared with existing methods (e.g., Tree-Ring, Stable Signature) and evaluated on several image-prompt datasets with theoretical justification.

### Strengths
1. **Visual consistency with low-rank property**: The method utilizes the low-rank property of the PMP's Jacobian matrix to minimize visual impact, achieving visual consistency within a training-free watermark framework (Reviewer 1). This yields smaller impact on generated images compared to existing diffusion-based watermarking methods like Stable Signature and Tree-Ring (Reviewer 3).
2. **Robustness against distortions**: The injected watermark is more robust against several common image distortions than existing baselines (Reviewer 1). Robustness is evaluated (Reviewer 3).
3. **Theoretical foundation**: The paper provides rigorous theoretical proof and a substantial number of computational formulas (Reviewers 2, 4). The method has the support of a rich theoretical basis (Reviewer 2).
4. **Quality and clarity**: Despite limited originality, the paper has good quality, clarity, and significance (Reviewer 2 – minority positive on these aspects).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited attack experiments**: The robustness evaluation covers only four fixed-parameter attacks (Reviewer 4), fewer than the six distortions used in Tree-Ring (Reviewer 1). Missing attacks include rotation, regeneration (Reviewers 3, 4), adversarial perturbations (Reviewer 3), and purification (e.g., DiffPure) (Reviewer 1). The method can be viewed as a variant of TreeRing, so more diverse attacks are needed (Reviewer 4).
2. **Incomplete comparisons**: The paper mainly compares with methods that embed watermarks into the semantic space (e.g., Tree-Ring), which change the image significantly. There is no comparison with post-generation watermarking methods that introduce small perturbations (e.g., StegaStamp) (Reviewer 3). More existing watermarking methods should be evaluated (Reviewer 3).
3. **Presentation quality**: The paper has poor presentation (Reviewer 1). Tables are very difficult to read, especially headers (Reviewer 2). The experimental part is not detailed enough; it is unclear whether comparison methods use reproduced results or pre-trained models (Reviewer 2).
4. **Ablation and parameter details missing**: Ablation studies (Appendix C) are incomplete (Reviewer 1). Appendix C only provides results without any analysis (Reviewer 4). Key parameters for *Shallow Diffuse* in Section 5 (e.g., embedding channels, watermark radius) are not specified (Reviewer 4). The filter size for Gaussian blurring (8×8) and its standard deviation selection are not explained (Reviewer 2).
5. **Questionable performance on CLIP-Score**: In Table 1, the reported CLIP-Score for *Shallow Diffuse* is 0.3285, which appears to be the worst among methods. This requires further explanation (Reviewer 2).
6. **Low PSNR/SSIM and visual quality concerns**: Table 2 shows that PSNR and SSIM for most methods (including *Shallow Diffuse*) are very low, making it easy for human eyes to detect modification traces, increasing the risk of malicious removal. The visual quality of the watermarked images needs further explanation (Reviewer 2).
7. **Channel averaging results confusion**: Table 4 suggests that channel averaging (averaging clean and watermarked images across channels) enhances robustness, but the reviewer expected it to reduce robustness. The authors need to explain this observation (Reviewer 1).
8. **Discrepancy between theoretical and experimental t values**: The theoretical assumptions are built on [1], but experimental results yield a different range of t values than the theoretical analysis in [1]. Although attributed to DDIM-Inv errors, this remains perplexing (Reviewer 4).
9. **Generalizability concerns**: The method relies on the properties of DDIM and DDIM-inverse, which may limit its generalizability; it might not perform well for attacks executed in the latent space (Reviewer 4).
10. **Missing scenario analysis**: For the user scenario with multiple users re-watermarking the same image, can the watermark from a specific user be detected? (Reviewer 3). Multi-key identification performance is also missing – can *Shallow Diffuse* inject and distinguish multiple watermarks? (Reviewer 1).
11. **Formula error**: The formula on line 360, \(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0, t}:=\boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}, t}\left(\boldsymbol{x}_{t}+\lambda \Delta \boldsymbol{x}\right)\), should likely be \(\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0, t}:=\boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}, t}\left(\boldsymbol{x}_{t}\right)\) (Reviewer 4).
12. **Limited technical contribution**: Compared to Tree-Ring, the technical contribution of *Shallow Diffuse* is limited (Reviewer 3 – minority negative). However, Reviewer 2 notes that quality and clarity are good despite limited originality (minority positive).
