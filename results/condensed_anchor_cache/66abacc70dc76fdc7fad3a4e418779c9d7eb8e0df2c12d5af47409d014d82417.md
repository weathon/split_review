## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FedDFQ, a personalized federated learning method that uses a Data Identity Extraction Module (DIEM) to quantify data heterogeneity via channel summation and cosine similarity, and an Automatic Gradient Accumulation Module (AGAM) to regularize classification layers. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and FashionMNIST with 50 and 100 clients show improvements over several baselines. The paper also includes an ablation study.

### Strengths
- The problem of personalization under data heterogeneity is important. (Reviewer 2)
- Compared methods in the experiments are plenty. (Reviewer 2)
- An ablation study is included, demonstrating the effectiveness of different modules. (Reviewer 2)
- The proposed AGAM aims to regularize the classification layer through personalized gradient accumulation, enhancing generalization on out-of-distribution samples; its plug-and-play design allows easy integration into existing FL frameworks. (Reviewer 1)
- Reviewer 3 found the paper well-organized and the language clear, and noted that the experiments, including ablation studies, are comprehensive with compelling results. (Reviewer 3 – contrast: other reviewers criticized presentation and formatting.)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and contribution.** The core idea (DIEM) is simply averaging an image over the \(H\) and \(C\) dimensions into a \(W\)-dimensional vector, and using cosine similarity to measure heterogeneity. This approach is overly simplistic and fails to capture nuanced feature variations, leading to suboptimal quantification. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Weak experimental validation.** Tests are limited to two simple datasets (CIFAR-10 and FashionMNIST), which restricts generalizability. (Reviewer 1) The experimental setup section is too brief, lacking details on baseline selection rationale, evaluation metrics, and hardware specifications. (Reviewer 3) Further, no experiments with different sampling rates are provided. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing theoretical support.** No convergence proofs or stability analysis are given for the weighted aggregation and regularization modifications. (Reviewer 1) The claimed “theoretical proof” that data identifier similarity represents heterogeneity (line 215) is trivial: setting \(W\) and \(b\) in Eq. (6) to identity and zero reduces \(S(z_i,z_j)=S(x_i,x_j)\) without establishing anything deeper. (Reviewer 2)
- **Presentation and clarity problems.**  
  - The method description is hard to understand; e.g., the formation of matrix \(A\) (shape \(N\times C\times C\) vs. tensor) and the meaning of Eq. (5) are unclear. (Reviewer 2)  
  - AGAM lacks any equation description, leaving its implementation vague. (Reviewer 1)  
  - The introduction of FedDFQ is abrupt, with insufficient context and motivation. (Reviewer 3)  
  - The criticism of existing personalized FL methods (line 45–49) is vague and lacks citations. (Reviewer 2)  
  - There are numerous typos, even in equations; formatting is rough (Figure 1 arrows misaligned, text unreadable). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)  
  - *Contrast:* Reviewer 3 described the paper as well-organized and clear, but Reviewer 1 and 2 found presentation poor. The combined evidence indicates serious readability issues.
- **Privacy concerns not addressed.** DIEM transmits “data identity vectors” (distribution information) from clients to the server, yet the paper provides no privacy-preserving guarantees or mitigation analysis. (Reviewer 1)
- **High communication overhead.** Each client must upload model weights, gradients, and a data identity vector, and download packets from all other clients. This is extremely expensive and not discussed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Puzzling ablation result.** Table 2 shows that each individual component performs *worse* than local training, while the combination yields improvement. The paper offers no explanation for this counterintuitive phenomenon. (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient experimental comparison.** The comparison with baselines is too general; there is no detailed discussion of differences between FedDFQ and each distinct FL method, and only one dataset is compared (contradicts claim of two datasets – likely a per-dataset analysis gap). (Reviewer 3)  
- **Unclear motivation for DIEM.** Why not use features from the global model as the distribution representation? What bias would that introduce? (Reviewer 2)  
- **Missing rationale for baseline selection and client numbers.** Why are 50 and 100 clients chosen? (Reviewer 3)
