## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Perceptual Consistency in Diffusion Models (PCDM), a zero-shot NR-IQA method that extracts multi-scale and multi-timestep features from a pre-trained latent diffusion model (LDM) via Perceptual Manifold Guidance (PMG) and uses them to predict quality scores. While the idea of leveraging diffusion models as feature extractors for IQA is considered novel and interesting (R1, R2, R3, R4), the reviewers raise substantial concerns about the source of performance, true zero-shot nature, missing comparisons, insufficient justification, limited ablations, presentation clarity, and potential overfitting.

### Strengths
*   **Novel application of diffusion models to NR-IQA:** The exploration of using latent diffusion models for zero-shot NR-IQA is interesting and differs from traditional reference‑based or large‑dataset methods (R1, R2, R3, R4). R3 explicitly calls it “pioneering”.
*   **Effective multi-scale feature extraction:** The method extracts multi-scale features from LDMs that yield excellent experimental results, validating the approach’s effectiveness (R2, R3).
*   **Solid theoretical foundation:** The paper provides detailed theoretical proofs for the proposed algorithm (R3).
*   **Strong zero-shot performance:** Extensive experiments on multiple IQA datasets demonstrate state‑of‑the‑art zero‑shot generalization capabilities (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
*   **Unclear source of performance and justification:**
    *   It is not explained why intermediate features from a denoising U‑Net (especially noisy ones) are beneficial for a discriminative task like IQA. The justification used for depth/pose (guidance during denoising) does not apply to IQA (R1, R4).
    *   Figure 5 shows that correlation improves significantly at smaller (cleaner) timesteps, implying that the performance may stem from near‑clean features that could be extracted directly from the VAE rather than the diffusion process (R1).
    *   The phrase “perceptually consistent region” lacks a specific definition, and it remains unclear why Perceptual Manifold Guidance (PMG) is beneficial for NR-IQA (R4).
    *   The authors claim the method enables “guidance and transition on the latent manifold” (Figure 1), but provide no experimental evidence for optimizing noisy latents to improve image quality (R1).
*   **Zero‑shot claim is inaccurate:**
    *   The method is trained on IQA datasets (L358–L364), contradicting the “zero‑shot” label (R4). No few‑shot validation is provided to support the claim (R3).
*   **Missing or insufficient comparisons:**
    *   The paper does not compare against recent methods such as LIQE and DEIQT (R3).
    *   There is no comparison with other NR-IQA methods that also use diffusion models, including pixel‑diffusion vs. LDM approaches (R2, R4).
    *   Table 3 lists only three methods; the reason for this limited selection is not explained (R3).
*   **Insufficient ablation and cross‑dataset validation:**
    *   Ablation experiments are shown only on the FLIVE dataset; effects of different components on other datasets are missing (R3).
    *   Cross‑dataset validation does not include experiments on FLIVE when training on other datasets, nor pairs like Koniq↔Kadid or CLIVE→FLIVE (R3).
    *   No ablation studies are presented to determine whether an optimal subset of diffusion hyper‑features exists, or if aggregating all features is the best strategy (R3).
*   **Performance discrepancy on synthetic vs. real datasets:**
    *   The method performs better on synthetic than on real datasets, raising concerns about overfitting to specific distortions. The authors should discuss this discrepancy and provide visualizations of feature extraction from distorted regions across different domains (R3).
*   **Lack of model parameter count and scalability analysis:**
    *   The number of model parameters is not compared with other methods (R3).
    *   Scalability with increasing image resolution or dataset complexity, and the associated computational trade‑offs, are not addressed (R3).
*   **Concerns about VAE‑induced degradation:**
    *   The VAE in stable diffusion can introduce color shifts and texture loss, adding further degradation to already impaired images. The impact of this on IQA evaluations is not discussed (R1).
*   **Ambiguities in method details:**
    *   The structure of the lightweight regression network \( g_\phi \) (Eq. 16) is not defined; the number of parameters and the effect of different architectures should be provided (R2).
    *   The function \( \psi_p \) in Eq. (13) is not clearly explained (R2).
    *   The range [0–100] in Figure 5 is unclear—does it represent the mean SRCC across those timesteps? (R1)
    *   It is confusing what “sdv1.5” or “just‑sdv1.5” refers to as a perceptual metric; which IQA metric provides the labels when using BRISQUE? (R1)
    *   The algorithm is inspired by the readout method and hyper‑feature network, but the readout citation is missing (R1).
    *   The paper is difficult to follow; the presentation should use clear pipeline figures (showing trainable vs. inference components) instead of pseudo‑code or text (R1, R4). An appendix with all mathematical formulas on one page would help (R1).
    *   The reason why peak IQA performance occurs when some noise remains (timesteps 0–100) needs explanation (R1).
