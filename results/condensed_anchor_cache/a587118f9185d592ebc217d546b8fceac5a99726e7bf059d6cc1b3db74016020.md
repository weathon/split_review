## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates decentralized training of deep neural networks as an alternative to synchronous data-parallel methods like All-Reduce, focusing on the classic data-parallel setting. It proposes a runtime model to determine when decentralization yields shorter per-iteration runtimes based on three key factors (overlapping communication and computation, heterogeneous communication costs, sensitivity to varying computation times). It also introduces a decentralized variant of Adam (DAdam) with convergence guarantees and an accumulation technique to mitigate high variance from small local batch sizes. Experiments are conducted on clusters with up to 64 GPUs, with a focus on transformer-based architectures. Reviewer opinions diverge: two rate it 6 (confidence 4 and 3) citing significant weaknesses, while one rates it 8 (confidence 3) praising the thoroughness and important problem.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important, impactful problem: the practical gap between decentralized training and All-Reduce.
- Provides a runtime model that quantifies key environmental parameters and estimates potential speedups; its accuracy in capturing real-time speed discrepancies is well illustrated.
- Offers theoretical analysis (convergence guarantees) for the proposed DAdam algorithm.
- Three factors leading to speedups are identified and each is supported by numerical experiments.
- Experiments are extensive and thorough, particularly on transformer-based architectures (relevant to modern LLMs), with a clear focus on comparisons of decentralized Adam variants.
- The paper includes detailed hyper-parameter settings for reproducibility.
- At 64 GPUs, the scale is suitable for demonstrating the method’s potential.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing important related work**: Several relevant papers are overlooked, including Koloskova et al. (ICML 2020 and NeurIPS 2021) on gossip SGD with changing topologies and local updates, Nadiradze et al. (NeurIPS 2021) on asynchronous decentralized SGD with quantized and local updates (which also decouples communication from computation), Li et al. (IEEE TPDS 2020) on wait-avoiding group averaging, Zhang & You (2019) on asynchronous distributed optimization, and Singh et al. (2020) on communication-efficient momentum SGD. Missing MLsys works [1,2] on dynamic gossip and communication compression are also noted. The paper cannot be properly positioned without this context.
- **Limited novelty of DAdam**: The contribution is more system optimization than a novel optimizer; overlapping communication and computation is already widely used in many ML systems [3,4]. There are no improvements to Adam itself.
- **Lacks detailed ablation studies** to isolate individual components (overlapping, accumulation technique, decentralized Adam). It is unclear how much performance each component contributes.
- **Statistical significance not established**: The claimed loss improvement (2.841 vs 2.846, line 488) is not shown to be statistically significant.
- **Limited communication topologies tested**: Only complete and alternating-exp-ring are evaluated; performance on other topologies (e.g., torus, expander graphs) is unknown, limiting generalization.
- **Missing convergence curves for GPT-2 on OpenWebText**: Without these, it is unclear whether DAdam can match All-Reduce Adam when training with more iterations, undermining assessment for large-scale language models.
- **No clear link between the decentralized Adam optimizer and the runtime model** (noted by one reviewer).
- **Implementation details insufficient**: The paper does not describe how communication is launched (torchrun, MPIRUN, etc.), how synchronization is managed (e.g., distributed.barrier()), how overlapping of communication and computation is achieved, or how communication topologies are configured. These details are crucial for reproducibility.
- **Runtime model explanation in Appendix A.5 could be clearer** (one reviewer suggests a presentation style similar to Algorithm 1).
- **Relevance questioned**: Some experiments operate in a parameter range that may be obsolete given modern single-node systems (e.g., training ImageNet in minutes on a single 4-GPU node using FFCV). This raises the question of whether the proposed method can outperform large-batch SGD on such a system.
- **Missing attribution for runtime model intuition**: It is unclear whether the assumptions behind the runtime model are from prior work or solely from the authors.
