## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes bioFAME, a frequency-aware masked autoencoder for multimodal pretraining on biosignals. It uses a frequency-aware transformer with a fixed-size Fourier-based operator for global token mixing (independent of input length and sampling rate) and a frequency-maintain pretraining strategy that performs masked autoencoding in the latent space to preserve frequency components per channel. The model is pretrained on SleepEDF (EEG, EMG, EOG) and evaluated on unimodal and multimodal transfer experiments across five biosignal datasets, reporting an average of +5.5% improvement in classification accuracy over previous state-of-the-art. Robustness tests under modality dropout and substitution are also conducted. Reviewers expressed mixed views: some found the approach novel and the results impressive (R2, R4), while others considered the novelty limited, evaluation setups unclear, and analyses insufficient (R1, R3).

### Strengths
- **Novelty and effectiveness (R2, R4):** The combination of a frequency-based Transformer block with a masked autoencoding pretraining task that enables multimodal processing appears novel and effective. The approach is well-motivated and addresses a practical problem (multimodal biosignal pretraining under distributional shifts).
- **Clear presentation (R1, R2, R3, R4):** The paper is well-written, easy to follow, with good storyline, illustrations, and informative tables/figures.
- **Extensive evaluation and robustness (R1, R2, R3, R4):** Thorough experiments on both unimodal and multimodal settings across diverse datasets, including robustness tests (modality dropout/substitution). Results show substantial performance gains in several transfer tasks (e.g., significantly higher than reported baselines) and demonstrate practical utility.
- **Significance of the problem (R3, R2):** The paper tackles an interesting and practically important problem of multimodal pretraining for time-series biosignals with resiliency considerations, which could be useful for real-world applications.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and differentiation (R1, R3 vs. R2, R4):** While some reviewers (R2, R4) find the overall combination novel, others (R1, R3) argue that key components have been extensively studied: frequency-domain analysis for biosignals, transformer blocks resembling FNO and Ge²AE, masked autoencoding in the frequency domain for neurological signals (similar to neuro2vec 2022), and channel-independent designs. The paper lacks a thorough discussion of differences from these works and justification of the necessity of the proposed approach.  
- **Insufficient architecture analysis (R1, R2, R4):** No details on layer number, embedding dimension, multi-head frequency filter operation, or computational cost (parameters/FLOPs) compared to baselines like TS-TCC or PatchTST. Architecture choices (depth, dimensionality) are not justified. Missing ablation on channel-independent learning (R3) and impact of normalization/positional encoding (R2).  
- **Evaluation limitations and clarity (R1, R2, R3):**  
  - Pretraining only on SleepEDF (sleep data); downstream performance may reflect properties of the specific dataset rather than general representation transfer. More diverse pretraining datasets are needed (R2).  
  - Unimodal vs. multimodal setup is confusing: e.g., ExpEMG is single-channel EMG, yet a multimodal approach is applied. The definition of “multimodal” is questioned (R1, Q1).  
  - Improvements are marginal in some comparisons; not all baselines use multimodal training, making comparisons potentially unfair (R1).  
  - Lack of comparisons to multimodal contrastive frameworks (e.g., TS-TCC) (R3).  
  - No linear probing evaluation to assess learned representation quality (R1, R2).  
  - No t-SNE or reconstruction visualizations of learned embeddings (R1).  
- **Variance and reproducibility (R1, R4):** Results are reported on fixed splits without mean/variance across seeds despite small dataset sizes. Prior work (Zhang et al.) reported variance; the current results may be biased by specific splits. Need more rigorous randomization, variability reporting, and open-sourced code for reproducibility (R4).  
- **Missing hyper-parameter and sensitivity analysis (R1, R3):** Pre-training and fine-tuning settings, hyper-parameters, and sensitivity to these choices are not provided. The fine-tuning strategy (e.g., only classification head or full encoder update) is unclear (R3 Q1).  
- **Clarifications needed (R2, R3, R1):**  
  - What does the initial MLP learn? Is it shared across modalities? How are patch sizes N and P chosen for different sampling rates? (R2 Q1)  
  - Architecture and role of the “second encoder” (is it the lightweight encoder of Section 4.2?) (R2 Q2)  
  - The approach is positioned as specific to biosignals, but the design appears generally applicable to any time-series; this positioning is questioned (R3 W2).  
- **Channel-independent design concerns (R3):** Learning per channel independently may be less computationally efficient (reduced parallelizability) and may not capture global cross-channel information well, besides concatenation. This requires analysis and ablation (R3 Q3).  
- **Motivation–approach link (R1):** The claim that frequency-aware MAE specifically addresses distributional shifts is not theoretically justified. The connection between frequency masking/reconstruction and handling shifts is vague.
