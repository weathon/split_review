## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a one-step sampling method for flow matching that bypasses ODE integration at inference time. Prototype points in the base distribution are computed for target samples (using the exact velocity from (Ryzhakov et al., 2024)), then a network is trained to map base to target directly. Reviewers are split: one reviewer (score 6) finds the speed improvement promising with acceptable performance loss, while the other three (scores 3, 1, 3) judge the contribution as weak due to severely limited experiments, absent comparisons, unclear theory, and poor presentation.

### Strengths

- The single‑step sampling approach addresses the key bottleneck of long inference time in flow‑based models by eliminating the need for ODE integration during generation (R1, R2, R3).  
- One reviewer (R1) further states that “the single step sampling results in a greatly improved speed without significantly affecting the model performance.”  
- Reviewer 4 agrees that the algorithm “could potentially provide more computationally efficient generative models” and that it “seems to be reasonable, even though a few theoretical justifications are unclear.”

**Contrast**: Although speed is cited as the main advantage, other reviewers note that no efficiency measurements (wall‑clock time, FLOPs) are provided to substantiate this claim (R4, also implied by R2’s “lack of numerical results”).

### Weaknesses

- **Experiments are extremely limited.** Only an 8‑Gaussian toy example and a color‑transfer task with four example images are shown (R3). No comparisons to any alternative methods (flow matching, diffusion models, consistency models) or ablated versions are made (R2, R3, R4). For a publication, comparisons with baselines that share the same goal – e.g., consistency models (R2), other flow matching approaches (R4) – are essential.  
- **No evaluation of computational efficiency.** The main motivation is faster inference, yet no runtime, step‑count, or complexity comparison is given (R4).  
- **Theory is unclear and hand‑wavy.**  
  - The definition of “prototype” is ambiguous: is it the exact point in ρ₀ that would be reached with a perfect ODE solver, or any point one happens to converge to? Are there many potential prototypes per target point? (R3).  
  - The role of the tolerance parameter `tol` is not explained in relation to the generation process: how does it affect the shape of the base distribution? The paper does not state that `tol` is a tunable hyperparameter that influences ODE solver accuracy (R1, R3).  
  - The interaction between tolerance and the added noise σ is unsatisfactory. The noise is introduced to compensate for early solver stopping, yet no convincing argument is given that the errors are reduced (R3). It remains unclear why σ is needed and how it interacts with tolerance (R4).  
  - Importance sampling is used to estimate the integral in Eq. (1), but the paper does not specify which distributions are the sampling vs. target (R4).  
  - Convexity is mentioned but its exact role in the problem is not justified (R2).  
  - The σ in Eq. (1) (making the mapping invertible) and the σ in Eq. (3) (related to tolerance) appear to be different but use the same symbol without clarification (R3).  
  - It is unclear why no noise is added in Eq. (4) while it is added in Eq. (3) (R3).  
- **Missing details in the color‑transfer task.** The problem description is absent: the section starts with “For *the* color transfer problem” without prior introduction (R1). The task setup, metrics, and procedure need elaboration (R1, R4).  
- **Language and presentation are poor.** Numerous spelling and grammatical errors, missing articles, and awkward sentences make parts of the paper incomprehensible, especially the introduction (R1, R3). Presentation ratings are low (R1: 1, R3: 2, R4: 2).  
- **Redundant algorithms.** Algorithm 1 and Algorithm 2 differ only by discrete labels; a single algorithm would suffice (R3).  
- **Notation inconsistency.** The buffer B varies across the text (R3).  
- **Unclear reference to previous work.** The method relies heavily on Ryzhakov et al. (2024), but both the motivation and details of that earlier work are insufficiently explained (R2).  
- **Minor issues.**  
  - Footnote 2 says “up to a constant” without clarifying whether this is a constant factor or constant term (R1).  
  - The paper claims that “Section 4.2” refers to a common method for image colorization but does not provide a reference or sufficient explanation (R1).  
  - The related work does not mention consistency models or other approaches that also bypass inference integration (R2).
