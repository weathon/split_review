## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a lightweight Transformer guided by features from multiple receptive fields (LT-FMRF) for few-shot fine-grained image classification (FSFGIC). It combines CNN-based multi-scale local feature extraction with self-attention to capture both local and global dependencies, and fuses these features to enhance inter-class differences. The model supports end-to-end episodic training from scratch. Experiments on five FSFGIC datasets show consistent improvements over multiple state-of-the-art methods.

### Strengths
- The proposed method achieves consistent improvements across five diverse FSFGIC datasets in both 5-way 1-shot and 5-way 5-shot tasks, demonstrating broad applicability and robustness (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The ablation results verify the effectiveness of the proposed LT-FMRF module (Reviewer 1).
- The fusion of multi-scale features (global and local from different receptive fields) effectively amplifies inter-class differences, which is critical for fine-grained classification (Reviewer 2).
- The method addresses the issues of long-range dependencies and insufficient few-shot data simultaneously (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is well-organized with a clear problem statement and well-structured experimental results and tables (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 5 also finds the paper easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty.** The LT-FMRF module largely combines existing techniques: adding multi-scale local features (via CNNs with varying kernel sizes) to a standard self-attention module. Using convolutional kernels of varying sizes to capture multi-scale features is a well-established approach (e.g., [1-4] cited by Reviewer 5) and has been extensively explored in few-shot learning. The similarity measurement uses feature reconstruction as in FRN, contributing little new architecture or metric design. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5)
- **Unclear motivation for fine-grained specificity.** The design does not include explicit adaptations for fine-grained classification (e.g., handling subtle inter-class differences). The goal of learning high-quality features from limited data aligns with general few-shot learning, not the unique challenges of fine-grained images. The claims about enhancing inter-class differences lack quantitative evidence or analysis (e.g., no feature distance or confusion matrix analysis). (Reviewers 4, 5)
- **No experimental support for “lightweight” claim.** The paper does not provide concrete benchmarks on parameter count, FLOPs, inference time, or memory usage compared to other models (CNNs, ViTs, or other few-shot methods). Lightweight implies reduced computational and storage cost, but this is not validated. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Insufficient and non-informative ablation studies.** (i) Ablations focus only on combinations of self-attention and receptive field features; missing analyses on the impact of different receptive field sizes (e.g., why exactly three?), hyperparameter stability, and scalability across datasets (Reviewer 4). (ii) No incremental experiments comparing the proposed module against existing public multi-scale modules to truly verify its effectiveness (Reviewer 5). (iii) No internal ablation on components of the convolution layer in LT-FMRF (Reviewer 5). (iv) Table 5 experiments on the number of LT-FMRF modules show that more modules improve 1-shot but reduce 5-shot accuracy, yet no explanation is provided (Reviewer 5). (v) Figure 3 compares training curves with a very low-performing baseline (FRN, >10% accuracy difference), which is not meaningful; comparison should be with incremental or similar models. Figure 2 heatmap compares proposed method with a lower-accuracy baseline (FRN) instead of comparing LRF with LRF+LT-FMRF (Reviewer 5).
- **Missing rationale for architectural choices.** No explanation why exactly three receptive fields are used or why this particular combination of CNN and Transformer features is optimal (Reviewer 4). Similarly, the principle behind maintaining convolutional inductive bias to reduce training samples is asserted but not elaborated (Reviewer 3 question).
- **Poor presentation and missing details.** The paper lists detailed processes rather than providing an organized high-level overview of module connections and interactions (Reviewers 1, 3). Some architectural details of the LT-FMRF module and fusion mechanisms are hard to follow (Reviewer 4). The paragraph starting with “The designed LT-FMRF network contains four modules” has an abrupt transition (Reviewer 3). Grammatical errors present (Reviewer 5). No feature map or t-SNE visualizations to give insight into model behavior (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of discussion on key related works.** The Related Work section misses discussing key limitations of 1-2 relevant works (Reviewer 3 question). No clear contrast of the MRF module with other multi-scale local feature extraction modules (Reviewer 1 question).
- **Unclear whether pre-trained models were used** (Reviewer 5 question). In few-shot experiments, this is critical for fair comparison.
- **Unanswered questions about design choices.** (i) Why deeper networks underperform in FSFGIC? (overfitting vs optimization issues) (Reviewer 2 question). (ii) Why not modify self-attention itself to enhance local information (e.g., hybrid CNN-attention mechanisms) rather than using a separate MRF module? (Reviewer 2 question). (iii) What is the principle behind maintaining convolutional inductive bias to reduce training sample requirements? (Reviewer 3 question). These points overlap with missing experiments or analysis.

**Reviewer disagreement:** Three reviewers (scores 5) highlight the method’s consistent empirical gains and relevance, while two reviewers (scores 3, confidence 5) strongly criticize the lack of novelty, missing evidence for key claims, insufficient ablation, and unclear motivation for fine-grained specificity. The negative reviewers view the contribution as minimal.
