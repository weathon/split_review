## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes “task augmentation,” a pipeline to generate multiple VLM evaluation tasks from a single existing dataset by re-annotating images with metadata from humans, heuristics, and pre-trained models. The method is applied to COCO (kitchen, wildlife domains) and KITTI (autonomous driving) to create 37,171 tasks on 1,704 images, with 6 human raters per question (162,946 answers) to quantify ambiguity. 21 open and closed VLMs are benchmarked. Key findings: model performance varies across domains, open models have narrowed the gap, Qwen2 72B is the strongest open model, human raters outperform all VLMs, and 56% of open models are worse than random. The paper’s contribution is framed as a resource-efficient way to build domain-specific benchmarks, but reviewers question the novelty and generalizability of the pipeline.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-structured and easy to follow (Reviewer 1).
- The re-annotation pipeline is a smart, resource-efficient way to create multiple tasks from a single benchmark while controlling data quality and diversity (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Human validation reduces question ambiguity (Reviewer 3).
- Addresses a critical need: developing evaluation datasets for domain-specific VLM benchmarking (Reviewer 3).
- Extensive evaluation across 21 diverse VLMs of varying model sizes strengthens robustness of findings (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The generated benchmark is challenging; even powerful models struggle on some tasks, demonstrating its difficulty (Reviewer 3).
- Observations such as considerable model performance variation across related domains are interesting (Reviewer 2) and some conclusions are useful (Reviewer 1). Human raters strongly outperforming all VLMs and many open models below random are notable (implied by Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concern (multiple reviewers):** The core idea of repurposing existing imagery and metadata (using off-the-shelf models or human input) to generate additional tasks or augment training sets is well-explored in prior work (Reviewer 2 cites [1,2,3]; Reviewer 3 cites [1,2,3,4]). The concept of “task augmentation” is not new.
- **Pipeline not automatic and limited generalizability (Reviewers 1, 2):** The process requires significant human effort—metadata annotation, rule-writing, task template design, multi-round prompt refinement (Reviews 1, 2). It is demonstrated only on COCO and KITTI (instance-level annotations); how to generalize the pipeline to other domains (e.g., pathology, autonomous driving) is unclear (Reviewer 1). Resource-efficiency is not concretely demonstrated (Reviewer 2).
- **Motivation mismatch with evaluation (Reviewer 3):** The paper targets domain-specific specialist models (pathology, autonomous driving) but evaluates only generalist VLMs. No data or models from high-stakes domains are included, leaving suitability for such tasks uncertain.
- **Unclear advantage over existing benchmarks (Reviewers 1, 2):** The paper does not demonstrate why the proposed benchmark is more useful than existing VLM benchmarks. Missing comparisons in terms of task quantity, question diversity, and problem difficulty (Reviewer 2). One reviewer noted: “I do not see a reason why the proposed approach can be more useful than existing evaluation benchmarks” (Reviewer 1).
- **Conclusions not new (Reviewer 1):** Similar observations (e.g., performance variation, open vs. closed gap) have been reported in other VLM evaluation papers. More novel analysis of results is lacking (Reviewer 1).
- **Writing and presentation issues (Reviewers 1, 3):** Sections (introduction, 2.2, 3.3) could be streamlined (Reviewer 3). Figure 4 is crowded; grouping models by parameter count or family would improve clarity (Reviewer 3). The proposed metric is not widely recognized; more standard metrics should be included for broader adoption (Reviewer 3). \(I(C_{i,q,m})\) in Eqn. (1) is not explained (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing details (Reviewers 1, 2, 3):**  
  - No statistical data on the 25 tasks (e.g., number of questions per task) (Reviewer 2).  
  - The task generation method via metadata is unclear: is it pre-set templates, LLM-generated, or manual writing? (Reviewer 2).  
  - A detailed breakdown of human effort per creation stage would clarify automation claims (Reviewer 2).  
  - The paper frames itself as providing evaluation datasets; releasing the generated dataset (not just code) would offer greater community utility (Reviewer 3).  
- **Minority reviewer (Reviewer 1) was notably more critical:** rated soundness 2, presentation 2, contribution 1, and explicitly questioned the significance of the conclusions and the pipeline’s value relative to existing benchmarks. This contrasts with the more positive assessments from Reviewers 2 and 3 (both score 5).
