## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the Predict+Optimize (P+O) framework, which uses learning algorithms to predict parameters for optimization problems in an end-to-end fashion. The paper proposes the Adaptive Proximal Gradient Optimizer (AProx) to handle inexact surrogate gradient backpropagation. The method adds a smooth function (squared loss on cost parameters) to the regret and uses subgradients to approximate the proximal operator, integrating momentum, adaptive learning rates, and parameter smoothing. The authors provide theoretical convergence analysis and experiments on combinatorial optimization benchmarks.

### Strengths
- The topic is interesting and aims to tackle a well-known issue in the non-differentiability of the loss function in Predict & Optimize. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- The paper provides a review of related works. (Reviewer 1)
- The introduction and related works are well-written. (Reviewer 2; contrast: other reviewers found writing quality poor overall)
- The numerical experiments show promising results for the proposed approach. (Reviewer 2; contrast: other reviewers raised concerns about experimental clarity and statistical rigor)
- Proving convergence is not straightforward, even in a simple setting with exact gradients. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
**Major Theoretical Flaws:**
- The proof of Theorem 1 contains a critical error: on line 772, a lower bound is incorrectly combined with an upper bound, invalidating the subsequent steps. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- The inequality in line 834 does not hold (e.g., if η is close to 1/L, the LHS approaches zero while the RHS becomes negative). (Reviewer 3)
- In line 836, 'higher order terms' Lη²δ² cannot be simply 'neglected' without a limiting process; the assumption that δ is small is incorrectly applied. (Reviewer 3)
- The proof of Corollary 2 is flawed: Equation 44 does not guarantee convergence of the sum (e.g., sums ∑1/k and ∑1/√k both diverge, counterexample given). (Reviewers 1, 3)
- The proof relies on R(ĉ) being convex, but this is not justified; the paper does not prove convexity of the regret function or discuss implications if it does not hold. (Reviewer 2)
- The definition of d_k is unclear, as ∇R(ĉ_k) is not uniquely defined. (Reviewer 3)
- Equation 13 includes a constant δ that is not properly quantified; δ = sup∥g(ĉ)∥ is problematic because the true gradient of the LP solution is either zero or undefined. (Reviewer 3)

**Algorithm and Method Issues:**
- The claim that the update "implicitly a proximal update" is not justified; the given update (ĉ_{k+1} = ĉ_k - η(∇f(ĉ) - g(ĉ))) resembles subgradient descent, not a proximal update. The connection to proximal operators is unclear. (Reviewer 2)
- The core contribution essentially adds a squared loss on cost parameters to an existing surrogate loss, paired with a customized Adam optimizer and weight decay; this lacks novelty and does not significantly advance the field. (Reviewer 3)
- Algorithm 1 has inconsistencies: line 3 defines ĉ_k = ĉ(θ_k), while line 10 computes ĉ_{k+1} as an update based on gradient; line 12 computes smoothed parameters that are not utilized. (Reviewer 1)
- The smoothing function f(ĉ) = ½∥ĉ - c∥² requires knowledge of the true cost parameters c, which are unknown in real-world scenarios, undermining practical applicability. (Reviewer 1)

**Experimental and Implementation Concerns:**
- Experimental setup is not clearly defined; Tables 1, 3, 5 lack description of metrics, training process, and statistical significance (no standard deviations reported in many cases). (Reviewer 3)
- Table 7 is unreferenced and entries are "yes"/"no" without description. (Reviewer 2)
- Line 368 refers to Table 1 showing training with "several different regrets" but Table 1 shows optimization algorithms, not regrets; possibly wrong table referenced. (Reviewer 2)
- Hyperparameter tuning (η, λ, β_s, β_m, β_p) is not explained. (Reviewer 2)
- The large standard deviations in tables make it difficult to draw meaningful conclusions. (Reviewer 3)
- The metric (relative optimal gap) measures performance of the trained model, not the optimizers directly. (Reviewer 3)

**Writing and Presentation Issues:**
- The paper is hindered by poor writing quality: awkward sentences, confusing notation, typos. Examples: "non-differentiate" (line 52), "gradient inexact" (line 68), "approachfocuses" (line 233), "introduces" (line 161). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- In Section 2.1, equations (3) and (4) are stated with no relation to the previous paragraph and no discussion. (Reviewer 1)
- Nonstandard terminology: "agent function/gradient" instead of surrogate loss/gradient; "discovergence" is nonstandard; "gradient" used for objects that are not true gradients; "training rounds," "step size," "calendar hours" instead of epochs. (Reviewer 3)
- Inaccurate or imprecise statements: Line 67 claim that "gradient inexact ... has not been emphasized, and research is lacking" is inaccurate as referenced papers address this. Line 36 "end-to-end approaches are also an emerging topic" is overly broad. Line 165 "R(ĉ) is not trivial" is vague. Line 172 incorrect explanation of coefficient 1/2. Line 188 inaccurate claim about implicit proximal operator integration. (Reviewer 3)
- References not updated (e.g., "Differentiation of Blackbox Combinatorial Solvers" is a published article, not cited properly). (Reviewer 2)
- ∇R and g are used interchangeably; should distinguish true gradient vs approximation (e.g., line 5 of pseudocode, line 259). (Reviewer 2)
- R(ĉ) is never explicitly written; should be defined in Section 2. (Reviewer 2)
- Line 233 "approachfocuses" missing space. (Reviewer 2)
- Line 240 claim that "most constructed surrogate functions can satisfy convexity" is not entirely true (e.g., DBB uses linear interpolations, IMLE; convexity not guaranteed). (Reviewer 3)
