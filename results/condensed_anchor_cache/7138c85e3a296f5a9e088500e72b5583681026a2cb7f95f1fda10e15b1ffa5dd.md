## Merged Review

### Summary

The Chain-of-Action (CoA) framework decomposes complex questions into reasoning chains via systematic prompting and three "Plug-and-Play" actions (web querying, knowledge encoding, data analyzing) to retrieve and verify information from heterogeneous sources. A multi-reference faith score (MRFS) is introduced to detect conflicts and improve answer reliability. Two reviewers (rating 8) find the contributions strong, empirically validating CoA on QA benchmarks and a Web3 case study, with notable gains in accuracy and token efficiency. A third reviewer (rating 5) acknowledges the innovation but raises concerns about the framework’s generality beyond text and tabular data, scalability to real-time data streams, and ability to handle higher-order reasoning. There is disagreement on presentation clarity: reviewer 1 rates presentation 1 (poor) and reviewer 3 rates it 4 (clear). The soundness is rated 2, 3, 3.

### Strengths

- **Core reasoning mechanism**: The divide-and-conquer approach decomposes complex queries into manageable sub-questions, improving precision. The use of "actions" (web query, data analysis) over pure "thoughts" is a natural extension that significantly improves QA capabilities over baselines (reviewers 2, 3).
- **Empirical performance**: CoA significantly outperforms existing reasoning and QA methods on multiple datasets, with reduced API calls and token usage compared to prior methods, demonstrating both effectiveness and cost-saving potential (reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Verification mechanism**: The multi-reference faith score (MRFS) effectively identifies and mitigates information conflicts by cross-validating LLM answers against external sources, enhancing reliability (reviewers 1, 3).
- **Practical impact**: Real-world deployment in a Web3 QA system increased user engagement and positive feedback, validating the method’s applicability (reviewer 3).
- **Innovative framework**: The structured decomposition into sub-questions and domain-adaptable plug-and-play actions represent a significant step toward faithful, retrieval-augmented QA (reviewer 3).
- **Use of multimodal LLMs**: The method leverages newer multimodal LLM abilities to perform actions such as web query and data analysis, creating a new QA mechanism (reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

- **Terminology and clarity** (majority concern): Key concepts are poorly defined or misapplied. Terms like "multimodal" (used for text and tabular data, which is heterogeneous rather than multimodal), "chain-of-action" (more akin to data collection and verification), "plug-and-play" (integration complexity may not be low-cost), "node" (sub-question), and "knowledge boundary" (what the model knows) lack precise definitions and cause confusion. While reviewer 3 found the presentation clear (score 4), reviewer 1 rated presentation 1, citing inaccurate terminology, loosely connected methodology, and a mix of abstract workflows and technical details. A clearer renaming of sections (e.g., "Data Collection" and "Data Verification" for Section 2.2.1 and 2.2.2) is suggested.
- **Ablation studies missing** (all reviewers): Several ablations are absent:
  - Removal of specific action types (e.g., only web search removed, only local knowledge base removed) to isolate their impact (reviewer 2).
  - Comparison of CoA with and without the ability to add external "plugs" to understand how much gain is from tools versus the new framework (reviewer 2).
  - Contribution of tabular data specifically is not analyzed (reviewer 1).
  - No results for CoA without MRFS (reviewer 1, Q1).
- **Limited discussion on question decomposition** (reviewer 1): Decomposition strategies and comparisons with existing baselines (e.g., CoT) are not elaborated, leaving a critical component underexplored.
- **Handling of modalities** (reviewer 3): The framework’s adaptability to non-text and non-tabular data (audio, video, complex sensor data) is unproven. Mechanisms for integrating such modalities into the reasoning chain are not defined. The claim of "multimodal" is weakened because tabular data is linearized as text for LLM input.
- **Scalability and latency** (reviewers 2, 3): The time to execute multiple actions and retrieve external sources could be significant. No analysis of average response time, computational overhead, or behavior with high-velocity data streams is provided, limiting the understanding of practical deployment constraints.
- **Higher-order reasoning** (reviewer 3): The method has not been demonstrated on tasks requiring abstract reasoning, counterfactual analysis, recursive logic, or other non-factual, complex multi-step dependencies. It remains unclear how the system handles a reasoning path that is not straightforward.
- **Expert evaluation details missing** (reviewer 1): Section 3.2 lacks information on expert recruitment, qualifications, and inter-rater reliability metrics, reducing transparency.
- **Contradictory source handling** (reviewer 3): How the system manages discrepancies when sources provide conflicting information is not fully detailed (though MRFS partially addresses this, further explanation is needed).
- **Comparison fairness** (reviewer 1): CoA uses GPT-4, while baselines DSP and SearchChain used older models (text-davinci-002, gpt-3.5-turbo), making direct comparisons questionable.
