- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 1, 1, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes ThreadsGAN, a GAN-based model (using BERT, Transformer, CNN) for generating discussion threads, conditioned on multi-topic and social response intention tags. It collects data from the Taiwanese forum PTT and evaluates against SeqGAN, TAIDE, and GPT-4 via automatic metrics and human evaluation. Scores are 1 (Reviewers 1 & 2) and 3 (Reviewers 3 & 4). All reviewers identify major weaknesses; the more positive reviewers note some novelty but concur that the method is flawed and performance is weak.

### Strengths

- The work examines an interesting application of discussion thread generation (R1, R3).
- The model uses relatively lightweight models (BERT, CNN) and performs a human evaluation (R1).
- A new customized method is proposed for a special task, integrating GAN with topic and response tags to enhance coherence and diversity (R3, R4).
- The dataset is collected from PTT across multiple subjects and has a large size of threads, useful for training and evaluation (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Weak motivation**: The importance of thread generation is not well justified; no recent related work is cited (Line 35 claims prior work without citations), and the task is not clearly differentiated from conversation generation or question answering (R1, R3, R4). The purpose of using GANs is not well motivated (R1), and the paper reads as an academic exercise without connection to real outcomes (R1).
- **Lack of clarity in methodology**: The task is not formally defined – input and output expectations are ambiguous (R4). The encoder takes the next response as input (eq. 3.2.3) while the decoder generates the next response (eq. 3.2.9), raising a potential circularity issue (R3). The variable “year” appears without explanation (R4). The description of the model is not clear enough to resolve validity questions about the generation process (R3).
- **Undefended design choices**: Using BERT with a decoder-only transformer backend is not defended; a more natural compatible embedding model is not chosen (R1). Using a CNN for token sequence generation (a patch-oriented model) is not justified (R1). No comparison or motivation is given for choosing BERT over alternatives like RoBERTa, or CNN over LSTM (R4). Alternative architectural choices and ablations are missing.
- **Data provenance and quality**: The source platform and topic selection are unclear (L140-161); quality guidelines and inter-annotator agreement for the dataset are not reported, undermining data veracity (R1). Fig. 1 shows 4 of 10 boards have fewer than 10 viable posts, raising generalizability concerns (R1).
- **Insufficient baseline descriptions and comparisons**: Baseline descriptions (SeqGAN, TAIDE, GPT-4) lack reproducibility details (R1). How LLMs are used (prompt design, adjustments) is not explained (R3). GPT-4 is used off-the-shelf without prompt tuning or SFT (R4). The proposed method does not outperform GPT-4 and sometimes performs worse than SeqGAN (R1, R3, R4). No explanation is given of any benefit (e.g., cost, explainability) over weaker baselines or LLMs (R4).
- **Human evaluation concerns**: Inter-annotator agreement is not discussed, undermining evaluation quality (R1).
- **Related work and writing quality**: The related work section lacks organization and contextualization before diving into individual works; the GAN subsection (Sec. 2.2) presents only single-sentence summaries without a coherent argument (R1). The paper has poor logical coherence and does not conform to conventional paper writing style (R2). Old references are used, not representing state-of-the-art text generation (R3).
- **Limited contribution and innovation**: The method lacks innovation (R2); the results do not show advantage over currently dominant LLMs (R3). The math (equations 209-213) is not a particularly good use of space (R1). Only a simple adaptation of SeqGAN is offered, without clear differentiation (R1).
- **Missing experiments and justifications**: No ablation studies or comparisons to stronger alternatives (RoBERTa, LSTM) are provided. The conclusions mention shortcomings with multilingual output that were not discussed earlier (R1).
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms**: Why is GAN beneficial for thread generation compared to SoTA LLMs? (R1, R4). What are the practical applications of thread generation? (R3). How is the proposed GAN different from standard SeqGAN? (R1). How exactly are LLMs used to generate threads (prompt details)? (R3).
