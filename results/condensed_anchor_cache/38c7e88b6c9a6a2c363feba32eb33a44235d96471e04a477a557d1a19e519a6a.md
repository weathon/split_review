## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Hot-Pluggable Federated Learning (HPFL), a framework bridging generic and personalized federated learning via Selective Federated Learning (SFL). HPFL uses a shared backbone with client-trained plug-in modules, uploaded with markers to a server for selection during inference. Differential privacy is applied to markers. Experiments on four datasets and three neural networks show performance gains.

### Strengths
- **Problem identification**: The authors identify a substantial gap between GFL and PFL and formulate SFL as a new learning problem to bridge them; the optimization functions of both GFL and PFL are special cases of SFL. (Reviewer 2, strength 1; also noted by Reviewer 4)
- **Novel framework design**: HPFL innovatively introduces a plug-in selection mechanism into federated learning, enabling bridging between general and personalized models – a new attempt in FL. (Reviewer 1, originality) The modular approach (shared backbone + plug-ins) enables efficient communication and computation, suitable for resource-constrained clients. (Reviewer 3, strength 2)
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Evaluation covers four datasets and three neural networks (Reviewer 1, quality; Reviewer 2, strength 3; Reviewer 3). Ablation studies are provided. Results demonstrate HPFL’s performance advantages. (Reviewer 1)
- **Clarity and structure**: The paper is well-written, clearly describes the background, problem, algorithm details, and experimental procedures, and fairly cites prior works. (Reviewer 1 clarity; Reviewer 4 strength on writing and after response responsiveness)
- **Potential for broader FL problems**: The paper discusses HPFL’s applicability to continual learning, one-shot FL, anarchic FL, and a plug-in market. (Reviewer 1 significance; Reviewer 3 mentions continual learning)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and contribution of the selection mechanism**: While Reviewer 1 considers the plug-in selection mechanism a novel contribution, Reviewer 2 and 4 find it insufficiently novel. Reviewer 2 notes that the training process is identical to split federated learning, and the selection step (relying on MMD) is a minor addition; core idea of selecting pre-trained modules based on data similarity is not new. The paper does not adequately justify why the specific implementation offers a substantial improvement over existing model selection techniques. (Reviewer 2 weakness 1; Reviewer 4: “methodology seems combination of existing works with minimal advances”)
- **Theoretical analysis is weak**: The treatment of SFL relies only on a loss function statement, insufficient to validate effectiveness. The greater-than sign in Eq.4 may be incorrect (should be ≤ ? because better should have less loss). The connection between theory and empirical results is weak; no rigorous analysis of generalization bounds or convergence. Function s(·) in Theorem 2.3 is not defined. (Reviewer 2 weakness 2; Reviewer 4: “It is not clear how the mentioned theorems add value to the literature”) Reviewer 1 did not comment on theory.
- **Privacy protection lacks originality and analysis**: Adding Gaussian noise to markers is a standard DP technique; the paper does not explore novel ways of applying DP, nor does it provide new insights into privacy-utility trade-offs. (Reviewer 2 weakness 3) It is unclear which type of DP is used (local, central, or both) and how plug-ins interact with DP mechanisms. (Reviewer 4 weakness; also question 6) The effectiveness of DP against sophisticated adversaries (e.g., inferring information from selection patterns or perturbed scores) is not empirically validated or rigorously analyzed. (Reviewer 3 weakness 3)
- **Insufficient details of the selection mechanism**: The selection algorithm (MMD, SVCCA, CKA) is described without specific algorithm steps, mathematical expressions, or pseudo-code, making it hard to assess robustness and reproducibility. (Reviewer 1 weakness 1) Notation is confusing: introduction of auxiliary information H in Section 2.4’s SFL definition is vague. (Reviewer 2 weakness 4)
- **Missing comparisons with more advanced baselines**: The paper compares with several GFL and PFL algorithms but lacks newer solutions that specifically address heterogeneous data distributions. For example, FedAlt/FedSim (Krishna et al., 2022@ICML) and Hu et al. (2020@IEEE IoT Journal) for PFL with DP are suggested. (Reviewer 1 weakness 2; Reviewer 4: “Figure 2 should include HPFL results; comparison should cover more advanced PFL studies”) Table 2 is hard to read; it is confusing why both best and second-best results are highlighted, and why HPFL does not achieve the best result in some cases (e.g., compared to FedSAM). (Reviewer 2 weakness 5)
- **Backbone dependency**: The framework relies on a common backbone; if the backbone fails to generalize well across heterogeneous client data, feature space misalignment can lead to suboptimal plug-in performance even after fine-tuning. (Reviewer 3 weakness 1)
- **Computational overhead of selection**: The plug-in selection process during inference could introduce additional delays for clients with limited resources. The paper lacks a detailed analysis of computational complexity and impact on different client hardware. (Reviewer 3 weakness 2; also Reviewer 1 question on overhead)
- **Lack of clarity on plug-in markers and how they bridge GFL-PFL gap**: The role of plug-in markers in bridging the gap is not adequately explained; a concrete example is needed. (Reviewer 4 weaknesses; question 5) The phrasing “Solving SFL means clients achieve performance in GFL as high as in PFL” is vague. (Reviewer 4)
- **No comparison of plug-in module vs. vanilla personalized model**: The difference between plug-in modules and standard personalization (e.g., fine-tuned local models) needs clarification, as the contributions may overlap. (Reviewer 4, question 2)
- **Potential performance impact of DP**: It is not examined whether DP (or other privacy mechanisms) significantly degrades model performance, especially under noise for selection markers. (Reviewer 1 question; also overlaps with Reviewer 3’s weakness 3)
