## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a polybasic speculative decoding framework that generalizes the dualistic (two-model) paradigm to a chain of multiple draft models. The authors derive theoretical results (Lemma 3.1, Theorem 3.2, Theorem 3.3) to characterize inference time and acceptance length, and they introduce an “ideal forward count” φ_i to guide model selection. Empirically, they evaluate on Vicuna-7B, LLaMA2-Chat 7B, LLaMA3-8B across tasks (MT-bench, math, summarization, translation, QA, RAG) and report latency speedups of 3.31×–4.01×, up to 3.87×, and up to 4.43×, respectively, comparing against EAGLE and vanilla autoregressive decoding.

### Strengths
- The empirical speedups are impressive (3–4× over vanilla), and the paper conducts experiments on three different model families under a variety of tasks. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- The idea of arranging multiple draft models in a chain is interesting and attempts to extend speculative decoding beyond a single drafter. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- The limitations section is well written and highlights implementation challenges. (Reviewer 1)
- The paper claims the approach is orthogonal to existing methods and can be combined with other techniques; the code is provided. (Reviewer 1, Abstract)

### Weaknesses
**Theoretical rigor and clarity**
- The core definition of “ideal forward count” φ_i is not properly justified. The paper states it “through empirical analysis” but provides no details on how φ_i was derived; all subsequent theory depends on this undefined quantity. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- Lemma 3.1 is imprecise: the statement “We can substitute L with its expected value E[L]” lacks context (substitute into what?), and the proof in Appendix A.3 uses a “small coefficient of variation” assumption that is not stated in the lemma. The authors neglect randomness of L_i with a vague claim about low variability; a proper development would require concentration inequalities and error quantification for Taylor expansion and dropped higher-order terms. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- The assumption that acceptance token length L ~ N(μ, σ²) (line 190) is not justified. L is a discrete variable; the normality assumption is not used elsewhere in the paper, and it is unclear if this simplification yields realistic insights. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- Theorem 3.2 fails to define T₂′, L₂′, T_i vs. T_i′, L_i vs. L_i′ clearly in the theorem statement. The conditions in the theorem are stated without practical interpretation. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- Theorem 3.3 makes qualitative claims (“very low relative variability”) without formal quantification. It is unclear if this theorem is actually proved or if it is a simple characterization of a capped geometric random variable. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- The concept “acceleration ratio” (Section 3.1) and “unstable acceptance token lengths” (line 273) are not formally defined. (Reviewer 4)
- The claim that speculative sampling leads to “more stable acceptance token length” (line 275–277) is unsupported. (Reviewer 2)

**Theoretical disconnection from experiments**
- The theoretical results (Theorem 3.2) are not used to guide the number of models n or the selection of M₁, M₂, …, Mₙ. For example, one would expect the authors to compute T_i and L_i from a pool of candidate models. (Reviewer 2)
- The theory is developed for n models, but experiments only consider three-model cascades (n=3). (Reviewer 2)
- The definition of φ_i appears after Lemma 3.1, but the lemma is supposed to justify the substitution that leads to φ_i. No objective function is written before Lemma 3.1 to clarify the substitution. (Reviewers 2, 3)

**Novelty and related work**
- The cascaded speculative decoding idea is not new; prior work already proposes similar multi-draft pipelines: Cascade Speculative Drafting (arXiv:2312.11462) and Staged Speculative Decoding (arXiv:2308.04623). The paper does not adequately differentiate from these works. (Reviewer 2)
- Reviewer 1 asks how the approach compares to token-tree methods like SpecInfer or Medusa, and how it handles self-speculative decoding with intermediate-layer exits. (Reviewer 1)
- The acceptance length μ changes between “ours” and “EAGLE” in Table 2; the paper does not explain whether the draft models are different and why μ changes if the draft distribution of M₂ should be unchanged by adding M₃. (Reviewer 2)

**Algorithm and presentation clarity**
- Algorithm 1 does not appear to use M₁. (Reviewer 4)
- The terms “polybasic” and “dualistic” are never defined in the introduction; the reader is left uninformed about the proposal until later. (Reviewer 3)
- The abstract contains a grammatical error: “then serve” should be “then serves” or “then serve as” is ungrammatical. (Reviewer 4)
- Numerous typos: “acceptance token length” → “accepted token length” (line 189); “lemma” vs “Lemma” inconsistent; “Limitation and discuss” → “Limitation and discussion” (Section 4.3 title); “As show in Figure” → “As shown in Figure” (line 483); “We approach consistently” → “Our approach consistently” (line 423). (Reviewer 4)

**Missing details**
- How is the success probability 1−α computed at inference time? (Reviewer 1)
- Why is EAGLE faster than the proposed method in some cases (Figure 3)? (Reviewer 1)
- In Section 3.1, the paper states φ_i is obtained “through empirical analysis” but provides no further detail. This is a major hole. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- The paper assumes that all theoretical analysis is independent of prefix (input). This is never stated. (Reviewer 3)
- The definition of inference time T = T_{M₁} + T_{D₂} (line 217) does not define T_{M₁} and T_{D₂}; the unit of time is unspecified. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- Line 330 asks if the authors deliberately lower M₁’s quality to be comparable to M₂; this needs clarification. (Reviewer 4)
- Line 53 (related work) claims existing work only uses a single draft model, but Figure 1 of arXiv:2312.11462 (Cascade Speculative Drafting) shows multiple draft models with a single target. The discussion of related work should be expanded. (Reviewer 4)
