## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes D2Coder, an LLM-based code agent for automated debugging that integrates fault localization (using SBFL and AST), program call graphs, and a simulated debugging mode. On SWE-bench, it reports a 27.3% improvement in resolution rate over SWE-agent and AutoCodeRover. Reviewers agree the problem is important and the approach intuitively clear, with some novelty in combining program graphs with LLMs. However, major concerns dominate: insufficient technical detail throughout (fault localization implementation, LLM choices/prompts, debugging loop budgets, costs); the source of performance gains is unclear (ablation shows marginal drop when the two main components are removed); the claimed 27.3% improvement is misleading (actual improvement is 6 percentage points over AutoCodeRover); lack of a dedicated related work section and positioning; scalability issues with whole-repo parsing; missing robustness validation for fault localization; and incomplete reporting of context size differences and dataset selection. One reviewer was more positive (score 3) about the innovation and potential, while two found the paper not ready (score 1). Overall, the paper requires substantial revision to substantiate claims and clarify methodology.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an important and well-motivated problem: improving LLM-based code agents for complex debugging tasks. (R1, R3)
- The proposed approach is intuitively clear and integrates program semantics (abstract syntax trees, program call graphs) with LLMs in a structured pipeline. (R1, R2)
- There is some algorithmic novelty in incorporating call graphs and a simulated debugging pattern. (R1, R2)
- The method shows preliminary promise and achieves a tangible improvement over two recent baselines (SWE-agent, AutoCodeRover): 28% resolution vs. 22% (AutoCodeRover). (R1, R3)
- One reviewer (R2) found the approach innovative and contributing to automated debugging applications, praising the integration of ASTs and program call graphs.

### Weaknesses
- Insufficient technical detail undermines reproducibility and evaluation. Missing specifics include: how the SBFL suspiciousness score weight \(w_{i,t}\) is computed (R1, R3); the exact implementation of refining symbol indexing, referencing analysis, mapping problem description to code segments, and ranking (sec. 2.1.4) (R1); the definition of \(R\) and its use in `UpdateTests` (R1); LLM details (choice, prompts, context size) and how it is used in Algorithm 1 (R1, R3); debugging loop budgets, timeouts, token/time costs, and number of patches per example (R1). The paper should include expanded implementation details in appendices. (R1, R3)
- The source of performance improvement over baselines is unclear. Ablation studies (Table 3) show only marginal resolution drop when call graphs and simulated debugging are removed, contradicting the claimed importance of these components (R1, R3). The paper states the ablation highlights their importance, but the results do not support this (R1). The actual performance gains may stem from other components or hyperparameter differences (R1).
- The reported 27.3% improvement in the abstract is misleading. The actual improvement over the best baseline (AutoCodeRover) is 6 percentage points (28% vs 22%), not 27% (R3). This inflates the contribution and should be corrected.
- The paper lacks a dedicated related work section and fails to adequately position itself among recent advances in LLM-based debugging. Referencing recent works (e.g., Majdoub et al., Tian et al., Lee et al., Yang et al., Cheryl et al.) and discussing novelty versus reuse are necessary. (R1, R3)
- Fault localization relies on SBFL and AST, but it may lack robustness when handling complex dependencies across multiple modules or files (R2). The method may be limited to specific code structures and programming styles; its effectiveness for significant code variation or cases requiring deep dynamic analysis remains untested (R2).
- Constructing symbolic indices and call graphs requires parsing the entire codebase, which is time- and resource-intensive, especially for large projects. No optimization measures are discussed, raising practical scalability concerns. (R2)
- How is simulated debugging implemented? The paper should detail how simulated debugging compares to on-machine debugging in terms of efficiency, coverage of error types, and implementation (R3). Also, differences in context sizes between approaches are not reported but directly impact cost (R3).
- The choice of SWE-bench subset is unclear: Tables 1 and 3 use SWE-bench lite while Table 2 uses SWE-bench; the selection criteria for the subset (apparently 300 problems) need justification. (R1, R3)
- No author response was provided during the review period, leaving reviewer questions unanswered and reinforcing concerns about paper readiness. (R1)
- Despite one reviewer (R2) noting innovation, the two other reviewers found the overall contribution low due to lack of detail and unclear evidence. The presentation quality was rated low (R1:2, R3:1), with calls for a comprehensive running example and improved writing. (R1, R3)
