## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes CeGNN, a cell-embedded GNN with learnable cell attributions and a Feature-Enhanced (FE) block for learning spatiotemporal dynamics from PDEs and real-world data. One reviewer (score 10, confidence 5) finds the paper well-written and the results sound, praising the ablation study. The other three reviewers (scores 6, 5, 5; confidences 5, 3, 4) are substantially more critical, citing insufficient novelty relative to prior topological deep learning works, weak and outdated baselines, unclear experimental rigor, and lack of theoretical justification for the core innovations.

### Strengths

- Ablation study is excellent and thoroughly justifies the proposed framework (R1).  
- Paper is well-organized, easy to follow, and provides detailed technical descriptions (R2, R4).  
- Addresses a core problem in learning physical dynamics (R2).  
- Good empirical results with abundant experimental results and visualizations; CeGNN achieves superior performance over the compared methods (R3, R4; R1 notes results are sound).

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of novelty**: The core idea of cell-attribution / higher-order message passing has been previously proposed in topological deep learning, e.g., cell complex neural networks (Hajij et al., 2020), simplicial complex networks (Bodnar et al., 2021), and the survey “Topological Deep Learning: Going Beyond Graph Data” (Papillon et al., 2022). The paper does not adequately differentiate CeGNN from these existing approaches; application to physical systems is new, but the methodological contribution is marginal (R3). R4 requests an explicit summary of CellMPNN’s contributions over existing higher-order graph methods.

- **Insufficient theoretical justification**: The effectiveness of CeGNN, particularly the cell feature and the FE block, lacks theoretical analysis. The cell feature is a learnable feature initialized by distance to cell center—its significance is not verified via theoretical analysis or by measuring the distance between cell and node features. The benefit may simply stem from added position awareness; replacing distance to cell center with distance to nearest PDE boundary might also yield improvements (R2). There is no theoretical discussion on why CeGNN improves over standard MPNNs (R4).

- **Weak baseline selection**: Compared baselines are from before 2022, omitting many recent breakthroughs in graph-based and neural operator approaches (R2, R4). This raises concerns about the effectiveness of the proposed method, as it may not outperform more recent baselines.

- **Missing experimental details and rigor**: Code is not available. It is unclear whether training data is self-generated or from public datasets, and training dataset size is not reported. Long-term simulation is claimed, but all experiments are one-step predictions, which is insufficient (R2). R2 also asks how much improvement would be observed if the cell-attribution feature were integrated into MP-PDE—no such ablation or comparison is provided.

- **Feature-Enhanced block poorly explained**: The methodology and motivation for the FE block are insufficient; more equations and explanation are needed (R1, R4). The physical or mathematical inspiration behind Algorithm 1 is not provided; the paper only shows that it works without explaining the reasoning that led to its design (R1). FE modules are not clearly defined (R4).

- **Incomplete experimental benchmarking**: The paper does not compare against a baseline where the cell feature is replaced by a simple positional encoding (e.g., distance to boundary), leaving doubt about whether the improvement is due to the cell-embedding concept itself or just extra positional information (R2).
