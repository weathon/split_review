## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes IQ-MPC, a model-based, reward-free imitation learning method that combines inverse soft-Q learning (IQ-Learn) with learned latent dynamics models (TD-MPC2) and model predictive control (MPP1). The method learns implicit rewards in Q-function space using an inverse soft-Q objective, enabling planning in latent space without explicit reward reconstruction. Evaluations on DMControl, MyoSuite, and ManiSkill2 (state and vision) show sample efficiency gains and stable expert-level performance compared to model-free baselines.

### Strengths

- **Novelty of reward-free, model-based IRL**: Integrating IQ-Learn’s inverse soft-Q with MPC to eliminate adversarial reward estimation is an original and scalable approach.
- **Clear formulation and grounding**: The soft-Q learning objective within a learned model is explained clearly and well-grounded in prior work (IQ-Learn, TD-MPC).
- **Comprehensive empirical evaluation**: Covers locomotion (DMControl), dexterous manipulation (MyoSuite), and object handling (ManiSkill2), including both state-based and visual (image-based) imitation learning tasks.
- **Compelling sample efficiency**: IQ-MPC shows superior sample efficiency over selected baselines and addresses instability of prior interactive IL approaches.
- **Ablation and analysis experiments**: Ablations on design choices, number of expert trajectories (e.g., 100–500), and correlation with learned rewards demonstrate thoughtful experimental design.
- **Latent-space reasoning without reconstruction**: Avoids reconstruction objective overhead, enabling efficient handling of high-dimensional inputs like images.
- **Training stability documentation**: Authors clearly describe techniques used to improve training stability.

### Weaknesses

- **Mathematical errors and unclear derivations** (R1): 
  - Eq. (4): Expectation over policy state-action distribution appears incorrect; V(s) should be Q(s,a) or derivation missing.
  - Transition from Eq. (6) to (12) is not explained; same V vs Q confusion.
  - Eq. (12): Sum should be inside the expectation.
  - Eq. (13) reward signal V(zt) − γV(z′t) is just reward shaping, which is policy-invariant and cannot serve as a valid reward signal.
- **Problematic causality and writing clarity** (R1):
  - Inverse Q-learning is not the novel formulation that mitigates out-of-distribution errors and bias accumulation; those are general advantages of IRL.
  - “In the objective function, p0 is the initial distribution of states. In this way, we can perform imitation learning by leveraging actor-critic architecture.” p0 not mentioned in equations; unclear why initial state distribution enables actor-critic.
  - α described only as “scalar coefficient to ensure stable training and prevent overfitting” without explanation or citation.
  - Section 4 first paragraph: storyline unclear; claims conventional world models cannot directly perform imitation learning, but many model-based IL methods exist without rewards (contradictory).
  - Lines 202–204: claim “reward-free world model … without requiring explicit reward data” but lines 204–206 say “our model can decode dense rewards … learn a reward function”. Self-contradictory language needs clarification.
- **Fundamental technical concern on Q-function transfer** (R2): Recovered Q-function entangles reward with environment dynamics. As the learned model changes across iterations, the Q-function may fail to transfer. Authors should provide theoretical analysis or discuss potential failure modes.
- **Limited novelty** (R2, R4): IQ-MPC blends IQ-Learn and TD-MPC without substantial novel theoretical insights beyond removing reward modeling. Core idea of using learned model to plan with learned Q-function is not entirely novel. Missing explicit technical contributions that differentiate from existing methods (e.g., addressing compounding errors in model-based planning, theoretical guarantees).
- **Incomplete baselines and comparisons** (R2, R4):
  - Missing comparisons with other model-based IRL methods (e.g., Hybrid Inverse Reinforcement Learning (Ren et al., 2024) which has SOTA on MuJoCo/D4RL; see [2402.08848]).
  - No comparison with offline IL methods (e.g., Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023)) in the same setting with 100 expert demos; offline methods do not suffer same stability issues.
  - No apples-to-apples comparison with reward-based model-based IRL to isolate benefit of reward-free formulation.
  - Claim that reward-based world models cause training instability not strongly supported empirically; IQ-Learn itself is known to be unstable in prior work.
  - Missing baseline performances from [1] (IQ-Learn paper) for other online IL methods and offline BC.
- **Computational overhead** (R2, R4): MPPI planning is expensive; inference-time cost is higher than baselines, making comparisons potentially unfair. Authors should either distill the planner into a policy class or report interactions (environment interactions vs world+model interactions). Provide empirical metrics of computational cost (time complexity, memory) compared to baselines.
- **Expert demonstration count** (R2): IQ-MPC uses 100–500 expert trajectories, while IQ-Learn originally learned with as few as 5–10 in MuJoCo. Discrepancy should be resolved by running IQ-Learn in same mujoco settings as Hybrid IRL baselines.
- **Misleading “state-only” claim** (R2): Mention “state-only” data is misleading because state-action pairs are needed to learn Q-function and policy.
- **Incomplete related work** (R2): Missing citations of fundamental IRL papers (Ziebart 2008) and recent model-based IRL methods (see long list in R2’s review). Including these would contextualize IQ-MPC properly.
- **Missing explanations in experiments** (R3):
  - Section 5.2: Medium reward prediction: why does model restore rewards closer to ground truth? Variance increases sharply when real rewards are high; how does deviation in reward prediction affect MPPI results?
  - Section 5.3: Sampling complexity comparison with baselines for small expert data is lacking; only planning ability is focused.
  - No experiment comparing reward recovery performance of other baseline methods.
- **Instability and noise sensitivity** (R4): 
  - Why does IQ-MPC handle instability better than IQ-Learn despite similar framework?
  - How does stochastic dynamics noise affect IQ-MPC (since IQ-Learn suffers under stochastic transitions)?
  - Sensitivity of IQ-MPC to hyperparameters (detailed in appendix but not discussed).
- **Minor issues**:
  - Line 212: typo – “Q” appears twice (R1).
  - R1 also notes important missing work: Al-Hafez et al., LS-IQ [link between SQIL and IQ-Learn, offering solution to instability].
  - p0 mentioned without being in equations (R2 line 155).
