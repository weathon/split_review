- Scores: 3, 5, 3, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces P2PCFR+, a variant of PCFR+ that downweights the predicted regret by a factor α to reduce strategy oscillation between iterations. The authors prove a slightly better constant in the regret bound and show empirical improvements on several small games. Reviewers are split: two find the idea clean and effective, two consider the theoretical and practical contributions too marginal for acceptance.

### Strengths
- The proposed modification to PCFR+ is simple (one line change) and yields noticeable empirical speedups on several benchmark games (Kuhn, Leduc, Liar’s Dice, Goofspiel).
- The paper is well written and provides a comprehensive background.
- Experiments are fairly comprehensive across the tested games, and the method converges faster than PCFR+ in most of them.
- The theoretical analysis proves a stronger constant in the regret bound for some regime of α.
- The method is scale‑invariant (like PCFR+), which is a valuable property.
- One reviewer (R4) notes that beating PCFR+ in practice is not easy, making the empirical results surprising and interesting.

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical contribution is minor** – the improvement is only in a multiplicative constant (e.g., from √8 ≈ 2.828 for α=0 to √6 ≈ 2.449 for α=1), which does not justify the strong claim of “faster theoretical convergence rate” (R1, R4). Moreover, Theorem 3 of Farina et al. (2021) already achieves a better constant of √2 (R4).
- **Proof errors and lack of clarity** – Multiple reviewers point to unresolved issues in the theoretical proof:
  * Equation (7) does not match the update description in the main text (R3).
  * Application of Lemma A.1 (line 684) uses the wrong variable (R_t+1 instead of ˆR_t+1) (R3).
  * The negative‑definiteness argument in lines 701–706 is not justified; for α=1 an extra term appears whose sign is not proven (R3, R4).
  * Theorem 4.1 and 4.3: R_I is written as an element of ∆^{|A(I)|} but should be ℝ_{\ge 0}^{|A(I)|} (R2).
  * Theorem 4.2: may be missing a division by T (R2).
- **Unexplained hyperparameter α** – The theoretical bound improves as α increases, but the algorithm reduces to CFR+ (which performs worse than PCFR+) when α→∞. Experiments use α=5, which is outside the range [0,1] where theory applies. No guidance is given for selecting α, requiring grid search in practice (R1, R2, R4).
- **Claim of being parameter-free is incorrect** – The algorithm introduces a new hyperparameter α (R4). The paper incorrectly contrasts itself with Smooth PCFR+ as being parameter‑free.
- **Instability argument is not supported** – The claim that prediction errors cause instability is hard to reconcile with the fact that scale‑invariance (which induces instability) is generally considered beneficial for fast convergence. It is unclear that P2PCFR+ resolves instability beyond a small approximation (R4).
- **Missing experimental comparisons** – The paper does not test on:
  * Large‑scale poker games (e.g., HUNL subgames) where DCFR is known to be strong (R2).
  * Non‑poker games such as Battleship and Pursuit‑Evasion, which were used in the original PCFR+ paper (R1).
  * Quadratic averaging for PCFR+ (the original PCFR+ paper uses it and obtains significant speedups) (R1).
  * Last‑iterate convergence performance (R4).
- **Inconsistency with prior literature** – Line 268‑269 claims PCFR+ converges more slowly than CFR+, contradicting the original PCFR+ paper which states the opposite (R2).
- **Example in Section 4.1 is flawed** – The construction with R^t_I = [2,0] cannot produce the described instantaneous regret r^t_I = [-1,1]; a correct example would need R^t_I = [0,0] (R4).
- **Typos and presentation issues** – Several reviewers noted undefined abbreviations (e.g., APCFR+ in lines 416/417, should be P2PCFR+ or PCFR+) (R1, R2). The name “P2PCFR+” is unnecessarily complex (R4). Eq. (6) and Theorem 4.2 place absolute constants inside big‑O notation, which is confusing (R4).
- **Question about discrepancy guarantee** – Lines 300‑303 claim that the discrepancy ∥σ^{t+1} − σ^t∥ decreases, but the bound also depends on ∥σ^{t+1} − ˜σ^{t+1}∥ which may increase (R2).
- **Question about cross‑iteration differences** – Line 297 claims that ∥˜σ^{t+1} − ˜σ^t∥ is unchanged from PCFR+, but this is not obvious because ˆR^{t+1} depends on σ^t which differs between methods (R4).
