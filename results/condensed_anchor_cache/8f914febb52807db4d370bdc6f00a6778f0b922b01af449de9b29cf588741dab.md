## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies model merging by focusing on compatible specialization. Using centered kernel alignment (CKA), it shows that as models fine-tune on distinct domains (e.g., math vs. code), their internal feature representations diverge, causing feature-averaging merging methods to perform poorly. The authors propose a dynamic routing-based merging strategy (inspired by Mixture of Experts) that routes across layers rather than using static interpolation. Experiments on GPT-2 (math and code datasets, evaluated by cross-entropy loss) demonstrate that routing improves over basic feature averaging, but performance plateaus when representational incompatibility grows. The paper advocates for new approaches that operate on well-defined input/output spaces, analogous to human language.

### Strengths
- **Insightful identification of compatibility issues**: Highlights that representational divergence during fine-tuning is a fundamental obstacle to model merging; well-motivated.
- **Fresh perspective**: Introduces compatible specialization as a lens for merging, with novel use of CKA to analyze representation similarity.
- **Clear writing** (Reviewers 1, 2): Well-organized, with helpful diagrams (Figures 1 and 4); analogies aid intuition.
- **Experiments on both in-domain and out-of-distribution settings**: Assess knowledge transferability; includes analysis of routing variants (multi-layer, cross-layer) showing better performance when models share similar internal structures.
- **Valuable direction**: Investigating impact of expert similarity in MoE merging (Reviewer 5) and cross-layer routing is intriguing and holds potential for new methodologies.
- **Significance**: Relevant as fine-tuned domain-specific models proliferate; addresses computational efficiency and compatibility (Reviewer 2).
- **Minority positive view (Reviewer 3, score 5)**: The MoE-based approach introduces a fresh perspective; routing between domain-specific models could address limitations of parameter interpolation when alignment is poor.

### Weaknesses
- **Concept of "compatible specialization" is ill-defined** (Reviewer 1): No clear connection between routing mechanisms and a model’s "ability to communicate knowledge"; lacks literature review on relevant past methods (e.g., L²-SP, linear mode connectivity [LMC]).
- **Limited experimental scope** (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5):
  - Only cross-entropy (CE) loss used for evaluation; insufficient to capture language model quality (perplexity, accuracy, diversity, coherence). Need well-defined benchmarks requiring multiple specializations (Reviewers 3, 4).
  - Comparisons only with feature averaging, not with other benchmark model merging or MoE methods (e.g., recent works). Missing ablations against past techniques (Reviewers 1, 2).
  - Changes in parameter count: MoE routing effectively doubles model size; observed loss reduction may be partly due to larger model rather than merging efficacy. Should compare models of similar size (Reviewer 3).
  - Only routing function studied is a linear transformation (Reviewer 4); plateau in Figure 5 is unconvincing as it uses only 3 points (standard, 2 layers, 3 layers) (Reviewer 4).
  - Small GPT-2 model trained with SFT on math/code tasks; code and math are challenging for this scale, raising concerns about adverse effects on results. Recommend larger model or simpler tasks (Reviewer 5).
- **CKA analysis limitations** (Reviewers 1, 2):
  - CKA is performed pre-merging only; does not explain post-merging performance dynamics. No analysis of how CKA changes post-merging across different routing configurations (would be necessary to correlate with performance plateau, but CKA remains constant pre-merging, undermining claimed correlation).
  - Recent work raises concerns about CKA reliability for similarity analysis; should include additional metrics or validate findings (citations needed).
  - Section 4.3 claims correlation between representational similarity and performance plateau, but Figure 6 only shows a single snapshot (likely "Routing with 3 Layers"; unclear). No evidence that CKA increases across configurations as claimed; merely reconfirms that adjacent layers have high CKA (known from prior work [9,10]).
  - CKA not a commonly used metric for feature distance; questions about differences from cosine similarity or PCA (Reviewer 5).
- **Lack of concrete vision**: Section 5.2 ("Routing Models in Input and Output Spaces") lacks concrete steps or preliminary experiments to advance this vision (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing experiments** (Reviewer 3):
  - No direct test of claim that larger CKA divergence degrades interpolation performance. Should compare model pairs with varying CKA (e.g., math vs. code vs. two code models) controlling for merging method.
  - No analysis of CKA pattern for MoE-merged model compared to interpolation-based model; could reveal advantages.
- **No discussion of time/space complexity** (Reviewer 1).
- **Fig. 5 plateau not well-supported**: Only three data points; need more routing configurations (Reviewer 4).
- **Choice of math and code tasks** (Reviewer 4): These tasks may have more in common than other pairs; rationale unclear.
- **Lack of action on incompatibility observations**: Figure 6 shows layer incompatibility, but no concrete steps taken to handle relative layer positions; only conjectures (Reviewer 1).
- **Typos** (Reviewers 1, 2, 5): Figure 4 caption incomplete; line 411 contains double "is"; conspicuous error in Figure 3.
- **Trade-off question**: Would improving representational compatibility come at the cost of individual specialization performance? (Reviewer 4)
- **Questions on routing complexity**: Figure 5 shows diminishing returns; ask about architectural modifications or efficiency improvements (Reviewer 2).
- **Scope**: Conclusions drawn from MoE-based feature merging may not generalize to parameter merging or other feature merging methods; recommend clearly specifying focus on MoE in abstract/introduction (Reviewer 5).
