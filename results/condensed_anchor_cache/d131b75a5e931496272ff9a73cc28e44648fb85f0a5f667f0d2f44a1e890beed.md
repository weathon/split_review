## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes RetinexGDP, a training-free, zero-shot personalized low-light image enhancement model that integrates Retinex domain knowledge into a pre-trained diffusion model. The method starts with a single Gaussian convolutional layer that incorporates total variation optimization for zero-shot Retinex decomposition. The decomposed reflectance is then used as a condition in the DDIM sampling process, which is guided by user-provided text instructions via the CLIP model. Patch-wise DDIM inversion ensures content and structure consistency. RetinexGDP supports any image size and produces noise-suppressed results. Experiments across nine low-light image datasets show performance comparable to state-of-the-art models. Reviewer scores are 3, 5, 5, 6, indicating significant disagreement: one reviewer (score 3) found the paper limited in novelty and effectiveness, while another (score 6) found it well-designed and experimentally validated.

### Strengths
- Training-free, zero-shot personalized enhancement allowing style personalization via user text instructions (R2, R3, R4).
- Integration of Retinex information (reflectance) into the diffusion model enhances interpretability (R2, R4).
- Incorporation of the CLIP model into the reflectance-conditioned DDIM sampling enables text-guided enhancement, increasing flexibility (R3, R4).
- Edge-aware property of total variation optimization is embedded into a single Gaussian convolutional layer for zero-shot Retinex decomposition (R4).
- Experiments cover nine different low-light datasets, providing a thorough evaluation (R1, R4).
- In addition, the abstract notes support for any image size and noise suppression without extra noise constraints (mentioned by R4 in summary).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns:** The method mainly combines existing techniques (GDP, Retinex, patch-wise DDIM inversion). The total variation layer borrows from Yeh et al. (2022). The overall idea is not inspiring (R1). Another similar work, Reti-diff (He et al. 2023), adopts a similar core idea – the paper does not clearly state its significant advantages over it (R4).
- **Effectiveness is disputed:** One reviewer (R1) finds the performance not state-of-the-art, with inconsistent metric results across Tables 1, 2, and 3. Qualitative examples show unnatural colors/styles (Figure 5) and over-smoothing (Figure 15). Another reviewer (R4) states that a large number of experiments have proved effectiveness. In comparison to training-free methods, RetinexGDP does not outperform RetinexDIP, and differences between results with different text instructions appear minimal (bottom line of Figure 5) (R2). The paper should also compare recent unsupervised SOTA methods, e.g., LightenDiffusion (ECCV2024) and FourierDiff (CVPR2024) (R3).
- **Metric inconsistency:** NIQE is used in ablation but not in the comparison experiment, though the cited NIQMC could be used on the LOL and VELOL datasets (R4). Additionally, R1 notes performance is inconsistent across metrics in the comparison tables.
- **Missing experimental details:** Hyperparameters for each loss function term (including gamma factor in Eq. 4 and λ in Eq. 3) are not provided (R2, R4). The values for gamma correction factor and λ are not given (R2). The paper lacks specific explanation of the image content and style distance metrics L_c and L_s in Equation 5 (R3). A diagram of the gradient flow would clarify which components are fixed/learnable (R2).
- **Methodological concerns about Retinex decomposition:** Decomposing illumination and reflectance from low-light images is challenging; using a Gaussian filter and TV proximity operator may not ensure effective decomposition. The paper does not justify why reflectance alone is used for enhancement rather than the entire image, which could lose illumination information (R2, R3). Gamma correction is applied after estimating illumination, but it is unclear why and whether the illumination shown in Figure 3 is before or after correction. The reflectance appears over-saturated, possibly due to gamma correction (R2). The claim that the Gaussian convolutional layer is a "total variation layer" is questionable because fixed Gaussian kernels do not correspond to a standard total variation layer (R1).
- **Figure-related issues:** In Figure 1, the gray line representing the loss function is not visible; blue and yellow lines are unclear (R2). The colors mentioned in the caption about the loss function do not match the colors used in the figure (R3).
- **Textual control ability:** Textual control ability and granularity are not fully demonstrated; content consistency across different prompts is not clearly explained (R1, R2). More examples would help (R1).
- **Computational complexity:** The patch-wise DDIM inversion has high computational complexity, making the method less appealing for applications (R1).
- **Training-free vs. loss functions:** The method is claimed training-free but introduces several loss functions. How the loss can be optimized in a training-free setting is unclear (R2, R3).
