## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MIND (Math Informed syNthetic Dialogues) to generate large-scale synthetic math dialogues for continued pretraining. Using LLaMA3-70B-Instruct and persona pairs (e.g., teacher-student, professor-professor), raw math text from OpenWebMath (14B tokens) is converted into multi-turn conversations that embed step-by-step reasoning. Experiments show models pretrained on MIND-generated dialogues outperform those trained on raw data: GSM8K +13.42%, MATH +2.30%, MMLU +4.55%, MMLU-STEM +4.28%, and General Reasoning +2.51%. Ablations highlight that knowledge gaps between dialogue participants are crucial for data quality.

### Strengths
- Clear articulation of contributions, methodology, and results; well-written and easy to read (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- Strong experimental results on multiple benchmarks with substantial gains across math reasoning, specialized knowledge, and general reasoning (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). Numbers: GSM8K +13.42%, MATH +2.30%, MMLU +4.55%, MMLU-STEM +4.28%, General Reasoning +2.51%.
- Thorough evaluation across dimensions: different conversational styles, scaling behavior, and applicability to three different seed corpora (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- Ablations provide good insights, especially showing that knowledge gaps between participants (e.g., teacher-student vs. professor-professor) are essential (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- Originality in using structured conversational data to add semantic variations and step-by-step reasoning, going beyond simple rephrasing (Reviewer 2).
- Method can also be used to clean pretraining corpora (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Distillation from LLaMA3-70B-Instruct not isolated**: It is unclear whether improvements come from the MIND-generated dialogue structure or from distillation of the powerful underlying LLM (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). The authors should compare with a weaker generator (e.g., LLaMA3-8B or an SFT model based on the pretrained model used in experiments) to isolate the effect of MIND (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- **Single pretrained model base**: Experiments use only one in-house checkpoint; improvements might be less significant with a highly-optimized model. Testing on multiple strong baselines (LLaMA-3, Mistral, Gemma) would strengthen generality (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing stronger baselines**: Only compared against a rephrasing baseline. A more advanced baseline for continued pretraining would be automatically generating follow-up questions and answers from text (e.g., as in other continued pretraining studies [1]) (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing related work discussion**: Should compare and contrast with Dialog Inpainting (Dai et al., 2022), which also synthesizes conversations from documents, though not specifically for math (Reviewer 1).
- **Evaluation details lacking**: Unclear whether results are zero-shot or few-shot; gains may decrease in few-shot setting. Also, why was "student-student" the only style kept for the 14B experiments and ablations when Table 1 shows the longest conversation (multi-turn across styles) achieves the best performance? (Reviewer 3).
- **Synthetic data quality concerns**: Need average length difference between original text and generated conversation; degree of hallucination in generated dialogues unclear (Reviewer 3).
- **Narrow domain focus**: Only math is tested; the approach should be evaluated on other technical domains needing step-by-step reasoning (physics, engineering, coding) to show broader applicability (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3 also suggests more diverse persona combinations).
- **Impact of conversation length and data ratio**: Reviewer 2 asked about optimal conversation length and the relative ratio of math vs. general data during continued pretraining. These affect performance and should be addressed.
- **Minority reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 8, confidence 4) found the paper very strong, clear, and convincing, praising the experimental results and methodology. Two other reviewers (score 5, confidence 4) raised the above concerns about distillation, baselines, evaluation details, and generalization, and rated soundness 2–3. This contrast should be noted.
- **Data release**: Reviewer 2 asked whether the generated dataset will be released (important for reproducibility).
