## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ACTIONPIECE, a method to dynamically expand the action space with temporally extended action subsequences discovered by byte pair encoding from high-reward trajectories. It is applied to generative flow networks (GFlowNets) and RL samplers to improve sample efficiency and mode discovery in long-horizon tasks. The approach is evaluated on synthetic tasks and an RNA sequence generation task. Scores range from 6 to 8; two reviewers (scores 8,8) are more positive about novelty and empirical thoroughness, while three reviewers (scores 6,6,6) raise concerns about novelty, generalizability, and experimental rigor.

### Strengths
- Multiple reviewers find the core idea of iteratively extracting and chunking action subsequences online to expand the action space interesting and novel for amortized sampling (R2, R5).
- The method is compatible with both RL and GFlowNets samplers (R1). The focus on GFlowNets is justified by their unique need for structured exploration and mode discovery (R1).
- Empirical evaluation is comprehensive and thorough, with insightful analyses and visualizations demonstrating improved sample efficiency and mode discovery across three synthetic environments and one real-world RNA task (R1, R2, R3). Reviewers appreciate the real-world orientation and informative discussion (R3).
- The paper is of good methodological quality, conducting careful and meticulous experiments (R4). It shows broad applicability and robustness across tasks (R5).
- The exploration of action abstraction has potential to lead to more efficient sampling techniques (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited comparison and differentiation from prior work**: The discovery of macro-actions has been extensively studied (options, unsupervised skill discovery, hierarchical RL). The paper does not clearly differentiate its approach from existing chunking mechanisms (e.g., variational formulation, clustering) and prior use of BPE for action abstraction (Zheng et al.). Two reviewers note this: R1 (macro-actions), R2 (prior chunking mechanisms), R4 (BPE not new, unclear novelty). The authors should provide a detailed comparison and clarify their unique contributions (R1, R4).
- **Deterministic assumption and generalizability to stochastic environments**: Line 159 assumes deterministic state transitions (s'=s+a). This is a strong assumption; the method may not apply to stochastic environments common in RL and GFlowNets. The authors should address how the method can accommodate stochastic transitions (R1).
- **Experimental limitations**:
  - Experiments are conducted on simple environments; no tests on complex benchmarks like Atari games (R1). Three seeds per setting (line 348, 507) are insufficient for statistical significance, especially for mode discovery where variance can be high (R3).
  - No comparison with existing macro-action discovery methods (R1). Only two chunking variants (Increment and Replace) are compared against each other, but not against prior chunking techniques (R2).
  - The use of a fixed BPE tokenizer for chunking fixed target distributions may limit generality and flexibility (R5). Reviewer asks how other tokenization techniques would perform (R3 Q2).
  - The proposed algorithm exhibits varied performance across tasks and samplers, lacking consistent indication of robustness (R4).
- **Unsupported claim about interpretability**: The claim that abstracted high-level actions are interpretable is not supported by evidence in the paper (R2).
- **Clarity and presentation issues**:
  - The Preliminaries section lacks a comprehensive introduction to "amortized samplers" (R1).
  - The heavy focus on GFlowNets as background is unclear when the method also applies to RL; authors should clarify why GFlowNets were chosen and how the method specifically addresses challenges unique to GFlowNets (R1).
  - Details on training the action encoder (line 304) are missing (R1).
  - The abstract lacks quantitative results (R5).
  - Minor typo: "the the" at line 319 (R3).
  - Conclusions are not effectively communicated; results appear highly case-specific without clear actionable takeaways (R4).
- **Lack of theoretical analysis**: No rigorous theory or proofs support the observed improvements; empirical results alone are insufficient (R5).
- **Missing exploration of adaptive combination**: The two proposed chunking mechanisms show contrasting strengths—whether and how they could be adaptively combined is not explored (R3 Q1).
- **Applicability to offline RL not discussed**: The paper does not address whether the method can be applied to offline-training RL tasks (R5).
