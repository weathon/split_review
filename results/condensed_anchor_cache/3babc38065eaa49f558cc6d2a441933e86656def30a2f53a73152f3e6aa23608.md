## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a hybrid anomaly detection framework combining a familiarity branch (feature comparison) and a novelty branch (B-cos network explanations) to detect anomalies with novel features. The reviewers find the idea interesting but criticize the unclear definitions of familiar/novel features and poor presentation in Section 3. There is disagreement on experimental validation: Reviewer 3 (score 5) considers the experimental results good overall, while Reviewer 1 (score 3) finds the method performs poorly on sensory anomalies and lacks comparisons, and Reviewer 2 (score 3) questions the fairness of figures and missing ablations. Additional concerns include insufficient ablation of hybrid components, unclear design choices, and missing related work.

### Strengths
- Introducing novel features for anomaly detection is an interesting and relatively under-explored approach (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). The idea of defining novel features according to absence of explainability is specifically noted as interesting (Reviewer 3).
- The manuscript deals with an important issue of anomaly detection (Reviewer 3).
- Anomaly detection via explanation is a relatively under-explored approach (Reviewer 2).
- Considering performance for both semantic and sensory anomaly detection with a single methodology is beneficial and often neglected; the method appears to give the best overall performance across both types of AD tasks (Reviewer 2).
- The presented experimental results are good overall (Reviewer 3). (Reviewer 1 disagrees regarding sensory anomaly performance, see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear definitions and poor presentation of familiar/novel features** (all reviewers).
  - Reviewer 1: Terminology is inappropriate (e.g., calling anomalies “background set” confuses with normal regions in defect detection) and the description of familiar/novel features is hard to follow.
  - Reviewer 2: Section 3 is longwinded, definitions are not rigorous, heavy notation obscures intuition, and illustrative examples are needed.
  - Reviewer 3: Despite claiming a formal definition as a contribution, the presentation in Sec. 3 is poor – definitions are bundled, notation is non-standard, and the section is hard to comprehend; the authors should follow a more structured format with definitions, lemmas, and theorems as in [a].
- **Potential redundancy between branches**. 
  - Reviewer 1 asserts the novelty and familiarity branches are similar; the novelty branch merely transfers from feature space to B-cos explanations.
  - Reviewer 2 questions how the B-cos network ensures it focuses on novel features rather than relying on deviations in familiar features like the familiarity module.
  - Reviewer 3 requests an ablation of the contribution of each component in the hybrid anomaly score.
- **Experimental validation concerns**.
  - **Limited sensory anomaly evaluation**: only MVTec; Reviewer 1 notes the method performs poorly on sensory anomalies and cannot compare with anomaly localization methods; consequently, the visual inspectable explanations are not very valuable if the method focuses only on semantic anomalies.
  - **Figure 5 is misleading**: Reviewer 2 points out that the selection of samples showing the maximum deviation from the training set upon adding the novelty score artificially amplifies the effect; a random sampling or distribution of impact is needed. Reviewer 1 states that Figure 5 does not explain the method’s effectiveness because adding novelty only increases distance for already classifiable anomalies, while confusion regions remain.
  - **Figure 6 baseline unclear**: Reviewer 1 argues that the familiarity-based baseline in Figure 6 is unspecified; a fair comparison requires using the authors’ own familiarity branch to demonstrate false negative reduction.
  - **Claim about reducing reliance on background class (Section 5.2) is not substantiated**: Reviewer 2 notes there are no experiments measuring performance with and without the background class; only the way the background class is generated is changed.
- **Missing ablation of the hybrid anomaly score**: Reviewer 3 explicitly requests ablation of each component’s contribution; this ties to the need for also clearly stating the final anomaly score definition with an equation (Reviewer 3).
- **Design choices not well explained**.
  - Reviewer 3 questions why two nearest train features are used in Eq. 3, and what are the benefits/issues of using more or fewer neighbors.
  - Reviewer 2 asks about the intuition behind using the normal approximation to simulate anomalies, compared to other augmentation strategies in prior work.
- **Missing details on near-AD experiments**: Reviewer 2 asks which classes were chosen, how they were chosen, whether other splits were tested, and how performance varied.
- **Missing relevant related work**: Reviewer 3 notes that a hybrid formulation for anomaly detection has been introduced in [b] but is not cited, and should be discussed.
