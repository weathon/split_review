## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies that Fourier feature embeddings in implicit neural representations (INRs) introduce high-frequency noise due to finite frequency sampling. Through Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) analysis, the authors interpret this as a truncated Fourier series. They propose a bias-free MLP as an adaptive linear filter to locally suppress unnecessary frequencies and amplify essential ones at the coordinate level, plus a line-search algorithm to dynamically adjust the filter’s learning rate. The method is validated on image regression (256×256), 3D shape regression, and Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), showing marginal improvements over positional encoding and random Fourier features.

### Strengths
- **Novel idea**: Suppressing/amplifying frequencies in Fourier feature embeddings is a valid and underexplored direction (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Theoretical analysis**: An NTK-based explanation attributing noise to finite frequency sampling is provided (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). (Note: Other reviewers question the connection to the method; see Weaknesses.)
- **Adaptive filter and line-search**: The bias-free MLP as a piecewise linear filter and the line-search algorithm aim to balance frequency learning and noise reduction (Reviewers 1, 3). (Note: Reviewer 4 notes misuse of “Pareto efficiency.”)
- **Multi-task validation**: Experiments on 2D image regression, 3D shape representation, and NeRF demonstrate generality; NeRF results are the most compelling (Reviewers 1, 4). Improvements are reported over baselines with positional encoding and random Fourier features (Reviewers 1, 4). (But Reviewers 2 and 4 note marginal gains in 3D shape.)
- **Complementarity insight**: The paper touches on the complementary representation power of Fourier series and ReLU networks, a potentially impactful idea (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical analysis vs. method mismatch**  
  - The NTK analysis is presented as central, but Theorem 2 does not require NTK and Theorems 1, 3, and Lemma 1 appear tangential or unmotivated (Reviewer 1, confidence 3).  
  - There is a contradiction: the theory claims noise arises from *unsampled* frequencies, while the adaptive filter suppresses *sampled* high frequencies in low-frequency regions; this mismatch weakens the motivation (Reviewer 2, confidence 5).  
  - Theorem statements lack self-contained notation, clear interpretation, and connection to the method (Reviewer 4).  
  - Related work on NTK and spectral bias omits recent training-dynamics methods and does not compare to prior strategies (Reviewers 1, 4).

- **Clarity and innovation of the proposed adaptive filter**  
  - “Bias-free” refers to removing additive bias in linear layers, not to avoiding spectral bias; the rationale is deferred to page 6 and lacks evidence (Reviewers 2, 4).  
  - The core idea (masking high-frequency embeddings in low-frequency regions) is similar to SAPE (Spatially-Adaptive Progressive Encoding), which is not cited or compared (Reviewer 2).  
  - No precise mathematical formulation of the adaptive filter is given; only a simplified description (Reviewer 1).  
  - Visualization of filtered embeddings (Figures 6, 13–15) is unexplained given the high dimension (H×W×embedding channel) (Reviewer 1).  
  - The impact of filter size (width, number of layers) on training and stability is not explored (Reviewer 2).  
  - The method adds parameters compared to baselines, but no controlled experiment with equal parameter count is provided (Reviewers 2, 4). In image fitting, the filter alone has at least 384×384×3 parameters, while the target is 256×256×3 – a strongly overparameterized regime where a pixel grid achieves zero error (Reviewer 4).  
  - The line-search algorithm is described as achieving “Pareto efficiency” between two components, but Pareto efficiency properly refers to tradeoffs between multiple objectives, not model components (Reviewer 4).

- **Insufficient and potentially unfair experimental comparisons**  
  - Missing comparisons with recent state-of-the-art INRs: WIRE, FINER, Gaussian-based INRs, and the related work “On the frequency-bias of coordinate-mlps” (Reviewers 2, 3). Without these, the significance of the improvements is unclear (Reviewer 3).  
  - Image regression experiments use only 256×256 resolution, whereas the standard INR benchmark is DIV2K at 1024×1024. The reported ~50 dB PSNR is surprisingly high and hinders fair comparison with WIRE/FINER (Reviewer 2).  
  - In 3D shape regression (Table 2), the baseline MLP+PE achieves IOU 0.96189, while the original BACON paper reports ~0.980 IOU, suggesting insufficient training or unfair settings. The improvement from the adaptive filter is marginal (Reviewers 1, 4). Visual results (Figure 9) show no clear advantage (Reviewer 4).  
  - The ablation study (Table 4) shows the line-search benefit almost exclusively in image regression (the overparameterized setting); it is unclear whether it helps in underparameterized (compression) regimes or for larger images (Reviewer 4).  
  - No experiments on restoration tasks (inpainting, denoising, super-resolution) are provided, which are standard INR benchmarks (Reviewer 3).  
  - Performance on larger signals (full-resolution DIV2K) and runtime/memory usage are not reported (Reviewer 1).

- **Presentation and writing quality**  
  - Grammar issues and sentence fragments (e.g., lines 044–045, 188; theorem statements) (Reviewer 4).  
  - Figures: axis labels, tick marks, and color bars are often illegible without zooming (Reviewer 4). Figure 1’s y-axis scales do not match and the caption is inconsistent with the data (Reviewer 4). Figure 4 could illustrate the phenomenon with INRs; Figure 7 is unclear and the algorithm description is mostly in the appendix; Figure 8 lacks dynamic range information; Figure 10 caption may describe the wrong scene (Reviewer 4). Table 3 appears before Table 2 (Reviewer 4).  
  - The term “projection” for Fourier features is inaccurate; “embedding” or “encoding” is preferred (Reviewer 4).  
  - Related work lists summaries but does not analyze strengths/weaknesses relative to the proposed method (Reviewer 4).
