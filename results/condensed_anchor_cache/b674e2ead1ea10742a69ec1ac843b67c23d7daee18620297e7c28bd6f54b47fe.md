## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper tackles the Generalized Schrödinger Bridge (GSB) problem, which extends standard Schrödinger Bridge Matching by incorporating an additional state cost (e.g., obstacles, repulsion). The authors propose GSBM, an iterative algorithm that alternates between optimizing the drift and the marginals, using a spline-based Gaussian approximation for the conditional probability paths. The method is tested on crowd navigation, opinion depolarization, LiDAR manifolds, and image domain transfer, with comparisons primarily against DeepGSB. Reviewers generally agree the problem is important and the approach is theoretically grounded, but they raise concerns about framing, the strong Gaussian-path assumption, computational limits, and the restricted class of state costs (differentiability and quadratic control cost required). The method is noted to improve feasibility over prior GSB solvers by preserving a transport map throughout training.

### Strengths

- Addresses an important and natural generalization of the Schrödinger Bridge problem that incorporates task-specific state costs.
- Theoretically sound; the variational formulation and the use of conditional stochastic optimal control are well-motivated.
- Tested on a diverse set of problems: crowd navigation, opinion depolarization, LiDAR manifolds, and image domain transfer.
- Improves upon previous methods (DeepGSB) by ensuring guaranteed feasibility of the transport map throughout training, enabling stable convergence and better scalability in high dimensions.
- Rigorous logic, clear exposition (with minor adjustments needed), and abundant experimental details.
- Broadens the scope of Schrödinger Bridge matching and converts existing optimal transport and SBM methods into special cases of the variational framework.
- Opens new algorithmic opportunities for training diffusion models with task-specific optimality structures.

### Weaknesses

- **Framing and claims**: The paper oversells its contributions by implying it solves optimal transport (OT) or the Schrödinger Bridge problem exactly. The Gaussian approximation (Eq. 7) is a strong assumption and the constraint (6b) is hard to enforce; the method should be presented as an approximate stochastic optimal control approach rather than an exact solver for OT/SB.
- **Gaussian-path assumption**: The Gaussian approximation in (7) is a key limitation. It is not clear from Theorem 5 that the loss can always be decreased under this assumption. Other assumptions that yield closed-form expressions like (8) are not explored. The method’s applicability to problems where the conditional density is far from Gaussian is not demonstrated.
- **Missing comparisons and experiments**: 
  - No investigation of computational limits (scaling with dimensionality and sample size).
  - No analysis of how the placement of spline control points affects accuracy.
  - The method is not shown to work directly on image data; it is only applied in a latent space (Sec. 4.2). No suggestions for improvement are given.
  - Limited evidence that a different coupling \(p(x_0, x_1)\) emerges at the minimizer—Figure 1 shows identical empirical densities even after learning.
- **Theoretical limitations**: 
  - Convergence guarantees are only local; no proof of convergence to a global optimum for any class of state costs.
  - The method requires a differentiable state cost \(V_t\) and relies on quadratic control costs for convergence analysis, excluding non-smooth/discontinuous costs and more complex control cost structures. Does (6) have an analytic solution only if \(V_t\) is quadratic and \(\sigma > 0\)?
- **Missing citations and attribution**: 
  - At the start of Sec. 3.1, both Liu (2023b) and Peluchetti (2022) are cited for entropic optimal transport, but neither proposes such a setting (Liu proposes OT when the field is a gradient field, but nothing entropic).
  - Missing references for matching algorithms (e.g., Tong et al., 2023) and the Schrödinger Bridge problem should be attributed to the original decades-old literature (Chen et al., 2020) rather than to machine learning papers alone.
- **Other adjustments needed**:
  - The feasibility discussion (Table 1) is not useful since feasibility is assumed in the problem statement; Table 1 could be moved to an appendix.
  - Semantic inexactness: “\(\nabla s^\theta_t(X_t)\) matches \(p_t\)” should state that the pushforward density matches \(p_t\), not the gradient field itself.
  - The first paragraph on page 2 should note that optimal transport considers many other costs beyond the \(\mathcal{W}_2\) cost.
  - No attempt to quantify the “semantically meaningful” coupling in image translation.
- **Remaining questions** (overlap with criticisms above):
  - How do computational efficiency and accuracy scale with dimensionality and sample size?
  - How do strategies for spline control point placement influence accuracy?
  - What strategies exist for initialization of the spline optimization (addressing local convergence)?
  - Can the authors provide a problem where the conditional density is far from Gaussian, or show that any of the current examples are far from Gaussian?
