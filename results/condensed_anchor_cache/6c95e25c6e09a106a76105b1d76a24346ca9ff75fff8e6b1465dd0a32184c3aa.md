## Merged Review

### Summary
Four reviewers acknowledge that the paper proposes SuMi for test-time adaptation (TTA) under a new “wild multimodal TTA” setting with both weak and strong out-of-distribution (OOD) contamination. The method uses interquartile range (IQR) smoothing, unimodal assistance for sample selection, and mutual information sharing to align modalities. Experiments on video-audio datasets show significant performance gains over existing TTA methods. The work is timely and extends prior multimodal TTA (e.g., READ). However, reviewers raise concerns about limited modality diversity, insufficient baseline comparisons, unclear novelty and component synergy, missing experiment details, and hyperparameter sensitivity. Two reviewers (scores 5,5) are less confident in the motivation and soundness; two (scores 6,6) find the problem and results compelling but note presentation and generalizability issues.

### Strengths
- The paper introduces a more challenging and realistic “wild multimodal TTA” scenario that considers both weak and strong OOD samples, extending prior work like READ (Yang et al., 2024) and advancing the TTA field.
- Two main contributions are clear: a dual-filtering sample selection mechanism (IQR smoothing + unimodal assistance) and a mutual information loss for cross-modal alignment.
- The method is validated across various corruption scenarios, including challenging OOD settings, demonstrating robustness and effectiveness.
- Writing is straightforward and easy to follow; the related work review is comprehensive.
- The proposed SuMi achieves significant performance improvements over existing TTA methods on two popular multimodal datasets.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited modality diversity & generalizability**  
   - Experiments use only video-audio modalities. It is unclear whether SuMi generalizes to other multimodal pairs (e.g., image-text, image-point cloud, vision-language models).  
   - Real-world multimodal TTA often involves more than two modalities. The paper shows a general multimodal form in Eq. (5) but later focuses on two modalities; the method and experiments should be validated on datasets with three or more modalities.  
   - Missing modality is more common with >2 modalities; experiments on simulated two-modality datasets are too narrow.

2. **Insufficient comparative analysis**  
   - Comparable sample-selection-based TTA methods (e.g., DEYO, EATA) are not sufficiently compared. The ablation study should include: (IQR + unimodal assistance) vs. DEYO/EATA, and ablation adding mutual information sharing (MIS) to those baselines, to isolate distinct benefits of SuMi’s components.  
   - Experiments focus almost exclusively on corruption shifts; other realistic distribution shift types (e.g., natural domain shifts) should be tested.

3. **Clarity and missing details**  
   - **Figure 2** is unclear: how IQR smoothing is applied and what is input to the fusion layers (raw features or transformed) must be explicitly shown.  
   - **Figure 3(c)** lacks axis labels and a self-explanatory title (e.g., what metric is plotted—accuracy, loss?).  
   - **Tables 1 and 3** do not label weak/strong OOD settings for each modality, making performance assessment difficult.  
   - **Equation 3**: the vector \(h\) is used for sample ranking but the comparison metric (e.g., magnitude, norm) is not specified. Definition of IQR in that context is also unclear.  
   - **Equation 4**: variable \(\mu\) is not found; likely \(\gamma_{\mu}\) is meant. Typos and inconsistent variable definitions should be fixed.  
   - **Section 3.2.1**: the sample selection ranking process is never introduced before using \(h\) for comparison.  
   - The motivation figure (Figure 3) is more vivid than Figure 1; it should be placed in the Introduction.

4. **Novelty and component synergy**  
   - The three components (IQR smoothing, unimodal entropy selection, mutual information sharing) appear orthogonal; there is no clear interaction or synergy among them. The rationale for combining exactly these techniques is insufficiently justified. How do separate parts benefit each other, or are they independent contributions?  
   - The IQR smoothing is essentially a linear process over iterations; no theoretical or more effective smoothing mechanism is discussed. Why interquartile specifically rather than other quantile ranges?

5. **Hyperparameter sensitivity and prior knowledge**  
   - The hyperparameter \(\mu\) (or \(\gamma_{\mu}\)) relies on prior knowledge about which modality is dominant. In true TTA, such prior information is unavailable; this could directly impact performance across datasets. A more robust hyperparameter selection mechanism is needed.

6. **Motivation feasibility (minority concern, Reviewer 2)**  
   - One reviewer questions whether the wild multimodal TTA task is truly novel or a pseudo-motivation combining modality missing and TTA subtasks. Is it realistic that algorithms would continue to be used under sensor failures? This point should be clarified with real-world application scenarios.

7. **Missing modality handling**  
   - The method does not explicitly state how it handles cases where one modality is completely missing. If a modality is absent, correspondence predictions may be impossible; imputation or alternative strategies should be described.
