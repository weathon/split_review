## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a new optimizer to train binary neural networks (BNNs) without retaining latent real-valued weights, thereby reducing memory footprint. The update uses a bit-flip probability for binary weights, derived from the current binary weight and real-valued gradients, intended to match the binary weights obtained by SGD in real-space training in expectation. The method is evaluated on a 4-layer fully-connected network on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and Tiny-ImageNet. The claimed memory reduction is up to 33× compared to standard real-weight training.

### Strengths
- The idea of training BNNs directly in binary weight space is novel and addresses an important direction for efficient training and memory reduction on edge devices.
- The paper reads well, with clear motivations for design choices and a good analogy to the SGD algorithm that eases understanding.
- The theoretical analysis is sufficient, with mathematical claims made under reasonable practical assumptions.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental scope and high error rates**: All reviewers note that the experiments are restricted to a simple 4-layer fully-connected network. Error rates on CIFAR-10 and Tiny-ImageNet (Tab. 3) are extremely high (e.g., CIFAR-10 test error over 50%) for both the proposed method and the STE baseline, indicating severe underfitting. This makes comparisons between training methods questionable. One reviewer (score 3) explicitly states the results are “far from satisfactory” and questions whether abandoning real-valued weights is reasonable given the large accuracy sacrifice for training efficiency. In contrast, two reviewers (scores 5 and 6) are more positive overall but also emphasize that the experimental evaluation is insufficient for a clear picture.
- **Missing realistic benchmarks and architectures**: No experiments are conducted on modern architectures such as ResNet, convolutional layers, or transformer layers. This limits the demonstration of practical applicability. One reviewer (score 5) specifically asks what obstacles the authors foresee in applying the method to BNNs with convolution layers.
- **STE still needed for activation binarization**: The method does not eliminate the need for real-valued gradients in propagating through binary activations; Straight-Through Estimator (STE) is still used. This limits the claimed scope of fully eliminating real-valued computations during training.
- **Unclear justification of the binary target weight**: The analogy from Figure 1(a) to 1(b) is more like binarizing gradients in real-valued space. It is not adequately justified how the binary target weight \( w_t^* \) is determined and whether it truly represents a descent direction in the binarized loss landscape. The method projects real-valued gradients onto binary space, which may not be a valid optimization strategy.
- **Missing baseline: BOP (Binary Optimizer)**: The closely related work BOP [1] also aims at training BNNs without latent weights and should be compared, but is not included.
- **Memory reduction claim not properly validated**: Although the authors claim 33× memory reduction, real-valued gradients are still stored during training. One reviewer (score 3) challenges this claim and notes that memory consumption is not explicitly reported in any table. The same reviewer suggests that a more realistic use case for edge devices is finetuning a pre-trained BNN model with binary weights only, rather than training from scratch.
- **Minority negative assessment**: One reviewer (score 3, confidence 5) is strongly critical of the practical effectiveness, while the other two reviewers are more favorable (scores 6 and 5, with a medium and high confidence respectively). The negative reviewer stresses that the method’s results are far from competitive, the memory reduction justification is insufficient, and the experimental setup does not support the claims.
