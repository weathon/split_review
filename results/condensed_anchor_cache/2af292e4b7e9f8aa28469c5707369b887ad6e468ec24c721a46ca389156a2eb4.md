## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Generative Entropic Neural Optimal Transport (GENOT), a unified framework that solves entropic regularized optimal transport problems (balanced/unbalanced OT, Gromov-Wasserstein, Fused-GW). It uses conditional flow matching to learn continuous couplings by distilling discrete OT solutions computed via a solver. Experiments are performed on synthetic low-dimensional data and single-cell biology tasks (cell development, drug response, cross-modality translation). The paper is well-written and addresses practical challenges in OT.

### Strengths
- Unified framework that covers multiple OT problem statements (balanced, unbalanced, GW, Fused-GW) within a single approach (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Flexible with respect to cost functions and can map across incomparable spaces (R1, R3, R4).
- Novel handling of unbalanced OT via a learned reweighting function (R4).
- Use of conditional flow matching enables stochastic mappings and is effective for generative modeling (R1, R3).
- Practical experiments on relevant single-cell biology tasks demonstrate applicability and show improvements over linear regression baseline (R1, R3). (R2 and R4 are less impressed by the baselines.)
- Presentation is clear, well-written, and rigorous (R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Reliance on discrete OT solver and approximation errors:** The method does not directly solve continuous OT; it regresses discrete approximations from a black-box solver. This limits the learned map’s quality to the discrete resolution, especially in high dimensions (R1, R2, R4). The algorithm uses a biased loss due to finite batch sizes (batch size fixed at 1024, no ablation); the expectation of the discrete estimator is not equal to the true loss in Equation (7) (R4). Proposition 3.1 assumes access to the ground-truth conditional plan, but the algorithm uses discrete mini-batch estimates, so convergence to the true continuous optimal plan is unclear (R1, R2, R4). For GW, the non-convex objective may change at each iteration, making training unreliable (R2). The effect of batch size on this biasedness is not discussed (R4). The discrete solver’s errors propagate without analysis (R4).
- **Lack of quantitative evaluation of coupling accuracy:** Only qualitative visualizations (Figs 1, 4) in low dimensions are provided. No quantitative metrics (e.g., Wasserstein distance between transported source and target) compare estimated couplings to true optimal plans (R3). In biological experiments, only marginal distributions are evaluated, not the joint coupling quality (R1, R2). The authors are encouraged to evaluate on high-dimensional benchmarks (Gushchin et al., 2023; Korotin et al., 2021) (R1, R2). No comparison with other neural OT methods (e.g., Korotin et al., 2023) for direct modeling of conditional generators (R3).
- **Insufficient baselines in single-cell biology:** Only linear regression is compared; missing comparison with established single-cell alignment methods (Schiebinger et al., 2019; Demetci et al., 2022) (R3). For unbalanced OT, comparisons with (Yang & Uhler, 2018) and (Lübeck et al., 2022) are missing (R2). For GW, (Bunne et al., 2019) should be discussed (R2). The biological significance of the results is not clearly demonstrated, and the complex experimental setup makes it hard for non-specialists to assess (R1, R2, R3).
- **Incremental contribution:** The core idea of distilling discrete OT solutions with CFM was already proposed in Tong et al. (2023); extensions to GW and unbalanced settings involve only minor changes (R2). Little of the method is specific to OT—any measure-mapping task could use the same regression framework (R4). Theoretical results are disconnected from the practical algorithm (R4).
- **Limited scope of experiments:** No application to image domains; the method likely struggles there due to the need for large batch sizes to approximate continuous distributions (R1, R4). Computational scaling with dimensionality and sample size is not investigated (R1). The appendix contains important experiments but may not receive thorough review (R2).
- **Reproducibility issues:** Code is not provided, and the biological protocol appears complex, hindering reproducibility (R1, R2).
- **Minor issues:** Typo on page 30: "choose alpha = 0.7m=, but ..." (R1). Incorrect links to Figures 5.1 and 5.2 in Sections 5.1 and 5.2 (R2). Notation GW-LR in Figure 5 not introduced in main text (R2). Notation $\hat \pi$ under Equation (7) is undefined (R4).
