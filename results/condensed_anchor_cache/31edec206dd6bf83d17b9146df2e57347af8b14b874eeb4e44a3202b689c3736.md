## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Video Q-Former, a connection module for multimodal large language models that uses an Attentive Pooling Module and a spatio-temporal querying transformer (spatial, temporal, summary queries) to extract video features for LLM input. It is evaluated on zero-shot VideoQA, video captioning, and video summarization. Two reviewers (score 5) found the approach straightforward, efficient, and well-evaluated, while two (score 3) considered it lacking in novelty, missing comparisons with recent work, and raising concerns about training data fairness and information loss from pooling.

### Strengths
*   The idea of using separate spatial, temporal, and summary queries for spatio-temporal representation is straightforward and makes sense. The computation cost is acceptable (Table 1) and not higher than BLIP-2, while achieving better performance.
*   The Attentive Pooling Module decouples spatiotemporal features with cross-attention layers, differing from the average pooling used in Video-ChatGPT.
*   Three expert FFNs (SP-FFN, T-FFN, SM-FFN) are introduced into the Q-Former to handle spatial, temporal, and summarization queries respectively.
*   Ablation studies are conducted to verify each component of Video Q-Former (e.g., Tables 6, 8).
*   The method is evaluated on multiple tasks: video captioning (MSRVTT, MSVD), zero-shot question answering, and video summarization.
*   The writing is clear and the illustration is good. The method is straightforward and easy to understand.

### Weaknesses
*   **Missing comparisons with recent works:** The paper claims state-of-the-art performance but omits many recent video MLLMs, including VideoChat2, LLaVA-Video, Chat-Unity, LLaVA-OneVision, MiniCPMv, and PLLaVA. The related work section also lacks these recent methods. Two reviewers (rating 3) note this significantly undermines the novelty and fairness of the evaluation.
*   **Comparison fairness with baselines:** It is not clarified whether Video Q-Former was trained on the same video data as the main comparison targets (Video-LLaMA, Video-ChatGPT). The primary baselines use much less video data to train their connectors, raising doubt about whether performance gains come from the architecture or from additional training data. The versions of the LLMs used should be consistent and specified.
*   **Limited novelty and re-use of existing ideas:** The approach of decomposing video into spatial and temporal representations for efficient modeling is a general idea in video understanding (e.g., TimeSFormer, Uniformer, Dual-AI) – proper credit and literature review are missing. Specialized efficient video Q-Formers have already been explored in UMT and VideoChat2; the paper should clarify differences. The Attentive Pooling Module has been widely used in prior work (e.g., Temporal-attentive Covariance Pooling Networks, NeurIPS 2021). Some reviewers (rating 3) view Video Q-Former as simply adding a Q-Former module to Video-ChatGPT with three query types, lacking sufficient innovation and technical depth.
*   **Insufficient ablation and marginal improvements:** Key ablations are missing, particularly the effect of each query type (spatial, temporal, summary) – the core contribution. The paper should ablate using only spatial queries, only temporal queries, etc., and evaluate on QA benchmarks (not captioning), since captioning metrics like B@4 and CIDEr may not reflect LLM performance well. The reported improvements are limited: in Table 6, SP Q-Former improves CIDEr by only 0.37; in Table 8, the full Video Q-Former improves over “Spatial only” by only 0.07 CIDEr.
*   **Information loss from attentive pooling:** The computational efficiency gain comes from applying attentive pooling to compress video tokens, whereas Video-LLaMA computes over all tokens. This may discard detailed information. Furthermore, with 8–16 frames and 32 tokens per frame (following BLIP-2), the input to the LLM is only 256–512 tokens – well within the capacity of most LLMs (e.g., LLaVA-1.5 uses 576 tokens per single image). Therefore, the need for further compression is questionable.
*   **Missing technical and implementation details:**
    *   Training data specifics: which datasets, how many videos, and sampling strategy are not provided.
    *   The design of the expert vision transformers used in ST Q-Former is not described; implementation details needed for reproduction.
    *   Confusion about the attentive pooling module: the “temporal representation” is derived from spatial queries attending to in-frame features – why is this called temporal representation?
    *   Table 1 separates Q-Former and BLIP-2 as two columns, but Q-Former is part of BLIP-2; the basis for comparison and the FLOPs formula for each should be clarified.
    *   It is unclear whether the processing of summary queries is identical to spatial and temporal queries (including cross-attention operations).
    *   Table 6 uses “SP QFormer” – clarify whether this includes all three query types and which features are used (as in Figure 3).
*   **Lack of motivation and visualization:** The paper does not adequately explain the motivation behind the Mixture-of-Experts design or the rationale for decoupling spatiotemporal features via attentive pooling. Visualizations (e.g., attention maps) of the Attentive Pooling Module would help demonstrate its effectiveness.
*   **Additional missing experiments:** It would be useful to show performance variance on an image benchmark after ST Q-Former tuning compared to a normal Q-Former. For video summarization, recent benchmarks like Shot2Story could be included (optional but recommended).
*   **Introduction overhead:** The introduction (lines 30–50) spends excessive space on general background only tangentially relevant to the main contribution (efficient video connection module for MLLMs). This could be streamlined.
