## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a method to improve instruction-following in language models by deriving activation steering vectors from the difference in activations between inputs with and without instructions. The method is applied to constraints like output format, length, and word inclusion/exclusion across four models (ranging from ~3b to ~9b parameters). Experiments test steering alone, steering combined with prompts, composition of multiple vectors, negation (e.g., reversing word inclusion to exclusion), and cross-model transferability from instruction-tuned to base models. Reviewers found the method interesting and experiments comprehensive, but raised concerns about task breadth, novelty, missing comparisons, uneven presentation, omitted negative results, and insufficient analysis of failure cases.

### Strengths
- Clear presentation and writing; the method is simple, effective, and well explained.
- Derivation of steering vectors from instruction modifiers is clever and novel as an extension beyond deriving vectors from binary states or ICL prompts (Reviewer 4). However, Reviewer 3 notes that activation steering itself is not novel and previous work on writing style/topic changing is related to response format and word mention – these two views reflect a minority contrast on novelty.
- Experiments cover multiple constraint types (format, length, word-specific) across several models, highlighting robustness and adaptability.
- Cross-model transferability experiments showing that steering vectors from instruction-tuned models improve base models are valuable and suggest a cost-effective way to get instruction-following improvements.
- Positive results on negation and composition of multiple steering vectors are interesting.
- The paper investigates nearly all natural applications with useful measurements, including quality degradation in the presence of steering.
- The steerability of LLMs is an important research area with real-world implications.

### Weaknesses
- Task breadth is limited: the three tasks (format, length, word inclusion/exclusion) focus on output structure rather than semantic understanding or complex reasoning. Format covers 13 sub-instructions but does not test nuanced interpretation or multi-step reasoning (Reviewer 1). The method may not be general and requires adjustment per instruction (Reviewer 3).
- Novelty is debated: Reviewer 3 states the method of activation steering itself is not new; previous works on writing style and topic changing are related. Reviewer 4 considers the derivation from instructions clever and novel, indicating a minority contrast. The authors should clarify exactly which parts are novel (e.g., combining steering vectors) (Reviewer 3).
- Motivation is unclear. Why use this method when explicit instruction in words seems simpler and works better? The proposed method can lead to nonsensical responses (Reviewer 3).
- Missing negative/unpromising results: word exclusion is described as unpromising due to embedding signal, but no measurements of failure are shown (Reviewer 4). Specific numeric length steering vectors should be computed and compared; if they do not work, that should be quantified (Reviewer 4). Appendix tables 8 and 9 omit many configurations with the explanation “steering was unnecessary as the models typically followed the instruction”; negative or unhelpful results should be included (Reviewer 4).
- Uneven presentation suggesting curation: Figure 3 does not provide breakouts by task for all four cases (bare prompt, prompt+modifier, prompt+steer, prompt+modifier+steer) (Reviewer 4). Quality degradation is measured differently across tasks; a uniform table or computation for all comparable tasks should be provided (Reviewer 4). Quality evaluation is only in one main experiment; results in the appendix should be discussed in the main text (Reviewer 3).
- No limitation or discussion section (Reviewer 1).
- Very low sample size: the format task has only 12 samples; Reviewer 3 asks if that is correct.
- Dev set uses the same base queries as the evaluation, making generalization to unseen queries unclear (Reviewer 3).
- Cross-model transferability analysis needs more quantitative depth: test degradation when applying steering vectors from instruction-tuned models to base models of different architectures or parameter sizes (e.g., from 1B to 100M, or different attention mechanisms) (Reviewer 2).
- Quality degradation is noted but not mitigated; a detailed analysis of the types of errors introduced (e.g., incoherence, limited diversity) is needed (Reviewer 2).
- Full grid of performance measurements for every modifier task (both format and language) in all four cases across models should be included (Reviewer 4). Quality degradation measurements should be shared uniformly over all modifiers (Reviewer 4).
- No projection plot of raw steering vectors to see cluster separation; it would be informative to see how related vectors (e.g., “answer of length n”) are arranged in representation space (Reviewer 4).
- Scaling factor c is not fully justified; it may mediate a tradeoff between efficacy and quality degradation. A plot of this tradeoff over a sweep of c would be informative (Reviewer 4).
- In the length experiment, using fixed c=20 raises the question whether steering makes responses generally shorter or adheres to the specific length constraint; clarification is needed (Reviewer 3).
- Plots are too small and use similar colors (dark blue vs. light blue) (Reviewer 3).
- Missing related work: a paper on “capitalization” (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.06668) should be cited (Reviewer 3).
- Figure 4 is confusing: how the delta is computed should be clarified; the quality score metric appears almost opposite to accuracy results (Reviewer 1).
- Grid search details missing (line 159): how examples are chosen, number of examples, granularity of the grid, and whether it is done for every layer (Reviewer 1).
- Scalability for diverse instructions: the need for unique vectors per instruction could impact scalability; clarification on handling highly variable instructions would add practical insights (Reviewer 2).
- How base models follow instructions (e.g., few-shot prompting?) should be clarified (Reviewer 3).
