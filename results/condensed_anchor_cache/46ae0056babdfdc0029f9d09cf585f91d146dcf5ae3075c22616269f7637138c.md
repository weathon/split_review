## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents an empirical study of text-to-audio (TTA) diffusion/flow-matching models, introducing AF-Synthetic (a 1.35M synthetic caption dataset filtered by CLAP score) and the ETTA model built on a Latent Diffusion Model with a Diffusion Transformer backbone. The authors conduct extensive ablations on data, architecture, training objectives, and sampling strategies. Reviewer scores are 5, 5, 6, 8, indicating strong disagreement: one reviewer (score 8) finds the work state-of-the-art and impactful, while the other three (scores 5, 5, 6) consider novelty limited, evaluation insufficient, and improvements marginal.

### Strengths
- **Dataset contribution**: AF-Synthetic is a million-scale, high-quality audio-caption dataset. Its filtering pipeline (using CLAP score to select the best of ten generated captions per sample) demonstrably improves TTA performance over prior open-source datasets.
- **Comprehensive experimental evaluation**: The paper systematically compares architectural choices (DiT vs. U-Net), training objectives (OT-CFM), sampling methods, and hyperparameters, providing a practical guide for the field.
- **Open-source commitment**: The authors will release source code, enabling reproducibility and community follow-up.
- **Presentation clarity**: Key conclusions are highlighted in purple-bordered boxes, making the paper easy to navigate.
- **Subjective evaluation for the final model**: Table 9 includes subjective ratings showing ETTA’s advantage for complex/imaginative captions, though subjective tests are absent for ablation studies.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient isolation of dataset vs. architecture contributions**: Multiple reviewers note that experiments do not control for the effect of AF-Synthetic (e.g., training baseline models like Tango or AudioLDM on AF-Synthetic, or training ETTA on AudioCaps only). Without these, it is unclear whether performance gains come from the dataset or the model design. Training ETTA solely on AudioCaps yields an FAD of 3.00, far below current state-of-the-art—a result that demands explanation.
- **Lack of subjective evaluations for ablations**: Only Table 9 uses human ratings; all other design choices are evaluated solely via objective metrics. Given known limitations of these metrics, the real perceptual impact of the proposed techniques remains uncertain.
- **Questionable objective metrics**: 
  - CLAP score used for dataset curation may introduce bias toward the metric, making the filtered dataset not representative of true audio-text distribution.
  - Older feature extractors (VGGish for FAD, PANNs/PaSST for KL) are used, while more advanced alternatives exist, potentially underestimating model quality.
  - The three Frechet Distance variants are not clearly explained, making it hard to interpret what each captures about quality or alignment.
- **Inconsistent improvements across metrics**: Many proposed changes (e.g., OT-CFM, t-sampling) improve some metrics while worsening others (e.g., FAD improves but FD_P declines), raising doubts about robustness and whether changes are genuinely beneficial.
- **Limited novelty and surprising findings**: The paper does not propose novel techniques; the core components (DiT with AdaLN, T5 text encoder, flow matching) are adopted from prior work. Key conclusions (e.g., larger models improve performance) are unsurprising, and the much larger AF-Synthetic yields results on AudioCaps/MusicCaps similar to the smaller AF-AudioSet (Tables 6–7).
- **Missing baseline comparisons**: Important baselines (Make-an-Audio, AudioLDM) are absent from Tables 2 and 3, making it difficult to benchmark ETTA against prior open-source methods.
- **Minority positive contrast**: One reviewer (score 8) finds ETTA achieves state-of-the-art on AudioCaps and MusicCaps and praises the dataset and subjective evaluation; this view is more positive than the consensus.
- **Additional missing experiments and clarifications** (overlap with criticisms):
  - Test a Min-SNR Weighting Strategy for training (Reviewer 2).
  - Experiment with a CFG schedule that removes guidance for the first 40% of steps then adds it for the remaining 60% (Reviewer 2).
  - Analyze why mode-collapsed models still yield good FAD/FD scores despite different backbones (Reviewer 3).
  - Explain the loss divergence issue noted on line 510, which seems inconsistent with normal evaluation metrics (Reviewer 1).
  - Clarify the differences between AF-AudioSet and AF-Synthetic beyond using the highest-CLAP caption per sample, and whether AF-AudioSet and Sound-VECaps overlap after CLAP filtering (Reviewer 4).
  - Distinguish the AdaLN implementation in ETTA-DiT from the original DiT paper (Reviewer 4).
  - Provide details on zero-initializing the final projection layer of DiT (Reviewer 1).
