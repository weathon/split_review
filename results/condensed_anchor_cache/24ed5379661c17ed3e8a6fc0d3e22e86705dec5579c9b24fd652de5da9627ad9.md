## Merged Review

### Summary

This work explores the geometry of attention maps in transformers by viewing them as weighted graphs and applying Ollivier-Ricci curvature (ORC). The authors claim a theoretical link between ORC and gradient descent convergence rate, and observe that more negative ORC correlates with faster convergence while more positive ORC correlates with greater robustness. They propose a variance-adjusting regularization method to manipulate attention curvature and report experiments on vision and language tasks. Reviewers note the novelty of the geometric approach and the breadth of empirical studies, but raise major concerns about the validity of the theoretical results due to unrealistic assumptions, flawed proofs, and a lack of rigorous justification.

### Strengths

* The idea of using Ollivier-Ricci curvature to study transformer training dynamics and robustness is innovative and timely (Reviewers 1,2,3,4,5). One reviewer (4) particularly highlights the originality and the direct link established between curvature and gradient descent convergence.
* Empirical investigations are extensive, covering multiple types of perturbations (adversarial, common corruptions, distribution shift), data augmentation, and generalization robustness (Reviewers 1,2,5).
* The paper is generally well-written, with a clear flow, precise statements, and illustrative figures (Reviewers 1,5).
* Theoretical results are discussed alongside substantial empirical justification (Reviewer 1).
* The proposed variance-adjusting regularization method is sound and illuminates the trade-off between performance and robustness (Reviewer 4).
* The overparametrized perspective connects well with recent optimization theory, and analyzing training dynamics from this angle is promising (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

* **Unrealistic and invalidating theoretical assumptions:** (a) Attention matrix A is assumed symmetric (Assumption A0), ignoring asymmetry from the value projection (AV) in practice (Reviewer 1). (b) A is assumed invertible (Assumption A1), but attention matrices can be rank-deficient (Reviewer 1). (c) A is assumed independent of input X, whereas in transformers A depends on X through Q and K; this dependence invalidates the proofs (Reviewer 1). (d) The assumption of identical attention matrices across layers is unjustified and unrealistic; even with shared blocks, A depends on each layer's input, breaking the proof of Lemma 4.2 (Reviewers 3,5). (e) These restrictions reduce the model to a linear transformation, a severe oversimplification (Reviewer 3). (f) The claim that relaxing assumptions is straightforward is unsupported; e.g., Eq. (6) immediately fails without constant A (Reviewer 3). (g) The assumptions are justified by citing Donovan et al. 2024, a paper rejected at ICLR 2024 precisely for these limitations; this undermines novelty and validity (Reviewer 3). (h) Several assumptions are relegated to the appendix, despite the paper's theoretical claims (Reviewer 3).

* **Fundamental errors in the main theoretical proof (Lemma 4.2):** (a) For Laplacian L = I - A, the largest eigenvalue of L is 1 - smallest eigenvalue of A, not 1 - largest eigenvalue as stated (line 774); this invalidates the bound from Bauer et al. 2011 that follows (Reviewer 3). (b) Differentiating an inequality without justification is mathematically invalid (Reviewer 3). (c) A minor typo: "2L-3" should be "2L-2" (Reviewer 3). (d) The proof assumes constant A across layers, but Eqs. (5) and (9) require layer-dependent A, causing the proof to collapse (Reviewer 5). (e) Theorem 4.1 mis-cites Liu et al. 2022, which holds only for mean squared error loss, yet experiments use cross-entropy; extension to general losses is non-trivial and missing discussion (Reviewer 3).

* **Lack of novelty in curvature–robustness connection:** The relationship between curvature and robustness is already established in Demetrius & Manke (2005) and Pouryahya et al. (2017). The paper does not sufficiently differentiate its findings from these prior works (Reviewer 1).

* **Unclear choice of Ollivier-Ricci curvature over more fundamental graph notions:** The paper does not justify why ORC is specifically beneficial compared to (weighted) graph cuts or the Laplacian spectral gap, which directly control graph sparsity and convergence. Extensive prior work on sparse and low-rank attention is not mentioned (Reviewer 5). Similarly, the advantages over other interpretability tools like CKA are not explained (Reviewer 2).

* **Proposed regularization method is weakly motivated and under-justified:** (a) Using SoftMax temperature to modulate attention variance is a more natural alternative that provides layer-specific, continuous, and learnable adjustments—with three clear benefits—yet no comparison is provided (Reviewer 5). (b) The link between curvature and weight variance is unclear despite the appendix (Reviewer 3). (c) Computational cost and hyperparameter sensitivity of the proposed method are not analyzed (Reviewer 2). (d) Comparison to other attention map distribution regularizations is missing (Reviewer 4).

* **Limited and inconclusive experimental evaluation:** (a) Vision experiments use only small datasets (CIFAR10, MNIST, Fashion-MNIST); larger, more realistic benchmarks are needed (Reviewer 1). (b) Table 1 is not conclusive: training loss should be used instead of training accuracy to measure convergence, and test accuracy (not train–test difference) to measure generalizability (Reviewer 5). (c) Hyperparameters for the regularization terms are not specified (Reviewer 5). (d) Figures 3–4–6 lack error bars or averaging over multiple runs, reducing statistical rigor (Reviewer 3). (e) Figure 3 is difficult to interpret; axes and colored lines need clear explanation in the main text (Reviewer 2). (f) Empirical comparisons to baseline methods are missing (Reviewer 1).

* **Ambiguous scope and overly self-promotional writing:** The paper straddles theory and experiment without clear focus: too much space is devoted to technical background summarizing existing results, while key algorithmic details are lacking (Reviewer 2). The related work section in the appendix ends each paragraph with "To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to…", which is self-promotional and interferes with objective evaluation (Reviewer 3).

* **Minor issues:** (a) Line 176: "positive-learning" should be "positive leaning" (Reviewer 5). (b) Line 716: "positively" should be "negatively" (Reviewer 5). (c) Citation formatting: "Laplacian Bauer et al. (2011)" missing parentheses (Reviewer 2).
