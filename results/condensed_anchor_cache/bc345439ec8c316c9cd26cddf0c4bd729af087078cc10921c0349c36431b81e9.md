## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Vision Search Assistant, a framework that integrates VLMs with web agents to enable handling of unfamiliar visual content via web-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation. It comprises three steps: Visual Content Formulation (extracting object-level descriptions), Web Knowledge Search via a Chain of Search algorithm (iterative web search and progressive summarization), and Collaborative Generation (integrating visual and textual information). Experiments on the LLaVA-Wild closed-set benchmark and human-evaluated open-set QA show performance improvements over baselines.

### Strengths

- Tackles an important practical problem: enabling VLMs to handle unseen images and novel concepts through collaboration with web agents. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- Identifies three critical research questions in visual search: what to search, how to search, and what to conclude. (Reviewers 2,4)
- Introduces the Chain of Search algorithm for progressive summarization of web knowledge, enhancing the system’s ability to handle novel and unseen data. (Reviewer 2)
- Conducts ablation studies that attempt to validate each component (e.g., what to search, how to search, by what to conclude). (Reviewers 1,2)
- Achieves good performance on both closed-set and open-set benchmarks. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses

- **Limited method novelty**: Several reviewers view the framework as a straightforward combination of captioning, searching, and RAG (A+B+C) without clearly highlighted new contributions. (Reviewer 1) Others praise the three-part framing but note the approach is essentially a typical application of LLMs. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing implementation details**: The specific search engine used is not mentioned. (Reviewer 1) For closed-set evaluation, whether the Vision Search Assistant is allowed to access the internet and whether its search scope differs from comparison models is unclear. (Reviewer 2)
- **Insufficient definition of open-set evaluation metrics**: The terms “factuality”, “relevance”, and “supportiveness” are not explained in sufficient detail, and no prior work citations are given for these criteria. (Reviewers 1,4) One reviewer requests detailed explanations and asks if human evaluation results are statistically significant and consistent. (Reviewer 4)
- **Ablation study lacks quantitative results**: The ablation experiments are described only qualitatively; providing numerical results (e.g., in tabular form) would enhance clarity. (Reviewer 1)
- **Unclear score type**: The specific type of score reported in experiments is not clarified, making interpretation difficult. (Reviewer 1)
- **Careless experimental section**: Figure 5 is provided but not discussed in the main text. (Reviewer 2)
- **Incomplete open-set dataset description**: The open-set evaluation dataset is not described in sufficient detail, and how fairness is ensured during subjective evaluation is not explained. (Reviewers 2,3)
- **Unexplained strong result against GPT-4 web**: Figure 6 shows the proposed method significantly outperforms GPT-4 web, yet no analysis or justification for this improvement is given in the method section. (Reviewer 2)
- **Insufficient comparisons**: The paper lacks comparisons with more open-world/retrieval-augmented methods, such as New Bing (Copilot), which is known as an excellent RAG system. (Reviewers 2,3) One reviewer states this omission is unacceptable for research. (Reviewer 3)
- **No efficiency analysis**: No information is provided on computational efficiency, average latency, number of LMM calls, or token consumption, especially for the iterative Chain of Search algorithm. (Reviewers 2,4)
- **Unclear formulation extraction**: The mechanism by which \(F_{VLM}\) outputs “correlated formulations” from an image with two captions is not explained; it is unclear whether this function is specifically trained for this task. (Reviewer 3)
- **No analysis of open-set detector impact**: How open-set detectors affect overall performance is not examined. (Reviewer 3)
- **Lack of generalization experiments**: Experiments are only conducted on LLaVA 1.6; the method appears training-free, so validation across different VLLMs is essential. (Reviewer 3)
- **Overclaim on Chain of Search**: The claim that the Chain of Search algorithm answers “how to search” is not supported by quantitative results. Additionally, the phrasing “By what to conclude” should be “How to conclude” as it focuses on procedure, not content selection. (Reviewer 3)
- **Reliance on qualitative results**: The paper uses many qualitative examples to prove efficacy, which is not convincing. When VLMs have never seen the objects, the extracted captions may be poor and unable to retrieve relevant web results. (Reviewer 3)
- **Political examples**: Using examples related to politics is inadvisable; many VLLMs avoid discussing such topics, leading to unfair implications about model performance. (Reviewer 3)
- **Unexplained design choice: text descriptions instead of images**: The motivation for transforming visual regions into text descriptions for search (rather than using images directly) is not adequately justified. Two reviewers note that critical information may be lost in the descriptive process and that many models struggle with entity recognition, potentially affecting search accuracy. (Reviewers 2,4) One reviewer asks how web knowledge search performance is measured and what measures are in place to handle inaccuracies in the search results. (Reviewer 2)
- **Baseline ambiguity in Table 1**: It is unclear whether “LLava-1.6-7B (Baseline)” is zero-shot; the decline in Conversation performance when naive Google Image Search is introduced requires explanation. (Reviewer 4)
- **Insufficient reproducibility**: Sampling parameters (temperature, top-p) and prompts used (e.g., Eq. 4, Eq. 7) are not provided. (Reviewer 4)
- **Lack of detailed qualitative analysis**: No case study shows the descriptions of each region, the results of each search chain step, and the final response to illustrate the benefits of the proposed method. (Reviewer 4)
- **Human evaluation criteria undefined**: The definitions of factuality, relevance, and supportiveness for the human evaluation in Fig. 6 are not stated; if these definitions follow prior work, citations should be given; otherwise detailed explanations are needed. (Reviewer 4)
