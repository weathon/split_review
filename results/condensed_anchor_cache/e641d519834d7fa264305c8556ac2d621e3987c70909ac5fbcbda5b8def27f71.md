## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper reexamines the role of "number-detector" units in CNNs (CORnet-Z and CORnet-S) for numerosity discrimination. Using pruning (Tarigopula et al., 2023) guided by a human behavioral number RDM, the authors identify units that best align with population-level numerosity representations. They find that these pruned units have minimal overlap with traditional ANOVA-identified number-detector units, challenging previous claims about the importance of specialized number-detector units. The paper also shows that pruned units better align with semantic number embeddings (GloVe).

### Strengths

- The paper addresses a concrete question about number representation in CNNs and challenges prior findings, offering a population-level perspective (reviewer 3, score 5, finds this contrast significant, while reviewers 1 and 2, both score 3, are less convinced of the novelty).
- Adapts a pruning method (Tarigopula et al., 2023) to use human behavioral data (number RDM) to identify behaviorally relevant units, providing an alternative to ANOVA-based unit-level analysis.
- Demonstrates that pruned units align better with human behavioral RDMs and GloVe embeddings than ANOVA-identified number-detector units, suggesting population-level coding may be more important.
- The paper is generally well-written and easy to follow (reviewers 1 and 3).
- Results could help reconcile conflicting evidence from prior methods (reviewer 1).
- The use of human behavioral data to guide pruning makes the work relevant to representation learning and human-aligned AI (reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

- **Circularity in Figure 2**: Pruning guided by the number RDM is expected to yield the best correlation scores, making the result tautological (reviewer 1).
- **Lack of robustness**: Tables 1 and 2 lack confidence intervals; only one network initialization is used, so the stability of patterns across seeds is unclear (reviewers 1 and 2). Overlap scores for ANOVA units (often just 1 unit) need a null model (e.g., random selection with same percentage) for meaningful interpretation (reviewer 2).
- **Findings may be unsurprising**: The network could develop both population coding and detector units; ablating units from each method and testing impairment on numerosity detection would be a stronger test (reviewer 1).
- **Limited impact and scope**: Results address only numerosity in a single model family (CORnet); broader evidence across model classes (AlexNet, ViTs, ResNets) and tasks is needed, especially because CORnet does not fully replicate ventral visual cortex (reviewers 1 and 3; reviewer 3 emphasizes this limitation).
- **Potential artifacts from pruning**: Removing non-numerosity units could increase RDM correlation akin to regularization; the paper does not address whether pruned units are truly numerosity-coding or merely removing noise (reviewer 3).
- **GloVe comparison issues**: Visual and textual representations differ fundamentally (Erk, 2016); a control experiment randomizing written number forms is needed (reviewer 2). The comparison method is unclear: GloVe has 4 conditions (written number forms), CORnet has 32 (4 numerosities × 4 areas × 2 fields). Which subset was used? How does varying area/field affect the comparison? The GloVe RDM is not shown (reviewer 2).
- **Number RDM origin and rationale**: No reference or description is provided; the construction of the behavioral RDM is unclear. Adding labels and a citation to Figure 1 is necessary (reviewer 2). Furthermore, using a psychophysics RDM to prune early visual layers (V1, V2, V4, IT) is questionable—it would be more expected for a readout layer matched to behavioral benchmarks (reviewer 2).
- **Missing performance metrics**: Classification performance after pruning is not reported; pruning typically aims to maintain accuracy, but this is not evaluated (reviewer 2).
- **Missing image preprocessing details**: Original images were 500×500, but CORnet expects 224×224; operations performed (resizing/cropping) and their effect on average item area and total field area conditions are unspecified (reviewer 2).
- **Missing pruning references**: Introduction lacks historical and review references (Janowsky 1989, Mozer & Smolensky 1989, Karnin 1990, Blalock et al. 2020) (reviewer 2).
- **Writing and presentation issues**: Paper appears hastily written, using only 6 of 9–10 pages, with small figures (reviewer 2). Typo at line 214: “ANOVA results in significantly more units in CORnet-S …” (reviewer 3).
- **Unaddressed questions overlapping with criticisms**:
    - Are number-detector neurons sensitive to specific numbers or do they show graded selectivity? Would relaxing the criteria for number detection change results? (reviewer 1)
    - What is considered a significant RDM drop (line 167)? (reviewer 1)
    - Is the large number of retained units in untrained networks concerning? (reviewer 1)
    - Where is the human behavioral data from? How do we know humans are invariant to total field and average item area? (reviewer 1)
    - When sequentially removing units (lines 165–6), are significance scores corrected for multiple comparisons? (reviewer 3)
