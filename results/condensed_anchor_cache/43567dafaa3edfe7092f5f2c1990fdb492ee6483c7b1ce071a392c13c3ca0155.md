## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes R-Sparse, a training-free activation sparsity method for LLMs that replaces linear layers with a rank-aware sparse inference approach. It leverages input channel sparsity and singular value decomposition to avoid predicting active channels. Experiments on Llama-2/3 and Mistral over ten tasks show 50% model-level sparsity with comparable performance and up to 43% end-to-end speedup using custom kernels. The method is compatible with weight quantization and applicable to both attention and MLP modules.

### Strengths

- Training-free: does not require pre-training or continual training, making implementation simpler.
- Achieves high sparsity (~50%) without significant performance loss, leading to practical speedups.
- Compatible with weight quantization for further efficiency gains.
- Applicable to different LLM families (Llama-2/3, Mistral) and a variety of tasks.
- Applies to both attention and MLP modules, unlike some prior work.
- Designs different sparsity ratios per layer to improve overall performance.
- Method is easy to follow (noted by Reviewers 2 and 4; Reviewer 3 raises clarity concerns – see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses

- **Table 1 interpretation and claim refinement**: R‑Sparse 40% is compared with CATS 22% and GRIFFIN 33%, but it does not consistently outperform CATS (e.g., PIQA 78.24 vs. 79.00). The authors should refine claims to avoid being misleading. Moreover, on PIQA performance sometimes increases at higher sparsity (79.49 → 79.92 for R‑Sparse 40% vs. 50%); the authors should provide insights into this phenomenon.
- **Missing sensitivity analysis of hyperparameters** (e.g., the sparse‑rank α in Algorithm 1, which appears fixed; how is it optimized?). A thorough investigation of hyperparameter impact is needed.
- **Missing comparison with [1]**: the authors discuss this work in related work but do not include it in experiments. Why?
- **Missing complexity and running‑time analysis** of the evolutionary search algorithm used to find optimal sparse component ratios. The paper should include the overhead and running time of the search itself.
- **Generality of empirical analysis**: Figures 1 and 3 are based only on the C4 dataset and 16 randomly sampled training examples. The authors should demonstrate that observed sparsity patterns hold on other datasets (especially very different ones) and across varying sample sizes. Robustness under data distribution shift is unclear.
- **Need deeper analysis of layer‑wise importance differences**: Figure 3 shows clear variation (e.g., self_attn.k_proj vs. self_attn.up_proj). The underlying reasons (architectural role, functional differences) should be explored.
- **Missing mapping of sparsity ratio to actual inference acceleration**: The paper should explicitly state the practical speedup corresponding to a given sparsity ratio, accounting for memory access patterns and computational overhead.
- **Counterintuitive generation‑speed trend in Figure 6**: Under “Dense”, when generation length increases from 1024 to 2048, speed slows down, but from 128→256→512 speed accelerates. This needs a clear explanation (prefilling, weight computation, KV‑cache access factors).
- **Clarity issues (noted by multiple reviewers)**:
    - Figure 1 is difficult to interpret until reading Section 3.3.
    - Section 3.2: the term “bias” is ambiguous—does it include non‑sparse components greater than 0? Lines 183‑185 define “sparse” when \(H_k \ge T_0\), which conflicts with intuition.
    - The most confusing term is “sparse components”; the authors should clearly state (as done in the rebuttal) that “positive = sparse = not pruned” and “negative = non‑sparse = to be pruned”.
    - Sparsity is defined as the ratio of \(x < T_0\), but in the method these components are replaced with the nearest discrete value, not zero; is this correctly called “sparsity”?
    - The definition of multi‑phase ReLU on line 170 appears inconsistent: as inputs become more negative, the output should approach zero, but the formula defines the opposite direction. An example with \(T_0=0, T_1=-1, T_2=-2\) would clarify.
- **Weak connection between motivation and method**: The first motivation (non‑sparse inputs as biases) is not explicitly linked to a specific design choice in R‑Sparse. The paper should explain which component is motivated by this observation.
- **Incremental contributions**: R‑Spare directly applies existing techniques (CATS for sparsity, SVD for weight decomposition, genetic algorithm for hyper‑parameters). The authors should more clearly articulate novelty, especially any specific design in the SVD step.
- **Limited activation function generality**: Experiments only cover models using SiLU (Llama‑2/3, Mistral). Adding results on a GELU‑based model (e.g., Gemma) would support the claim that the method applies to non‑ReLU activations beyond SiLU.
- **Minor issues**: Switch the positions of `mlp.up_proj` and `mlp.gate_proj` of layer 0 in Figure 3 (suggested by Reviewer 3). The recommended reference (Alizadeh et al., “LLM in a flash”) may be relevant.

*Contrast in readability opinions*: Reviewer 2 finds the paper “very clearly written”, Reviewer 4 says it is “easy to understand”, while Reviewer 3 reports it is “difficult to read (clarity)” – the weaknesses above capture the specific points causing confusion.
