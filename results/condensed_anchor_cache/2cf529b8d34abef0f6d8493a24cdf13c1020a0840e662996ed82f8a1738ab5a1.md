## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper analyzes limitations of two-stage prompt-based Class-Incremental Learning (CIL): (1) errors can arise in the second stage even when the first-stage prediction is reliable; (2) forcing identical architectures in both stages restricts performance. To address these, the authors incorporate a self-supervised learning objective (self-distillation loss from DINO) to improve first-stage feature discriminability, a threshold‑filtering strategy that bypasses the second stage when the first-stage confidence is high, and the use of self‑supervised pre‑trained models as task‑identity providers. Experiments are conducted on both in‑distribution (ID) and out‑of‑distribution (OOD) CIL benchmarks, showing comparable ID results and substantial OOD gains (e.g., +6.34% on Split Aircrafts, +5.15% on Split Cars-196). The paper is generally well‑written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 3), but Reviewer 4 states that the writing is “very poor” and requires careful revision.

### Strengths
- The analysis of self‑supervised pre‑trained models for the first stage (task‑identity prediction) is insightful and reveals that self‑supervised models (e.g., DINOv2‑LVD142M) provide more reliable task‑id predictions in OOD scenarios, leading to substantial accuracy gains (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).  
- The proposed threshold‑filtering strategy selectively passes data to the second stage, preventing errors when the first‑stage prediction is already reliable and saving computational resources; this is a simple yet effective step toward real‑world application (Reviewers 2, 3).  
- The method demonstrates significant performance improvement in OOD scenarios (e.g., up to 6.34% and 5.15% on Split Aircrafts and Split Cars‑196), consistent with the observation that self‑supervised models excel in OOD settings (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).  
- The self‑distillation loss boosts first‑stage accuracy, which in turn improves final accuracy on OOD benchmarks (Reviewer 3).  
- The paper reveals that using diverse pre‑trained models (especially self‑supervised) for the first stage can improve accuracy; these observations can inspire different backbone choices for different domain scenarios (Reviewer 3).  
- “Exploring prompt‑based CIL in OOD scenarios is interesting” (Reviewer 1).  
- The method is simple and achieves a performance gain on several OOD CIL benchmarks (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparison with prompt‑generation methods:** Some prompt‑based CIL methods (e.g., [a] Generating Instance‑level Prompts for Rehearsal‑free Continual Learning, ICCV2023; [b] Open‑World Dynamic Prompt, ECCV2024) directly generate instance‑wise prompts and do not require a two‑stage “select+prediction” pipeline. These important works are not discussed or compared. A detailed analysis (performance, memory cost) on both ID and OOD benchmarks is needed (Reviewer 1).  
- **Limited uniqueness of the paper’s summary:** Prior works (e.g., [c] Continual Learning with Pre‑Trained Models: A Survey, IJCAI2024; [d] Revisiting CIL with Pre‑Trained Models, arxiv2023) have already summarised prompt‑based CIL methods, making the contribution of this paper limited. The key differences should be highlighted, and performance comparisons on ID and OOD scenarios should be provided to show the necessity of combining self‑supervised learning with prompt‑based CIL (Reviewer 1).  
- **No experiments on complex/challenging CIL benchmarks:** The paper does not evaluate on the four new challenging ID CIL benchmarks proposed in [d] (e.g., cross‑domain CIL benchmark VTAB) (Reviewer 1).  
- **The 10% error‑rate claim is unsupported:** The paper states “the second‑stage model may still misclassify … this situation occurs frequently in OOD scenarios, with an occurrence rate of around 10%.” No experimental evidence or theoretical analysis is provided to support this 10% figure (Reviewer 2).  
- **Performance decline on ID datasets is not explained:** The method shows performance drops on several ID datasets (e.g., –1.06% on CIFAR‑100, –0.61% on ImageNet‑R). The authors do not analyse why performance improves in OOD but declines in non‑OOD settings, and no ablation studies are conducted on ID scenarios to determine whether the self‑distillation loss or the threshold‑filtering strategy is harmful (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).  
- **The self‑supervised task‑identity provider depends on pre‑training data gap:** The effectiveness of using a self‑supervised pre‑trained model for task‑id prediction depends on the gap between pre‑training data and incremental tasks. If the gap is large, the model may not provide reliable task identities. A discussion or comparison across datasets with varying similarity to pre‑training data is missing (Reviewer 1).  
- **Observation 2 (“identical architecture not required”) has marginal significance:** It is obvious that better task‑identity predictions improve second‑stage predictions irrespective of architecture. A more meaningful insight would be “Self‑supervised models provide more reliable first‑stage task‑identity predictions than supervised models” (Reviewers 3, 4).  
- **Potential test‑information leakage in threshold determination:** Section 5.2 requires updating the Beta‑distribution parameters using *all* test samples, which could leak test information (Reviewer 4).  
- **Section 4 does not support the claim in Section 5.2:** The paper claims “even if the first‑stage model correctly predicts the class label, the second‑stage model may still misclassify.” Section 4 only reports task‑ID prediction results, not correct class‑label predictions. A correct task‑id does not imply a correct class label (Reviewer 4).  
- **Lack of theoretical proof for different architectures:** The paper shows experimental evidence that first‑ and second‑stage models can use different architectures and pre‑trained weights, but provides no theoretical justification (Reviewer 2).  
- **The threshold‑filtering strategy is a straightforward, common technique:** The Beta‑distribution choice is not clearly motivated, and the strategy does not provide novel ideas for filtering (Reviewer 3).  
- **Inconsistent and poorly defined notation:**  
  - In Section 5.1.2, parameters θₛ and θₜ are not clearly defined (whole model or only added params?). If added parameters, Equation 4 is problematic because the added component does not take input x.  
  - Symbols K and i in Equation 4 are not explained (number of classes or tasks?).  
  - θ is used for network parameters in Eq.4 and then for a distribution in Eq.5/6; τ is used for temperature in Eq.4 and for filtering threshold in Eq.9.  
  - Punctuation missing after all equations (Reviewer 4).  
- **Conflict results between tables:** The result for Sup‑21K on Split Aircrafts is 47.47 in Table 3, but 47.37 in Tables 5, 6, and 7 (Reviewers 3, 4).  
- **Self‑supervised training for the first stage does not bring novel insights:** It has been shown in prior works (e.g., [1] Prototype Augmentation and Self‑Supervision for Incremental Learning, CVPR2021; [2] Steering Prototypes with Prompt‑Tuning, WACV2024) that self‑supervised techniques learn generalizable features beneficial for continual learning. The self‑distillation loss is from DINO, so the contribution is limited (Reviewer 3).  
- **The first “empirical analysis” question (decoupled models) has marginal significance:** In prompt‑pool‑based CL, the two‑stage models are originally decoupled and not enforced to be identical. Thus this observation is not surprising (Reviewer 3).  
- **No ablation on ID scenarios:** Ablation studies are conducted only on OOD scenarios, leaving unclear whether both the self‑distillation loss and the threshold determination harm ID performance (Reviewer 4).  
- **Reviewer 2 requests deeper analysis:** Provide theoretical justification for why different architectures/weights work, and analyse why ID performance declines rather than simply listing results (Reviewer 2).  
- **Reviewer 1 questions the task‑id prediction module:** The reliability of self‑supervised pre‑trained models for providing task‑ids depends on the gap between pre‑training data and incremental tasks – a discussion is needed (Reviewer 1).  
- **Reviewer 3 questions the Beta‑distribution choice:** Why Beta distribution is adopted for threshold filtering is not clear (Reviewer 3).
