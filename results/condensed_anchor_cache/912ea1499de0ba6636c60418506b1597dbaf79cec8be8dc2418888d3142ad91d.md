## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Condition-Annealed Diffusion Sampler (CADS), a sampling strategy for pretrained conditional diffusion models that increases generation diversity, especially at high classifier-free guidance scales. CADS works by gradually annealing the conditioning signal: adding scheduled, monotonically decreasing Gaussian noise to the conditioning vector during inference. It requires no retraining, no additional model modifications, and is compatible with any pretrained model and sampling algorithm. Experiments on class-conditional ImageNet generation (achieving FID 1.70 at 256×256 and 2.31 at 512×512), pose-to-image, identity-conditioned face generation, and text-to-image generation show consistent diversity improvements with minimal quality loss. The method is simple, effective, theoretically motivated, and validated through extensive ablation studies.

### Strengths

- **Simple and effective**: The method is intuitive, easy to implement, and requires no fine-tuning of pretrained models. It outperforms the straightforward baseline of “adaptive CFG,” confirming its technical contribution.
- **Broad applicability**: CADS works with various diffusion models and samplers (DDPM, DDIM, etc.) and can be applied to multiple conditional generation tasks, making it of broad interest to the community.
- **Theoretical grounding**: The paper provides a theoretical explanation for why the annealing procedure improves diversity. A toy dataset experiment further supports the theory (Appendix). (Reviewer 1 recommends moving this explanation to the main paper.)
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Extensive validation on class-conditional, pose-to-image, identity-conditioned face, and text-to-image generation tasks. Ablation studies clearly show the influence of each hyperparameter.
- **Strong results**: Consistent improvements over baselines across all tasks, with the diversity improvements in pose-to-image generation being particularly impressive. New state-of-the-art FID scores on ImageNet are reported (1.70 at 256×256, 2.31 at 512×512).
- **Writing clarity**: The paper is well-organized and understandable. (Reviewer 1 suggests moving Appendix C. to the main article.)

### Weaknesses

- **Motivation could be stronger**: The rationale for the specific annealing schedule seems retroactively justified rather than derived from first principles. A derivation from the problem formulation would improve clarity.
- **Terminology concern**: The term “Adaptive” in “adaptive CFG” may be misleading because the adaptation is based solely on the timestep `t`, not on the input `z_t`. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing DDIM comparison**: All experiments use only the original DDPM sampler. Since DDIM is more widely used in practice, comparisons with DDIM are needed to fully demonstrate the method’s generality. (Reviewer 2)
- **Unclear hyperparameter selection**: The values of key hyperparameters (τ₁, ψ, s) appear to vary across experiments without a clear justification. For example, the sampling settings for ImageNet 256 differ between Table 1 and Table 6a (see Table 12 in Appendix G). It is unclear whether these values were chosen via systematic search or heuristic tuning, which hinders reproducibility and assessment of robustness. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing comparison to isolate CFG effects in Table 2**: A direct comparison between DiT‑XL/2 with CADS at `w_CFG=1.5` and DiT‑XL/2 at `w_CFG=2` (or a similarly matched baseline) is absent. This makes it hard to disentangle the benefits of CADS from the effects of simply using a different guidance scale. (Reviewer 3)
- **Lack of analysis at limited NFEs**: All experiments use large numbers of sampling steps (≥100 NFEs). It is unclear how CADS performs under a limited number of function evaluations, e.g., with fewer sampler steps. (Reviewer 1, request)
- **Missing ablation on noise injection location**: For pose-to-image generation, noise is injected into the pose image, while for other tasks it is injected into the conditioning embedding. No ablation study explores the effect of noise injection into internal layers of the conditioning extractor or whether the embedding‑injection scheme would work for other tasks. (Reviewer 1, request)
- **Failure modes at extreme guidance scales**: The paper does not investigate whether, at very high guidance weights, CADS simply delays the onset of artifacts/divergence or truly prevents them. A discussion of potential failure modes and the method’s limitations at extreme settings is missing. (Reviewer 3)
- **Clarification needed on diversity metric**: The definition of “diversity” is given in Section 3, but it is unclear whether “random seed” refers to the seed of the random number generator or a different initial latent `x_T`. The claim that samples from different seeds are more diverse should be clarified (e.g., comparing `h(f(x_T⁽⁰⁾), f(x_T⁽¹⁾))` vs. `h(g(x_T⁽⁰⁾), g(x_T⁽¹⁾))`). (Reviewer 2, request)
- **Justification needed for higher IS of DDPM in Fig. 5**: It is unclear why the baseline DDPM achieves higher Inception Score (IS) than CADS across most CFG scales in Fig. 5. (Reviewer 2, request)
