## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Distance-based Tree-Sliced Wasserstein (Db-TSW) distance that generalizes the splitting maps used in Tree-Sliced Wasserstein on Systems of Lines (TSW-SL) to be Euclidean-invariant and to incorporate distance to the tree lines. A new tree sampling process is introduced for efficient GPU implementation. Theoretical results include injectivity of the associated Radon transform and metric properties. Experiments on gradient flows, image style transfer, and generative models show competitive or improved performance against recent SW variants. Reviewers found the work well-motivated and the writing clear, but raised several concerns about missing analyses, ablation studies, and inconsistent baselines.

### Strengths
- Well-written and easy-to-follow (Reviewers 1,2).
- Generalization of splitting maps addresses a key limitation of TSW-SL (lack of Euclidean invariance and disregard for tree/domain information) (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- Introduction of a simplified, root-concurrent tree sampling process that enables GPU-friendly implementation and linear runtime (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- Comprehensive theoretical analysis: injectivity of the Radon transform under Euclidean-invariant splitting maps, and proof that Db-TSW is an E(d)-invariant metric (Reviewers 1,4).
- Extensive empirical evaluation on multiple tasks (gradient flows, image style transfer, diffusion-based generative models) demonstrating better or competitive performance compared to recent SW variants, particularly in high-dimensional settings (Reviewers 1,2,3,4). Reviewer 4 notes that Db-TSW outperforms all previous SW variants in generative modeling.

*Note: One reviewer (score 8) was particularly positive about the thoroughness of experiments and clarity; three reviewers (score 6) had more critical remarks.*

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of computational/memory complexity analysis.** The paper does not provide explicit runtime or memory scaling with number of samples, dimensionality, or number of lines. The point-line distance computation scales linearly with both, which could be a bottleneck for large or high-dimensional data (Reviewers 1,2).
- **Insufficient ablation study.** The contributions of the three main novelties (E(d)-invariance, distance-dependent splitting map, new tree sampling) are not disentangled. It is unclear which component drives performance gains over TSW-SL. The impact of key hyperparameters (number of lines \(k\), inverse temperature \(\delta\), root sampling strategy) is not systematically explored. Specifically, the choice \(\delta=10\) yields a very sparse splitting map and should be ablated (Reviewers 2,3,4).
- **Inconsistent baselines across experiments.** The set of comparison methods varies between tasks, making it difficult to holistically evaluate Db-TSW. Comparisons with non-linear slicing methods (GSW, ASW) are missing (Reviewers 2,3).
- **Missing statistical analysis.** Results from multiple runs (e.g., gradient flow) report only means without variance / standard deviation. There is no analysis of how Db-TSW behaves with increasing number of samples or projections (Reviewers 2,4).
- **Gradient flow experiment confounding.** Different ground costs are used (\(L_2\) vs. tree metric) across methods, making it hard to attribute differences to the proposed distance. The step size used for each variant is not reported; using the same step size may be unfair due to different scale/smoothness properties (Reviewers 3,4).
- **Flexibility limitations of root-concurrent tree structure.** The root-concurrent design may struggle to capture complex spatial arrangements (e.g., non-convex shapes, spirals). The paper should discuss such failure cases and whether the new sampling method is justified solely by computational efficiency or also improves performance (Reviewers 1,3).
- **Lack of sample/projection complexity and error bounds.** The paper does not discuss convergence rates or error bounds for the Monte Carlo approximation, which are important for understanding theoretical guarantees (Reviewer 2, implied by Reviewer 3’s question on high-dimensional performance).
- **Limited discussion of splitting map alternatives.** Only softmax is used as an E(d)-invariant splitting map; other possibilities and their trade-offs are not discussed (Reviewer 2).
- **Notation issues and missing references.** Equation (2) uses \(Rf_\mu\) which is not a probability; equation (8) applies \(R_{\mathcal{L}}^\alpha\) to measures, but it was defined only for \(f\in L^2\). The orthogonal sampling idea in Section 5.2 has parallels in prior SW works (Rowland et al., Dai & Seljak) that are not cited (Reviewer 4).
- **Typos.** Figure 2 legend has repeated sentences; line 371: “\(\mathcal{L}\) is a tree system consists”; line 476: “Db-TSW and \(\mathrm{Db-TSW}^\top\) is” (Reviewer 4).
- **Open questions left unaddressed.** (i) Is the transform injectivity or stability affected when tree lines are closely aligned or distances are nearly uniform across lines? (Reviewer 1). (ii) Can components of the framework be made learnable? How can prior knowledge guide optimal tree design? (Reviewer 2). (iii) Is sampling trees near the means necessary to avoid numerical instability for large \(\delta\)? (Reviewer 3). (iv) Do the reported wall-clock times reflect the parallel implementation? (Reviewer 3).
