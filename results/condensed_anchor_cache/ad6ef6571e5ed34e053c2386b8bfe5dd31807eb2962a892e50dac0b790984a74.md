## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces SlimKV, a KV cache pruning method that preserves large activations in both Key and Value caches, specifically designed to be compatible with Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) architectures. By computing importance scores S1 (sum of attention weights across heads and queries within an observation window) and S2 (max of absolute values in the Value cache per token) and combining them multiplicatively, SlimKV retains tokens with top importance scores. Experiments on models like Llama-2-7B and Mistral-7B on multiple benchmarks claim comparable accuracy while using significantly less KV cache (down to 1-2% of total tokens in some cases, or 1/10 of SOTA methods’ cache). One reviewer (score 5) was notably more positive, finding the idea clear and the method potentially very useful, while the other three (scores 3) expressed significant concerns about theoretical justification, evaluation rigor, and practical overhead.

### Strengths
- Targets an important practical problem: reducing KV cache requirements for LLMs with long contexts.
- Addresses GQA compatibility, which is relevant for modern models like Llama-3 and Mistral.
- Highlights that Value cache activations matter, an aspect neglected in prior work.
- Shows that SlimKV can achieve accuracy close to existing methods (e.g., SlimKV vs. SnapKV: 29.78 vs. 29.22 at KV size=256, and 31.52 vs. 31.42 at KV size=2048 on average) while using fewer cache tokens.
- Reduces KV cache to 1-2% of total tokens on some benchmarks while maintaining reasonable accuracy.
- Experiments cover 4 models and 6+ datasets, demonstrating broad applicability.
- One reviewer (score 5) considered the idea clear and the method potentially very useful.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient theoretical justification**: The paper claims large activations in the Value cache lead to more substantial output contributions (Section 2.2) but provides no mathematical proof or rigorous empirical study (e.g., variance of V, contribution of tokens with large S2). Reviewers request: an ablation comparing performance with and without preserving large V activations, and evidence that large V activations are crucial.
- **Methodological ambiguity**: No clear rationale given for:
  - Why summation is used for S1 (attention weights aggregated over heads and queries) vs. max for S2 (max of absolute V values) – seems arbitrary.
  - Why multiplication is chosen to combine S1 and S2 (Equation 5).
  - How Equation (3) effectively identifies important Key positions via attention weight summation.
  - How Equation (4)’s max operation on absolute V values is justified.
- **Observation window (Section 3.1)**: Length not specified; unclear if this window is included in KV cache budget; how does SlimKV handle tasks where the instruction/question falls outside the window (e.g., multi-turn conversations)? No discussion on impact.
- **Missing latency / throughput analysis**: No discussion of inference time improvements, computational overhead of importance scoring, clustering, or re-reading attention scores. Using such scoring may defeat the purpose of methods like FlashAttention. Need analysis of how SlimKV affects end-to-end execution.
- **Limited evaluation scope**:
  - Only two compression levels shown (256 and 2048, with an inconsistent change to 128+2048 in later tables); major gap between levels – should show a range.
  - No experiments on larger models (e.g., 70B scale); only <10B models tested.
  - In many cases accuracy drops significantly compared to full KV (FullKV). One reviewer questions whether using a smaller uncompressed model would be better than a larger compressed one.
  - Performance gains over baselines are marginal (e.g., <0.6% over SnapKV in multiple settings).
- **Incomplete comparisons**:
  - For GQA models, only compared against StreamingLLM; missing comparisons with other methods like H2O, Ada-KV, FastGen, SparQ Attention, or activation-based eviction strategies (e.g., from arxiv 2407.18003).
  - No direct comparison of KV-cache reduction vs. accuracy trade-off (like the plots in H2O).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: `window_size` and `max_capacity_prompt` (Listing 1) likely have golden values per model/dataset? If not, tuning overhead during inference is high and unaddressed.
- **Ablation studies inadequate**: Only evaluates removal of S1 or S2; does not explore sensitivity to window size, kernel size, or different clustering strategies. Also, the S2-only (V-only) ablation showing robust performance seems counterintuitive – needs explanation.
- **Presentation issues**: Numerous grammatical errors and typos (e.g., “lenghts”, “thus sustain large activations in KV cache is essential”); inconsistent terminology (“Observation Window” vs. “observing window”, “KV cache” vs. “KVs”); pseudocode lacks comments and variable explanations; no figures to illustrate design or results.
- **Reproducibility**: No mention of code availability or detailed experimental settings (e.g., exact hyperparameters, observation window length, clustering mechanism).
- **Novelty concerns**: One reviewer (score 3) finds the method only an incremental improvement over existing activation-based pruning and lacking sufficient contribution for a top venue.
- **Cross-layer application**: Not discussed how SlimKV works across layers – are tokens evicted in the first layer automatically evicted later, or is per-layer eviction performed?
