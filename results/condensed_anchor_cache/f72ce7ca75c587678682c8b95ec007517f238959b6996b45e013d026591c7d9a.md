## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Predictive Inverse Dynamics Models (PIDM), an end-to-end paradigm that predicts actions using inverse dynamics conditioned on forecasted visual states. The model Seer uses Transformers to process visual states and actions, pre-trained on large-scale robotic datasets (DROID) and fine-tuned for real-world tasks. All reviewers acknowledge strong empirical results: 13% improvement on LIBERO-LONG, 22% on CALVIN ABC-D, and 43% in real-world tasks. Reviewers 1–3 rate the paper highly (8), emphasizing effective pre-training, thorough ablation, real-robot evaluation, and scalability. Reviewer 4 rates 6, noting insufficient novelty (sub-goal prediction is already explored), missing limitations, unclear network architecture, and lack of MACs/network size comparisons. All reviewers agree the problem is important and the paper is well-written.

### Strengths

- Effective pre-training objectives: joint forward (conditional visual foresight) and inverse dynamics prediction, demonstrated via thorough ablation studies (Table 3(b)). Reviewers note the synergy between vision and action at each step.
- End-to-end training: both modules are trained together, enabling scalable use of visual, temporal, and action features via Transformers with unidirectional attention masks.
- Extensive evaluation: both simulation (LIBERO-LONG, CALVIN ABC-D) and real robot experiments, with strong empirical gains (13%, 22%, 43%). Ablation studies show benefits of pre-training, loss functions, and data efficiency (Fig. 3) are convincing.
- Scalability via pre-training on DROID and fine-tuning with modest data (R1, R2). The data efficiency study is particularly convincing (R4).
- The paper is well-motivated, well-written, and easy to follow (R1, R3, R4); figures are informative (R3).
- Contributions are of interest to the community (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty and prior work context**: Several reviewers criticize omitted comparisons. R1 notes that learning inverse dynamics from visuals was explored earlier (e.g., RPT [Radosavovic et al. 2023]) and asks for explicit comparison. R3 states GR-1 does the same high-level goal (synergistic vision+action prediction). R4 argues the central idea is not novel, pointing to works like [1][2][3] that use sub-goal prediction for manipulation (e.g., Gen2Act, This&That, VideoAgent, IGOR). The claim in line 71 of being “first to optimize vision and action prediction synergistically” is challenged. R4 also notes the GR-1 prior.
- **Limited generalization across embodiments**: R2 highlights that pre-training only on DROID (Franka Panda) restricts generalization to different robot morphologies (degrees of freedom, joint configurations, end-effectors). Suggests training on a more diverse dataset like Open-X-Embodiment. Overfitting to DROID’s specific tasks is a concern. R1 similarly notes scalability along the model capacity axis is not shown.
- **No discussion of limitations**: Both R3 and R4 note the paper lacks a limitations section. R4 adds specific concerns: difficulty in high-frequency control tasks (e.g., ALOHA), and absence of evaluations on precision tasks requiring rich contacts (insertion, screwing). R3 notes the low number of experimental trials (e.g., 20) and no indication of statistical significance; performance differences may not be significant. R4 also questions applicability to high-precision tasks due to video prediction imprecision.
- **Network architecture unclear**: R4 states the architecture (especially sub-goal selection, action prediction frequency) is not clearly explained, even in the supplementary. R3 asks for clarification on action frequency relative to image prediction, number of observation history frames, number of predicted frames, and whether temporal ensembling was used. R1 notes the formulation does not use history (past observations), limiting applicability to more complex environments requiring task planning.
- **Missing comparisons in terms of computational cost**: R4 states only success rates are reported, not network sizes and MACs; it is unclear if performance gain comes from larger model or better method. R1 asks about fine-tuning approach (full fine-tune vs. LoRA) and scaling with parameter count. R3 asks about GPU resources for pre-training and fine-tuning.
- **Missing ablations requested**: R1 asks to evaluate importance of forward dynamics prediction (Table 3(b)): pretrain with only inverse dynamics? R2 asks about OpenVLA baseline checkpoint version. R4 asks how sub-goals are selected during training set preparation, and how fine-tuning/pretraining works when both loss functions are not used (Table 3).
- **Minor issues**: Typo lines 67 and 267 (R2, R3). R4 requests quantitative comparison with existing sub-goal prediction works.
