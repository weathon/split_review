## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Entropy-MCMC (EMCMC), a method to bias posterior sampling in Bayesian deep learning towards flat basins for improved generalization. It introduces an auxiliary variable whose stationary distribution is a smoothed posterior (convolution with a Gaussian kernel). The joint distribution enables efficient sampling via SGLD without inner loops. Theoretical analysis gives convergence rates in the strongly convex setting. Experiments on synthetic data, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet show improved performance over SGD, SGLD, Entropy-SGD, and Entropy-SGLD.

### Strengths
* Core idea of the augmented model/auxiliary variable is interesting, novel to the best of reviewers’ knowledge, and simple yet effective.
* Resolves a key computational limitation of local entropy methods (Entropy-SGD) by removing the inner loop, with essentially the same cost as SGD.
* Preserves an unbiased marginal posterior (advantage over the Robust Ensemble (Baldassi et al. PNAS ’16) with \(y>1\) replicas).
* Good empirical performance on classification, calibration, and out-of-distribution detection benchmarks.
* Theoretical comparison with entropy-SGD and entropy-SGLD is insightful; favorable convergence bound for log-concave targets.
* Paper is well organized, well written, and easy to follow.
* Synthetic experiments and measurement of flatness metrics (eigenspectrum, interpolation/extrapolation) corroborate the method’s ability to focus on flat minima.
* Extensive experimental coverage across multiple common benchmarks.

### Weaknesses
* **Lack of rigorous theoretical justification for why the method biases towards flat basins.** The claim that \(\theta_a\) provides additional paths for \(\theta\) to traverse is not rigorously reasoned; only empirical support from synthetic examples is given (Reviewer 5). Reviewer 4 questions the convergence result (Theorem 1): if the marginal converges to the true posterior, the sampler must eventually visit sharp modes—contradicting the claim of staying in flat basins. Is the result only local due to the strongly convex assumption? To what extent does the sampler still converge to the true posterior under non-convex violation? A simple long-run experiment on the Figure 2 mixture would help clarify. The practical utility of the theoretical statements is difficult to judge (Reviewer 1).
* **Novelty concerns.** The method is highly similar to previous work: Entropy-SGD (Chaudhari et al., 2019) and the Robust Ensemble (Baldassi et al. PNAS ’16). Reviewers 2 and 3 note a lack of novelty, only partly justified by the Bayesian setting. The core idea of using an auxiliary variable is not entirely novel, and experimental settings are fairly commonplace.
* **Weak SGD baselines and experimental setup issues.** Reviewer 2 questions the fairness of comparisons: SGD baselines appear to perform significantly below what is achievable with standard training procedures; hyperparameters may not have been tuned to optimal values. Reviewer 3 points out that all main experiments use \(T=1\times10^{-4}\) instead of the Bayesian \(T=1\); Table 4 shows only minimal performance degradation at \(T=1\), so results should either be presented with \(T=1\) only or follow the burn-in/sample protocol of Zhang et al. ICLR 2020b. Hyperparameter tuning for \(\eta\) and \(T\) is performed on the test set rather than a validation set (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 notes that in Table 1(a), entropy-SGLD is almost consistently worse than SGLD, whereas Entropy-SGD consistently outperforms SGD—why?
* **Unclear behavior and choice of hyperparameter \(\eta\).** Reviewers 1 and 5 ask whether \(\eta\) is a free variable, why \(\eta\) disappears in Eqn 21 during integration, and whether the method is independent of \(\eta\) (Reviewer 5: if \(\eta\to0\), the smoothed target equals the exact posterior; if \(\eta\) is too large, MCMC convergence slows). Reviewer 3 notes an irregular, sharp-peaked dependence of test error on \(\eta\) in Fig. 6 and requests an intuition; Reviewers 3 and 5 ask about adaptive tuning for \(\eta\) (e.g., “Focusing” in Pittorino et al. ICLR ’21). Reviewer 5 requests visualization of target and smoothed target slices for various \(\eta\).
* **Synthetic example and MCMC details.** Reviewer 5 finds the definition of the “ridge” in the mixture unclear and wants justification that the initial point on the ridge does not favor one mode. Details on \(\eta\), number of MCMC iterations, and step sizes are missing for the synthetic experiment. Figures 2, 7, and 8 suggest both SGLD and EMCMC do not mix well.
* **Clarity gaps.** Reviewer 1 requests clear specification of prior and likelihood before inference; notation \(f(\theta)\) (no data dependence) is confusing and should be replaced. Need arguments that Eqn 4 integrates to a finite quantity. Assumption 1 could be a remark rather than an assumption. Reviewer 3 notes a typo in Eqn 16 (missing \(+\)) and says more references to the appendix (e.g., Appendix B for proofs) are needed. The “Related Work” section should correct that local entropy was introduced in Baldassi et al. PRL ’15, not Entropy-SGD (Reviewer 3). No line numbers are present (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 4 is confused by framing the method as Bayesian sampling when it intentionally avoids exploring sharp modes. Reviewer 5 asks how sampling-based and optimization-based results are compared (e.g., point estimates from MCMC samples?) and whether a systematic way to choose \(\eta\) exists.
* **Limited scope and generalizability.** Reviewer 2 notes that experiments are limited to standard datasets and architectures; the flatness-generalization link is debated (Dinh et al. 2017 cited). Reviewer 1 finds data augmentation’s influence on empirical performance unclear. Reviewer 3 asks whether more replicas could be used (as in Baldassi et al.) while keeping one marginal equal to the original measure, and whether only \(\theta\) samples should be collected (not \(\theta_a\)).
