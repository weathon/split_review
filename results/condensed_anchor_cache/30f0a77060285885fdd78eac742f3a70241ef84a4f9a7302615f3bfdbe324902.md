## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper evaluates zero-shot robustness of instruction-tuned LLMs to rephrased instructions. It finds that using novel (unobserved) but appropriate instruction phrasings consistently degrades performance and yields high variance, despite semantic equivalence. Instruction-tuned models are not especially robust to rephrasing. The authors propose a mitigation: introducing soft-prompt parameters and optimizing a KL divergence to align representations of semantically equivalent instructions. They release a dataset of 319 instructions manually written by NLP practitioners for 80+ tasks. Experiments cover models up to 11B (Llama finetunes, Alpaca, etc.) on MMLU, BBL, and NLG benchmarks.

### Strengths
- Significant and important result that exposes a critical weakness in instruction-tuned models; raises an important issue for the field.
- Experiments are well-executed, clear, and broad across models (Llama finetunes, Alpaca) and datasets (MMLU, BBL, NLG); results hold for different model families and sizes.
- Analysis of representation similarity between instructions and performance correlations is interesting, even if the correlation is somewhat weak.
- Proposed soft-prompt + KL penalty method is well-motivated and performs reasonably, especially for Alpaca-7B; it functions as a form of regularization.
- Figure 4 helps intuition for the method.
- Release of 319 human-written instructions facilitates further research, although some reviewers noted prior datasets exist.

### Weaknesses
- The novelty of robustness experiments is somewhat limited: prior work has observed variance based on prompt formatting (acknowledged in paper). Work adds insights for modern models (Alpaca) and representation distance analysis, but is not fully novel.
- Releasing only prompt rephrasings may not be a sufficiently significant contribution; similar datasets already exist (e.g., Mishra et al., 2022).
- The claim that models perform worse on novel phrasings is potentially too strong. In Tables 3 and 7, the variance on unobserved instructions is often larger than the difference in average performance, questioning whether the difference is statistically significant. Significance tests are missing.
- The proposed PT+KL method may not be significantly better than prompt tuning alone. In Appendix B.3, PT alone often performs within one standard deviation of PT+KL (e.g., BBL unobserved and average). No statistical tests for significance are reported.
- Details about the proposed solution are insufficient: the soft-prompt size (variable N) is not specified in the main text; batch size and the number of paraphrases per batch / total dataset size are not given.
- Fine-tuning on synthetic paraphrases (FT baseline) results in severe unexplained performance degradation (Table 7). This is surprising—continual fine-tuning on the same data should not damage performance that much. Reviewers suspect synthetic prompt quality is poor, raising the question: would better synthetic instructions mitigate this? No analysis is provided.
- The paper only evaluates models up to 11B; the robustness trend may be unpredictable for state-of-the-art LLMs like GPT-3.5 or GPT-4. Reviewer 3 notes this is a limitation given the title claims generality.
- The alignment procedure (PT+KL) might make the model insensitive to irrelevant or negated instructions, echoing concerns from Webson & Pavlick (2022) about whether models truly understand prompts. This possibility is not tested.
- Missing experiments/comparisons/ablation requests: (a) How does the proposed method compare to introducing paraphrases during initial instruction fine-tuning? (b) Could RLHF after instruction tuning mitigate the sensitivity? (c) The results in Table 7 should be broken down into BBL subcategories (QA/BC/MC as in Table 6) to see how closest distance affects performance differences. (d) What is the effect of different prompt distances (as in Table 7) across those subcategories?
- One reviewer (reviewer 3) is notably more skeptical, scoring the paper 6 vs. the other scores of 8. That reviewer emphasizes the significance concerns and the lack of generalization to larger models as major weaknesses. Their concerns about insensitivity to negated instructions and the absence of significance tests are included above.
