## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces the Deep Compression Autoencoder (DC-AE) family for latent diffusion models, achieving spatial compression ratios up to 128× while maintaining reconstruction quality and enabling substantial speedups. Two key techniques are proposed: (1) residual autoencoding, which uses non-parametric space-to-channel residual connections in down/upsampling layers to alleviate optimization difficulties, and (2) decoupled high-resolution adaptation (DHRA), a three-phase training strategy that mitigates generalization penalties when switching from low- to high-resolution inputs. On ImageNet 512×512, DC-AE provides 19.1× inference speedup and 17.9× training speedup for UViT-H on an H100 GPU with better FID than the widely used SD‑VAE‑f8. Experiments span multiple datasets, diffusion architectures (UViT, DiT, PixArt), and both reconstruction and generation metrics.

### Strengths
- Well written and easy to follow.
- Reduces computational requirements for training/evaluating high-resolution diffusion models, making the topic more accessible.
- Evaluated on multiple datasets and metrics (rFID, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID, CLIP score).
- Downstream latent diffusion training and sampling results are provided, showing that reconstruction improvements translate to generation gains.
- Qualitative examples clearly demonstrate improvements.
- The architectural modifications (residual space‑to‑channel connections) are simple, effective, and well motivated.
- The three‑stage training recipe is straightforward yet impactful.
- Speedups are substantial (up to 19× inference, 17.9× training on ImageNet 512×512 with UViT‑H).
- Tested across a broad range of datasets and diffusion model families, including text‑to‑image (PixArt).
- Baselines (stable diffusion VAEs) are widely recognized by the community.
- Both reconstruction and generation evaluations are provided, strengthening the practical relevance.
- The method addresses a real‑world deficiency that affects all researchers and products using autoencoders.

### Weaknesses
1. **Missing sampling parameters for diffusion models** – sampler type, number of steps, guidance scale are not reported; impact on sampling quality requires further investigation.
2. **Insufficient ablations** – no ablation of the constituent parts of DC‑AE (e.g., removing residual connections, alternative connection strategies). Specifically, the choice of space‑channel residual connections over purely spatial or purely channel‑wise connections lacks justification or empirical comparison.
3. **Missing comparisons with recent autoencoders** – Tables 3 and 4 omit comparisons with SD‑XL, SD3, and the Asymmetric Autoencoder.
4. **Table 5 discrepancy** – PixArt trained with DC‑AE shows a lower CLIP score than with SD‑VAE, yet the text claims the opposite; the authors should clarify whether this is a typo and report results after equalizing memory usage (e.g., by increasing batch size for SD‑VAE) to see if gains persist or stagnate.
5. **Missing baseline with pixel shuffle** – a simple pixel‑shuffle (space‑to‑channel) operation could also reduce spatial resolution; the paper should compare such a baseline (using SD‑VAE with pixel shuffle) against DC‑AE.
6. **Missing shift/scale details for LDM latents** – the paper does not explain how the shift and scale factors applied to latents before diffusion are computed.
7. **Architecture details incomplete** – Fig. 4 gives a high‑level overview but omits specifics of “Encoder/Decoder Stages”; full architecture and training details should be provided (e.g., in the appendix) instead of only in the code.
8. **Explanation for DHRA is hand‑wavy** – Section 3.3’s conjecture that DC‑AE helps because “the diffusion model needs to simultaneously learn denoising and token compression” is not rigorously argued; while empirical validation is acceptable, a clearer rationale would strengthen the paper.
9. **Generalization across resolutions not fully disentangled** – the paper does not test whether single‑stage training on random 256×256 crops from a high‑resolution dataset (instead of downscaled images) could close the gap, nor investigate whether the generalization issue stems from different pixel statistics or boundary artifacts (e.g., zero‑padding effects at high compression). The benefit of DHRA keeping the GAN from influencing the encoder is not isolated.
10. **No ablation of depth in Stages 2 and 3** – how the number of trainable layers in these stages affects speed vs. quality is not studied (no graph or analysis).
11. **Limited range of experiments** – most experiments rely on stable diffusion backbones; although several datasets are used, the evaluation would benefit from a broader set of diffusion model families.
12. **Lack of gradient‑propagation analysis** – no quantitative analysis of why standard staged training suffers from poor gradient propagation (e.g., loss landscapes or gradient norms) is provided.
13. **Incremental nature of modifications** – the residual connection with space‑to‑channel is close to removing skip connections and adding a reshape operation in the standard SD autoencoder; the fine‑tuning procedure is more detailed but necessary only because of the architecture change.
14. **No shallow‑compression evaluation** – Table 2 compares autoencoders only at high downsampling ratios (f32, f64, f128); the behavior of the proposed architecture at lower compression (e.g., f8) is not shown.
15. **Only FID metric (Inception V3) for generation** – additional metrics (e.g., sFID, IS, precision/recall) would provide a more complete picture.
16. **Loss and training details missing** – the paper does not specify optimizers, learning rates, schedules, or the exact loss functions (including GAN loss) used in each of the three training stages, which is critical for reproducibility.
17. **Absence of original images in side‑by‑side comparisons** – visual comparisons lack the ground‑truth image as reference.
18. **Prior work not cited** – the space‑to‑channel operation has been used in super‑resolution (Shi et al. CVPR 2016); this connection is not discussed.
19. **Artifacts not analyzed** – how often does the proposed AE produce visible artifacts? Are all figures random samples or cherry‑picked?
20. **Design choices for space‑to‑channel operations** – the role of averaging (vs. simple space‑to‑channel) is not ablated; without averaging, would the channel dimension grow too fast? Similarly, the channel‑duplication in upsampling is not tested against alternatives.
21. **Impact of EfficientViT blocks** – how does the use of EfficientViT blocks affect reconstruction/generation quality compared to standard blocks, especially under high compression?
22. **Inference throughput/latency** – the paper states “we assume the number of sampling steps is 1” for speedups, but does not report actual throughput or latency numbers for full image generation.
23. **Figure resolution unclear** – the resolution of images in Figure 7 is not indicated in the caption.
24. **Clarification on “convert the latent to higher spatial compression”** – Line 161 is ambiguous: do f8 through f64 AEs start with the same output latent size, with f32 and f64 further squeezing spatial dimensions into channels?
25. **VAE improvement not fully translating to LDM** – while DC‑AE shows little FID loss from f32 to f64, the corresponding latent diffusion models (Table 3) degrade; this may indicate a need for higher‑capacity diffusion models at extreme compression.
