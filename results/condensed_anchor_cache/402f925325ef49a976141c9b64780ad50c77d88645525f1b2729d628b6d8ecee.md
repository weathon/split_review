## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper pioneers a data-centric perspective for partial-label learning (PLL) by proposing a new task called candidate label set pruning (CLSP), which aims to filter out false candidate labels in a training-free manner. The first CLSP method is based on the inconsistency between the representation space and the candidate label space: a candidate label of a training instance is pruned if it is not among the candidate labels of the instance’s k-nearest neighbors in the representation space. Theoretically, an upper bound of the pruning error rate is proved, along with an analysis of how representation quality and candidate generation affect the bound. Empirically, extensive experiments on CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet, and PASCAL VOC under uniform, label-dependent, and instance-dependent candidate generation settings, using ten state-of-the-art PLL algorithms, show significant performance improvement after applying CLSP. All four reviewers rate the paper highly (score 8) and praise its novelty, theoretical contribution, and comprehensive experiments, but raise concerns about the potential risk of removing the true label, unclear details regarding feature extractors and numerical simulations, and missing analyses of bad cases and sensitivity.

### Strengths

- **Novelty and significance**: The proposed CLSP task is the first to approach PLL from a data-centric angle, shifting attention from designing complex training algorithms to improving label quality before training. This fresh perspective is considered highly significant for the PLL community (Reviewers 1–4).
- **Simple yet effective method**: The kNN-based pruning method is easy to understand and implement, yet achieves impressive empirical gains across diverse settings (random, LD, ID), benchmarks (CIFAR, Tiny-ImageNet, VOC), and ten PLL algorithms (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Theoretical contribution**: The proof of an upper bound on the per-example pruning error rate, along with analysis of the effects of feature quality, label ambiguity, and pruning proportion, is solid and novel. The numerical simulation experiment provides practical guidance for selecting parameters k and τ (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Comprehensive experiments**: The paper evaluates on four datasets with three candidate-generation mechanisms and ten baselines, demonstrating consistent improvements especially in harder settings. The experiments are extensive and well-executed (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Clarity and organization**: The paper is well-structured, the motivation is clearly stated, and the related work is adequately covered. The formal definition of CLSP (Definition 1) is explicit (Reviewers 2, 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Risk of removing the correct label**: The proposed method transforms a PLL problem into an UPLL problem where the true label may be inadvertently eliminated. Although an upper bound on the pruning error is provided, the negative impact of such removal and the trade-off between reducing noisy labels and preserving the true label are not explored. This is a major concern (Reviewer 3; also echoed by Reviewer 1’s request for bad-case analysis).
- **Unclear dependency on feature extractors**: The method relies on a kNN algorithm applied to features from a pre-trained or trained feature extractor. The paper does not clearly state whether this extractor is pre-trained independently or comes from the classifier during training—the latter would contradict the “training-free” claim. The choice of distance metric in kNN and its impact on pruning performance are not discussed (Reviewer 3; related questions from Reviewer 2 about ResNet-SSL and ResNet-S details).
- **Missing analysis of bad cases and algorithm sensitivity**: Several experiments (Table 1 and Table 8) show performance drops after pruning. The paper lacks an analysis of why these occur (e.g., over-pruning, limitations of kNN for certain data distributions, or the nature of the data points). It also does not break down which PLL algorithms are more sensitive to pruning, nor provide a clear relationship between the degree of pruning (τ) and performance gain (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 2 also notes the unusual loss curve on VOC in Figure 5).
- **Incomplete discussion of related work**: References are missing for some state-of-the-art PLL methods that also attempt to filter incorrect candidate labels, such as “A Unifying Probabilistic Framework for Partially Labeled Data Learning” and “Mutual Partial Label Learning with Competitive Label Noise.” Including these would strengthen the positioning of CLSP (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Unclear numerical simulation**: The calculation of δ_k and ρ_k values and the conclusions drawn from the numerical simulation experiment (Figure 3) are not presented clearly enough. The values of δ_k and ρ_k for the real-world VOC dataset are omitted (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 1 asks to show these for VOC).
- **Definition and scope issues**: Definition 2 may need clearer motivation. Additionally, the abstract’s description of PLL (“equipped with a set of candidate labels”) is vague regarding the standard assumption that only one label is the ground truth; this should be clarified, especially as some PLL variants waive that limitation (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Missing details and potential typos**: 
  - The PASCAL VOC dataset is not well introduced for the PLL setting (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 1 also asks how it is used).
  - A potential typo in Definition 1: the formulation of β may be missing a division by n (Reviewer 4).
  - The way k and γ_i are calculated in Figure 1 is not explained (Reviewer 2).
- **Limitations and future work**: The paper does not discuss potential limitations of the proposed approach (e.g., what happens when the true label is outside the candidate set, i.e., noisy PLL data; whether an adaptive τ per instance would be better; the difference between a data-centric method and simple pre-processing) (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
