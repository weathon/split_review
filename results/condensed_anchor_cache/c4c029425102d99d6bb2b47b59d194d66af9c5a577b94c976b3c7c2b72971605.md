## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Probabilistically Rewired Message-Passing Neural Networks (PR-MPNNs), which learn to add relevant edges and omit less beneficial ones via differentiable \(k\)-subset sampling (SIMPLE) to address under-reaching and over-squashing in MPNNs. Theoretical analysis identifies conditions where PR-MPNNs outperform purely randomized approaches in probabilistically separating graphs. Experiments on synthetic (EXP, CSL, 4-CYCLES) and real-world datasets (ZINC, ALCHEMY, OGBG-MOLHIV, etc.) show competitive or superior predictive performance. **Reviewer disagreement:** Reviewers 1 and 2 rated the paper 6; Reviewer 3 rated 8, indicating significant difference in overall assessment.

### Strengths
- The proposed PR-MPNN model is simple yet effective (Reviewer 2, high confidence).
- The paper provides theoretical analysis proving that PR-MPNN is more effective in probabilistically separating graphs than randomized approaches (e.g., dropping nodes/edges uniformly) under certain conditions (Reviewer 2, high confidence).
- Experimental results on node and graph classification tasks show competitive or better performance compared with baselines (Reviewer 2, high confidence).
- The method is verified with both theory and experiments (Reviewer 1, low confidence).
- The work is well-written and interesting; learning rewiring is intuitively preferable over arbitrary heuristics (Reviewer 3, high confidence).
- Appreciated discussion of techniques such as the Differentiable Graph Module, which in principle might seem similar (Reviewer 3, high confidence).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation for \(k\)-subset constraint unclear:** It is not explained why a \(k\)-subset constraint is preferred over simply sampling edges independently with a learned probability; the specific benefits for expressiveness and learning dynamics need elaboration (Reviewer 2, high confidence).
- **Insufficient differentiation from prior work:** The difference between PR-MPNN and existing graph rewiring methods (e.g., graph structure learning, heuristic rewiring) is not explicitly discussed in the related work section (Reviewer 2, high confidence).
- **Over-squashing claim insufficiently supported:** It is unclear how probabilistically separating graphs directly translates to mitigating over-squashing (Reviewer 2, high confidence). Moreover, the rewiring appears to only remove edges (no new edges are added; see Question Q1), which may reduce information flow rather than alleviate bottlenecks; a detailed analysis (e.g., examining path lengths, effective resistance) is needed (Reviewer 3, high confidence).
- **Robustness to noise/missing information not validated:** The paper claims PR-MPNNs make MPNNs less vulnerable to noise and missing information, but provides no empirical or theoretical evidence (Reviewer 2, high confidence).
- **SIMPLE method not explained:** The paper is not self-contained; it should include a brief introduction to how SIMPLE works and why it is suitable for this task (Reviewer 2, high confidence).
- **Experimental weaknesses (Reviewer 2, high confidence; partially echoed by Reviewer 3):**
  - It is unclear which of the two methods for handling multiple priors (aggregation of sampled adjacency matrices) was used in the experiments; the choice should be stated and justified.
  - Baseline selection is inconsistent across datasets (e.g., on EXP, CSL, and 4-CYCLES different baselines are used); claims about outperforming DropGNN on 4-CYCLES are inaccurate (PR-MPNN is only comparable in some cases).
  - Evaluation metrics in Table 1 are not clearly stated; some datasets use higher-is-better (OGBG-MOLHIV) while others use lower-is-better (ZINC, ALCHEMY) without explanation.
  - No ablation studies to validate the contribution of individual components (learned edge priors, \(k\)-subset sampling, SIMPLE gradient estimator).
- **Computational overhead and efficiency claim unsupported:** The paper claims computational efficiency over baselines but provides no comparison of training/inference time; the overhead of the sampling procedure should be discussed (Reviewer 1, low confidence; Reviewer 2, high confidence).
- **Limited benchmark breadth:** The evaluation does not include comparisons with deterministic rewiring techniques such as FOSR and SDRF, nor with random rewiring (DropGNN) on real-world tasks (Reviewer 3, high confidence).
- **How expressiveness guarantee translates to practical performance:** The theoretical expressiveness improvement is not clearly connected to observed performance gains; it remains unclear whether it explains the practical benefits (Reviewer 1, low confidence).
