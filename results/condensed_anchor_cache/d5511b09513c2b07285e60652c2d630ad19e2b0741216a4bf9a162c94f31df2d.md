- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 6, 6, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Dynalang, an agent that learns a multimodal world model (extending DreamerV3) to predict future text and image representations, and acts from imagined rollouts. Reviewers find the motivation compelling and the method neat, but raise concerns about limited technical novelty (incremental over DreamerV3), insufficient ablations, simplistic evaluation environments, overclaimed title, and lack of comparison with alternative model-based and SOTA methods.

### Strengths
- Clear writing and comprehensible method descriptions. (R1, R3)
- The problem of enabling RL agents to understand diverse language beyond simple instructions is important and understudied. (R2)
- The multimodal alignment mechanism and the idea of using future prediction as a unifying objective is interesting and sound. (R3, R4)
- The proposed method is neat: consuming all modalities in one conditional generative model is elegant. (R3)
- Experimental results consistently show improvements over evaluated baselines across multiple environments (Homegrid, Messenger, Habbit, LangRoom). (R2, R4)

### Weaknesses
- The technical contribution is limited: the main change from DreamerV3 is adding text tokens to observations. (R1, R3, R4) There is no ablation study to disentangle whether the world model genuinely integrates language into dynamics or whether the language token is only used by the policy network after latent state generation. (R1)
- The experiments lack ablations validating the importance of each component (e.g., language token in world model vs. policy). (R1)
- The title overclaims: the model learns dynamics for one environment at a time, not a general world model. No experiment demonstrates generalization to a held-out world, which would justify "modeling the world". (R2)
- The method’s handling of instructions that only occur at episode start (e.g., ALFRED, TEACh) is unclear—multimodal alignment would only occur from initial frames. How does the method benefit under such conditions? (R2)
- In Section 4.4, actual SOTA methods are not reported as baselines, even if Dynalang is inferior. Reasons why modularization or foundation models benefit long-horizon real-world tasks are not addressed. (R2)
- The evaluation environments (Homegrid, Messenger, Habbit, LangRoom) are toy-like with low visual fidelity, except the navigation task. More realistic embodied environments (e.g., Crafter, Minecraft, ALFRED, TEACh) are needed to demonstrate scalability and robustness. (R1, R2)
- The paper does not compare with other model-based methods that are more similar (e.g., video-guided planning like Dai et al. 2023). (R3, R4) The claim of outperforming language-conditioned methods is insufficient without such comparisons.
- The unique importance of language vs. images/video for world modeling is not justified. Since the model is multimodal (text+images), one could argue that images alone could also convey world knowledge; evidence distinguishing language’s role is missing. (R3, R4)
- The setting of using only one text token per time step is limited and conflicts with the paper’s motivating example (“I put the bowls away”), which is a multi-token sentence. (R4) It is also unclear how tokens are padded when language is exhausted. (R3)
- Minor: Typo in Section 3.1 (“whih” should be “which”). (R3)

Two reviewers (R1, R4) were more critical of the technical novelty and experimental depth, while two reviewers (R2, R3) acknowledged the method’s neatness and consistent improvements but raised similar concerns about overclaiming, missing ablations, and lack of realism.
