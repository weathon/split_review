## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DilateQuant, a quantization-aware training framework for diffusion models with three components: Weight Dilation (WD), Temporal Parallel Quantizer (TPQ), and Block-wise Knowledge Distillation (BKD). WD reduces activation range by equivalently scaling unsaturated in-channel weights without changing weight range. TPQ assigns per-timestep quantization parameters. BKD uses block-wise reconstruction with gradient updates. Experiments on UNet-based diffusion models show improved FID vs. existing methods.

### Strengths
- The Weight Dilation idea is considered novel and interesting (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 2 finds it "novel and interesting"; Reviewer 3 calls it the most innovative point; Reviewer 4 says it introduces a novel technique.
- Extensive experiments on diffusion models, consistently outperforming SOTA baselines (Reviewer 1). Ablation studies and efficiency analysis are provided (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is well-written with clear motivation and easy to follow (Reviewers 2, 4). Reviewer 4: "written with exceptional clarity."
- WD costlessly absorbs activation quantization errors into weight quantization (Reviewers 3, 1). The activation range decreases while weight range remains constant, aiding convergence (Reviewer 3).
- However, two scorers (3,4) gave rating 3 vs. 6 from reviewers 1 and 2, indicating disagreement on overall contribution and performance lift.

### Weaknesses
- **WD improvement is small in ablations.** Reviewers 1, 2, and 3 note that Table 3 shows WD contributes little to the final FID. Adding WD to BKD-only only improves FID from 9.63 to 9.13 (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 doubts WD and TPQ contributions; the last two rows of Table 3 fail to isolate WD+TPQ from WD alone. Both Reviewers 1 and 4 request an ablation of WD alone (without TPQ and BKD) and also a combination WD(No)+TPQ(No)+BKD(Yes) to isolate BKD's effect (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 4: ablation insufficient.
- **Scaling activations to weights is not new.** Reviewer 1 points out this idea appears in AWQ (Activation-aware Weight Quantization for LLMs). Reviewer 4 notes equivalent scaling like SmoothQuant and Outlier Suppression are not compared. The paper should compare with AWQ (Reviewer 1) and also SmoothQuant/Outlier Suppression (Reviewer 4).
- **WD may fail when activation outliers coincide with max-min weight indices.** Reviewer 4: If the outlier index aligns with the in-channel index corresponding to a channel’s max/min weight, the method may not handle the outlier effectively. The paper does not analyze how common this is.
- **Constraint of maintaining max-min values unchanged may be too restrictive.** Reviewer 4 suggests slight adjustment could allow better handling of outlier coincidence and suggests jointly minimizing activation and weight quantization errors.
- **WD scales solely based on weight magnitude, ignoring activations.** Reviewer 4: this may not sufficiently mitigate extreme activation outliers; the paper should compare with methods that consider both activation and weight magnitudes (SmoothQuant, Outlier Suppression).
- **Novelty of TPQ and BKD is limited.** Reviewer 2 calls contributions of TPQ and BKD modest; BKD is well-explored in previous quantization/NAS literature. Reviewer 3: TPQ and BKD lack innovation; TPQ resembles TLSQ in EfficientDM (He et al., 2023) – requests explanation of difference. Reviewer 4: assigning per-timestep quantization parameters is already in prior works (e.g., PTQD, TFMQ-DM); block-wise reconstruction is known in PTQ for diffusion models (PTQD, TFMQ-DM, etc.); BKD’s only distinction (gradient-based updates) may not be sufficient for novelty. Reviewer 3 also asks for comparison with BK-SDM and explanation of block splitting.
- **No experiments on DiT-based models (e.g., SD3, Pixart, FLUX).** Reviewer 2 states these newer models predominate; experiments only on UNet. Existing equivalent scaling works for DiT quantization exist and should be compared.
- **Missing high-resolution results (1024x1024).** Reviewer 3 notes the paper lacks image generation at higher resolution.
- **Missing comparisons requested.** Reviewer 3 asks for comparison with PTQD (He et al., 2024) and TFMQ-DM (Huang et al., 2024). Reviewer 1 notes Table 4 baselines are outdated; need efficiency comparison from Table 2 baselines.
- **Ablation missing combination WD(Yes)+TPQ(No)+BKD(Yes) and WD(No)+TPQ(No)+BKD(Yes)** (Reviewer 1). This would clarify whether WD is the key component when BKD is present.
- **Potential numerical issue in Eqs. (10) and (11).** Reviewer 4: denominator may be zero; needs safeguard (e.g., epsilon).
- **Calibration sample number for EfficientDM may be incorrect.** Reviewer 4: EfficientDM is data-free (uses random noise), so calibration sample count should be 0; possible confusion with training steps.
- **Additional experiments requested.** Reviewer 3 asks for Δx′/Δx in Table 8 and W6A6 results in Table 9 to demonstrate generalization.
- **Motivation of WD is confused.** Reviewer 2: Paper states outliers exist in all channels of DMs, so LLM scaling methods unsuitable. But if outliers are in all channels, can they still be called outliers? The difference in activation values across input channels appears much less salient than in LLMs.
