## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Alice benchmarks for synthetic-to-real domain adaptation in person and vehicle re-identification. It provides real-world datasets (AlicePerson, AliceVehicle) designed to have unconstrained, non-manually-guaranteed clusterability, making them closer to real-world test scenarios. Synthetic source domains reuse existing PersonX and VehicleX. The benchmark includes baseline results from well-known UDA methods, an online evaluation server, and analysis of the domain gap. Motivation is clear and writing is good.

### Strengths
- Provides a good benchmark specifically for synthetic-to-real domain adaptation in person/vehicle re-ID, with many experiments and interesting findings (R1, R2).
- Well-written, clear motivation, nice structure and flow (R2, R4).
- Unified evaluation framework (online server) ensures fairness across results (R2).
- Baseline results from well-known UDA methods are provided, offering a consistent basis for comparison (R2).
- Important finding: strong clusterability in existing re-ID benchmarks is a risk that can bias UDA methods; the paper shows that unsupervised clustering gains from existing benchmarks do not translate to AlicePerson/AliceVehicle, highlighting overlooked biases (R2, R3).
- Synthetic data approach avoids privacy invasion in data labeling (R4).
- Insightful discussion in “Discussion and Future Work” section (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Title overclaims scope**: title says “object re-identification” but the paper only covers person and vehicle re-ID. Although future expansion is claimed, current work is limited to these two domains (R1).
- **Missing investigation of synthetic data generation strategies**: No analysis of how different rendering engines (Unity vs. Unreal), rendering parameters (lighting, texture resolution, camera parameters), or attribute generation methods (GANs, procedural) affect realism and domain transferability. Impact of varying noise levels is also ignored (R1).
- **Quality of generated data and labels not clearly assessed**: Only FID is used as a high-level measure. Metrics for sharpness, contrast, artifacts are absent. Potential subtle errors in 3D model alignment or annotation are not discussed, and their influence on training is not evaluated (R1).
- **Synthetic data reused from existing datasets (PersonX, VehicleX); real-world data offers little advantage over off-the-shelf datasets** (R3). The only novel aspect is the strong clusterability issue, but:
    - For AlicePerson, adding distractors/junk is not a satisfactory solution to break clusterability.
    - For AliceVehicle, Table 4 shows almost no difference from VeRi776 on the clusterability issue (R3).
- **UDA methods compared are outdated**: Fig. 3, Table 3, and Table 4 use older UDA methods; newer, more effective methods should have been included (R3). This limits the benchmark's relevance for current research.
- **Contribution is insufficient** (R3, R4): The main claimed novelty is the unclustered real-world data. No novel method or algorithm is proposed to specifically handle the new benchmark. Other contributions (benchmarking existing work, discussion) are not seen as significant beyond what existing benchmarks already provide.
- **Domain adaptation may be the wrong paradigm for synthetic-to-real** (R4): Given synthetic data is cheap, the paper should consider domain generalisation by generating more diverse synthetic data (cite ClonePerson, RandPerson) rather than adapting a single synthetic source. This direction is not explored.
- **Privacy issues in data capture remain** (R4): Although synthetic data avoids labeling privacy concerns, the real-world datasets (AlicePerson, AliceVehicle) are captured from passersby and vehicles without their knowledge. A license from local government is insufficient to address human rights and consent.
- **Understandings are intuitive, not mathematically rigorous** (R4): The discussion and analysis provide only high-level intuitions without formal proofs or theoretical grounding. More theoretical analysis of domain gap is expected.
- **Potential inherent limits of synthetic data benefits** not discussed (R2): There is no discussion of whether there are fundamental barriers to how much synthetic data can help real-world re-ID, even with improved rendering.

*Reviewer disagreement*: One reviewer (R2) is more positive (rating 8, contribution 4) and emphasises the unified evaluation framework and the bias-hiding finding as strong contributions. The other three reviewers (R1, R3, R4) rate the paper 6 and find contribution weaker (R3: 1 poor, R1/R4: 2 fair). Even the positive reviewer notes a missing discussion on potential limitations of synthetic data.
