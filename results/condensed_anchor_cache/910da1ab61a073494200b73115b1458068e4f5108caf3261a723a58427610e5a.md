## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Agreement-Aware Iterative Preference Optimization (AIPO) to mitigate length exploitation in Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for LLMs, using iterative synthetic data training. The approach is evaluated on MT-Bench, AlpacaEval 2.0, and Arena-Hard, showing performance gains while controlling response length. One reviewer (score 5) finds the topic important and the empirical results solid, but agrees on many clarity and framing issues. Two reviewers (score 3) are more critical, raising serious concerns about limited novelty (similar ideas already exist), weak evaluation (only instruction-following benchmarks, underperformance relative to SPPO/SimPO in several settings), insufficient formal definition of length exploitation, high hyperparameter complexity, and poor writing quality.

### Strengths
1. The paper tackles a relevant and under-explored issue—length exploitation in iterative DPO—providing a practical motivation and thorough investigation (R1, R3).
2. Extensive experiments are conducted on standard benchmarks (MT-Bench, AlpacaEval 2.0, Arena-Hard) with comparisons to logical baselines (R1, R3).
3. AIPO demonstrates performance gains while controlling response length, achieving notable improvements on AlpacaEval 2.0 and Arena-Hard (R2).
4. The use of a synthetic data curation pipeline offers a scalable alternative to human-labeled data and provides a complete iterative training pipeline without additional human intervention (R2).
5. The proposed AIPO training objective is original and introduces innovation in the form of an agreement-aware adjustment (R3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Clarity and novelty of contributions (R1, R2, R3):**  
   - The term “length exploitation” is never formally defined, despite being the central motivation (R1 Q4).  
   - The three listed contributions (data pipeline, finding, AIPO) are poorly framed; novelty relative to prior work (R-DPO, SimPO, etc.) is entirely unclear, especially for the data curation pipeline and the AIPO loss (R1 Q1–Q3).  
   - Technical contributions are very limited: similar ideas (introducing a margin, adding SFT regularization) have already been proposed by multiple prior works (SimPO, Disentangling Length from Quality, DPO with an Offset, Contrastive Preference Optimization, Iterative Reasoning Preference Optimization) (R2).  
   - The related work section is meager; it lacks a comprehensive survey of preference alignment methodologies, hindering understanding of the broader field (R3).  

2. **Evaluation scope and methodology (R1, R2, R3):**  
   - Experiments are limited to instruction-following benchmarks (MT-Bench, AlpacaEval 2.0, Arena-Hard) that are sensitive to sampling and decoding hyperparameters; evaluation should include diverse tasks from the Hugging Face Open Leaderboard, as well as safety/honesty datasets such as HH-RLHF (R2).  
   - No experiment combines synthetic and human-generated data; the paper only contrasts synthetic vs. human data, leaving open the possibility that a hybrid approach could be more effective (R3).  
   - In Figure 3, AIPO appears to perform the worst while SimPO performs best—this requires clarification (R3).  
   - In Table 4, AIPO frequently falls short of SPPO, e.g., a staggering ~9.7% decline on Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 + UltraFeedback + PairRM's AlpacaEval 2.0; the authors do not explain this large disparity (R3).  
   - Table 5 omits discussion of cases where SPPO outperforms AIPO (e.g., possibly on the 123B Mistral-Large-Instruct-2407), and the presentation is inadequate (R3).  
   - The observed empirical differences in response length are not egregiously large, making length exploitation as the central problem less convincing; it seems more like an interesting byproduct (R1).  
   - The motivation for AIPO is handwavy and only indirectly addresses length exploitation, suggesting the real problem may be something else (R1).  
   - The assertion that degraded learning leads to “lengthy and less informative” responses is insufficiently justified; other outcomes of degraded learning are possible (R1 Q6).  
   - The analysis of SimPO’s length normalization component is unclear; the paper does not adequately explain why this specific part does not work (R1 Q5).  

3. **Practicality and complexity (R2):**  
   - AIPO introduces three additional hyperparameters that require manual tuning to achieve optimal performance, significantly increasing experimental complexity and limiting real-world applicability (R2).  

4. **Writing quality (R1, R2, R3):**  
   - The paper contains numerous grammatical errors; the overall presentation is poor, with contributions and findings not clearly communicated (R1, R2).  

5. **Reviewer disagreement (R1 vs. R2/R3):**  
   - One reviewer (score 5) finds the approach interesting and the empirical results solid, but agrees on many clarity and framing limitations. Two reviewers (score 3) are substantially more critical, particularly regarding novelty, evaluation breadth, and the extent of performance drops relative to SPPO/SimPO. The positive reviewer still notes that the paper requires substantial modifications before it can be considered acceptable for a top venue.
