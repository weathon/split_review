## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the product-patent retrieval task in the image modality, targeting the domain gap between patent images (typically line drawings/sketches) and product images (photographs). The authors construct a dataset (PRIR) including product-patent pairs and unlabeled data (3.7M training images, 240 test pairs). They systematically evaluate unsupervised strategies for domain alignment and propose a supervised pre-training paradigm: training a classifier on edge maps (computed via edge extraction) to improve feature extraction across backbones. Experimental results are presented for several retrieval pipelines.

### Strengths
1. **Practical and novel problem**: The task of image-based product-patent infringement detection is highly relevant for intellectual property protection and has been insufficiently explored. All reviewers agree the motivation is well-grounded and of application importance.
2. **Dataset contribution**: The introduction of the PRIR dataset (product-patent pairs plus unlabeled data, 3.7M images) provides a valuable resource for future research. This is considered a clear positive across reviewers.
3. **Reproducibility and clarity** (Reviewer 2): The paper is “easy to follow” and the approach seems reproducible.
4. **Focused framework** (Reviewer 3): The two posed questions (domain alignment and powerful backbone) give a clear structure to the investigation.
5. **Practical pre-training pipeline** (Reviewer 1): The supervised pre-training on edge maps is a practical solution for domain alignment.

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient comparison with relevant prior work** (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
   - No comparison with state-of-the-art patent image retrieval methods such as **DeepPatent** (Kucer et al.) is provided, despite being highly cited (25+ citations) and directly related. This undermines the claimed superiority of the proposed method.
   - Methods from sketch-based image retrieval (e.g., Deep Sketch Hashing, SketchyGAN, CartoonGAN, survey papers) are not discussed or compared, even though they address a very similar cross-domain gap (line drawing to photograph). Such baselines could be adapted or cited to strengthen the conclusion.
   - Related work section is missing a comprehensive review of image-modality pre-training that is central to the paper’s main contribution.
2. **Motivation of image-only retrieval** (Reviewers 2, 4).
   - It is questionable whether aligning product and patent images in image space is meaningful given the extreme content differences: patent images are binarized line drawings, product images are color photographs with rich texture. No comparison to a text‑based retrieval baseline is provided, so the advantage of image‑only matching over text‑based retrieval is unclear. A multimodal approach (combining image and text) might be more direct and effective.
   - The assumption that “patent images are all line drawings/hand-drawn” may not hold in practice (e.g., 3D rendered images in modern patents). A more general domain adaptation module could be preferable.
3. **Dataset construction and documentation is insufficient** (Reviewers 1, 4).
   - It is not clearly described how the product-patent pairs were built (e.g., how Amazon product images were collected, which views of patent images were used, whether multiple patent views were filtered or combined in evaluation).
   - Paired data construction must ensure semantic or instance-level identity; this process and validation are missing.
   - Lack of systematic statistics, sample visualizations, and analysis of the dataset (e.g., number of classes, distribution of types) limits understanding.
4. **Method lacks theoretical innovation** (Reviewers 2, 4).
   - The core contribution is merely applying standard pre‑training (classifier on edge maps) to patent images; the description of the pre-training paradigm in Sec. 3.3 is too brief and lacks novelty.
   - The attention to theoretical grounding and detailed methodology is weak.
5. **Reliance on edge extraction and choice of extractor** (Reviewer 1).
   - The method critically depends on the edge extractor; it is unclear how different edge detection algorithms would affect retrieval performance and how to pick the best one. An ablation over edge extractors is missing.
   - Pre-training on ImageNet‑1K edge maps may still carry a domain gap because ImageNet contains many natural images, not just line drawings.
6. **Writing and presentation issues** (Reviewer 1, title confusion in Reviewer 4).
   - Text contains typos, redundant sentences, and unexplained terms (e.g., lines 307‑313 nearly identical to lines 323‑326; “SRC” vs “SCR” in legends; “contrastive learning methods” mentioned but undefined; references to tables in line 402 that do not appear).
   - The title includes both “Supervised” and “Unsupervised”, which is confusing.
7. **Limited experimental validation** (Reviewers 2, 3).
   - Experiments are scarce; more rich and in‑depth visualizations and analysis (e.g., retrieval results, failure cases, t‑SNE embeddings) are needed to prove dataset quality and the advantage of the pre-training mechanism.
   - The test set size is small (240 pairs), raising concerns about statistical significance and generalizability.
   - No experiments are conducted to compare against alternative training strategies (e.g., fine‑tuning directly on product images instead of ImageNet‑1K as raised in Reviewer 1’s question).
   - The relationship between model size and performance is acknowledged but not explored.
8. **Limited broader applicability** (Reviewer 3).
   - The work is very specific to patent protection and does not discuss how the approach could transfer to other domains (e.g., technical diagram interpretation, historical document analysis), limiting its appeal to the broader community.

**Note on reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 5) is more positive, noting that the results are “promising” and the framework is focused. However, the same reviewer also points out missing comparisons with sketch‑based retrieval methods and suggests broader applications. The other three reviewers (score 3) are more critical, especially on the motivation, lack of innovation, insufficient baselines, and dataset documentation. The paper’s weaknesses are thus severe but not uniformly dismissed—the strongest advocates still identify substantial gaps in evaluation and related work.
