## Merged Review

### Summary

SeerAttention augments standard attention with a learnable gating network that predicts block-level importance via pooled embeddings, enabling block-sparse attention. A customized FlashAttention kernel extracts block-level ground truth attention with minimal overhead. Experiments show up to 90% sparsity at 32k context and 5.67× speedup over FlashAttention-2. The method is applied in post-training and long-context fine-tuning.

### Strengths

- **Block-level sparsity** enables high efficiency potential and seamless integration with FlashAttention. (R1)
- **Learns attention sparsity** instead of relying on predefined patterns or heuristics, offering better adaptation to different inputs, heads, and tasks. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Customized FlashAttention implementation** provides real-device speedups (5+× over FlashAttention-2) and efficient extraction of block-level ground truth with minimum overhead. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Paper is well-organized and intuitive** – crystal clear methodology and presentation (R3, R4). (Minority contrast: R2 and R3 also had presentation criticisms; see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses

- **Training cost and generalizability concerns** (multiple reviewers):
  - Requires additional training, which is expensive for large models (70B/405B) and may limit practical adoption; similar trained-sparsity approaches (TDSA, Reformer) have not been widely used. (R4)
  - Comparison against training-free methods (QUEST, MInference, Self-Extend) is missing; would training-free heuristics (averaging, absolute max, QUEST-style reduction) work equally well? (R4)
  - Generalizability is questionable: perplexity explodes at 128k in Table 1 even though 128k is within Llama-3.1’s pretrained context; attention gating fails when context length exceeds the trained gate length (64k). (R4)
  - Training data requirements (size, nature, preprocessing) are not specified. (R2)
  - It is unclear whether training with MSE loss instead of end-to-end LM loss is optimal. (R1)
  - No quantification of training cost (GPU hours, FLOPs, memory) or memory overhead of the gating network during inference. (R3)
- **Asymptotic memory complexity is quadratic** (R4):
  - The method must store an O(T²) attention buffer before converting to a sparse mask; for a 1M sequence with block size 64, peak memory is ~15.6GB (values) plus indices and sorted values up to ~93.7GB.
  - Memory profiling in Figure 8 only considers max pooling in the training kernel, while the proposed approach uses average+max+min pooling – would that increase overhead? (R3)
- **Limited context length evaluation and generalization** (R3, R4):
  - Evaluated only up to 128k (the pretraining length of Llama-3.1); no results for contexts significantly exceeding the pretraining limit (e.g., 256k, 512k) to demonstrate generalization.
  - Downstream tasks evaluated only up to 32k in Table 2 (LongBench); no evaluation on longer-context benchmarks (InfiniteBench, LOFT, RULER, PassKey Retrieval, Needle-in-a-Haystack). PPL alone does not capture retention of token-level information. (R2, R4)
  - Missing impact on general benchmarks like MMLU. (R1)
- **Pooling details and rationale insufficient** (R2, R3, R1):
  - “Max and min pooling on K matrix” is not precisely defined – dimension (sequence or hidden?), kernel size, stride. (R2)
  - No theoretical or intuitive explanation for using average pooling on Q and max+min pooling on K. (R3)
  - Would pooling on RoPE’d QK embeddings remove the need for separate RoPE in AttnGate? (R1)
  - Row-normalization of the max-pooled attention map (scaling to sum to 1) may produce out-of-distribution scores; alternatives (leaving unaltered, redirecting to attention sink) not explored. (R3)
- **Missing implementation details and applicability** (R2, R3):
  - Handling of sequence length not divisible by block size B: padding strategy not discussed; unclear whether method applies only to prefilling or also to decoding. (R2)
  - Inference-stage Triton kernel pseudo code missing – specifically how top-k blocks are selected and attention is computed only on those blocks. (R2)
  - Applicability to other architectures (e.g., Mixture of Experts) not discussed. (R3)
  - Inconsistent algorithm symbols (A vs D for attention scores). (R2)
- **Choosing the sparsity threshold (Top-k)** is not guided; no discussion on how to set it to maintain high accuracy. (R1)
- **Figure 8** does not clearly state whether speedups are for prefilling or decoding stage; experimental setup for latency measurement is insufficient. (R2)
- **Inference delay** is not reported in Table 1 for complete comparison. (R2)
- **Related work discussion incomplete** (R3): should include KV cache eviction methods (predefined and dynamic patterns, attention sinks, learnable sparse attention) and their trade-offs.
- **Methodological novelty limited** (R4): closely related to TDSA but with different pooling dimension (sequence vs hidden); need justification for why pooling sequence dimension is more effective than alternatives (hidden, head) and why additional training is beneficial compared to existing reduction methods (QUEST, MInference).
