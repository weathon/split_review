## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MGDC-UNet, which combines multi-group deformable convolution with transformer components for 3D volumetric medical image segmentation. Testing was performed on BraTS21, FLARE21, and AMOS22. Reviewer opinions varied considerably: one reviewer (R4, score 8, confidence 4) was notably more positive, praising clarity and experimental support. The other three reviewers gave scores of 5–6 (R1 and R2 confidence 4, R3 confidence 2) and raised concerns about limited novelty, insufficient improvement over baselines, questionable computational efficiency claims, missing experimental details, and the need for evaluation on more challenging or small datasets.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a challenging problem in medical image segmentation and demonstrates improved accuracy on three public datasets (BraTS21, FLARE21, AMOS22). (R1, R3)
- The network can adaptively adjust the offset of sampled locations, focusing attention on semantically relevant organ positions. (R2)
- Dynamic adjustment of offsets and modulation scalars helps mitigate inductive biases inherent in traditional CNNs, achieving transformer-like spatial aggregation. (R2)
- The MGDC blocks introduce a hybrid deformable convolution and MLP structure for effective channel scaling and enhanced feature learning. (R2)
- The paper is well organized and clearly written. (R4)
- The effectiveness of the proposed method is well supported by experiments. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty is limited:** The method is a combination of widely used techniques. The reverse bottleneck (page 5) closely resembles the inverted residual block from MobileNetV2. 3D deformable convolution has already been applied to videos [2]. The integration of the multi-group mechanism with deformable convolution is not sufficiently detailed, making it unclear how this configuration differs from existing grouped convolutions with deformable kernels. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Improvements over prior methods are marginal or not significant:** Figure 1 shows self‑attention performance comparable to MGDC. Figure 3 and Figures 4 and 5 show only very small improvements. (R1, R3)
- **Computational efficiency claims are not supported:** The only complexity‑reducing component is depthwise convolution, but many added components (linear layers, SoftMax, transformer, extra group loop) increase complexity. Tables 1 and 2 show SegResNet inference time 0.78s with memory 3.3G versus MGDC‑UNet 2.08s with 9.4G memory. Time and memory are not reported in Table 3, which is inconsistent with earlier tables. (R1)
- **Claim about kernel size is contradicted:** On page 6 the authors claim that increasing kernel size from 3 to 7 yields enhancements, but some results in Table 2 disprove this. (R1)
- **Missing experimental details needed for reproducibility:** Batch size, learning rate, loss function, learning rate schedule, optimizer, and loss‑function parameters are not provided. (R2)
- **Data strategy is unclear:** The division of training, validation, and test sets, implementation of 5‑fold cross‑validation, and criteria for selecting the best model in each fold are not specified. (R2)
- **Lack of evaluation on datasets with complex pathological features:** All tested datasets have objects with clear boundaries. To better assess the claim of capturing complex pathological features, experiments on datasets such as RETOUCH (retinal OCT fluid detection) are recommended. (R2)
- **No results on small datasets:** The method was not tested on small datasets like MRBrainS or MICCAI iSEG (minor issue). (R4)
- **No discussion of limitations.** (R4)
- **Title is too general:** The paper only evaluates on three datasets (brain tumor, CT multi‑organ, cross‑modality MR/CT) and cannot represent all medical image segmentation tasks; the title should be changed accordingly. (R1, R4)
