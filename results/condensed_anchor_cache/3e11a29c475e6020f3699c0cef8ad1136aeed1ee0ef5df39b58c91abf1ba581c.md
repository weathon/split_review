## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PINCO, a physics-informed graph neural network (GNN) combining GNNs and PINNs to solve the AC optimal power flow (AC-OPF) problem. The goal is to obtain fast, feasible solutions without using an optimization solver for training. While reviewers acknowledge the relevance of the problem and the conceptual combination of GNNs and PINNs, the overall assessment is critical. Major concerns include limited novelty (the approach combines existing tools without significant architectural or algorithmic advances), insufficient experimental validation (no comparisons against other ML methods, small-scale systems up to 118 buses, unrealistic data distribution), large constraint violations (equality loss up to 16 MW, 10% violations in Q/V), scalability issues (10–24 hour training times, no demonstration on 10,000+ bus systems), incomplete literature review, and unsupported claims (e.g., “without violations”). One reviewer (R2, score 5) rated the paper higher, praising its clarity and inference speed, but the majority view (three scores 1, one score 3) is that the contributions are insufficient and the feasibility results are not convincing.

### Strengths
- Addresses a relevant and important optimization problem in power systems (AC-OPF).
- Combines graph neural networks (GNNs) and physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) in a single framework.
- Uses a graph-based architecture that, in principle, supports changes in grid topology (though not experimentally demonstrated).
- Employs a self-supervised (unsupervised) training scheme that does not require expensive solver-generated data.
- One reviewer (R2) found the paper clear and well-structured, with effective use of established methodologies, and highlighted that the model achieves feasible solutions (quantified via Equality Loss) while offering faster inference times compared to the MIPS solver.
- Another reviewer (R4) noted promising numerical performance relative to the MIPS solver.
- Attempts to enforce inequality constraints to zero violation (though several reviewers question the significance and efficacy of this).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty.** The approach primarily combines existing tools (GNNs and PINNs) without substantial novel architectural or algorithmic modifications. Use of GNNs for AC-OPF is not new (reviewers note prior works); self-supervised training has also been used before. The paper incorrectly claims to be the first to use PINNs for AC-OPF – several existing works have already done so (R1: [1]–[2]). The claim about handling multiple generators per bus is not new; prior works such as *Confidence-Aware Graph Neural Networks for Learning Reliability Assessment Commitments* (TPWRS 2023) and *CANOS* (arXiv:2403.17660) already address this, and multiple generators can be easily aggregated (R5).
- **Insufficient experimental comparisons.** The paper does not compare against any existing ML methods for AC-OPF (e.g., GNN-only, PINN-only, CANOS, Compact Optimization Learning). Comparisons are limited to the MIPS solver. Missing comparisons against other optimization-based solvers such as SDP relaxation, SOCP relaxation, and linear-OPF initialized Newton–Raphson (R1, R3, R4, R5).
- **Large constraint violations undermine feasibility claims.** The paper claims “without violations,” but equality constraint violations reach up to 16 MW (Table 2) and 20 MW when comparing to MIPS on the 118-bus system (R1, R3). Figure 3 shows violations in reactive power (Q) and voltage magnitude (V) up to ~10%, which is unacceptable for power system operators (R3). The augmented Lagrangian formulation in Eq. (5) is theoretically unsound: the quadratic penalty term \(\mu_k g_j(w_u^k)^2\) drives all inequality constraints toward being binding (\(g(w)=0\)), even when the constraint should remain slack. A counterexample given (R5): \(\min_x x\) subject to \(x\le0\) leads to a finite solution for an unbounded problem. The claim of zero inequality violation is not substantial because inequality constraints here are simple variable bounds or l2-norm constraints that can be enforced by activation functions; the real challenge is satisfying both equality and inequality constraints simultaneously (R5).
- **Scalability concerns.** Experiments are limited to very small systems (max 118 buses), 100× smaller than real-life instances (10,000+ buses). Training times are 10–24 hours, which is too large for practical deployment; a reasonable target would be ≤8 hours for 10,000‑bus systems. Several existing works (e.g., *Spatial Network Decomposition*, *Compact Optimization Learning*, *CANOS*) have demonstrated AC-OPF learning at 10,000+ bus scale. The data distribution is not described; generating data by independently perturbing individual loads leads to unrealistic, narrow variability. Open-source datasets such as OPFData and OPFGenerator exist but are not used (R2, R5).
- **Incomplete literature review and missing context.** The paper does not cite relevant works on physics-informed GNNs for power system state estimation (PSSE) that motivate deep learning for power systems (R4). Key references related to end-to-end learning for constrained optimization are missing (R1). The model in Figure 1 appears identical to existing PINN models; if it differs, this should be clarified and cited (R1).
- **Lack of adaptability to topology changes.** The current formulation assumes a fixed grid topology; any change in circuit breaker status or maintenance would require retraining from scratch. The paper claims GNNs support topology changes but provides no experiments to demonstrate this. A more flexible framework accommodating varying topologies within a single model would be far more practical (R2, R5).
- **Missing ablation and interpretability studies.** No analysis is provided on the contribution of individual architectural components. Visualizations of training dynamics (e.g., equality loss and relative cost difference over epochs) are absent (R2). The purpose of Figure 3 is unclear; it does not directly relate to the OPF objective of minimizing generation cost (R2).
- **Notation and formulation issues.** Not all notation is defined before use: the formalism for equality/inequality constraints is not introduced before the augmented Lagrangian in Eq. (5), and the parameter \(w_u^k\) is undefined (R5). The use of only 500 demand samples for an unsupervised model is unexplained (R1). The abstract’s claim about addressing the limitation of interior-point methods regarding global optimality is not addressed – how does the current method improve upon this? (R1)
- **Practical concerns about infeasibility detection.** If the AC-OPF problem is infeasible, the neural network would still output a solution; it is unclear how an operator could detect infeasibility (R3).
- **Minority contrast.** One reviewer (R2, score 5) rated the paper higher, praising its clarity, structure, and inference speed. However, the majority (three reviewers with score 1, one with score 3) found the contributions insufficient, the experimental validation weak, and the feasibility claims unsupported.
