## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces InCoDe, a framework for interpretable control of diffusion image generators using compressed, human-interpretable descriptions. InCoDe selects informative query-answer pairs via Information Pursuit, then conditions a diffusion model (SD 1.4) on the resulting concept vectors. It is evaluated on CLEVR, LSUN Bedroom, LSUN Church, and two new custom datasets (LSUN Bedroom and Church augmented with query-answer pairs, to be released). Reviewers find the work relevant and mostly well-presented, but raise concerns about limited flexibility due to fixed query sets, reliance on VQA accuracy, evaluation scope, and limited novelty beyond prior work.

### Strengths
- **Well-written and polished**: The paper is clear, with well-explained equations and diagrams (Fig. 3, 4) that make the training procedure easy to follow (R1). Examples throughout provide good intuition (R3).
- **Novel, user-friendly interface**: The query-answer framework allows intuitive control without technical prompts, enhancing interpretability and user experience (R1, R2). Users can see how each attribute shapes the final image (R2).
- **Solid baselines and significant gains**: InCoDe is compared against strong baselines (Fig. 8, 9) and achieves notable improvements in image quality (R1). Comprehensive experimental validation across diverse datasets (R2).
- **Information-theoretic foundation**: The method leverages information-theoretic principles to prioritize the most informative attributes, which is principled (R3).
- **Conditioning pre-trained diffusion models**: The approach of conditioning existing diffusion models while smoothing training is interesting and practical (R3).
- **New datasets**: The authors promise to release two new datasets (LSUN Bedroom and LSUN Church with QA pairs), which fills a gap in currently available resources (R1).
- **Relevant and timely contribution**: The work focuses on interpretability and controllability, which are important for the community (R4). It offers a solid extension of Chattopadhyay et al. (2023) into image generation (R4).
- **Reproducibility details**: The appendix (A.3) provides thorough descriptions of model architecture, datasets, and hardware setup (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited flexibility due to fixed query sets**: All four datasets rely on a small, hand-selected set of 40–58 queries. Users cannot generate images with attributes or combinations not present in the predefined set, which severely limits real-world applicability (R1, R2, R3). For example, an unusual combination of attributes not covered by the query set cannot be achieved (R2). This makes the method unfeasible for large real-world datasets like ImageNet or COCO (R3).
- **Evaluation scope and outdated models**: The method is demonstrated on SD 1.4, while newer, more capable diffusion models exist (R3). Image quality metrics such as FID are missing; relying solely on BLIP does not assess generation quality – it is possible InCoDe follows attributes better but decreases overall image quality (R3). A comparison to an off-the-shelf diffusion model conditioned on, e.g., CelebA facial attributes, is missing (R4). The paper claims generative models struggle with multi-concept composition but only shows a qualitative example (Fig. 2); quantitative analysis and comparison to established work are absent (R4). The status of Table 2 in this context is unclear (R4).
- **Reliance on VQA accuracy**: The framework depends on accurate VQA responses; errors propagate to image generation. For instance, misinterpreting a query about object color leads to an incorrectly colored generated image (R2).
- **Limited theoretical novelty**: The theoretical foundations are largely taken from prior work (Chattopadhyay et al., 2023) and adapted to the image generation domain (R3, R4).
- **Oversimplified related work**: The discussion of related methods is too brief; it lacks details on how InCoDe compares to attention-based control methods, disentangled representations for semantic manipulation, or other interpretability approaches (R1).
- **Writing and presentation issues**: Some parts are hard to follow (e.g., lines 342–351) and numerical results are not clearly linked to tables (R4). Links to supplementary material are broken; single-file submission was encouraged (R4). Figures 8 and 9 appear as raster graphics and look blurry when zoomed (R1). Figure 8 has potentially incorrect axis names (R3). Acronyms in figures: “DT-IC”, “TopK-IC”, “Stab. D”, “Stu. D” are not defined (R4). Are these related to lines 342–351? (R4)
- **Unanswered technical questions**: In Figure 3, why does the QueryAnswerer compute \(\hat{Q}(X)\) instead of \(\hat{Q}(\hat{X})\)? (R4) How is the constraint in Equation (2) enforced in practice? (R4) Is the Decoder module necessary if the goal is to produce a modified image? (R4)
