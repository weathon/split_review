## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces BioBridge, a parameter-efficient framework that bridges independently trained unimodal foundation models (FMs) for biomedical entities (proteins, drugs, diseases) by learning cross-modal transformations via a knowledge graph (KG). The transformation is parametrized by a transformer module and learned with a contrastive (InfoNCE) loss, while all underlying FMs remain frozen. BioBridge outperforms baseline KG embedding methods in cross-modal retrieval tasks (on average by ~76.3%), demonstrates out-of-domain generalization, and shows potential for biomedical QA and drug generation. Reviews are divided: two reviewers rate the paper 8 (high confidence), two rate it 6; the higher-rated reviews are more enthusiastic about the problem framing and experiments, while the lower-rated ones raise concerns about baselines, missing ablations, and limited multimodality.

### Strengths
- The problem of aligning large unimodal biomedical FMs (for proteins, drugs, diseases) is highly relevant and well motivated. The paper is clearly written and easy to follow.  
- The core idea – learning cross-modal transformations in embedding space while keeping unimodal FMs frozen – is sensible, novel, and parameter efficient (only the bridge module is updated).  
- Extensive experiments are conducted on cross-modal retrieval, protein–protein interaction, protein–phenotype matching, and tail entity prediction across several benchmarks. The method beats baseline KG embedding methods (by ~76.3% on average) and is sometimes the only applicable solution.  
- Interesting applications are demonstrated: cross-modal retrieval, gene–phenotype matching, biomedical QA, and guided drug generation.  
- The method shows out-of-domain generalization ability by extrapolating to unseen modalities or relations.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited scope of multimodality**: The term “multimodal” is confined to biomedical entity types in the KG; experiments lack text–image tasks (e.g., medical imaging). The method is more accurately described as a biomedical KG foundation model, as it relies on KG structure and cannot handle modalities outside the KG.  
- **Weak baseline comparisons**:  
  - Baselines in Section 4.1 are several years old and not state-of-the-art. Recent multi-modal KG embedding methods (e.g., MMKRL, cross-modal KG contrastive learning, DFMKE) and GNN-based link prediction methods are missing.  
  - Comparisons are predominantly with KG link prediction methods, making it unclear whether the advantages stem from the transformation learning or from the KG supervision itself. Baselines that do not use KG supervision are needed to isolate the contribution.  
- **Missing ablations and hyperparameter analysis**: No ablation studies on the contrastive learning objective (InfoNCE vs. SimCLR, MoCo, etc.) or on key hyperparameters (temperature, batch size, learning rate). The creation of negative samples is not described.  
- **Unmotivated design choices**:  
  - No justification for using a transformer over simpler alternatives (MLP, vanilla autoencoder) to encode the transformation.  
  - Equation 1 makes the transformed embedding depend on (1) target modality type, (2) relation type, and (3) individual node in the target modality. The authors do not motivate this design or explain how similarity search works with node-dependent embeddings; mapping to a single unified space is not discussed.  
  - The contrastive loss (Eq. 2) is one of many options (e.g., self-supervised link prediction); no experiments with alternative transformation techniques are provided.  
- **Data leakage concern for semantic similarity**: Protein nodes are connected to GO terms in PrimeKG, so the embedding may already implicitly know labels during training. Holdout of protein–GO links is needed to address this.  
- **Molecule generation case study lacks rigor**: No quantitative assessment (comparison with existing molecule generation methods or a baseline of retrieving top K entities from the KG). It is unclear whether retrieved molecules are novel predictions or existing links.  
- **Clarity gaps in methodology**:  
  - Related work lacks coverage of embedding alignment literature (e.g., deep CCA).  
  - The methodology section is very short; assumptions and the theorem from the appendix should be in the main text.  
  - It is unclear which parameters are optimized with the contrastive loss since the FMs are frozen.  
  - For PPI (same modality), why is a bridge module needed?  
  - For the cross-species task, why is phenotype not available in PrimeKG?  
  - Is a single model trained on all bridge transformations, or is a separate model trained per task?  
- **No discussion of limitations or failure modes**; the paper would benefit from an analysis of scenarios where BioBridge might underperform.  
- **Generalizability concerns**: The strong dependence on KG structure raises questions about applicability to novel entities or other domains where such structured knowledge is unavailable or incomplete.  
- **Further open questions**:  
  - Can the method be applied to image captioning or visual question answering?  
  - How would a model trained on paired multimodal data compare to BioBridge’s approach of training on large unimodal data?  
  - What challenges would arise when adapting BioBridge to non-biomedical domains?
