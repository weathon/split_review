## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers generally acknowledge the novelty and potential of the proposed Recurrent Neuro-Symbolic Forward Chaining Network (RNS-FCN) for discovering latent predicates and rules, but they raise significant concerns about technical contribution, experimental evaluation, and performance. The framework is described as interesting and creative, yet similar to prior work (R5, Lu et al. ICLR 2022). One reviewer (score 5) is notably more positive about the architecture and its ability to handle latent variables; the other three (scores 3) are critical of insufficient validation, weak empirical results, and lack of comparisons.

### Strengths
- The proposed RNS-FCN bridges the gap between traditional rule induction and latent variable modeling, offering a novel architecture with well-defined variables and justified design choices (recurrent unit, backpropagation, softmin to prevent diminishing values while preserving differentiability) (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- The framework provides a robust tool for discovering hidden structures and generating new concepts, with practical value for handling unobservable latent variables in real-world reasoning tasks (Reviewers 1,2).
- The end-to-end approach enhances the ability to uncover latent factors, applicable to user behavior analysis and advanced decision-making in AI (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is well written and easy to follow; the solution is creative and promising (Reviewer 4).
- Simulations on synthetic data help readers gain an intuitive understanding of the approach; evaluations on both synthetic and real datasets are performed (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty**: Section 4.3 merely covers standard backpropagation, which does not warrant inclusion as a significant contribution (Reviewer 1). The framework is very similar to R5 (Lu et al., ICLR 2022), including a forward inference stage, backward rule refining stage, invented predicates, and analogous Figure 1b; the paper does not distinguish itself from or compare with R5 (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient evaluation**: The method is demonstrated on only two small real-world datasets (Heloc, Adult) and one synthetic dataset. The synthetic experiments are too limited—they do not study varying number of rules, noise, or additional datasets. The correct set of rules was not recovered in the synthetic study despite small size and no noise (Reviewers 1,4).
- **Weak empirical performance**: On the Adult dataset, accuracy and F1 of RNS-FCN are inferior to several baselines, yet the method is marked as best in Table 1. Performance improvement on Heloc and Adult is incremental. No explanation is provided for the poor Adult performance (Reviewers 2,3,4). The baselines are very old (Reviewer 3). The method does not seem to work effectively according to the results (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of interpretability analysis**: It is unclear how the approach is more interpretable than neural network baselines; the introduction of nonlinear functions (softmin) and optimization via backpropagation may complicate interpretability rather than enhance it (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing ablation study**: An ablation study is necessary to verify the contribution and impact of each component in the framework (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing experimental details**: Hyperparameter tuning and selection, use of a validation set, determination of embedding dimensionality \(d\), and computational complexity are not reported. The number of iterations is considered critical but not justified (Reviewers 2,4).
- **Incomplete analysis of latent factors**: The motivation for latent conditions is not completely clear; no explicit real-world examples (e.g., in healthcare) of factors that cannot be directly measured are given. The meaning of inferred latent values is not analyzed—e.g., whether they relate to removed factors in synthetic data or if latents are actually needed for Adult (Reviewer 4).
- **Code and data not provided**: The only file in the supplementary pack is `untitled.ipython`, which does not look like valid code. The synthetic dataset generated by the authors is not provided (Reviewer 3).
- **Citation style is incorrect** (Reviewer 3). The paper should tone down overclaims (e.g., “multi-step inferencing akin to human thought”) and include a proper discussion (Reviewer 4).
- **Unaddressed questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - How does the method compare to Diffnaps (NP Walter et al., AAAI 24), one of the most recent predictive rule-mining methods? (Reviewer 4)
  - Is “latent predicate” first invented? If not, original citations must be given; if yes, explain necessity (Reviewer 3).
  - How does post-hoc labeling of latents work, and is it used in real-world data? (Reviewer 4)
  - How close are predicted rule embeddings to predicate embeddings (e.g., cosine similarity)? (Reviewer 4)
  - What is the runtime complexity, average number of recurrences \(T\) until convergence, and effective runtime? Are there cases where \(v^t\) does not converge? (Reviewer 4)
  - What is the difference between Figure 5a and 5b? (Reviewer 4)
  - Why was sampling to a 1:1 negative/positive label ratio necessary? Is the method not robust to imbalanced data? (Reviewer 4)
  - Is it optimal to initialize unobserved predicates with a random guess? Is there analysis supporting this? (Reviewer 2)
  - How can predicate embeddings be pretrained to capture meanings and relationships? (Reviewer 2)
  - How are conjunctive combinations of predicate embeddings defined? (Reviewer 2)
