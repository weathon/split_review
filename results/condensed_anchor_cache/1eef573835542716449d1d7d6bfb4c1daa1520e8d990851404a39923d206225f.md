## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Lyapunov Uncertainty Control (LUC), an offline RL method that uses Lyapunov stability and control-invariant sets to constrain the agent to a low-uncertainty state region, thereby reducing Bellman uncertainty. LUC employs a Q-ensemble to regulate uncertainty growth. The authors provide theoretical analysis and empirical results on MuJoCo tasks (D4RL suite), with additional experiments on OOD states and ablations. One reviewer (rating 6) views LUC as a novel combination of tools with thorough experiments and strong performance; two reviewers (ratings 3, 3) find the contribution limited, the theoretical results hard to interpret, and the empirical evaluation insufficient.

### Strengths
- Novel approach combining Lyapunov stability with offline RL, offering a theoretically grounded mechanism for uncertainty management. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- Clear paper structure. (Reviewer 1)
- Strong performance in the Walker2D task (MuJoCo). (Reviewer 2)
- Clear visualization of uncertainty resolution in OOD states. (Reviewer 2)
- Experiments cover the full D4RL suite, with additional OOD and ablation studies, demonstrating soundness and the ability to outperform some existing offline RL methods. (Reviewer 3)
- Theoretical results show conceptual soundness and how errors in algorithm components influence downstream performance. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and similarity to existing methods:** The contribution is limited given the well-studied problem of partial data coverage/distributional shift. The Q-ensemble regularization closely resembles EDAC’s approach without clearly articulating how it differs in mechanism or how it contributes to Lyapunov stability beyond standard ensemble regularization. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Inadequate evaluation scope and missing baselines:** Experiments are restricted to MuJoCo tasks; key benchmarks such as AntMaze or Android-based tasks are omitted, limiting generalizability claims. Several relevant and state-of-the-art offline RL baselines are missing, including Conservative Q-Learning (CQL), the method from “Pessimistic Q-learning for offline reinforcement learning: Towards optimal sample complexity” (Shi et al., 2022), and distributionally robust offline RL methods (e.g., Panaganti et al., 2022; Shi & Chi, 2022). The choice of included baselines (e.g., RORL) is questioned without explanation for omitting other robust/conservative approaches. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Theoretical results lack interpretability and practical connection:** Assumptions are not well-motivated, and their implications for algorithm performance are not discussed. The connection between theory and the practical LUC algorithm is unclear, and the guarantees are not compared to those in other offline RL papers. (Reviewer 1; also noted by Reviewer 3 as needing more discussion)
- **Insufficient experimental details:** The experimental settings and evaluation metrics are not sufficiently detailed, hampering reproducibility and fair comparison with other studies. (Reviewer 1)
- **A relevant related work is omitted:** The paper “In-Distribution Barrier Functions: Self-Supervised Policy Filters that Avoid Out-of-Distribution States” should be discussed. (Reviewer 3)
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms:**  
  - How does the Q-ensemble in LUC differ from EDAC’s in terms of policy stability and uncertainty control? (Reviewer 2)  
  - What are the specific similarities and differences between LUC and CQL in handling OOD data/distributional shift? (Reviewer 2)  
  - Why are distributionally robust RL works (e.g., DRRL foundations and offline DRRL methods) not compared or discussed? (Reviewer 1)  
  - Is the theoretical guarantee comparable to those in other offline RL papers? (Reviewer 1)  
  - Have the authors considered testing on AntMaze or Android-based environments to demonstrate generalizability? (Reviewer 2)
