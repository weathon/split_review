## Merged Review

### Summary
LaMPlace is a learning-based macro placement method that optimizes cross-stage metrics (WNS, TNS, HPWL) by training an offline predictor to generate a pixel-level mask that quantifies placement impact. Experimental results on commonly used benchmarks show average 9.6% improvement across key metrics, with 43.0% improvement in WNS and 30.4% in TNS.

### Strengths
- Directly optimizes final chip quality metrics (cross-stage) rather than intermediate surrogate metrics, addressing a critical gap in existing placement methods. [R2,R4]
- Strong zero-shot generalization: the offline predictor generalizes to unseen benchmarks without requiring extensive fine-tuning or retraining. [R1,R2]
- The method concurrently improves multiple cross-stage metrics including HPWL, WNS, and TNS. [R1]
- The mask-based placement enables efficient optimization of cross-stage metrics without the high computational cost of repeatedly running post-placement stages. [R2,R4]
- Novelty of the L-flow and L-mask formulation was appreciated. Experiments are comprehensive and cover multiple design metrics. [R3]
- The offline training setting is practical for industry due to abundant chip data from design projects. [R4]

### Weaknesses
- **Handling of non-edge-connected macros**: The algorithm struggles when macros are not edge-connected (i.e., have sparse or no interconnections). The graph-based approach may produce suboptimal placements for designs with heterogeneous macro connectivity patterns. *[R1, minority reviewer, high confidence; other reviewers did not raise this issue.]*
- **Offline data reliance**: The method depends on substantial offline chip data for training, raising questions about its adaptability to entirely new chip architectures or design paradigms not represented in the training data. *[R4]*
- **Black-box predictor**: The predictor lacks transparency; it is difficult to interpret placement decisions. The authors did not discuss if there were strange or counter-intuitive placement examples that could provide insight into the predictor’s behavior. *[R4]*
- **Scalability concerns**: The paper does not analyze computational complexity or memory requirements for very large-scale designs (millions of cells). Details on how mask generation runtime and memory scale with number of macros and grid size are missing. *[R4]*
- **Experimental gaps and missing explanations**:
  - The claim of translation-invariance with respect to macro positions needs clarification: macro positions relative to chip boundaries affect standard-cell placement and thus metrics like congestion and timing; the extent to which this assumption holds and when it might be violated is not explained. [R3]
  - Pair-wise ranking implementation details are insufficient: how pairs of macro placement solutions are selected, the relative ranking criteria, loss function, and optimization procedure should be described. [R3]
  - HPWL is a surrogate metric; results for post-route wirelength would be a more complete evaluation. [R3]
  - Macros smaller than the grid size: how does LaMPlace handle them? [R1]
  - Some tables (Table 4, Table 8) are missing units. [R1]
  - DREAMPlace results (Figure 9) show blank rectangular areas and overlapping macros; timing-driven option in DREAMPlace does not seem to support macros effectively – controls or comparisons with that option disabled are needed. [R1]
  - An ablation study on the size of the offline dataset is missing; the paper does not show results for different dataset sizes. [R4]
  - Implementation details and code release for review would strengthen the work. [R4]
