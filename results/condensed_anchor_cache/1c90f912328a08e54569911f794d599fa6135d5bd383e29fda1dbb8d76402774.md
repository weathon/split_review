## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces ADer, a modular benchmark library for multi-class visual anomaly detection that implements 15 state-of-the-art methods, 9 evaluation metrics, and 11 datasets across industrial, medical, and general-purpose domains. It also proposes ADEval, a GPU-accelerated evaluation package. There is notable disagreement among reviewers: one reviewer (score 8) is highly positive, praising the extensive benchmarking, scalability, and acceleration, while three others (scores 3, 3, 5) are critical of novelty, framework/algorithmic depth, experimental rigor, and missing coverage.

### Strengths

- Addresses the critical gap of unfair comparisons in multi-class anomaly detection by providing a unified, standardized evaluation protocol with integrated experimental setup (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Comprehensive benchmark: 15 state-of-the-art methods, 11 datasets, 9 metrics, covering industrial, medical, and general-purpose domains (R1, R2, R3).
- ADEval GPU acceleration package significantly improves evaluation speed for time-consuming metrics like mAU-PRO on large-scale datasets (R1, R2).
- Source code provided and committed to open-source; library also supports single-class settings and semantic AD datasets such as CIFAR-10 (R2).
- Well-organized and clearly written (R2).
- Thorough experimental results enabling intuitive comparison of method effectiveness, including stability analysis and cross-domain dataset correlations (R1, R4).
- Emphasis on method stability—important because prior works often report best results over multiple trials (R4).
- Tables are easy to read and well presented (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty and scope**: The paper is primarily an integration of existing methods, datasets, and metrics, which does not meet the novelty expected for ICLR. Multiple reviewers (R1, R3) noted this limitation; one suggested incorporating representation learning techniques or discussions to strengthen relevance to the ICLR audience (R1).
- **Insufficient justification and coverage of multi-class setting**: Lack of clear rationale for focusing solely on multi-class AD; challenges mentioned in Section 2.2 are not clearly addressed by ADer (R1). Additionally, the benchmark does not cover conventional one-class-one-model UAD settings (R2) or zero-few-shot AD models such as WinCLIP (R2).
- **Superficial descriptions of methods, datasets, and metrics**: Descriptions of the 15 methods are limited to one paragraph and four broad categories, lacking key innovations and the specific deficiencies each method attempts to correct. Datasets are only briefly introduced in a paragraph and table; qualitative differences (e.g., MVTec-AD being simple and solved vs. more challenging VisA and RealIAD) are not explained early. The 9 evaluation metrics lack detailed explanation of their meaning, advantages, limitations, and complementarity. This makes the analysis inaccessible to non-experts (R4). A similar concern about metric explanation was raised independently (R2).
- **Insufficient framework details** (R3): The ADer framework is described only via a brief Figure 3; the process and method for integrating existing or self-developed algorithms are not elaborated. No experimental comparison of the integrated vs. non-integrated usage is provided. Reference to the more detailed description in MMDetection is recommended.
- **ADEval insufficiently described** (R3): Only a brief example of time improvement is given, without algorithmic details (e.g., the iterative-accumulating algorithm used) or supporting experimental data for time reduction. Reviewer 3 requested clarification on the optimizations ADEval brings and experimental data for metrics like mAU-PRO on large-scale datasets.
- **Limited standardization in experimental setup** (R3): Only image resolution and training epochs are fixed; other parameters remain as defined by the original methods. This does not fully demonstrate ADer’s role in ensuring fairness, and results could be compared using original code with the same parameters.
- **Reproducibility and statistical rigor** (R2): Benchmark results are based on single runs without calculating standard deviation. The rationale for this choice is not provided; multiple runs are needed to assess randomness and reproducibility.
- **PatchCore description inaccuracies and missing evaluation** (R4): Describing PatchCore as “training-free” is misleading—it involves feature extraction and sub-sampling which constitute a training procedure. Consequently, Table 1 should include training time for PatchCore. Furthermore, the claim that PatchCore cannot run on datasets larger than MVTec-AD is contradicted by published works using PatchCore on VisA (Fucka et al., Zhang et al.). PatchCore should be evaluated on all datasets or a clear technical/resource limitation should be provided.
- **Missing recent high-performing methods** (R4): Diffusion-based methods (e.g., TransFusion, DiffusionAD) and EfficientAD are not included despite reported state-of-the-art results on VisA. The criteria for method selection should be explicitly explained.
- **Presentation issues**: Missing values in Table 6 (R2); broken links in lines 374 and 430 (R2); text in Figure 1 is too small (R2). Section 3.5 could be better placed earlier to highlight ADer’s advantages (R1).
