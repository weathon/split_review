## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an unsupervised, source-free alignment method to reuse a pre-trained sequential VAE (seqVAE) across different neural recordings. The approach freezes the learned latent dynamics and encoder, learns a new linear decoder for new observations, and learns an implicit nonlinear mapping from new observations to the space of old observations. Validation is done on simulations and a monkey reaching dataset. Three reviewers rated the paper 6 (fairly positive but with significant concerns), and one reviewer (R3) rated it 8 (very positive, noting strong motivation and qualitative improvements). There is agreement on the importance of the problem but disagreement on the strength of the experimental validation.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important neuroscience problem: aligning multiple datasets across sessions/animals to avoid retraining models from scratch and to leverage available data. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- The method is theoretically well-motivated, analytically tractable, and provides mathematical proof of optimality in a simplified case. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and clearly motivated. (R1, R2)
- The approach is original – to the reviewers’ knowledge, a new way to reuse pre-trained generative models for new neural data. (R2, R4)
- The approach produces real qualitative improvements in learned embeddings, as shown in Figures 3 and 4 (R3; minority more positive view: R3 found experiments convincing, while others found them limited).
- The method is fairly flexible: apart from the linear decoder assumption (a data-availability limitation), it does not require a specific architecture. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient baselines**: Only ERDiff is compared in Table 1. Missing standard methods such as Procrustes alignment, canonical correlation analysis (CCA), adversarial training, and other classical cross-session alignment techniques. (R1, R3)
- **Limited experimental scope**: Only one real neural dataset (monkey reaching) is used. Needed: diverse datasets, varying noise levels, different recording modalities (e.g., LFP, calcium imaging). (R1, R4)
- **Unclear novelty over ERDiff**: ERDiff also uses a generative model (score-based) for alignment. The paper does not clearly articulate what specific limitations of ERDiff it overcomes or what novel aspects are added beyond the existing framework. (R1)
- **Proposition 1 guarantee is weak**: The guarantee says the learned alignment is a linear offset away from optimal, but the offset \(B\) could be large, especially when noise characteristics differ. The proposition does not provide a meaningful bound. (R2)
- **Alignment function \(g_\theta\) not evaluated**: The choice of \(g_\theta\) is critical. If it is a pointwise nonlinear function, it implicitly assumes latent dynamics are *identical* between old and new observations – a strong assumption that may not hold in real-world scenarios. If too flexible (e.g., another full RNN), it defeats the purpose of reusing the pre-trained model. No analysis of trade-offs is provided. (R2)
- **Strong assumption of shared dynamics limits applicability**: The approach assumes the same qualitative latent dynamics across datasets, which essentially implies the same task setup. It is unlikely that a task-free dataset could be transferred to a task-based one – this limitation is not acknowledged. (R3)
- **Missing details on latent dynamics model**: How is \(p_\theta(x_t|x_{t-1})\) modeled? Are smoothness constraints added? Figure 2b shows the proposed method produces much smoother latent trajectories than ERDiff – the reason is unclear. (R4)
- **Missing description of FA+LDS baseline**: The method "FA+LDS" compared in Figure 2 is not explained. How is it implemented? (R4)
- **Counterintuitive result in Figure 2e**: The mean k-step \(r^2\) for Re-training *decreases* as the number of training samples increases. Intuitively, more samples should improve \(r^2\). (R4)
- **Table 1 results unclear**: What results are shown (simulation or real data)? The \(r^2\) numbers do not match those in Figure 4. Also, why is the k-step MSE standard deviation smaller than the standard deviation of MSE for both ERDiff and the proposed method? (R4)
- **Contradictory performance in Figure 4**: The proposed method performs better than ERDiff for Poisson observations but not for smoothed observations (especially Figure 4b top-left). This discrepancy is not explained. (R4)
- **Assumptions of fixed prior and encoder not checked**: The method fixes the prior dynamics \(p(x_t|x_{t-1})\) and the encoder \(q(x|y_{embed})\). Would jointly training on two datasets produce similar latent dynamics and encoder? The paper does not verify this. (R4)
- **Clarification needed on ELBO and dynamics**: Why is measuring log predictive probability several steps ahead important for good embedding? (R3)
- **Clarification needed on equation details**: Reviewer 2 asks if \(q_\phi(x_t,x_{t-1}|y_{1:T}) = q_\phi(x_t|y_{1:T}) q_\phi(x_{t-1}|y_{1:T})\) in Eq. 3 and if the equation should use \(p_\theta(x_t|x_{t-1:t-k})\) instead of \(p_\theta(x_t|x_{t-1})\). (R2)
