## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a 3D-aware image generation framework that adapts a pre-trained 2D GAN (e.g., GANSpace) for multi-view consistent image generation. It uses a self-calibrated depth and pose estimation network (Monodepth2, trained unsupervised) and a latent refiner based on a diffusion model that refines latent codes with warp and consistency losses, enabling view-consistent manipulation without requiring 3D data or camera distribution priors. Experiments on FFHQ and SDIP Elephants datasets show qualitative and quantitative (FID, KID) improvements over existing 2D GAN-based, 3D-aware GAN, and diffusion-based methods.

### Strengths
1. Novel method that adapts a pre-trained 2D GAN for multi-view consistent generation without requiring large-scale 3D datasets or prior camera pose knowledge.
2. Comprehensive comparisons with 2D GAN-based (e.g., GANSpace), 3D-aware GAN (EpiGRAF, EG3D, GMPI), and diffusion-based (SyncDreamer, Zero123) baselines.
3. The approach is simple—using an additional warp loss for supervision—yet significantly improves multi-view consistency over the basic 2D GAN + GANSpace pipeline, combining the high quality of 2D generative models with the consistency of 3D-aware models.
4. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear and informative figures.
5. The proposed latent refiner with multi-view and geometry-preserving capabilities achieves high 3D consistency; visual results and quantitative evaluations (FID, KID) are good.

### Weaknesses
1. **Technical details insufficient**: The main text lacks specific details on the latent refiner (guided diffusion model and latent manipulation). It does not explain why guided diffusion was chosen as the refiner (Reviewers 1, 3).
2. **Missing ablation experiments**: The abstract states that LATENT REFINEMENT provides better view consistency, but there are no ablation experiments in the main paper to verify this key contribution. The only ablation is in supplementary material (Reviewer 1). Similarly, no ablation isolates the effect of the latent refiner vs. other components (Reviewer 1).
3. **Equation 3 inconsistency**: In Equation 3, the left side input is w+, the right side is w; the text above mentions warping G(w+), which is inconsistent with both the equation and Figure 2 pipeline (Reviewer 1).
4. **Unconvincing visual results for 3D-aware baselines**: The visual results for EpiGRAF and EG3D appear much poorer than in their original papers. For face or car datasets where camera distribution is known, 3D-aware models using explicit 3D representations should perform well; the proposed warp-based method looks superior, raising questions about the experimental setup (Reviewer 2).
5. **Missing comparison with related unposed 3D-aware GANs**: The paper claims multi-view consistency without camera prior but does not compare with methods like [1] (Learning 3D-Aware GANs from Unposed Images with Template Feature Field) that also avoid camera priors using DINO features trained unsupervised on 2D data (Reviewer 2).
6. **Dataset selection and generalization concerns**: Experiments focus primarily on faces and elephants. For multi-view face generation, the standard benchmark includes AFHQv2-Cat; results on this dataset are missing. For other tasks, only SDIP Elephants is used, which may lack persuasiveness. Comparisons with SyncDreamer and Zero123 suggest additional experiments on similar cases (e.g., Objaverse) are needed. The method’s advantage on more complex datasets (e.g., human figures) is not demonstrated (Reviewers 2, 3). Reviewer 4 calls the dataset choice “cherry-picked” and suggests applying the method on datasets used by SyncDreamer.
7. **Training time reporting unfair**: Table 1 reports training time only for the proposed components (e.g., 2 days) but omits the full training time including the base 2D GAN model. This makes the comparison misleading (Reviewer 2).
8. **Unreasonable 3D reconstruction claim**: The depth maps shown in Fig. 3 are poor quality, which would theoretically lead to a subpar point cloud. Therefore the claim of significant superiority in Section 4.4 (3D reconstruction) appears unreasonable (Reviewer 3).
9. **Discrepancy in GMPI FID**: The paper reports GMPI’s FID at 512 resolution on FFHQ as 54.95, but the original GMPI paper reports 8.29. This discrepancy raises concerns about the experimental setup and fairness of comparisons (Reviewer 3).
10. **Unsubstantiated claim about GAN vs. diffusion interpretability/speed**: Line 79-80 states that GAN latent spaces are more interpretable and faster than diffusion models, but the paper does not prove this. Reviewer 4 suggests applying the method on a diffusion backbone instead to verify.
11. **Unclear whether pretrained models were used for comparison methods**: For SyncDreamer and other baselines, it is unclear if a pretrained model was used or if it was retrained on FFHQ/Elephants. If pretrained, domain gap makes performance comparisons unfair, and training time comparisons are particularly unfair (Reviewer 4).
12. **Figure 2 ambiguity**: In Figure 2, the image d_w after depth estimation appears significantly different from G(W), which may be a schematic rather than an actual effect. This causes misunderstanding (Reviewer 1).
13. **Minority positive review (Reviewer 4, score 5) acknowledges several of the above weaknesses** (cherry-picked datasets, unsubstantiated claim, training time fairness, missing diffusion backbone test), but remains more positive overall about the method’s logic and writing.
