## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper studies cooperative asynchronous multi-agent multi-armed bandits where the subset of active agents at each round is unknown and time-varying. It proposes a fully distributed algorithm, SE-AAC-ODC, based on Successive Elimination with an Accuracy Adaptive Communication (AAC) protocol. The algorithm achieves near-optimal regret with constant (time-independent) communication rounds under adversarial asynchronicity. The paper also extends the algorithm to provide local differential privacy guarantees. Numerical simulations on synthetic data show communication efficiency improvements over baselines. Reviewers generally find the paper well-written and the constant communication result interesting, but raise concerns about originality, communication cost comparisons, missing experimental details, large constant factors in regret bounds, and several technical clarifications.

### Strengths

- Well-written, clearly organized, and easy to follow (R1, R2, R3).
- The algorithm design using an event-trigger/accuracy-adaptive communication protocol based on confidence radius is reasonable and promising (R1, R3, R4).
- Achieving constant communication rounds independent of the time horizon while maintaining near-optimal regret is a significant advantage; this is novel compared to previous explore-then-exploit approaches that achieve constant communication but not optimal regret (R1, R3, R4).
- Theoretical analysis appears correct (R2).
- Synthetic experiments demonstrate superior communication efficiency over existing baselines (R1, R3).
- Extension to local differential privacy is included (R2, R3).
- The problem setting (asynchronous multi-agent bandits) has real-world applications and is of interest to the research community (R1, R2).
- Algorithm is easy to implement (R1).

Note: While most reviewers find the work novel, one reviewer (R2) considers the algorithm and analysis extensions of prior works (Yang et al. 2023, Chen et al. 2023) and rates originality lower. See Weaknesses.

### Weaknesses

1.  **Communication cost claim potentially misleading.** Reviewer 2 notes that the paper claims its communication cost $O(KM \log \Delta^{-1})$ is much smaller than that of AAE-ODC (Chen et al. 2023) claimed as $O(KM^2 \Delta^{-2} \log T)$. However, this comparison omits the fact that AAE-ODC with exponential buffer thresholds has communication cost $O\big(M^2 \log (K \log T / \Delta^2)\big)$ (Corollary 2 in Chen et al. 2023). For large $K$ (where $K>M$), this latter scaling can be smaller than that of SE-AAC-ODC unless $T$ scales doubly exponentially in $K$, making the constant communication benefit vacuous in such regimes. This also puts the validity of the numerical experiments into question.

2.  **Lack of originality (contrast).** One reviewer (R2) states that the algorithm and analysis are extensions of known works (Yang et al. 2023 and Chen et al. 2023) and rates contribution as fair. In contrast, other reviewers (R1, R3, R4) consider the protocol novel and interesting. Reviewer 2 rated the paper 5 after discussion (initial score 3).

3.  **Missing details in numerical simulations.** 
    - The values of the buffer threshold used for the AAE-ODC baseline (from Chen et al. 2023) are not reported (R2).
    - Experiments use synthetic data only; real-world validation is absent (R1).
    - The number of agents in experiments is lower than the number of arms; impact on generalization not discussed (R4).

4.  **Large constant factor in regret bound.** Reviewer 3 notes that the constant term in the regret bound can be very large (e.g., with $\alpha=6$ used in experiments, the constant is 392), which significantly impacts practical performance for shorter time horizons. The paper does not compare theoretical regret bounds (not just orders) with those of alternative methods, nor does it plot regret and communication complexity bounds.

5.  **Unclear trade-off between regret and communication.** Reviewer 3 asks for clarification on the key metric (time vs. communication). In Figures 1-2, the proposed method has higher regret than UCB-IBC, and it is unclear in which practical scenarios communication efficiency outweighs regret minimization. Moreover, if $\alpha$ is reduced to match UCB-IBC's regret, it is uncertain whether communication savings remain significant. The effect of the $\alpha$ trade-off is not investigated. The paper also does not provide an intuitive explanation for how constant communication can still yield near-optimal regret (i.e., small $R(T)/T$ as $T$ increases).

6.  **Algorithm clarity and technical issues.**
    - Algorithm 1 lines 21–24 are unclear (R4).
    - Why agents send both an elimination message (line 7) and their estimates (line 15) is not motivated (R4).
    - The definition of the confidence radius in Eq. (1) (Section 3.2.1) may be missing a min(1, …) operator (R2).
    - The expectation in the pseudo-regret definition appears unwarranted as Algorithm 1 is deterministic (R4).
    - Remark 10's bound $\frac{e^\epsilon+1}{e^\epsilon-1} \leq 1 + \frac{1}{\epsilon}$ is incorrect; the right-hand side should be $1 + \frac{2}{\epsilon}$ (R2).
    - In the proof of Lemma 1, $M_t$ is not defined and the notation is misleading (R4).
    - In the proof of Theorem 2, step 3, the last equation on page 17 needs more detailed derivation (R4).

7.  **Missing lower bound on communication rounds.** Reviewers 1 and 2 note the absence of a lower bound for the number of communication rounds required in the considered model, making it difficult to assess optimality.

8.  **Strong assumption on the best arm.** The paper assumes there is a unique best arm ($\mu_1 > \mu_2 \geq \ldots$), which limits the scope of the study (R4).

9.  **Minor issues.** 
    - Typo: "Success Elimination" should be "Successive Elimination" (R1).
    - The superscript $+$ in $\mathbb{N}^+$ is redundant (R2).
    - Missing citation for gossip-based communication in Chawla et al. 2020 in the related work (R2).
