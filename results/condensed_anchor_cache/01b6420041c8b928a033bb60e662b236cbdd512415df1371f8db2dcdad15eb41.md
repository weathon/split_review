## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Diffusion Image GNN (DiG), which replaces CNNs or Transformers with graph neural networks as the backbone of diffusion models for image generation. Two variants are introduced: DiG_parallel and DiG_mix, each processing multi-scale graph representations of image patches. The authors argue that GNNs capture long-range dependencies efficiently while maintaining 2D grid structure.

### Strengths
- The writing is simple and easy to understand (R1).  
- The motivation is well-structured and logical; replacing CNNs/Transformers with GNNs is a good attempt (R1, R3, R4).  
- The paper is pioneering in applying GNNs as a backbone within diffusion models for image generation, which is a significant contribution (R3, minority positive reviewer, score 5).  
- Two distinct graph models (parallel and unified) are introduced to effectively model relationships between 2D image patches (R2).  
- The authors notice oversmoothing in deeper GNNs and address it by adding a special time-and-context node connected to all nodes (hyper node); this is an interesting and effective design (R2, R4).  
- Experimental results show that DiG can reduce computational consumption (R1; note this claim is contested by other reviewers on specific comparisons).  

### Weaknesses
- **Performance relative to transformers**: DiG does not show clear advantages over ViT-based backbones. In Tables 1 and 2, U-ViTs achieve better FID with lower flops in almost every case, and DiTs also outperform DiG (R1, R2, R3, R4). DiG_parallel often requires as many (if not more) parameters and flops than transformer baselines (R2, R3). The claim that DiG_parallel achieves a strong FID‑10K of 6.88 (patch size {2,4}) comparable to U‑ViT’s 6.07 is misleading because DiG introduces more parameters and complexity; when compared with ViT methods of similar parameter count or flops, DiG lags significantly (R3). This trend is consistent across all datasets in Table 2 (R3, R4).  
- **Insufficient novelty**: With the widespread use of large models, the computational overhead of attention mechanisms is no longer the primary limitation, and GNN performance does not match ViT; there are already many studies on computational efficiency (R1). The positive reviewer (R3) also acknowledges that results do not fully support the claims.  
- **Lack of in-depth explanation**: The paper does not analyze why improvements in FID occur—e.g., whether gains come from multi-scale graphs, or how GNN characteristics affect generation. Deeper analysis of each component’s actual contribution is needed (R1).  
- **Limited scale**: All experiments are conducted on relatively small images (maximum 64×64). It is unclear how DiG would scale to higher resolutions (R2).  
- **Missing comparisons**: No comparisons are made with hierarchical backbones trained for diffusion, such as UNet or Swin Transformer. Such baselines would help justify why a GNN approach is beneficial (R2).  
- **Variant justification**: DiG_parallel consistently outperforms DiG_mix in the experiments. The authors should explain the motivation for introducing DiG_mix and under what circumstances it would be advantageous (R4).  
- **Architecture and design questions** that overlap with criticisms or missing justifications:  
  - The motivation, main innovation, and difference from existing methods need to be clarified (R1).  
  - Why is 1‑dimensional positional encoding used? (R4).  
  - Given that GNN layers already use local aggregation and implicitly contain structural information, why is positional encoding still required? (R2).  
  - Provide deeper insight into why the graph is necessary for image generation; multi‑scale patch correlations are already modeled by transformer methods, and claims of fewer parameters or lower complexity are not supported by experiments (R3).
