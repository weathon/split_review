## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes IF-PCA+, an enhanced version of the Influential Features PCA (IF-PCA) algorithm for high-dimensional subject clustering. It integrates diffusion-based manifold fitting (DMF) to address IF-PCA limitations: sample independence assumption, feature selection ignoring correlations, and linear handling. Experiments on 10 microarray and 8 single-cell datasets show improved feature selection and clustering accuracy/ARI over IF-PCA, DESC, Seurat, and others. Reviewers span scores 3–6; the lower score criticizes limited theory and modest improvement, while higher scores praise originality and empirical performance.

### Strengths
- Clustering high-dimensional data, especially single-cell RNA-seq, is an important and unsolved problem (R1, R2).
- Section 2.3 explains the IFPCA+ method well (R1).
- The improvement of DMF over IF in Table 5 is impressive (R1).
- Originality: novel integration of manifold fitting into the IF-PCA framework, specifically using diffusion-based manifold fitting (DMF) to improve robustness against noise and address limitations of IF-PCA (sample independence, feature correlation, noise handling) (R2, R4).
- Significance: better insights into biological processes and disease mechanisms, with broad applications in cancer research and personalized medicine (R2).
- Systematically identifies and addresses limitations, progressively enhancing IF-PCA+ through integration of DMF and adaptive K-means (R3).
- Achieves the best average rank and regret across single-cell RNA-seq datasets, consistently outperforming other competitive methods (R3).
- Comprehensive numerical comparisons with many alternative methods (R4).
- Well-motivated extension of IF-PCA (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Method description clarity**: The description is difficult to follow; it includes unnecessary history and assumes strong familiarity with prior work (ysl23, yao2, IF-PCA, etc.) without explaining their core mechanisms or limitations (R1, R3). Abbreviations like “KS” (line 209) are undefined; citing experimental results (lines 174–185, 288–289) within the algorithm description blurs methodology and validation (R3). A separate background section is needed (R1). Numerous English mistakes (subject-verb disagreement, missing articles, verb form errors) and typos in mathematical formulas are present, e.g., “bandwith” → “bandwidth”, “tunning” → “tuning”, “matlab” → “MATLAB” (R2, R4). Some references are incorrect (R1). Questions about basic terminology: what is “subject clustering” vs. clustering? Is manifold fitting just another name for manifold learning? What is a “nonlinear dataset”? (R1).
- **Theoretical and simulation limitations**: There is no theory suggesting when IF-PCA+ would work; the method is essentially a preprocessing step for IF-PCA with slight parameter modifications (R1). Simulations are limited: no comparison to other methods in the simulation, and it is unclear which metrics are computed (R1).
- **Modest improvement vs. cost**: One reviewer (score 3) found that the method takes longer, improves only a little in some metrics, and provides no more insight into the data (R1). [Note: Other reviewers (score 6) consider the improvement significant; this contrast is preserved.]
- **Comparison fairness**: Runtime comparisons use different languages (Python vs. R), making results biased; theoretical complexity analysis would be fairer (R3). Abbreviations of comparative algorithms (e.g., Xs) are unclear and only explained in the appendix, harming readability (R3).
- **Parameter tuning**: The paper lacks sufficient guidance on tuning parameters for IF-PCA+ (n_0, knn_f, knn_s; diffusion map bandwidth, K-means parameters) without prior knowledge of the number of clusters or data structure (R2, R3, R4). For example, line 270 asks how to select n_0, knn_f, knn_s (R2). Scree plots and spectral embedding are not definitive for checking the low-dimensional nonlinear manifold assumption in high-dimensional noisy data (R4).
- **Scalability and variants**: No discussion of computational complexity or data size limits (n and p, e.g., p in millions) – it is unclear how the method scales with extremely large datasets where memory is an issue (R4). On Page 6, several variants are mentioned but no clear recommendation or trade-offs in computational cost vs. accuracy are given (R4).
- **Missing experiments and explanations**: 
  - Table 4 shows a large regret gap between IF-PCA+ and IF-PCA that needs further discussion (R3).
  - Table 5’s IF-sf results reach 1.0 in settings A and B without explanation, raising robustness concerns (R3).
  - Single-cell data often have zero-inflation; the paper claims manifold fitting helps handle this but does not explain why, and no simulation varying zero proportions is provided (R4).
  - The numbers in Table 1 are not explained (what they represent or how they were obtained for real data) (R4).
  - For the PCA clustering step, the authors use max(4, K) top left singular vectors to run K-means – why 4 rather than a higher number? (R4).
  - IF-PCA+ uses log transformation; it is unclear if the method only works for non-negative data. A “nolog-IFPCA+” variant is mentioned but no recommendation is given for which version to use in different applications (R4).
  - Questions about zero-inflation, tuning parameter selection, and scalability overlap with the above criticisms and missing experiments/ablations (R4).
