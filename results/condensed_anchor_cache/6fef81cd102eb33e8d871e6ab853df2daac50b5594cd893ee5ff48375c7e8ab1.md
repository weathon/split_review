## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates likelihood displacement in DPO: the probability of preferred responses often decreases during training, counterintuitively. The authors demonstrate that this can be catastrophic, e.g., training a model to prefer "No" over "Never" can increase the probability of "Yes", and aligning Llama-3-8B-Instruct to refuse unsafe prompts reduces the refusal rate from 74.4% to 33.4%. They attribute the phenomenon to embedding similarities between preferred and dispreferred responses, quantified by a centered hidden embedding similarity (CHES) score. The CHES score identifies training samples that contribute most to likelihood displacement; filtering them out mitigates unintentional unalignment in experiments. The paper provides theoretical analysis in a simplified setting, shows that adding SFT loss on preferred responses as regularization can mitigate displacement, and validates findings on SORRY-Bench.

### Strengths
- Provides valuable insights into the phenomenon of likelihood displacement and its catastrophic consequences.  
- Develops a sound theoretical understanding of how and why likelihood displacement occurs, with detailed analysis connecting it to token unembedding geometry (difference of unembeddings of preferred and dispreferred outputs).  
- The CHES score offers a useful tool to identify problematic training samples and guide data curation for DPO.  
- The paper is clearly written, with the theoretical results well presented.  
- Analyzes variants of direct preference learning that explicitly add SFT loss on preferred responses and shows they can mitigate likelihood displacement.  
- Experiments on SORRY-Bench demonstrate that CHES-based filtering effectively improves refusal rates, with strong performance.  

### Weaknesses
- **Experimental scope is limited.** The experiments focus primarily on refusal responses, which are often single-token. Multi-token responses and diverse types of responses are not tested. The only metric reported is refusal rate; there are no evaluations on broader alignment benchmarks (e.g., Ultrafeedback, evol-instruct). This raises concern about whether the proposed filtering harms other alignment objectives.  
- **Theoretical contribution is weak according to one reviewer.** Theorems 1 and 3 are essentially gradient computations; Theorem 3 depends on factors α_{k,k'} whose sign is unknown (only an empirical study is provided). The unconstrained feature model is acknowledged as an oversimplification, and the analysis only covers the local change in log-probability of preferred labels, not the full training dynamics.  
- **Missing experiments and comparisons:**  
  - No results on realistic tasks like finetuning on Ultrafeedback with comparisons to other baselines.  
  - No investigation of whether unintentional unalignment also appears in RLHF algorithms beyond direct preference learning (e.g., PPO) and whether the same mechanism explains it.  
  - No analysis of how likelihood displacement evolves over longer training—does it diminish or intensify?  
  - No recommended threshold for CHES filtering.  
  - The paper does not compare against a baseline that directly optimizes the refusal rate (e.g., only using two responses: "Acceptance" vs. "Refusal"), which could achieve the same objective more explicitly.  
- **Clarity gaps in the theoretical exposition:**  
  - The intuition behind the orthogonal component in Section 4.2.1 (Line 297) needs more explanation—why is the projection orthogonal to the preferred unembedding attributed to the dispreferred unembedding?  
  - The interpretation of the CHES score as the similarity of preferred and rejected responses is vague; the expression includes a negative term of the norm of the preferred response summed embeddings. How should this be interpreted mathematically?  
  - The CHES score does not include terms related to the unembedding geometry of the responses, yet Theorem 1 shows that unembedding geometry influences the change in log-probabilities. This mismatch is a gap.  
- **Implications for complex reasoning (e.g., math problem solving, code generation) are not discussed**—it is unclear whether the data filtering method would help or hinder reasoning capability.  
- **A typo is noted:** Line 959: $\mathbf{W}_{\mathbf{y}_!}$ should be $\mathbf{W}_{\mathbf{y}_1}$.  
- **One reviewer is significantly more critical of the theoretical contribution** (rating contribution 2 vs. 3 from others), while others praise the theoretical insights; this contrast should be noted.
