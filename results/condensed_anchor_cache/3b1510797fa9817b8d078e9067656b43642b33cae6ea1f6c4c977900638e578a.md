## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a diffusion-based framework for solving inverse problems by assuming the observation arises from a stochastic degradation process that gradually degrades and noises the clean image. The method learns to reverse this degradation to recover the clean image, maintaining consistency with the original measurement throughout the reverse process. An early-stopping scheme is introduced to control the distortion-perception trade-off. Experimental results on synthetic deblurring and inpainting tasks demonstrate competitive performance. The method is well-presented and sound, but reviewers raise concerns about practical applicability (known degradation assumption), generality to other tasks, incomplete comparisons, and missing experimental details. Reviewer 1 is more critical of the assumptions and scope, while Reviewers 2 and 4 are more positive about the contribution and presentation.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an interesting and relevant problem of solving inverse problems using a diffusion strategy.
- The method is sound, the theoretical presentation is clear, and the paper is generally well-written.
- The proposed method well maintains data consistency with the original measurement in the reverse process, and the early-stopping scheme provides flexibility for distortion-perception trade-off.
- Experimental results show that the method is competitive with relevant works and achieves better trade-offs between perceptual and distortion metrics. New SOTA results are reported for these trade-offs.
- Analysis on the accuracy and limitations of the proposed method is provided and verified by experimental results.
- The technique for enforcing measurement consistency outperforms existing approaches in the same area, making a valuable contribution with practical relevance.

### Weaknesses
- The method relies on the assumption that the degradation model \(A\) is known a priori, which is unrealistic in many practical applications (e.g., deblurring and inpainting). Experiments are only on synthetic data where \(A\) is easily specified; no evaluation on practical tasks with unknown degradation is provided. (Reviewer 1, strong concern)
- Each degradation requires retraining of the inverse network, limiting flexibility. The ability to extend to more complex restoration tasks (e.g., compressive sensing) is unknown. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- The early-stopping scheme for perception-distortion trade-off is quite straightforward and heuristic. (Reviewer 1)
- When the degradation model \(A(·)\) is linear (e.g., deblurring), the network effectively learns to reconstruct \(x_0\), making Dirac equivalent to DDPM. The deblurring experiment does not clearly demonstrate superiority over DDPM in such linear cases. (Reviewer 1)
- Comparison and discussion to relevant published work is incomplete. Specifically, the paper should compare to Daras et al. (2022) (which can also solve inverse problems in a non-blind formulation), Delbracio and Milanfar (2023) (which also discusses perception-distortion trade-off via bridge formulations and requires retraining for specific degradations), and Bridge methods like \(I^2\)SB (Liu et al., ICML 2023). (Reviewer 2)
- Experimental results should include methods that train a dedicated model for a given degradation (like the proposed method) for fair comparison, not only general frameworks that do not require retraining. Additionally, including a state-of-the-art supervised method like NAFNet as a distortion-prioritized reference would strengthen baselines. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- Experiment details are lacking in the Appendix. In training, the degradation function \(A_t(·)\) is needed, but for deblurring, only clean image \(x\) and degraded \(\hat{x}\) are available; how to design \(A_t(·)\) from only those is unclear. (Reviewer 1)
- The experimental setting is limited to only two degradations (deblurring and inpainting); generalization to other degradations such as Gaussian denoising or compressive sensing is unclear. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- The question of what happens under a mismatch of the degradation process between training and testing is not addressed. (Reviewer 4)
- There is a minor formatting issue: “projectionSong” lacks a space (line 11, second paragraph of Introduction). (Reviewer 1)
