## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces BoneMet, the first large-scale (over 50 TB) publicly available multi-modal dataset for breast tumor bone metastasis (BTBM), comprising 2D X-ray images, 3D CT scans, and biological/medical records from thousands of mice collected over five years. The dataset is structured into six components (Rotation-X-Ray, Recon-CT, Seg-CT, Regist-CT, RoI-CT, MiceMediRec). The authors provide standardized APIs (PyPI, HuggingFace, GitHub), code, and tutorials, and conduct benchmark experiments with various deep learning models to demonstrate usability. One reviewer (score 8, confidence 3) is notably more positive than the other three (scores 5,6,5; confidence 4).

### Strengths
- First open, large-scale (>50 TB), multi-modal dataset specifically for BTBM, including 2D X-rays, 3D CT scans, and biological data (medical records, quantitative analysis). (All reviewers)
- Comprehensive documentation of data collection protocols and analytical procedures. (R1)
- Standardized APIs on PyPI and HuggingFace, plus code and tutorials on GitHub, ensuring broad accessibility and ease of use. (R1, R3, R4)
- Extensive experimental validation with multiple deep learning benchmarks (BTBM diagnosis, CT generation, reaction force prediction) demonstrating dataset usability. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Clear motivation and well-structured presentation of the dataset and its components. (R2, R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Translational gap between murine and human BTBM is insufficiently addressed.** The dataset is from mice; differences in skeletal structure and disease progression may limit direct applicability to human patients. (R1, R3, R4) The abstract emphasizes human health implications, but no experiment or analysis demonstrates transferability or clinical value for humans. (R1, R4) The authors should discuss or evaluate whether models trained on this dataset can be transferred to human BTBM and how. (R3)
- **Insufficient experimental details and data partitioning.** Data partitioning (train/val/test) is not reported. (R2, R3) No external validation on other datasets is performed, raising concerns about generalization. (R3) Several experiments (e.g., comparing ViT with/without STA) do not clearly tie their purpose to demonstrating dataset applicability. (R2)
- **Limited technical novelty and method selection.** Most deep learning models are built on existing methods; the contributions are primarily dataset-centric. (R2) The selection of benchmark methods (e.g., Swin-base vs. ViT with STA) is not justified. (R1, R4) For example, why not compare ViT with STA directly against ViT without STA? (R1)
- **Label quality and data processing uncertainties.** Pixel-wise annotation of bone and the manual cropping of fibula (via CTAn®) may be questionable; the effectiveness of automated tools is not validated. (R2) For RoI-CT, assigning tibiae ROI with fixed pixel values (180,240,60,0) turns the original CT into a segmentation-like mask, losing most information. (R2) No manual annotation of organs or tumors is involved except fibular cropping. (R2)
- **Missing comparisons with prior datasets (especially human-derived).** The authors should compare BoneMet with existing available datasets (including human bone metastasis datasets) to clarify differences and scale. (R4)
- **Clinical significance of reported metrics is not evaluated.** Precision/Recall/F1/Accuracy values for BTBM diagnosis are not compared to expert clinical performance. For CT generation, SSIM values of 0.767–0.860 from 3D-GAN, T-VAE, ST-VAE are reported but their clinical role is unclear. (R2)
- **Inconsistencies and missing details.** Typos: “detials” should be “details” (line 454); “BigThansfer” should be “BigTransfer” (Figure 5). (R1) Table 1 “Spatial Resolution” column inaccurately filled (mostly indicates organs). (R2) The number of mice is not given. (R2) The label assignment level (slice vs. animal) for positive/negative categories is not specified. (R2) The count of Seg-CT images exceeds Recon-CT, even though Seg-CT is derived from Recon-CT. (R2) Pre-Op and Post-Op are not defined. (R2) Figures have small text, reducing readability. (R4)
- **Multi-modal learning application is not truly multi-modal.** In Section 3.3, only CT data is used as model input; the “multi-modal” aspect is not realized. (R2)
- **MiceMediRec feature list is incomplete.** Not all features are provided (only a subset in Table S2). The method to obtain predicted reaction force values from the multi-modal prognostic model is unclear. (R2)
- **Dependence on external software without proper references.** The authors used existing software (e.g., CTAn®) for data processing; corresponding references and whether they are free of charge should be specified. It is also unclear when the self-developed Seg-API, Regist-API, and RoI-API are used versus the external tools. (R2)
