## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents GECo, a post-hoc explainability method for Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) on graph classification tasks. GECo uses community detection (Blondel modularity optimization) to identify subgraphs, evaluates each community’s contribution to the prediction, and selects those above a threshold (mean/median of probability values) to build an explanation mask. The method is tested on six synthetic and four real-world datasets for Graph Convolutional Networks, compared against GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, PGMExplainer, and SubgraphX using necessity, sufficiency, accuracy, and fidelity metrics. The authors claim GECo outperforms baselines on most datasets and is computationally efficient.

### Strengths
- GECo enhances the interpretability of GNNs by identifying influential subgraphs, offering a more intuitive explanation than node-/edge-level methods.
- The method is simple and straightforward, making it easy to understand and implement.
- The paper is well-organized and clearly written (noted by some reviewers).
- The problem of explainability in GNNs is important and highly relevant.
- GECo achieves competitive explainability performance across both synthetic and real-world datasets, with reported efficiency (faster computation times) in some experiments.
- The community-based approach is novel in focusing on key subgraphs rather than individual nodes or edges.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty and insufficient differentiation from existing methods** – Multiple reviewers note that using communities/substructures for explanation is already known. The paper does not adequately analyze limitations of existing explainability models (GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, SubgraphX, PGMExplainer) to argue the necessity of a community-based approach. Work cited as overlapping: Martínez Mora et al. 2024 "Community-aware explanations in knowledge graphs with XP-GNN", Chen & Ying 2023 "TempME: Towards the Explainability of Temporal GNNs via Motif Discovery". One reviewer calls the approach “straightforward” and lacking novelty (R1, R3); another views simplicity as a strength (R4) – this disagreement is preserved.
- **Weak theoretical justification** – The authors hypothesize that GNNs learn community structures but do not formally prove this claim or provide evidence. Section 3.2 focuses only on technical steps without analysis of why the method works theoretically (e.g., mathematical formulation for subgraph detection). No theoretical foundation for why communities are the optimal substructures for explanation.
- **Insufficient comparison with recent baselines** – Baselines (GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, etc.) are not the latest. Reviewers recommend comparing with instance-level explanation models from the last two years, e.g., Xie et al. "Task-agnostic graph explanations", Chen & Ying "TempME", and standard dataset comparisons like BBBP, BACE, NCI1, BA-Shapes, Graph-Twitter.
- **Lack of robustness verification for community detection** – GECo uses only Blondel’s modularity optimization. No discussion of how different community detection algorithms might affect explanation results. An ablation study with alternatives is necessary.
- **Threshold selection issues** – Threshold 𝜏 is determined by mean or median of community probability values. This may be unsuitable for uneven graph structures or varying community sizes. Adaptive threshold adjustments are recommended.
- **Metric limitations and missing evaluations** – Fidelity-based metrics suffer from the OOD problem; reviewers suggest adding Fid_{α1,+} and Fid_{α2,-} (Zheng et al., ICLR 2024). Sparsity as a metric and fidelity vs. subgraph size plots are also needed. Runtime performance experiments are missing.
- **Presentation and writing weaknesses** – (a) Introduction's third paragraph is too brief; lacks clear motivation for using graph communities. (b) Related work should be systematically organized (e.g., classification by perturbation/gradient/decomposition methods) and compare strengths/weaknesses. (c) Section 3.3 is too lengthy; Section 3.2 (method) is too short (less than one page). (d) Figure 1 lacks clarity: how threshold is determined, how communities are identified, and which communities are influential are not visually explained; font is too small. (e) Tables and text on page 8 are not professionally formatted.
- **Insufficient references** – Many references are not up-to-date. Missing citations: Sangaiah et al. 2023, Sadler et al. 2022 on explainable community finding, and earlier community-based explanation works like Aviyente & Karaaslanli 2022.
- **Method scope limitations** – Focusing on communities may miss important individual nodes within a community. The method is designed for graph classification and may not extend well to node classification tasks. The inference process (whether all communities are fed individually to the GNN) needs elaboration.
- **Required experiments** – (a) Visualizations of identified subgraphs, especially on synthetic data with known ground truth. (b) Ablation study with different community detection algorithms. (c) Adaptive threshold experiments. (d) Comparison with task-agnostic and motif-based explainers. (e) Formal mathematical formulation of subgraph detection process.
