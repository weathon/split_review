## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses two key gaps in statement autoformalization: the absence of a faithful automated evaluation metric and the lack of contextual information causing hallucination. It proposes **BEq** (Bidirectional Extended Definitional Equivalence), a neuro-symbolic method using Lean 4 features to determine equivalence between formal statements. It also introduces **RAutoformalizer**, a retrieval-augmented autoformalization pipeline that retrieves dependent formal objects via a trained retriever and uses topological informalization to structure training data. A new OOD benchmark, **Con-NF** (961 informal-formal pairs from frontier mathematics), is contributed. Experiments show BEq improves accuracy (82.50%→90.50%) and precision (70.59%→100.0%) over baselines, and RAutoformalizer outperforms SOTA on both ProofNet (12.83%→18.18% BEq@8) and Con-NF (4.58%→16.86% BEq@8).

### Strengths
1. **Novel and important contributions**: BEq is a pioneer metric grounded in human intuition about statement equivalence, leveraging Lean 4 definitional features and being more interpretable than prior metrics. RAutoformalizer introduces a well-motivated dependency retrieval and topological informalization pipeline to reduce context agnosia. The Con-NF benchmark emphasizes OOD evaluation, which is critical for research-level autoformalization.
2. **Strong empirical results**: BEq significantly improves human alignment over other metrics (Table 1), with 100% precision. RAutoformalizer substantially outperforms SOTA baselines on both in-distribution (ProofNet) and OOD (Con-NF) settings. Ablation studies validate technical choices.
3. **Well-designed training protocol**: The dependency retrieval is trained on pairs of (theorem, its dependencies), and the autoformalizer is fine-tuned with retrieved contexts. The use of topological order for informalization is reasonable.
4. **Clear motivation and writing**: The paper is well-organized and easy to follow. The two problems are clearly articulated, and the solutions are well-explained.
5. **High significance**: Both the metric and the retrieval-augmented approach address fundamental limitations in autoformalization and can significantly impact future research.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited applicability of BEq**: BEq requires gold-standard formal statements and Lean 4 tactics, restricting its use to benchmarks with human-verified ground truths. Its reliance on a 20B LLM (InternLM2-Math-Plus-20B) also limits accessibility; smaller models (e.g., 7B) were not tested, and no performance comparison across model sizes is provided. The metric cannot be directly applied to benchmarks without human-checked pairs (including Con-NF), and its formal definition overlooks the assumptions that ground-truth statements are aligned with informal statements and are correctly type-checked.
2. **Insufficient human evaluation documentation**: For the BEq human equivalence benchmark, critical details are missing: expert numbers, competence levels, annotation protocols, inter-annotator agreement rates, payment structures, and how ground-truths were validated. The number of cases (200) is small given Con-NF’s size, and the potential overlap between pair creators and evaluators raises independence concerns. Label distribution and sampling bias (non-uniform in Fig. 3) are not adequately addressed.
3. **Lack of prompt and implementation details**: The few-shot prompts for the transformation function T (line 261) and for LLM graders (line 303) are not provided; the cited reference (Ying et al., 2024a) does not contain the exact templates. This hampers reproducibility.
4. **Concerns about benchmark construction rigor for Con-NF**: Con-NF is generated by LLM informalization without human verification. Potential issues include: (a) discrepancy between informal statements and ground-truth formal statements; (b) formal identifiers leaking into informal statements (e.g., tactic names, variables); (c) some theorems may not be translatable. No filtering or quality control procedures are reported. The construction details, topic distributions, and how Con-NF differs from existing OOD datasets (e.g., why not use [1]?) are insufficiently described.
5. **No human evaluation of RAutoformalizer results**: Table 3 relies on BEq and Typecheck, but BEq’s robustness is limited (see point 1). Without human evaluation, the significance of the performance gains is undermined.
6. **Limited ablation of retrieval component**: The benefits of the retrieval component are not isolated; Table 3 only shows overall gains, but does not quantify how much hallucination reduction (especially for nonexistent dependencies) is achieved. It is unclear how close the retrieval is to ground-truth dependencies.
7. **Weak baseline selection**: ICL baselines are weak; frontier models (DeepSeek, GPT-4o) with retrieval in-context should be compared. Standard reasoning baselines (CoT, ToT, Self-Consistency) are missing. Comparison with ReProver [1] (a RAG method for theorem proving) is needed to distinguish novelty.
8. **Potential bias from using a single LLM family**: Both BEq and RAutoformalizer rely on InternLM2-Math-Plus-20B; the same model family is used for evaluation, backbone, and fine-tuning. This raises concerns about fairness and generalizability. Results with different LLM backbones (e.g., DeepSeek, GPT) are missing. In Table 1, DeepSeek V2.5 actually outperforms InternLM2-Math-Plus-20B in majority voting; the choice of model is not justified.
9. **Minor clarity issues**: The formal setup of BEq (transformation primitives, derivation of Unidirectional Definitional Implication) is unclear. The statement in Introduction about type checking being "too weak" lacks citations or empirical evidence. The critique of LLM graders as "non-determinant" is not sufficiently distinguished from BEq’s own LLM dependence and few-shot prompt design.
10. **Typos and table errors**: Line 097: "A baseline is build"; Line 199: "with how humans instincts." In Table 3, for Con-NF Typecheck@1, LW scores 28.10% but RA is marked bold at 20.50% – likely a mistake.
