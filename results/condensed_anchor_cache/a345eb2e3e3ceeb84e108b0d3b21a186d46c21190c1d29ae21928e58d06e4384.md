## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SnLH, which decouples low- and high-frequency spectral signals for unsupervised graph domain adaptation (UGDA), using mutual information maximization on low-frequency features and contrastive learning on high-frequency features. One reviewer (rating 6, confidence 2) finds the problem important, the writing clear, and the spectral signal idea interesting and effective. The other three reviewers (ratings 3, 3, 3; confidences 4, 5, 4) acknowledge strong empirical performance and the novel perspective of spectral signals, but raise substantial concerns about limited novelty (predecessors in node-level tasks), theoretical flaws (mislabeling KL-divergence as mutual information, unsubstantiated invariance claims), inconsistency in treating high-frequency signals, missing baselines and analyses, and scalability issues.

### Strengths
- Unique take on UGDA by distinguishing low- and high-frequency spectral components, addressing a previously overlooked aspect of GDA. (R1, R4)
- Strong empirical performance across diverse datasets, surpassing several baselines; "model performs well in most databases." (R1, R3)
- Well-structured approach: mutual information maximization for low-frequency signals, contrastive learning for high-frequency signals. (R1) Idea of incorporating spectral signals is interesting and effective. (R2)
- Interesting and important problem; paper is well-organized and clearly written. (R2)
- Finding that low-frequency topology signals represent shared cross-domain features while high-frequency indicates domain-specific knowledge is intuitively reasonable. (R1, R3)
- Highlights the spectral signal information discrepancy in graph-level domain adaptation. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: Separating low/high-frequency signals has been explored in node-level tasks; extension to graph-level is incremental, not groundbreaking. (R3) Core idea is same as in [1][2] (different tasks). (R4) Low-high-frequency signal and interclass consistency are existing losses; the combination is not innovative. (R3)
- **Theoretical issues with Mutual Information**: Equations 9 and 10 are KL-divergence, not mutual information; need to clarify how joint distribution is estimated to justify claim of mutual information maximization. (R1) Claim that maximizing mutual information ensures global domain invariance is unsubstantiated; need theoretical or empirical evidence. (R1) D. (R3) I. (R3) Doub (R3) Doubt that performance improvement is due to mutual information rather than other alignment methods. (R3)
- **Inconsistency in treatment of high-frequency**: Initially authors argue high-frequency is domain-specific, but then align it via contrastive learning, contradicting the original intent; why aligning high-frequency features helps if they are domain-specific? (R1) Motivation for contrastive learning on high-frequency is insufficient; need theoretical basis and empirical evidence. (R1)
- **Missing baselines**: Lacks recent SOTA baselines such as "Multi-View Teacher with Curriculum Data Fusion for Robust Unsupervised Domain Adaptation". (R2) Also lacks related baselines [3]. (R4)
- **Implementation clarity**: Need details on how classifiers P_s and P_t are expressed/estimated: are they shared or independent? Architecture? Training procedure? (R1) Why A2GNN is introduced as baseline? It appears a wrong citation and is for node classification? (R2)
- **GNN encoder influence**: How do different GNN encoders affect performance? (R2)
- **Computational complexity**: Use of mutual information and contrastive learning with frequency filters adds significant complexity; scalability on large graphs uncertain. Provide computational complexity analysis or runtime comparisons on larger graph datasets. (R2, R4)
- **Writing improvements**: First paragraph of Introduction too long; separate discussion of graph data and graph DA into different paragraphs. Some words are too long. (R4)
- **Graph-level DA impact limited**: Most GDA methods focus on node-level; paper does not adequately address specific challenges and nuances of graph classification tasks; justification for focusing on graph-level is not sufficiently compelling. (R3)
