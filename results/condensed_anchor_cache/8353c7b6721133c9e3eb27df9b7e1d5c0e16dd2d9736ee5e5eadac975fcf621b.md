## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewer 1 (score 3, high confidence) is critical, citing several missing algorithmic details, insufficient justification for design choices, and gaps in experimental evaluation. Reviewers 2 and 3 (score 8, moderate-to-high confidence) are positive, commending the extensive experimental analysis, clear presentation, and convincing results, but each point to a few shortcomings (lack of theoretical guarantees, need for more detail on permutation alignment, and scalability concerns). All reviewers agree the problem of consolidating experts in Mixture-of-Experts models is important and well-motivated.

### Strengths
- **Important problem and motivation:** The paper addresses the memory bottleneck of deploying MoE models by exploiting known expert redundancies (R1).
- **Extensive experimental analysis:** The authors provide thorough ablations for each design decision – averaging strategy (Table 8), necessity of permutation alignment (Table 6), similarity functions and superiority of router logits (Table 4) – and compare on multiple text datasets (R2, R3).
- **Clear presentation:** Figures are well designed (Figure 1, Figure 2, Figure 3) and the motivation is clearly written. The large array of experimental results and ablations is convincing (R3).
- **Insightful visualizations:** Figure 3’s depiction of activation frequencies per dataset is clear and suggestive (e.g., potential for per-dataset compression aggressiveness) (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Expert permutation alignment is under‑explained:**  
  - The intuition, exact definition of “alignment,” algorithm for computing the permutation, and the objective function are missing. It is unclear whether the alignment is a one‑time or iterative operation (R1, R3).  
  - The search space for possible permutations is not described (exhaustive vs. heuristic), nor is the computational cost or its scaling with the number of experts (R3).  
  - A chicken‑and‑egg problem arises: alignment is needed to identify similar experts, but a reference expert is required for alignment. How this is resolved is not addressed (R3).

- **Lack of theoretical foundations:**  
  - No proof of optimality or error bound on information loss / performance degradation from the merging algorithm (R2).  
  - Theoretical justification is missing for why merging should be performed *before* compression (R2, question 1).  
  - The claim that similar experts exhibit similar router logits lacks theoretical backing (R2, question 2).

- **Missing details and justifications in the method:**  
  - **Figure 3:** How are activation frequencies computed? Were models fine‑tuned per task, and with the load balancing loss? Is the observed imbalance due to fine‑tuning or inherent? How does this affect the compression method? (R1)  
  - **Stable‑rank computation:** In a layer with 32 → 6 experts, is the stable rank averaged per expert or computed on merged experts, and on weight matrices or activations? (R1)  
  - **Expert grouping:** The similarity metric for assigning non‑dominant experts to dominant ones is not explained. Imbalanced group sizes are possible; their implications are not discussed (R1).  
  - **Pruning of S:** Why are cumulative importance scores used instead of individual scores? The pruning schedule in Appendix A2 appears ad‑hoc and is not justified (R1).  
  - **Role of knowledge distillation:** All models in Table 2 use distillation. Results without distillation are needed to isolate the compression contribution. Is the teacher the full SMoE model? (R1)  
  - **Performance drop after compression (M‑SMoE → MC‑SMoE):** More analysis is required on alternative pruning strategies, schedules, and the trade‑off between compression ratio and performance degradation (R1).

- **Insufficient benchmarking of speed and memory:**  
  - Table A10 shows only marginal speed gains. Comprehensive measurements of inference throughput and memory usage for both M‑SMoE and MC‑SMoE, compared with the dense and full SMoE models, are missing (R1).  
  - The computational cost as the number of SMoE layers grows is not analyzed (R2).
