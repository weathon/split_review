## Merged Review

### Summary
Magic123 proposes a two-stage coarse-to-fine approach for high-quality, textured 3D mesh generation from a single unposed image. The key novelty is combining SDS losses from a 2D diffusion prior (StableDiffusion) and a 3D-aware 2D diffusion prior (Zero1-to-3). Stage 1 optimizes a hash‑grid NeRF for coarse geometry; Stage 2 uses a DMTet mesh representation for high‑resolution texture refinement. A trade‑off parameter balances the two priors. Textual inversion and monocular depth regularization are used for appearance consistency and to prevent degenerate solutions. Experiments are conducted on the NeRF synthetic dataset and 15 real objects.

### Strengths
- **High‑quality reconstruction**: The method produces 3D assets with impressive quality, significantly better than previous single‑view lifting approaches (R1, R3). Qualitative and quantitative improvements are shown on both synthetic and in‑the‑wild images (R1, R2).
- **Clear novelty**: The idea of jointly using 2D and 3D diffusion priors is a key insight and is proven useful. The 2D prior (trained on billions of real images) enhances texture realism, while the 3D prior (trained on limited 3D data) improves geometric plausibility (R1, R3). Two reviewers (R1, R3) view this as a clear and important contribution. *(Minority: two other reviewers (R2, R4) consider the combination straightforward, noting most components already appear in prior works like Magic3D, RealFusion, and Zero1-to-3.)*
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Ablations clearly demonstrate the benefit of combining both priors (Figures 6,7) and the effect of varying their weights (R1, R3). Quantitative metrics include LPIPS, PSNR, SSIM, and CLIP similarity. Results are shown on NeRF synthetic objects and 15 real objects, with turntable videos in the supplementary (R1, R2, R3).
- **Well‑written and reproducible**: The paper is easy to follow, provides sufficient implementation details, and the code is attached (R1, R2, R3). All network and training details are described, aiding reproducibility (R3).
- **Related work**: Adequate treatment of text‑to‑3D and image‑to‑3D literature (R3).
- **Supplementary materials**: Turntable videos and key ablations provide valuable insight (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Performance ceiling due to segmentation**: The final asset quality is limited by the segmentation model (acknowledged by the authors). Questions arise: (a) Do the diffusion priors correct segmentation inaccuracies? (b) How does the method handle objects in cluttered environments? (c) How does DMTet’s texture mapping behave at inaccurate segmentation boundaries where object and background blend? (d) What happens when segmentation outputs disjoint segments – does the NeRF’s inductive bias overcome this? (e) Is a segmentation network required even on a plain background? (R3)
- **Dependence on depth / normal estimation**: Performance is capped by the effectiveness of pre‑trained depth and normal networks, limiting the type of input images. Depth and normals may be unreliable until the NeRF has converged to a reasonable geometry; is there a schedule for these losses? The sensitivity of the final geometry to depth map accuracy, especially near depth discontinuities or occlusions, is unclear (R3).
- **Frontal view assumption**: Zero1-to-3 assumes a centered frontal view. The authors removed four examples from the training dataset that did not meet this assumption. This limits versatility for real‑world objects viewed from arbitrary angles. How does the method handle off‑center objects even if segmentation is accurate? Is a pre‑processing centering step needed, and how does that affect the final model for non‑symmetrical objects? (R3, R4)
- **Insufficient comparison with Zero1-to-3**: The claimed “significant improvements” are not strongly supported. The qualitative comparison in Figure 5 shows quality/sharpness gains but also a potential trade‑off in multi‑view consistency. Supplementary videos lack direct comparison with Zero1-to-3, and the quantitative results are comparable on the limited 3D examples. The trade‑off between quality and consistency needs more thorough investigation with metrics that quantify multi‑view discrepancies (R4). *(Minority: R2 notes the combination is straightforward and evaluation lacks diversity, further weakening the claim.)*
- **Ill‑motivated weighting of priors**: The paper does not detail how the trade‑off parameter is chosen. Are the weights fixed per instance or adaptable? If fixed, sensitivity analysis and justification are missing; if adaptable, the mechanism should be described (R4).
- **Novelty concerns from some reviewers**: Two reviewers (R2, R4) consider the work largely an A+B combination of existing components: coarse‑to‑fine from Magic3D, text‑conditioned SDS from RealFusion, and image‑conditioned SDS from Zero1-to-3. The combination of the two SDS losses is seen as straightforward given that Zero1‑to‑3 already provides 3D awareness and StableDiffusion provides better appearance (R2). *(Disagreement: two reviewers (R1, R3) find the combination novel and a clear contribution.)*
- **Lack of diversity evaluation**: Given that multiple 3D reconstructions are possible from a single image, the paper does not evaluate diversity of outputs (R2).
- **Missing details on textual inversion vs. DreamBooth**: How does textual inversion compare to DreamBooth for the 2D‑SDS loss in terms of training time, memory, and texture quality? (R3)
- **Assumption of relightability**: The diffuse shading model bakes lighting into texture; the method does not reconstruct relightable materials (limitation acknowledged) (R1).
- **Scalability of DMTet**: The tetrahedral mesh representation is best suited for objects and may not scale to complex scenes or large environments (R4).
- **Reconstruction speed**: The speed of stage one could be improved by progressive resolution; stage two could benefit from a smaller range of sampling time steps (R1).
