## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces FT-Shield, a watermarking method to detect unauthorized fine-tuning of text-to-image diffusion models. FT-Shield embeds optimized watermarks into images that are quickly learned during fine-tuning, and uses a trained binary classifier (with Mixture of Experts) to detect unauthorized use. Experiments are conducted on multiple fine-tuning methods (DreamBooth, LoRA, Textual Inversion). The idea of optimizing watermarks for faster learning is novel and interesting. However, several reviewers raise concerns about novelty, experimental assumptions, and missing evaluations.

### Strengths
- The studied problem—preventing unauthorized diffusion-model-based image mimic—is important and timely (R1, R3, R4).
- Experiments incorporate a variety of fine-tuning approaches (DreamBooth, LoRA, Textual Inversion) (R1, R2).
- The watermarking objective is intuitive and the proposed method outperforms recent baselines (including a concurrent ICLR 2024 submission) (R3).
- Ablation studies are comprehensive (R3).
- The methodology is assessed in realistic scenarios (R4).
- The manuscript is clearly written and well-articulated (R4).
- The idea of optimizing watermarks such that they are easier to learn than style/object content is novel and interesting (R2 summary; note minority disagreement on novelty in weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**:
  - The problem setting, overall workflow, decoder construction/training, and use of fine-tuning generated images for decoder training closely resemble Gen-Watermark [1]; only the watermark creation process differs (R1).
  - The bi-level optimization (Equation 1) for generating watermarks is similar to the method in DiffusionShield [2], further weakening the contribution (R1).
  - Reviewer 2 states the idea does “not seem novel” (R2, despite describing it as novel in summary).
- **Missing empirical evidence for core claim**: No quantitative or qualitative evidence is provided that existing methods learn style before watermark while FT-Shield learns watermark before style; the claim is unsupported (R2). Figure 1 lacks supporting data and the definition of “completion of style learning” is unclear; the offender’s motivation for using fewer fine-tuning steps is not clarified (R1).
- **White-box access assumption**: The bi-level optimization (Equation 1) requires white-box access to the text-to-image model. The protector is assumed to use the same model as the offender; evaluation is needed when models differ in size or architecture (R1).
- **Unrealistic assumptions in main experiments**:
  - High performance in Table 1 assumes the protector knows the fine-tuning method used by the offender and augments decoder training with images from that same method. In realistic settings (Table 4) accuracy drops to ~75%, and IM is not assessed in Table 4 (R1). More extensive evaluation under realistic scenarios is needed (R1, R4).
  - The detection task only considers a single generated image per target model; generating multiple images could yield stronger detection (R3 question).
- **Missing transferability experiments**: It is not tested whether watermarks trained with one diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion 1.5) remain effective when fine-tuned with another (e.g., SDXL) (R3).
- **Lack of robustness against known attacks**: The watermark’s robustness under the attack from Zhao et al. (2023) [1], which uses a diffusion model to denoise watermark perturbations, is not evaluated (R3 question). Combined disturbances should be tested (R4 question from Section 4.5).
- **Incomplete experimental details and reporting**:
  - The images used to compute TPR and FPR (prompts, models) are unclear (R2).
  - Table 3 should include FID scores to substantiate claims about stopping fine-tuning (R4).
  - In Section 4.4, “one fine-tuning” is ambiguous; clarification is needed on the transferability evaluation (R4).
  - disturbance intensity in Section 4.5 should be quantified and its effect on TPR discussed (R4).
  - It would be beneficial to report TPR at a fixed FPR (e.g., TPR@FPR=1%) for easier method comparison (R3 suggestion).
- **Risk of false positives and multi-watermark management**:
  - There is a risk of false positives: a watermarked style A could cause its watermark to appear in generated images of an unrelated style B (R4 question).
  - The framework’s ability to handle multiple independent watermarks simultaneously is unexplored; challenges include overwriting previously learned watermarks and increased FPR (R4 question).
- **Limited qualitative results**: The appendix shows only a single style (Figure 3). More diverse examples—including objects, additional styles, and human faces (e.g., public figures like Nicolas Cage)—should be provided (R4).
