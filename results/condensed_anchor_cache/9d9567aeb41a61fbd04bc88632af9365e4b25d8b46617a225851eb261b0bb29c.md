## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces VisCoIN, a method that maps learned concept features from concept-based interpretable networks (CoIN) to the latent space of a pretrained generative model (StyleGAN2). The goal is to improve visualization and interpretability of unsupervised concept dictionaries, especially for large-scale images. The approach uses three adapter modules and a pretrained classifier and generator, with losses for output fidelity, reconstruction faithfulness, concept sparsity, and viewability. Experiments on several large-scale recognition benchmarks evaluate prediction accuracy, reconstruction fidelity, concept faithfulness, consistency, sparsity, and viewability. The method shows promise but has several limitations regarding reconstruction quality, the need for a secondary unconstrained representation, missing baselines, and unclear justification of design choices.

### Strengths
- The idea of mapping unsupervised concepts into the latent space of a pretrained generative model is interesting and novel (R1, R4). It builds upon CoINs with a new interpretation modality (R4).
- The definition of viewability and the overall methodology are clear, well-founded, and intuitive (R2). Visualization of learned concepts with varying λ provides compelling evidence of effectiveness (R2).
- The method addresses the limitation of using training-set examples for concept visualization; generated visualizations are simpler to understand than concept attribution maps and image patches (R3).
- Leveraging pretrained generative models rather than training an additional decoder improves efficiency by using a less complex concept translator (R3).
- The measure of consistency of concept visualization is novel and an appropriate proxy for human experiments (R3).
- The paper is well-written, with good treatment of relevant literature and detailed implementation parameters for reproducibility (R1, R5).
- Numerous evaluation methods (prediction accuracy, reconstruction fidelity, faithfulness, consistency, sparsity, viewability) provide a thorough validation of the proposal (R5).
- Correctness and reproducibility are sound (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**: Several important baselines are not included: Label-free CBMs, LaBo, Posthoc CBMs, Sparse CBMs (R1). Also missing a closely related work: Garg et al., “Advancing Ante-Hoc Explainable Models through Generative Adversarial Networks,” AAAI-W’24 (R1). Comparing on metrics beyond visualization is needed for completeness. (R1 – major concern)
- **Reconstruction quality and reliability**: Many reconstructions are quite far from the original, sometimes altering images drastically (Fig. 4b, appendix examples). Quality is inconsistent, raising concerns about the reliability of concept visualizations (R3). The method’s reliance on a generative model introduces a bottleneck; concept attribution maps avoid this by highlighting on the original image (R3). (R3 – significant weakness)
- **Secondary unconstrained representation Φ'(x)**: The need for an additional unconstrained representation in reconstruction (Section C1.1) implies that reconstruction purely from interpretable concepts is not possible. This should be stated in Section 3.2, as it undermines the claim that concepts are fully interpretable and indicates the concept translator does not learn a complete mapping to the generator’s latent space (R3). (R3 – major concern)
- **Justification of output fidelity loss L_of**: The rationale for using L_of (comparing output logits from pretrained classifier f) instead of direct cross-entropy with ground-truth labels is unclear. This introduces dependence on a potentially flawed pretrained classifier and seems unnecessarily complicated (R2). (R2)
- **Autoencoder limitation for compositional representations**: The integration of Ψ and Ω is an auto-encoder objective, which is inherently insufficient for learning compositional/disentangled concept representations. Modern architectures (e.g., transformers for f, diffusion models for G) are not explored; the simple autoencoder may limit capturing complex relationships between concepts (R2). (R2)
- **Lack of depth in analysis**: The appendix reports many ablation studies but with too little discussion of inferences and take-aways. Important analyses are brief, making it hard to parse (R1). (R1)
- **Potential bias from generative model**: Including a pretrained generative network may introduce bias; concept functions are learned with the concept translator, but the generator’s latent space affects concept learning. It is unclear whether we are explaining the feature extractor or the generative model (R5). (R5)
- **Concept count K and semantic meaning**: The choice K=64 is empirically set but ablation for smaller K is needed. How do visualizations change with fewer concepts? The naming of concepts (e.g., “red eye”) appears deductive rather than rigorous (R5). (R5)
- **Faithfulness and consistency evaluation**: Faithfulness (feature removal) is standard; insertion-based evaluation (e.g., XRAI AUC) should be added (R5). For consistency, the classifier architecture (linear? layers?) is not specified (R5). An additional experiment with part-label datasets (e.g., CUB-200) could verify if removed concepts correspond to same parts across class instances (R5). (R5)
- **Sparsity and viewability analysis**: Discussion of these metrics goes little beyond the training loss. A qualitative viewability analysis and human evaluation compared to baseline approaches are missing (R5). (R5)
- **By-design interpretability concerns**: The output depends on the entire feature set, so less-contributing features can still change the label, breaking the by-design property (R4). Additionally, the mapping via concept translator may be imperfect. Addressing this (e.g., using only top features, or distilling the latent space to coincide with the generator’s) is not explored (R4). (R4 – clarity/limitation)
- **Generalizability and overhead**: It is unclear if the method generalizes to transformer-based architectures. The computational overhead compared to CoIN and ResNet is not clarified (R4). (R4)
- **Visualization in Figure 4**: The red-box visualization in Fig. 4 is not clearly explained; it is difficult to follow what “Visual modifications of more local concepts indicated by red boxes” means (R4). (R4 – clarity)
- **Additional modeling concerns**: (R1) Asks about restrictions on pre-training datasets for f and G – how semantically close must they be to the target dataset? (R3) Notes that maximum reliable λ is 3-4; is this due to training magnitude, and does setting φ_k(x)=0 for faithfulness fall outside that range? Also, the simple architecture of Ω might benefit from multiple layers. (R5) Asks why in Fig. 4 the bird head and red eye both enlarge – is there concept overlap? (Questions included as weaknesses when overlapping with criticism.)

*Reviewer disagreement*: One reviewer (R3, score 8) is notably more positive, praising the novelty of consistency and the efficiency of leveraging pretrained models, but also raises the strongest concerns about reconstruction quality and the unconstrained representation. Other reviewers (R1, R2, R4, R5, all score 6) share a more reserved view, emphasizing missing baselines, lack of depth, bias risks, and by-design limitations. The minority positive view does not negate the majority’s criticisms; rather, the paper is seen as promising but with several unresolved issues.
