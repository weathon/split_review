## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes PromptTrack, a video-level RGB-T tracking paradigm using prompt learning. It models spatial-temporal relationships in multimodal contexts by learning streaming temporal prompts from historical frames (capturing target appearance changes and motion trajectory) and multimodal spatial prompts conditioned on those temporal prompts. A Spatial Prompt Generation (SPG) module is inserted into a one-stream backbone (OSTrack) to enhance inter-modal interaction. Prompt tokens are stored in template memory (max 4 templates) for future use. Experiments on RGB-T (LasHeR: Precision 76.2%, Success 60.7%), RGB-D, and RGB-E benchmarks achieve state-of-the-art performance at 35 FPS. The framework is simple and effective.

### Strengths
- The prompt-based temporal information learning effectively integrates motion information for tracking (R3).
- Motivation is clear, experiments are comprehensive, and results are convincing (R2, R1, R4 on SOTA).
- PromptTrack captures target appearance changes and motion trajectories via streaming spatiotemporal prompts, leading to more accurate and robust tracking in complex environments (R1, R4).
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance (e.g., Precision 76.2%, Success 60.7% on LasHeR) while maintaining real-time speed (35 FPS), demonstrating both accuracy and practicality (R1, R4).
- Framework is simple and effective, with good readability and use of notations (R1).
- Exhibits scalability by extending naturally to RGB-D and RGB-E tasks (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Contribution heavily disputed (minority reviewer).** Reviewer 4 (rating 3, score minority) judges the contribution as weak, arguing the paper essentially adds an extra modality to ODTrack (Zheng et al., AAAI 2024), that the motivation is almost indistinguishable from ODTrack, and that it also uses a similar template sampling strategy. Other reviewers (R1–3, all ratings 6) did not cite this as a major weakness. This contrast should be noted.

- **Missing ablation studies and analyses:**
  - No ablation on number of search images during training; would increasing it improve temporal modeling? (R1)
  - No ablation on number of MSP blocks versus trade-off in MACs/Parameters. (R1)
  - Insufficient explanation why the prompt formulation is suitable to learn motion features. (R3)
  - Lacks a detailed comparison/analysis of how the proposed module is integrated into OSTrack (R3)—the backbone used.

- **Unclear or insufficient implementation details:**
  - Figure 3: various operations lack annotations (e.g., whether the plus sign indicates concatenation or addition), leading to ambiguity. (R2)
  - Template memory: maximum 4 templates is stated, but its size and how templates are updated are not described. (R2)
  - How is \(p_0\) obtained in Fig. 2? (R3)
  - Why is subtraction used to obtain the TIR spatial prompt in Eq. 9? Is this operation important? (R3)
  - Minor: the notation “k” appears both in “40k samples” and “k template images”; this should be unified. (R1)

- **Computational and memory costs insufficiently reported:**
  - The paper mentions the limitation of substantial storage for all historical templates but provides no detailed comparison of computational and memory requirements during training and inference versus baseline methods. (R2)
  - Reviewer 4 adds: for fair comparison, the paper should clearly indicate which prior trackers use a memory mechanism and which do not (ODTrack also uses one). Since adding memory typically reduces FPS, this information (including the impact on FPS) must be listed. (R4)

- **Incomplete experimental comparisons:**
  - On RGB-E and RGB-D benchmarks, only a few recent trackers are compared; more latest methods should be added to demonstrate advancement. (R2)
  - Performance of OSTrack (RGB-T) in Table 6 of Appendix C: is it tested with fine-tuning or without any retraining? (R1)
  - Are the reported performances on three RGB-T datasets based on a single set of model parameters or separate parameters for each dataset? (R1)
  - Reviewer 4 reiterates the need to clearly separate methods with and without memory mechanisms and to report FPS accordingly (already listed above).
