## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses multi-objective Bayesian optimization (BO) in the large-batch setting (batch sizes up to 1000s). It proposes LBN-MOBO, which uses a Bayesian neural network (BNN) surrogate (specifically a modified Deep Ensemble) and a new acquisition function (2MD) that performs non-dominated sorting of both predicted objectives and their epistemic uncertainties via NSGA-II. Experiments are conducted on synthetic functions (1 in the main paper, 4 in appendix) and two real-world problems (airfoil design, 3D printing) with batch sizes of 15,000 and 20,000 over 10 iterations (150,000 and 200,000 total evaluations).

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an important and practical problem: multi-objective BO with extremely large batches, where traditional acquisition functions (qEHVI, qParEGO, qNEHVI) become computationally intractable.
- The proposed method is simple and elegant, combining established components: BNN surrogates (popular in BO), NSGA-II (well-known in multi-objective optimization), and a concatenation of predictions and uncertainties as inputs to NSGA-II. This avoids sophisticated new engineering.
- Real-world evaluation on two interesting benchmarks (airfoil design, 3D printing) is a strength; releasing them in open-source would be a valuable contribution to the community.
- Section 5.4 (running the algorithm without uncertainties) clearly demonstrates the benefit of using uncertainty estimates.
- The concept is intuitive: encouraging exploration by also maximizing uncertainties.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns across multiple reviewers**: The idea of constructing a batch by finding the Pareto front of concatenated predictions and uncertainties is very similar to UCB (R1, R3, and R4 note it seems obvious; R1 and R3 say novelty is insufficient). The difference to UCB is that 2MD treats prediction and uncertainty as separate objectives rather than a weighted sum, but the reasoning behind this choice is not clearly justified (R1, R2). Some related work using the same multi-objective acquisition idea (Gupta et al., 2018) and large-batch Thompson sampling (Hernández-Lobato et al., 2017; Deshwal et al., 2021; Vakili et al., 2021) is not discussed (R2).
- **Unclear technical details and missing explanations**: The formal problem statement is not provided (R1). The background does not explain epistemic uncertainty, yet the proposed method only uses epistemic uncertainty—no motivation or insight for this choice (R1). The overall BO process description is placed in the method section rather than background (R1). The functioning of 2MD is not clearly explained: NSGA-II is a key component but is not described in the main paper; the only implementation statement is the last three lines of page 6 (R3). The magnitude/range of F is typically unknown, and there is no advice on balancing prediction and uncertainty (R3). The paper does not discuss issues with BNN surrogates, such as hyperparameter tuning being another optimization problem or inaccurate uncertainty estimates (R1).
- **Limited and unconvincing experimental evaluation**: 
  - The choice of surrogate model (Deep Ensembles) is based on only one synthetic experiment; its generalization to other tasks is not established (R1, R3).
  - In the two real-world tasks, only two methods are compared (modified Deep Ensemble + 2MD and dropout + 2MD); more baselines, including GP-based acquisition functions (qNEHVI, qParEGO, qEHVI) and evolutionary methods (MOEA/D, NSGA-II), are missing (R1, R3). Given the large number of function evaluations (150k–200k), EC methods may outperform, but they are not compared (R1).
  - For Figure 1, it is unclear why SGHMC, HMC, and Deep Ensembles are not paired with qNEHVI and qParEGO, and the total number of function evaluations is not reported (R1). The definition of “time” in Figures 1 and 3 is not given (R3).
- **Related work omissions**: The paper should mention other surrogate models beyond GP and BNN (e.g., TPE, Random Forests) and justify why BNN is preferred (R1). Also missing the aforementioned batch TS and multi-objective acquisition works (R2). Calling deep ensembles a Bayesian neural network is not entirely correct (R2).
- **Limited theoretical analysis**: No theoretical development or analysis of the proposed method is provided (R3).
- **Minor issues**: Citation style should use \citep{} and \citet{} appropriately (R1). The title is somewhat cluttered (R4).
- **Minor disagreement**: One reviewer (R4) found the paper clearly written (strength), while others (R1, R3) noted unclear organization and missing details. The paper's contribution is rated 2 by three reviewers and 3 by one (R4).
