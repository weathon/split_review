## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Physics-Informed Gaussians (PIGs), which replace the MLP in PINNs by learning feature embeddings via a mixture of trainable Gaussian functions (with learnable mean and covariance). This parametric mesh representation dynamically adjusts the position and shape of Gaussians during training, aiming to overcome spectral bias and the limitations of fixed-grid parametric methods. Experiments on PDEs such as Allen-Cahn, Helmholtz, and Nonlinear diffusion show competitive accuracy, supported by a universal approximation proof and error bars.

### Strengths
- Provides a theoretical proof of universal approximation capability for the proposed method.
- Experiments are diverse, baselines are sufficient, and the ablation study is detailed.
- Writing is overall clear, well-structured, and easy to follow, with clear motivation and well-discussed comparisons to previous works.
- The method offers a more concise and parameter-efficient feature embedding compared to prior parametric grid representations (e.g., grid-interpolation methods).
- Code is provided for reproducibility.
- Error bars are included, enhancing reliability of results.
- The approach overcomes limitations of traditional PINNs, maintains the PINNs optimization framework, and can be applied to various PDEs.

### Weaknesses
- **Originality concerns:** Mathematically, the Gaussian embedding is in the same spirit as a KAN layer with RBF, and Gaussian embeddings are not uncommon in ML. One reviewer found this similarity diminishes novelty, while another (more positive) noted there may be room to further expand discussion on novelty and potential impact.
- **Overclaimed dynamic adaptation:** The claim that the method “dynamically adjusts the positions and shapes of the Gaussians during training” gives an impression of adaptive sampling based on the residual or loss landscape, but the approach does not actually take into account the residual or loss function. A more precise description is needed.
- **Unfair comparisons and overstated results:** In experiments, the authors report best results for PIG without running baselines again under identical setup and parameter counts. The average error of PIG is generally higher, yet only the best results are highlighted. The claim of “more accurate and efficient” is not substantiated—efficiency (computation time) is not reported, and only solution accuracy is provided without timing comparisons.
- **Lack of evidence for spectral bias alleviation:** The paper states that locality encourages capturing high-frequency details by alleviating spectral bias, but no experimental or analytical evidence (e.g., frequency analysis) is provided. It is also unclear if this can be mathematically proven.
- **Missing experimental details and computational costs:** For 2D/3D cases, the mathematical formulation of Gaussian embedding, parameter initialization, and how boundary conditions are constrained are not described. The computational costs per iteration are mentioned only qualitatively vs. JAX-PI, but a detailed report would be beneficial. Applicability to high-dimensional PDEs (e.g., d≳100) and associated computational cost is not addressed.
- **Questions about Gaussian basis choice:** Multiple reviewers ask why Gaussian functions are chosen over other embeddings (e.g., SIREN’s sine, RBFs) and whether other functions would fail. Gaussian functions are local and may lack representation capability for PDEs with long-range interactions; this limitation is not discussed.
- **Mode collapse and training stability:** There are potential issues with mode collapse (μ_i → μ_j or σ_i → 0/∞) and significantly different update scales between W/θ and μ_i/σ_i that may depend on simulation scale. The paper does not address these.
- **Architecture choices:** The use of tanh activation (vs. sin or swish) is not justified. The title of Section 3.2.2 may be irrelevant.
- **Theoretical gaps:** Although universality is discussed, theoretical understanding of convergence properties is missing, as noted by one reviewer.
- **Minor presentation issue:** Figures should be vector images to avoid pixelization.
- **Contrast among reviewers:** One reviewer (score 8) found no significant weaknesses and was highly positive, while the other three (scores 6) raised the above concerns about novelty, fairness, and missing analyses. This divergence is not fully reconciled in the paper.
