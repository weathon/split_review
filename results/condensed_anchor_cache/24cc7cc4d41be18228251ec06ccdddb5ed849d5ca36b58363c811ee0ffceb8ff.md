## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DAEFR, a dual-branch framework for blind face restoration. It introduces an auxiliary low-quality (LQ) encoder trained on LQ data to capture domain-specific features, alongside a high-quality (HQ) encoder. Feature association (CLIP-like constraint) and multihead cross-attention fusion bridge the domain gap, and an HQ decoder reconstructs the image. Evaluations on synthetic and real-world datasets show promising performance.

### Strengths
- **Innovative dual-branch design**: The auxiliary LQ encoder trained specifically on LQ data is an original solution to the domain gap problem, differing from prior single-encoder HQ-pre-trained methods. The approach is intriguing and may inspire future work.
- **Feature association and fusion**: The use of CLIP-like association and multihead cross-attention to align and combine LQ and HQ features is novel and effectively bridges the domain gap.
- **Thorough ablation studies**: The significance of each component (LQ branch, association loss, fusion module) is extensively verified.
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: The method is evaluated on both synthetic and real-world datasets, with comparisons to state-of-the-art methods, demonstrating robustness and fidelity under severe degradation.
- **Well-structured and clear**: The paper is well-written, with logical flow, clear explanations, and effective visual aids (figures, tables).
- **Addresses common and severe degradations**: DAEFR handles various degradation types effectively.
- **Strong downstream performance**: The method outperforms baselines in face recognition tasks, indicating preserved identity.

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal contribution (minority view)**: Reviewer 3 questions whether adding another stream is a major contribution for a top-tier venue, as all components are well-established. Reviewers 4 and 5 (rating 5) also consider the contribution limited.
- **Missing full name**: The full name of DAEFR is not given at its first occurrence.
- **Motivation for domain gap not intuitive**: The current explanation of the domain gap issue (Figure 1 and text) does not make the motivation sufficiently clear.
- **Training process confusion**:
  - Why not combine the two training stages (autoencoder training, then encoder training with association loss) into one, or apply association loss only in stage 2?
  - In stage 3, the text says only MHCA and transformer are trained, but Figure 2(c) suggests the two encoders are not frozen.
- **Results not significantly outperforming on synthetic CelebA-Test** (Table 1): The proposed method does not substantially outperform others on this dataset.
- **LPIPS performance worse than alternatives** (Table 2): All other methods achieve better LPIPS scores. The paper lacks discussion or visualization to explain this.
- **Missing differentiation from CodeFormer**: No clear explanation of the key differences between DAEFR and CodeFormer.
- **No discussion of limitations or failure cases**: The paper does not analyze situations where DAEFR might fail or perform poorly.
- **No future research guidance**: No suggestions for potential improvements or extensions.
- **No analysis of extreme/uncommon degradations**: Missing insights into performance under diverse or rare degradations.
- **No computational efficiency details**: Processing time, resource requirements, and scalability for larger datasets/higher resolutions are not provided.
- **No user study for perceptual quality**: A user study could validate real-world applicability.
- **Missing ablation clarification for low-quality feature effectiveness**: The ablation study on the auxiliary branch could be more detailed (e.g., quantitative comparison without the branch).
- **Generalization to non-facial images**: Not investigated.
- **High training resource requirements**: The dual-encoder-decoder setup doubles training resources.
- **Spelling error**: “recently” used incorrectly at the beginning of the second paragraph in “Vector Quantized Codebook Prior” (Related Work).
