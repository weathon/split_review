- Scores: 8, 8, 8, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary

Motif uses an LLM (Llama-2-70B) as a preference annotator over pairs of event captions to train an intrinsic reward model, which is then combined with extrinsic reward to train an RL agent via standard RL. Tested on the NetHack Learning Environment, Motif’s agents outperform those trained solely to maximize the game score, solve tasks where no progress had been made without demonstrations, and exhibit human-aligned behaviors that can be steered via prompt modifications. The method scales with LLM size and prompt information. Three reviewers (scores 8,8,8) find the idea interesting and the results strong; one reviewer (score 5) argues the contribution is overstated and that the key insight is joint optimization of preference-based and extrinsic reward rather than the LLM itself.

### Strengths

- The paper is clear, well-written, and well-organized. (All reviewers)
- Figure 1 provides a clean bird's‑eye view of the approach; the spider graph (Figure 4) adds helpful qualitative assessment. (Reviewer 1)
- The ablation experiments are exhaustive, covering scaling laws, prior vs. zero knowledge, prompt rewordings, etc. (Reviewer 1)
- The idea of using an LLM as a preference annotator for preference-based RL is innovative, interesting, and practically useful. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3)
- The method scales well with LLM size and is sensitive to prompt modifications, offering flexibility. (Reviewer 2)
- The paper provides a comprehensive analysis of both quantitative and qualitative agent behaviors. (Reviewer 2)
- The loss function for training the intrinsic reward model (Equation 1) is clearly presented. (Reviewer 3)
- Empirical results are strong – Motif solves difficult, sparse-reward NetHack tasks that previous intrinsic‑motivation methods could not solve. (Reviewer 4 – minority reviewer still acknowledges this significance)
- Training agents with intrinsic rewards is very effective; intrinsic‑only agents surprisingly outperform score‑only agents. (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty and contribution (minority reviewer makes this explicit):** One reviewer (score 5) argues that the core contribution is joint optimization of preference-based and extrinsic reward, not the use of an LLM. They note that the method is nearly identical to standard preference‑based RL (e.g., Christiano et al., 2017) and that existing works (e.g., [2] using GPT‑4, [3] incorporating LLM rewards) already explore LLM‑guided decision‑making. This reviewer believes describing the approach as “LLM’s contribution” may be an overstatement. (Reviewer 4)
- **Computational cost and efficiency:** Using a 70‑billion parameter LLM to generate a preference dataset from captions is expensive. The paper lacks details on the time and resources required for caption generation, preference elicitation, and agent training. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2)
- **Missing baselines and comparisons:**
    - No baseline using a simpler model (e.g., sentiment analysis) to generate preferences. (Reviewer 1)
    - Insufficient comparison to other methods that integrate LLMs into decision‑making agents, especially those using LLMs for reward functions or exploration guidance. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4)
    - Reviewer 4 explicitly cites [1] (preference‑based RL), [2] (GPT‑4 for games), and [3] (LLM‑based rewards) as related work that should have been compared or acknowledged more clearly.
- **Generalizability concerns:** It is unclear whether Motif can be applied outside the NetHack Learning Environment. NLE’s structured, discrete actions and constrained visual space may make LLM preference annotation easier; complex continuous environments (robotics, driving) or those with high‑dimensional visual scenes may pose significant challenges. (Reviewer 3)
- **Terminology issue:** Classifying the preference‑based reward as an “intrinsic” reward is questionable – it originates from external knowledge (humans or LLM), not the agent’s own experience. (Reviewer 4 – minority)
- **Lack of discussion on limitations and potential drawbacks:**
    - No discussion of biases in LLM preferences, the impact of prompt engineering on generated rewards, or the possibility of the agent exploiting the intrinsic reward in unintended ways. (Reviewer 2)
    - No robustness evaluation against adversarial attacks or environment variations. (Reviewer 2)
- **Unanswered questions that overlap with criticisms or missing analyses:**
    - Is the alignment with human intuition a consequence of the strong LLM or the wording of the prompt? (Reviewer 1)
    - Why is the coefficient for the intrinsic reward α₁ fixed at 0.1? Only an ablation over α₂ is provided. (Reviewer 1)
    - In Figure 6c, the reworded prompt yields low score but steadily rising dungeon level – could the agent be hallucinating its dungeon level due to a very high intrinsic reward? (Reviewer 1)
    - Why do agents trained on extrinsic reward only perform worse than those trained on intrinsic reward only? (Reviewer 2)
    - What is the optimal strategy to balance intrinsic and extrinsic rewards during training? (Reviewer 2)
    - Which RL algorithm is used for fine‑tuning? (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4 – both note this is missing from main text)
    - Why use LLaMA 2 rather than GPT‑3.5/4? (Reviewer 4)
    - How robust are Motif‑trained agents against adversarial attacks or when deployed in varied environments? (Reviewer 2)
    - Can Motif be applied to other environments beyond NLE? (Reviewer 3 – aligns with generalizability weakness)
- **Minor issue:** In Section 2, “O the observation space” should be “O is the observation space”. (Reviewer 4)
