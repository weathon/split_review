## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents GOLD, a framework for graph OOD detection that synthesizes pseudo-OOD data via implicit adversarial learning without requiring pre-existing OOD datasets or pre-trained models. A latent generative model (LGM) is trained to mimic in-distribution (ID) embeddings from an evolving GNN, while a GNN encoder and OOD detector are trained to classify ID data and maximize energy divergence between ID and synthetic embeddings. Experiments on five benchmark graph datasets show GOLD outperforms non-exposure methods and matches/exceeds real-OOD exposure baselines. The framework supports both LDM and VAE variants.

### Strengths
- **Novel adversarial latent generation**: The idea of generating pseudo-OOD samples from ID embeddings through implicit adversarial learning is creative and addresses the lack of OOD data without relying on pre-trained generative models.
- **No OOD data or pre-trained models required**: The method alleviates the need for real OOD samples or external models, making it broadly applicable.
- **Strong empirical performance**: Extensive experiments on five benchmark graph datasets demonstrate robustness; improvements on FPR95 are particularly impressive (noted by multiple reviewers). The method outperforms non-OOD baselines and matches/exceeds real-OOD exposure baselines.
- **Comprehensive evaluation with solid baselines**: Multiple datasets and metrics are used, supporting the method’s effectiveness across diverse graph types and OOD scenarios.
- **Clear ID/OOD embedding divergence**: Experimental visualizations verify the energy-based approach produces a clear separation between ID and synthetic pseudo-OOD embeddings.
- **Well-structured paper with theoretical proofs**: The paper is easy to follow, well-organized, and includes theoretical justifications.
- **Implementation flexibility**: Supports both LDM and VAE variants, offering trade-offs between performance and computational cost.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty of individual components**: One reviewer (score 6) notes that using generative models to synthesize samples for downstream tasks (e.g., continual learning) and energy-based detection are widely adopted; the main contribution appears to be the new divergence regularization (Eq. 12). This reviewer questions what distinguishes GOLD’s use of the generator from prior works and asks for a clearer articulation of the key novelty.
- **Missing ablative studies**: The same reviewer requests ablations on the loss terms (\(L_{DReg}\), \(L_{Unc}\), \(L_{EReg}\))—removing each or applying individually—to better demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed divergence regularization. This overlap with a criticism is placed here as a weakness.
- **Potential confusion from overly close pseudo-OOD samples**: If synthetic embeddings are too close to the ID distribution, the OOD detector may misclassify ID samples as pseudo-OOD. The authors are asked to clarify specific techniques used to control distributional proximity. This reviewer question overlaps with a criticism about the generation process.
- **High variance in FPR95 on Amazon and Coauthor datasets**: One reviewer observes substantial variance and asks whether it is linked to the distributional control during generation. This is a missing analysis/explanation.
- **Blank figure**: Figure 1(d) appears blank, hindering interpretation of the paper’s content.
- **Missing extension to graph-level OOD detection**: A reviewer asks what modifications would be needed to extend beyond node-level to graph-level detection.
- **Training computational cost**: While acceptable and discussed in the limitations section, the cost induced by pseudo-OOD generation is noted.
- **Clarity issues**: The subscript notation (e.g., \([0], [1]\)) in Eq. (10) is unclear and should be explained.
- **Limited backbone diversity**: The paper could benefit from trying other backbone GNN models beyond those used.
- **Deeper analysis of FPR95 results**: One reviewer suggests an in-depth analysis of why FPR95 improvements are especially strong and why similar gains are not as pronounced on other datasets (though improvements are still good). This minor missing analysis is placed as a weakness.
- **Presentation improvement needed**: One reviewer (score 6) assigns Presentation 2 and suggests overall presentation needs improvement.
