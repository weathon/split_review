## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviewers largely agree that the paper addresses an important problem (multi-agent online submodular maximization) and makes significant contributions: improving the approximation ratio from \(1/(1+c)\) to the tight \((1-e^{-c})/c\), relaxing the complete-graph requirement to a connected graph, and providing a projection-free variant (MA‑OSEA). Theoretical results are considered strong and sound. However, several weaknesses are noted: insufficient clarity in the problem formulation and assumptions, lack of practical justification for the marginal-gain oracle, no formal analysis of communication complexity, high computational cost (2∑|V_i| function evaluations per round), limited experimental validation (no error bars, only 5 iterations, no direct verification of theoretical bounds), and numerous presentation/notation errors. Reviewers 2 and 5 raise many clarity issues; Reviewer 1 questions venue relevance. The computational cost, oracle reliance, and unclear novelty relative to prior offline methods are recurring concerns.

### Strengths
- Novel and sound contribution with strong theoretical results: achieves the tight approximation ratio \((1-e^{-c})/c\) (vs. \(1/(1+c)\) for state-of-the-art OSG) and regret \(\widetilde{O}\bigl(\sqrt{C_T T/(1-\beta)}\bigr)\). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5)
- Relaxes the requirement of a complete communication graph to a connected graph, enabling sparse communication. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4, 5)
- Effective use of a novel non‑oblivious surrogate function for the multi‑linear extension to avoid sub‑optimal stationary points, incorporating curvature information. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- The projection‑free variant (MA‑OSEA) eliminates the computationally expensive projection step using KL divergence and mixing a uniform distribution. (Reviewer 2)
- Paper is well‑structured overall, with coherent flow and a well‑organized appendix. (Reviewers 1, 3, 5)
- Empirical validation via a multi‑target tracking simulation demonstrates effectiveness against prior methods. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- Advances prior work to near‑optimal approximation and substantially relaxes assumptions. (Reviewer 5)

### Weaknesses
1. **Problem formulation and assumptions**:
   - Assumptions (e.g., lines 159, 177) are not listed explicitly. (Reviewer 1)
   - The connection to the motivating example is unclear: why the objective is revealed after decisions and why it is guaranteed submodular. (Reviewer 1)
   - The assumption that each agent has a mutually disjoint action set (line 157) seems odd; in tracking, agents could execute the same action. (Reviewer 1, Q1; also Reviewer 2: Section 2.1 claims unique discrete sets yet simulations use identical action sets like up/down.)
   - The requirement that the objective must be monotone submodular is strong; how restrictive is this? (Reviewer 1, Q4)
   - The paper assumes a static communication graph, but applications like multi‑target tracking may involve dynamic topologies; no discussion of adaptation. (Reviewer 3)

2. **Theoretical concerns and comparisons**:
   - Reliance on curvature parameter \(c\): it may be unknown a priori; sensitivity to \(c\) and algorithm behavior under different \(c\) settings not analyzed. (Reviewer 4; also Reviewer 1 Q3 on novelty of curvature info)
   - Unclear why existing offline algorithms (Vondrak, Bian et al.) cannot achieve the \((1-e^{-c})/c\) bound, requiring the new surrogate approach. (Reviewer 2)
   - The regret bound degrades from \(\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{C_T T})\) to \(\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{C_T T/(1-\beta)})\) compared to prior work; the trade‑off is not theoretically justified. (Reviewer 5, Q2)
   - The relationship between spectral gap \((1-\beta)\) and approximation ratio is not discussed; there appears to be a trade‑off between regret and approximation that is not explored. (Reviewer 2)
   - Novelty of Theorem 2 compared to Corollary 7 of Zhang et al. 2024 is unclear (Remark 3). (Reviewer 5, Q4)
   - The analysis of the non‑oblivious surrogate function’s benefits over existing surrogates could be clearer. (Reviewer 1, Q3)

3. **Algorithmic concerns (oracle, computation, communication)**:
   - The algorithm requires querying a local marginal‑gain oracle multiple times per step (2∑|V_i| evaluations in Algorithm 1 line 11 and Algorithm 2 line 12). It is not evident how such an oracle exists in practice when the submodular function is unknown or expensive. (Reviewers 3, 4)
   - Computational cost is high: evaluating the objective 2∑|V_i| times per iteration can be prohibitive for large action spaces or complex \(f_t\); no complexity analysis or strategies to reduce evaluations. (Reviewer 4)
   - Communication efficiency is claimed in the title but not formally analyzed: no discussion of number of communication rounds or information per round; tightness of communication complexity not addressed. (Reviewer 3)
   - The explanation of avoiding Lipschitzness of KL‑divergence via “mixing a uniform distribution” is unclear (Theorem 5, Remark 9); no uniform‑distribution parameters appear in the theorem. (Reviewer 2)
   - How to sample from actions under the constraint \(\sum x_a \le 1\) without normalization? (Reviewer 5, Q3)
   - The difference between the two proposed algorithms (MA‑OSMA vs. MA‑OSEA) is not clearly explained beyond Table 1; more insight needed on the cost of projection and how they differ beyond a \(\log T\) factor. (Reviewers 2, 5)
   - Comparison with Distributed‑CG (appendix only) should be in the main paper. (Reviewer 5, Q5)

4. **Experimental evaluation**:
   - Simulations lack error bars; only 5 iterations are used, which is insufficient for robust conclusions. (Reviewer 5)
   - No direct verification of theoretical bounds: e.g., plotting ratio of empirical objective to optimal value across instances would strengthen claims. (Reviewer 4)
   - Validation limited to one scenario; should include varying graph topologies, numbers of agents, numbers of actions, and different (non‑homogeneous) target types. (Reviewers 1, 4)
   - Figure 3: rows representing different target types are not labeled in the figure or caption; the meaning of the two non‑utility columns is unclear. (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 4 Q3)
   - The comparison with OSG in simulations would benefit from a more direct explanation of why OSG only achieves \(1/(1+c)\) approximation and how the proposed methods differ. (Reviewer 2)

5. **Presentation and clarity**:
   - The abstract contains overly technical details (e.g., “skillfully harnessing the KL divergence”) and should be more high‑level. (Reviewers 2, 5)
   - Numerous notation issues and typos: MA‑OSM introduced twice (line 49, line 154); line 138 typo “lowercase”; line 142 notation \(s\) undefined; line 225 notation \(F\) unclear; Theorem 2 missing “for any \(x,y\)”; Algorithm 1 uses adaptive \(\eta_t\) yet Remark 8 sets constant; Assumption 5 expectation derivation or assumption unclear and \(G\) undefined; \(\gamma\) redefined; \(C_1,C_2,C_3\) conflict with Theorem 3; multiple \(F^A\) vs. \(F^s\) typos (lines 1029, 1291); line 992 typo \(y\) should be \(x\); line 1058 “argmin?”; line 1100 bracket typo; line 1126 typo; line 1183 references unspecific; line 1374 typo “first equation of Lemma 12”; line 1420 typo “\(f\) missing”. (Reviewer 5)
   - Related work section is not informative enough; more detailed explanation and comparison of works in Table 1 needed. (Reviewer 5)
   - Line 83: citations needed for “well‑established consensus techniques.” (Reviewer 5)
   - The OSG method should be described and compared to MA‑OSMA/MA‑OSEA more directly, including assumption differences. (Reviewer 2)
   - The contribution’s novelty relative to existing methods (e.g., online mirror ascent, consensus techniques) could be better specified. (Reviewer 1; also Reviewer 5 Q1)
   - Reviewer 1 notes the problem seems more specific to robotics, less relevant to the venue.
   - Minor: line 70 hyperlink redundant. (Reviewer 1)
   - The need for Assumption 5’s expectation derivation or assumption unclear. (Reviewer 5)
