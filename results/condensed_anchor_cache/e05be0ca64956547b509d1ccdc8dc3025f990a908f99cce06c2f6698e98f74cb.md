## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces ViDiT-Q, a quantization method tailored for Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) in text-to-image and video generation. It addresses challenges like time-varying channel imbalance and data variation via fine-grained dynamic quantization, static-dynamic channel balancing, and a metric-decoupled mixed-precision approach. Experiments show significant memory (2–2.5×) and latency (1.4–1.7×) reductions with minimal quality loss at W8A8 and W4A8.

### Strengths
- Provides a detailed analysis of quantization error sources in DiTs, leading to principled solutions; the paper is well-structured and clearly explains each component (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Unique combination of scaling (addressing static channel imbalance at early denoising) and rotation (handling dynamic distribution variations over time) for channel balancing (Reviewer 1).
- Dynamic quantization tailored to temporal variations in video sequences reduces color deviations (Reviewer 1).
- Extensive validation on multiple text-to-image and video generation models; qualitative results show high image quality and text-image alignment, while naive PTQ produces blurred/noisy outputs (Reviewer 1).
- Achieves lossless W8A8 quantization and W4A8 with negligible degradation; outperforms existing diffusion quantization methods significantly (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Demonstrates hardware efficiency gains (2–2.5× memory, 1.4–1.7× latency on A100), making deployment on constrained devices feasible (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited exploration of lower bit-widths**: Performance drops noticeably at W4A4 and W4A2; configurations like W4A4 or W2A8 are not studied, limiting applicability for aggressive compression on edge devices. (Reviewer 1, a self-declared non-expert, and Reviewer 3, with high confidence, both raise this point.)
2. **Complex parameter management**: ViDiT-Q introduces multiple quantization parameters (e.g., different α values per timestep), increasing model complexity. (Reviewer 1)
3. **Insufficient clarity on differences from prior work**: The precise distinctions between “channel-wise” and “output-channel-wise” grouping, and why “timestep-wise” parameters are more costly than the proposed dynamic quantization, are not quantitatively explained. (Reviewer 2)
4. **Missing ablation studies**: The paper lacks ablations isolating the individual contributions of fine-grained dynamic quantization, static-dynamic channel balancing, and metric-decoupled mixed precision. (Reviewer 3)
5. **Limited baseline comparisons**: Only diffusion-specific quantization methods are compared; general techniques (e.g., adaptive quantization, knowledge distillation for compression) are not benchmarked. (Reviewer 3)
6. **Restricted task scope and hardware testing**: Evaluation covers only text-to-image and text-to-video; applicability to other DiT tasks (e.g., super-resolution, image manipulation) is unexplored. Hardware results are limited to a single setup (NVIDIA A100); testing on lower-power devices is missing. (Reviewer 3)
7. **Metric-decoupled sensitivity analysis open questions**: The paper does not clarify whether some modules are sensitive to multiple metrics simultaneously, nor how sensitivity analysis adapts to different models/layers. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3)

**Reviewer contrast**: Reviewer 1 (confidence 2, self-declared non-expert) noted the complexity and suggested integrating 8-bit attention (SageAttention) or linear attention mechanisms; this suggestion is not echoed by the other reviewers. All reviewers assign a score of 6, indicating no disagreement in overall assessment, but confidence varies (2, 2, 5).
