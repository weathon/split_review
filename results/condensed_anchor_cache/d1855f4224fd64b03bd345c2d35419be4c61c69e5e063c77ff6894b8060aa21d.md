## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Gradually Pruned Queries (GPQ), a post-training method that incrementally prunes redundant object queries in DETR-based 3D detectors by sorting queries by classification scores and iteratively removing the lowest-scoring one. Experiments on nuScenes with several PETR-based architectures show that pruning can reduce FLOPs and inference time (up to 67.86% FLOPs reduction, 76.38% time reduction on Jetson Nano) while maintaining or slightly improving detection performance.

### Strengths
- This is the first study to explore query pruning specifically in query-based 3D detectors.
- The idea of using classification scores to prune queries is simple and reasonable.
- The approach is well motivated: Fig. 2 demonstrates that query selection across different detectors is highly imbalanced (a few queries are used very often, many are rarely selected).
- GPQ is straightforward, requires no additional learnable parameters, and can be applied to any existing checkpoint after training, making it universally applicable.
- The paper is well structured and illustrated, with clear figures (Fig. 1, Fig. 3) that support the text.
- Experiments are thorough for the proposed method itself, evaluating on four current PETR-based architectures (StreamPETR, FocalPETR, etc.) and showing consistent throughput improvements without degradation in mAP/NDS.
- Results on the nuScenes validation set are promising, including positive surprises where performance slightly improves after pruning.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with related work:**
  - No comparison with prior pruning methods or implemented baseline pruning techniques (e.g., magnitude-based weight pruning, structural pruning).
  - No comparison with 2D DETR acceleration methods such as QueryDet (Yang et al., CVPR 2022) or SRDD (Zhu et al., 2022), nor a discussion of what challenges exist in transferring such methods to 3D.
  - No quantitative comparison with alternative efficiency techniques: Token Merge ([A]), model quantization, or the strong camera baseline SparseBEV with 640×1600 resolution on the nuScenes leaderboard.
- **Lack of ablation studies:**
  - No ablation on design choices: e.g., incremental pruning vs. pruning directly to the target number of queries in one go.
  - No oracle-based pruner baseline (prune the worst query when matched against ground-truth boxes) to understand how close the score-based approach is to optimal.
  - No investigation into why training with a large set of queries and pruning post hoc is better than training with fewer queries from the start. Which queries get pruned? Do they attend to similar spatial areas? Inverting the algorithm (prune the most confident queries) would help understand whether low-confidence queries still produce reasonable OBBs.
- **Limited experimental scope:**
  - Experiments only on the nuScenes dataset; no validation on other 3D detection datasets (e.g., Waymo, KITTI).
  - All main experiments use ResNet50 backbone and relatively low input resolutions (e.g., 256×704). Missing evaluations on higher resolutions (512×1508, 640×1600) and with different backbone architectures to assess scalability.
  - Missing evaluation on detectors that use both content and spatial queries (e.g., PETR variants with explicit spatial query components); the paper should add such experiments.
- **Inconsistent training epochs:**
  - In StreamPETR and FocalPETR, ablation studies use 24 epochs while comparisons with other methods use 60 epochs. A direct comparison with 60-epoch training is needed to ensure fairness.
- **Missing evaluation metrics:**
  - The paper reports only accuracy and FLOPs/time. Model parameters and memory cost are also important for pruning; these should be reported (e.g., in Table 1 and Table 2). Similarly, providing time and memory costs alongside accuracy in Table 1 would help readers see the efficiency-accuracy trade-off.
  - Timing on Jetson Nano is valuable, but desktop GPU timings (common in related work) are not reported, making direct comparability difficult. Tables 3 and 4 could be combined to make room for additional analyses.
- **Analysis gaps:**
  - Using fewer queries may slow down network convergence; the same number of epochs for different query counts may therefore unfairly penalize the fewer-query baseline. The paper should discuss or compensate for this.
  - The reason why high-score queries perform better than low-score queries (and why pruning after training is beneficial) is not explained. The analysis of Fig. 2 could be extended.
  - Fig. 4 shows only reprojections of 3D boxes into frames, which hides depth errors; top-down 3D visualizations would be more informative.
- **Writing and presentation issues:**
  - Numerous typos: e.g., "3D objec detection" (L351), "each query as as the fundamental" (L264), "it is the queries" (L268), "The reason of why our method" (L300). English writing needs improvement.
  - Figure 1: font inconsistent with other figures, Y-axis label unclear.
  - Figure 2: renders poorly, text hard to read.
  - Figure 3: the last column label should be "Iteration n+1" instead of ambiguous "Iteration n" run twice.
  - Algorithm 1 is too detailed and does not clarify the high-level concept; it should be simplified.
