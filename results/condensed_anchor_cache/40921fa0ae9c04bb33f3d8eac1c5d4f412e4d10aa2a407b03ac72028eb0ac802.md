## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a heuristic framework for early stopping in semi-supervised deep learning, combining three criteria: model stability (weight convergence), generalizability (validation cross-entropy loss), and pseudo-label confidence (sliding window over most reliable labels). Experiments are conducted on a single mHealth dataset (4,700 observations, 62% unlabeled) and compare against a baseline without these heuristics, showing improved accuracy and F1.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-motivated and addresses a practical, real-world problem in healthcare (mHealth) where missing labels are common and semi-supervised learning is widely used.
- The combination of weight stabilization, generalizability tracking, and pseudo-label confidence is an interesting approach that could guide SSL training.
- The application to mHealth data is novel and the framework could be valuable for other domains with similar label-sparsity challenges.
- The paper is clearly written and the problem is well-motivated with relevant healthcare examples.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experiments**: Only one dataset (4,700 obs, ~1,800 labeled) is used, with no error bars reported, so results may lack statistical significance and generalizability.
- **Insufficient baselines**: The only baseline is a model without the heuristics. Missing comparisons to:
  - Related early stopping methods (e.g., Bai et al.: Understanding and Improving Early Stopping for Learning with Noisy Labels).
  - State-of-the-art SSL methods (e.g., UPS, FlexMatch, SLA, CSA) that change pseudo-label selection and may interact differently with the heuristics.
- **Lack of ablation studies**: The contribution of each criterion in the combined approach is not isolated.
- **No theoretical analysis**: No justification for why these three specific criteria were chosen, and no analysis of how the criteria interact (Section 2.5.4). The heuristics are not new and are already widely used in practice.
- **Limited coverage of SSL paradigms**: The paper incorrectly refers to properties of pseudo-labeling (PL) methods as characteristics of all deep SSL methods, ignoring consistency-regularization approaches that do not use pseudo-labels during training.
- **Potential architecture and data sensitivity**: The early stopping criteria may be highly dependent on model architecture and training dynamics. For example, in Figures 2-4, validation loss does not increase during training as typically expected in real-world settings, raising concerns about whether experiments reflect realistic conditions.
- **Scalability concerns**: The combined criteria method relies on ranking, which may not scale well with larger datasets or models.
- **Missing related work section**: No discussion of prior SSL/early stopping literature.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity not explored**: Questions about how results depend on the confidence threshold θ, sliding window size w, dataset size, label percentage, and weighting scheme for the combined criteria. Also, how the confidence heuristic should adapt when different PL selector functions (vanilla PL vs. UPS) are used.
