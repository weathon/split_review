## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a transfer evasion attack against learning-based image watermarks in the no-box setting (no access to the target watermark model or detection API). The attacker trains multiple surrogate watermark models (encoders+decoders) and uses PGD to find a perturbation that causes the extracted watermarks from these surrogates to deviate. The perturbation is then applied to a watermarked image, and it is shown to transfer to the target watermark detector, evading detection. Experimental results across several learning-based watermarking schemes demonstrate effectiveness, with the attack outperforming common post-processing methods and some existing transfer attacks. The paper also provides theoretical analysis for the inverse-decode scenario. The reviewers generally find the problem timely, the motivation clear, and the presentation good, but raise several concerns about computational cost, comparison fairness, transferability to non-learning-based and architecturally different watermarks, and the practical utility of the theoretical bounds.

### Strengths
- The robustness of image watermarks in the no-box setting (no access to model or API) is a relatively overlooked and practical area; the paper helps highlight this risk.
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow; motivation and method are intuitive.
- The proposed attack is effective across multiple learning-based watermarking schemes (e.g., inverse-decode, forward-decode) and outperforms several baselines (e.g., JPEG, Gaussian noise/blur, Brightness/Contrast, MI-CWA, DiffPure) at certain budget levels.
- The theoretical analysis (though limited) and experimental results support the method design; one reviewer (Reviewer 4) found the experimental studies comprehensive and the method works very well, giving a higher rating than the others. [Note: one reviewer (Reviewer 2) also noted that theoretical results support claims.]
- The issue of watermarking generative model outputs is timely and interesting.

### Weaknesses
- **Computational cost**: The proposed method requires training many surrogate models and solving the optimization (Eq. 3), which is computationally expensive. This is a major concern for practical deployment. Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R4) request an explicit analysis and empirical comparison of FLOPs and time with other attacks. R2 further notes that cost escalates with complex architectures and longer watermarks, hindering practical implementation for high-capacity watermarks.
- **Fairness of baseline comparison**: The comparison is inconclusive because it selectively compares at certain budget levels (e.g., r<=0.1, SSIM>0.9) without showing the full trade-off between evasion rate and perturbation budget (L2, Linf, SSIM). Some attacks may work better at other budgets. A proper comparison should include evasion rates across a range of representative budgets for all baselines. (R1)
- **Transfer to non-learning-based watermarks**: The paper’s experiments mainly target learning-based methods that are architecturally similar to the surrogates. The claim that the attack works on non-learning-based methods (Figure 13) lacks detail about the target methods. Moreover, non-learning-based variants (e.g., Tree-Ring variants, 0-bit watermarks) may exhibit greater robustness than the baselines evaluated. The paper does not discuss whether the victim could use a fundamentally different scheme designed to resist this attack. (R1, R2)
- **Architecture transferability**: For the transfer attack, if the target uses ResNet-18, did the surrogate models use similar architectures (e.g., ResNet-X)? The paper should also consider attacks when the target uses a totally different architecture, such as transformer-based watermark models. (R2)
- **Theoretical analysis limitations**: The theoretical results have limited practical utility. (a) The independence assumptions about decoder outputs are questionable because the perturbation δ is optimized to simultaneously fool multiple surrogate decoders, introducing correlations that are unaddressed. (b) The analysis only applies to inverse-decode, not forward-decode or other schemes. (c) The bounds depend on quantities (a, b, β, p) that involve the unknown target decoder T and data distribution, making them incalculable by the attacker in the no-box setting. (R4) One reviewer (R4) specifically found the theoretical contribution insufficient, while another (R2) considered it supportive—there is disagreement on its significance.
- **Perturbation visibility**: To ensure high image quality, the L∞ bound should be kept small (e.g., 0.03, 0.05). The paper tests larger values (e.g., 0.2) which visibly degrade image quality and reduce practicality. The paper should include comparisons at small L∞ bounds to demonstrate effectiveness under minimal visual impact. (R3)
- **Placement of theoretical section**: Section 5 (theory) is a claimed main contribution but is placed in the appendix; it should be in the main text. (R3)
- **Lack of insight for superior empirical performance**: The proposed method significantly outperforms SOTA, but no justification or insight is given for why it works so much better. (R4)
