## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Goal-conditioned Data Augmentation (GODA), a diffusion-based method for offline RL that generates synthetic transitions conditioned on return-to-go (RTG). GODA incorporates three selection mechanisms for conditioning, a controllable goal scaling factor λ, and adaptive gated conditioning. Evaluations on D4RL (diverse tasks) and Traffic Signal Control (TSC) tasks show that GODA improves performance over several baselines, including SynthER, when used to augment the original offline dataset (mixed training). The method aims to improve data quality in suboptimal datasets by generating higher-return transitions.

### Strengths
- **Innovative integration of return (goal) conditioning with diffusion models for offline data augmentation.** Conditioning on return-to-go is a sensible direction for improving sample quality (Reviewer 1, 3). The adaptive gated conditioning and adjustable scaling (λ) are designed to improve accuracy and diversity (Reviewer 3).
- **Thorough experimental evaluation on diverse D4RL tasks and real-world TSC tasks**, covering different dataset quality levels and multiple offline RL algorithms (Reviewer 1, 2). GODA outperforms SynthER in these settings (Reviewer 2).
- **The method enhances data diversity and quality** without generating excessive synthetic data (Reviewer 3). Clear explanation of essential features, though some technical details (e.g., adaptive gated conditioning) need further elaboration (Reviewer 3).
- **Extension to practical TSC tasks** is appreciated (Reviewer 4), as offline RL benchmarks often remain narrow.

### Weaknesses
- **Evaluation mismatch:** For baselines, offline policies are trained *only* on generated data, while GODA uses the *original data mixed with generated data*. This discrepancy makes reported comparisons unfair to baselines (Reviewer 1, 2). Ablations on the proportion of mixed synthetic data are needed, as well as experiments where all methods use 100% real + x% generated data (Reviewer 1, 2). Reviewers note this is a major flaw that obscures true performance differences.
- **Limited novelty / incremental contribution:** GODA essentially extends SynthER by adding reward-to-go conditioning and a conditioning mechanism. The structure of the conditioning (e.g., adaLN) and classifier-free guidance have been previously explored (Reviewer 2, 4). A highly similar prior work, GTA (Lee et al., NeurIPS 2024), also uses classifier-free guidance with a scaling factor for augmentation—the paper does not adequately distinguish its contributions from GTA (Reviewer 4).
- **Related work gaps:** The paper omits discussion of established hindsight relabeling methods and generative trajectory augmentation techniques (e.g., GTA, Furuta et al., ICLR 2022, Liu & Abbeel, ICML 2023) that are highly relevant (Reviewer 2). Additional experimental comparisons with hindsight relabeling methods for offline RL are warranted (Reviewer 2).
- **Methodology clarity insufficient:** 
  - The rationale for proposing three distinct condition selection mechanisms is unclear (Reviewer 2).
  - Controllable goal scaling (λ) lacks a systematic selection approach; it is not specified how λ is tuned across environments (Reviewer 2, 4).
  - The conditioning structure (adaptive gated conditioning) is not sufficiently detailed and has been used in prior work (Reviewer 2, 3).
  - **Abuse of “goal-conditioning”:** The term “goal” actually refers to return-to-go, not a typical goal vector (e.g., desired state). This is highly confusing and should be renamed “return-conditioning” (Reviewer 4).
- **Generating transitions conditioned on cumulative future returns is unnatural:** Cumulative future rewards are an unbiased but high-variance estimator; conditioning a *transition* on such a noisy variable is fundamentally challenging. Successful return-conditioned generative models typically generate entire trajectories (e.g., Diffuser, Decision Transformer) (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of visual/image-based task experiments:** Several real-world offline RL scenarios involve visual inputs; the absence of such experiments limits applicability (Reviewer 3).
- **No investigation of data efficiency:** The paper does not examine whether GODA reduces the need for large datasets or extensive generation; efficiency should be evaluated (e.g., performance vs. dataset size) (Reviewer 3).
- **Experiments mostly on dense-reward environments,** deviating from typical goal-conditioned RL scenarios that feature sparse rewards (Reviewer 2).
- **TSC performance differences are small across algorithms** (Reviewer 4); random goal condition is claimed to increase diversity but no validation is provided (Reviewer 4).
- **Notation issues:** Noise ε is replaced with n (Reviewer 2); L2 distance notation should use ||·||² (page 8, line 410) (Reviewer 2).
