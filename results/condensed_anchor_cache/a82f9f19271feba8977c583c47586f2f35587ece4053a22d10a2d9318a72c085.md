## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an inference-time method to improve LLM faithfulness in rationale generation using a dual-reward probabilistic inference framework. A local reward (from a domain-specific expert model) and a lookahead reward (via rollouts similar to MCTS) reweight token logits. Experiments on three reasoning tasks (using Llama-3-8B and Mistral-7b-Instruct) show gains in both accuracy and faithfulness. However, multiple reviewers raise serious concerns about clarity, fairness of baselines, novelty, and fundamental assumptions about faithfulness.

### Strengths
- Faithful rationales are important for explainability and model control; the problem is well-motivated.
- The dual-reward mechanism (local token-level reward + lookahead/global reward) is a novel combination and an interesting direction for inference-time improvement.
- The ablation study (Section 5.1) clearly demonstrates the benefit of the lookahead reward over only local rewards.
- The method is training-free with respect to the backbone LLM (though it relies on pre-trained expert models), making it portable.
- A comprehensive set of experiments across different datasets shows consistent improvements in accuracy and faithfulness metrics.
- The experimental results at a surface level show large gains, and the approach addresses a hot topic (MCTS-type decoding for LLMs).

### Weaknesses
- **Poor clarity and missing details in method description**: Section 3 is badly written. The connection to Feynman-Kac formulae is not explained. Equations 1–3 are confusing: notation (e.g., \(t \wedge T\), \(P(s_t)\) posterior over what?, \(\pi_t\) as a potential function) is ambiguous. Equation 2’s indicator function is unclear (is the intent to restrict logits to tokens in \(C\)?). Equation 3’s equality \(s_{t+l}=s_{t-1}||w_t\) is inconsistent (left side has \(t+l\) tokens, right side \(t\) tokens). The motivation for the local constraint (e.g., “attributes conveyed over longer spans” vs. “domain experts have better accuracy”) is hard to follow. The discussion in lines 329–342 that helped understanding should appear earlier.
- **Fundamental faithfulness concern**: The method generates the answer *before* the rationale, so the rationale may be post-hoc and not the actual basis for the decision. The approach does not address spurious correlations or inherent biases.
- **Limited to constrained answer spaces**: The method requires a predefined answer set (e.g., classification labels), limiting generalization to open-ended tasks.
- **Unfair baseline comparisons**: Expert models (e.g., in Table 8) are fine-tuned on the target tasks, some with access to external datasets, giving them more signal than the backbone pre-trained model. A fair comparison would include vanilla fine-tuning of the backbone LLM. The performance and faithfulness of the expert models alone are not reported—if they are better, why use them only as guides?
- **Novelty concerns**: The method is reminiscent of existing controlled decoding works (ILQL, Dola) which already do more sophisticated token reweighting by reward. The lookahead reward is essentially a simplified MCTS and a “poor man’s version of ILQL”. The main novelty claimed (focus on faithfulness) is limited and more of an application.
- **Missing baselines, metrics, and experimental details**: No comparison to the expert model alone. Evaluation details (prompts, answer extraction, exact task format) not provided. Hyperparameter \(n\) (number of rollouts) not specified or justified. Inference time cost not measured; the claim that computational costs are similar to existing decoding techniques is unsupported, especially if the expert model is as large as the base model.
- **Evaluation reliability**: Standard faithfulness metrics (word inclusion, perturbation) may be noise-prone and unreliable.
- **Missing experiments**: Only two backbone LLMs are used (Llama-3-8B, Mistral-7b); more sizes and models needed. Only three reasoning tasks; more datasets required. Figure 2’s distribution of domain-specific words is unclear and its relevance to results is not explained.
- **Writing and scope issues**: The paper claims to ensure “logically coherent and comprehensive” rationales but provides no evaluation of logical coherence or comprehensiveness. The faithfulness definition in Section 3.2 is not clearly stated. The NLI example (“submergible” does not imply submerged) is a poor illustration.
- **Missing related work**: Papers on measuring faithfulness and human alignment of LLM rationales are not cited (e.g., “Evaluating Human Alignment and Model Faithfulness of LLM Rationale”, “On Measuring Faithfulness or Self-consistency of Natural Language Explanations”).
- **Unresolved questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - What exactly does \(q_\phi\) reward? Does it simply reward completions that contain tokens in \(C\)?
  - How is the set \(C\) of classification label words constructed? What is the motivation behind token masking?
  - Are rollouts done on the backbone model or the expert model?
  - Can the method generalize to settings where the rationale is generated before the answer?
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 1) is highly critical of the method’s fundamental approach and soundness, while two reviewers (scores 5,5) are positive but note significant flaws. The middle reviewer (score 3) emphasizes missing details and poor presentation. The paper’s contributions are not convincingly established.
