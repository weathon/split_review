## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes federated vanilla and entropy-regularized natural policy gradient (FedNPG) methods under softmax parameterization for multi-task reinforcement learning in the infinite-horizon tabular MDP setting. Each agent has a private reward function but shares the same transition kernel. Agents communicate over a prescribed graph topology without sharing trajectories. The authors establish non-asymptotic global convergence guarantees under exact policy evaluation and show robustness to inexact evaluation.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and clearly presented, with a well-defined problem formulation (R1, R3).
- To the best of the reviewers’ knowledge, this is the first work on decentralized FedNPG with convergence analysis (R4).
- The convergence results are shown not to degrade significantly despite the absence of trajectory sharing (R4).
- The tabular setting is a wise choice for NPG because the update has a simple form without higher-order matrix operations, simplifying analysis (R4).
- The authors provide a clear comparison of their findings with existing results (R3).
- The theoretical analysis is solid and the work is complete, with well-designed algorithms (R2, R3).
- The paper addresses a less common but pertinent issue—multi-task RL (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of numerical experiments**: The paper provides no simulation results to validate the efficacy of the proposed algorithms, which is considered a critical omission by multiple reviewers (R2, R3, R4).
- **Restrictive tabular assumption**: The framework is limited to discrete state-action spaces with deterministic gradients. It does not consider function approximation, stochastic transitions, or high-dimensional environments, which significantly limits practical applicability (R3, R4).
- **Missing multiple local updates**: The algorithms do not incorporate multiple local update steps, a key feature of federated learning. This omission means that the effect of agent heterogeneity and the communication efficiency benefits typical of FL are not examined (R3, R4). One reviewer (R4) specifically asked whether local computation for several iterations without communication is possible.
- **Unclear motivation for decentralized topology**: Several reviewers found the motivation for decentralized communication over a graph (rather than a central server) insufficient. The paper would benefit from concrete practical examples and an explanation of how the mixing matrix is determined in practice (R1, R2, R4). One reviewer (R4) further questioned why agents with individual reward functions should use a single global policy instead of local policies.
- **Theoretical results need more intuition and are possibly overclaimed**: The convergence rates in Theorems 1 and 2 lack explanation of how they relate to MDP properties, policy parameterization, and network topology (R1). A reviewer (score 3, R2) argued that the global convergence claim may be overclaimed because it relies on additional assumptions on the policy structure (e.g., equation 107). Another reviewer (R4) noted that assumptions about the mixing matrix **W** are missing—it is not stated whether **W** must be ergodic or satisfy the connectivity rule from (Nedic & Ozdaglar, 2009)---without which the claim of "illuminating impacts of network size and connectivity" is unclear. (Note: one reviewer (score 6, R4) considered this the first work on decentralized FedNPG, but still flagged the missing assumptions.)
- **Insufficient discussion of network topology and communication efficiency**: The paper does not discuss how convergence rate changes with network connectivity, nor the trade-offs between communication cost and convergence speed (R1, R4). Comparison with existing convergence results from distributed optimization (which may cover multi-task RL as a special case) is also missing (R2).
- **Practical synchronization difficulty**: In a fully distributed setting, being synchronous in each iteration is challenging, especially if agents randomly select communication partners (R4).
- **Unexamined heterogeneity and speedup**: The paper does not investigate behavior when transition kernels differ between agents (R3). Moreover, it does not check whether the algorithms achieve the expected linear or sublinear speedup in the number of agents, as studied in prior federated RL works (R3 cites [1–4]).
- **Novelty concern**: One reviewer (score 3, R2) felt the work may be a combination of existing works with insufficient motivation, while others (scores 5 and 6) considered it novel and well-structured—this disagreement is noted.
