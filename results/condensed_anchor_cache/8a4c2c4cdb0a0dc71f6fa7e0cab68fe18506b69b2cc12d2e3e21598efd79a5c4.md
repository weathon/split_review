## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces OR-Bench, the first large-scale over-refusal benchmark. The authors propose a pipeline that uses Mixtral 8×7B to generate toxic seeds, then rewrites them into seemingly toxic but benign prompts, filtered by an ensemble of moderators (GPT, Llama, Gemini) validated against human experts. OR-Bench contains 80,000 prompts across 10 categories, a hard subset of 1,000 prompts (OR-Bench-Hard-1K) rejected by multiple strong LLMs, and 600 toxic prompts. They evaluate 25-32 LLMs from 8 families, plus defensive augmentation techniques. The work highlights a trade-off between safety and over-refusal, although GPT-4-turbo appears to deviate from this trend.

### Strengths
1. **Importance and timeliness**: Over-refusal is a critical issue that impacts model usefulness, and the benchmark fills a missing perspective in LLM safety evaluation. (R1, R6, R7, R8)
2. **Comprehensive evaluation**: Extensive study of 25-32 popular LLMs across 8 model families, including open-source and closed-source models, with granular analysis across refusal categories and defensive techniques. (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5, R7, R8)
3. **Public dataset and tools**: The dataset (80K + hard 1K + toxic 600) is publicly released on HuggingFace, along with an interactive demo, facilitating reproducibility and future research. (R1, R8)
4. **Solid pipeline for dataset generation**: The automated pipeline (toxic seed generation via Mixtral, rewriting into over-refusal prompts, ensemble moderation) is a clear methodological contribution, with steps and reasons explained convincingly. (R2, R5, R7)
5. **Validation and bias discussion**: Potential sources of bias and rejected alternatives are discussed; the ensemble moderator is validated against human experts and ScaleAI workers. (R8)
6. **Dataset scalability and regeneration**: The pipeline can be re-run rapidly, mitigating concerns about benchmark memorization over time. (R5)
7. **Clear presentation and motivation**: The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and motivates the distinction between safety and over-refusal effectively. (R3, R8)
8. **Fine-grained categories**: The benchmark covers 10 refusal categories, enabling detailed analysis of over-refusal across topics. (R4)

### Weaknesses
1. **Ambiguous definition of key concepts**: The definitions of “over-refusal,” “dual-use,” and “safe prompt” are subjective and not falsifiably defined. The paper’s stance that prompts with dual-use potential (e.g., legal self-defense tools, political manipulation) should never be refused if framed benignly is questionable; such prompts are a known jailbreaking technique. (R1, R5, R6, R8) *One reviewer (R5, score 1) strongly argues the problem is poorly defined; another (R8, score 8) finds the dual-use motivation insufficiently motivated.*
2. **Bias in dataset construction**: 
   - The hard subset (OR-Bench-Hard-1K) is selected based on rejection by a subset of LLMs, potentially biasing performance against models not in the selection pool. (R3)
   - The whole dataset uses only three model families (GPT, Llama, Gemini) as moderators, introducing systematic bias. (R3)
   - Many prompts follow simple patterns (e.g., adding “, while being unethical”) limiting diversity and risking overfitting. (R3)
   - The pipeline relies on LLMs (Mixtral) to generate prompts, potentially biasing towards those models’ perceptions of toxicity. (R1)
3. **Insufficient detail and methodological gaps**:
   - Missing details on the generation process: how seed questions are selected, how many rewrites per seed, duplicate removal criteria, number of human experts involved, inter-annotator agreement, and exact prompts for replication. (R1, R4, R5)
   - The evaluation of expert agreement in Table 1 uses the ensemble moderator’s majority vote as ground truth, creating circularity. (R2)
   - The paper does not report inter-annotator agreement for the human evaluation, which is a methodological requirement. (R5)
   - Testing via public APIs without system prompts is inconsistent across models; some models require system prompts for proper context. (R5)
   - Using temperature 0 is methodologically inappropriate given the stochastic nature of LLMs. (R5)
   - Few details on how the ensemble moderator distinguishes between toxic and benign prompts, and how errors (e.g., false positives) are mitigated. (R6)
4. **Lack of deeper analysis of over-refusal causes**: The paper focuses on aggregated refusal rates without exploring why specific prompts trigger refusals or when safe alternative responses could be provided. (R1, R4) *This is echoed by multiple reviewers.*
5. **Refusal detection limitations**: Keyword matching may misclassify cases where a model starts refusing but then answers, or other nuanced refusal behaviors. The paper does not verify how well keyword matching approximates GPT-4 judgment. (R7)
6. **Limited technical novelty**: The prompt generation and moderation methods build on existing techniques with limited innovation beyond the domain. (R3)
7. **Sustainability concerns**: The hard subset is defined based on current models’ rejection behavior; as models evolve, the criterion may become outdated and require a time-consuming re-run of the entire pipeline. (R2)
8. **Ethical concerns**: The paper lacks an explicit ethics section and does not address the ethical implications of subjecting human annotators to highly problematic LLM outputs, especially when using a third-party service (ScaleAI). (R5) *This is a minority but strong criticism.*
9. **Unbalanced dataset**: The benchmark focuses only on prompts near the refusal boundary (high false positive rate potential) without including a comparable set of prompts that should be refused (true positive rate), limiting its ability to measure the full safety-helpfulness trade-off. (R5)
10. **Missing analysis of sample-wise correlations**: The paper does not study whether specific prompts are consistently rejected across models, or if models from the same family show higher agreement. (R8)
11. **GPT-4-turbo anomaly**: GPT-4-turbo does not exhibit the same strong trade-off between safety and over-refusal, but this is barely mentioned despite being an important exception. (R8)
12. **Specific questionable entries**: Reviewers note that some prompts in OR-Bench (e.g., discussing political tactics without endorsing, or controlling someone through manipulation without breaking laws) may not be truly benign, as they resemble jailbreaking techniques. (R6)
