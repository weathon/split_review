## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Puppeteer, a hierarchical world model for visual whole-body humanoid control using RL without predefined rewards or skill primitives. A high-level agent generates commands from visual observations for a low-level tracking agent. The method is evaluated on 8 tasks with a 56-DoF humanoid and a user study. Reviewers praised the clear writing, interesting approach, and evaluations, but expressed concerns about: (1) underperformance in asymptotic return vs. TD-MPC2 despite improved naturalness, with insufficient justification of why hierarchy improves naturalness; (2) missing key baselines (hierarchical Dreamer, TD-MPC2+AMP, HumanoidOlympics); (3) lack of quantitative metrics for tracking performance and physical plausibility (torques, energy, stability margins); (4) subjective naturalness evaluation without comparison to real motion capture; (5) limited comparison for zero-shot generalization claims; (6) missing resource efficiency details and model reusability discussion. Two reviewers gave high scores (8), while two were more critical (5), raising issues about fair comparison and insufficient contributions.

### Strengths
- The hierarchical world model integrating high-level visual guidance with low-level proprioceptive control is novel in its simplicity and efficacy, achieving natural motion without predefined rewards or skill primitives.
- Puppeteer advances visual whole-body humanoid control, with zero-shot generalization to unseen tasks.
- The research addresses a significant and practical challenge: controlling humanoid agents from visual observations using generalizable world models.
- The low-level agent trained on trajectory tracking is adaptable across a range of tasks, showing promising generalizability.
- The high-level agent controlling from visual observations is task-specific but broadly applicable.
- A user study validates that the proposed method enables more natural humanoid control preferred by participants.
- The paper is well-written, well-organized, with clear figures and plots.
- The approach is interesting and a natural next step to extend TD-MPC2.
- Evaluation includes multiple tasks with proprioception and visual observations, and ablations evaluate design choices, especially the role of planning.
- Baselines include model-free and model-based approaches.
- The proposed visual whole-body high-dimensional humanoid control benchmark enriches evaluation platforms in the area.

### Weaknesses
- **Asymptotic performance vs. naturalness tradeoff**: The method significantly underperforms TD-MPC2 in episodic return across most tasks (Figure 5), while improving naturalness. The paper fails to justify *why* hierarchy improves naturalness beyond the “reward hacking” argument; intuitions and validating experiments are missing. *(Minority reviewers 3 and 2 note that TD-MPC2 shows comparable or superior returns.)*
- **Lack of quantitative metrics for tracking and physical plausibility**:
    - No evaluation of tracking accuracy or success rate for the low-level controller. Reviewer 1 cites works [1–4] that evaluate tracking performance but are not mentioned nor their metrics used: [1] Luo et al. (CVPR 2023), [2] Won et al. (TOG 2022), [3] Cheng et al. (2024), [4] He et al. (2024).
    - No metrics for joint torques, energy consumption, or stability margins; raises concerns about practical applicability and objective assessment of naturalness.
- **Missing baselines**:
    - No hierarchical RL baseline included (e.g., hierarchical Dreamer [Hafner et al. NeurIPS 2022]). *(Minority reviewer 3)*
    - Missing TD-MPC2+AMP baseline to compare naturalness from human motion priors. *(Reviewer 1)*
    - More standard baselines from the literature (e.g., HumanoidOlympics) should be compared, as it also uses human motion data and RL. *(Minority reviewer 4)*
    - Baselines added during rebuttal are made-up combinations of high-level algorithms with low-level TD-MPC, not established methods; no measures taken to ensure fairness in hyperparameter tuning. *(Minority reviewer 3)*
- **Subjective naturalness evaluation**: The user study only compares with regular RL methods, not with real motion capture data, limiting assessment of true naturalness. *(Minority reviewer 4)* Reviewer 2 also notes naturalness depends on subjective human judgment and lacks a clear definition; the paper does not define ‘naturalness’ beyond qualitative human perception.
- **Zero-shot generalization claims not compared to baselines**: The paper claims zero-shot generalization to larger gap lengths but does not compare these results with TD-MPC (or other baselines). A quantitative analysis of performance drop-off as gap length increases is needed. *(Reviewer 2)*
- **Lack of interface design comparison**: The high-level command interface (keypoint positions) is not compared to alternative designs from prior works (e.g., CALM [5] Tessler et al. 2023, Universal Humanoid Motion Representations [6] Luo et al. 2023). The interface choice is critical for skill reuse but is underexplored. *(Reviewer 1)*
- **Resource efficiency and reusability not reported**:
    - No comparison of memory usage, training duration, or inference times across methods. *(Reviewer 2)*
    - The low-level agent’s applicability to different humanoid models with varying DoF is unclear. *(Reviewer 2)*
- **Limited task diversity**: The benchmark primarily evaluates visio-locomotive capabilities; could expand to more diverse scenarios (e.g., manipulation tasks in HumanoidOlympics). *(Minority reviewer 4)*
- **Unaddressed questions that overlap with criticisms**:
    - Why does the humanoid always lean forward when moving? *(Reviewer 4, relates to motion naturalness)*
    - Can the method be validated in real-world experiments? *(Reviewer 4, relates to practical applicability)*
    - How exactly does the low-level policy track high-level commands (per-step or until tracking error threshold)? *(Reviewer 3, relates to missing interface explanation)*
    - Is the low-level tracking effectively transferable to different humanoid models? *(Reviewer 2, relates to reusability)*
    - Provide details on memory usage, training time, inference time across methods. *(Reviewer 2)*
    - Include comparative study on survival rate/survival time for final-trained model. *(Reviewer 2)*
    - Include baseline experiments focused on “zero-shot generalization.” *(Reviewer 2)*
    - How relevant is naturalness for real-world humanoid control? *(Reviewer 2, relates to metric validity)*
- **Minor issues**:
    - Line 079: “end-effector joints” should be “end-effector links”. *(Reviewer 3)*
    - Typo in Problem Formulation (page 2): environment transition function should be L, not S. *(Reviewer 2)*
    - Punctuation missing in equations (style matter). *(Reviewer 3)*
