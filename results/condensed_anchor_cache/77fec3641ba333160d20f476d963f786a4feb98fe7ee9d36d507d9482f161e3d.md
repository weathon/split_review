## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a heterogeneous graph representation for robotic manipulation of rigid and deformable objects (ropes, cloths) with one or two end-effectors. It introduces the Heterogeneous Equivariant Policy (HEPi), which uses SE(3)-equivariant message passing networks (EMPNs) with separate parameters for each interaction type to exploit geometric symmetry. A new benchmark in IsaacLab is presented with six tasks: Rigid-Sliding, Rigid-Insertion, Rigid-Insertion-Two-Agents, Rope-Closing, Rope-Shaping, and Cloth-Hanging. HEPi is shown to outperform Transformer and non-heterogeneous EMPN baselines in average returns, sample efficiency, and generalization to unseen objects. The paper includes thorough evaluations (10 seeds, interquartile mean, 95% CI), detailed appendix with hyperparameters and reward functions, and theoretical analysis of global information propagation.

### Strengths
- **Novel and well-motivated combination**: Merging heterogeneous graphs with equivariant networks is a clever design that unifies rigid and deformable object tasks and reduces the SE(3) search space. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Clear writing and formulation**: The paper is well-structured, with solid mathematical grounding and intuitive presentation. (R1)
- **Comprehensive and statistically robust experiments**: Results are averaged over 10 seeds with interquartile mean and 95% CI. Detailed ablations and discussions support the findings. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Reproducibility and benchmark**: The appendix provides full experimental details (reward functions, hyperparameters). Tasks are clearly defined and demonstrated in video, making them easily adoptable by the community. (R2, R3)
- **Use of TRPL**: Replacing standard PPO with TRPL simplifies hyperparameter tuning, a significant practical advantage. (R2)
- **Potential for extension**: The representation could be extended to fluid and rigid/soft/fluid-coupled systems. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of real-world validation and reliance on perfect keypoints**: The method assumes ground-truth object coordinates are available; no experiments with noisy perception or physical robots. This is a significant limitation for real-world applicability. Several reviewers note that keypoint extraction from cameras is prone to errors and occlusions, and the policy’s sensitivity to such noise is not addressed. (R1, R2, R3, R4)  
  - R2 suggests adding a visual capture pipeline (camera + pose estimation for rigid tasks, point cloud for deformable tasks) to test robustness.  
  - R3 highlights that self-occlusions during deformable object manipulation make vertex extraction especially difficult.
- **Scalability and computational cost**: All experiments use simple shapes (ropes, triangles, stars) with fewer than 100 nodes. The paper does not discuss how graph construction (k-NN) and message passing scale to complex objects requiring thousands of nodes (e.g., cabinets, dresses), nor the impact on computational complexity. (R1)
- **Simplicity of tasks and end-effector model**:  
  - The 2D rigid-sliding task (suction gripper) is too trivial, reducing it to pick-and-place. R4 suggests replacing it with a pushing task (one/two end-effectors, contact dynamics) to better test the method.  
  - The end-effector is modeled as a point mass, ignoring kinematic/dynamic constraints of real robots (joint limits, torque, self-collisions, multi-link arm dynamics). Transfer to real hardware is not discussed. (R4)
- **Incomplete coverage of deformable object types**: The paper only handles codimension-2 (ropes) and codimension-1 (cloths) deformable objects. Fuller 3D soft objects (codimension-0, e.g., rubber balls) require internal deformation state that cannot be captured by sparse keypoints; the method’s capacity to handle twisting, bending, or compression is not demonstrated. (R4)
- **No investigation of generalization to different physical properties**: The paper does not test performance with varying stiffness, friction, or other material properties. (R3)

  - Line 74: "equivariacne" -> "equivariance" (R3)  
  - Line 182: left quotation mark on "lifts" (R1)
