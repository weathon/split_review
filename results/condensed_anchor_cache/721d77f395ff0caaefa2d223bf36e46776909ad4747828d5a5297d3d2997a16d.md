## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses client drift in heterogeneous federated learning. The authors present a theoretical bound on the generalization contribution of local training using the conditional Wasserstein distance between client data distributions. They propose FedImpro, which decouples the model into low-level (feature extraction) and high-level (classifier) components, and trains the high-level part on reconstructed feature distributions to create similar conditional distributions across clients. Experiments on FL benchmark datasets show improvements in final accuracy, convergence speed, and weight divergence. The method is also evaluated under different levels of client participation, local epochs, data heterogeneity, and both cross-device and cross-silo settings.

### Strengths
- The problem of client drift in FL is highly relevant and well-motivated.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with a clear logical flow.
- Detailed discussion of related work, including an analysis of previous methods.
- Theoretical claims are supported by proofs, including a bound on the generalization contribution via Wasserstein distance.
- Extensive empirical analysis: FedImpro is compared with several state-of-the-art (though some older) baselines in terms of final performance, convergence speed, and weight divergence. Ablation studies (e.g., depth of gradient decoupling) are included.
- The combination of model decoupling with feature distribution estimation and privacy protection is novel and effective for client drift mitigation.
- The method of sampling synthetic features to increase distribution similarity is interesting and shows promise.
- Limitations (communication overhead, privacy) are explicitly addressed. The paper shows FedImpro does not lead to privacy leakage in gradient inversion attacks.
- Reproducibility details are provided.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with recent relevant baselines.** The paper compares only with relatively old methods (~3 years old). Several state-of-the-art methods for data heterogeneity are not included: FedDyn [R1,R2], FedSpeed [R1], ETF [R1], SphereFed [R1], and CCVR [R1]. These methods are highly correlated (CCVR and SphereFed share the idea of feature distribution alignment) and their comparison on client drift reduction, final performance, and communication cost is essential.
- **Increased communication cost not quantified.** FedImpro notably increases both the number of communication rounds and message size. The paper suggests using less communication-heavy estimation methods but does not provide a quantitative comparison against FedAvg or other baselines (R1). Reviewer 1 specifically asks for the impact on number of communications and message size.
- **Theoretical connection to algorithm is unclear.** The analysis uses the bound on generalization contribution via conditional Wasserstein distance, but how this directly translates into the specific steps of FedImpro (e.g., decoupling, feature reconstruction) is not well explained (R3). The logic flow from theory to algorithm needs to be clearer.
- **Conditional distribution assumption conflicts with label-non-IID scenario.** The theory conditions on label y. However, in label-imbalanced partitions (e.g., CIFAR-10 non-IID), the conditional distribution p(x|y) is the same across clients, yet client drift still occurs (R4). This requires more explanation or a revised theoretical foundation.
- **Missing analysis on the impact of number of clients per round.** Unlike standard FedAvg, the number of clients per round influences the feature distribution estimation in FedImpro. An ablation study on this effect is lacking (R4).
- **Limited weight divergence reduction.** From Fig. 2, the actual reduction in weight divergence achieved by FedImpro appears very small (R1). This weakens the claim of mitigating drift.
- **Missing hyperparameter sensitivity analysis.** The paper does not adequately explore sensitivity of FedImpro to hyperparameters, especially those related to feature extraction and distribution matching components (R3). This affects assessment of robustness.
- **Time cost not compared.** Reviewer 2 asks for CPU-time comparisons to reach target accuracy, as FedImpro increases training time.
- **Missing experiments on NLP and large-scale datasets.** Only vision benchmarks are used. Reviewers suggest including Shakespeare, StackOverflow, Landmarks-User-160k (R1), or a natural partition like FEMNIST (R4).
- **Minor: Typo in Section 5.1.** Cross-device (M=100) and cross-silo (M=10) settings are described incorrectly (R1, R4). The explanation should be reversed.
- **Minor: No comparison with previous split-training methods.** The idea of decoupling lower/higher-level networks is not new; comparisons to such prior work are missing (R3).
- **Reviewer 1 and 3 are more positive (ratings 8), while Reviewer 2 and 4 are more cautious (ratings 6), raising concerns about baselines, theory-to-algorithm gap, and experimental scope.**
