## Merged Review

### Summary
Three reviewers assess the paper proposing Meta Ranking (MR), a cross-query-comparison method that enables weak LLMs to evaluate response reliability by pairwise ranking a target query-response pair against multiple reference pairs. Reviewers agree MR is simple, novel in approach, and shows strong performance in error detection, model cascading, and instruction tuning, with weak LLMs (e.g., Phi-2) outperforming stronger baselines. However, they raise significant concerns about limited novelty (viewed as a special case of ICL), high inference cost (O(N) vs O(1) for ICL), vague definition of “reliability”, insufficient experimental scale and model coverage (missing LLaMA-3, GPT-4o-mini), and unclear ablation of the reference samples. Disagreement exists: Reviewer 2 (score 5) finds the method solid and evaluation thorough despite novelty concerns, while Reviewers 1 and 3 (score 3) emphasize writing quality, interpretability, and experimental limitations.

### Strengths
- Novel cross-query-comparison method (Meta Ranking) that demonstrates superior performance in error detection, model cascading, and instruction tuning (R1, R3).
- Effective even with weaker LLMs (e.g., Phi-2) enabling weak-to-strong generalization; surpasses strong baselines like GPT-3.5-turbo with only five reference samples (R1).
- Simple and easy to understand (R2).
- Solid evaluation showing improvement in reliability evaluation (micro precision) and through downstream applications (model cascading, instruction tuning) (R2).
- MR uses a weighted voting system that compares target and reference pairs, incorporating reliability scores for accurate response assessment (R3).
- MR outperforms traditional uncertainty methods (e.g., entropy, confidence) with better generalization and robustness across tasks and datasets (R3).
- Cross-query comparisons reduce overconfidence in LLMs, improving accuracy especially in weak models (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and framing**: The method can be viewed as a special case of ICL: instead of concatenating N reference pairs in one query, each reference pair + query is sent separately to the LLM (R2). One reviewer finds it novel (R1), but another finds novelty limited (R2). Theoretical innovation lacks depth; the paper does not clearly explain why the MR voting mechanism leads to improved performance and judgment efficiency (R3).
- **Definition of “Reliability”**: The definition is vague (“attributes such as correctness and quality as required by the context”), making it hard to evaluate results and understand improvement (R2). A more rigorous definition is needed (R2’s question).
- **Efficiency and scalability concerns**: The method requires N times inference (O(N) vs O(1) for ICL), significantly increasing inference cost (R1, R2). The assumption that N is small may not always hold (R2). The method should be compared with other inference-scaling methods like self-consistency (R1). In Figure 3, P(T) w/ Phi-2 has much higher inference time than MR w/ Phi-2, yet MR requires N=5 comparisons while P(T) directly outputs correctness; this discrepancy is unclear (R1). Similarly, Reviewer 3 asks whether the drastic inference time drop in Figure 3 can be explained solely by shorter input text (R3).
- **Experimental coverage and model recency**: The paper should include more recent models such as LLaMA-3 and GPT-4o-mini (R1). Experimental scale is limited; not enough exploration of how different MR configurations and settings impact performance improvement (R3).
- **Table 1 issues** (R1):
  - It is odd that Phi-2 obtains zero accuracy on GSM8K and performs close to random guessing (~25%) on MMLU and CMMLU.
  - The task performance of base models (LLaMA-2, ChatGLM-2, Yi) should be included for comparison.
  - What does “Random Selection” mean?
  - If MR uses 5 few-shot samples (some correct, some incorrect), do baselines (e.g., TP of Phi-2, P(T) w/ OpenChat-3.5) also include 5 few-shot samples (same questions but all correct)? Otherwise, the benefit cannot be attributed to MR alone.
- **Figure 4**: Performance increases as the number of reference pairs grows; what would the curve look like beyond 5? (R1).
- **Downstream applications**: The two introduced applications (model cascading, instruction tuning) are not sufficiently developed to enrich the second half of the paper; they lack substantial innovation and need more exploration (R3).
- **Writing quality**: Section 4 is poorly written and difficult to follow (R1).
