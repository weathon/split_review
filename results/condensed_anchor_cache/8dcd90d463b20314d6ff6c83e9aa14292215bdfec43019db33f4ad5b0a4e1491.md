## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents FLAT, an LLM unlearning method that requires only forget data and no retain data or reference model. It maximizes the \(f\)-divergence between a template answer distribution and the forget answer distribution. Experiments on Harry Potter, TOFU, and MUSE benchmarks demonstrate competitive unlearning performance and retained model utility. One reviewer (score 8) praised the practical novelty and theoretical grounding, while three reviewers (score 6) found the performance gains marginal, the methodology unclear, and several experimental concerns unanswered.

### Strengths
- Addresses a critical practical limitation: eliminates the need for retain data or a reference model (all reviewers).
- Provides a novel perspective by framing LLM unlearning through \(f\)-divergence, which is missing in prior work and could benefit future research (R1, R2, R4).
- Offers a thorough theoretical foundation with proof, supporting the loss adjustment mechanism (R1, R4).
- Conducts extensive experiments on multiple benchmarks (Harry Potter, TOFU, MUSE) and diverse LLMs, covering popular unlearning settings (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Situates the method well within the existing literature, clearly delineating its advantages (R1).
- Includes informative appendices (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal or non‑significant performance gains**, especially on TOFU. In Table 3, FLAT(KL) only slightly outperforms simpler baselines like Mismatch, and on TOFU (Tables 4‑5) all methods achieve similar forget quality (FQ) and model utility (MU) (R1, R2, R3). Reviewer 2 notes that the reported FQ for NPO on TOFU‑1% is 6e‑3, whereas the NPO paper reports >0.8; this discrepancy raises concerns about result reliability (R2, R3). Retain performance is also not significantly improved (R2).
- **Missing comparisons and baselines**: Mismatch is not consistently included in later experiments (e.g., Tables 6‑7) (R1). Several related works that also perform unlearning without retain data are omitted, especially the “name change” algorithm [1‑3], which reportedly achieves higher FQ while maintaining MU (R2). The SimPO baseline is not fully discussed (R3).
- **Methodology is unclear and poorly justified**: The derivation from Eq3 (maximizing \(f\)-divergence) to the weighted loss is not clearly motivated; it is not explained how maximizing \(f\)-divergence translates to better unlearning compared to simpler weighting strategies (R3). Notation is confusing: \(\theta\) does not appear in Eq3, and the relation between \(\mathcal{D}_e,\mathcal{D}_f\) and \(\theta\) is ambiguous; Eq4’s derivation is unclear; the symbol \(h\) is used inconsistently (sometimes token probability, sometimes token) (R2). There are suspected errors: line 235 may need a log‑prob sign; line 371’s claim about PO PPL may be incorrect; lines 512‑515 appear to be repeated content (R4).
- **Lack of ablation on template response generation (Step 1)**. Variations in template design could significantly impact performance, and the authors did not study these effects (R1, R3). It is also unclear how the alternative (“template”) answer is sampled (R3).
- **Scalability concerns**: experiments on TOFU are limited to the 1% forget subset (2 authors out of 200); results on 5% or 10% are missing. Most prior work evaluates all splits, and 10% is considered more difficult (R2, R3). This raises doubts about the method’s generalizability to larger forget sets.
- **Experimental setting clarity issues**: On TOFU, it is not explained that base LLMs are first fine‑tuned on fictitious data (forget, retain, held‑out) before unlearning. The forget quality metric measures closeness to a model trained only on retain data, but it is unclear why the unlearned model should resemble that model and why the input is forget data (R4). Metrics are inconsistent: Harry Potter uses Forget Quality Gap (difference in ROUGE‑L and BLEU), while TOFU directly compares ROUGE‑L between unlearned and retained model; low ROUGE‑L does not necessarily indicate better performance, so the use of a gap measure is questionable (R4). VerbMem is computed with only one prefix token, which is unusual (R2). Table 6 reports different metrics than other TOFU experiments (R2). The phrase “satisfy a criterion” in Table 5 is undefined (R2).
