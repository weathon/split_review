## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Generalization-Aware Minimization (GAM), a generalized version of SAM that uses multiple perturbation steps instead of SAM’s single-step perturbation to align training updates with the expected test loss gradient. The theoretical contribution shows that for quadratic losses, the test loss landscape is a rescaled version of the training loss landscape; a practical online algorithm adapts the perturbation steps during training. Experiments on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and SVHN with MLP, CNN, and WN networks report consistent but small improvements over SAM and SGD.

### Strengths
- Provides a rigorous theoretical analysis of the relationship between training and test loss landscapes, including Theorem 1 on curvature direction alignment and Theorem 2 on a series of transformations.
- Offers insights into why SAM works, moving beyond the simple “flat minima” explanation and motivating new understanding of sharpness-based algorithms.
- The theoretical analysis is plausible with clear explanation of assumptions (reviewer 3); the paper is written clearly and theoretical insights are supported empirically.
- The idea of multiple perturbation steps and learning a perturbation coefficient via the difference between perturbed and original gradient is novel and interesting.
- Originality: the theoretical demonstration that the test loss landscape is a rescaled version of the training loss landscape for quadratic losses is a meaningful perspective.
- The algorithm is grounded in an interesting theoretical framework that could motivate further advances, despite limitations in the current analysis.

### Weaknesses
- **Computational cost and practicality:**  
  – The multiple perturbation steps require several forward/backward passes per iteration. The paper dismisses the overhead with a brief statement that “the overhead remains manageable” but lacks a thorough treatment.  
  – Several reviewers request a detailed analysis: time complexity per perturbation step, how it scales with model size and input dimensionality, comparison of wall-clock time against SGD and SAM on various hardware, and the trade-off between computational cost and performance.  
  – The significant computational cost makes the method less practical for large-scale applications; the marginal gains do not clearly justify the added cost (reviewers 4 and 5 are especially critical).  
  – Reviewer 1 asks: “Have you explored any techniques to reduce the computational cost while maintaining the benefits of multiple perturbation steps?”

- **Experimental evaluation is limited:**  
  – Experiments are conducted only on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and SVHN with small architectures (MLP, CNN, WN). Missing commonly used models such as ResNet, ViT, WideResNet, or EfficientNet, and larger datasets like CIFAR-100.  
  – Reviewer 2 notes that results on language tasks or other modalities are absent (OOD performance would also be relevant).  
  – Reviewer 3 requests comparison to SAM with larger γ, noting that as γ increases SAM’s performance also increases; the paper does not show that GAM outperforms such stronger SAM baselines.  
  – Reviewer 5 points out that the method only demonstrates less than 1% improvement, which is relatively minor, and lacks comparison with other state-of-the-art generalization techniques.  
  – Reviewer 5 also notes that results on WN are omitted and asks for clarification.

- **Theoretical assumptions and clarity:**  
  – The theory is restricted to quadratic loss functions. Section 3.4 mentions extension via local quadratic approximations, but the paper lacks a deeper investigation of how the results generalize to broader loss functions, what additional assumptions are needed, and how the local quadratic approximation relates to true loss curvature.  
  – Theorem 1 is preceded by 14 lines of dense assumptions without adequate motivation. The presentation would benefit from intuitive explanations of assumptions (e.g., quadratic loss in non-convex landscapes, rotational and location invariance) and their practical implications.  
  – Reviewer 5 raises a potential issue: Theorem 1 may be trivial because, for any x and y, E[x|y] can be written as a function of y, allowing D=0. The authors need to clarify the non-trivial aspects, e.g., specific constraints on D or c̃.  
  – Reviewer 3 asks for more explanation on why the true loss landscape is a rescaled version of the observed loss landscape, especially when D(Λ̃)=Λ̃ the analysis seems trivial; intuition/examples for when and why D(Λ̃) differs from Λ̃ are needed.  
  – Reviewer 5 requests clarification on Equations (16) and (17) and whether (15) is equivalent to both.

- **Writing and presentation issues:**  
  – Line 159: vertical relationships and ‘p’ are not clearly defined.  
  – Line 264: the definition of Δ should be clarified in the main paper.  
  – Line 409: “γ1 ∈ 0.001, 0.01, 0.1” should be “γ1 ∈ {0.001, 0.01, 0.1}”.  
  – Reviewer 3 asks for more explanation of Equation (7), especially D(Λ̃).

- **Missing ablation studies and sensitivity analysis:**  
  – The effect of batch size, number of perturbation steps T, and ε on performance and the trade-off with computational cost is not analyzed.  
  – Reviewer 5 requests sensitivity analysis for key hyperparameters ε and T.  
  – Reviewer 4 asks how the discrepancy function used in the method was determined.  
  – Reviewer 1 and reviewer 4 ask for ablation studies on the number of GAM steps T.  
  – Reviewer 5 also asks for a concrete example showing that SAM is a special case of GAM.  

**Minority reviewer contrast:** Reviewers 4 and 5 are notably more negative, emphasizing that the experimental scope is insufficient and the computational burden undermines practical value. They rate the paper 3, while reviewers 1–3 rate it 6 and consider the theoretical and conceptual contributions valuable despite the limitations. This disagreement underscores that the strengths (theoretical novelty and insights) are seen as stronger by the majority, whereas the weaknesses (limited experiments, high cost, marginal improvement) are more critical to the minority.
