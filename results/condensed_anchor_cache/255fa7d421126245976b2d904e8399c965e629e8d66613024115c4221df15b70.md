## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper provides the first comprehensive study of black-box detectability of LLM watermarks. It develops rigorous statistical tests to detect the presence and estimate parameters of three popular watermark families (Red-Green, Fixed-Sampling, Cache-Augmented) using only a limited number of black-box queries. Experiments on seven schemes and five open-source models confirm effectiveness, and tests on real-world APIs validate feasibility. The findings challenge prior assumptions by showing that current watermarking schemes are more detectable than previously believed.

### Strengths
- This is the first work to examine detectability in a practical black-box setting, addressing a significant gap in the literature. The task is highly relevant: if detection fails, that provides unusually strong evidence that a watermark is quality-preserving under all use-cases.
- The paper is extremely well-written, concise, and clear. The statistical formulations for each watermark family are well explained and easy to follow.
- The experimental results are rigorous, spanning multiple schemes, open-source models (five), and practical APIs. The cross‑detection tests (verifying that tests for one family do not falsely trigger on another) are a valuable addition.
- The methods are cost‑effective: detection works with limited queries, and parameter estimation is also demonstrated.
- The contribution is significant: it challenges the widespread assumption that watermarks are undetectable without the key, and provides a foundation for future robust watermark design and advanced detection.

### Weaknesses
- **Scope and justification** – Only three watermark families are considered, none of which are designed to be undetectable. The paper’s claim that provably undetectable schemes (e.g., Gumbel‑max with key) “lack experimental validation” and are “not yet practical due to slow generation speed” is disputed. Practical implementations exist (e.g., Christ et al., 2024; “Excuse me, sir? Your language model is leaking (information)”) that are no slower than the tested schemes. The paper’s message would be far more compelling if it had benchmarked against at least one such scheme, even with a simple entropy‑threshold modification to Aaronson’s scheme. The critical reviewer (score 6) explicitly argues that the paper should either demonstrate difficulty or accept that truly undetectable schemes may already be practical.
- **Lack of generalization and unified detection** – Each family requires a distinct detection technique (Table 1). This poor generalizability means that as new watermarking methods emerge, detection cost will increase. A unified approach was not explored, and cross‑detection tests only compared across families, not across hyperparameter variations within a single family.  
- **Detection algorithms for Fixed‑Sampling and Cache‑Augmented are trivial and easily circumvented**  
  - The detection based on unique outputs can be defeated by simply skipping the first few tokens (as already proposed in Algorithm 3 of Christ et al., 2024).  
  - The cache‑based detection is often impractical: waiting for a global cache to reset can take too long, and per‑user cache is usually not applicable due to many users.  
  - The cache mechanism itself is only a minor component of those schemes; removing it often does not degrade performance (Hu et al., 2023).  
- **Evaluation metrics and real‑world limitations**  
  - Reporting median p‑values over 5 watermarking keys is unrealistic – in practice a single key is used per model. The median is not a good metric; it does not reflect the false positive rate and is difficult to interpret.  
  - False positive rates for detection are not provided, despite testing multiple watermark types (which increases the risk).  
  - Table 2 shows suboptimal detection on production APIs; the paper cannot conclude the presence of a watermark in those real‑world cases, raising doubts about practical utility.  
  - Figure 3 shows large deviations from the true δ, indicating that parameter estimates may not be accurate enough for downstream tasks.  
- **Missing details and vague descriptions**  
  - The key algorithm for calculating p‑values (lines 197‑240) is too vague.  
  - The algorithm for estimating context size (lines 781‑791) lacks precise description and experimental results.  
  - Efficiency is not quantified: how many tokens are required for reliable detection?  
  - No analysis of whether the watermarking key itself can be recovered (Appendix C hints at parameter estimation but does not address the key).  
- **Missed experiments** – The tests were not evaluated on adversarially manipulated texts (e.g., after post‑processing attacks), leaving open whether detection is robust to intentional removal attempts.  
- **Lack of explicit application scenarios** – The paper does not clearly motivate why black‑box watermark detection matters in practice, which would help readers understand the contribution’s real-world relevance.
