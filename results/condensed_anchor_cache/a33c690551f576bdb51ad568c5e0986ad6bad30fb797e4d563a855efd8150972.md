## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a dense RGB-based neural implicit mapping system, FMapping, using a factorized NeRF (TensorRF) representation and a learned depth covariance function (Dexheimer & Davison 2023). It operates in two stages: an initialization stage that processes all frames to optimize NeRF, poses, and depth priors, and an on-the-fly stage that refines depth maps using Gaussian process inference with the covariance prior, achieving ~5 Hz depth estimation. Evaluation is on the Replica dataset, reporting L1 depth error with median scaling. The method claims photometric and geometric accuracy comparable to RGB-D methods.

### Strengths
*   **Factored scene representation for efficiency:** Using a factorized tensor field (TensorRF) as the scene representation for online neural implicit mapping is noted as novel, and qualitative comparisons show advantage over alternative efficient representations like Tri-plane.
*   **Motivation for RGB-only dense SLAM:** The paper addresses well-founded issues: unstable initial pose tracking due to poor initial mapping, the challenge of real-time dense mapping, and the desire for high-quality maps without depth sensors.
*   **Leveraging depth covariance for RGB convergence:** Integrating a learned depth covariance function to guide depth estimation and expedite convergence in an RGB-only setting is a reasonable and interesting approach.
*   **Competitive claims:** The method claims to reach photometric and geometric accuracy comparable to RGB-D methods while using only RGB input.

### Weaknesses
*   **Poor technical exposition and readability:** The draft requires thorough revision. Several reviewers found the writing vague, with incomplete sentences, missing variable definitions, and typos throughout (e.g., “scarify”, “on-the-fly continue”, “warpping loss”, missing parentheses for citations, and many others listed by R1 and R3). This makes it difficult to judge the merits of the core ideas.
*   **Inaccurate claim of online / real-time mapping:** The method is not truly online because the initialization stage processes all frames (including pose optimization) before the on-the-fly stage. Claiming the whole system runs in real-time is incorrect (R1). The two-stage decomposition itself is not new and has been used by prior work like DIM-SLAM (R2).
*   **Unclear camera pose handling:** It is unclear how camera poses are computed or refined during and after the initialization stage. The paper does not adequately explain the pose optimization process (e.g., SE(3) parameterization, whether poses are refined in the on-the-fly stage) (R1, R3). Equations 2 and 3 are confusing: Eq. 2 is called a posterior but looks like a likelihood; Q and Σ are poorly defined and their dimensions seem mismatched (R2, R3). The optimization variables in Eq. 3 are not listed, making the loss unclear (R3).
*   **Questionable evaluation and missing benchmarks:**
    *   The evaluation lacks 3D map visualizations, novel view synthesis results, pose trajectory plots, and any 3D metric (e.g., accuracy, completeness, F-score). Only a single 2D depth L1 error with median scaling is reported in Table 1, which does not penalize scale drift (R1, R4).
    *   Only two RGB-based baselines are compared; important methods such as DIM-SLAM, DROID-SLAM are missing (R2).
    *   The comparison with NICE-SLAM in RGB mode is unfair because the proposed method uses a strong pre-trained depth prior while NICE-SLAM does not (R2).
    *   The reported PSNR for iMAP appears lower than NICE-SLAM (even than the RGB version of NICE-SLAM) which needs explanation. Per-scene results for Orbeez-SLAM and the averaging methodology are not provided (R4).
    *   Speed measurement is unclear; the method seems slower than NICE-SLAM, contradicting efficiency claims, and the bottleneck is not identified (R4).
*   **Missing ablation studies:** Numerous proposed components lack empirical justification:
    *   The self-supervised SDF supervision (Eq. 7) is very similar to prior pseudo-SDF losses but uses rendered instead of measured depth. Since rendered depth comes from the same predicted SDF, an analysis or ablation is needed to show it works better (R2).
    *   The covariance-guided sampling uses σ<sub>n</sub> instead of diag(Σ<sub>l*</sub>) to measure depth uncertainty—possible typo? No ablation (R2).
    *   The truncation distance Tr is modulated per-ray by the uncertainty, but should be scene-specific. No analysis or ablation of this dynamic modulation (R2).
    *   The multi-stage sampling process (uniform stratified + importance + covariance depth-guided) is complicated with no ablation showing its benefit. Additionally, depth variance depends only on pixel location, not on sampling stages, so multi-stage guided sampling seems unnecessary (R2).
    *   The advantage of factorization over other representations is asserted (Fig. 4) but with vague description; a proper ablation comparing memory and time with other works (e.g., Tri-plane) is requested (R3).
*   **Theoretical analysis is not novel:** The problem setup analysis does not provide new insights beyond existing practice (R2). The description of “learning mapping from scratch without poses” is contradicted by the use of initial tracker poses (R3).
*   **Unclear definitions and technical gaps:** Several key terms and equations are unclear:
    *   The map \( \tilde{m} \) and decoders \( \Phi, \mathcal{G} \) are not properly defined (R1).
    *   In Eq. 4, the visibility mask B is not defined; indices j, l are unclear; the term \( \tilde{P} \) is replaced without explanation; \( \delta_i, t_i \) in Eqs. 5-6 are undefined (R3).
    *   “Large window size” in Sec. 4 is not explained (R3).
    *   The network architecture for the SDF branch is not specified (R3).
    *   The difference between \( \tilde{D}_{j \rightarrow l} \) and \( m(\tilde{D}_{j \rightarrow l}) \) is not clarified (R3).
*   **Misleading statements about explicit vs. implicit representations:** The claim that explicit methods are not compact or cannot extend to unobserved regions is not true and needs clarification (R1).
*   **Relative scale consistency:** Guaranteeing scale consistency across frames in monocular RGB reconstruction (without known scale) is not explained (R1).
