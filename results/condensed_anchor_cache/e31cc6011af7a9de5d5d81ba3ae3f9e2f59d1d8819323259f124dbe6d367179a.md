## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Mani-WM, an action-conditioned video generation model for robot manipulation. It uses a diffusion transformer with a novel frame-level conditioning technique to generate high-resolution, long-horizon videos from an initial frame and a given action trajectory. Evaluations on RT-1, BRIDGE, Language2Table, and RoboNet show consistent improvements over baselines in video prediction metrics. The model also supports interactive teleoperation (keyboard/VR) and model-based planning for real-robot tasks. Two reviewers find the work strong (scores 5 and 6); one reviewer (score 3) has fundamental reservations about the practical utility of pixel-level video prediction for robot learning.

### Strengths
- Clear, well-structured writing with nice figures and a comprehensive appendix. (R1, R2, R3)
- Extensive video prediction experiments across four challenging datasets. (R1, R3)
- High-resolution video generation results that outperform baselines consistently across datasets and metrics. (R2, R3)
- Thorough ablation studies on training steps and model sizes. (R3)
- Interesting interactive applications (policy trajectory preview, teleoperation with keyboard/VR) and model-based planning demos. (R1, R2, R3)
- Teleoperation results suggest potential for easier data collection, though generalizability is unclear. (R1)

### Weaknesses
1. **Fundamental utility for robot learning** (R1, score 3, strong negative): Pixel-level video reconstruction may not improve robot learning; latent-space world models (e.g., DreamerV3) already perform well without generating every pixel. The paper overemphasizes video prediction metrics and underemphasizes downstream policy performance. Generalization to unseen action trajectories is a more important metric for model-based planning. (R1, R3 also questions: why higher resolution/longer horizon matter if closed-loop short-horizon replanning suffices.)
2. **Missing explicit evaluation of trajectory following** (R2, strong): The model is conditioned on end-effector trajectories, but the paper only evaluates overall video quality, not how accurately the generated end-effector pose matches the given trajectory. This should be measured directly. (R1 also asks about zero-shot generalization to OOD trajectories; R2 suggests comparing to methods with explicit trajectory loss like Geng & Owens (2024).)
3. **Unfair comparison baselines** (R2): Baselines (e.g., UniSim, Dreamitate, OpenSora) are not designed for explicit trajectory conditioning. A fairer comparison would include video generation models enhanced with VLM/human feedback or self-conditioning techniques. (R1 notes missing citations/discussion of these baselines.)
4. **No explicit loss for trajectory conditioning** (R2): The trajectory condition is only implicitly learned from training data; an explicit loss (e.g., from differentiable motion estimators) could improve alignment.
5. **Trajectory extraction limitations** (R2): The method requires extracting end-effector trajectories from video, but real-world issues are not addressed: occlusion of the end-effector (common in long-horizon data), stochastic environment dynamics (e.g., billiards: same trajectory yields different outcomes), and inapplicability to diverse manipulations (dexterous hands, unscrewing caps where rotation matters). This conflicts with the claim of scalability.
6. **Weak policy learning evaluation** (R1, R3): The real-robot planning section is unsubstantial. Missing: baselines using the same video prediction methods for planning, other policy learning baselines (e.g., diffusion policy, model-based RL as in iVideoGPT), details on the custom robot training dataset (size, whether tasks/objects overlap with test), and whether planning is open-loop or closed-loop (and if closed-loop, the frequency). (R3 additionally asks about generalization to new objects/tasks.)
7. **Marginal numerical improvements** (R1): Gains in metrics (e.g., ~1 point PSNR) are small; more context is needed to interpret significance.
8. **Missing qualitative comparisons with baselines** (R1): The qualitative section (Fig. 5) compares only to ground truth, not to baseline generated videos, making it hard to see if gains are meaningful.
9. **Limited results for RoboNet** (R3): RoboNet is the only multi-embodiment dataset; more qualitative/quantitative results should be included.
10. **Scalability not demonstrated** (R3): Ablation on dataset size is missing; it would strengthen claims about scaling these large models.
11. **Minor presentation issue** (R3): In Table 3, SSIM and PSNR are scaled by 100; this should be explicitly noted in the caption or text to avoid confusion.
12. **Additional questions as weaknesses** (converted from reviewer questions that overlap with missing experiments/analysis):
    - Action preprocessing: Is the action (delta) normalized before feeding into the model? (R3)
    - Robustness to action noise: Adding noise to the trajectory should produce a different video if the world model is accurate; the claim that "video remains very similar" seems contradictory and needs explanation. (R3)
    - Keyboard/VR input: How are discrete keys (e.g., up/down arrow) converted to continuous action trajectories? (R3)
    - Qualitative samples: Are those from training or validation set? (R1)

Note: Reviewer 1 (score 3) is considerably more skeptical about the core contribution, while Reviewer 3 (score 6) is more supportive but still requests additional planning baselines and analyses.
