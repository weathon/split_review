## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GraphSHAP-IQ for efficient exact computation of any-order Shapley Interactions (SIs) for graph neural networks. By exploiting GNN architecture, it shows that SI complexity depends only on the receptive field (ℓ-hop neighborhood) rather than the full graph. The method assumes linear global pooling and output layers. Experiments on benchmark and real-world datasets (including WAQ) demonstrate substantial complexity reduction and low approximation error. Reviewers overall found the method novel and sound, but raised concerns about novelty relative to prior work, experimental comparisons, writing clarity, and applicability to non-linear readouts. One reviewer (score 3) gave a strongly negative assessment.

### Strengths
- **Novel and sound theoretical contribution** (R1, R4). The invariance property that restricts SI computation to receptive fields is a novel use of GNN structure (R4). R1: method is sound with robust theoretical results.
- **Efficiency and theoretical complexity guarantees** (R2, R4). GraphSHAP-IQ achieves high efficiency; proof that complexity is linear in number of nodes and exponential only in receptive-field size (R2).
- **Comprehensive experiments across diverse datasets and GNN architectures** (R1, R2, R4). Includes qualitative and quantitative results; introduction of WAQ dataset is a valuable addition (R2).
- **Well-constructed and informative figures** (R3, R4). Particularly Fig. 2 is excellent (R4). R3: figures clearly convey intended information.
- **Well-written and easy to follow** (R2). (Note: R3 strongly disagrees on writing quality; see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty; prior work already uses receptive field for Shapley values**. R2: Section 5.4 of [1] states that for a k-layer GNN, only k-hop neighbors can influence a node’s prediction and receive non-zero Shapley value. Extending from values to interactions is incremental. R3: related work is disorganized and lacks detailed comparison with similar methods (e.g., TreeSHAP).
- **Experiment in Figure 4 does not fairly isolate the contribution**. R2: GraphSHAP-IQ’s better MSE at same number of model evaluations could be simply due to discarding irrelevant nodes. A fairer comparison would apply the same receptive-field optimization to all baselines to test whether GraphSHAP-IQ still outperforms. R3: experiments do not convincingly demonstrate effectiveness in reducing complexity; additional/targeted experiments needed.
- **Restriction to linear global pooling and output layers limits applicability**. R1, R4: Many GNN models use non-linear readouts (e.g., activation functions). The method may produce inaccurate explanations for such models.
- **Exponential dependence on receptive-field size for dense or large graphs** (R1). While linear in number of nodes, the exponential term in the largest ℓ-hop neighborhood remains prohibitive for highly connected graphs.
- **Writing clarity and flow are poor** (R3). R3: overall logic unclear, several grammatical errors, motivation not compelling. R4: extensive notations without a notation table make paper hard to follow. (Contradicts R2’s positive writing assessment; majority still found writing acceptable but noted notation issues.)
- **Visualization of higher-order interactions is challenging** (R4). Interpreting 3-way or 4-way SIs becomes exponentially harder; the practical utility for end-users is unclear.
- **Missing running time comparisons** (R1). The paper does not report algorithm running times across datasets or compare with baselines, making it hard to evaluate applicability.
- **Missing experiments and ablations** (from questions that overlap with criticisms):
    - Effectiveness for **node classification** task not addressed (R1).
    - Impact of using different baselines (random, learned) instead of mean not explored (R1).
    - In Fig. 4, comparison of top‑k most important groups between exact and approximated SHAP is missing (R4).
    - No figure with computational expense on x‑axis and MSE on y‑axis for all methods to help users trade off accuracy vs cost (R4).
    - Extension to other models (e.g., CNN with pixel receptive fields) not discussed (R4).
    - Purpose of showing “GNN vanilla” in Table 1 is unclear (R3).
