## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Linear Oscillatory State-Space models (LinOSS), a recurrent architecture derived from discretizing second-order linear ODEs modeling forced harmonic oscillators. Two variants are proposed: LinOSS-IM (fully implicit) and LinOSS-IMEX (implicit-explicit). The design uses diagonal state matrices, requires only non-negative diagonal elements for stability, and exploits fast associative parallel scans for efficient processing. Theoretical results include a proof of universal approximation for continuous causal operators and a stability analysis. Experiments on three categories of time-series tasks (mid-to-very-long-range classification/regression, long-horizon forecasting) show consistent improvements over SOTA models (e.g., outperforming Mamba by 2x and LRU by 2.5x on a 50k-length task). All four reviewers recommend acceptance (scores 8,8,8,8). The work is praised for its theoretical rigor, algorithmic cleverness, and strong empirical results, but several concerns are raised about experimental clarity, interpretability, novelty, and the practical validity of theoretical claims.

### Strengths
- **Strong theoretical foundations.** Rigorous analysis of stability (only non-negative diagonal state matrix needed), universal approximation, and connections to Hamiltonian systems and symplectic integration. Proofs are sound, though one reviewer questions their applicability after training.
- **Clever implementation.** The ODE design simultaneously enables: proven stabilization, fast matrix inversion (via Eq. 3), and parallel scans of the sequence recurrence. The constraint to diagonal A is elegantly exploited.
- **Simple yet effective parameterization.** Requires only non-negative diagonal elements for stability, contrasting with the restrictive parameterizations of many previous SSMs.
- **Excellent empirical results.** Consistently beats SOTA (Mamba, LRU, Transformers, etc.) across diverse time-series tasks. The improvement on the 50k-length task is particularly striking (2x over Mamba, 2.5x over LRU).
- **Clear writing and motivation.** The work is well motivated by cortical dynamics and is generally clear and concise. Supplementary material is comprehensive.

### Weaknesses
1. **IM vs. IMEX distinction not empirically validated.** The core claim (Line 298) that LinOSS-IM enforces “forgetting” and LinOSS-IMEX is better for long memory is not clearly supported by experiments. In Table 1, the two variants show no difference except on Worms; the Worms result may be a fluke. Table 2 (where memory over a long sequence should be important) shows IM performing better, contradicting the claim. The authors should explain when to use each variant and verify the claim with targeted ablations.
2. **Over-reliance on integrator choice for stability/forgetting.** Using an ODE framework, it would be more natural to introduce dissipative terms directly into the ODE rather than relying on the discretization. It is unclear whether the theoretical stability properties (Section 3) hold after training, especially if the model learns “through” the discretization (c.f. Krishnapriyan, “Learning continuous models for continuous physics”). The authors should inspect whether the assumptions on A and S (e.g., \(A_{kk}>0\)) hold after training, and test LinOSS-FE (forward Euler) as an ablation to isolate the effect of the IM/IMEX stabilization. A simple experiment: can LinOSS-IM learn a conservative harmonic oscillator?
3. **Missing runtime and efficiency comparisons.** Despite efficiency being a major motivation for SSMs, no runtimes, FLOPs, or memory usage are reported. The authors should provide: runtimes vs. baselines (especially Mamba and S5), runtimes with/without the matrix inversion trick, and runtimes with/without parallel scan.
4. **Architecture details insufficiently described.**  
   - How are LinOSS layers stacked? In Figure 1, which parts are \(u, y, z\)?  
   - What effect do the nonlinearities (e.g., $\sigma$ in the Figure) have on the stability analysis?  
   - What are the exact hyperparameters, nonlinear layers, and model graphs for each experiment in Section 4?  
   - How is the output of the SSM mapped to classification/forecasting (loss functions, head architecture)?  
   - Appendix A should report parameter counts, the architecture of “nonlinear layers,” and hyperparameters for the forecasting problem.
5. **Clarity and presentation issues.**  
   - Section 3.2 (universality) can be written more accessibly; more proof steps (e.g., Line 292) should be shown (appendix is fine).  
   - Figure 1 should be more explanatory.  
   - Typo: “UAE” → “UEA” (Table 1); “UEA” and the cited “Walker 2024” need definitions.  
   - Color highlighting in tables is not colorblind-friendly; use symbols.  
   - Line 289: “constraint” → “constrained”.  
   - The title “extreme long ranges” is exaggerated (current SOTA uses millions of tokens).
6. **Differentiation and integration details.**  
   - How does the fast matrix inversion formula (Eq. 3) differentiate during training? Is it done via automatic differentiation or a custom rule?  
   - Can parallel scans be applied with integrators other than first-order IM/IMEX (e.g., forward Euler, higher-order IMEX)?
7. **Model interpretability lacking.** Despite being based on oscillatory dynamics, the paper does not discuss learned frequencies or how the model captures different timescales. No analysis of the learned parameters.
8. **Lack of ablation on initialization.** No experiments on the impact of different initialization schemes for the state matrix.
9. **Universality proof – limited novelty?** Section 3.2 proves universality for LinOSS, but it may apply more broadly to other SSMs. The connection to specific previous work (parametrization from Rusch & Mishra 2021; universality from Lanthaler et al. 2024) is acknowledged by one reviewer, which limits novelty.
10. **Inability to model chaotic or synchronized dynamics.**  
    - The model cannot produce chaotic dynamics (due to linearity and stability). What if the task requires chaos? How does this square with the universality claim?  
    - The oscillators are independent; can the architecture model transient synchronization/desynchronization? The linear formulation may limit capturing non-linear interactions in real-world time series.
11. **Numerical stability assumption.** The stability analysis assumes \(M\) is diagonalizable. The paper briefly mentions that non-diagonalizable matrices with real eigenvalues may not cause exponential instability, but practical implications (especially during training) are not explored.
12. **Clarification needed on Equation 7 / Hamiltonian.** If the underlying ODE is Hamiltonian (energy-conserving), does the IM discretization (which is dissipative) become inappropriate?
13. **Unclear impact of time step \(\Delta t\).** How does the choice of \(\Delta t\) affect model performance and stability? A sensitivity analysis is missing.
14. **Section 3.2 accessibility and specialization.** The universality proof would benefit from more concrete examples (e.g., what are \(a, b\) in Line 222?).
15. **PPG model memory.** Why does the PPG model have higher memory demands even though the models seem small?
