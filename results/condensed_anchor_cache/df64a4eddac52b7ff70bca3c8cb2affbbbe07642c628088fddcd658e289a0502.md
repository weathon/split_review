## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces RealChat-1M, a large-scale dataset containing one million real-world conversations with 25 state-of-the-art LLMs, collected from 210K unique IP addresses over five months via the LMSYS Chatbot Arena website. The authors provide an overview of the dataset's curation, statistics, and topic distribution. They demonstrate four use cases: developing content moderation models comparable to GPT-4, building a safety benchmark from jailbreak conversations, training instruction-following models, and creating a challenging benchmark (Arena-Hard-200) that reveals performance gaps between open-source and proprietary models. The dataset is publicly available.

### Strengths

- The dataset is unparalleled in scale among public real-world LLM conversation datasets (1M conversations, 25 LLMs, 210K IPs), and its real-world provenance makes it extremely valuable given that most training data for LLMs is proprietary and private.
- The four use cases are creative, well-designed, and demonstrate the dataset's multifaceted utility: content moderation models, a safety benchmark (repurposing jailbreak data), instruction tuning, and a challenging prompt benchmark (Arena-Hard-200) that highlights performance gaps between open and proprietary models.
- The analysis is interesting and supported by past work—e.g., the cluster analysis in Figure 3 and the observation that many users now use LLMs for coding help.
- The comparative analysis across all use cases leverages both open-source LLMs (e.g., Vicuna) and proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4), revealing large performance gaps and safety concerns, especially among open-source models (Vicuna, Alpaca).
- The authors have a plan to regularly update the dataset, which is crucial given the frequent release of newer LLMs.
- The dataset captures authentic user prompts and highlights potential safety concerns, paving the way for future research on safety and robustness.

### Weaknesses

- The responses in the dataset are synthetic and lack human quality ratings, so additional processing (e.g., annotation, filtering) is necessary before the data can be used for many purposes. This is a weakness compared to related datasets like Anthropic HH (Table 1) that include human preference judgments.
- The dataset is not as diverse as the abstract suggests: Vicuna-13B dominates as the most frequently used model by a large margin, and the vast majority of conversations are in English. The authors should consider redrawing Figures 1 and 2 by leaving out Vicuna/English to allow interpretation of the long tail of other models and languages.
- It is unclear whether the conversation format (single-model vs. side-by-side) and IP addresses have similar balance issues. For instance, do a small number of IP addresses account for a large fraction of the dataset, and do most users interact via single-model mode rather than the potentially more interesting side-by-side mode? Providing these statistics would help researchers evaluate the dataset.
- Deeper analysis of data quality is needed. For example, how many prompts are derived from existing benchmarks like MMLU or MT-Bench? Was any human annotation performed to detect duplicates or assess quality? What is the number of prompts remaining after the filtering described in Section 3.2?
- One reviewer (the most negative, rating 6) questioned the unique value of RealChat-1M relative to existing datasets (OpenAssistant, SharedGPT, Anthropic HH, Chatbot Arena) that have already proven useful for fine-tuning, safety evaluation, and performance assessment. The paper does not provide compelling reasons why researchers should choose a subset of RealChat-1M over these alternatives for the same purposes.
