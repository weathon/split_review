## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a training-free method (R2F) that leverages LLMs to guide diffusion models in generating rare concept compositions (e.g., objects with unusual attributes). The approach rewrites a rare prompt into frequent variants and alternates between them during denoising, based on theoretical and empirical analysis showing that exposing frequent concepts improves composition. The method is extended with layout guidance (R2F+) and evaluated on multiple benchmarks, including a new RareBench dataset, outperforming SD3.0 and FLUX by up to 28.1% in T2I alignment.

### Strengths
- The idea of using frequent-to-rare prompt rewriting with alternating guidance during diffusion is novel and effective for generating rare-concept images.
- The observation that alternating between frequent and rare prompts during denoising is important; both global and region-based generation are proposed.
- Solid empirical results with promising quantitative and qualitative performance, including state-of-the-art results on multiple benchmarks.
- A new benchmark (RareBench) is introduced to facilitate research on rare composition generation.
- The method is training-free and flexible across pre-trained diffusion models (SD3.0, Flux, RPG) and LLMs, with detailed visualization and code provided for reproducibility.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear theoretical and empirical analysis.

### Weaknesses
- **Acceleration incompatibility**: The method alternates prompts across many denoising steps, making it incompatible with accelerated models (e.g., consistency models using ≤4 steps). A reviewer asks how the method could be adapted to such settings. [R1]
- **Theory-practice gap**: The theoretical analysis studies linear interpolation of score functions, but the empirical method uses alternating prompts across steps; the connection is not fully tight. [R1]
- **Ad-hoc scheduling**: The transition from always using the frequent concept at early steps to randomly selecting among rare/frequent after a fixed point is arbitrary. A principled way is needed—e.g., using noise variance or semantic similarity between frequent and rare concepts to dynamically determine the transition point. [R2]
- **Limited concept complexity**: All examples involve only two concepts (e.g., adj+noun). It is unclear how the method generalizes to more complex rare compositions (e.g., "an agent rabbit with a gun in a casual suit")—which sub-combination would be used for frequent guidance, and how to handle combinatorial explosion. [R2]
- **Missing reverse-order experiments**: The intuitive explanation (early steps determine rough structure) would be strengthened by directly comparing the proposed order (frequent first, then random) with the reverse (rare first, then frequent). [R2]
- **No automatic handling of frequent compositions**: The method requires manual determination of whether a composition is rare enough to need guidance. A clear threshold or criterion is needed to avoid degrading generation of already well-formed frequent compositions. [R2]
- **Niche application**: Rare composition generation is a relatively narrow area; the authors could discuss broader application scenarios. [R3]
- **Computational overhead**: R2F+ involves substantial latent and gradient computations (similar to LMD). A detailed comparison of computational and memory costs with other methods is essential to assess feasibility. [R3]
- **LLM’s lack of diffusion priors**: The LLM determines the “visual detail level” and guidance length, but it has no knowledge of diffusion model priors (e.g., difficulty of generating certain objects/attributes). This raises questions about reliability. [R3]
- **Suitability of LLM-provided frequent concepts**: The example “plants made of glass” is not a frequent concept. Moreover, LLMs may output frequent concepts that differ significantly in shape/size (e.g., “furry bird” vs. “furry tiger”), which could misguide early structure generation. [R3]
- **Necessity of R2F+**: Layout-based methods like LMD trade off image quality for spatial awareness; R2F already achieves higher spatial metrics than both LMD and RPG on T2I-CompBench (Table 3). Expanding to R2F+ may unnecessarily increase cost and degrade image quality; a comparative evaluation of image quality is needed. [R3] *Note: the minority reviewer (R3, score 6) is notably more critical on this point.*
- **Suggested alternative**: The reviewer suggests testing IterComp (a backbone designed for compositional generation) as it may yield larger improvements. [R3]
- **LLM lacks diffusion concept distribution**: The LLM does not have access to the training concept distribution of the diffusion model, so it may replace a rare concept with another rare one instead of a frequent one. Providing the concept distribution from LION could improve the results. [R4]
