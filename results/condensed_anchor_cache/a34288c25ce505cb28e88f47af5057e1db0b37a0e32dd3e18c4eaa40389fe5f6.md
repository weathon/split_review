## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses imbalanced generalization in meta-RL by (1) identifying "critical" training tasks whose adaptation score is poor, (2) augmenting only those tasks via bi-level optimization (upper level learns augmentation coefficients; lower level computes meta-prior), with the goal of making the meta-prior pay more attention to critical tasks. The method is evaluated on a real-world drone navigation task, a stock market simulation, and three MuJoCo environments, using MAML as the base meta-RL algorithm. Theoretical convergence and generalization guarantees are provided.

### Strengths
- **Important problem**: Tackles the well-recognized issue of imbalanced generalization in meta-RL (R1, R4, R5). Some reviewers (R1, R4) highlight this as a significant motivation; R5 agrees it is a feasible explanation.
- **Real-world experiments**: Inclusion of a physical drone navigation task and a stock market environment is praised by multiple reviewers as impressive and rare for a theoretical paper (R1, R4, R5). R1 calls the experiment section “impressive”; R4 says it is “surprisingly expansive”.
- **Theoretical analysis**: The paper provides convergence guarantees (O(1/√K)) and generalization proofs. Reviewers R1, R2, and R5 state these are sound and thorough (R2: “convergence guarantees are sound”; R5: “thorough theoretical analysis”). *However, some reviewers question specific claims (see Weaknesses).*
- **Related work discussion**: The paper reviews explainable ML and generalization in meta-learning (R1, R2, R4). R2 and R4 note the paper shows command of related work in those areas, though R2 and R5 identify major omissions (see Weaknesses).
- **Clarity of contributions**: R2 notes that contributions are “clearly presented in the introduction”.

### Weaknesses
- **Poor presentation and vague/unsupported claims**: Multiple reviewers criticize writing quality. R1 calls writing “repetitive” (e.g., every section reiterates “paying attention to critical tasks”). R2 identifies several vague or false claims: (a) “first explainable meta-RL method” is ill-defined; (b) “generalization is likely to improve” violates No Free Lunch theorems; (c) “no compromise on non-critical tasks” is unsupported theoretically and empirically; (d) augmentation “adds information” is false – it injects noise; (e) “outperforms state-of-the-art baselines” is misleading because MAML is far from SOTA. R2 also flags anthropomorphism (“paying attention”). R4 states presentation needs work: unclear notation, too many references to appendix, lack of intuition, insufficient discussion of results. R1 and R4 both want more explanation of the experimental setting.
- **Method ambiguity and validity of data augmentation**: R2, R3, and R5 question the core augmentation. R2: linearly interpolating state-action-advantage triplets produces invalid samples; it adds noise, not information; the claim that augmentation does not hurt non-critical tasks is unsupported and contradicts No Free Lunch. R3: the mapping from state-action to reward is nonlinear, so mixture samples are invalid; the method is not RL-specific – it is just bilevel optimization for Mixup coefficients and applies equally to supervised meta-learning. R5 is skeptical of using conditional mutual information to prove no performance change on non-critical tasks; if augmented data quantity becomes large, the algorithm could overfit critical tasks and ignore others. R2 and R3 both emphasize that the method’s effectiveness is not properly justified.
- **Missing related work**: R2 and R5 point out that two highly relevant fields are omitted: black-box meta-RL (e.g., VariBAD [Zintgraf 2020], PEARL [Rakelly 2019], RL² [Duan 2016]) and unsupervised environment design (UED) (e.g., PAIRED [Jiang 2021], ADR [Jackson 2023]). R2 notes that UED’s minimax-regret objective is very similar to the paper’s objective. R5 adds that context-based meta-RL with information-theoretic approaches (Li et al.) is not discussed. R2 and R5 argue that inclusion would strengthen the contribution.
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation and ablations**: Several reviewers call for more experiments. R1: missing ablation on *Ncr* (number of critical tasks); why always 5? How were baseline hyperparameters tuned? R2: core algorithm MAML is not SOTA; comparison to black-box meta-RL and UED methods is needed. R4: task distributions are narrow (only MuJoCo, drone, stock; Meta-World would be better); results lack discussion; no quantification of added computation. R5: need ablation to show that augmentation is better than simply re-weighting critical tasks; impact of number of tasks? training instability from solving two bilevel problems? R1 (question 2) asks for experiment to verify that weighted meta-policy alone cannot improve generalization.
- **Conceptual issues and significance**: R3 and R4 question novelty and impact. R3: the observation “poor generalization because meta-prior does not pay enough attention to critical tasks” is well-known, yet claimed as a contribution. R4: the method is “academically interesting” but unclear how much impact it will have. R5: the justification for abandoning weighting in favor of augmentation is insufficient; the paper does not convincingly show why augmentation is needed if critical tasks are identified. R3 also notes that knowledge of poorly-adapted tasks may not be available in practice.
- **Theoretical concerns**: Although theoretical analysis is generally praised, R5 questions the use of conditional mutual information to prove no performance change on non-critical tasks and flags a potential typo in Appendix D. R2 reiterates that the “no impact on non-critical tasks” claim is unsupported and contradicts No Free Lunch. R4 wishes proofs were less appendix-heavy.
- **Missing conversion of reviewer questions**: Several questions that overlap with criticisms are listed as weaknesses above (e.g., ablation on Ncr, weighting vs augmentation, comparison to UED, computation cost, stability tricks). Additional unanswered questions include: (R4) why not use held-out tasks directly for outer-loop optimization? (R5) applicability to offline meta-RL? (R3) online or offline RL? (R1) hyperparameter search details?
