## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Geometric Bayesian Flow Networks (GeoBFN) for 3D molecule generation, applying the recently introduced Bayesian Flow Networks (BFN) framework to model both continuous atomic positions and discrete atom types/charges via a latent parameter space. The method incorporates an SE(3)-equivariant GNN to maintain invariance, achieves state-of-the-art performance on QM9 and GEOM-DRUG benchmarks (e.g., 90.87% molecule stability, 85.6% atom stability at 1k steps), and enables flexible trade-off between sampling speed and quality. Reviewers generally find the approach strong and promising, but raise concerns about the theoretical formulation and proof of equivariance, clarity of the noise-sensitivity motivation, and novelty.

### Strengths
- Strong empirical performance, exceeding prior diffusion-based and other generative models on multiple benchmarks (Reviewers 1–4). Specific numbers: 90.87% molecule stability in QM9, 85.6% atom stability in GEOM-DRUG at 1k steps.
- First application of Bayesian Flow Networks to molecular geometry generation (Reviewer 3).
- Naturally handles the multi-modality of molecular data: continuous coordinates and discrete atom types/charges within a unified probabilistic framework (Reviewer 1).
- Sampling quality improves consistently with more steps, and the model allows any number of steps to trade off efficiency and quality (e.g., 20× speedup without performance loss) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- Provides a clear and readable introduction to BFN and well-formulated mathematical foundations for the proposed method (Reviewer 4).
- Comprehensive empirical evaluation with comparisons to many benchmarks and ablation studies (Reviewer 4).
- The lower variance of the parameter space compared to sample space of diffusion models is nicely visualized (Figure 3) and argued to be advantageous (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical rigor and proofs:** Several key derivations are incomplete or missing. The variational bound (Eq. 8) and its reduction to Eq. 19 are not derived; the meaning of \(L_\infty\) and why minimizing it yields correct parameters is unclear (Reviewer 2). The proof of Theorem 3.1 (translational equivariance) ends mid-sentence; it does not address translation invariance—only rotation invariance via matrix \(\mathbf{R}\) is shown, and the concept "Zero of Mass" is undefined (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2). The proof of Proposition 3.2 and the transition from Lemma C.1 to Eq. 23 skip major steps (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear motivation for BFN's advantage:** It is not entirely clear why Bayesian Flow Networks improve modeling of 3D molecular tasks over diffusion models. The authors' explanation in Section 3.3 is unclear to multiple reviewers: the connection between entropy increase of marginals and "smoother information changes" is subjective and not convincingly linked to improved performance (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). Specifically, the claim that marginals in GeoBFN follow an entropy-increasing procedure (from \(\delta(\theta_0)\) to data distribution) while diffusion follows entropy-decrease (from high-variance Gaussian to data) is not well explained, and the term "objective" therein is ambiguous (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). Additionally, the effect of noise on interatomic distances and chemical validity needs a more rigorous explanation (Reviewer 4).
- **Clarity of design choices:** The handling of discretized charges and why it outperforms discrete atom-type modeling is not elaborated (Reviewer 1). The objective modification in Eq. 21 and how the weighted average plus nearest-neighbor selection addresses sparsity of center buckets is unclear (Reviewer 4). The subscripts GeoBFN\(_{50},\dots,\)GeoBFN\(_{2k}\) in Table 1 are not defined (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing details:** Conditional molecule generation details are claimed to be in the appendix but were not found (Reviewer 1). A clear formulation of the training algorithm (e.g., algorithmic block) is missing (Reviewer 2). Possible architectural or preprocessing differences between GeoBFN and diffusion baselines (e.g., EDM) are not discussed; thus it is unclear whether all improvements are attributable to the BFN formulation, especially since the training loss (Eq. 19) is similar to diffusion (Reviewer 2).
- **Inconsistencies:** There appears to be an inconsistency between the definition of \(p_U\) in Eq. 5 and that in Graves et al. Eq. 6—the manuscript samples \(y\) from \(p_O\) while Graves samples from \(p_S\) (Reviewer 1). The bold numbers in the \(V\times U\) and Novelty columns of Table 1 appear incorrect (Reviewer 1). EDM results in Table 1 are worse than originally reported in the EDM paper; this discrepancy needs explanation (Reviewer 1).
- **Questions about results:** Larger numbers of generation steps seem to decrease novelty—why? (Reviewer 4). Validity on the GEOM-DRUG dataset decreases with more steps and is overall lower than some baselines; is there something special about this dataset? (Reviewer 4). Is atom stability easier to achieve than molecular stability? (Reviewer 4).
- **Minor:** Typo: "transitional" should be "translational" (Reviewer 1).

Note: While Reviewer 2 raised the most serious theoretical concerns, Reviewer 3 and 4 found the presentation otherwise sound (Reviewer 3 gave 4/4 for soundness and presentation, indicating a minority disagreement on severity). Reviewer 1's score was raised after discussion, acknowledging resolution of some concerns.
