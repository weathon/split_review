## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a framework leveraging exposure bracketing photography (multiple raw images) to unify denoising, deblurring, high dynamic range imaging, and super-resolution in low-light scenes. The solution includes a temporally modulated recurrent network (TMRNet) and a self-supervised adaptation method. A synthetic data simulation pipeline is built (using UPI-based RGB-to-raw conversion with RIFE frame interpolation) and real-world images from 200 nighttime scenes are collected. The model is pre-trained on synthetic paired data then adapted to real-world unlabeled images. Experiments on both datasets show favorable performance against state-of-the-art multi-image processing methods.

### Strengths
- The first method to jointly perform denoising, deblurring, HDR, and super-resolution using exposure bracketing; this paradigm is orthogonal to mainstream research and can inspire subsequent studies (R1, R3, R4).
- Good/impressive performance: experimental results show clear improvements in both visual quality and quantitative metrics (R1, R2, R4).
- The work is well-complete: it covers data simulation, real-world data collection, method design, and extensive experiments. The dataset creation pipeline is solid and the self-supervised adaptation method is clever (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The paper is well-written and well-organized, with clearly articulated problems and solutions (R3, R4).
- The necessity of the TMRNet architecture and the proposed loss functions is clearly explained and supported by ablation experiments (R3).
- The constructed synthetic and real datasets will be helpful for researchers in the related field (R3).
- **Minority reviewer (R4, score 8) is notably more positive**, emphasizing that the core contribution lies in the dataset construction and training methods, and that the approach is a practical, orthogonal technique. R4 also found the experiments impressive and the self-supervised adaptation design clever.

### Weaknesses
- **Claim of unifying tasks is exaggerated / insufficient technical novelty**: The ability to handle four degradations together stems simply from training on a dataset that contains those degradations; the network itself does not present significant novelty compared to existing video restoration networks (R3). HDR methods already address noise, blur, and ghosting; inputs with combined degradations have appeared in prior work (R2 cites R1, R2). The authors should adjust the wording of this contribution (R2).
- **Architecture lacks originality**: TMRNet is similar to a common conv-LSTM with different convolution layers per layer; temporal modulation networks have already been explored (e.g., “Temporal Modulation Network for Controllable Space-Time Video Super-Resolution,” CVPR 2021) (R3). The BracketIRE and BracketIRE+ settings are also similar to all-in-one methods (e.g., AdaIR, Perceive-IR); a direct comparison with these all-in-one approaches is needed (R1).
- **Self-supervised loss functions are not very different from existing losses**: For instance, the Exponential Moving Average Normalization loss from “Exponential Moving Average Normalization for Self-supervised and Semi-supervised Learning” (CVPR 2021) is closely related (R3).
- **Computational cost is high**: TMRNet’s FLOPs are huge compared with competing methods, raising doubts about the fairness of the comparison (R1).
- **Unfair comparisons in Table 2**: The compared methods are only trained on synthetic data, while TMRNet was fine-tuned on real-world data. The authors should include results for TMRNet *without* real-world fine-tuning, and add a note about this discrepancy (R2).  
  **Related missing comparison with AI-ISP approaches**: The self-supervised adaptation method still faces challenges compared to supervised methods when paired data are available. It would be helpful to compare against an AI-ISP pipeline that addresses RAW denoising, super-resolution, deblurring, and HDR as independent subtasks (possibly obtaining paired real data), or to demonstrate on synthetic data that the joint exposure-bracketing approach offers clear advantages over handling each subtask separately with specialized networks (R4).
- **RGB-to-raw conversion is insufficiently justified**: The method using UPI requires camera-specific parameters, but the paper does not specify the CMOS sensor type of the videos or whether it matches the UPI setting. Moreover, using RIFE for frame interpolation can introduce artifacts (especially in areas with significant motion and complex textures), creating unrealistic motion trajectories and ghosting. This may cause the model to overfit synthetic artifacts and limit generalization to real-world blurred scenes (R1, R4).
- **Unclear if raw packing operations are used**: Many raw-image processing methods pack color channels; the paper does not specify whether this is performed (R1).
- **Exclusion of invalid pixels not explained**: The paper excludes 10 and 4 invalid pixels around the input without justification; it is unclear whether the method can handle marginal image areas (R1).
- **More frames may not always help**: The paper claims that more frames improve performance, but it does not discuss whether over-exposed frames or large-blur frames could make restoration more challenging (R2).
- **Need validation on other public datasets**: The effectiveness of the TMRNet module and the loss functions should be justified on public datasets beyond the authors’ own (R3).
- **Unclear motivation for adding super-resolution**: Why is super-resolution included in BracketIRE+? To match the “restoration” title or to demonstrate low-resolution capability? This needs better justification (R1).
- **Datasets must be made publicly available**: Without public release, the contribution is not essential (R1). The paper states the datasets will be released, but the current work’s reproducibility is in question.
