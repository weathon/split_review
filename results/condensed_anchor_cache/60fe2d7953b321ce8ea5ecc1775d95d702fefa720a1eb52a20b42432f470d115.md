## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes TOTEM, a two-stage approach for universal time series representation. Stage 1 learns a VQ-VAE (TimeVQ) to discretize continuous time series into tokens from a learned codebook. Stage 2 feeds these tokens into a Transformer encoder with task-specific heads for forecasting, classification, and translation tasks. The method is tested on multiple domains (neuroscience, seismology, meteorology, power grids, traffic) and includes new large-scale datasets. The authors claim universality and scalability. All three reviewers assigned a rating of 3 (weak reject). One reviewer rated soundness 3 (good) while the others rated it 2 (fair); presentation scores were 2, 2, and 1 (poor).

### Strengths
- The paper addresses the important problem of learning generalizable representations for time series across tasks and domains.
- The method is simple and presented clearly with good visualizations and a commendable literature review.
- Using VQ-VAE for tokenization is interesting and reasonable, and experiments verify that learned embeddings generalize across domains.
- The experiments cover multiple downstream tasks (forecasting, classification, translation) across diverse domains, demonstrating broad applicability.

### Weaknesses
- **Overclaimed universality and scale**: The "universal" claim is overstated. The representation is learned per dataset ("we only train one TimeVQ per dataset"), so there is no single model across all data—contradicting the claim. Cross-domain zero-shot experiments test only small domain gaps (e.g., training on one set of human domains and transferring to another) with no evaluation on significantly different domains. The newly introduced datasets are not convincingly larger than existing ones; e.g., TimesNet uses anomaly detection datasets with over 500k series. Moreover, different tasks require retraining the model and task-specific heads, so the method does not provide a truly universal model across tasks. The title claims "at scale" but the dataset sizes are not exceptionally large compared to prior work.
- **Low novelty and insufficient citations**: The method heavily relies on VQ-VAE with only minor modifications, limiting novelty. Several highly relevant prior works are not properly cited: BeiT (vision VQ-VAE for pre-training), VQ-AR (vector quantized autoregressive time series forecasting), Crossformer (first to flatten channels for univariate time series representation), Non-stationary Transformer (processes input series by removing mean/std and recovers scale via MLP, identical to the Scale Reinstater design), and TimesNet (temporal 2D-variation modeling for general time series analysis, evaluated on forecasting, classification, and imputation). These should be discussed and compared.
- **Insufficient experimental methodology**: (1) The paper does not report multiple random runs or standard deviations for different experiment settings, raising reliability concerns. (2) Comparison to baselines is incomplete; notably, TimesNet is not compared in forecasting, classification, or translation tasks despite being a task-general backbone. (3) The proposed method performs worse than existing models on Weather, Traffic, and Electricity datasets, undermining the universality claim. (4) Efficiency comparison (GPU memory, running time, parameter size) is missing, especially concerning given that flattening sensor channels leads to extremely high dimensionality for multivariate datasets like Traffic. (5) An ablation study is missing; critically, replacing TimeVQ with a simple linear layer on patches would test whether discrete tokenization is necessary. (6) The translation task is poorly defined: what are "envelopes"? Is it simply time series regression? A detailed mathematical formulation is needed. (7) Imputation, a related challenging task requiring handling of missing data, is not discussed. (8) The selection of new datasets (mainly human-brain ECoG) seems arbitrary without clear motivation. (9) The usefulness of forecasting on Neuro and Earthquake datasets is questionable; these may not represent practical real-world scenarios.
- **Presentation ambiguities**: (1) Figure 2 shows both Transformer encoder and decoder, but Section 3.2 states only the XEncoder (Time Transformer Encoder) is used; this needs clarification. (2) How attention is applied (presumably along the token dimension) is not explicitly described—clearer architecture figures are needed. (3) The "Scale Reinstater" is used inconsistently: in forecasting it reinstates future mean/std, but in classification/translation it is used as a scale embedding, which does not correspond to reinstatement. A clearer naming or explanation is required. (4) Unannotated arrows in Stage b of Figure 2 (e.g., above the normalization block and below the tokenization block) are unclear. (5) The paper should clarify whether normalization methods differ between Stage a and Stage b, and why CNN is used in pretraining while Transformer is used in downstream tasks.
- **Lack of clear motivation for quantization**: The paper does not convincingly justify why discretizing continuous time series into tokens is beneficial. Time series are inherently complex and continuous, and tokenization may lose information. The authors should provide evidence that the discrete representation outperforms simpler continuous embeddings.

*Note on reviewer disagreement*: One reviewer (R2) rated soundness 3 (good) while others rated it 2 (fair), and one reviewer (R3) rated presentation 1 (poor) versus 2 for the others. These differences reflect varying assessments of experimental rigor and clarity, though all agreed on the overall rating.
