## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Topological Positional Encoding (ToPE), which integrates persistent homology (PH) with positional encodings (PE) to boost GNN expressivity. ToPE uses PEs to induce graph filtrations, computes persistent homology embeddings, concatenates them with base PEs, and feeds the combined vector into GNN layers. Theoretical analysis claims increased expressivity over Laplacian PE and a connection to the k-FWL test. Experiments are conducted on molecular property prediction, out-of-distribution generalization, and synthetic tree tasks.

### Strengths
- The method is straightforward and the manuscript is generally well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1,2,3,4,6).
- The idea of introducing topological-aware positional encoding is interesting (Reviewer 4).
- The paper presents extensive experiments that could guide practitioners, with ToPE outperforming existing methods on most tasks (Reviewers 2,3). The insights are didactic and well communicated (Reviewer 5).
- Some reviewers found the theory well-written and self-contained, and appreciated the expressivity comparison of positional embeddings (Reviewers 3,5).
- A novel positional encoding that encodes topological features via learnable persistence diagrams is proposed (Reviewer 6).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and unclear contribution vs. prior work** (Reviewers 1,2,4): The paper does not clearly articulate how ToPE differs fundamentally from existing approaches like VC and RePHINE. Using positional encodings to induce filtration is noted as the key difference, but this distinction from layer-wise GNN embeddings or node features is not adequately emphasized. Multiple reviewers found the combination of PE and PH arbitrary, more like an ad-hoc engineering trick than a principled method, lacking a clear theoretical motivation.
- **Shallow and insufficient theoretical analysis** (Reviewers 1,2,3,4): 
  - Proposition 1 (stability) is considered trivial or its statement problematic: the constant $L_{\psi}$ depends on the graph (order of quantifiers), which could lead to instability even for close graphs (Reviewer 3). The purpose of the stability analysis is unclear (Reviewer 4).
  - Lemma 1, Propositions 2 and 3 (expressivity) do not analyze the actual final GNN, only components, and rely on contrived examples. It is not clear that ToPE is strictly more expressive than Laplacian PE within aggregate-combine GNNs – for any ToPE GNN, there might exist a Laplacian PE GNN that distinguishes the same graphs (Reviewer 3). The connection to k-FWL feels tacked on and does not provide deeper understanding (Reviewer 1). The proof that ToPE is more expressive than PE alone is considered trivial because PH expressivity is already known; the important comparison to PH-only methods (e.g., RePHINE) is missing (Reviewers 2,4). The statement of Proposition 3 is confusing regarding the role of the hash function (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited and potentially overstated experiments** (Reviewers 2,4,5,6):
  - Experiments are conducted only on molecular graphs and a synthetic tree task, yet the abstract claims “several graph and language processing applications” – an overstatement (Reviewer 4).
  - Improvements over baselines are often marginal; given reported variance, differences may not be statistically significant (Reviewer 4).
  - No baseline without positional encodings (e.g., RePHINE) is included to verify whether ToPE is more effective than PH-only methods (Reviewers 2,4). Comparisons to other PH-based methods and structural approaches (e.g., graph kernels) are absent (Reviewer 5).
  - The tables are confusing because the proposed method name “ToPE” never appears (Reviewer 4).
  - **Missing experimental documentation**: No model selection procedure, hyperparameter grid, or split details are provided (Reviewer 4). The hyperparameter sensitivity (e.g., dimension of topological features, number of persistence intervals, encoding method) is not analyzed, despite prior work showing such hyperparameters significantly affect performance (Reviewer 5). Computational cost of persistent homology is not addressed (Reviewer 5).
- **Motivation for using positional encodings for filtration is unclear** (Reviewers 1,6): Why build filtration on PEs rather than other node features? If PEs only serve for better distinguishability, would using high-order GNNs or random features lead to an even more expressive model? The role of PEs in this context is not justified.
- **Training with persistence diagrams** (Reviewer 6): Persistence diagrams are discrete functions of graph filtrations; how are they trained with gradient descent? The paper does not explain this crucial implementation detail.
- **Disagreement among reviewers**: While some reviewers found the theoretical analysis well-written and the experiments extensive (Reviewers 3,5,6), others considered the theory shallow and the experiments insufficient, with Reviewer 4 giving a score of 1 and Reviewer 1 a score of 3. The positive reviews (3,5,6) still expressed significant weaknesses regarding novelty and theoretical depth.
