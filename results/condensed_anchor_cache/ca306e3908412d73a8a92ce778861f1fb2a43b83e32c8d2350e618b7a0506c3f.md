## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Network-based Active Inference (NetAIF), integrating Active Inference with network dynamics for robotic valve-turning. It claims superior computational efficiency and task performance compared to Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL), with results showing over 99% accuracy and dramatic reductions in iteration count and compute cost. The reviewers agree the problem is well-motivated and the approach is novel, but they raise significant concerns about clarity, experimental detail, fairness of comparisons, and limited benchmarking.

### Strengths
- The paper is well motivated and addresses an important issue in robotics: alternative algorithms to DRL that are more sample-efficient for specific applications.
- NetAIF's ability to learn in real-time without extensive pre-training offers a significant advantage over traditional RL methods.
- The evaluation supports the claims of sample efficiency and improved performance of the proposed algorithm on the considered task.
- The method is evaluated on both simulated and real-world tasks, providing evidence of practicality.
- The incorporation of feedback control law in the network model enables stability, and the use of random pullback attractor for weight updates is interesting.
- Performance is very good: it achieves over 99% accuracy in goal position and orientation in untrained dynamic environments, with a 45,000-fold reduction in computational costs and approximately 100,000 times more efficient iteration count than DRL.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of experimental and algorithmic details**: The paper is missing critical information needed for reproducibility and understanding.
  - The state space of the robot for the valve-turning task is never explicitly mentioned, even in supplementary material.
  - The prediction error metric is not defined: is it the difference between current and desired robot joint angles?
  - For DRL baselines, the state and action spaces and the reward function are not provided.
  - Section 2.2 mentions a “random attractor” but it is not explained how it fits into Algorithm 1.
  - The algorithm box is missing many critical pieces: how new weight values are set, over what prediction errors are computed, how the learning rule works, and what sets the network architecture apart from a linear RNN.
  - There is no clear description of the network architecture, input, output, neuron weights, or the design of the feedback controller and its parameters.
  - The implementation of the random pullback attractor and how it contributes to the learning process is unclear.
  - Noise processing is not described.
- **Limited benchmarking and scalability**: The experiments are considered too simple for a machine learning conference.
  - The paper would be significantly strengthened by evaluating on more benchmarks to determine generalizability.
  - The motion speed of the manipulator is low, not enough to verify effectiveness.
  - The method is only demonstrated on one real-world task (valve turning); there is no evaluation on alternative tasks (e.g., different valve shapes, frictional properties, highly dynamic systems like quadcopters).
  - Failure cases are not shown or analyzed. What are the failure modes and why?
  - No real-world deployment of DRL policies is shown, so it is unclear if they are so bad that deploying them was not feasible.
- **Fairness of comparison**: It is unclear whether NetAIF leverages additional information compared to model-free RL algorithms.
  - The paper must explicitly state the input representation for both NetAIF and DRL baselines to ensure that the comparison is not skewed by differences in available information (e.g., NetAIF may have access to the goal state while RL receives only sparse rewards).
  - In Table 1, adding noise and HER improves SAC performance; why were those not applied to PPO in Table 2? And why wasn't PPO tested in Table 1 for consistency?
  - The paper does not discuss comparison to model-based RL approaches.
- **Insufficient related work and background explanation**: The paper is difficult to process, especially for readers without an Active Inference background.
  - There is no related work section, making it hard to situate the contribution within existing literature (e.g., predictive coding networks, other AIF-based methods, standard neural networks).
  - A detailed explanation of how NetAIF differs from standard neural networks (particularly weight updates compared to backpropagation) is necessary.
  - The feedback control loops and how they influence weight adjustments are described only at a high level.
- **Presentation issues**: The expression is weak and key issues are not explained clearly.
  - The first paragraph of the introduction feels disconnected from the core concept.
  - A summary of contributions at the end of the introduction would be helpful.
  - The paper lacks a clear problem statement.
  - Figure 3 needs clarification: what are the external, internal, and blanket states for the valve-turning task?
  - Literature cited is considered old and the literature research is insufficient.
- **Unaddressed limitations**: The paper does not discuss sensitivity to initial conditions or performance in highly complex environments, nor does it provide a discussion of the method’s stability and robustness given the missing feedback controller details.
- **Code not provided**: The authors are asked to release the related code.

**Note on reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 5) was more positive, emphasizing the innovative application and good performance, but still noted insufficient literature research, weak expression, and too simple experiments. The other three reviewers (score 3) were consistent in their criticism of missing details, limited benchmarking, and unclear mechanisms.
