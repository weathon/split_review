## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies domain-adapted sentence embeddings for question-answering document retrieval. It proposes a systematic method to determine similarity-score thresholds, introduces bootstrapped confidence intervals and distributional overlap metrics (COE, ROE), and analyzes isotropy. Experiments on telecom, health and science datasets show that fine-tuning and pretraining-then-fine-tuning improve retrieval accuracy and tighten confidence intervals. One reviewer (score 8) finds the study solid and clear; the other three (scores 3 each) see limited novelty, weak narrative cohesion, and insufficient justification for the proposed methodology.

### Strengths
- (R1) The paper is generally understandable and the figures/charts are exceptionally clear.
- (R2) Proposes novel evaluation metrics: confidence intervals over bootstrapped samples and distributional overlap metrics (COE, ROE), which are well-explained.
- (R3) Addresses a relevant problem for RAG applications; demonstrates that fine-tuning improves retrieval performance and tightens confidence intervals, indicating reduced expected performance variance in the domain.
- (R4) Proposes a systematic method to introduce similarity-score thresholds for document retrieval, supported by extensive experiments showing that fine-tuning and pretraining-then-fine-tuning help improve accuracy.
- Contrast: R1 is markedly more positive about the contributions and presentation than the other three reviewers, who consider the paper’s novelty low and its narrative weak.

### Weaknesses
- (R1, also echoed by R3) Insufficient details on isotropy scores; insufficient motivation for the threshold computation approach – it is unclear why existing methods struggle with this issue and how the paper effectively addresses it.
- (R2) Lacks specifics on the pre-training corpus for each domain. For example, in the health domain, pre-training on a large corpus like PubMed abstracts is standard; the current setup risks conflating pre-training effects with simply adapting to training-data distribution. Comparisons are limited to models from BAAI and OpenAI; state-of-the-art domain-specific baselines (e.g., models pre-trained on legal corpora) are missing, making it hard to assess true effectiveness.
- (R3) The confidence interval estimation techniques are direct applications of common methodologies. The proposed threshold assessment is not compellingly justified against simpler approaches like hyper-parameter cross-validation (which is inexpensive if the similarity matrix is computed once). The connection of isotropy findings to prior work is insufficiently explained, and the narrative lacks cohesion – sections are not tied together, and the methodology is not systematically contrasted against baseline approaches.
- (R4) Lack of novelty: fine-tuning or continued pretraining for domain performance and the isotropy problem have been well studied. Too many research questions – RQ2 and RQ3 appear loosely connected to the main topic of domain-adapted sentence embeddings. Poor organization: the introduction functions as a literature review rather than clarifying the research problem, gap, and contributions.
- (R2 asks) How does test/dev set accuracy compare to bootstrapped accuracy? Could it be compared with existing QA model results as a relaxed version of exact answer retrieval? (Missing comparison)
- (R4 asks) Why is the confidence interval set to 95%? Is there supporting evidence? (Missing justification)
- (R4 asks) Why is nDCG not used, given it is widely employed for information retrieval evaluation? (Missing metric comparison)
