## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a unified theoretical framework for robust supervised contrastive losses against label noise. It derives a general condition (risk difference must be constant) for robustness, analyzes InfoNCE, InfoNCE-NN, and RINCE under that condition, and proposes SymNCE (Symmetric InfoNCE) as a robust variant. Reviewers acknowledge the importance of the problem, the unifying perspective, and theoretical grounding, but raise significant concerns about novelty, strong assumptions (class-balanced, symmetric noise), unfair/unreliable experimental comparisons, missing baselines, lack of instance-dependent noise evaluation, and clarity issues.

### Strengths
- Unifies prior techniques (nearest-neighbor selection, RINCE) under a common theoretical framework. (R1, R2, R3)
- Well-motivated from classic robustness theory (Ghosh et al.). (R1)
- Proposed theoretical condition is extendable to other losses. (R3, R5)
- SymNCE is simple to implement without large computational overhead. (R2)
- Solid theoretical analysis. (R4)
- Focuses on a practically important problem with a theoretically grounded solution. (R5)
- Baselines implemented in the same code base, enabling fair comparisons. (R3)
- Empirical robustness studied under realistic/natural noise (Clothing1M). (R3)
- Well-organized structure with clear contributions. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns:** The symmetry condition is already known from Ghosh et al. for supervised losses; Theorem 3.2 is a straightforward adaptation of Theorem 1 from Ghosh et al. (R1). RINCE already extended the theory; this work is an incremental step. (R3)
- **Strong/unrealistic assumptions:** Corollary 3.3 assumes class-balanced clean distribution, which is not guaranteed without ground-truth labels. (R4, R5) Symmetric label noise is unlikely in practice; most methods now tackle instance-dependent noise. (R2, R4) The unified threshold \(t\) for positive selection in InfoNCE-NN is unreasonable because clustering quality may vary per class. (R2) No experiments show effectiveness under imbalanced data. (R4)
- **Missing comparisons with latest methods:** The paper does not compare with Selective-supervised Contrastive Learning (CVPR22), Twin Contrastive Learning (CVPR23), NGC (ICCV21), or methods combining SimCLR and sCL (e.g., arxiv 2206.01206, 2306.04160). (R2) Also missing asymmetric robust losses, Generalized Jensen-Shannon divergence loss, f-divergence losses, and Bregman divergence losses. (R3)
- **Unfair experimental comparisons:**
  - Warmup with SupCon is used only for SymNCE, not for baselines. (R3)
  - SymNCE’s weight \(\beta\) is selected per noise rate and dataset (e.g., 0.2, 1.0, 0.6, 1.0 for CIFAR-100 at \(\gamma=0.0,0.2,0.6,0.8\)), while baselines use fixed hyperparameters per dataset. (R3)
  - Temperature \(\tau\) is changed per dataset for SymNCE/SupCon but fixed for RINCE. (R3)
  - Learning rate is changed per dataset for SymNCE/SupCon but fixed for all other baselines. (R3)
  - Batch size 512 may favor contrastive losses over non-contrastive baselines. (R3)
  - No mean and standard deviation from multiple runs are reported; results may be unreliable. (R3)
- **Clothing1M concerns:** Only 1,440 samples (a small subset) are used, while many baselines originally use the full 1M noisy dataset. CE result (53.48) is far below literature (68–70), suggesting implementation differences. (R4, R3)
- **Label flip dependence:** Success may depend heavily on which labels are flipped (e.g., dogs vs. cats vs. orthogonal classes). How flips are generated should be shared for reproducibility. (R1)
- **Missing noise types:** No experiments with instance-dependent label noise on CIFAR (e.g., using [1,2] from ICLR21, NeurIPS20) or with real-world human annotations [3,4]. (R4) Asymmetric noise is tested but no theoretical insight is given for why the framework should/handles it. (R5) Sample-dependent noise not considered. (R1)
- **Theoretical clarity issues:**
  - Missing derivation steps from Eq. 39 to 40 in the proof of Corollary 3.3; the constant must be independent of \(f\), which is critical. (R3)
  - Under Eq. 14, should it be \(\lim_{K\rightarrow\infty} \log K\) (infinite)? If so, does Corollary 3.3 hold when constants are infinite? (R3)
  - For InfoNCE-NN, robustness argument relies on a threshold \(t\) chosen per \(f\), but Corollary 3.3 requires the constant to be independent of \(f\). (R3)
  - Discussion after Corollary 3.3 is confusing; relationship with Ghosh et al. 2017 needs clarification. (R5)
  - Intuitive explanations for terms in Section 3.4.1 would improve understanding. (R5)
- **Missing baselines and experiments:**
  - SimCLR should be compared because it is label-agnostic, making it a natural robust baseline. (R1)
  - No experiments with \(M=1\) views (single positive like SimCLR); what happens with noisy augmentations then? (R1)
- **RINCE at high noise rates:** Claims of 80% noise effectiveness surprising; likely depends on label flip construction; verification via multiple runs needed (little y-axis variation in Fig. 1c). (R1, R3)
- **No discussion of limitations.** (R2)
- **Presentation improvements needed:** Main contributions unclear until Section 4; overall readability can be improved. (R1, R5)
