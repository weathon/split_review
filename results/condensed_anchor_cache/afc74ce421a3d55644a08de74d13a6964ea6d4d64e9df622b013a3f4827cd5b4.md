## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a text-augmented Video-Language Model (VLM) method that uses LLM-based text rewriting to generate diverse positive/negative samples, combined with multi-level contrastive learning to improve video-text alignment. The approach is designed as a plug-and-play module and is evaluated on video grounding, QA, and retrieval tasks. Reviewers rate the paper 5–6 (mostly 5, one 6). They agree the motivation is clear and the experiments extensive, but raise major concerns about limited novelty (LLM augmentation + contrastive learning is well-studied), insufficient validation of the core claim that VLMs “don’t understand language input,” missing baselines (larger models, zero-shot, non-LLM augmentation), and methodology gaps (loss formulation, hyperparameters, scalability). The more positive reviewer (R5, score 6) stresses the value of text diversity and structure-level augmentation, but still notes added cost and similarity to retrieval-augmented methods.

### Strengths
- Clear motivation: the issue of video-text misalignment is underexplored and practical (R1, R3, R4, R6).
- The method is plug-and-play, easily integrable into existing VLMs, and shows performance gains across multiple tasks (R1, R2, R3, R5, R6).
- Extensive experiments covering video grounding, video QA, and video-text retrieval, with multiple VLM backbones (e.g., All-In-One, Just Ask) – strong empirical evidence (R2, R3, R5).
- The contrastive learning approach (positives vs. negatives) is intuitive and sound, especially the idea of attribute-level selection and fine-grained text reasoning (R3, R5, R6).
- Structure-level augmentation (beyond word/sentence) is a valuable addition; ablation studies provide insight into word-type importance (R3, R5).
- Paper is well-organized and easy to follow (R4, R6).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty**: The core ideas – LLM-based text rewriting for data augmentation and contrastive learning with positive/negative samples – are already standard in NLP and vision-language research. The work is largely a combination of existing techniques (R1, R3, R6). The text rewriting approach is considered simplistic and common knowledge (R3, R6).

2. **Motivation not convincingly validated**: The claim that “VLMs don’t understand language input” is not fully supported by the analysis. The observed performance drop under text rewriting may be due to domain-specific overfitting (e.g., Charades-STA is template-based, larger models like BLIP-2 and InternVideo show less degradation) rather than a fundamental limitation (R1, R2, R4). No statistical evidence (e.g., relationship between text representations and output logits) is provided to back Figure 1’s negative examples (R6). The paper does not adequately review existing methods that use text representations and show strong generalization in training-free settings (e.g., [a,b,c,d,e]) (R4).

3. **Missing critical baselines and comparisons**:  
   - Comparison with larger video-LLMs (e.g., VideoChat, Video-LLaVA) that have stronger generalization is absent; their inclusion is essential to assess the method’s added value (R1).  
   - Zero-shot evaluation is missing – the degradation might disappear without supervised fine-tuning, which would undermine the motivation (R1).  
   - No comparison with non-LLM text augmentation methods (e.g., synonym/antonym replacement, back-translation, paraphrasing) that are simpler and have been widely used (R2).  
   - Should be compared to image-text alignment literature; no discussion of how video-text misalignment differs (R3).  
   - No cross-data evaluation to check whether improvements come from text invariance or better video encoding (R4).

4. **Benchmark choices are weak or incomplete**:  
   - ActivityNet-Caps and Charades are not the most comprehensive or current benchmarks; recent ones like VATEX (longer, multi-reference captions), YouCook2 (dense captioning), and Video-MME (for Video-LLMs) would be stronger choices (R2).  
   - The paper lacks a distribution of augmented text lengths, so scalability to longer, real-world text is unclear (R2).

5. **Methodology gaps and unclear implementation details**:  
   - It is unclear whether both video encoder and text encoder are updated during training; Figure 2 shows no gradient backpropagation (R3).  
   - The “most discriminative word” selection and the claim that the loss is a “Weighted contrastive loss” are not properly justified – equation (4) does not match that description (R3).  
   - Implementation details are missing: which datasets are used for fine-tuning? For ActivityNet Captions, does the method generate augmentations on the training set and then fine-tune open-source VLMs? (R2).  
   - Ablation on the use of negative samples (especially for generation tasks like captioning) is missing (R2).  
   - No t-SNE visualization to confirm that positive/negative samples diverge in embedding space as claimed (e.g., Line 93) (R6).  
   - The statement “predefined text inputs are too strict and user-unfriendly” is not well-justified – for captioning, natural captions are not template-based (R2).

6. **Hyperparameter and cost considerations**:  
   - Hyperparameter ablation (e.g., on contrastive loss weighting, number of augmented samples) is missing (R3).  
   - The method incurs additional training cost and data scale from using multiple off-the-shelf LLMs (R5).  
   - No running time including LLM rephrasing is provided in Table 5; baseline times are not included (R5).  
   - Dataset noise may be amplified by LLM-generated text, worsening misalignment (R5).

7. **Presentation and figures**:  
   - Figures need refinement (e.g., Fig. 4(3) contains unnecessary coordinate points) (R6).  
   - The storyline and frame are not well-aligned with the core question (R2).
