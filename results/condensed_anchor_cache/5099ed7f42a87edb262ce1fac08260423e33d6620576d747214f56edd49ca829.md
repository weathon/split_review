## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents MolReFlect, a teacher-student framework for fine-grained molecule-caption alignment. It uses a large teacher LLM (Llama-3-70B) to zero-shot extract substructure-phrase alignments, then applies In-Context Selective Reflection (retrieving prior extractions for self-correction) and Chain-of-Thought In-Context Molecule Tuning (CoT-ICMT) to train a smaller student LLM (Mistral-7B). Experiments on the ChEBI-20 dataset show state-of-the-art performance on Molecule-to-Caption (Mol2Cap) and Caption-to-Molecule (Cap2Mol) translation tasks, outperforming baselines including ICMA and SFT. The approach aims to enable explainable predictions and effective learning with limited resources.

### Strengths
- Novel MolReFlect teacher-student framework that addresses a critical gap in fine-grained molecule-caption alignment, focusing on molecular substructures and their textual descriptions.
- Achieves good generation performance (Mol2Cap and Cap2Mol) without domain-specific pretraining, and allows finetuning of small models (Mistral-7B) with limited resources (Reviewer 1).
- Includes plenty of ablation studies to validate the effectiveness of different components (Reviewer 2), and provides a thorough description of the methodology and experimental setting (Reviewer 4).
- Well-motivated to enable fine-grained alignments for improved explainability (Reviewer 3).
- One reviewer (score 6) was particularly positive about the framework’s novelty and the practical advantage of training small models.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited dataset and task evaluation**: All experiments are conducted only on the ChEBI-20 dataset; the model is not evaluated on other datasets (e.g., Mol-Instructions) or other molecular tasks such as molecule retrieval, property prediction, or novelty scoring. Novelty scores are missing, raising concerns about memorization rather than generalization (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Missing baselines**: The comparison set is limited and does not include more recent state-of-the-art chemical language models such as nach0, Text+Chem T5, SciFive, PRESTO, GitMol, or MoMu (which is mentioned in Related Work but absent from experiments). The only baseline differing by more than an order of magnitude in parameter count (BiT5 252M vs. MolReFlect’s ~77B) is ICMA, making direct performance comparisons potentially unfair (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Ablation concerns**: The ablation studies are partially unconvincing. The “w/o In-Context Reflection” and “w/o Selection” variants are practically indistinguishable from the full model (Table 4), and the ablations are only shown for one of the two tasks (Cap2Mol or Mol2Cap) (Reviewer 1).
- **Data leakage risk**: The paper’s statement on data leakage is not convincing. Both the teacher model (Llama-3-70B) and the student model (Mistral-7B) may have encountered ChEBI-20 or similar data during pretraining, and the pre-trained Mole-BERT graph encoder used for retrieval may also introduce leakage. The claim that an unsupervised metric avoids leakage from Galactiva-125M (Line 265) needs justification (Reviewer 3).
- **Computational complexity**: The proposed pipeline uses at least 77B parameters (Llama-3-70B teacher + Mistral-7B student) and requires multiple forward passes for in-context reflection. No computational cost comparison with baselines (e.g., BioT5 with 252M parameters) is provided. Training code is not released, hampering reproducibility (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Single configuration and limited student/teacher variation**: Only one setup is tested (teacher: Llama-3-70B-Instruct, student: Mistral-7B). No experiments with larger student or smaller teacher are performed to assess generalizability (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear intermediate task performance**: The framework uses NER-like substructure extraction and entity linking within In-Context Selective Reflection, but the paper does not report the accuracy of these intermediate steps (Reviewer 4).
- **Choice of SMILES over SELFIES unexplained**: Given that SELFIES guarantee 100% validity, the exclusive use of SMILES without explanation is a gap (Reviewer 4).
- **No human evaluation**: For molecule captioning, which can benefit from expert judgment, human evaluation is absent (Reviewer 2).
- **Other specific criticisms**: (a) The paper does not cite arxiv 2408.11866 which uses a similar framework (Reviewer 1). (b) Robustness to chemically invalid outputs (e.g., Ganeeva et al. 2024) is not tested (Reviewer 1). (c) The justification in Related Work that 2D/3D graphs matter less than fine-text alignment is unfair given the limited scale of molecular data (Reviewer 3). (d) Bold numbers in Table 4 for Validity need correction (Reviewer 1). (e) Notation for “alignments K” (Line 220) is unclear (Reviewer 4). (f) Abbreviation ICMA is not introduced before use (Line 192) and “Mo2Cap” should be “Mol2Cap” (Line 399) (Reviewer 4).
