## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the concept of "epistemic miscalibration" in LLMs—the misalignment between a model's linguistic assertiveness (external certainty) and its internal certainty. The authors develop a new human-labeled dataset and a metric for measuring linguistic assertiveness, claim to cut error rates by over 50% relative to previous benchmarks, and present human evaluations that show a significant gap between expressed confidence and actual accuracy. Three reviewers rated the paper 3 (weak reject), one rated it 6 (weak accept), highlighting a wide disagreement on overall quality.

### Strengths
- Addresses an increasingly relevant and important issue in LLM deployment, particularly for high-stakes domains (e.g., healthcare, misinformation) where overconfidence can mislead users.
- Clearly identifies and formalizes the problem of epistemic miscalibration, providing a useful framework for understanding internal vs. external certainty.
- The human survey component adds empirical support for the existence and severity of miscalibration, bridging computational outputs and user perceptions.
- One reviewer (score 6) found the question intriguing, the results interesting, and the literature survey thorough, with careful definitions and clear conceptual separation of certainty types.

### Weaknesses
- The primary evaluation is limited to a single context (misinformation detection), and several over-claims appear, especially in the introduction and related work. Multiple existing papers (e.g., arXiv:2404.00474) have already demonstrated misalignment between internal and expressed uncertainty, and the research gaps presented (lines 91–102) are not substantiated.
- The method for measuring internal certainty relies on only one approach (from Rivera et al., 2024) without justification for why it is superior to other established methods (e.g., logits, training probabilities, or methods from [2–5] in Reviewer 3's list). This is a fundamental limitation because there is no ground truth for internal certainty. The experiments need to test multiple internal certainty estimators to rule out method-specific artifacts.
- The paper claims to introduce a new human-labeled dataset, but its construction, statistics, and sample data points are not provided. The dataset is derived from news articles dating back to 2017 (from Wang, 2017), so some statements may now be factually outdated, which could influence conclusions. Annotator expertise and recruitment details are missing (e.g., what makes the three annotators experts? how were they paid?).
- The claim of a "novel method for measuring linguistic assertiveness" is unclear; the description in Section 3.2 appears to use previous methods without explaining differences. The core calculation of both internal certainty and linguistic assertiveness is not explained clearly; the paper is not self-contained, just referencing previous work.
- The paper's structure is confusing: dataset and models appear before the method (Section 3), and Sections 5 and 6 should be merged. There is no dedicated background/related work section; related content is scattered across the introduction, Section 2.2, and elsewhere, making it hard to separate contributions from prior work.
- The choice of Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct fine-tuned with LoRA as the sole representation is unusual and lacks justification. Results from other Llama variants (3B, 8B) are needed to study scaling law effects on epistemic calibration.
- The empirical evidence of miscalibration (Main Contribution 2) merely confirms a widely acknowledged phenomenon (see [2,6,7] in Reviewer 3's list); the paper would benefit from proposed solutions or a deeper discussion of methodological approaches to correct miscalibration.
- Several writing and presentation issues: inconsistent decimal precision in tables (e.g., Table 1), full-page figure breaking text flow (Page 10), asterisk not explained in Table 2, author column centered, Figure 2 legend should use model names on x-axis, and Figure 4a tick values not clearly indicated. Line 41 references "Figures 1 and 5" without indicating they are in the appendix.
- The paper claims the method cuts error rates by over 50% relative to previous benchmarks, but the experiments only show MSE results; which specific result supports this claim is not identified.
- Additional specific weaknesses: The reference to Rivera et al. 2024 (line 296) does not specify which method was used (they employ multiple methods). "Coder" should be "annotator" (line 270). The exact gap in performance (line 314) and the exact value for "around 0.7" (line 273) should be stated. The best-performing model should be explicitly named (line 303). The external certainty calculation method is unclear regarding whether multiple generations are considered. It is unexplained how MSE can exceed 1 when values are between 0–1 (Figure 2). Statements in line 414 lack provenance. "Silicon sampling" (line 469) is not a standard term and needs a citation.
- One reviewer (score 6) acknowledges the strengths but still notes limited experiments (single certainty estimation method) leading to overstatements, and writing that needs more detail and examples for clarity.
