## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses catastrophic forgetting in Multimodal Continual Instruction Tuning (MCIT). Existing methods like EMA use fixed weights, but LLaCA dynamically adjusts EMA weights using Taylor expansion and Lagrange multiplier, achieving a better plasticity-stability balance. Experimental results on LLaVA-1.5 show significant improvements in anti-forgetting (forgetting reduced from 22.67 to 2.68) and continual tuning performance (average accuracy from 41.31 to 61.89) with modest extra computational cost.

### Strengths
- The paper is generally well-written and structured, with detailed mathematical formulations and theoretical derivations (R1, R3).
- The model can be easily applied across a wide range of fine-tuning methods and achieves parameter-efficiency during training, overcoming previous MCIT methods that require a large number of trainable parameters (R1, R2).
- LLaCA exhibits good performance on various QA datasets (ScienceQA, TextVQA, GQA, VizWiz, VQAv2, OCRVQA) with the lowest forgetting score under both instruction types, and significantly reduces catastrophic forgetting while improving average accuracy compared to baseline (R1, R2, R3).
- Compared to basic LoRA fine-tuning, LLaCA adds only slight extra computational cost (R1, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of modality-specific adaptation for MCIT** (R1, R3): The method is more aligned with general continual learning and does not leverage modality-specific capabilities (e.g., distinct features for the feature encoder and LLM, interaction between modalities, managing diverse instruction types). It remains unclear whether the dynamic balance weight β_t should be calculated to balance across different modalities.
- **Limited evaluation task coverage** (R2, R3): Only QA tasks are used; tasks like Visual Entailment (VE), classic classification (CIFAR100, MNIST), and the Referring Expression Comprehension (RefCoCo) and Image Classification (ImageNet) datasets from the CoIN benchmark are omitted without explanation. Including them would provide a more complete evaluation. The authors should reproduce the experiment themselves rather than copying full fine-tuning results from CoIN (R3).
- **Insufficient comparison with state-of-the-art methods** (R1, R3): The paper does not compare with SOTA methods for mitigating catastrophic forgetting in MLLMs (e.g., [3] cited in R1). Most baselines used are designed for LLMs. The term “baseline” is used inconsistently (R3); stronger baselines like PGP should be compared instead of only LoRA. (R1 further notes that catastrophic forgetting in LLMs is well-studied, questioning the novelty of the problem.)
- **Single backbone and PEFT method** (R2): All experiments are on LLaVA-1.5 only. More backbone models (Mini-GPT4, OpenFlamingo) should be tested, and other PEFT methods (Prompt-tuning, Adapter) should be explored, with discussion of their inherent differences.
- **Missing justification for L1-Norm approximation** (R1): The paper introduces the L1-Norm to approximate β_t and claims it demonstrates exceptional performance, but provides no explanation or empirical results to support this claim.
- **Incomplete static-weight comparison** (R1): The paper only compares with a stable EMA weight of 0.99. Additional results with other static weights or weight decay should be provided for a more comprehensive evaluation.
- **Missing zero-shot/unseen task results** (R3): The paper discusses zero-shot performance in Section 5.3 but does not provide detailed results for unseen tasks. Including these results would clarify how training on certain tasks affects zero-shot performance and explain improvements over the baseline and original LLaVA-v1.5.
- **Unclear parameter preservation vs. loss preservation** (R1): In Eq(3), the second equation preserves parameters θ_t^* after training on previous datasets. It would be more appropriate to maintain consistent loss values rather than preserving the parameters themselves when tuning on a new dataset.
- **Implementation and clarity issues** (R3):
  - Subsection “Multimodal Continual Instruction Tuning Definition” in Section 2 focuses more on problem formulation than related work; moving it could improve clarity.
  - The “checkpoints” mentioned in Figure 3 and Algorithm 1 are unclear; corresponding loading operations should be clarified.
  - The framework caption in Figure 3 needs more detailed explanations of each module and process.
  - Implementation inconsistency: Appendix A.8 mentions 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs, while Table 3 reports 8 A6000 GPUs.
  - Many references are arXiv preprints; some have formal publications that should be cited instead.
  - Equation numbering in the appendix is inconsistent (e.g., Eqs. (20)-(21) and (23)-(25)).
- **Lack of optimization analysis** (R2): Discussion on Attention Activation Pattern Analysis and Loss Landscape could be beneficial to explain why LLaCA holds superior performance compared to baselines.
