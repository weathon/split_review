## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SimDiffPDE, a plain diffusion model with Transformers for solving PDEs, reformulating the task as image-to-image translation. It introduces multi-scale noise and a test-time ensemble method. Results show strong performance across six benchmarks (Darcy Flow, Airfoil, Pipe, Plasticity, Elasticity, Navier-Stokes) with average improvements of ~22%. Two reviewers (scores 5,5) are more positive about the empirical results and simplicity; two reviewers (scores 3,3) raise significant concerns about theoretical foundations, novelty, and evaluation rigor.

### Strengths
- **Strong empirical results across diverse benchmarks**: SimDiffPDE achieves consistent top results on multiple datasets and grid types, with an average relative improvement of 22.0% (R1). It beats many architectures over a diverse range of tasks and data formats (structured meshes, regular grids, point clouds) (R4). For the challenging Navier-Stokes equations, improvement is +51.4% (abstract).
- **Multi-scale noise strategy**: The multi-scale noise is reasonable because independent pixel/mesh noise can phase out low-frequency components (R2). This enhances the capabilities of diffusion models in PDE solving (R1).
- **Simple overall pipeline**: The method is easy to read and simple in design (R3, R4).
- **Early example of diffusion for PDEs**: While not the first, it is one of the earliest applications, with a Transformer architecture (R4).
- **Flexibility and scalability claims**: The paper addresses simplicity, feasibility, and flexibility in model size and training paradigm (R4). The results on three geometry types show flexibility (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Multi-scale noise – theoretical and practical issues**:
  - The multi-scale noise is a linear combination of interpolations across scales; it does not follow a standard Gaussian distribution. This raises a mismatch: if sampling starts with standard Gaussian noise, the final forward noise distribution does not match the initial sampling distribution; there may be no closed-form \(q(x_t|x_0)\) (R1). Also, after T steps, can one certify that multi-scale noise yields standard Gaussian \(\mathcal{N}(0,I)\)? (R1, R2).
  - Remarks 3.3.1 and 3.3.2 and Fig. 4 are unconvincing: how can noise not “destroy” data when added via Eq. 1? After 100 steps of multi-scale noise, columns 2 and 3 still show no visible Gaussian noise (R4).
  - Multi-scale feature handling is already intrinsic to U-Net-based diffusion; the novelty of this specific multi-scale noise approach may be limited to Transformer architectures (R4).
- **Test-time ensemble – justification and fairness**:
  - The test-time ensemble assumes predictions converge under scale and shift parameters; this needs more theoretical explanation and justification (R1). The iterative optimization may be time-consuming; does it offer significant advantage over simple averaging? Comparison needed (R1). Reviewers question if “additional calculations after the output” make the comparison fair to deterministic alternatives (R2, R3).
  - No comparison of inference efficiency (time, parameter counts) with baselines; the ensemble overhead likely makes inference much larger than deterministic models (R1, R3).
- **Motivation and novelty**:
  - The method is a direct application of common diffusion models; aside from multi-scale noise, there are no technical contributions (R3). The novelty is limited: first Transformer-based diffusion for PDEs, but overall pipeline is an application of existing diffusion architectures (R4). Two reviewers (score 3) find this insufficient.
  - Good results may arise because diffusion models generally work well, not from an original approach to PDEs (R4).
  - No theoretical explanation why diffusion (a stochastic process) is needed for deterministic PDE mappings (R2, R3). A direct comparison to a plain Transformer with the same architecture but without noise is needed to verify that fitting deterministic PDEs is challenging enough to justify a diffusion model (R3).
- **Missing contextualization**: The paper fails to cite and discuss relevant diffusion-based PDE solvers, especially DiffusionPDE (Huang et al., 2024) (R4).
- **Evaluation and comparisons**:
  - **Missing baselines**: On Navier-Stokes, comparisons are needed with DeepONet, PeRCNN, PDERefiner, LI, and TSM (R1). Already included baselines may be insufficient.
  - **Inference efficiency**: No comparison of training time, inference time, and model parameter counts for different baselines (R1, R3). Scalability with input size and computational complexity are not analyzed (R4).
  - **Physical metrics**: For Navier-Stokes, statistical metrics alone are insufficient; PDF and energy spectrum curves should be used (R1).
  - **Additional NS tests**: Does the model only apply to simple datasets (e.g., Li et al.)? Long-term prediction, error propagation plots, correlation curves, and adaptation to changes in Reynolds number and external forces are needed (R1).
  - **3D applicability**: Can the model handle 3D problems? (R1)
  - **Traditional solvers**: No comparison with traditional numerical solvers at different resolutions; scalability with grid resolution is unclear (R1).
- **Flexibility and data handling**:
  - **Resolution**: The paper does not explain how the model handles different resolutions—single model trained on one resolution and tested on others? Separate models? What architectural adaptations? (R3)
  - **Image-to-image claim vs. point cloud data**: The Elasticity benchmark uses point cloud data; the paper provides no explanation of implementation details. If inputs are reorganized into regular grids, then the claim of handling irregular/unstructured data is contradicted (R3).
  - **Problem setting**: Definitions of variables are too generic. What are \(x\), \(y\), \(x_g\), \(x_u\) in practice? Geometric inputs and observed quantities are unclear (R4). The mesh notation (1D) contradicts the 2D problem in Fig. 2 (R4).
- **Writing and presentation**:
  - The writing style is unrefined; many issues: “Guassian” used 27 times instead of “Gaussian”; grammar errors (“The two designed…improves”); naming inconsistency (SimDiffPDE-X vs X-SimDiffPDE); typo in Eq. 3 (subscript of \(y\)) (R4, R2).
  - Fig. 8 (interesting) should be moved to the main body and labels added (R4).
