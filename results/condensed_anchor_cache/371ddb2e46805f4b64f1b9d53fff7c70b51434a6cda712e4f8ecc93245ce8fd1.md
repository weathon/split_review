## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a stagewise pretraining framework, progressive subnetwork training, instantiated as **Random Part Training (RaPTr)**. RaPTr trains random subnetworks (depth-wise, width-wise) and progressively increases subnetwork size until the full network. It claims to be competitive or better than stacking methods (Progressive Stacking, Gradual Stacking) and layer dropping, providing up to 33% FLOPs savings on BERT and UL2 and better downstream performance on UL2 (e.g., 1–5% on Tydi QA, SQuADv2, SuperGLUE). Theoretical analysis attempts to justify stability of subnetwork training in residual networks. The paper is clearly written by most reviewers, but one reviewer (score 5) found presentation issues, inconsistent improvements, and novelty concerns.

### Strengths
- Simple and effective stagewise training paradigm, easy to implement on top of standard training. (R1,R2,R3,R4)
- Strong empirical gains over baseline and stacking methods, especially in short training regimes (e.g., up to 33% FLOPs savings for BERT and UL2). (R1,R2)
- Downstream improvements on UL2 (Tydi QA, SQuADv2) over stacking and baseline by 1–5%. (R1,R2,R4)
- Novel rescaling of outputs and fixing first/last layers help training stability. (R4)
- Theoretical analysis provides justification for stability of subnetwork training in residual networks and loss stability across stage transitions. (R2,R3,R4)
- Detailed ablations on fixed layers and scale. (R2)
- Demonstrates that layer dropout as regularization can improve stability and downstream performance. (R1)
- Well-written and easy to follow (R2,R3); though one reviewer (R4) had clarity concerns.

### Weaknesses
- **Inconsistent improvement across tasks and settings.** Table 1 and 2 show very limited improvement over stacking for the same FLOPs (R4). Table 3 improvements on Tydi QA and SQuADv2 are not mirrored on TriviaQA and SuperGLUE (marginal or non-existent); the alternative schedule in Table 3 is not detailed, making it impossible to compare RaPTr with stacking trained under the same schedule. Without this schedule, RaPTr’s advantages appear small (R4). R2 also notes varied performance, e.g., multilingual QA hurt when adding 30k steps. (R2,R4)
- **Baseline quality and setup unclear.** R1 questions how competitive UL2 baselines are; hyperparameter references needed. R3 asks for training setup of baselines and RaPTr in Table 2. R4 notes “Layerdrop” in Table 2 is not defined (could be confused with existing method). (R1,R3,R4)
- **Lack of analysis on compute-efficient frontier and scaling.** R1: model sweep needed to quantify improvement; no frontier analysis. R2: gains diminish in long training; scalability with model sizes and fit to LM unknown. (R1,R2)
- **Real wall-clock time not provided**, may disagree with FLOPs savings. (R2)
- **Theoretical results limited.** R2: analysis is in simplified linear residual network setting. R4: Section 4.2 is unclear—normalization dependent on initialization (not discussed); what to conclude from Lemma 4.3 unclear; link to experiments unclear; Fig 2.b goodness of fit low; claims like “worst-case bound O(L^{-0.88})” or “gap scales O(L^{-c})” not explained. Notation issues: schedule notation never used; definition of I reversed; I_s undefined. (R2,R4)
- **Novelty concerns.** R3 and R4 note the idea is not novel—very close to Zhang et al. 2019 (layer dropping); main difference is dropping at start of training and different rescaling. (R3,R4)
- **Many hyper-parameters** (number of stages, steps per stage, schedule of p). R3: tuning difficult in pretraining. R4: schedule selection unclear; why only p varies and stages differ between RaPTr and stacking in Table 2? (R3,R4)
- **Presentation issues** noted by R4: Figure 1 could be a table; Figure 2 never referred; Figure 3 hard to read and poorly explained; multiple typos and unclear expressions (“lower better loss”, “Equiflop” not defined, “O(L ≥ 0.88)” meaningless, reversed names in intro). (R4)
- **Questions overlapping with criticisms** (missing experiments/analysis): R2 asks about wall-clock time and varied performance in Table 3. R3 asks about hyper-parameter determination and effect on learning rate schedule. R4 asks whether first k or last k layers should be fixed, and why BERT experiments are called “short horizon”. (R2,R3,R4)
