## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates whether using generated data from generative models in iterative self-consuming loops can amplify bias across generations in image classification. It proposes a simulation environment where generative and classification models are trained synergistically over multiple generations. Extensive experiments are conducted on Colorized MNIST, CIFAR-20/100, CIFAR-100, and Hard ImageNet with models including LeNet, ResNet50, VGG-19, and pre-trained variants. Bias is examined across multiple subgroups and fairness metrics (e.g., Equal Opportunity, Disparate Impact, Maximal Disparity). The paper reports mixed trends in performance and bias across datasets, model capacities, and generative steps, and provides a high-level conjecture that bias dynamics depend on multiple factors such as data quality and model initialization.

### Strengths
- **Timely and important problem:** The paper studies a highly relevant and under-explored question—how the growing use of synthetic data in retraining loops may affect model fairness. This is crucial as generated content becomes prevalent online (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Realistic simulation and broad experimentation:** The iterative self-consuming pipeline mirrors practical deployment scenarios. Experiments cover datasets of varying complexity (easy to hard), multiple classification models (LeNet, ResNet50, VGG-19, pre-trained), both unbiased and biased initializations, and a range of fairness metrics. This enhances the generalizability of the empirical framework (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Notable observations with experimental support:** Several findings stand out: (i) low correlation between dataset subgroup bias and resulting model bias (Section 4.2); (ii) higher susceptibility of pre-trained models to integration bias; (iii) bias dynamics are influenced by factors such as dataset complexity, model capacity, and data quality across generations. These are supported by the extensive experiments (Reviewers 1, 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient analysis of mixed and contradictory results:** The paper presents many inconsistent trends without adequate explanation. For example: (a) ResNet50 baseline accuracy on CIFAR-20/100 (~50%) is much lower than on CIFAR-100 (~80%), despite having fewer classes; (b) in Fig. 6(a), ResNet50 and LeNet show opposite trends in Equal Opportunity and Disparate Impact while VGG-19 remains unchanged; (c) comparing Figs. 5(a) and 6(a), ResNet50 on CIFAR-20/100 versus CIFAR-100 shows completely different trends for Equal Opportunity, Disparate Impact, and Maximal Disparity. Similar unexplained discrepancies appear in other sections. The discussion (Section 5) remains high-level and does not connect root causes to these specific results, e.g., why pre-trained models are more susceptible, or why certain datasets yield more consistent behavior. The final conclusion merely conjectures multiple influencing factors without clear experimental evidence or theoretical grounding (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Limited scale of datasets and models:** Experiments are confined to relatively small datasets (Colorized MNIST, CIFAR-20/100, CIFAR-100, Hard ImageNet) and small models (LeNet, ResNet50, VGG-19). The largest model is ResNet50, which is modest compared to modern architectures like ViT. This raises serious concerns about whether the observations hold in realistic settings with large-scale data, many classes (e.g., ImageNet-1K), and state-of-the-art models. No experiments with transfer learning or foundational models are included, even though such settings are dominant in practice and would better validate the paper’s motivation (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Missing experiments on generative model choice:** The study uses only one type of generative model (unspecified family). Different generative models (e.g., GANs, diffusion models) produce samples with varying quality and bias properties, which likely affect downstream results. Ablations over generative model families are absent (Reviewer 2).
- **Critical methodological details omitted:** Several aspects hinder reproducibility and interpretation: (i) the human study and expert-guided filtering in Section 3.2 are not described—criteria for filtering are undefined, and potential biases from subjective filtering are not discussed; (ii) the fraction of synthetic data added per generation (r% or p) is not reported for any dataset; (iii) the CLIP filtering procedure (e.g., whether similarity between label text and image is used) is not specified; (iv) the construction of subgroups (described only as “share similar semantics”) is too vague; (v) generated images are not visualized to illustrate quality degradation or distribution shifts across generations (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Unexplained FID trends:** Table 1 shows inconsistent FID evolution: Color-MNIST and CIFAR-20/100 fluctuate, while Hard ImageNet jumps from 50.9 to 186.4 in the second generation. The paper does not analyze whether this indicates distribution drift (generative model losing fidelity) or increased diversity, nor does it discuss how these data quality changes affect the observed bias and performance metrics (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Missing ablations and comparisons:** Key ablation studies are absent: (i) impact of different loss functions on bias dynamics; (ii) experiments with tasks having a larger number of classes; (iii) analysis of different amounts of synthetic data (ratio p) to understand sensitivity. These are needed to strengthen causal claims (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Generalizability not addressed:** Observations such as “dataset bias does not amplify model bias” (Section 4.2) are not tested on other datasets or tasks. The paper does not discuss how conclusions might generalize to other modalities (e.g., NLP, object detection) or different subgroup definitions (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Missing citations:** Several original works in the introduction and later sections are not cited (Reviewer 4).
- **Weak overall contribution:** The conclusions drawn (e.g., bias depends on multiple factors) are not significant; the paper lacks novel theoretical insights or actionable guidelines for practitioners. The significance of the observed trends is unclear (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Conclusion and future work underdeveloped:** The final section does not tie back key findings to the main problem or suggest concrete next steps; it merely hints at extensions (Reviewer 4).
