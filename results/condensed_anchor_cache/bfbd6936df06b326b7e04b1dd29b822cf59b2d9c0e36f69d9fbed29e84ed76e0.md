## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Video Language Planning (VLP), a framework that combines vision-language models (VLM) as policies and value functions with text-to-video models as dynamics models to plan long-horizon robot manipulation tasks. A tree search optimizes a sequence of language subgoals and corresponding video clips. The approach is evaluated on simulated and real-robot platforms (3 hardware setups) against 4 baseline methods, with claims of improved success rates. Reviewers are split: two rate 8 (positive), two rate 6 (more critical). All agree the paper is well-written, but concerns center on task complexity, missing baselines, computational cost, and reproducibility.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-structured and easy to follow, with clear contributions and grounding in related work (R1, R2, R4).
- Strong performance on simulated and real-robot long-horizon manipulation tasks, including multi-object rearrangement and bi-arm dexterous manipulation, demonstrated with substantial qualitative and quantitative results (R1, R2, R3).
- VLP leverages Internet-scale pretrained VLMs and text-to-video models, avoiding the need for expensive robot data collection and enabling zero-shot generalization to novel lighting, objects, and tasks (R2, R3).
- Real-world evaluation across three hardware platforms, despite task simplicity by some reviewers’ standards (R1, R4).
- Experimental results on the relationship between planning budget and execution accuracy provide valuable insights into algorithm efficiency (R2, R3).
- Discussion of limitations and failure cases (e.g., video model failures in appendix) is insightful and adds transparency (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Task complexity and long-horizon assessment**: Several reviewers find the chosen tasks insufficiently challenging to demonstrate long-horizon capabilities. Reviewer 1 notes that in the block-grouping task there is no dependency between subgoal successes/failures, making it solvable without retaining information over long horizons. Reviewer 4 argues the tasks are too simple and doubts the convincing value of the video dynamics model over cheaper alternatives (e.g., text-based LLM dynamics like SayCan or procedural planning). A more challenging suite (e.g., procedural planning) would better showcase the method’s edge (R4).
- **Missing baselines and ablations**: Reviewer 4 finds the baselines insufficient to isolate the contribution of the video dynamics model. Suggested baselines include: a text-only LLM dynamics model (e.g., SayCan or RAP’s MCTS+LLM), Visual Language Planner (finetuned LLM), VIMA (multimodal planner without dynamics), and HiP (closest approach, not compared). Reviewer 4 also requests an ablation without the video dynamics model (Table 1) to assess its added value. Reviewer 3 asks for experiments on incomplete videos without language labels, which the introduction highlights as a capability but is not demonstrated.
- **Computational cost and runtime**: All reviewers highlight that video model inference is expensive. Reviewer 2 notes no runtime measurements are provided (e.g., in Table 3), limiting assessment of real-time applicability. Reviewer 3 and 4 stress that scalability, memory usage, and inference time are absent from the main paper (only mentioned in appendix), making practical deployment unclear. Reviewer 2 suggests optimizing only the text sequence via tree search and generating video per-clip as a more efficient alternative.
- **Generalization to other platforms and domains**: Reviewer 3 questions whether VLP can adapt to more diverse or complex robotic systems (e.g., different kinematics, sensor modalities) beyond the three relatively simple or self-designed platforms tested. The lack of experiments on widely adopted platforms reduces the paper’s impact.
- **Limitations of video prediction models**: Reviewer 1 asks whether generated out-of-manifold frames during long-horizon prediction pose a limitation. Reviewer 3 connects this to overestimation of the value function, and asks if out-of-distribution images/goals compound the issue (e.g., could offline RL methods like CQL help?). Reviewer 2 asks if the replanning rate can be made dynamic to detect divergence from the video plan.
- **Specific methodological questions** (considered as weakness because they point to missing analysis or clarity):
  - PaLM-E value function: how is it trained (regression vs. language modeling)? If the latter, is the mode used for tree search? (R2)
  - Multi-modal policies: In tasks with multiple valid trajectories (e.g., “make a horizontal line”), can PaLM-E be fine-tuned to accommodate multi-modality? (R3)
  - Task horizon limit: The paper gives 1500 timesteps for all tasks—does VLP handle longer horizons (e.g., moving an object 10m)? (R3)
  - Video frequency: how many seconds do 16 frames correspond to? (R2, R4)
  - Domain definition: Are tabletop manipulation in simulation and real world considered the same domain? (R4)
  - Open-sourcing: Reviewer 4 requests clarification on code/model release plans, as lack thereof reduces reproducibility and community value.
