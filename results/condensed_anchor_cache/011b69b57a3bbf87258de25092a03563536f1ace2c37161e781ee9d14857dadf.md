## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces LucidFusion, a feed-forward framework for generating 3D Gaussians from arbitrary, unposed multi-view images. It uses a Relative Coordinate Map (RCM) to achieve cross-view geometric alignment without explicit camera pose, and includes a refinement step. The method fine-tunes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model to predict point coordinate maps and Gaussian parameters. While the approach is interesting and shows promise, reviewers identify significant issues with experimental design, fairness of baselines, limited generality to truly arbitrary poses, lack of key evaluations, and incremental technical novelty. One reviewer (Rating 5) finds the paper well-written and the RCM extension valuable, but the other three (Rating 3) are critical of the contributions and evaluation.

### Strengths

- The RCM eliminates the need for explicit input camera poses, enabling pixel-level cross-view geometric alignment and improving flexibility over pose-dependent LRMs (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Leveraging a pre-trained 2D diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) for RCM prediction is an interesting approach that benefits generalization (R2, R4).
- The method outperforms prior works like LGM, iFusion, and CRM (R1). Specifically, it outperforms iFusion and includes ablation studies (R2).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1, R3).
- The method can be extended to 8 views (R1).
- Thorough ablation studies were conducted (R4).
- Experimental results demonstrate effectiveness (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Missing related works and incremental novelty**: The RCM is a pixel-level extension of relative pose; prior works on relative pose (RelPose, RelPose++, Few-view object reconstruction, Cameras as Rays) and on predicting point maps relative to a main view (Dust3r, SpaRP) should be discussed and differentiated (R1, R2). SpaRP is claimed as concurrent but was submitted to ECCV 2024, weakening that assertion (R2). The contributions are largely incremental: RCM alignment and VAE decoding closely parallel Dust3r and LGM (R3); predicting 3D coordinate maps with a diffusion model is not novel (similar to CRM and SpaRP) and simply adding a Gaussian prediction head is incremental (R4).
- **Inappropriate/unfair baselines and incomplete comparisons**: Many baselines (LGM, CRM, InstantMesh) are trained on fixed structured views, while LucidFusion is trained on random viewpoints, making the comparison unfair (R1). The method should be compared with pose-free sparse-view reconstruction methods like LEAP, PF-LRM, UpFusion (R1). Results for InstantMesh and LGM are based on poses from iFusion, which may be of bad quality; the authors should include results using ground-truth poses or pose predictions from LucidFusion via PnP (R4). Only NVS metrics (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS) are reported, with no 3D metrics (Chamfer Distance); this is unfair because the proposed method includes an additional refinement with rendering losses, and lightweight optimization should also be applied to baselines (R2). Experimental comparisons are limited with few ablations (R3). The test set is only 100 objects from GSO and ABO, too small to be convincing (R4).
- **Method does not truly handle arbitrary random poses**: Training data is limited to fixed elevation angles {-20°, 0°, 20°} and evaluation uses the same fixed elevations, contradicting the claim of arbitrary poses (R1, R2). The method assumes fixed FoV, fixed up direction, and look-at-center, which may not generalize to real-world images (R2). It should be evaluated on real-world datasets like OmniObject3D and MVImageNet (R2). Robustness to elevation is questionable: the model is trained on fixed elevations and may fail on arbitrary elevations (R1). The evaluation setting should use random elevations between -20° and 20° (R1).
- **Missing evaluation of RCM quality**: The paper should report 3D metrics for the predicted RCM (Chamfer Distance, L1/MSE distance to GT RCM, correspondence accuracy) (R1, R4). Comparisons with dense correspondence methods like DKM and RoMa are needed (R1). A suggested experiment: use ground-truth RCM to compare stability versus rigid pose and robustness to noise (R1). Robustness to different diffusion random seeds should be discussed and tested (R4).
- **Presentation and clarity issues**: Figure 2 and Figure 3 are inconsistent: "Stage 2" in Figure 2 refers to training a network for other Gaussian parameters, while Figure 3 labels Stage 2 as refinement optimization (R2). It is unclear which submodules of Stable Diffusion (VAE encoder, UNet, VAE decoder) are used for predicting RCM vs Gaussian parameters, which are frozen vs fine-tuned, and why two separate training stages are needed (R2). The term "DPT" in Table 4 is not defined (R4). Line 157 wrongly claims PF-LRM requires optimization (R1).
- **Performance concerns**: Supplemental videos show significant shape distortions (e.g., "bell") and multi-view inconsistency (e.g., "bear", "mario") even for simple objects, indicating limited practicality (R4).
- **Other limitations**: The method assumes input views cover all object regions; if not, it may struggle (R2). The rationale for using a VAE decoder to predict Gaussian parameters over LGM’s approach is unclear (R3). The source and reliability of depth D in training data are not explained (R3).
