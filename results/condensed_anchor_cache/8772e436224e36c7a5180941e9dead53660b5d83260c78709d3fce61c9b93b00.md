## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes FedLoRU, a low-rank update framework for federated learning that enforces low-rank client-side updates and accumulates them into a higher-rank model. It also presents variants for statistical and model heterogeneity (pFedLoRU, mFedLoRU). The authors provide theoretical analysis of Hessian rank structure and experimental results on computer vision and language model fine-tuning.

### Strengths

- The paper investigates the rank properties of client and server losses, analytically showing that under stochastic sampling, the rank of the Hessian increases with smaller sample sizes. Reviewers 2 and 4 consider this analysis rigorous and well-motivated; Reviewer 4 notes it establishes interesting asymptotic results within a mathematically general framework. Reviewer 1, however, finds the theoretical contribution limited, arguing it only reveals the high-rank nature of small datasets and does not directly support the algorithm’s design.

- Empirical results demonstrate evidence of the higher rank structure of client losses and show that restricting the rank of local updates aids in implicit regularization (Reviewer 2). Reviewers 1 and 4 note that FedLoRU achieves performance comparable or superior to other methods while reducing communication overhead.

- The presentation is well-organized; the motivation and methodology are clear (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty is limited.** Multiple reviewers (1, 2, 3, 4) note that FedLoRU is essentially a federated extension of existing low-rank accumulation methods such as ReLoRA. Reviewer 3 further states that the non-personalized version is a federated version of ReLoRA that aggregates low-rank modules for higher-rank training. Reviewer 4 notes the extension to the federated setting appears straightforward and lacks deep discussion of specific FL challenges and benefits. Reviewer 1 comments that the idea of using low-rank updates in FL has been explored before.

- **Lack of theoretical analysis for the algorithm itself.** Reviewers 1, 3, and 4 all note the absence of convergence guarantees. Reviewer 4 specifically asks for an analysis of how the low-rank constraint affects convergence rate and whether it introduces bias. Reviewer 3 calls the algorithm fully heuristic, with no analysis of whether the personalized variant (pFedLoRU) converges to a meaningful solution, and questions whether the introduced L and U modules cancel out A and B. Reviewer 1 notes that algorithmic convergence is not established.

- **The connection to implicit regularization is insufficiently explained.** Reviewer 4 points out that the title mentions a connection to implicit regularization, but FedLoRU explicitly enforces a low-rank subspace constraint, which is an explicit regularization; the paper does not provide a clear explanation of how this connects to the implicit regularization phenomenon (e.g., SGD bias). Reviewers 1 and 3 also question whether the theoretical insight (high-rank Hessian on clients) logically leads to constraining updates to low rank.

- **Experiment setup and results have issues.** Reviewer 1 finds Figure 2(a) hard to interpret (both 50 and 500 samples show high stable rank at the 15th epoch, which is counterintuitive) and suggests repeating experiments multiple times. Figure 2(b) is unconvincing without a detailed description linking it to Theorem 3.2. Reviewer 2 notes that the least client participation ratio is set at 0.5, while realistic settings often involve lower ratios. Reviewer 3 argues that the algorithm is still full-parameter training because it initializes W every τ steps, making comparison to LoRA unfair; the algorithm should also be compared to conventional FL algorithms, not just LoRA.

- **Flexibility and efficiency concerns.** Reviewer 3 emphasizes that FedLoRU sacrifices the flexibility of LoRA: LoRA stores separate adapters (A, B) and can serve multiple tasks in parallel, while FedLoRU needs to store full model parameters. Also, the step of solving argmin_{A,B} f is computation-heavy, even approximate, making the method unsuitable for LLM fine-tuning.

- **Insufficient baselines.** Reviewer 2 recommends adding more baselines that apply low-rank factorized updates, such as FedPara (Hyeon-Woo et al., ICLR 2022).

- **Lack of justification for stable rank metric.** Reviewer 1 notes the claim that stable rank "serves as a continuous proxy for rank and is robust" is made without sufficient references or supporting literature; more discussion on its adaptation from related fields and appropriateness for FL is needed.

- **Missing large-scale evaluation.** Reviewers 3 and 4 note that most experiments are on small computer vision datasets; more evaluations on large language model fine-tuning are needed to demonstrate practical applicability and scalability.

- **Theoretical connection between loss Hessian rank and weight matrix rank is unclear.** Reviewer 4 asks how the rank analysis of loss Hessians relates to low-rank updates on weight matrices, which the algorithm directly modifies.

- **Theorems are presented in long paragraphs without summaries** (Reviewer 2).

### Additional Reviewer Questions (overlapping with above or requiring answer)

- How does the low-rank constraint affect the optimization bias? (Reviewer 4)
- Will the personalized variant converge to a solution where L and U fully cancel out A and B? (Reviewer 3)
- What is the conceptual justification for calling the method “implicit regularization” when the rank constraint is explicit? (Reviewer 4)
- Does the algorithm accurately solve argmin_{A,B} f? The step is computation-heavy. (Reviewer 3)

### Reviewer Disagreement

Reviewers 1 and 3 (scores 3,3) are more critical of the theory, novelty, and experimental justification, while Reviewers 2 and 4 (scores 5,5) are more positive about the motivation, theoretical analysis, and empirical results. The positive reviews emphasize the analytical rigor and empirical evidence for rank structure, whereas negative reviews question whether the theory supports the algorithm and note limited novelty and missing convergence guarantees.
