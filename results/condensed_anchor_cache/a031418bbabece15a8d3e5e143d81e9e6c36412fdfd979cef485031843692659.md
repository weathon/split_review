## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper analyzes the incremental learning behavior of gradient descent (GD) for symmetric matrix factorization, focusing on large initialization regimes. Using a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) argument, the authors derive theoretical results showing stage-wise linear convergence and an implicit incremental learning phenomenon. The framework extends prior rank‑1 analyses to general rank, with a focus on the transition between learning successive components.

### Strengths
- **Novel theoretical framework and incremental learning analysis** – The SNR-based approach and inductive arguments are novel; they provide a first theoretical explanation of stage-wise dynamics (fast decrease, crawl, fast decrease) under large initialization. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Clear motivation and presentation of key ideas** – Figure 1 and the first paragraph of Section 4.2 effectively illustrate the problem; the paper is well written and follows explanations with helpful proof sketches. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Relevance to understanding implicit bias in deeper models** – The studied simple setting is a tractable proxy for two‑layer linear networks, and the results are potentially important for the field of implicit bias in matrix factorization, contrasting with existing small‑initialization results. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Honest about limitations** – The paper explicitly states when an assumption (e.g., Assumption 8) is needed and provides justification, which is appreciated. (Reviewer 1)
- **Comprehensive literature review** – The related work section covers relevant prior results. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental validation** – Only one experiment on rank‑2 matrix factorization is provided (Figure 1). The paper lacks experiments with varying dimension \(d\) and rank \(r\) (e.g., rank‑10) to demonstrate the claimed phenomena more broadly. (Reviewer 3)
- **Ambiguity about “large initialization”** – The proof for the main result requires a “reasonably small” \(\alpha\) such that \(\sigma^2_1(K_r)\le\lambda_r-3\Delta/4\), which depends on \(r\). When \(r\) scales with \(d\), this effectively reduces to a small initialization. The text sometimes states the theorem does not require \(X_0\in S\) yet Lemma 5 assumes \(X_0\in S\), creating contradictory statements. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **No explicit convergence theorem for the general setting** – The paper does not clearly state a main theorem or corollary that with large random initialization GD converges at a certain rate under explicit parameter choices. The notation is dense and the results are presented in an isolated manner (e.g., the rank‑2 case is not stated as an independent theorem). (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Confusing connection to implicit bias** – The introduction claims the focus is on implicit bias, but the analysis of dynamics (Figure 1) does not directly relate to the limit point that GD converges to, which is the usual meaning of implicit bias. (Reviewer 1)
- **Unclear definitions and missing justifications**
  - “Incremental learning” is not clearly defined; it could be confused with streaming or online learning. (Reviewers 1, 3)
  - The assumption that \(\Sigma\) is diagonal is introduced without explanation; it should be justified via orthogonal equivariance of GD. (Reviewers 1, 3)
  - The “benign set” \(R\) in Section 3.1 changes for higher ranks; it is unclear how the proof ensures GD stays in such a set. (Reviewer 4)
  - Assumption 8 is not well explained; its relation to the arguments in Section 4.2.3 is unclear. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing comparisons and references**
  - No clear comparison with state‑of‑the‑art results for the same matrix factorization setting. (Reviewer 2)
  - The seminal work by Gunasekar et al. (2017) on implicit regularization in matrix factorization (conjecturing global convergence to minimal nuclear norm with small initialization) is omitted. (Reviewer 3)
- **Presentation issues**
  - Section 3 organization: the title “challenges in examine general rank solutions” does not match the content (3.1 is local convergence, 3.2–3.3 discuss large initialization). (Reviewer 4)
  - The SNR decomposition appears similar to previous works (e.g., Stöger & Soltanolkotabi); the differences should be highlighted more. (Reviewer 4)
  - Minor: “period” (p.7) should be “time”; “ascend to infinity” → “diverge”; “takes infinity at 0” and “the rest elements” are typos; “top three rows” in Figure 1 legend is ambiguous; Section 3.3 repeats earlier text. (Reviewers 1, 3)
  - The claim that symmetric MF mirrors a two‑layer linear network is inaccurate: symmetric MF is closer to quadratic regression. (Reviewer 1)
- **Open questions requiring clarification**
  - Does the proof require the initialization to be aligned with each component (as in prior work, e.g., Stöger & Soltanolkotabi)? (Reviewer 4)
  - How does the definition of \(\sigma_1(u_{k,t})\) reduce to the norm of \(u_{k,t}\)? (Reviewer 3)
  - For non‑diagonal \(\Sigma\), what is the key difficulty in extending the analysis? (Reviewer 1)
