## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces DeCo (Dynamic Correction Decoding), a lightweight, model-agnostic decoding method to mitigate hallucinations in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The authors empirically demonstrate that MLLMs can recognize visual objects in earlier transformer layers but this recognition is suppressed in deeper layers by strong language model priors, leading to hallucinations. DeCo addresses this by adaptively selecting preceding layers and proportionally integrating their logits into the final layer. Experiments on CHAIR, POPE, MME, and GPT-4o-assisted evaluation across models like InstructBLIP, MiniGPT-4, LLaVA-1.5, and Qwen-VL show significant hallucination reduction with manageable latency increase, outperforming existing methods such as VCD and OPERA.

### Strengths
- The paper provides a novel insight and empirical analysis showing that MLLMs correctly recognize objects in earlier layers but the visual information is suppressed by language priors in later layers, offering a new layer-wise perspective on the hallucination mechanism.
- Figures illustrating token probability trends across layers (e.g., hallucinated vs. non-hallucinated tokens) make the analysis accessible and informative.
- The proposed DeCo method is lightweight, efficient, and model-agnostic, achieving similar or better hallucination suppression than VCD and OPERA with lower latency overhead, enhancing practical applicability.
- Evaluation across diverse benchmarks (CHAIR, POPE, MME) and multiple models (InstructBLIP, MiniGPT-4, LLaVA-1.5, Qwen-VL) provides a well-rounded assessment of effectiveness.
- Probing experiments across transformer layers effectively reveal how hallucinations emerge in later layers, helping understand MLLM behavior better.
- The main technical pipeline is clearly described and easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- The methodology (observations and proposed layer selection/logit correction) is modality agnostic; the findings are largely empirical and it is unclear whether this is a general phenomenon for other models of the same size or for models of different sizes (Reviewer 3).
- The method design appears ad-hoc with multiple thresholds in Eq2, Eq3, layer interval a, b in Eq4, and weight α in Eq7, raising concerns about the generalizability of the proposed method (Reviewer 3).
- There is insufficient connection to existing literature on LLM internal representations and hallucination (e.g., [1-5] in Reviewer 3’s comments) and to the unique property of conditional multimodal modeling. A comparison with Favero et al. (2024) [6], which studies similar visual information grounding, is suggested.
- Concerns about text quality: the response in Figure 9 exhibits awkward repetition (“The horse statue is positioned on top of the chair” stated multiple times), questioning the effectiveness of the chosen α in avoiding repetitive language (Reviewer 1).
- In Figure 10, DeCo reduces a major hallucination (misidentifying a lift as “chair”) but the output still contains a hallucination (“several other people visible in the background”), suggesting a gap between benchmark performance and consistent real-world accuracy (Reviewer 1).
- There is a lack of investigation into how DeCo influences language tokens that are not related to the visual input but are essential for sentence generation (Reviewer 1).
- The coherence and richness of text generated from preceding layers is not evaluated; additional metrics such as BLEU or other text quality scores are requested (Reviewer 2).
- Discrepancy in optimal layer intervals: Figure 1(b) suggests [10,20] is optimal, while Figure 7(b) shows [17,28] performs better. Clarification is needed (Reviewer 2).
- More evidence and ablation studies are needed to demonstrate how dynamic soft modulation prevents abrupt changes in logits (Reviewer 2).
- Detailed MME results per subtask are missing (Reviewer 2).
- The experimental results on POPE (Table 3) do not fully align with the settings from LLaVA 1.5; clarification on the specific settings followed and how they differ from LLaVA is needed (Reviewer 4).
- Missing evaluation on comprehensive benchmarks like MMBench and MMVet, which are important for assessing the model’s overall performance (Reviewer 4).
- It is unclear how the affine layer φ (L104) is initialized and trained. If it requires training, each layer would need such a layer; if not, how is consistent mapping across layers ensured? (Reviewer 3)
- For POPE (Yes/No evaluation), it is unclear how a decoding strategy can have an impact on performance; further explanation is needed (Reviewer 3).
- The selection process for the 500 images used in experiments and which splits were used for hyperparameter determination/evaluation need more details (Reviewer 1).
- The layer range (e.g., 20-28) may need tuning for different MLLMs; the paper does not address this (Reviewer 1, implicit in method)
