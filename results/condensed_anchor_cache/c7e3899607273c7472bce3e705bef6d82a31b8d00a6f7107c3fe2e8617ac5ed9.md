## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes WISE-GNN, a framework that enhances GNNs by incorporating Wise embeddings (capturing attribute proximity to class-specific landmarks) and a topological module based on persistent homology (encoding local subgraph structures). It aims to address long-range dependencies and oversmoothing in heterophilic graphs. Experiments are conducted on small benchmark datasets. Reviewer ratings are 1, 3, 5, 3, indicating strong disagreement: one reviewer (rating 5) found the work promising, while three others (ratings 1 and 3) raised substantial concerns.

### Strengths
- The background is introduced clearly (R1).
- The visualization results are interesting (R1).
- One reviewer found the paper generally clear (R2), though other reviewers strongly disagreed on clarity.
- Promising performance improvements have been achieved (R2).
- WISE-GNN addresses both attribute and topological aspects of graph data, aiming for a more comprehensive representation (R3).
- The framework is model-agnostic, allowing easy integration with any existing GNN architecture (R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear motivation**: The paper claims to address oversmoothing, oversquashing, and underreaching but does not explain how the proposed topology embedding solves these problems. Experiments focus on heterophilic graphs, confusing the objective (R1). The use of Wise embeddings over alternative strategies (e.g., clustering) lacks sufficient justification (R3).
- **Limited novelty**: The Wise embedding resembles existing class landmark/prototype methods (R2, R3). The topological module is adopted from established persistent homology work, offering limited originality (R3). Similar ideas (e.g., LinkX, position embeddings in graph transformers) have been previously explored (R1). The approach is incremental rather than groundbreaking (R3).
- **Methodological issues**:
  - The Wise embedding seems handcrafted and time‑consuming; runtime is not discussed (R2).
  - Computing distances to multiple cluster centers makes similar nodes more alike, but message‑passing still influences heterophilic nodes (R4).
  - It is unclear why the Wise embedding is used for GNN input while the Betti vector is processed through an MLP (R2).
  - The topology module does not effectively interact with the Wise embedding but functions as a simple combination (R4).
- **Insufficient explanation**: The objective function and how Wise embeddings are integrated with GNN training are not clearly explained (R3). The details of building the Wise embedding are difficult to follow (R1). The paper lacks discussion on why the method is effective for heterophilic graphs (R1).
- **Experimental concerns**:
  - All datasets used are small (a few thousand nodes) (R1, R2, R4). Chameleon and Squirrel datasets are known to have information leakage issues; filtered versions should be used (R2, R4).
  - The GCN baseline on Cora yields only ~59% accuracy, contradicting widely reported ~72% (R1, R4), making claimed improvements unconvincing.
  - Missing comparisons with strong recent heterophilic baselines (e.g., ACM-GCN, GREAD, M2M-GNN, Geom-GCN) (R4) and other recent methods (R2).
  - Experiments on large‑scale heterophilic datasets (e.g., from Platonov et al. and Lim et al.) are needed (R2, R4).
  - Specific results (e.g., T‑H2GCN performance) require clarification (R3). Comparisons with clustering‑based methods are needed to validate the Wise embedding’s distinct advantage (R3).
- **Reproducibility**: No code or hyperparameters are provided (R2).
- **Poor writing and citation style** hinder understanding (R1).
