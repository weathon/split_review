## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Q-Bench, a benchmark for evaluating Multi-modality Large Language Models (MLLMs) on low-level visual perception, description, and quality assessment. It constructs three datasets: LLVisionQA (2,990 images with human-asked low-level attribute questions), LLDescribe (499 images with expert golden descriptions), and a compounded IQA dataset. Using a softmax-based strategy to enable quantifiable quality scores from MLLMs, the paper evaluates 10 recent MLLMs. The experiments are extensive and comprehensive, and the findings indicate that current MLLMs possess preliminary but unstable low-level visual skills, suggesting a need for specific enhancements. The benchmark is well-organized and the paper is clearly presented.

### Strengths

- The benchmark specifically targets low-level visual tasks, addressing a gap in existing MLLM evaluation (rather than a holistic evaluation of general abilities).
- The three-task design (perception classification, expert-aligned description, probability-based quantitative quality assessment) is well-organized and thorough.
- The datasets are solidly constructed: LLVisionQA with 2,990 diverse-sourced images and questions, LLDescribe with expert annotations, and a compounded IQA dataset with a novel softmax-based strategy.
- The experiments are extensive and comprehensive, diagnosing the strengths and shortcomings of current MLLMs; the findings may inspire further research.
- The paper is well-written and clearly presented, with clear details.

### Weaknesses

- **Evaluation trustworthiness (severe concern per Reviewer 1, score 6):** How is the reliability and confidence of the GPT-assisted evaluation pipeline ensured, given that ChatGPT itself suffers from hallucination? This concern is not fully addressed.
- **Metric design issues:**
  - Reviewer 1 asks how the proposed softmax-based score differs from simply using perplexity (only considering good/poor) and whether perplexity is effectively equivalent.
  - Reviewer 2 notes that the softmax between “good” and “poor” ignores synonyms (e.g., great, excellent, fine, bad, low) and suggests merging logits (e.g., great+excellent+fine into good; bad+low into poor) to compute a more robust score.
- **Data contamination:** Reviewer 1 asks how the paper avoids contamination of the evaluation dataset in MLLM training sets, given that current models use large amounts of data.
- **Missing recent SOTA MLLMs:** Reviewer 2 notes that the leaderboard should be updated with recently released models: QWen-VL, InternLM-XComposer, and LLaVA-1.5.
- **Missing GPT-4V baseline:** Both Reviewer 3 (score 8, confidence 4) and implicitly Reviewer 2 request that the performance of GPT-4V on Q-Bench be included to establish a commercial SotA baseline. This is a necessary missing experiment.
