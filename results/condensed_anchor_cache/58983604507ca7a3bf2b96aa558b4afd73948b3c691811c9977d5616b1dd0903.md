## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Curated LLM (CLLM), which uses GPT-4 to generate synthetic tabular data in ultra-low-data regimes (n < 100) and then curates the generated samples using learning dynamics (confidence and aleatoric uncertainty metrics). Experiments show performance gains over conventional generators. Reviewers agree the problem is important and timely, and the presentation is clear, but disagree sharply on the technical contribution, validity, and ethical safety. Reviewer 1 (score 8) is enthusiastic, praising the curation mechanism, gains for underrepresented samples, and promising results. Reviewer 3 (score 6) finds the topic interesting and the framework useful, but raises substantial doubts about the role of the LLM prior and the robustness of curation. Reviewer 2 (score 5) is highly critical, questioning the technical novelty, the risk of LLM biases in medical applications, and the experimental setup. All three request additional experiments to separate in-context learning from memorization and to test on controlled datasets.

### Strengths
- The problem of data augmentation for ultra-low-resource tabular data (n < 100) is important, well-motivated, and timely, with potential impact for disadvantaged groups and rare scenarios.
- The approach (using LLM background knowledge for generation + post-generation curation) is intuitive and well-motivated; the curation idea (discarding samples with low confidence and high aleatoric uncertainty) is smart and improves performance for both LLM and non-LLM generators.
- Promising experimental results across multiple real-world datasets, with coverage of representative baselines (including CTGAN, TVAE, SMOTE, etc.).
- LLM-based generation is shown to be especially helpful for generating underrepresented samples, which traditional methods struggle with.
- The paper provides interesting (though anecdotal) insight into GPT‑4’s ability to extrapolate to unseen regions of the manifold (via t‑SNE plots).
- Presentation is excellent: clear language, beautiful formatting, well‑organized, well‑contextualized.
- Concepts such as data prior and data quality flags offer a useful framework for thinking about the problem.
- Related work is well introduced and comparisons are thorough.

### Weaknesses
- **Technical contribution and novelty:** The core idea (few‑shot generation + curation) builds heavily on existing work; Reviewer 2 argues there is little technical advance beyond standard few‑shot generation. Reviewers question whether the gains come from the LLM’s background knowledge or from a strong data model, and whether simpler baselines (e.g., adding random noise to existing samples before curation) would match performance.
- **Role of LLM prior and in‑context learning vs. memorization:** It is unclear whether the LLM learns the nuances of the tiny dataset or its prior overwhelms them. Table 3 shows LLM performance does not improve much as n increases, consistent with either scenario. The fact that GPT‑4‑generated data often outperforms the real training set (D_train) in the train-on-synthetic test-on-real setting (Table 3) may indicate memorization rather than genuine generation. Reviewers request experiments on datasets that are definitively not in GPT‑4’s training data (e.g., by manually manipulating column dependencies and marginal distributions) to distinguish in‑context learning from memorization.
- **Dataset and experimental rigor:** 
  - Dataset selection logic is not justified; it is unclear whether datasets were chosen to highlight strengths or are representative. More datasets from baseline papers (e.g., STaSy [1]) should be included.
  - Only GPT‑4 is tested; results should be verified with other LLMs (Claude 2, LLAMAv2).
  - Lack of error bars / statistical significance in reported results (e.g., Table 2). Multiple seeds or resampling should be used, and statistically significant winners should be bolded. Average performance with standard deviation is needed for easy comparison.
  - The dataset behind Table 2 is not named, and it is unclear whether similar results hold for other datasets/figures.
  - In Table 3 the “Adult” row in the first section misses a bolded winner.
  - Table 1 does not specify what quantity the performance numbers represent.
  - The dip around 6 samples in Figure 3 is unexplained; the actual performance after augmentation needs to be reported.
- **Counterintuitive results:** For GPT‑4‑no‑context, utility drops when moving from 40 to 200 samples (Table 2), which contradicts the expectation that more data should help. This may indicate volatility or a flaw in the method/experiment and requires investigation.
- **Curation model‑dependence:** The curation method (Eq. 1–2) depends on the specific ML model via \(f_e(\mathbf{x})\), potentially amplifying that model’s inductive biases. The authors should evaluate curation with multiple downstream models and include additional metrics like resemblance. The selection criteria (high confidence, high aleatoric uncertainty) bear similarity to active learning; it may be worth clarifying whether the two criteria are essentially equivalent (both derived from \([f_e(\mathbf{x})]_y\)).
- **Ethical and trustworthiness concerns (medical focus):** The paper targets medical applications but does not discuss the risk of LLMs reproducing social biases (e.g., associating demographic attributes with STDs, COVID outcomes). Factual tracing in LLMs is an open problem; using black‑box model outputs without such analysis is irresponsible and poses ethical risks, especially for historically disadvantaged groups. The authors may not be fully aware of these limitations.
- **Missing discussion of pitfalls and failure modes:** Because the method is easy to use, it is important to understand when it breaks down. No such analysis is provided.
- **Additional missing details:**
  - “Ultra‑low‑resource” is not defined; “low‑resource” appears in abstract/intro, and the range n < 100 may be considered few‑shot, not a standard notion.
  - The paper does not discuss decoding strategies (temperature, sampling) to ensure diversity.
  - The acronym “ICL” is used but never introduced.
  - Figure 2 is hard to read due to solid markers occluding each other.
- **Related work omissions:** Besides citing STaSy [1], the paper should reference tabular deep‑learning works that address extreme low‑data regimes, including transfer learning [2,3] and knowledge‑graph‑augmented methods [4,5] (idea similar to using an LLM prior).
