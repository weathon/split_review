## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a two-stage dynamic structure learning method for deep SNNs that uses the PQ index to adaptively determine rewiring ratios for pruning and regrowth. Overall, reviewers found the motivation and combination of techniques interesting but raised significant concerns about the novelty relative to existing ANN pruning methods, the unclear connection to SNN-specific properties, incomplete performance comparisons, missing analyses (energy consumption, large-scale datasets), and minor presentation issues. One reviewer (score 3) was substantially more critical, describing the method as a direct application of ANN techniques without adaptation to SNN characteristics, while three others (scores 6, 6, 5) saw more merit but still had significant reservations.

### Strengths

- Adaptive pruning and sparse training are applied to deep SNNs, which can further enhance energy efficiency for neuromorphic implementation.
- The two-stage method appears feasible based on the experimental results presented.
- Pruning is essential for making SNNs practical on neuromorphic hardware, with different constraints from ANN pruning that is GPU-oriented.
- The proposed method achieves competitive or better performance than the dense model with fewer connections and reduced redundancy.
- Dynamically calculating the rewiring ratio during training is a novel idea.
- The paper provides a detailed algorithm pseudo-code.
- Combining the sparse structure learning framework of SNNs with the PQ index and rewiring techniques is interesting.
- Various rewiring scopes (layer-wise and neuron-wise) were considered.

### Weaknesses

- The method appears to be a direct application of ANN pruning techniques (e.g., RigL, DSR) without targeted adaptations to the binary spiking characteristics of SNNs, such as discrete event-driven computation and temporal dynamics. The paper does not explain why the method is specifically suited to SNNs versus Binary Neural Networks (BNNs).
- The PQ index is not defined or explained; only a citation is given. Reviewers found it unclear how the PQ index relates to SNNs, how it is calculated, and how it guides the dynamic pruning process. One reviewer (score 3) considered this a major omission; others also noted the lack of connection to SNNs.
- The methodology lacks theoretical discussion. There is no justification for why the pruning and rewiring strategies are particularly beneficial or suitable for SNNs, and no analysis of how they interact with temporal dynamics.
- The training process described is inconsistent: Figure 2 shows decreasing density over iterations, but Algorithm 1 uses the same removal and regrow fraction c_i, implying density should remain constant. This discrepancy requires explanation.
- The performance reported in Table 1 is incomplete and lags behind previous works. For DVS-CIFAR10 VGGSNN, the proposed method achieves 78.4% Acc, 30% Conn, 2.76M Param, while UPR can achieve 81.0% Acc, 4.46% Conn, 2.50M Param, and STDS can achieve 79.8% Acc, 4.67% Conn, 0.24M Param. (One reviewer noted no substantial improvement in accuracy or network connectivity.)
- The paper lacks analysis of energy consumption (e.g., synaptic operations, power saving ratio), which is essential for evaluating SNN efficiency in neuromorphic applications.
- Experiments are limited to small-scale datasets; no results on large-scale datasets such as ImageNet-1k are provided to demonstrate scalability.
- It is unclear whether the proposed method requires longer training time compared to existing methods; a comparison of training time is needed.
- It is unclear if the method can be applied effectively to attention-based and Transformer-based SNNs, which have shown strong performance in the field.
- Minor technical issues: duplicate definition of c_i in Algorithm 1 (used both for an input data label and the rewiring ratio); undefined symbols n^k and epsilon in Equation 1; Figure 3 is noticeably different in size from other figures, affecting presentation quality. Including additional figures to illustrate the method would aid comprehension.
