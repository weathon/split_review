## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces the notion of *interaction rank* to characterize game structure in offline multi-agent RL (MARL). It shows that low interaction rank makes functions more robust to distribution shift, and proposes a decentralized policy gradient algorithm with $\chi^2$ regularization that converges to a coarse correlated equilibrium (CCE). The sample complexity scales exponentially with the interaction rank rather than the number of agents. Experiments on a contextual game illustrate the benefit of low‑interaction‑rank critic architectures.

### Strengths

- **Novel structural assumption**: The interaction rank is a new and interesting idea. It provides a clear research question and explicitly quantifies how low‑interaction‑rank games are easier to learn.
- **Decentralized algorithm with guarantees**: The work is the first to propose a decentralized algorithm for offline general-sum MARL with provable statistical guarantees. The algorithm is no‑regret and can be deployed in realistic settings.
- **Theoretical contributions**: Statistical convergence and sample complexity bounds are derived, scaling with the interaction rank instead of the number of agents. The proof sketch for the contextual game (K=2) is clear and technically sound.
- **Presentation**: The paper is well‑written and easy to follow. The toy contextual‑game example helps intuition.
- **Research framing**: The paper draws a clean conclusion that low interaction rank games are easier to learn, confirming and quantifying an intuitive expectation.

### Weaknesses

- **Restrictive assumptions**:
  - The assumption that Markov games have **decoupled transitions** is very restrictive and not representative of many multi‑agent settings.
  - The assumption that reward functions and transition kernels belong to a **finite function class** is not practical.
  - The paper should have stated the transition‑decoupled assumption as part of the problem formulation, not as a technical assumption.
- **Practical difficulty of algorithm**:
  - In Algorithm 1, line 3, **estimating the transition kernel and solving the arg max** is difficult in practice.
- **Limited empirical evaluation**: Only a single toy problem is tested, with no real‑world experiments. The code is not released (encouraged by reviewer).
- **Insufficient related‑work discussion**:
  - The paper does not discuss how the interaction rank relates to existing MARL/RL structural assumptions (e.g., exponential decay of transition dynamics, [Qu et al. 2022; Ying et al. 2024]). Is low interaction rank a similar property but on reward rather than dynamics? Could exponential decay alleviate the decoupled‑transition requirement?
  - It is unclear what the state of the art is for offline, decentralized MARL and how the proposed algorithm compares to it.
- **Ambiguities in theoretical results**:
  - The **constant hidden by “≲”** in the sample complexity bound: does it scale with problem size? In the extreme case of **full interaction rank**, does the bound reduce to the usual scaling with number of agents? If not, what explains the difference?
  - The **on-support and off-support components** in Theorem 2 need explanation. The off‑support component depends on a $\chi^2$‑divergence ball; it is not clear whether this regularizer is a wise choice despite analytical convenience, as it limits policy performance.
  - In the definition of $C_{\sin}$ (and Assumption 4), the max over $\mu_i$ can be infinite when $\nu_i$ is zero for some action‑context pair. Can a **single‑policy concentration coefficient** be defined here, and the optimality gap expressed as a function of it?
- **Questions about setting**:
  - Does the same property apply to **cooperative MARL**? If there are differences, they should be discussed.
  - Could a **centralized** setting achieve better performance guarantees than the decentralized one?
- **Claim of decentralization advantage**:
  - The paper claims that prior work is not decentralized, but the advantage may stem from the **decoupled transition assumption** rather than from the method itself. The reviewer doubts the novelty of the decentralized benefit.
- **Minor suggestion**: Consider using “Markov Game” or “MG” in the title/abstract to avoid the cooperative connotation of “MARL”.

*Low‑scoring reviewers (rating 6) particularly note the limited empirical evaluation and the restrictive assumptions as major weaknesses.*
