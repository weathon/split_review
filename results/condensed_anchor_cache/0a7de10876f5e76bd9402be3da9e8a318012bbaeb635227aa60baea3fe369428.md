## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces the "Neuron Path" concept for Vision Transformers (ViT), proposing a Joint Attribution Score (JAS) and a layer-progressive algorithm to identify the most influential neurons in the feed-forward network (FFN) across layers. Five reviewers generally find the problem well-motivated and the method innovative, but raise concerns about limited scope (FFN-only, image classification only), insufficient comparison with prior attribution work, missing ablation studies, lack of downstream task validation, and unclear exposition of JAS. Ratings are all 6 (weak accept), with some reviewers more critical about novelty and methodological rigor.

### Strengths

- **Innovative concept and method:** The "Neuron Path" and JAS are novel ideas for tracing influential neuron pathways in ViT FFNs. The layer-progressive locating algorithm efficiently selects neurons by gradient magnitude (Reviewers 2, 3, 5). Reviewer 5 notes the method is simple yet effective.
- **Clear motivation and presentation:** The paper is well-motivated (understanding ViT internals is crucial) and easy to follow, especially the Introduction and Related Work sections (Reviewers 1, 3, 5). Figures and clearly defined terms aid readability (Reviewer 3).
- **Comprehensive experiments and application demonstration:** Comparisons against baselines (Activation, Influence Pattern) are provided in Table 1. Class-level analysis in Figure 4 shows distinct pathways per class; Figure 5 shows class relationships via neuron paths. A model compression (pruning) experiment demonstrates practical utility (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5). Reviewer 2 highlights that the method can visualize differing neural pathways and assess class similarity.
- **Potential for broader application:** The gradient-based approach may extend to other architectures (Reviewer 2). The pruning experiment shows the method can identify redundancy, valuable for model optimization (Reviewer 3).
- **Minority positive view (Reviewer 5):** The method is considered simple yet effective, and the writing is strong.

### Weaknesses

- **Incomplete scope – FFN only:** The analysis is limited to neurons in the Feed-Forward Network, excluding the self-attention mechanism. This may overlook interactions between self-attention and FFN, leading to an incomplete understanding of information flow (Reviewers 3, 4). Reviewer 4 further questions the motivation for focusing only on the first linear layer of each FFN.
- **Narrow task and model focus:** The method is evaluated only on image classification (ViT). Applicability to other vision tasks (e.g., object detection) or to other architectures (e.g., CNNs, NLP transformers) is not explored (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). Reviewer 1 asks if it can be used in NLP; Reviewer 3 asks about generalization to other tasks.
- **Insufficient comparison with prior work / lack of novelty clarity:** The paper does not adequately compare with established attribution methods like HINT, Neuron Shapley, or WWW (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 2 finds it difficult to discern the method’s significance relative to recent studies without a comparative analysis.
- **Missing ablation study for JAS:** The Joint Attribution Score requires multiple back-propagation iterations and is computationally expensive, but Table 1 varies search methods and scores simultaneously, making it impossible to isolate the contribution of JAS. A comparison with a simpler baseline (e.g., zero-order derivatives or single-neuron attribution) is needed (Reviewers 2, 3). Reviewer 2 asks why not use zero-order derivatives; Reviewer 3 asks if approximations can reduce computational load.
- **Lack of quantitative evaluations beyond Table 1:** Figures 5 and 6 (class relationships, pruning) lack comparisons with baseline methods; only qualitative visualizations are provided. Quantitative metrics are needed to demonstrate superiority (Reviewer 2).
- **Downstream task validation missing:** The abstract claims that neuron paths preserve model capability on downstream tasks, but no downstream task experiments are conducted (Reviewer 2).
- **Theoretical foundations unclear:** The rationale for JAS (sum of gradients) is not explained; Equation 2 is not described. The paper does not link JAS to information theory or causal inference (e.g., mutual information, information bottleneck) to justify its use in identifying influential pathways (Reviewers 2, 3). Reviewer 2 asks for meaning and rationale of Eq 2.
- **Notation and exposition issues:** The notation in Equations 2 and 3 is confusing (e.g., double subscript, need clearer definitions of original and assigned values). A pseudo-code (like Algorithm 1) for JAS would help understanding (Reviewer 5).
- **Unaddressed pattern difference between supervised ViT and self-supervised MAE:** In the preliminary study (Sec 3.1), an inverted distribution pattern appears – the neuron path in MAE plays a much greater role (larger Accuracy Deviation) than in supervised ViT. This phenomenon is noted but not explained or targeted in later experiments (Reviewer 5).
- **Assumption of FFN redundancy not rigorously tested:** The paper assumes redundancy in FFN layers as basis for pruning, but prior work (e.g., "Intriguing Properties of Vision Transformers" NeurIPS 2021) shows FFNs encode high-level visual features essential for performance. This hypothesis warrants more thorough testing (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of in-depth property analysis:** The stability of neuron paths across inputs, their evolution during training, or correlation with human-interpretable concepts is not examined, limiting interpretability insights (Reviewer 3). The computational expense is noted as a challenge (Reviewer 3, also implied by Reviewer 2 on JAS cost).
- **Minority critical view on novelty (Reviewer 4):** The approach significantly overlaps with existing attribution techniques, and the authors should clarify what is new. Also, relying solely on joint attribution scores lacks breadth – additional metrics like concept coherence or task-specific performance are missing.
