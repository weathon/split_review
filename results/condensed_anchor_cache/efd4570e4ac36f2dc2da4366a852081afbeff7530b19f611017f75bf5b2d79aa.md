## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper unifies state-of-the-art GNNs for KG reasoning (RED-GNN, NBFNet) into a common framework (QL-GNN) and analyzes their expressivity in terms of rule structures they can learn. The analysis identifies rule types (T(h,x), U(h,x)) that existing GNNs cannot learn, leading to a novel entity-labeling strategy (EL-GNN) that extends the learnable rule set. Theoretical results are supported by experiments on synthetic and real datasets, showing improvement over baselines. All four reviewers find the theoretical analysis sound and the contributions novel. However, the presentation, experimental scope, and scalability discussion receive criticism from three reviewers (scores 5,6,6), while the fourth reviewer (score 8) is strongly positive and notes no weaknesses, only a query about time complexity.

### Strengths
- **Novel theoretical perspective**: The paper provides a systematic way to understand GNN expressivity for KG reasoning through rule structures, using a CML-based formalism that describes which rule structures each model class can represent (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- **Rigorous analysis**: Theoretical results (Theorem 4.4, Proposition 5.1, Corollary 5.2) prove limitations of existing GNNs (e.g., CompGCN cannot learn structural rules in Figure 2) and advantages of the proposed methods. The formalism generalizes existing methods to represent broader classes of rules (Reviewer 1).
- **Effective proposed method**: EL-GNN outperforms state-of-the-art models on multiple datasets (WN18RR, NELL-995, YAGO3-10). The method is simple—assigning initial representation to entities with out-degree above a threshold—yet effective (Reviewer 4).
- **Empirical support**: Experiments on synthetic datasets (Table 1) confirm theoretical claims (Reviewer 1,3,4). Results align with the identified rule structures.
- **Innovative approach**: QL-GNN framework and EL-GNN model address gaps in understanding GNNs for KG reasoning, offering a structured approach to rule learning (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing experiments on FB15K-237**: Section 6.2 experiments omit FB15K-237, the most popular link prediction benchmark, making the evaluation of EL-GNN incomplete (Reviewer 2). *Question: "What about the performance on FB15K-237?" overlaps with missing experiment.*
- **Presentation of theory not sufficiently accessible**: The definition of expressivity should be presented earlier, and the related work section is too high-level for non-experts. Theorem statements (e.g., Theorem 4.4) need more handholding: e.g., "The structural rules in Figure 2 cannot be learned by CompGCN due to Theorem 4.4" should be explained more clearly (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of generalization/expressivity explanation**: It is unclear how the analysis relates to number of parameters, graph size, aggregation functions, etc. Theorem C.2 appears to depend only on L, but how do other factors affect learning? (Reviewer 1, question).
- **Insufficient motivation/connection to real data**: The paper would benefit from more real-world examples showing how often the identified rule patterns occur in empirical datasets and how gains correlate with those patterns (Reviewer 1).
- **Scalability and complexity not addressed**: There is no detailed analysis of time and space complexity for EL-GNN vs. QL-GNN, especially regarding the entity-level computations from the labeling strategy. Experiments use relatively small datasets; larger KGs (e.g., with millions of edges) are needed to demonstrate practical applicability (Reviewer 3). *Question from Reviewer 4 about the claim that "additional time complexity is marginal" also asks for numeric values or more discussion—this overlaps with the missing scalability analysis.*
- **Limited discussion of labeling strategy limitations**: The paper does not explore scenarios where the labeling strategy may introduce biases or fail to capture underlying rule structures, e.g., for specific KG structures or relation patterns (Reviewer 3). *Questions about transferability to other KGs and robustness to noise (Reviewer 3) are also not addressed, but they are less overlapping with weakness—include them as they relate to underspecified limitations.*
- **Minor**: First sentence missing "A" (Reviewer 1).
