## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Masked VAE, combining a VAE-inspired variational encoder for masked tokens with MAE’s deterministic encoder for visible tokens, producing distributionally-aware masked completions. A new evaluation benchmark, Context-Completion, measures contextual understanding via object inpainting. The method achieves comparable ImageNet classification fine-tuning to MAE while outperforming MAE on the proposed benchmark.

### Strengths
- Clearly identifies a fundamental limitation of masked image modeling: the under-constrained prediction problem is forced into a deterministic point estimate, whereas multiple plausible completions exist (e.g., a partial kitchen could contain spoons, cups, pizzas). Introducing stochasticity via a VAE distribution directly addresses this gap.
- The paper is well-organized and easy to follow; motivation for probabilistic masking is intuitive and well-articulated.
- Proposing contextually-aware representations introduces a novel perspective to self-supervised vision learning, distinct from typical discriminative objectives.
- MaskedVAE achieves competitive performance with MAE on ImageNet fine-tuning (e.g., linear probing, fine-tuning), demonstrating that adding stochasticity does not harm classification representations—a reassuring sanity check.

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear practical benefit for self-supervised representation learning:** The method only matches MAE on downstream tasks (e.g., ImageNet classification), offering no improvement. If the flaw in masked prediction does not translate to better representations, it raises the question of whether the flaw is truly meaningful for SSL. The paper’s position is ambiguous: if the goal is merely better inpainting, why not use diffusion models (which already excel at multi-modal completion) instead of a VAE-based approach?
- **Context-Completion benchmark is not a fair evaluation against MAE:** MAE’s lightweight decoder is intentionally weak for generation—it is designed to encourage representation learning, not inpainting. Comparing MAE’s raw pixel reconstruction with MaskedVAE’s variational decoder is therefore an uneven comparison. A more appropriate baseline would be specialized image completion models, such as diffusion-based methods.
- **Benchmark metrics are outdated** and the shown image completion results do not match the quality of current popular models (e.g., diffusion-based inpainting).
- **No evidence that stochastic completion improves context understanding over existing methods:** MAE already performs well on dense-prediction tasks (e.g., object segmentation), suggesting that deterministic masking captures context effectively. The paper does not show that a model with good inpainting ability yields better representation for any discriminative task. In fact, MAE can already produce realistic completions via a finetuned decoder (e.g., using GAN loss) or by adding diffusion models (like I-JEPA), without requiring a variational encoder during SSL pretraining.
- **Overall impact on the field is unclear:** The practical applications of MaskedVAE within self-supervised learning are not well defined. The core motivation—to enable multiple plausible completions—appears to serve a generative purpose rather than representation learning, yet the paper is framed as a SSL method. This tension weakens the contribution.
- **Minority contrast on classification performance:** One reviewer notes that competitive ImageNet results are a strength (showing no degradation), while two others view the lack of improvement as a weakness—the method does not surpass MAE in any discriminative setting, limiting its value as a SSL technique.
