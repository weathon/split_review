## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Libra, a vision-language model (VLM) for radiology report generation (RRG) that leverages temporal images (current + prior chest X-rays). Libra uses a radiology-specific image encoder (RAD-DINO), a medical language model (Meditron), and a novel Temporal Alignment Connector (TAC) containing a Layerwise Feature Extractor (LFE) and a Temporal Fusion Module (TFM). Experiments on MIMIC-CXR show that Libra outperforms existing VLM methods of similar scale, with a 12% improvement in the RadCliQ metric.

### Strengths
- **Clinically important and well-motivated problem**: Integrating temporal information from multiple radiology images for report generation addresses a real clinical need (R1, R4).
- **Innovative temporal processing**: The TAC module (LFE + TFM) provides a sophisticated approach to capturing and synthesizing temporal changes across different time points (R2, R3). One reviewer highlighted the interesting local and global learning mechanism (R1).
- **Comprehensive ablation studies**: The paper thoroughly ablates each component (TFM, LFE, PIPB, training stages, prompts), demonstrating their individual contributions (R1, R2, R4).
- **Detailed documentation and appendix**: The appendix provides extensive descriptions of datasets, training configurations, and additional experiments, supporting transparency and reproducibility (R2). The qualitative comparisons and heatmaps further validate the method (R3).
- **Empirical superiority**: Libra outperforms reasonable baselines on MIMIC-CXR, with a thorough evaluation that includes both standard metrics (e.g., RadCliQ) and clinical efficacy measures (R1, R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Overstated novelty and insufficient acknowledgment of prior work**  
   - The claim of being the first VLM for temporal report generation is false. Multiple prior works address temporal report generation, e.g., [2023.findings-emnlp.325], [2023.findings-emnlp.140], [arXiv:2403.13343], and [arXiv:2301.04558] (which the authors cite but do not compare against) (R1).  
   - The paper lacks a thorough review of recent vision-language models for medical image interpretation, making it hard to contextualize contributions (R2).  
   - The vision-language alignment mechanism (cross-modal attention) is not clearly novel compared to existing methods; detailed comparisons highlighting innovations are needed (R2).

2. **Marginal temporal improvement despite central framing**  
   - The paper is pitched as a temporal alignment method, but most performance gains come from other changes (LFE, training pipeline, prompt, VQA), with temporal fusion only improving metrics by 1–2%. Other components impact metrics by 5–10% (R4).  
   - The authors claim "significant enhancements across all metrics" for the TAC, but ablation results (Table 3) show that using dummy prior images yields comparable results except for F1. If true prior images provide only marginal improvement, the effectiveness of temporal fusion is overstated (R3).  
   - One reviewer noted that the model's reliance on prior images remains a challenge; the proposed dummy-image solution may not fully resolve temporal information gaps when prior images are unavailable (R2).

3. **Inflexible input handling and limited number of prior scans**  
   - The model requires exactly two images (current + one prior). When no prior scan exists, the current scan is used as a dummy. Many patients have multiple prior scans, and extending to more than one prior would increase complexity (cross-attention head) and likely require multiple dummies (R4). This inflexibility makes the approach less attractive than more flexible methods (R1, R4).  
   - It is unclear from the description whether the method is designed for one or multiple prior scans (R1).

4. **Incomplete model description and presentation issues**  
   - The TFM diagram omits the final MLP layer, causing ambiguity in the module's integration (R2).  
   - RAD-DINO is described too briefly; more details on its architecture, pre-training, and specific contributions are needed (R2).  
   - The effect of using Rad-DINO over a classical pre-trained encoder is not clear; an ablation replacing it would help (R1).  
   - The writing could be more concise and clear; some sections are overly wordy while others lack detail (R4).

5. **Unbalanced comparisons and questionable experimental settings**  
   - Comparison with MIMIC-Diff-VQA is unfair: Libra was trained on both MIMIC-CXR and MIMIC-Diff-VQA, while the compared model was only trained on MIMIC-CXR (R3).  
   - In Q2 of Section 4, the encoder and LLM should align with Libra's (RAD-DINO and Meditron) to ensure fair comparison (R3).  
   - Table 5 is unclear: using only Findings in Stage 1 yields better Clinical Efficacy, but adding VQA data (expected to help) actually decreases performance (R3).

6. **Limited dataset and evaluation**  
   - Experiments rely solely on MIMIC-CXR; generalizability to other datasets (e.g., CheXpert) or clinical settings is not demonstrated (R2). Some evaluation metrics are insufficiently explained (R2).  
   - Inference time and hardware specifications are not reported (R4).  
   - The use of multi-layer features from the image encoder (a claimed contribution) is not separately evaluated; the LFE ablations do not isolate this choice (R4).  
   - More detailed explanation is needed for Figure 5 heatmap, particularly why the Prior LFE shows more focused attention (R3).

*Note*: One reviewer (R3, score 6) was more positive overall, describing the approach as innovative and sophisticated, but still raised the concerns listed above (particularly points 2, 3, and 5). The other three reviewers (scores 5) were more critical of the novelty, framing, and experimental rigor.
