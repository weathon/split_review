- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 6, 3, 6, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes DUET, a single-loop decentralized bilevel optimization (DBO) algorithm that removes the need for lower-level strong convexity (LLSC) by introducing a diminishing quadratic regularization to the lower-level objective. DUET incorporates gradient tracking to handle data heterogeneity and achieves convergence to an approximate KKT-stationary point with iteration complexity \(O(1/T^{1-5p-\frac{11}{4}\tau})\). An extended version (DSGT) is also provided under an upper-level strong convexity assumption. The paper claims to be the first to address DBO without LLSC under decentralized settings with data heterogeneity. Numerical experiments on a pedagogical example and decentralized meta-learning on MNIST are presented.

### Strengths

- **First algorithm for DBO without LLSC**: DUET is the first single-loop DBO algorithm with provable convergence that does not rely on lower-level strong convexity, a common but restrictive assumption in prior methods (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Handles data heterogeneity without gradient homogeneity assumptions**: The algorithm integrates gradient tracking and consensus updates to overcome consensus errors in DBO with heterogeneous data, and avoids the double-loop computational overhead of traditional bilevel methods (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Novel convergence metric**: The paper proposes approximate KKT stationarity as the convergence measure for DBO without LLSC, providing a detailed convergence rate analysis that is expected to facilitate further research (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Clear comparison with existing methods**: The paper compares convergence rates and demonstrates improvements over current approaches (Reviewer 1). Table 1 summarizes complexity comparisons (though a concern about metric clarity is noted).
- **Weaker, standard assumptions**: The assumptions used (e.g., smoothness, bounded variances) are standard and do not impose strong convexity on the lower-level problem (Reviewer 4).
- **Well-written methodology**: The methodology section clearly explains how each algorithmic step addresses specific challenges (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited and overly simple experiments**: Experiments are confined to a pedagogical example and decentralized meta-learning on MNIST, which may not represent the non-convex nature of many bilevel optimization problems (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Reviewer 2 additionally notes the system is small-scale with few clients, raising scalability concerns. Reviewer 4 points out missing architectural details for the neural network in the meta-learning experiment, requiring readers to inspect code. Reviewer 2 asks for larger-scale distributed systems and more complex tasks such as bilevel NAS (e.g., DARTS).
- **Need for stochastic analysis**: The algorithm is presented in a deterministic setting, whereas stochastic gradient methods are far more common in machine learning. Reviewer 2 questions whether analyzing convergence under a stochastic setting poses challenges and asks for justification of the deterministic focus.
- **Reliance on convexity (not just LLSC removal)**: Although the paper removes LLSC, it still assumes lower-level convexity. Reviewer 2 notes that ML objectives are often non-convex and asks for theoretical analyses that do not depend on convexity assumptions.
- **Justification for relaxed stationarity metric**: Reviewers 2 and 4 raise concerns about using approximated KKT-stationarity without adequate justification. Reviewer 2 points out that this relaxation may yield significantly different solutions compared to the original problem, and that the stationarity measure differs from traditional bilevel optimization works. Reviewer 4 notes that Table 1 uses an \(\varepsilon\)-stationary metric while the paper uses the LLSC-less metric, which is not directly visible.
- **Communication complexity incomplete**: Reviewer 2 asks for analysis of the number of bits required (communication complexity) in addition to the number of communication rounds (Corollary 2), and requests experimental comparisons with existing distributed bilevel methods on this dimension.
- **Theory novelty is modest**: Reviewer 4 states that while the metric and algorithm are novel, the analytical techniques are quite standard in the distributed and bilevel optimization literature.
- **Misleading naming**: Reviewer 4 notes that the name “DSGT” conflicts with established terminology for distributed stochastic optimization with gradient tracking and suggests a rename.
- **Presentation and readability issues** (raised strongly by Reviewer 2 and in detail by Reviewer 3):
  - The paper has an excessive number of notations and poor readability; improvements are needed (Reviewer 2).
  - The feasible region for parameters \(p\) and \(\tau\) (line 81) should be clearly delineated (Reviewer 3).
  - Definitions of communication costs/complexity (lines 105, 117) need clarification (Reviewer 3).
  - The decentralized bilevel optimization problem formulation should be introduced earlier (Reviewer 3).
  - Precise definitions of \(\nabla \mathcal{L}\) (line 231) and \(\nabla \Phi\) (line 347) are needed, including specifying the norm type (Frobenius vs. 2-norm) for matrix-valued gradients (Reviewer 3).
  - Notation \(x_{i,t}\) and \(y_{i,t}\) (line 242) is ambiguous; should be clarified as vectors at iteration \(t\) (Reviewer 3).
  - The notation \(\mathbf{v}_{i,\mu_t}^*(x_{i,t})\) for the solution of the linear system and multiplier \(v_t\) is confusing; reviewer suggests replacing \(\mathbf{v}\) with a different symbol to avoid ambiguity with other uses of \(\mathbf{v}\) (Reviewer 3).
  - The rationale for projection parameters \(r_v^t\) and \(r_y^t\) (line 301) is unclear (Reviewer 3).
  - Definitions of \(\otimes\) and \(\bar{\beta}, \bar{\mu}\) (lines 347, 362) are not clearly stated (Reviewer 3).
  - Citation format in the references is incorrect (e.g., missing publication location, page numbers) (Reviewer 3).
  - Typo: line 402 "for all \(k\)" should be "for all \(t\)" (Reviewer 3).
- **Similarity to existing algorithm**: Reviewer 3 notes that DUET’s update mode is similar to the algorithm in [Liu et al., 2022] and asks for a clear distinction. They also question whether the step updating the multiplier mirrors the gradient descent step for solving the linear system in the hypergradient, and whether the boundedness assumption can be consolidated.
- **Theoretical nuance about complexity rates**: Reviewer 4 questions the correctness of claiming \(O(\varepsilon^{-3.5})\) and \(O(\varepsilon^{-2.5})\) in Corollaries 2 and 4, since \(\tau\) cannot be zero and the “\(\tau \to 0\)” limit that yields these rates is problematic.
- **Lack of lower bound insight**: Reviewer 4 suggests it would be interesting to study the lower bound of DBO under the LLSC-less metric, as the current \(O(\varepsilon^{-2.5})\) seems not optimal.
- **Missing comparison for Theorem 5**: Reviewer 3 recommends providing a comparative analysis of Theorem 5 with other algorithms.
- **Supplementary question from Reviewers**: Could more experiments on other problems/datasets be provided? (Reviewer 1)
- **Contrast among reviewers**: While three reviewers rated the paper 6, one reviewer (Reviewer 2, score 3) expressed significantly more critical concerns about the algorithm’s necessity in ML (non-convexity, deterministic-only analysis), inadequate justification of the stationarity measure, and insufficient experimental validation; they also found the presentation poor. This minority view indicates substantial disagreement on the paper’s overall contribution and soundness.
