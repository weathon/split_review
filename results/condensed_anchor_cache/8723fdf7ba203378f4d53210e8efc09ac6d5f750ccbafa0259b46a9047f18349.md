## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Autoverse, a domain-specific language for 2D grid-based games using cellular-automaton-like rewrite rules, enabling GPU-parallelizable environment simulation. Environments (rules and initial map) are evolved to produce curricula of increasing complexity. The authors propose bootstrapping open-ended RL: first evolve environments to maximize greedy tree search iterations, collect expert playtraces, distill them via behavior cloning, then continue training with RL in environments evolved to maximize the agent’s value function error. Experiments study observation range effects and environment dynamics. Three reviewers (scores 3) found the idea interesting but raised major concerns about presentation, experimentation completeness, and lack of comparisons. One reviewer (score 5) was more positive, highlighting originality, efficiency, and significance of the IL+RL approach.

### Strengths

- **Originality and expressiveness**: Autoverse’s combination of a domain-specific language with cellular-automaton-like rewrite rules is original and interesting, allowing practically limitless environments by modifying initial state and update rules, enabling more complex dynamics and environmental diversity than other open-ended learning environments. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Computational efficiency**: Using JAX to implement rewrite rules as convolutions enables batch GPU processing, providing at least an order-of-magnitude speedup for RL training. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Curriculum and structured learning**: Gradual evolution of environment complexity based on search ability provides a natural curriculum avoiding local optima. Combining imitation learning from expert search trajectories with subsequent reinforcement learning gives agents a solid starting foundation and improves generalization. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Relevance for foundation models and open-endedness**: CA-based environments are valuable for generating training data for foundation models in RL. (R2)
- **Clarity of core method**: The presentation of Autoverse (Section 2.1) is clear and Figure 1 helps visualize generated environments. (R2)

### Weaknesses

- **Presentation and formatting issues**:
    - The introduction lacks references despite many non-trivial claims (e.g., L31, L32, L38, L40). (R2)
    - Figures 1, 3, and 4 have problems: Figure 1 is referenced only on page 8; Figure 3 has extremely small text (especially 3a) and wastes space; Figure 4 has ambiguous elements (e.g., meaning of gray box) and layout issues with full-page figures. (R2, R3, R4)
    - Tables 1 and 2 do not follow ICLR style. (R2)
    - Incorrect parenthesis in references (Earle et al. 2023, L271; multiple in Section 4). (R2)
    - Chapter structure is weak; transition from method to results is not natural. (R1)
- **Missing baseline comparisons and ablation studies**:
    - No direct comparison with other advanced methods or open-ended learning benchmarks, making it hard to evaluate competitiveness. Claims that Autoverse environments are more diverse/complex than others lack citations or comparisons. (R1, R3, R4)
    - The combined imitation+RL approach is not compared against using either technique alone. (R3, R4)
    - No justification provided for choosing greedy tree search over alternatives. (R4)
    - Only mutation is used for environment evolution; crossover is omitted, limiting exploration of diversity. (R1)
- **Insufficient experimentation and missing details**:
    - No performance numbers reported after behavior cloning converges, leaving its contribution to final RL results ambiguous. (R3, R4)
    - Tables 1 and 2 lack interpretability: no reference values (e.g., random agent) and missing metadata (number of evaluations, number of train/test environments, repetitions). (R2)
    - The claimed scalability and computational efficiency are not backed by any benchmarks or experiments. (R2, R3, R4)
    - Long-term training effects are not studied: no trends after many cycles, no analysis of policy stability after large environmental changes, no evidence of long-term adaptability. (R1)
    - The evolutionary process generates very unstable (unusable) environments in most cases (L71, L413), but the ratio of usable to unusable environments and the computational cost to obtain usable ones are not analyzed. (R2)
- **Interpretability and applicability limitations**:
    - Rewrite rules are hard to understand and interpret, hampering manual intervention and broader adoption. (R1)
    - The framework is limited to grid-like, 2D, seemingly small environments; relevance to more realistic open-ended scenarios (e.g., Craftax, MineDojo) is unclear. (R2)
- **Missing details on environment and search**:
    - Whether grid size is constant and can be varied is not addressed. (R2)
    - How the greedy tree search scales with grid size and number of actions is not discussed. (R2)
    - Whether reward update rules always depend on agent actions or can arise purely from cell interactions is unclear. (R2)
    - No quantitative, objective indicators for evaluating diversity of generated environments beyond qualitative analysis. (R1)
    - No mechanism described to dynamically regulate evolution speed to avoid overly fast or slow progression. (R1)
- **Overall innovation questions**: The framework of combining imitation learning with reinforcement learning is not novel and has been used in many related works. (R1)
- **Minority reviewer contrast**: One reviewer (score 5) found Autoverse original and significant, praising its environment diversity and the IL+RL pipeline as a structured approach to generalization. The other three (score 3) had substantially more critical assessments, particularly regarding presentation, missing experiments, and lack of comparisons.
