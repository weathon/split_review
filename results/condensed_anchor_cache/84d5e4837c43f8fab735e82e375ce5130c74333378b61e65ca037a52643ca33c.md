## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses backdoor attacks on Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for image-to-text generation using only Out-of-Distribution (OOD) data, a realistic scenario where the attacker lacks access to the original training dataset. The proposed VLOOD framework introduces two loss functions—Clean Knowledge Preservation (CKP) and Conceptual Consistency Preservation (CCP)—along with a dynamic weighting mechanism to balance clean and poisoned data. Experiments on image captioning (Flickr8k, COCO) and VQA (VQAv2) across three VLM architectures (BLIP2, MiniGPT4, and others) demonstrate near 100% attack success rate (ASR) with minimal degradation of clean-task performance. All three reviewers agree the OOD setting is novel and practical, and the approach reveals a critical security vulnerability. However, they raise concerns about the justification of the loss functions, sensitivity to hyperparameters (λ, trigger size/position), and the need for broader architectural validation.

### Strengths
- **Realistic and practical scenario**: Using OOD data for backdoor attacks on VLMs is both novel and aligns with real-world constraints where attackers cannot access original training data. (R1, R2, R3)
- **High attack effectiveness**: The method achieves near 100% ASR while preserving the original performance (e.g., BLEU, CIDEr, accuracy) on clean images across image captioning and VQA. (R1, R3)
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Validation across three VLM architectures and three datasets (Flickr8k, COCO, VQAv2) shows robustness and transferability. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Simple and accessible implementation**: The proposed losses and dynamic weighting require minimal modification to standard fine-tuning. (R1)
- **Clear exposition**: Technical components (CKP, CCP, λ adjustment) are presented with visual examples and structured explanations. (R3)
- **Security implications**: Reveals VLM vulnerabilities in realistic settings, contributing to multimodal security research. (R2, R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient justification of loss function choices** (R1, R2):
  - CKP uses KL divergence but serves a role similar to the standard LM loss on clean data (minimizing divergence from the ground truth vs. from the benign model’s output). If the benign model is well-trained, both targets should be nearly identical. Experiments replacing CKP with $\mathcal{L}_{LM(clean)}$ are needed. (R1)
  - CCP uses scaled $\ell_1$ distance for embedding alignment, while the LM loss on poisoned data uses KL divergence. The paper’s justification (line 283) that $\ell_1$ is reliable across most dimensions is insufficient; $\ell_1$ may yield sparse results in high-dimensional spaces. Alternatives like $\ell_2$ or cosine similarity might better capture conceptual consistency. Experiments replacing CCP with $\mathcal{L}_{LM(poisoned)}$ are needed. (R1, R2)
- **Dynamic weight λ lacks robust evaluation** (R2):
  - Initialization and adjustment rate of λ are not stated; these can significantly affect convergence and stability, especially given varying OOD data distributions. Ablation studies on λ’s initial value and empirical convergence data (e.g., number of iterations until stabilization) are missing. (R2)
  - No ablation study isolating the λ mechanism: e.g., comparing fixed vs. dynamic λ, or different initial values, to clarify its impact on ASR, CACC, and PACC. (R2)
- **Trigger size and position sensitivity not systematically analyzed** (R2):
  - Ablation shows that conceptual consistency (CACC, PACC) fluctuates with trigger size; trigger position is randomly chosen (line 773 in Appendix). A systematic study of trigger placement (corners, center) and combined size-position effects is required to understand robustness. (R2)
- **Limited exploration of architectural universality** (R1, R3):
  - Only BLIP2 and MiniGPT4 (early VLMs) are tested; no experiments on more recent models like Llama3.2 Vision or Qwen2-VL. Further analysis on different architectures would strengthen claims about VLOOD’s universality. (R1, R3)
- **Missing comparisons and baseline clarifications** (R1):
  - Table 1 omits a direct comparison between “Default + CKP” and “Default + CKP + CCP”; the difference between “Default + Dynamic” and “VLOOD” is unclear. Table 2 should report clean performance of the model before any OOD fine-tuning rather than performance after fine-tuning on clean OOD data. (R1)
- **Data quantity sensitivity** (R3):
  - How does VLOOD’s effectiveness vary when OOD data drops below 3000 samples? (R3)
- **Limitations of CKP and CCP when visual content deviates strongly from original training data** (R3):
  - The paper does not clarify how the losses perform if OOD images are semantically very different from the pre-training distribution. (R3)
- **Reproducibility and presentation issues** (R2):
  - Some parameter settings are not fully stated; open-sourcing code and configurations is recommended. (R2)
  - Typo on line 490: “wo Defense” should be “no Defense”. (R2)

**Note on reviewer disagreement**: Reviewer 2 (score 5) is more critical than Reviewer 3 (score 8) and Reviewer 1 (score 6). R2 particularly questions the loss function design (both necessity and metric choice), λ mechanism robustness, and trigger sensitivity, and rates soundness as 2. R3 is overall positive (contribution 3) with only one weakness about architecture diversity. R1’s concerns are moderate, focusing on ablation experiments and clarity. All reviewers agree the OOD setting is a strength.
