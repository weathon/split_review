## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents ImmersePro, a framework for converting single-view videos into stereo videos using a dual-branch architecture (disparity and context branches) with spatial-temporal attention and implicit disparity guidance to avoid explicit disparity maps. It also introduces the YouTube-SBS dataset of 423 stereo videos (over 7 million stereo pairs). Experiments report improvements over stereo-from-mono by 11.76% (L1), 6.39% (SSIM), and 5.10% (PSNR). Reviewer 1 gave a positive rating (6); the other three reviewers were critical (3 each).

### Strengths
- Introduction of a large-scale YouTube-SBS dataset for stereo video generation, with careful preprocessing that is a significant contribution (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- Reasonable design of implicit disparity and layered disparity, consistent with findings that explicit optical flow is problematic for synthesis (Reviewer 1, also Reviewer 4).
- Dual-branch architecture with implicit disparity guidance (Reviewer 2).
- Ablation studies on various modules (Reviewer 2).
- Method is clear and intuitive; results look good (Reviewer 3).
- Timely problem (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Dataset completeness and quality**:
  - Missing dataset details: frames per video, length distribution, temporal consistency measurement (Reviewer 1); no temporal consistency metrics reported (Reviewers 1,3).
  - Suspected vertical distortion: RAFT-Stereo (designed for horizontal disparity) performs worse than RAFT, implying vertical offsets in the dataset (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 1 notes that optical flow from RAFT is inaccurate for stereo due to vertical movement and suggests constraining to horizontal or using better disparity estimation.
  - License breakdown unclear: whether dataset includes standard YouTube license or only public domain/CC-BY (Reviewer 3).
  - GT disparity in Figure 5 is clearly not GT; predicted L2R disparity is below modern standards (Reviewer 3). Suggests using MPI Sintel for GT disparity and temporal evaluation (Reviewer 3, references Luo et al. SIGGRAPH 2020, Kopf et al. CVPR 2021).

- **Insufficient evaluation and comparisons**:
  - Need to re-train/fine-tune SOTA methods on the new dataset for fair comparison (Reviewer 1); also evaluate image-based models trained on the dataset on prior image-based datasets (Reviewer 1).
  - Missing comparison with explicit disparity, which is vital to show motivation (Reviewer 4).
  - Missing comparison with recent SOTA mono-to-stereo methods: Tucker et al. 2020, Han et al. 2022, Wang et al. 2023 (Reviewer 4). Also missing a simple baseline using Depth Anything v2 forward warp (Reviewer 3).
  - Baselines are old; proposed method uses Depth Anything, giving an advantage (Reviewer 3).
  - Scale invariance concern: care needed when comparing metrics between methods that require scale alignment (Reviewer 3).
  - No evaluation on other benchmark datasets (Reviewer 2).
  - Lacking complexity analysis: computational complexity of view prediction, latency, and cost of stacking horizontally shifted copies not discussed (Reviewer 2).

- **Ablation and module necessity doubts**:
  - Ablation on context fusion shows PSNR drop <0.2 dB, not conclusive (Reviewer 2).
  - Figure 3 rows 2 and 3 look like a bug, not just “blurring” as stated; this affects ablations in Table 3 (Reviewer 3).
  - Method fails to converge when “implicit disparity” is removed; no explanation why, and whether it can be replaced (Reviewer 2).
  - Disparity index selection is similar to Fast-ACV (Xu et al. 2023); novelty unclear. Need theoretical analysis why implicit disparity (warp then weighted-sum) works better than explicit (weighted-sum then warp) (Reviewer 4).

- **Presentation and methodological clarity**:
  - Main architecture figure not readable; need zoom-in on implicit disparity (Reviewer 2).
  - Method section has poor presentation: typo in Eq.3, missing definition of \(D_{i,j}^d\), how dimension D=7 is obtained, insufficient illustration of how layered disparity selects the layer (Reviewer 4).
  - Problem of existing methods (Line 051-052) is unclear; should show visualization or experiment at the start (Reviewer 4).
  - PSNR reported as percentage is not professional; should use dB differences (Reviewer 1).
