## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses the lack of behavioral diversity in offline reinforcement learning by introducing an intrinsic reward mechanism that maximizes the mutual information between actions and policies. This allows agents to learn varied behaviors even from homogeneous datasets, unlike prior methods that require diverse data. To mitigate the risks of out-of-distribution actions, the method incorporates the ensemble-diversified actor-critic (EDAC) framework for Q-value uncertainty estimation. Experiments on D4RL MuJoCo benchmarks demonstrate improved diversity while maintaining performance across both heterogeneous and homogeneous datasets.

### Strengths
- **Novel intrinsic reward mechanism:** The mutual-information-based approach effectively promotes behavioral diversity across both heterogeneous and homogeneous datasets, which addresses a significant limitation of existing methods (R1, R2, R4). One reviewer considered this a significant advancement (R2).
- **Technical soundness:** The mutual information formulation is intuitive and well-motivated (R1, R4).
- **Integration with EDAC:** The use of ensemble-diversified actor-critic to manage out-of-distribution risks ensures diversity does not come at the cost of performance (R4).
- **Clear writing:** The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1, R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited empirical scope:** Only three MuJoCo tasks are evaluated; no discrete-action environments (e.g., Atari) or more complex robotic manipulation tasks are included (R1, R2 Q4, R4 Q15). Additionally, the diversity of strong baselines like EDAC, CQL, and IQL is not reported, and the observed action differences between methods may partly reflect insufficient training rather than true diversity (R4 Q15). Missing hyperparameter analysis for λ and N (R1 Q2), a metric to quantify the diversity–performance trade-off (R4 Q14, Table 4), and a study of downstream task adaptation (e.g., few-shot) (R1 Q3). Applicability to online RL is not discussed (R4 Q16).
- **Missing comparison and novelty clarification:** The idea of mutual information in offline RL is previously proposed in Ma et al. 2022 (R3). The paper does not clearly differentiate its objective from Eq.5–6 of that work nor include it as a baseline (R3). A similar concern arises with DIVEOFF, which also uses mutual information; the reasons for the proposed method’s superior diversity–performance balance over DIVEOFF are not adequately explained (R4 Q4, Q8). A minority reviewer (score 5) was less positive due to this prior-art overlap, while others (score 6) found the work novel.
- **Insufficient theoretical justification:** The transition from path diversity to behavioral diversity lacks explicit conditions and assumptions (R1). The derivation of the unique-behavior (UB) reward is not rigorous, and it is unclear why rewarding policies for behaving differently leads to exploration beyond the dataset distribution (R4 W4). The explanation for how distant latent vectors are decoded into similar actions is missing (R4 Q7). The estimation formulas for \(p(a_t|s_t)\) and \(p^t(s_t)\) (Eq.8) may suffer from bias or high variance; the rationale for these estimates is not justified (R2 Q3, R4 Q9). The inequality in Eq.8 of the third term (path diversity) being positive is not explained (R4 Q10). The analysis of \(p^t(s_t|\pi)\) appears to ignore the dependence on \(\pi\) (R2 Q2).
- **Implementation and algorithmic details missing:** How the minimum Q-value from the EDAC ensemble is selected for policy updates is unclear (R4 W2). The policy update step in Algorithm 1 (line 8) lacks details (e.g., whether policy gradient is used) (R4 Q12). The same notation \(\phi(\pi_i)\) is used for both state diversity and action diversity (R4 Q13). The possibility of incorporating information exchange between policies (e.g., Wu et al. 2023) is not considered (R2 Q5).
- **Conceptual clarity and experimental presentation:** The title phrase “out of the box” is vague and not clearly connected to the method’s contributions (R4 W1). The definition of heterogeneous vs. homogeneous datasets is not provided (R2 Q1, R4 Q2). The importance of diversity in offline RL is not elaborated (R4 Q3). Figure 2’s example does not convincingly show distinct behaviors—policies appear similar (R1). Figure 1 shows little visible difference between the proposed method and SORL, and the connection of Figure 2 to the main text is not given (R4 Q5, Q11).
