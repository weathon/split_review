## Merged Review

### Summary
Four reviewers (R1-R4) gave a score of 3 (weak reject), while one reviewer (R5) gave an 8 (accept). The majority sees the dataset as having significant flaws: the use of Glide docking scores as ground truth is unreliable and limits real-world relevance; the evaluation lacks proper statistical rigor (no error bars, nested cross-validation); the dataset covers only 10 targets and 14K unique molecules; and the paper does not compare against MoleculeNet or show correlation with experimental data. R5, while positive about the dataset’s potential and the experimental insights, also notes the need for wet‑lab validation and explicit limitations. All reviewers agree that the benchmark addresses an important need but disagree on its execution and significance.

### Strengths
- MoleculeCLA avoids label noise, inconsistencies, unbalanced label distribution, and data volume constraints present in MoleculeNet (R1, R3, R5).
- The dataset includes nine properties (chemical, physical, biological) computed via Glide, enabling multi‑faceted evaluation (R1, R2, R3).
- The paper evaluates nine representative deep‑learning models (UniMol, GraphMAE, etc.) with varied architectures and representation approaches (linear probe, fine‑tuning, DTI tasks), yielding meaningful insights (R1, R5).
- The authors provide a relatively comprehensive analysis of the dataset’s chemical space (diversity, statistical description) (R2, R5).
- Valid criticisms of MoleculeNet are presented, motivating the need for a new benchmark (R2, R5).
- Reviewer 5 specifically found the experiments sound, the dataset sizes sufficient, and the insights derived valuable.

### Weaknesses
- **Computational labels not ground truth:** Glide docking scores are based on molecular‑mechanics empirical formulas, not first‑principles calculations like DFT. ML models can never exceed Glide’s accuracy, and the labels do not necessarily correlate with real experimental outcomes (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5). The claim that the dataset is “reliable” is disputed (R5).
- **No comparison to MoleculeNet:** The paper claims improved stability/reliability but provides no direct comparative results on MoleculeNet benchmarks (R1).
- **Limited diversity and scale:** Only 10 protein targets (lower than MoleculeNet); only ~14K unique molecules from 140K total data points; tasks are restricted to docking‑related properties (R1, R2, R3).
- **Severe evaluation flaws:** No nested cross‑validation, no proper hyperparameter selection, no training reruns, and no error bars, confidence intervals, or statistical tests. Reported results could arise by chance (R4). Additionally, baseline comparisons are unfair because pretraining datasets differ in size (e.g., UniMol 19M vs. GraphMAE 2M) (R2).
- **Missing baselines and related datasets:** Benchmark lacks classic methods GEM, Molformer, KGPT, and KANO (R2). Related work omits ExCAPE‑db, LSC, and FS‑Mol (R4).
- **Unsupported claims:** The statement that target selection “ensures broad coverage of potential interactions” (line 168) lacks supporting experiments or citations (R2). The conclusions about the effectiveness of 3D coordinate information are biased because the dataset’s properties rely heavily on 3D geometry (R2). The claimed superiority of UniMAP is not consistent across appendix results (R2).
- **Ignoring inherent field characteristics:** The dataset removes the noise, imbalance, and small data volumes that are intrinsic to drug discovery; a benchmark that eliminates these characteristics may give a biased and unrealistic view of method performance (R4). The design does not reflect real‑world scenarios (R1, R4).
- **Inappropriate citations:** The first paragraph on molecular representation learning neglects pioneering work from the 1990s (Huuskonen 1998) and 2014 (Unterthiner, Dahl, etc.), and misattributes the origin of graph neural networks for molecules (R4).
- **No validation on experimental data:** Models trained on MoleculeCLA have not been tested on wet‑lab datasets; the paper does not show that training on this computational benchmark improves performance on real tasks (R1, R3, R5). A practical verification of correlation with experimental labels is required (R3, R5).
- **No discussion of limitations or future directions:** The paper does not mention MoleculeCLA’s limitations, potential improvements, or plans to expand coverage (R3, R5).
- **Specific reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms:** “Why Glide over Autodock Vina?” (R5); “How do models perform on experimental data?” (R3); “Can the dataset be used for pretraining and then fine‑tuned on experimental labels?” (R3); “What are the dataset sizes of LSC, ExCAPE‑db, FS‑Mol relative to MoleculeCLA?” (R4). These remain unanswered and highlight missing comparisons and experiments.
