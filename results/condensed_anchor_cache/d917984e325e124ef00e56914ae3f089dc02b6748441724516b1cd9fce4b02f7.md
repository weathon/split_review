## Merged Review

### Summary
StreamingT2V proposes an autoregressive framework for generating long videos (80–1200+ frames) from a single text prompt, using a Conditional Attention Module (CAM) for short-term chunk transitions, an Appearance Preservation Module (APM) to maintain scene/object consistency via first-frame CLIP features, and randomized blending to fix noise mismatch when applying a video enhancer chunk-wise. Reviewers acknowledge the method’s ability to produce long, natural videos with high motion and smooth transitions. However, major concerns include: missing standard quantitative comparisons (FVD/FID/LPIPS on MSR-VTT, UCF-101, K600), limited novelty (previous chunk conditioning in ViD-GPT; APM similar to ART-V’s anchored conditioning), insufficient analysis of APM’s content restriction, lack of ablation comparing CAM to alternative designs (W.A.L.T, SEINE), missing training data details, unclear metric definitions (warp error), and formatting/writing issues. One reviewer (score 6) was notably more favorable than the other three (score 5), yet their listed criticisms were similar.

### Strengths
- Autoregressive framework effectively leverages both short-term (CAM) and long-term (APM) dependencies to create seamless long video content with smooth transitions and continuity (Reviewer 3).
- Generated videos are sufficiently long, natural, and exhibit relatively large motion, outperforming existing methods in consistency and motion amount (Reviewer 2).
- Identifies the noise mismatch problem when enhancing long videos via chunk-wise SDEdit and proposes a randomized blending method to address it (Reviewer 2).
- The framework, with CAM and APM, ensures natural continuity and consistent scene/object characteristics across the entire video (Reviewer 3).
- Stable video quality is achieved in the autoregressive generation process (Reviewer 4).
- The overall autoregressive long video generation framework is considered novel (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing standard quantitative benchmarks**: Comparisons on common metrics (FVD, FID, LPIPS) on datasets like MSR-VTT, UCF-101, and K600 are absent (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- **Limited novelty**: Conditioning on previous frame chunks has been explored (ViD-GPT, Gao et al. 2024). The Appearance Preservation Module (APM) is similar to the anchored conditioning method in ART-V (Weng et al. 2024) (Reviewer 2).
- **APM restricts content variation**: The APM, while maintaining object consistency, also limits semantic variation, making it less suitable for very long videos where dynamic content is expected. No metric like CLIP-I is provided to quantify subject consistency. The authors should demonstrate that the model can still generate semantic variation despite APM (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- **CAM design and missing ablations**: The Conditional Attention Module significantly increases parameters. The paper does not justify why a simpler autoregressive prediction (e.g., W.A.L.T with classifier-free guidance, Gupta et al. 2023) was not used, nor does it provide an ablation comparison (e.g., FVD on UCF/K600) against alternative designs like SEINE (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- **Training data details not provided**: The paper states training data is from public sources but gives no URLs or citations (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- **Writing and formatting issues**: Citations should use brackets; Table 6 (likely Table 1) exceeds the page width; the table labeling is incorrect (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- **Missing analysis of error accumulation**: Autoregressive generation suffers from error accumulation; the paper does not discuss how StreamingT2V addresses it or what the upper-bound generation length is (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing analysis of anchor frame influence**: The impact of randomly sampled anchor frames on final generation is not analyzed; it is unclear why all frames from the first chunk cannot serve as anchor frames (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear definitions**: The warp error metric W(V) is not clearly defined (whether it is between anchor frame and all other frames, or between consecutive frames) (Reviewer 4). The fusion operation described in line 299 and Figure 3 requires clarification (Reviewer 4).
