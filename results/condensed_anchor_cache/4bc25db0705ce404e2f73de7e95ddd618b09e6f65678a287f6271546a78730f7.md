## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Q-SFT, an offline RL method for LLMs and VLMs that casts Q-learning as a modified supervised fine-tuning problem. A weighted cross-entropy loss embeds Q-values into token probabilities, allowing the method to leverage pretrained logits without adding new value heads or reinitializing weights. Theoretical analysis provides performance bounds and conservative Q-estimation. Experiments cover text-based games, dialogue, robotic manipulation, and navigation from images, comparing against prior value-based RL and SFT baselines.

### Strengths
- Targets the multi-turn RL scenario for LLMs, which is vital for complex reasoning and agentic use (R1).
- Solid theoretical motivation with guarantees (R1, R3).
- Broad experimental coverage across LLM and VLM tasks, including robotic manipulation and navigation (R1, R3, R4).
- Novel approach: casting Q-learning as a modified cross-entropy loss, distinct from typical value-head methods, and particularly beneficial for large vocabulary/action spaces (R2, R3, R4).
- Straightforward implementation: learning two models (behavior policy and Q-policy) with different objectives, used together at inference (R2).
- Clear motivation: addressing instability of the Q-learning objective (R4).
- Strong empirical performance, sample efficiency, and scalability; performance gap increases with model scale (R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- Conceptual confusion about multi-turn vs. token-level actions: The math treats each token as an action (vocabulary space), but the value function should be per utterance; the multi-turn aspect is merely repeated generation, not directly captured in token-level Q-values. This undermines the claimed multi-turn focus (R2). *Reviewer 2 is notably more critical on this point than others.*
- Experimental setup limitations:
  - Text-based games have small, unambiguous vocabulary and well-defined rewards that do not translate to practical problems (R2).
  - No supervised fine-tuning (SFT) results from prior work are included as direct comparisons in tables; the paper states "offline RL fine-tuning of LLMs does not reliably outperform SFT," but many datasets already show RL outperforming SFT, making the baseline choice unclear (R2).
  - Some tasks train the model from scratch, conflicting with the main motivation of reusing pretrained weights; no explanation is given for why the method works better in that setting (R3).
- Missing ablation studies:
  - Effect of hyperparameter γ: how is γ set in experiments? Sweeping γ would study the weight ratio between demonstration tokens and dummy actions (R1).
  - Impact of behavior policy quality: if the initial behavior policy is near perfect, does the weighted Q-policy merely imitate it? Only a 10% data experiment is shown (R1).
  - What happens if the value head is initialized from scratch but the same loss is used? (R3)
  - Training details (number of steps, etc.) are sparse (R3).
- Method description is too short (half page) and lacks intuition on why it should be more stable than conventional Q-learning (R3).
- The theoretical lower bound (Theorem 4.1) gives p_theta ≥ Q* · π_β; if π_β is small, the policy can be overly conservative. The paper does not discuss how this affects practical performance or exploration. *Reviewer 3 separately asks about bound tightness for large vocabulary* (R4, also R3).
- Inference requires double forward passes (original frozen LM + fine-tuned LM), increasing latency significantly. The paper provides no analysis of computational overhead or applicability in real-time settings like robotic manipulation (R4).
- Memory usage during training: how many models are loaded (main, target, original)? (R3)
- Baselines in tables use different base models; no specification of the base model for each, making comparisons less clear (R3).
- Writing clarity and accuracy issues (R2, R3):
  - Table 1: -2.08 (not -2.11) should be bold in the Wordle column (R3); R2 also flags bolding error.
  - Line 162: `h` not defined (R2).
  - Line 218: prime should be on `a`, not on `argmax` (R2).
  - Typo: "RI" instead of "RL" (R2).
  - Citations missing parentheses (e.g., lines 037, 039) (R2).
  - Equation (2): may need a negative sign (R3).
  - Figure 1 too small and hard to read (R3).
  - Line 153 defines RL as MDP, but what about POMDP? (R3)
  - Figure 3: do error bars correspond to different seeds? (R3)
- *Reviewer 2's rebuttal response* emphasizes the need for a clear definition of "step" vs. "turn," better explanation of scaling potential, and inclusion of comparable SFT baselines.
