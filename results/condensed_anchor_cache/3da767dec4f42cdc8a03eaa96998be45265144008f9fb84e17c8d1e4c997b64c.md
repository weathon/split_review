## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers found the paper’s Filter-Recycle-Interguide (FRI) strategy and the LM/GM design novel, and acknowledged good performance across multiple SNR levels and noise types, with a favorable balance of model size and latency. However, several reviewers raised concerns about the lack of theoretical justification for how the proposed operations separate target and non-target features, missing comparisons with related methods (e.g., TaylorSENet) and datasets (DNS Challenge test set, CHiME-3 real-world recordings), insufficient qualitative analysis, and the need for clearer demonstration that the method addresses the high-similarity speech-noise scenario stated in the motivation.

### Strengths
- **Novel Filter-Recycle-Interguide (FRI) strategy:** A creative approach that recycles both target (speech) and non-target (noise) features and uses them for mutual refinement, offering a fresh perspective beyond typical filtering-only methods.
- **Effective local and global module design:** Local Module (LM) and Global Module (GM) utilize convolutional module outputs and self-attention energy distributions to extract and refine target/non-target features, respectively.
- **Strong performance across diverse conditions:** Experiments on WSJ0-SI84+NOISEX-92 and AVSpeech+AudioSet show substantial improvements in PESQ and SI-SNRi over existing models at various SNR levels (including -5 to 5 dB) and across noise types (including challenging babble noise).
- **Good computational efficiency:** The model achieves competitive results with relatively small size (e.g., 1.74M parameters in the base version) and low latency, as shown in Tables 3 and 4.
- **Comprehensive ablation study:** Multiple ablation experiments (Tables 3, 8, 9) validate the contribution of each key component (LM, GM, IMs) and explore design variants.
- **Careful experimentation and good overall results** (Reviewer 1). The model surpasses comparable models in most settings.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical justification for feature separation:** Several reviewers questioned why the subtraction operation in the LM modules (e.g., Eq. 3) yields noise features and addition yields speech features, rather than the reverse. Without constrained feature value ranges, the model could adjust parameters to treat addition and subtraction identically (Reviewers 1, 4). The role of normalization layers in ensuring this separation is not clearly explained (Reviewers 4, 5). Similarly, it is unclear how the convolution in Eq. (1) reliably separates target and non-target features (Reviewer 1).  
- **Missing comparison with related methods:** The paper does not compare with TaylorSENet [1], which also uses a cascaded feature-refinement strategy (Reviewer 3). More broadly, the baseline selection lacks specificity regarding feature-interaction techniques, making it hard to assess novelty (Reviewer 2).  
- **Insufficient generalization tests:**
  - The model is tested only on synthesized noisy speech. Real-world recordings (e.g., CHiME-3) should be used, with subjective listening or ASR as evaluators since ground-truth would be unavailable (Reviewer 4).  
  - Noise types and SNR levels used in training may overlap with testing; unseen SNR levels and unseen noise conditions were not tested (Reviewer 1).  
  - Reverberation data was included during training but not in test sets; its removal from training should be considered (Reviewer 1).  
- **Missing evaluation on DNS Challenge test set:** The model is trained on DNS Challenge training data but tested on other datasets (WSJ0+NOISEX-92, AVSpeech+AudioSet). Testing on the public DNS Challenge test set would better demonstrate performance (Reviewer 3).  
- **Inconsistent MP-SENet results in the appendix:** The reported VoiceBank-DEMAND results for MP-SENet differ from those in the official repository (https://github.com/yxlu-0102/MP-SENet), and these discrepancies are not acknowledged (Reviewer 3).  
- **Inadequate demonstration of the high-similarity motivation:** The paper’s starting point is that “when noise and speech components are highly similar, it is difficult for the SE model to learn effective discrimination patterns,” but neither the methodology analysis nor the experiments explicitly show how the FRI strategy addresses this challenge (Reviewer 2).  
- **Lack of qualitative and interpretability analysis:** No visualizations or case studies are provided to explain how the model separates features or why it outperforms baselines, reducing insight into the method’s inner workings (Reviewers 2, 3). The visualization in Figure 3 should also include better-performing methods like MP-SENet (Reviewer 3).  
- **Ablation study and presentation issues:**
  - Table 3 (LM ablation) is not referenced in the text (Reviewer 3).  
  - It is unclear why IM-E Modification 3 (d) has smaller model size (1.64M) than the original (1.74M) despite removing only a Sigmoid operation (Reviewer 4).  
  - The question of whether incorporating both IM-E Modification 2 and IM-D Modification 2 simultaneously would yield better performance with fewer parameters was raised (Reviewer 3).  
- **Unclear experimental choices:**
  - Why do many methods have identical STOI values in Table 6? (Reviewer 1)  
  - Loss hyperparameters (0.2, 0.9, 0.1, 0.3 in Eq. 14) were not optimized systematically, and it is unclear whether they were tuned while looking at test tables (reviewer 1).  
  - The paper does not specify whether PESQ is narrowband or wideband in the appendix (Reviewer 3).  
- **Minor writing and notation issues:** Misuse of “convolution operation” instead of “convolutional operation” (Reviewer 3); dimension symbol \(L\) not explained (Reviewer 3); possible typo in Eq. (6): “\(f_n = f_4 + f_{2,n} + f_{n,t}\)” should be “\(f_n = f_4 + f_{2,n} + f_{3,n}\)” (Reviewer 4).  
- **Novelty concerns:** The ideas appear collected from previous CV publications; the introduction should better highlight novel technical contributions (Reviewer 1). The energy-based attention module’s originality is unclear and lacks comparison with existing energy-based attention methods (Reviewer 5).  
- **Minor questions requiring clarification:** How does the mask usage in Fig. 4 (a) differ from a direct multiplication? (Reviewer 4). How does the “Network for speech extraction” in Fig. 1 correspond to the modules in Fig. 2? (Reviewer 4).
