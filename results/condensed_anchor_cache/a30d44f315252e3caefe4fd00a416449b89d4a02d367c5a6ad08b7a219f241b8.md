## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a Region Transformer (Regformer) for single image deraining, built on a Region Transformer Block (RTB) consisting of a Region Masked Attention (RMA) mechanism and a Mixed Gate Forward Block (MGFB). The key motivation is to explicitly process rain-affected and unaffected regions independently and consider their combined effects, improving deraining by avoiding confusion between rain streaks and background. Experiments claim state-of-the-art results on multiple datasets with fewer parameters and comparable compute to existing methods.

### Strengths
- **Motivation and design**: The paper is well-written, well-organized, and clearly presented. It provides a strong motivation by first pointing out the importance of explicitly and individually processing rain-affected and unaffected regions. The design of region masked attention is simple and effective.
- **Performance**: The proposed method consistently outperforms previous baselines on multiple benchmark datasets, achieving new state-of-the-art results. It does so with fewer parameters and equivalent computation costs compared to Restormer and DRSformer. Visual results are much better, especially in regions with fine details covered by raindrops, and the method performs well on real-world cases.
- **Evaluation and support**: The experimental evaluation is adequate and the results convincingly support the main claims. Ablation studies verify the contributions of the RMA and MGFB components.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and region‑mask justification**: The region‑based attention mechanism has been used in other areas before, and the paper does not clearly demonstrate its specific benefit for deraining. The crucial mechanism for identifying true non‑rain features and generating high‑quality region masks is insufficiently explained. One reviewer (score 5) questions whether the approach has advanced beyond prior work; another (score 6) requests more insight into how the features of unaffected regions guide restoration, and whether the mask generation can be visually verified.
- **Mask generation clarity**: The mask generation process needs detailed clarification. The textual description in Sec. 3.2.1 appears to conflict with Figure 3. It is unclear how a 3×3 convolution produces the three types of region masks, and the relationship between shallow features and decoder features in the mask computation is not sufficiently described to allow replication. Adding intermediate feature-map visualizations of the masks would strengthen the paper.
- **Generalization to real‑world data**: One reviewer (score 6) expresses serious concern that the method may only be effective on synthetic datasets. On the real‑world SPA‑Data dataset, Regformer gains only 0.06 dB PSNR over DRSformer, which is marginal. Testing on a more realistic and diverse dataset (e.g., WeatherStream) is recommended to validate robustness. (Other reviewers do not raise this specific concern, but it is a significant minority point.)
- **Ablation and completeness**: Ablation studies (similar to Table 3) should be provided on additional datasets, especially SPA‑Data, to ensure the contributions are consistent. More perceptual metrics (e.g., LPIPS) should be added to Table 1 to reflect restoration quality from different aspects. Inference time comparisons with baselines are also requested.
- **Technical details and implementation**: Several points need clarification or justification:
  - Equation (6): the multiplication of element‑wise additions under the large Π is not clearly explained—why is multiplication used, and how should it be performed?
  - The meaning of “n and k_i are all parameters” (last line of page 6) is unclear.
  - The specific effect of the parameter \(k_i\) in the MGFB should be discussed.
  - The implementation details of the baseline methods used in Table 1 (e.g., how their scores were obtained) need to be described fully.
