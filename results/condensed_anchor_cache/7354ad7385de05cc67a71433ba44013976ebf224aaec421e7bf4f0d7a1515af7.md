- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes Nash-GBML, which formulates gradient-based meta-learning as a Single-Leader Multi-Follower (SLMF) game with a Nash game among task-specific parameters at the inner level. The authors introduce penalty terms (centroid and robust) to model interactions among tasks, provide convergence analysis under convex assumptions, and evaluate on sinusoid regression, image completion, and few-shot classification. Reviewers agree the game-theoretic perspective is novel and potentially interesting, but find the motivation unclear, the framework unnecessary for the proposed penalties, the theoretical assumptions too strong, and the experimental gains marginal. The paper is also criticized for poor clarity, missing comparisons and related works, and limited algorithmic contribution.

### Strengths

- The use of a game-theoretic formulation (SLMF + Nash game) for meta-learning is novel and understudied; it could help bring meta-learning to the attention of a different community.
- The authors demonstrate their method on non-traditional tasks (image completion), which is encouraging and goes beyond standard benchmarks (mini-ImageNet, CIFAR-FS).
- The approach is tested with two meta-learning algorithms and shows minor gains in both.
- Experimental results are complete (Reviewer 4).
- Reviewer 3 notes the game-theoretic approach is a potential interesting direction and that the paper demonstrates some experimental advantage.

### Weaknesses

- **Unclear motivation and formulation:** The paper claims to model “interactions among tasks” but never defines what this means concretely. The penalty terms are very similar to those in prior meta-learning works that assume independent tasks (e.g., [1,2,3] from Reviewer 2). The motivation for why tasks should interact in standard few-shot benchmarks (where tasks are statistically independent) is missing. No application scenario for non-independent tasks is provided.
- **Game-theoretic framework is distracting and unnecessary:** Multiple reviewers find that the game-theoretic language does not add value. The proposed penalties and convergence results can be motivated without it, and the framework obscures the exposition. Reviewer 1 asks: what benefit does the game theory offer for the proposed penalties?
- **Theoretical results are limited:** The convexity assumptions (for Theorems 3.2 and 3.3) are too strong—gradient-based meta-learning is non-convex even for linear regression. The assumptions and notations in the theorems are unclear and poorly defined (Reviewer 2). The theory only shows correctness, not any advantage over existing methods.
- **Empirical improvements are marginal and not statistically significant:** In most results, the baselines fall within the confidence intervals of Nash-GBML despite having one fewer hyperparameter. Reviewer 3 notes “some experimental advantage” but Reviewers 1 and 2 find the improvements very limited. Reviewer 4 questions why the method achieves both better robustness and better average performance (Tables 3,4) when the paper claims a trade-off.
- **Missing comparisons and baselines:** If the penalty terms are meant to address robustness, the paper should compare with existing robust meta-learning methods (Reviewer 4). Many related works are omitted, including probabilistic meta-learning [1-3], meta-learning theory with and without i.i.d. assumptions [4-9], convergence of meta-learning [10,11], and robust meta-learning [12] (see Reviewer 2’s list).
- **Algorithmic novelty is weak:** The Nash-equilibrium approach in Algorithm 2 is not clearly different from standard gradient descent. The overall contribution to algorithm design is limited, as the Stackelberg game interpretation of MAML is well-known.
- **Clarity issues:** The paper is poorly written with vague claims, undefined notations, and poorly formatted theorems. Figure 2’s green “interactions among tasks” are not explained, and using a log-scale y-axis could improve readability.
- **Code is not provided.**
- **Meta-test stage confusion:** If the inner level involves iteration among tasks, it is unclear how the method handles a single new task at meta-test time (Reviewer 4).
- **Questions overlapping with criticisms:** Reviewers ask for clarification on: the difference between the traditional SLMF formulation and the proposed one; the exact definition of the convex assumptions; the meaning of \( w(\cdot) \) in eq (13); whether the penalty targets average or worst-case (line 208); and whether it is accurate to say traditional meta-learning is modeled as an SLMF game (suggested: present as this paper’s formulation, not as a fact).
