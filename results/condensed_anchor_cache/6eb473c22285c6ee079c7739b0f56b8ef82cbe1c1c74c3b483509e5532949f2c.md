## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents a novel rank-based latent causal discovery algorithm (RLCD) that identifies Markov equivalence classes of directed acyclic graphs with both measured and latent variables, in linear causal models. The authors establish necessary and sufficient conditions for identifiability of latent structural patterns using rank information of the covariance matrix, and prove asymptotic correctness of the algorithm. The framework is more general than existing methods that restrict latent variable patterns. Experimental results on synthetic and real-world personality data demonstrate efficacy.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a very important problem in causal discovery – identifying graphs with latent variables.
- The graph pattern considered is quite general compared to existing methods; the approach allows causally-related hidden variables almost everywhere in the network.
- The learning algorithm has theoretical guarantees (asymptotic correctness) under certain graphical conditions.
- The paper is very well written, clearly structured, and a pleasure to read (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Claims are well justified (R1).
- Results are technically sound and every result is accompanied by examples that aid understanding (R4).
- The real-world example is convincing (R2).
- The approach is an original extension of previous work on hierarchical structure and has potentially significant application (R3).
- Differences from related work (e.g., PC, FCI, hierarchical methods) are clearly explained according to one reviewer (R3), while another reviewer (R1) found the description of related work insufficient – see Weaknesses.
- The introduction is neat and the contribution is expressed clearly (R4).
- Experimental studies are conducted extensively, providing strong empirical benefits (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Assumptions hard to verify and not justified**: The assumptions (Condition 1 and 2) are on unmeasured latent variables and graphical structure; it is unclear how one would verify them before carrying out the graph search procedure. The authors do not provide sufficient justification for why these assumptions are relatively weak compared to previous work (R1). [R1]
- **Relation to previous work lacks detail for one reviewer**: R1 states that the Related Work (moved to appendix) does not contain enough information to judge the exact contribution. In particular, Hier.rank is not described in sufficient detail, leaving questions about what specifically allows identification of children of latents and mediator latents as opposed to this work. Another reviewer (R3) found the differences clearly explained, indicating a disagreement. [R1 vs R3]
- **Potential inaccuracy in claim about FCI**: The paper claims that algorithms like FCI “rely on the assumption that all hidden variables are independent of each other.” R3 questions this, noting that any maximal ancestral graph (MAG) is learnable from conditional independence using FCI, and a MAG is also valid if obtained from marginalizing over dependent latent variables. This claim may be inaccurate. [R3]
- **Missing details in algorithm description**: The steps in Algorithm 1 after Phases 1–3 are not described in detail; it is not clear how cluster information is aggregated after the first three phases or how additional information is added into the CI skeleton. The output of Phase 3 (𝒢'') and how it leads to the Markov equivalence class is confusing (R3). [R3]
- **Evaluation insufficient**: The experimental section appears sparse; it is not clear why RLCD performs so much better than competitors based solely on F1 scores. It would be helpful to see the output of different algorithms on a running example (R3). [R3]
- **Use of F1 metrics questioned**: R1 suggests that SID and SHD are more natural metrics for graphical evaluations. [R1]
- **Method limited to linear SCM**: The proposed method is confined to linear structural causal models. It is unclear if the framework can contribute when variables are mixtures of discrete and continuous, or follow nonlinear SCM (R4). R2 also raises a related question: Corollary 1 states the algorithm degenerates to PC when there are no latent variables, but this is under the linearity assumption; if the truth is not linear, PC is asymptotically correct but the proposed algorithm may not be. [R2, R4]
- **FCI skeleton F1 score of 0.00 unexplained**: R3 questions how FCI can obtain a skeleton F1 of 0.00 even for observed variables (Tables 2 and 3 suggest no edge got correct). This requires explanation. [R3]
- **Questions about contribution novelty**: R1 asks whether Lemma 10 and Theorem 4 are contributions or have been stated by previous work; a reference is missing for Lemma 10. This raises a concern about the novelty of certain theoretical results. [R1]
- **Need for examples of graph operators**: R2 requests examples to explain the minimal-graph operator and the skeleton operator, to help readers understand what equivalent graphs they entail. [R2]
- **Minor issues** (R3): many textual errors (e.g., duplicated references, word choice “leverage”, repetition “mainly three-fold”, unusual constructions, “uesful”, missing definitions of “triangle structure”, typos); Algorithm 1 and Figure 3 introduced too early in the paper; Table 2 appears before the experimental section; page 9 has an unfinished thought. [R3]
- **Running time comparison**: R4 is curious about running speed and time complexity compared to FCI or LiNGAM. [R4]
