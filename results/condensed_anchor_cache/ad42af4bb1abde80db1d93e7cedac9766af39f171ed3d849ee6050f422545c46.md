## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GOttack, an adversarial attack framework on Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) that manipulates graph orbits (topological patterns) to generate subtle yet effective structural perturbations. It narrows the search space by targeting nodes belonging to specific orbits, achieving high misclassification rates and better time efficiency (~55% of the fastest competitor). The method is evaluated on multiple datasets, GNN architectures (GCN, GIN, GraphSAGE), and four defenses (RGCN, GCN-Jaccard, GCN-SVD, MedianGCN). Reviewers find the topological angle novel and the complexity-motivated design interesting, but raise significant concerns about scalability, outdated defenses, limited improvement over baselines, lack of evaluation on large graphs, and insufficient validation of theoretical claims. One reviewer (rating 8) was markedly more positive, praising the new angle, transferability, and qualitative insights, while still noting a severe limitation regarding the indirect (surrogate) attack setup.

### Strengths
- **Novel use of graph orbits and graphlets** for adversarial attacks on GNNs – provides a topology-driven angle rarely explored in the literature (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Comprehensive experimentation** across five graphs, three GNNs, and four defense mechanisms (R1).
- **Time efficiency**: GOttack completes training in approximately 55% of the time required by the fastest competitor (PRBCD), and the paper targets the complexity challenge that is often neglected in adversarial attack work (R1, R3).
- **Transferability**: Found perturbations transfer well between different GNN architectures (R2).
- **Well structured and easy to follow** except for some details (R2, R3).
- **Qualitative insights** via explainers (e.g., GNNExplainer) in addition to misclassification rates (R2).
- **Theoretical analysis (Theorem 1)** relating orbit selection to hitting times is interesting (R3).
- **Comparison with several attack baselines** including PRBCD, Metattack, and others (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Indirect (non-adaptive) attack using a surrogate**: The attack is evasion-to-poisoning transfer from a GCN surrogate, which is a severe limitation. It is not evaluated against adaptive attacks (e.g., directly attacking GIN/GraphSAGE with PRBCD) except for GCN. This limitation is not prominently discussed (R2). One reviewer (R2, who otherwise rated the paper highly) considered this a severe weakness.
- **Outdated and incomplete defense evaluation**: Only RGCN, GCN-Jaccard, GCN-SVD, and MedianGCN (all pre-2021) are tested. Missing more recent and effective defenses such as GCNGuard, GCORN, GARNET, and SG-GSR. Moreover, the budgeted attack results focus on undefended models; the evaluation should primarily assess attack success against defended models, as undefended GNNs are known to be vulnerable (R2, R3, R4).
- **Scalability concerns due to orbit discovery**: Orbit discovery on graphlets is extremely time-consuming. Although it is a preprocessing step, the authors do not demonstrate scaling to large graphs (e.g., OGB-arxiv, Reddit, Products, Papers100M). No runtime for the preprocessing is reported, and it is unclear whether the times in Table 4 include preprocessing. There is no discussion of dynamic graph updates. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Modest and inconsistent improvement over baselines**: In many cases in Table 2, GOttack outperforms baselines by less than 1% – an improvement smaller than the reported standard deviations. This raises questions about whether the ranking would hold with a different random seed. Furthermore, GOttack underperforms compared to baselines in most cases when the budget exceeds 1 (see Tables 9–13). (R4)
- **Ineffectiveness on heterophilic graphs**: The method implicitly assumes homophily (line 256) and appears ineffective on heterophilic graphs as shown in Appendix D. (R4)
- **Poor scalability discussion regarding PRBCD**: The paper states that PRBCD has quadratic scalability (Section B.2), which seems imprecise. PRBCD’s properties (e.g., linear-time approximation) are ignored in the comparison. (R2)
- **Over-reliance on specific orbits (15 and 18)**: It is unclear whether orbits 15 and 18 are special regardless of the number of graphlet nodes (k=5). The authors do not discuss whether the method would work if these structures are absent from the target graph. (R1, R2)
- **Lack of experimental validation for Theorem 1**: The theoretical claims about the effect of choosing nodes in orbits 15/18 (hitting times) are not empirically validated. An experiment comparing different orbits would strengthen the paper. (R3)
- **Topological analysis independent of node features**: The attack ignores node features, implicitly assuming they are sufficiently discriminative for misclassification to succeed. (R2)
- **Minor issues**: The term "universal" may be misleading; the attack focuses on small budgets (1–5) without relating them to node degree. The reliability of GNN explanations is debated (R2). Metattack is mentioned in related work but differences are not discussed (R2). The bilevel nature of poisoning (Eq. 2) is inaccurately described (R2). Standard deviations are only in the appendix; they should be in main tables (R3). The significant difference (over 30%) between GOttack-GCN and GOttack-GSAGE on BlogCatalog in Table 2 is unexplained (R4).
