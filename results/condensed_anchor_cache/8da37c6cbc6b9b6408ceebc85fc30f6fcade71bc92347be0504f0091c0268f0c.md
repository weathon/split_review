## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Self-Distillation Through Time (SDTT), a training strategy that reduces the number of sampling steps required for discrete diffusion language models. SDTT iteratively distills a teacher model into a student that predicts ahead in time, halving the number of steps per round. Starting from a pre-trained discrete diffusion model, the student is trained to match the teacher’s token distribution at a later denoising timestep. Through multiple rounds of SDTT, the model can generate text with up to 32–64× fewer inference steps (e.g., 32 tokens simultaneously) while exceeding autoregressive models in text quality and on the LAMBADA benchmark. Wall-clock generation speed reaches up to 8× faster than an AR model with KV caching (without caching; faster with caching). The method is evaluated up to 860M parameters, and the authors promise to release the model. Extensive ablations justify key design choices.

### Strengths
- **Improved generation quality and speed**: Achieves remarkable improvements in both quality and efficiency, with up to 32–64× fewer steps, 8× faster wall-clock time than AR with KV caching, and better text quality / LAMBADA scores (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Clarity of writing**: Well‑written with clear explanations of methodologies and results (Reviewer 1). Note: other reviewers find presentation lacking (see Weaknesses).
- **Simple and intuitive method**: SDTT trains a student reconstruction model to predict ahead in time given a teacher, and can be applied iteratively (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Thorough ablations and design choices**: Extensive experiments justify the divergence measure, number of steps compressed per round, and other components; solid empirical performance (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Scaling to 860M and model release**: Demonstrates effectiveness up to 860M parameters; promise to release the model is a valuable contribution for future research (Reviewer 3).
- **Strong empirical evidence**: Large set of experiments shows significant decoding speed improvement; compressing discrete diffusion sampling to this degree is an important contribution (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Valuable adaptation of existing ideas**: Successfully extends progressive distillation techniques to discrete diffusion language models, achieving faster generation with improved quality (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient validation of speed claims**: The settings for the claimed 8× speedup (Section 4.4, Figure 2b) are unclear (e.g., whether based on 32 steps, 1.3B, batch size 8). It is not clarified if quality (generation perplexity) is comparable between 1.3B DLM and AR models (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing analysis of batch size and model size impact**: How batch size affects memory usage and decoding speed for both diffusion and AR models; how model size impacts generation speed (Reviewer 1).
- **Effect of flash-attention not addressed**: Whether the speed advantage over AR persists when flash-attention is employed (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited downstream tasks**: Only LAMBADA is used; math reasoning and other tasks are encouraged. Conditional likelihoods/perplexity alongside MAUVE are also missing (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Self-distillation effect on diversity not examined**: Potential model collapse from training on recursively generated data is not discussed (Reviewer 1).
- **Decision to set the number of rounds to 7 is not justified**: Why 7 rounds, and why not progressively grow the distillation step size (Reviewers 1, 3). The number of distillation iterations is only inferable from `dt`, causing confusion (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing related citations**: (1) A Reparameterized Discrete Diffusion Model for Text Generation; (2) Diffusion of Thoughts: Chain-of-Thought Reasoning in Diffusion Language Models (Reviewer 1).
- **Presentation and writing issues** (Reviewers 2, 4):
  - Notation: Equation 3 could be written as `log p(x|z_t)` instead of an inner product; the efficient computation in the fully‑factored discrete case is not mentioned (Reviewer 2).
  - Ambiguous terminology: “steps” (sampling, decoding, training) used inconsistently (Reviewer 2). “Rounds” used both for distillation iterations and for denoising steps within a round (Figure 2a) (Reviewer 4).
  - Writing organization: Iterated SDTT (multiple rounds) is introduced only in experiments, not in Methods; should be moved there and formally described (e.g., telescoping sum of divergences) (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
  - `dt` and `Δ` undefined – need formal definitions; in MDLM `Δ` denotes a simplex, not a timestep (Reviewer 2).
  - Inconsistent x‑axis labels: some figures use “sampling step size” (1/512), others “number of sampling steps” (e.g., 1024). Unifying to “number of sampling steps” would clarify (Reviewer 4).
  - Figure references: Are Figures 2 and 3 referred to in the body text? (Reviewer 2)
  - Typos: Line 96: “(MDLM.)” → “(MDLM).”; Line 785: “the the MAUVE” and “resuts” (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient comparison to prior work**: The paper does not adequately discuss how SDTT differs from progressive distillation for continuous diffusion models [1]. The core idea (student mimics teacher at a later timestep) is conceptually similar, and specific novelties for discrete diffusion should be highlighted (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Missing conditional likelihoods/perplexity**: MAUVE alone is insufficient; conditional log‑likelihood or perplexity should be reported (Reviewer 2).
- **Odd distillation behavior not explained**: Distilling more than 2 steps at a time hurts the student; more training steps per round also hurt (Figure 11). Why this happens is not discussed (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Crucial figure missing**: A figure showing latency vs. generative perplexity is essential. The claim of reproducing a 4× improvement (Deschenaux & Gulcehre, 2024) while retaining quality should be visualized. Section 4.4 should be the first result discussed (Reviewer 2).
- **Surprising MSE performance**: Using MSE loss yields better generative perplexity; the paper does not discuss why (e.g., relationship to chi‑square divergence) (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of deterministic mapping distinction**: The paper claims SDTT does not rely on DDIM, but discrete diffusion already lacks such deterministic processes; the implication should be elaborated (Reviewer 4).
