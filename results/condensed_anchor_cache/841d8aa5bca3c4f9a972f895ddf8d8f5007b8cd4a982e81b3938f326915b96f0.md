## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a data augmentation method that maps data to a statistical manifold via an energy-based model on posets, projects to a lower-dimensional submanifold, samples using kernel density estimation, and reconstructs augmented points via a backward projection algorithm using nearest neighbors. The method is presented as an interpretable alternative to black-box generative models, with motivation from information geometry and Riemannian geometry.

### Strengths
- The approach is highly original and novel (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- The method is motivated by information geometry and provides necessary background (Reviewer 1).
- The method is interpretable, standing out from black-box generative models; interpretability is demonstrated on MNIST (Reviewers 2,3).
- The paper is mostly well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 2,4); Figures 1 and 2 help build intuition (Reviewer 3).
- The approach is applicable to any kind of structured data, giving it broad potential (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
**Method clarity, theoretical grounding, and motivation**
- Several reviewers found the method section difficult to parse without specialized background; a global outline and earlier introduction of Figure 2 are needed (Reviewer 2). Substantially more background on information and Riemannian geometry is required (Reviewer 3).
- The paper should use a concrete numeric example (e.g., Example 4.2) as a running example throughout Section 4 to clarify each step (Reviewer 3).
- The theoretical grounding promised in the abstract is not delivered; the reconstruction ultimately uses the closest element in an arbitrary norm, raising questions about the claimed interpretability (Reviewer 4).
- The necessity of the Riemannian geometry framework is questioned: Example 4.2 ends with an embedding that is simply a normalized flattened tensor; the projection to a flat base submanifold and the reconstruction via a “local data submanifold” are not explained properly (Reviewer 4). One reviewer contends the whole approach could be described with simpler linear algebra (Reviewer 4).
- Key design choices are undiscussed: e.g., when modeling with posets or using the many-body tensor approximation is challenging, and why alternatives were not chosen (Reviewer 3).

**Experimental validation is insufficient**  
- All experiments are limited to small-scale datasets (MNIST with 500 samples, three UCI datasets with <10k samples and <1000 dimensions). There is no evaluation on higher-resolution natural images (e.g., CIFAR-10, CUB) or beyond 2D images (e.g., audio) (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- Evaluations only use a linear classifier on MNIST; no demonstration of compatibility or improvement with standard data augmentation (flipping, cropping, color jitter), deep classifiers (e.g., ResNet-18), or in combination with standard image-level perturbations (e.g., Gaussian noise, blurring) (Reviewer 2).
- No comparison to a simple baseline like PCA (Reviewer 4).
- Performance gains are marginal and not statistically significant: on MNIST, accuracy improves from 81.8% to 83.4% (0.7% over AE’s 82.7%); on Connectionist Bench the gain is 6.6%, but on Taiwanese Bankruptcy Prediction and Musk the method performs worse than AE (Reviewer 1). The improvements may be within error margins (Reviewer 4). One reviewer notes that any test accuracy gain could be due to the blurring effect, which acts as a standard data augmentation, rather than the proposed geometric machinery (Reviewer 3). Another reviewer questions whether blurring is desirable, since it is often considered a failure mode of VAEs (Reviewer 1).
- There is no quantitative analysis of how augmentation performance changes with the number of generated samples (Reviewer 1).
- Visual quality of generated images is poor and highly similar to the closest training examples, suggesting overfitting; no improvement is seen when increasing the submanifold dimension (Reviewer 3).  
- One reviewer states the experiments are “very nonconvincing” and “cannot be said with any statistical significance” (Reviewer 4).

**Scalability and applicability**  
- The method requires partial ordering, which cannot capture invariant or equivariant structures (Reviewer 1).  
- It is unclear whether the method scales computationally to larger, more complex datasets (Reviewer 1). Practical applicability in terms of performance gain and computational cost is not addressed (Reviewer 1).

**Minor issues**  
- Reference to Kingma & Welling should be 2014 (Reviewer 3).  
- In line 395, clarify whether “100 images per digit” was intended (Reviewer 3).

**Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 5) is more positive, praising the originality and potential, while another (score 1) is highly critical of the theoretical contribution and experimental rigor. The remaining reviewers (scores 3) are cautiously negative, citing insufficient validation and clarity.
