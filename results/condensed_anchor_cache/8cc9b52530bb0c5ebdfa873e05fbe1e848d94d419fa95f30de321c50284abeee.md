## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Comprehensive Accurate Assessment (Compass), a docking evaluation metric that integrates PoseCheck (strain energy, steric clashes, interactions) and AA-Score (empirical binding affinity). Compass is used during inference to assess DiffDock poses and in a fine-tuning paradigm that adds a Compass-based loss (LAN-MSE) to DiffDock’s training. Experiments on the PDBBind dataset analyze dataset noise and show that fine-tuning with Compass yields limited RMSD improvement but better physicochemical/bioactivity favorability.

### Strengths
- Developing representative and comprehensive docking evaluation metrics is crucial; Compass offers a valuable contribution in this direction (Reviewer 1, score 5).
- The study reveals limitations of the PDBBind dataset, which is widely used in docking research (Reviewer 1, score 5).
- Integrating Compass with DiffDock (a SOTA method) is practical and insightful; it shows potential to improve specific aspects of docking quality beyond RMSD (Reviewer 1, score 5).
- Incorporating stronger physical priors about ligand quality is a reasonable approach to improving docking quality (Reviewer 3, score 3).
- The idea of combining existing pose quality evaluation techniques into a unified fine-tuning framework is novel (Reviewer 4, score 3) and has potential as an important component (Reviewer 3, score 3; Reviewer 4, score 3).
- The paper presents a new paradigm for fine-tuning with Compass Scores encompassing binding affinity energy, strain energy, and steric clashes (Reviewer 2, score 1 – minority more positive note).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and method concerns:**
  - Compass combines two existing metrics (PoseCheck, AA-Score), so its novelty is limited (Reviewer 1, score 5).
  - LAN-MSE loss function introduces no significant new insights (Reviewer 1, score 5).
  - The definition of LAN-MSE is confusing: (a) Equation 1’s first two conditions (favorable/unfavorable) are mutually exclusive, making the third and fourth conditions unreachable (Reviewer 2, score 1; Reviewer 4, score 3). (b) Taking absolute value of log is problematic if y can be positive/negative; it may incentivize prediction and true value to diverge (Reviewer 4, score 3). (c) The definition of `buffer` as a constant but then used as a function of `|y|` leads to a discontinuous `f(|y|)` (Reviewer 4, score 3). (d) Calling the stability of log a “theorem” is overstated (Reviewer 4, score 3).
  - The statement “calculated based on translational, rotational and torsional movements” (near Eq 3) is unclear (Reviewer 4, score 3).
  - The term “Non-Gradient-Tracked-Loss” (line 351) is unexplained (Reviewer 4, score 3).

- **Implementation flaw and fine-tuning effectiveness:**
  - The Compass loss is implemented as a non-gradient-tracked penalizer: it is constant in PyTorch’s computational graph, so its gradient is zero during backpropagation, rendering the fine-tuning meaningless (Reviewer 2, score 1 – code inspection; Reviewer 3, score 3 – source code confirmation).
  - The most likely explanation for observed fine-tuning improvements (especially on a small training/test set) is statistical artifacts; no assessment of statistical significance is provided (Reviewer 3, score 3). Further scaling to more structures could clarify (Reviewer 4, score 3).

- **Insufficient evaluation and support for claims:**
  - Inference mode is described but not evaluated; no results are provided showing how Compass improves pose selection over DiffDock’s own confidence model (Reviewer 3, score 3; Reviewer 4, score 3). What is the point of inference mode? (Reviewer 3, score 3)
  - The key claim that improving physicochemical and bioactivity favorability is beneficial is not adequately supported (Reviewer 2, score 1). The authors only provide a few anecdotal examples without large-scale evaluation (Reviewer 2, score 1; Reviewer 3, score 3).
  - The conclusion that many crystal structures in PDBBind have unreasonable conformations is based solely on PoseCheck, which itself is insufficiently validated (Reviewer 2, score 1).
  - AA-Score is an empirical scoring function; its correlation with experimental binding affinity for crystal structures is low, and the authors do not address how the discrepancy between predicted AA-score and actual binding affinity is handled (Reviewer 2, score 1).

- **Specific technical problems with PoseCheck:**
  - PoseCheck uses element-based van der Waals radii for steric clash detection, which is overly simplistic for crystal structures and not precise enough to reliably determine favorable physicochemical properties (Reviewer 2, score 1).

- **Missing details and unclear methodology:**
  - Equation 1: what does “stop” mean? Which pose is returned? (Reviewer 4, score 3)
  - The test set of 261 structures is not listed; selection criteria are not explained (Reviewer 2, score 1).
  - How Equation 7 (CS_total score) is optimized during fine-tuning of a diffusion model is not explained: (1) how to train the per-step diffusion process with a loss only computable on the endstate; (2) how to make a loss differentiable on the submanifold when Compass scores are computed in Euclidean space (Reviewer 4, score 3).

- **Limited performance improvement:**
  - The reported fine-tuning improvements are marginal: 4.21% → 4.98% and 11.38% → 11.42% (Reviewer 4, score 3). Stronger evidence for the benefit is needed (Reviewer 4, score 3).
  - DiffDock’s performance on this version of the PDBBind test set appears much lower than on the traditionally used test set (Reviewer 4, score 3). No explanation is given.

- **Hyperparameter tuning concerns:**
  - Only a limited number of weights and learning rates were tested; an existing hyperparameter optimization tool would have been more efficient (Reviewer 1, score 5).
  - The weighted-sum combination of LAN-MSE with DiffDock’s loss increases training complexity because weights require careful tuning (Reviewer 1, score 5).

- **Missing comprehensive comparison:**
  - How representative/comprehensive is Compass compared to the broader landscape of docking metrics? What are its limitations? (Reviewer 1, score 5)
