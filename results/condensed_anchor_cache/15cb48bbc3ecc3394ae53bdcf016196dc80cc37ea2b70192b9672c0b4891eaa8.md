## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Structured Inverse-Free Natural Gradient Descent (SINGD) to address memory inefficiency and numerical instability of KFAC. It makes KFAC inverse-free (inspired by INGD) to enable low-precision training, and imposes sparse Kronecker-factored structures on INGD to reduce memory costs. The authors provide a theoretical connection between INGD and KFAC, and show on transformer- and convolution-based models that SINGD is more memory-efficient and numerically robust than KFAC.

### Strengths
- Well-written and organized; provides a clear and detailed description of KFAC and INGD methods. (R1, R2, R4)
- Establishes a theoretical bridge between INGD and KFAC, closing the gap between these two methods. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Method is well-motivated and explained; makes KFAC inverse-free, eliminating costly matrix inverses and enabling low‑precision training. (R1, R2, R4)
- Imposes Kronecker‑factored structure on INGD (SINGD) and leverages sparse structures (e.g., block diagonal, hierarchical) to reduce memory footprint. (R1, R2, R4)
- Provides a plausible reason for the better empirical performance of INGD compared to KFAC and IKFAC. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- One reviewer finds the contribution mostly incremental – combining existing algorithms without demonstrating completely new elements. In particular, the original INGD already discussed sparse group structures in K and C, so imposing a Kronecker‑factored structure on INGD is seen as trivial and not a substantial advance. (R4; other reviewers are more positive but also note limited novelty: R3 states the inverse‑free part is undermined by INGD and the structured part lacks depth.)
- Empirical evaluation is weak:
  - Visualization makes it hard to see how proposed methods compare to Adam near the end of training. (R3)  
  - Evaluations should use settings where KFAC outperforms Adam (as in previous work [1]) to convincingly demonstrate the method’s benefits. (R3)  
  - Fig. 6 should show test error vs. training time, and peak memory for the four networks should be reported. (R1)  
  - Overall training times compared to non‑matrix‑free variants and to SGD/Adam are missing. (R2)  
  - From the figures, methods do not outperform Adam in test‑loss convergence; it is unclear what advantage SINGD offers over Adam. (R2)  
  - Comparisons with other state‑of‑the‑art optimizers like Shampoo and NGPlus are missing. (R1)
- Insufficient details on sparse Kronecker factors, derivations, proofs, and the hierarchical approach:
  - Some derivations and proofs are relegated to the appendix; they should be in the main text for a more natural presentation. (R1)  
  - The hierarchical approach is not well explained, even with the supplementary material. (R2)  
  - A specific example of how to compute the projection \(\hat{\Pi}_K\) and how to use the structures \(\hat{\mathcal{L}}\) is needed. (R1)  
  - For CNN, it is unclear how model weights with more than two dimensions are merged into two dimensions and the resulting dimensionality. (R3)  
  - Papers like [1] suggest for CNN one Kronecker factor can be small and dense while the other is large but diagonal; this structure should be considered/compared, and setting the same sparsity parameter for both factors may not be optimal. (R3)  
  - The simplification of the NGD update for BLR (end of page 4) needs explanation. (R1)  
  - The condition that \(O(\beta_1^2)\) is “small enough” in Theorem 1 needs clarification. (R1)  
  - The block diagonal structure is straightforward and existing; the paper should show that the proposed hierarchical structure solidly outperforms block diagonal or other simpler structures to justify its contribution. (R3)
- Fig. 1 memory footprints could be better described and contextualized; timings would be a welcome addition. (R2)
- The hyperparameter search space for KFAC on VGG16/CIFAR100 may not be large enough – concerns about whether KFAC was tuned carefully. (R1)
