## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes the new task of dense video object captioning (Dense VOC) – detecting, tracking, and captioning object trajectories in videos, unifying spatial and temporal localization with natural language description. The authors introduce an end-to-end model integrating detection, tracking, and captioning, and a training strategy that leverages disjoint supervision from multiple datasets (e.g., VidSTG, VLN, SMiT, Visual Genome). This enables zero-shot generalization and strong performance after fine-tuning. New evaluation metrics (e.g., CHOTA) are designed for the task. The model outperforms multi-stage pipelines combining state-of-the-art components, and achieves state-of-the-art results on video grounding without explicit training for it. Extensive experiments and open-sourced code support the findings.

### Strengths

- **Novel and important task:** Dense VOC fills a missing gap in video understanding, setting higher standards for holistic spatio-temporal interpretation. The task unifies detection, tracking, and captioning, ensuring more temporally consistent results than multi-stage pipelines (R1, R2, R4).
- **Effective end-to-end framework:** The proposed model integrates detection, tracking, and description generation in a streamlined, end-to-end trainable architecture, with efficient ground truth construction for tracking (R1, R2). It outperforms strong baselines and even specialized models on tasks like video grounding and multi-object tracking (R4).
- **Innovative training from disjoint supervision:** By decomposing the task and combining data from diverse sources (e.g., detection from Visual Genome, video-level captions from SMiT), the model achieves noteworthy zero-shot capability and strong initialization for fine-tuning (R1, R2, R4). The approach demonstrates effective generalization beyond direct annotation availability.
- **Comprehensive evaluation and new metrics:** The paper conducts extensive experiments with carefully designed metrics (e.g., CHOTA) that jointly capture detection, tracking, and captioning. The model is shown to surpass prior state-of-the-art on VidSTG and VLN for spatial grounding without explicit training (R1, R2, R4). The work contributes valuable benchmarks and tools to the community (R2).
- **Clear presentation and open source:** The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear motivation, architecture, and training strategy. Code is open-sourced, strengthening credibility (R1, R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Tracking component lacks novelty and analysis:** The end-to-end tracking algorithm is relatively simple and not sufficiently novel compared to methods that perform identity association within the model (e.g., MinVIS, CAROQ, trackformer). The paper does not compare with alternative association strategies such as query vector propagation (R3). The reliance on frame-by-frame association may limit robustness under occlusions, rapid object movements, or frequent interactions; no detailed analysis is provided for such challenging conditions (R3, R2, R4). The absence of a breakdown of failure cases (e.g., occlusions, fast motion, complex interactions) limits understanding of the model's limitations (R2, R4).
- **High computational demands and no efficiency analysis:** The framework requires more than 32 GPUs (GPU model not specified), posing challenges for reproduction and deployment. The experimental section lacks analysis of inference efficiency per stage (detection, tracking, captioning) and comparison with other backbone architectures (R4).
- **Limited dataset diversity and potential biases:** Evaluations focus on a few datasets (e.g., VidSTG, VLN) that may not fully represent real-world scenarios. The heavy reliance on disjoint datasets not fully covering Dense VOC introduces domain discrepancies and biases (e.g., between SMiT video-level captions and Visual Genome detector proposals) that could limit generalization. The paper does not discuss how these differences affect caption quality or the model's ability to capture interactions between targets (R1, R2, R4). Additionally, the term "Dense" is ambiguous and may misrepresent the core focus on unified processing rather than high object density (R4).
- **Missing qualitative and event-type analyses:** No human evaluation of captioning quality is provided. The caption tracking process is event-oriented but the paper does not analyze performance across different event types (e.g., actions, interactions, state changes) (R2, R4).
- **Clarification of task significance and generalizability:** The paper does not clearly articulate the practical benefits of Dense VOC through specific challenging application scenarios (e.g., sports commentary, animal monitoring). A more in-depth discussion on domain differences between datasets and the rationale for task decomposition would strengthen claims of broader applicability (R1, R2).
- **Typos and formatting inconsistencies:** There are errors such as symbol mismatch in Equation (1) (Section 3.5) and inconsistent citation formats (e.g., Table 2) (R1).

*(Note: Reviewer 3, who rated the paper 6, is notably more critical of the tracking component's novelty and analysis, while the other reviewers (all rating 8) are more positive overall. This contrast is reflected in the first weakness point above.)*
