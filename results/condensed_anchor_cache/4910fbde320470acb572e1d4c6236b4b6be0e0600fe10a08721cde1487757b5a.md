## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper reframes scene flow as estimating a continuous space-time PDE with a neural prior (EulerFlow). The velocity field representation allows multi-frame bi-directional consistency via Euler integration, enabling self-supervised training. The method outperforms all prior art on the Argoverse 2 2024 Scene Flow Challenge (by >2.5× over the next-best unsupervised method and >10% over the next-best supervised method), handles small fast-moving objects (birds, tennis balls), and exhibits emergent 3D point tracking over long time horizons. Evaluations also include Waymo Open Dataset and qualitative depth-camera tabletop scenes without domain-specific tuning.

### Strengths
- **Strong empirical results**: EulerFlow surpasses all prior art on Argoverse 2 by a large margin (2.5× vs unsupervised, 10% vs supervised) – a very significant improvement.
- **Novel, simple, and technically sound formulation**: Casting scene flow as a PDE with neural prior is an innovative direction; the multi-frame bi-directional extension of NSFP is natural and effective.
- **Works out-of-the-box across domains** (autonomous driving, tabletop dynamic scenes) without per-domain tuning of most hyperparameters.
- **Effective on small, fast-moving objects** (birds, tennis balls) that previous methods struggle with; also demonstrates emergent 3D point tracking.
- **Extensive evaluation** with comprehensive ablation studies (neural prior depth, temporal window, etc.) and thorough comparisons.
- **Clear writing and effective visualizations** that make the method easy to understand.

### Weaknesses
- **Computational speed is a major concern** (noted by multiple reviewers); current runtimes prevent real-world deployment.
- **Temporal encoding in [-1,1] may not extrapolate** to time steps outside that range; the paper lacks explicit discussion of the limitations of this encoding for longer sequences.
- **Only evaluated on sequences of length 20**; experiments with longer sequences are needed to fully demonstrate the claimed benefit of arbitrary-time query and continuous-time representation.
- **Need for better baselines to isolate contributions**: It is unclear whether performance gains come from the PDE formulation or the neural network parameterization. A baseline that applies pure Euler integration on top of NSFP output would help isolate the effect of PDE modeling.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The neural prior depth needed tuning (e.g., increased to 18 for the Argoverse 2 challenge), suggesting dataset-specific adjustments that may limit out-of-the-box applicability.
- **Missing formal definitions and implementation details** (Reviewer 4):
    - The PDE definition on line 189/195 is incomplete: “∂L*/∂t” alone is not a PDE; equation (2) should explicitly depend on x.
    - The Euler integration formula (Euler_θ(P_t, d, k)) should be defined formally for reproducibility.
    - Equation (3) calculations: it is unclear whether separate loss terms are computed independently or if intermediate Euler steps can reuse previous estimates to reduce computation.
    - The direction argument may be a legacy from NSFP; training without it should be tested to verify necessity.
- **Ablation studies are insufficient in scope** (Reviewer 4):
    - No experiments with different numerical solvers (higher-order schemes) or smaller time steps (Δt < inter-observation gap).
    - Sensitivity of the method to discretization step size is not investigated.
- **More failure case analysis needed** – especially for occlusions, rapid depth changes, and complex object interactions (Reviewer 2). The paper should show explicit examples of where the method fails.
- **The method’s main contribution is not precisely positioned** (Reviewer 3):
    - The title and introduction are too general; the formulation of scene flow as a PDE has been proposed earlier (e.g., [1], [2] – references omitted here). The scope should be narrowed to the neural representation and self-supervised optimization.
- **Evaluation is mostly on autonomous driving datasets**; quantitative results on synthetic datasets with diverse motion patterns (e.g., FlyingThings3D) would strengthen claims of generality.
- **The last stated limitation – “EulerFlow does not understand ray casting geometry” – is not demonstrated** with examples or metrics; concrete failure cases tied to ray casting should be provided.
- **Paper tries to cover too many aspects and could better highlight its main advantage** (long-term flow trajectory and small fast object handling) through reorganization.
- **Activation function ablation shows Gaussian non-linearity gives very bad performance** – possibly due to spectral width mistuning; this unexplained sensitivity warrants further discussion.

*Note*: One reviewer (Reviewer 2, score 8) is more positive than the others (scores 6,6,6) and emphasizes the strong improvement over prior art while still acknowledging speed and hyperparameter tuning as weaknesses.
