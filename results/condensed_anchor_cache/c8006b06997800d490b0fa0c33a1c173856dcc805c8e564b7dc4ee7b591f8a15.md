## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies supply-demand matching on dynamic platforms where worker attributes are uncertain and evolving. It proposes a two-stage approach: first, a latent factor model with a multi-centroid grouping penalty learns and clusters latent features for workers and jobs from historical data; second, these features are used for dynamic matching. Theoretical guarantees for predictive consistency, group robustness, and convergence rates are provided. Experiments include a small simulation and a kidney exchange case study, with comparison to a point process model. Reviewer opinions diverge: two reviewers (scores 5) find the work well-structured and practical; three reviewers (scores 3) consider it straightforward, insufficiently novel, and not adequately differentiated from prior work, particularly Xu et al. (2023).

### Strengths
- **Problem importance**: The dynamic supply-demand matching problem is practically important, with applications in labor markets and organ transplantation (Reviewers 1, 5).
- **Methodological appeal**: The multi-centroid grouping penalty is an appealing solution for clustering heterogeneous workers and jobs (Reviewer 2). The two-stage design is clear and ingeniously structured (Reviewer 5).
- **Theoretical guarantees**: The paper provides proofs of predictive consistency, group robustness, and convergence rates, which help establish confidence in the model’s adaptability (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Real-world validation**: The kidney exchange case study shows applicability to a critical matching problem with medical and operational constraints (Reviewers 1, 4, 5).
- **Writing and structure**: The introduction motivates the problem well (Reviewer 2). The manuscript is clearly structured and uses formulas rigorously (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of related work and unclear novelty**: The paper lacks a cohesive overview of related literature; it does not explain its contributions with respect to existing work, especially Xu et al. (2023) which uses the same multi-centroid grouping penalty for a similar two-stage model (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5). Some reviewers consider the latent factor learning straightforward and trivial (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation**:
  - The simulation in Section 6.1 uses only 4 worker types and 3 job types; scalability to larger, more realistic type spaces is untested (Reviewers 2, 4).
  - For the kidney exchange dataset, only one accuracy figure (60%) is reported for the proposed LFGP model; no direct performance comparison (e.g., AUC, ROC, accuracy) is made with the point process model on the same data (Reviewers 2, 4).
  - The comparison is confusing: the point process model is evaluated via a ROC curve, while LFGP is reported only as a single accuracy number (Reviewer 2).
  - The paper claims that LFGP’s accuracy decreases with more label types, but no evidence or analysis of label types is provided (Reviewer 2).
  - Only one real-world dataset is used, limiting generalizability; there is a context gap between the general problem statement and the kidney exchange application (Reviewer 3).
  - Dataset statistics for the kidney exchange data are missing (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing ablations and sensitivity analysis**:
  - No analysis of how the hyperparameters \(C\) and \(D\) (number of job/worker clusters) affect performance, especially when they do not match ground truth (Reviewer 2).
  - No discussion or experiment on handling new workers with no historical record (Reviewers 2, 5).
  - No evaluation of how the model handles distribution shift between the historical and matching stages (Reviewer 2).
  - No strategy for dealing with label noise or class imbalance (e.g., rare blood types in kidney exchanges) (Reviewer 4).
- **Notation and presentation issues**:
  - Notation is inconsistent and confusing: e.g., \(\mathbf{V}/\mathbf{U}\) vs \(V/U\), \(A \leftrightarrow \mathbf{A}\), \(B \leftrightarrow \mathbf{B}\), \(L \leftrightarrow \mathcal{L}\), \(G \leftrightarrow \mathcal{G}\); also \(A\) denotes both a collection of latent factors and the latent factor of the \(i\)-th worker; “benefit matrix” and “reward matrix” are used interchangeably (Reviewers 2, 5).
  - Proofs in the appendix skip steps; for example, on line 655 it claims \(\Vert \hat{\Theta} - \Theta^\star \Vert = O_p(n^{-1/2})\) without prior introduction, and on line 692 claims \(R(\hat{V},\hat{U}) \to 1\) without clear justification (Reviewer 2).
  - Minor spelling errors and indentation issues (Reviewer 5).
- **Questions that expose missing experiments or analyses**:
  - How does the model handle irregular regeneration patterns or unpredictable availability of workers/jobs? (Reviewers 4, 5)
  - How does the model scale to online platforms with many worker and job types (e.g., recommender systems)? (Reviewer 4)
  - How does the model handle distribution shift, and is there a lifelong learning component? (Reviewer 2)
  - What is the effect of the hyperparameters \(C\) and \(D\)? (Reviewer 2)
  - What happens to regenerated workers/jobs—do they keep their original latent factors? (Reviewer 5)
  - Is the “FLGP model” in Section 6.1 the same as the point process model? Provide explanation. (Reviewer 3)
