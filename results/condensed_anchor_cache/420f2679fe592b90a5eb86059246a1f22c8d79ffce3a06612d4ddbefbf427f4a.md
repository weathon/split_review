## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces MMDocBench, an OCR-free document understanding benchmark with 15 tasks, 4,338 QA pairs, and 11,353 supporting regions. It evaluates 13 open-source and 3 proprietary LVLMs, with results showing considerable room for improvement. Reviewers acknowledge the focus on fine-grained visual understanding and the inclusion of supporting region annotations, but raise significant concerns about novelty relative to existing document benchmarks (DocVQA, InfoVQA, DUDE, ChartQA, MMLongBench-Doc), the definition of “fine-grained,” baseline selection, IoU metric interpretation, and lack of deeper analysis (instruction sensitivity, model architecture, human performance, error analysis). One reviewer (R1, rating 6) is more positive about the contribution than the others (R2: 3, R3: 3, R4: 5); the minority positive view is that the benchmark’s fine-grained, OCR-free focus and inclusion of representative SOTA models are valuable.

### Strengths

1. **Fine-grained visual and OCR-free document understanding**: The benchmark targets localized regions and details, increasing difficulty compared to global-image benchmarks. (R1, R4)
2. **Supporting region annotations**: The provision of 11,353 supporting regions improves interpretability for result analysis—rare among previous benchmarks. (R3)
3. **Comprehensive evaluation**: 13 open-source and 3 proprietary models are tested, covering a broad set of tasks (15 main tasks) and diverse document types (papers, receipts, tables, charts, infographics). (R1, R3, R4)
4. **Well-written and clear**: The paper is easy to follow. (R2, R3)
5. **Grounding-like task in dense documents**: The challenge of predicting bounding boxes alongside answers is noted as interesting and under-explored. (R2)

### Weaknesses

1. **Lack of novelty and insufficient comparison with existing benchmarks**:
   - Existing document understanding benchmarks (e.g., DocVQA, InfoVQA, DUDE, TextVQA, ChartQA, MMLongBench-Doc) already contain fine-grained QAs that require locating regions. The paper does not include a comparative analysis table (task types, annotation methods, metrics) to clarify MMDocBench’s unique contributions. (R2, R3, R4)
   - The definition of “fine-grained” is unclear: if it simply means answers reside in a part of the image, it is indistinguishable from prior benchmarks. (R2)
   - The paper does not discuss real fine-grained referring tasks such as PointQA or other document-level referring benchmarks. (R2)

2. **Baseline model selection – missing or homogenous models**:
   - Several recent LVLMs specialized for document/chart/OCR understanding are not discussed or benchmarked: TableLlama, Vary, UReader, LLVAR. (R3)
   - Proprietary models are homogeneous (GPT-4o and GPT-4v); Gemini-1.5 and Claude-3.5 should be included. (R3)

3. **IoU metric issues and implementation concerns**:
   - All models show very low IoU scores (GPT-4: 2.44%; best: 11.44%), yet the same models achieve high EM (GPT-4: 66.40%) and F1 scores. This strong discrepancy undermines the IoU metric’s validity for this task, or suggests measurement error. (R3)
   - Coordinate normalization to [0, 1000] is used, but LVLMs have vastly different image-processing strategies (tile sizes, resizing, coordinate normalization methods). Some models (e.g., QWen2-VL) use special grounding tokens (<|box_start|>, <|box_end|>). The paper does not detail how different prompts/image processing strategies were accounted for, and it is unclear whether the authors tailored instructions per model (Line 402) – potentially compromising fairness. (R3)
   - The authors claim LVLMs “face significant challenges in predicting supporting regions” without sufficient evidence; an analysis of the relationship between IoU and answer accuracy is needed, as is an evaluation of whether the IoU metric is appropriate. (R3)
   - Visual examples of predicted bounding boxes plotted on images are missing (only coordinate numbers in Appendix A.4), making it hard to judge correctness. (R3)

4. **Missing experimental analyses**:
   - No experiment on instruction sensitivity – small prompt modifications can cause large result differences. (R1)
   - Exact Match (EM) may be inappropriate for diverse LVLM responses; F1 is better but still limited. (R1)
   - The analysis does not examine how model architectures, training data, or model sizes affect performance. (R1)
   - Impact of document information density on fine-grained VQA performance is not investigated. (R2)
   - No human performance results are provided to gauge benchmark difficulty. (R4)
   - Specific error case analysis (e.g., reasoning errors, performance variation across models) is lacking. (R4)

5. **Missing implementation details and unclear data provenance**:
   - Details about the chain-of-thought (CoT) instructions used are missing. (R1, R3)
   - It is unclear whether all QA pairs are from human annotation or derived from existing benchmarks (e.g., DUDE). (R3)
   - The paper does not discuss the impact of bounding box output on hallucination problems. (R2)
   - No discussion of methods to improve LVLM fine-grained capabilities. (R2)
