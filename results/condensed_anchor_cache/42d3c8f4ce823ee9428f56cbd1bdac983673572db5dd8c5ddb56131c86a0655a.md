## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GenSim, a framework that leverages LLMs (GPT-4, Code Llama) to automatically generate diverse simulation tasks and expert demonstrations for robotic manipulation. Starting from 10 human-written tasks (CLIPort), it generates over 100 tasks via two modes: task-directed (curriculum for a target task) and bootstrapping (iterative novel task proposal). Generated tasks are validated through syntax checks, policy success rates, and minimal human inspection. Multi-task policies trained on these tasks show improved zero-shot and few-shot generalization on held-out simulation tasks and transfer to real-world long-horizon tasks, outperforming baselines. The work is praised for its novelty, thorough evaluation, and practical impact, but concerns are raised about the simplicity of tasks (table-top pick-and-place), limited diversity compared to CLIPort, the required human-in-the-loop, scalability, and gaps in metric details and comparisons.

### Strengths
- **Novel and impactful idea**: Using LLMs’ reasoning and code generation to automatically increase task-level diversity in robotic simulation, addressing a key bottleneck for multitask policy training.
- **Well-written and easy to follow**: The paper is clearly structured, with figures (e.g., Fig. 3, Fig. 6, Fig. 7) that aid understanding; detailed prompts are provided in the appendix.
- **Strong experimental evaluation**: Convincing results in both simulation and real-world transfer:
  - Table 1 shows that policies pre-trained on 70 generated tasks significantly outperform those on 50 or the original 10 CLIPort tasks when fine-tuned on few real demonstrations.
  - Zero-shot and few-shot generalization to unseen tasks is demonstrated (e.g., Fig. 6 left, Fig. 7 right).
  - Multitask policies pretrained on generated tasks exhibit stronger real-world transfer to unseen long-horizon tasks with minimal sim-to-real adaptation.
- **Thorough ablations and comparisons**: Ablations study prompt design (e.g., single-prompt vs. two-stage chain), LLM models (GPT-4, Code Llama, finetuned GPTs), and the impact of task library size.
- **Tailored evaluation metrics**: Metrics for code correctness (“runtime-verified”) and task completion are used to assess generated task quality.

### Weaknesses
- **Task simplicity and limited diversity**:
  - All tasks are table-top top-down pick-and-place with a suction gripper; no dexterous manipulation (sweeping, folding, ropes) or more complex scenes are considered.
  - The held-out evaluation tasks mostly involve blocks and omit more complex objects (e.g., scissors, chess pieces) present in CLIPort.
  - Generalization within this narrow domain is relatively easy; it remains unclear if the method scales to more varied or realistic tasks.
  - Only one environment (setting) is used; trends may not hold across different simulation platforms or robot morphologies.
- **Human-in-the-loop requirements**:
  - Figure 3 shows human inspection is needed for generated tasks. The paper does not specify how many tasks were manually annotated, the granularity of feedback, or the total human effort.
  - The “automatic” nature is partially undermined; the frequency and cost of human intervention are not quantified.
- **Missing details and unclear metrics**:
  - The “few-shot policy generalization to related tasks” experiment (Section 4.2) is ambiguous: it is unclear whether policies were pre-trained on all generated tasks or only a subset, and how those subsets were selected.
  - Metrics in Fig. 6 (e.g., “code reasoning capability”) lack explanation of how “runtime-verified” is measured and whether “task completed” is averaged over multiple environment resets.
  - The claim that policies pretrained on only 10 CLIPort tasks do not generalize well to GPT-4 tasks should be mitigated, as Fig. 7 (right) shows only 15–20% relative improvement.
- **Scalability and practical concerns**:
  - Computational resources, time, and effort to generate over 100 tasks are not discussed; this limits insight into the framework’s practical utility and real-world applicability.
  - Imperfections in evaluation metrics (e.g., misaligned language descriptions) are acknowledged but not deeply analyzed regarding their impact on task quality and downstream policy training.
- **Missing comparisons and unaddressed questions**:
  - No comparison with existing data augmentation methods using foundation models (e.g., ROSIE, CACTI) which are simpler to implement.
  - Handling of syntax errors / lack of grounding in generated code is not described; how such errors affect policy training is unexplored.
  - The amount of prompt engineering required is not discussed.
- **LLM fine-tuning and selection issues**:
  - In Fig. 6, GPT-4 without fine-tuning outperforms all fine-tuned smaller models (Code Llama), raising the question of whether fine-tuning is only for inference cost reduction.
  - The paper does not specify which LLM is used for the “exploration” generation mode or whether newly generated tasks are used only as prompt examples or also for fine-tuning the LLM.
  - Fine-tuning the LLM on only 10 initial tasks may cause overfitting; how this is handled is not explained.
- **Sim-to-real evaluation is shallow**: Only minimal adaptation is discussed; the effectiveness and robustness of policies in real-world settings beyond the reported tasks are not deeply evaluated.
- **Failure cases not sufficiently addressed**: The paper could benefit from more examples and analysis of generation failures to delineate the method’s limitations.
