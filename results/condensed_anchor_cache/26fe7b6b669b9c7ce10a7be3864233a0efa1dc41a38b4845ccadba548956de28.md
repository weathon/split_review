## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces MathVista, a benchmark for evaluating mathematical reasoning of foundation models in visual contexts. It comprises 6,141 examples from 28 existing datasets plus three newly created datasets (IQTest, FunctionQA, PaperQA). A taxonomy covers 7 reasoning types and 5 task types. The authors evaluate 12 models (LLMs, LMMs, context-augmented LLMs) and find that Multimodal Bard achieves 35% accuracy and GPT-4V (tested later) achieves 49.9%, still 10.4% below human performance (60.3%). The paper includes in-depth analysis and qualitative examples. The reviewers generally see value but raise several concerns.

### Strengths

- The construction of a visual-based mathematical reasoning benchmark is well motivated and novel, with a broad coverage of reasoning types (7), data sources (28), and task types (5). (R1,R2,R3,R4)
- The identified taxonomy clarifies and organizes the capabilities relevant to visual/mathematical reasoning. (R2,R4)
- The three newly collected datasets (IQTest, FunctionQA, PaperQA) address gaps not covered by past works. (R1,R2)
- Evaluation is performed on prominent LLMs (GPT-4, ChatGPT, Claude) and LMMs (mPLUG-Owl, InstructBLIP, LLaVa, Multimodal Bard) under zero-shot, few-shot (2-shot for LLMs), Chain-of-Thought, and Program-of-Thought settings, and when augmented with visual context. Human performance is computed and qualitative analysis and fine-grained comparisons are provided. (R1,R2,R3,R4)
- The paper is well written with appropriate figures, dataset examples, analysis breakdown, model prompts, and annotation methods. (R2,R4)

### Weaknesses

1. **Data collection for new datasets lacks rigor details.** Inter-annotation consistency checks are not reported; the "rigorous review process" is not described. (R2)
2. **Few-shot evaluation is incomplete.** LMMs (e.g., Multimodal Bard, Flamingo, mPLUG-Owl) support few-shot learning but are only tested zero-shot. LLMs are only tested with 2-shot; broader evaluation over {2,4,8,16,32} would better assess few-shot learning of mathematical reasoning. (R2)
3. **Disentangling visual/text understanding from math reasoning is missing.** The benchmark amalgamates visual and reasoning steps, making it unclear whether errors stem from perception or reasoning. Annotations for rationales or intermediate sub-questions would help. (R3,R4 — R4 calls this a potential downside but not critical.)
4. **Low human performance (~60.3%) raises concerns about data quality or task difficulty.** The paper does not analyze the 40% of examples humans answered incorrectly or whether annotation noise explains the gap. (R3,R4)
5. **Fine-grained reasoning types (Tab-2) yield limited takeaways beyond "Bard is best."** The analysis does not reveal which models excel at which reasoning types, beyond overall rankings. (R3)
6. **Results and methods have been partly superseded.** Reviewer 1 (minority, score 5) notes that the results (e.g., Bard as best) are superseded by GPT-4V (reported later in paper but not included in initial experiments) and that the prompting methods (CoT, PoT) have been superseded by more recent approaches (e.g., hypothesis refinement, multiple expert agents). The paper also lacks relevant references:
   - Prompting: Phenomenal yet puzzling (Qiu et al. 2023), Hypothesis search (Wang et al. 2023), LLM as multi-agent for ARC (Min & Motani 2023).
   - GPT-4V: Lost in Translation (Zhang et al. 2023).
   - Program-of-Thought: Solving Linear Algebra (Drori & Verma 2021), Solving Probability/Statistics (Tang et al. 2022), A neural network solves... (Drori et al. 2022).
   (R1; other reviewers did not raise this as a major weakness but R1 considers it significant.)
7. **Missing analysis of why specific models perform better.** The paper does not identify which features (e.g., visual encoder, training data, model size) contribute to performance differences. (R1)
8. **Benchmark is relatively small (6141 examples) and is test-only** without a training/finetuning subset, limiting its utility for improving models. (R2)
9. **GPT-4V results are absent from the main evaluation** (only added later; reviewer notes it would be good to have). (R3; also implicitly noted by R1)
10. **The sampling strategy for "testmini" is unclear.** The use of KL divergence and TV distance to compare testmini to the full set is not explained (what distributions are being compared?). (R4)

*Note:* Reviewer 1 (score 5) is more critical than the other three reviewers (scores 8 each), especially on the issues of being superseded and lacking in-depth analysis. The majority found the benchmark and evaluation solid but with actionable flaws.
