- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 1, 5, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes augmented flow matching (AFM), which reduces the conditional variance of the flow matching loss by introducing auxiliary variables correlated to the training pair (constructed via linear random projection). The authors show theoretically (Proposition 1) and empirically that this reduces variance. Experiments are on 2D synthetic data, CIFAR-10 image generation, and single embryonic cell evolution (Waddington OT dataset).

### Strengths

- **Topic and motivation**: The investigated topic is interesting; the paper is well motivated and provides useful examples to support intuition. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Simplicity and computational efficiency**: The proposed method is very simple, easy to implement, and has low computational and memory overhead. It requires only a slight modification to the training phase. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Generality**: The variance reduction strategy is general and can be adapted to different settings by different designs of the auxiliary variable Y. It has potential, though unexplored, to combine with other flow matching improvement techniques (e.g., rectified flows, optimal transport, curvature-minimizing approaches). (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Multiple domains**: Experiments are provided for synthetic data, images, and cell evolution data. (Reviewer 3)
- **Presentation**: The paper is well written and the idea is clear. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient comparison to existing methods**: 
  - For CIFAR-10, the paper’s best FID is ~3.5, but rectified flow reports 2.58. This comparison is omitted. The paper does not compare to other flow matching modifications that create straighter paths (e.g., rectified flows evaluated with Euler/2nd-order Heun, minibatch or multisample flow matching). (Reviewer 2)
  - For the embryonic cell evolution dataset (Waddington OT, Schiebinger et al. (2019), ~1000 citations), the paper does not compare to or reference any previously published results, making it impossible to assess experimental progress. (Reviewer 2)
  - The paper claims the method can be plugged into other training methods (e.g., optimal transport, curvature minimization) but provides no evidence that the combination is useful. Experimental comparison to ReFlow on CIFAR-10 is missing. (Reviewer 3)
- **Sensitivity to augmentation dimension and NFE; lack of selection guidelines**:
  - The effect of the method strongly depends on the chosen augmentation dimension (K); without judicious selection the technique can even worsen performance at slightly higher cost. The paper lacks a criterion to determine K beforehand. (Reviewers 1, 3)
  - Table 2 stops NFE at 100 (Euler) and 60 (2nd-order Heun) exactly where the improvement from augmentation disappears, so it is possible the FID is hurt when integrating with a highly accurate ODE solver. (Reviewer 2)
  - The paper should investigate how to choose the augmentation dimension and NFE and propose an effective strategy. (Reviewer 3)
- **Inconsistent experimental results and lack of statistical rigor**:
  - No measure of variability is reported; it is unclear if results are from a single run or averaged. No statistical significance tests are performed to establish meaningful differences. (Reviewer 3)
  - Inconsistent behaviour: Table 2 (Heun) shows AugDim=3 is consistently worse for all NFE than both AugDim=2 and AugDim=4. This does not make sense and raises concerns about consistency. (Reviewer 3)
  - The abnormally bad result with 2nd-order Heun and AugDim=3 in Table 2 needs explanation. (Reviewer 1)
- **Cell evolution experiment needs more thorough evaluation**:
  - Qualitatively, the distributions are better but still very different from the original. The discussion is limited to “better follows the distribution of true points.” Are they good enough? How to tell? (Reviewer 3)
  - The Wasserstein distance increases as NFE increases (Figure 5), which is not observed for CIFAR-10 (metrics monotonically decreasing). This behaviour needs explanation. (Reviewer 3)
  - Results in Table 3 need more explanation. (Reviewer 1)
- **Unclear variance reduction magnitude and robustness**:
  - It is unclear how much variance can be reduced by the proposed method, especially when the data distribution varies. (Reviewer 1)
  - The robustness to the choice of the random projection matrix P needs more demonstration. (Reviewer 1)
- **Presentation and notation issues**:
  - Arrangement of figures and tables is messy. (Reviewer 1)
  - Notations in Section 3 need further clarification. (Reviewer 1)
  - Typo: line 205, “where $X_0$ is drawn” should be “where $(X_0, X_1)$ is drawn”. (Reviewer 4)
- **Limited technical contribution**: The method is simple but many important issues (parameter choice, robustness, thorough comparison) remain unresolved. (Reviewer 3)
- **One reviewer noted a major concern mentioned in the paper’s discussion but did not elaborate.** (Reviewer 4)
- **Minority contrast**: One reviewer (Reviewer 4, score 6) found the method appealing and clear, emphasizing its simplicity and generality, while another (Reviewer 2, score 1) gave a strong reject based on insufficient experimental results and lack of competitive comparisons. The overall experimental evidence is seen as unconvincing by most reviewers.
