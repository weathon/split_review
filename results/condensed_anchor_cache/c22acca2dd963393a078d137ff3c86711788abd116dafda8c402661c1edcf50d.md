## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes AdaWarp, a neural network module that integrates a differentiable bilateral grid to enforce a piece-wise smooth (P-S) prior on deformation fields for medical image registration. The method uses a low-resolution latent representation and a guidance map generator to achieve edge-preserving, efficient filtering. Evaluations are performed on cardiac MRI and abdomen CT datasets, showing improved accuracy-efficiency and accuracy-smoothness trade-offs over selected baselines. Multiple reviewers highlight the reasonable motivation and clear methodology but raise concerns about novelty, experimental breadth, and visual evidence.

### Strengths
- **Motivation & Assumption**: The piece-wise smooth (P-S) prior is derived from observed patterns in daily CT/MRI scans, grounding the method in realistic physical priors (R1). This bridges a gap in the literature by enforcing global smoothness while respecting local discontinuities (R2).
- **Innovative Module**: Integrating a differentiable bilateral grid into a learning-based registration framework is highly innovative (R4). The module effectively replaces traditional smoothness constraints and demonstrates connections with self-attention (R3).
- **Thorough Comparative Experiments**: The method is tested on two datasets covering different modalities (cardiac MRI and abdomen CT) and input constraints, demonstrating robustness and broad applicability (R1, R3).
- **Clarity**: The paper is well-structured with a clear description of the methodology, including the encoder, guidance map generator, and adaptive filtering process (R2, R4). Visual aids (Figures 4, 5) help clarify complex comparisons (R4).
- **Promising Trade-off**: The method presents a promising alternative for balancing accuracy, efficiency, and smoothness (R2, R4), outperforming existing methods in accuracy-efficiency and accuracy-smoothness trade-offs (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty Concerns**: The use of a latent representation for efficiency is not unique; such approaches are already common (R1). The core addition—a differentiable bilateral grid—is viewed as an incremental modification (R1). In contrast, another reviewer (R4) finds the integration highly innovative, highlighting disagreement on novelty.
- **Limited Justification for Efficiency Focus**: Deep learning registration methods (e.g., VoxelMorph) already achieve real-time speeds on GPUs; the paper does not adequately justify why further efficiency gains are a pressing problem (R2).
- **Missing Brain MRI Evaluation**: The P-S assumption is only demonstrated on cardiac and abdominal data, which have relatively simple anatomy. The method should be evaluated on well-benchmarked brain MRI registration, where the assumption may fail for highly convoluted structures (R2). Reviewer 1 notes the two datasets cover different modalities, but brain MRI remains absent.
- **Lack of Visual Results**: The paper does not provide qualitative results (warped images or segmentation masks) to substantiate the claim that the model preserves sharp boundaries (R3, R4). Numerical metrics (Dice, HD95, SDlogJ) alone are insufficient to demonstrate edge preservation (R4).
- **Inconsistent Model Structures Across Datasets**: Different architectures and training strategies (unsupervised vs. semi-supervised) are used per dataset, raising concerns about generalizability (R3, R4). Why are Ada-Res and Ada-Cost different? Results of swapping them are not shown (R4). The abdominal dataset uses Convex Adam’s framework with the same segmentation model as a feature extractor (R3, R4). It is unclear whether this segmentation model is pre-trained, making the method semi-supervised and potentially unfair when compared to unsupervised baselines (R4). The role of the guidance map generator vs. raw intensities is not justified (R3).
- **Insufficient Baselines**: Several important baselines are missing. VoxelMorph is widely benchmarked but omitted from Figures 4 and 5 (R2). CorrMLP (Meng et al. 2024) has a conflicting motivation (full-resolution details) and should be compared (R2). Multi-scale and patch-wise registration methods that also manage local deformations while maintaining smoothness are not discussed or used as baselines (R4). Different baselines are chosen for the two datasets without clear rationale (R3).
- **Missing Ablation Studies**: No ablation studies are performed to demonstrate the effectiveness of each component (e.g., guidance map generator, bilateral grid, latent resolution) (R4).
- **Unanswered Experimental Details**:  
  - Whether the same λ (smoothness weight) values are used for all methods and how they were tuned (R3).  
  - Whether baselines use diffeomorphic transformations; a table of hyperparameters and transformation models for all methods is requested (R3).  
  - t-tests are only reported for Dice, not for HD95 (R3).  
  - The reason learning-based methods show slightly higher SDlogJ than traditional ones, despite expected higher accuracy from iterative optimization, is not explained (R3).  
  - Inference and training times are not provided (R4).  
  - Code is not available (R3).
- **Presentation and Writing Issues**: The experiments section is disorganized, and the clarity could be improved (R4, R3). The paper’s primary area is listed as “interpretability and explainable AI,” but no interpretability work is presented (R4).  
- **Clarification of Claims**:  
  - The statement that iterative optimization-based methods are “time-consuming and lack contextual information” requires elaboration: discrete methods can converge quickly for large deformations, and contextual information (e.g., segmentation maps) can be incorporated (R3).  
  - The justification for extending the bilateral grid by only one additional dimension to account for object boundaries is insufficient; why one dimension is enough to capture complex relationships is unclear (R3).  
  - The role of the guidance map generator is ambiguous: why not use raw intensities directly as the additional coordinate? (R3).
