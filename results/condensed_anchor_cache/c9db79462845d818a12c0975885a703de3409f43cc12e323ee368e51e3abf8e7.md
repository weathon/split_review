## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents HelloBench, a long-text-generation benchmark grounded in Bloom’s Taxonomy and covering 5 subtasks (open-ended QA, summarization, chat, text completion, heuristic text generation) across 38 subcategories (647 examples). The authors also propose HelloEval, a checklist-based LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation method that uses regression-trained weights derived from human annotations to produce a numerical score. Experiments on ~30 mainstream LLMs reveal that most models struggle to generate text beyond 4000 words and exhibit issues such as repetition and quality degradation at longer lengths. The paper reports that HelloEval achieves the highest Spearman correlation with human evaluation (0.32, p-value not discussed per reviewer) compared to traditional metrics (ROUGE, BLEU, etc.).

### Strengths
- HelloBench covers a diverse array of long-text generation tasks (open-ended QA, summarization, chat, text completion, heuristic generation) grounded in Bloom’s Taxonomy, offering a holistic evaluation framework.
- HelloEval provides an automatic, labor‑saving evaluation method that uses human‑derived weights for checklist items, showing higher correlation with human judgments than traditional metrics (ROUGE, BLEU) and other LLM-as-a-Judge methods.
- The experiments are conducted across a wide range of popular proprietary and open-source LLMs (about 30 models), making the findings (e.g., most LLMs generate ~1000 tokens even with large max_tokens; quality degrades beyond typical lengths) more reliable and convincing.
- The benchmark focuses on a timely and under‑investigated aspect of LLM evaluation (long text generation), and the findings offer insights into current models’ insufficiency in this capability.
- The metric design (using human judgments to induce evaluation parameters) is innovative and intuitive for better human alignment.

### Weaknesses
1. **Omission of prior work (ProxyQA)** – The paper fails to acknowledge or compare with ProxyQA, a pioneering framework for evaluating long‑form text generation. Both approaches use evaluators (checklists) to assess generation quality, but ProxyQA employs query‑specific proxy questions while HelloBench uses a general checklist that is not query‑ or semantics‑adaptive. The motivations and insights of the two works align closely, and the paper should include ProxyQA in Table 1 and conduct a correlation analysis of results between the two benchmarks to demonstrate HelloBench’s added value.  
   *[R1: Strong criticism of novelty overlap; R1 and R4 agree that novelty is limited.]*

2. **Low Spearman correlation of HelloEval (0.32)** – Although HelloEval shows the highest and statistically significant correlation with human evaluation, the overall Spearman correlation of 0.32 is not high enough to assume that improvements in HelloEval reliably indicate real gains in long‑text generation. A scatter plot of HelloEval scores vs. human scores should be provided to illustrate the spread.  
   *[R2: Direct weakness, questions reliability.]*

3. **Potential overfitting and annotation details** – It is unclear whether the correlation analysis was performed on the same annotations used to derive the checklist weights in the preparation stage. If so, there is a risk of overfitting. The paper should report the number of annotations used for correlation analysis and whether it is large enough to reach a significant conclusion.  
   *[R2: Question that overlaps with criticism of methodology.]*

4. **Unclear interpretation and comparability of HelloEval scores** – The paper does not explain what an increase of 1 in HelloEval means, what quality gains are considered significant, or whether scores can be directly compared across different sub‑tasks (e.g., QA vs. summarization). Comparisons in lines 363–364 (e.g., HelloEval scores between QA/text‑completion and summarization/chat) may not be valid without justification.  
   *[R2: Questions that double as methodological weakness.]*

5. **Data leakage concern** – The claim in line 1021 that “By doing so, we guarantee that the data are not leaked for the reason that they are all test samples” is false; test samples can still be part of training data if leaked from other sources.  
   *[R2: Criticism of an inaccurate claim.]*

6. **Benchmark scale, coverage, and source policy** – The benchmark is not large‑scale: e.g., open‑ended QA contains only 200 samples from a single source (Quora). The paper does not use existing large‑scale human‑collected benchmarks such as ELI5. Furthermore, Quora may not allow data crawling, which could cause policy violations.  
   *[R4: Criticism of coverage and legality; R4 also notes the overall contribution is incremental.]*

7. **Superficial mapping to Bloom’s Taxonomy** – The assignment of cognitive levels to tasks (e.g., “we have selected the most suitable task for each cognitive level”) is not justified; the mapping appears shallow and contains overlaps. The paper needs proof or reasoning.  
   *[R3: Weakness in conceptual grounding.]*

8. **Lack of robustness analysis for HelloEval** – Beyond correlation with human evaluation, the paper should conduct win‑rate analyses or confidence‑interval tests to determine whether the proposed method yields consistent and reliable judgments, as even top‑performing LLMs or human evaluators can produce inconsistent results.  
   *[R1: Missing experiment that overlaps with a criticism.]*

9. **Shallow analysis of findings** – The paper notes that current LLMs struggle with long‑text generation (e.g., inability to exceed 4000 words, repetition, quality degradation) but does not explore the underlying reasons for these limitations or suggest potential improvements. The implications of these findings for future model development are also inadequately discussed.  
   *[R4: Criticism of insufficient depth.]*

10. **Missing license information** – The benchmark’s license is not stated, which is important for an evaluation benchmark.  
    *[R2: Question that constitutes a practical weakness.]*
