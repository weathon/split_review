- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 5, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents FroSSL, a self-supervised learning objective that minimizes covariance Frobenius norms to avoid collapse and mean-squared error for augmentation invariance, with the aim of combining sample- and dimension-contrastive approaches. Reviewers appreciate the clear structure, good motivation, and focus on convergence speed as an important problem. However, the majority of reviewers find the experimental evidence insufficient to support the claims: the method never outperforms established baselines in full training, the reported faster convergence is not convincingly demonstrated in practical performance regimes, and important ablations (e.g., role of the logarithm term) and comparisons to closely related methods (kernel-based approaches, I-VNE+) are missing. One reviewer (score 5, moderate confidence) is more positive, finding the faster convergence “seems convincing” and the explanation via dimensional collapse sensible, but this reviewer also notes the lack of mean±std and that FroSSL never beats any existing method. Overall, reviewers question whether the contribution is significant given the weak empirical evidence.

### Strengths
- **Novel combination and clear exposition**: FroSSL proposes a new SSL objective that unifies sample- and dimension-contrastive ideas, following the framework of Garrido et al. (2023). The manuscript is well-structured, with clear motivation and necessary background. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Addressing convergence speed**: The paper tackles the less explored aspect of SSL convergence speed, which has implications for compute requirements. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Mathematical analysis**: The analysis and explanation via dimensional collapse (eigenvalue equalization through Frobenius norm minimization) are sensible and well presented. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Experimental coverage**: Experiments are conducted on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, and ImageNet using ResNet-18, and results are often in the top 3 among compared methods. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Minority positive view**: One reviewer (Reviewer 2, score 5) found the faster convergence “seems convincing” and the dimensional collapse explanation sensible. (Contrast: other reviewers disagree; see Weaknesses)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient experimental scope and missing ablations**:
  - Missing ablation on the logarithm term to show its necessity. (Reviewers 1, 4)
  - No experiments on larger models where fast convergence would be critical. (Reviewer 4)
  - Convergence speed results for CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 are not provided. (Reviewer 4)
  - No analysis of the role of the log term in the loss. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Missing mean±std**: Results are reported without standard deviation over multiple runs. Given that accuracy numbers are close in Table 2, this is important to assess significance. (Reviewer 2)
- **Does not outperform existing methods**: FroSSL never beats any already established method in full training (Table 2). The paper should discuss limitations and why it underperforms. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **Faster convergence not convincingly demonstrated**:
  - On STL-10 (Table 3), at 30 epochs SwAV reaches nearly identical performance; from 50 epochs FroSSL is not in the top‑3. (Reviewer 3)
  - On ImageNet (Figure 4), at 30 epochs FroSSL and Barlow Twins (BT, not SOTA) reach similar performance; at 100 epochs FroSSL has a slight advantage over BT but BT is not SOTA. Faster convergence is only evident when performance is ≤50%, which is impractical. (Reviewers 3, 4)
  - FroSSL is derived from rotational invariance, not directly from fast convergence; no explanation of how enforcing equal eigenvalues via Frobenius norm minimization translates to faster convergence. (Reviewer 4)
  - The experiments use full training lengths, not exploiting the claimed fast convergence property. (Reviewer 3)
  - *(Contrast: Reviewer 2 found the faster convergence convincing, but other reviewers disagree based on the above.)*
  - Missing explanation for why FroSSL is eventually bested by other methods over longer training. (Reviewer 2)
- **Unclear figure/table explanations**:
  - Figure 2: what are the “variants”? (Reviewer 4)
  - Figure 3: dataset not specified. (Reviewer 4)
  - References to “Figure 5.1, Figure 5.2, Table 5.2” are unclear. (Reviewer 4)
  - Links to figures/tables reference sections rather than the actual figures/tables. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing comparisons to closely related methods**:
  - Kernel-based methods discussed in Sec. 4.3 (“Entropy in SSL”) are not included in the benchmark. (Reviewer 3)
  - The paper does not compare to I-VNE+ (VNE, CVPR 2023), which also minimizes covariance Frobenius norm. What sets FroSSL apart and why is it superior? (Reviewer 4)
- **Insufficient motivation and theoretical justification**:
  - Why does Barlow Twins not push representations to be rotationally invariant, given it pushes the cross-correlation to identity? (Reviewer 1)
  - Intuition and necessity of the logarithm term are not explained. (Reviewer 1)
  - How can FroSSL be simultaneously dimension-contrastive (no negative samples) and sample-contrastive (explicit negative samples)? This seems contradictory. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minor issues**:
  - Missing citation for SimMER. (Reviewer 3)
  - No convergence speed experiments for CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, though they are used in the full evaluation. (Reviewer 4)
