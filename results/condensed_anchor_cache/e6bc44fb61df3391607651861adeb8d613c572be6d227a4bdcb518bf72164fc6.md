## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes to improve off-policy RL by conditioning the value function on a low-dimensional representation of the policy parameters, using a learned encoder (LPE-DM) and a generalized off-policy evaluation method (GOPE). Empirical results on continuous control tasks show improvements over SAC and TD3 baselines. Three reviewers rated the paper 3 (fair) and one 5 (positive), but all expressed significant concerns about novelty, theoretical justification, method clarity, and experimental rigor. The more positive reviewer (score 5) found the analysis and results interesting but still noted the idea is not new, the theoretical analysis is weak, and statistical significance is questionable. The other three reviewers emphasized lack of clarity, insufficient motivation, and limited comparisons.

### Strengths
- The idea of augmenting value function inputs with low-dimensional policy representations is conceptually interesting and leads to empirical improvements across several tasks.
- The dynamic masking (DM) technique for policy encoding is persuasive in principle, and the ablation study is informative and well-executed.
- The approach is simple to grasp and shows decent performance gains over standard off-policy methods (SAC, TD3) in continuous control benchmarks.
- The paper provides an ablation study (LPE vs. LPE-DM) and investigates the relationship between policy representation and value function quality.

### Weaknesses
**Novelty and Related Work**
- The core idea of conditioning a value function on a policy representation is not fundamentally new; it closely resembles the policy-extended value function (Tang et al., AAAI22). The proposed Bellman operator is a straightforward extension of that prior work.
- The method of policy encoding (except for dynamic masking) mirrors the representation learning used in the same prior work (OPR). No comparisons are made against that or other policy representation approaches.
- GOPE (keeping a replay buffer of past policies and retraining on all data) is a natural and previously used technique; the paper does not position it as a new contribution.
- Several reviewers noted that the claim that “policy representations optimized via TD learning contain the most relevant features” is not adequately supported.

**Theoretical Analysis**
- Theorem 3.3 relies on a very strong assumption: that the value learning error satisfies ‖Qt – Q^{π_t}‖ + ‖Q_{t+1} – Q^{π_{t+1}}‖ ≤ ‖Q^{π_t} – Q^{π_{t+1}}‖. This condition is not realistic (the error is not directly estimable from data), and the bound may be trivial if error stays constant. The analysis does not capture the generalization benefit that the authors intend.
- Definition 3.2 (representation-conditioned value function) is problematic as written: a function conditioned only on policy representation (not state/action) cannot approximate the state-conditional Q-function. Even if the intended form is Q(s,a,X_π), the theorem’s condition seems unlikely to hold.
- No formal justification is given for why conditioning on policy parameters should improve performance, either theoretically or through a post-hoc experimental argument.

**Method Clarity and Justification**
- The main method (LPE-DM) is insufficiently described, particularly in Section 4.2. Key notation (χ, ψ, ψ⁻) is not clearly defined; the reader must guess their roles. Figure 2 lacks explanation (e.g., what l₀, l₁ denote).
- The purpose of Section 3.1 (theoretical framework) is unclear and does not connect well to the empirical results.
- The rationale for dynamic masking (masking nodes based on frequency of parameter changes) is not intuitive: parameter importance is not solely defined by change frequency. The paper does not justify why this is a suitable proxy.
- The claim in Section 4.1 (“policy representations can ignore parameters associated with zero-output activations without affecting performance”) is not clearly stated or supported. The average parameter change does not directly indicate neuron relevance.
- The paper uses “ave-evaluation” as a metric, which is confusing: it is the average score across all trials over training, which can be uninformative because different training curves can produce the same average.

**Experimental Concerns**
- Standard deviations in Tables 1–2 are large (e.g., SAC vs. SAC-GOPE on Hopper), making it difficult to assess statistical significance. The authors should not claim improvement without significance testing.
- Some learning curves (Fig. 8) show odd dips for TD3 and slightly higher starting points for GOPE methods, suggesting potential evaluation artifacts or non-standard implementations.
- Figure 5 shows minimal difference between LPE and LPE-DM (well within error). This undermines the necessity of dynamic masking; an ablation on the masking ratio η would be beneficial.
- Table 4 reveals that omitting historical policy representation actually improves max-evaluation performance, which contradicts the motivation for GOPE. The paper does not address this.
- The title emphasizes “sample efficiency,” but the main text focuses on final performance. Only the appendix (Fig. 9) shows learning curves relevant to sample efficiency; these should be referenced in the main text.
- Figure 6’s purpose and relevance (plotting value generalization vs. average return) are unclear.
- Spacing between Figures 4 and 5 is too small, causing confusion with captions.
- Minor errors: incorrect definition of Q^* (should be max over π, not a), notation in Equation (7), and references to Table B/C instead of Table 3/4 in the appendix. “RA” and “RanP” are inconsistent.

**Missing Comparisons/Ablations**
- No comparison to prior work on policy-augmented value functions (e.g., Tang et al. 2022) despite being cited.
- No ablation of the masking ratio η to justify the dynamic masking design.
- No investigation of alternative masking strategies (e.g., Bayesian neural networks to assess weight importance).
- No analysis of computational/memory cost: storing all historical policy parameters for GOPE likely incurs high overhead, which is not discussed.
