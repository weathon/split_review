## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ReMoE, a fully differentiable Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture that replaces the standard TopK+Softmax routing with a ReLU activation function. A training recipe with sparsity and load balancing losses, plus dynamic penalty adjustment, is introduced. Experiments on autoregressive language modeling (up to 30B tokens, model sizes up to ~7/8B total parameters) show ReMoE consistently outperforms standard TopK MoE on perplexity across varying model sizes, expert counts, and granularity. The routing exhibits domain specialization and flexible token-to-expert allocation. Reviewers note the idea is conceptually clean and results promising, but raise concerns about novelty attribution, lack of downstream evaluation, computational overhead in early training, and limited experiment scale.

### Strengths
- **Conceptually simple and effective**: Replacing TopK+Softmax with ReLU is a clean drop-in modification that improves performance across all comparisons (Reviewer 2, 4, 5). The method consistently outperforms TopK MoE baselines on perplexity (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
- **Thorough analysis and ablations**: Ablations on sparsity regularization, load balancing, domain specialization, token-frequency routing, and training stages are presented (Reviewer 1, 3, 4). Figure 4 clearly depicts the three-stage training dynamics (warm-up, sparsifying, stable) (Reviewer 2, 3). Reviewer 1 and 4 highlight the insightful correlation between expert allocation and token frequency as well as the effect of load balance loss.
- **Novel and promising idea**: The ReLU routing provides full differentiability, eliminating the hard requirement for each token to be processed by exactly k experts, potentially improving conditional computation (Reviewer 2, 5). Reviewer 5 emphasizes that the approach unlocks better capabilities in MoE models by alleviating the discrete routing difficulty.
- **Well-written and easy to follow**: The method section is thorough, and the presentation is clear (Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 4, 5). Reviewer 2 particularly appreciates Figure 4.

### Weaknesses
- **Attribution of success unclear**: The main concern (Reviewer 2) is whether gains come from ReLU or from the near-dense training in the first ~100 steps (sparsity as low as 20%). Reviewer 2 suggests a controlled baseline: train a dMoE with k=int(0.8*E) for 100 steps then switch to k=1. Reviewer 5 similarly questions if the key is continuity or the flexible sparse regularization enabling multiple training phases. Reviewer 1 notes stage I effectively trains a nearly dense model, impacting memory/computation efficiency.
- **Missing evaluation on downstream tasks**: Multiple reviewers (1, 2, 3, 4) emphasize that only perplexity/loss is reported. It is critically important to show results on common-sense reasoning, LM evaluation benchmarks, or other downstream tasks to justify performance improvements.
- **No training/inference cost analysis**: The computational overhead of dynamic expert selection is not quantified. Reviewer 3 requests speed analysis for both training and inference, noting that stage I activates many experts. Reviewer 2 flags high memory consumption early in training (should be explicitly noted in section 3.5 line 269). Reviewer 3 also questions GPU utilization for dynamic expert selection compared to TopK’s better parallelization.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and scalability**: The method introduces two new hyperparameters (λ₀, α). Reviewer 3 notes MoE training is already tricky; the ablation at 20k steps (~10B tokens) for 182M model may not replicate for larger models, and some α values cause rapid regularization changes and oscillation. Reviewer 2 asks if the 30B token budget is compute-optimal for all model sizes and whether results hold for longer training (beyond compute-optimal, as in Llama3).
- **Limited experiment scale and missing baselines**: The largest model uses a batch size of 512k tokens, which Reviewer 2 considers small for LLM pre-training. Reviewer 3 and 2 point out missing comparisons with recent MoE variants addressing routing bottlenecks, e.g., sparse-mixer-v2, and others improving expert utilization.
- **Conceptual and theoretical gaps**: Reviewers request deeper explanations. Reviewer 5 wants a detailed breakdown of how L1 regularization, load balancing, and dynamic adjustment interact and prevent suboptimal solutions. Reviewer 1 and 4 ask why ReLU routing enhances domain specialization and whether it captures semantic information beyond token frequency. Reviewer 2 corrects the claim about "non-differentiable training objective" – the objective is differentiable; the issue is sparse gradients. Reviewer 4 asks for clarification on the symbol N (active parameters) and total model size.
- **Additional concerns**:
  - **Latency issues**: Uneven load distribution across a sequence could cause latency during autoregressive generation (Reviewer 2).
  - **Flexibility of regularization**: Whether the regularization terms truly allow leveraging the flexible routing (Reviewer 2).
  - **Suggested improvements**: Reviewer 1 suggests investigating shifted-ReLU or learnable thresholds. Reviewer 3 recommends domain vocabulary coverage analysis for experts. Reviewer 4 suggests providing more theoretical evidence on why differentiability helps. Reviewer 2 suggests a writing correction: "Scaling in parameters N" → "Scaling in active parameters N".
