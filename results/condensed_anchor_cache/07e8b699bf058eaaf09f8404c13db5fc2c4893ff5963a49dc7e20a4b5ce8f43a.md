## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Decoding by Contrasting Knowledge (DeCK), a contrastive decoding strategy to improve in-context editing (ICE) of knowledge in LLMs. DeCK addresses the problem of "stubborn knowledge" – facts with excessive pre-training confidence that resist editing – by contrasting logits from the edited context with those from the unedited parametric knowledge. Experiments on MQuAKE show large improvements (e.g., 219% on Multi-hop QA). Four reviewers diverge: two (scores 5) find the method insightful and effective, while two (scores 3) highlight serious experimental and safety shortcomings. All agree the idea is simple and clear, but the evaluation is inadequate.

### Strengths

- **Insight into ICE mechanisms.** The paper explores ICE through a decoding lens, enhancing understanding of why ICE works and where it fails, particularly regarding stubborn knowledge. (Reviewer 4)
- **Simple and efficient method.** DeCK does not require model parameter access; it is a plug-in decoding component for any ICE method. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Effectiveness on stubborn knowledge.** Extensive experiments show significant performance gains, especially on hard-to-edit facts, with ablation studies confirming the design. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Clear writing and presentation.** The approach is well-organized and easy to follow. (Reviewers 2, 4)

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient and outdated baselines.** Only naive versions of ROME and IKE (plus MeLLo) are compared. Missing are established KE methods such as SERAC, MEND, RAE, Pokemqa, and newer developments like MEMIT. Also missing are related contrastive decoding methods that also aim to increase reliance on edited knowledge (e.g., CAD, DoLA, SAE). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Narrow evaluation metrics.** The evaluation overlooks standard editing metrics such as locality (whether unrelated facts are preserved). (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Safety risk from increased context reliance.** By amplifying the model’s reliance on in-context text, DeCK also increases the chance of following malicious instructions or jailbreak prompts, a security risk that is not discussed or mitigated. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Limited dataset generality.** Experiments are confined to MQuAKE. It is unclear whether DeCK generalizes to other knowledge-conflict datasets (e.g., NQ-Swap, MacNoise, ConflictBank) or to other domains. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Lack of precise definition for "stubborn knowledge".** The central concept lacks a quantifiable definition, making it hard to assess how DeCK specifically differs from conventional methods. (Reviewer 4)
- **Incomplete analysis of ICE failures.** The paper claims to be the first to elucidate ICE performance, but prior work (e.g., arxiv 2306.09306) already showed ICE’s advantages over parametric editing. A deeper analysis of why IKE methods fail in certain cases and how DeCK overcomes them is needed. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Computational overhead.** DeCK requires two inference passes per instance (edited vs. unedited), doubling inference cost, which may hinder practical deployment. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing study on model size.** It is unknown how DeCK scales to larger models (e.g., LLAMA 3 70B). (Reviewer 4)
- **DeCK is evaluated only on multi-hop QA (MQuAKE) but targets single-fact editing;** should be compared with traditional KE benchmarks and methods (e.g., IKE variants) more thoroughly. (Reviewer 3)

*Reviewer disagreement:* Two reviewers (scores 5) see DeCK as a novel and effective contribution, while two (scores 3) consider the experimental evaluation too weak to support the claims, citing missing baselines, metrics, and safety concerns. The positive reviewers also note limitations, but view the core idea as promising; the negative reviewers argue the shortcomings substantially undermine the paper’s conclusions.
