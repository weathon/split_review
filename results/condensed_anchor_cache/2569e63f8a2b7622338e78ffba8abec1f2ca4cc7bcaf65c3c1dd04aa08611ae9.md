## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Repetitive Contrastive Learning (RCL) method to enhance Mamba's sequence selection capability for time series forecasting. It uses repeating sequence augmentation with Gaussian noise and both inter-sequence and intra-sequence contrastive learning to pretrain a single Mamba block, then transfers parameters to Mamba-based models. Experiments show performance gains on several Mamba-based forecasting models.

### Strengths
- The work is one of the first to explore contrastive learning in Mamba models for time series.
- The proposed repeating sequence augmentation and contrastive learning methods are simple, easy to implement, and transferable to various Mamba-based TSF architectures.
- Extensive experiments demonstrate that Mamba-based TSF models show significant performance improvement after applying the proposed pretraining and augmentation methods.
- Visualizations (Figures 3 and 4) indicate that the pretraining is effective.
- The paper addresses the important gap of teaching models to select and prioritize key time points, which is vital for prediction accuracy.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing/Insufficient baselines**:  
  - No comparisons with non-Mamba methods, making it unclear where the proposed method stands against current state-of-the-art.  
  - Missing comparisons against recent SOTA baselines from arXiv:2405.14616, arXiv:2403.09898.  
  - Missing comparisons against univariate baselines.  
  - Missing comparisons with existing time-series representation learning/data augmentation + downstream forecasting methods (e.g., arXiv:2303.11911, arXiv:2312.16424, arXiv:2206.08496).  
  - Missing comparisons with MLP-based architectures (arXiv:1905.10437, arXiv:2201.12886) in discussion and as baselines.  
  - The baseline set is too small and not representative; more Mamba-based and non-Mamba-based methods are needed.
- **Key claim unsupported**: The paper claims the pretraining enhances Mamba's “selectivity,” but this claim is not substantiated. “Selectivity” appears only in the title and in statements about freezing matrix A; there is no empirical or qualitative evidence that selectivity is improved. The performance gain may instead stem from contrastive learning's effect on latent-space structure rather than enhanced selection ability. Proposed needed: define selectivity, measure improvement, or rebrand the explanation (e.g., “improving representation quality”).
- **Technical novelty concerns**:  
  - The repetitive augmentation simply creates negative pairs by concatenating duplicated noisy sequences, which increases input size and computational cost. Traditional contrastive learning can generate negative pairs more efficiently.  
  - The idea of contrastive pretraining for time series is not entirely novel; the related work misrepresents novelty by focusing on image/text domains while omitting prior time-series contrastive works.  
  - It is not clear how the proposed augmentation improves upon conventional contrastive learning approaches.
- **Insufficient ablation and analysis**:  
  - No ablation of the two proposed techniques (intra-sequence contrast and repetitive contrastive learning) individually. Which is more powerful? Do they have synergistic effects?  
  - No ablation of the effect of varying Gaussian noise levels.  
  - No analysis to demonstrate that the model actually achieves effective selection and prioritization of key time points, as claimed.  
  - No analysis of whether pretrained matrix A indeed improves selectivity; analysis techniques from LLM literature (e.g., induction heads) could be used.
- **Methodological concerns**:  
  - Duplicating time points and adding noise may create large numerical differences and disrupt temporal correlations – why not use patch operations instead?  
  - Inputting the full augmented sequence \(X_{aug}\) (with all duplicated steps and noise) directly through an MLP may amplify noise and break temporal correlations.  
  - The paper uses vanilla Mamba; without input shuffling during the repeat process, sequential SSM characteristics could cause information leakage in noise generation. Bidirectional Mamba may be more appropriate.  
  - The term “selective scan mechanism” is misleading: the “scan” in Mamba refers to hardware parallelization, not algorithmic selection.
- **Experimental setup lacking clarity**:  
  - Missing basic forecasting settings: input lookback length, forecasting horizon, train-test split.  
  - How exactly are metrics in Table 1 computed? Why is the presentation format different from prior works on the same datasets?  
  - The experimental evaluation is insufficient for venue standards; recent works include more datasets or more comprehensive setups.  
  - Improvements over weak baselines do not provide strong evidence of contribution.
- **Generality not shown**: The contrastive techniques appear not to be specific to Mamba; applying them to other model families (e.g., Transformers) would help assess significance. The paper does not explore this.
- **Parameter sharing not clarified**: It is unclear whether all MambaPB blocks share the same parameters or are trained independently during self-training—if shared, the approach would be more interesting.
- **Poor presentation and writing**:  
  - Many mathematical symbols in Figures 1 and 2 do not render correctly.  
  - Citation format is inconsistent; frequent textual citations where parenthetical would be better.  
  - Symbols are ill-defined, operational flowchart is unclear, and there are typos and repeated sentences.  
  - Overall readability and comprehension need significant improvement.
- **Notable reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer rated the paper 3, citing deep skepticism about the selectivity claim, lack of soundness, and insufficient novelty. Other reviewers (5,5,6) acknowledged potential but noted similar issues, especially regarding baselines and support for core claims.
