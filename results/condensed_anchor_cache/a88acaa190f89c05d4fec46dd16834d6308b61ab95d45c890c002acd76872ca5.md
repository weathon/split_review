## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces ParallelSpec, a parallel drafter for speculative decoding that replaces auto-regressive drafting with a single forward pass predicting multiple future tokens using [MASK] tokens as placeholders. A group-wise parallel training strategy aligns the drafter with the target model. ParallelSpec can be integrated into frameworks like Medusa and EAGLE. Experiments on Llama-2-7B/13B and Vicuna-7B/13B (SpecBench) show latency reductions up to 62% and overall speedups up to 2.84× (Llama-2-13B). The method is lossless via rejection sampling.

### Strengths

+ The parallel drafter is a novel alternative to auto-regressive speculative decoding, enabling efficient multi-token generation in a single forward pass. (R1,R5)
+ Simple, elegant, and clearly presented method with intuitive motivation for reducing speculation overhead. (R2,R3,R4,R5)
+ Integrates seamlessly with state-of-the-art frameworks like Medusa and EAGLE. (R1,R2,R5)
+ Strong empirical results: consistent speedup gains over Medusa (~37‑62%) and EAGLE (~9‑17%), with up to 1.1× improvement on EAGLE alone. (R1,R2,R3,R4,R5) — *One minority reviewer (R5, score 8/contribution 4) finds these gains substantial and the method highly effective.*
+ Well-designed experiments with clear presentation of hyperparameters, ablation studies (e.g., effect of K, self-distillation), and mainstream benchmarks. (R1,R5)
+ Lossless decoding preserved via rejection sampling, maintaining the target model’s distribution. (R1)
+ Problem well-motivated: speculation overhead is a critical bottleneck in end-to-end speedup. (R4)

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty concerns**: The core idea of parallel drafting in speculative decoding has been explored before (Stern et al. “Blockwise Parallel Decoding”, Monea et al. “PaSS”, Medusa). ParallelSpec’s architecture using [MASK] tokens closely resembles prior work, with the incremental difference of applying it with a separate drafter. (R3,R4)
- **Unfair comparison with Medusa**: In Medusa+ParallelSpec, the draft model is a single-layer Transformer (lines 266‑267), whereas standalone Medusa uses MLP heads. It is unclear what fraction of the speedup comes from the architecture change versus the parallel drafting itself. A baseline controlling for architecture is needed. (R5)
- **Inconsistent baseline results**: Reported speedups for standalone Medusa and EAGLE are lower than in the official SpecBench leaderboard (e.g., Medusa on Vicuna-7B: 1.42× vs 2.42×; EAGLE on Vicuna-7B: 2.18× vs 2.39×). This discrepancy affects the perceived improvement of ParallelSpec (the 62% speedup claim in the abstract is based on a particularly weak Medusa baseline). R3 recommends tempering claims; the practical significance hinges on a fair comparison. (R3,R5)
- **Marginal gain over EAGLE**: The speedup over EAGLE is only ~9‑17% (roughly 1.1×), which is modest given the added complexity of a new training paradigm and attention mechanism. (R2, echoed by R3)
- **Incomplete temperature=1 evaluation**: Table 1 includes temperature=1 only for a subset of baselines and target models. The comparison between Medusa and Medusa+ParallelSpec is missing at temperature=1, which is critical for assessing robustness under stochastic decoding (more realistic usage). (R2)
- **Reproducibility issues**: No public code is provided (R1). The tree construction details in Appendix A.3 are insufficient—only “top-k highest probabilities” with “manually crafted rules” are given, without specifics on how k is chosen per depth. (R3)
- **Training efficiency**: The group-wise parallel training leads to a larger processed dataset than EAGLE, making training less efficient. (R4)
- **High-temperature behavior**: Reduced alignment at higher temperatures is not well explored; the paper lacks analysis of speedup‑accuracy tradeoffs and how rejection sampling performs when the draft distribution diverges from the target. (R1)
- **Missing evaluation on larger models** (e.g., LLaMA-2-70B) where speculation overhead matters less but inference speedup is most critical. (R4)
- **Unclear presentation of EAGLE adaptation**: The description of how hidden states are processed in ParallelSpec+EAGLE (lines 355‑357) is insufficient; more details are needed (even in the appendix). (R5)
- **Algorithm 1 issues**: Several inaccuracies (e.g., inconsistent notation $x_{<n}$ vs $x_{<t}$, unclear tree generation, missing use of output O, ambiguous notation $H(s)$, normalization residual line with extra “+”). These can be fixed but reduce clarity. (R3)
- **Minor issues**: Figure 1’s wall-time trace is misleading: latency savings are only guaranteed if the parallel draft model has the same speculation accuracy as the auto-regressive model; this assumption should be stated. (R4) The left side of Figure 1 could better distinguish auto-regressive vs. parallel drafting (R3). End of intro—relative improvement for EAGLE should be stated alongside the Medusa number (R3). Notation inconsistencies in Section 3 (e.g., $x_k$ definition) should be corrected (R3). Table 2’s upper and lower halves should be split into separate tables (R5). No comparison against EAGLE-2 is provided (R4).
