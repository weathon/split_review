## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a framework for human-like communication in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL). It uses a text-to-mask model to focus on relevant observation features and incorporates imitation of human demonstrations as an auxiliary objective. The hybrid approach combines RL (PPO) with rule-based strategies and is tested on two newly proposed multi-agent tasks. The authors claim faster learning and improved interpretability.

### Strengths
- The idea of object-oriented, mask-based messaging is relatively novel to a small extent (Reviewer 2) – it addresses key MARL challenges like non-stationarity and interpretability.
- The text-to-mask model facilitates interpretability by grounding communication contextually (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is comprehensively written overall (Reviewer 2 – though others disagree on writing quality).
- Reviewer 4 notes the research question at the beginning looks interesting, but otherwise finds no strengths.

### Weaknesses
- **Writing quality is poor** (Reviewers 1, 4): Numerous grammatical mistakes, informal wording, typos. Examples: Line 58 “it could be beneficial assimilating to human beings”; Line 87 “problems is both interesting and challenging, thus gained some attentions”; Line 128 “centralized → decentralized”; Line 202 “need → needs”; Line 242 “is can be → can be”; Line 239 “would want to → would like to”. This significantly harms presentation.
- **Lack of motivation in introduction** (Reviewers 1, 2): The analogy to human communication (prior knowledge, contextual awareness, efficient exchange) is not specific enough. Section 4.1 partially addresses it, but the introduction needs concrete examples of how these strategies apply to MARL. Reviewer 2 notes the motivation could be more specific about which aspects of human-like communication are considered.
- **Missing comparisons to standard MARL baselines** (Reviewers 1, 2, 3): Not compared against IPPO, MAPPO, QMix (Reviewer 1), nor against MARL communication baselines like AEComm (Lin et al., 2021) (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 says the experimental section lacks comprehensive benchmarks against state-of-the-art communication strategies, making comparative advantage unclear.
- **Experiments on toy, non-standard environments** (Reviewers 1, 2, 3): Only 2 agents in 2 relatively simple, newly proposed tasks. Reviewer 1 asks for validation on standard environments (e.g., OpenAI Multi-Particle Environment suite, StarCraft). Reviewer 2 says the method’s effectiveness is poorly supported with only simple environments. Reviewer 3 notes limited demonstrated applicability to complex real-world MARL scenarios.
- **No hyperparameter details provided** (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited original theoretical contribution** (Reviewer 3): The framework mainly integrates existing methods (text-to-mask, PPO) – lacks novel theoretical insights.
- **Weak analogy to human communication** (Reviewer 3): Proposed strategies focus on rule-based data exchange, not capturing depth of real human interactions.
- **Overlooks communication policy instability during training** (Reviewer 3): This could pose challenges in dynamic environments.
- **Missing citation** (Reviewer 2): The “complex” problem description in the introduction matches the cheap talk discovery problem (phone booth maze) from Lo et al. (ICLR 2023) – should be cited.
- **Hand-designed message space limits generalization** (Reviewer 2, question): The method seems hard to generalize given need to hand-design message space; authors should elaborate.
- **Reviewer 4 raises critical technical concerns:**
  - Line 162: Switching observations of two agents may not be valid when reward function is invariant to observations or symmetric; relation between reward and observations always exists.
  - Lines 193-195: Why does representing observations as collections of feature-sets (each corresponding to a textual term for an object) enhance identification of object relevancy?
  - Lines 199-200: Inaccuracy from pretrained object detection models (used for image observations) can cause unexpected errors, making embeddings uninterpreted.
  - Lines 208-210: Why choose decoupled policies for actions and communications? If unjustified, this only adds more parameters.
  - Lines 237-239: Why is the design for π^comm (communication policy) reasonable? What special considerations?
  - Lines 243-244: Why is the encoder network disregarded at inference? Needs more clarification.
  - Lines 289-292: How is the rule-based policy or human demonstrations guaranteed to exist for every task? Also, using behavioral cloning contradicts the claim in lines 65-67 that collecting demonstrations is hard.
- **Reviewer 1 (minority positive) gave score 3 while others gave 3,3,1.** Reviewer 2 (score 5) is the most positive, but acknowledges weaknesses in scope and missing baselines. Reviewer 4 (score 1) is extremely negative and finds no strengths beyond the initial question.
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms or missing experiments** (converted to weaknesses above): missing standard baselines/environments, hyperparameter details, generalization of message space, justification of decoupled policies, existence of demonstrations.
