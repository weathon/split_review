## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces PAdaMFed, a federated learning algorithm that combines a SCAFFOLD-like client-drift control mechanism, normalized client updates, and global momentum to achieve hyperparameters that are independent of problem-specific parameters (e.g., smoothness constant \(L\), gradient variance bounds, heterogeneity bounds). A variance-reduced variant, PAdaMFed-VR, is also proposed. The authors provide convergence guarantees without assuming gradient dissimilarity, with sample complexity \(\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-4})\) and communication complexity \(\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})\) for the standard version, and improved \(\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})\) and \(\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})\) for PAdaMFed-VR. Experiments on EMNIST and IMDB (the latter in the appendix) show that PAdaMFed outperforms SCAFFOLD even after hyperparameter tuning for SCAFFOLD. The paper is well-motivated and addresses important practical issues in FL, but several reviewers raise concerns about the clarity and extent of the “problem-parameter free” claim, the limited empirical evaluation, missing comparisons, and the practical overhead. One reviewer (Reviewer 5) is notably more critical, questioning the independence claim and the reproducibility of experiments.

### Strengths

1. **Problem-parameter independence.** The algorithm’s hyperparameters depend only on system-defined constants (number of local steps \(K\), total rounds \(T\), number of sampled clients \(S\)) and not on unknown problem-specific constants such as the smoothness constant \(L\) or gradient dissimilarity bounds. Multiple reviewers (1,2,3,4,5) agree this is a significant contribution.
2. **Convergence without heterogeneity bounds.** The analysis does not require any bound on data heterogeneity (gradient dissimilarity), a standard assumption in many FL works (Reviewers 2,3).
3. **State-of-the-art theoretical rates.** The paper provides rigorous sample and communication complexity bounds (\(\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-4})/\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})\) for the standard version, \(\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})/\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})\) with variance reduction) and linear speedup in the number of local steps and participating clients (Reviewers 3,5). Reviewer 1 notes the analytical convergence bounds as a strength.
4. **Variance-reduced variant.** PAdaMFed-VR is introduced to further reduce variance and achieve improved complexities (Reviewer 1).
5. **First fully parameter-independent algorithm.** Reviewer 3 considers this the first work to provide hyperparameters completely free of problem-specific parameters.
6. **Empirical outperformance.** The experiments show PAdaMFed (with theory-given hyperparameters) outperforms SCAFFOLD even after tuning SCAFFOLD’s parameters (Reviewer 3). The results validate the method on image classification (EMNIST) and sentiment analysis (IMDB).
7. **Well-motivated and well-written.** The paper clearly motivates the need for parameter-free FL and the presentation is generally clear (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

1. **Clarity of “problem-parameter free” claim.** Several reviewers question the extent of independence. Reviewer 5 argues that although the stepsizes themselves are independent of \(L\), the theoretical analysis still implicitly relies on \(L\) through Assumptions A3/A4 and Lemmas 3/4, which bound error terms based on the interaction of the stepsizes with smoothness, making the claim questionable. Reviewer 3 asks for a more detailed explanation of *what exactly* enables hyperparameter independence, particularly how the normalization (Algorithm 1, line 8) interacts with momentum and control variates. Reviewer 4 notes that the gradient normalization discards magnitude information, which is likely why \(L\) is not needed, but this comes at the cost of a worse convergence rate comparable to sub-gradient methods, since smoothness is not exploited.
2. **Theoretical comparison and optimality.** Reviewer 2 notes that the convergence bound is not optimal compared to SCAFFOLD-M; a fair comparison requires deriving the equivalent formulation for SCAFFOLD-M under the same conditions, especially when \(K\) (local updates) is a function of \(T\) (total rounds). Reviewer 1 requests a detailed comparison table (like Table 2 in the SCAFFOLD paper) showing convergence rates/communication complexities of PAdaMFed, PAdaMFed-VR, SCAFFOLD, and related algorithms, and an explanation of the gain from momentum and variance reduction. Reviewer 3 notes that empirical outperformance over SCAFFOLD is not reflected in the theory; an explanation of this gap is needed.
3. **Insufficient explanation of variance reduction in PAdaMFed-VR.** Reviewer 3 finds the discussion in Section 3.2 unsatisfactory; it is unclear how the specific form (difference of gradients at different points) reduces variance. Questions arise about its resemblance to STORM and how \(\beta\) should be chosen to balance variance reduction and client drift control.
4. **Limited and not fully reproducible empirical evaluation.** 
   - **Scope:** The experiments are limited to two datasets (EMNIST and IMDB) and a single architecture; Reviewer 2 recommends testing on a wider range of architectures and datasets.
   - **Reproducibility:** No code is provided, and details of hyperparameter tuning for baselines (grid search) are missing; Reviewer 4 states the experiments are not reproducible.
   - **Concerns about baseline performance:** Reviewer 5 notes FedAvg achieves only 0.8 accuracy on EMNIST IID, which seems low; Reviewer 4 is surprised that PAdaMFed (with theory-given hyperparameters, no grid search) outperforms grid-searched baselines so significantly, and asks whether tuning PAdaMFed’s parameters would give even better results.
   - **Metrics:** Reviewer 4 points out that only accuracy (a machine learning metric) is reported, while all theory concerns optimization metrics (gradient norm); a plot showing gradient norm over iterations would help align theory with experiments.
   - **Missing ablation:** Reviewer 5 requests an ablation study to isolate the contributions of adaptive stepsizes, momentum, and the control variate to the overall performance.
5. **Communication overhead.** Reviewer 5 notes that the algorithm requires communication of three vectors per round (\(\theta_i\), \(c_i^{t-1}\), \(g_i^{t-1}\)), which is a substantial increase; a detailed analysis and comparison with other methods is needed. Reviewer 2 suggests that since \(c^{t-1}\) and \(g^{t-1}\) are only used as \(\beta c^{t-1} + (1-\beta) g^{t-1}\), the weighted sum could be sent instead, saving communication.
6. **Dependence on number of sampled clients \(S\).** Reviewer 4 asks what happens if the number of sampled nodes changes between iterations, since \(S\) is used in hyperparameter selection.
7. **Missing comparisons.** 
   - Reviewer 4 mentions that many FL algorithms already use diminishing step-sizes (e.g., step-decay) that do not require knowledge of \(L\) and come with theoretical guarantees; these are not compared.
   - Reviewer 2 notes that a related work (citation [1], likely on SCAFFOLD-M variants) is overlooked; a comparison on handling client drift is needed.
8. **Proof novelty and clarity.** Reviewer 1 states that the idea is a direct extension of SCAFFOLD with momentum added to each client, and that the challenges in the proof compared to SCAFFOLD are unclear. Reviewer 1 asks for a detailed explanation of the proof challenges and how momentum/variance reduction improve upon SCAFFOLD.
9. **Minor.** Reviewer 2 points out that most related works use \(\|\nabla f(x)\|^2 \le \epsilon\) for \(\epsilon\)-stationarity, while the paper uses \(\|\nabla f(x)\| \le \epsilon\), which can be confusing.

*Note: The above weaknesses incorporate reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms or requests for missing comparisons/experiments (e.g., “What if S changes?”, “Why not compare optimization metric?”, “Can the optimal bound be recovered with extra assumptions?”).*
