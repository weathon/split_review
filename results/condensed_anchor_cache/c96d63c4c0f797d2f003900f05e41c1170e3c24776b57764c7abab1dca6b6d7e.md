## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper applies Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) to visual data by first encoding images into discrete token IDs via VQ, then training a BPE tokenizer to merge frequent visual token sequences. The approach is tested with LLaMA-3.1-8B and evaluated on VQAv2, MMBench, VizWiz, etc. The three reviewers diverge in overall assessment: one (score 8) finds the method creative and promising, while two (score 5) consider the experimental evidence weak, the comparisons unfair, and the performance far behind current MLLM SOTA.

### Strengths
- **Novelty**: Adapting BPE for image tokenization is creative and attempts to make visual data work more seamlessly with text in multimodal models (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Structural Prior**: Integrating structural information directly into image tokens could help models better understand cross-modal alignment (Reviewer 3).
- **Theoretical Analysis**: Section 3 provides a theoretical analysis on how BPE tokenization benefits transformer learning (Reviewer 2).
- **Scaling**: Performance improves when scaling to larger datasets (e.g., ShareGPT4) during SFT (Reviewer 2).
- **Some improvement**: The results show some improvement over the VQ-only baseline (Reviewer 1).
- **Clear experiment settings**: The experimental setup is clearly described (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Performance far behind SOTA**: The best model achieves 60.6 on VQAv2, 44.0 on MMBench, and 48.2 on VizWiz, which is far behind similar-sized 7B LLaMA-based MLLMs (Reviewers 1, 2). This raises the question of the practical benefit of VQ+BPE compared to standard MLLM practices (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision) (Reviewer 2, question).
- **No projector in experiments**: The pipeline lacks a projector, making the comparison against classical MLLM pipelines (which use a projector) unfair (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient ablation**: Only one table compares LLM+VQ vs. LLM+VQ+BPE; the implementation details of the “LLM+VQ” baseline are not illustrated (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing baseline**: Should include a setting where the visual encoder is frozen during SFT (Table 5.1) (Reviewer 1, question).
- **Theory limitations**:
  - Multimodal fusion analysis is absent; the theoretical analysis only covers 2D image data, not how BPE facilitates cross-modal attention or alignment of latent spaces (Reviewer 3).
  - No theoretical exploration of information loss from BPE tokenization (e.g., high-frequency visual details); the trade-off between vocabulary size, compression, and information preservation is unquantified (Reviewer 3).
  - A simplified 2D Markov process may not adequately capture real-world image structure (Reviewer 3, question).
  - The proofs are not helpful for understanding experimental outcomes (Reviewer 1).
- **Vocabulary size drop**: Performance drops when vocabulary increases from 8k to 16k, with no theoretical explanation (Reviewer 1, question).
- **Convergence behavior**: In Figure 2(b)(c), all settings converge after ~150 iterations, making differences negligible (Reviewer 1, question).
- **Questionable numbers**: Unclear whether the value 0.99 in Lines 770 and 253 is fabricated (Reviewer 1, question).
- **Similarity to existing methods**: The proposed BPE is very similar to superpixel or clustering algorithms; the paper neither discusses the difference nor compares performance (Reviewer 1, question).
- **Limited evaluation scope**: Evaluation is restricted to VQA-like tasks (requiring broad semantic alignment). The method is not tested on tasks demanding fine-grained visual details or spatial understanding, such as image segmentation, object detection, or complex image captioning (Reviewer 3). Consequently, generalization to precision-demanding tasks is unclear (Reviewer 3, question).
- **Not tested on other LLMs**: The approach is only evaluated with LLaMA-3.1-8B; observations on other LLMs are missing (Reviewer 2, question).
