## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates the emergence of Massive Activations (MAs) in attention layers of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), focusing on Graph Transformers (GraphTransformer, GraphiT, SAN). It proposes a detection method using activation ratios between untrained and trained models, defines MAs via gamma distribution fitting, and introduces the Explicit Bias Term (EBT) as a countermeasure. Experiments on ZINC, TOX21, and PROTEINS show MAs are widespread across architectures and that EBT reduces MAs and improves robustness against a gradient ascent attack. Reviewer assessments range from 3 to 5, with disagreement on the significance of contributions and experimental scope.

### Strengths
1. Provides a rigorous definition and detection of Massive Activations (MAs) and experimentally confirms MAs as a widespread phenomenon across multiple attention-based GNNs (GraphTransformer, GraphiT, SAN) and datasets (ZINC, TOX21, PROTEINS).
2. Proposes the Explicit Bias Term (EBT) to mitigate MAs, which is shown to reduce MAs effectively and enhance model robustness against a gradient ascent attack; EBT is applicable across downstream tasks.
3. Includes comprehensive empirical experiments and various visualizations (activation ratio distributions, gamma distribution fitting) that offer insights into MA behavior.
4. The study is beneficial for the community working on graph transformer improvements, improving understanding of attention in graph structures.
5. Writing is logically clear and coherent.

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient theoretical justification for EBT**: The mechanism by which bias terms counteract MAs is not explained; no comparison to alternative mitigation strategies is provided. One reviewer notes that the detection methodology and EBT may not constitute a significant contribution.
2. **Limited experimental scope**: Only three datasets (ZINC, TOX21, PROTEINS) and older models (GraphTransformer, GraphiT, SAN) are used. Missing evaluations on more real-world datasets (e.g., OGBN for graph-level tasks) and more recent attention-based GNNs (e.g., GATv2, Graph conjoint attentions networks, Adaptive structural fingerprint GNN, SGFormer, models that compute attention for non-adjacent nodes). One reviewer found the experiments comprehensive, but others strongly disagree.
3. **Poor presentation quality**: Figures 1 and 2 show activation ratios with inconsistent baselines (untrained vs. trained), hindering direct comparison. Figure 3 fits a single gamma distribution across different models, layers, and datasets without justifying its appropriateness. Tables lack essential formatting (e.g., at least two lines) and do not report variance across multiple trials, making results statistically unconvincing.
4. **Unclear motivations and definitions**: The reason for focusing on Graph Transformers over conventional attention-based GNNs (e.g., GATs) is not explained. The term "edge feature" is not precisely defined, and the connection between MAs and downstream performance is not clearly established – tables provide indirect evidence. The paper does not define "unimportant feature" nor analyze correlation with feature importance.
5. **Contribution may be overstated**: The work primarily transfers concepts from prior work on LLMs (Sun et al., 2024) without significant novelty in the graph domain.
6. **Technical motivation for detection method is weak**: The use of an untrained model to estimate MAs vs. using the median activation value within a layer is not justified; the normalization procedure is unclear, raising concerns about the reliability of MA detection.
7. **Insufficient ablation studies**: The ablation study in Table 2 is not extended to all three Graph Transformer variants on all test datasets, limiting validation of EBT's generalizability.
8. **Claims about dataset independence of MAs are contradicted**: The paper claims MAs are independent of dataset choice (line 197), but Figure 1 shows significant variation across datasets for the same architecture, requiring clarification.
9. **Vague statements**: On page 5 line 258, the statement “When MAs appear, we have found two possible phenomena” is vague; a more detailed mechanistic explanation is needed.
10. **Constraint on attention not justified**: The paper only considers attention between adjacent nodes, but many graph transformers compute attention for all node pairs; this constraint is not justified.
