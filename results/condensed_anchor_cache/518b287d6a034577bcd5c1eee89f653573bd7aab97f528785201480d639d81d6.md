## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Integrative Decoding (ID), a decoding method that extends self-consistency to open-ended generation tasks. ID first samples N responses to a prompt, then uses each response as a prefix when re-answering the same question, and finally selects the next token by aggregating predictions across all such inputs at each decoding step. Experiments on TruthfulQA (+11.2%), Biographies (+15.4%), and LongFact (+8.5%) across six LLM series (Llama3, Gemma2, GLM4, and others) show improved factuality. The method requires no additional prompting or training. One reviewer was notably more positive (score 8, confidence 4) than the other three (score 6). All reviewers agree the method is simple and effective, but raise concerns about cost, evaluation rigor, and missing comparisons.

### Strengths
- The idea is simple and effective, with a clear theoretical formulation that is straightforward to implement in practice. The method’s writing is easy to follow.
- ID does not require additional prompting or training, making it more applicable than previous self-consistency methods that often constrain task format.
- Experimental results clearly support claims of factuality improvement: significant gains on TruthfulQA, Biographies, and LongFact over multiple LLM families and scales.
- Performance consistently improves as the number of sampled responses increases, demonstrating stability. One reviewer highlighted that other methods (e.g., USC, SR) do not show this monotonic improvement.
- Rather than selecting a response from candidates, ID integrates multiple responses through generation, which is an interesting and novel approach.

### Weaknesses
- **Computational cost**: The method incurs high cost—roughly double the context length (each response prepended to the prompt) and as many inference steps as the number of sampled responses. For long-form generation, this is inefficient. One reviewer noted the paper does not discuss this drawback or provide inference-time comparisons even as context. Another reviewer asked for a more detailed explanation of why the context length is exactly ×2.
- **Missing comparisons with atomic self‑consistency methods**: The paper only compares against older baselines (USC, SR) and does not include recent atomic self‑consistency approaches (references [1][2][3] in the review), which have significantly surpassed those older methods. One reviewer also suggested the related work should discuss self‑consistency for uncertainty estimation (e.g., [4]) to better motivate the research direction.
- **Weaknesses in factuality evaluation**: Factuality is evaluated using GPT-4 as a judge, with no human annotation. One reviewer stated that actual improvements could be misestimated due to potential mismatches between the automatic judge and true factuality. Another reviewer remarked that factuality improvement seems plausible only if the model is well-calibrated, and the paper lacks discussion on that perspective.
- **Need for deeper analysis of the method’s principles**: The assumption behind the approximation in Equation 4 (sum of two terms approximated via the integrative prompt) is not well-justified. One reviewer asked whether the authors attempted to recover the two separate terms (f and G) to check if the motivation holds. Another reviewer requested more detailed explanation and experimental support for the reasonableness of the core assumption. A third reviewer questioned the claim that performance “progressively improves” with more samples, noting that for Llama3, Gemma2, and GLM4 in Figure 3, accuracy increases then decreases—contradicting the stated monotonic trend.
- **Missing examples in case study**: Section 3.5 (“Case Study”) does not provide baseline examples for SR and USC, making it hard to compare qualitative differences. One reviewer raised this as a missing detail.
- **Lack of coherence/fluency evaluation**: The paper mentions that ID maintains coherence, but no quantitative evaluation of coherence or fluency is provided. One reviewer noted this absence as a weakness.
