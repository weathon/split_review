- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 5, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an over-memorization method that trains a model on both the standard training set and a separately optimized dummy set of non-sensitive instances. The dummy set is initialized with random noise, then optimized via SGD on a separate model to improve generalization. The goal is to occupy the model’s limited memorization capacity with dummy data, thereby reducing memorization of sensitive training examples. The approach is evaluated on CIFAR-10 (image classification) and Wikitext-103 (text generation) using memorization scores and the LiRA membership inference attack. Reviewers found the idea novel and the presentation clear, but raised major concerns about the lack of formal privacy guarantees, insufficient comparisons, modest empirical improvements, and unclear mechanisms.

### Strengths
- **Novel and interesting idea**: Using a dummy set to absorb memorization and protect private data is a creative direction (R2, R3).
- **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-written, well-organized, and easy to follow (R1, R2, R4).
- **Multi-task evaluation**: Experiments cover both image classification (CIFAR-10) and language modeling (Wikitext-103), demonstrating generality (R1, R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **No formal privacy guarantee** – The method lacks any theoretical privacy accounting (e.g., differential privacy). Reviewer 1 notes that prior work [r4] used dataset condensation for privacy, but [r5] showed that approach can be easily broken because it lacks formal guarantees. The proposed method may similarly give a false sense of security. Reviewer 3 also highlights the absence of a theoretical analysis of memorization reduction. The empirical evaluation should be extended beyond LiRA to include stronger attacks such as attribute inference or model inversion, and per-sample success rates (R1, R4). Even the more positive reviewer (R3) raised this concern.

2. **Insufficient comparisons with relevant baselines**  
   - **Dataset condensation**: The dummy-set idea closely resembles “dataset condensation” / “dataset distillation” (first appearing in 2021). The authors should discuss differences in optimization objective, data type (discrete vs. continuous), constraints (e.g., pixel bounds), computational cost, and provide quantitative privacy–utility comparisons with recent condensation methods [r1, r3, r4] (R1).  
   - **DP-SGD**: Tables 2 and 3 should include DP-SGD as a baseline to compare the privacy–utility trade-off at various privacy budgets (R1).  
   - **Conventional regularization**: Reviewer 2 asks for the attack AUROC when using dropout or label smoothing.  
   - **Prior dummy-data approaches**: Reviewer 4 points out that using dummy data to overcome overfitting has been studied before (e.g., [2–4] in their references); the paper must clarify how it advances beyond those works.

3. **Modest empirical privacy improvements with questionable statistical significance**  
   - **Memorization scores (Table 1)**: The reported reductions are small and the standard deviations are large, making it unclear whether the effect is statistically significant. A more rigorous analysis (confidence intervals, hypothesis testing) is needed (R2).  
   - **MIA resistance (Tables 2,3)**: For CIFAR-10, AUROC only drops from 0.6373 to 0.5995; for Wikitext-103, from 0.9688 to 0.7972. The CIFAR-10 drop is very small, and the Wikitext-103 value is still high, indicating substantial remaining memorization. Per-sample attack success rates (e.g., on easily memorized samples) are missing (R4). Reviewer 3 requests MIA results on both training and dummy samples to verify that the dummy set indeed occupies capacity.

4. **Unclear necessity and effect of dummy set optimization**  
   - Reviewer 2 questions the claim (line 248) that optimizing the dummy set to minimize its loss improves generalization. Intuitively, this optimization may cause dummy samples to resemble each other or the training data, reducing diversity and their ability to consume capacity. A comparison of MIA accuracy and test accuracy *with and without* this optimization is required.  
   - In Algorithm 2 (line 4), the model parameter \(\tilde{\theta}\) is updated using gradients computed on the dummy set itself. Reviewer 1 asks why this is needed and requests an ablation study examining the impact of this update (or alternative mechanisms).  
   - Reviewer 4 adds that using randomly initialized dummy data optimized via SGD may harm model utility; further discussion and experiments on utility are needed.

5. **Missing experiments on CIFAR-100** – Adding results on CIFAR-100 would demonstrate robustness across dataset complexity and number of classes (R1).

6. **Issues with dummy set scaling and correlation with training data**  
   - Reviewer 2 asks how the optimal dummy set size scales with model size (e.g., does a larger model require a proportionally larger dummy set?).  
   - Reviewer 3 notes that correlation between dummy samples and training samples is not addressed. The paper should analyze mutual information or other statistical measures and explore methods to minimize this correlation during generation (e.g., adversarial training or explicit decorrelation constraints).  
   - Reviewer 3 further notes that performance is affected by dummy set size; a detailed analysis of the relationship (including computational cost) and methods for adaptive sizing (e.g., early stopping) are needed.

7. **Missing evaluation of side effects** – The paper does not study how the dummy set affects model robustness (adversarial attacks), prediction uncertainty, calibration, or fairness (R3). These are important for practical deployment.

8. **Experimental setup concerns** – The reported clean-trained ResNet-18 test accuracy on CIFAR-10 is 87.58%, which is much lower than the expected >90%. Reviewer 1 asks for clarification of the training procedure to ensure fair comparisons.

9. **Overlooked related work on memorization mitigation** – Reviewer 4 states that important prior work on reducing memorization via dummy data insertion is not discussed; the authors should rectify this.

10. **Minor inconsistencies and missing details**  
    - Notation inconsistency: \(S_t \cup S_d \setminus \{z_t\}\) vs. \(S_t \cup S_d \setminus z_t\) (R3).  
    - Potentially misleading statement (line 200): “adding \(S_d\) to \(S_t \cup S_d\) should not cause a significant change” – likely a typo; please clarify (R3).  
    - “Unintended memorization” should be clearly defined and distinguished from normal memorization (R3).  
    - No figures of the final dummy samples; showing them would help interpret whether they resemble natural images or random noise (R3).  
    - Details on MIA construction missing: number of reference models and how they are chosen (R3).  
    - Data augmentation usage during training not specified; its effect on memorization should be discussed (R3).
