## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes OmniBind, a framework that fuses 14 pre-trained multimodal models (supporting 3D, audio, image, video, and language) into a unified representation space via a learnable dynamic weight router. The router is trained with cross-modal overall alignment and language representation decoupling to mitigate knowledge interference. The method is lightweight and training-efficient. Extensive experiments demonstrate strong zero-shot generalization and cross-modal alignment across diverse benchmarks and applications.

### Strengths
- **Novel dynamic routing approach**: Moves beyond fixed-weight averaging by introducing a learnable routing mechanism that dynamically assigns weights to different input combinations, enhancing cross-modal alignment and reducing interference between knowledge sources. This offers a flexible way to integrate multiple pre-trained spaces. (Rev1, Rev4)
- **Thorough evaluation**: Performance is validated across a wide range of modality combinations and benchmarks (Tables 2, 3), often outperforming the “individual best” specialist source model on the target modality. This strengthens claims of improved generalization and versatility. (Rev1, Rev3)
- **Practical applications**: Multiple examples (any-query object localization, audio separation, 3D-audio retrieval, composable multimodal understanding) demonstrate real-world potential. (Rev1, Rev4)
- **Training efficiency**: The binding and routing rely on lightweight networks, making the approach extremely training-efficient (line 041). (Rev4; note: Rev4 questions this claim—see Weaknesses)
- **Extensibility and significance**: The idea can be easily adapted to new modalities and may lead to more exciting applications. (Rev3)
- **Clarity**: Key ideas are clearly presented with text and figure illustrations; the paper is relatively easy to follow (Rev3; note: Rev4 disagrees—see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Pseudo-pair construction limitations**:
  - The method relies on pseudo-paired data (e.g., audio from AudioSet, then retrieving most similar texts from AudioCaps/Clotho), which raises validity concerns in real-world scenarios. Predictions from other models introduce compounding errors and biases that may propagate into OmniBind. (Rev1)
  - For audio, using AudioSet audio and retrieving texts from AudioCaps is somewhat redundant, because AudioCaps’ audio comes from AudioSet. Moreover, the training set includes AudioCaps data, so comparisons in Table 2 may be unfair; also, AudioCaps annotations are highly correlated with AudioSet category annotations, raising label leakage concerns for zero-shot classification on AudioSet (Table 3). (Rev2)
  - Missing ablation: No side-by-side comparison of pseudo-item pair retrieval vs. pseudo-embedding aggregation (as in FreeBind) to demonstrate claimed efficiency and robustness. (Rev3)
  - No direct or indirect quality measurement of the curated pseudo item-pairs is provided. (Rev3)
- **Computational overhead not quantified**: The dynamic routing adds complexity (memory, latency, inference time) that may become prohibitive with scaling. The paper lacks a discussion of training/inference speeds, computational cost of the router, and trade-offs between performance gains and increased costs (e.g., for resource-constrained devices or real-time applications). (Rev1, Rev4)
- **Language representation decoupling issues**:
  - The need for decoupling is not supported with statistics on the number of such cases. (Rev2)
  - The approach separately aligns image-text and audio-text encoders, which seems contradictory to the goal of a universal representation. (Rev2)
  - The claim of significant biases in textual descriptions across modalities (line 258) lacks supporting evidence, references, or quantitative analysis (e.g., feature distribution visualizations). (Rev4)
- **Missing comparisons and ablations**:
  - The method falls under multi-task learning; comparisons with explicit multi-task methods are needed to demonstrate advantages. (Rev2)
  - No ablation on expert load balancing in the Mixture of Experts framework; the paper does not discuss whether the router equally distributes load or favors certain experts, nor the implications. (Rev4)
- **Generalizability concerns**: The paper does not explain how to incorporate new modalities (or more granular representations) without significant retraining or re-engineering. It is unclear whether adding a new space requires re-computation of all existing spaces (Equation 4 suggests all embeddings are adjusted). (Rev1, Rev4; Rev3 asks for clarification on iterative/incremental binding feasibility)
- **Dataset recency**: The benchmarks used are mostly quite old; more updated ones should be considered. (Rev2)
- **Incremental contribution**: The main differences from prior work (e.g., FreeBind) are the pseudo-item generation and learnable weight routing, which some reviewers view as a natural extension (Rev3; note: Rev3 also calls the approach novel, but acknowledges the incremental nature. This contrast is preserved.)
- **Presentation issues**: The paper is hard to follow and not well-written; motivation and method sections are unclear, and there are inconsistencies in notation (e.g., “item pair {p, a, v, t}” on line 211 is not defined). Citation errors occur at L53, L60, L124, L155, etc. (Rev4, Rev3)
- **Missing qualitative examples**: While results show binding sometimes improves over individual best, no qualitative examples of such improvements are provided. (Rev3)
