## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates how the choice of probability path model affects latent flow matching for spatio-temporal forecasting. The authors propose a new probability path model designed to exploit continuity and correlation in spatio-temporal data, claiming faster convergence and improved predictive performance. Reviewers generally find the paper well-written and its experiments extensive, but raise significant concerns about vague motivation, missing comparisons, lack of theoretical grounding, and the appropriateness of the framework and metrics for probabilistic forecasting.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and accessible, providing a unified framework for probability path models in flow matching and diffusion models (Reviewer 1).
- It offers a thorough discussion and comparison of various probabilistic forecasting models, which is informative and insightful (Reviewer 2).
- The proposed probability path model accounts for spatio-temporal continuity and correlation, leading to faster convergence and more stable training (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Experiments are intensive and provide support for the proposed model, including a comprehensive ablation study (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The ability to perform spatio-temporal forecasting is considered a major strength; the approach formalizes flow-based forecasting via a neural-network-parametrized probabilistic path that reduces to second-order regression (Reviewer 3).
- Reviewer 3 also notes a novel theoretical framework (though this is later criticized as system-building; see weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Vague motivation and insufficient formalization:** The motivation for the new probability path is presented vaguely and lacks a rigorous mathematical illustration (Reviewer 1).  
- **Missing comparisons with existing diffusion-based methods:** Numerous diffusion-based spatio-temporal forecasting methods exist but are not compared (Reviewer 1).  
- **Ablation on latent space needed:** All baselines use an encoder; an ablation study demonstrating the benefits of modeling in latent space is missing (Reviewer 1).  
- **Encoder training unknown:** If the encoder is trained separately from flow matching, the objective function used to train the encoder is not stated (Reviewer 1, question overlapping with missing detail).
- **Notational confusion:** It is unclear whether $v_t$ in Algorithm 1 and $v_s$ in Algorithm 2 refer to the same vector field (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of theoretical justification for faster convergence:** The observation that the proposed path yields faster convergence and requires fewer sampling steps is purely empirical; theoretical derivations or insights are missing (Reviewer 2).  
- **Unclear sampling details:** How the $s_n$ values in the sampling algorithm are determined is not explained (Reviewer 2, question).  
- **Unjustified variance schedule:** The reasoning for setting highest variance at the middle and lowest at start/end is not provided (Reviewer 2, question).  
- **No extension to other diffusion formulations:** It is unclear whether the proposed probability path has an equivalent form for score matching or noise-prediction objectives and whether it would bring the same benefits (Reviewer 2, question).
- **Inappropriate framework and distributional assumptions:** Stochastic processes and ODE-based methods are known to underperform for non-Gaussian and non-stationary data; the use of Gaussian probability paths cannot deal with real-world fat-tailed distributions (Reviewer 3).  
- **Standard techniques presented as contributions:** Conditional probability paths and marginalization are standard in generative AI (Reviewer 3).  
- **Second-order metrics are suboptimal for probabilistic models:** All evaluation metrics are second-order (MSE, Frobenius norm, PSNR), which are inadequate for a probabilistic model; higher-order probabilistic metrics should be used (Reviewer 3).  
- **System-building rather than deep framework:** The approach integrates too many concepts (encoders, MLPs, MSE regression, optimal transport, diffusion, ODEs, Gaussian models) and appears more like an engineering exercise than a fundamentally new methodological contribution (Reviewer 3).  
- **Specific questions from Reviewer 3:** Have the authors considered elliptical probability distributions or elliptical mixture models to handle non-Gaussian data? Which higher-order probabilistic metrics would they use? (These overlap with the general criticism of distributional assumptions and metrics.)

**Reviewer disagreement:** Reviewer 3’s view that the work is a “system-building exercise” with limited novelty contrasts with Reviewer 2’s appreciation of the framework as informative and with Reviewer 1’s praise of the unified perspective. Additionally, Reviewer 3 considers the experiments extensive, while Reviewer 1 finds the experimental evaluation incomplete (missing comparisons and ablations).
