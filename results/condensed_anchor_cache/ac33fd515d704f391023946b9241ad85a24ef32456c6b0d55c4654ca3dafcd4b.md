## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a three-stage fine-tuning method to adapt an English CLIP model (using QWEN 1.5 for translation and a Translation Filtering Network) to a Chinese CLIP model, called YouCLIP. The stages are: embedding layer alignment, token fusion (training first half of the text encoder), and contrastive learning fine-tuning. The model achieves new SoTAs on Chinese benchmarks (e.g., COCO-CN Recall@1 from 63.4 to 73.1) with minimal loss on English tasks (ImageNet Top-1 76.9) and lower training resources than previous Chinese CLIP models trained from scratch.

### Strengths
- Clear writing and well-demonstrated experiments showing effectiveness of the proposed method.
- State-of-the-art results across almost all Chinese benchmarks, with significant gains (e.g., COCO-CN text-to-image R@1 from 63.4 to 73.1) while retaining strong English performance (ImageNet Top-1 76.9).
- The three-stage training pipeline is described in detail and uses much less computing resources than training a Chinese CLIP from scratch.
- Knowledge-distillation framework for converting an English CLIP to other languages is easy to understand and addresses an interesting research question.
- Experimental results are technically sound (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Heavy reliance on the translation system:** The high performance depends on QWEN 1.5, a much larger LLM than CLIP itself, which was trained with enormous resources. Thus the claim of “least training resources” is misleading—the translation model itself required huge resources. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limited analysis of the translation system’s impact:** The effect of the translation step on the final performance is not sufficiently analyzed; only the AFN (translation filter) is analyzed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing pipeline baseline:** Reviewer 1 suggests adding a baseline that first translates Chinese captions to English using the same translation system, then uses the original English CLIP. Would YouCLIP outperform this pipeline?
- **Multilingual claim is unsupported:** The paper’s title and motivation mention multilingual, but it only supports Chinese and English (two languages). It is unclear how the model performs on other languages, especially low-resource ones. (Reviewer 3; also Reviewer 2 asks about applying to other languages and robustness.)
- **Limited technical novelty:** Aligning languages by training embedding layers and using non-English image-text pairs is a common approach. (Reviewer 3)
- **Arbitrary architectural design choices:** The assumption that the primary difference between text encoders is in the embedding layer, yet stage 1 trains only embeddings while stage 2 trains the first half of the text encoder. The rationale for splitting at half rather than other choices is not justified. (Reviewer 3)
- **Unclear experimental details and missing comparisons:**
  - Figure 1 (Left) needs scale/values for Wukong and CN-CLIP results. (Reviewer 2)
  - Table 2’s formatting: “Tab.2” should be “Table 2” for readability. (Reviewer 2)
  - OpenCLIP’s ImageNet (EN) result may be missing in Table 3. (Reviewer 2)
  - Few-shot experiment results related to Tables 2 and 3 are not shown. (Reviewer 2)
  - No detailed comparison of computational overhead (data volume at billion level) with other methods. (Reviewer 3)
- **More ablation studies needed:** For example, comparing different architectural choices for the text encoder. (Reviewer 3)
- **Minority lower score (3) reviewer** finds novelty limited and method design arbitrary, while two reviewers (scores 5,5) are more positive. This contrast is preserved.
