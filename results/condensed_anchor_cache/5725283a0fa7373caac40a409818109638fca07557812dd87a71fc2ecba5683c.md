## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviewers acknowledge the paper’s focus on inter-layer link prediction in multiplex graphs and note that experiments show improvements on this task. The paper is generally described as well written and easy to understand. However, all three reviewers identify significant concerns: the motivation for inter-layer prediction is unclear, important baselines (heterogeneous methods, attention-based multiplex methods, simple single-layer embedding concatenations) are missing, the model is seen as an incremental application of GAT, and experimental analyses are insufficient (especially the failure to explain why GATNE outperforms on intra-layer tasks). The contribution is assessed as limited, with soundness rated fair to good and contribution fair across reviewers.

### Strengths
- The paper shows interesting and measurable performance gains on inter-layer link prediction (R1, R2).
- The writing is clear and the method is easy to follow in general (R1, R2; but see weakness on clarity below).
- The multiplex graph setup is motivated by real-world applications (R3 S1).

### Weaknesses
1. **Unclear motivation and insufficient framing**  
   - The importance of inter-layer link prediction is not justified; what real applications require it? Does it reduce to graph alignment? (R1, R2)  
   - A multiplex network is a special case of a heterogeneous graph; the paper does not argue why multiplex methods are necessary instead of treating the problem as heterogeneous link prediction (which would cover both intra- and inter-layer links) (R1, R2).  
   - The authors do not discuss or compare with attention-based multiplex embedding methods such as [1,2,3] nor with general heterogeneous graph learning methods [4,5] (R2).  
   - The paper only compares with other multiplex methods, not with heterogeneous graph models; a benchmark on heterogeneous datasets is requested (R1).

2. **Model novelty and design**  
   - The model is a straightforward application of GAT to multiplex networks; the contribution is incremental and lacks methodological novelty (R2, R3).  
   - No architectural details are given for the number of MPXGAT‑H and MPXGAT‑V layers or the number of alternating H‑V rounds. Is the default 1 H → 1 V → 1 H? How many rounds? (R3 D3).  
   - Terminology is imprecise: “Equations 1 use the convolutional layer as described in the GAT model” – GAT is not typically considered convolutional; a reference is needed (R3 D2).  

3. **Methodology description is unclear (contrast with clarity strength)**  
   While two reviewers find the paper clearly written (R1, R2), one reviewer (R3) states the methodology description is confusing. Specifically:  
   - Definition of the vertical network: “consists of a single-layer graph formed by the set of edges connecting nodes across different layers”. Is this a single layer or multiple layers? (R3 D1)  
   - The assumption “a node i on layer α can be connected to at most one node j on another layer β, i.e., the two nodes represent the same unit of the system”. Does this mean node i and j are the same node? (R3 D1)  
   - The notation N₁+…+N_L = N suggests N_i and N_j may have overlapping nodes; this needs clarification (R3 D1).  
   - No toy or real‑world example is provided to illustrate horizontal and vertical subnetworks (e.g., people on multiple social media platforms). Such an illustration is strongly recommended (R3 W1, D1).

4. **Insufficient baselines and missing analyses**  
   - A simple and relevant baseline is missing: applying a single‑layer graph embedding method (DeepWalk, Node2vec, LINE, GCN, GAT) to each layer independently and concatenating the embeddings for link prediction (R3 D4).  
   - GATNE consistently outperforms the proposed method on intra‑layer link prediction, but the authors provide no analysis of why this happens (R2, R3 D5).  
   - Overall, the experimental results are not convincing; too few baselines are included, and the results lack clear analysis (R3 W3).  
   - Evaluating on a heterogeneous benchmark is also requested (R1).
