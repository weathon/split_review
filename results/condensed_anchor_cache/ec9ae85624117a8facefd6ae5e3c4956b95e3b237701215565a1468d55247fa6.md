## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes DiSF (Diversified Submodular File Selection) to combat dimensional collapse in LLM pre-training data selection. The method formulates data selection as a submodular optimization problem, aiming to ensure uniform eigenvalues in the feature covariance matrix by maximizing a submodular information function over the unlabeled corpus. A classical greedy algorithm with mini-batch sampling is used. Experiments on TinyLlama models (120M to 1.1B parameters) across nine Harness tasks show consistent improvements over six baselines. DiSF saves 98.5% of 590M training files in SlimPajama, outperforms full-data pre-training within a 50B token budget, and achieves roughly 1.5× training efficiency and 5× data efficiency. All four reviewers gave a score of 8; Reviewer 2 raises more detailed theoretical and novelty concerns while others are broadly positive.

### Strengths

- Well-motivated research: addresses the critical issue of dimensional collapse in LLM data selection, which degrades general performance despite boosting domain-specific results (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Clear presentation: the methodology and narrative are easy to follow, facilitating understanding of complex concepts (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Strong experimental results: DiSF consistently outperforms six baselines across nine tasks and three model sizes (120M, 560M, 1.1B), with convincing evidence (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- Thorough comparisons and extensive ablation studies: analysis of data selection budget, architectural backbone, feature extractors, etc., providing valuable insights (Reviewer 1).
- Simple yet effective method: converts the uniformity of eigenvalues of the covariance matrix into minimizing the F-norm of the covariance matrix, then solves via submodular optimization (Reviewers 3, 4).
- Clear demonstration of the drawbacks of existing methods: they produce narrow feature space, limiting multi-task performance (Reviewer 4).
- Data efficiency: achieves up to 5× data efficiency and 1.5× training efficiency, saving substantial training files (Reviewer 1, supported by abstract).
- Robust methodology: the diversity-driven selection is well supported by experimental evidence across benchmarks (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

- Limited scale of experiments: tests only up to 1.1B parameters; scalability to larger models commonly used in current LLM research is unclear (Reviewer 1).
- Scalability concerns: whether the method maintains efficiency and effectiveness when applied to much larger datasets is uncertain (Reviewer 1).
- Lack of detailed computational cost analysis: the paper does not provide time/space complexity for feature extraction, covariance matrix computation, and the greedy optimization process, nor how these scale with dataset size and selection budget. The "Selection scale" experiment is insufficient to demonstrate computational efficiency (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). Specific requests: provide time and space complexity metrics on Table 2 (Reviewer 3) and compare computational cost against other baselines, not just full data (Reviewer 4).
- Missing proof of submodularity: the paper shows only empirical monotonicity via Figure 4, but does not prove that the function \(F_M^{DiSF}(U)\) satisfies the diminishing marginal returns property required for submodularity (Reviewer 2).
- Missing comparison with prior diversity-driven methods: prior work such as INGENIOUS (Renduchintala et al., 2023) should be compared to study the impact of dimensional collapse and the diversity selection algorithm (Reviewer 2).
- No justification for choosing only \(F_M^{DiSF}(U)\) over known submodular functions: functions like Log-Determinant and Facility-Location have been proven effective for diversity selection in summarization and subset selection; their performance should be compared with DiSF (Reviewer 2).
- Small performance gain when budget increases from 10B to 50B (5× increase): the overall improvement is quite small; additional experiments or theoretical analysis are needed to justify this result (Reviewer 2).
- Limited performance improvement: in Table 2, most improvements are within ±0.5, which could be within the error range; a reasonable analysis is needed to illustrate the effectiveness of the method (Reviewer 3). (Reviewer 3 identifies this as a weakness; other reviewers note consistent improvements despite this.)
- Observation of dimensional collapse (Figure 2) is only on SlimPajama; need verification on other pre-training corpora to confirm the phenomenon is general (Reviewer 4).
- Minor presentation issues: Section 2.3 could be moved higher for better readability; repeated phrases like "diversified submodular file selection" and "decorrelated text files" reduce readability (Reviewer 2). Reviewers also request sample size and performance analysis under full data vs. data selection across different datasets (Reviewer 3).
