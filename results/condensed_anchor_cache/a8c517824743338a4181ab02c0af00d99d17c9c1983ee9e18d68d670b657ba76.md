## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes L-MSA, a layer-wise fine-tuning method that uses a metric derived from the Method of Successive Approximations (MSA) to select which layer to update and then fine-tunes that layer. It provides a theoretical analysis within deep linear networks based on Pontryagin’s Maximum Principle. Empirical evaluations are conducted on CIFAR-100, CIFAR-C, CIFAR-Flip, Living-17, and ImageNet-C, comparing against full fine-tuning, LIFT, LISA, surgical fine-tuning, and Auto-RGN.

### Strengths
- **Novel layer-selection metric**: The MSA-based metric is theoretically grounded and offers a fresh perspective on which layers to fine-tune (R1, moderate confidence).
- **Theoretical analysis**: A formal analysis is provided in the context of deep linear networks and connected to optimal control (R1, R3). However, R2 and R3 find the analysis shallow and not clearly linked to the method’s benefits.
- **Empirical validation across multiple datasets and methods**: Experiments include a good variety of datasets (CIFAR-100, CIFAR-C, CIFAR-Flip, Living-17, ImageNet-C) and baselines (full fine-tuning, LIFT, LISA, surgical fine-tuning, Auto-RGN) (R3). An ablation study is also present (R3).
- **Ablation and limitation discussion**: The paper includes an ablation analysis and explicitly discusses limitations (R3).
- **Important task**: Parameter-efficient fine-tuning is practically relevant (R2).
- **Well-written and easy to follow** (R4, moderate confidence; contrary to R1 and R2 who cite disjointed presentation – see Weaknesses).
- **Promising results on some datasets**: L-MSA outperforms full fine-tuning on part of the datasets (R4; but R3 notes the improvement is modest – see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited originality and unclear contributions**: The idea that fine-tuning different layers yields different outcomes is already widely explored (R2, high confidence). The paper does not clearly articulate what the main contribution is or how L-MSA differs from existing layer-wise methods (R1, R2). The Method of Successive Approximations is introduced without sufficient motivation for why it is suitable for layer selection or fine-tuning (R2, R3).
- **Insufficient comparison with state-of-the-art PEFT methods**: The paper omits widely-used parameter-efficient methods such as LoRA (Hu et al.) (R1, high confidence). Reviewer 4 also questions why only image datasets are used when the compared methods (LIFT, etc.) are from NLP/LLM contexts; no experiments on NLP datasets are provided (R4).
- **Weak empirical improvements**: In Table 1, L-MSA outperforms baselines in only 2 out of 4 settings (R3). The only method it clearly surpasses is LIFT, which is not yet peer-reviewed (R4). Reviewer 3 notes that improvement is not outspoken.
- **Missing computational cost analysis**: The paper claims reduced computational cost but provides no measurements of memory usage, training time, or peak memory compared to full fine-tuning or other PEFT methods (R1, R3, R4). Reviewer 4 specifically asks: does L-MSA introduce additional overhead (e.g., computing state and co-state variables per layer)? One reviewer expects that L-MSA may require similar memory to full fine-tuning (R4).
- **Disjointed presentation and lack of self-containment**: Several parts are delegated to the appendix; protocols and model details are not always explicit (e.g., models used in Sec. 4.2) (R3). The paper references equations out of order, forcing readers to navigate back and forth (R1). Reviewer 2 rates presentation as 1, citing poor writing and insufficient motivation.
- **Shallow theoretical analysis**: The theoretical analysis (Sec. 3) is too shallow to be informative (R3). Reviewer 2 notes it is unclear how the theory connects to improved layer selection or fine-tuning. The analysis could be moved to the appendix to free space for more details (R3).
- **Generalization concerns**: The approximated update loss may not guarantee strong generalization; a deeper analysis of conditions for validity is missing (R1).
- **Weak positioning in related work**: Related work sections discuss topics not directly linked (e.g., large architectures, prompt-based methods), while methods compared in experiments (LIFT, LISA, Auto-RGN) are not covered. Missing references: AutoLR (Ro & Choi, AAAI 2021) and Block-Wise Fine-Tuning (Barakat & Huang, ICMLA 2023) (R3).
- **Questionable presentation of empirical results**: Figure 6 averages over datasets, hiding per-dataset variation (R3). Figure 4 shows point estimates, so the claimed “consistent” identification of layers is not observable (R3). In Figure 5 (ImageNet→CIFAR-100), L-MSA appears to converge earlier while full fine-tuning and Auto-RGN continue to improve; reviewer 4 asks for longer training epochs (R4).
- **“No Adaptation” baseline appropriateness**: For datasets with different class counts (CIFAR-Flip) or reduced labels (ImageNet-C), the “No Adaptation” baseline may not be a fair reference (R3).
- **Contradictory views on presentation**: While R4 finds the paper well-written, R1 and R2 cite serious presentation issues (disjointed, unclear, not self-contained). This disagreement is preserved.
