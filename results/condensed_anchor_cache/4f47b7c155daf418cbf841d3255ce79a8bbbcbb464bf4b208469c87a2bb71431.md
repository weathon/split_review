## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces DeepROCK, a method for detecting pairwise feature interactions in deep neural networks (DNNs) while controlling the false discovery rate (FDR). It uses knockoff features (generated via a KnockoffGAN) together with a novel DNN architecture that includes a pairwise‑coupling layer and a calibration procedure for existing interaction importance measures. Experiments on simulated and real datasets are presented to demonstrate the method’s ability to identify interactions with improved FDR control and power relative to prior techniques.

### Strengths
- The problem of detecting feature interactions in DNNs with FDR control is important for interpretability in high‑stakes applications.
- The idea of integrating knockoffs with interaction detection algorithms is interesting and promising; the pairwise‑coupling layer and calibration procedure are novel contributions that make interaction detection methods usable for scientific discovery.
- The need for calibrated interaction scores is surprising and highlights a key insight.
- Empirical results on simulated and real data show clear advantages over existing methods in terms of both power and FDR (reviewer 2 notes this strongly; reviewers 1 and 3 also confirm the potential).
- The presentation of the approach and required mathematical background is sound.
- The experiments include meaningful simulated scenarios and two real‑world datasets.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical justification for FDR control (major concern raised by reviewer 2, score 3; also noted by reviewer 1):**  
  No proof is given that the knockoff swap property holds for the interaction measures used, nor that the calibration procedure yields valid FDR control. The heuristic motivation in section 3.2 (“Intuitively, the interaction between two marginally important features naturally has a higher importance score…”) is not formally justified. The choice of the maximum interaction score from knockoff features as a threshold (rather than the mean or a quantile) is not motivated. There is no clear definition of interaction importance analogous to conditional independence for main effects (e.g., \(Y \perp (X_i, X_j) \mid X_{-ij}\)). One reviewer (score 3) considers these theoretical gaps severe enough to undermine the core claim; the other two reviewers (score 6) also note the heuristic nature but are less critical overall.

- **Limitations of the method:**  
  – The method is specialized to pairwise interactions; scaling to \(n\)-wise interactions would incur significant computational cost (reviewer 1).  
  – Transitive interactions are not addressed: e.g., if A–B and B–C interact, the method may incorrectly identify A–C as an interaction (reviewer 1).  
  – The approach appears to work only with MLPs; generality to other architectures (e.g., deep residual networks) is unclear, and concerns about weight‑matrix multiplication in such architectures are raised (reviewer 2).

- **Missing empirical comparisons and ablation study (reviewer 3):**  
  In the real‑world experiments (Fig. 3 and 4) there is no comparison to existing methods. It is unclear how DeepROCK performs relative to simply using interaction scores from the network without the knockoff framework, calibration, or coupling layer. A detailed ablation study isolating the impact of each component is missing.

- **Concerns about the underlying interaction importance measure (reviewer 2):**  
  The method builds on integrated gradients, which have been shown to lack fidelity in explaining models. This raises questions about the conclusions drawn from the resulting scores.

- **Minor issues:**  
  – Typo “withcovariance” in section 2.2 (reviewer 3).  
  – No code publication mentioned (reviewer 3’s question, relevant for reproducibility).
