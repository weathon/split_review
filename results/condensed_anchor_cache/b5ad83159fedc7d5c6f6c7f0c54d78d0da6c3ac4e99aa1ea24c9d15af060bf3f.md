## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper extends double sampling randomized smoothing (DSRS) to smooth with Exponential General Gaussian (EGG) and Exponential Standard Gaussian (ESG) distributions, deriving certified radii and reporting improved certified accuracy on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. Reviewers note that the theoretical and experimental framework closely follows Li et al. (2022), with the exponent η as the main addition. Opinions diverge strongly: one reviewer (score 8, confidence 3) finds the extension valuable, the theoretical analysis novel under a more general concentration assumption, and the ImageNet results significant (4–6% improvement at larger radii). Another reviewer (score 1, confidence 4) argues the work is mostly identical to Li et al. (2022), the theoretical contribution incremental, and the claims misleading. The other two reviewers (scores 5, 5; confidence 4 each) acknowledge some theoretical findings and thorough experiments but raise concerns about marginal improvements, missing variance reporting, unclear definitions, and limited novelty in the constant-factor improvement.

### Strengths

- Extends DSRS to a larger family of distributions (EGG) and provides theoretically better certified radii in the constant multiplier under Li et al.’s concentration assumption (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Proposes a more general concentration assumption under which EGG yields polynomially better certified radii (Reviewer 3).
- Derives novel theoretical results (Theorems 1 and 2) (Reviewer 1).
- Experiments are thorough and show improved certified accuracy over DSRS on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet; on ImageNet at larger radii, improvement is 4–6% (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is easy to follow (Reviewer 2) and has a concise introduction with clearly outlined contributions (Reviewer 1).
- Preliminaries section provides a nice introduction to the formalisms (Reviewer 1).
- The work contains both theoretical analysis and corresponding empirical verification (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty / overlap with Li et al. (2022):** The paper largely reproduces Li et al. (2022) with the only addition being the EGG distribution. Theorem 1 is essentially the same as Theorem 2 in Li et al. (2022) (EGG with η=2 is the generalized Gaussian from that work). The proofs (Lemmas C.1–C.4, Section 4.3) are nearly identical except for the exponent η. The extension to η≠2 is straightforward and does not add significant novelty (Reviewer 4). One reviewer (score 8) disagrees, viewing the extension as meaningful.  
- **Theoretical effectiveness of η is questionable:**  
  - The lower bound in Theorem 4 is Ω(d^{1/η}), but the required (σ, p, η)-concentration assumption becomes stronger as η decreases. The authors acknowledge that η does not tighten the bound (page 6, Eqn. (54)), yet they claim “much tighter constant factors,” which is misleading (Reviewer 4).  
  - Independent of η, the certified radius still scales only as √d, so improving the constant factor yields only incremental gains in high dimensions (Reviewer 2).  
- **Empirical improvements are marginal, especially on CIFAR-10:** The certified accuracy improvements are ≤0.5% in most cases in Table 2. On CIFAR-10 the improvements are only marginal, and it is unclear why this happens (Reviewers 2, 3). The claim that all EGG with η∈(0,2) break the curse is contradicted by the experiments, which use η=8.0 and show only very small gains (Reviewer 4).  
- **Missing variance estimates:** The experiments lack reported variance. The theory suggests EGG should never yield lower certified accuracy than DSRS, yet Table 2 shows some cases where it is slightly lower, hinting at variance issues. Without variance, the statistical significance of the observed differences cannot be assessed (Reviewer 2).  
- **Unclear or insufficient definitions:**  
  - The term “point-to-point certified accuracy” in the Abstract is unclear and should be clarified (Reviewer 1).  
  - The introduction of DSRS (Section 3) glosses over how A and B are defined and estimated (no formulas given), and how the certified radius is related to optimization (4) is vague (Reviewer 1).  
  - Theorem 2 is described as solving the dual problem, but it only gives specific formulas for the objective and constraints based on EGG densities; it is not a closed-form solution, and it is unclear how the dual variables ν₁, ν₂ are solved in practice (Reviewer 1).  
- **Theoretical and notational issues in Theorem 1:**  
  - Why is η restricted to a finite set? Why is d−2k required to be less than 30? This is not explained (Reviewer 1).  
  - The lower bound does not depend on k or η, which is surprising and suggests possibly missing dependence (Reviewer 2).  
  - The typo “sufficiently large” should be “sufficiently large” (Reviewer 2).  
- **Presentation issues:**  
  - The paper is hard to follow at times, with cryptic or vague language (Reviewer 1).  
  - Mislabeling of probability density functions and substitution variances: the order of ESG and EGG is swapped (Reviewers 1, 3).  
  - “Prospective” in “prospective in providing much tighter lower bounds” is unclear (Reviewer 1).  
  - Lemma C.3 should refer to “at labeled example (x₀, y₀)” not just “at input x₀” (Reviewer 1).  
- **Missing comparison to Li et al. (2022):** The authors should provide a detailed comparison of methods, theoretical results, proofs, experiment settings, and conclusions with Li et al. (2022) (Reviewer 4).
