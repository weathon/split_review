## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers generally acknowledge the paper addresses an important gap—safety in curriculum learning—and is clearly written with thorough background. The method (SCG) extends CURROT with safety constraints and achieves lower constraint violations during training with comparable performance. However, reviewers note limited novelty (essentially adding a constraint to CURROT), evaluation on only three simple 2D environments, lack of comparisons with safe-RL baselines or environments from prior curriculum learning work, and lack of scalability demonstration. One reviewer (score 3) finds the method trivial and the evaluation insufficient, while others (score 6) see value but note limited impact and incremental contribution.

### Strengths
- The paper clearly identifies a gap in safe curriculum learning and motivates the need for safety during training. (R2, R3, R4)
- Exposition is clear, well-organized, with thorough background and related work. (R2, R3, R4)
- SCG achieves state-of-the-art performance across the tested benchmarks (Safety‑Maze, Safety‑Goal, Safety‑Push), obtaining comparable task success to CURROT while significantly reducing constraint violations during training. (R1, R2, R3)
- Compared to DEFAULT, SCG improves sample efficiency. (R1, R2)
- The approach is pragmatically presented and the experiments convey main results effectively. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty is limited**: The method is essentially CURROT with an added safety constraint; several reviewers consider the contribution incremental or trivial. (R1, R3, R4) Reviewer 1 explicitly states “The method seems trivial to me. It is mostly based on CURROT.”
- **Empirical evaluation is narrow**: Only three simple 2D environments are tested (Safety‑Maze, Safety‑Goal, Safety‑Push). No comparisons are made to environments used in prior curriculum learning works (e.g., Point Mass, Bipedal Walker Stump Tracks). (R1, R3) The scalability to higher‑dimensional or combinatorially large task spaces (e.g., Brax) is not demonstrated. (R2)
- **Missing baselines**: No comparison with other safe‑RL algorithms (R3). The paper does not include a baseline that uses a weighted sum of safety and performance as a reward (R1). It only compares to CURROT, DEFAULT, and naively modified safe versions; the rationale for selecting these baselines is not clearly justified (R4). It is unclear whether “Default” is simply RL without any curriculum. (R4)
- **Parameter sensitivity not analyzed**: The impact of the newly introduced trade‑off parameter \(\alpha_k\) is not studied. (R3)
- **Clarity issues in figures and text**:
    - Figures 3 (right) and 5c show tasks that appear to be constraint‑violating, but the colour‑coding for tolerance radius is ambiguous; adding circles to scale would clarify. (R2)
    - Sentences at lines 188 and 193 do not fully describe the objective when “finding the optimal policy” and “minimising the constraint violation regret” are in tension. (R2)
    - In Figure 4, it is unclear whether boxes denote standard deviations and bars denote min/max. (R4)
- **Questions indicating missing experiments or ablations** (merged into Weaknesses as they overlap with criticisms or requests for more analysis):
    - Was the cost annealed in the Naive approach as well? (R3)
    - What is the motivation for choosing Safety‑Maze, Safety‑Goal, and Safety‑Push as benchmarks to demonstrate SCG’s advantages? (R4)
    - Why not include a baseline that uses a weighted sum of safety and performance rewards? (R1)
    - Could making more mistakes early in training help learn to avoid future constraint violations? (R1)
    - How does SCG compare to other safe‑RL algorithms in these domains? (R3)
- **Scalability and impact concerns**: The method currently appears far from application to robotics or other domains where safety during training is critical; the gains on safety and performance are not dramatic. (R2) The underlying CURROT may not scale, making the broader applicability of SCG unclear. (R2)

**Note on reviewer disagreement**: Reviewer 1 (score 3) was more negative than the other three reviewers (scores 6), particularly regarding the method’s triviality, the lack of comparisons to prior curriculum environments (Point Mass, Bipedal Walker Stump Tracks), and the desire for a baseline with a weighted safety+performance reward. The other reviewers also noted limited novelty and narrow evaluation but found the contributions more acceptable.
