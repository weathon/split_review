## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper addresses the coherence-quality tradeoff in multi-modal generative modeling by introducing Multimodal Latent Diffusion (MLD). MLD uses independently trained, deterministic uni-modal autoencoders, concatenates their latent variables, and trains a score-based diffusion model on the joint latent space. A multi-time training method conditions the diffusion on random subsets of modalities, enabling conditional generation from an arbitrary subset. Experiments compare MLD against multi-modal VAEs on MNIST variants, CUB, and PolyMNIST datasets.

### Strengths

- The paper tackles a classical problem (multi-modal learning) and offers a new perspective via latent diffusion, effectively addressing the coherence-quality tradeoff observed in existing multi-modal VAEs (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- Comparisons to multi-modal VAE baselines are thorough and show encouraging results (Reviewers 2, 3).
- An ablation study preemptively addresses why in-painting fails relative to the proposed conditional masking training, and shows it is not significantly better (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is clear and well-organized; the appendix substantially supplements technical and experimental details (Reviewer 4).
- Experimental results provide concrete evidence supporting the claims (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

- **Weak/outdated baselines**: The compared methods (MVAE, MoPoE) are considered weak – e.g., MVAE cannot generate good digits (Fig. 20), MVAE and MOPOE cannot generate legible birds (Fig. 22, p. 22). Reviewers 1 and 4 question whether these are meaningful benchmarks in 2023. Missing baselines include MMVAE+ (Palumbo et al., 2023) and parallel work on score-based multimodal autoencoders (Wesego et al., 2023) (Reviewer 4). No comparison to other multi-modal diffusion models, notably CoDi (Tang et al., 2023), which uses latent diffusion with similar conditioning on arbitrary subsets (Reviewer 3).

- **Limited dataset diversity**: Experiments are primarily on small/low-resolution datasets (MNIST variants, PolyMNIST) or two-modality CUB. Missing high-resolution datasets (e.g., Bimodal CelebA) and datasets with more than two modalities (e.g., video+audio+text) to test scalability and real-world applicability (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). The lack of more challenging benchmarks makes it hard to assess robustness.

- **Visual quality concerns**: MLD’s generated images are not always good – e.g., “painted birds” in Fig. 22 (Reviewer 1). On CUB, the visual representations are unclear; coherence for image→caption did not improve and case demonstrations of caption generation are lacking (Reviewer 4).

- **Presentation clarity**: The paper lacks an overall model diagram to illustrate the architecture (Reviewers 1, 3). Mathematical sections (Eq. 4, 6, 7) are presented without clear intuitive explanations, assuming high familiarity with diffusion models and VAEs (Reviewers 2, 3). Some equations (e.g., Eq. 5) add little understanding of the contribution (Reviewer 3).

- **Methodological concerns**:
  - **Latent distribution mismatch**: Concatenating latents from modalities with diverging distributions may cause the shared noise schedule to over-perturb low-variance modalities and under-perturb high-variance ones, leading to suboptimal generation. An analysis of latent space distributions is needed (Reviewer 4).
  - **Optimization speed imbalance**: Varying convergence rates of autoencoders could bias the diffusion model toward faster-converging modalities, degrading performance on slower ones (Reviewer 4).
  - **Lack of conditioning intensity control**: The model does not allow weak vs. strong conditioning; the mapping from conditioning modality to generated modality is always deterministic (Reviewer 4).
  - **Training/inference modality mismatch**: When training on three modalities (A, B, C) but inferring only A→B, would a masked C still be required? The paper does not discuss flexibility for varying numbers of observed modalities during inference (Reviewer 4).

- **Missing experimental details**:
  - How is text generation performed in the proposed method? (Reviewer 1)
  - Autoencoder architecture design (e.g., for images, is it like Stable Diffusion’s convolutional AE?), latent dimension choices per modality, and rationale (Reviewer 2).
  - Scalability to many modalities and high-dimensional data (Reviewer 2).
  - How is the concatenated latent space kept cohesive given independent training of uni-modal autoencoders? (Reviewer 2)
  - Training specifics: 4 A100 GPUs for ~4 months total experiments – more details needed (Reviewer 2). Results should be compared to those in original baseline studies (Reviewer 2).
  - Code is not provided, hindering reproducibility (Reviewer 4).  
  - Quantitative comparisons on CUB should appear in the main text (Reviewer 4).

- **Formatting**: The citation format deviates from the official ICLR 2024 style (Reviewer 2).
