## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a knowledge distillation method for incremental object detection (IOD) that combines hard and soft pseudo-labels via an image-level hybrid knowledge representation (HKR) and a task regularized distillation (TRD) loss to balance old and new tasks. The approach mixes instance-level hard (one-hot) and soft (logits) knowledge using adaptive thresholds based on prediction confidence. Extensive experiments are claimed on MS COCO and Pascal VOC, with state-of-the-art results and a reduced mAP gap to joint learning.

### Strengths
- Unlike methods using only hard or soft pseudo‑labels, this paper combines both via mixup/adaptive thresholds, allowing the student to selectively learn beneficial teacher knowledge.
- Extensive experiments on two popular benchmarks (MS COCO and Pascal VOC) under multiple incremental scenarios demonstrate effectiveness.
- No major codebase changes are needed to implement the method; it is easy to try.
- Addresses catastrophic forgetting in IOD with explicit strategies for knowledge selection and transfer.
- One reviewer (score 5) additionally highlighted that the paper is clearly written, the motivation of using sample confidence in knowledge distillation is interesting, and the results on standard benchmarks look good.
- Another reviewer (score 3) acknowledged the paper’s focus on a real problem and the introduction of hybrid knowledge representation and task regularized distillation as “innovative strategies” (though other reviewers found them less novel).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty** – The proposed extensions of existing knowledge distillation (weighted labels, pseudo‑labels, semi‑supervised concepts) are seen as incremental improvements. One reviewer (score 5) also noted “simple alternative to knowledge distillation” and that loss balancing is not new in continual learning.
- **Unfair comparison** – The method uses YOLOX as the base detector while most competing methods use different detectors (e.g., Faster R‑CNN). Reviewers suggest reimplementing other methods on YOLOX or showing results on a common detector for fair comparison.
- **Missing Pascal VOC results?** – One reviewer states that experimental results on Pascal VOC are missing, contradicting the abstract which claims experiments on both datasets.
- **Unclear figures and equations**:
  - Figure 1 confusingly uses “w.o.” and “w/o” (assumed same) and lacks a description of feature distillation arrows and NMS.
  - Eq. 7 and 8 are identical, and Eq. 10 is a regular L2 loss; the “task regularized” mechanism needs clearer explanation.
  - Eq. 4 and 5 are almost identical – why not merge them?
- **Missing ablations and analyses**:
  - Ablation on the proposed `Loss_diff` is needed to verify the claimed balancing effect.
  - Motivation of “knowledge fuzziness” and “imbalance learning” as causes of forgetting is not supported by data or training observations.
- **General rather than detection‑specific** – Strategies appear general and not tailored to the unique challenges of object detection (e.g., bounding box characteristics, multi‑scale issues). Reviewers ask for more specific adaptations to object detection.
- **Need for more thorough discussion of related work** – Introduction lacks citations for relevant methods.
- **Novelty questions from reviewers**: What aspects are particularly novel compared to existing continual learning KD techniques? How does the method compare to other IOD approaches in terms of advantages/limitations?
