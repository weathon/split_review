## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes two new randomized-smoothing-based robust deep reinforcement learning algorithms—DS-DQN for discrete actions and AS-PPO for continuous actions—to address the utility-robustness trade-off observed in previous smoothed agents like CROP. The authors claim that their methods outperform prior state-of-the-art robustly-trained agents in robust reward and provide a stronger adversarial attack for smoothed DQN. The four reviewers (all rating 5, reject) generally find the problem interesting but raise major concerns about limited novelty, missing baselines, unclear method presentation, weak experimental evidence, and insufficient discussion of related work.

### Strengths
- The problem of achieving robustness in DRL via randomized smoothing is interesting and timely (R1).
- The motivation from shortcomings of existing smoothed agents (e.g., CROP) and the trade-off between utility and robustness is intriguing; the introduction of a robustness guarantee sets this paper apart from simple robust RL training methods (R2).
- The methods show versatility across different task types (discrete and continuous action spaces) with empirical contributions demonstrating robust performance under attacks and robustness guarantees (R2).
- Empirical results appear better than baselines on the tested environments (R3).
- The methods appear easy to implement on top of existing algorithms (DQN, PPO) (R3).
- The paper is clearly written and easy to follow (R4) – **contrast**: R2 and R3 criticize the presentation as confusing and suboptimal, so this positive view is a minority.
- The paper targets a “mis”-evaluation in CROP, providing a clear motivation (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and over-reliance on CROP:** The paper heavily relies on the existing CROP method, limiting its novelty (R3). The work is completely based on CROP and appears to be a correction of CROP, raising doubt about its suitability as a full research paper (R4). Fig.1 only compares with CROP; results from other methods would be useful (R1). No discussion or comparison with another concurrent randomized smoothing work [1] (R4).
- **Missing baselines and incomplete literature survey:** The paper does not include recent robust RL baselines such as ATLA [1] and WocaR-RL [2] (R2). The literature survey on robust RL baselines appears not comprehensive, especially regarding recent works (R2). Baselines used are all before 2022; more recent methods should be compared (R1). For PPO-based comparison, Table 3 only compares SA-PPO and vanilla PPO, missing Radial-PPO and other PPO-based baselines (R2). The paper introduces a new attack method based on randomized smoothing but does not compare it with the strongest evasion attacks such as PA-AD [3], nor does it discuss prior attack methods in the related work (R2).
- **Unclear method description and inconsistent approaches:** The method description is dispersed throughout the paper, complicating comprehension (R3). The approaches for discrete (DS-DQN) and continuous (AS-PPO) cases appear to differ: a denoiser is used in DS-DQN but not in AS-PPO, raising consistency questions (R3). The smoothing component in AS-PPO is unclear; specifically, what does ΔS_t represent in the paragraph following Equation 9 (R3)? It would be helpful to explain the common components of the schemes (R1). Using pseudocode diagrams would enhance clarity (R2). It is also unclear whether the attack models for discrete (DQN) and continuous (PPO) cases are the same (R3).
- **Weak experimental presentation and evidence:** The experiment section has suboptimal writing and presentation; results lack strong evidence to robustly support the claims (R2). In Table 5, the terms “high” or “highest” are vague and do not provide an intuitive representation of contributions (R2). For AS-PPO, it appears to perform better only on larger attack budgets in Table 3, yet Table 8 shows no significant advantage or even any advantage on large budgets (R2). The connection between theoretical guarantees (robust certificates) and empirical results is not well-established (R3).
- **Unclear practical implications of robustness certification:** It is not clear how the certified radius and reward lower bounds are practically useful in the context of Atari games or continuous control tasks (R3). How is the certificate computed for experimental evaluation, e.g., for Atari Pong (R3)?
- **Denoiser purpose unclear:** In DS-DQN, random Gaussian noise is added to the state during training, and a denoiser network reconstructs the original state. Does the denoiser essentially mirror the added noise? What is the purpose of training such a denoiser when the noise model is known? How does it contribute to better rewards and enhanced robustness (R3)? Is a denoiser model used for AS-PPO (R3)?
- **Lack of limitations discussion:** The paper falls short in discussing potential limitations (R2).
