## Merged Review

### Summary

This work introduces Neural Rule Learner (NeurRL), a differentiable Inductive Logic Programming (ILP) system for learning interpretable rules directly from raw sequential data (e.g., time series, images). It combines a VAE for embedding extraction, a differentiable k-means clustering module for symbolization, and a fuzzy neural rule learner (resembling ∂ILP) for rule induction. The system avoids explicit label leakage by not requiring pre-trained networks to map inputs to symbolic labels. The pipeline is trained end-to-end (though with two-stage pre-training of the autoencoder). Rules are extracted post-hoc from the learned weights. Experiments on UCR time-series datasets and MNIST highlight data-efficient learning and competitive accuracy compared to baselines.

### Strengths

1. **Originality and problem framing**: Tackles an important and timely problem – learning symbolic rules from raw sequence data without explicit label leakage. The approach of integrating differentiable clustering (k-means) and VAE with gradient-based rule learning is noteworthy (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). The paper formally establishes the ILP learning task from raw data using the interpretation transition framework (Reviewer 4).

2. **Clear presentation and writing**: The paper is generally clear, explains the method compellingly, and clearly outlines the research question and core idea (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Code is available (Reviewer 3).

3. **Effectiveness**: Experimental results show notable improvement over previous methods in terms of predictive accuracy on several UCR datasets, especially in low-data regimes (Reviewer 1, 4). The system provides human-readable rules (Reviewer 1).

4. **Significance**: The work is a valuable contribution to the neuro-symbolic community, paving the way for systems that learn with fewer priors on raw input (Reviewer 2). It appears important for learning symbolic rules about raw sequential data (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses

1. **Claim about end-to-end training and grounding is insufficiently supported** (Reviewers 2, 3). The paper claims to be the first to enable joint training without pre-training, but the proposed method still uses a two-stage process (pre-training the autoencoder) and work [5] already achieves joint learning of neural network and rule learner (Reviewer 3). More fundamentally, it is unclear how NeurRL actually grounds cluster-based symbols so that the rule learner can form meaningful rules beyond simple conjunctions of cluster assignments (Reviewer 2). Without a clear mechanism for grounding, the method may be confined to the rule format in Eq. (5) and unable to handle more complex rule structures (Reviewer 2). The paper also claims to uncover underlying rules without identifying conditions for failure; previous works [3,4,6,7] show that representation learning through clustering is necessary but not sufficient, and that "reasoning shortcuts" can occur (Reviewer 3).

2. **Insufficient context and comparison to related work** (Reviewers 3, 4). The paper lacks adequate discussion of related neuro-symbolic work, e.g., neuro-symbolic autoencoders [1,2], differentiable clustering [3,4], and other works that achieve similar goals (e.g., autoencoder+discriminator [1,2] or more technically sophisticated methods [3] – Reviewer 4). Without comparison to existing methods (e.g., on datasets from [5]), the novelty appears incremental (Reviewer 3). An experimental comparison with existing methods on arithmetic tasks or datasets in [5] is needed (Reviewer 3).

3. **Experimental methodology and reproducibility concerns** (Reviewers 3, 4). Details on hyperparameter selection are lacking: the model has many hyperparameters (number of categories K, subsequence length l, number of regions P, temperature parameters, loss weights, biases) and it is unclear how they were chosen, whether tuning was separate per dataset, and whether reported results are best or averaged (Reviewers 3, 4). No standard deviations or statistical reporting are provided (Reviewer 4). A sensitivity analysis for hyperparameters is missing (Reviewer 3). Post-hoc rule extraction leads to accuracy drops up to 15 percentage points (Table 1), underscoring its heuristic nature and raising questions about faithfulness of the extracted rules relative to the learned weights (Reviewer 3).

4. **Limited complexity of tasks and scalability concerns** (Reviewers 2, 3). Experiments are conducted on small datasets with simple symbolic tasks; it is unclear how the method scales to larger, more complex datasets (Reviewer 2). Runtime comparisons or discussion of scalability challenges are missing (Reviewer 2). The MNIST demonstration may rely on superficial image cues (e.g., identifying regions that are black or white) rather than semantic digit understanding; more challenging tasks like arithmetic on handwritten digits or sequences of integers (even/odd, counting) would better validate the approach (Reviewer 3). The memory consumption issue common to ∂ILP and αILP is not addressed (Reviewer 2).

5. **Learned predicates lack high-level abstraction and translation invariance** (Reviewers 1, 4). The rules capture low-level spatial relationships (neighboring pixels) rather than abstract, semantically meaningful concepts (Reviewer 4). The approach requires significant data invariance (orientation, sampling regularity), and many real-world datasets violate this (Reviewer 1). The model does not consider translation invariance – patterns should be detected regardless of region; fixed regions lead to brittle rules that fail to generalize to shifted instances (Reviewer 4). The coordinate sensitivity table (datasets Coffee, ECG, Gun Point, etc.) illustrates potential fragility to data collection errors (uncalibrated sensors, aspect/focus variation, frame rate) (Reviewer 1). It would be helpful to have more detailed interpretation of rules on each UCR dataset to assess interpretability (Reviewer 1).

6. **Specific technical concerns** (Reviewers 2, 3). Are the codes in the differentiable clustering layer learnable and how is collapse avoided? (Reviewer 3). Does NeurRL share the intense memory consumption of ∂ILP/αILP? (Reviewer 2). The definition of "label leakage" needs further clarification, especially whether coordinate dependence (e.g., limited field-of-view, imaging orientation) constitutes an example of leakage (Reviewer 1). The paper’s two-stage training contradicts the claim of avoiding pre-training (Reviewer 3).

7. **Reviewer disagreement**: While some reviewers (e.g., Reviewer 1) see strong originality and significance (score 8), others (Reviewer 3) find the contribution incremental and claims unsupported (score 5). The strengths listed above reflect the positive views, but the weaknesses are substantiated by multiple reviewers.
