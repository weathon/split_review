## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Imit-Diff, a diffusion transformer-based imitation learning framework for robotic visuomotor control. Three key components are introduced: (1) Dual Resolution Fusion to integrate high- and low-resolution visual features efficiently using attention; (2) Semantics Injection that provides input images masked by manipulation-target segments using open-vocabulary models (Grounding DINO / MixFormerv2 / MobileSAM); (3) Consistency Policy (adopted from Prasad et al., 2024) to speed up denoising via few-step sampling. Experiments on a real robot arm show that Imit-Diff outperforms ACT and Diffusion Policy, especially in cluttered scenes and under task interruptions.

### Strengths
- The method incorporates richer visual cues through multi-resolution features, semantic masks, and faster sampling, which are promising directions for enhancing diffusion-based policy learning. [R1,R3]
- The presentation is clear, with reasonable motivation and explanation of the three modules. [R2,R3]
- Real-hardware experiments demonstrate improvements over ACT and Diffusion Policy, including robustness in cluttered scenes and task interruptions. [R1,R3]
- The proposed visual enhancements (dual resolution fusion, semantic injection) are potentially widely applicable to various manipulation tasks. [R1]
- The ablation study (Table 4) shows that each introduced component contributes to overall performance. [R3]

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns:** The core diffusion policy learning mechanism remains largely unchanged from prior work; the main contributions are at the system level by combining existing components (ConvNext, DINOv2, Grounding DINO, etc.) and adopting the consistency policy from Prasad et al. (2024). [R1,R2] Reviewers note that the method lacks sufficient novelty for a top ML venue. [R1,R2]
- **Unfair comparison due to larger backbone:** Imit-Diff uses a much larger vision backbone (ConvNext + ViT-S) compared to the ResNet-18 used by ACT and Diffusion Policy, making it unclear whether improvements come from the proposed modules or increased model capacity. [R3] This concern is amplified by results in Table 4: when using ViT-S alone (without the proposed modules), the success rate is 30%, lower than the baselines, raising questions about the reliance on pre-trained DINOv2 weights. [R3]
- **Semantic injection not convincingly demonstrated:** The “unseen” targets are only blocks of new colors, insufficient to assess open-vocabulary generalization. [R1] Furthermore, Table 4e does not show substantial benefits from the semantic injection module. [R4] It remains unclear which foundation model (e.g., CLIP, DINOv2, SAM2) performs best for this injection. [R4]
- **Insufficient ablation study:** The ablation in Table 4 lacks an overall ablation of the three main modules (Dual Resolution Fusion, Semantics Injection, Consistency Policy). [R2,R4] The study focuses on detailed settings (loss, FPN levels) rather than isolating the contributions of high- vs low-resolution inputs. [R2] More importantly, it is unclear whether the improvements stem from fusing two pre-trained encoders. [R4] Additionally, when varying FPN layers in Tab 4e, it is not specified whether the models were retrained. [R4]
- **Efficiency concerns:** Despite the consistency policy, the model likely remains inefficient due to the many added modules (Grounding DINO, MixFormerv2, etc.). [R2,R4] Table 5 only compares the CTM component, not the full system’s inference time. [R2] There is no comparison of overall framework speed, and no analysis of how efficiency and performance change after applying the consistency policy. [R2,R4]
- **Limited baselines and evaluation:** Only ACT and Diffusion Policy are compared; no comparisons to recent vision-language-action models (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA) that also aim for generalization. [R2] The ablation study uses only 20 real-world trials per setting, which may lead to high variance; simulated environment results would be more reliable. [R4]
- **Presentation issues:** The paper contains errors (e.g., the policy is called Rep-Diff in Figure 1(c)). [R2] Section 3.2 lacks motivation for why ConvNext and DINOv2 were chosen for low/high-resolution encoding. [R2] Section 3.3 spends significant space describing the consistency policy without addressing specific implementation challenges or unique difficulties in applying it to the proposed algorithm. [R4]

**Reviewer disagreement note:** Reviewers R1 and R3 (each rating 5) are more positive, viewing the method as technically sound and well-motivated based on clear ablation and real-world gains. Reviewers R2 and R4 (each rating 3) are more critical, particularly on novelty, fairness of comparison, insufficient ablations, and efficiency concerns.
