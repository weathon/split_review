## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces CIDA-3D, a method for unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) for 3D-aware classification and multiclass 3D pose estimation. It extends prior work (3DUDA, Kaushik et al. 2024; Jesslen et al. 2023) to a multi-class setting by incorporating weighted conformal prediction to handle covariate shift. The method uses local part plurality and robustness, and requires only unlabeled target domain images. Experiments are conducted on OOD-CV and synthetic-to-real settings. Reviewers are divided: two rate 5 (confidence 2 and 3), two rate 3 (confidence 2 and 3). The main points of disagreement are the novelty of the extension, the depth of experiments (missing baselines, ablations, qualitative results), and clarity of presentation.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a challenging and relatively unexplored problem: adapting 3D-aware models from synthetic data to real-world domains without any labels in the target domain. It is the first fully unsupervised approach for 3D-aware domain adaptation (Reviewer 2).
- CIDA-3D introduces unsupervised domain adaptation into the tasks of 3D-aware classification and multiclass 3D pose estimation (Reviewer 1).
- The local part plurality and robustness hypothesis is well motivated and sound; the model builds on previous method 3DUDA by utilizing local part plurality and robustness to enhance adaptation across different classes in noisy or occluded environments (Reviewer 1).
- The creative combination of conformal prediction with neural mesh models for handling uncertainty in unsupervised domain adaptation is highly original (Reviewer 2). Weighted conformal prediction is introduced for covariate shift (Reviewer 3).
- The method outperforms existing methods by a large margin as shown in Tables 1–3 (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Experiments demonstrate robust synthetic-to-real domain adaptation, bridging the sim2real gap and enhancing robustness against complex real-world nuisances such as texture, shape, and environmental variations (Reviewer 1). Experiments are conducted across various source and target domains (Reviewer 3).
- The paper addresses a highly relevant problem for applications in robotics, autonomous driving, and augmented reality (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing baselines:** Source-only and Oracle baselines are omitted, which would provide lower and upper performance bounds and help future benchmarking (Reviewers 2, 3). Why were these baselines omitted? (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient experimental comparisons with prior work:** Compared to UDA3D, CIDA-3D operates in a multi-class setting with conformal prediction and local part plurality, but pose estimation results when a known category is given to CIDA-3D, or when a category estimated by CIDA-3D is given to UDA3D, are not reported (Reviewer 3).
- **Novelty concerns:** This work is highly based on Kaushik et al. (2024) and Jesslen et al. (2023). It appears to only extend existing methods to a multi-category UDA setup (Reviewers 3, 4). Comparing Fig.2 of this paper with Fig.2 of Kaushik et al. (2024), the only difference is conformal prediction based on Tibshirani et al. (2019) (Reviewer 4). The contribution of this extension is not clearly justified (Reviewer 4). The claim of being the first to attempt UDA for 3D-aware classification and pose estimation (L.93) is not convincingly supported given the prior art (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of depth in ablation and analysis:** Ablation studies lack depth regarding key components like the choice of neural mesh and conformal prediction; detailed analysis of design choices, parameters, and alternative solutions is needed (Reviewer 1). The paper does not explore how different levels of local feature robustness influence performance (Reviewer 1). It would be better to show intermediate results (e.g., feature map visualizations, intermediate 3D matching) during optimization to see how classification and pose results change (Reviewer 4). No metric is provided to evaluate local part robustness and plurality across datasets (Reviewer 1). Efficiency comparisons (training time) are missing (Reviewer 4).
- **Weak justification for conformal prediction:** The theoretical justification for why conformal prediction is particularly well-suited for UDA is underdeveloped (Reviewer 2). The paper explains how conformal prediction generates confidence sets but does not explore why it is superior to other uncertainty quantification methods (e.g., Bayesian approaches or dropout-based uncertainty) (Reviewer 2). Are there empirical evidence to support the claims in L.270-282 about why conformal prediction was chosen? (Reviewer 1). The description of conformal inference in the Related Work is unclear and not connected to the proposed method (Reviewer 4).
- **Clarity and writing issues:** The paper is hard to follow (Reviewers 2, 4). The overall logic is poorly organized; key terms like OOD inputs are not expanded or explained when first mentioned (Reviewer 2). The Introduction lacks coherence: L.31-37 introduces 3D representation and cognitive science independently without conjunctions, and does not provide specific motivation for the proposed framework (local part plurality, robustness, conformal prediction); the contributions and their details are insufficiently described (Reviewer 4). Citations are incorrectly formatted throughout (e.g., \citep vs \cite in L.32,33,154) (Reviewer 4). Captions in Figs.1 and 2 are too small; Fig.2(b) title and descriptions are indistinguishable (Reviewer 4). Punctuation errors in Eqs.2,4,5, L.361; typo in L.427 (Reviewer 4). Notation is re-defined in L.287-307 after being defined in L.158, making formulation hard to follow (Reviewer 4). The first paragraph of the methodology places too much emphasis on the source model; Sections 3.1 and 3.2 are not central contributions and could be streamlined (Reviewer 3). Both Tables 1 and 3 highlight the best-performing metrics, but Table 2 does not—was this an oversight? (Reviewer 2).
- **Minority concerns about reliance on domain classifier:** CIDA-3D relies on a domain classifier; this could introduce dependency on the classifier’s accuracy and increase complexity. The system is vulnerable to misclassification, potentially leading to less effective conformal prediction. It is unclear how robust the domain classifier is, or whether alternative classifier-free UDA solutions are possible (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of visual evidence for key claims:** L.264 claims that the hypothesis also stands in multi-class settings, but this is not intuitive from Fig.1. The key observations in L.65-67 are stated without clear support or evidence (Reviewer 4). Statistical or visual evidence should be provided (Reviewer 4). Qualitative results similar to Fig.3 in Kaushik et al. (2024) are missing (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited generalization scope:** To fully establish generalizability, the method should be tested on large-scale classification datasets like ImageNet or OpenImages with more object categories; the current scope limits assessment for real-world applications (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing interpretability:** It would be helpful to demonstrate interpretability of the conformal prediction module to better understand how well it works (Reviewer 1).
