## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Fourier-based ensemble training method that diversifies non-robust frequency components across sub-models via a weakness allocation strategy. Using random noise and targeted-attack transformations, it aims to reduce adversarial transferability. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 with small architectures (e.g., ResNet-20) show improved robust accuracy over existing ensemble methods while preserving clean accuracy. The method is independently trained per sub-model, reducing GPU memory. Overall, the approach is innovative and addresses a key challenge, but has notable limitations in reproducibility, scalability, and competitiveness with adversarial training.

### Strengths
- Innovative use of Fourier transformations to manipulate frequency components for ensemble adversarial robustness (R1, R2, R3).
- Effectively addresses adversarial transferability by diversifying non-robust features across sub-models (R1, R4).
- Solid theoretical backing supporting the proposed framework (R2).
- Comprehensive experiments across various adversarial attacks and multiple baseline methods demonstrate effectiveness (R2, R3).
- Independent training of sub-models reduces GPU memory requirements and simplifies the training pipeline (R1).
- Motivation of leveraging diverse non-robust features is natural for achieving a better clean-robust accuracy trade-off (R4).
- Comparison with baselines shows the method's superiority (R4).
- One reviewer (R4) found the paper well-written and easy to follow, though another (R2) strongly disagreed on readability (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Complexity and scalability concerns**: The targeted-attack transformation is computationally intensive and intricate to implement, potentially limiting adoption and scalability to larger datasets or real-time applications (R1). The dependency on specific frequency thresholds (τ1, τ2) lacks a systematic methodology for setting them on new datasets with different resolutions or feature distributions (R3). R2 questions whether the method scales to larger models or more complex datasets given the Fourier transforms.
- **Limited architecture scope**: Experiments are primarily on small architectures (ResNet-20); effectiveness on larger or more diverse model types is unclear (R1).
- **Lower robust accuracy compared to adversarial training (AT)**: Ensemble learning with the proposed method still significantly underperforms popular adversarial training methods in robust accuracy, though it maintains higher clean accuracy (R2). This restricts practical applicability.
- **Poor readability and unclear presentation** (R2): specific issues include: (i) no detailed explanation of the feature extractor in Definition 3.1 (R2); (ii) inappropriate use of \(\hat{y}\) for one-hot vector, conflicting with notation for prediction; (iii) confusion in Definition 3.2 second formula (missing \(y^t\)); (iv) Equation 8 is broken across plain text and equation mode; (v) unclear how "high-amplitude frequency features" are identified (R2). These issues hinder reproducibility and comprehension.
- **Insufficient reproducibility details**: The frequency selection and transformation processes are not sufficiently detailed (R3). The specific application of the Fourier transform and manipulation of frequency components are not clearly described, making replication difficult (R3).
- **Lack of justification for amplitude-based selection**: The motivation for choosing high-amplitude frequency components to identify non-robust features is not well explained, lacking theoretical or empirical evidence (R4).
- **Missing ablation studies**: No ablation study verifies the contribution of individual components such as weakness set allocation, amplitude-based selection, and new dataset construction (R4).
- **Strange baseline results**: The reported baseline results in Table 2 differ from those in Table 1 of the cited TRS paper (R4). The authors should clarify this discrepancy.
- **Questions from reviewers that overlap with weaknesses**: (R2) How is the feature extractor different from a standard classification model? (R2) Clarify the incorrect second formula in Definition 3.2. (R3) How do frequency thresholds τ1 and τ2 affect robustness and clean accuracy? (R4) Provide empirical evidence for amplitude-based selection and clarify Table 2 differences.
