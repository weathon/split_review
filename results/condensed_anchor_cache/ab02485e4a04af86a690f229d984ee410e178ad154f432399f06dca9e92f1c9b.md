## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates the selection of teacher models in knowledge distillation (KD) and finds a strong correlation between teacher calibration error (measured by ECE/ACE) and student accuracy. It proposes a simple temperature-based calibration method to reduce teacher calibration error, thereby improving KD performance. The method achieves new state-of-the-art results on image classification benchmarks when applied on top of the existing SOTA method MLLD. Reviewers agree the paper is well-motivated and clearly presented, with extensive experimental validation. However, the technical contribution is considered small (temperature scaling already used in traditional KD), and the scope is limited to image classification. Two reviewers rate the contribution as good (3), while one rates it fair (2), reflecting disagreement on novelty and breadth.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-motivated and clearly written, with a logical structure that is easy to follow. (R1, R2, R3)
- The correlation between teacher calibration error and student accuracy is convincingly demonstrated via careful experiments (Fig 1). (R2, R3)
- Comprehensive experimental validation across multiple benchmarks and domains (R1); strong empirical results that establish new SOTAs when combined with MLLD (R2).
- The use of adaptive calibration error (ACE) instead of expected calibration error (ECE) is a convincing methodological choice. (R3)
- The proposed temperature-based calibration is practical and effective, offering a simple plug-in improvement for existing KD methods. (R1, R2)
- The paper covers most related work in knowledge distillation and is accessible to a wide audience. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and simplicity**: The method is essentially temperature scaling, which has already been proposed in traditional KD; technical contribution is small. (R1, R2) One reviewer (scoring 5) was particularly critical of the limited novelty and narrow scope, while others were more positive.
- **Limited task scope**: All experiments are on image classification; object detection or other tasks are not considered. (R2)
- **Lack of theoretical insight**: The paper would benefit from theoretical analysis explaining why reducing teacher calibration error improves student performance. (R1)
- **Missing experiment**: The authors should use the same student with different teachers of varying depth to disentangle the effects of teacher accuracy and ECE on KD quality, and explore potential trade-offs. (R1)
- **Incomplete related work discussion**: Prior work studying temperature and KD (reference [1] in reviewer comment) should be discussed to clarify similarities and differences. (R1) Additionally, the paper should assess whether the method works well with various calibration studies and cover those in related work. (R3)
- **Experimental concerns raised**:
    - Real-world deployment often involves choosing a student that can best learn from a fixed teacher, rather than selecting a teacher for a fixed student; this scenario should be addressed. (R3 Q1)
    - The trend in Fig 2(a) may reverse between T=2 and T=4, and the variance is large, weakening the claim about the relationship between temperature and calibration. (R3 Q2)
    - In Table 2, the student model (ResNet56) is larger than some teacher models (e.g., VGG19); this raises questions about the appropriateness of the KD setup. (R3 Q3)
    - After applying the proposed calibration-based KD, the calibration (ECE/ACE) of the student model itself is not reported; evaluating this (e.g., in Table 7) would clarify whether the student inherits calibration benefits. (R3 Q4)
