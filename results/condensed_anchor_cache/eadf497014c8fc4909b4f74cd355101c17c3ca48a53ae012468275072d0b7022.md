## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes ADMP-GNN, an adaptive depth message-passing framework that dynamically adjusts the number of message-passing layers for each node. It introduces a progressive training strategy and heuristic layer-selection methods (k-core, PageRank, Walk Count) for test nodes. The approach is model-agnostic and aims to improve node classification performance.

### Strengths
- The idea that different nodes require different numbers of message-passing steps is well-motivated and sensible. (R1, R2, R4)
- The adaptive mechanism itself is simple and the architecture is efficient—the same complexity as a normal GNN. (R1, R4)  
- The synthetic dataset experiment is well-designed and convincingly demonstrates the need for adaptive depth. (R4)
- The sequential training approach helps reduce computational complexity. (R3, R4)
- The paper is well-written and clear. (R2, R4)
- The empirical experiments are promising, show the model-agnostic property, and include extensive ablations. (R4)
- The use of heuristics (k-core, PageRank, Walk Count) for test-time layer selection addresses the challenge of generalizing beyond validation nodes. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and related work.** Several prior works propose adaptive message passing for GNNs (e.g., [R1] Adaptive Message Passing; [R2] Cooperative Graph Neural Networks; [R3] GwAC; [R4] APGCN; others), yet they are neither discussed nor compared in experiments. This calls into question the paper’s novelty. (R1, R4)
- **Missing comparisons.** The method is not compared with graph transformers, graph attention layers (which also provide adaptivity), or rewiring methods. (R1)
- **Limited depth range.** Experiments are only up to 5 layers. It is more interesting to see performance with many layers (e.g., 64, 128, 256) to test robustness to over-smoothing and over-squashing. (R1)
- **Unclear experimental setup.** The data splits are not reported; they appear not to follow standard splits for the datasets used (e.g., as in Geom-GCN). This makes it impossible to fairly compare with published results. (R1)
- **Incomplete ablation study.** The ablation study is mentioned in the main paper but results are missing. (R1, R4)  
- **Heuristic layer selection issues.** The proposed clustering-based method uses validation accuracy per cluster, which may be unreliable if the validation set in a cluster is too small or not representative. (R2)  
- **Limited improvement on some datasets.** Gains are modest on Cora and Citeseer; overall accuracy is not much better than the GCN baseline. (R2, R3)
- **Disentangling heuristics from adaptation.** The Walk Count heuristic is very similar to the well-known RWSE encoding; it is unclear how much of the performance is due to the adaptive mechanism versus these heuristics. (R1)
- **No discussion of fundamental GNN problems.** The paper does not address which problems the method solves (over-smoothing, over-squashing, under-reaching) or its expressiveness. (R1)
- **Runtime concerns.** Although the authors discuss time complexity, they do not compare runtimes with other methods, especially more expensive ones like graph transformers and subgraph methods. (R1) [One reviewer (R4) claims the architecture has the same complexity as normal GNN, but another reviewer (R1) notes the method’s overall complexity is high. This contrast remains unresolved.]
- **Unanswered questions about the training design and figures.**  
  *Figure 1:* For the synthetic experiment, are the L different GNNs trained with the same hyperparameters? Deeper models may be harder to train (see ref [5]). Furthermore, the blue curves show a V-shape (accuracy drops first then improves)—this is not intuitive and not explained. (R4)  
  *Exit function:* Line 193-194: why are hidden representations not included in the exit function? (R4)  
  *Gradient conflicts:* Line 236: is there any proof or reference for the gradient conflict issue? (R4)  
  *Stochastic depth:* Could stochastic depth training help as a simpler alternative to the proposed sequential training? (R4)  
- **No graph-level experiments.** The method’s applicability to graph property prediction is not discussed; if it applies, why no experiments on graph classification/regression? (R1, R4)  
- **Unexplained poor results.** In Table 1, on the CS dataset with 2 layers, ADMP-GNN ALM performs much worse than GCN; the cause (e.g., gradient conflicts) is not explained. (R4)  
- **Terminology unclear.** What does “ADMP-GNN PT” stand for? (R4)  
- **Paper feels incomplete.** The paper is under 9 pages. (R3)
