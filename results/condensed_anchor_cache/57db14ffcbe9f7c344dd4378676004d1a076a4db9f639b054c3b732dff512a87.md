## Merged Review

### Summary
All six reviewers acknowledge the paper’s thorough model development, extensive experimentation, and focus on explainability in multimodal crop yield prediction using Transformer-based architectures. Confidence is moderate (3–4), and the work is described as well-structured and comprehensive in analyses. However, the majority of reviewers (4 out of 6) rate the contribution low (scores 3,3,3,3), citing lack of novelty, unclear takeaways, and insufficient validation of interpretability claims. Two reviewers (scores 6 and 5) are more positive, highlighting practical utility and systematic evaluation, but they also note significant weaknesses around generalization, missing statistical rigor, and absence of domain expert verification.

### Strengths
- Thorough model development and analysis, including ablation studies comparing LSTM, ALSTM, 1D-CNN, and Transformer architectures (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- Extensive comparative analysis of interpretability techniques: Attention Rollout, Generic Attention, Shapley values, linear probes across layers, and attention weight distributions (Reviewers 2,4,5).
- Evaluation on real-world data for three crops (corn, soybean, wheat) in Argentina, demonstrating that interpretability does not compromise performance (Reviewers 2,4,6).
- Well-structured and clearly written paper with accessible explanations and visual aids (Reviewers 4,5,6).
- Large dataset with geographical units (fields, farm, subfields) enabling multi-level analysis (Reviewer 5).
- Novelty in applying explainability methods to the crop yield prediction domain (Reviewers 3,4).
- Referenced related work thoroughly and used visualizations that suggest data-specific model behavior (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of clear novel contribution:** The prediction model uses existing Transformer and explanation methods (SVS, AR, GA) without adaptation or modification for multimodal crop yield prediction. Application appears straightforward (Reviewer 1,3). The technical contributions are unclear; the paper does not demonstrate significant innovation (Reviewer 3).
- **Experiments seem disjointed and lack coherent high-level research question.** Individual assumptions are tested in isolation; no clear takeaways for model builders or decision-makers (Reviewer 1). Actionability is mentioned but not demonstrated (Reviewer 5).
- **Unsubstantiated claim about intrinsic explanations** (Lines 46–47): “Intrinsic explanations are inherently more faithful and less prone to errors introduced by surrogate models.” No references provided (Reviewer 1).
- **Rationale for comparing attention-based attribution and Shapley Values is unclear.** Are Shapley Values treated as ground truth? This needs justification (Reviewer 1,2). The paper highlights discrepancies between weighted modality activations and Shapley values (e.g., soil gets 37.8% weight but only 1.1% in Shapley for corn; satellite 72.3% in Shapley vs. lower in weight). This inconsistency raises reliability concerns (Reviewer 2,6). The lack of alignment between intrinsic and post-hoc methods makes it unclear which modality consistently influences predictions (Reviewer 6).
- **Choice of baseline models is insufficient.** Models like ConvLSTM, STATT, 3D-CNN are more suitable for satellite image time-series data (Reviewer 2). Also missing comparison with contemporary multimodal approaches (e.g., Multimodal Variational Autoencoders, Multimodal Contrastive Learning) (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited generalizability:** Only one dataset from Argentina is used; no experiments on different geographic regions or crop types (Reviewer 2,4,5). No concept drift analysis across fields or time (2017-2023) (Reviewer 5). Model shows variability in capturing yield variance across fields (e.g., Field-B vs. Field-A) and error maps reveal inconsistent spatial patterns (Reviewer 6).
- **Interpretability results lack validation from domain experts.** Explanations are speculative without agronomic knowledge confirmation (Reviewer 2,5). For example, temporal layer-wise attention shows no correspondence with growing/harvesting seasons (Reviewer 2). Authors claim data influence without experimental verification (e.g., claim about daily series in line 419 could be tested by fitting models on aggregated 5-day averages) (Reviewer 5).
- **No statistical significance testing** despite large sample sizes; subfields could be used as units for t-tests or ANOVA on attention score distributions (Reviewer 5).
- **Qualitative evaluation of interpretability only:** Missing functionally-grounded metrics such as fidelity and sparsity (Reviewer 4).
- **Computational cost not discussed:** Transformer has 109,345 parameters vs. ALSTM’s 38,017, with modest improvement (R² 0.41→0.46, MAE 2.00→1.90). No report of inference time or resource requirements (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing statistical details:** Table 1 lacks standard deviations or significance tests to assess if performance differences are meaningful (Reviewer 1).
- **Reproducibility concerns:** No code provided (Reviewer 5). Preprocessing steps for each data type are not described in sufficient detail (Reviewer 4). Missing reference link (line 1122) (Reviewer 4).
- **Question about linear probing setup (line 264):** It is unclear if training data from Transformer model is reused as test data for linear probing, which would risk evaluating overfit representations (Reviewer 5).
- **Need to better articulate why crop yield prediction is critical** and its impact, especially given computational demands of transformers (Reviewer 3). The paper does not adequately convey the problem significance.
- **Multitask learning ambiguity:** It is unclear if the three crop yields are predicted in a multi-task manner; ground truth for Table 1 needs clarification (Reviewer 3).
