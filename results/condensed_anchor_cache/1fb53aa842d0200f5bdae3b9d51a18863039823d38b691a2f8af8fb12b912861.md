## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Influence-Guided Diffusion (IGD) for dataset distillation. It uses a guided diffusion process with two terms: trajectory influence (gradient matching between generated and original samples) and deviation guidance (diversity among generated samples). The method is claimed to be training-free, leveraging pretrained diffusion models. Experiments on ImageNette, ImageWoof, and ImageNet subsets achieve state-of-the-art results (e.g., 60.3% on ImageNet-1K at IPC=50). All five reviewers agree the paper is well-written and easy to follow.

### Strengths
- **Readability and clarity.** The paper is well written, easy to read and understand (R1, R2, R4, R5).
- **Motivation is clear and reasonable.** The problem is well framed and the method’s motivation is sensible (R2, R3, R5).
- **Novelty of the idea.** Applying guided diffusion for dataset distillation is an interesting and neat application. The bridge between diffusion models and importance-based sample selection is reasonable (R4, R5). One reviewer (R5) particularly praised the idea as “reasonable and neat” and found the performance improvements “remarkable.”
- **Well-motivated, simple guidance terms.** The two guidance terms (gradient similarity and sample diversity) are intuitive and well justified (R2, R4).
- **Strong performance.** The method achieves significant improvements over baselines and state-of-the-art results on ImageNet subsets, with a 60.3% accuracy on ImageNet-1K at IPC=50 (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5). The improvement with DiT and Minimax-finetuned DiT is especially obvious (R3).
- **Comprehensive ablation studies.** Ablations cover all proposed components and hyperparameters (R3, R4). Cross-architecture validation and visualizations are provided (R5).
- **Training-free for the diffusion model itself.** IGD can be plugged into any pretrained diffusion model without additional diffusion model training (R1, R3). (But note the “training-free” claim is contested – see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with recent diffusion-based works.** Several important baselines are missing: RDED [1] (R3, R5), other efficient diffusion sampling strategies (e.g., DPM solvers) (R3), and more recent methods that also use pretrained diffusion models (R5). Time or generation cost comparisons with RDED are also absent (R3).
- **Experiments limited to ImageNet subsets.** No results on classic datasets like CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 (R1).
- **The “training-free” claim is misleading.** The method still requires training a surrogate model (ConvNet-6) to collect checkpoints for computing trajectory influence, plus storing gradients and using a pretrained diffusion model. This is not “training-free” in the usual AIGC sense; the paper should clarify the scope and report the overhead (R1, R3, R4). One reviewer (R1) asked how IGD differs from existing train-free AIGC approaches.
- **Weak motivation of “Influence” in the introduction.** The term “Influence” is not clearly explained or motivated before its use; a diagram contrasting with prior methods would help (R1).
- **Mathematical writing issues (R4).**
  - Derivation from stepwise (Eq.4) to epochwise (Eq.5) is unclear; the approximation of total loss reduction using summed stepwise influences is not rigorously justified, especially for non-convex losses.
  - Duplicate definition of C (L096 and L110).
  - Inconsistent bold/non-bold notation for latent variable \(z\) (Sec 2.2).
  - Decoder D (L132) is not clearly defined.
  - Parameters \(\theta_e\) and \(\theta_E\) in Eq.5 are not defined.
- **High computational and storage cost.** Gradient calculation for all generated samples and similarity computations are expensive. The paper lacks a detailed analysis of time, memory, and storage costs across all experiments (R4). The need for per-architecture distillation (one distillation per target model) raises concerns about real-world applicability and computational expense (R2).
- **Lack of diversity measurement and risk of distribution collapse.** No quantitative diversity metrics (e.g., FID, Recall) are reported (R2). Despite the deviation guidance, there is concern that the method could cause collapse of the generated data distribution (R5).
- **Design of Equation (7) is insufficiently justified.** Why is cosine similarity used instead of product? Is this purely empirical or based on a hypothesis? (R2). The model choice (ConvNet-6) for the surrogate checkpoints: would a larger model (e.g., Swin Transformer) improve performance, or is the choice insensitive? (R3).
- **Performance still far from full dataset.** The paper does not study what IPC would be needed to match the original dataset performance (R2).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity not studied.** The algorithm has many hyperparameters (influence factor, deviation factor, scale, guided range, etc.); their sensitivity and the search procedure are not reported (R5).
- **Generalization only to classification tasks.** It is unclear how the method would perform on other tasks (R2).
- **Necessity of the proposed guidance terms is questionable.** As shown in Table 5, even when the influence and deviation guidance are removed from the sampling (Eq.9), good results are still achieved. This undermines the claimed contribution of these terms (R1).
