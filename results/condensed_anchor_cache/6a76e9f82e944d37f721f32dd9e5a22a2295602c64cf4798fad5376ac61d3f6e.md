## Merged Review

### Summary
KARPA is a training‑free KGQA framework that operates in three steps: pre‑planning (LLM generates initial relation paths using KG relations), retrieving (semantic embedding model finds candidate paths in the KG that are similar to the LLM‑generated paths), and reasoning (LLM uses candidates to produce the final answer). The authors claim state‑of‑the‑art performance on WebQSP and CWQ. Reviewer ratings are mostly 5 (four reviewers), with one reviewer (R1) giving a score of 3, reflecting stronger concerns about novelty and fairness of comparison.

### Strengths
- The three‑step process (pre‑planning, retrieving, reasoning) is clearly described and easy to follow.
- Training‑free nature allows easy integration with various LLM architectures without fine‑tuning or pre‑training on specific KGs, offering flexibility and adaptability.
- KARPA reduces the number of interactions between the LLM and the KG, improving efficiency. The semantic embedding‑based retrieval helps avoid local optima that can occur in stepwise traversal methods.
- The idea is reasonable and well‑motivated for leveraging external KG knowledge.
- Strong experimental results on the WebQSP and CWQ datasets, consistently outperforming baseline methods.
- The heuristic value‑based relation path retrieval method is interesting for computing semantic similarity between paths of different lengths.

### Weaknesses
- **Unfair comparison**: Baseline methods mostly use 3–7B LLMs while KARPA uses GPT. Authors should test with smaller LLMs (e.g., 7B) to enable a fair comparison (R1, R2). One reviewer (R1) specifically notes that RoG used instruction‑tuning, but KARPA’s comparison does not use the same backbone.
- **Lack of novelty and technical contribution**: The planning‑retrieval‑reasoning framework closely resembles RoG; KARPA merely expands the planning phase into pre‑planning and re‑planning (R1). The claimed “training‑free” contribution is essentially relying on the abilities of a pre‑trained LLM, so substantive technical advances are minimal (R2).
- **Heavy reliance on LLM abilities**: KARPA requires the LLM to propose candidate paths or rank candidates in one single call. This may fail when the candidate subgraph is complex or large (R2, R3). If the LLM outputs hallucinations, meaningless paths, or paths that do not match the KG, performance degrades because there is no mechanism to control output quality (R2). One reviewer (R2) argues that stepwise methods like ToG are more realistic and efficient in such cases.
- **Dependence on the semantic embedding model**: Performance heavily depends on the quality and choice of the embedding model (R1, R5). The use of a sentence transformer may fail in multilingual scenarios, e.g., when the LLM proposes English relations but the KG surface form is Chinese (R3). It is not proven that embedding‑based selection is better than LLM‑based selection, especially on dense KGs with many paths (R2).
- **Generalization and robustness concerns**: The method is evaluated only on KGQA; its effectiveness on other knowledge‑intensive tasks is not addressed (R5). WebQSP and CWQ are likely seen during LLM pre‑training, so performance on unseen KGs or domains (e.g., when the KG is not in the LLM’s training scope) remains unknown (R4, R5). Performance may degrade with incomplete or noisy KGs (R5).
- **Missing baselines and metrics**: Robust agent‑like methods such as Interactive‑KBQA are not compared (R1). Many baselines report the strict Exact Match (EM) metric, but KARPA does not report EM (R1).
- **Experimental details insufficient**: The meaning of “sampling 300 KGs instances across datasets to optimize computational cost” (lines 408–409) is unclear (R3). The results in Table 3 need more explanation, and token usage would be a more effective measure than call frequency when comparing efficiency with ToG (R3).
- **Unexplained design choices**: Figure 1 is unclear; it does not show how KARPA resolves the claimed limitations of local search and unseen KGs (R4). The advantage of extending relation paths to different lengths is not empirically proven; experiments demonstrating this are needed (R4).
- **Missing references**: Several relevant works are omitted, such as “Knowledge Graph‑Enhanced Large Language Models via Path Selection” (ACL 2024 Findings), “KnowledgeNavigator”, “Paths‑over‑Graph”, and “LightRAG” (R5).
- **Scalability and efficiency**: The framework’s computational efficiency and scalability on larger KGs or more complex queries have not been evaluated (R5).

**Minority critical viewpoint**: One reviewer (R1, score 3) is particularly concerned about the lack of novelty, the unfair comparison with RoG, and questions the motivation that instruction‑tuning (as in RoG) cannot generalize to unseen KGs—arguing that few‑shot generalization can mitigate hallucinations and that this claim needs experimental validation.
