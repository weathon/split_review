## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GrowLength, a pretraining strategy that progressively increases the training sequence length (e.g., from 128 to 4096) using RoPE and direct positional extrapolation. Reviewers acknowledge that the method targets an important problem (LLM training cost) and is presented clearly, but they raise strong concerns about insufficient experimental validation, lack of novelty relative to existing work (e.g., BERT two-phase pretraining, curriculum learning), missing comparisons and ablation studies, and presentation issues. Reviewer 1 (score 6) found the logic easy-to-understand and the ablations convincing; Reviewer 3 (score 5) considered it simple but effective with plenty of experiments. In contrast, Reviewer 2 (score 3) and Reviewer 4 (score 3) questioned the validity of the experiments and the originality of the core idea.

### Strengths
- **Important problem**: The paper tackles the high computational cost of LLM pretraining.
- **Clear logic and writing**: The use of RoPE and insights from position interpolation/extrapolation is straightforward. Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, and Reviewer 2 all note good presentation.
- **Plenty of experiments**: Results cover multiple model scales (160M, 410M, 1B parameters) and show that GrowLength reduces training loss and time compared to constant-length baselines (Tables 1–3, Figures 1–5). Reviewer 1 highlights ablations on training schedules showing limited dependence.
- **No additional engineering effort**: The method modifies only the data loader, making it practical.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient experimental validation**:
  - Training is far from convergence: the longest run is ~20,000s (5.56h) on 16 V100s, whereas a 160M LLM typically requires several days to converge (Reviewer 2). No evidence that the model has reached a reasonable level of convergence.
  - Experiments use only a single neural architecture (unspecified, likely a LLaMA-like model) and small model sizes (up to 1B parameters). The paper does not address scaling to models with billions (e.g., 7B+) or how the method works with other acceleration techniques (GQA, FastAttention) – Reviewer 4, Reviewer 3.
  - No final testing results on downstream tasks or standard benchmarks (e.g., zero-shot or fine-tuned evaluation). Only training loss and perplexity are reported, which is insufficient to demonstrate general efficacy – Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3.
  - The number of extra tokens used by GrowLength-2 is not reported (Reviewer 2, Q1; also Reviewer 4 Q1: where are GrowLength-1 and GrowLength-2 defined?).

- **Lack of novelty and missing relevant comparisons**:
  - The core idea – using shorter sequences to reduce computation – is well-established; the paper does not clearly articulate a unique contribution beyond that (Reviewer 2).
  - The method is essentially an application of context-window extension techniques from fine-tuning to pretraining, but the distinct challenges of pretraining (data distribution, optimization landscape, resource constraints) are not discussed (Reviewer 3).
  - Missing comparisons to: BERT two-phase pretraining with a growing context window (Reviewer 4), curriculum learning for NLU (Xu et al., ACL 2020) which also uses length-based difficulty (Reviewer 3), and efficient pretraining methods like stacking/layerdrop (e.g., [1–4], especially [4] which also proposed a GrowLength within Stacking) and optimization-based acceleration (e.g., [5,6]) – Reviewer 1.
  - No comparison to different position embeddings to justify RoPE’s role (Reviewer 1).

- **Insufficient study of growth schedule and hyperparameters**:
  - The exponential growth schedule (128, 256, 512, …) is not rigorously justified. There is no analysis of faster vs. slower growth, diminishing returns, or instability at later stages. The potential for negative transfer when growing too fast is not addressed (Reviewer 3).
  - It is unclear how many sequence batches are used per stage and whether they are proportional to sequence length (Reviewer 1). If batch sizes change, it is unclear whether baseline hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size) were optimally tuned for a fair comparison (Reviewer 1).
  - The method’s sensitivity to the length distribution of the training data is not explored (e.g., if the dataset has few short sequences) (Reviewer 3).

- **Presentation issues**:
  - Figures 3 and 5 are confusing because different arrows point to lines with the same colors (Reviewer 4).
  - Typos: “while GrowLength-1 is trained with more tokens” should be “GrowLength-2” (Reviewer 2, also Reviewer 3); missing conference name for reference [1] (Reviewer 2); extra full stop at end of third line in Sec. 4.4 (Reviewer 3).
  - Tables 1–3 might be better presented as figures (Reviewer 3).
