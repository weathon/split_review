## Merged Review

### Summary

ControlVAR introduces a controllable visual autoregressive (VAR) framework that jointly models image and pixel-level conditions using next-scale AR prediction. By treating controls (e.g., mask, canny, depth, normal) as RGB tokens with the same tokenizer as the image, and employing a teacher‑forcing guidance (TFG) strategy during inference, the model handles control‑to‑image, image‑to‑control, and even unseen control‑to‑control tasks. Experiments across multiple tasks show competitive performance against diffusion‑based methods such as ControlNet and T2I‑Adapter.

### Strengths

- The alternating prediction of image tokens and control tokens is a novel and promising direction for AR‑based conditional generation; addressing how to control AR models is valuable for the community as AR applications grow.
- The framework uses pixel‑level controls and the TFG mechanism, both innovative within the AR domain.
- Experiments are well‑designed and demonstrate effectiveness across many conditional generation tasks, outperforming popular diffusion models (ControlNet and T2I‑Adapter).
- The paper is generally well‑written and easy to follow, with clear visualization that helps illustrate the framework and generation results.

### Weaknesses

- **Major concern – fine‑tuning requirement and limited flexibility:** ControlVAR requires fine‑tuning the pre‑trained VAR model to enable control. Unlike ControlNet for Stable Diffusion (which does not modify base model weights), switching to a new base AR model would necessitate retraining or extensive fine‑tuning, incurring substantial computational and resource overhead. This reduces the method’s flexibility, scalability, and practical adoption. (All three reviewers note this; Reviewer 2 stresses that this limitation is a core drawback.)  
    *Reviewer 2 and Reviewer 3 ask whether freezing the base model could be explored, and whether there are plans to reduce the dependency on model‑specific tuning.*

- **Late introduction and insufficient theoretical depth:** The core methodology is not introduced until Section 3.2, and the overall network modeling section lacks rigorous, specific theoretical grounding. The explanation of how control is achieved at the pixel level is shallow.  
    *Reviewer 1 points to unclear notation (C vs. c), lack of early examples, and confusing derivation in Eq. (6). Reviewer 2 and Reviewer 3 both note that the method description is too late and not sufficiently in‑depth.*  
    *Reviewer 2 asks for a more detailed theoretical explanation of how control works in the AR setup.*

- **Computational overhead:** ControlVAR sequences are twice as long as standard VAR, leading to at least double training and inference time. The comparison of training speed with ControlNet and T2I‑Adapter is provided but without model configurations, making the speed comparison unconvincing (Reviewer 1).

- **Limited novelty of Teacher‑Forcing Guidance (TFG):** TFG appears similar to existing guidance methods (e.g., classifier‑free guidance) and does not introduce a substantially distinct technique for controllable generation (Reviewer 2).

- **Theme ambiguity and naming mismatch:** The paper includes tasks like image‑to‑control and control‑to‑control prediction, which diverge from its primary focus on control‑to‑image generation. This blurs the core contribution and may confuse readers. The name “ControlVAR” implies a heavier emphasis on control‑focused multi‑task learning than the actual scope (Reviewer 2).  
    *Reviewer 2 asks how ControlVAR could handle unconditioned tasks given its reliance on control conditions.*

- **Organizational and presentation issues:**  
    – Section 3.1 contains extensive details on a specific control extraction method that would be better placed in experiments or supplementary material (Reviewer 3).  
    – The tokenization description is minimal (only states “same tokenizer as VAR”), offering little informational value (Reviewer 3).  
    – Citation format should use `\citep` or `\citet` instead of bare `\cite` (Reviewer 1).  

- **Missing insight from depth exploration:** The authors explore different model depths, but the necessity of this analysis and the insights drawn from it are not clearly motivated.  
    *Reviewer 1 questions what specific conclusions can be derived from varying model depth.*

- **Missing discussion on AR vs. diffusion advantages:** The paper would benefit from a clearer articulation of the advantages of the autoregressive approach for conditional generation compared to the more mature diffusion methods.  
    *Reviewer 1 asks for an explicit perspective on this comparison.*

- **Minority reviewer (Reviewer 2, score 3) is notably more critical** on the issues of limited novelty of TFG, theme ambiguity, and the overall contribution being insufficiently novel for controllable generation. Reviewers 1 and 3 (both score 5) found the contributions more convincing, but all agree on the fine‑tuning limitation and shallow theoretical depth.
