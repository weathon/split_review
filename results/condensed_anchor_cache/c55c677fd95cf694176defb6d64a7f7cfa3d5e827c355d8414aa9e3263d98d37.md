## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces SADE (Scene-text Autoregressive Diffusion Engine), an autoregressive diffusion model that generates synthetic scene-text images to improve OCR accuracy in data-scarce settings. SADE addresses the failure of standard diffusion models to place characters in unseen positions by generating overlapping image parts sequentially, conditioned on previous parts and the target character sequence. Experiments on mock number plates and real-world vehicle type labels show OCR accuracy gains, especially for characters at positions absent from the original limited training data. Reviewers agree on the core idea and its practical motivation but diverge widely in their assessment of its execution and evidence: two reviewers (scores 5) find the method effective and promising, one (score 3) sees potential but notes missing validation, and one (score 1) considers the evaluation too narrow to support claims.

### Strengths
- **Innovative combination of autoregression and diffusion** to overcome the position-memorization problem of standard diffusion models, enabling generation of diverse character sequences in unseen positions while maintaining visual coherence. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Clever segmentation and overlap design**: SADE partitions limited labeled images into overlapping parts, then autoregressively generates new parts conditioned on earlier ones, effectively increasing the number of training examples and sequence diversity from a small labelled set. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Clear architectural explanation**: the paper provides a comprehensive description of the conditioning on character sequences, the generation of overlapping parts, and the training/sampling procedures. (Reviewer 1)
- **Practical demonstration on two real-world tasks** (license plate and vehicle trim label recognition), showing that SADE can improve OCR accuracy in data-scarce scenarios. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4; Reviewer 2 notes this is a strength but with limited scope).
- **Addresses a concrete limitation**: standard diffusion models trained on whole images memorize character positions; SADE’s autoregressive design reduces the effective number of positions the model must handle, making it able to generalize to novel positions. (Reviewers 2, 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental evaluation and missing ablations**. Multiple reviewers call for ablation studies of each component (autoregressive generation, overlapping parts, character sequence conditioning) to quantify their individual contributions. (Reviewers 1, 2) Quantitative analysis of how well generated images respect conditioned characters is absent. (Reviewer 2) Error analysis and discussion of common failure cases are missing. (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient comparisons with relevant methods**. The paper lacks comparisons with: state-of-the-art OCR methods (Reviewer 1), non-autoregressive diffusion models that generate whole images (Reviewers 2, 3), traditional render-based synthesis engines (Reviewer 2), GAN-based synthetic data generation (Reviewer 4), and recent diffusion-based text generation methods such as **Textdiffuser** and **AnyText** (Reviewer 3). Only bar-chart results without precise accuracy numbers are provided, making quantitative comparison hard. (Reviewer 3)
- **Generalizability concerns**. The method is tested only on two specific, short alphanumeric tasks (license plates and vehicle trim labels). Reviewers question whether SADE would generalize to more complex OCR scenarios (e.g., variable‑length text, multi‑language, arbitrary fonts). (Reviewers 1, 2, 4) The model generates **fixed-size images**, limiting its applicability to text sequences of varying length. (Reviewer 2)
- **Limited justification of data quality and controllability**. The paper does not discuss how the quality of generated images is controlled or assessed, nor does it evaluate the match between generated characters and background or the overall realism of the synthetic images. (Reviewers 4) There is no mention of when generation quality is poor or how to avoid such cases. (Reviewer 4)
- **Narrow related‑work coverage**. Important recent text recognition and generation methods (Textdiffuser, AnyText) are omitted, undermining the positioning of the contribution. (Reviewer 3)
- **Presentation issues**. The paper lacks a pipeline diagram or process overview, making the method hard to follow. (Reviewers 3, 4) Qualitative visual examples comparing SADE outputs with those from other methods are missing. (Reviewer 1) Results are presented in bar charts without explicit accuracy numbers, reducing interpretability. (Reviewer 3) The abstract contains a typo: “data-scare” should be “data-scarce”. (Reviewer 2)
- **Computational inefficiency**. The autoregressive generation of sequential image parts is likely time‑consuming compared to holistic generative models. (Reviewer 1)
- **Minority negative view**: Reviewer 2 (score 1, confidence 4) is particularly critical, stating that the method’s evaluation on only two highly specific, relatively straightforward OCR tasks does not demonstrate practical value, and questions whether a standard render‑based engine would suffice—indicating that the contribution may be incremental. This reviewer also highlights the lack of analysis on how well the generated images match the conditioned characters as a serious omission.
