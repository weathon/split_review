## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Shift Split Channels (SSC) layer to replace convolutional layers, aiming for faster and more memory-efficient operation by splitting input channels, shifting, and subtracting. Reviewers acknowledge the clear motivation and evaluation across multiple applications (image classification, sequence modeling, speech separation). However, they raise serious concerns: the method’s effectiveness is disputed—one reviewer claims comparable accuracy and slight efficiency gains while others note significant accuracy drops (e.g., 78%→71% on CIFAR100) and negligible speed/memory improvements. The method lacks mathematical formalization, proper literature review (prior shift-based and MLP approaches), and sufficient validation on large-scale datasets and standard backbones. Presentation quality varies from “well-described” to “poor,” with figures and abbreviation definition criticized.

### Strengths
- The key idea (splitting channels, shifting, subtracting) is clearly described (R1, R2). The attempt to decouple computational complexity from kernel size is somewhat enlightening (R1).
- The proposed method is evaluated on a number of applications including image classification, sequence modelling, and single-channel speech separation (R2, R4).
- The experimental results seem believable and the motivation is good (R1, R3).
- One reviewer (R4) notes that SSC achieves comparable accuracy with slightly less computations and memory consumption, though this is contested by others.

### Weaknesses
- **Effectiveness and performance trade-off**: The proposed method lacks real effectiveness, contradicting the authors’ claimed conclusions. The claimed replacement of convolutional layers is not plausible (R1). In image classification (Table 4), the training time and memory are close to standard convolution but accuracy drops significantly (78% vs 71%), which is unacceptable (R2). This suggests the method may be ineffective even on small-scale datasets like CIFAR100 (R3). The improvement over existing methods is not significant; speed and memory improvement is minor and performance drops over convolution layers (R4). One reviewer (R4) however found comparable accuracy with slightly less computations, creating disagreement.
- **Depthwise separable convolution is a better alternative**: The experimental results only support that depthwise separable convolutional layers, not the proposed SSC layer, have a good balance between accuracy and computational cost (R1).
- **Lack of mathematical formalization and theoretical discussion**: The paper lacks a formalization of the SSC operation and does not discuss in depth the relation and difference to convolutional layers, especially when stacking multiple layers (R2). The “shift and subtract” operation seems not reasoning and convincing; the method is just a special case of convolution with hand-crafted weighting (R4).
- **Insufficient literature review**: Several existing works remove convolutional layers using similar ideas (e.g., splitting channels and shifting). The authors should have reviewed prior works such as MLP-Mixer and MAXIM (R3). The approach is straightforward and has been proposed previously (R3).
- **Limited experimental validation**: Experiments are only done on small-scale datasets like CIFAR100; conclusions may not generalize to large-scale datasets (R3). The authors should validate on more network architectures, large-scale image classification tasks, and classical backbones (R4).
- **Presentation issues**: Figure 3 is not informative; without reading the main text it is difficult to understand (R2). The abbreviation “SSC” is not defined in the abstract, and “SOTA” should be written out (R3). The quality of figures could be improved (R3). Overall presentation needs significant improvement; operation details are unclear and fail to convince the reader of the advantage (R4).
- **Missing ablation/hyperparameter details**: Do all layers use the same value of K? More generally, how to set the value of K? (R2) (This is a missing experiment/ablation related to the method.)
