## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Spectral Highways, a data augmentation technique that adds supernodes (one per spectral cluster) and fully connects them, along with edges to original cluster members, to improve GNN performance on heterophilic graphs. Experiments on seven small-scale heterophilic datasets show significant gains on ChameleonF and SquirrelF. The paper also analyzes homophily shifts and shortest-path changes. However, reviewers raise major concerns about dataset choice, scalability, justification, ablation, baselines, writing quality, and reproducibility. One reviewer (score 5) is notably more positive, praising the story and dataset coverage, while the other three (score 3) find the method insufficiently motivated and validated.

### Strengths
- Method and illustrations are clear.
- Approach is simple and shows significant results on ChameleonF and SquirrelF.
- Paper organizes existing motivations for heterophilic graphs and addresses them in the analysis section.
- One reviewer (score 5) finds the story interesting.
- That same reviewer considers the datasets rich, though others disagree.
- Idea is reasonable and improves on the concept of virtual nodes (e.g., Hwang et al. (2022)).
- Idea is simple and works well on the tested datasets.

### Weaknesses
- **Dataset limitations (multiple reviewers).** Cornell, Texas, Wisconsin are extremely small and unbalanced, making accuracy an inappropriate metric. Squirrel Filtered and Chameleon Filtered were introduced by Platonov et al. (2023) to illustrate flaws and are not recommended for further use; the new datasets from that paper are omitted. Only one dataset from Lim et al. (2021) is used. The authors report out‑of‑memory errors for pokec, genius, wiki and resource constraints for twitch‑gamers. The absence of realistic, larger datasets reduces the reliability of conclusions.
- **Scalability.** The algorithm depends on spectral clustering, which prevents scaling to larger graphs. The paper acknowledges large variance in results, likely due to the small size of Cornell, Texas, Wisconsin. No experiments on larger datasets are provided.
- **Lack of justification for why the method works (multiple reviewers).** The core intuition is insufficiently motivated. Adding edges independently of homophily creates neutral edges, which moves adjusted homophily toward zero – this is not obviously beneficial for heterophilic graphs. The specific choice of spectral clustering over other connectivity‑enhancing methods is not justified. Lines 208‑232 show that the intuitive option does not always work and all variants must be tried, undermining the claimed intuition.
- **No ablation study.** The contribution of different components is not assessed: number of virtual nodes, spectral clustering parameters, connectivity patterns between virtual nodes and original nodes, and especially the edges between spectral nodes (which one reviewer argues seem redundant).
- **Insufficient comparisons.** Few rewiring, data‑centric, or graph‑augmentation methods are compared. The method is not compared to stronger baselines in heterophilic graphs (e.g., Table 1 in [1]). Table 2 mixes baselines with different base GNN models, making results not directly comparable; Dir‑SAGE and SH(Dir‑SAGE) are statistically tied. Table 7 shows that SH hurts performance on homophilic graphs, yet the practical challenge of knowing whether a graph is homophilic or heterophilic is not discussed.
- **Missing computational complexity analysis.** No time/space complexity analysis is provided. The construction time of Spectral Highways (spectral decomposition, adding nodes/edges), its impact on downstream GNN runtime, and the memory footprint of the augmented graph versus the original are not reported.
- **Writing and presentation issues (multiple reviewers).** The paper is written poorly. The introduction lacks a paragraph on existing challenges of node classification on heterophilic graphs, making the contributions abrupt. The related works section provides little context or motivation. Section 2.2 is redundant with Section 2.1. Equation (1) is claimed to be a formal description but is not a valid mathematical expression, complicating reading. Line 158 describes vertices with high similarity as “far away,” which is ambiguous. Figure 1 does not look like a heterophilic graph (most edges connect same‑label nodes).
- **Analysis depth.** Section 6 (analysis of homophily and shortest‑path shifts) should be moved forward to justify the design, yet most analyses are indirect and do not explain why the method improves performance. Theoretical analysis is lacking. Table 2 could be expanded to show performance gains across dataset characteristics (homophily levels, graph sizes, class distributions).
- **Novelty concerns.** Adding nodes and connections is a common graph augmentation approach. The paper does not clearly delineate unique spectral properties compared to similar methods (e.g., [1] or the virtual‑node work of Hwang et al. (2022) and Qian et al. (2024)). Hwang et al. also cluster and assign virtual nodes but omit edges between them.
- **Missing code.** Code for reproducing results is not provided.
- **Specific reviewer disagreement.** One reviewer (score 5) finds the story interesting and datasets rich, while the other three (score 3) consider the datasets small and flawed, the method insufficiently justified, and the evaluation incomplete. This positive assessment does not override the multiple substantive concerns raised by the other reviewers.
