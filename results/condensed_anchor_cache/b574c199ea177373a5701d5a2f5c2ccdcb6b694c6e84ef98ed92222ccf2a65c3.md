## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers are split between enthusiastic (scores 10,8) and critical (scores 5,5,1). Positive reviewers praise the first diffusion model for full-length human genotypes, clear writing, and empirical evidence that synthetic data matches real data in downstream classifiers. The negative reviewers (especially score-1) find the paper falls short of ICLR standards due to missing technical details, weak experimental design, poor figures, and lack of comparison to prior work. All reviewers agree the problem is important and the approach of working at the complete-genome level is promising, but disagree sharply on execution and novelty.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and importance**: First diffusion model to generate complete synthetic human genotypes (R1,R2,R5). Addresses a significant challenge in human genetics: private data limits public availability (R1,R2). The problem of synthetic data generation is significant (R3).
- **Clarity and prose**: The paper is incredibly well-written with clear, enjoyable prose (R1). Well-structured and technically sound (R2). Relatively easy to follow (R3).
- **Useful comparative study**: Compares four diffusion architectures (CNN, CNN+MLP, Transformer) with insights relevant beyond biomedicine, e.g., understanding diffusion on different data types (R1).
- **Empirical evidence**: Demonstrates near-identical accuracy when classifiers are trained on synthetic vs. real data, and that augmenting small real data with synthetic data improves performance (R1,R2,R5).
- **Privacy discussion included**: Evaluation of privacy loss via membership inference (R3). Data privacy is a clear motivation (R2).
- **Dataset choice**: The two datasets (ALS, 1000 Genomes) are well-justified (R4).
- **Complete-genome focus**: Working at the complete genome level to represent full individuals, which is a limitation of most sequence-level models (R4). The embedding technique to reduce dimensionality is a promising approach (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons to prior work**: The paper does not compare to other methods (R1,R3,R4,R5). Specific missing references: Szatkownik et al, HyenaDNA (R1); D3, DNADiffusion, Enformer, Borzoi (R4). The previous works section has only a table with no main text, making it unclear how the current approach relates (R5).
- **Insufficient technical details**:
  - The embedding (PCA) is not novel and lacks thorough discussion of how complete genomes are reconstructed from latent space and the impact on final sequence quality (R4). Reconstruction metrics at the sequence level are missing (R4).
  - Model architecture description is difficult to follow; ablations lack proper introduction (R4). The MLP is very shallow (first downscale 147k→1024) and its loss doesn’t move – unclear if it learns more than regularization (R1).
  - Training duration and process are not detailed (R4). The classifier architecture (input, architecture) is not specified (R4). Data generation with conditions (classifier-free guidance) is not explained – how are conditions selected for generation? (R5).
  - Privacy metrics (Section 3.4.2) are poorly introduced; the notation ‘d’ (distance) is not defined (R4). Fréchet Distance was not used despite relevance for distributional similarity (R2). The data splitting procedure is unclear (R2).
  - The claim that DDIM is beneficial but DDPM is necessary is confusing; DDPM is a special case (R5). Classifier-free guidance was developed for conditional generation, not specifically quality (R5).
- **Experimental flaws**:
  - In the data-scarcity augmentation experiment, the diffusion model should be trained on the same k% as the classifier to reflect actual use, not on 100% (R4).
  - Evaluation limitations are not discussed: generalizability to other genomic datasets (R2), potential negative implications (R3). A more thorough ethical discussion (beyond privacy) is needed (R3).
- **Model design concerns**:
  - Using CNNs is questionable due to lack of translational symmetry: the same vector can represent entirely different information across the many distinct PCA latent spaces (R5).
  - The Transformer comparison is unfair: the claim that transformers lack spatial bias ignores positional encoding (R5). The Transformer performs surprisingly poorly on long sequences, and the explanation is limited (R1).
  - DDIM was not explored; only inference-time use and its quality impact (R1).
- **Poor figure quality**: Figures have small fonts, are blurry, and not vector (R1,R3,R4,R5). Some figures appear to be screenshots from run monitoring software (W&B) – unacceptable (R4,R5). The UMAP experiment is distracting and subjective (R5). Figure 2 has non-horizontal lines and misplaced markers (R5).
- **Writing and structure issues**:
  - The paper uses too many small sub-paragraphs; a denser presentation is expected (R4). The previous works section lacks main text (R5). Grammar issues: " that do not only" → "that not only", missing quotation-mark style (R3). Redundancy in lines 453 and 483 (R1). Unnecessary comma line 492 (R1).
- **Minority strong negative view**: Reviewer 4 (score 1, confidence 5) states the paper falls short of expected quality and contribution standards for ICLR and confidently recommends strong rejection. They find the literature review incomplete, figures unprofessional, experimental setup unconvincing, and the overall writing lacking crucial details.
