## Merged Review

### Summary
This work explores speculative decoding (SD) for multimodal language models, specifically LLaVA 1.5. It analyzes four drafting strategies—multimodal (M), text-only (T), caption (C), and pooled multimodal (P)—and finds no single method consistently best. To address this, the paper proposes In-batch Ensemble Drafting (IbED), which combines probability distributions from all four strategies via a single small draft model (68M parameters) and uniform averaging, aiming to improve block efficiency without additional model parameters.

### Strengths
- **Comprehensive analysis of drafting strategies across diverse image settings** (1, 2, and 5 images per sample) with good dataset coverage. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- **IbED is effective and insightful:** it yields more consistent decoding speedups than any single strategy and eliminates the need to manually choose a drafting method. [R1, R4]
- **Interesting findings:** caption drafting often outperforms multimodal drafting; multimodal drafting degrades rapidly with more images; no single drafting method covers all tokens correctly predicted by others. [R1]
- **Among the first papers** to specifically study speculative decoding for MLLMs. [R2]
- **Clearly written and well-motivated:** the problem framing and central contributions are easy to follow. [R3, R4]
- **Extensive preliminary experiments** and strategy variation, though limited to one model. [R2, R4]

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty:** Prior work (e.g., Gagrani et al., 2024) already demonstrated speculative decoding for multimodal models and showed text-only drafting can achieve acceleration. The discoveries (e.g., pooling, ensemble) are largely seen in prior literature, and IbED is a reasonable but not particularly novel extension. [R2, R3, R4]
- **Missing output quality evaluation:** Only block efficiency is reported; the accuracy or quality of generated outputs is not evaluated. Table 12 shows very low accuracy with significant drops (e.g., from 79.5% → 35% on COCO Caption), and benchmarks like VQAv2 are simple, making the setting potentially favorable for SD. The paper does not verify whether the verification model accepts wrong tokens. [R2, R4]
- **Generalizability unclear:** All experiments use a single model (LLaVA 1.5); findings may not generalize to other MLLMs. Validation on at least two more models is needed. [R4]
- **Caption drafting introduces significant additional latency:** It uses Florence 2 Large FT (0.77B parameters, >10× the draft model size), which cannot be parallelized with the draft phase. The slight block efficiency improvements (e.g., +0.02 in the 1-image case for MTC over MT) may not be practical when captioning time is accounted. A metric incorporating total speedup (including captioning) would be more appropriate. [R3]
- **Multimodal drafting’s poor performance with 5 images may be due to model or size limitations:** LLaVA 1.5 may not natively support >5 images, and the draft model (68M) is smaller than the 115M model used in Gagrani et al., 2024. Ablations with larger draft models are needed. [R1, R3]
- **Presentation issues:** Section organization is confusing (Section 4 and 5 overlap in drafting method discussion); section titles are misleading (e.g., Section 5.1 discusses M vs. P, not the necessity of image modality; Section 5.2 covers caption drafting, but text-only drafting is also a non-image modality). [R1]
- **Overstated naming:** The paper uses “multimodal LLMs” but only addresses image-text models; “vision and language models” is more accurate. [R4]
- **Pooled drafting is not a distinct method:** It is a variant of multimodal drafting, common in MLLMs (see McKinzie et al., 2024). [R3]
- **Simple benchmarks:** Tasks like VQAv2 with short answers may be particularly favorable for speculative decoding; harder benchmarks like MMMU should be included. [R2, R3]
- **Unaddressed questions:** [R3, R4]
  - Given the complexity/latency of captioning, could an MTP ensemble or different pooling targets be used instead?
  - Can IbED generalize to other modalities (audio, video)?
- **Typos:** “mcuh less than” (line 458); consider “drafting strategies” instead of “draftings”. [R4]

**Note:** Reviewer 1 (score 5) is significantly more positive about the findings and IbED’s contribution than the other reviewers (scores 3, 3, 3). This minority view is reflected in the strengths but the majority concerns about novelty, generalizability, and missing evaluation are highlighted above.
