## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper reassesses layer pruning in LLMs through extensive experiments across multiple dimensions. Key findings: (1) reverse-order pruning (simply pruning the last 25% of layers) performs as well as or better than more sophisticated layer selection metrics; (2) partial-layer fine-tuning (freezing most layers and fine-tuning only the last few remaining layers plus the lm_head) outperforms LoRA-based fine-tuning; (3) one-shot pruning is more beneficial than iterative pruning. Following these insights, the authors prune Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct to create Llama-3.1-6.3B-It models that outperform several popular LLMs of similar size (e.g., ChatGLM2-6B, Vicuna-7B-v1.5, Qwen1.5-7B, Baichuan2-7B). The paper releases code and pruned model weights on Huggingface. Reviewer opinions are split: two reviewers rate the paper highly (6/6, citing thorough benchmarks and valuable insights), while two rate it low (3/3, citing limited novelty and insufficient generalization).

### Strengths
- The paper is well-structured and easy to follow, with rich background information (including math equations) that helps readers unfamiliar with the field.
- Extensive empirical study with diverse datasets and models (multiple LLMs around 7B parameters); results are presented in clear graphs and tables.
- An important and inspiring finding: fine-tuning only the last few remaining layers (e.g., 3 layers + lm_head) outperforms LoRA in post-pruning fine-tuning.
- The pruned Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct model shows competitive performance against other popular LLMs of similar or larger size.
- Release of model weights and code contributes to open science, facilitating reproducibility and further research.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and incremental insights**:  
  - The main findings confirm existing intuitions rather than introducing new methodologies. Pruning the last layers and fine-tuning specific parts aligns with established practices in model compression and transfer learning.  
  - Similar conclusions to Insights #1 and #3 have been noted in prior work: [1] (ShortGPT) shows deeper layers are less effective, and [2] shows iterative pruning provides no added benefit. The paper does not clearly differentiate itself from [1].  
  - The paper does not propose any new method; it only provides findings from an ablation study. Reverse-order pruning is presented as a discovery, not a novel technique.
- **Missing comparisons and insufficient evaluation on larger models**:  
  - The study compares simple pruning metrics only with Block Influence from ShortGPT [1]; it does not include more recent advanced methods such as SLEB [2] or FinerCut [3].  
  - The ablation studies (Tables 1,2,5,8) are conducted only on models with ~7B parameters or less. No experiments on larger models (e.g., LLaMA-2 30B, 70B, LLaMA-3 80B) are performed, limiting claims of generalizability. As model size increases, redundancy may change, making pruning on large models especially important.  
  - The proposed method is not tested on the OPT model, which (as noted in [3]) exhibits high redundancy in shallow layers rather than deeper layers, providing a different test case.
- **Lack of analysis and missing ablations**:  
  - The paper does not explain *why* reverse-order pruning outperforms other metrics, nor does it analyze the role and importance of the last layers in LLMs. This lack of mechanistic understanding raises concerns about generalizability and robustness.  
  - Results are shown only *after* fine-tuning; performance before fine-tuning is not provided, making it unclear whether the proposed pruning strategy consistently outperforms others prior to fine-tuning.  
  - The fine-tuning dataset is shown (Table 7) to be sensitive to model performance, yet the paper does not discuss the calibration dataset used for other pruning methods, potentially biasing results. Ablation on the number of SFT samples (or training samples) is missing.  
  - Some reviewers noted that the paper does not specify exact hyperparameters for all experiments, detailed fine-tuning configurations, or the procedures for selecting and processing calibration samples for data-driven pruning metrics.
- **Presentation details**:  
  - Some metrics used lack an indication of whether higher or lower values are better, making interpretation harder.
- **Additional questions from reviewers** (overlapping with missing experiments/comparisons):
  - How does the method perform on large LLMs like LLaMA-2 30B, 70B, LLaMA-3 80B?  
  - What is the generation speed of the pruned model compared to other models in Table 8?  
  - What are the exact experiment setups for other pruning methods, especially the number of calibration samples?  
  - Could the authors provide performance before fine-tuning and ablation on the number of training samples in SFT?
