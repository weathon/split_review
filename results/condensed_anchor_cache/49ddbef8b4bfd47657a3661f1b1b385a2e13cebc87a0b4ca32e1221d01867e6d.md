## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces DriveGPT4, a system that fine-tunes LLaMA 2 with a visual instruction tuning dataset derived from BDD-X using ChatGPT. It processes multi-frame video, text, and historical control signals to output both natural language explanations and low-level vehicle control signals. The model is compared with ADAPT on QA and action prediction metrics, and ablation studies examine the impact of historical control signals and out-of-domain data.

### Strengths
- Novel application of LLMs (LLaMA 2) to create an interpretable end-to-end driving system that handles multi-modal inputs (video, text, history) and outputs both textual responses and control signals.
- Creation of a visual instruction tuning dataset specifically for autonomous driving, based on BDD-X and enriched by ChatGPT, which provides a resource and baseline for future research.
- The system demonstrates zero-shot generalization capabilities to other datasets (e.g., video game scenes), mirroring the robustness observed in ChatGPT.
- The presentation is clear, well-organized, and easy to follow (noted by three reviewers).
- The model is compared against ADAPT on fixed QA, open-ended QA, and control prediction tasks, with quantitative results including a fixed QA score of 82.4 vs. 82.1 for ADAPT.
- (Minority positive: Reviewer 1, score 6, found the zero-shot generalization compelling and the baseline contribution strong.)

### Weaknesses
- **Control prediction relies on historical control signals, not LLM.** The model takes past control signals as input; the ablation study (“No control signals”) yields performance close to ADAPT-32, suggesting the improvement comes from history information rather than the LLM itself. The baselines were not given historical control signals, making the comparison unfair. A simple transformer with the same inputs should have been compared. Only speed and steering angle are predicted, not full end-to-end actions like lane changes or braking.
- **ChatGPT evaluation bias.** Training data was partially generated by ChatGPT, and evaluation uses ChatGPT to score responses. ChatGPT may exhibit style or position bias, favoring responses similar to its own generation. Standard metrics (e.g., BLEU, METEOR) show DriveGPT4 performs consistently worse than ADAPT, which undermines the claimed improvement.
- **Interpretability claim unsupported.** There is no explicit training constraint linking textual explanations to the predicted control actions, and no experiments validate that the explanations reflect the model’s actual reasoning. The paper does not provide quantitative or qualitative analysis of this connection.
- **Narrow evaluation scope.** Experiments are limited to the BDD-X dataset, which contains only salient, front-view images and does not represent real-world diversity (occlusions, varying lighting, non-critical elements). Evaluations on Waymo Open, NuScenes, or general multi-modal benchmarks are missing. The model’s ability to handle sensor modalities like LiDAR or radar is unknown.
- **Limited improvement and high cost.** On the fixed QA task, the improvement is marginal (82.1 → 82.4), and the LLM-based method incurs much longer inference time and higher computational cost. The added value of generating flexible, verbose answers over compact, fixed responses is not demonstrated.
- **Overclaims and misplaced motivation.** The title “DriveGPT4” implies a Level 4 solution, but the system only generates control signals, omitting perception, planning, and behavior. Several reviewers noted it is not the first interpretable end-to-end driving system, and the claim that interpretability is the key bottleneck for autonomous driving is not well-supported.
- **Technical contribution is modest.** The video tokenization follows Valley, and the action prediction component is similar to RT-2. The core contribution is the instruction dataset generation.
- **Behavior cloning limitations.** The model uses behavior cloning, which is known to suffer from distribution drift; this issue is not addressed.
- **Missing ablations and clarifications.** Questions from reviewers about the contribution of global features (F0^G ⊕ ...), why not fine-tune Video-LLaMA directly, the use of YOLOv8 vs. open-set detectors, and the lack of a clear summary table for training stages, tasks, and frozen/trainable parts remain unanswered. The paper does not evaluate on the DriveLLM dataset to compare high-level planning decisions.
- **Minority negative perspective.** (Reviewer 3, score 3, confidence 5) strongly questioned the control prediction (may simply extrapolate history) and interpretability claims, and noted the ChatGPT evaluation bias and inferior standard metrics.
