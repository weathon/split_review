## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes detecting adversarial attacks on vision systems (object detection, instance segmentation) by measuring inconsistency between outputs of multiple tasks via a consistency score. Evaluated on BDD100k against PGD attacks, the method shows high detection accuracy. One reviewer (score 6) was significantly more positive than the other three (score 3 each); all points from that reviewer are preserved.

### Strengths
- Novelty: leveraging multi-task perception for adversarial detection, differing from single-task or attack-specific defenses.
- Practical potential: efficient detection mechanism suitable for resource-constrained autonomous driving applications.
- Clear presentation: writing is easy to follow, method and motivation well-explained.
- Well-justified consistency score with a preliminary study.
- Logical technical choice: multi-task consistency for driving is reasonable.
- Authors discuss limitations and reasons for using different backbones.
- Extendable idea: could apply to other multi-task learning scenarios.

### Weaknesses
- Insufficient attack evaluation: only PGD attacks considered. Need evaluation against:
  - Transferable attacks
  - Attacks focusing on a single object (important for autonomous driving)
  - Sensor data spoofing (e.g., LiDAR spoofing)
  - Physical-realizable patch attacks
  - Data poisoning/backdoor attacks
- Absence of adaptive/defense-aware attacks: a worst-case attack could incorporate the consistency score into adversarial optimization to deceive both task heads and the consistency metric (e.g., see Sobh et al., arXiv:2107.07449).
- Threat model too restricted: white-box assumption with full model knowledge may be unrealistic. Black-box or limited-knowledge attacks (e.g., blackbox LiDAR or camera spoofing) not considered.
- Limited comparison with prior defense methods. Only compared with RobustDet (on different datasets) and adversarial training (different threat model). Need comparisons with preprocessing methods or other attack detection approaches on standard datasets.
- Claim of handling local adversarial attacks not experimentally validated.
- Evaluation focus on attack performance rather than defense performance. Should include more defense-centric studies: various attacks, comparison with prior defenses, ablation studies.
- Computational cost of running multiple models with varied backbones insufficiently discussed; raises concerns about real-world applicability in autonomous vehicles.
- The comparison in Table 4 appears inequitable: the proposed method requires querying a stronger model when reliable detection is needed, yet is compared as if only two weak models.
- Significance of releasing an adversarial dataset (PGP on BDD100k) is questionable; it merely applies an existing attack.
- Only object detection and instance segmentation considered. Applicability to other tasks (depth estimation, lane detection) is not discussed; need comment on challenges for such tasks.

These weaknesses reflect concerns from multiple reviewers, including the more positive reviewer (#1) who also questioned the threat model and adaptive attacks.
