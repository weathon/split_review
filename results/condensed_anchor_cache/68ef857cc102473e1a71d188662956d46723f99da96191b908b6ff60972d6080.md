## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes xMLP, a DNN architecture using only square activations (\(x^2\)) for efficient private inference (PI). It argues that replacing ReLU with \(x^2\) reduces PI latency because square activations are amenable to Beaver Triples, but accuracy drops in CNNs due to an “information compounding” effect. Using an MLP-mixer-style backbone, the authors claim that xMLP matches ReLU-based models in accuracy and efficiency, achieving a 0.58% accuracy improvement over prior PI models with 7× speedup, and up to 700× faster GPU offloading.

### Strengths
1. **Important and practical problem.** The paper targets accelerating private inference by replacing expensive ReLU activations with more MPC-friendly square activations. (R3, R4)  
2. **Novel architecture choice.** Using an MLP-mixer-like architecture to make square activations effective is novel, and the analysis of why this architecture avoids the accuracy drop seen in CNNs is sound. (R2)  
3. **Informative ablation studies.** Comprehensive ablation experiments (e.g., Table 5) provide useful insights into the effects of different normalizations (BN vs LN) and the interplay between ReLU and \(x^2\) on PI performance. (R1, R2)  
4. **Competitive results on standard datasets.** On CIFAR-100 and ImageNet, xMLP outperforms ResNet models with fewer activations and parameters and maintains accuracy comparable to ReLU-based variants. (R4)  
5. **Strong PI performance gains.** Compared to prior PI methods, xMLP shows a 0.58% accuracy improvement with 7× faster PI speed; when offloaded to GPU, it is up to 700× faster than the previous state-of-the-art PI model with comparable accuracy. (R4)  
6. **Thorough comparison of activations.** The paper includes a comprehensive comparison of ReLU vs. \(x^2\) on ImageNet. (R2)

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of novelty and insufficient related work.**  
   - The core insight that ReLU induces sparsity and square activations avoid it is well-known (e.g., Zhao et al. ICPR 2018, Serra et al. ICML 2018). (R2, R3)  
   - The claim of being the first to replace all ReLUs with polynomials is false; prior works (Lee et al. IEEE Access 2023, Chrysos et al. CVPR 2023, Xu et al. MLSys 2022, and AESPA arXiv:2201.06699) have done so, some on ImageNet. (R1, R2)  
   - The contributions are marginal, showing improvement only on MLP-mixer-type models, not on CNNs (Table 4 shows poor results on ResNets). (R1)

2. **Missing comparisons with state-of-the-art PI methods.**  
   - The paper does not compare with recent PI-specific architectures such as SENets (Kundu et al. ICLR 2023), DeepReShape, Making Models Shallow Again, MPCViT, SAL-ViT, and PriViT. This undermines the claim of pushing SOTA. (R2, R3)  
   - Comparison with prior polynomial-replacement works (e.g., AESPA) is omitted. (R1)

3. **Missing implementation details and ambiguity.**  
   - The coefficient of the square function is not specified. (R1)  
   - Offline-phase computation and communication overhead for Beaver Triples are not quantified: how many triples are needed, how they are distributed, simulation environment and time. (R1, R4)  
   - Timing analysis in Section 4.3.2 is ambiguous: the paper mentions using a 2PC protocol but includes CrypTen (which uses 3PC with a trusted third party), mixing benchmarks. (R2)  
   - It is unclear if knowledge distillation or fine-tuning was used for the reported results (e.g., Figure 1). (R2)  
   - Runtime for depth-wise convolution is not evaluated. (R2)  
   - The paper claims GPU offloading for PI is unavailable in prior work, but GPU-based secure computation platforms exist (Orca, Piranha, CryptGPU). (R2)

4. **FLOPs overhead and offline-cost realism.**  
   - FLOPs are excluded when comparing CNN-based PI in Table 2, but they contribute to offline latency. The assumption that all FLOPs can be processed offline only holds for isolated single inference requests; in real-world scenarios with non-zero arrival rates, offline costs affect real-time performance (Garimella et al. ASPLOS 2023). The proposed architecture’s high FLOPs may exacerbate this issue. (R2, R4)  
   - Linear operations (including transpose rotations in HE) add significant offline cost and communication overhead. (R4)

5. **Incomplete activation comparison in Table 4.** The table omits \(\text{ReLU}(x)^2\), making it unclear whether it is better or worse than \(\text{ReLU}(x)\), despite claims in Section 3.1. (R3)

6. **Superficial analysis of the core phenomenon.** The discussion of sparsity and information compounding in Section 3.1 lacks new depth and is already covered in prior works. (R2, R3)
