## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a boundary-based watermarking method for protecting DNNs against model extraction attacks. It selects trigger samples on the base model's decision boundary, assigns them labels with the least probabilities, and provides a tight bound via the Lovász Local Lemma to improve the distinguishability between benign and stolen models. Experiments on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet with ResNet34 and VGG11 show improved trigger set accuracy and p-values over SOTA methods.

### Strengths
- Novel boundary-based trigger selection strategy that optimizes distinguishability between benign and stolen models, addressing limitations of randomly selected trigger sets.
- Theoretical analysis proving guarantees under Lovász Local Lemma constraints on watermark-related parameters, adding rigor to the approach.
- Strong empirical results on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet, demonstrating state-of-the-art trigger accuracy and p-values.
- Ablations showing the effectiveness of the proposed trigger selection and labeling approach.
- The paper is well-written, well-organized, and easy to follow, with good coverage of related work.
- Model extraction is a relevant and practical issue for DNNs in production.
- The use of perturbed models for trigger set generation and certification is known to be effective and the paper provides some experimental results on the fingerprinting task.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and missing comparisons**: The idea of using perturbed models for trigger set generation and certification is not new, which notably limits the novelty. The paper lists several other watermarking approaches but does not compare with them (e.g., Mikhail Pautov et al., "Probabilistically robust watermarking of neural networks", IJCAI-2024). The paper has high similarity with previously published work and no comparison with it; one reviewer argues the experimental results are notably below the SOTA.  
- **Performance degradation on simple datasets**: The method leads to notable degradation of the fingerprinted model's performance even on simple datasets (CIFAR-10/100), making feasibility questionable.  
- **Limited evaluation scope and scalability**:  
  * Tested only on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet with ResNet34 and VGG11; lacks evaluation on larger datasets (e.g., LAION, ImageNet-21K) and production models (e.g., CLIP, SAM, VIT-high, SigCLIP).  
  * Tested only against distillation-based attacks; only models of the same architecture are included in the perturbed set. Doubts remain about efficiency against other extraction attacks and adaptive adversaries who could circumvent boundary-based triggers.  
  * No exploration with larger networks and other architectures (ResNet-101, ResNet-152, DenseNet, ConvNeXt, MobileNetV2, VGG), raising questions about scalability.  
- **Theoretical guarantee practicality**: The approach requires achieving the Lovász Local Lemma parameter constraints (e.g., setting α values), but it is unclear how difficult this is in practice. The paper lacks a clear methodology for determining the critical α value or how it changes with different architectures/datasets. No guidance is provided on techniques to optimize α for larger models.  
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and analysis**: Sensitivity to s (number of perturbed models), δ (perturbation range), and boundary thresholds a and b is not deeply explored. More ablation studies are needed to understand the effect of these parameters.  
- **Computational cost and real-world deployment**: The process involves multiple perturbations, decision boundary identification, and label selection, which may introduce significant computational overhead. The paper does not compare computational cost with other watermarking techniques, nor analyzes the cost of generating perturbed models and selecting boundary samples. Resources may limit real-world deployment.  
- **Missing methodological details**: The paper does not provide a clear algorithm for boundary sample selection, especially when multiple samples fall within defined thresholds or have the same/very similar low probabilities. It is unclear how labels are assigned in such cases. This lack of clarity hinders reproducibility.  
- **No false positive rate study**: Crucially, the paper provides no study or results about false positive detection of benign models. If a non-fingerprinted model is often detected as fingerprinted, it indicates an inappropriate choice of the trigger set. One reviewer asks how one can guarantee the method does not falsely detect a non-fingerprinted model as fingerprinted.  
- **No code is provided**.  
- One reviewer (score 1) is significantly more critical, questioning the novelty, SOTA performance, and feasibility due to performance degradation, while other reviewers (scores 5 and 6) find the approach valuable but note similar limitations.
