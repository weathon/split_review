## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a framework to inform denoising diffusion models of physical constraints (PDEs, boundary conditions) during training by introducing a virtual observable (Rixner & Koutsourelakis, 2021). A residual term \(R(x) = \mathcal{F}(x)\) is added as a regularization objective, modelling \(p(r \mid x) = \mathcal{N}(r; R(x), \sigma^2 I)\) and maximizing the expected log-likelihood of \(r=0\). The residual is evaluated on \(x_0^*\), obtained via one-step score-based mean estimation or two-step DDIM sampling, avoiding full sampling from the model. Experiments on Darcy flow and topology optimization show improved PDE residual satisfaction compared to baselines like PG-Diffusion (Shu et al., 2023) and CoCoGen (Jacobsen et al., 2023), but suboptimal performance on other metrics.

### Strengths
- **Original approach**: Incorporates virtual observables into the score-matching denoising diffusion training objective to embed physical constraints explicitly, contrasting with prior methods that use auxiliary models or post-training adjustments (PG-Diffusion, CoCoGen).
- **Explicit PDE residual optimization**: Introduces PDE residuals as virtual observables and directly optimizes on the physical constraints, rather than projecting residuals into a latent space.
- **Theoretical grounding**: Provides coherent theoretical arguments for the adaptation (Appendices A.1, A.3), including a justification for ignoring the likelihood ratio.
- **Clear experimental setup**: Follows configurations from established related work (Appendices A.6.2, A.6.3) on benchmark tasks (Darcy flow, topology optimization).
- **Demonstrated effectiveness**: Outperforms state-of-the-art methods in satisfying physical constraints (PDE residual reduction), and the added objective acts as a regularizer against overfitting without compromising data likelihood.
- **Interesting combination**: The setting of combining a data-based diffusion loss with a data-free regularization loss is a worthwhile question (Reviewer 4, more positive on this aspect).
- **Potential for impact**: Could enhance generative modeling for scientific applications where physical consistency is essential.

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental contribution**: The approach is substantially grounded in prior work on virtual observables (Rixner & Koutsourelakis, 2021). Should be explicitly framed as an adaptation of that framework to denoising diffusion models. Adding a physical residual term to the loss is straightforward and offers limited innovation.
- **Overlooked prior work**: The core challenge—sampling from an unnormalized target distribution via diffusion models—has extensive prior literature not cited (e.g., Sanokowski et al. [4], references [1]–[3], [5]). The paper’s loss is a special case of Eq. 6 in Sanokowski et al. [4] with temperature \(0\); that work emphasizes that annealing is crucial to avoid local minima (Fig. 2, middle). The “Noise Distribution” (Eq. 12) is essentially the “Annealed Noise Distribution” from Sec. 3.2 of [4]. Also not cited is [5] on training neural networks with PINN losses in a generative manner without data. Proper citation and studying annealing-based losses would strengthen the work.
- **Writing clarity**: The paper is not self-contained. Key quantities (\(R(x) = \mathcal{F}(x)\), the differential operator \(\mathcal{F}\)) are not explicitly defined; the likelihood function \(p(r \mid x)\) lacks detail on how residual \(r\) is obtained and its dimensionality. Notation is inconsistent (e.g., \(x\) in Sec. 2.1 vs. \(\xi\) in Eqs. 5 and 6). The abstract is vague, making it hard to grasp contributions at a glance.
- **Experimental evaluation limited**: Only two benchmark tasks (Darcy flow, topology optimization) are shown; the proposed method underperforms on other metrics (e.g., data likelihood, structural metrics). More comprehensive evaluation is needed.
- **Missing ablations and clarifications**:
  - Effect of sampling step in DDIM: How does performance change with more steps? (Reviewer 3)
  - Effect of scaling \(c\) in the loss function: No experiments with different scaling values. (Reviewer 3)
  - Rationale for \(x_0^*\): Why not sample \(X_0\) directly? What advantage does the one-step/two-step estimate provide? No ablation on this choice is provided. (Reviewer 4)
  - Performance without the data-based loss term: How does the method behave when only the physics loss is used? (Reviewer 4)
  - For topology optimization, what advantage over traditional methods (SIMP)? Generating a distribution is not obviously necessary; can the method uniquely provide displacement fields? (Reviewer 3)
- **Minority critical view**: One reviewer (score 5) finds the core contribution insufficiently novel and the experimental evaluation limited; the other reviewers (all score 6) recognize the potential but share concerns about clarity and incremental nature. This contrast should be considered when addressing weaknesses.
