## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GTMGC, a Graph-Transformer model for end-to-end prediction of a molecule’s ground-state 3D conformation from its 2D graph. It introduces a novel self-attention mechanism (MSRSA) that incorporates adjacency and interatomic distance matrices as biases, and uses MoleBERT for initial embedding and Laplacian positional encoding. An iterative refinement of a cached conformer in the decoder and a regularization loss on the predicted distance matrix are also contributions. Evaluated on Molecule3D and QM9, the model is reported to outperform previous baselines and RDKit.

### Strengths
- The architecture extends classical attention to 3D molecular graphs in an elegant way with minimal complexity, making it straightforward to adapt existing transformer intuitions.
- The iterative refinement of the cached conformer in the decoder is a notable innovation that improves performance.
- Ablation studies clarify the contribution of each component.
- The paper is very clear, well‑written, and pleasant to read.
- The integration of existing ideas (adjacency and distance biases, weighted sum) for the specific task of ground‑state prediction is seen as original to some extent.
- The reported performance against baselines is significant and demonstrates effectiveness for accelerating ground‑state conformation computation, which is useful for chemistry and drug discovery.
- One reviewer found the model novel in capturing spatial structures and well‑executed; another noted the work serves as a foundational step toward full conformer generation.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and overclaim:**  
  Many prior Transformer works already use the adjacency matrix, the distance matrix (e.g., [2] cited in the manuscript), and weighted summation of attention biases; the contribution is mostly an integration of existing methods. One reviewer (score 3) considered the novelty limited, while the other two reviewers (scores 8) found the architecture novel and elegant.
- **MoleBERT tokenizer and featurization:**  
  The assertion that the MoleBERT tokenizer is innovative is overstated. Results in Table 2 show only a shallow advantage (or even slightly worse) compared to standard atomic encoding. A thorough comparison with other featurizations (e.g., MolGPS, learned embeddings, physicochemical properties) is missing.
- **Simple Transformer baseline already strong:**  
  The ablation study (Table 3) shows that a simple Transformer without any additions (no adjacency, no distance biases, no regularization) achieves an MAE of 0.4395, which already outperforms *all* baselines cited in the paper. The final improvement of the full GTMGC over this bare Transformer is only about 1%. This raises the question of whether the good performance comes almost solely from a large, powerful standard Transformer (the model appears much larger than other methods).
- **Ablation methodology concern:**  
  Table 3 appears to report the lowest MAE for each metric from different ablated models, rather than evaluating a single consistent model choice—this is incorrect.
- **Missing comparisons:**  
  - No comparison with generative molecular conformation methods (e.g., Torsional Diffusion [NeurIPS 2022]) that could be retrained to produce a single conformation.  
  - No comparison with other Transformer‑based methods for molecules, such as Relative Molecule Self‑Attention Transformer, 3DTransformer, or Geometric Transformer.  
  - The property prediction results are relegated to the appendix and do not outperform state‑of‑the‑art across the board. It is unclear whether these tasks were tackled as single‑task or multi‑task.  
  - Scalability across different graph sizes is not assessed.
- **Missing ablations and analysis:**  
  Ablation of the Laplacian positional encoding is not reported. The learned weighting parameters (γ) that combine the attention contributions should be examined to understand the role of each component.
- **Paper structure:**  
  The introduction and preliminary sections (multi‑head attention, transformer basics) are too lengthy and could be shortened or moved to an appendix. The related work section is in the appendix and should be in the main text, where it would allow a detailed discussion of why previous graph transformers are ill‑suited for the task.
