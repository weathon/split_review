## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes CtrlA, an adaptive RAG framework that extracts honesty and confidence features from LLM representations (via contrastive prompts, PCA, and linear combination) to steer generation and trigger retrieval, aiming to solve when and what to retrieve dynamically. Experiments on short-form and long-form QA tasks show it outperforms existing adaptive RAG methods like FLARE and DRAGIN. The paper is well-written and includes exquisite figures. A minority reviewer (score 3) raised significant concerns about assumptions, fairness, and missing baselines, while the three majority reviewers (scores 5) found the method interesting and effective but noted several technical and clarity issues.

### Strengths
- Introduces a novel representation-based method for adaptive retrieval focusing on honesty and confidence, departing from conventional uncertainty-based approaches.
- The empirical evaluation is thorough: multiple LLMs evaluated across major benchmarks (short-form and long-form generation), providing good reference points for future work.
- Outperforms well-known baselines such as FLARE and DRAGIN in both retrieval timing and content accuracy.
- Optimizes retrieval timing, resulting in fewer unnecessary retrievals.
- The framework is lightweight and does not heavily depend on external fine-tuning, potentially applicable across various LLMs.
- Interpretable approach analyzing internal mechanisms (honesty, confidence), offering clearer insights into model behavior.
- The research question is timely; adaptive retrieval for modern LLMs remains an open problem.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with exquisite figures.

### Weaknesses
- The core contribution is misaligned with the motivation: honesty steering and query formulation aim to improve RAG response quality rather than solving the adaptive retrieval trigger problem. The retrieval triggering criterion remains fundamentally uncertainty-based (multiple reviewers).
- The proposed method may fail on the motivating example where the model confidently says "I don’t know", because it still relies on confidence monitoring (reviewer 4).
- The linear representation assumption (honesty and confidence features linearly separable) is oversimplified for modern LLMs, referencing Templeton et al. (2024) which is based on early RNN-era findings. A minority reviewer (score 3) was particularly concerned, also questioning independence in complex contexts and performance if features are entangled. Reviewer 1 raised the same issue regarding strong assumptions.
- The method requires access to internal LLM structure, limiting applicability to commercial models like o1. Heavy reliance on PCA and layer-wise feature extraction may cause high computational costs; principal component selection (first PC may not represent desired direction) is not discussed. No token expenses reported (reviewers 1, 2).
- Query formulation strategies lack clarity for handling ambiguous/noisy outputs. Masking unconfident tokens is insufficiently detailed, risking loss of important context and affecting retrieval accuracy (reviewer 2).
- Modifying representation space can harm generation quality: Figure 3b shows a case where the answer is correct but the sentence is not fluent (reviewer 2).
- Algorithm 1 is incomplete: an outer loop that takes the query and document as input and outputs the final response is missing; the current algorithm only outputs a single segment (reviewer 4).
- Missing related work: the paper fails to cite knowledge conflict literature (Xie et al. 2023, Jin et al. 2024) both for introduction and as baselines. Section 3 (3.1, 3.2.4) should cite FLARE and decoding-stage feature monitoring works (reviewers 1, 4).
- Experimental setup is unclear: definition of "segments" is not explained. Ablation studies do not isolate the effect of confidence monitoring on adaptive retrieval improvements. A full ablation with one technique ablated at a time is missing (reviewers 3, 4).
- Inconsistent results: Figures 2 and 3 show different effects of λ on accuracy across tasks, but no discussion is provided beyond dataset category (reviewer 2).
- Hyperparameter determination (retrieval threshold, λ) is unclear: are they tuned on in-domain validation sets and kept constant across test sets? (reviewer 4).
- Fairness concern: feature extraction relies on the model's own internal knowledge, which may embed biases from training data, potentially affecting certain groups or topics disproportionately (reviewer 1, minority).
- Multi-step pipeline may increase computational burden; overall complexity is not discussed (reviewer 1).
