## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a probabilistic framework for domain generalization (DG) using information-theoretic bounds that relate generalization error to mutual information between domain index and inputs/representations (Theorems 3, 5). Based on these bounds, the authors introduce Inter-domain Distribution Matching (IDM), which simultaneously aligns inter-domain gradients and representations via per-dimension sorting and matching, and Per-sample Distribution Matching (PDM). Experiments on DomainBed (ColoredMNIST, DomainNet, PACS, OfficeHome, TerraIncognita) compare against baselines. The theory separates training vs. test-domain population risk but assumes infinite samples per domain; the algorithm is justified by the analysis but the connection is debated. Model selection using test-domain validation yields stronger results than training-domain selection.

### Strengths
- The paper is well written with clearly listed assumptions (R1, R3, R4).
- The information-theoretic analysis is sound and inspiring, separating \(L_{tr}\) and \(L_{te}\) using \(L(W)\) and providing interpretable bounds that align with previous empirical findings (R2, R4).
- The probabilistic DG formulation (random testing domain) covers more general cases than worst-case optimization (R2).
- The Per-sample Distribution Matching (PDM) idea and its connection to slicing is original and cute (R4).
- The proposed algorithm is theoretically justified and works well on multiple datasets (R2, R4). Authors are transparent about hyperparameter selection and discuss failures (e.g., TerraIncognita) (R1).
- Extensive experimental results with thorough analysis (R2). Note: R1 and R3 found the empirical performance unexceptional or only strong on synthetic data.

### Weaknesses
- **Theory–algorithm gap**: The information-theoretic bounds (involving \(I(W,D), I(Z,D)\)) do not directly justify the specific gradient- and representation-matching choices; IRM would more directly minimize \(I(W,D)\). The encoder–classifier distinction is arbitrary for deep nets (R1). Minimizing only \(I(R;D)\) may be insufficient; one should also minimize \(I(Y;D|R)\) (related to invariant risk minimization or sufficiency) (R4). Assumption 1 (“target domains independent of source domains”) is not well-defined—sets of distributions are not random variables (R3).
- **Algorithmic novelty and comparisons**: The algorithm is a combination of existing gradient matching (similar to Fishr) and per-dimension representation matching (somewhat new but general idea is not novel) (R1, R3). The method aligns marginal distributions per dimension assuming independence between dimensions, which is unrealistic; it is unclear if this suffices for joint alignment (R3). Why gradient alignment is needed when representations are already aligned is not explained mechanistically (R3). An important baseline, SWAD, is missing from Table 2 (R3).
- **Empirical performance concerns**:  
  - IDM shows the largest improvement on CMNIST (a half-synthetic dataset with manual concept shift), which weakens demonstration on real-world covariate-shift datasets (R3).  
  - Average accuracies across methods in Table 2 are not substantially different (except CMNIST); practical significance is limited (R2).  
  - When model selection is done on training domains (Table 10), IDM does not outperform baselines (R1, R2). This is partially defended by authors (R1) but still raises concerns about utility.  
  - Ablation study (Table 6) shows that removing gradient alignment (GA) improves performance on OfficeHome, questioning its necessity (R1, R3). More ablations on realistic datasets are needed (R1).  
- **Scope and assumptions**: The framework assumes infinite samples per domain (only domain generalization gap considered), ignoring finite-sample generalization error—this should be acknowledged (R4). Theorem 7’s assumption seldom holds in practice (R4).  
- **Missing baselines, tasks, and datasets**: No results on WILDS, non-vision tasks (R2). Missing baseline SWAD (R3).  
- **Writing and presentation issues**: Algorithm implementation details should be in the main body; subscript “ma” in \(X_{ma}\) (Algorithm 1) is ambiguous (R3). Equation (4) has a typo: “when I(Y;D|X)” should be “when I(Y;D|X)=0” (R4).  
- **Disagreement among reviewers**: R3 is strongly negative (score 1, after rebuttal lowered from 3) citing fundamental concerns with assumptions, algorithm justification, and empirical validation. R1 (score 5) found novelty lacking and performance only marginal. R2 (score 8) and R4 (score 6) were more positive on theory and algorithm but acknowledged limitations in empirical gains and scope.  
- **Unaddressed or underdiscussed connections**: The information-theoretic bound (Theorem 2) resembles multi-domain versions of standard bounds (Bu et al. 2020); connections to meta-learning generalization bounds (Jose et al. 2021, Chen et al. 2021, Hellström et al. 2022) should be discussed (R4).  
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms**:  
  - Are the bounds vacuous for deep neural networks? (R1)  
  - How is warm-up done when gradient matching is removed (Table 6)? More ablations on realistic datasets needed. (R1)  
  - Can relative improvement over ERM or worst-case accuracy show clearer differences than averages? (R2)  
  - Do the authors have any ideas for a decent model selection strategy for IDM? (R2)  
  - Plans to share code? (R2)  
  - Why does per-dimension sorting assume dimension independence? Is marginal alignment sufficient for joint alignment? (R3)  
  - How does the proposed gradient alignment differ from Fishr specifically? (R3)  
  - Is gradient alignment truly needed given ablation results? (R3)  
  - Why minimize only \(I(R;D)\) rather than also \(I(Y;D|R)\)? (R4)
