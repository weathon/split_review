## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies an important limitation of Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) for distribution closeness testing (DCT): the same MMD value can arise for distribution pairs with different norms in the reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS), making it less informative. To overcome this, the authors propose a norm-adaptive MMD (NAMMD) that scales MMD by the RKHS norms of the distributions. Theoretically, they prove that NAMMD achieves higher test power than the standard MMD test, provide asymptotic distribution analysis, upper bounds on sample complexity, and guarantee Type-I error control. Experiments on five datasets (synthetic and real-world) show that NAMMD outperforms both MMD and Canonne’s tests.

### Strengths
*   **Important issue identified**: MMD can be identical for distribution pairs with different RKHS norms, a fundamental problem for closeness testing.
*   **Simple and effective method**: NAMMD is a straightforward scaling of MMD that directly addresses the identified issue.
*   **Rigorous theoretical analysis**: Includes proofs of higher test power, asymptotic distribution, sample complexity bounds, and Type-I error control.
*   **Solid numerical evaluation**: Experiments on five datasets demonstrate superiority over MMD and Canonne’s tests.
*   **Well-written and comprehensive**: The background and technical assumptions are clearly explained, with explicit proofs and justification.
*   **Convinces reader**: The theoretical and empirical evidence together make NAMMD a reasonable framework (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
*   **Missing connection to kernel tuning literature** (Reviewer 1): The paper does not compare NAMMD against existing methods that adapt kernel hyperparameters (e.g., Gaussian length-scale γ) to mitigate the same MMD issue, such as MMD-FUSE [1], MMD Aggregated Test [2], and Efficient Aggregated Kernel Tests [3]. Specifically:
    - Can the problem with MMD be reduced by manually adjusting kernel hyperparameters? How does NAMMD compare to such approaches in terms of strengths and weaknesses?
    - Does the same issue occur for unbounded kernels (e.g., polynomial kernels on bounded data) or kernels with a positive limit at infinity (e.g., a truncated Gaussian with constant c for large distances)? The paper only considers kernels that vanish at infinity.
*   **Lack of real-world high-dimensional demonstration** (Reviewer 2): The paper claims NAMMD works on complex high-dimensional data (e.g., images), but the experiments do not include an application on actual high-dimensional images that demonstrates a meaningful improvement in practice.
*   **Insufficient theoretical justification of empirical threshold estimation under the “ϵ-close” case** (Reviewer 3): When NAMMD(P,Q)=ϵ, the threshold is estimated using the variance of the asymptotic distribution, approximated by an empirical variance estimator \(\sigma_{X,Y}\). No analysis of the bias, variance, or consistency of this estimator is provided to justify the approximation.
*   **Unclear motivation for NAMMD definition and alternative designs** (Reviewer 4):
    - The paper does not explain why NAMMD is defined as Equation (1) and why this particular form is sufficient for the goal. After introducing NAMMD, no justification is given for its design.
    - It is not explained why the authors did not instead redesign a kernel-based closeness testing method that directly circumvents the inherent MMD problem, rather than scaling MMD.
*   **Minor issue** (Reviewer 1): Line 475–476: The notation \(\|z_0, z_1\|\) should likely be \(\|z_0 - z_1\|\) (missing minus sign).

**Reviewer disagreement**: Two reviewers (R1 and R4) assigned scores of 8 and are highly positive about the theoretical rigor and problem identification. Two reviewers (R2 and R3) assigned scores of 6, expressing concerns about missing real-world high-dimensional validation and insufficient theoretical support for the threshold estimator, respectively.
