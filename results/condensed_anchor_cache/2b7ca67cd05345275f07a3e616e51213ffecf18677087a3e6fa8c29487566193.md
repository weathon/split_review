## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses spatially guided text-to-motion synthesis. It proposes Cycle Consistent Diffusion, which improves controllable generation by optimizing frame-level cycle consistency between generated motions and conditional controls (spatial constraints). A test-time optimization framework directs the pre-trained cycle-consistent model toward user-defined sparse constraints. The authors also introduce a probability regularization for the latent noise of the diffusion model to mitigate reward hacking. Experiments on the HumanML3D dataset report ~5–10% improvement in controllability metrics and reduced foot skating compared to Omnicontrol and DNO.

### Strengths
1. The cycle consistency mechanism is new in the motion community and provides a novel perspective for controllable generative models (Reviewers 1, 3).
2. The test-time optimization framework is effectively motivated and includes a novel loss to address reward hacking (Reviewers 1, 2).
3. The paper describes the application of chained motion generation in 3D scenes in detail, enabling diverse coherent movements with practical applicability for HSI (Reviewers 1, 3).
4. Quantitative results on HumanML3D show clear improvement over SOTA methods (Omnicontrol, DNO) across multiple metrics (Reviewers 1, 2).
5. The literature review in the related works section is thorough (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited technical novelty.** The spatial-conditioned diffusion model is straightforward and seen in previous work (e.g., Omnicontrol). The cycle consistency training closely resembles methods from image generation (ControlNet++) without specific motion-domain insight. Optimizing diffusion noise has also been explored (ODN, GMD). The modifications appear incremental (Reviewer 1).
2. **Poor writing and presentation.** The method section contains typos, mismatched symbols, and wrong hyperlinks (e.g., eq7 has an extra right parenthesis; Line 320’s “Fig. 3.4” links to subsection 3.4 instead of a figure). Figures 2 and 3 are very similar and not informative, making the method difficult to understand (Reviewers 2, 4).
3. **Ablation study fails to prove effectiveness.** The full model does not achieve optimal performance across all metrics; removing cycle consistency or other components often yields better R-Precision, diversity, and foot-skate scores (Tables 1, 2). This raises concerns that performance improvements may stem from parameter tuning rather than the proposed components (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). For example, in Table 2, the w/o cycle model is less than 1 percentage point lower on Loc. Error and Avg Err but significantly outperforms the full model on R-Precision, diversity, and foot-skate (Reviewer 3).
4. **Insufficient experimental evaluation.** Experiments are conducted on only one dataset (HumanML3D), lacking cross-dataset validation (e.g., KIT-ML). Only two baseline methods are compared (Omnicontrol, DNO), which is inadequate compared to comprehensive evaluations like Omnicontrol’s. The experiment section is only two pages long. Overall motion quality metrics (e.g., FID) are missing. The paper also lacks analysis of failed cases and insightful observations (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
5. **Qualitative results are not comprehensive.** The provided images do not clearly show improvement beyond baselines (DNO, Omnicontrol). Only limited video results are available for evaluating motion quality. More visual comparisons with SOTA methods are needed (Reviewers 1, 2).
6. **Unsubstantiated claims about computational overhead.** The paper states that cycle consistency loss introduces substantial GPU burden and mitigates this with localized noise addition and single-step sampling, but it does not demonstrate the effect of these mitigations, whether motion quality is preserved, nor report exact training and inference times (Reviewer 3).
7. **Data mislabeling in tables.** In Table 1, DNO’s Trajectory Error and Location Error for “Fight Foot” are lower than HuMouS’s but the best results are not bolded, and there are other formatting issues (Reviewer 3).
8. **Lack of clarity on contributions and mechanisms.** The authors should clarify how their contributions differ from Omnicontrol, GMD, and ODN. The interplay between spatial constraints and cycle consistency across different cycles is not explained. Strategies for maintaining temporal dependencies during optimization and robustness in complex real-world environments are missing (Reviewers 1, 2).
9. **Missing analysis of key design choices.** The effects of Probability Regularization and different DDIM inversion initializations are not analyzed (e.g., using a 2D t-SNE map) to demonstrate their necessity (Reviewer 1).
