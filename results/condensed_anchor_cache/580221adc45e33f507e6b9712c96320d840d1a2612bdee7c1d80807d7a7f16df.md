## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MuHBoost, a multi-label boosting method that combines LLM prompting (building on SummaryBoost) with multi-label classification (LP and CC variants) to jointly predict multiple health/well-being outcomes from longitudinal ubiquitous health data. MuHBoost is designed to handle heterogeneous feature types (continuous measurements, categorical responses) and missing values while being resource-efficient. Two enhanced variants address LLM hallucinations. Experiments on 13 tasks from 4 datasets (LifeSnaps, GLOBEM, etc.) compare against multiple baselines using standard MLC metrics. Reviewers generally found the application important and the method effective, but raised concerns about novelty, limited background, lack of statistical significance, narrow evaluation (binary-only labels, low-dimensional data), and insufficient computational analysis.

### Strengths
- Important application: longitudinal human behavior modeling for patient monitoring, drug user intervention, student performance, etc. (Reviewers 1,4).
- MuHBoost is a resource-efficient multi-label classification method that jointly predicts multiple health outcomes with reduced computing time/cost (Reviewers 1,4).
- The method handles heterogeneous data formats (time series + ancillary tabular data) and missing values, addressing realistic aspects of ubiquitous health data (Reviewer 1).
- Comparative analyses show MuHBoost generally outperforms a diverse set of baselines (Random Forest, XGBoost, MLkNN, MLTSVM, etc.) across multiple standard MLC metrics, evaluated on 13 prediction tasks from 4 datasets (Reviewers 1,4).
- Multiple robustness checks are performed, including on SummaryBoost’s extract-then-refine procedure and comparing GPT-3.5 vs GPT-4 (Reviewer 4).
- The enhancement for small sample size problems (N < 2^Q) via the two MuHBoost variants is helpful (Reviewer 2).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow, even for readers outside the area (Reviewer 2).
- Demonstrates how LLMs can be used as a multi-label boosting method for temporal and auxiliary tabular data (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty:** The approach is seen as iterative improvements on prior SummaryBoost work. The data conversion (two-step LLM prompting) and the follow-up variants MuHBoost[LP+] (motivated by Nam et al., 2017) and MuHBoost[CC] (motivated by AdaBoost.C2, Li et al., 2023) appear to be straightforward adaptations of existing techniques. The novelties differentiated from these prior methods are not sufficiently emphasized (Reviewers 2,3). One reviewer specifically found the overall contribution low (score 5) while others rated it higher (scores 8,6,8).
- **Poor handling of high-dimensional / high-frequency time series:** The experiments consider features with dimensionality less than 30. The method likely cannot process realistic high-frequency sensor data (e.g., movement, heart rate at second/minute resolution). The paper does not discuss this limitation or test on finer-grained data (e.g., LifeSnaps high-frequency data). Comparison to methods that handle such data (e.g., dynamic latent factor models like Zhang et al., 2024) is missing (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of statistical significance:** Results in Table 1 report only average performance across splits; no significance tests are provided to establish whether MuHBoost’s outperformance over baselines is robust (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing comparison to multilabel text classification LLM methods:** The paper notes prior use of LLMs for MLC in text classification but does not compare MuHBoost to those approaches or differentiate its advantages for heterogeneous/data types (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient background and derivation:** The paper lacks a rigorous mathematical foundation for MuHBoost (step-by-step derivation of loss function, optimization). Descriptions of SummaryBoost, Label Powerset, and Classifier Chains are too brief, making it hard to assess novelty and understand the underlying mechanisms (Reviewers 1,4).
- **Only binary classification tasks evaluated:** All experiments involve binary labels. The claim that MuHBoost can handle different feature types (e.g., categorical/continuous predictions) is not empirically supported; the paper should demonstrate performance on non-binary outcomes or acknowledge this limitation (Reviewer 4).
- **Scalability and computational complexity not analyzed:** The paper lacks detailed analysis of time complexity, memory footprint, and computational resources (e.g., FLOPS) in relation to number of labels, time series length, and dataset size. This is crucial for understanding practical limitations (Reviewers 1,2).
- **Small sample sizes of some datasets:** The limited sample sizes in certain datasets may affect generalizability. The paper only briefly acknowledges this; a more thorough discussion of potential biases and limitations when applying MuHBoost to broader contexts is needed (Reviewer 1).
- **Hallucination proof insufficient:** The claim that LLM hallucinations increase with longer/complex prompts lacks solid experimental or theoretical proof. How the proposed method specifically addresses this issue is not clearly demonstrated (Reviewer 1).
- **Baselines outdated:** Some compared baselines (e.g., Random Forest, XGBoost) are too old; state-of-the-art results are not convincing. More recent methods (2023-2024) should be included (Reviewer 1). Additionally, comparison to RNN-type methods for longitudinal data is missing (Reviewer 2).
- **Questions on implementation details:**
    - The data transformation process (Section 3.1) should be graphically illustrated for clarity (Reviewer 1).
    - It is unclear how each step of the two-step LLM prompting approach helps; which aspects of the textual description are most important (Reviewer 3).
    - The input for MLkNN and MLTSVM is not specified (original measurements or embeddings?) (Reviewer 2).
    - The paper uses incorrect citation style (\cite instead of \citep) (Reviewers 3,4).
    - Footnotes referenced in the main text are placed in the appendix; each footnote should appear on the page first referenced (Reviewer 4).
    - Several typos: “have the two following” (Line 081), “require” instead of “requires” (Line 206) (Reviewer 4).
