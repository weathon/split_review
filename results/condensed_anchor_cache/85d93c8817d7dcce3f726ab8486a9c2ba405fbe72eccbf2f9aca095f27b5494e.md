## Merged Review

### Summary
TRACE is an encoder-decoder transformer that models temporal reconstruction of single-cell gene expression as a sequence-to-sequence task. It represents a cell’s expression profile as a sequence of gene tokens and uses an iterative unmasking generation strategy (rather than autoregressive) to predict expression at unseen time points, incorporating zero-inflated negative binomial (ZINB) losses. The model is evaluated on three datasets across interpolation and extrapolation tasks, outperforming existing baselines (e.g., OT-CFM, other competitors). Ablation studies and biological validation on immune-response data are also presented. Reviewers note that TRACE avoids high-dimensionality issues of some alternative methods and directly captures gene-gene interactions across time.

### Strengths
- **Originality**: The main novel contribution is the use of sequence-to-sequence modeling to directly capture gene-gene interactions across time points, rather than relying on cell embeddings as intermediates. Related work is adequately cited and the improvement over existing methods is stated. (Reviewer 3)
- **Quality**: The method is benchmarked against relevant baselines on three datasets and generally shows superior performance for interpolation and extrapolation. Ablation studies are included to justify the chosen architecture. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Clarity (Introduction & Related Work)**: The motivation for temporal interpolation/extrapolation of scRNA-seq data and the advantages of seq2seq modeling are clearly articulated in these sections. (Reviewer 3)
- **Documentation**: Experiments and hyperparameters are thoroughly documented. (Reviewer 1)
- **Evaluation Metrics**: The use of principal component (PC) plots (instead of UMAP) for qualitative overlap assessment between inferred and ground-truth data is appreciated. (Reviewer 2)
- **Biological Relevance**: TRACE recapitulates known biology in immune-response activation stages, demonstrating utility beyond quantitative metrics. (Reviewer 2)
- **Architectural Justification**: The iterative unmasking approach avoids the high-dimensionality problem that methods like OT-CFM face. (Reviewer 4)
- **Contrast**: Reviewer 2 (rating 5) found the paper well-written and clear overall, whereas other reviewers (ratings 3) identified substantial clarity issues in the Methods and experiments sections.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity of Methods (Majority Concern)**: The description of the training and inference pipeline is insufficiently detailed and disjointed. Multiple reviewers requested a step-by-step explanation, including an algorithm for training and for interpolation/extrapolation inference. The description of how the problem is set up (inputs, outputs for interpolation vs. extrapolation) lacks specificity. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Clarity of Key Components**:
  - What exactly is a “gene token” and how are genes tokenized? (Reviewer 1)
  - How are target embeddings \(Y_t\) assigned and gene tokens \(X_t\) generated? (Reviewer 4)
  - Should target tokens be masked in Figure 1? (Reviewer 4)
  - Do the encoder and decoder use the same gene token embeddings? (Reviewer 3)
  - How are the \(Y_i\)s computed? Are they from decoder’s gene token embeddings? (Reviewer 3)
  - What do the \(C_t\)s represent – gene embeddings for time \(t\), [CLS] token embeddings, or a combination? (Reviewer 3)
  - How are the two losses (\(\ell_{SSL}\) and \(\ell_{ZINB}\)) combined during training? (Reviewer 3)
  - How exactly are the time positional encodings computed? Lines 235–237 are unclear. (Reviewer 3)
- **Baseline Comparisons and Metrics**:
  - Motivation for using metrics like EMD and MMD is lacking; why not simply use MSE of the full gene expression vector? The metrics used should be meticulously described for unfamiliar readers. (Reviewers 1, 3)
  - The paper reports results for only four time points; other time points are not shown (even in appendix). (Reviewer 1)
  - A baseline comparison to **RNA velocity** is missing; the authors should add such a baseline or explain why it is not possible. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing Experiments**:
  - The introduction extensively discusses in silico perturbation models, yet experiments only cover interpolation and extrapolation. Experiments on **scPerturb-Seq data** (e.g., Lotfollahi et al. 2019, or identifiability guarantees from causal disentanglement) are needed to support the claimed perturbation relevance. (Reviewer 2)
  - Only the first three of five listed advantages of TRACE (handling high dimensionality without reduction, learned embedding space, integration with foundation models) are experimentally supported; points (d) avoiding VAE posterior collapse and (e) benefiting diffusion/flow-matching models lack validation. (Reviewer 4)
- **Uninformative Analyses**:
  - Section 4.5 (analysis of cell embeddings) does not align with the overall narrative of temporal reconstruction and provides no insights into TRACE’s predictive capabilities. (Reviewer 3)
  - The ablation studies are uninformative and lack motivation; a more insightful ablation would examine performance when excluding data points closest to unseen time points. (Reviewer 3)
- **Methodological Questions Overlapping with Criticism**:
  - Why is \(t_0\) data exclusively used for computing source embeddings? How is data from other time points incorporated during training? (Reviewer 3)
  - Why are only source embeddings computed via the encoder, while other embeddings use a frozen TRACE decoder? (Reviewer 3)
  - One motivation is direct gene-gene interaction learning, yet final gene counts are still predicted via the cell embedding. Wouldn’t using gene embeddings directly be more effective? (Reviewer 3)
  - The iterative unmasking approach resembles discrete diffusion with an absorbing transition matrix – did the authors explore this connection? (Reviewer 4)
  - How does TRACE work on scPerturb-Seq experiments? (Reviewer 2)
