## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies in-context learning (ICL) of Markov chains (binary states, next-token prediction) using linear self-attention (LSA) transformers. The authors derive the closed-form global minimizer for a single-layer LSA on length‑2 Markov chains, give upper/lower bounds for arbitrary-length chains, and show multilayer LSA performs preconditioned gradient descent on a multi-objective problem. Numerical experiments with LSA and GPT-2 are provided. Reviewers agree the topic is important and the paper is well‑organized, but they diverge in overall assessment: two reviewers rate it 5 (accept) while a third rates it 3 (reject, more confident). The negative reviewer finds the theoretical contributions too specific, disjointed, and lacking insight beyond the simplest setting.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an important problem—ICL beyond linear regression with i.i.d. inputs—and studies a probabilistic Markovian setting that is closer to real-world sequential data.
- The theoretical results are novel: most ICL theory assumes deterministic input-output mappings, while this work considers a random mapping given by a Markov chain.
- The analysis of the first two propositions is sound (checked by a reviewer).
- The paper is well-written, clearly presented, and well-organized.
- Numerical experiments using both LSA and GPT-2 architectures back the theoretical findings.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited scope and generalizability:** The setup is highly restrictive: binary states, length‑2 chains for the closed-form result, and the bounds in Theorem 1 are of unclear tightness. One reviewer notes that simply writing the closed-form optimum for a 2×2 case does not give insight into the general problem; the contrast with the sparser minimum for linear regression is valid but not surprising (there is no a priori reason to expect sparsity). Another reviewer questions how the work differs from existing papers on ICL for Markov chains (Rajaraman et al. 2024, Nichani et al. 2024) and whether the setting is too simplistic for broad applicability.
- **Theorem 1 is too trivial/abstract:** The theorem essentially states that if \(f=g\circ h\) then \(\min f\ge\min g\) and \(\min f\le f(x')\) for an appropriate \(x'\). This is a generic fact; strict convexity of \(\tilde{f}\) is never used. Moreover, the only application (Example 2 in the appendix) appears incomplete—missing computations and no concrete loss bounds are derived. It is also unclear how Theorem 1 connects to Propositions 1 and 2, which studied the structure of the optimum rather than loss values.
- **Disconnected theoretical pieces and lack of landscape analysis:** The paper claims to analyze the loss landscape but only characterizes the global minimum. One reviewer points out that, unlike work on ICL for linear regression that proves a global PL condition and gradient descent convergence, this paper offers no optimization dynamics results. Propositions 1 and 2 are very specific to the length‑2 binary setting; the general bounds do not give structure. The overall contributions feel disjointed.
- **Proposition 3 uses an unjustified sparsity constraint:** Proposition 3 assumes a modified sparsity structure (Eq. 12) by zeroing out the last row of \(Q\), which is different from the dense structure found in Propositions 1 and 2. This constraint is only justified by aligning dimensions and seems to exist solely to derive another “forward pass = preconditioned GD” result. No justification is given for why this is relevant to the dense optimum of the Markovian setting.
- **Clarity issues (questions that overlap with criticism):**
  - The term “next-token prediction” in Table 1 and elsewhere is misleading: the task is still a supervised regression, not autoregressive prediction as in other works (e.g., Sander et al.). The authors should change this terminology.
  - In Eq. (10), what does \(X_i^*\) represent?
  - How to pass from Lemma 1 to Proposition 4? A short proof sketch is needed.
  - The dense/sparse terminology is unclear before seeing Figure 1; the authors should clarify in the introduction why Markovian data yields a denser minimum.
  - Line 198: LSA is not directly linked to Eq. (6); the notation should be aligned.
  - The square-bracket notation from Eq. (11) onward is hard to read; double indices would be better.
  - The experimental section (3.3) would benefit from an introductory paragraph outlining the experiments.
- **Missing experiments and comparisons:**
  - Can the authors train the LSA as in Eq. (6) and show the attention maps (\(P\) and \(Q\)), and also train a standard LSA (with \(W_k, W_q, W_v\) as in Eq. 5) and compare all three scenarios (Eq. 6, Eq. 5, and the constructed model Eq. 9)?
  - Can attention maps from Section 4 be visualized and compared to those in Rajaraman et al. 2024 and Nichani et al. 2024? Even if exact matches are hard, such a comparison could reveal whether MSE vs. cross-entropy training leads to similar patterns.
- **Minor weaknesses:**  
  - Propositions should be labeled as theorems (being main contributions).  
  - Line 135: “sampled from” should be “belong to”.  
  - Line 229: “(1) Data” should be “(a) Data”.  
  - In Proposition 2, the definition of \(c_1\) should have \(n+1\) instead of \(j\).  
  - \(|\mathcal{S}|\) in evaluation metrics is undefined; are these still binary values?  
  - Figure 1 could be improved to convey the message more clearly.
