## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes TGeo-KD, an adaptive method for learning sample-wise knowledge fusion ratios in knowledge distillation (KD). It leverages trilateral geometry among student prediction (S), teacher prediction (T), and ground truth (G) at the intra-sample level, and extends to inter-sample relations by incorporating the teacher’s global average prediction per class. A simple neural network maps these geometric relations to adaptive fusion ratios via bilevel optimization. Experiments on image classification (CIFAR-100, ImageNet), attack detection (HIL), and CTR prediction (Criteo) show improvements over other loss re-weighting methods.

### Strengths
- Novel method TGeo-KD for adaptive sample-wise knowledge fusion ratio, exploiting trilateral geometry among S, T, and G (R1, R3, R4). The problem of learning the fusion ratio per sample is less studied and the solution is simple and effective (R2, R4).
- Captures both intra-sample and inter-sample geometric relations, helping mitigate outliers and providing robust knowledge transfer (R1, R4).
- Versatile and adaptable across architectures and model sizes; applicable to multiple tasks (R1, R2, R4).
- Solid experimental results on standard benchmarks (CIFAR-100, ImageNet, HIL, Criteo) showing consistent improvements over other loss re-weighting methods (R2, R3, R4). Some reviewers found performance gains good and experiments comprehensive (R3, R4), while others noted marginal gains on CIFAR-100 (R1).
- Practical: introduces little additional training time (R3).
- Well-written paper with clear motivation and illustrative example (R2, R4).
- Sample-wise fusion ratio provides insights into how much knowledge to learn from teacher vs ground truth per sample based on ST discrepancy and teacher correctness (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal performance gains:** Some reviewers observed that improvements in Table 1 and Table 2 are marginal, raising skepticism about the underlying motivation (R1). This contrasts with the positive assessment by other reviewers (R3, R4).
- **Insufficient theoretical proof:** Motivation is illustrated by only one experimental case in the Introduction; comprehensive validation across different datasets is lacking (R1). How outliers hurt KD is not empirically shown (R3).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons:** Important related works like Decoupled Knowledge Distillation (CVPR’22) and Knowledge Distillation via Softmax Regression Representation Learning (ICLR’21) are not compared; if included, improvements may not be significant (R2).
- **Need for larger-scale evaluation:** Experiments on CIFAR-100 use same architecture and are limited; claims would strengthen with results on larger datasets like ImageNet (R2). The HIL and Criteo datasets are considered low impact for demonstrating generalizability (R2). Evaluating on real OOD datasets like DomainNet would be more convincing (R3).
- **Motivation for inter-sample relations unclear:** Since the teacher may underperform on outliers, simply decreasing α could also address this; the need for inter-sample relations is not clearly justified and requires further clarification (R3). The paper does not show that inter-sample relations on incorrectly predicted samples contribute significantly to decreased fusion ratios; more experiments are needed (R4).
- **Impact of sampling method on HIL/Criteo results:** The paper mentions oversampling minority class but does not explore its direct influence on performance improvements (R4).
- **Understanding of learned fusion ratios:** It is unclear how the neural network assigns larger/smaller fusion ratios to specific samples and how the learning process unfolds (R4).
- **Design-choice questions:**
  - Why Euclidean distance is used instead of cosine similarity for computing similarities of class probability distributions? Advantages unclear (R3, R4).
  - Eq. 4 is described as an implicit function of ω (θ* depends on ω); how is the alternating optimization performed? (R3)
  - What is the specific reason for formulating into bilevel optimization? Motivation is not clear and may lead to less efficient training (R4).
  - How are outliers defined in the datasets used (ImageNet, CIFAR)? (R2)
  - Does the method show consistent performance across different numbers and categories of samples? (R4)
- **Computational complexity:** Introducing a neural network to learn fusion ratios may increase computational cost compared to simpler KD techniques, potentially a drawback in resource-constrained environments (R1).

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** Lower-scoring reviewers (R1 and R2, scores 5) emphasized marginal gains, lack of theory, missing baselines, and need for larger-scale experiments, while higher-scoring reviewers (R3 and R4, scores 8) considered the method novel, experiments solid, and practical.
