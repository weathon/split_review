## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LOCO (low-rank cluster orthogonal weight modification), a perturbation-based learning algorithm that extends node perturbation (NP) by projecting layer inputs into a low-rank, task-dependent orthogonal subspace. The aim is to improve the efficiency and biological plausibility of learning without backpropagation (BP). The authors provide theoretical analysis of gradient estimation variance and convergence, and test LOCO on spiking and artificial neural networks using MNIST, NETtalk, and a catastrophic forgetting task. Reviewers acknowledge the algorithm’s novelty and connection to neuroscience (e.g., orthogonal activity subspaces in motor control), and note that empirical results convincingly show improvements over vanilla NP for deep networks. However, reviewers raise major concerns about mathematical inaccuracies, insufficient biological justification for the projection matrix, lack of comparisons with other non-BP algorithms, missing tests on hard tasks, unaddressed computational overhead, and missing citations. Scores are 3, 3, 5, 3, with one reviewer (R3) being significantly more positive (score 5) and finding the combination of theory and experiments valuable, while others (R1, R2, R4) see significant flaws.

### Strengths
- Numerical experiments are thoroughly and clearly explained, and results convincingly demonstrate the benefits of LOCO over conventional node perturbation for deep neural networks with more than five hidden layers (R1).
- The algorithm is interesting and has connections to neuroscientific data on orthogonal activity subspaces, e.g., in motor control (R2).
- Testing performance across both spiking neural networks and artificial neural networks is a strength (R2).
- The pursuit of biologically plausible alternatives to backpropagation is a significant problem for computational neuroscience (R3).
- Authors support findings by combining empirical results on well-known benchmarks with theoretical analysis, which strengthens the validity of the approach (R3).
- The theoretical aspects of the paper are clearly articulated (R4 – note that other reviewers raised mathematical inaccuracies in these aspects; this disagreement is preserved implicitly via Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Gradient bias and mathematical inaccuracies (R1):**
  - Abstract states LOCO provides an unbiased estimate of the BP gradient, but the LOCO update becomes ΔW^{LOCO}_l = ΔW^{BP}_l P_l^T in the limit of infinite perturbations; unless P_l is full-rank, LOCO introduces bias relative to BP. The conditions under which this bias is acceptable or negligible are not clarified (R1).
  - Line 894 incorrectly refers to ℓ as the Hessian matrix; according to Malladi et al. (2024), ℓ is the smoothness (Lipschitz) parameter (R1).
  - Eq. 17 is missing the trace operation in the Frobenius inner product, making the equation mathematically incorrect (R1).
  - Eq. 18: a matrix P satisfying this equation exists, but it is no longer a projection matrix; no rigorous justification is given for why P remains a projection matrix under this condition (R1).
  - Appendix A.3: inequalities are missing throughout the proof, making the mathematical derivations difficult to verify (R1).
  - Lines 965-966: the origin of tr[∑(θ_t)] is unclear and the equation appears incorrect; no definition or justification is provided (R1).
  - Line 1221: although g follows a Gaussian distribution if the perturbation is Gaussian, the covariance is not necessarily the identity matrix; the implications are not discussed (R1).
  - Appendix A.15: there is confusion between subspaces in activity space and weight space; even if pre-unit activity lies in an r-dimensional space, the gradient g may not be constrained to r dimensions; the relationship is not clarified (R1).
  - Lines 1239-1241: it is implicitly assumed that σ² = ||μ_g||² without explicit statement or justification (R1).

- **Biological plausibility concerns (R1, R3, R4):**
  - The projection matrix P lacks clear biological implementation: computing P requires a global view of network activity to enforce orthogonality, which is not biologically realistic (R3). The low-rank constraint also has no obvious biological counterpart (R3).
  - It is not clarified how LOCO addresses the three issues that make backpropagation biologically implausible: (a) weight transport (symmetric forward/backward weights), (b) signed and potentially extreme error signals, and (c) feedback altering neural activity. The paper should explain how LOCO avoids these problems compared to BP (R4).
  - The method trades off one aspect of biological plausibility for another (the cluster-orthogonal projection algorithm on inputs) (R2).

- **Insufficient comparisons and missing citations (R2, R3, R4):**
  - The related work section is insufficient; many non-backpropagation algorithms are not compared, such as neural sampling, predictive coding, contrastive divergence, equilibrium propagation, target propagation, the forward-forward model, scaling forward gradient with local losses, and gradients without backpropagation. Both experimental and theoretical comparisons (e.g., convergence rates, computational complexity, robustness to noise) are lacking (R4).
  - A closely related work by Duncker and Driscoll et al. (NeurIPS 2020) also uses a projection matrix P to promote orthogonal subspace learning (minimizing interference in continual learning) and is not cited; a comparison is essential (R3).
  - Hebbian learning based orthogonal projection for continual learning of spiking neural networks is also missing (R4).
  - Comparisons to BP are not always made (R2).

- **Task complexity and scalability not tested (R2, R3):**
  - The method seems to rely on simple task structure: low-dimensional projection (assuming inputs can be projected to low rank without losing task-relevant info) and k-means clustering for orthogonalisation (relying on data prototypes). Neither assumption is validated on harder tasks; the submission does not test on appropriately hard tasks such as those in Bartunov et al. 2018 (Assessing the Scalability of Biologically-Motivated Deep Learning Algorithms and Architectures) (R2).
  - The paper does not discuss limitations: scenarios where low-rank and orthogonality assumptions might not hold, or how performance would degrade in more complex, high-dimensional tasks (R3).

- **Efficiency and complexity not adequately evaluated (R1, R2, R3):**
  - The proposed approach’s efficiency is not tested (e.g., FLOPs), and it does not appear efficient because the projection matrix must be calculated at every update (R2).
  - The computational cost of maintaining the projection matrix P, especially in larger networks, is not discussed (R3).
  - How was the learning rate controlled when estimating learning efficiency? Algorithmic complexity may be less relevant in inherently parallel systems (brain, neuromorphic chips, GPUs); the conclusions need to consider this (R1).

- **Missing formal theorem statements (R3):**
  - Theoretical analyses are not framed within formal theorem statements. It is unclear what specific conditions or assumptions (e.g., convexity) apply, making it difficult to assess the generality and robustness of the theoretical results. The smoothness assumption is mentioned but its implications for convergence are not explicitly stated (R3).

- **Overly general title (R3):**
  - The title (“Efficient Learning in Neural Networks without Gradient Backpropagation”) does not distinguish this work from the extensive literature on learning without backpropagation; it should more accurately reflect the low-rank and orthogonality constraints (R3).

- **Presentation issues (R2, R3):**
  - Figures are small and difficult to read at standard zoom, in particular Fig. 1 (R2).
  - Several typos: “weights” misspelled (lines 102, 156), “to obtained” (line 160), “we varies” (line 319), “performance” (line 399), and mixing up \citep vs \citet in the introduction (R2, R3).

- **Alternative approach not justified (R1):**
  - Would it not be more effective to consider low-rank perturbations of the postsynaptic units rather than projecting presynaptic units into low-rank space? The motivation for projecting x_{l-1} is not explained (R1).
