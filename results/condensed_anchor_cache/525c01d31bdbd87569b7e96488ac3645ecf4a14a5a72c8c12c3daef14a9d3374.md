## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Domain-Inspired Sharpness-Aware Minimization (DISAM) to address the inconsistent convergence of SAM across different domains under domain shifts. DISAM introduces a constraint that minimizes the variance of domain losses, which automatically weakens gradient perturbations for well-optimized domains and strengthens them for less-optimized ones. Theoretical analysis indicates faster overall convergence and improved generalization. Experiments on multiple domain generalization benchmarks demonstrate superiority over state-of-the-art methods, and the approach also shows improved efficiency when fine-tuning CLIP models for open-class generalization.

### Strengths

- **Novelty of applying SAM to domain shifts:** The paper identifies that SAM has a detrimental impact under domain shifts due to inconsistent convergence across domains, and proposes the first SAM methodology specifically designed to handle distribution shifts (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). Reviewer 2 notes that there has been a lack of thorough investigation into sharpness-based optimization in the context of domain shifts, and the idea of constraining the variance of losses among training domains is interesting.
- **Computational efficiency:** DISAM does not incur additional computational cost compared to standard SAM, making it practical for resource-constrained scenarios (Reviewer 3). It is compatible with various sharpness-variants (Reviewer 3).
- **Theoretical and empirical support:** The paper provides theoretical evidence showing faster convergence, complemented by comprehensive empirical validation. Analytical experiments demonstrate efficacy in convergence speed and mitigating model sharpness (Reviewer 2). Numerical results show improvement over state-of-the-art by a large margin (Reviewer 4), and the method outperforms several methods across domain generalization benchmarks (Reviewer 2).
- **Application to CLIP fine-tuning:** The study investigates DISAM for fine-tuning a CLIP-based model, aiming for improved open-class generalization, and shows superior efficiency in parameter-efficient fine-tuning combined with pretraining models (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty concerns:** Several reviewers question the novelty of the core idea. Reviewer 1 states that convergence consistency is a general issue and the solution is normal, making the novelty not clear for ICLR. Reviewer 3 argues that minimizing variance between losses is not entirely novel, as similar concepts appear in vREX (Out-of-Distribution Generalization via Risk Extrapolation) and Fishr (Invariant Gradient Variances for Out-of-Distribution Generalization); DISAM appears to be an incremental adaptation of vREX principles applied to SAM. This contrasts with positive novelty assessments by Reviewers 2 and 4. (Disagreement preserved.)
- **Missing comparisons with standard optimizers:** DISAM is compared to SAM but not to standard optimizers like SGD or Adam. Reviewer 2 notes that SAM-based methods incur twice the computational overhead and additional storage compared to SGD/Adam, and a thorough evaluation should include wall-clock time and resource usage to assess practical efficiency gains.
- **Insufficient comparison with domain generalization methods:** Reviewer 2 calls for a more comprehensive comparison with recent state-of-the-art domain generalization techniques, especially methods addressing domain alignment or feature disentanglement, to strengthen the claims.
- **Parameter sensitivity of ρ and λ:** 
  - Reviewer 2 notes that ρ significantly influences convergence speed and generalizability, but the paper lacks a clear methodology for selecting the optimal ρ. A sensitivity analysis across datasets and architectures is needed. Reviewer 4 also questions why larger ρ relates to better generalization (Q3) and how the theoretical convergence rate links to generalization (Q2).
  - Reviewer 4 points out that λ in eq. (7) is a critical parameter but does not appear in Theorem 1, and its influence on convergence and generalization is not sufficiently explored. A sensitivity study is needed (W2).
- **Motivation for focusing on early-phase convergence:** Reviewer 4 states that Fig. 1(b) shows inconsistent convergence early in training, but losses become similar after ~30 epochs. It is unclear how the difference in convergence *degree* (as opposed to loss value) in the early phase directly impacts final generalization. An explicit link between early-stage convergence behavior and the final minima across domains is needed (W1).
- **Marginal improvement in OOD fine-tuning:** Reviewer 3 notes that improvement in out-of-distribution performance with DISAM is not intuitive, and when comparing enhancements to CLIPOOD, the difference seems marginal, raising questions about effectiveness in fine-tuning.
- **Missing discussion of related work:** Reviewer 4 asks about the relation with a recent work (arXiv:2309.15639) that also proposes variance reduction for finding perturbations, though not designed for domain generalization (Q1). This comparison is missing.
- **Theoretical-practical gap for ρ:** Reviewer 2 notes that the theoretical analysis suggests larger ρ should lead to improved generalization given convergence, but this aspect is not reflected in experiments (Q1). Reviewer 4 also asks why the last sentence in Sec. 3 claims larger ρ yields better generalization (Q3).
- **Need for further analysis of CLIP fine-tuning:** Reviewer 2 requests further experimental analysis to elucidate why DISAM performs better in CLIP fine-tuning (Q2). Reviewer 3 raises similar doubts about marginal gains.
- **Clarification on loss value vs. minimization speed:** Reviewer 1 asks whether the important factor is the values of losses across domains or the minimization speed (Q1). This distinction affects the interpretation of the variance constraint.
- **Relation to multi-objective optimization:** Reviewer 1 asks whether domain-level convergence consistency is a general issue under domain shifts or in multi-objective optimization (Q2).
- **Reproducibility:** Reviewer 3 requests that reproducible code be provided during the rebuttal period.
