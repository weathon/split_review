## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces zero-shot in-context adversarial learning for LLMs to improve research idea generation. It uses a multi-agent system (Proposer, Reviewer, Area Chair) that iteratively refines ideas through an adversarial process inspired by GANs, and introduces a relative quality ranking metric (using GPT-4o as a judge) to evaluate novelty and feasibility. Experiments on a dataset of 500 biomedical papers report a 21% improvement in novelty and 322% in feasibility with GPT-4o. Reviewer opinions diverge: Reviewers 2 and 3 see a novel, well-executed contribution with clear empirical gains, while Reviewer 1 finds the theoretical grounding weak, the evaluation insufficient, and the experiments lacking rigor. All three agree that the evaluation metric needs validation against human judgment, and that broader domain testing is needed.

### Strengths

- **Original approach and framing:** Drawing inspiration from GANs for LLM-based research ideation is creative and timely. The multi-agent architecture (Proposer, Reviewer, Area Chair) mimicking peer review is intuitive and well-illustrated (R1, R2, R3).
- **Clear presentation:** The paper is well-structured, with documented prompts, clear figures, and coherent writing (R1, R2, R3).
- **Empirical results:** The method shows substantial gains over baselines (initial idea, self-reflection) in novelty and feasibility, supported by ablation studies and convergence analysis (R2, R3). The constructed dataset of 500 biomedical papers provides a reasonable testbed (R2).
- **New evaluation metric:** The relative quality ranking metric offers a scalable way to assess open-ended LLM generations, addressing an important evaluation gap (R2, R3).
- **Significance:** The work contributes to LLM-driven scientific discovery and could generalize to other creative tasks (R2, R3). Reviewers 2 and 3 consider the contribution valuable, though Reviewer 1 finds it weaker (contribution scores: R1=2, R2=3, R3=2).

### Weaknesses

1. **Theoretical framing is weak and inconsistent with actual method.**  
   - The GAN analogy lacks mathematical justification. The minimax objective and the assumption of a neighborhood \(B_\epsilon(y)\) for the discriminator are not grounded in the procedure, which is essentially a dialogue generating tokens, not parametric memory updates (R1, R2).  
   - The notation of thetas and theta-stars for altering parametric memories is not coherent with the iterative prompting being performed; this makes the theoretical analysis unconvincing and a distraction (R2).  
   - *Reviewer 2 notes the framing is “more metaphorical than actually a rigorous description”; Reviewer 1 finds the lack of mathematical foundation a major weakness.*

2. **Evaluation is insufficient and potentially biased.**  
   - **No human validation of the ranking metric:** The relative quality ranking metric is only evaluated by GPT-4o itself, raising concerns about bias (favoring its own model family) and alignment with human judgment (R1, R2, R3). All three reviewers request a small-scale human evaluation study or comparison with established metrics.  
   - **Limited scope of evaluation:** Experiments are confined to biomedical papers; generalizability to other domains is not demonstrated (R2, R3).  
   - **Only two baselines:** The comparison includes only an initial idea baseline and self-reflection; additional baselines (e.g., prompt engineering methods like DSPy, fine-tuning approaches) are missing (R2).  
   - **Lack of statistical rigor:** Reported numbers lack confidence intervals; reproducibility is unclear given deterministic setups are not guaranteed across API calls (R1). Only GPT-series models are tested; open-source models should be included (R1).  
   - **Novelty and feasibility are assessed separately** without analyzing their interplay or trade-offs (R2).

3. **Practical concerns not addressed.**  
   - **Computational cost:** The multi-agent system, especially with GPT-4o, is likely expensive; the paper does not discuss cost or optimization strategies (R2, R3).  
   - **Prompt sensitivity:** The system’s performance may vary significantly with prompt phrasing; no analysis of prompt engineering robustness or best practices is provided (R2).

4. **Missing analyses and unanswered questions.**  
   - The role of the Area Chair: Its removal impacts feasibility more than novelty; the paper offers no insight into why, what cues the AC uses, or how its judgments align with human assessments (R2).  
   - Selection of reference papers (\(k\) papers as background) is not clearly justified (R1).  
   - Reliability of GPT-4o as an evaluator is untested: does it produce consistent rankings when repeated? (R1).  
   - How the system handles the tension between high novelty and low feasibility is not examined (R2).  
   - The paper does not relate its ranking metric to existing evaluation methods for open-ended text generation, nor discuss its advantages/limitations (R2).  
   - Concrete adaptation paths for other tasks are not provided (R2).  
   - *Reviewer 1’s question about the comparison \(\hat{y}_{i-1} < \hat{y}_i\) (Line 206) is noted but not elaborated.*
