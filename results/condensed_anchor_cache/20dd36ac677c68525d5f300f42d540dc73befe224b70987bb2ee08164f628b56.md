## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a framework to control whether a language model answers from context or from its parametric knowledge. The authors create a counterfactual dataset (e.g., "The capital of France is London") with instructions to either follow or ignore the context. They fine-tune instruction-tuned Llama-3.1, Mistral-v0.3, and Gemma-2 on this task (85-95% accuracy) and then use patchscopes to localize layers important for context vs. prior decisions. They identify a 1-D subspace in a single layer that encodes this choice and show it can be used as a “knob” to intervene during inference. The same subspace works in fine-tuned, instruction-tuned, and base models of the same family, and correlates with model performance.

### Strengths
- **Originality and significance:** Creative combination of counterfactual dataset creation, patchscopes, activation patching, and distributed alignment search. The problem is well-motivated (RAG, QA, misinformation avoidance) and of substantial interest to the community.
- **Quality of experiments:** Comprehensive – tests multiple instruction prompts, multiple model families (Llama, Mistral, Gemma), and three domains. Careful validation that models robustly handle in-context vs. parametric knowledge beyond the training dataset.
- **Interesting findings:** Demonstration of a 1-D subspace that generalizes across models and even to non-fine-tuned versions, suggesting a fundamental mechanism.
- **Clarity:** Generally well-written, especially the problem setting and motivation. Mathematical notation is clear and easy to follow.
- **Practical implications:** Findings could advance model interpretability and control, e.g., avoiding hallucination or misinformation. One reviewer (R4) highlights potential for real-world robust models.

### Weaknesses
- **Overclaimed novelty:** The claim that a simple fundamental mechanism modulating context vs. prior knowledge is new overstates the contribution. Prior work (e.g., EMNLP 2023 / ACL anthology 2023.emnlp-main.615, arXiv:2402.11655) already showed particular attention heads can be reweighted to change this behavior. The paper would benefit by toning down the novelty claim and focusing on what the methodology adds. *(R1, strong concern)*
- **Missing related work and citations:** The related works section lacks several important references. The paper should compare/discuss differences with Yu et al. 2023, Jin et al. 2024, and other papers on models handling conflicting knowledge (e.g., arXiv:2402.11655, 2305.16572, 2404.06283) as well as irrelevant context (e.g., arXiv:2302.00093, 2310.01558). A dedicated paragraph on models handling conflicting knowledge is missing. *(R1)*
- **Method limitation – only MHSA patching:** The analysis restricts patching to multi-head self-attention outputs, ignoring MLPs that are known to contribute to parametric knowledge. This could explain why some localizations fail (e.g., Figure 2d). A broader approach would first intervene on the final hidden state (residual stream) of each layer before narrowing down. *(R1)*
- **Method – only final token position:** Focusing only on the last token position may miss effects across other token positions (e.g., which positions the causal attention heads attend to). Though computationally justified, this is a limitation. *(R1)*
- **Lack of patchscope details:** The paper shows only the best set of layers in Figure 2; it would be valuable to show how the iterative algorithm built up to that result. *(R1)*
- **Unclear motivation for the “knob”:** The paper claims a knob is desirable for controlling context sensitivity, but it’s unclear why a practitioner would want a fixed knob (e.g., per-sample judgment needed) rather than just excluding the context when it should be ignored, or why not simply finetune for controllability in the first place. The proposed steering methodology does not consistently outperform finetuning (R3 notes it doesn’t for arithmetic; only helps for Llama-3.1 ICL and Llama-3.1-Instruct 0-shot). *(R2, R3)*
- **Missing comparison with prior techniques:** No comparison against earlier intervention methods (e.g., those mentioned in Section 2) to showcase usability of the proposed technique. *(R4)*
- **Side effects not investigated:** The study does not examine whether this “knob” affects other behaviors (safety, bias, consistency) in unwanted ways. *(R4)*
- **Minor reviewer concerns:**
    - R1: The term “one-dimensional subspace” is used without concrete definition; the learned projection matrix \(P\) is not obviously rank-one or orthogonal (Equation 4 appears to use a full matrix; explanation needed).
    - R1: The paper should clarify the hypothesis at lines 69-70, 178-179 – is it from prior work (Jin et al. 2024)? 
    - R1: Activation patching was first proposed in earlier work (NeurIPS 2020 / arXiv:2004.14623), not Meng et al. 2022.
    - R1: Duplicate sentence in lines 060-065, typo line 130.
    - R1: The upper figures for 2a-2d are not meaningful if not patching directly into the residual stream; the dotted line explanation is unclear.
    - R1: Notation \(a_c\) and \(a_p\) should clarify that they are used only for correctly predicted instances (hence equal to correct labels).
    - R2: The framing around contributions can be made more precise.
    - R2: For Figure 3, compute a baseline for instruction models without intent instructions (expect low SIA) as a sanity check.
    - R3: How often does the projection matrix \(P\) need to be re-learned? Does it work for larger models like Llama-3 70B?
    - R3: Why does finetuning not help on the Arithmetic task compared to ICL?
    - R4: Does the subspace affect other behaviors of the LLM (e.g., safety, bias, consistency)?
