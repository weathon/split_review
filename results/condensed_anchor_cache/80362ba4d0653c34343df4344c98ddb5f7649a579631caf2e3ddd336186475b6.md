## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SpecAR-Net for time-series representation, which uses STFT to transform 1D time series into 2D spectrograms, followed by multi-scale 2D complex convolutions and pooling. It aims to jointly model time and frequency to capture periodicity, trends, and transient patterns. Experiments cover five downstream tasks: classification, anomaly detection, imputation, long- and short-term forecasting.

### Strengths
- The SpecAR-Block is designed as a plug-in component that could be integrated into CNNs, RNNs, or Transformers (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 3 notes the plug-in manner as a strength).
- The framework is general and competitive against many SOTA baselines across multiple benchmark tasks (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 2 agrees it outperforms most SOTA in 5 tasks; Reviewer 3 appreciates the inclusion of many SOTA models).
- Time-frequency modeling via STFT enables capturing time-dependent trends and periodic components, which is a benefit over pure time-domain methods (Reviewer 1).
- The use of various complex convolutions in the frequency domain is a proposed technical contribution (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and differentiation**: The approach is a straightforward combination of STFT and multi-scale convolutions, offering limited new insights (Reviewer 1). It is very similar to existing frequency-domain methods like TimesNet and Fedformer, and the paper does not clearly explain what differentiates SpecAR-Net besides moving convolutions into the frequency domain (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 3 notes unifying time-frequency is not novel, citing Woo et al. 2022).
- **Lack of justification for design choices**: No justification is given for using STFT instead of Wavelet transforms (which provide variable resolution), nor for choosing the Hamming window over other windows (e.g., Hann, Blackman, Gaussian) (Reviewer 1). Similarly, the proposed components (time-frequency transform, multi-scale convolution, order-preserving loss) are claimed to address periodicity, mutagenicity, and trend patterns, but it is not clearly explained how each component solves its corresponding problem (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing ablation studies**: No ablation study is provided on the individual components (STFT, 2D convolutions, pooling, order-preserving constraint), making it impossible to assess their contributions (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Plug-and-play claim unsupported**: Although the paper claims SpecAR-Net is a plug-and-play module, no experiments demonstrate this by plugging it into other existing models (Reviewer 2).
- **Statistical significance and experimental rigor**: No statistical significance tests or error bars are reported for the results in Tables 3–7, leaving the improvements unconvincing (Reviewers 1, 3). The experimental setting is not clearly stated (Reviewer 3), and it is unclear whether the compared models have comparable parameter counts, making the fairness of comparison questionable (Reviewer 2). Hyperparameters not listed are not described, and the full search space is not provided, nor is the tuning procedure clear (Reviewer 3).
- **Code not open**: Code for reproducibility is not provided (Reviewer 3).
- **Writing and presentation issues**: The Abstract and Introduction contain sentences that are not directly relevant to the contribution (Reviewers 2, 3). Figure 1, 2, and 3 have low resolution and are difficult to read; font sizes are too small, and vector images are recommended (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). A typo in Table 3: “2..051” should be “2.051” (Reviewer 3).
- **Specific unanswered questions** (representing missing experiments or justifications):
  - In the TF Resolution experiment, why does a larger window length provide only marginal improvement for shorter prediction lengths (96, 192) but benefit longer horizons (336, 720)? (Reviewer 2)
  - Do the proposed methods (time-frequency transform, multi-scale convolution, order-preserving) individually address the stated problems of periodicity, mutagenicity, and trend? An ablation study is needed (Reviewer 3).
  - What is the whole search space of hyperparameters, and how were the listed and unlisted hyperparameters tuned? (Reviewer 3)
  - Are the number of parameters comparable across all baseline models in the experiments? (Reviewer 3)

**Minority positive view**: Reviewer 1 rated the paper 5 (fair confidence), noting the framework is “quite general” and competitive, but still rated contribution low (1) and soundness fair (2). Reviewer 2 and 3 both gave 3 and raised more substantial concerns. The strengths acknowledged by Reviewer 1 (generality, competitiveness, time-frequency modeling) are included above, but the overall evidence from all reviewers highlights significant methodological and experimental gaps.
