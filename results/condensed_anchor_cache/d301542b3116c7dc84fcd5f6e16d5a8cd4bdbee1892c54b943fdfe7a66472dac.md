## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Uncertainty-aware Reward Model (URM) and its ensemble variant (URME) to address the inability of standard reward models to capture stochasticity in human preferences and to evaluate prediction reliability. URM uses a probabilistic value head over multiple reward attributes to model aleatoric uncertainty, and URME quantifies epistemic uncertainty through ensemble discrepancies. The authors argue that the Bradley-Terry loss cannot learn aleatoric uncertainty even with a probabilistic head, motivating the shift to a multi-attribute rating objective. Experiments on RewardBench show improvements over deterministic baselines, and best-of-n (BoN) experiments on AlpacaEval demonstrate that uncertainty-based filtering can improve generation quality. However, the reviewers identify substantial concerns about novelty, data leakage, missing baselines, insufficient motivation of design choices, and the absence of RL experiments.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles an important challenge—incorporating reward model uncertainty into policy learning (Reviewer 3).
- It is generally well written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 3), and it is honest about limitations without overselling (Reviewer 1).
- Reviewer 2 found the results on RewardBench nice and the figures well done, and specifically praised Figure 5 (higher variance for out-of-distribution data than in-distribution data with similar means) as encouraging for uncertainty quantification. This positive view is more favorable than that of the other reviewers.
- Reviewer 1 liked the experiments in Section 5.2.3.

### Weaknesses
- **Data leakage**: The Skywork-Reward-Preference-80k dataset (v0.1) used to train the gating layer overlaps with RewardBench, the evaluation benchmark. A cleaned version (v0.2) was released shortly before review. Claims of state-of-the-art performance on RewardBench need to be rerun with the corrected dataset (Reviewer 3).
- **Novelty and contribution**: The idea of uncertainty over reward models has been explored in prior work; the paper does not propose a truly novel way of parameterizing uncertainty (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 noted that learning reward weights is likely not new.
- **Minor improvements**: The performance gains over baselines in Table 1 are relatively small (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing baselines in Table 2** (generation results): No baseline without uncertainty quantification is provided, making it impossible to attribute improvement to the proposed method. The model used to produce reference answers is not specified. Baselines from Table 1 could have been reused (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Only Best-of-N, no RL experiments**: BoN results are not informative for studying overoptimization; RL exerts much stronger optimization pressure. Experiments with PPO or direct alignment would be much more compelling (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Insufficient motivation of design choices**:
    - Why sample only once from each URM? Why not use the full distribution (e.g., overlap between densities) to quantify epistemic uncertainty? (Reviewer 2)
    - Equation 7 is problematic because a single URM could wrongly give a very high or very low reward while others are correct. Similarly, Equation 8 is problematic with a URM outputting large variance (Reviewer 2).
    - The use of SELU (line 362) is not justified (Reviewer 2).
    - The selection of examples for Figure 6a and b is unclear; plotting the full uncertainty distribution would be more informative (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing experiments and ablations**:
    - Need to disentangle the benefit of ensembling from that of uncertainty quantification. Evaluate individual URMs from URME (Reviewer 2).
    - Compare learned gating weights to uniform, expert, or random weights (Reviewer 2).
    - Study the correlation between high uncertainty (from URM/URME) and loss/evaluation of answers (Reviewer 2).
    - Report performance for different numbers of URMs in URME (Reviewer 2).
    - Show best-of-1 win rates for higher N and whether improvement saturates (Reviewer 2).
    - Could reward weights be learned jointly with the individual URMs? (Reviewer 2; overlaps with missing experiment.)
- **Insufficient explanation of the Bradley-Terry uncertainty limitation**: The paper should state more clearly that the difference between r_w and r_l drives the loss to zero, so sigma (the variance) cannot be accurately learned (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 questioned whether this issue is fundamental to the BT loss or only true for symmetric distributions like Normal (asymmetric distributions like Gumbel might work). More discussion is needed.
- **Why only aleatoric uncertainty?** The claim that only aleatoric (not epistemic) uncertainty is captured by the probabilistic value head is not substantiated and appears false on the surface (Reviewer 1).
- **Clarity and writing issues**:
    - The introduction does not discuss RLHF (Reviewer 2).
    - The three-stage description (SFT, RM, PPO) is misleading; PPO is only one instance of RLHF methods (DPO, REINFORCE, etc. should be mentioned) (Reviewer 2).
    - The PPO description is insufficient (clipped loss, GAE, etc. are left out) (Reviewer 2).
    - Writing is sub-par in places (multiple examples given, e.g., “log probility”, “Thourgh”, “Bradley-Terry-model loss”) (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3 for typos).
    - The transition from Bradley-Terry to multi-attribute training is jarring; more background on multi-attribute reward models and uncertainty quantification is needed. The label R in Equation 4 must be defined (Reviewer 3).
    - Terminology: “gating” is a poor name for learning coefficients of rewards (Reviewer 2).
- **Evaluation limited to one benchmark** (RewardBench). Missing assessment of an “alignment tax” on standard NLP tasks such as MMLU (Reviewer 3).
- **Filtering results not meaningful**: The claim that filtering OOD data improves reliability is not backed by quantitative results; no measure of downstream impact is provided (Reviewer 3).
- **Typographical and formatting issues**:
    - Section 5.2.2 title “Ablation” uses backwards quotation marks (Reviewer 3).
    - Line 490 should include a pointer to the earlier-described validation set (Reviewer 3).
    - Table 1 should label models as multi-attribute, BT, or generative, and bold the best result in each column (Reviewer 3).
    - Figure 2 should use a legend for colored lines instead of arrows (Reviewer 3).
    - “parameter$\alpha$” missing a space (line 280) (Reviewer 3).
    - Appendix not included in the same PDF as the main paper (Reviewer 1).
- **Remaining questions** (overlap with criticisms or missing experiments):
    - How is aleatoric vs. epistemic uncertainty distinguished? The offline vs. online RL distinction is unclear (Reviewer 3).
    - To what extent does preference stochasticity arise from true randomness vs. partial observability? (Reviewer 3)
    - Could the reward weights be learned jointly with the individual URMs? (Reviewer 2)
