## Merged Review

### Summary

This work introduces ChannelViT, a modification of the Vision Transformer (ViT) for multi-channel imaging (e.g., microscopy, satellite). The method splits an image into patch tokens independently per channel and adds a learnable channel embedding. Hierarchical Channel Sampling (HCS) is proposed as an additional regularizer to improve robustness when only a subset of channels is available at test time. ChannelViT is evaluated on ImageNet, JUMP-CP (microscopy), and So2Sat (satellite) and is shown to outperform standard ViT on classification tasks and generalize well with partial channels. Reviewers generally agree on the well-motivated problem and effectiveness of ChannelViT and HCS, but some found the novelty limited and the evaluation lacking in certain areas such as baselines, scaling, and dataset relevance. Minor reviewers (scores 5,5) were more critical of novelty and evaluation methodology; major reviewers (scores 8,8) were more positive but still noted missing baselines, computational cost, and biological relevance concerns.

### Strengths

- **Well-motivated problem and application.** The work targets an important and realistic scenario in multiplexed imaging and satellite sensing where channels carry semantically independent information and may be partially missing. This has potential for enabling biological/clinical findings.
- **Simple and effective method.** Decoupling channels into separate tokens with learnable channel embeddings is an intuitive extension. The method outperforms standard ViT in almost all experiments and shows consistent improvement on JUMP-CP with partial channels (Table 2, Figure 4).
- **HCS as a powerful regularizer.** Hierarchical Channel Sampling enhances robustness to missing channels and is effective even when applied to standard ViT, suggesting it can serve as a generic regularization technique independent of architecture.
- **Extensive ablation and analysis.** Thoughtful experiments cover partial-channel settings, DINO pretraining compatibility, different model variants (ViT-S/16, ViT-S/8), and qualitative attention map analysis that demonstrates improved explainability. Most claims are supported by experiments or ablations in the main text or appendix.
- **Diverse evaluation.** Evaluation spans natural images (ImageNet), microscopy (JUMP-CP, C17-WILDS), and satellite (So2Sat), providing evidence of general applicability.
- **Well-organized presentation.** The paper is easy to read with clear illustrations and tables.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty.** The core modification is separate processing of channels, which resembles multi-modal tokenization and input channel dropout techniques already known (R1, R4). The method does not significantly diverge from the standard ViT framework, and HCS is a variant of input channel dropout. The novelty is considered fair to moderate by most reviewers.
- **Missing baselines and comparisons.** The paper compares only to vanilla ViT. No comparisons to established CNN architectures (e.g., ResNet variants) or other transformer-based methods adapted for multi-channel data are provided, making it difficult to assess relative performance (R3). For multiplexed imaging, label-free approaches (channel synthesis) should be discussed and compared (R2). State-of-the-art baselines on the specific datasets (e.g., C17-WILDS shows >95% accuracy with ViT-B/16) are not contextualized (R2).
- **Computational cost is underexplored.** Training cost scales linearly with the number of channels (as reported in appendix), and the attention complexity is quadratic in the number of tokens (which now includes channels). This cost is not analyzed in the main text (R3). The DINO experiment shows training is ~3.64× more expensive than the baseline (R2). The quadratic increase can be prohibitive in practice with many channels (R3, R4).
- **No evaluation at larger model scales.** Only ViT-Small variants are tested. Experiments with ViT-B and ViT-L are absent, and the title parallels the original ViT paper without demonstrating scalability (R2). The efficiency concern may be the reason for avoiding larger models (R2).
- **Evaluation protocol issues.** ImageNet results for ViT-S/16 with DINO linear probing (72.62%) and supervised training (71.49%) are substantially lower than published results (77.0% and 79.8%, respectively). This suggests the linear probe protocol may not follow recommended practices, undermining the claim that ChannelViT surpasses standard ViT in full-channel scenarios (R2).
- **Dataset choice and relevance.** Only one dataset (JUMP-CP) directly targets multiplexed imaging; more relevant benchmarks (RXRX1, TissueNet) should be used (R2). C17-WILDS is H&E pathology, not a multi-channel problem unless stains are separated (R2). The JUMP-CP task (treatment prediction) is a pretext representation-learning task, not a biologically relevant endpoint; a more meaningful task (e.g., cell type classification) would strengthen evaluation (R3). So2Sat is satellite, but the overall selection of out-of-domain tasks could better highlight ChannelViT’s strengths (R2).
- **Potential information loss from independent channel processing.** Splitting input into separate channels and processing them independently may disrupt spatial coherence and cross-channel alignment. The patch embedding does not account for correlations between channels, and the reassembly method after processing is not clearly described (R1).
- **Lack of theoretical justification for HCS equity claim.** The claim that HCS “guarantees equitable sampling across each m” is empirically supported but lacks intuitive or theoretical justification; uniform sampling of m may not equally represent all channel combinations (R1).
- **Missing experimental details.** Table 3: the random selection of images with varying channel counts should be repeated with different partitions and standard deviation reported (R3). Figure 6 lacks error bars and the procedure is unclear (R3). Figure 5 has a swapped label (KRAS and KCNH76 rows) (R3). Minor typos (demonstrats, utiliz, involvs) (R3).
- **Failure to discuss related ViT adaptations for video/3D.** Existing ViT extensions that increase input dimensions along a new axis (e.g., video, 3D) are not discussed; the paper should compare and justify why those are not suitable for channels (R3).
- **Lack of clarification on HCS during testing.** It is not explicitly stated whether HCS is used at test time or how channels are selected for partial-channel testing (Table 2). If only a subset of combinations is used, mean and standard deviation across combinations should be reported (R1).
- **Gains limited to data with many channels.** The improvement is most evident for multi-channel data; the method offers less benefit for conventional three-channel images, while incurring significantly higher processing time (R4).
- **Terminology “hierarchical” not clearly motivated.** The name “Hierarchical Channel Sampling” is not intuitively justified—the sampling process does not appear hierarchical in the usual sense (R4).
