## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a continuous loss function called 'tube loss' for prediction interval (PI) estimation. The loss is piecewise linear (resembling an extension of Huber loss with four pieces) and simultaneously learns upper and lower bounds of the PI, minimizing width while aiming to cover a target fraction of response values. The authors provide theoretical asymptotic guarantees and evaluate the method on synthetic data, kernel machines, LSTMs, and real-world time series (ocean wave height, plus benchmark datasets from Pearce et al. 2018). The main claim is that tube loss yields narrower and more accurate PIs compared to existing methods.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles an important problem: estimating reliable prediction intervals.
- The proposed loss function appears interesting and is continuous and differentiable almost everywhere, enabling the use of modern optimization algorithms.
- Simultaneously learning both boundaries of the prediction interval allows minimizing the mean interval width in a single objective, which is simpler than existing approaches.
- The tuning parameter \(r\) lets the PI tube capture denser regions of the response, potentially sharpening the width, especially for skewed distributions.
- Experiments show improvements over the QD loss from Pearce et al. 2018 (Reviewer 1).
- Reviewer 3 notes the prediction interval estimation problem is relatively significant.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and motivation**: The loss function is a modification of the Huber loss (four pieces instead of two) and is not technically novel (Reviewer 1). The loss is not well motivated; it appears arbitrary, and the paper fails to explain why this particular form is chosen (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing baselines and literature**: No comparison or discussion of quantile regression, the de facto approach for producing confidence intervals (Reviewer 2). The only baseline is a single paper from 2018 (Pearce et al.), which is weak (Reviewer 1). The paper lacks a section on related work, making it hard to discern the challenges and contributions (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited real data experiments**: Experiments are mostly on synthetic data (Reviewer 1). Real data experiments are limited and do not indicate clear superiority (Reviewer 2). The ocean wave height dataset has no baseline comparisons (Reviewer 1). The skewed distribution claim is not clearly shown in experiments (Reviewer 4).
- **Table 3 interpretation and disagreement**: The proposed method does not consistently outperform QD loss. On Concrete and Energy datasets, QD achieves similar coverage but significantly lower width, indicating QD is better (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 4 also notes that on Energy, QD has better MPIW. The paper’s claim that QD does not reach desired coverage is inaccurate for these datasets (Reviewer 2). On other datasets, coverage is different, making the methods not comparable rather than tube loss being better (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 also found Table 3 unclear.
- **Writing and organization**: The paper is poorly written and badly organized (Reviewer 2). Multiple reviewers note that the writing does not meet academic publication standards (Reviewer 3). Symbols and concepts are not properly introduced (e.g., \(E\) and \(\mu\) in the first paragraph) (Reviewer 3). Equations contain undefined variables (e.g., Eq. (8) has no index \(i\) in the second summation and unclear \(l\)) (Reviewer 4). Figures lack captions (Figures 6, 7) or are not referenced in the main text (Figure 1) (Reviewer 4). The proofs and Page 6 do not contribute much and would be better replaced with real experiments (Reviewer 2).
- **Optimization and tuning**: The optimization is two-stage, and the parameters \(r\) and \(\lambda\) require manual tuning without automatic determination, relying on human experience (Reviewer 4). How many iterations are used and how to find a proper \(r\) for a new dataset are not specified (Reviewer 4).
- **Experimental details missing**: Section 4.2 does not describe the neural network architecture used (Reviewer 4). The title of Section 4.3 needs revision (Reviewer 4). The experimental section is cursory and lacks detailed descriptions (Reviewer 3).
- **Reviewer 2 (score 1, confidence 5) is strongly negative**: This reviewer considers the paper not up to conference standard due to missing quantile regression baselines, poor writing, and failure of experiments to demonstrate advantage. The other three reviewers gave scores of 3 (fair) with lower confidence, indicating they were less certain. Reviewer 4 rated contribution as 3 (good) but presentation as 2 (fair).
