## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies out-of-distribution (OOD) inverse problems where a diffusion model trained on one distribution is used to reconstruct images from a different test distribution. Two settings are considered: (1) only a single measurement from the test distribution is available, and (2) a very small sample from the test distribution is available. The authors propose using a patch-based diffusion prior (building on DDIP and PaDIS) with a self-supervised loss for the single-measurement case. Experiments on CT and natural image datasets show that patch-based models are more robust to distribution shifts than whole-image models. One reviewer (R2) found the writing clear, while others (R1, R5) raised significant clarity issues; overall the contribution and experimental evaluation drew strong criticism.

### Strengths
- The problem of handling mismatched training and test distributions in inverse problems is important and timely, with clear practical relevance. [R1, R2, R3, R4, R5]
- The key idea of using patch-based diffusion models instead of whole-image models is intuitive and sensible; patches are less prone to overfitting and memorization of the source distribution, making them naturally suited for OOD settings. [R1, R2, R3, R5]
- Experimental results demonstrate that the patch-based approach achieves better reconstructions than whole-image models in both the single-measurement and small-sample settings, showing its effectiveness. [R4, R5]
- The paper is well motivated and addresses an important practical problem. [R2] (Note: R2 found writing clear and motivation reasonable, in contrast to other reviewers who noted clarity issues.)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and insufficient distinction from prior work.** The proposed method combines existing components (DDIP/SCD adaptation with the PaDIS patch-based diffusion model), which constitutes only a replacement of the whole-image backbone. The finding that patch-based models are more robust to OOD data and avoid memorization/overfitting was already documented in prior work on patch-based diffusion (e.g., [1] in R3, [1] in R1). The paper does not clearly articulate what is novel beyond this combination and lacks a clear, unique contribution. [R1, R3, R4]  
- **Lack of theoretical analysis.** There is no theoretical justification for why patch-based models generalize better under distribution shifts, nor a clear intuition for the design of Algorithm 1. The argument would be stronger with a theoretical framework explaining the generalization properties. [R3, R4]  
- **Clarity and presentation issues, especially in Section 3.1.** The description of patch extraction and score fusion (Eq. 8) is hard to follow: the roles of \(M\) and \(k\) are not defined before use, the outer product range is ambiguous (should be \(P^2\), not \(M^2\)), and the patch offset is not given two indices. The bordering region is introduced without prior explanation. Figure 1 and text in Section 3 appear to be copied from the original PaDIS paper with minimal adaptation and without clear attribution; it should be repainted and properly cited. Equations 2 and 3 contain errors. [R1, R3, R5]  
- **Unclear explanation of how Eq. (11) addresses distribution mismatch.** The loss in Eq. (11) only enforces data consistency with the measurement; the connection between minimizing this term and altering the learned prior to accommodate a different test distribution is not explained. More detailed justification is needed. [R2]  
- **Experimental setup is insufficiently explained and lacks statistical rigor.** The results in Table 1 are reported on only 10 samples, which is too few to be statistically meaningful. The paper does not state which sampling algorithm is used for the "Whole image, correct*" model, nor how key hypothetical baselines are constructed. "Best baselines" in Table 2 is undefined. The discussion of diversity (e.g., "some unrealistic features" in Fig. 7) is vague and unsupported by quantitative diversity metrics (e.g., Recall). It is counter-intuitive that the proposed method, without training on test data, outperforms diffusion approaches that leverage training data. [R5]  
- **Missing important baselines.** The paper does not compare with strong diffusion-based inverse problem solvers that are relevant in OOD settings, such as DPS, DDS, DDNM, and DAVI. In particular, DAVI (Ref. [4] in R4) specifically addresses OOD settings and should be included as a benchmark. [R4]  
- **Relationship to prior experiments on small datasets is unclear.** The appendix of [1] (PaDIS paper) already provides experiments on training patch-based and whole-image models on different dataset sizes and observes that PaDIS remains visually more consistent. The paper should elaborate how its OOD experiments relate to and complement those results. [R1, R3]  
- **Scalability concerns.** Experiments are limited to CelebA 256×256; patch-based models are intended for large images, so experiments on higher resolutions would be needed to demonstrate scalability. [R3]  
- **Missing experimental details and metrics.** The paper should report perceptual metrics such as LPIPS, especially for deblurring and super-resolution. The dataset used for Figures 4 and 5 is not specified. Table 3 is placed in the appendix but referenced in the main text; the discussion of that table should either be moved to the appendix or the table should be brought into the main paper. [R5]  
- **Figure 6 uses inconsistent notation compared to Figure 3, causing confusion.** [R3]
