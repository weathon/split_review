## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes BALD, a framework for backdoor attacks on LLM-based decision-making in embodied AI (autonomous driving, home robotics). It introduces three attack mechanisms: word injection, scenario manipulation, and knowledge injection. Experiments on GPT-3.5, LLaMA2, PaLM2 over HighwayEnv, nuScenes, VirtualHome show near-100% success rates for word/knowledge injection and 65–90% for scenario manipulation while preserving benign performance. Two reviewers (scores 8,8) highlight the contribution as novel and important; a third (score 6) finds it open and relevant but not fully thorough; a fourth (score 3) questions technical novelty and real-world applicability, calling the threat model imaginary.

### Strengths
- First comprehensive framework (BALD) for backdoor vulnerabilities in LLM-based decision-making for embodied agents. (R1, R2, R4)
- Three systematic attack mechanisms (word injection, scenario manipulation, knowledge injection) covering diverse pipeline components. (R1, R3)
- Extensive evaluation across multiple LLMs (GPT-3.5, LLaMA2, PaLM2) and platforms (HighwayEnv, nuScenes, VirtualHome) with solid results and validated conclusions. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Real-world relevance: misbehaviors in autonomous driving and robotics (e.g., accelerating toward obstacles, placing knives on beds) highlight critical safety risks. (R1)
- Clear methodology with diagrams, data preparation details, and structured experiments improving reproducibility. (R1, R2)
- Benign performance unaffected, demonstrating stealthiness. (R4)
- Discussion format in experimental section provides valuable insights for the community. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Defense analysis is limited.** Only tested benign demonstrations in in-context learning and ONION (word outlier removal), both old defenses (newest from 2021). Missing evaluation against more robust strategies: input sanitization, adversarial training specifically adapted for LLMs, dataset sanitization, or newer backdoor defenses. (R1, R4; Q7 from R1; Q3 from R4)
- **Narrow comparison baseline.** Only compared to BadChain; no wider SOTA comparison (e.g., AgentPoison). (R4; Q1 from R4)
- **Missing evaluation on more recent LLMs.** No experiments on GPT-4, GPT-4o, Gemini, or advanced multimodal models. (R2, R4; Q2 from R4)
- **Lack of model-specific vulnerability analysis.** No discussion of how architectural differences (attention mechanisms, depth, pre-training data, open-source vs. proprietary) affect susceptibility. (R1)
- **Long-term effectiveness not considered.** The study does not address whether model updates, adaptive learning, or defenses over time could reduce attack efficacy. (R1)
- **Scenario manipulation relies on fine-grained context.** Success depends on precise environmental descriptions (e.g., “red Mazda CX-5 with hazard lights”); reproducibility in less controlled settings is uncertain. (R1)
- **Lack of cross-domain generalization discussion.** No explicit discussion of applicability to high-stakes domains beyond driving/robotics, such as healthcare or industrial automation. (R1)
- **Minority view (Reviewer 3): threat model is imaginary.** Embodied AI is still a concept; real-world validation is absent, and operating vehicles via LLMs is currently impractical. The motivating scenario does not match current deployment. (R3)
- **Minority view (Reviewer 3): limited technical novelty.** Attack strategies share similarities with existing adversarial/backdoor literature; the contribution is systematic categorization, not new techniques. (R3)
- **No real-world or physical robot experiments.** All evaluations are in simulation, leaving open questions about practical feasibility and challenges (e.g., physical hardware). (R4; Q4 from R4)
- **Suggestions for future work** (not necessarily weaknesses but requested improvements):
  - Explore complex visual backdoor injection (patches, object combinations, viewpoints) beyond text modality. (R2)
  - Evaluate on physical robotic systems and identify anticipated challenges. (R4)
  - For BALD-scene: consider adversarial attacks against the VLM stage; address multi-modal LLMs that take images directly (no text insertion point). (R1, Q2 and Q3)
  - Clarify whether BALD-word differs from classical backdoor poisoning (just pairing trigger with malicious output) vs. existing agent-poisoning work. (R1, Q1)
  - Explain logical consistency of retrieval rate and ASR calculation in nuScenes (why 100% ASR despite low retrieval? Should ASR = retrieval_rate × conditional_ASR?). (R1, Q6)
  - Discuss higher FAR for PaLM2. (R1, Q5)
- **Minor issues:** Typo “resilience” → “resilient” (line 508). Figure 1 clarity could distinguish word injection and knowledge injection more clearly. (R4)
