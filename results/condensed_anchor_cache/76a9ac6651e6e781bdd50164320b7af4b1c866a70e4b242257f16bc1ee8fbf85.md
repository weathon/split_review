## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Outcome-Driven Action Flexibility (ODAF), an offline RL method that shifts constraints from actions to the consequences (subsequent states) of actions. A reward mechanism evaluates whether successor states meet performance requirements (e.g., safety) rather than whether actions imitate the behavior policy. The method includes a novel Bellman operator, theoretical convergence analysis, and empirical evaluation on D4RL and PointMaze using uncertainty quantification techniques. The paper is supported by theoretical and experimental results (R1), and the outcome-driven concept is intriguing (R5). However, several reviewers raise concerns about presentation, theoretical clarity, evaluation rigor, and practical novelty.

### Strengths
- **Motivation and novelty**: The idea of constraining consequences rather than actions is relatively new and promising (R1); it can be seen as a relaxation of action-based constraints (R2). The comparisons between ODAF, action-supported methods, and pessimistic constraints are insightful (R5).
- **Theoretical foundation**: Lemma 1 establishes contraction of the outcome-driven Bellman operator; Theorem 1 provides a lower bound for the learned Q function (R3, R4). The theoretical analysis and performance bounds are well-grounded (R4). The paper includes a sizable amount of theoretical analysis (R2).
- **Empirical performance on D4RL**: ODAF achieves 87.9 on all 15 D4RL tasks and ~81.7 on 12 non-expert tasks (R5); good empirical performance overall (R1). The experiment in §5.1 is easy to reason about (R2).
- **Trajectory stitching**: ODAF’s focus on outcomes enables effective combination of action segments from sub-optimal trajectories, a real challenge in offline RL (R3).
- **Design and experiments**: Integrating performance measurement into state uncertainty is a good way to optimize policy (R4). The experimental design (including detailed appendices) is well-executed (R5), and experiments effectively illustrate the design of ODAF (R4). Many baselines are compared (R2).
- **Detailed supplementary material**: The code and appendices provide substantial detail (R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation clarity**: Using SAC-N as the base algorithm already addresses distribution shift, making it difficult to demonstrate whether ODAF itself resolves that issue (R1). No direct theoretical results (e.g., performance guarantees) are provided to show ODAF is better than action-constraint methods (R1).
- **Theoretical analyses issues**:
  - Dense and unclear: The analyses in §4.1 are hard to follow; it is unclear what the final goal is at each step (R2). A high-level outline is needed, and analyses should be separated from algorithm design (R2).
  - Theorem 1 bounds the distance between learned and optimal Q, but requires N→∞ and full coverage (d^{π_β}(s,a)>0) for convergence to zero—conditions under which standard Q-learning also converges, trivializing the ORL problem. The condition in Corollary 1 (sup_s d^{π*}(s)/d^{π_β}(s) ≤ C) is not enough to reduce the distance to zero; a concrete counterexample is provided (R2). How will the analyses change without full coverage (the actual ORL setting)? (R2)
  - Assumption 1 for Theorem 2 is not correctly presented (R1).
  - Discussion 1: It is unclear how Eq.8 leads to the argument in line 228; also, the action support set not satisfying the consequence set may imply the consequence set can be stricter (R1).
  - Notation and proof errors: Eq.6 last term should be Q(s',a'), not just Q(s') (R2, R5). In Eq.6, the expectation term on the RHS does not include s' (R2). δ is not stated in Theorem 1 (R2). Eq.25 typo: the first term should use hat{T}, not T (R2). L136 uses hat{Q} but equations use Q; L138 inappropriately writes P(r|s,a) (R5). In Lemma 1 proof, u and v should be in the state-action value space (R5). Notations for U^π(s) and perturbation ball B_s^{ε_odaf} are inconsistent between Section 4.2 and Appendix (R5).
- **Practical algorithm novelty**: Despite lengthy theoretical proofs, the practical algorithm is relatively simple; it builds heavily on RORL with an additional pretrained VAE environment model and an auxiliary ODAF loss (R5). The derivation of the optimization objective (Eq.10) is abrupt, and the constant β_ssb is introduced without explanation; intermediate steps should be in the Appendix (R5).
- **Evaluation rigor and tuning**:
  - Hyperparameters were tuned separately per environment; the tuning procedure (especially how test-set evaluation was avoided) is unclear (R2). How was ODAF tuned per environment? (R2)
  - Confidence intervals are missing for baselines in Tables 1 and 2 and Figure 3, making comparisons uninformative (R2, R3). Bold font is used without statistical significance; many results (e.g., half-cheetah m-e, hopper m, walker2d m/m-e/m-r/r) are not statistically significant; SVR scores better on walker2d m-r; in medium-diverse/large-diverse rows of Table 2 baselines outperform ODAF but ODAF is still bolded (R2). Please include confidence margins and report how many results are statistically significant (R2).
  - MBRCSL, the closest baseline in §5.1, is not compared in §5.2 and §5.3; the reason is not justified (R2). Baselines are inconsistent across Tables 1 and 2 (R3).
  - RORL is not compared in the PointMaze and AntMaze experiments; why? (R5)
- **Missing comparisons and related work**: A recent work [1] also constrains consequences (next states) rather than actions to address distribution shift; the idea is very similar but algorithms differ; a comparison should be included (R1). All baselines (SPOT, OSR, COMBO, etc.) should be discussed in the Related Work section in their appropriate subsections (R3). Would other model-based methods like MOREC contribute further improvements to ODAF? (R5)
- **Implementation details insufficient**:
  - Specific choices of uncertainty estimation method, adversarial attack mechanisms, and hyperparameters are not fully detailed (R3). 
  - Sensitivity analysis for the ODAF weight is only in the appendix; should be in the main paper along with discussion of other hyperparameters (R3). Are there specific hyperparameter settings crucial for good results, or is ODAF generally robust? (R3) How sensitive is performance to the choice of uncertainty estimation method? (R3)
- **Limitations not adequately discussed**:
  - The acknowledged limitation of generalizing to states far from the dataset is brief and lacks concrete examples, conditions for failure, or potential mitigation strategies (R3). The limitation should be discussed in the main paper (R4). 
  - Generalization with small sample sizes and inaccurate pretrained dynamics model is not addressed; how much performance degradation occurs? (R4) Are there modifications to mitigate this? (R3)
- **Presentation and writing quality**:
  - Major grammatical errors: line 140 sentence never ends; line 403 "MuJoCo (D4RL)" is redundant (R3). 
  - Narration is overly complicated, making the paper hard to follow (R3). 
  - Poor presentation overall; text in Section 3 overlaps with prior work (RORL and SVR) (R5). 
  - Reference formatting issues: incomplete citation for RORL (NeurIPS 2022), duplicate references for "Behavior regularized offline reinforcement learning" and "Is pessimism provably efficient", inconsistent format across references (R5).
- **Missing experiment information**: The range (standard deviation, confidence interval, etc.) is not stated for ODAF’s results in any table or figure; baselines also lack this information (R2, R3). Performance on other D4RL domains (Maze2D, Adroit) is not evaluated; would the outcome-driven approach need modifications there? (R3)
