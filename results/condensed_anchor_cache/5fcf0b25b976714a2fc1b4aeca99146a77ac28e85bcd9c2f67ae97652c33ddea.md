## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper identifies an interpretability illusion in subspace activation patching methods: even when a subspace intervention appears to change a model’s output as if a specific feature were altered, the effect may actually arise from activating a dormant parallel pathway that is causally disconnected from the feature of interest. The illusion is demonstrated in a distilled mathematical example, in the indirect object identification (IOI) task, and in factual recall (including a link to rank‑1 fact editing and inconsistencies noted by Hase et al. [2]). The paper also shows a success case in IOI where manual circuit analysis can avoid the illusion. All three reviewers recognize the timeliness and importance of the work, though one reviewer (Reviewer 2, score 3) has strong concerns about the paper’s scientific framing, assumptions, and presentation, while the other two (Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 3, both score 8) consider it highly significant and well‑executed.

### Strengths
- Highly relevant and timely: addresses a critical issue in the growing field of mechanistic interpretability (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- The interpretability illusion is well motivated, clearly introduced, and intuitively framed using the concepts of dormant and causally disconnected pathways (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- The formal definition of the illusion (p. 5) is sound, and the paper argues convincingly that the illusion is not a mere artifact of specific experimental settings but can occur with high probability (Reviewer 1).
- The experimental design for demonstrating the illusion is well‑crafted (Reviewer 1).
- The discussion on how to prevent the interpretability fallacy (e.g., through manual circuit analysis) is important, sound, and paves the way for future work (Reviewer 1).
- The mechanistic viewpoint explaining the presence of the illusion is sound (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is clearly written, well‑structured, and figures help illustrate the concepts (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- The paper is significant both for identifying a practical problem and for developing deeper intuitions about interpretability methods (Reviewer 3).
- Even the more critical reviewer (Reviewer 2) acknowledges that the hypothesis that subspace activation patching can be misleading because of dormant pathways is an interesting one that needs to be considered for future applications.

### Weaknesses
- **Overly strict definitions and unvalidated assumptions**: The definitions of “causally disconnected” (requiring exact kernel membership) and “dormant” (requiring exactly zero effect from patching) are brittle and unlikely to hold in practice due to numerical imprecision or non‑linearities. The paper does not justify the implicit assumption of linear behavior along the decomposed subspaces (i.e., that the output effect adds linearly from “disconnected” and “dormant” directions). Both issues seriously limit the applicability of the theoretical framework (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3; Reviewer 2 gave Soundness 1).
- **Missing falsifiable hypothesis and unclear implications**: The paper does not present an explicit, falsifiable hypothesis, reducing its scientific validity. It is unclear what empirical predictions could distinguish a genuine subspace from an illusory one (Reviewer 2). Similarly, Reviewer 3 asks for a set of empirical predictions (e.g., about activation or gradient directions) that could support or falsify the theoretical model.
- **Weak connection between theory and experiments**: The theoretical decomposition of a subspace into disconnected and dormant parts is not explicitly linked to the experimental case studies (IOI, factual recall). Terms like “disconnected”, “dormant”, “ker W_out”, “ABB”, “BAB”, and “connected” in Table 1 are introduced without clear definitions in the main text, making it difficult to see how the experiments verify the hypothesis (Reviewer 2).
- **Main paper not self‑contained**: The core arguments and experimental details rely heavily on the appendix, hindering assessment of the claims (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing comparison with component‑level activation patching**: The paper focuses solely on subspace activation patching but does not clarify whether the identified illusion also holds for standard (component‑level) activation patching. The toy example in Appendix A.3 uses usual activation patching, yet the messaging is specific to subspace patching (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- **Omitted code and presentation issues**: No code is provided (Reviewer 1). The writing in sections 4–7 is dense and sloppy; for example, “ker W_out” is never defined, and the relation of results in Table 1, Figure 3, and Figure 4 to the description in Section 4.3 is unclear (Reviewer 2). Figure 1 is hard to parse despite being central (Reviewer 1). There is an error in the indices of the toy example in Appendix A.3 (Reviewer 1).
- **Relaxation of definitions needed**: Even if the illusion exists conceptually, the strict equality requirements should be relaxed to allow approximate thresholds (e.g., small effect sizes rather than zero) to make the framework practically useful (Reviewer 3).
