## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes to perform posterior sampling for inverse problems by simulating Langevin dynamics in the noise (latent) space of a pre-trained generative model, specifically a consistency model (CM) or distilled flow. The method leverages a fixed deterministic mapping from noise to data (from CM) so that the posterior in noise space is close to unimodal Gaussian, enabling MCMC exploration without restarting the full generative chain. Theoretically, a guarantee in total variation distance is provided. Experiments on image restoration (inpainting, deblurring, super-resolution) with noisy linear and nonlinear forward operators on LSUN-Bedroom (256×256) and ImageNet (64×64) demonstrate high fidelity and diversity. The authors claim low NFE and training-free inference.

### Strengths

- Well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 4, 5).
- The idea of projecting posterior sampling to noise space is good because the posterior in noise space is close to a single-modal Gaussian, which is friendly to MCMC (Reviewer 1).
- Theoretical analysis (approximation guarantee in total variation distance) is provided and welcomed (Reviewers 1, 3, 5).
- Leveraging consistency models significantly reduces sampling costs compared to previous data-prediction diffusion models (Reviewer 4). One reviewer (5) considers this the first work to perform posterior sampling in the latent space of CMs.
- The method is straightforward to implement and works on both linear and nonlinear inverse problems (Reviewer 3).
- Well-motivated problem with a good literature review (Reviewer 3).
- Experiments on ImageNet 64×64 and LSUN-Bedroom 256×256 support claims of diverse posterior sampling (Reviewer 5).
- One reviewer (4, score 6) finds the experiments solid and the idea simple but effective.
- Clarity of exposition and key results (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty**: Running MCMC in the latent space of one-step pushforward generative models is not new. Similar ideas were explored in NeuTra (Neutra-lizing Bad Geometry in HMC) and later adapted in VAEBM and “MCMC Should Mix” (Reviewers 1, 5). The contribution is a direct adaptation of posterior sampling with a CM and fixed noise (Reviewer 1). Lack of insight into efficient posterior sampling with multi-step priors (diffusion models, hierarchical VAEs) (Reviewer 5). Another reviewer (2) notes Langevin dynamics posterior sampling has been extensively studied in EBMs (cites [1]–[4] for image generation, translation, saliency) without discussion.
- **Overstated claims**: The method is *not* applicable to all types of generative priors – only implicit/pushforward models (Reviewer 5). Practically, it is *not* applicable to diffusion priors because backpropagation through thousands of neural network compositions is too expensive; adjoint methods are mentioned but no experiments presented (Reviewer 5).
- **Practical performance concerns**: Total runtime and wall-time are not reported. Backpropagation through the whole consistency model \(\phi\) at each step is costly in memory and time; NFE alone is insufficient (Reviewer 3). The warm-start step for initial noise \(z^0\) (line 294–300, Appendix B.1) makes the method not entirely training-free and its runtime should be included (Reviewer 3).
- **Diverse samples claim unsubstantiated**: The paper claims that accumulating samples leads to diversity, but does not analyze convergence or correlation between adjacent sampling steps. Starting from independent noise (as in baselines) guarantees independence (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**: Missing strong baseline FPS (Dou & Song 2024) for linear inverse problems (Reviewer 3). The authors argue that replacing DPS/LGD’s diffusion backbone with a CM backbone makes the comparison fair (line 370–374), but critics disagree: if DPS-DM and LGD-DM are faster and better, it is not unfair to include them (Reviewer 3).
- **Empirical results not fully convincing**: Some samples in the top-right row of Figure 1 appear oversaturated, potentially indicating chain instability or poor mixing (Reviewer 1).
- **Ablation missing**: No ablation on the Euler–Maruyama step size \(\tau\); it is unclear if optimal \(\tau\) is consistent across datasets and dimensions (Reviewer 5).
- **Questionable design choices**: The method assumes sampling from the CM with fixed noise, which lacks justification and may explain suboptimal quality (Reviewer 1). Why are 1-step CM results empirically better than 2-step CM? (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited scope**: Experiments are restricted to image reconstruction tasks (inpainting, deblurring, super-resolution); unclear if the method generalizes to complicated Bayesian inference or other domains (Reviewers 2, 4). One reviewer (2) asks explicitly whether it can be applied beyond image reconstruction.
- **Prior work omissions**: No discussion of efficient sampling based on molecular dynamics (e.g., diffusion recovery likelihood, flow contrastive estimation, cooperative diffusion recovery likelihood) (Reviewer 2, cites [5]–[7]).
- **Reviewer questions overlapping with weaknesses**:
  - Can the inpainting mask be shown in Figures 1 and 5? (Reviewer 1) – relates to missing experimental details.
  - What is the difference between the proposed method and an Energy-Based Model? (Reviewer 2) – relates to novelty and positioning.
  - How were posterior samples checked for distinctness? (Reviewer 5, Q1) – relates to diversity claim.
  - What are the unconditional FIDs/Inception scores of the diffusion models and consistency models used? (Reviewer 5, Q2) – missing baseline quality numbers.
  - Have the authors tried Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) for faster mixing? (Reviewer 5, Q3) – relates to unexplored alternatives.

**Note on reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (4, score 6) considers the experiments solid and the method effective, finding only minor weaknesses (lack of novelty and limited likelihood complexity). The other four reviewers (score 3) raise more fundamental concerns about novelty, performance, missing comparisons, and overstatements.
