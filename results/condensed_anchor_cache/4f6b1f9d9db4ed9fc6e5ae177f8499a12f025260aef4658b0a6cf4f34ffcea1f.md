## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper defends against unbounded unlearnable datasets (UDs) via COIN, a random pixel-based image transformation (bilinear interpolation) that disrupts class-wise multiplicative noise. The authors first propose EPD for detection, then COIN for corruption. They also design two new attacks (VUDA, HUDA) to broaden evaluation. The paper is well written and timely. Two reviewers assign low scores (3,3) and one a higher score (6), indicating strong disagreement about the contribution. The high-scoring reviewer finds the technical novelty good and the experiments convincing; the two lower-scoring reviewers raise fundamental concerns about overclaim, questionable motivation, missing analyses, and limited generalizability.

### Strengths

-   The paper is the first to specifically explore defenses against unbounded UDs, a timely and novel problem. (Reviewers 2, 3; Reviewer 1 notes good technical novelty.)
-   COIN is a new defense mechanism that employs random pixel-based transformations, adding to the arsenal against UDs. (Reviewer 2)
-   The paper is very well written, with clear algorithm details. (Reviewers 1, 3)
-   Experiments are extensive in terms of compared baselines (11 SOTA), datasets (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet subset), and architectures (Multiple). (Reviewers 1, 3; Reviewer 1 finds them convincing and comprehensive.)
-   The paper clearly explains the matrix-multiplication formulation of unbounded UDs and the motivation for using interpolation. (Reviewers 1, 3)

### Weaknesses

-   **Overclaim and misinterpretation of existing defenses.** The claim that existing defenses are not effective against unbounded UDs is inaccurate. In Table 2, ISS-J consistently surpasses COIN across all architectures (e.g., COIN accuracy 80.99% vs. ISS-J 86.37% on ResNet-18 under CUDA, and OPS: COIN 67.17% vs. ISS-J 72.24% on ResNet-18). Moreover, the OPS authors themselves have acknowledged that OPS is vulnerable to median filtering. (Reviewer 3, supported by data from Tables 1, 2)
-   **Missing explanation for COIN underperforming ISS-J on OPS while outperforming it on CUDA.** COIN is ~5% worse than ISS-J against OPS (CIFAR-10, ResNet-18) but much better against CUDA. The paper does not explain this discrepancy. (Reviewer 1)
-   **Questionable motivation and relevance of intra-class matrix inconsistency / inter-class matrix consistency.** The observation that classification requires intra-class consistency and inter-class inconsistency is trivial and already exploited by prior bounded UD defenses (e.g., orthogonal projection by Segura et al.). It is unclear why the orthogonal projection method fails on CUDA while COIN succeeds; the authors suspect COIN works because it disrupts the matrix structure of CUDA specifically, rendering the consistency metrics irrelevant. Consequently, the claimed motivation for COIN is weak. (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 1 adds that the observation is not new and essentially replicates prior findings on linear separability.)
-   **Lack of motivation for the specific COIN design.** The paper claims COIN enhances intra-class matrix inconsistency and inter-class matrix consistency, but does not explain how random matrix multiplication explicitly achieves that. The design appears tailored to CUDA’s matrix structure, raising concerns about generalizability to other possible unbounded attacks that use different matrix formulations. (Reviewer 3)
-   **Limited evaluation on ImageNet.** The ImageNet experiments use only 20 classes, 2% of the dataset, with images resized to 64×64. This is still a low-resolution, small dataset. The authors should test on ImageNet-100 at 224×224 resolution to demonstrate scalability and superiority on larger high-resolution data. (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 2 also points out missing analysis of computational overhead for larger images.)
-   **Missing evaluation against OPS on CIFAR-100.** (Reviewer 1)
-   **Missing computational overhead analysis.** The paper mentions COIN is efficient but does not provide a time complexity breakdown for each step (pixel transformations, consistency calculations). The impact of varying image size and batch size on cost is also absent. (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 2 also asks: “What is the computational overhead for the intra-class matrix inconsistency and inter-class matrix consistency, compared with the computation cost of the baselines?”)
-   **Limited generalizability to other architectures and data modalities.** Experiments only use ResNet-18 on image tasks. The paper should test on other architectures like VGG or transformer-based models, and discuss whether the pixel-based transformation is transferable to non-image data (time-series, text). (Reviewer 2)
-   **Missing tests against bounded UDs and sample-wise OPS.** In real scenarios defenders do not know the attack type. The paper does not evaluate whether COIN is effective against bounded UDs (e.g., standard OPS) or when OPS uses sample-wise perturbations. (Reviewer 1 questions; these overlap with missing experiments and are thus listed as weaknesses.)
