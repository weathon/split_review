## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces a conceptor-based framework for affine steering of LLM activations, deriving optimal linear and affine steering functions from concept-conditional covariance matrices and a Boolean algebra for composing conceptors. Experiments on 5 function vector tasks (IO, entity gender, sentiment, capitalize, present-past) show that conceptor steering outperforms standard additive steering baselines (Turner et al., 2023; Todd et al., 2024) on 3 tasks and matches on 2, across GPT-2 and Pythia models. Reviewers are mixed: one (score 3) finds the paper unclear and poorly explained, while others (scores 5,6,6) see novelty and promise but raise concerns about limited experiments, lack of error bars, potential hyperparameter advantage, missing comparisons to affine steering (Singh et al., 2024), computational cost, and insufficient justification for the optimality definition. A minority reviewer (3) also questions the significance and toy nature of the tasks.

### Strengths
- The approach offers a theoretically motivated alternative to existing additive activation steering methods (Turner et al., 2023; Todd et al., 2024). (Reviewers 2,3,4)
- Derives optimal affine steering functions from first principles, connecting conceptor theory (Jaeger, 2014) to LLM activation engineering. (Reviewers 2,4)
- Outperforms additive steering baselines on multiple function vector tasks (IO, entity gender, sentiment) as shown in Table 1 and Figures 2-3. (Reviewers 2,3,4)
- Introduces a Boolean algebra for composition of steering objectives, with promising initial results. (Reviewers 3,4)
- Bridges the gap between prior work on RNN control (conceptors) and modern LLM activation steering, providing a unified perspective. (Reviewer 2)
- The paper is well-written and easy to read (Reviewer 4) – though this is contrasted by clarity concerns from Reviewers 1 and 3.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and presentation:**  
  - Figure 1 is never referenced in the main text and lacks description (what green/yellow dots represent, difference between additive and conceptor steering). (Reviewer 1)  
  - Many terms are poorly explained: φ(s), c' in Equation (5), “guardedness”, “optimal” in line 203, motivation for relying on covariance matrix over concept function φ, difference from procedure in Todd et al. (2024). (Reviewer 1)  
  - The derivation in Section 3 is hard to follow due to numerous ill-described definitions (1-4, 8-9); it is suggested to summarize in the main text and move derivation to an appendix to build reader intuition. (Reviewer 3)  
  - Minor formatting issues: Figure 3 placement (Reviewer 2); line 184: “→” instead of “↦”, line 230: h → H. (Reviewer 1)  
- **Incremental improvement over baselines:**  
  - In Table 1, conceptors do not meaningfully outperform addition baselines on 2 of 5 tasks (capitalize, present-past). On the other 3 tasks, a substantial fraction of improvement is due to mean-centering (compare Addition-MC with Addition). The benefit of affine over linear conceptors is negligible. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Potential hyperparameter advantage:**  
  - Conceptor steering introduces an “aperture” parameter optimized per dataset, while the additive baseline has no such parameter. A single global aperture across tasks should be used for fair comparison, and the effect of non-optimal aperture must be discussed. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Missing comparisons:**  
  - The paper claims to unify affine steering functions (Singh et al., 2024) and additive steering (Turner et al., 2023), but experiments only compare to additive baselines; affine steering baselines are absent. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Limited experiment scope:**  
  - Only 5 function vector tasks are tested; tasks are toyish and not representative of realistic alignment use cases. (Reviewers 1,3,4)  
  - Additional tasks needed: persona tasks (e.g., arXiv:2312.06681, arXiv:2407.12404) and generative style-based tasks. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Only outdated, small open models (GPT-2, Pythia) are used; evaluation on larger recent models (e.g., Llama) is missing. (Reviewer 2)  
  - No measurement of general performance degradation from steering; this is important because conceptors apply a more substantial transformation than additive steering (see Anthropic’s evaluating feature steering). (Reviewer 4)  
  - No error bars are reported in any plots or table; variation across runs should be shown. (Reviewer 2)  
- **Computational cost concerns:**  
  - The method may be more expensive than additive steering; a big-O computational complexity analysis is needed, and cost may become prohibitive with larger models. (Reviewers 2,4)  
- **Unclear justification and motivation for method design:**  
  - Why is Jaeger’s definition of optimal conceptor the correct one for activation steering? (Reviewer 4)  
  - The motivation for the optimization problem in Definition 4 is unclear: why steer toward H_c ≈ CH_c, and why minimize ∥C∥? (Reviewer 3)  
  - The interpretation of conceptor steering as projecting activations into an ellipsoid is not justified in terms of the language model’s representational geometry. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Lack of central takeaway insight:**  
  - Unlike the function vectors paper (Todd et al., 2024), which yielded the crisp insight that “language models represent concepts as vectors,” this paper does not provide a similarly clear geometric insight about the representation space. (Reviewer 3)
