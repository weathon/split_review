## Merged Review

### Summary
MQL4GRec transforms item content from multiple domains and modalities (text, images) into a unified “quantitative language” via quantitative translators (e.g., RQ-VAE per modality) and enriches it through generation tasks (masked item token prediction, multi-modal similarity alignment). This shared vocabulary aims to bridge the gap between PLMs’ general linguistic knowledge and recommendation-specific needs, enabling cross-domain and cross-modal knowledge transfer. Extensive experiments on three datasets (with improvements over baselines by 11.18%, 14.82%, and 7.95% on NDCG) and ablation studies demonstrate effectiveness. Code is open-source.

### Strengths
- **Innovative concept**: First proposal of a unified quantitative language to integrate multimodal content into a shared vocabulary for generative recommendation (R1, R2, R3, R4). This addresses knowledge transfer from different domains and modalities (R1, R2, R3).
- **Elegant and effective architecture**: Straightforward design (quantitative translators + generation tasks + pre-training/fine-tuning) yet yields strong performance (R1, R4). One reviewer particularly appreciates this type of work and believes it can make a meaningful impact (R4).
- **Superior performance**: Outperforms baselines on NDCG across all datasets (up to +11.18%, +14.82%, +7.95%) (R1, R2). Experiments include thorough ablation studies (R3) and demonstrate robust improvement (R1, R2, R4).
- **Well-structured pre-training**: Includes both generative prediction and alignment tasks, enhancing robustness and multimodal semantics (R1).
- **Open-source code**: Code repository provided (R2, R4), enhancing reproducibility (R4). (R2 notes this as a strength.)
- **Thorough experimental validation**: Multiple datasets, ablation studies, and detailed analysis (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Computational complexity and resource analysis missing**: No discussion of training time complexity, especially for vector quantization (layers/nodes in MLP), collision resolution, and overall efficiency (R1). High complexity/storage demands could lead to significant real-world deployment costs (R3). Training costs (time, VRAM, inference) vs. baselines are not compared (R4 question → weakness).
- **Unclear item mapping from quantitative vectors**: It is not explained how generated tokens are mapped back to specific items during next-item generation (R1). The mapping algorithm/data structure is omitted, hampering reproducibility (R1).
- **No simultaneous multimodal fusion**: The model does not fuse text and image information at the input of the transformer to predict the next clicked item; modalities are processed independently and then concatenated (R1). This limits capturing cross-modal interdependencies and may yield suboptimal performance when multimodal context is crucial (R1).
- **Similarity to existing tokenization approaches**: The unified vocabulary closely resembles GenRet’s discrete auto-encoding and MMGRec’s Graph RQ-VAE (R2). RQ-VAE is applied independently to each modality without cross-modal interaction during tokenization (R2). The motivation and figures align with GenRet, diminishing originality (R2).
- **Lack of comparative analysis with MMGRec**: The paper does not clearly distinguish how MQL4GRec’s tokenization differs from MMGRec’s Graph RQ-VAE, especially regarding multimodal representation and user-item interactions, making unique contributions unclear (R2).
- **User ID collisions**: With only 2000 tokens for a large user base, collisions may cause recommendation inaccuracies (R2 question → weakness).
- **Domain transferability limitations**: Performance on the Games dataset declines with increasing pre-training data, suggesting pre-training strategy limitations for cross-domain differences (R2, R3). The model performs poorly on some domain-specific datasets (R2), and zero-shot capability is limited (R4).
- **No statistical significance testing**: Statistical significance of results is not reported (R4).
- **Incomplete related work**: The “Multi-modal Recommendation” section should include more recent state-of-the-art multimodal recommender papers (e.g., [2,3]); the paper lacks discussion on multimodal codebooks from other communities to clarify distinctions between the proposed approach and existing methods (e.g., [4,5]) (R4).
- **Incremental advance**: While valuable, the framework is rooted in the generative recommendation paradigm and aligns with a preprint in a similar direction, thus not groundbreaking (R4, minority – this reviewer still finds it valuable).
