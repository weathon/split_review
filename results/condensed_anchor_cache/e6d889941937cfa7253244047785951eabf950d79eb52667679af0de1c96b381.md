## Merged Review

### Summary
ChartBench is a large-scale benchmark for evaluating MLLM chart comprehension, covering 42 chart categories, 66.6k charts, and 600k QA pairs. It focuses on unannotated charts, forcing models to reason using visual elements (colors, legends, coordinates) rather than direct data points. The authors introduce an Acc++ evaluation metric that reduces lucky guesses by substituting values from the same metadata into negative queries. Two baselines (CoT and SFT) are provided, and 18 open-source and 3 proprietary MLLMs are evaluated. The paper is well-structured with a detailed appendix.

### Strengths
- Comprehensive dataset: 42 chart types (9 major categories), 66.6k charts, 600k QA pairs—substantially surpassing existing benchmarks like ChartQA, MMC, ChartX. Unannotated charts force visual reasoning, better mirroring real-world application scenarios. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4,5)
- Acc++ metric mitigates chance-level correct answers by generating negative queries via random value substitution from the same meta table, providing a more robust evaluation than previous metrics (e.g., Acc+ from MME). (Reviewers 1,2,4,5)
- Baseline methods (Chain-of-Thought and supervised fine-tuning) are effective and clearly demonstrate current MLLM limitations on unannotated chart reasoning. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- Well-organized manuscript and presentation; the appendix includes numerous visual examples, quality inspection, and additional experimental analyses, making the work comprehensive. (Reviewers 2,5)
- 200 question templates were employed during QA construction to ensure diversity (Reviewer 5; note: Reviewer 1 disagrees on diversity – see Weaknesses).
- The focus on an important gap in MLLM evaluation (chart comprehension lacking diverse types and proper metrics) is noteworthy. (Reviewer 4)
- Thorough experimental evaluation across 18 open-source and 3 proprietary models provides valuable insights into model capabilities and limitations. (Reviewers 2,4)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty beyond dataset construction.**  
  Acc++ is a minor modification of MME's Acc+, differing only in random replacement of ground truth values; the improvement is insufficient to stand as a core technical contribution. The CoT and SFT baselines are well-established techniques in the LLM/VLM community. The overall generation pipeline also lacks substantial novelty compared to existing benchmarks like ChartX.  
  (Reviewers 1,2,3,5; Reviewer 1 is most critical – score 5 vs. 6)
- **Dataset generation and generalization concerns.**  
  - Charts are generated via standard plotting libraries, lacking the sophisticated design elements and custom modifications found in real-world charts and infographics; this raises questions about representativeness and alignment with actual data distributions. (Reviewer 2)  
  - Heavy reliance on synthetic data: The benchmark may not effectively evaluate real-world performance, as the gap between synthetic and real distributions is large. (Reviewers 2,3)  
  - No experiments demonstrating that models trained on ChartBench's synthetic data improve performance on real-world chart tasks (e.g., fine-tuning on ChartBench and testing on ChartQA test set). (Reviewers 2,5)  
  - The significant distribution difference between ChartBench and ChartQA (Fig. 3) is likely due to the simulation-based nature of ChartBench; this should be explicitly discussed. (Reviewer 5)
- **Insufficiency of Acc++ for holistic chart understanding.**  
  Acc++ treats value extraction as binary classification, which cannot fully capture an MLLM's ability to comprehensively recognize all chart elements (colors, legends, coordinates, etc.). A more complete evaluation should assess understanding of each chart component. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing data point annotations.**  
  The intentional exclusion of data point annotations limits the benchmark's utility: (a) annotations could serve as auxiliary information for QA interpretability, and (b) they are important for chart information extraction tasks (e.g., recent GOT). The rationale for excluding them needs stronger justification. (Reviewer 5; not raised by other reviewers)
- **Question diversity concerns.**  
  Despite claiming 200 templates, the template-based generation approach still leads to limited diversity in instruction formats (as seen in Fig. 3c, where the query distribution difference between ChartBench and other benchmarks is less significant). (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 4 also asks whether explicit efforts were made to ensure diversity; Reviewer 5 considers the 200 templates a strength, indicating disagreement)
- **Missing evaluations on recent state-of-the-art VLMs.**  
  Tables 3 and 4 should include newer top-tier models such as InternVL2, Qwen-VL2, Qwen2-VL, and DeepSeek-VL for a more up-to-date comparison. (Reviewers 2,3,5)
- **Lack of model categorization in baselines.**  
  Specialized OCR/document models (e.g., mPLUG-Owl, DocOwl) should be evaluated separately from general-purpose VLMs (e.g., Qwen-VL) to better analyze performance drivers. (Reviewer 2)
- **Need for out-of-domain generalization tests.**  
  The paper does not test models' generalization to charts from completely different domains or visual styles not seen during training. (Reviewer 3)
- **Open questions about computational cost and representativeness.**  
  No comments on the compute/time required for evaluating the full benchmark, nor any suggestion of a smaller subset that could be representative. (Reviewer 4)
- **Lack of side-by-side justification against existing benchmarks.**  
  The paper does not provide clear side-by-side comparisons or examples showing how ChartBench specifically addresses the limitations of prior datasets (e.g., ChartX) in a way that yields new insights. (Reviewer 4)
- **Claim about visual grounding vs. ChartX needs clarification.**  
  Table 1 claims ChartBench is visually grounded, but ChartX also appears to be visually grounded; this requires verification and clearer differentiation. (Reviewer 1)
