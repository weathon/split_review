## Merged Review

### Summary

Reviewers acknowledge that RetCompletion applies RetNet (from NLP) to image completion, achieving fast inference (10× faster than ICT, 15× faster than RePaint). The two-stage design (Bi-RetNet for structure, CNN upsampling for texture) is clearly described and runs efficiently across mask types (Center, Expand, Half). Strengths center on speed, bidirectional design, and empirical validation. However, most reviewers (except one with rating 6) find the innovation limited because the core idea is a direct transfer of RetNet from NLP, the architecture closely resembles prior works (ICT, TFill, Vision Mamba), and comparisons are only against methods from 2022 or earlier. Quantitative improvements over older baselines are marginal; no comparison with recent SOTA (2024) such as BrushNet, Latent Code Inpainting, DiffIR, StrDiffusion, or InpaintAnything. Presentation issues include unclear equations, typos, wasted space, and confusion between pluralistic and deterministic completion. Multiple results expected for pluralistic tasks are not provided. There is a strong call for ablation studies (e.g., on Bi-RetNet) and more complex mask evaluation. One reviewer (R2) is notably more positive, praising restoration quality and practicality for real-time use.

### Strengths

- **Fast inference speed**: Achieves 10× faster than ICT, 15× faster than RePaint, and demonstrates potential for real-time applications (R1, R2, R5). Strength noted with high confidence by R2 and moderate confidence by R5.
- **Bidirectional structure (Bi-RetNet)**: Novel adaptation of RetNet for parallel training and recursive inference, integrating bidirectional contextual information without high computational burden (R2, R3). R2 and R3 both highlight this as a key contribution.
- **Empirical validation**: The paper is well-structured and easy to read; quantitative, qualitative, and inference-time results are provided, with Figure 2 showing outcomes across Center, Expand, Half masks (R1, R2, R5). R4 agrees that the method produces reasonable images on various masks.
- **Quantitative and qualitative advantages**: Outperforms most existing SOTA models (per R3, though others dispute). R2 affirms high restoration quality; R4 notes reasonable visual appearance.
- **Interesting task**: Pluralistic image completion is an important and interesting problem (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty**: Main contribution is applying RetNet (already proposed in NLP) to image completion, which most reviewers deem insufficient for ICLR standards. Architecture closely resembles ICT, TFill, and shares similarities with Vision Mamba (R1, R3, R4, R5). The motivation to transfer RetNet from NLP to CV is not well justified (R4).
- **Outdated and incomplete comparisons**: Only compared with works from 2022 or earlier. Missing recent SOTA: BrushNet, Latent Code Inpainting (CVPR 2024), DiffIR, StrDiffusion (CVPR 2024), InpaintAnything (2023). Should also compare with TFill (for deterministic results) and StrDiffusion, InpaintAnything (R1, R3, R4). Quantitative improvements over old baselines are marginal (R4).
- **Performance not state-of-the-art**: While R2 rates high quality, R1, R4 clearly state performance does not reach current SOTA. R3 claims advantages, but this is contradicted by others; the contrast must be noted. Reviewer confidence: R1 (1), R4 (5) – the more critical view has higher confidence.
- **Missing ablation study**: No ablation to assess contribution of Bi-RetNet structure (R2). Request from R2.
- **Architectural similarity / unclear distinction from prior work**: The two-stage framework is nearly identical to ICT/TFill, only replacing the transformer with RetNet; authors do not clarify differences or demonstrate effectiveness (R4). Questions about motivation (“why must we use RetNet in CV?”) (R4).
- **Pluralistic vs deterministic confusion**: Paper claims “pluralistic image completion” but does not show multiple diverse outputs; results are deterministic. If deterministic, ICT is not a good baseline; TFill would be better (R4). Related works listed are not all pluralistic (R4).
- **Poor presentation of equations**: Equations lack explanation of symbols (e.g., eq. 6: what are \(\hat{I}\) and \(I\)?); merge sign is wrong in eq. 16; subscript formatting in eq. 6; overall need rewriting (R3, R4).
- **Typos and language errors**: “?” on line 30; “It is clear demonstrate that…” (line 348-350); citation error (L29-L30: “CNN-based methods?”) (R1, R4).
- **Insufficient visual results**: In Figure 2, difference between proposed method and RePaint on faces is unclear (R5). More visual results needed (R5).
- **Wasted space in manuscript**: Page 8 and Figure 1 – space could be used more efficiently (R5).
- **Encoding via K-Means**: K-Means clustering loses high-frequency information; codebook (discrete) or Gaussian (continuous, as in latent diffusion) would be better (R4).
- **Mask diversity**: Only simple mask types tested; more complex/patterned masks needed for real-world practicality (R2).
