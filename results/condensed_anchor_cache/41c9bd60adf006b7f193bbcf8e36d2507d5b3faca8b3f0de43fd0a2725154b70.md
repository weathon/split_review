## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes UNPrompt, a zero-shot generalist Graph Anomaly Detection (GAD) approach that trains a one-for-all model on a single graph dataset and generalizes to others without retraining or fine-tuning. It uses coordinate-wise normalization to align node attribute dimensions and semantics across graphs, and neighborhood prompt learning to leverage latent node attribute predictability as an anomaly score. Reviewers note the problem is timely and challenging, but raise significant concerns about novelty, experimental rigor, baseline comparisons, and clarity. Scores: 3, 6, 3, 3 (one reviewer notably more positive at 6, confidence 4; others at 3 with confidence 4).

### Strengths
1. **Problem novelty and relevance** (Reviewers 1, 2, 4): The zero-shot generalist GAD setting is timely, challenging, and intriguing—training one model on one graph and testing on others is a valuable direction that may inspire further innovation.
2. **Method design** (Reviewer 2, Majority): The proposed UNPrompt is thoughtfully designed with a balance of sophistication and simplicity, making it accessible for future researchers. Two main components (coordinate-wise normalization and neighborhood prompt learning) are clearly presented.
3. **Experimental demonstration** (Reviewers 2, 3, 4): Experiments show that UNPrompt outperforms baselines on the datasets included, with detailed results. Reviewer 4 notes the experiments are detailed and demonstrate strong performance. Reviewer 2 says experiments across diverse scenarios demonstrate practical effectiveness and versatility.
   * **Minority positive contrast**: Reviewer 2 (score 6) considers the experiments extensive and demonstrating versatility, while other reviewers (3, 4) find datasets and baselines insufficiently diverse.*
4. **Paper organization** (Reviewers 3, 4): The paper is easy to follow and well-organized.

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient novelty / limited theoretical contribution** (Reviewers 1, 3, 4): Multiple components are not sufficiently distinguished from prior work.
   * Coordinate-wise normalization is directly taken from previous work and applied without addressing unique GAD challenges (Reviewers 3, 4). Reviewer 3 states it is “hardly considered a contribution” and asks for explanation of differences.
   * Latent Node Attribute Predictability is similar to Local Affinity in TAM (Qiao and Pang 2023) (Reviewer 2 questions this; Reviewer 3 says the anomalous score function shares a similar form). Reviewer 3 asks whether the TAM comparison in Figure 3(b) used the same hyperparameters or tuned them optimally.
   * Neighborhood Prompting follows typical graph prompt learning frameworks; Reviewer 3 asks for explanation of differences from other graph prompt learning works. Reviewer 4 notes it appears to encompass an all-in-one method [1] (reference not given in review) and does not address unique challenges of prompt learning in GAD.

2. **Weak experimental rigor and insufficient evidence for generalization** (Reviewers 1, 2, 3):
   * Only 4 datasets are evaluated (Amazon-all and YelpChi-all are extended versions of original ones), all from social networks or co-review domains. More diverse datasets (e.g., financial networks like T-Finance, large-scale like Elliptic) are needed to verify generalization (Reviewers 2, 3).
   * Baseline results are reported as suboptimal (as acknowledged in rebuttal). The dimensionality hyperparameter (chosen as 8, a heuristic from minimum 25) is arbitrary and has significant impact on results (Reviewer 1). Excessive reliance on heuristic settings for baselines undermines credibility of comparisons (Reviewer 1).
   * Missing comparisons with recent strong baselines: ARC (Liu et al 2024) for generalist GAD (Reviewer 2 asks for discussion and comparison), XGBGraph and GADAM (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 also suggests including coordinate-wise normalization for baselines to test if UNPrompt still outperforms.
   * No code provided (Reviewer 4).

3. **Inadequate consideration of graph anomalies** (Reviewer 4): The design (Formulas 4–5) focuses on graph uniformity or high node connectivity as criteria for anomalies, but neglects attribute features. Anomalous nodes may also be related to node attributes; the treatment appears superficial.

4. **Unclear or poorly justified experimental details** (Reviewers 1, 2, 3):
   * Which graph datasets are used for pre-training and prompt tuning? This is not clearly stated (Reviewer 2).
   * Choice of pseudo-label threshold (40th percentile of normal scores) is unexplained. Real anomalous rate is far less, leading to many false pseudo-labels in unsupervised setting. Hyperparameter sensitivity is provided only for number of prompts, not for other critical hyperparameters (Reviewer 3).
   * Coordinate-wise normalization role: why it is critical? More explanation expected (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 4 questions how Formula 2 can project datasets with large feature differences into same space (mean will be large), and how potential negative values are handled.

5. **Thoroughness of literature review** (Reviewer 1): The related work section is not thorough, which obscures clarity of novelty.

6. **Writing quality** (Reviewer 4): The paper contains too many lengthy sentences, making it difficult to read and understand.

7. **Questions incorporated as weaknesses**:
   * What is the difference between zero-shot and inductive settings? (Reviewer 1) – implies lack of clarity in problem definition.
   * If prompts alter neighbour features of a node, could this affect anomaly detection? (Reviewer 4) – a potential methodological flaw.
   * Can the pre-trained model use a dataset other than Facebook and still get good performance? (Reviewer 3) – generalization concern.
   * Can UNPrompt outperform baselines if they also use coordinate-wise normalization? (Reviewer 3) – missing ablation.
   * Unique challenges of prompt learning in GAD? (Reviewer 4) – missing motivation.
