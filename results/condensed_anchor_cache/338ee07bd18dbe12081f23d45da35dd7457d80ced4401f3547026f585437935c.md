## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ACER, a two-stage approach to enhance long-context language model capabilities without human supervision: (1) automatic data synthesis using a retrieval model and a short-context generator to produce synthetic long-context data, and (2) fine-tuning a short-context LM on that data. Experiments on long-context RAG and reading comprehension tasks show ACER outperforms generalist long-context models and the retrieval-read pipeline.

### Strengths
- The topic of long-context modeling is compelling and critical, with valuable new insights.
- The method effectively alleviates the need for extensive human-annotated data.
- The pipeline for automatically generating RAG training data is effective; it achieves significant performance improvements even when using only an 8B model as the data generator.
- Experimental results on standard benchmarks (Natural Questions, TriviaQA, NarrativeQA) are positive, with detailed comparisons against other models.
- The paper is well-written, well-structured, and easy to follow; methodology is clear and effective.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation scope and missing comparisons:** Evaluation is conducted almost exclusively on long-context RAG tasks (NQ, TQA, NarrativeQA), where improvement is natural given the methodology. Missing evaluation on more general long-context sets such as LV-Eval and Needle in a Haystack. Also only a narrow comparison of long-context benchmarks (e.g., Infinibench, LongBench) was provided, and important approaches like self-extend and lm-infinite are not compared or discussed. Missing comparisons with other RAG strategies beyond long-context models.
- **Lack of novelty:** Several reviewers view the approach as a straightforward/engineering trick rather than a research idea, lacking innovation and contribution.
- **Limited model scale and type:** Experiments are conducted on only one size (8B) and one type (Llama-3-8b-it) of language model. It is unclear whether a larger backbone (e.g., Llama-3.1-70b-it) would provide greater benefits, and how the limitations of Llama-3-8b-it (e.g., instruction-following consistency) impact performance.
- **Missing case studies and in-depth analysis:** No case studies on synthetic data, attention pattern analysis, or examples demonstrating that the model genuinely improves long-context handling beyond benchmark scores. Absence of data statistics such as context lengths and chain-of-thought answer lengths (e.g., from Figure 1). Also lacks ablation studies on experimental settings, especially the impact of chunk size and its relationship with context length.
- **Chunking and parameterization concerns:** Chunking strategies in long documents disrupt contextual flow, leading to incomplete chunks and potentially causing LLMs to reference unrelated contexts or fall back on parametric knowledge. The method does not address how to handle this fragmentation or how to set the number of retrieved chunks (\(M\)) for different questions.
- **Figure quality:** Figure 1 would benefit from a higher-resolution version (preferably PDF).
- **Minority reviewer (score 3) more negative:** While three reviewers gave a score of 5, Reviewer 4 rated the paper 3, citing insufficient generalizability, lack of novelty, and missing in-depth analysis.
