## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a model-free distributionally robust reinforcement learning (DRRL) algorithm using a three-timescale stochastic approximation framework, with asymptotic convergence guarantees and experiments on tabular and DQN-based environments. Reviewer scores range from 3 to 8 (6,8,6,3,3). While most reviewers find the problem interesting and the algorithm novel, several raise serious concerns about the presentation, justification of assumptions, missing comparisons with prior work, and lack of sample-complexity guarantees. The two low-scored reviewers (3,3) are particularly critical of the paper’s clarity, the validity of theoretical claims, and the lack of key justifications; the high-scored reviewer (8) is positive but notes missing ablation details.

### Strengths
- **Novel model-free DRRL with single trajectory**: The paper is the first to propose a model-free DRRL algorithm that does not assume access to a simulator or repeated sampling, making it significantly more practical (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5).
- **Three-timescale stochastic approximation**: The introduction of an additional timescale to handle unbiased estimation is interesting and novel (Reviewers 2, 4, 5).
- **Clear motivations**: The reasons for not using SAA or multilevel Monte Carlo are well explained (Reviewer 3). The extension of the two-timescale framework to DRO is also noted (Reviewer 4).
- **Reasonable experiments**: A good amount of experiments on tabular environments and larger control tasks (Cartpole, LunarLander) illustrate the algorithm’s performance compared to robust baselines like SR-DQN (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5). The tabular example and DQN-based implementation (Algorithm 4 in Appendix) are recognized (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Asymptotic convergence guarantee**: The paper provides theoretical asymptotic convergence (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 finds the results sound (though did not check proofs thoroughly).
- **Promising experimental results**: The performance in perturbed environments is shown to be superior to non-robust methods; some perturbations demonstrate clear advantage over SR-DQN (Reviewers 2, 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Only asymptotic convergence, no sample-complexity**: Only asymptotic guarantees are provided; no finite-sample or sample-complexity analysis. A comparison with previous work (e.g., Panaganti et al. 2022) on sample efficiency is requested (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 5 doubts the soundness due to many assumptions.
- **Missing comparisons and claims of novelty**:  
  - The claim of being the “first model-free DRRL” is incorrect. Panaganti et al. (2022) is model-free (offline). The paper must explicitly compare and discuss differences, especially the choice of uncertainty set and problem setting (Reviewer 3).  
  - The claim that the uncertainty set is not covered by Badrinath & Kalathil (2021) and Roy et al. (2017) is poorly justified. Reviewers 4 and 3 note that those works study online settings with general uncertainty sets; the paper’s argument about containing elements outside the simplex does not prove superiority nor equivalence to the R-contamination model. Empirical comparisons with these online methods are missing (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear practical implementation and ablation**:  
  - The DQN-based implementation (Algorithm 4 in Appendix) looks nearly identical to the tabular Algorithm 1; a full description and explanation of modifications and connection to the tabular version are needed in the main text (Reviewer 3).  
  - The learning rates used in the three-timescale algorithm are not reported. An ablation study on learning rate sensitivity is missing; without it, the robustness of the algorithm is unclear (Reviewer 2).  
  - Reviewer 2 notes that in Figure 5, DDQR and SR-DQN show very similar performance under some perturbations (e.g., FMP in Cartpole, EPP in LunarLander); more discussion of when DDQR outperforms and the specific perturbation characteristics is needed.
- **Writing, clarity, and typos**: The paper is hard to follow in places; many notations and terms (e.g., “misspecified MDP”) lack definitions (Reviewer 4). There are factual errors and typos that undermine credibility (Reviewer 4). Presentation is rated “fair” or “poor” by Reviewers 3, 4, and 5.
- **Overly strong assumptions without justification**:  
  - The three-timescale stochastic approximation framework requires more than 10 assumptions, most unexplained. It is not shown that these assumptions hold for the specific DR-RL problem — e.g., that the associated ODE has a unique global asymptotically stable equilibrium (Reviewers 4, 5).  
  - The use of gradient descent to solve the support function is not justified: why is the problem smooth? Does the gradient exist? (Reviewer 5).  
  - The theoretical section lacks explanations for why the three-timescale regime stabilizes training (Reviewer 4).
- **Conceptual concerns**:  
  - The combination of online RL with distributional robustness seems unnatural; offline RL is a more natural setting for DRO. Why learn a robust policy in the testing environment when it is suboptimal there? (Reviewer 3).  
  - Reviewer 2 asks whether the independent η update for each (s,a) pair works under s-rectangular uncertainty sets.
- **Proof and theoretical details**: Reviewer 4 could not verify the proofs due to unclear presentation; the soundness of convergence results is questioned (Reviewers 4, 5). Reviewer 5 doubts whether the required ODE properties hold for the DR-RL problem.

**Minority disagreement highlights**: The two low-scoring reviewers (4 and 5) are far more negative: they emphasize the lack of justification for assumptions, writing issues, and missing comparisons, while the high-scoring reviewer (2) acknowledges weaknesses but finds the algorithm interesting and well-presented. Reviewer 1 and 3 are intermediate (score 6), with Reviewer 3 noting the novelty but also pointing out the incorrect “first” claim and the need for clearer comparisons.
