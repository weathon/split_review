## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MMIU, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating large vision-language models (LVLMs) on multi-image tasks. It covers 7 types of multi-image relationships (semantic, temporal, spatial), 52 tasks, 77,659 images, and 11,698 multiple-choice questions. The evaluation of 24 popular open-source and closed-source LVLMs finds significant challenges, with GPT-4o achieving only 55.7% accuracy. Reviewers commend the benchmark's thoroughness, originality, and insightful analyses, but raise concerns about presentation clarity, potential biases from GPT-4o-generated data, limited architectural comparisons, and missing practical guidance.

### Strengths
- **First comprehensive multi-image benchmark:** MMIU fills a critical gap by evaluating LVLMs across diverse multi-image relationships (semantic, temporal, spatial) and image modalities.
- **Well-organized presentation:** The paper is clearly structured with detailed background, methodology, and experimental results; figures and tables effectively enhance understanding.
- **Meticulous design and data curation:** Great effort is evident in curating the dataset following a logical main plot line (Reviewer 2).
- **Interesting research insight:** The finding that LVLMs pre-trained on single-image content can outperform multi-image counterparts is valuable (Reviewer 2).
- **Originality:** The benchmark creatively spans cognitive processes from low-level perception to high-level reasoning, highlighting areas where models struggle (Reviewer 3).
- **Rigorous methodology:** 24 LVLMs are tested with multi-dimensional analysis (SFT, task maps), providing reliable and depth-rich results (Reviewer 3).
- **High significance:** MMIU has strong potential impact for guiding future LVLM research, especially for video understanding and 3D reasoning (Reviewer 3).
- **Extensibility:** The benchmark design allows easy addition of new tasks and image modalities (Reviewer 1).
- **Diverse task coverage:** Tasks range from low-level visual perception to high-level reasoning, offering a holistic capability view (Reviewer 5).
- **Insightful analysis:** Detailed analytical experiments identify key performance gaps and limitations (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Cumbersome presentation of detailed results:** Table 3 lists every fine-category result per subtask, making it hard to derive clear comparisons; Figure 4 is similarly overcrowded. Reviewers recommend showing summarized coarse-category (Semantic/Spatial/Temporal) performance in the main text and relegating finer details to the appendix (Reviewer 2, also noted by Reviewer 4).
- **Potential GPT-4o bias in data generation:** Questions and answers are generated by GPT-4o, which itself has multi-image reasoning limitations. This may introduce systematic bias in task construction and answer choices. Human verification may not fully eliminate subtle biases; the paper lacks a clear discussion or safeguards (Reviewer 3, strong concern).
- **Unaddressed position bias for unanswerable tasks:** MMIU places the "None of the above" option always as Option D, but known LLM position bias (Zheng et al., 2023) favors early options. This could deflate results for unanswerable tasks (Table 12). The authors do not report shuffling option orders or otherwise controlling for this effect in Section 3.2 (Reviewer 2, high confidence).
- **Limited architectural comparison:** The evaluation does not analyze how specific visual encoding strategies or model architectures affect performance, especially on spatial and temporal tasks (Reviewer 4).
- **No proposed methods to address identified gaps:** While MMIU highlights performance shortcomings, the paper does not explore or suggest alternative training techniques, architectural adjustments, or data augmentations to overcome them, limiting its practical utility for guiding future model development (Reviewer 4).
- **Large scale may require heavy computation:** The dataset includes 77,659 images and 11,698 questions, which may demand significant resources for experimentation and analysis (Reviewer 1).
- **Questionable practical significance of comparisons on temporal/spatial tasks:** Most current LVLMs do not model temporal or spatial capabilities during pre-training, so comparing them on these tasks may lack real-world grounding (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing details on data collection and potential duplicates:** The paper does not specify which data sources or processing techniques were used (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 5 asks whether there are overlaps with existing multi-image datasets—this duplication risk is unaddressed.
- **Missing baseline: pure language models on single-image understanding:** The authors could strengthen the analysis by reporting performance of pure language models (e.g., MMMU) as a foundation for multi-image comprehension (Reviewer 1).
- **Requested experiment: impact of adding interleave or video data in pretraining/post-training:** The paper would benefit from evaluating models that incorporate interleaved image-text or video data during training to see if multi-image reasoning improves (Reviewer 1).
- **Need for deeper granular analysis:** The current analysis focuses on general trends but could examine why specific models excel or struggle on particular relationship types (spatial, temporal, high-level semantic) (Reviewer 4).
- **Unexplained counterintuitive result:** Models without multi-image supervised fine-tuning (SFT) sometimes outperform those with multi-image SFT. The paper does not explain this phenomenon (Reviewer 5).
- **Writing polish needed:** Typo on line 173 (“multimodal large language models (LVLMs)” abbreviation error); inconsistent figure references (Fig. vs Figure) (Reviewer 5). Also, a missing table reference on line 1389 (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited discussion on real-world applicability:** While tasks are drawn from practical scenarios, the paper does not explicitly connect them to high-stakes applications (autonomous driving, surveillance, medical imaging) or discuss how MMIU could be adapted for emerging domains (Reviewer 3).
