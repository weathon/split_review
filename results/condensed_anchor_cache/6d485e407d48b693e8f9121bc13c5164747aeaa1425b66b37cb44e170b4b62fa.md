## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a one-hot prior for HMM-GLMs (OHG) to model dynamic functional neural interactions while respecting anatomical constraints. Evaluations on synthetic and real neural datasets show improved interpretability and predictive likelihood over baselines. Two reviewers are highly positive (score 8), praising originality, clarity, thorough evaluation, and neuroscience grounding. One reviewer is notably more critical (score 3), raising concerns about model necessity, biological validity of state divisions, and missing analyses. All reviewers agree on the need for better scalability analysis and clearer exposition of methods.

### Strengths
- Original contribution extending GLMs with a learnable one-hot prior to incorporate anatomical constraints, enhancing biological plausibility (R1, R2, R3).
- Clear presentation with well-structured figures and equations (R1, R3).
- Consistent and strong results across synthetic data and two real datasets (motor cortex and whisking), with highest predictive likelihood (R1, R2, R3).
- Meaningful latent state inference that could be significant for neuroscientists studying dynamic interactions (R1).
- OHG yields better connectivity patterns and hidden states compared to baselines (R2).
- Technically robust: well-defined problem building on substantial prior research, straightforward and effective approach (R3).
- Comprehensive evaluation including many baselines (GLM, HMM-GLM, GHG, etc.) and varied datasets; whisking dataset is particularly interesting (R3).
- Detailed description of the expectation-maximization algorithm (R2).

### Weaknesses
- Synthetic experiments are limited: only test a single global state scenario; do not consider networks with multiple independently evolving subpopulations that communicate rarely (R1).
- No principled model selection criterion for the number of states; the paper notes that additional states are rarely occupied and distinctions become subtle, which undermines the model’s reliability when the true state count is unknown (R1).
- Missing analysis of decoding task information from the latent states in real data, which would help demonstrate utility (R1).
- Scalability concerns: does not test with hundreds to thousands of neurons as in modern recordings; inference speed after fitting is not reported, leaving real-time application (e.g., stimulus control) uncertain (R1).
- Confusion between the two proposed frameworks (GHG and OHG): GHG is described after OHG, yet it is a simpler variant that performs worse. The value of GHG is unclear, and the naming/ordering is confusing (R2).
- Evidence for biological validity of state division is weak: in Table 2, multi-state OHG results are similar to one-state GLM, suggesting that only the global prior matters or that only one state exists; this needs stronger justification (R2).
- Claim that generative hyperparameters are noninformative and insensitive across datasets lacks supporting evidence (R2).
- Sections 2 and 3 (method description) need more intuitive explanations; the transition from HMM to GLM and how the one-hot prior is incorporated into parameter estimation is not sufficiently clear and may cause confusion (R3).
- Prior work by Glaser et al. (focusing on cluster/population structures) is not acknowledged or compared, despite thematic relevance to underlying structures (R3).
