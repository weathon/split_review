## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GLOV, a framework that uses LLMs as implicit optimizers for VLM prompt optimization. An LLM is meta-prompted with task descriptions and in-context examples (with accuracy feedback) to generate VLM prompts; additionally, a feature-level offset vector (difference of positive/negative prompt embeddings) steers the LLM’s generation. Evaluated on 16 datasets for dual-encoder (CLIP) and encoder-decoder (LLaVA) VLMs, reporting up to 15.0% and 57.5% improvement (3.8% and 21.6% on average).

### Strengths
- Motivation is sound and clear.
- Steering the LLM response during prompt optimization is novel and effective; the design by analogy with gradient update has good engineering intuition (Reviewer 2).
- Use of meta-prompts and LLM steering provides fresh insights (Reviewer 3).
- Experimental results are transparent: search outcomes are in the appendix and code release is promised.
- Discovered prompts generalize within the same category (dual encoder) of VLMs.
- Highly readable, well-defined, clear structure.
- Practical topic: improving VLM generalization with low cost (no parameter update, little data, fast training) (Reviewer 4).
- Some experiments demonstrate the idea’s performance and the value of optimal prompt search (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty dispute**: One reviewer (R4, score 3) considers the extension of meta-prompts from [1] to few-shot trivial and of minor technological contribution. Another reviewer (R2, score 8) praises the steering strategy as novel and effective. The optimality of the meta-prompt design itself is not verified (R4).
- **White-box constraint**: Feature-level guidance requires access to LLM intermediate features, which is hard for closed-source or very large LLMs; this may limit advantages over black-box methods and could be harder than accessing VLM parameters/gradients (R1).
- **LLM sensitivity**: More evidence needed that LLM reasoning plays an important role, especially given result fluctuations (Figs 1,3,6) and the limited reasoning of 7B-level LLMs. Suggested: show higher accuracy or fewer steps with a stronger LLM (R1).
- **Computational impracticality**: LLM iterative inference is costly compared to encoder-based single-forward models; this makes the approach unrealistic for real-world applications (R4). Only downstream performance is reported; missing inference time (seconds) and GPU memory (GB) for all methods in Tables 1-2 (R4). Also missing a curve of iteration steps vs inference time to illustrate the performance-cost trade-off (R3).
- **Incomplete comparisons**: No comparison with prior VLM prompt optimization works [1,2,3] that achieve promising results at lower cost (R3). No comparison with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (prompt-tuning, LoRA, adapter) in terms of performance, parameters, training time, and inference speed under the same settings; Table 5 showing CoOp worse than CLIP is suspicious and needs explanation (R4).
- **Generalization unclear**: If GLOV is not competitive with other VLM classification methods, the authors should demonstrate generalization to visual tasks beyond image classification (R3).
- **Clarity issues**: Figure 2 is difficult to understand—needs clearer flow of iterative optimization (R3). Algorithm 1 is unclear: meaning of square brackets at lines 9,12,28,29 (when K is an integer) and how the top-3 ensemble prompts are selected (last step, best step, or all steps?) (R1).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The two steering hyperparameters (layer l, scaling factor α) are not analyzed for sensitivity across datasets; unknown whether they are searched per dataset or kept fixed, and how optimal choices vary (R1).
- **Placeholder ambiguity**: Some searched prompts (appendix Ln 1187-1192) contain extra placeholders (angle brackets); it is unclear if these come from dataset metadata and whether the search process accounts for them (R1).
- **Missing average accuracy**: Main tables (Table 1, 2) lack average accuracy, making cross-referencing with ablations (Table 4) harder (R1).
- **Embedding reliability for encoder-decoder models**: For LLaVA, the method relies on sentence embeddings of the LLM-generated prompts for steering. The reliability of these embeddings for complex, lengthy, or syntactically varied prompts (e.g., elaborate instructions/descriptions) is unverified. Potential for lengthy ambiguous symbolic representations; measures to ensure sentence transformer efficacy are missing (R2).
- **Applicability constraint**: GLOV requires an objective fitness function for the task; this limits applicability to tasks where such a function exists (R2).
- **In-context selection concerns**: Using top-k/bottom-k for in-context examples may cause a large disparity between positive and negative samples in later stages, hindering subtle refinements (analogous to a large fixed learning rate). A dynamic selection threshold is suggested (R2). Similarly, steering might benefit from dynamically modifying the rank interval between P+ and P− (R2).
- **Statistical significance**: In few-shot learning, the impact of the single labeled sample on performance is significant. How are samples selected to ensure results are statistically meaningful? Variance over five repeated experiments is not reported (R4).
