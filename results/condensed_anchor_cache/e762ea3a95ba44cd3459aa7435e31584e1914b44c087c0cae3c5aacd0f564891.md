## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies non-parametric identifiability of latent variables in causal representation learning under distribution shifts, building on Liu et al. (2022; 2024) by relaxing the requirement that causal functions among latents be linear or polynomial. It establishes a necessary and sufficient condition (Assumption (iv)) for full identifiability in latent additive noise models, provides partial (component-wise) identifiability results when only some shifts meet the condition, and extends the theory to latent post-nonlinear models. A practical algorithm based on iVAE with sparsity constraints is proposed and evaluated on synthetic data, image datasets, and fMRI data.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-structured, relatively easy to follow, and provides a good overview of existing work on assumptions for identifiability in CRL (R1, R3). The explanations of intuition and insights are particularly excellent (R3).
- The necessary and sufficient condition (iv) is a meaningful theoretical contribution that seems both practically relevant and theoretically significant (R4). The approach of applying nonlinear ICA to noise variables and using ANMs as a bridge is an interesting direction (R4).
- The complete and partial identifiability results – when not all distribution shifts adhere to Ass. (iv) – make the results more general and give some hope for practical applications (R2). The partial identifiability of latent causal variables provides valuable insights for domain generalization and domain adaptation (R3).
- The structure of theoretical results (additive noise case first, then extension to postnonlinear) reduces complexity and is easy to follow (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and relation to prior work**: The core motivation of leveraging distribution shifts to assess identifiability based on changes in coefficients of *z_i* overlaps substantially with Liu et al. (2024); this paper is an incremental improvement from polynomial to nonlinear causal functions while keeping the same essential idea (R3). The paper does not contrast its results with the multi-environment setting in [1]; it is unclear how this is a generalization (R2). The learning method resembles iVAE with added order and sparsity constraints, which were already considered in Liu et al. (2022; 2024) (R4). Similarities to Liu et al. (2022; 2024) in the causal graph and inference process should be explicitly acknowledged (R4).
- **Assumption (iv) and its restrictiveness**: Assumption (iv) appears extremely restrictive and close to a hard intervention; it is unclear how it is more general than a hard intervention (R2). The justification via PPI inhibition through small molecules is not convincing: full inhibition (partial derivative zero) is unlikely, small molecules have other effects, PPI is not compatible with the DAG or additive noise assumption (R1). The claim that assumptions have been "verified to be practicable" in cited works is not supported (R1). Even after rebuttal, the notion of soft intervention remains unclear and seems no more than a notational tweak (R4).
- **Clarity of theoretical claims and proofs**: The proofs lack mathematical rigor in places (R1). There are multiple references to a "hatted" function *must belong to the same function class* without clarifying what this means technically (R1). The sufficiency proof (Thm. 3.5) is not fully convincing: the required equality for matching marginals may hold even when the Jacobian has a non-zero value, but counterexamples may exist where the partial derivative is zero except on a set of zero probability (R1). The claim that the theory "eliminates the need to make assumptions about the dimensionality of latent causal or noise variables" is not supported – the two-parameter exponential family requirement is per *z_i* and *l* is unrestricted, similar to prior work (R4). For Ass. (ii), one at least needs to know there are *2l+1* different values of *u*, so some implicit knowledge of *l* is required (R1).
- **Experimental evaluation and baselines**: The experimental evaluation lacks important details; e.g., the mixing function *f* for synthetic data is not stated, making reproducibility hard (R2). No CRL method (such as [1] or others) is compared against, so it is difficult to assess practical applicability (R2). Most baselines are misspecified regarding the problem due to assumptions of independent latents, but this is not mentioned (R2). The synthetic and image data are limited to chain structures; more complex structures would be valuable (R4). It would be informative to compare against "iVAE to recover *z* and then existing causal discovery methods to build causal graphs among *z*" (R4). Why does β-VAE perform so well on the fMRI data for reasonable β values? (R4)
- **Hard vs. soft intervention confusion**: The concepts of hard vs. soft/natural interventions are unclear and add little to understanding the model (R4). The assignment on *u* still formally models distribution shifts, which is a hard intervention; the paper does not convincingly show that "soft interventions" are inherently broader (R4). The distinction seems unnecessary and confusing, similar to the need for specific *u_i* values which depends on proof construction (R4).
- **Writing and presentation issues**: The paper could be more transparent about its limitations and relationship to prior work (R4). Remark 3.2 is confusing – better examples would help illustrate the condition, especially nonlinear examples satisfying (iii) but only one satisfying (iv) (R4, R3). The "Model Capacity" section is confusing, conflating Thm. 3.1 and Cor. 4.1; examples are not clearly explained (R4). Remarks 4.2 and the intuition for Corollary 4.1 could be removed (R4). Several minor typographical errors: L63, L116 ("we explores"), L142, L175, L208, L213, L235, L237, L239, L250, L356/L358, L382, L387, L393, L437, L445, L483, and missing articles (R1, R3, R4). Some clarifications are needed: the number of latent causal variables and causal order – are they prior information? (R3) The performance with a larger number of latents (experiments use 3–6) is not addressed (R3). The statement "we assume two-parameter exponential family members" should be made explicit rather than "we focus on" (R4).
