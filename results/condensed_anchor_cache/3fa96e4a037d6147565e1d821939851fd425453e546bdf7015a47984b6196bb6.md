## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper generalizes the result of Koehler et al. (2022) to a broader class of generalized score matching losses. It shows that for Markov processes with generator $\mathcal{L}$ and stationary distribution $p$, an appropriately chosen linear operator $\mathcal{O}$ yields a generalized score matching loss whose statistical efficiency is governed by the Poincaré constant $C_P$ of the process, analogous to the original result. The authors use a lifted diffusion (CTLD) that introduces a temperature variable to construct an annealed score matching loss, and they analyze the sample complexity for learning Gaussian mixture models (GMMs) with shared covariance, showing that the bound depends polynomially on dimension $d\), diameter of means, and eigenvalues of the covariance, avoiding the Poincaré-constant-based lower bounds.

### Strengths
- The problem of improving the statistical efficiency of score matching is well-motivated (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The paper generalizes Koehler et al. (2022) to arbitrary linear operators and corresponding generalized score matching losses, providing a novel framework connecting mixing times of a broad class of Markov processes to statistical efficiency (Reviewer 1).
- The authors introduce CTLD based on annealing and show provable statistical benefits for GMMs; the framework is novel and interesting (Reviewer 1).
- Results are new and non-trivial; the statements are clear, and comparisons with prior work on score matching are presented (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Assumptions in Theorem 2** are strong and restrictive. Assumptions 1 and 2 may be hard to verify when $\mathcal{O} \neq \nabla_x$ and is not derived from CTLD. The paper would benefit from a discussion on practical implications, how one might validate these assumptions in real-world scenarios, and concrete examples beyond CTLD (Reviewer 1; echoed partially by Reviewer 2’s question about technical novelty).
- **Lack of experimental validation**: No experiments are provided, even on simple datasets. This makes it difficult to assess the practical performance of the proposed approach (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited technical novelty relative to Koehler et al. (2022)**: The proof of Theorem 2 appears to be a straightforward generalization of the proof in Koehler et al. (2022). It is unclear what interesting technical contribution is added beyond that work (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 1 also asks whether Koehler et al. can provide an upper bound for GMMs, suggesting a need for clearer distinction).
- **Computational efficiency of the estimator for GMMs (Theorem 5)**: The estimator does not appear to be efficiently computable, unlike the score matching estimator in Koehler et al. (2022) for exponential families. The motivation for using this estimator is unclear (Reviewer 2).
- **Interpretation of the bound in Theorem 5**: The bound is on $\|\Gamma_{SM}\|_{OP}$ but $\|\Gamma_{MLE}\|_{OP}$ may depend on $K$ (number of mixture components). It is not clear why avoiding dependence on $K$ in the constant $C$ is important if the MLE error itself depends on $K$. It would be more informative to bound the largest diagonal entry of $\Gamma_{SM}$ in terms of the largest diagonal entry of $\Gamma_{MLE}$ to control per-component estimation error independently of $K$ (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of comparison with prior works on Gaussian mixtures**: The paper refers to some related work but does not provide a direct comparison of the scaling improvements or how the result compares with existing bounds for learning GMMs (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Generalization to non-identical covariances**: It is unclear whether the approach can be extended to mixtures where components have different covariance matrices (e.g., all with bounded condition number) (Reviewer 2).
- **Technical presentation**: The paper is fairly technical and may pose challenges for readers not expert in this area (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor issue**: Definition 1 uses linear operators between spaces of all functions, but Lemma 1 relies on adjoint operators assuming Hilbert spaces; clarification is needed (Reviewer 2).

**Disagreement among reviewers**: One reviewer (score 8, confidence 3) rated the paper highly, praising the novel framework and the GMM analysis. Two reviewers (score 5, confidence 2) were more critical, citing concerns about technical novelty, computational efficiency, lack of experiments, and insufficient comparisons with prior work.
