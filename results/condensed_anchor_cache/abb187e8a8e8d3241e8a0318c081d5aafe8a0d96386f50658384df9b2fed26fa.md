## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces LaTRO (LaTent Reasoning Optimization), a framework that treats chain-of-thought reasoning as sampling from a latent distribution and optimizes it via variational methods. LaTRO uses the model’s own probability of generating the correct answer as an implicit reward, enabling the LLM to improve both its reasoning process and its ability to evaluate reasoning quality without an external reward model. Experiments on GSM8K and ARC-Challenge with Phi-3.5-mini, Mistral-7B, and Llama-3.1-8B show accuracy gains over base models and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) baselines. Two reviewers (scores 5) found the topic innovative and the paper well-written; three reviewers (scores 3) considered the evaluation insufficient and the baselines too weak.

### Strengths
1. **Important and novel research question**: Improving LLM reasoning during training without external feedback is a significant goal. LaTRO offers a fresh perspective by framing reasoning as sampling from a latent distribution and optimizing it via variational methods (R1, R3, R4, R5). This approach is distinct from prompt-based methods (e.g., CoT) and moves toward unsupervised learning (R4).
2. **Clear writing and organization**: The paper is well-structured, with clear motivation and easy-to-follow presentation (R2, R3, R5).
3. **Well-motivated variational objective**: The variational objective is thoroughly justified in Sections 3 and 4.1, and its feasibility is supported by mathematical proof (R1, R4).
4. **Self-reward mechanism without external models**: Using the model’s own probability of generating the correct answer as an implicit reward unifies policy and reward training, allowing self-improvement without an external reward model (R3, R4).
5. **Empirical gains with ablations**: LaTRO shows accuracy improvements over base and SFT baselines on GSM8K and ARC-Challenge across three architectures (Phi-3.5-mini, Mistral-7B, Llama-3.1-8B). Ablations on greedy decoding vs. self-consistency and inference-time scaling of reasoning samples demonstrate additional gains (R1).

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient and weak baselines**: The paper compares only against base models and a basic SFT baseline. Missing baselines include: (a) a stronger SFT baseline that filters correct reasoning paths using ground-truth answers (R3); (b) DPO fine-tuning using correct and incorrect rationales (R3); (c) self-play methods such as Self-Play Fine-Tuning and Self-Rewarding Language Models, adapted for 7B models (R1, R3, R5); (d) STaR (bootstrapping reasoning with reasoning) (R5); (e) prompting-based reasoning methods like Tree-of-Thought and RAP (R5); (f) CoT-SC, which the paper itself discusses as motivation (R4); (g) prior latent-variable reasoning methods (Hu et al. 2023, Hoffman et al. 2024) that also use EM-like algorithms (R5). Comparisons on computational cost and accuracy are needed (R5).
2. **Limited experimental scope**: Only two datasets (GSM8K, ARC-Challenge) and small models (7B scale) are used. More datasets are needed: arithmetic reasoning (beyond GSM8K), commonsense reasoning (CSQA, Hellaswag), symbolic reasoning (R1, R3), and code reasoning (HumanEval, MBPP) (R5). Testing on larger model scales is also necessary (R3). The number of case study examples (only Figure 4 and appendix) is too limited (R2).
3. **Lack of confidence intervals or robustness analysis**: No results from repeated runs are reported, making it unclear how robust LaTRO is to initialization and randomness. At least three repetitions should be provided (R5).
4. **Insufficient related work discussion**: The paper should discuss and contrast with methods that leverage external LLMs (e.g., Distilling Step-by-Step, Zephyr) (R1), self-play techniques (e.g., Self-Play Fine-Tuning, Self-Rewarding Language Models) (R1, R5), self-improvement without feedback (e.g., "Small language models can self-correct", "Think Thrice Before You Act") (R4), and prior latent-variable reasoning formulations (Hu et al., Hoffman et al.) (R5). A dedicated related-work subsection is advised (R4).
5. **Lack of implementation details and clarity on key components**: The paper uses many formulas but does not include a step-by-step pipeline description or flowchart, making reproduction difficult (R4). Specific unclear points: (a) How the first gradient term in Eq. (4) optimizes the policy to generate better rationales (R1). (b) What the "update parameter" refers to during training and how it is implemented (R4). (c) How LaTRO evaluates the probability that each reasoning path produces the correct answer, what the conditional probability in Section 4.1 means, whether this evaluation has task restrictions, and what its accuracy is (R4). (d) Why CoT-SC is introduced in Proposition 1 (R3). (e) The definition of "golden rationales" and why ARC-Challenge cannot have them (R3). (f) Why rationales as long as 500 tokens are needed and how longer rationales improve performance before saturating (R1).
6. **Concerns about the self-reward mechanism**: The stability of the self-reward component is not adequately considered (R2). Additionally, ground-truth answers effectively serve as implicit external feedback, partly contradicting the claim of training without external feedback (R3).
7. **Questions about ARC-Challenge results**: ARC-Challenge shows smaller improvements. Reviewers question whether this indicates limited reasoning scope in the dataset (R1) and what the results on ARC-Challenge actually demonstrate given that the SFT baseline did not use rationales (R3).
