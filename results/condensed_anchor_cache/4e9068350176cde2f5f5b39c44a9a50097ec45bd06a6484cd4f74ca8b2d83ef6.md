## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a modified TransformerXL architecture that uses Automatic Chunking, ForgetSpan, and Similarity-based Forgetting to improve memory efficiency for long-horizon episodic decision-making in cognitively inspired robots. It tests on visual and audio-visual tasks requiring long-horizon recollection and navigation. Multiple reviewers (1,2,4) consider the paper an early-stage technical report with major clarity and experimental flaws; one reviewer (4) gave a score of 3 while others gave 1. There is disagreement on novelty and overall contribution.

### Strengths
- The problem of long-horizon episodic decision-making for cognitively inspired robots is considered important (Reviewer 2), and the set of tasks focusing on memory aspects with progressive difficulty is ideal for evaluating such claims (Reviewer 4).
- Some reviewers found the memory implementation (Automatic Chunking, ForgetSpan, Similarity-based Forgetting) to be original, innovative, and timely (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 3 specifically highlights the trio of techniques addressing unique memory optimization challenges and the comprehensive testing across diverse tasks.
- **Contrast**: Reviewer 2 states “novelty is considerably low,” and Reviewer 4 notes that prior work on non-parametric memory with forgetting heuristics (e.g., refs [2], [3] in that review) is not contrasted, making the techniques seem lacking in originality. The positive view of novelty is a minority opinion.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and presentation are major flaws** (all reviewers). The paper is poorly written with many grammar errors and typos (Reviewer 2). The structure is confusing: no clear separation between preliminaries, methodology, and experimental setup; descriptions of techniques are repeated (Reviewer 4). Figure 1 is confusing (Reviewer 4). Mathematical formulas need rewriting and proper symbol definitions (Reviewer 4). Part of Section 3.1 is cut off (Reviewer 4). The paper requires careful proofreading before submission (Reviewer 4).
- **No proper related work / literature review** (Reviewers 1,2,3,4). Claims about human cognition in the Introduction lack citations (Reviewers 3,4). The paper does not discuss the active area of memory in RL or contrast with prior forgetting heuristics and memory architectures (Reviewer 4 provides specific references). The Reference section contains works not cited in the text (Reviewer 4).
- **Experimental shortcomings**:
  - Only compared to own ablated versions and baseline TransformerXL; no comparison to other state-of-the-art models or memory architectures (Reviewers 1,2,3,4). In Figure 8, no baseline is shown at all (Reviewer 4).
  - No confidence intervals are reported, raising questions about statistical significance in RL experiments (Reviewer 4).
  - Discussion is limited to stating “our method is better than baseline” without further analysis (Reviewer 3).
  - Concern about overfitting: testing modifications may not be significantly different from original tasks (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing details for reproducibility and justification**:
  - What is PPO2 and how does it differ from PPO? (Reviewer 4)
  - In Section 2.2, why are forgotten elements masked out rather than deleted entirely to save memory? (Reviewer 4)
  - In Section 2.3, the dynamic threshold is updated every n timesteps—what is n in experiments? How are bins constructed? (Reviewer 4)
  - No pseudocode for the proposed heuristics (Reviewer 4).
  - The choice of transformer architecture is not motivated, especially given known optimization difficulties in RL (Reviewer 4 cites [1]).
- **General assessment**:
  - Reviewer 1: “This paper is not ready yet for a conference presentation, it is an early stage technical report.”
  - Reviewer 2: “The paper does not show an understanding of the state of the art. … Experimental results cannot justify the novelty or contribution.”
  - Reviewer 4: “The work brings several major flaws and needs a careful revision with rewriting.”
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - “Model is compared to its own ablated versions, but not to existing models – why is that?” (Reviewer 1) → highlights lack of comparative analysis.
  - “How do you tackle the overfitting issue?” (Reviewer 3) → concern about generalization.
  - “What is PPO2?” and other design-choice questions (Reviewer 4) → missing clarity and justification.
