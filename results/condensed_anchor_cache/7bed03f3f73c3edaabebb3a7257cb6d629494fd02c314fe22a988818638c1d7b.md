## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers diverge on the paper’s novelty and significance. One reviewer (score 6, confidence 4) finds **DVCL innovative** in preserving neighborhood relationships among noisy pseudo-labels and demonstrates consistent gains across four datasets. Three reviewers (scores 5, 3, 3; confidence 4,5,5) view the contribution as limited, noting that voxel-wise contrastive learning is not new and the **core idea of pulling similar voxels together and pushing dissimilar apart has been widely explored**. They question whether the claimed problem (disruption of semantic relationships by complementary-label VCL) is convincingly demonstrated with real data rather than a cartoon illustration. While the entropy-based selection module is considered an original practical contribution, the main distance‑aware mechanism is criticized for assuming that initial feature‑space neighborhoods are correct—an assumption that may not hold for unreliable pseudo‑labels. Evaluation is extensive on four CT datasets (FLARE2022, AMOS, etc.), but one reviewer mistakenly believes only FLARE2022 was used; others note the small labeled sample sizes (e.g., 90 in AMOS) and raise concerns about generalization to more complex tasks (pathology, vessels). Language issues and unclear technical details are flagged by multiple reviewers. Overall, the paper is rated weakly to moderately, with significant concerns about the fundamental motivation and the small performance gains over prior VCL methods.

### Strengths
1. **Voxel-wise contrastive learning for semi‑supervised segmentation** is an interesting and practically important direction, even if previously explored. The problem of handling noisy pseudo‑labels is highly relevant to the community.  
2. **Preserving neighborhood relationships** among unreliable pseudo‑labeled voxels (neighbors vs. outsiders) is seen as an innovative adaptation of contrastive learning (Reviewer 2).  
3. **The entropy‑based selection module (ESM)** is acknowledged as an original and dynamic way to distinguish reliable from unreliable voxels based on prediction entropy (Reviewer 3).  
4. **Quantitative improvements** are reported consistently across four datasets (FLARE2022, AMOS, BTCV, WORD), with ablation studies and comparisons (e.g., Table 1) supporting that DVCL modifications yield meaningful gains (Reviewer 2).  
5. **The paper is well-written and easy to understand** (Reviewers 1, 3). Explanations of complex concepts (neighbors/outsiders) and illustrations (Fig. 1b) effectively contrast DVCL with existing methods (Reviewer 2).  
6. **Extensive experiments and ablations** are presented, including comparison with multiple state‑of‑the‑art methods (Reviewer 3).  
7. Performance evaluation includes both quantitative and qualitative results on four datasets (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty.** The concept of voxel‑wise contrastive learning has been widely used in previous work, and the paper does not clearly articulate what specific modifications or innovations distinguish DVCL from prior approaches, especially for handling unreliable pseudo‑labels (Reviewer 1, 3). Reviewer 3 stresses that the paper does not adequately describe prior complementary‑label VCL methods for segmentation; a dedicated related‑work paragraph and a comparison table are missing.  
2. **Unconvincing demonstration of the claimed problem.** The paper relies solely on a cartoon illustration (Fig. 1) to argue that complementary‑label VCL disrupts semantic relationships. Reviewers (3, 4) require real‑data evidence:  
   * Feature‑space visualizations before and after applying complementary‑label VCL.  
   * Quantification of how often semantically similar voxels end up far apart after complementary‑label VCL.  
   * Measurement of the impact on segmentation performance when this issue occurs (e.g., how often the top‑k nearest neighbors do not share the same ground‑truth label).  
3. **Questionable assumption that initial feature‑space neighborhoods are correct and should be preserved.** The main contribution assumes that the feature representations of voxels (especially unreliable ones) and their neighborhoods prior to VCL are reliable. Yet the purpose of VCL is to correct potentially incorrect representations; it may be that those initial neighborhood relationships are wrong and should *not* be maintained. The paper does not validate this assumption (Reviewer 3).  
4. **Neighbors in feature space may cross organ boundaries.** Voxels from different organs can appear close in the feature space; the proposed method would then incorrectly pull them together, assigning the same label. The paper does not acknowledge this as a limitation or quantify how often such misalignment occurs (Reviewer 3). This directly contradicts the goal of learning discriminative features (Reviewer 4).  
5. **Small performance margins over existing methods.** Gains are modest, and the paper does not report statistical significance or confidence intervals. Methods like Monte Carlo dropout could quantify uncertainty over predictions (Reviewer 3).  
6. **Evaluation concerns:**
   * One reviewer (1) states that evaluation is limited to the FLARE2022 dataset, lacking experiments on more complex tasks (pathology, vessel structures) and thus raising doubts about generalization. (Note: other reviewers confirm four datasets, but this disagreement underscores the need for clearer reporting.)  
   * The maximum number of labeled images used (90 in AMOS) is small, making the semi‑supervised motivation less compelling. It is unclear how much annotation effort is saved (i.e., percentage of labeled data needed to match fully supervised performance) and how the method scales with hundreds or thousands of labeled images (Reviewer 4).  
   * The small test set may limit statistical power (Reviewer 4).  
7. **Lack of feature‑space visualizations.** The paper does not show the distribution or clustering of prototypes or voxel features, making it difficult to assess whether contrastive learning effectively separates classes (Reviewer 1).  
8. **Insufficient investigation of key hyperparameters:**
   * The entropy threshold τ for reliable/unreliable pseudo‑labels is not thoroughly studied across datasets; it is unclear whether it is fixed or dynamically adjusted (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 4 finds it surprising that the optimal τ is 1, which may cause the high‑quality contrastive loss to vanish during training.  
   * The numbers of neighbors (K) and outsiders (K′) are not examined for their effect on segmentation of small or anatomically complex organs (Reviewer 2). The straightforward choice of K nearest and K furthest may make the loss term trivial (Reviewer 4).  
9. **Contradictions in method design.** Reviewers (3, 4) point out that maintaining semantic relationships among unreliable voxels may conflict with learning discriminative features: pulling together voxels that are semantically similar but belong to different classes undermines separation.  
10. **Clarity and technical errors:**
   * The definition of “prototype” is unclear; a figure illustrating it and the feature space is needed (Reviewer 1).  
   * Equation (4) may have the terms for M_r and M_u swapped (Reviewer 3).  
   * Equation using L2 norm between probabilities (||p_T^m – p_n||₂) is incorrect since these are probabilities, not feature vectors (Reviewer 3).  
   * In Fig. 1b, the complementary‑label assignment (A∉1, B∉1, C∉2, D∉2) appears inconsistent with the prototypes; the text states otherwise (Reviewer 4).  
   * The method using bounds (Eq. 17) is not clearly explained; it still depends on U_r (Reviewer 3).  
   * Language issues are frequent (e.g., “fearest” should be “furthest”, line 338) (Reviewer 4).  
11. **Missing details in the experimental setup:**
   * Why are reliable pseudo‑labeled voxels not used as negative candidates? Only labeled voxels are used (Reviewer 3).  
   * The Cross Pseudo Supervision (CPS) framework is used but not motivated or described in the methods overview (Reviewer 3).  
   * In Table 2, the “EBL” component appears equivalent to the entropy‑weighted cross pseudo supervision loss, but the proposed high‑quality VCL loss is listed separately—this is confusing (Reviewer 4).  
12. **Minor issues:** Reference Wang et al., 2022a is incorrect (does not discuss complementary‑label contrastive learning). Visual distinction between nearest neighbors and farthest outsiders in Fig. 2 is poor (Reviewer 3). The statement “Experiments demonstrate that VCL based on complementary labels helps to learn good voxel representations” lacks substantiation and references (Reviewer 3).
