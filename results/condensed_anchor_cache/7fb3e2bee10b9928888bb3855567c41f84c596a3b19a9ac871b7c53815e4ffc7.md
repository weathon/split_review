## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a method for sinogram inpainting in CT by integrating a Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) with physics-based loss functions and a blending algorithm. It uses an autoencoder with physics-guided losses to stabilize training, then a diffusion model for generation, and finally blends the output with the original sparse sinogram. Reviewers are split: one is positive (score 6), two are moderately critical (scores 3), and one is very negative (score 1). Core disagreements concern data authenticity, complexity, organization, and the real need for the proposed pipeline.

### Strengths
- Incorporation of physics-domain knowledge (CT acquisition physics) into loss functions for the autoencoder is a valuable direction (R1, R3, R4).
- The method addresses a practical problem: sparse sinograms reduce dose/acquisition time in CT (R2, R3).
- The approach was tested on a real-world dataset (R3; contradicted by R2 and R4 – see Weaknesses).
- The work shows originality in combining a Latent Diffusion Model with CT-specific physical properties, enhancing stability and accuracy of the autoencoder (R1, R4).
- The method can recover sinograms with different masks and sampling ratios, and is suitable for sparse data acquisition and the missing wedge problem (R2).
- The physics-informed loss functions make the model easier to adapt to new tasks and experiments (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Organization and writing**: The paper is highly disorganized. Section 2 has incorrect citation formatting (almost all citations are wrong, especially in Section 4.4), and the authors misuse `\citet`/`\citep` (R1). Figure 9 is severely blurred, making it impossible to read results (R1, R4). The logic is hard to follow, with abrupt topic shifts (e.g., line 171-176) and vague/unprofessional expressions such as “During a CT experiment”, “CT experimental time”, “The object is rotated stepwise around a central axis” (misleading—source/detector rotate, not object), “Combining several such projections, a specialized algorithm”, and “morphological deformation … due to extended radiation exposure” (suggests motion artifacts incorrectly) (R4). The paper only lists existing methods without clearly identifying their limitations or explaining the motivation for the proposed approach (R4).
- **Data authenticity and geometry limitations**: There is a major conflict among reviewers: R2 states the method uses simulated parallel-beam projection data, not real-world data; R3 claims it was tested on a real-world dataset; R4 questions the term “realistic simulated data” and notes the use of “re-project” suggests data are re-generated, not genuine. No detailed imaging parameters are provided (R4). Additionally, the method is only demonstrated for parallel-beam geometry; extension to fan-beam or cone-beam is unclear (R2).
- **Complexity and hyperparameter issues**: The pipeline (autoencoder + diffusion model + blending) is very complex. Training an autoencoder is hard, there are many sub-modules, and the loss functions have many hyperparameters with no ablation study or sensitivity analysis to justify choices (R2, R3, R4). An ablation of every loss component is necessary (R2). Reviewer 3 questions whether a simpler model (e.g., a diffusion model with all losses) could be used instead, and whether the approach is end-to-end trainable.
- **Lack of reconstructed CT image evaluation**: Though the primary task is sinogram inpainting, the final goal is CT image reconstruction. Several reviewers strongly criticize the absence of quantitative and qualitative results for the reconstructed CT images (R1, R2, R4). Even minor errors in sinograms can cause streaky artifacts in the reconstructions (R2). Baseline comparisons with reconstruction methods (e.g., dual-domain reconstruction, DRONE, Learnable Multi-scale Fourier Interpolation) are missing (R2).
- **Blending algorithm novelty and limitations**: Reviewer 4 states the “novel blending algorithm” is actually a common practice in CT reconstruction from incomplete measurements. Reviewer 3 notes the blending technique is not suitable for lower mask ratios.
- **Missing comparisons and limited literature review**: The literature review is limited; many relevant CT reconstruction works (iterative reconstruction, deep learning image-domain methods, unrolling like ADMM-Net, plug-and-play methods) are not covered (R2). Comparison with sparse-view and limited-view reconstruction methods is necessary (R2).
- **Specific minor errors and unclear notation**: The phrase “perceptual loss term, $L_P$ in addition” has incorrect comma placement (R1). The statement that “ramp filtering operation for noise removal” is inaccurate—it removes blurring (R1). Notation in equation (1) (e.g., `sg`, `z_qn`) is never defined (R2). The term “phantom features” is unclear (R3).
- **Training/inference time and resource concerns**: Reviewer 2 requests training and inference time comparison (diffusion sampling is time-consuming). Reviewer 1 questions how fine-tuning with 25,000 real-world samples can be done “with limited GPU resources” (contradiction).
