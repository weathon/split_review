## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper empirically compares combinations of Losses from Learning with Noisy Labels (LNL) and Domain Generalization (DG) on three real-world datasets and one synthetic noise dataset under a unified “In-N-Out” setting. The main findings are: no single method consistently outperforms others across all benchmarks; LNL sample-selection methods degrade when data come from multiple domains because they can skew domain distributions; and combining LNL and DG losses can sometimes be worse than using each individually. Three reviewers gave score 5, one gave score 1.

### Strengths
- Addresses the realistic intersection of label noise and domain shift, an underexplored combination.
- Provides a large‑scale, thorough empirical comparison of multiple LNL and DG methods and their combinations; experiments appear well‑executed.
- Observation that LNL sample‑selection strategies (e.g., UNICON) become less effective when data come from diverse domains – the sampling can skew domain distributions, hurting generalization.
- Some interesting results (e.g., method combinations can perform worse than individual approaches) provide useful insights.
- In‑depth analysis of how algorithms like ELR, MIRO, and UNICON behave under out‑of‑distribution generalization, including showing that the ranking of LNL algorithms can change between single‑domain and multi‑domain settings.
- The authors appear to have thoroughly studied the related literature (noted by Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty.** The paper is essentially a large‑scale benchmark; no new technical solutions are proposed. The combination of LNL and DG losses is a straightforward sum of existing objective functions, and the paper does not introduce mechanisms tailored to the interaction between noise and domain shift. All three score‑5 reviewers consider the contribution low, and the score‑1 reviewer sees no novelty beyond naively combining losses.
- **Lack of a clear motivation for a unified task.** The paper does not convincingly demonstrate why handling noise and domain shift separately is insufficient, nor does it provide concrete real‑world examples or theoretical analysis that justify a combined framework. The task feels artificial rather than driven by application needs.
- **Selection of methods appears arbitrary.** Only MIRO/SWAD are used as DG components and only ELR/UNICON as LNL components, with insufficient justification for excluding other established methods. This limits the generalizability of the conclusions.
- **Key findings are not surprising.** The result that no single method wins across all settings is already known from OOD generalization benchmarks (e.g., WILDS, spurious‑correlation studies), so it is unclear what new practical advice emerges beyond the sample‑selection insight.
- **Presentation and flow are weak.** The problem statement is not well motivated, the analysis sections are hard to follow, and the main points are buried in chunks of information. Minor issues: line 186: missing `\` before `textit`; the name “In‑N‑Out” conflicts with an ICLR 2021 paper.
- **Inadequate ablation and baseline coverage.**
  - Figure 3 shows only ID performance but the discussion makes claims about OOD performance; a direct OOD comparison is needed.
  - Figure 4b compares only a few baselines; SWAD and MIRO could be combined but that combination is not shown, and other LNL/regularization options are missing.
  - Section 3.3.3: it is unclear whether the noise rate is fixed across sampling ratios; the arguments for drastic ID performance degradation are not reasonable. More justification is needed.
- **Hyperparameter burden.** The objective involves α, β, λ, γ; the reviewer with score 1 questions how to balance so many hyperparameters. (The paper does not discuss tuning or sensitivity.)
- **Specific unanswered questions that overlap with criticisms.**
  - Which of the four loss types in Eq. (3) is most important for ID/OOD under label noise? What do the tuned weights look like?
  - What model/checkpoint selection strategy is used? (This is important for DG.)
  - What is the domain definition for Clothing1M? Why is the dataset reduced to 100,000 images?
  - Line 52 claims DG methods (GroupDRO, Fishr) show implicit OOD‑robustness under noise. Is that claim empirical or theoretical? Where in those papers is it shown?
  - The distinction between “Out‑of‑Domain” and “OOD Robustness” categories is unclear.
- **Minority reviewer (score 1) highlighted additional severe concerns:**
  - No novelty beyond naïvely summing objectives.
  - Prior work (SAM adjusted for DG) already exists; why is ℛ_SAM used?
  - Too many hyperparameters with no guidance.
  - Lack of clarity on domains in Clothing1M.
