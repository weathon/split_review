## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes to improve conformal classification efficiency (smaller prediction sets) by applying test-time augmentation (TTA) to the scoring function.  Instead of using a single classifier output, the conformity score is computed from probabilities aggregated over multiple augmentations of the test input.  Two aggregation variants are studied: uniform averaging (TTA-Avg) and learned weights (TTA-Learned) trained on the calibration set via cross-entropy loss.  The method is model-agnostic, requires no retraining, and yields reported reductions in prediction set size of up to 30%.  Evaluation spans three datasets (including iNaturalist with many classes), three model architectures, two conformal scoring methods (APS and RAPS), multiple coverage levels, and simple vs. expanded augmentation policies.

### Strengths
- **Simple and practical**: The approach is easy to implement, understand, and model-agnostic, and consistently improves CP efficiency across diverse settings (datasets, models, scores, coverage levels).  No model retraining is needed.
- **Thorough empirical evaluation**: Experiments cover three datasets, three models, two conformal scores (APS, RAPS), multiple coverage values, and two augmentation policies.  Ablation analysis contrasting uniform vs. learned weights and simple vs. expanded policies is informative.
- **Insights**: The paper provides analysis into why TTA helps (e.g., improved top-k accuracy) and includes a discussion of when the method is most effective.
- **Presentation**: The paper is well-organized and easy to follow (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Validity concerns for learned weights (TTA-Learned)**: The calibration set is used to train the aggregation weights, which breaks exchangeability between calibration and test sets with respect to the conformal score.  The conformal guarantee has not been formally proved for this variant; empirical coverage matches nominal but does not constitute a proof.  To guarantee validity, three separate datasets (train, weight-learning, calibration) would be required, reducing calibration set size.  (Raised by R1, R2, R4, R5)
- **Double-dipping of calibration data**: The calibration set is used both for learning the augmentation weights and for threshold selection, violating the standard one-time use principle.  (R5)
- **Limited novelty**: Several reviewers (R5, R6) note that it is well-known that a better classifier produces smaller prediction sets.  TTA with learned weights was previously introduced by Shanmugam et al. (2021).  While some reviewers (R3) find the approach innovative and timely, the incremental contribution over existing work is questioned.  (Contrast preserved.)
- **Lack of direct link between training objective and CP efficiency**: The aggregation weights are trained to maximize accuracy via cross-entropy, but this objective does not explicitly minimize prediction set size.  Alternative losses (e.g., Stutz et al., 2022) might better align with CP goals.  The paper does not justify why cross-entropy should improve efficiency.  (R2, R4)
- **Insufficient explanation of mechanism**: The paper does not fully explain why simple test-time augmentation yields such large improvements.  Additional analyses (e.g., top-k accuracy breakdown, calibration of non-top classes) are needed.  (R6)
- **Missing comparisons to relevant baselines**: The evaluation omits comparisons with other methods for improving CP efficiency, such as ensemble conformal prediction, adaptive conformal prediction (Romano et al., 2020), and conformal training approaches.  (R3, R4, R6)
- **Computational cost undiscussed**: The impact of TTA on inference time, especially for real-time applications, is not addressed; multiple augmentations increase per-sample cost.  (R3)
- **Applicability depends on augmentation choice**: The method’s effectiveness relies on the specific augmentations used, and no guidance is provided on how to select them for different datasets or problems.  (R1, R3)
- **TTA-Learned vs. TTA-Avg performance similar for APS**: Table 4 shows that on APS, TTA-Learned and TTA-Avg achieve nearly identical efficiency; the paper offers no intuition for this observation.  (R1)
- **Learned weights are near zero**: As reported, the learned weights are close to zero, suggesting fixed weights might suffice and would restore validity.  (R2)
- **Presentation errors**: Typographical issue with the notation $k_{reg}$ in the related work; indexing from 0 to N in Section 3 should give N+1 samples; a large part of Section 4 is preliminary material that should be placed in a separate section.  (R6)
