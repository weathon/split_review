## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes BrightDreamer, an amortized text-to-3D Gaussian generator that produces 3D objects in a single feed-forward pass (~77 ms). It consists of a Text-guided Shape Deformation (TSD) network that deforms anchor points into Gaussian centers and a Text-guided Triplane Generator (TTG) that predicts the remaining four Gaussian attributes (scale, rotation, opacity, SH). Training uses SDS loss with rendering. The method sparks mixed assessments: two reviewers rate it 5 (praising speed and architecture), while two rate it 3 (citing quality, novelty, and experimental gaps).

### Strengths
- **Fast feed-forward generation**: Produces a 3D Gaussian object in 77 ms and renders at 705 fps, directly addressing the low speed of per-prompt optimization methods.
- **First amortized text-to-3D using 3D Gaussians**: Novel application to the amortized generation task, avoiding per-prompt optimization.
- **Reasonable architecture**: Reframing 3D Gaussian generation as deformation from an anchor shape and using a triplane for other attributes is a logical design. TSD and TTG contribute technical novelty (Reviewer 2).
- **Stabilizing design**: Reparameterization of Gaussian scaling helps stabilize optimization (Reviewer 1).
- **No 3D data required**: Training relies only on 2D rendered images with SDS (Reviewer 2).
- **Clear exposition**: The paper is well-written; Table 1 clearly contrasts with prior work (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).  

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient and unfair experimental comparisons**  
   - Missing baselines: Table 1 omits ATT3D (Reviewer 1) and GAN-based text-to-3D generators (Reviewer 2); Latte3D’s reported timing is incorrect (it should be <1 s) (Reviewer 1).  
   - Incomplete comparison set: Latest 3D diffusion methods (Latte3D, LGM) and improved SDS variants (MVDream, ProlificDreamer) are absent; the ProlificDreamer examples shown are below its typical quality (Reviewer 2).  
   - Should compare against other amortized text-to-3D methods, not only 3D‑GS methods (Reviewer 4).  
   - Ablation study (Fig. 9) shows only one qualitative pair; FID/CLIP scores on a holdout set are needed to demonstrate the complete design’s advantage (Reviewer 1). Improvement between (a), (b), (c) is unclear (Reviewer 3).  
   - Ablation terms (“Triplane Generator Division”, “Coordinate Shortcut”) are not defined, making the table hard to interpret (Reviewer 4).  

2. **Limited generation quality**  
   - Textures are over‑smoothed and lack realism (e.g., the rabbit in Fig. 8) (Reviewer 2).  
   - Geometry is incomplete (e.g., the car in Fig. 9(a)) (Reviewer 2).  
   - Results appear blurry, with overly saturated colors (Reviewer 3).  
   - There is a clear fidelity‑speed trade‑off; quality lags behind per‑prompt optimization (Reviewer 3).  
   - The number of anchor points and their expressive capacity are not discussed – 3DGS typically requires millions, but the fixed smaller set may limit detail (Reviewer 4).  

3. **Novelty concerns**  
   - The deformation‑from‑anchor idea is not new; similar concepts appear in Pixel2Mesh and Pixel2Mesh++ for explicit 3D generation. Applying it to 3D Gaussians is seen as an incremental adaptation (Reviewer 4).  
   - The network structure closely resembles TriplaneMeetsGaussian (Zou et al. CVPR 2024); the claimed improvements lack an ablation study to validate them (Reviewer 4).  

4. **Training constraints and generalization**  
   - Training appears category‑specific (e.g., vehicle, animal); it is unclear why categorization is needed, how many samples per class are required, and how the model handles truly arbitrary prompts (Reviewer 3).  
   - Diversity and generalization to unseen prompts are not well demonstrated; the training scale is far smaller than image diffusion models (Reviewer 3).  
   - Training is expensive: 30 + hours on 8 × 80 GB GPUs (Reviewer 3).  
   - Missing information: training iterations and parameter counts for TSD/TTG modules are not reported (Reviewer 1 questions).  

5. **Writing and missing details**  
   - Several recent 2024 works are not cited (e.g., “Taming Mode Collapse in Score Distillation”, “DiverseDream”) (Reviewer 3).  
   - Overuse of bold/italic formatting and dense paragraph layout reduces readability (Reviewer 3).  
   - Unanswered questions about quality‑speed trade‑off, generalization, training prompt selection, and similarity to prior triplane‑GS networks need clarification (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
