## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes RANKCLIP, a vision-language pretraining method that extends CLIP’s pair-wise contrastive loss to a list-wise ranking consistency loss. It enforces cross-modal rank consistency (similarity rankings of texts given an image align with rankings of images given a text) and in-modal rank consistency (rankings within each modality are consistent). The goal is to capture many-to-many relationships between images and texts. Experiments on zero-shot classification, linear probing, and image-text retrieval show improvements over CLIP and ALIP, especially on zero-shot classification. The method uses a ResNet50 backbone and is trained on datasets including CC12M, RedCaps, and YFCC15M.

### Strengths
- **Reasonable motivation:** The paper clearly identifies the limitations of rigid one-to-one matching in CLIP and argues for capturing many-to-many relationships, providing a solid rationale for RANKCLIP.
- **Novelty of the rank consistency objective:** The proposed rank consistency loss directly leverages the inherent similarity structure among samples (within and across modalities), which has not been explicitly used in prior CLIP-style methods. This is a novel way to improve embedding quality.
- **Extensive evaluation:** Experiments cover zero-shot classification, linear probe classification, and image/text retrieval across multiple datasets (ImageNet, CIFAR-10/100, Caltech101, Food101, GTSRB, etc.).
- **Robustness and semantic understanding:** RANKCLIP shows improved performance under natural distribution shifts (e.g., ImageNet variants) and better semantic understanding in retrieval tasks.
- **Scalability with data size:** The gains become more pronounced as the training dataset size increases (CIFAR → ImageNet → YFCC), suggesting promising scalability.
- **Significant gains in zero-shot classification:** RANKCLIP achieves notable improvements over state-of-the-art methods (CLIP, ALIP) on several zero-shot benchmarks.
- **Well-written and easy to understand:** The paper is clearly structured, and the method is explained in an accessible manner.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of discussion and comparison with related works:** The paper omits or insufficiently discusses several relevant methods that also improve CLIP’s alignment or construct many-to-many relationships. Missing references include:
  - Progressive self-distillation using image-to-text logits [1] and in-modal consistency [2] (raised by Reviewer 1, who notes RANKCLIP closely resembles [1]).
  - SaCo Loss [A] and ProtoCLIP [B] (Reviewer 4).
  - CyCLIP, SoftCLIP (Reviewer 2), and SigLIP (Reviewer 3).  
  This makes it difficult to assess the novelty and relative significance of the proposed approach.
- **Limited backbone evaluation:** Experiments are conducted only with ResNet50, while many contemporary CLIP works use ViT-B/32. This misalignment reduces comparability of results. Additional backbones are needed to verify generalization (Reviewers 1 and 4).
- **Unconvincing retrieval results:** There is a large disparity between image retrieval (84.1%) and text retrieval (8.1%), raising reliability concerns (Reviewer 1).
- **Performance degradation on fine-grained datasets in linear probing:** On GVGAircraft, Food101, and GTSRB, RANKCLIP underperforms; this phenomenon is not analyzed or explained (Reviewer 1).
- **Lower top-3/top-5 accuracy on CIFAR-10:** On CIFAR-10, RANKCLIP achieves lower top-3 and top-5 accuracy than CLIP (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient ablation and analysis of loss components:**
  - The interaction between the rank consistency loss and the original CLIP contrastive loss is not analyzed (e.g., gradient dynamics, whether the rank loss refines or significantly alters the embedding space, or if it alone could learn cross-modal alignment). (Reviewer 3)
  - Ablation in Table 5 shows that cross-modal consistency alone performs nearly as well as the full objective, questioning the necessity of in-modal consistency. A weighting ablation (varying the relative strengths of the two losses) is missing. (Reviewer 3)
- **Concern about noisy self-supervised ranking:** Without manual labels, the reference ranking used for consistency may be noisy, potentially making the learned objective unreliable for capturing true semantic similarity (Reviewer 4).
- **Too few baseline comparisons:** The paper only compares with CLIP and ALIP; more recent methods (e.g., CyCLIP, SoftCLIP, SigLIP) are needed to convincingly demonstrate superiority (Reviewers 2 and 3).
