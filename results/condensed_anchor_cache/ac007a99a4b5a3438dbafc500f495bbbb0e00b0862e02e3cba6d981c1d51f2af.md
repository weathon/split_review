## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper analyzes ReLU-based MLPs by interpreting the network as a continuous piecewise linear function and decomposing the input space into convex polytopes (a polytopal complex). The authors propose an algorithm (a variation of the classical Region Subdivision algorithm) that computes this decomposition using both H- and V-representations of cells. They then use the complex to compute properties such as maxima, minima, number of cells, local span, curvature, and star volumes. Empirical experiments on toy functions (e.g., Himmelblau, Griewank) examine the effects of width, depth, regularization, and initialization on these measures. The paper emphasizes validity checks (Section 3.2) and provides detailed runtime analysis (Figures 5 and 6). The motivation is to extend the validity of a neural network beyond its test set to a neighborhood, but this claim is not fully realized in the experiments.

### Strengths

- **Novel idea**: Using a set of polytopes to represent ReLU MLPs for geometric understanding of layer compositions and to align training/testing data points with polytope cells. (Reviewer 1, 4)
- **Algorithm contributions**: The variation of Region Subdivision allows both H- and V-representations; the validity checks in Section 3.2 are a necessary and appreciated step towards trustworthy methodologies, and the detailed runtime analysis (Figures 5, 6) is thorough – only Serra et al. (2018) does something similar but less detailed. (Reviewer 3)
- **Analysis capabilities**: The polytopal complex enables computing maxima, minima, number of cells, local span, curvature, and star volumes; the analysis of the effects of width, depth, and regularization on the complex is new, especially showing the impact of regularization on the number of activation regions (first work to do so). (Reviewer 1, 3, 4)
- **Visualization**: The paper uses level sets to make three-dimensional functions clearer (a not very common approach in the field). Figure 8 looks awesome, though it lacks axis labels and a legend. (Reviewer 2, 3)
- **Theoretical analysis not limited to shallow alignment**: Translating polytope properties into behaviors of NN layers. (Reviewer 1)
- **Motivation**: The idea of extending validity beyond test data is interesting and could inform data augmentation/pruning strategies. (Reviewer 3 – but also a weakness that the paper does not deliver on this claim)

### Weaknesses

#### 1. Motivation and contributions mismatched / overstated
- The paper claims to “extend the validity of a neural network beyond a discrete test data point to its neighbors” but does not include experiments that perform testing beyond the test set or demonstrate how the polytopal decomposition changes perceived generalization capabilities. The main focus is instead on analyzing properties of linear regions. This makes the story inconsistent. (Reviewer 3)
- It is unclear why the community would benefit from polytopal analyses over standard techniques (e.g., examining Hessians for convexity, or directly analyzing piecewise linear nature). The connection between geometric properties (curvature, hyperplanes, stars) and their implications for real-world performance is missing. (Reviewer 1, 2)
- The paper does not specify what “assess the network” means; metrics for evaluating generalization, robustness, or other capabilities are not defined. (Reviewer 1)

#### 2. Novelty concerns
- The algorithm in Section 3.1 is an incremental modification of the classical Region Subdivision algorithm. The validity and time checks are pleasant but do not provide significant novelty on their own. The contribution would be much stronger if the paper included experiments that extend testing beyond the test set, as the motivation suggests. (Reviewer 3)
- For acceptance to a venue like ICLR, the current novelty is borderline; Reviewer 3 explicitly states rating is unlikely to change beyond a boundary reject (5) without deeper experiments (e.g., on real-world datasets). Note: Reviewers 1 and 4 found the algorithm novel, but Reviewer 3’s reservation is a minority, stronger skepticism.

#### 3. Incomplete literature review
- Essential prior works on activation/linear regions are missing, including Hanin & Rolnick (2019), Wang (2022), Liu et al. (2023), Arora et al. (2018), Serra et al. (2018), Raghu et al. (2017), Novak et al. (2018), Gamba et al. (2022), Hanin & Rolnick (2019) [9], Zhang & Wu (2020), Croce et al. (2019), Xiong et al. (2020). (Reviewer 3)
- As a result, the paper does not cite that Liu et al. (2023) already showed adjacent activation regions differ in only one bit of their activation sequence (cf. L232). (Reviewer 3)
- The paper should clarify whether it works on activation regions or linear regions; convexity (C_k = conv(v_1,…,v_p)) does not hold for linear regions as noted by Hanin & Rolnick (2019), so this should be stated early. (Reviewer 3)
- Additional references on bounds of cells and vertices are needed. (Reviewer 4)

#### 4. Practical limitations and scalability
- The time complexity is O(|vertices|), and the number of vertices can be exponential in the number of neurons/layers/input dimension. The paper does not provide a clear analysis or bounds on how vertex count scales with network parameters (layers, neurons, input dimension), making it unclear if the algorithm is feasible for deep networks or high-dimensional input. (Reviewer 4)
- All experiments use only two-dimensional inputs and simple toy functions (four target functions with two inputs each). Scalability to higher dimensions or more complex network architectures (e.g., convolutional, recurrent) is not discussed. The paper needs to acknowledge limitations of toy examples and how results might generalize. (Reviewer 1, 2, 3)
- Although the authors mention their method works for higher dimensions, the experiments primarily focus on two-dimensional cases, similar to the work of Humayun et al. (2023). The comparison with Humayun et al. should detail specific advantages in higher-dimensional settings where computational complexity may be limiting. (Reviewer 2)
- The assumption that the network is a continuous piecewise linear function (ReLU MLP) is not useful for DNNs in practice that use other non-linearities (sigmoid, tanh) or complex architectures (conv, recurrent). The paper should discuss limitations and whether approximations are needed for other activations like ELU. (Reviewer 1, 4)

#### 5. Clarity, detail, and presentation issues
- **Introduction**: The example and Figure 1 are vague – “symmetry of the data” is not defined, and the preference for the right plot is not justified beyond visual intuition. (Reviewer 1)
- **Algorithm explanation**: The transition from polytope properties to NN behavior is insufficient; more concrete examples are needed. The details of (α³) in Section 3 are confusing; sketches would help. (Reviewer 1, 3)
- **Figure 8**: Lacks axis labels and a legend, making it difficult to interpret despite being visually appealing. (Reviewer 2)
- **Minor typos**: 
  - Line 150: “MLP” should be “MPL”. (Reviewer 2, 3)
  - Line 234: missing final “s” in “assess”. (Reviewer 2)
  - Line 318: “the the”. (Reviewer 2)
  - Lines 134, 150, 235-236, 265, 266: typos. (Reviewer 3)
  - Line 150: does not mention which appendix to go to. (Reviewer 3)
- **Figures 4 and 5**: Do not specify unit for time; Figure 4b has wrong y-label; digits in yellow cells of Figure 6 are unreadable. (Reviewer 3)
- **Equation (1)**: Should mention ∑λ_i = 1, otherwise it is not true. (Reviewer 4)
- **Unclear quantities**: What are ρ and ν* from L128? (Reviewer 3)
- **Not all readers will be acquainted with topology**; visualizing what a star is would make the paper easier to read. (Reviewer 3)

#### 6. Specific missing experiments and unresolved questions
- No experiments extend testing beyond the test set as promised; the paper only shows theoretical possibility. A concrete future direction would be to estimate neighboring linear regions via linear search from a data point, and then measure robustness or accuracy on extended test and training sets. (Reviewer 3)
- Question: What does “checking can further check if any derived vertex lies outside of the input cube” (L235) mean? Why are the validity checks in Section 3.2 needed? Does the algorithm not guarantee validity? If checks fail, what do we do? (Reviewer 2)
- Question: In L369-370, what do the authors mean by “until the training collapses”? (Reviewer 3)
- Question: What did the authors mean by “until the training collapses” in L414? (Reviewer 3)  
  *Note: This appears duplicated in the text; likely the same question.*
- Question: In L429, how is the scope different from Balestriero & LeCun (2023)? (Reviewer 2)
- Have the authors considered generalizing to classification? It would be interesting to see the relation between robustness of activation regions and sample counts. (Reviewer 3 – curiosity question but overlaps with missing direction)

#### 7. Minority reviewer disagreement
- Reviewer 2 (rating 3, confidence 2) is notably more negative than Reviewers 1, 3, and 4 (ratings 5, 5, 5). Reviewer 2 questions the overall community benefit, missing comparison details, and lack of real-world application, while the other reviewers find the idea novel and the analysis valuable. This contrast should be preserved: Reviewer 2’s lower confidence and stronger skepticism regarding usefulness of the approach should be noted. Reviewer 3 also expresses novelty concerns but still gave a 5, while providing the most detailed criticism.
