## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a variant of Joint PSRO (JPSRO) that incorporates diversity measures to solve for correlated equilibria in n‑player general‑sum games. It defines a new equilibrium concept – Diverse (Coarse) Correlated Equilibrium (D(C)CE) – and an algorithm, Diverse Correlated Oracle (DCO). Reviewers find the research direction interesting and the theory sound, but raise significant concerns about writing clarity, missing algorithmic details, unconvincing experimental results, and the marginality of contributions. The paper received ratings of 3 from all reviewers.

### Strengths
- The paper examines an interesting domain and introduces ideas about diversity that have not been previously precisely formulated for n‑player games (R1).
- Experimental results show promise and positive effects on convergence rate in some games (R1, R4).
- The research direction is interesting and the theory appears sound (R2).
- The paper studies an important problem: the role of diversity in strategy exploration methods for empirical game solving (R3).
- The ablation study covers both reward and behavioral diversity measures (R3).
- The paper theoretically and empirically shows that the solution can be computed analytically (R3).
- The concept of using policy diversity as a metric in selecting a meta‑strategy for PSRO is interesting (R4).

### Weaknesses
**Experimental Evaluation Concerns**
- The experimental results do not showcase the actual diversity of the found solutions; the paper focuses only on low exploitability, which is a critical weakness (R1).
- The reported performance relies on internal parameters “selected appropriate through extensive hyper‑parameter tuning”; it is unclear whether this is a fair comparison—other techniques were not similarly fine‑tuned (R1). When the hyperparameter \(k\) is small, the solution optimizes for maximizing \(\sigma^\top \sigma\) rather than diversity, so success may not be due to diversity (R1).
- The results are not convincingly strong: only in 3‑Player Kuhn Poker is the novel method clearly better; two of the three games (Blotto, First‑Price Sealed‑Bid Auction) are 2‑player, not multi‑player, and the method shows only marginal improvement or worse (R2, R3).
- In the most comprehensive test (ablation of diversity metrics on Kuhn Poker), the proposed method (a combination of prior methods) performs second‑worst, being outperformed by both prior methods individually (R3, R4).
- The ablation study of different diversity metrics is shown only on a single game (3‑player Kuhn Poker) (R3). It is unclear how the methods compare on other games (R3).
- The experiments only consider CCE and measure the CCE gap; it is not shown whether the method can be used for CE as well (R2). The paper frames itself as solving for CE and CCE generally but only evaluates for Maximum Welfare CCE (R3).
- Missing baselines: NE is not included as a meta‑strategy solver (R3). For the Blotto game (2‑player zero‑sum), comparison to standard PSRO with a Nash equilibrium meta‑solver is possible but not done (R2).

**Writing and Clarity Issues**
- The algorithm is not clearly explained. Section 4 and Algorithm 1 do not define the meta‑solver (MS) or the diversity measure (DM); the paper does not state that the output of DCO is supposed to be a D(C)CE, nor prove that the termination condition yields a D(C)CE (R2). The choice of the function \(f\) used in the experiments is never described (Section 3.1 defines \(f_{i|l,m,\dots,q}\) in terms of \(f\), but the specific \(f\) is unspecified; Section 2.4 lists two diversity measures but the method of combination is left undefined) (R2).
- The game definition in Section 2.1 describes a complete‑information, alternating‑move sequential game, but the paper claims generality to incomplete‑information or simultaneous‑move settings (R4). Notations for game and meta‑game need improvement (R2).
- Several symbols are undefined or incorrectly used: \(\Pi^{O:T}_{-i}\) from Marris et al. is not defined (R1); \(\rho\) in Equation (4) is not defined (R1); “MGCE” is mentioned but not defined (R2); “meta‑strategy” may be confused with “joint meta‑strategy” (R2); \(A_i\) and \(\sigma\) not clearly defined as vectors (R2); “recommended policy” in CCE definition not defined earlier (R2); the \(\epsilon\)-CE definition is non‑standard and may cause confusion (R4).
- The diversity metrics in Equations (3) and (4) suffer from issues: (a) the metric depends on the ordering of profiles (which strategy is new) and should be expressed as a two‑argument function (R4); (b) the occupancy measure is not defined (R4); (c) since Nash equilibria are not generally unique, Equation (4) is not well‑defined until a specific NE is selected—this should be discussed (R4); (d) the relationship between the diversity metric \(\text{Div}(\cdot)\) and the function \(f(\cdot)\) used later is unclear; \(\text{Div}(\cdot)\) seems designed to affect the best‑response oracle, while \(f\) is used in the meta‑solver (R4).
- The paper states that “the sampling probability of the policy is closer to the uniform distribution, the entropy or Gini impurity is larger” but the term “sampling probability of a policy” is not precisely defined (R2).
- The statement that “calculating entropy can be challenging” is not justified—entropy computation is \(O(|N| \times |\Pi|)\) (R2).
- Many typos and formatting issues: “frameowrk” (R1, R3, R4); “Itertions” (R1, R2); spacing before citations (R3); quotation marks in Section 2.1 used incorrectly (R2); grammar errors (“used to denoted”, “The NE is located”, “only consider deviating until”) (R2).

**Conceptual and Theoretical Concerns**
- It is not clear why we should want to “increase the sampling probability assigned to diverse policies” nor that this is well‑defined (R3). Diverse policies are not necessarily profitable in equilibrium; if the population is already diverse, a uniform distribution would be preferred, and if not, increasing sampling of diverse policies could reduce return (R3). The paper does not explain how this applies to deterministic versus stochastic policies (R3).
- The paper does not provide intuition for why maximizing the sum of each player’s policy diversity expectations achieves the goal of assigning higher probability to diverse policies (R2). The diversity expectation is defined with \(f_{i|l,m,\dots,q}\) which depends on the set of policies but not on their distribution, yet the objective is about the distribution (R2).
- The contribution appears marginal: the method essentially uses existing diversity measures to solve for MWCCE, and the theoretical analysis is not deep (R3, R1).
- The citation for correlated equilibrium is incorrect—the standard citation is Aumann 1974, not Farina et al. 2020 or Marris et al. 2022 (R4).
- The definition of \(\epsilon\)-CE is non‑standard; it implies that the uniform‑random policy is a \(1/N\)-CE and that not every \(\epsilon\)-CE is an \(\epsilon\)-CCE (R4). This may be confusing.
- Proposition 3.1 uses the term “elementary matrix” in a non‑standard way (R4).
- The paper claims that solving Equations (7‑9) is “unrealistic”, but if the game is manageable, it is just a convex program (R4).
- Theorem 3.4 may actually hold for all \(\epsilon>0\), not just small \(\epsilon\) as implied (R4).
- The phrase “isolation diversity term is meaningless” is not clarified (R3).
- The paper states that “diversity has the … and being computationally tractable” but diversity is not generally guaranteed to be tractable (R3).

**Missing Analyses and Comparisons**
- The paper does not provide qualitative examples of the policy diversity expectation value for different strategy profiles, or show the difference in distributions over strategy profiles for D(C)CE vs. MG(C)CE (R2).
- The hyperparameter tuning process for \(k\) is not described; when \(k\) is very small the solution does not optimize for diversity but for maximizing \(\sigma^\top \sigma\) (R1). It is not shown whether fine‑tuning other techniques would improve their performance (R1).
- The paper does not explain how the diversity metric \(\text{Div}\) relates to the function \(f\) used in the meta‑solver (R4).
- The paper does not compare methods in terms of finding an equilibrium as opposed to the maximum welfare equilibrium (R3).
- The paper does not clarify what “CCE Gap evaluated under Maximum Welfare CCE” means (R2).
- For the First‑Price Sealed‑Bid Auction, which is described as multi‑player, only a 2‑player experiment is shown (R2).
