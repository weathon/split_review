## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents RaT-BPTT (Random Truncated Backpropagation Through Time) for dataset distillation, addressing bilevel optimization by coupling truncation with a random window to stabilize gradients, reduce computational burden, and capture long dependencies. It also introduces Boosted Dataset Distillation (Boost-DD) to mitigate intercorrelation among distilled samples. Empirical results show state-of-the-art performance on CIFAR, CUB, and Tiny-ImageNet under multiple images-per-class (IPC) settings. Note: Reviewer 1’s confidence is low (2) and score is 5, while Reviewer 2 is highly confident (5, score 8) and Reviewer 3 is fairly confident (3, score 6). All agree the method is simple and effective.

### Strengths
- **Simple, effective, and easily applicable** – The core idea of random truncation in BPTT is straightforward and can be integrated with other bilevel optimization-based dataset distillation methods (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).  
- **Thorough empirical evaluation with SoTA results** – Outperforms most baselines across multiple datasets (CIFAR, CUB, Tiny-ImageNet) and IPC settings (Reviewers 2, 3).  
- **Improved efficiency for meta-gradient computation** – Compared to full BPTT (Deng & Russakovsky), reduces both memory and time (Reviewer 2).  
- **Hardness-stratified analysis (Figure 10)** – Provides insight into which examples benefit most from distillation; appreciated by Reviewer 2.  
- **Ablation studies** – Clearly demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed components (Reviewer 3).  

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical justification** – No theory explaining why RaT-BPTT outperforms BPTT or truncated BPTT. Reviewer 1 suggests a simple setting to analyze when/why it works; Reviewer 3 echoes difficulty in analysis.  
- **Questionable stability metric** – Using meta-gradient norm change (Figure 3) as an indicator of training stability is insufficient. Reviewer 2 recommends measuring gradient variance or Hessian eigenvalues instead, and suggests gradient clipping or scaling as a straightforward alternative (also noted by Reviewer 3).  
- **Boosting not studied with other distillation frameworks** – Boost-DD is presented as optimization-agnostic but only tested with RaT-BPTT. Reviewer 2 asks whether it would work with DSA, MTT, etc., and expects an ablation comparing RaT-BPTT with SGD vs. Adam (since all existing methods use SGD; maybe Adam alone explains improvements). Reference [5] (Farzi Data) recommended.  
- **Incomplete presentation of boosting section** – Section 5 lacks key details (shifted to appendix). Reviewer 2 suggests keeping a subset of results from Figures 7–10 in the main text with full experimental details.  
- **Missing robustness study of boosting strength \(\beta\)** – No comprehensive analysis of how varying \(\beta\) affects final performance (Reviewer 2).  
- **Scalability concerns** –  
  - Storing a trajectory of length \(M\) may hinder scaling to large datasets (Reviewers 2, 3). Reviewer 3 notes that backpropagating through the truncated inner trajectory remains memory-intensive and existing reversible learning techniques [6] may not apply. Empirical demonstration of memory reduction without performance degradation is requested.  
  - No experiments on ImageNet-1000 or on architectures beyond shallow-to-wide convnets (e.g., VGG, EfficientNet, ResNet) (Reviewer 3).  
- **No computational cost comparison with baselines** – Reviewer 3 points out that RaT-BPTT is likely much more expensive than methods like FRePO that simplify the inner loop; such a comparison is missing.  
- **Missing discussion of general bilevel optimization applicability** – Reviewer 1 asks whether RaT-BPTT transfers to other bilevel problems beyond dataset distillation, suggesting a discussion.  
- **Other missing experiments / ablations** (from reviewer questions):  
  - What happens if meta-gradients of BPTT are scaled by gradient norm (clipping) instead of random truncation? (Reviewer 3)  
  - What if the total number of unrolling steps is gradually increased rather than fixed \(T\)? (Reviewer 3)  
  - Full-data performance should be included in Table 1, and the efficient computation of meta-gradients for SGD inner loops [6] should be cited (Reviewer 2).
