## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes SiDA (Score identity Distillation with Adversarial Loss), which extends SiD by adding an adversarial loss using real images. The discriminator is built by reusing the encoder of the fake score network (averaging encoder feature maps). The method achieves state-of-the-art one-step generation FID scores on CIFAR-10 (1.499 unconditional, 1.396 conditional), ImageNet 64×64 (1.110), and surpasses the teacher EDM2-XXL on ImageNet 512×512 (1.366 vs. 1.81 with CFG and 63 steps). Training remains relatively stable, and the method converges faster than SiD from scratch and improves pre-distilled generators after warmup.

### Strengths

- **Strong empirical results:** Achieves state-of-the-art one-step generation on CIFAR-10, ImageNet-64, and sets record-low FIDs when distilling EDM2 models on ImageNet 512×512, surpassing the teacher EDM2-XXL (1.366 vs. 1.81 with CFG and 63 steps) (R1, R2, R4).  
- **Efficient discriminator design:** Repurposes the fake score network’s encoder as a discriminator (averaging encoder features), adding minimal parameter cost (R1).  
- **Improvement over teacher and baseline:** The use of real images allows generation quality to exceed the teacher model (R2).  
- **Training stability:** Despite the adversarial loss, training remains relatively stable (R2).  
- **Comprehensive experiment analysis:** The paper provides detailed training statistics and analysis of the training dynamics (R3).  
- **Well-presented background:** The exposition of SiD and adversarial loss in diffusion is clear (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited generalization to transformer architectures:** The discriminator design relies on U-Net encoder–decoder structure (averaging encoder feature maps); it is not straightforward to adapt to transformer-based diffusion models (e.g., DiT), and the paper provides no discussion or preliminary results on such adaptation (R1).  
- **Lack of high-resolution evaluation:** Experiments are restricted to small-scale datasets and low resolutions (CIFAR-10 32×32, ImageNet 64×64, FFHQ-64, AFHQ-64). The paper does not evaluate on the more common ImageNet 256×256 setting or on text-to-image generation, which limits assessment of scalability and fairness with recent methods (R1, R2, R3, R4). Only a few comparisons are given at 512×512 (ImageNet 512) (R3).  
- **Missing comparisons with recent methods:** Several state-of-the-art generative/distillation methods are absent: DiT, RDM, MAR (R4). Also missing is a comparison with OSGAN, another one-step adversarial training approach (R4). More generally, the paper does not include many recent one-step methods at larger scales (R3).  
- **Reproducibility concerns:** The paper does not mention the release of code or trained weights, raising doubts about reproducibility (R4).  
- **Ambiguity in novel contribution:** Adding an adversarial term to SiD is a straightforward combination of existing ideas (UFOGen, SD3-turbo); the novelty is limited (R3).  
- **Efficiency advantage not clearly demonstrated:** Although Figure 3 shows faster FID decrease in later training, each iteration incurs larger gradient magnitude and additional computational cost (adversarial loss + discriminator), so overall training time may not be shorter than SiD (R3).  
- **Insufficient ablation and hyperparameter justification:**  
    - Only the balancing factor α is ablated; the core hyperparameters (λ_sid=100, λ_adv=0.01) are set arbitrarily without discussion or ablation, despite likely having significant impact (R2).  
    - The necessity of the L1(θ) term and the role of α are not explained: “Why is L1(θ) still needed with the additional discriminator, i.e., why SiDA still needs tuning α?” (R1).  
- **Unclear formulation and motivation:**  
    - The “Loss Construction that Respects the Weighting of Diffusion Distillation Loss” (Section 4) is unclear—how Eq. 7/8 differ from PatchGAN and how the proposed weight “honors existing weighting” is not explained (R3).  
    - The motivation for “incorporating average fakeness within each GPU batch” is not well clarified. Why does this specific design solve the gap between diffusion distillation and adversarial learning? (R4).  
    - The reason for faster convergence when training from scratch is not adequately explained (R4).  
- **Unaddressed performance instability at certain settings:** In Figure 1, for α=1.2, after ~1000 iterations the Inception Score drops and FID spikes; this setting is also missing from Table 1. Does this indicate a failure to converge? (R2).  
- **Missing experiments on data efficiency and teacher generality:**  
    - How does performance change when using only a subset (e.g., 10%) of real images? (R2).  
    - Does the approach generalize to teacher models other than VP-EDM? (R2).  
- **Presentation improvements needed:** A schematic of the framework would significantly improve readability (R2). Some equations (e.g., between lines 149 and 151) are unnecessary and can be removed (R4).
