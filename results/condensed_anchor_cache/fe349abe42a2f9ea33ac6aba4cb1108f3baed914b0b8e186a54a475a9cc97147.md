## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses Federated Long-Tailed Learning (Fed-LT) where data from clients is globally long-tailed. The authors propose FedLoGe to improve both local personalized models and a generic global model by integrating representation learning and classifier alignment within a neural collapse framework. The method consists of a Static Sparse Equiangular Tight Frame Classifier (SSE-C) to enhance representations, and Global and Local Adaptive Feature Realignment (GLA-FR) via an auxiliary global classifier and personalized norm transfer. Experiments on CIFAR-10/100-LT, ImageNet-LT, and iNaturalist demonstrate advantages over state-of-the-art pFL and Fed-LT methods. Reviewers generally agree the problem is important and the method shows promise, though concerns about clarity, missing details, and novelty were raised.

### Strengths
- **Problem significance**: The paper tackles an important and emerging problem of federated learning with long-tailed data distributions (Reviewer 1, 3, 4).
- **Method design**: The key idea of using an Equiangular Tight Frame (ETF) to build a classifier as a regularized consensus is well-motivated (R1). The integration of neural collapse and realignment for local-generic performance is novel (R2). The proposed SSE-C effectively addresses feature degeneration (R3).
- **Experimental strength**: Experiments are well-designed and comprehensive, with ablation studies (R1), multiple baselines (R2), and evaluation on various backbones and datasets of different scales (R4). The proposed method significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines (R2, R4).
- **Presentation**: Figure 2 provides a clear illustration of the method (R2, R4). The paper is generally well-structured (R3, R4). (Note: some reviewers found the paper hard to follow; see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing technical details and motivation**:
  - The mechanism of personal classifier realignment (Eq. 7) and how local classifier norm influences the global classifier to address local heterogeneity is not clearly explained (R1).
  - The motivation for keeping two classifier heads per client (ϕ_k and ψ_k) and the realignment of the global classifier (Eq. 6) is insufficient; it is unclear why a single personalized classifier would not suffice and why a fixed classifier (as suggested by neural collapse) cannot be used for all clients (R1, R2).
  - The purpose of introducing sparsity to SSE-C beyond its empirical effectiveness is not justified (R1).
  - The method of fixing the classifier and backbone alternatively for training has been proposed in previous personalized FL papers; novelty is not clearly articulated (R4).
  - The optimization of Eq. (4) (which contains a maximization over j) is not detailed (R1).
  - The paper does not clearly explain why the proposed method improves performance on long-tailed FL (R4).
- **Clarity and presentation issues**:
  - Figure 1 is difficult to comprehend: axes are not clearly defined, and the relationship between the plotted mean/variance of class means and the claimed feature collapse/degeneration is not explained (R1, R2, R4). The connection between variance changes and improvement in feature quality is not substantiated (R4). The message from the first paragraph of page 3 about large/small means is unclear (R4).
  - Notation is inconsistent: number of clients is K in Section 3 but N in Section 4 (R4). Typos: "multiple the norm" should be "multiplying" (R4). Citation formats need fixing (R1).
  - The paper is hard to follow overall (R2, R4), although Reviewer 3 found it well-written.
- **Missing comparisons and discussion**:
  - The paper lacks a detailed comparison with related long-tailed FL work, especially FedETF, and does not clearly specify the technical contributions over prior work (R1). The related work section is merely a list; the differences with previous work should be added to clarify contributions (R3).
- **Missing analyses**:
  - Convergence analysis (both experimental and theoretical) is missing (R3).
  - Privacy discussion is lacking; the method has risk of gradient disclosure (R3).
  - Communication overhead is likely large and requires experiments and analysis (R3).
- **Unclear experimental settings**:
  - The number of clients participating in each round and the number of local epochs are not stated (R4). It is unclear whether the paper focuses on cross-silo or cross-device FL (R4).
- **Concerns about problem setting** (minority reviewer contrast):
  - Reviewer 2 raised that the generic-local setting appears self-contradictive: in personalized FL, the goal is models adapted for each client’s distribution, so the necessity of a model for global/generic distribution is not adequately justified. However, after rebuttal, Reviewer 2 noted that the authors’ responses addressed these concerns. Relatedly, this reviewer initially doubted the significance and broad applicability but later was satisfied.
