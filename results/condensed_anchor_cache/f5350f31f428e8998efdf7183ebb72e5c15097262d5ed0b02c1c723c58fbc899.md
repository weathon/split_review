## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MDGS, a GNN-based method for rigid body dynamics simulation that incorporates force analysis (contact and non-contact forces) and enforces physical constraints derived from Kane’s equations. The model uses object-centric and contact-centric graphs and is evaluated on simulated datasets including the Cubli robot and other multibody systems. Reviewer opinions are sharply divided: Reviewers 1 and 4 rate the paper low (3/10) citing poor presentation, questionable physics rigor, missing comparisons, and flawed baseline implementations; Reviewer 2 is moderate (5) with concerns about dataset description and evaluation; Reviewer 3 is strongly positive (8) praising originality, solid theoretical proof, thorough experiments, and significance for real-world applications.

### Strengths
- **Modeling complex mechanical structures:** The paper addresses an underexplored area—learning dynamics of objects with intricate mechanical structures (e.g., the Cubli robot), which reviewers 1, 3, and 4 agree is valuable. Reviewer 3 states no prior work specifically learns such dynamics and provides compelling evidence (Figure 1) that previous approaches struggle.
- **Generalization experiments:** The evaluation includes varying shapes, motion intensities, and density parameters (Reviewer 1).
- **Theoretical grounding:** Reviewer 3 finds the theoretical proof solid (even if Kane’s equation part is not fully understood), and the integration of force analysis and graph construction well-conceived. Reviewer 4 acknowledges the focus on embedding physical constraints is relevant.
- **Dataset and code release:** The paper provides a robust benchmark dataset with statistical validation, and releases code and data, which Reviewer 4 considers a valuable resource (Reviewer 4, also noted in abstract).
- **Strong empirical performance:** Reviewer 3 highlights that MDGS outperforms previous approaches by a good margin, with thorough qualitative and quantitative results.
- **Clarity of exposition:** Reviewer 3 finds the paper well-written and clear with detailed explanations and intuitive figures (contrasting with Reviewer 1’s view on presentation).

### Weaknesses
**1. Poor presentation and clarity of core contributions**
- Kane’s equation section (line 146) lacks motivation and connection to GNN design; feels like a textbook insertion without context (Reviewer 1).
- Teaser figure is confusing; does not clearly separate this work from GNS or SGNN (Reviewer 1).
- Coordinate system is unclear: how Euclidean particle graphs are constructed under generalized coordinates is not transparent. If minimal coordinate knowledge is needed, the advantage over first-principle simulators like Drake is questionable (Reviewer 1).
- Minor typo: line 228 should refer to \(\tilde{G}(t)\), not \(\bar{G}(t)\) (Reviewer 4).

**2. Questionable physics rigor and missing validation of constraints**
- Authors do not state that Kane’s equations derive from variational principles; visualizations of predicted forces \(\tilde{F}\) and \(\bar{F}\) against Kane’s definitions are absent (Reviewer 1).
- No analysis of conserved quantities or whether the learned system adds/removes energy (Reviewer 1).
- How boundary conditions (e.g., floor contacts) are represented under “non-contact forces” is unclear (Reviewer 1).
- Without ground-truth force labels, predicted forces cannot be verified against real forces; Theorem 3.1 relies on accurate force predictions, which is not established (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 2 suggests analyzing relative magnitudes of learned forces across datasets, locations, and timesteps.
- Reviewer 2 requests a metric to evaluate whether constraints are better satisfied by MDGS (e.g., constraint violation error) beyond MSE.

**3. Missing critical experiments and ablations**
- **Baseline implementation flawed:** The GNS baseline is implemented with a GCN and node features, not the original MPNN architecture; results thus do not reflect a fair comparison to the state-of-the-art (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing comparison to FIGNet:** The paper should compare to FIGNet (Allen et al., 2023), a leading model for rigid body simulation (Reviewer 4).
- **Ablation on GNN architectures:** Only GCN is tested. If GNS were used, Figure 7(d)’s results could change (MDGS would match GNS+rigid constraint). The structure constraint module appears model-agnostic, but this is not sufficiently explored (Reviewer 2).
- **Node feature encoding:** Using absolute positions loses shift invariance; relative positional encoding (as in original GNS) is standard and should be ablated (Reviewer 4).
- **Graph construction for contacts:** The contact-centric graph treats each contact as independent, limiting receptive fields (e.g., force propagation in a chain of objects). An ablation with a fully connected graph for contact force prediction and final \(Z\) prediction is needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing runtime comparison:** A key advantage of neural dynamics is speed; the paper lacks runtime comparisons with analytical methods (e.g., finite element analysis) and optimized rigid body simulators (Reviewers 3 and 4).
- **Partitioning details:** No description of dataset partitioning method, control variable variation between train/test, number of nodes/objects, or ablation on partition size (visualizations would help) (Reviewer 2).
- **Figure 1 analysis questioned:** Claim that predicting mechanical structure is harder based on error comparison across different tasks may be invalid without normalization consistency (Reviewer 4).

**4. Scope and applicability limitations**
- The MSE loss requires ground-truth object states, restricting the method to simulation; training on real-world data is not addressed (Reviewer 3).
- The method may be limited to certain mechanical systems; challenges for humanoid robot dynamics are not discussed (Reviewer 3).
- The model currently relies on analytic decomposition (Kane’s equations); its generalizability to systems where such decomposition is not available is unclear (from Reviewer 1’s comment on coordinate systems).

**5. Dataset and protocol ambiguity**
- Table 1: Is \(t=40\) the MSE at that specific timestep or averaged over all \(t \leq 40\)? Unclear (Reviewer 2).
- The impact of the scaling factor \(\lambda\) on input \(F_t\) is not explained; theoretical scaling should be mitigated by network weights but its actual effect on performance is unexamined (Reviewer 4).
