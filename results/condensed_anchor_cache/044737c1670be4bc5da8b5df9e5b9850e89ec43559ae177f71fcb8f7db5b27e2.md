## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a learning-based framework to generate complete feasible solutions for Integer Programming (IP) end-to-end. It uses contrastive learning (inspired by CLIP) to embed IP instances and solutions, then a diffusion model (DDPM/DDIM) conditioned on IP representations to sample solution embeddings, with a guided sampling strategy to account for constraints and objectives. Experiments on four IP datasets show high feasible ratios (≥89.7%) without solver intervention, and when combined with SCIP’s CompleteSol heuristic, solutions outperform Neural Diving and SCIP heuristics by 3.7–33.7% in gap to optimal, with ≥99.7% feasibility. The method also scales from small training instances to larger ones. Reviewers disagree: one is strongly positive (score 8, confidence low), while the other three are more critical (scores 5,6,6) and point to significant weaknesses.

### Strengths
- **Important direction**: Generating complete feasible solutions end-to-end is a valuable attempt that goes beyond prior partial-solution methods (Reviewer 3, confidence 4).
- **Technical components**: The combination of contrastive learning, diffusion models, and IP-specific guidance is sound and tailored to IP (Reviewer 1, confidence 2; Reviewer 2, confidence 3).
- **Experimental results**: The method shows high feasible ratios and better objective values than Neural Diving and SCIP heuristic on four datasets; scalability to larger instances is demonstrated (Reviewer 3, confidence 4; Reviewer 4, confidence 4).
- **Ablation and hyperparameter studies**: Ablation confirms the benefit of IP guidance; hyperparameter experiments on gradient scale \(s\) and leverage factor \(\gamma\) are included (Reviewer 3, confidence 4).
- **Presentation**: The paper is well motivated and easy to follow (Reviewer 3, confidence 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparison to optimal solutions**: The paper reports only relative comparisons to baselines; without absolute gap to the global optimum (or a known lower bound), a high feasible ratio is insufficient—feasible solutions may be far from optimal (Reviewer 1, confidence 2; Reviewer 4 confidence 4, major item 3).
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons**:  
  - Only Neural Diving and SCIP are compared; several more recent learning-based diving or solution generation methods are omitted (e.g., “A general large neighborhood search framework…”, “Learning large neighborhood search policy…”, “MIP-GNN”). The advantage over these lines is unclear (Reviewer 2, confidence 3).  
  - Reviewer 3 (confidence 4) suggests adding [1] as a baseline.  
  - The claim of “first to generate complete solutions” is questioned (Reviewer 3, confidence 4).  
- **Choice of SCIP over Gurobi**: SCIP is often slower/less powerful than Gurobi, yet the paper uses SCIP as the main solver baseline. Justification is lacking, and Gurobi comparisons would be more convincing (Reviewer 2 question, confidence 3; Reviewer 4 major item 2, confidence 4).
- **Fairness of SCIP comparison**: The paper takes the first feasible solution found by SCIP heuristics, not solutions within a fixed time budget. This may understate SCIP’s capability and is an unfair comparison (Reviewer 4 major item 1, confidence 4).
- **Missing ablation for contrastive learning**: The benefit of contrastive learning over supervised learning (with labeled solutions) is not evaluated; an ablation is needed (Reviewer 2 question, confidence 3).
- **No training time/resource reporting**: Training and inference time (especially for diffusion models) are not reported, making it hard to assess practical efficiency and scalability (Reviewer 2, confidence 3; Reviewer 3, confidence 4).
- **Missing details on modified diffusion model**:  
  - The prediction loss (Eq. 3) differs from standard diffusion; intuitive explanation and the resulting inference algorithm are not provided (Reviewer 3, confidence 4).  
  - The claim that “GCN always gains feasible solutions” in Gasse et al. (2019) contradicts the paper’s statement that GCN often yields infeasible solutions (Reviewer 2 question, confidence 3).  
- **Contradiction about solver reliance**: The paper claims “no reliance on an IP solver” yet uses SCIP’s CompleteSol heuristic to complete partial solutions; this hybrid nature needs clarification (Reviewer 4 minor item 1, confidence 4).
- **Unclear/weak explanations**:  
  - The reason for setting coverage \(C\) to 0.1/0.2 due to “difficulty” when \(C>0.2\) is vague—does it mean zero feasible partial solutions in 30 generated samples? Exact failure conditions should be given (Reviewer 4 minor item 2, confidence 4).  
  - The method’s use case is not described; it is unclear when this approach is preferable to existing learning-based IP solvers (Reviewer 2, confidence 3).  
  - Potential for repeated solutions without performance improvement is not addressed (Reviewer 4 minor item 3, confidence 4).  
- **Typo**: “DDIM then” on page 3 last paragraph (Reviewer 4 minor item 4, confidence 4).
