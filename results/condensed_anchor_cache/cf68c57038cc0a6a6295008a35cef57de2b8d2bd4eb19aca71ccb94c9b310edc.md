## Merged Review

### Summary
Ratings range from 5 to 8, with one reviewer (Rating 8) strongly positive about originality, quality, and significance, while the other three (Ratings 5–6) have reservations about task complexity, method abstraction, comparisons, and scalability. The paper introduces BaB-ND, a GPU-accelerated branch-and-bound framework for long-horizon motion planning that uses bound propagation from neural network verification (α,β-CROWN) to handle non-linear neural dynamics. It is tested on contact-rich manipulation tasks (planar pushing, object sorting, rope routing) in simulation and the real world, and supports various neural architectures (MLPs, GNNs). Reviewers appreciate the novel integration of verification ideas into planning, the clear writing (with helpful appendix and 1D toy example), and the challenging experimental setup. However, concerns revolve around limited 2D task diversity, missing details (bounding step, GPU acceleration, closed-loop definitions), lack of baselines (MIP for optimality, MPPI/CEM for runtime, traditional motion planners, broader comparisons), absence of ablation studies, hyperparameter sensitivity, reliance on a well-designed simulator, and unclear novelty relative to verification literature. Several specific questions about planning horizon, action space, pruning guarantees, data collection, and real-time applicability are raised as missing elements.

### Strengths
- **Originality**: The adaptation of branch-and-bound with bound propagation from neural network verification (α,β-CROWN) for motion planning over neural dynamics is novel (R1, R2, R3). One reviewer (R1) highlights this as a possibly new direction combining verification methods with planning.
- **Clear exposition**: The paper is well-structured, with a helpful 1D toy example, effective diagrams, and an appendix that supports understanding (R1, R4). The step-by-step example clarifies the method (R1).
- **Extensive experiments**: The framework is evaluated on multiple challenging contact-rich tasks (planar pushing with obstacles, object sorting, rope routing) in both simulation and the real world, with comparisons against Mixed-Integer Programming (MIP) and sampling-based methods (R1, R3, R4). A video demonstrates the improvements (R1).
- **Architecture flexibility**: BaB-ND supports various neural dynamics models, from simple MLPs to graph neural networks (GNNs), and scales with model size (R1, R2, R3).
- **GPU acceleration**: The use of GPU acceleration is noted as a potential advantage for handling extended horizons and complex models (R1, R2).
- **Addresses important problem**: The work tackles long-horizon, contact-rich manipulation planning, a critical challenge in robotics (R1, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited task diversity and complexity**: Most experiments are 2D; even the 3D rope manipulation is simple (R2). Uncertainty remains for full 3D manipulation with the complete robot kinematic chain, constrained environments, deformable objects, and dynamic obstacles/goal regions (R1, R2, R4). The method assumes static initial poses, making adaptation to dynamic changes difficult (R4). One reviewer (R1) gives the example of book reordering across shelves.
- **Scalability and runtime concerns**:
  - GPU acceleration implementation is not detailed, and its benefits are not demonstrated (e.g., runtime comparison with/without GPU) (R3).
  - Bound propagation and branching may become bottlenecks; no runtime comparisons with MPPI or CEM are provided (R1, R3).
  - The effect of increasing planning time on baseline performance is not analyzed; baselines may not have reached optimality (R1).
  - Scaling to a full 3D action space with translations and rotations could dramatically increase computation (R4).
  - The paper would benefit from latency experiments and discussion of modifications for real-time application (R1, R4).
- **Insufficient method detail and abstraction**:
  - The method section, especially the bounding step, is too abstract; it is difficult to connect concepts like objective function, input \(u\), bounds, and sub-domains to practical robotic tasks (R3). The approach assumes familiarity with the CROWN method (R3).
  - Robot experiments lack clarity: definitions of open-loop and closed-loop are ambiguous (R4), making Figure 5 results hard to interpret. The y-axis of Figure 6 is unclear (R2).
  - Action space appears limited to 2D translations; the controller’s planning horizon (20 steps) and execution frequency are not reported (R4).
  - Closed-loop control feedback mechanism is not explained (R1).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**:
  - Comparisons with traditional motion planners using full state information would help clarify trade-offs (computational cost including training vs. performance) (R1).
  - No benchmark against MIP for solution optimality, nor against MPPI and CEM for runtime (R3).
  - Broader baselines from other methodologies are needed to validate extensibility (R2).
  - No ablation study isolating the contribution of the branching heuristic or modifications to bound propagation (R1, R3).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The choice of branching heuristic, bound propagation depth, and sampling rates likely affect performance, but no analysis of their impact is provided (R1, R2).
- **Data collection and model training**:
  - Action sampling strategy for generating training episodes is unclear; why not train an RL agent in the simulator? (R4).
  - Sim-to-real gap is not addressed (R4).
  - Each task requires a custom neural network, which may be time-consuming and resource-intensive; expert design is needed for optimal performance, limiting practicality (R2).
- **Novelty ambiguity**: The distinction from neural network verification methods (beyond the application to planning) is unclear from a machine learning perspective (R3, R4).
- **Missing limitations discussion**: The paper does not discuss limitations or potential future directions (R4).
- **Code unavailable**: The provided code URL is inaccessible (R2).
- **Specific unanswered questions (overlapping with weaknesses)**:
  - Can sufficient sub-domain pruning be guaranteed? When does the method perform poorly compared to non-sampling methods? (R1)
  - Why is there no sampling-based method for scalability comparison? (R1)
  - Data collection: how are task variations achieved? (R1)
  - Figure 6 inconsistency: rope routing reports open-loop cost but closed-loop success rate; why? (R1)
  - Could early-stopping in bound propagation be optimized adaptively? (R1)
  - What modifications are needed to extend to more complex 3D tasks or deformable objects? (R2)
  - How practical is the custom network design for new scenarios in real-world applications? (R2)
  - With a 20-step horizon, how many actions are executed and what is the planner’s operating frequency? (R4)
