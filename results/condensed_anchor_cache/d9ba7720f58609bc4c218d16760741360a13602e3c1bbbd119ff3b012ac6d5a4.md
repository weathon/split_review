## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents a single-image face relighting method that explicitly models the relationship between light source properties (position, intensity, color) and the face by integrating a light feature embedding with cross-attention and self-attention layers in a residual convolutional autoencoder. Training is done on a synthetic OLAT dataset rendered in Blender with seven maximally separated light colors. The method achieves state-of-the-art quantitative results on Multi-PIE and Real Human datasets. Most reviewers, however, found the approach very incremental to Pidaparthy et al. (2024), with limited novelty, insufficient ablations, and unconvincing qualitative results. One reviewer (rating 1) was highly critical of data quality and novelty; another (rating 5, low confidence) noted the method surpasses baselines but lacks depth.

### Strengths
- The general idea of explicitly modeling the light–face spatial relationship is considered essential for physically based relighting with accurate shadows and speculars.
- The lighting embedding jointly encodes position, intensity, and color, enabling the method to handle different light colors—a capability absent in many methods trained without real light stage data or environment maps.
- The method achieves state-of-the-art quantitative performance on Multi-PIE and Real Human against several baselines.
- Writing and presentation are clear and easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of ablation studies** – The paper contains no ablation experiments (e.g., removing cross-attention between image and lighting features, varying the lighting embedding design). This makes it impossible to gauge the significance of claimed contributions.
- **Limited novelty and incremental contribution** – The overall pipeline (learned lighting encoding + residual conv autoencoder) is essentially unchanged from Pidaparthy et al. (2024). The use of a 7D vector with positional encoding is a standard technique (common in NeRFs and transformers); the architecture is a simple encoder–decoder. One reviewer (3) rated novelty as very low (contribution score 1) and considered the method incremental; another (4, confidence 2) found no new insights.
- **Missing baseline comparisons** – No comparison against DiFaReli, one of the most recent in-the-wild face relighting methods. The paper claims higher inference time as a reason for exclusion, but no runtime benchmarks are provided to support this.
- **Qualitative results are not convincingly better** – Relit images lack natural specular highlights and exhibit irregular shadows; visual quality in Figure 5 is comparable among all methods (including baselines). The model struggles on real-world images with non-black backgrounds, likely due to synthetic training data bias.
- **No video results** – No sequences showing a moving light source from a fixed viewpoint are presented, which is standard for relighting papers to demonstrate stability and spatially varying effects.
- **Justification for avoiding spherical harmonics (SH) is insufficient** – The paper claims SH cannot model light color or spatially varying effects, but SH can be extended by concatenating color coefficients, and the method itself does not demonstrate spatially varying relighting. One reviewer (3) notes that shadow/visibility depends only on lighting position, not color.
- **Data quality concerns** – The synthetic dataset uses 3D models without physically based rendering (PBR) features (specular modeling, subsurface scattering). Lighting colors are discretely sampled (seven maximally separated colors) rather than continuously, which may cause interpolation problems. The reference data source paper is below ICLR quality bar.
- **Synthetic-to-real adaptation insufficiently validated** – Only limited qualitative examples on real images are shown; robustness to diverse in-the-wild lighting conditions and backgrounds is not demonstrated.
- **Unsupported edge device claims** – The paper mentions lower inference time for edge devices but provides no runtime, model size, or power consumption comparisons.
- **Factual errors and missing details** – Hou et al. (2021, 2022) datasets are incorrectly referenced (2021 used DPR/Yale, 2022 used CelebA-HQ; errors appear in Table 1 and Introduction). The Real Human dataset lacks description of its lighting range, poses, expressions, and augmentations. Typo at L147.
- **Important citations missing** – COMPOSE (ECCV 2024), SwitchLight (CVPR 2024), NeRFFaceLighting (SIGGRAPH 2023) are omitted.
- **Design justification missing** – Cross-attention uses key/value from lighting features and query from image features, contrary to the intuitive reversed assignment; no rationale is provided.
- **Segmentation mask input inconsistency** – Section 4 does not mention the segmentation mask described in Section 6; unclear if it is used.
- **Disagreement among reviewers** – One reviewer (rating 1, high confidence) strongly criticizes novelty and data quality, while another (rating 5, low confidence) acknowledges the method’s performance but also finds it lacking in novelty and evaluation depth.
