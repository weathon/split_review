## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Highlight Diffusion, a training-free acceleration method for text-to-image diffusion models. It uses cross-attention maps to identify salient regions in the image that are most relevant to the text prompt, and selectively computes these "highlighted" regions at high resolution during the denoising process while roughly computing less important areas. A caching mechanism reduces redundant computations. On MS-COCO with SD1.4 and an NVIDIA RTX 3090, Highlight Diffusion achieves a 1.52× speedup with minimal quality loss (FID +0.65, CLIP −0.02). While the core idea is interesting, reviewers raise significant concerns about limited experimental validation, narrow applicability, insufficient comparisons, writing quality, and modest speedup.

### Strengths

- The core idea of leveraging cross-attention maps to identify salient regions and focus computation on those areas is interesting and provides a training-free approach to accelerate diffusion model inference.
- The paper includes a caching mechanism to avoid redundant computations.
- The method was validated on the MS-COCO dataset, showing inference speedup without significant performance degradation.
- The appendix explores feature redundancy in detail.
- The refinement of the cross-attention mask is a useful contribution for further research on T2I diffusion models.
- The method is presented clearly, and the supplementary code verifies implementation details, enhancing reproducibility.
- The paper addresses an important issue (diffusion model acceleration) and proposes an automatic selective mechanism via cross-attention maps.
- One reviewer noted the paper is well-written and easy to follow (minority positive view on presentation).

### Weaknesses

- **Writing and organization are major issues.** Most reviewers found the writing unclear, convoluted, and poorly organized. Section 3.1 was seen as unnecessary background. Too much space is devoted to background knowledge at the expense of experiments. There are empty spaces in the main paper, giving an impression of poor space utilization, and some important content (e.g., on feature redundancy) is relegated to the appendix. Algorithm 1 contains a typo: unclear what $x_{t-n}$ refers to. (One reviewer disagreed, finding the paper well-written, but this is a minority opinion.)
- **Insufficient experimental validation.** Only SD1.4 is tested, which is considered outdated (SD1.5 is more commonly used; newer models like SDXL, SD3, Pixart-α, FLUX are not evaluated). The only competing method compared is FRDiff; many other acceleration works are missing: DeepCache (CVPR 2024), SpeedUpNet (ECCV 2024), Diff-Pruning (NeurIPS 2023), SnapFusion (Arxiv 2023), Spectral Diffusion (NeurIPS 2023) should be included. The hyperparameter $N$ (number of timesteps for full resolution) is set empirically based on Table 1 but not explored in depth (e.g., different schedules like [1,3,5,7,9] or [9,7,5,3,1]).
- **Limited applicability and generalization.** The method selects the most salient region from cross-attention maps, making it object-centric. This may fail in multi-object scenarios, images focused on background/style, or when the object is very large (covering most of the image), in which case the method cannot reduce computation effectively and resembles DeepCache. A failure case is shown with the “cat” example (line 798). The method's generalizability to other architectures (SDXL, SD3, FLUX) and adapters (ControlNet, IP-Adapter) is not demonstrated.
- **The reported speedup of 1.52× is modest.** This does not seem very convincing compared to other acceleration techniques.
- **Limited technical contribution.** The primary idea of selective computation based on attention maps may collapse when the prompt object is large; the approach then appears similar to DeepCache. The distinction from DeepCache is not clearly explained. The paper also lacks a detailed explanation of exactly how the speedup is achieved (e.g., which tokens are skipped from previous steps) and what trade-offs or limitations exist.
- **Handling text-image misalignment is not addressed.** The paper does not discuss how the method performs when the prompt and image are misaligned, or what strategies ensure consistent performance in such cases.
- **Narrow application scope.** The method seems to work only for certain cases (e.g., object-centric prompts), limiting its practical value. The example on the “cat” shows it can fail.
- **Missing experiments on complex prompts.** The method should be tested on benchmarks like DPG-Bench that provide a more comprehensive evaluation of text-to-image capabilities, rather than only MS-COCO.
