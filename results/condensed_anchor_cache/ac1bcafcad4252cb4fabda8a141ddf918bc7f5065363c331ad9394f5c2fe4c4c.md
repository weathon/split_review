## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes DIRECT, an active learning method for classification under class imbalance and label noise. DIRECT uses a one-vs-rest reduction to multiclass, estimates class separation thresholds via the VReduce algorithm, and queries samples near those thresholds. The paper claims 60-80% annotation budget savings over baselines including GALAXY. Reviewers overall found the problem important but expressed serious concerns. Three reviewers (scores 3,3,3, confidence 4) identified fundamental theoretical flaws, limited technical contribution, poor presentation, and insufficient handling of label noise. One reviewer (score 6, confidence 3) was more positive, praising the innovative combination of class separation thresholds with 1D active learning and the extensive experimental results, though this reviewer also noted presentation issues and suggested additional experiments.

### Strengths
1. Addresses an important and practical problem: active learning under both class imbalance and label noise (R1, R2, R4).
2. Improves upon the existing method GALAXY by enabling parallel annotations and robustness against label noise (R1); specifically, R1 notes that DIRECT “could enable parallel annotations and be robust against label noises.”
3. The idea of identifying and querying near the decision boundary, especially for minority classes, is technically sound and shows promise for improving performance in imbalanced data (R2).
4. The approach of combining class separation thresholds with one-dimensional active learning is considered innovative and provides a fresh perspective (R3).
5. Extensive experiments on imbalanced datasets provide a strong foundation; results indicating 60–80% reduction in annotation budgets are compelling and demonstrate effectiveness compared to recent active learning methods (R2, R3).
6. The paper is well-organized with clear explanations of methodology and results, making it accessible (R3).
7. The related work section provides a thorough summary of classical active learning methods, informative for readers less familiar with the field (R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Presentation and readability are poor** (R1, R2, R3, R4):
   - The paper does not utilize the main page limit well; figure caption of Figure 3 overlaps with main text (R1).
   - The algorithm is not clearly presented, with too much text and no equation numbers (R1).
   - Typos at lines L269 and L278 (R2), and a formatting error at the bottom of page 5 (R3).
   - Overall presentation score is 2/4 from all reviewers.
2. **Results are poorly presented and unreliable** (R1, R3):
   - It is unclear where active learning starts; different starting points of curves are confusing (R1).
   - In some cases it is difficult to see the difference between variants of DIRECT and GALAXY (R1).
   - More visualizations are needed to effectively demonstrate experimental results and clarify method performance (R3).
3. **Limited technical contribution; method heavily leverages previously established algorithms** (R2, R4):
   - The theoretical contributions are modest (R2); the approach largely employs existing methods (VReduce, threshold learners) without substantial new theory (R4).
   - R4 asserts that the “sample around the optimized threshold” strategy is “merely an intuition”; there is no proof that sampling around that threshold is optimal for active learning.
4. **Fundamental theoretical flaws** (R4):
   - The “optimal threshold” is based on true labels, which are inherently inaccessible during active learning; accurate computation of this threshold is infeasible in practice (R4).
   - The hypothesis class is defined solely as a threshold classifier over the output of a deep learner. Without analyzing the behavior of this deep learning output and the underlying data distribution, any claims about a hypothesis learned by empirical risk minimization (ERM) within this class are meaningless (R4).
   - The proof in the appendix only establishes equivalence between the ERM solution on the training data and an “optimal threshold” on that same training set. It does not show that this threshold generalizes to unlabeled data or is optimal for active learning (R4).
   - Sampling near the threshold distorts the training distribution, which undermines learning guarantees; from a statistical learning perspective, if the majority class has a more complex distribution, naturally more data would be required (R4).
5. **Handling of label noise is unclear or absent** (R2, R4):
   - The algorithm itself does not explicitly manage or mitigate label noise. The paper claims to address label noise, but lacks a formal definition of label noise and no specific strategies are proposed to mitigate noisy labels (R4).
   - R2 notes confusion: Section 5.2 experiments with different levels of label noise, but the method description does not explain how noise is handled; R2 suggests noise may relate to agnostic learning but the connection is unclear.
6. **Threshold estimation for minority classes is challenging and not addressed** (R2):
   - Estimating the prediction threshold is particularly difficult for minority classes due to limited sample sizes; this issue is not thoroughly discussed in the paper (R2).
7. **Missing experimental details and incomplete comparisons** (R1, R2, R3):
   - **Starting point of active learning**: It is unclear what the initial labeled set is and how to explain the initial differences in learning curves (R1).
   - **Dataset construction**: The rationale for grouping certain classes into a larger class to create imbalanced datasets is unclear, resulting in an inconsistent number of classes compared to the original dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10/100). A simpler approach (e.g., using CIFAR-10-LT or selective sampling) would have been more straightforward (R2).
   - **Inconsistency in comparison methods**: The methods used for comparison appear inconsistent across different datasets; the criteria for selecting comparison methods and any reasons for discrepancies should be clarified (R2).
   - **Budget inclusion of B_parallel**: When plotting learning curves, it is unclear whether the budget spent on B_parallel (for threshold estimation) is included in the overall labeling budget; this affects interpretation of the curves (R2).
   - **Label noise levels**: Experiments only include noise at 0%, 10%, 15%, and 20%. Including 5% or exploring higher noise levels would strengthen the study’s comprehensiveness and make the noise experiments more comparable (R3).
8. **Needs more detailed explanation of improvements over GALAXY and robustness sources** (R3):
   - The authors identified three limitations of GALAXY and addressed them by optimizing the separation threshold. A more detailed explanation of this approach would help readers understand the proposed improvements (R3).
   - The authors should clarify the sources of robustness in each scenario (class imbalance vs. label noise) to highlight the model’s adaptability (R3).
9. **Reviewer disagreement**: While three reviewers (R1, R2, R4) rated the paper 3 (weak reject) with substantial skepticism, one reviewer (R3) rated it 6 (weak accept) and considered the approach innovative and the experiments compelling. R3’s confidence was 3 (somewhat confident), lower than the other reviewers’ confidence of 4. The fundamental theoretical flaws raised by R4 and the limited contribution noted by R2 and R1 are not addressed in R3’s review, creating a clear split.
