## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Uni-IR, a unified inverse rendering framework that jointly estimates geometry, materials, and lighting by integrating physically-based volume rendering (PBVR) and physically-based rendering (PBR). Unlike two-stage methods that optimize components separately, Uni-IR imposes mutual constraints through a shared material and lighting representation. The framework is evaluated on synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating improvements over baseline methods like NeRO.

### Strengths

1. **Unified joint optimization**: The approach of simultaneously estimating geometry, materials, and lighting through a single framework reduces ambiguity between components and enables global optimization. Integrating PBVR with PBR within one pipeline is a reasonable design that can derive a better optimal solution.

2. **Seamless integration of rendering models**: The paper tackles the rarely-addressed problem of combining PBVR (as in NeRF-based methods such as TensoIR, NeRO) and deterministic PBR (as in Nvdiffrec-like methods). Directly applying PBR on stochastic geometry often causes artifacts (see Fig. 11, NeRO with PBR); joint optimization of both losses provides valuable insights.

3. **Performance and extensive evaluation**: Extensive quantitative and qualitative experiments on multiple datasets show Uni-IR outperforms its baselines by a significant margin. The paper provides rich details with thorough formulations and implementation descriptions.

4. **Quality and clarity**: The paper is commendably detailed, including both quantitative and qualitative results. The presentation is fair.

### Weaknesses

1. **Unclear novelty and missing related work**: The technical contribution is unclear because much of the writing reiterates details already introduced by NeRO and other prior works. Several closely related methods are not cited, notably TensoIR (CVPR 2023) and TensoSDF (SIGGRAPH 2024). TensoSDF already combines volume and surface rendering based on NeRO with roughness-aware IDE, achieving superior quality over NeRO. The proposed method appears very similar to TensoSDF in high-level idea and many low-level design choices. Without direct comparison to TensoSDF, it is hard to determine whether material decomposition in volume rendering is essential, especially given TensoSDF’s success.

2. **Reproducibility concerns**: No code or reproducibility statement is provided, making it difficult for others to replicate the results. The method is complex with many integrated components.

3. **Writing and presentation issues**: The paper contains multiple typos and grammar errors (e.g., “Eq. equation” throughout; line 261: “where i indicate” → “indicates”; line 913: “??” in the Appendix). Table 1 caption does not explain which metric is used. The real images in Figure 8 look unnatural/synthetic; more natural real images with background are suggested. The metric used in Table 1 is not clearly explained.

4. **Incomplete comparisons and missing results**:
   - Qualitative results focus primarily on lighting reconstruction (Fig. 4, Fig. 5), while material decomposition is only shown in the ablation study (Fig. 6) without comparison to baseline methods.
   - Geometry comparisons are limited to NeRO (Fig. 7); other baselines are missing. In Figure 7, Uni-IR bakes shadow into the albedo – it is unclear whether this is a failure case or common; a visualization of the visibility term or more albedo results would clarify.
   - No video results with changing viewpoints and lighting are provided, which are critical for evaluating inverse rendering methods.

5. **Ambiguities in method details**:
   - The lighting estimation experiment (lines 364-372) uses known materials rather than estimating all components simultaneously. The reviewers question whether all scene parameters (geometry, materials, lighting) are estimated jointly in each evaluation or if some are assumed given; this affects interpretation of results.
   - The term “unified” in the claims is ambiguous: does it refer to unified optimization vs. two-stage, or unified rendering (PBVR + PBR) vs. separate renderings? Does “seamless integration” mean that during training the PBR and PBVR colors converge to the same value?
   - It is unclear how the surface points \(x_s\) are identified for evaluating the rendering equation (line 255), since the surface is not known a priori.
   - Table 5 terms “NeRO (PBVR)” and “NeRO (PBR)” are confusing – NeRO originally used PBVR only, so it is unclear what these variants represent.
   - The paper mentions extracting mesh via NeuS, but does not report on whether the final mesh is textured or its quality.

6. **Minority stronger criticism (Reviewer 4, score 3)**: Reviewer 4 evaluates the novelty as low (contribution score 2), arguing that the method is essentially NeRO combined with surface rendering, very similar to TensoSDF, and that material decomposition in volume rendering may be unnecessary. The lack of video results and unclear positioning reinforce this lower assessment.
