## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates diffusion modeling for anomaly detection (AD) and proposes Diffusion Time Estimation (DTE), which avoids full reverse diffusion by modeling the posterior distribution over diffusion timesteps for a noisy input. Anomaly scores are derived from the mode or mean of this distribution. Three DTE variants are introduced: a non-parametric kernel density estimate and two parametric DNN-based models (regressive and categorical). Experiments on 57 ADBench datasets (tabular) and additional image datasets (VisA, CIFAR-10, MNIST) show that DTE achieves competitive detection performance with orders-of-magnitude faster inference compared to DDPM, though it often underperforms simple kNN. The work is well-motivated and easy to follow, but reviewers disagree on the novelty relative to existing diffusion-based AD methods and on the generalizability of results.

### Strengths
- Novel perspective: Using diffusion-time distribution estimation for anomaly detection avoids the computational overhead of the reverse diffusion process; anomalies are assumed farther from the data manifold, leading to higher density at larger timesteps.
- The DTE framework provides both non-parametric (KDE) and parametric (regressive/categorical) models, allowing a trade-off between accuracy and inference efficiency. The parametric DNN model substantially reduces inference time relative to DDPM.
- Comprehensive evaluation on 57 datasets from ADBench (tabular) plus additional image datasets (VisA, CIFAR-10, MNIST) demonstrates that DTE generally outperforms DDPM and shows comparable results to a large set of 19 baseline/SOTA methods in both semi-supervised and unsupervised settings.
- The presentation is well-organized and easy to follow; the analysis of the inverse Gamma posterior and the derivation of the analytical density are contributions.

### Weaknesses
- Performance of DTE is often upper bounded by a simple kNN-based AD method (using raw features). The authors should quantitatively demonstrate where DTE offers distinct scalability advantages over kNN and should compare with advanced kNN variants (e.g., deep kNN methods that learn optimized embedding spaces for kNN-based AD). [Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2]
- The method’s reliance on distances in the original feature space raises concerns for very high-dimensional data (e.g., hundreds of thousands of features). While pre-trained embeddings are mentioned as a possible workaround, the core dependency on original feature distances is not explicitly addressed or tested on such high-dimensional scenarios. [Reviewer 2]
- In the unsupervised setting, DTE is less competitive than in the semi-supervised one. This suggests a potential overfitting to normal data when labeled normal samples are available, making the method sensitive to the training data composition. The effect of varying the ratio of labeled data in the semi-supervised setting needs investigation. [Reviewer 3]
- The contribution relative to existing diffusion-model-based AD works (e.g., [ICCV23] for surface anomaly detection, [ICCV23] for video AD, DiffusionAD, medical anomaly detection) is unclear. The paper does not clearly articulate why the reverse-diffusion perspective (or DTE) is more suitable for AD compared to other generative models (e.g., GANs) or non-generative methods. [Reviewer 4]
- The experimental comparison lacks recent diffusion-based AD methods and the latest 2023 AD baselines. While the evaluation on ADBench is comprehensive, the proposed DTE does not consistently outperform all other methods on each dataset—it achieves best performance only on a subset—raising questions about its generalizability and practical applicability across diverse datasets. [Reviewer 4]
- The claim of evaluation on “tabular, image, and natural language datasets” is misleading because the experiments only use tabular data and embeddings from pre-trained models; raw image/NLP data are not directly used. The current evaluation scope does not fully represent the diversity implied. [Reviewer 2]
- The visualization in Figure 3 could be improved; showing residuals between the non-parametric estimate and the analytical posterior would make the small differences more apparent. [Reviewer 1]
- Missing discussion on how the method could be extended to anomaly localization (a more challenging task). [Reviewer 3]
