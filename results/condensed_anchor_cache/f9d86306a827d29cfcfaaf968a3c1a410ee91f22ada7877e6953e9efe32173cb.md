## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a low-rank plus diagonal (LR+D) covariance matrix to jointly model aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty in high-dimensional regression tasks (image inpainting, colorization, optical flow). The method reduces memory and computational cost compared to full covariance, and is evaluated on MNIST, CelebA, and Flying Chairs using U-Net with Monte Carlo Dropout, SVI, and Deep Ensembles. Results show improved negative log-likelihood (NLL) and out-of-distribution (OOD) detection. Two reviewers rated 3 (low confidence in contributions/evaluation), two rated 5 (overall positive). This divergence centers on whether the separate aleatoric/epistemic components are properly evaluated and whether the novelty is well-articulated.

### Strengths

- Novel application of the low-rank plus diagonal decomposition to separate aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty estimates, going beyond previous uses for aleatoric-only (Zepf et al. 2023 applied jointly) (R1, R4).
- Reduces memory and computational requirements compared to full covariance matrices while preserving essential output correlations (R3, R4).
- Well derived; shows performance improvements over simple Bayesian baselines on NLL and pixelwise error (R1, R2).
- Better separation of in-distribution and OOD data as measured by differential entropy (R2) and OOD detection on MNIST inpainting (leave-one-class-out) (R4).
- Good selection of datasets representing high-dimensional regression outputs (inpainting, colorization, optical flow) (R4).
- Baselines cover Monte Carlo Dropout, variational inference (SVI), and deep ensembles (R4).
- Method scales with number of model samples and SVD components (shown in ablation) (R1).

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of separate evaluation of aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties**: The paper explicitly models both types (Equation 1, 2) but evaluates only the joint uncertainty (e.g., NLL, OOD detection). This makes it unclear what each component contributes and whether the disentanglement is meaningful. Suggested experiments: vary training set size to test epistemic uncertainty, add label noise for aleatoric uncertainty, and plot diagonal of each covariance matrix qualitatively (R4, R1). Reviewer 2 questions how disambiguation is possible if Equation 2 is sum-decomposable. Reviewer 1 references known failure cases (Wimmer et al. 2023, Mucsanyi et al. 2024). The OOD detection results are a missed opportunity to evaluate epistemic uncertainty separately (R4).
- **Evaluation issues**:
    - The NLL interpretation is incomplete: stating “NLL measures how well a model predicts the observed data” is not fully correct because NLL also accounts for predictive uncertainty (R4).
    - Comparisons between methods with and without epistemic uncertainty (e.g., diagonal vs. ensemble) conflate epistemic and aleatoric components, so the metric does not isolate what is being evaluated (R4).
    - Training loss is never defined in the main text (only in Appendix D.3, which is not referenced) (R1).
    - “Ablation studies” (Sec 3.5) are actually a hyperparameter sweep; “Hyperparameter” section label is inappropriate (R1). Qualitative results lack samples from competing methods (R1).
- **Insufficient novelty framing and related work**: Low-rank plus diagonal has been used previously for aleatoric uncertainty, and Zepf et al. (2023) applied it to both aleatoric and epistemic jointly. The paper should clarify the specific methodological changes that lead to improved performance (R1). Reviewers ask how Equation 1 relates to existing decompositions (Pfau 2013, Depeweg et al. 2018, Abe et al. 2022, Gupta et al. 2022) (R1, R2).
- **Numerical stability and scaling**: The method may suffer from numerical stability issues, especially with a high number of retained low-rank components (R3). Reviewer 2 asks about condition numbers of predicted covariance matrices and what numerical issues occur exactly. Scaling to very large output spaces or complex tasks may require further adjustments (R3).
- **Necessity of SVD**: Reviewer 2 questions why SVD is used to truncate the rank when the rank hyperparameter R^W could be set low directly, saving the SVD cost.
- **Limited experimental scope**: Experiments use only U-Net on relatively small datasets (MNIST, CelebA, Flying Chairs) (R3). Only Monte Carlo Dropout, SVI, and Deep Ensembles are evaluated; other recent methods (e.g., Mukhoti et al. 2023 DDU, Daxberger et al. 2021 Laplace Redux) could provide deeper insights (R3). Testing on larger datasets and transformer architectures is suggested (R3).
- **Missing sensitivity analysis**: No study of how different ranks and sample sizes affect performance and computational requirements (R3 Q1). The paper would benefit from computational complexity analysis or empirical runtime comparisons on larger datasets (R3 Q2).
- **Missing reference**: The paper should cite “A deeper look into aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty disentanglement” (CVPR 2022 workshops) (R4).
