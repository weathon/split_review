## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces GAOKAO-Eval, a closed-book benchmark based on China’s 2024 Gaokao exams, to evaluate LLMs released before the exam, aiming to avoid data leakage. Using the Rasch model from cognitive psychology, the authors identify two mismatches between LLM scores and human-aligned capabilities: anomalous consistent performance across difficulty levels, and high variance on similarly difficult questions. They also report inconsistent human grading of LLM outputs and note that o1’s reasoning tokens partially mitigate the mismatch. Reviewers appreciate the dataset’s timeliness and the evaluation effort, but raise concerns about clarity, novelty, experimental insights, and justification of key methodological choices.

### Strengths
- Addresses data leakage by using an annually updated exam administered after model release; the closed-book setup is a useful workaround (Reviewer 1, 2, 4).
- Evaluates several popular LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, WQX, InternLM, Qwen, Mixtral) across multiple subjects and difficulty levels (Reviewer 1, 4).
- Identifies a performance mismatch between LLMs and humans, which is a timely and relevant finding (Reviewer 1, 2, 4).
- Introduces the Rasch model from cognitive psychology to analyze scoring patterns—a novel perspective for LLM evaluation (Reviewer 2, 4).
- Discovers “semi difficulty-invariant scoring” and high variance on similar-difficulty questions, along with inconsistent teacher grading of LLM answers; these insights could guide future alignment research (Reviewer 2, 4).
- Examines the relationship between o1’s reasoning tokens and performance consistency, providing a promising direction (Reviewer 4).
- Non-trivial evaluation effort: thorough comparison across models, construction of a specialized WQX model, and inclusion of human grading (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear human evaluation process**: How were the 54 graders selected? What are the grading guidelines, task distribution, and validation steps? Inter-rater agreement is missing (Reviewer 1, 3). Ethical considerations for human involvement are not disclosed (Reviewer 2).
- **Rasch model justification**: Why is the Rasch model the best fit for modeling LLM behavior? No alternative psychometric models are explored or discussed. The paper assumes Gaokao performance can be simulated by the Rasch model without evidence that actual human performance distributions might look similar to the LLM’s (Reviewer 1, 2). The hybrid approach to difficulty (human annotations + LLM scores) in line 274 is unclear (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited novelty and insight depth**: Variants like GAOKAO-Bench and GAOKAO-MM already exist; the authors claim non-leakage but those datasets also avoid leakage. If the dataset is not the key novelty, the insights derived should be deeper. Currently, results are aggregated and the paper repeats the general message that LLM performance does not align with human abilities, which is well-studied. Key takeaways for the community are lacking (Reviewer 3). The claim that “difficulty ratings are well-aligned with human perception” (line 357) is unsupported (Reviewer 3).
- **Poor interpretability of figures and experiments**: Figures 4, 5, 6, 7a, 8, and 11 lack clear explanations, captions, and takeaways. For example, Fig 4 shows overall performance by subject but does not clarify what insight to draw. Fig 5’s incorporation of human ratings and ELO is unexplained. Fig 7a’s grey area and “across models” phrasing are ambiguous. Fig 6 fits IRT across all models instead of per-model. Fig 8’s purpose is unclear. Fig 11’s “incorporating O1 tokens” lacks detail given o1’s opaque reasoning (Reviewer 3). Eq 3’s application is not shown (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons**: Why not compare with stronger multimodal open-source models like LLavaOneVision or LLavaNext? (Reviewer 3). For the o1 reasoning discussion, the internal process is opaque; no ablation with CoT or other reasoning techniques on white-box models is performed to test whether CoT could reduce inconsistency (Reviewer 4).
- **WQX model necessity**: The specially trained WQX model is only marginally better than GPT-4o on Science Total and Art Total. What if GPT-4o were fine-tuned on the same corpus? The key message from including WQX is unclear (Reviewer 4). How does the paper conclude WQX improves over InternLM based on accuracy if high scores are not reliable indicators? (Reviewer 4 question).
- **Motivation questioned**: Aligning LLM performance to human capability distributions on reasoning tasks may not be a good idea, unlike safety/toxicity alignment. The paper assumes “human-like reasoning” is desirable but does not define it or argue why it matters if the LLM already achieves “human-like performance” (Reviewer 1, 4).
- **Exaggerated scope**: The benchmark primarily assesses knowledge-based QA within a constrained exam format, not comprehensive LLM capabilities as claimed in Section 2 (Reviewer 2).
- **Grammar and typos**: Examples: “consistant”, “difficultiess” (line 23); “we” (line 25) not capitalized; “cabilities” (line 459); “discoverys” (line 527); “phenomenon” should be plural (Reviewer 1). Minor: line 23 errors noted by Reviewer 4.
- **Can the observed phenomena be verified on non-Gaokao tests?** The analysis is confined to one exam format (Reviewer 2).
