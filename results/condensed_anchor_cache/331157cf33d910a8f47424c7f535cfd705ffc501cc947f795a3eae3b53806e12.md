## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a Quantization-Aware Conversion (QAC) algorithm for ANN-to-SNN conversion. It establishes a theoretical equivalence between the quantization bit-width of ANN activations and the timesteps in SNNs with soft reset, then uses mixed-precision quantization (inspired by ANN mixed-precision QAT) to convert an ANN into a mixed-timestep SNN where different layers can have different timesteps. A calibration step for initial membrane potential and firing threshold under frozen pretrained weights is also introduced. Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet show improved accuracy and reduced average timesteps compared to prior conversion methods.

### Strengths
- Novel insight linking ANN activation quantization bit-width to SNN timesteps under soft reset, providing theoretical support for mixed-timestep SNNs.
- Mixed-precision quantization-based conversion algorithm that yields SNNs with few timesteps, reducing inference cost while maintaining accuracy.
- Technically solid analysis: detailed equivalence derivation (theorem), four cases of gradient estimation during QAT (Eqs. (7)–(10), Fig. 2), and calibration of initial membrane potential and firing threshold under frozen pretrained weights (R4 finds this interesting).
- Experimental validation on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet, outperforming previous conversion methods in both accuracy and timestep efficiency.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty**: The method primarily combines existing ANN mixed-precision quantization with ANN-SNN conversion techniques. Similar insights (equivalence of quantization levels and timesteps) have been discussed in prior works [2][3]. The discussion of gradient cases closely aligns with referenced work [1]. (Reviewer 1)
2. **Unclear motivation**: If quantized ANNs are equivalent to SNNs with soft reset, why not simply use the quantized ANNs? The paper does not adequately highlight the advantages of using the converted SNN over the quantized ANN itself. (Reviewer 3)
3. **Data flow in mixed-timestep structure not explained**: The paper does not specify how the data flows between layers with different timesteps, how modules with different timesteps are connected, whether hardware implementation is feasible, and whether asynchronous SNN behavior is maintained. In particular, assuming vanilla IF neurons emitting binary spikes: if layer \(l\) uses \(T_l\) timesteps and layer \(l+1\) uses \(T_{l+1}\) timesteps, how do IF neurons in layer \(l+1\) receive the spike sequence \([\mathbf{s}^l(1),...,\mathbf{s}^l(T_l)]\) from the previous layer? (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4)
4. **Experiments limited to static datasets**: Only static image classification datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet) are tested. The omission of dynamic/event-based datasets may be related to the unresolved asynchronous behavior issue. (Reviewer 1)
5. **Additional training cost from calibration step**: QAC involves two phases: training a quantized ANN (via QAT) and then calibrating membrane potentials/thresholds. This effectively doubles training cost. An ablation study showing the impact of calibration before and after application is needed to evaluate its necessity and to enable fair comparison with other methods. (Reviewer 2)
6. **Not a direct conversion method**: QAC trains a quantized ANN first (using QAT) rather than converting an arbitrary pre-trained ANN. This makes it more of a specialized training method than a pure conversion technique. Comparative experiments against direct SNN training methods should be conducted to clearly show the trade-offs and position QAC among SNN training methodologies. (Reviewer 2)
7. **Performance behind SOTA with more timesteps**: While QAC works well with few timesteps, its performance lags behind state-of-the-art methods that use more timesteps. (Reviewer 3)
8. **Presentation issues**: Some figures occupy excessive space with limited information; text in some figures is too small to read clearly. (Reviewer 1)
