## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes HyperLLaVA, which uses HyperNetworks to generate dynamic parameter shifts for the vision projector and LLM based on visual and language guidance, aiming to address static mapping issues in MLLMs like LLaVA. Two-stage training is employed. Experiments on MME, MMBench, SEED-Bench, LLaVA-Bench, and a new CMT benchmark show improvements. Reviewers note clear writing and extensive experiments, but raise major concerns about motivation, small performance gains, unfair comparisons, the CMT benchmark’s value, missing baselines (e.g., MoE methods), insufficient ablations, and unclear implementation details. Two reviewers gave 5, three gave 3.

### Strengths
- Clear, fluent writing that is easy to follow (R1, R2, R3).
- Extensive experiments and detailed ablations validating how each component works and that the overall method is effective (R1, R2, R4).
- Method is simple to implement (R4).
- Identifies two specific challenges in applying hypernetworks to MLLMs: weak correlation and unstable optimization (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation is ill-defined.** The claim of negative transfer in MLLMs is not convincingly demonstrated. The paper relies on Wang et al. 2019 (small-scale models) without theoretical analysis or recent literature showing that static parameters hurt large-scale MLLMs (R5). The core principle of MLLMs is to project modalities into a shared semantic space; why task-specific projections are better is not justified (R5). R5 believes the problem greatly contradicts the main principle of existing MLLMs.
- **Performance gains are very marginal and may not be significant.** In Table 4, improvements are almost all below 0.5% on SQA, MMB, etc., and are described as “ignoble” (R2). Table 5 shows limited gains (R1). From 7B to 13B, improvements mostly range 0.1–1.0%, suggesting poor scalability (R1). Such small gains could be achieved by simply training LLaVA-1.5 for additional steps (R5). More rigorous statistical analyses and a direct comparison with LLaVA-1.5 trained for extra steps are needed (R5).
- **Missing comparisons with relevant dynamic/MoE baselines.** The paper does not compare with MoE-LLaVA or LLaVA-MoLE, which are also dynamic methods for task transfer (R2). Recent works like CogVLM, Wings, and Mono InternVL are not compared (R3).
- **The CMT benchmark is of questionable value.** It simply combines existing open-source datasets (R1, R2). Its zero-shot claim is incorrect (line 300–301) because it uses supervised data (R2). Existing benchmarks (MMVet, MMBench) already offer more complex and diverse zero-shot evaluations (R2). Only a limited set of models is tested, and no insightful conclusions are drawn (R1).
- **Unfair comparison with LLaVA.** HyperLLaVA uses an additional 505K CMT training data not present in the baseline, making direct comparisons invalid (R4). It is unclear whether the LLM is fully fine-tuned or trained with LoRA (R4).
- **Insufficient ablation and analysis.** Tables 3 and 4 show little improvement on SQA, SEED, MMB; there is no ablation on combining visual and language experts (R1). The paper does not specify the number of additional parameters introduced (e.g., as a percentage of the original network) (R1, R3). Performance gains may partly be due to increased parameters rather than the method itself (R1). The “unstable” optimization mentioned in Table 5 is not defined — is it due to the simple projector structure or drastic parameter changes? (R3). If adding an expert to the projector laterally helps, why not use a multi-head projector? (R3). Additional experiments directly testing the benefit of dynamic tokens and qualitative visualizations are missing (R3, R5).
- **Implementation details are unclear or questionable.** Symbols like ξ, E^n(), H_M in Eqs (2),(3),(5) are not consistently defined (R4). “L1” in line 250 is unexplained (R4). Why does the last input token h^{L/2} at the L/2-th layer fully perceive the whole multimodal context? (R4). The formulation of V (line 246) and Eq. (6) appears to be an extra residual output, not dynamic; the network structure of ξ in Eq. (6) is not given (R4). The visual expert is similar to LoRA and only at the input layer, while many MLLM studies show connectors contribute minimally (R3). For the language expert, using half-layer language guidance as hypernetwork input is unusual; it may just add extra structure rather than generate effective parameters (R3). The choice of using half the layers is not justified, potentially creating a highly coupled network (R3). Whether any pruning of the hypernetwork occurs during inference is not stated (R3). The complexity and extra steps of dynamic token projection should be highlighted (R5).
- **Minor technical errors.** In Table 5, the bolded MME accuracy seems incorrect (R3). “LoRA” should be capitalized consistently (R3). The use of “L” in Equation 6 may cause misunderstanding (R3). Figures are difficult to understand (R2).
