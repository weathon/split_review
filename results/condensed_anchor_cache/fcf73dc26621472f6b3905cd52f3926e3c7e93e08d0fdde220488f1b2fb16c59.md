## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Pseudo-label Regularized Optimization (PRO), a method that combines multiple existing regularization techniques (marginal consistency, test entropy minimization, label accumulation) to prevent catastrophic accuracy collapse during test-time training (TTT) of CLIP models on unlabeled test data. Experiments on ViT-B-32 and ViT-L-14-336 across 18 datasets report an average improvement of 2.5% and a best-case improvement of 6.1% over zero-shot baselines.

### Strengths

*   **Well-written and easy to understand:** The paper clearly motivates the problem of catastrophic collapse in test-time adaptation and explains how PRO addresses it. The problem setup (Sec. 3) is clearly described with formulas and diagrams. [Reviewers 1, 2, 3]
*   **Practical significance:** Addressing hyperparameter sensitivity and collapse in TTT is an important and timely problem for deploying large pre-trained models in real-world settings where labeled data is scarce and test distributions shift. [Reviewers 1, 2]
*   **Thorough experimental investigation:** The paper conducts extensive experiments on 18 datasets to illustrate the failure modes of prior TTT methods and to ablate the contribution of each regularization component. [Reviewers 1, 2, 4]
*   **Minority (Reviewer 1, score 6) found originality and practical value in the heavily regularized pseudo-labeling approach**, while other reviewers (scores 3) considered the method primarily a combination of existing ideas (see Weaknesses). [Disagreement preserved]

### Weaknesses

*   **Limited novelty:** PRO is an empirical combination of several existing techniques (marginal consistency, test entropy minimization, label accumulation) with no new theoretical insight. Reviewers 2, 3, and 4 specifically noted the absence of a detailed description of PRO beyond stating it is a cherry-picked combination from ablation experiments. Reviewers 2 and 4 requested clearer specification of how the loss functions are combined. [Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4]
*   **Missing statistical significance:** Results are reported without means and standard deviations (e.g., over multiple runs), making it unclear whether improvements are statistically significant. [Reviewer 1]
*   **Incomplete comparisons:** PRO is not benchmarked against other test-time adaptation methods that use semi-supervised learning strategies (e.g., self-training [2]), and the "Text Tower" component lacks a clear explanation. [Reviewer 3; Reviewer 2 additionally requested explanation of “Text Tower”]
*   **Limited architectural generality:** Effectiveness appears architecture-specific; PRO yields noticeable gains on ViT models but only a marginal 0.1% improvement on ResNet architectures (see Appendix B). [Reviewer 3]
*   **Limited domain generality:** The method is only evaluated on CLIP-based image classification tasks; no experiments on text problems, image segmentation, or other modalities. Reviewers 1, 2, and 4 questioned whether PRO generalizes beyond CLIP and/or to settings where the pre-training data and test classes share significant overlap (Reviewer 1 questioned fit with low-data learning). [Reviewers 1, 2, 4]
*   **Limited empirical gain on standard datasets:** PRO-CLIP shows little performance gain on commonly used image datasets (Figure 3). [Reviewer 2]
*   **Lack of theoretical understanding:** The paper provides no theoretical explanation for why the combined regularizers prevent collapse (e.g., beyond stating that class imbalance can be mitigated). [Reviewer 4]
*   **Missing details in algorithm description:** Details of how the loss functions are combined (weighting, scheduling) are unclear, and the full experimental setup and results from Section 5 are omitted. [Reviewer 4]
*   **Figure 2 (sketch of PRO) is not intuitive and does not convey the method composition at a glance.** [Reviewer 2]
*   **Missing description of failure modes:** Other than class imbalance, what are the failure modes of PRO? [Reviewer 4]
*   **Reviewer 2 noted the paper reads as a relatively complete experimental report, better suited for a workshop.**
