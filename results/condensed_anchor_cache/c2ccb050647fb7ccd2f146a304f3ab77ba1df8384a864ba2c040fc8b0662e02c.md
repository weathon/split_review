## Merged Review

### Summary
Hi-Gaussian addresses single-view 3D reconstruction using feed‑forward 3D Gaussians, a Normalized Spherical Projection module to enlarge field‑of‑view, and a Hierarchical Gaussian Sampling strategy to handle occluded regions. Training is self‑supervised (no 3D ground truth) and the method outperforms baselines on Semantic KITTI and BundleFusion datasets. One reviewer (score 8, confidence 5) views the technical contributions as solid and the experiments as comprehensive, while three others (scores 5, confidence 4) consider the contributions incremental, raise concerns about heavy reliance on a pre‑trained depth model, unfair comparison with the self‑supervised SceneRF, and limited practical utility for occluded regions.

### Strengths
- The paper is well‑written, clearly presented, and tackles a problem of general interest (Reviewers 3,4).
- Quantitatively surpasses all compared methods on Semantic KITTI and BundleFusion, with clear rendering in unbounded street views (Reviewer 1).
- Technical contributions are solid: Hierarchical Gaussian Sampling effectively models occluded areas (a critical challenge), and the Normalized Spherical Projection captures outside‑FOV information, improving upon previous spherical projection techniques used in methods like SceneRF. The Gaussian here is from 3DGS, not a NeRF point‑sampling tool (Reviewer 3).
- Experiments, including main results and ablation studies on novel view synthesis, are comprehensive and demonstrate advantages (Reviewer 3).
- Figures 8 and 9 are particularly helpful for understanding the impact of the proposed components (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- Contributions are seen as incremental: using a pre‑trained monocular depth network, mapping pixel coordinates to spherical coordinates, and a leader‑follower Gaussian approach do not provide deep insights (Reviewer 1).
- Heavy dependence on a pre‑trained depth model (UniDepth). The comparison with the self‑supervised SceneRF (Table 1) is unfair; performance gains may come from the depth prior rather than the proposed architecture. The method cannot be applied when a pre‑trained depth estimator is unavailable or performs poorly. Reviewers request: (i) ablation showing robustness to bad depth predictions, (ii) zero‑shot generalization on datasets like Tanks and Temples and LLFF, and (iii) clarification whether compared methods were retrained with the same evaluation cameras (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- The Normalized Spherical Projection module is heavily influenced by SceneRF’s Spherical U‑Net. The novelty is limited unless a detailed analysis of differences and a dedicated ablation study are provided. Specifically, the claim that normalizing x,y reduces spatial compression needs intuition and an explanation (e.g., how it reduces black areas in the image; this should be illustrated by referencing Figure 8 in Section 3.2) (Reviewers 2,3,4).
- The practical significance of single‑view Gaussian Splatting is not convincing: occluded regions are unseen and not hallucinated by learned priors, yielding blurry or nonsensical content. Without a mechanism to infer plausible structures, the reconstruction is incomplete and of limited use (Reviewers 2,4).
- No evaluation of 3D reconstruction is provided despite the title claim. The paper should include 3D reconstruction metrics or novel depth synthesis (like SceneRF) or qualitative visualizations of 3D Gaussian distributions and depth maps (as in pixelSplat) (Reviewer 3).
- Hierarchical Gaussian Sampling: leader Gaussians are initialized from depth on visible surfaces, so it is unclear how follower Gaussians diverge to cover occluded regions when sampled from the leader’s distribution (Reviewer 3).
- Qualitative results are insufficient: only zoom‑in views are shown, indoor range is limited, and no videos demonstrate 3D consistency (Reviewer 1). Figure 7 would benefit from an additional column highlighting occluded and outside‑FOV areas in the novel view (Reviewer 3).
- The method should be explicitly described as self‑supervised (no 3D ground truth) to distinguish it from monocular methods that require 3D supervision. This is claimed to be a selling point but is not made clear (Reviewer 3).
- On line 116 the authors claim faster rendering than NeRF, but no speed comparison is provided (Reviewer 3).
- Missing details: how training and testing cameras are selected (Reviewer 2).
