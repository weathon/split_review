## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a unifying axiomatic framework that bridges graph and topological message-passing by viewing simplicial and cellular complexes through the lens of relational structures. This framework generalizes existing architectures (R-GCNs, simplicial neural nets, higher-order GNNs) and extends oversquashing theory from graphs to topological domains, introducing tools like the aggregated influence matrix and influence graph. Rewiring methods are adapted to the relational setting. Two reviewers (scoring 8) praise the strong theoretical contributions, clarity, and unifying perspective. Three reviewers (scoring 6) raise substantial concerns about limited novelty (arguing the paper largely re-derives results from Topping et al. (2022) with little innovation), weak empirical validation (restricted to TUD datasets without node/link prediction, ablations, or depth experiments), specific flaws in Definition 2.6, and insufficient complexity analysis.

### Strengths
- Well written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 2, 5).
- Relational structures provide a general unified framework that connects graph, relational, and topological GNN communities; includes simplicial and other higher-order structures (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Strong theoretical contribution: the aggregated influence matrix and influence graph successfully extend oversquashing results to topological domains, enabling analysis of local geometry, depth, and hidden dimension effects (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Propositions and theorems are mathematically sound (Reviewer 1).
- Addresses an important gap in topological deep learning, highlighted in a recent position paper (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- Introduces new rewiring heuristics for relational and topological GNNs (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty.** The paper reduces simplicial complexes to an influence graph and re-derives oversquashing results that closely mirror existing work (Topping et al., 2022). Definition 2.6 and Lemma 3.2 are considered straightforward extensions lacking significant innovation (Reviewers 3, 4, 5). Many formulas are not original and need proper references (Reviewer 5).
- **Weak empirical validation.** Experiments are limited to TUD datasets, which are outdated, small-sample, and problematic for evaluation. Reviewers strongly recommend including newer benchmarks (e.g., Zinc, OGB, atomistic sets like QM9, MD17, peptides) and additional tasks (node/link prediction) that are the primary targets for relational GNNs. The effects of rewiring on real-world data are inconclusive (Reviewers 2, 3, 5).
- **Missing complexity-accuracy trade-off.** The paper omits discussion of computational complexity of the rewiring techniques relative to number of relations, their arity, and elements in the structure (Reviewer 2). Questions about whether rewiring should prune edges (given redundancies from lifting) or modify higher-order edges remain unanswered; insight from the framework on how to formulate such algorithms is missing (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Insufficient ablations and depth analysis.** No experiments with varying numbers of message-passing layers, and no ablation studies on the impact of local complexity, hidden dimension size, or model depth (Reviewer 5).
- **Issues in Definition 2.6.** (a) The substitution of \(R_i\) with \(\mathcal{S}^{n_i}\) has minimal impact, effectively treating all possible \(n_i\)-tuples as related rather than focusing on specific relations defined by \(R_i\) (Reviewer 4). (b) The shift operator \(A^{R_i}\) only accounts for signal passed from the tuple to \(\sigma\), not for other permutations, limiting expression of the relation (Reviewer 4). (c) Terminology is unclear: variables such as \(p_t\) need proper introduction (Reviewer 2).
- **Reduction is combinatorial, not topological.** Analysis that leverages topological properties (e.g., topological curvature) would be more insightful for understanding oversquashing in topological domains (Reviewer 3).
- **Outdated literature review.** Coverage of relational GNNs focuses on work before 2020, lacking recent advances in heterogeneous graphs and dynamic relational structures. State-of-the-art comparison with methods that explicitly address oversquashing (e.g., spectral techniques, adaptive message passing) is missing (Reviewer 5).
- **Content imbalance.** Too much space is devoted to simplicial complexes and their influence on oversquashing, while the solution part (how the proposed framework directly addresses oversquashing) is less detailed (Reviewer 5).
- **Missing cyclic lifting.** Experiments only use clique lifting, but recent TDL baselines like CIN and CIN++ use cyclic lifting; including cyclic lifting across all experiments would increase relevance (Reviewer 3).
- **Unanswered theoretical questions.** (a) Is there a correspondence between the message-passing graph of Bodnar et al. and the aggregated influence matrix? (Reviewer 1). (b) Could tighter bounds, e.g., equation (8), be achieved by considering matrices \(B^{R_i}\) rather than a single \(B\)? Conversely, can all relations be combined into a single union relation? (Reviewer 3). (c) Can relations be treated as a directed set, with the direct limit serving as a terminal object for defining message functions? (Reviewer 4).
