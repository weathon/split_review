## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SIIHPC, an incomplete multi-view clustering (IMVC) method. It avoids explicit missing sample recovery by instead imputing similarities: it separates observed parts via bipartition learning transformation and relaxes non-negative constraints via sample regularization. Learnable consensus graphs provide a unified structure, and missing similarity parts are imputed based on the relationship between consensus graphs and exclusive similarities. Instead of a single prototype quantity for all views, SIIHPC assigns a group of hybrid prototype quantities per view, then performs spectral grouping on the resulting multi-scale graphs to produce labels. An auxiliary function with theoretically proven monotonic-increasing properties solves the optimization problem. Experiments on six public datasets with varying missing ratios show competitive performance against multiple IMVC methods.

### Strengths
- **Novel similarity-level imputation**: SIIHPC bypasses traditional missing-sample recovery, reducing computational overhead and allowing clustering without requiring fully complete data structures. This is an efficient alternative to recovery-based techniques. (All reviewers; particularly highlighted by Reviewer 1)
- **Hybrid-group prototype construction**: Replaces single prototype with a flexible, view-specific set of prototype quantities, enabling each view to capture unique features and improving adaptability to heterogeneous data. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Extensive experimental evaluation**: Tests on multiple datasets (e.g., VGGFACEHUND, YOUTUBEFACE, FASHMINST) with varying missing ratios and multiple metrics. Ablation studies validate individual component contributions. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Favorable memory and computational efficiency**: SIIHPC shows lower resource consumption compared to several baselines. (Reviewer 2; also noted by Reviewer 4 in the context of PSIMVC comparison)
- **Clear and well-organized presentation**: The framework is logically structured, with Figure 1 providing an intuitive overview. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **Interesting auxiliary function**: Designed to equivalently solve a quadratic programming problem with orthogonal constraints and proven to be monotonic-increasing, supporting optimization convergence. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Terminology and notation clarity**: Terms in Figure 1 (T-PBL, SLI, IVHGP, MSVSG) are not explained in the caption or text, making the framework hard to follow without additional context. Several notations are undefined: \( ()_{+} \) in Eq. (11), “N/A” in Tables 2 and 3. The Symbol Summary in the Appendix is incomplete. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)  
  *Minority reviewer (Reviewer 1) found this especially problematic; others also noted missing definitions.*
- **Algorithm process and parameter details**: The overall workflow, especially the final steps for obtaining clustering results and initial parameter settings, is insufficiently described. Hyper-parameter choices (e.g., λ from [10⁻⁷,…,10⁻²], β from [10²,…,10⁷]) lack justification. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Missing theoretical explanations for baseline failures**: Some comparison methods (e.g., matrix factorization, tensor decomposition, graph-based) perform poorly on VGGFACEHUND, YOUTUBEFACE, FASHMINST even though these datasets are not exceptionally large. No discussion of computational bottlenecks or algorithmic limitations is provided. (Reviewer 2)
- **Insufficient space complexity analysis**: The memory requirements of Algorithm 2 are not broken down per variable (considering matrix/tensor dimensions, number of samples, views, prototypes). How memory scales with these factors is unclear. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Lack of interpretability and practical discussion**: The clustering outputs and prototype structures are not interpreted in real-world terms; the broader utility of hybrid-group prototypes is not explored. (Reviewer 1, minority)
- **Ablation study lacks granular analysis**: The contributions of “Similarity Level Imputation” and “Hybrid-group Prototypes” are not mechanistically discussed. For example, does similarity imputation primarily help under high missing rates? How does the number of prototypes affect performance? (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing standard deviation metrics**: Experimental results are presented without standard deviations, making it hard to assess stability and reliability across runs. (Reviewer 1, minority)
- **Missing comparisons and analyses**:
  - No comparison with deep learning-based IMVC methods. (Reviewer 2)
  - Differences with PSIMVC (also prototype-based) are not explained; PSIMVC consumes less time/memory in Table 3 – why? (Reviewer 4)
  - Theoretical complexity of GSRIMC vs. SIIHPC not provided. (Reviewer 2)
  - Reasons for sub-optimal results in Table 2 are not discussed (unlike Table 3). (Reviewer 4)
- **Convergence behavior question**: Figure 4 shows the objective of auxiliary function \( g \) increases monotonically within each iteration of Algorithm 2, but not across different iterations (e.g., 3rd vs 4th vs 5th). This discrepancy is not explained. (Reviewer 4)
- **Dataset description insufficient**: The composition of each view (e.g., image vs text, features extracted, preprocessing steps) is not specified, limiting assessment of generalizability. (Reviewer 3)
- **Sensitivity to imputed similarity quality and high missing ratios**: No discussion of potential limitations when missing rates are very high or imputation quality degrades. (Reviewer 1, minority)
- **Complexity reduction not justified**: The claim that the complexity of \(\mathbf{H}_{v,s}\) is reduced from square to linear after transformation lacks explanation. (Reviewer 4)
- **Performance with elicitation sampling prototypes**: Not evaluated, leaving an open question about alternative prototype initialization. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minor organization issue**: The title of Section 5.5 is identical to that of Section G in the Appendix. (Reviewer 2)
