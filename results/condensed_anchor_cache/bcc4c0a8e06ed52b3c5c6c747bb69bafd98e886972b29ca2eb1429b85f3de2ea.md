## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates in-context reinforcement learning (ICRL) for LLMs, where context contains (query, prediction, reward) triplets instead of ground-truth labels. The authors show that naive ICRL fails due to lack of exploration, and propose two sampling-based variants—Explorative ICRL (introduces randomness via dropout of context entries) and Approximate ICRL (a cheaper approximation). Experiments on text classification benchmarks (e.g., SST-2, AG News, etc.) show that these methods improve over naive ICRL and zero-shot, but generally underperform supervised in-context learning (ICL). The paper also ablates reward type (positive-only best), keep probability, and subsampling strategies.

### Strengths
- The paper studies an interesting and promising research direction. (*One reviewer scored 6 and found it promising; others gave 3.*)
- It identifies two key causes for naive ICRL failure: lack of exploration and inability to learn from negative examples. These insights are useful for future research.
- The proposed Explorative ICRL and Approximate ICRL are novel, simple to implement, and show significant performance improvements over the naive baseline on the benchmark tasks. Approximate ICRL reduces computational cost with little performance loss.
- Extensive ablations and benchmark experiments are provided, covering types of rewards, sampling probabilities, context subsampling strategies, and the effect of negative rewards. Each design choice is well analyzed and supported.
- The paper is clearly written and well organized. The angle of leveraging LLM stochasticity for exploration is interesting.

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and setting are unclear.** The paper only studies stateless, single-step binary-reward tasks (text classification). This is very similar to supervised ICL: the reward is derived from ground-truth labels (line 252), and filtering for positive rewards effectively reconstructs the supervised training set in context. Why use ICRL over ICL when ICL performs better on all benchmarks (Fig. 2)? One reviewer noted that the setting resembles a random search followed by ICL, which is basic and straightforward.
- **Performance relative to supervised ICL is weak.** In 3 of 5 benchmarks, ICRL (even the proposed variants) underperforms supervised ICL in both accuracy and convergence speed. The reviewer expected comparable performance on easy text classification if the method were promising.
- **The handling of negative rewards contradicts prior ICRL literature.** The paper shows negative rewards hurt performance, whereas prior work on in-context RL (e.g., Laskin et al., Lee et al., Dai et al., Zisman et al.) suggests that transformers can learn from suboptimal demonstrations and that diverse priors improve posterior approximation. This may indicate LLMs lack the ability to maximize reward in-context, a crucial feature of ICRL.
- **Missing baselines.** The paper does not compare against:
    - RLHF on the binary rewards.
    - Sampling with higher temperature (given the claimed benefit of stochasticity for exploration).
    - Reordering context examples (a simple exploration baseline).
- **Limited scope weakens novelty.** The work focuses only on single-step bandit problems, while prior work already studies inference-only in-context RL for multi-step sequential decision making (e.g., Brooks et al., Mirchandani et al.). The title claims "Reinforcement Learners" but the scope is more akin to contextual bandits; one reviewer suggested renaming. Additionally, STaR-like methods that sample → filter by reward → add to context are already known to work well in-context (Agarwal et al.), reducing the novelty of the proposed algorithms.
- **Lack of more complex RL environments.** Experiments are confined to text classification with binary rewards. Evaluating on contextual bandits or MDP environments (e.g., from Lee et al.) would be more convincing, where rewards come naturally from the environment rather than being derived from ground-truth labels.
- **Minor presentation issues:** typos (lines 72, 186, 187, 426, 539) noted by one reviewer.
