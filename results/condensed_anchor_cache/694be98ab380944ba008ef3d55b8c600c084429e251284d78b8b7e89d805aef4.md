## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces PoseDiffusion, a diffusion-based framework for generating 2D human pose skeletons from text descriptions. The denoising model GUNet combines graph convolutional networks (GCNs) with a U-Net architecture to capture spatial relationships among keypoints. The authors claim this is the first generative framework for text-to-pose based on diffusion models, and they also consider downstream use of generated poses with ControlNet for controllable image generation.

### Strengths
- Novelty of using graph convolutional networks within a diffusion model for human pose skeleton generation, exploiting the inherent graph structure of keypoints (reviewers 1, 2, 3). To the reviewers’ knowledge, GNNs have not previously been used in human-specific diffusion-based generation (reviewer 1). Some reviewers viewed this as creative and reasonable (reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Consideration of downstream applications, such as using generated pose keypoints to control image generation with ControlNet (reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Downstream motivation not substantiated.** The introduction motivates PoseDiffusion by improving 2D pose skeletons for controllable human image generation, but experiments only evaluate at the heatmap level. It remains unclear whether heatmap improvements translate to better final image generation (reviewer 1). The proposed ControlNet-based evaluation has its own biases and does not convincingly show improvement over baselines (reviewer 3).
- **Unconvincing qualitative results.** In Figure 5, the baseline SD 1.5 T2P sometimes produces more coherent and realistic poses than GUNet (reviewer 1). In Figure 7, GUNet does not differentiate left vs. right keypoints, resulting in flipped/mirrored skeletons (reviewer 1). Visualizations fall short of top-conference standards (reviewer 2). The ControlNet qualitatives do not look convincingly better than baselines (reviewer 3).
- **Flawed evaluation protocol.** Using MSE to compare generated heatmap coordinates to ground truth penalizes valid variations in a generative task (reviewer 1, line 455). Applying HPSv2, a metric designed for natural images, to keypoint heatmaps is questionable and lacks explanation (reviewer 1, Table 2). The paper lacks quantitative evaluations that directly measure generated pose quality, such as FID, CLIP-image similarity, or cycle consistency error using an off-the-shelf pose detector (reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient baselines and missing comparisons.** Baselines are limited: WGAN-LP (2018), SD1.5 T2P, PoseAdapter. UNet-T2H is an ablation, not an independent baseline (reviewer 3). Descriptions of baselines (especially the “pose coding layer”) are too vague to understand what they are and where they are added (reviewer 3). The paper does not compare to recent works specifically designed for text-to-pose, such as “Adversarial Synthesis of Human Pose From Text” by Yifei Zhang et al. (reviewer 3). A comparison to a pure transformer-based architecture is needed to justify the benefit of graph convolutions (reviewer 3).
- **Disagreement on novelty.** Some reviewers found the GCN+UNet approach novel (reviewers 1, 3), but reviewer 2 considered the novelty insufficient to distinguish the model from existing work, and reviewer 3 explicitly required a transformer baseline comparison to justify the graph convolution component. The claimed contributions may be overstated (reviewer 2).
- **Presentation and clarity issues.** The manuscript needs a thorough language revision (reviewer 2). Descriptions of baselines and evaluation protocols are lacking (reviewers 1, 3). The paper requires considerable improvements to meet top-conference standards (reviewer 2).
