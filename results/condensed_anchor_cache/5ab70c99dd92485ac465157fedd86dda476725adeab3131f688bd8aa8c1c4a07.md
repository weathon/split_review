## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Tensor-Var, which uses kernel Conditional Mean Embedding (CME) with deep feature (DF) learning to linearize nonlinear dynamics and state-observation mappings in 4D-Var, producing a convex cost function. It is evaluated on chaotic systems and a global weather prediction task using ERA5 data. Reviewers praise the theoretical grounding and originality but raise concerns about limited real-world validation, baseline choices, unfair runtime comparisons, missing connections to dynamical systems theory, and writing quality. Reviewer 1 (score 8) is highly positive, emphasizing innovation and efficiency gains; Reviewers 2, 3, 4 (score 6) have more reservations, especially regarding the gap to practical assimilation.

### Strengths
- The Tensor-Var method is innovative, applying CME to 4D-Var to significantly improve optimization efficiency for nonlinear dynamical systems (R1). It avoids the pure DL trend and preserves theoretical guarantees (R3).
- Thorough background and theoretical analysis are provided, proving the existence of linear dynamics with consistent convergence between original and feature spaces (R2, R3). Each modification is well-motivated with theoretical justifications (R2).
- Competitive accuracy and efficiency are achieved compared to variational DA baselines (R1, R2). Several toy scenarios are used to compare approaches (R4). The work addresses the core task of forecasting dynamical systems (R3).
- Mathematical principles are presented clearly (R4). The use of established methods (CME, DFs, delay embeddings, overcomplete autoencoders) to improve existing DA methods is noted (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited real-world validation and applicability**:
    * Validation on real-world datasets is limited; additional tests in practical applications are needed (R1).
    * Feasibility is questionable due to the demand for ground-truth state data; a more elaborate discussion of how Tensor-Var improves over traditional DA in this setting would be welcome (R2).
    * Evaluation uses only synthetic data (simulated observations from ERA5 reanalysis), not true observations; this makes it hard to assess real-world performance (R3, R4). Resolution is too low and observations are assumed at grid points, greatly simplifying the problem (R4).
    * Using ERA5 (a reanalysis product) for simulated assimilation means only one data type is assimilated; the paper does not address assimilation of multiple observation sources or online/real-time operational scenarios (R4). Overall, the work is still far from real assimilation, reducing its relevance and value (R4).
- **Baseline choices and comparisons**:
    * On the chaotic system, only Frerix et al. 2021 is used as a DL baseline; more recent attempts (e.g., Bocquet et al. 2024) and the Latent-x-Var baseline (used in the NWP task) are omitted (R3).
    * Runtime comparison is unfair: Tensor-Var is run on CPU while variational baselines leverage GPU acceleration via JAX; this should be addressed by running each framework on suitable hardware or stating the limitation explicitly (R3).
- **Convexity and optimization concerns** (R2):
    * The claim that the entire loss is convex when DFs are learned jointly with linear operators is questionable, because feature maps are parameterized by nonlinear NNs, making the optimization non-convex.
    * Learning the inverse feature map φ† via loss (7) is akin to an overcomplete autoencoder, which can underlearn useful features and overfit; the influence of regularization weight w on performance should be discussed.
    * Linear approximation of nonlinear dynamics is only approximate; in chaotic systems, longer roll-outs will cause qualitative/quantitative differences between learned linear dynamics and true dynamics. The authors should comment on this in the context of their DA approach.
- **Missing theoretical connections to dynamical systems theory** (R2):
    * Using a history of observations connects to delay embedding methods; the paper should discuss heuristics for choosing time lag and embedding dimension, and how they compare to the cross-validation approach used.
    * Mapping nonlinear dynamics to a higher-dimensional space to linearize them is core to Koopman operator theory; at least a brief connection to this field is missing.
- **Writing quality and presentation**:
    * The paper contains grammatical errors, unclear sentences, and repetitions (e.g., lines 164–166, 270–271); the conclusion can be improved (R3). Minor typos: “Equation equation 1” (l.173–174), “global optimal” should be “global optimum” (l.356–357) (R2).
    * Author Contributions and Acknowledgments on p. 10 contain sample text from the ICLR template (R2, R3).
- **Need for explanation**: Why are NRMSE distributions of Latent 4D-Var more spread than those of Latent 3D-Var and Tensor-Var? (R2)

*Note: Reviewer 1 (score 8) is more positive than the other reviewers (score 6), particularly about the innovation and efficiency. The other reviewers, while acknowledging theoretical contributions, raise substantial concerns about the gap to real-world applications, baseline selection, and experimental rigor.*
