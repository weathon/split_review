## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes AccCtr, a method to accelerate training-free conditional diffusion models by retraining the condition extraction network (e.g., depth, segmentation, edge detectors) to refine the guidance loss. The authors introduce an alternative maximization framework to analyze manifold deviation as the cause of slow sampling, and they retrain \(C_\psi\) using two losses (\(\mathcal{L}_1,\mathcal{L}_2\)) on the latent space of a pre-trained LDM. The claimed result is faster generation and improved sample quality across multiple condition modalities. Reviewer opinions diverge sharply: two reviewers are positive (scores 5,5), one is mixed (score 3), and one is strongly negative (score 1). The positive reviews highlight the problem’s relevance, the manifold deviation analysis, and the theoretical alternative maximization, but also note limited validation, marginal improvements, and writing issues. The negative review identifies a fundamental error in the main theorem, a misleading “training-free” claim, missing comparisons with prior work (e.g., MPGD, FreeControl, DreamSampler), and numerous manuscript errors.

### Strengths
- The problem of slow sampling in training-free conditional diffusion models is relevant and well-motivated; the analysis of manifold deviation as a bottleneck is intuitive and provides a plausible motivation (Reviewer 2).
- Introducing an alternative maximization framework to analyze the conditional sampling process is interesting and provides a theoretical lens (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Training the condition extraction network with the proposed objective yields promising results without requiring multiple posterior mean updates per sampling step (Reviewer 1).
- The paper achieves state-of-the-art results on the evaluated tasks (Reviewer 4), and the figures and Algorithm 1 aid understanding (Reviewers 2, 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Misleading “training-free” claim and high training cost.** The title and framing claim “training-free” yet the method actually trains the condition extraction network from scratch (equation 14), which contradicts the name. Moreover, the reported fine-tuning cost is ~60 hours, which is deemed unacceptable for a “training-free” approach (Reviewers 1, 4).  
- **Fundamental theoretical error.** The derivation leading to equation (12) is invalid: \(\nabla_x\mathcal{E}(y,x,C_\psi)\neq\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\nabla_{f(x)}\mathcal{E}(y,f(x),C_\psi)\) because it ignores the Jacobian \(\partial\epsilon_\theta(x)/\partial x\). Consequently, the claim that equations (10)–(12) alternately maximize \(\log p(z_0)\) and \(\log p(y|z_{0|t})\) is incorrect, breaking the analysis in Sections 4–5 (Reviewer 1).
- **Mischaracterization of \(\hat{z}_{0|t}\) as a projection onto the image manifold.** The posterior mean \(\mathbb{E}[z_0|z_t]\) is conditioned on noise level \(t\); only as \(t\to0\) does it approach the true image manifold, not for large \(t\). This should be evident from visualizing \(\hat{z}_{0|t}\) during sampling (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing comparisons with relevant methods.** The paper does not compare against: MPGD (Manifold Preserving Guided Diffusion, ICLR 2024) which also trains an autoencoder in latent space using a loss similar to \(\mathcal{L}_1\); Decomposed Diffusion Sampler (ICLR 2024), DreamSampler (ECCV 2024), FreeControl (CVPR 2024), MasaCtrl (ICCV 2023), ControlNet (ICCV 2023), Ctrl-Adapter (arxiv 2024), and other training-free or adapter-tuning approaches (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Limited validation and marginal improvements.** Quantitative results (Table 2) show only small gains over baselines, and the FID values are not competitive with some baselines, suggesting a controllability-quality trade-off. Only one generative model (Stable Diffusion) is tested; no experiments with other diffusion models or flow-matching methods. No confidence intervals are reported (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Weak connection between alternative maximization and network optimization.** The alternative maximization framework is presented in Section 4, but its direct link to retraining \(C_\psi\) in Section 5 is not clearly established (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing ablation studies.**  
  - Ablations on the number of unconditional diffusion steps \(N\) and conditional correction steps \(M\) are visual only; quantitative evaluations are needed (Reviewer 2).  
  - Ablation on the hyperparameter \(\lambda\) in \(\mathcal{L}_2\) is missing, despite its importance for controlling guidance quality (Reviewer 4).  
  - The influence of the loss \(\mathcal{L}_2\) is questionable: optimizing \(\Psi\) requires computing a Hessian (\(\nabla_{z_{0|t}}\mathcal{L}_1\) then partial for \(\Psi\)), leading to high computational cost (Reviewer 4).
- **Writing and presentation issues.**  
  - Notation inconsistencies: \(z_t\) denotes pixel-level diffusion in Sections 1–4 and Algorithm 1, but latent code in lines 347–350.  
  - Equation (9) has errors (\(\bar\alpha_t\) should be \(\bar\alpha_{t-1}\); \(\hat{z}_t\) should be \(\hat{z}_{t-1}\)).  
  - “Time travel step \(K\)” in Algorithm 1 (lines 9–13) is ambiguous; with \(K=2\) the noise scheduler parameter \(\bar{a}_k\) would mismatch \(\bar{a}_{JN}\).  
  - Typos: e.g., in Table 1 the best CLIP score is from ControlNet, not bolded; \(\tilde{\beta}\) should be \(\bar{\beta}\) in line 139.  
  - The manuscript does not follow ICLR 2025 style.  
  - References have inconsistent formatting between introduction and other sections (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Lack of positioning to efficient sampling literature.** The paper claims improved sampling efficiency but does not discuss connections to prior acceleration methods (e.g., arxiv/2211.13449, arxiv/2310.19075) (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited task scope.** Figure 1 uses only image-to-image tasks (depth, edge, segmentation) without text guidance; the analysis of slow generation does not account for additional challenges from text conditioning. It remains unclear whether AccCtr works on style-transfer tasks (Reviewer 4).
