## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Sampling-Gaussian, a supervision method for stereo matching that uses Gaussian distribution sampling and a combined loss (L1 + cosine similarity) to address multimodal issues in soft-argmax-based disparity regression. The method is evaluated on KITTI and SceneFlow datasets across five baselines (PSMNet, MSN2D, etc.) and claims improved accuracy. Reviewer opinions diverge: one reviewer (score 3) finds the writing and explanation seriously lacking, while the other two (scores 6, 5) consider the method straightforward and motivation good. There is consensus that the main improvement comes from handling the disparity range below zero, but concerns about limited innovation, scene-dependent offset, and weak improvement on iterative SOTA frameworks (e.g., IGEV) are shared.

### Strengths
- Tackles an important problem in computer vision (multimodal distribution in soft-argmax stereo matching).
- Motivation is good: clearly identifies the shortcoming of soft-argmax and proposes sampling from Gaussian for supervision.
- Method is straightforward, easy to follow, and can be integrated into any soft-argmax-based method without extra inference cost.
- Performance improvement is significant on several well-known softmax-based methods (e.g., PSMNet, MSN2D).
- The method is universal and cross-domain generalization results (Table 6 on KITTI, SceneFlow) demonstrate transferability.
- Code is provided online.

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and clarity** (one reviewer, score 3, is particularly critical). The paper needs significant improvement in writing: many typos (e.g., Line85: "Taken", Line180: "analyse", Line212-213: "to calculates", Line289-290: "to further to"); Sections 3.2(a)(b) are unclear; Equations (5), (7), (8) are not properly written (e.g., what is exp^{z^*}?); Fig.6 visualisation differences are not highlighted. The idea is not well explained, and the link between the proposed method and the argument is unclear due to poorly explained equations.
- **Missing analysis of multimodal occurrence**. The paper does not analyze or discuss when multimodal distribution actually happens; it only states that soft-argmax fails under multimodality.
- **Limited innovation**. The main improvement appears to come from taking the disparity range below 0 into consideration, thereby eliminating the negative impact of distributed supervision in that range. However, the extended disparity range is fixed at 16 (offset), which is scene-dependent. It is unclear whether this fixed offset generalizes to other scenarios (e.g., Middlebury with disparity up to 800) or requires resetting for each new scene. This lack of analysis on offset selection weakens the contribution.
- **Small improvement on iterative SOTA frameworks** (e.g., IGEV-Stereo). The improvement over IGEV is limited, raising the question of whether iterative optimization schemes (which are now dominant, e.g., IGEV) suffer from the multimodal problem at all. The authors should analyze the disparity distribution of IGEV vs. other baselines to explain the lack of significant improvement. One plausible reason is that iterative frameworks are less dependent on soft-argmax.
- **Missing experiments and comparisons** (derived from reviewer questions):
    - Provide results showing improvements near disparity endpoints (e.g., disp < 10 px and disp > 180 px) to validate the claim about overlooked regression near boundaries (Line 110).
    - Test the proposed method on more recent methods (PCWNet, CFNet, GANet) to see if it can achieve state-of-the-art performance.
    - Explain why Sampling-Gaussian does not significantly improve IGEV-Stereo; include analysis of IGEV’s disparity distribution compared to baselines like PSMNet and MSN2D.
