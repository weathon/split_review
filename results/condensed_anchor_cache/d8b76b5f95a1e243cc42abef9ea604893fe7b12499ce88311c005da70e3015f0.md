## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes TabUnite, a framework that applies three encoding schemes (PSK Encoding, Dictionary Encoding, Analog Bits) to convert categorical features into continuous representations for use with diffusion (DDPM) and flow matching generative models. The goal is to unify heterogeneous tabular features in a single generative process. Reviewers generally find the paper well-written and the experiments extensive, but raise significant concerns about limited novelty, insufficient analysis of the encoding schemes’ individual effects, and missing comparisons to learned embeddings and the SOTA encoding i2b. The reported performance gains over baselines are seen as marginal or inconclusive. Two reviewers rated the paper 5 (positive, open to revision) while three rated it 3 (weak reject), highlighting a clear division on the significance of the contribution.

### Strengths
- **Addresses an important and current challenge**: Handling categorical variables in tabular generative models is a critical problem with many practical applications (Reviewer 2, 4).
- **Comprehensive experimental evaluation**: The paper provides extensive experiments on multiple datasets and against many baselines, with good insights into generative model performance and additional details in the appendix (Reviewers 1, 3, 4, 5).
- **Clear and well-organized presentation**: The paper is easy to follow, with thorough background on diffusion/flow matching and encoding schemes, logical structure, and effective use of figures to aid comprehension (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5).
- **Introduces three distinct encoding methods**: PSK Encoding, Dictionary Encoding, and Analog Bits are novel preprocessing strategies for categorical features, differing from traditional one-hot encoding (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Space complexity analysis is provided** (Reviewer 1).
- **Curation of a large-scale heterogeneous dataset** for comprehensive evaluation (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The paper primarily applies preprocessing techniques (encoding) to existing diffusion/flow models, which does not constitute a fundamentally new method. The novelty is considered overstated, especially for Dictionary Encoding (which is essentially min‑max scaling) and Analog Bits (which is a simple binary encoding) (Reviewers 2, 3, 5). The contribution is seen as more of a data processing trick rather than a core ML advancement (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient analysis per encoding method**: The paper does not adequately analyze the individual impact of each encoding scheme (Phase‑Shift Keying, Dictionary, Analog Bits) on generation quality or sampling behavior (Reviewer 5). An ablation study showing how each encoding affects results is missing.
- **Marginal improvement over SOTA encoding i2b**: The proposed encodings outperform i2b only on some datasets, and on others (e.g., Bank, Cardio, Stroke in appendices) i2b is better. The overall performance gains are marginal (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing comparison to learned embeddings**: The paper does not compare static encodings to learned embeddings (e.g., from FinDiff or TabDiff), which could better represent categorical semantics (Reviewer 4). This is a key missing baseline given the claim of unifying continuous and categorical data (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing comparison to TabSyn with latent diffusion**: A straightforward baseline would be to compare TabUnite (DDPM/Flow) with TabSyn (latent diffusion) using the same generative model; TabSyn is missing from Table 3 (Reviewer 3).
- **Encoding limitations**: PSK Encoding works well for periodic categorical values (e.g., days of the week) but fails for nominal categorical data (e.g., city names) where a circular representation is not meaningful. Dictionary Encoding preserves ordering but is not suitable for non‑ordinal categoricals (Reviewer 4). Analog Bits blurs categorical boundaries by implying distances between distinct categories, and its decoding via simple thresholding may lose precision (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of intuition and justification**: The reasons why the proposed encodings outperform one‑hot encoding are not clearly explained. For example, PSK Encoding uses a uniformly distributed phase without justification; it is unclear why this is optimal for arbitrary categorical features (Reviewer 1). The claim that generative models underfit in high‑dimensional spaces (due to one‑hot) is stated without supporting evidence (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing experiments for broader applicability**: Are these encodings effective for predictive tasks (e.g., classification, regression) beyond generation? Results on such tasks would demonstrate broader utility (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing integration with other generative models**: It remains unclear whether applying these encodings to other tabular synthesis models (beyond DDPM/Flow) improves performance (Reviewers 2, 5).
- **Design choices not motivated**: For PSK Encoding, why use Eq. (1) structure? Could a learnable phase bias improve performance? The design rationale is insufficient (Reviewer 1).
- **Minor weakness**: The examples explaining encoding schemes use numbers instead of categorical labels (e.g., “{1,2,3,4}”); using actual category names would be more intuitive (Reviewer 4).
