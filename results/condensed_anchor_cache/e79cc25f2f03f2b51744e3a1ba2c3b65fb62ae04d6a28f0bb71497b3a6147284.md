## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies uniform expressivity of message-passing GNNs with rational activation functions in terms of first-order logic. Building on Barcelo et al. (2020), it shows that not all GC2 queries can be captured by rational GNNs (contrary to ReLU GNNs) and proposes a fragment RGC2 that rational GNNs can capture. Experiments on two artificial queries test approximation stability. Reviewers found the work clearly presented and extending prior results, but raised concerns about the significance of rational activations, the arbitrariness of RGC2, and the limited scope of experiments. One reviewer (score 3) was notably more critical of the contribution and presentation; the other three (score 5) were more positive.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and pedagogy**: The paper is clearly written, makes well-motivated connections to Barcelo et al., and explains GC2 and related logic accessibly (Reviewers 1, 3). Section 2 is particularly helpful.
- **Novel theoretical contribution**: Extends GNN expressivity results to rational activations, showing that not all GC2 queries are uniformly expressible—contrasting with ReLU GNNs (Reviewers 2, 3). This is a useful step in understanding how activation functions affect expressivity.
- **Good positioning in literature**: References existing work appropriately and the problem is relevant to the ICLR community (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Adequate presentation of results**: Both negative (Theorem 3) and positive (Theorem 4) results are shown (Reviewer 2).
- **Experimental stability**: Experiments demonstrate that approximations for the two queries remain stable as the number of nodes grows (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Significance and motivation of rational activations**: One reviewer (score 3) found the contribution small and was not convinced why GNNs with rational activations are worth studying; the other three (score 5) also questioned this. References or experiments showing practical advantages are missing (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). The paper does not clearly justify why this fragment is important.
- **RGC2 appears arbitrary and incomplete**: There is no proof or strong argument that RGC2 is the maximal fragment captured by rational GNNs. The paper only shows that RGC2 is expressible, not that rational GNNs cannot express anything outside RGC2. Reviewers 1 and 2 request either a full characterization or a discussion of why it is infeasible, and a better explanation of what patterns are lost compared to GC2.
- **Presentation deficiencies** (especially noted by Reviewer 4, but also by Reviewer 3):
  - Background takes too much space; novel results first appear on page 7. Structure is poor: RGC2 is defined only later (line 416) but referenced earlier (Theorem 4, line 351). Proof sketches are separated from theorem statements.
  - Figure 1 caption is too sparse; lacks context.
  - Example 2 in Section 3 is ambiguous: the vector **c** appears unused, the index \(i\) in \(\xi^4(G,v)_i\) is unclear, and the notation \(A\) for universe conflicts with standard adjacency matrix usage.
  - Several typographical errors: line 181 extra “a”, line 309 missing subscripts (\(Q_1,Q_2,Q_3,Q_4\)), line 360 extra “and”, line 450 repeated “then”. Line 444 contains a typo claiming rational GNNs can uniformly express GC2, contradicting Theorem 3 (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited experimental scope**: Only two artificial queries are tested. There is no direct comparison with a ReLU GNN (the paper claims a ReLU GNN can approximate a CReLU GNN but does not show this). Training loss curves are not analyzed to corroborate theoretical difficulties. The results do not provide a practical advantage and undercut the theoretical message (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Missing extensions and discussion**: The paper does not discuss why rational activations matter beyond the niche setting, nor does it extend to higher-order MPNNs or other activation functions as suggested in related work (Reviewer 2). No comparison with alternative activation functions is given.

**Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (rating 3, confidence 4) was much more critical, emphasizing the small significance, poor presentation, and weak experiments. The other three (rating 5, confidence 3) appreciated the clarity and the theoretical contribution but shared similar concerns about completeness and motivation.
