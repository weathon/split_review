- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 6, 1, 5, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces COP (Competition Over Pixels) / SAMP, a non-iterative fully-differentiable method for unsupervised object-centric learning. It uses CNN and MaxPool layers to generate “primitive slots” from image features, then applies a single cross-attention layer (Soft-Winner-Takes-All, SWTA) where softmax is taken over queries and the row-normalization is skipped. The method is evaluated on Multi-dSprites, Tetrominoes, and CLEVR6, reporting FG-ARI.  
Reviewers strongly disagree on the paper’s value: one rates 6 (positive, important step), one rates 5 (positive but limited), one rates 3 (negative), and one rates 1 (very negative, rejects on novelty, experiments, and clarity). Key points of contention are the simplicity versus lack of novelty, the choice of datasets, and the strength of empirical evidence.

### Strengths
- **Performance on standard benchmarks**: COP surpasses the original Slot Attention on Multi-dSprites and Tetrominoes across all reported FG-ARI metrics (Reviewer 1). Results on these simple datasets are good (Reviewer 4).
- **Efficiency**:
  * Superior theoretical time and space complexity compared to Slot Attention (Reviewer 1).
  * Non-iterative design leads to small memory consumption and runtime (Reviewer 4); the single attention step may speed up training/inference (Reviewer 3).
- **Simplicity**: The method uses only Convolution, MaxPool, and a single Cross-Attention layer, is non-iterative, and the framework is well‑written and easy to understand (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Novelty of max‑pooling for slot initialization** (Reviewer 3): Using max‑pooling as a mechanism to learn object‑centric representations has not been done before, as far as the reviewer knows. (Other reviewers consider all components not new; see Weaknesses.)
- **Visualizations and appendix**: The detailed description of encoder and decoder architectures in the appendix, and the number of output visualizations, are appreciated (Reviewer 2).
- **Encouraging result with no attention**: COP without any cross‑attention still performs well on Tetrominoes (Reviewer 3). This suggests the primitive‑slot generation alone can capture some structure.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited dataset scope**:
  * All experiments use synthetic datasets (Multi-dSprites, Tetrominoes, CLEVR6) with clear object boundaries and limited variability. The ability to handle real‑world challenges (lighting, occlusion, background clutter) remains untested (Reviewer 1).
  * The object‑centric community has moved to more complex benchmarks such as ClevrTex, MultiShapeNet, and MOVi; those are necessary to assess the capacity of COP (Reviewer 2, 3, 4). Testing on only simple datasets makes it hard to evaluate scalability and generalizability (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
- **Weak baselines and incomplete comparisons**:
  * Baselines are somewhat dated (e.g., original Slot Attention). Comparisons with more recent methods like Implicit Slot Attention, BO-QSA, or transformer‑based alternatives are missing (Reviewer 1, 3). The paper mentions Implicit Slot Attention in Section 3.3 but does not include it in the results (Reviewer 3).
  * No comparison with methods that employ different slot initialization strategies, e.g., learned slot initializations or BO-QSA, making it difficult to isolate the impact of max‑pooling (Reviewer 3).
  * COP should be compared to variants where only one component is changed: (1) Slot Attention with max‑pooling query generation, (2) Slot Attention with a single attention layer, to fairly evaluate contributions (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient evaluation beyond segmentation**:
  * Only FG-ARI is reported. The quality of the learned slots should be assessed via downstream tasks, such as object property prediction (e.g., on CLEVR following Slot Attention’s protocol) or object tracking (Reviewer 2, 4).
- **Novelty concerns** (strong disagreement; only Reviewer 3 finds max‑pooling novel):
  * “Soft-Winner-Takes-All” is a slight variation of Slot Attention’s competitive attention: it applies softmax over queries but skips the normalization step. The core idea of competition between queries is already present in Slot Attention (Reviewer 2). The decoder is identical to the original (Reviewer 2).
  * The non‑iterative attention operation is common; SAVi already uses a single iteration of Slot Attention with conditional initialization (Reviewer 4). The proposed primitive slots are conceptually similar to that conditional initialization, differing only in source (image features vs. initial frame hints) (Reviewer 4).
  * The discussion about “competition through MaxPool layers” is misleading: CNN layers share weights across locations, so there are no independent sub‑networks; the diversity of primitive slots stems from local CNN filters and local max‑pooling (a well‑known property), not from competition (Reviewer 2).
  * The number of slots is hardcoded and manually tuned per dataset, and generating them via max‑pooling is even more limiting than the random initialization of Slot Attention (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear or incorrect technical descriptions**:
  * Section 3.1 is badly structured; the encoder and decoder are identical to Slot Attention and should be cited without re‑explanation. The novelty in the object‑centric bottleneck is not clearly presented (Reviewer 2).
  * The formulation of “Soft‑Winner‑Takes‑All” is not clearly defined. A mathematical definition, algorithm, code snippet, or side‑by‑side comparison with Slot Attention is needed (Reviewer 2, 4).
  * Section 3.3 misrepresents Implicit Slot Attention by implying it requires many iterations, while that work showed robustness to more iterations but not a necessity (Reviewer 3).
  * (Minor) The original Slot Attention uses the sqrt of the slot dimension, not $\sqrt{n}$ (Reviewer 3).
  * The term “Primitive slots” is inconsistently capitalized and hyphenated; its first mention should explicitly clarify how they differ from “queries” (Reviewer 2).
- **Ablation study is insufficient**:
  * The ablation should include a version of Slot Attention with only the query‑generation replaced by max‑pooling, and a version with only the iterative attention replaced by a single SWTA layer, to understand each component’s contribution (Reviewer 2).
  * No ablation comparing max‑pooling with other pooling strategies (e.g., average‑pooling, strided convolutions) to justify the choice of max‑pooling (Reviewer 3).
  * It is unclear whether the performance gains come from the novel initialization or from the overall architecture; an ablation that varies the slot initialization method is needed (Reviewer 3).
  * Table 1 introduces “L_comp” but its meaning is not explained (Reviewer 3).
- **Scalability claim unjustified**:
  * The abstract claims “scalable”, but scaling to larger/complex images would require adjusting the number of slots and thus the computation in the CNN+MaxPool stack (Reviewer 2). The non‑iterative design does not automatically imply scalability to complex scenes (Reviewer 2).
  * One reviewer (Reviewer 4) questions whether iterative attention is actually a feature, not a bug, because follow‑up works (STEVE, LSD) show it helps decompose complex data. It is unclear if non‑iterative SWTA can handle more complicated images (Reviewer 4).
- **Code quality**: The attached code is broken; `train.py` calls a non‑existing function `read_cli_args_and_get_config` (Reviewer 2). The code cannot be verified, and there are multiple implementations in the zip archive without instructions (Reviewer 2).
- **Writing and presentation**:
  * Several sentences are poorly written (e.g., “Similar to the encoder, we build the CNN layers in such a way that they preserve the spatial dimension. But, the MaxPool layers reduce it.”) The manuscript requires thorough revision (Reviewer 2).
- **Unanswered experimental questions** (overlap with missing experiments/ablations):
  * What is the exact formulation of SWTA? (Reviewer 2)
  * Is the “COP w/ Attn as in Slot Attention” ablation just adding weight normalization after softmax? (Reviewer 3)
  * Does performance improve if multiple iterations of SWTA‑Attention or Slot Attention are run after obtaining primitive slots? (Reviewer 3)
  * Has COP w/o attention been tested on other datasets? Is there a noticeable difference in segmentation masks? (Reviewer 3)
  * What is the actual wall‑clock time difference between COP and Slot Attention? (Reviewers 3, 4)
  * Why is SWTA significantly better than Slot Attention in the ablation? Slot Attention applies softmax over slots, which is more natural for competition (Reviewer 4).
- **Disagreements among reviewers**:
  * **Novelty**: Reviewers 2 and 4 consider all components not new; Reviewer 3 finds the use of max‑pooling novel. The majority view is that COP offers limited novelty.
  * **Scalability**: Reviewer 2 rejects the scalability claim; Reviewer 4 is skeptical; Reviewer 1 lists efficiency as a strength.
  * **Dataset choice**: Reviewers 2, 3, and 4 criticize the use of only simple synthetic datasets; Reviewer 1 acknowledges this as a limitation but still writes a positive evaluation.
