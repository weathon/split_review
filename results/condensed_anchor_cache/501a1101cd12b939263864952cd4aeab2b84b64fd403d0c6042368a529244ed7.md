## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a diffusion-based pretraining method for gait recognition in the wild. It conditions a latent diffusion model on the output of a gait recognition backbone, then fine-tunes the backbone on the original gait recognition task. Experiments on Gait3D and GREW across multiple backbones (GaitBase, GaitPart, GaitGL) show that the pretraining improves performance and reduces supervised fine-tuning iterations. The authors also introduce a modified Min-SNR weighting strategy for pretraining efficiency.

### Strengths
- First work to apply diffusion-based pretraining specifically to gait recognition; reveals an interesting and useful phenomenon. (Reviewers 2,3,4,5)
- Introduces a modified Min-SNR weighting strategy that enhances pretraining efficiency. (Reviewer 2)
- Extensive experiments on Gait3D and GREW across multiple backbones, with comprehensive ablation studies, demonstrate consistent performance improvement over training from scratch. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4,5)
- The pretraining serves as an effective initialization, allowing downstream models to achieve better performance with fewer supervised iterations. (Abstract, Reviewers 1,3)
- Paper is well-written, clear, and easy to understand. (Reviewers 1,2,4)

### Weaknesses
1. **Unclear motivation and mechanism**: It is not adequately explained how diffusion pretraining (which uses only a diffusion L2 loss in pixel space without identity supervision) improves discriminative feature separation in the embedding space, especially for inter-sequence recognition across different views. (Reviewers 2,3,4)
2. **Unfair comparisons and missing baselines**:
   - The gait extractor is pretrained for 120,000 iterations before fine-tuning, giving a cumulative training duration advantage over baselines trained only for fine-tuning iterations. (Reviewer 2,3)
   - No comparisons are made with other pretraining methods (e.g., self-supervision, other diffusion-based representation learning), making it hard to isolate the benefit of the specific diffusion approach. (Reviewer 3)
   - Reviewer 5 notes that the claimed reduction in supervised iterations is weak because pretraining itself requires substantial iterations (Table 1 shows original training iterations nearly equal to pretraining+transfer learning).
3. **Limited novelty and marginal performance gains**:
   - The framework combines existing components (diffusion models, conditioning) without substantial innovation; Section 3 contains no novel components. (Reviewer 2)
   - Several reviewers consider the performance gains marginal and insufficient to justify the added complexity of multi-stage pretraining (Reviewers 2,3). In contrast, Reviewer 5 considers the improvements considerable. Post-pretraining accuracies remain lower than existing state-of-the-art models on GREW and Gait3D. (Reviewer 2)
4. **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Hyperparameters such as \(r\) and \(l_g/l_D\) require careful tuning per dataset and backbone. Improvements on GaitBase (GREW) are incremental, and the need for per-case tuning limits practical generalizability. (Reviewer 3)
5. **Outdated backbones and lack of SOTA evaluation**:
   - The most recent backbone evaluated is GaitBase (2023); newer methods such as DYGait, SkeletonGait, CLASH, QAGait, and deepgaitv2 are not tested. (Reviewers 2,5)
   - Reviewer 5 explicitly recommends benchmarking against such stronger models to demonstrate the true value of the pretraining.
6. **Computational cost and scalability**: The multi-stage process (pretraining + fine-tuning) prolongs training time and demands substantial GPU resources, posing challenges for resource-constrained environments and scaling. (Reviewer 1)
7. **Data quality dependence**: The method relies on well-segmented and aligned input data; robustness to lower-quality or adversarially perturbed inputs is not investigated. (Reviewer 1)
8. **Conceptual issues**:
   - The claim of “transfer learning” is questioned because fine-tuning is performed on the same dataset as pretraining, resembling domain adaptation more than standard transfer learning. (Reviewer 2)
   - The pretraining resembles knowledge distillation from the diffusion model; it is unclear why one would not directly fine-tune the diffusion encoder (TAESD) for gait recognition instead of using it to pretrain other backbones. (Reviewer 5)
9. **Insufficient analysis**: The paper lacks analysis of why the diffusion pretraining helps, why certain backbones benefit more/less, and why the architecture components are chosen as they are. The experiments section mainly reports tables without explaining the underlying reasons. (Reviewers 3,4)
10. **Sampling inconsistency for temporal backbones**: For backbones like GaitPart and GaitGL, which require consecutive frames (e.g., 30 frames) for temporal learning, the method uses different frames for the encoder and the diffusion denoiser (\(s_\epsilon \neq s_g\)). It is unclear how this discrepancy is reconciled with the need for consecutive input frames, and the difference between the two inputs lacks clarity. (Reviewers 3,4)
11. **Missing cross-view evaluation**: Experiments are limited to in-the-wild datasets; validation on standard cross-view datasets (CASIA-B, OUMVLP) is absent, which would help assess generalizability to typical cross-view gait recognition tasks. (Reviewer 3)
12. **Figure clarity**: The figure could be improved to show different sampled frames rather than identical silhouettes. (Reviewer 4)
