## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes “Direct Inversion” (also called PnP Inversion), a simple three-line-code modification that disentangles the source and target diffusion branches to improve both essential content preservation and edit fidelity in text-guided diffusion-based image editing. The method is plug-and-play and applicable to many existing editing methods (e.g., P2P, MasaCtrl, PnP). To evaluate editing, the authors introduce PIE-Bench, a benchmark of 700 human-reviewed images across 10 editing types (object change/add/remove, pose change, color/material/background change, style change, etc.) with source/target prompts, source images, and editing-region masks. The paper reports extensive quantitative and qualitative results showing that Direct Inversion outperforms optimization-based inversion techniques across 8 editing methods while being nearly an order of magnitude faster. Three reviewers scored the paper 6 and one reviewer gave 8, reflecting broad agreement on the method’s simplicity and effectiveness but also shared concerns about presentation clarity and evaluation details.

### Strengths
- **Well-written and well-presented** with clear figures; results are pleasing and convincing.
- **Simple yet effective method**: Direct Inversion is a minimal (three lines of code) modification that improves both content preservation and edit fidelity across a wide range of diffusion-based editing methods.
- **Thorough investigation** of existing prompt-editing methods, identifying the key problem (previous methods try to unify source and target branches via fine-tuning, leading to a gap), and proposing a straightforward rectification.
- **Comprehensive experiments** applying the method to 8 recent editing methods (P2P, MasaCtrl, P2P-Zero, PnP, etc.), with universal improvements in both content preservation and edit fidelity; detailed ablation study and thorough documentation of setups in the supplementary material enhance reproducibility.
- **Benchmark contribution**: PIE-Bench provides 700 images across 10 well-defined editing categories (changing/adding/removing objects, altering poses, colors, materials, backgrounds, style, etc.), each with source/target prompts, source image, and editing-region mask. The evaluation criteria are well-organized, and the benchmark is expected to benefit future research.
- **Sound experimental results and rigorous analysis**; the method is shown to be nearly an order of magnitude faster than optimization-based baselines.
- **Plug-and-play nature** makes Direct Inversion easy to integrate into existing diffusion editing pipelines.

### Weaknesses
- **Algorithm and notation clarity**: The method description (Section 4.2, Algorithm 1) is hard to follow.
  - The bracket notation `[C^{src}, C^{tgt}]` in the `DDIM_Forward` call is not explained; it is unclear whether the forward function is called twice (once for each prompt) and why line 9 has only one output.
  - The subscripts `z_0, z_t', z_t''` are not meaningful; they do not clearly distinguish latents from the forward process with the source prompt, target prompt, and inverted latents.
  - It is not defined what `o_t` represents or whether `z_t` in the algorithm encompasses both source and target latents.
  - The interaction between the source and target branches is vague: line 9 appears to update `z_t^{tgt}` only from `z_{t+1}^{tgt}` and `C^{tgt}` without using source branch information, yet the role of the source branch in preserving content is central.
- **Figure 3 is largely irrelevant** to the paper’s core contribution; reviewers suggest replacing it with a more informative figure (e.g., Figure 5 from the supplementary material) and moving the benchmark/evaluation metrics explanation into the main text (since they are major contributions but currently absent from the main body).
- **Lack of per-category scores** for the 10 editing types in PIE-Bench. Specifically, the pose-change category is of high interest; one reviewer suspects most methods struggle there and requests at least supplementary reporting and discussion.
- **CLIP similarity evaluation concerns**:
  - The target prompt in PIE-Bench is a full description of the target image, not a localized description of the edited region. This may cause CLIP similarity to be dominated by unchanged regions, potentially failing to reflect local edit quality.
  - CLIP similarity scores across different methods (Tables 1, 4, 8) are very close, raising doubts about whether this metric can distinguish editing quality. Reviewers suggest including BLIP similarity or human evaluation for a more robust assessment.
- **Benchmark composition**: The number of images per editing type is not given. Most editing types appear to be local (e.g., change object, add object), while large-region editing is limited to style changes. It is unclear if the dataset adequately covers both local and global editing scenarios.
- **Missing theoretical motivation**: The paper shows promising empirical results but lacks theoretical grounding for why the proposed separation of branches works.
- **Missing comparison/discussion**: A related but concurrent work [1] is not discussed; also, the method name “Direct Inversion” clashes with an existing work [2], which could cause ambiguity.
- **Minor errors**: Typo in Algorithm 1 Part I (“Invert” should be “Invert”); typo in Section 4.2 (“optimization-based” was written as “optimization-based”).
- **Specific unresolved questions**:
  - In Figure 1 (PnP column): Direct Inversion corrects texture/shape hallucinations but does it also correct the background color (third row)? If not, why?
  - Following Figure 2: “...This results in a learned latent with a discernible gap between `z_0''` and the original `z_0`...” How exactly does the optimized `z_0''` deviate from the original distribution? What does this deviation look like, and why does it negatively affect performance? Concrete examples would help.
- **Presentation of results**: Figure 4 shows only Ours+P2P as the applied result, though additional results are in the supplementary; more examples in the main figure would be appreciated.
- **Algorithm similarity to CycleDiffusion**: The algorithm feels similar to CycleDiffusion (arXiv:2210.05559); a clear explanation and comparison are needed to disambiguate.
