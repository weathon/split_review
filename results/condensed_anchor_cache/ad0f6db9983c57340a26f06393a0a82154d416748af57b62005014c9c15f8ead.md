## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers describe the paper as proposing a reinforcement learning (RL) framework for node selection in branch-and-bound (B&B), using a graph neural network to encode the entire B&B tree and a root-to-leaf path aggregation to produce a probability distribution over open nodes. The policy is trained with PPO on synthetic TSP instances and evaluated on TSPLIB, UFLP, MINLPLib, and MIPLIB, often outperforming SCIP’s default node selector. One reviewer notes the method is well-written and the root-to-leaf design cleverly avoids tree growth issues; another appreciates the global tree view as a strong motivation. However, several reviewers raise substantial concerns about experimental rigor, missing comparisons, clarity of the RL definition, and heuristic choices.

### Strengths
- Global view of the B&B tree via GNN is a strong motivation, addressing a limitation of prior node-selection methods that consider only isolated nodes. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- The root-to-leaf path aggregated score is a clever design that avoids the computational challenge of a growing B&B tree by assuming that a good node implies good ancestors. (Reviewer 1)
- Positive results across multiple benchmarks (TSPLIB, UFLP, MINLPLib, MIPLIB) under strict time constraints, despite training only on synthetic TSP instances. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- The paper clearly states the issue of node selection in B&B and the limitations of conventional hand-crafted or imitation-learning-based selectors. (Reviewer 2)
- Helpful supplemental contents and provided code (not tested). (Reviewer 3)
- Potential significance: RL-based node selection could overcome the limitations of imitation learning when the expert policy struggles, and the proposal of representing the MDP state with GNNs is an original contribution. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Reward function and RL formulation are not rigorously defined.**  
  - The definition of the reward (Equation 5) is unclear; in particular, how the gaps for the node selector and SCIP are calibrated is not specified (e.g., gap at same number of nodes or same time budget). (Reviewer 1)  
  - The overall RL structure (states, actions, reward function) is not clearly stated, making it hard to understand how the method works. (Reviewer 2)  
  - The design of the reward formula (why that formula, why use “-1” instead of a constant C, and why shifting/clipping heuristics are needed) lacks motivation and study. (Reviewer 3)  
  - It is ambiguous whether the reward is received only at the end of the episode (end of the B&B solve). (Reviewer 4)

- **Missing comparisons against prior work**, especially other learned node selectors that use GNNs (e.g., Labassi et al., 2022). The authors only compare against SCIP’s default node selector, and the version compatibility issue preventing comparison is not a sufficient justification. (Reviewers 2, 4)  
  - Consequently, the paper does not directly test its key hypotheses: that RL offers an advantage over imitation learning, and that considering the entire tree state is better than considering only isolated nodes. (Reviewer 4)

- **Evaluation metrics and experimental setup are problematic.**  
  - The evaluation metrics (e.g., Utility/Node) do not directly reflect the quality of primal solutions found or the achieved duality gap, which are the primary goals of node selection. (Reviewer 1)  
  - The time limit of 45 seconds is too small; longer runs are needed to observe the long-term impact of the learned policy, especially on complex instances. (Reviewer 1)  
  - The metrics and benchmark datasets are not those used in prior papers in this area, making it difficult to interpret results within the current context. Issues with metrics (e.g., when zero gap is reached) could be addressed by Primal/Dual/Gap integral metrics (as in the ML4CO competition). (Reviewer 4)  
  - Performance on the training set is not reported, only on benchmarks. (Reviewer 1)

- **The method relies on numerous heuristic choices without sufficient justification or ablation studies.**  
  - Only the upper bound is used as a factor for node selection; the paper does not study or compare why this choice is made. (Reviewer 3)  
  - The method is broken into many phases; it is unclear if it can be made end-to-end, and the overall practicality (e.g., integration into a solver) is questionable. (Reviewer 3)  
  - The choices of PPO (versus alternatives like TRPO or SAC), reward clipping, shifting, and the removal of problems with >100% or 0% gap are presented without supporting evidence. The cumulative heuristics undermine the motivation that RL is driving the improvements. (Reviewer 3)

- **Generalization and practicality concerns.**  
  - It is unclear whether the RL agent must be trained from scratch for each problem instance; if so, the method may not be practical. (Reviewer 2)  
  - The possibility of using a pretrained RL agent that generalizes across problem types is not demonstrated; more insight into generalization capabilities is needed. (Reviewer 2)  
  - The learning cost (experience collection for RL convergence) is not discussed. (Reviewer 2)

- **Other technical issues.**  
  - In Table 3, the mean for “Gap Ours” and “Gap Base” is NaN, despite normal individual values; this is not explained. (Reviewer 3)  
  - The paper’s argument that $P \neq NP$ implies that provably hard instances are unreasonable for the proposed algorithm is not clearly justified. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Numerical instability problems encountered in the B&B solver are not clarified, which is important for reproducibility and insight. (Reviewer 3)
