- Scores: 3, 1, 5, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes FedBuff-LDP, a local differential privacy (LDP) extension of the asynchronous FL algorithm FedBuff, by adding Gaussian noise to local gradients. It claims to be the first LDP method for asynchronous FL. Experiments are run on CIFAR-10 and CINIC-10, studying the impact of staleness on privacy under a membership inference attack. The privacy definition uses pure ε-DP but the actual mechanism provides ε-δ-DP due to Gaussian noise. Reviewers disagree: one reviewer scores 5, finding the results interesting and the topic timely; the other three score 1–3, citing minimal novelty, missing formal proofs, and limited experiments.

### Strengths

- **Timely topic:** Asynchronous FL with LDP is an interesting and under-studied area; exploiting asynchronicity to improve efficiency and the privacy–utility trade-off is worth exploring. (R1, R2)
- **Interesting idea linking staleness to privacy levels:** The proposal to connect update staleness with different privacy budgets in an LDP context is conceptually interesting. (R4)
- **Clean presentation:** Diagrams and tables are clear, and the paper is easy to follow despite its brevity. (R2, R4)
- **Results in a limited but concrete setting:** Studying sample-level DP as a substitute for user-level DP yields interesting results, but these apply only when each client has many samples—a condition that often does not hold in practical FL scenarios. One reviewer found this contribution positive (R3, score 5), while others considered it insufficient for a top venue.

### Weaknesses

- **Minimal novelty:** The algorithm is a straightforward combination of FedBuff with basic Gaussian noise addition on the client side. Multiple reviewers note no technical challenge, no mathematical contribution, and that such sample-level DP is already implemented in many open-source FL libraries. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Missing LDP proof and definition mismatch:** The paper defines LDP as pure ε-DP (Section 2) but applies Gaussian noise, which inherently provides only (ε,δ)-DP. Moreover, the paper does **not** prove that Algorithm 1 satisfies the stated LDP condition; it merely cites prior work, which is insufficient. (R2, R3)
- **Unjustified staleness claim:** The claim that staleness can improve privacy or utility is not supported. Reweighting a perturbed update does not change the signal-to-noise ratio (R1). No theoretical analysis of how privacy loss evolves with staleness or what hypotheses are needed for gains is provided. (R1, R4)
- **Incomplete related work:** The paper does not fully cover prior FL methods that incorporate LDP. In particular, sample-level DP has already been implemented in open-source FL libraries, yet this is not discussed. (R2, R3)
- **Limited experiments:** Only CIFAR-10 and CINIC-10 are used; no comparison to relevant baselines (e.g., FedBuff without DP, other asynchronous algorithms) is provided. Results lack confidence intervals (only one run?). No supplementary material with proofs or additional experimental details is given. (R2, R4)
- **User-level vs. sample-level DP confusion:** The paper compares its sample-level DP approach to central DP without clarifying that the privacy protection and attack surfaces are fundamentally different, making the comparison invalid. (R3)
- **Unclear definitions:** Terms such as staleness τ_i are not formally defined. Equation (6) originates from [15] but its weighting adaptation for non-uniform device sampling is unexplained. (R2)
- **Missing composition analysis:** The paper applies noise in multiple steps per client but does not consider composition to calculate the final ε. (R3)
