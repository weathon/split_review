## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DNA-GPT, a training-free detection method for distinguishing human-written from machine-generated text. It truncates text, regenerates the remainder using an LLM, then compares original and regenerated parts via N-gram analysis (black-box) or probability divergence (white-box). Experiments on GPT-4, GPT-3.5-turbo, text-davinci-003, GPT-NeoX-20B, LLaMa-13B across four English and one German dataset show state-of-the-art performance, surpassing OpenAI’s own classifier. The method also provides explainable evidence and handles revised-text attacks and model sourcing.

### Strengths
- Simple, training-free approach that avoids needing millions of training texts. (R1, R2, R3)
- Strong empirical results, outperforming existing detection methods (e.g., GPTZero, OpenAI classifier) on multiple LLMs and datasets. (R1, R2)
- Original mathematical framework based on N-gram analysis and probability divergence. (R2)
- Well-written, easy-to-follow, claims follow clearly from experiments. (R1, R2)
- Provides explainable detection evidence (N-gram overlaps); addresses revised-text attacks and model sourcing. (R2, R1)
- The “one simple trick” insight gives an aha moment for an important, practical problem. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear term selection in BScore**: How is the function f(n) in BScore chosen? (R1)
- **Unclear calculation for human text**: How is P(Y_0 | X) computed when Y_0 is human-written? Does text-davinci-003 allow such calculation? (R1)
- **Likelihood-gap hypothesis basis**: The framework relies on the likelihood-gap hypothesis, but the paper does not formally acknowledge its basis in the literature or empirically verify it (e.g., from Fig 2). (R2)
- **Missing examples of n-gram evidence**: The claim that the method provides explanations/evidence would benefit from concrete examples. (R2)
- **Theoretical issue with Delta**: Delta is shown to be lower bounded but not upper bounded, which weakens the justification for using KL divergence / total variation. (R3) – Note: The rebuttal clarified R3’s misunderstanding about the logic flow; however, the issue of missing upper bound remains a concern about execution thoroughness.
- **Unknown model limitation**: The paper only tests a specific set of 5–10 models; in practice, one often does not know which model generated the text, yet the method assumes the generating model is known or among the tested set. (R3)
- **Lack of discussion on KL vs. likelihood-gap estimation**: The paper shifts from LLH gap to KL divergence then back to LLH gap; it should discuss why KL estimation is harder (e.g., smaller sample from human distribution) and how this relates to the method. (R3, after rebuttal clarification)
- **General feeling of insufficient thoroughness**: The problem is not studied thoroughly; e.g., could a stronger theoretical result be built (e.g., any LM trained with certain mechanisms always exhibits the likelihood-gap hypothesis)? (R3)

*Reviewer disagreement*: Reviewer 1 is strongly positive (score 8), while Reviewers 2 and 3 are positive but with moderate reservations (score 6). All three agree on the method’s novelty and effectiveness, but R2 and R3 raise more concerns about theoretical grounding and practical scope.
