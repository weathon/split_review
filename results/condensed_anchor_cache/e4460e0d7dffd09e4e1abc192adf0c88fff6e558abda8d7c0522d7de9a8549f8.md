## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces TFG-Flow, a training-free guidance method for multimodal generative flow models applied to molecular design. The method uses a predictor for a target property to guide generation without additional training, with theoretical contributions and experiments on four molecular design tasks.

### Strengths
- Training-free guidance makes the approach computationally advantageous over other flow-based molecular generation models (Reviewer 1).
- The problem of conditional generation is important and under-studied in the molecular generation context (Reviewer 2).
- The proposed method is straightforward and effective based on experimental validations (Reviewer 2).
- The paper is well-written with clear problem setup, motivation, and contribution description; theoretical contributions are precisely stated and proven (Reviewer 3).
- Encouraging experimental results across several tasks with transparency about limitations; code provided for reproducibility (Reviewers 3, 4).
- Strong formal framework with broad applicability beyond molecular examples (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Incomplete explanation of the multimodal flow model (Reviewers 1, 3):**
  * How is \(p_{1|t}\) parameterized (e.g., GAN, NF, VAE)? The notation “sample/likelihood” for \(g\) is ambiguous—does it return both sample and likelihood, restricting model choices?
  * If sampling from \(p_{1|t}\) is efficient, why not directly sample from \(p_{1|0}\)? Why not generate a collection of \(G_1\sim p_{1|t}(G_1|G_t)\) and pick the one with highest property predictor \(f\)? This baseline is missing.
  * The expectation under \(p_{1|t}\) (Eqs. 7,9,10) is used to estimate marginal velocities; it is unclear if Monte Carlo estimation is used, and if so, the number of samples and resulting variance are not reported.
- **Missing computational efficiency comparison:** Tables 1, 2, and 4 do not report model sizes, training runtime, or inference runtime; this hinders evaluation of the method’s claimed efficiency (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Missing comparison with optimization-based SBDD methods:** Table 4 should compare with methods like Autogrow4, as TFG-Flow is optimization-based rather than purely generative (Reviewers 1, 3; see also Reviewer 2: evolutionary algorithms in Schneuing et al. 2022).
- The paper’s main claim is “training-free,” but the benchmarks where results stand out use models trained for guidance (Reviewer 4).
- Conditional generation guidance is well-studied in other domains (e.g., Lu et al. 2023, Wu et al. 2024, Zhao et al. 2024) but not discussed; molecular optimization has many existing methods not compared (review by Du et al. 2024) (Reviewer 2).
- **Theoretical concerns (Reviewer 2):**
  * Guidance may not be exact: Eq. 15 appears to omit the original vector field (only guidance term). In diffusion, evaluating \(\nabla E(\mathbb{E}_{X_1|X_t}(X_1))\) is not exact.
  * Eq. 15 notation: should be \(X_{t+1}\leftarrow X_t+\dots\)? Rate matrix \(R\) not properly defined.
  * In Sec. 3.2, why does the expectation grow exponentially? Modeling per-atom rate matrix suggests order \(n|A|\).
  * Is the Monte Carlo estimator for Eq. 12 biased?
- **Presentation and clarity issues:**
  * Table 1 bold/highlighting is misleading: TFG-Flow is not best overall (e.g., worse than EEGSDE) unless categorized separately (Reviewer 3).
  * Lines 470-473: confusion about what is trained (drug quality prediction network) vs. what does not need training (oracle target predictor) (Reviewer 3).
  * Line 479: target definition matches quality to reference instead of maximizing it; why not maximize? (Reviewer 3).
  * Line 1499: assumption is incorrect, needs clarification (Reviewer 3).
  * Typo in Table 4: Vina score for TFG-Flow is falsely bolded (Reviewer 1).
  * Presentation is overly compact (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing practical details (Reviewer 1):**
  * How is hyperparameter \(\alpha_t\) chosen/tuned?
  * How does predictor quality/accuracy impact overall performance?
- **Theorem 3.4 estimator variance:** How does variance grow when using \(k<K\) Monte Carlo samples? Can the estimator be improved for other applications? (Reviewer 4).
