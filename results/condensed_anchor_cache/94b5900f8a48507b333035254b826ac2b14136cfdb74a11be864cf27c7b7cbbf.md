## Merged Review

### Summary
Frag2Seq uses fragment-based SE(3)-invariant tokenization to convert 3D molecules into sequences for LM-based generation in SBDD. It incorporates protein pocket embeddings (ESM-IF1) via cross-attention. Experiments on CrossDocked show strong performance on Vina score, QED, Lipinski, and up to 300× speedup. One minority reviewer (score 3) finds it underperforms IPDiff on binding affinity and lacks novelty; other reviewers (scores 8,6,6) highlight competitive results and efficiency.

### Strengths
- Effectively incorporates protein context via cross-attention with pre-trained protein embeddings (ESM-IF1), enabling target-aware generation (R1, R2, R4).
- SE(3)-invariant tokenization preserves geometric information; proofs of structural completeness and invariance are provided (R1, R3). Innovative use of canonical SMILES ordering for 3D-isomorphic molecules (R4).
- Strong performance on binding affinity (Vina score) and drug-likeness (QED, Lipinski) over baselines (R1, R4). *One reviewer notes IPDiff achieves higher Vina scores (R2).*
- High sampling efficiency: up to 300× speedup versus atom-based autoregressive and diffusion models (R1, R2, R4).
- Comprehensive baselines and sufficient ablations/analysis (R3).
- Fragment-based generation reduces computational complexity (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty:** Model structure closely resembles IPDiff, with the only change being diffusion replaced by a language model; SE(3)-invariant frames and cross-attention are common approaches, making the contribution incremental (R2, R3, R4). A minority reviewer (R2) considered this a severe limitation.
- **Insufficient binding affinity accuracy (minority view):** One reviewer (R2) points out the model underperforms compared to IPDiff on Vina scores and lacks a direct accuracy comparison with IPDiff on CrossDocked2020. *Other reviewers highlight strong binding affinity results.*
- **Missing critical comparative analysis:** No direct accuracy comparison with state-of-the-art models like IPDiff; more detailed results on binding affinity and molecular quality are needed (R2). Unclear why text-based representation and LM generation is needed for SBDD; choice of LM over 3D atom-based diffusion models should be justified with theoretical and practical benefits (R3, R4).
- **Quantization and bond inference:** Quantizing continuous structural information into discrete tokens causes precision loss (R4). The LM cannot directly generate bond information, requiring an additional model for bond inference; reliability of this model is not validated, and it is unclear if baseline models require similar inference (R4).
- **Fragmentation robustness:** Fragmentation by rotatable bonds may limit flexibility in positioning critical binding groups; robustness of fragmentation rules for structurally complex molecules is unclear (R1). Using canonical SMILES as fragment key raises question: would different conformations of the same fragment be represented by the same key? (R3).
- **Interpretability:** Scoring mechanism needs clarity on how fragment characteristics (size, functional groups) influence metrics like Vina scores; an attention map visualization between protein pocket and molecule would improve interpretability (R1).
- **Protein context integration:** The paper should compare alternate strategies for incorporating protein embeddings (R3). Direct cross-attention between entire sequences may be simplistic; experiments should demonstrate it effectively captures binding pocket information (R4). Consider integrating an interaction prior mechanism similar to IPDiff within the diffusion model to enhance accuracy (R2).
