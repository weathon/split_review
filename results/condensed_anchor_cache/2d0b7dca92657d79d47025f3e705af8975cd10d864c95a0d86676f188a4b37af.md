## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MediConfusion, a benchmark of 352 confusing medical image pairs (from the ROCO radiology dataset) designed to probe failure modes of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) in visual question answering. Each pair consists of images that are visually distinct to medical experts yet similar in feature space (using DINO and BiomedCLIP similarity). Questions were generated via GPT-4 and validated by a radiologist. All tested models (open-source and proprietary) perform below random guessing, exposing serious reliability issues. Common failure patterns include difficulties in differentiating normal vs. pathological anatomy, lesion characteristics, medical devices, and vascular conditions.

### Strengths
- **Novel focus on failure modes**: The benchmark systematically probes visual reasoning weaknesses of medical MLLMs, moving beyond typical accuracy-focused evaluations. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Rigorous dataset creation**: Automated similarity filtering combined with expert radiologist oversight ensures clinical relevance and accuracy of question-answer pairs. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: Extensive testing of diverse state-of-the-art MLLMs (general and medical-domain) with clear baselines, and metrics that accommodate models unable to answer multiple-choice questions directly. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Strikingly strong results**: The finding that all models perform at or below random guessing is impactful and highlights an urgent need for improvement. (Reviewer 4)
- **Actionable error analysis**: Detailed breakdown of common failure patterns (spatial reasoning, lesion characteristics, etc.) provides clear directions for future model improvements. (Reviewer 1)
- **Well-motivated and relevant**: Addresses an underexplored problem in a safety-critical domain with potential to become a standard evaluation tool. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Clear presentation**: Well-structured with helpful visual aids; writing is generally easy to follow. (Reviewers 1, 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited dataset size and diversity**: Only 352 questions across 9 categories, derived solely from the ROCO radiology dataset. This may be insufficient to capture the full spectrum of medical image complexity and diagnostic tasks. Expansion to other modalities (ultrasound, nuclear imaging, etc.) is needed. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Closely follows existing general-domain frameworks** (e.g., MMVP). The methodology – using feature similarity to find confusing pairs – is not new, and many findings align with known VLM challenges. The novelty contribution is modest. (Reviewer 2)
- **Reliance on feature similarity metrics (DINO, BiomedCLIP)** does not fully capture nuances of medical image interpretation. There may exist confusing pairs that are visually similar but differ in feature space – this direction is not explored. (Reviewer 2)
- **Potential biases in the selection process** and lack of diverse imaging modalities beyond those common in existing datasets may limit generalizability of the findings. (Reviewer 2)
- **Motivation and innovation are not clearly articulated**: The claim that existing benchmarks are “biased towards common/typical scenarios” is vague; the paper does not explain what these scenarios are or how MediConfusion overcomes them. The specific design choices for the medical domain – beyond using radiology images – are not justified. Why are two similar medical images different from two similar general images? (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing diagnostic experiments**: The paper reports below-random performance but does not analyze why. Varying similarity thresholds (e.g., *sim_med*, *sim_gen*) to test whether models can distinguish “easy” pairs would clarify if models fail on all pairs or only the hardest ones. (Reviewer 3)
- **Questionable task format**: The decision to frame the benchmark as multiple-choice questions is not justified. Open-ended VQA or report generation could be more realistic. (Reviewer 3)
- **No demonstration of improvement**: The paper does not train or fine-tune any model on MediConfusion to show that incorporating the benchmark can improve reliability. Such an experiment would strengthen the paper’s impact. (Reviewer 4)
- **Lack of discussion on image encoders**: The poor performance of LLaVA-med (compared to LLaVA) may be due to using encoders trained on everyday images. The role of specialized medical image encoders is not discussed, missing a key insight for model improvement. (Reviewer 4)
- **Writing clarity issues**: Phrases like “stress-tests the visual capabilities” are unclear. The introduction does not clarify that the benchmark uses image pairs or that the task is multiple-choice until later sections, making the motivation hard to follow. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing experiment on prompt engineering**: It is unclear whether alternative prompt formats or framing could improve model performance; this is relevant for assessing reliability. (Reviewer 1)
