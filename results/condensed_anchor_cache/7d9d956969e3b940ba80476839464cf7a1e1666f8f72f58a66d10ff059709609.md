## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates the compatibility of Mamba state-space models (SSMs) with mixed-precision fine-tuning (MPFT) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT). Using dynamical systems theory (Lyapunov exponents), the authors theoretically prove that Mamba’s recurrent dynamics are robust to small input perturbations. They empirically validate: (1) divergence in accuracy between FP32 and FP16/BF16 inference is lower for Mamba than for comparable Transformers (Pythia, OLMo); (2) Mamba fine-tuning with FP16/BF16 (full, LoRA on all/subset of linear layers) shows less MMLU accuracy divergence than similar-size transformers; (3) PEFT yields hardware throughput gains and reduced memory per token; (4) instruction tuning (Alpaca) narrows the in-context learning (ICL) gap between Mamba and Pythia (from 38% to 81% for Mamba; Mamba-2 goes from 82% to 132% of Transformer ICL improvements). The paper also claims Mamba exhibits emergent ICL abilities after instruction tuning.

### Strengths
- **Important problem addressed** – The work attacks a key barrier to wider SSM adoption: training resources have been higher than for Transformers because the latter are known to be compatible with efficient fine-tuning. Demonstrating compatibility brings SSMs within reach for more researchers. (Reviewer 2, rating 6, confidence 4)
- **Combined theory and empirics** – The theoretical results (Lyapunov exponents) are simple, elegant, and important; proofs in the appendix are easy to follow and solid (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 also finds the theoretical analysis solid. (Reviewers 1,2)
- **Good experimental setup** – Care was taken to use the same batch size across models and to compare the Mamba model with Transformer-based models in comprehensive scenarios. (Reviewers 1,2)
- **Well-written and easy to follow** – The paper is clearly structured. (Reviewer 1)
- **Promising evidence for instruction tuning** – The experiments show that fine-tuning Mamba on instruction-tuning data can improve its ICL abilities. (Reviewer 3, rating 3, confidence 5)

### Weaknesses
- **No error bars or randomness quantification** – Empirical results (especially experiment 2/Figure 1, experiment 4/Figure 3 for fine-tuned models, experiment 5/Figure 4) lack any measure of spread or variability, undermining confidence in the comparisons. (Reviewer 2, confidence 4)
- **Single dataset per experiment** – Most experiments use only one dataset (MMLU for stability; Alpaca for fine-tuning). Experiment 5 averages results across multiple datasets but does not report them separately, preventing readers from assessing per-task performance. (Reviewer 2)
- **MMLU as proxy metric at small scale** – At the model sizes tested, MMLU scores are around random (~25%), making the metric’s interpretability limited. Only differential scores are provided without the original MMLU results, further weakening the claim. (Reviewer 3, confidence 5)
- **Learning rate sensitivity not explored** – The same learning rate is applied to both Mamba and Pythia when comparing training stability. A better-tuned Transformer might show enhanced stability; an ablation study or sensitivity analysis on learning rates is needed. (Reviewer 1, confidence 3)
- **Theoretical contribution contested** – One reviewer considers Lemma 1 trivial (it directly derives the decay rate from \(dx_{t+1}/dx_t\) for any recurrent model) and Theorem 1 redundant; similarly, Theory 2 (concatenating weight matrices with LoRA) has no practical advantage or empirical basis. (Reviewer 3, confidence 5)  *In contrast, Reviewer 2 finds the theoretical results “simple, elegant and important” and the proofs solid.*
- **Inconsistent choice of transformer baselines across experiments** – For example, OLMo appears only in the first experiment (Table 1), OpenELM in all experiments except the first, and different sets are used in main results vs. other experiments. The reasoning is not explained. (Reviewer 2, question converted; Reviewer 1 also would benefit from clearer justification of model selections)
- **Instruction-tuning baseline and claims** – Pythia is a weak baseline for Mamba ICL comparison. The claim that instruction-tuned Mamba exhibits “emergent ability” for ICL is questionable given known SSM limitations (citations [1,2]), and Figure 3 shows a performance drop for Mamba-2 as shot count increases, likely reflecting state‑space models’ well‑known difficulties with long sequences and ICL. (Reviewer 3, confidence 5)
- **Missing concrete numbers in tables** – The graphs (Figures 1–4) are visually helpful, but the actual numerical results should be provided in tables in the appendix for precise reference. (Reviewer 2)
- **Overly long contextualization** – Section 2 (background) and Section 5 (related work) both function as related‑work sections, taking space that could be better used for more detailed empirical analysis. Some content should be moved to the appendix. (Reviewer 2)
- **Cross‑figure reference errors** – Line 345 refers to “Figure 5” but likely means Figure 1; line 379 refers to “Figure 5” but likely means Figure 2; Appendix Theorem 3 corresponds to main‑paper Theorem 2. (Reviewer 2)
- **Introduction lacks a clear contributions summary** – The paper would benefit from listing contributions explicitly at the end of the introduction to help the reader assess impact. (Reviewer 1)
- **Minor concern about MPFT novelty** – For some training frameworks that do not keep master weights in FP32, Mamba may already have been trained with mixed precision (AMP), so the claimed contribution regarding MPFT compatibility may be less novel than presented. (Reviewer 3, confidence 5)

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* There is significant disagreement on the value of the theoretical analysis (Reviewer 3 finds it shallow and redundant, while Reviewer 2 and Reviewer 1 find it solid and elegant). Similarly, the empirical evaluation is viewed as important but limited by Reviewer 2, whereas Reviewer 3 considers the baselines and metrics weak. The overall scores (5, 6, 3) reflect these divergent opinions.
