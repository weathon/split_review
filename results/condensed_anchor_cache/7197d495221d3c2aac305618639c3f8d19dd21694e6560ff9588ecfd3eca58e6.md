## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Sub-treatment Group Alignment (SGA) and Random Temporal Masking (RTM) for counterfactual estimation in time series. SGA identifies sub-treatment groups in representation space via Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs) and aligns corresponding sub-groups across treatment groups. RTM replaces input covariates at randomly selected time steps with Gaussian noise during training. Experiments on synthetic and semi-synthetic datasets (tumor growth PK/PD, MIMIC-III) show improvements over baselines. Reviewers generally find the problem important and the methods clearly described, but raise concerns about experiment validity, GMM assumptions, theoretical justification, missing ablations, and applicability limitations. One reviewer (score 3) is more critical on theoretical and practical aspects.

### Strengths
- The studied problem (counterfactual estimation in time series) is important and interesting.
- The core idea is clearly explained and easy to follow; code is provided for reproducibility.
- The paper is well written and theoretically sound, with proofs for all statements in the appendix.
- The approach is a universal methodology applicable to various existing models; applying it to CRN and CT improves outcomes (Tables 1, 2, 3).
- Experimental results support the method claims in the majority of setups.
- The research method is scientific, reasonable, and innovative; the logical structure is strict and conveys intentions clearly.

### Weaknesses
- **Experiment validity**: Tables 2 and 3 report only a single run, but both the data-generating processes (Appendix F) and the random masking algorithm (Appendix E) involve random sampling. Multiple runs are needed to assess variability and robustness under different sampling/mask ratios.
- **Missing ablations**: Tables 1 and 2 lack results for the combined SGA+RTM; Table 3 lacks results for +SGA and +RTM individually. Synergy between SGA and RTM is not demonstrated. One reviewer notes that if no synergy exists, the “time-series” title may be misleading since SGA alone could apply to non-time-series data.
- **GMM assumptions and limitations**: The framework relies on GMM; the paper should discuss whether this is a restrictive assumption and why GMM was chosen over other clustering algorithms (e.g., mixture of factor analyzers). For clinical data with categorical/binary covariates, covariance matrices of mixture components may collapse, and alternatives may be needed. In higher dimensions, it is unclear how sub-treatment groups from different GMMs (which can yield different results with random initial values) can be uniquely aligned; the uniform mixture calculation in Equations (Lines 377, 1035) may not guarantee consistent optimized results across runs.
- **Theoretical concerns**: Theorem 4.2 includes a small constant δ_c in assumption A2, so the weighted sum on the left is not always smaller than the W1 distance between full distributions. Additional conditions are needed to guarantee a tighter upper bound; an intuitive explanation is requested. The theorem proving for RTM is insufficient.
- **RTM assumptions and applicability**: RTM assumes continuity in time-series data (e.g., tumor growth). Its effectiveness for short, non-dense sequences (e.g., 12-week biweekly clinical trials) is unclear; the paper should specify data characteristics where RTM is effective.
- **Paper organization**: The main text requires referring to the Appendix to be complete, effectively exceeding the 9-page limit. Essential content should be in the main paper.
- **Clarifications needed**:
  - Figure 1: Is there a specific frequency for applying SGA and RTM? Why is RTM applied only at T=2 and T=5?
  - Distinction between sub-treatment group index k and treatment group a: Is k simply an index within each treatment group? Is the framework restricted to categorical treatments? How would it handle continuous treatments?
  - How is the number of sub-treatment groups determined (e.g., cross-validation)? This is not found in Tables 4/5 Appendix.
  - What is the ratio of random mask in the experiments? Does it impact performance?
  - How does the method get back from the sub-treatment group alignment to the representation space? What are the GMM coefficients in Figure 1?
  - Why were CRN and CT chosen as base models for applying SGA/RTM instead of other benchmarking methods?
- **Figure 3(b)**: The Normalized RMSE of RMSNs is similar to CT+SGA+RTM and CRN+SGA+RTM when γ=4. Higher γ values should be tested and the results explained.
- **Future work**: No direction for future research is provided; some guidance should be added.

*Note: One reviewer (score 3) is more critical on the theoretical justification (Theorem 4.2), GMM consistency in higher dimensions, RTM assumptions, synergy, and categorical data handling; these points are highlighted above.*
