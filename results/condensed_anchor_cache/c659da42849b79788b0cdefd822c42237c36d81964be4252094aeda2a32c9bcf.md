## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper applies Contrastive Decoding (CD, Li et al. 2022) – an inference-time method that searches for sequences maximizing a weighted likelihood difference between a strong and a weak (amateur) model – to reasoning tasks. The authors reformulate CD with a hyperparameter β controlling the contrastive penalty and evaluate on arithmetic word problems (GSM8K, MATH), commonsense reasoning (e.g., HellaSwag, WinoGrande), and multiple-choice reasoning. CD improves over greedy decoding on several tasks (e.g., GSM8K, HellaSwag with large models) but yields inconsistent results – it degrades performance on MATH, shows small or negative gains on commonsense reasoning with smaller models, and works reliably only with chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting. Ablations explore sensitivity to β, amateur model choice (weak vs. strong, earlier checkpoints), and provide qualitative analysis of generation patterns (e.g., reducing copying from the prompt). Two reviewers find the experimental scope and analysis valuable; one reviewer is more critical of the novelty and results.

### Strengths
- Refactoring the original Contrastive Decoding formulation into logit space is a nice idea (Reviewer 1).
- Exhaustive experimental results across arithmetic, commonsense reasoning, and ranking tasks, including parameter analysis and ablation studies on the factors affecting CD performance (β, amateur model type, negative prompting, earlier checkpoints) (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Good analytical section: interpreting gains/losses from CD compared to greedy decoding, especially the finding that CD reduces copying from the prompt and avoids some abstract reasoning errors (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 agrees that the ablations show conditions where CD works better or worse.
- The paper is well written overall; the general purpose and conclusions are clearly presented (Reviewer 2).
- Demonstrates that CD can improve over greedy decoding on some reasoning tasks and identifies key sensitivities (e.g., stronger amateur models hurt performance, CD works better with CoT) (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty:** The primary contribution is the application of an existing method (Contrastive Decoding, Li et al. 2022) to additional datasets without introducing innovative or original elements. Changes to the original formulation are minor (Reviewers 1, 2). Reviewer 1 rates contribution as poor; Reviewers 2 and 3 rate it fair to good but acknowledge limited novelty.
- **Missing baseline:** The method is very similar to Coherence Boosting (Malkin et al. 2022), which is not mentioned in related work and should be compared as a baseline (Reviewer 1).
- **Inconsistent results:** CD yields highly variable performance. It improves on GSM8K but degrades on MATH; yields small gains on commonsense reasoning only with large models and specific tasks (e.g., HellaSwag) while harming performance on tasks like WinoGrande; does not help on multiple-choice tasks without CoT. It is unclear when to use CD effectively (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Limited analysis of failures:** The paper lacks a detailed error analysis (e.g., wins/losses on specific examples), especially for tasks where CD performs worse (e.g., MATH). Providing samples and patterns of errors would strengthen the understanding (Reviewer 3).
- **Only works with chain-of-thought prompting:** CD shows improvement only when CoT prompts are used. This is a significant limitation; it is unclear whether this is due to task nature or an inherent limitation of CD. More ablation to understand why CD does not help without CoT is needed (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Limited model diversity:** Almost all experiments use LLaMA models (65B, 13B, 7B), with only one experiment on FLAN-T5. This makes it unclear whether CD’s behavior generalizes to other pretrained models (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 also suggests studying smaller amateur models and using cross-family strong/weak models.
- **Presentation and clarity issues:** (a) Section 2.1 should first recall the original Contrastive Decoding work before introducing the revised formulation – the current presentation requires consulting the original paper to understand the α-mask and logit-subtraction logic (Reviewer 2). (b) Notation should be clarified: α ∈ [0,1] (i.e., log α < 0) (Reviewer 3). (c) Figure 4: “GSM 8K EM” is undefined; Table 1 does not specify the metric shown (Reviewer 3). (d) Section on “CD reduces copying from the prompt” needs statistics from the experiment for both baseline and CD models (Reviewer 3). (e) The derivation that the distribution in Equation 1 collapses to the argmax of p_e/p_a as β → ∞ is not obvious; steps should be shown in the appendix (Reviewer 3). (f) Figure 1 could benefit from an “Average” row at the top (at least for greedy generations) (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing experiments:** (a) Study the effect of using smaller amateur models (e.g., from different families or mid-training checkpoints) (Reviewer 1). (b) Explore whether CD could be used during training itself (Reviewer 2). (c) In the FLOP comparison with self-consistency (Fig.9), self-consistency has no hyperparameter; the hyperparameter search cost for CD should be factored into the FLOP increase (Reviewer 1).
