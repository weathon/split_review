## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Partial-LLM Detector (PaLD), a black-box method for the mixed-text scenario where individual segments (e.g., sentences) may be written by either a human or an LLM. PaLD addresses two tasks: estimating the percentage of LLM-written text (PALD-PE via MAP estimation with Gaussian KDE confidence intervals) and identifying which segments are LLM-written (PALD-TI via combinatorial search using classifier scores, with a greedy variant). Experiments are conducted on the Writing Prompts and Yelp datasets with GPT-4o infilling. Reviewers note this departs from traditional binary detection and is an important innovation, but raise concerns about experimental scope, methodological assumptions, and clarity.

### Strengths
- **Important and realistic problem**: The paper is one of the first to address the mixed-text scenario (estimating percentage and identifying segments), moving beyond binary human/LLM classification. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Rigorous theoretical and statistical foundation**: In-depth formulation with Bayesian framework, MAP estimation, and KDE; provides confidence intervals and likelihood measures. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Informative ablations**: PALD-PE is thoroughly ablated, showing that methods with higher quantile slope are more useful in mixed-text settings. (R1)
- **Well-structured and clear presentation**: Figures are well-crafted, and the paper is overall well-written. (R3, R4)
- **Comprehensive experiments**: The paper includes both percentage estimation and segment identification tasks, with multiple baselines and evaluation metrics. (R4)
- **Handles texts of mixed origin**: The method offers a more nuanced detection capability suitable for realistic scenarios. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental domains and datasets**: Experiments are only on Writing Prompts and Yelp (two datasets). This is insufficient to validate generalizability. Reviewers suggest including more diverse and realistic datasets such as RoFT, RAID, and M4. (R1 [high confidence], R2, R3, R4)
- **Restricted LLM and generation diversity**: Only GPT-4o is used for infilling. Non-instruction-tuned LLMs and other models should be tested to assess robustness across generation styles. (R1)
- **Unfair comparison between PALD-TI and unsupervised baselines**: PALD-TI uses a supervised classifier (e.g., fine-tuned RoBERTa) while DetectGPT and FastDetectGPT are unsupervised. A zero-shot variant of PALD-TI (e.g., using an unsupervised T-score) is needed for fair comparison. (R1)
- **Baseline implementation issues**: 
  - Fine-tuned RoBERTa baselines were trained on paragraph-level text but tested on individual sentences, creating a distribution mismatch that likely deflates their performance. (R1)
  - Thresholds for DetectGPT and FastDetectGPT were not tuned on a validation set; a fixed threshold may not be optimal for segment-level identification. (R1)
- **Dependence on accurate segmentation**: The method assumes each segment is either entirely human or LLM-generated and uses predetermined segmentation (e.g., sentences). It does not evaluate performance under mismatched segmentation (e.g., boundaries not aligning with actual human/LLM parts) or in cases where segments contain mixed content. (R2, R4)
- **No robustness analysis**: The paper lacks sensitivity analysis regarding segment lengths, adversarial perturbations, or attacks aimed at undermining detection. (R3, R4) Also, analysis of false positives and false negatives (misclassification causes) is missing. (R4)
- **Computational complexity**: PALD-TI’s combinatorial search over 2^n – 2 segments is expensive; the greedy approximation is noted but its approximation errors are not discussed. (R1, R2, R3 – R3 considers this minor and encourages solutions to hard problems; others see it as a significant limitation)
- **Clarity and notation issues**: Several terms (e.g., “robust” in detection context, “Bayesian framework” without references, undefined χ) need better explanation. The determination of number of decomposed segments and whether parameters η, δ are segment-specific or global is unclear. (R2) KDE bandwidth and T-score threshold selection lack detailed justification. (R4)
- **Limited cross-domain evaluation**: Cross-domain experiments use only two domains (WP and Yelp), and no analysis of correlation between domains is provided. More distinct writing styles could show drastic performance drops. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Missing challenging cases**: The paper does not test scenarios such as humans mimicking LLM style or LLMs imitating highly stylized human writing. (R2)
- **Edge case evaluation**: It is unclear how PALD-PE and PALD-TI perform when there are no machine segments or only machine segments. (R1)
- **Related work section could be improved**: Should review existing studies on partially LLM-written text detection (which are few) and reduce coverage of watermarking methods (less relevant). (R3)
- **Minor typos**: “performnace” (line 297) and possibly notation “X_i” instead of “X” (line 191). (R1)
