## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies first-order methods for solving variational inequalities (VIs) with general constraints (linear equality and convex inequality constraints). The authors analyze the ACVI algorithm (from Yang et al., 2023) and make three main contributions: (1) proving last-iterate convergence of ACVI at a rate of \(\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{K})\) under only monotonicity (plus either strict monotonicity or strict convexity of a constraint), removing the earlier need for an \(L\)-Lipschitz operator assumption; (2) introducing an inexact ACVI variant that uses warm-starting and approximate subproblem solvers, with the same \(\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{K})\) rate when errors decrease appropriately; (3) for “simple” inequality constraints (fast to project), presenting a simplified ACVI variant with similar guarantees. Experiments on synthetic bilinear games and GANs show that inexact ACVI converges faster than exact ACVI and the lookahead-Minmax algorithm.

### Strengths
- Proves the same \(\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{K})\) last-iterate convergence guarantee for ACVI under strictly weaker assumptions (monotonicity instead of Lipschitz + monotonicity). A new analysis technique is developed to remove the Lipschitz requirement (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- Establishes convergence for both exact ACVI and an inexact ACVI with warm-starting, providing practical guidance on how subproblem accuracy can be traded off (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- Considers the special case of constraints that are easy to project onto, offering a simplified variant with the same theoretical guarantees (Reviewer 4).
- The paper is well-written and clearly motivated, drawing attention to the differences between VIs and standard optimization (e.g., limit-cycle behavior, monotonicity as convexity analogue) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- The algebraic proofs in Appendix C are thorough and valuable as a resource for others working on equilibrium-solving (Reviewer 2).
- Experiments on bilinear games and GANs show inexact ACVI outperforming lookahead-Minmax by implicitly solving linear systems in early steps (Reviewer 4).
- Reviewer 2, the most positive (score 8), emphasizes the importance of equilibrium-solving algorithms and the underdeveloped state of the field, calling the paper a welcome contribution.

### Weaknesses
- **Comparison with Yang et al. (2023) is insufficiently clear.** The last-iterate convergence metric (gap function + difference of primal iterates) differs from the optimality gap used by Yang et al., making direct comparisons difficult (Reviewer 1). The paper does not explicitly highlight the key differences in proof strategies, especially where the Lipschitz assumption is circumvented. Reviewer 3 states that many steps are similar and struggles to see where the main improvements occurred, despite the claim of a new analysis technique.
- **Limited experimental validation.** Experiments are restricted to synthetic bilinear games and GANs; no examples from computational game theory, multi-agent reinforcement learning, or economics are provided (Reviewer 2). This does not fully showcase practical applicability in the diverse real-world scenarios where VIs arise (Reviewer 2). Additionally, no concrete examples of operators that lack Lipschitzness are given to illustrate the importance of the relaxation (Reviewer 1).
- **Figure 1 lacks a legend** (what are the yellow vs. blue lines? which is the initial point?) and the vector fields are too small to read (Reviewer 2). Font sizes in figures are small (Reviewer 2). Equation numbers are not consistently applied; labeling all equations would help with cross-reference (Reviewer 2). Notation is inconsistent (e.g., min/argmin sometimes italicized) (Reviewer 2).
- **The barrier map is undefined when first used in Algorithm 1**, making the algorithm difficult to understand and implement (Reviewer 4). The role of the barrier function in Theorem 3.2 is unclear—does it require one of the two specific barriers, and would others work? (Reviewer 2).
- **Computational cost of the x-subproblem is not analyzed.** The efficiency of the overall algorithm depends critically on solving this general nonlinear system, but no complexity analysis or discussion of practical solution methods is provided (Reviewer 4). Questions from Reviewers 1 and 2 about controlling subproblem errors and the computational complexity of inexact ACVI remain unanswered.
- **Monotonicity assumption is restrictive.** The method assumes \(F\) is monotone (or strictly monotone), which is violated in many applications, including GANs (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4). The paper does not discuss limitations of this assumption or the possibility of extensions to non-monotone settings (Reviewer 1). Furthermore, the assumptions of monotonicity on \(\mathcal{C}_=\) and strict monotonicity on \(\mathcal{C}\) require better explanation and justification for practical scenarios (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing comparisons with existing last-iterate convergence results** in constrained optimization literature (Reviewer 1).
- **Typographical issues:** On page 23, the first two displays are missing squares on the norm terms (Reviewer 3). In the appendix, “subproblems and definitions” should have capital letters (Reviewer 2).
