## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a single-loop Hessian-free algorithm (LV-HBA) for constrained bilevel optimization where the lower-level problem has coupling constraints. It introduces a smooth proximal Lagrangian value function to reformulate the problem into a single-level optimization with smooth constraints. Non-asymptotic convergence analysis is provided, relaxing the lower-level strong convexity assumption to convexity. Experiments on synthetic and practical tasks (hyperparameter selection, data hypercleaning) demonstrate performance. Reviewer opinions diverge: Reviewers 3 and 4 find the contributions significant and the theory solid (scores 8), while Reviewers 1 and 5 raise concerns about strong assumptions, unclear equivalences, and limited novelty (scores 5); Reviewer 2 (score 6) acknowledges the novelty but notes practical limitations.

### Strengths
1. **Novel Hessian-free, single-loop algorithm** – LV-HBA is the first single-loop Hessian-free algorithm for constrained BLO (R1, R2, R3, R4). It avoids computationally intensive Hessian calculations and is suitable for machine learning applications (R3).
2. **Relaxed lower-level assumptions** – The analysis removes the traditional strong convexity assumption on the lower-level problem, requiring only convexity, and accommodates non-singleton lower-level solutions (R1, R3, R4).
3. **Proximal Lagrangian value function reformulation** – The introduction of the smooth proximal Lagrangian value function and resulting single-level reformulation is a noteworthy contribution, potentially enabling other efficient methods for constrained BLO (R3, R5). When lower-level constraints are absent, the function may revert to the classical Moreau envelope (R3).
4. **Non-asymptotic convergence analysis** – The paper provides a convergence rate (best rate \(O(1/K^{1/4})\) from Theorem 3.1 when \(p=1/4\)) using a newly defined potential function with monotonically decreasing step sizes, demonstrating sufficient descent (R2, R3, R4, R5). The theoretical analysis is thorough and well-structured (R3).
5. **Well-written and organized** – The paper is easy to follow, with clear assumptions and problem settings (R1, R3). Numerical experiments on five machine learning tasks validate practical performance (R2, R4, R5).

### Weaknesses
1. **Overly strong or unclear assumptions**  
   - Assumption 3.1(i) (functions convex jointly in \((x,y)\)) is too strong; typically only convexity in the lower-level variable \(y\) is assumed (R5).  
   - Assumption 3.3(ii) (global Lipschitz constants \(L_{g_1}, L_{g_2}\)) can be stringent since these constants are often not known in advance (R2).  
   - The exact form of \(\rho_f\) (weak convexity constant) in Assumption 3.2 is unclear; the paper only states it may be smaller than \(L_f\), but in general one may only have \(\rho_f = L_f\), leaving the potential benefit vague (R1).  
   - No Lipschitz continuity assumption is made on the upper-level function \(F(x,y)\) (common in BLO works like Xiao et al. 2023b); the paper does not justify its absence (R1).

2. **Equivalence and stationarity measure concerns**  
   - The proof of Theorem A.1 only shows that the feasible sets of the original problem (1) and reformulation (4) are equivalent, but does not demonstrate equivalence of optimal solutions (R1).  
   - The stationarity measure defined via (16) is not clearly connected to the original bilevel problem, making its significance questionable (R5).  
   - The techniques in the convergence proofs lack novelty, as they rely directly on standard penalty method analyses (R5).

3. **Excessive hyperparameters and practical tuning difficulty**  
   - The algorithm depends on many hyperparameters (\(\alpha_k, \beta_k, \eta_k, \gamma, c_k\)) and the parameter \(r\) in the proximal set; despite theoretical ranges, their choice is not easy in practice (R1, R2, R4).  
   - It is unclear whether algorithm performance is significantly affected by the choice of these parameters (R2).  
   - No guidance is provided for selecting \(r\) in the reformulation or in experiments (R1, R2, R4).  
   - The use of plain gradient descent/ascent may be slow compared to momentum or higher-order methods in practice (R2).

4. **Missing comparisons and references**  
   - Table 1 incorrectly states that BVFSM requires the lower-level objective to be convex; it does not (R3).  
   - Relevant value-function-based approaches for constrained BLO (Fliege, Tin & Zemkoho 2021; Fischer, Zemkoho & Zhou 2022) are not discussed or compared (R3).  
   - The motivation would be strengthened by listing specific machine learning bilevel examples with nonsmooth or weakly convex lower-level functions (R4).

5. **Minor issues**  
   - Typo on page 8: \(\|y^k - y^*(x)\|\) should be \(\|y^k - y^*(x^k)\|\) (R3).  
   - The benefit of using the Moreau envelope instead of the optimal value function in the technical analysis is not explained (R5).  
   - When the lower-level constraints are absent (\(g=0\)), the relationship of the proximal Lagrangian value function to the augmented Lagrangian function is not discussed (R3).
