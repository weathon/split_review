## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Vlearn, an off-policy actor-critic method that learns only a state-value function (V) via a surrogate importance‑sampling loss derived by moving importance weights outside the squared Bellman error. The algorithm combines multiple components: twin V-networks, trust‑region policy updates (TRPL), importance‑weight clipping, target networks, and a replay buffer. Experiments on Gym (MuJoCo) and DeepMind Control Suite (DMC) tasks show strong results on high‑dimensional tasks (e.g., 38‑D DMC dog tasks) but weaker performance on simpler tasks (hopper, walker, cheetah). Reviewers broadly agree that the empirical results on high‑dim tasks are promising, but they raise significant concerns about the derivation’s clarity and theoretical justification, insufficient ablation studies, an obscured connection to prior work (Mahmood et al. 2014), and the difficulty of isolating the contribution of the proposed value‑estimation objective from the many auxiliary techniques. One reviewer (Reviewer 3, confidence 5) is particularly critical of the mathematical rigor and contribution, while others (Reviewers 2 and 4, confidence 2–3) are more positive about the empirical strength but still identify substantial weaknesses.

### Strengths
- **Strong empirical performance on high‑dimensional tasks** – Vlearn achieves competitive or superior results on DMC dog tasks (38‑D) and several Gym tasks compared to a fair set of baselines, especially outperforming V‑trace. (Reviewers 1,2,4)
- **Clear motivation and comprehensive related work** – The paper clearly explains the problem (curse of dimensionality in action‑value functions) and credits relevant prior work. (Reviewer 1)
- **Extends on‑policy trust‑region methods to off‑policy setting** – Applying TRPL/PPO‑style updates to off‑policy learning is a meaningful step. (Reviewer 2)
- **Trust‑region stabilisation works well empirically** – The use of an explicit trust‑region constraint on policy updates helps training stability. (Reviewer 3)
- **Well‑written exposition** – The research question and narrative are easy to follow. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Derivation of the value‑loss function is unclear and potentially flawed** – The step from Equation (2) to Equation (4) using Jensen’s inequality is not explained. Reviewers question how the inequality is applied when importance ratios do not sum to one, what random variable the expectation is taken over, and how the summation over actions \(j\) is subsumed into the time‑step sum. Reviewer 3 (highly confident) finds the derivation unsound; others also note the lack of rigor. A step‑by‑step justification is missing. (Reviewers 1,3,4)
- **Notation problems and missing definitions** – Examples: \(K\) appears in the loss but is later defined as batch size; \(a_{t,j}\) and the sum over \(j\) are not clearly defined; the pseudocode uses “for i in {1,2}” ambiguously and introduces \(\theta\) (line 11) that is not part of the algorithm’s input. (Reviewers 1,4)
- **Claims of an “upper bound” and “same optima” are not justified** – The paper states that Equation (4) is an upper bound of the original Bellman error and shares the same minima, citing Neumann & Peters (2008). However, that proof is for on‑policy value estimation with a given Q‑function and does not directly apply to the off‑policy V‑learning setting with target networks. (Reviewer 3)
- **Algorithm combines many independent techniques; contribution of the value‑estimation objective is unclear** – Vlearn uses twin V‑networks, trust‑region projection, importance‑weight clipping, replay buffer, and target networks. The only ablation (replay‑buffer size) does not isolate the effect of the proposed loss. Reviewers request ablations that compare with/without the importance ratios in the loss or that use Monte Carlo estimates to verify whether the value estimates are more accurate. (Reviewers 1,3)
- **Weak performance on low‑dimensional tasks** – On simple environments (Hopper, Walker, Cheetah) Vlearn underperforms, raising questions about general applicability beyond high‑dimensional action spaces. (Reviewer 2)
- **Potential high‑variance importance ratios in high‑dimensional action spaces not addressed** – Although the policy is updated with a trust‑region constraint, the paper does not discuss how the importance sampling ratio \(\frac{\pi(a|s)}{\pi_b(a|s)}\) is reliably estimated in high‑dim spaces, nor does it analyse variance or propose mitigations beyond weight clipping. (Reviewer 2)
- **Twin V‑networks lack motivation** – The paper borrows double‑Q learning to combat overestimation bias, but overestimation bias is a known issue for Q‑functions, not V‑functions, so the rationale for twin V‑networks is unclear. (Reviewer 1)
- **Connection to prior work (Mahmood et al. 2014) is obscured** – The proposed loss (Equation 4) closely resembles the weighted importance‑sampling least‑squares objective of Mahmood et al. (linear case), extended to deep networks. The paper does not acknowledge this connection or clearly state any additional novel contributions beyond the extension. (Reviewer 4)
- **Ambiguous main contribution** – The conclusion claims “a novel approach applying trust region methods in the off‑policy setting,” but the paper also emphasises learning V instead of Q. It is not clear which is the primary contribution. (Reviewer 4)
- **Algorithmic details missing or inconsistent** – Algorithm 1 does not include the importance‑ratio truncation described in the text. (Reviewer 3)
- **Minor typos**: “Bellmann” → “Bellman”, “populate]”, “an novel approach” → “a novel approach”. (Reviewer 4)
