## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers agree that SelMix fine-tunes a pre-trained model for non-decomposable objectives by learning a selective sampling distribution over class pairs to perform mixup, with the distribution updated periodically from validation feedback. The method is evaluated in both supervised and semi-supervised settings on long-tailed benchmarks. Reviewers note strong empirical results but raise concerns about fair comparison baselines, missing ablations, theoretical rigor, and incomplete reporting of training time.

### Strengths
- **Simplicity, reasonableness, and theoretical guarantee:** Reviewer 1 highlights that SelMix is simple and reasonable with a theoretical guarantee. Reviewer 3 and Reviewer 4 also praise the theoretical analysis and convergence rate, and Reviewer 4 calls the theoretical analyses sufficient.
- **Strong and comprehensive experimental results:**  
  * Reviewer 2 details superior performance on matched label distributions (Table 2) across all metrics (mean recall, min recall, H-mean, G-mean, min coverage); on unknown label distributions (Figure 3); and on ImageNet-1k LT (Table 4).  
  * Reviewer 3 notes strong results across long-tailed benchmarks in both supervised and semi-supervised settings for different objectives.  
  * Reviewer 4 highlights comprehensive experiments under different supervision settings and distributions.
- **Efficiency through fine-tuning a pre-trained model:** Reviewer 3 emphasizes computational efficiency because the method only fine-tunes a pre-trained model, but Reviewer 2 notes that a detailed training-time comparison is missing (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient literature review and overclaims:** Reviewer 1 states that the core idea of optimizing a non-decomposable objective via a weighted sum of per-class gains has been explored in prior work (e.g., [1], [2]), and the paper should acknowledge this; the novelty lies in the specific mixup-based estimation. Lacks discussion of related “selective mixup” works [1–5] that also select specific pairs to mix (Reviewer 3).
- **Grammar errors:** Reviewer 1 points out multiple examples: “existing frameworks theoretical frameworks”, “Semi-Supervised Learning is are algorithms”, \(f: \mathbb{R}^{m \times n} \rightarrow n\), \(\rho_l=N_1/N_K, \rho_l=M_1/M_K\).
- **Unclear theoretical formalism:** Reviewer 1 finds theorems not formal with overly strong assumptions; \(z_k\) appears suddenly without explanation in Theorem 4.1 or in the appendix proof; “a reasonable directional vector for optimization” is vague. This contrasts with Reviewers 3 and 4 who considered the theoretical analysis a strength.
- **First-order Taylor approximation limitations:**  
  * Reviewer 1 questions whether first-order approximation on Eq. 5 can guarantee convergence for complex objectives, and asks about advantages over fitting a surrogate model.  
  * Reviewer 4 adds that neglecting the Hessian (curvature) may limit optimization, and suggests comparing with a two-sided approximation \(f(x+h)+f(x-h)-f(x)/(2h)\).
- **Missing training-time comparison:** Reviewer 2 notes that the paper only reports Gain calculation time in Appendix L; there is no comprehensive breakdown of total training time vs. existing methods, and pre-training vs. fine-tuning time is not separated.
- **Algorithm 1 clarity:** Reviewer 2 points out that Algorithm 1 says “update \(h\)” but Section 4.1 says “update \(W\)”; the function SGD-Update() obfuscates specific hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size) needed for reproducibility.
- **Unfair comparison with other methods:** Reviewer 3 observes that SelMix uses FixMatch + Logits Adjusted (LA) loss as its baseline, while competing methods use vanilla FixMatch. Tables H.2 and H.1 show LA alone already improves over vanilla FixMatch, making it hard to isolate SelMix’s contribution. A fair comparison would use vanilla FixMatch or upgrade all baselines to FixMatch + LA.
- **Missing definitions:** Reviewer 3 notes that the *prior distribution* \(\pi_i\) and the *unconstrained confusion matrix* \(\tilde{C}\) are never clearly defined, though they are crucial for defining the gain.
- **Missing hyperparameter ablations:** Reviewer 4 asks for ablation studies on the imbalance ratio and the number of samples used for validation feedback to understand sensitivity.
- **Lack of clarity on non-decomposable vs. non-differentiable objectives:** Reviewer 1 questions the distinction; the paper may conflate the two terms.
