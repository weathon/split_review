## Merged Review

### Summary
GeoX is a multimodal large language model for geometric problem solving (GPS). It uses unimodal pre-training (unsupervised autoencoder on a 120K geometry image dataset) to obtain a geometry-specific vision encoder (Geo-ViT) and symbol decoder, geometry-language alignment with formalized descriptions (instead of natural language), and a Generator-And-Sampler Transformer (GS-Former) to bridge the modality gap between diagrams and formal language. Instruction tuning allows GeoX to generate verifiable solutions. The model significantly reduces hallucinations compared to existing VLMs and outperforms both generalist and specialist baselines on GeoQA, UniGeo, Geometry3K, and PGPS9k. The approach provides interpretable formalized process descriptions.

### Strengths
1. **Novel design of visual sampler (GS-Former):** Simple yet effective; extracts more informative regions from geometric diagrams and substantially improves model performance. Attention maps confirm the model focuses on task‑relevant areas.
2. **Formal language over natural language:** Using formalized descriptions for geometry diagrams is an important contribution and significantly outperforms natural language in solving GPS tasks.
3. **Geometry-specific pre-training:** The unsupervised autoencoder for Geo-ViT and the curated 120K geometric image dataset are valuable resources for the community; no prior work has focused on a dedicated diagram encoder.
4. **Geometry-language alignment as fresh perspective:** This pre‑training paradigm effectively bridges the modality gap and provides a new direction for aligning geometric and semantic features.
5. **Strong empirical results:** Outperforms both generalists (e.g., InternVL2, QWENVL2) and geometry specialists by large margins on multiple benchmarks.
6. **Interpretability:** GeoX produces formalized process descriptions, enhancing both transparency and reasoning accuracy.
7. **Paper presentation:** Easy to follow with good‑quality figures.

### Weaknesses
1. **Novelty of the training pipeline:** The idea of separate math‑specific vision encoder and LLM training followed by alignment has already been introduced in EAGLE and MAVIS, so the overall pipeline is not entirely novel.
2. **Inconsistent experimental numbers:** On the Geometry3K dataset, the “Completion” score without GS‑Former is reported as 33.1 in Table 5 but as 55.0 in Table 6. The settings between tables must be clarified and made consistent.
3. **Missing ablation – vision encoder:** No comparison between the proposed Geo‑ViT encoder and the standard CLIP encoder to demonstrate Geo‑ViT’s effectiveness for geometric images.
4. **Missing ablation – overall architecture:** No ablation study on the architecture used for vision and language pre‑training, leaving unclear which components drive the reported gains.
5. **Narrow evaluation:** Performance on geometry problems from MathVista, We‑Math, MathScape, and geometric problems involving natural images is not reported. This raises concerns about generalization beyond math‑problem settings.
6. **Limited baseline comparisons:** The paper does not compare against recent state‑of‑the‑art multimodal LLMs such as InternLM‑Xcomposer, InternVL2, and QWENVL2 on the same benchmarks.
7. **Baseline LLaVA score seems unreliable:** LLaVA gets a Top‑1 score of 9.5 on GeoQA. It is unclear whether LLaVA was fine‑tuned on the GPS dataset, given that the original LLaVA fine‑tuning data lack GPS questions.
8. **Insufficient explanation of modality integration:** The paper does not adequately describe how geometric features are extracted, represented, and integrated with natural language in the framework. Specifics of the feature extraction process are missing.
9. **Missing resource / cost analysis:**
    - Computation time of the geometric solver is not reported.
    - No breakdown of parameters and computation costs in the performance comparison.
    - No strategies are discussed to alleviate the apparently resource‑intensive training and inference process.
10. **Metric justification missing:** The choice of metrics (All, Angle, Length) in Table 2 is not justified. Additional metrics may be needed for a comprehensive evaluation.
11. **Generalization to unseen data:** The paper does not discuss how well the model generalizes to unseen geometric structures or scenarios where performance is expected to decline.

*Note:* One reviewer (R1) criticizes the training‑pipeline novelty strongly; others (R2, R4) find the contributions substantial. R4 raised several resource‑related concerns but later increased their score after the rebuttal.
