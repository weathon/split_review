## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes ControlMM, a controllable text-to-motion generation framework that integrates spatial control signals into a generative masked motion model. The key innovations are (1) masked consistency modeling to minimize inconsistency between input control signals and generated motion, and (2) inference-time logit editing to refine control precision. ControlMM achieves real-time, high-fidelity, high-precision motion generation, outperforming state-of-the-art methods in motion quality (FID 0.061 vs 0.271) and control precision (average error 0.0091 vs 0.0108), while generating motions 20× faster than diffusion-based methods. It supports diverse applications like any-joint any-frame control, body part timeline control, and obstacle avoidance. Experiments and demos are provided on an accompanying website.

### Strengths
- The problem of spatially controllable text-to-motion generation is important and difficult.
- Results are impressive: better FID (0.061 vs 0.271) and lower control error (0.0091 vs 0.0108) compared to state-of-the-art.
- The method generates motions 20× faster than diffusion-based approaches and enables real-time, high-fidelity, high-precision generation.
- Introduces two novel components: masked consistency modeling (ensures high-fidelity motion, reduces inconsistency) and inference-time logit editing (improves control precision, enables new tasks without retraining).
- The ControlNet-like design is first applied to masked generation models, which is interesting and inspiring.
- Inference-time optimization achieves reasonable joint control error without sacrificing generation quality.
- Supports a wide range of downstream applications (any joint any frame, body part timeline, obstacle avoidance).
- Extensive experiments and ablation studies validate performance; videos are provided on the website.

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and presentation issues:** The write-up is challenging to follow due to inconsistent notation (e.g., \(\mathbf{e}\) in Eq. 1 undefined; \(L\) in Eq. 2 undefined; \(x_i\) used for both original motion frames and quantized components), unexplained terms, and details deferred to later sections. Figure 3 and Figure 4 are too small with unreadable fonts. Minor typos (e.g., "inference-timelogits" in line 145). Overall writing quality needs significant improvement (R1, R5).
- **Downplaying prior work:** The paper avoids clearly referencing building blocks (Masked Motion Models [Pinyoanuntapong et al. 2024], ControlNet mechanism). The contribution appears incremental: combining masked motion models with a ControlNet-like module plus additional tricks. The ControlNet for controllable motion was first introduced by MotionLCM, reducing novelty (R1, R5).
- **Control module effectiveness questioned:** Ablation (Table 3) shows the motion control module alone (fifth variation) has the second worst trajectory error; inference-time optimization alone on a vanilla model achieves better control error. This undermines the claimed contribution of the ControlNet-like module. Embedding editing appears to be the main component for precision (L1 vs. L3 in ablation), so sub-components are not all effective (R3, R5).
- **Unfair comparison with OmniControl:** OmniControl does not use inference-time optimization, yet ControlMM includes such optimization at test time. The comparison may be unfair (R3).
- **Missing important baselines:** CondMDI [Cohan et al. 2024], TLControl, and MotionLCM are not compared. Also, comparison with MoMask (the base masked generation framework) is missing from Table 1, obscuring ControlMM’s specific contribution (R1, R4, R5).
- **No evaluation on small datasets (e.g., KIT):** The model’s robustness in data-constrained settings is not explored (R4).
- **Lack of qualitative user studies:** Unlike other works (e.g., MLD), no user study is provided (R4).
- **Joint control data leakage concern:** The paper relies on specific joint control during both training and testing. It is unclear whether ground-truth joint data is used at test time (possible data leakage) and how the system would determine which joint to control in practical applications (R4).
- **Diversity not demonstrated:** No examples showing output diversity under identical control constraints (same prompt and control signals) (R4).
- **Reliance on pretrained models (MoMask):** Adaptability is limited; performance may carry over biases from the pretrained model. It is unclear if the model can be trained from scratch (R2).
- **Out-of-distribution robustness unclear:** Handling of extreme OOD cases (e.g., body part timeline control) is not thoroughly tested (R2).
- **Inference optimization issues:** Latent-space optimization adds time; efficiency is not well discussed. Over-fitting or parameter determination are not addressed. Demo videos show jittery, non-continuous motions (e.g., “a figure walks forward in a zig zag pattern”, “a person walks forward carrying something”), possibly due to the optimization (R5).
- **Technical contribution seen as “bag of tricks”:** The method combines logit editing, codebook editing, and ControlNet, but the ablation shows each component not independently effective. The main contribution may be the combination rather than novel individual components (R5).
