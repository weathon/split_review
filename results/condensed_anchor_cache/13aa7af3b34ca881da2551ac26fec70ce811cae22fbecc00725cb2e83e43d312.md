## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses the problem of generating realistic tabular data that complies with domain-specific constraints. It introduces Constraint Layers (CL) that can be integrated into deep generative models (DGMs) to enforce a set of linear constraints, guaranteeing compliant samples. The method automatically parses constraints into a post-processing layer and can also be used as a guardrail at inference time. Experiments on six datasets using GANs show that unconstrained DGMs violate constraints up to 95% of the time, while Constrained DGMs (C-DGMs) never violate them and achieve up to 6.5% improvement in utility/detection. The paper includes theoretical justification and proofs of correctness.

### Strengths
- Clear motivation and well-written narrative with illustrative examples (R1, R3, R4).
- The first work to handle constraints in tabular data generation; the method effectively enforces compliance (R2).
- Highlights an important and underexplored issue: that standard DGMs frequently violate obvious constraints in tabular data (R3).
- Theoretical justification and formal proofs (appear correct) add rigor (R1, R3).
- The experimental evaluation is thorough, covering multiple problems and comparing with three strong baselines (R1, R4).
- Results convincingly show zero constraint violation after applying CL and often improved utility over unconstrained models (R1, R2).
- The method also works post hoc (as a guardrail on pre-trained models) without retraining, still yielding improvements (all reviewers).

### Weaknesses
- **Outdated baselines and missing comparisons**: The generative models used (GANs) are outdated. The paper should evaluate CL on more recent and powerful tabular DGMs such as TVAE, STaSy, TabDDPM, GOGGLE, GReaT, and CoDi, both for training and post hoc experiments (R2, R3, R4).
- **Variable ordering in the Constraint Layer is unjustified** (Important): The assumption λ(i)=i is used for notational convenience, but different orderings can drastically change the constrained output. Example: with constraints x2≤10 and x1≤x2≤x3, the generated output ⟨10,5,5⟩ yields different CL results: ⟨10,10,10⟩ for λ=⟨1,2,3⟩ vs ⟨5,5,5⟩ for λ=⟨2,1,3⟩. The notion of optimality is not well-defined, and there is no discussion of how the ordering is chosen, its impact, or alternative strategies (R3). There may be cases where no valid ordering exists (R4).
- **Exponential growth of constraint reduction** (Important): The reduction from Π to Π₁ can make the number of constraints exponential in the original set. The experiments use only a small number of conditions, so the computational impact is untested. A complexity analysis with larger constraint sets and feature counts is needed (R3).
- **Limited to linear constraints**: Only linear constraints are considered. Extensions to non-linear constraints (e.g., via polynomial features) are relatively straightforward but not implemented or discussed. This limits the novelty; the paper should at least provide proof-of-concept experiments and discuss challenges and potential solutions for non-linear extensions (R3).
- **Missing distributional fidelity metrics**: The paper lacks a metric (e.g., negative log-likelihood, Wasserstein distance) to directly compare the generated distribution to the true distribution. The reported Wasserstein distances sometimes show that C-DGMs have higher distances than unconstrained models—even higher than the post-hoc P-DGMs—which is counterintuitive and raises concerns about distribution distortion (e.g., overpopulating boundary regions). Without such analysis, it is unclear whether CL improves or worsens the learned distribution; a practitioner might prefer to simply filter generated samples (R4).
- **Unclear applicability to other generative models and training integration**: It is not immediately clear how CLs affect training (e.g., gradient backpropagation through the layer) or whether they can be applied to non-GAN models such as VAEs, normalizing flows, or diffusion models (R4).
- **Categorical feature constraints not clarified**: The paper does not make clear whether CL can handle constraints between categorical variables or between numerical and categorical variables (e.g., if x1="category 1" then x2>5). The differences in Jensen-Shannon divergence for categorical features (Table 12) need explanation (R4).
- **Proofs difficult to follow**: The theoretical proofs are dense and could be reworded for better clarity and flow (R1).
- **Missed opportunity to motivate linear constraints**: The logical completeness of linear constraints (e.g., inconsistency detection via Farkas lemma) could be highlighted as an advantage (R1, suggestion).
- **Source code not released**: The supplementary material does not include source code; the paper only promises release upon publication (R3).
- **Performance improvement on state-of-the-art unclear**: Without testing on recent models, it is unknown how much CL improves upon already strong baselines (R2, question).
