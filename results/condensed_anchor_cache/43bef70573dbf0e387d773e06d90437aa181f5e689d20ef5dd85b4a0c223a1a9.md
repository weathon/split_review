## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the Item Language Model (ILM), a framework to bridge behavioral embeddings from recommendation systems with language understanding in Large Language Models (LLMs). It adapts a Querying Transformer (QFormer) with an additional item-item contrastive loss to integrate collaborative and semantic information, evaluated on 24 tasks from ELM using PALM-2. Reviewer scores: one positive (6/10), three critical (3/10).

### Strengths
- **Writing clarity.** The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 4). Reviewer 4 notes it is clear and well organized. However, Reviewer 3 notes structure and typo issues (see Weaknesses).
- **Addresses an important gap.** The work tackles the significant gap between behavioral understanding in recommendation systems and language understanding in LLMs (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). Reviewer 2 describes it as a “new direction to integrate these two types of knowledge,” though not the first alignment paper.
- **Architecture choice is sound.** The ILM-QFormer architecture is superior to a simple MLP model, reflecting the value of QFormer (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 4 agrees that using QFormer with additional contrastive loss is sound and effective.
- **Ablation studies are informative.** Ablation studies demonstrate the value of each component, showing that combining semantic and behavioral embeddings outperforms using either alone, with comparisons across MLP, QFormer, and QFormer with pre-training (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 notes “in-depth model analyses in Appendix” but criticizes missing ablations in main content (see Weaknesses).
- **General applicability.** The technique is domain-agnostic and can be applied to any domain with rich embedding representations (Reviewer 1).
- **Minority positive view.** Reviewer 2 rates the paper 6/10, emphasizing that “comprehensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the ILM framework” and that the proposal is valuable despite not being the first alignment work. This contrasts with the other reviewers’ criticism of experiments.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty.** The proposed method is mainly based on BLIP‑2 with two additional contrastive losses. Both the Querying Transformer and item-item contrastive loss are already widely adopted in self-supervised learning and recommendation systems [1,2]. The application is a straightforward adaptation of existing techniques with marginal methodological advancement (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Insufficient baselines and comparisons.**  
  - **Missing LLM‑based recommendation baselines.** Only one baseline (ELM) is included. Several related works should be compared:  
    * Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4 all cite missing comparisons. Named methods: LC‑Rec (compressing product descriptions), BinLLM (compressing user-product collaborative signals), EAGER, Learnable Tokenizer for LLM‑based Generative Recommendation, STORE, and works [3,4,5,6] from Reviewer 1.  
    * Reviewer 2 lists: Adapting Large Language Models by Integrating Collaborative Semantics for Recommendation, EAGER, Learnable Tokenizer, STORE, and asks what the differences are.  
    * Reviewer 3 adds: LC‑Rec and BinLLM specifically.  
  - **Missing traditional recommendation algorithms.** Comparisons with collaborative filtering, LightGCN, NGCF, SASRec, DIN, FM, DCN V2 are absent (Reviewers 1, 3).  
  - **Metrics.** No evaluation on widely used metrics such as AUC of ROC, Recall, or nDCG (Reviewer 1).  
- **Marginal improvement and significance concerns.**  
  - In Table 2, ILM‑Combined shows only marginal improvement over ILM‑Semantic; Reviewer 4 questions whether this is statistically significant (needs significance test). This suggests behavioral information may not be beneficial for ELM tasks, which are mostly language‑modeling related.  
  - In Table 4, the benefits of item‑item contrastive (IIC) and user‑item contrastive (UIC) are also marginal; significance testing is needed (Reviewer 4).  
  - Reviewer 1 also asks for ablation of each loss and in‑depth analysis of why the method works (e.g., alignment/uniformity [7]).  
- **Limited evaluation scope and generalizability.**  
  - The evaluation is restricted to 24 niche ELM tasks. Sequential recommendation results in the appendix show the framework cannot outperform OpenP5, limiting its application scope (Reviewer 4).  
  - Reviewer 2 questions practicality at industrial scale (1+ million users/items) and asks for scalability solutions.  
  - Reviewer 3 notes no analysis of time complexity or inference time, reducing industrial viability.  
- **Insufficient analysis and missing studies.**  
  - No justification for why QFormer was chosen as the backbone. Do the proposed losses generalize to other backbones? (Reviewer 1).  
  - Ablation of different losses (IT, IIC, UIC) should be presented in the main content, not only in the appendix (Reviewer 4).  
  - Reviewer 1 requests a main insight/takeaway: what should readers learn from this work?  
- **Technical concerns.**  
  - **Item ID conflicts.** Using 8-bit codes for each item may cause similar products to receive the same code, leading to conflicts (Reviewer 3).  
  - **Figure 3 drop.** Why does the result drop dramatically at query length 8? What factors (data size, etc.) affect the optimal query length? (Reviewer 4).  
- **Typos and presentation issues.**  
  - Missing punctuation at the end of the formula sentence in Section 3.2 (Reviewer 3).  
  - Incorrect table references in the appendix (Reviewer 3).  
  - “fullyfullyfinetune” should be “fullyfinetune” (Reviewer 4).  
  - Reviewer 3 also notes that the structure and sentences could be improved for logic and clarity.
