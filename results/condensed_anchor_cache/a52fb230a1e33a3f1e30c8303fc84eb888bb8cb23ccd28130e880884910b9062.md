## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents Grace, a client selection framework for federated learning that aims to simultaneously defend against backdoor attacks and improve model accuracy under data heterogeneity. Grace combines two components: a local model purification method using data augmentation to weaken backdoors, and a server-side representation combination sampling that selects clients based on their class representations. The paper claims Grace enhances defense performance by over 50% compared to state-of-the-art defenses and improves accuracy by up to 3.19% over existing selection schemes. Experiments are conducted mainly on CIFAR-10 with various attacks and defenses.

### Strengths
- The idea is easy to understand (R1).
- Many attacks/defenses are tested during evaluation (R1).
- The approach is innovative in that it improves accuracy while defending against backdoors, and maintains client privacy while enabling the server to select honest and high-quality clients (R2).
- The method demonstrates stable performance with increasing poisoning rates (R2).
- Grace aims to solve multiple challenges (client selection, backdoor defense, non-IID) simultaneously via client selection (R3).
- This is claimed to be the first solution that defends against backdoor attacks without sacrificing model accuracy, surpassing existing defenses under different attack scenarios and non-IID settings (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and contribution** (R1, R2, R3, R4): Several reviewers find the contribution incremental. R1 states the paper simply combines representation learning and data augmentation without significantly advancing qualitative understanding. R1, R2, R3 all note weak novelty, with R2 and R3 rating contribution 1–2.
- **Unclear threat model and scope** (R1, R4): The threat model is unclear and does not discuss Byzantine attacks, the possibility of bypassing local processing, or compromised model uploads. R4 notes the study only addresses horizontal FL and does not mention vertical FL or its limitations. R1 also flags that Section 2 (Threat Model) references many works without explaining their relation to the proposed model.
- **Weak experimental evaluation** (R1, R2, R3, R4):
  - R1: Evaluation results do not support the authors’ compelling claims; e.g., in Table 1, Grace underperforms Fed-CBS on feature-based data partitioning, which is not explained.
  - R2: Lacks experimental support for different heterogeneous settings (e.g., α = 0.05, 0.5, 0.1 for Dirichlet). Also needs more diverse datasets and scenarios (not just CIFAR-10).
  - R3: Baseline selection is out-of-date; recent 2023–2024 attacks/defenses are missing: Zhang et al. (2024) A3FL, Nguyen et al. (2024) IBA, Huang et al. (2024) Lockdown, Kumari et al. (2023) BayBFed.
  - R4: Evaluation is limited; impact of different numbers of clients is unclear. Mainly CIFAR-10 is used.
- **Missing theoretical support** (R2): Lack of convergence proof. The CMI component’s upper bound is not formally defined, and no convergence guarantee for GCLR is provided.
- **Poor presentation and clarity** (R1, R2, R3): Writing needs improvement (typos, grammar). R1 finds the paper neither well-written nor well-motivated. R2 requests a formal algorithm in Section 4. R3 states the proposed methods are confusing, especially how data augmentation works (as stated in the title) and the overall target of the paper (title emphasizes client selection, abstract focuses on defense, contribution mentions non-IID attacks but exact non-IID settings are not defined).
- **Potential flaw in client selection** (R3): The selection based on local representation learning with global class representation loss may directly kick out benign clients that hold minority classes, contradicting the claim of robustness to non-IID settings.
- **Unanswered critical questions that overlap with weaknesses**:
  - R2: How robust is the system when the majority of clients are malicious? Is the approach effective in extreme non-IID settings (e.g., when the class `y` is not entirely present in a client’s dataset for data augmentation)? The authors should validate impact with extreme non-IID experiments.
  - R3: What are the exact non-IID settings used in the paper (as claimed in contributions)? Authors claim robustness to non-IID backdoor attacks, but the method seems to remove non-IID clients to improve performance; need experiments with SOTA 2023–2024 methods.
  - R4: Does the threat model consider Byzantine attacks? Is there concern that local processing might be bypassed or that compromised models might be uploaded? Applicability limited to horizontal FL; limitations for vertical FL not discussed.
