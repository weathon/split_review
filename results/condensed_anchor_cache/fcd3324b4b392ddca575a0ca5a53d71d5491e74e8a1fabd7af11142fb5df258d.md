## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Neural-Symbolic Message Passing (NSMP) framework for answering existential first-order (EFO₁) complex queries over knowledge graphs. It re-uses a pre-trained neural link predictor (ComplEx-N3) and fuzzy logic operations to compute neural embeddings and symbolic fuzzy set vectors for each variable node during message passing, without training on complex query datasets. A dynamic pruning strategy stops message propagation from un-updated variable nodes to filter noise. Evaluations on BetaE and FIT benchmarks show competitive results and fast inference compared to prior fuzzy-logic-based methods like FIT. However, reviewers highlight major concerns regarding novelty, plagiarism, and the standardness of the fuzzy logic formulation, with scores ranging from 1 to 6.

### Strengths
- The framework integrates neural and symbolic reasoning in a novel way (according to R1, R2), using pre-trained link predictors with fuzzy set theory for aggregation and enabling interpretable intermediate representations.
- The dynamic pruning technique is interesting and novel (R1, R2); ablations confirm its effectiveness, especially for FIT queries (R1).
- NSMP achieves competitive performance on BetaE and FIT benchmarks without training on complex queries, demonstrating empirical significance (R1, R2, R3).
- The method is computationally efficient, showing significant speedups (e.g., 10× over FIT on NELL) while maintaining strong performance (R1, R2).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R2, R3), with comprehensive experiments across different KG benchmarks and query types (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and overlap with prior work:** Multiple components are near-identical to existing methods. The message passing mechanism is essentially the same as LMPNN (R3, R4). The neural-symbolic combination is very close to EmQL (R3) and the use of a pre-trained link predictor without training on complex queries follows CQD and FIT (R1, R3). The overall approach reuses LMPNN’s structure with minor modifications (R4). Some sections contain verbatim or near-verbatim text from LMPNN, FIT, and ENeSy without proper citation (R4). These issues are so severe that R4 considers the paper plagiarism.
- **Fuzzy logic formulation is non-standard:** The paper normalizes the fuzzy set after every operation (Eq. 7–15, 17), meaning De Morgan’s laws are not satisfied (R3). Consequently, NSMP must resort to disjunctive normal form (DNF) to handle disjunctions, which may not scale well (R3). This contrasts with CQD, FIT, and other works where each entity has an independent probability and logical operations form a closure (R3).
- **Inefficiency and scalability concerns:** The core operation involves `O(|V|²)` space (adjacency matrix) and `O(|V|²)` computation (sparse matrix multiplication) per step, raising questions about practical scalability for large KGs (R2). The claimed 10× speedup over FIT on NELL is not theoretically justified, as both methods share similar quadratic complexity (R2). The dynamic pruning should be called static or Bellman-Ford-like, as it only depends on the query graph and layer index, not learned representations (R3). Questions remain about the complexity reduction of pruning in terms of the number of messages (`O(Lm)` vs. `O(m²)`) (R2).
- **Incomplete justification for experimental choices:** The paper does not include the “pni” query type; reviewer 1 questions this omission, noting that Yin et al. (2024) defines it within EFO₁ and FIT includes it via resampling. The authors also omit FB15K, citing a paper that does not actually claim test leakage but proposes a cleaning procedure (R1). Most CQA benchmarks and methods use FB15K; this choice needs stronger justification (R1).
- **Interpretability not demonstrated:** The paper claims interpretability as a strength, but no qualitative experiments (e.g., visualizing intermediate variable states) are provided (R3).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** The hyperparameters of NSMP appear to be tuned per dataset; no analysis of sensitivity or robust defaults is given (R3).
- **Underperformance relative to baselines:** NSMP underperforms GNN-QE and CQD-A on BetaE datasets, and underperforms FIT on EFO-1 datasets (R4). The improvement over existing methods is marginal or nonexistent (R1, R4).
- **Technical questions and issues:**
  - How product t-norm aggregation handles non-homogeneous probability ranges across intermediate answers? (R1)
  - Can the authors explain the complexity of FIT vs. NSMP on acyclic and cyclic queries in terms of graph and query size? (R2)
  - Is DNF really scalable for handling disjunctions? (R3)
  - Equation 9 & 10: What if negative values result from subtraction? (R3)
  - Typographical errors: “graph representation” → “graph representations” (line 30), “A straightforward” (line 34), “symbolic representation” → “symbolic representations” (line 261) (R3).
- **Reviewer disagreement:** R1 and R2 see moderate novelty and empirical value (scores 6, 5), while R3 sees limited novelty and technical issues (3), and R4 sees no contribution and plagiarism (1). All reviewers except R4 express some open questions; R4’s criticism is particularly severe.
