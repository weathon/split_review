## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Robin3D, a 3D Large Language Model trained on a 1 million instruction-following dataset generated by the Robust Instruction Generation (RIG) engine. The dataset includes 344K adversarial samples (designed to improve discriminative power) and 508K diverse samples (enhancing generalization), plus 165K benchmark training samples. Two modules are proposed: a Relation-Augmented Projector (RAP) for better spatial understanding by integrating scene‑level, object‑level, and positional features, and ID‑Feature Bonding (IFB) that wraps object features with ID tokens to strengthen referring and grounding. Robin3D achieves state‑of‑the‑art or strong results across five 3D multimodal benchmarks (ScanQA, SQA3D, Multi3DRefer, ScanRefer, Scan2Cap) without task‑specific fine‑tuning, with notable gains reported on Multi3DRefer (+7.8%) and Scan2Cap (+6.9%). The paper is clearly written and well structured.

### Strengths
- **Large‑scale, high‑quality instruction‑tuning dataset:** RIG produces adversarial (negative/positive mixed) and diverse (expression‑varied) samples, which are expected to improve model robustness, reduce hallucination, and alleviate rigid outputs (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5). The 1M‑sample dataset is a major contribution (R3, R5).
- **Novel architecture components (RAP & IFB) for spatial and grounding enhancement:** RAP fuses 3D scene, object, and positional features to capture spatial relationships; IFB links object IDs to their features via token wrapping, improving referring and grounding (R1, R4, R5). Ablation experiments show clear gains, particularly for grounding tasks (R5).
- **Strong performance across multiple benchmarks:** Robin3D outperforms prior methods on five 3D multimodal tasks (ScanQA, SQA3D, Multi3DRefer, ScanRefer, Scan2Cap) without task‑specific fine‑tuning (R2, R3, R4, R5). The model is trained jointly on all tasks (R4). Zero‑shot improvements are evident (R5).
- **Clear motivation and fluent writing:** The problem of insufficient robust instruction data in 3DLLMs is well motivated, and the paper is easy to follow (R2, R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty of proposed modules:**  
  * RAP is essentially similar to the Chat‑Scene baseline: linear layers to connect 3D, object, and positional features; the only change is the interchange of inputs to linear layers (R4). The advantages of introducing Mask3D information via RAP compared to prior works are not clearly explained (R5).  
  * ID‑Feature Bonding (IFB) merely adds an end‑of‑object ID token and rearranges vision and prompt tokens – not a fundamentally new idea (R4). The paper should explain how object IDs are linked to features in earlier works and what benefit wrapping them with ID tokens brings (R5).  
- **Architecture and dataset concerns:**  
  * Reliance on off‑the‑shelf closed‑set 3D instance segmentation (Mask3D) trained on ScanNet; open‑vocabulary alternatives (e.g., Segment3D) are recommended (R1).  
  * Cropping instance‑level point clouds and applying object‑level 3D CLIP (Uni3D) limits receptive fields and is computationally heavy; scene‑level CLIP approaches (OpenScene, RegionPLC) are suggested (R1).  
  * The dataset is designed for indoor 3D scenes; generalization to outdoor environments (e.g., 3D street, DriveLM, LiDAR‑LLM) is not discussed (R2, R3). Extension to point‑level LLMs (PointLLM, GPT‑4Point) and outdoor 3D LLMs is not addressed (R2).  
  * The dataset release status is not stated; as the main contribution lies in the dataset, its public availability is important (R3).  
- **Insufficient ablation and analysis:**  
  * Ablation on adversarial data is incomplete: it is unclear whether performance gains come from the addition of adversarial samples or simply from increased data volume (R2). Ablations varying the proportion of adversarial vs. diverse samples are missing (R5).  
  * Ablation on the number of training examples is not provided (R3).  
  * The relative contributions of RAP and IFB are not balanced across tasks – they contribute little to 3D QA (ScanQA, SQA3D) but help grounding significantly; this is not explained (R2).  
  * The ablation study in the paper appears to have ordering confusion: “Baseline (+ RAP & IFB)” in Table 3 gives the same results as the benchmark in Table 2, but the “ABLATION STUDY” section (+ Adversarial & Diverse) should include RAP&IFB and all data. The inconsistency between ablation results and Table 1 is not clarified (R4).  
- **Evaluation and baseline issues:**  
  * Only traditional NLP metrics (BLEU, CIDEr, METEOR, Rouge) are reported; LLM‑based evaluation (e.g., GPT, Mistral) is recommended by R1.  
  * The best results in Table 2 may not be state‑of‑the‑art: prior to the submission deadline, the open‑source Chat‑Scene model already achieved ScanRefer accuracy >61% and >55%, outperforming Robin3D. The paper should compare with the most recent baselines (R4).  
  * The LLM backbone used by each compared method is not clarified in Table 1, which is important since MLLM performance strongly depends on the LLM (R1).  
  * The baseline network architecture in Table 3 is not clearly identified (R1).  
- **Missing comparisons and tasks:**  
  * Comparison with methods using video as a modality (VLM‑Grounder, Coarse Correspondences) is not included (R2).  
  * The paper should test whether the proposed dataset brings consistent gains when used to fine‑tune existing task‑specific or jointly‑trained models (R5).  
- **Lack of details and transparency:**  
  * The prompts used for adversarial data generation and the data creation process are not described in sufficient detail (R2).  
  * Training configurations and training time are not provided (R2).  
- **Minor discrepancy in Tables:** The grey sections in Table 1 indicating “ground truth question‑relative objects annotations” are ambiguous; clarification is needed on whether the explicit positional information P from Mask3D counts as such annotations (R4).
