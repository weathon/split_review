## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a Relative Distance Variance Constraint (RDVC) loss that regularizes deep metric learning by minimizing the variance of relative distances among triplets within a batch. The authors provide an empirical study showing that lower relative-distance variance correlates with better NMI/F1 performance, along with theoretical analysis. RDVC can be integrated with existing pair-based losses (e.g., triplet, multi-similarity, semi-hard triplet) and is evaluated on CUB200, Cars-196, and a zero-shot sketch-based retrieval dataset (Sketchy). While one reviewer (R2, rating 5) finds the combination with existing methods yields obvious improvement and the analysis comprehensive, three reviewers (R1, R3, R4, all rating 3) find the experimental validation limited and the motivation unconvincing.

### Strengths
- **Novelty of approach:** Analyzing the distribution of relative distances in DML is an interesting and new direction (R1, R2). The RDVC loss is a new formulation based on variance of relative distances per triplet (R3).
- **Easy integration:** RDVC seamlessly integrates with existing losses like triplet, multi-similarity, and semi-hard triplet without significant architectural changes, enhancing practicality (R1, R4). Table 1 shows performance improvements on CUB200 and Cars-196 (R1, R4).
- **Validation on sketch-based retrieval:** The method is tested on zero-shot sketch-based image retrieval (FG-ZS-SBIR), an interesting and challenging application (R1).
- **Comprehensive analysis:** The paper provides both empirical (Sec 3.1) and theoretical perspectives (R2). Writing is clear and well-structured, with a logical flow (R3, R4).
- **Minority positive assessment:** Reviewer 2 (rating 5) notes that the combination of RDVC with existing methods brings “obvious performance improvement” and the analysis is comprehensive, contrasting with the more critical views of other reviewers.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing standard benchmarks:** Evaluation is limited to small fine-grained datasets (CUB200, Cars-196) and Sketchy. Widely used and more challenging benchmarks such as SOP (Stanford Online Products) and InShop are not included (R1, R3). This weakens claims of general applicability (R4).
- **Outdated comparisons:** The main results (Table 1, Table 2, Table 3) mostly compare against methods from 2019 or older. Several stronger post-2022 baselines are missing, including HIER (Kim et al. 2023), Non-isotropy Regularization (Roth et al. 2022), Stop-gradient Softmax (Yang et al. 2023), and Contextual Similarity Optimization (Liao et al. 2023) (R1, R2, R3, R4). R2 and R4 explicitly request comparisons with the latest 2023–2024 competitors.
- **Weak motivation:** 
  - The empirical trend in Sec 3.1 (variance vs. performance) does not appear monotonic, making the motivation for directly minimizing variance questionable (R1).
  - No strong theoretical justification is provided. Enforcing the same relative distance for all dissimilar-example pairs, regardless of class variation, seems like a poor objective for metric learning, especially on datasets with diverse class hierarchies (R1).
  - The design choice to push anchor and positive apart for “easy” triplets (L288-290) is not justified; intuitive reasoning suggests this could harm embeddings (R1).
- **Limited ablation studies:**
  - The claim that RDVC removes the need for a margin hyperparameter is only supported by testing 2 margin values in Fig 6(b), which is insufficient (R1).
  - The hyper-parameter λ1 is only evaluated at four values (0.5, 1, 1.5, 2). A wider range (e.g., 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100) across datasets is needed to assess robustness (R2).
  - More in-depth ablation on the effect of varying lambda weights and comparisons with other regularization techniques are missing (R4).
- **Prior related work:** Distance variance regularization was previously addressed by Kan et al. (MVC, TPAMI 2023), which computes variance of pairwise distances directly. The paper lacks a thorough comparison and discussion differentiating RDVC from MVC (R3).
- **Unclear handling of informative pair selection:** The proposed loss does not address the issue of selecting informative pairs for triplet construction, leaving implementation details ambiguous (R1, R2). For example, are all triplets used or is sample mining involved? (R1)
- **Missing experimental details:** It is unclear whether embeddings are ℓ2 normalized and what image/crop sizes are used for CUB200 and Cars-196 (R1). Computational overhead of RDVC compared to standard DML losses is not reported (R4).
- **Disagreement on CLIP comparisons:** Reviewer 2 suggests including CLIP and its fine-tuned variations in Table 3, while Reviewer 1 argues that comparisons with zero-shot CLIP-based approaches are unfair due to very different training/model settings (R1, R2).
- **Limited innovation:** The overall research approach is somewhat conventional, offering limited theoretical depth and innovation within DML (R4).
- **Missing visualization:** A t-SNE visualization of learned embeddings across training stages would help illustrate RDVC’s effects on distribution uniformity and separation (R1). Fig 2(c) would be clearer if plotted as variance vs. performance directly (R1).
