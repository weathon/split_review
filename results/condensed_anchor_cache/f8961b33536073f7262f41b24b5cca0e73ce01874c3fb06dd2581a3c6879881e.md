## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes an efficient diffusion posterior sampling method for dose-reduced CT reconstruction. It enforces data consistency via posterior mean estimation (single-step gradient update) and data-enhancement projections (multi-step conjugate gradient), adds a forward resampling step, and accelerates sampling by initializing with FBP images instead of random noise (small time-step initialization). The method is evaluated on simulated AAPM data (8 training, 2 testing subjects) against MCG and DPS, showing higher PSNR and faster reconstruction.

### Strengths

- The paper is well organized and provides a good, self‑contained summary of DDPMs. (R1)
- Leveraging projection data to guide the denoising process yields more reliable reconstruction than unconditional image generation. (R2)
- Compared to MCG and DPS, the method achieves faster reconstruction with fewer denoising iterations by using DDIM. (R2)
- The design is reasonable and includes a clear section (3.2) summarizing advantages over MCG and DPS. (R3)
- The reported results show higher‑quality reconstruction with shorter sampling time. (R4)
- *Minority positive:* Reviewer 3 (score 6) finds the performance impressive compared to other diffusion‑based methods and the method well‑designed, contrasting with the more critical assessments. (R3)

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty and missing prior citations:**  
  – The core components are a combination of DPS (posterior mean estimation), DDS (data‑enhancement projections), and CCDF (small time‑step acceleration), none of which are claimed as new. [2,3] are not cited. (R4)  
  – Data consistency via conjugate‑gradient projection closely resembles ReSample (ICLR 2024 spotlight), which is not cited or benchmarked. (R1)  
  – FBP initialization is universally used in iterative and DL‑based CT reconstruction, so its contribution as an accelerating technique is unclear. (R1, R3)  
  – The method is essentially MCG combined with DDIM acceleration and CG projections; additional innovations beyond that are not clearly articulated. (R2)  
  – More refined work combining DPS and DDS already exists (Askari et al., 2024, [4]) and is not compared. (R4)  
  – No comparison to DDS, either theoretically or experimentally. (R4)

- **Insufficient and single‑dataset evaluation:**  
  – Only 2 subjects from a single simulated dataset (AAPM) are used for testing, with no held‑out validation set. (R1, R3)  
  – The training set is only 8 subjects (∼50 test images from 2 patients with potential slice overlap). (R1, R3)  
  – All experiments use simulated synthetic projections, committing the inverse crime (same forward model as in training). (R1)  
  – No real low‑dose or measured projection data (e.g., LDCT‑and‑Projection dataset) are tested. (R1, R2)  
  – Generalizability to other datasets is not demonstrated. (R2)  
  – Extremely challenging settings (e.g., 2 or 8 views, 5% dose) where diffusion methods excel are not included. (R2)

- **Missing ablations and baseline concerns:**  
  – No ablation study isolating the effect of FBP initialization; its speed and fidelity contribution is not quantified. (R1, R3)  
  – Hyperparameters for MCG and DPS baselines appear to be matched to the proposed method rather than tuned separately, potentially harming their performance. (R1)  
  – ADMM‑TV baseline is clearly over‑regularized; the TV weight was apparently not swept. (R1)

- **Methodological and experimental questions from reviewers:**  
  – Why were ReSample and similar works not cited or benchmarked? Can an enumerated list of differences and a comparison be provided? (R1)  
  – What specifically makes FBP initialization a new contribution given its widespread use? (R1)  
  – What are the benefits of using both MCG and CG (tangent‑space arguments from DDS)? Please provide a theoretical explanation or ablation. (R4)  
  – How does this method differ from MCG beyond DDIM and CG? (R2)

- **Minor presentation and formatting issues:**  
  – The abstract is written in an LLM‑assisted style that is hard to parse; a human‑edited revision is needed. (R1)  
  – Figure 3 x‑axis should extend to 1000 steps to show saturation of DPS and MCG. (R1)  
  – Table 2’s column “Computation cost/s” is unclear – should it be “Computation cost (s)”? (R1)  
  – Algorithm 1’s notation lacks explanation. (R4)  
  – “dB” is incorrectly written as “db” in the abstract. (R4)  
  – Punctuation is missing after some formulas (e.g., Eq. 21). (R4)  
  – Reviewer R4 notes that LDCT computation time being shorter than SVCT (96 views) is counterintuitive and needs clarification (full‑view vs. sparse‑view operations in CG). (R4)
