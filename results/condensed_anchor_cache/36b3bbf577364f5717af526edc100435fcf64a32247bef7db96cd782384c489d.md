## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviews are mixed: one reviewer (score 3) is considerably more critical, questioning the benchmark’s novelty, the evaluation setup, and the generalizability of the tool-based agent design. The other three reviewers (scores 5, 5, 6) are more positive overall, acknowledging the benchmark’s utility, the identification of 96 KG errors, and the well-aligned sub-tasks. Common weaknesses include missing comparisons with recent RAG/KBQA baselines, reliance on a single (2020) KG, lack of clarity in evaluation metrics and sub-task reporting, and concerns about scalability and generalizability. The performance gain of BKGAgent is largely attributed to the provided tools rather than the multi-agent design. Detailed methodological and metric descriptions are repeatedly requested.

### Strengths
- The benchmark proposes three distinct tasks: Knowledge Graph Question Answering (KGQA), Scientific Claim Verification (SCV), and the integrated agent task Knowledge Graph Checking (KGCheck) for evaluating biomedical AI agents (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- The study successfully identifies **96 factual errors** in the biomedical knowledge graph, marked as “Refute” annotations. This directly challenges the common assumption that KGs contain precise information (Reviewer 2).
- The sub-task decomposition of KGCheck (database queries + literature review) aligns well with the methodology of human scientific research, making the benchmark useful for future work (Reviewer 3).
- The constructed test cases for KGQA and claim verification may be helpful for evaluating clinical-domain models and agents (Reviewer 1).
- The designed evaluation mechanism (including metrics like understanding, reasoning, efficiency, KG process, information retrieval) serves as a reference for future agent evaluation design, even though the metrics themselves are not fully explained (Reviewer 1).
- The proposed baseline **BKGAgent** incorporates multiple tools and agent interactions (team leader, KG agent, validation agent) and achieves better overall performance on KGCheck compared to existing baselines (Reviewer 3).
- Extensive evaluation on current LLMs and AI agents is performed and compared across several settings (Reviewer 4).
- The overall benchmark BioKGBench is a novel contribution for evaluating AI agents in the biomedical domain (Reviewer 4; also implied by others).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and generalizability of the benchmark setup (Reviewer 1 – more negative):** When evaluating LLMs alone (Table 7), the LLMs do not have access to the KG or the literature database, making the task a straightforward, existing QA/claim verification setup that has been well-studied. When evaluating agents (Table 8), the same set of tools (for querying KG and literature) is provided by the authors rather than being part of the agent design; this assumption limits the benchmark’s generalizability and ties results to a specific tool design. The agent benchmark task is not designed to be result-oriented (e.g., outcome-based metrics are missing). (Other reviewers did not raise these points as strongly.)
- **Missing comparisons with related work (Reviewers 1, 2, 3):** The study lacks comparison with recent RAG-based baselines such as Self-RAG and KG-RAG (Reviewer 2). It also does not compare with KG-guided QA methods like Think-on-graph [1]; the unique contribution beyond applying KG-enhanced QA to the biomedical domain is unclear (Reviewer 1). The KGQA sub-task is closely related to KBQA (Knowledge Base QA), but this relationship is not discussed; it may be possible to benchmark KBQA methods in place of the KG agent (Reviewer 3). The difference between KGQA and the STaRK benchmark [1] is not clarified (Reviewer 2).
- **Clarity and soundness of evaluation metrics (Reviewers 1, 3, 4):** The metrics (understanding, reasoning, efficiency, KG process, information retrieval) are not explained, and what the numerical scores imply is unclear (Reviewer 3). For the KGCheck “refute” annotation: why is a relationship classified as refute if it is simply not present in the KG (which could be due to KG sparsity rather than an error)? What is the conceptual difference between an “existing” and a “potential” relationship? (Reviewer 1). For the SCV task: does the model have access to the entire corpus (5664 articles) or only the specific article that the claim is derived from? How are conflicts between global claims from different articles handled? (Reviewer 1). How are the SCV labels made? Were human agreement tests performed? Is the classification a self-critique/prompting process or a trainable process? If prompt-based, what is the scoring criteria? (Reviewer 4). What additional information does the Qwen evaluation LLM have compared to the evaluated LLMs that produce answers? (Reviewer 1). Does the KGCheck framework support unseen nodes not present in the KG, or does it assume all entities are already in the KG? (Reviewer 1).
- **Performance attribution and reporting gaps (Reviewers 3, 1, 4):** From Figure 5 and Table 8, the performance of **BKGAgent** is very similar to that of **AutoGen w/ our tools**, indicating that the key performance gain likely comes from the provided tools rather than the three-agent framework. The effectiveness of using three agents (team leader, KG agent, validation agent) is not demonstrated (Reviewer 3). The performance of the proposed framework on the individual sub-tasks (KGQA and SCV) is not reported and compared with baselines (Table 7 only shows LLM performance on SCV, not the agent’s performance) (Reviewer 3). For baseline agents that do not have access to the authors’ tools, how do they query the KG and literature pool? (Reviewer 1). The **recall rate** of errors detected by BKGAgent on the KGCheck task is not reported (Reviewer 3). **Cost analysis** (e.g., API costs, latency) of BKGAgent is missing (Reviewer 4).
- **Scalability and generalizability concerns (Reviewers 2, 4):** The approach may face scalability issues when applied to larger and more complex KGs (Reviewer 2). The study relies solely on a single biomedical KG proposed in 2020, which may not reflect the most current state of knowledge representation. As KGs evolve, accuracy and completeness can change, potentially making the findings less relevant over time (Reviewer 2). Results may not generalize to other KGs due to variations in structure, content, and accuracy (Reviewer 2). Generalizability of the pipeline to other domains (e.g., non-biomedical) is not discussed (Reviewer 4). The KGCheck task is narrowly focused on verifying KG entries, which restricts its utility in broader scientific agent contexts (Reviewer 2).
- **Minor issues:** There are minor writing issues (Reviewer 4). In line 363–364, the authors use the word “propose” for metrics that are common (Reviewer 4). The Clinical KG is cited with inconsistent references ([32], [33], [53]) (Reviewer 2).
