- Scores: 5, 3, 5, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper derives an expression for RNN loss gradients in terms of the Q vectors and R values from Finite-Time Lyapunov Exponent (FTLE) calculations of the hidden-state dynamics. Empirically (on MNIST), the authors show that the top singular vectors of the gradient matrix become increasingly aligned with the dominant Q vectors during training and that perturbing inputs at times of high state-space expansion (large R values) maximally affects the loss. Three reviewers rated the paper 5 (positive) and one reviewer rated it 3 (negative). Overall, the idea of linking forward and backward dynamics is considered novel and important, but concerns about novelty relative to existing theory, limited empirical scope (only MNIST), unconvincing sensitivity results (Fig 3, Table 1, Fig 4), and the need for deeper theoretical grounding are raised.

### Strengths
- Novel idea in an important, underexplored area: linking forward (activation) dynamics to backward (gradient) dynamics in RNNs.
- Theoretically and experimentally sound paper (Reviewer 3).
- Thorough experiments and detailed explanation of experimental setup (Reviewer 1).
- Elucidating how chaotic dynamics shapes computation in recurrent networks is a relevant and challenging topic, with a novel perspective (Reviewer 4).
- Novel observation that the gradient structure and (finite-time) Lyapunov vectors become aligned during training (Reviewer 4).
- Overall presentation is clear and easy to follow (Reviewers 2, 4).
- The focus on the connection between Lyapunov exponents and training dynamics of deep neural networks is important and interesting (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and related work**: The connection of RNNs to Lyapunov exponents is already studied in [arxiv 2110.07238] and [arxiv 2306.04406]; a detailed comparison is necessary to evaluate novelty (Reviewer 2, W1). The empirical results likely follow from well-understood adjoint Lyapunov vector theory (Rev 1): adjoint Lyapunov vectors (with the loss as observable) are orthogonal to backward Lyapunov vectors, and the FTLEs obtained with an adjoint basis are the same as from tangent-space QR iteration. This may explain all alignment and sensitivity observations (Rev 1). The original reference for QR-based Lyapunov vector calculation (Ginelli et al. 2007 PRL) is missing (Rev 1).  
- **Empirical scope**: Only MNIST is used; experiments on dynamical data should be considered (Rev 2, W2). Different RNN architectures, loss functions, etc. should be tested to see if dynamics differ (Rev 3; also Rev 4 asks whether for a large network the largest R-value becomes self-averaging).  
- **Sensitivity analysis (Fig 3, Table 1) not convincing**: Very small differences and large variance; unclear if the effect is robust (Rev 4). P-values should be viewed cautiously due to multiple testing and post-hoc selection of k-values; can the difference be measured beyond doubt (Δ > 3σ)? Why is there no difference for smaller numbers of pixel flips? (Rev 4). Also not clear how useful the analysis is if retraining is required for each number of flips (Rev 2, W3). Fig 3 legend (blue/orange meaning up/down or R-value/random flips) needs clarification (Rev 4).  
- **Fig 4**: The difference in logit size between R-value and random flips is not statistically quantified. Is there a theoretical argument for this behavior? (Rev 4)  
- **Numerical observations not derived mathematically**: While the gradient is expressed in terms of FTLE quantities, the alignment and prediction-flip observations are purely numerical; where evidence is not convincing (Fig 3, 4), conclusions rest on weak support (Rev 4).  
- **Inconsistencies in experimental setup**: Why are hidden states initialized to \(h_t=0\) in the sensitivity analysis but nonzero in Figs 1+2? (Rev 4). Below eq. 8, \(J_t\) is treated as a random matrix, but later analysis uses specific trained weights – why? (Rev 4). Why are FTLEs not put explicitly into eq. 12? (Rev 4). Given eq. 12, is there a mechanistic way to see that alignment of \(\Delta V\) and Q increases over training? (Rev 4).  
- **Computational cost claim (p.5)**: Why is computing gradients in each step considerable? Autodiff does this quickly; is the QR+SVD decomposition the expensive part? (Rev 4)  
- **Training dynamics questions**: Does alignment (Figs 1,2, after 5 epochs) vanish when training saturates (gradients become smaller and noise-dominated)? (Rev 4). Discussion claims alignment increases both over epochs and over input sequence, but figures/references only show epoch 5, not later epochs or later positions in the input sequence (Rev 4).  
- **Terminology**: p.7 “discriminatory power” is misplaced – sensitivity to a pixel does not imply it contains more label information (Rev 4).  
- **Notation**: Not consistent – un‑bolded \(h_t\) and bolded \(\mathbf{h}_t\) are used simultaneously to denote the gradient vector (Rev 2, W4).  
- **Real‑world relevance**: It remains unclear how the observed relations can build better RNN architectures, training algorithms, regularization, or loss functions (Rev 3; also Rev 1 asks for implications of dual basis Lyapunov vectors; Rev 4 asks about implications for building better RNNs).  
- **Minor contrast**: Reviewer 2 (score 3) is more negative, highlighting insufficient novelty vs. existing Lyapunov+RNN works, lack of dynamical datasets, and unconvincing usefulness of sensitivity analysis (W3); the other three reviewers (score 5) find the work novel and sound but share concerns about real‑world impact and empirical rigor.  
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms/missing experiments/ablations**:  
  - Can adjoint Lyapunov vectors (fixing the loss as observable) explain the experimental observations? (Rev 1)  
  - Does alignment persist at saturation? (Rev 4)  
  - Are the observations robust across architectures and non‑MNIST tasks? (Rev 3, Rev 2)  
  - Why does Fig 3 show no effect for small numbers of flips? (Rev 4)  
  - How do the results change if the cost of QR+SVD is considered? (Rev 4)
