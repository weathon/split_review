## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies that strongly connected subgraphs (e.g., cliques) can severely restrict information flow in common GNN architectures, counter-intuitively. To address this, the authors introduce the concept of multi-scale consistency: at the node level, retaining a connected propagation graph even if connectivity varies; at the graph level, ensuring distinct graphs describing the same object at different resolutions obtain similar feature vectors. They show popular GNNs lack these properties and propose ResolvNet, a spectral-based GNN using resolvents of the Laplacian. Theoretical proofs establish multi-scale consistency, and experiments on real-world datasets show that ResolvNet outperforms baselines on many tasks, both inside and outside the multi-scale setting. Scores range from 3 to 8, with one very positive reviewer (rating 8) and one very negative (rating 3).

### Strengths
- **Novel problem identification and solution**: The paper identifies a new issue (strongly connected subgraphs limiting information flow) and introduces a framework (ResolvNet) based on resolvents, which is considered a significant and innovative contribution. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Clear problem definition and motivation**: Multi-scale consistency is well-defined (Definition 2.1) and explained in detail using figures, text, and examples, making the problem and its importance accessible. (R1, R3)
- **Theoretical support**: Rigorous theoretical proofs and justification are provided for the proposed method, enhancing its credibility. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Promising experimental results**: The method demonstrates usefulness and outperforms several baselines on widely-used datasets; the approach is empirically validated. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Clarity and organization**: Reviewer 2 finds the paper exceptionally well-organized and lucid; however, Reviewer 4 reports poor readability, indicating a significant contrast in presentation assessment. (R2, R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental evaluation**:
  - Datasets are small to medium-sized; experiments on larger datasets (e.g., OGBN) are missing. (R3)
  - Important baselines are ignored: e.g., GCNII and GraphMAE2 are not compared on Cora, Citeseer, and Pubmed; GCNII can beat the proposed method on Cora and Pubmed. (R4) Additionally, missing comparisons with pooling methods (diffpool, gpool, eigenpooling) followed by a standard GNN, or Gaussian Mixture Models that separate scales. (R1)
  - The proposed method does not achieve the best performance in Table 3. (R4)
  - No synthetic experiments (e.g., stochastic block models) are conducted for controlled evaluation of parameters. (R1)
- **Lack of ablation and parameter analysis**: The impact of parameters ω and k (from the ResolvNet Layer) is not thoroughly investigated. It is unclear how performance changes when ω varies across orders of magnitude or how increasing k affects multi-scale capture. (R2)
- **Clarity and readability issues**:
  - Integration of Type-0 and Type-1 filters is unclear: does a node autonomously determine which filter to employ or is it a global parameter? (R1)
  - The concept of resolvents is not commonly understood; the paper lacks sufficient depth, practical examples, and intuitive explanation of how the resolvent of the Laplacian relates to multi-scale consistency. (R2)
  - Notation is sometimes introduced without adequate context (e.g., \( T \) in Section 3.2). (R2)
  - The split of an original graph into \( G_\text{high} \) and \( G_\text{reg} \) (related to Definition 2.1) is not clearly explained. (R3)
  - The sentence above Eq. 1 (“we would have a Lipschitz continuity relation…”) is confusing and not justified. (R3)
  - The abstract contains an unclear sentence and a concluding sentence that makes assertions not fully substantiated by the experiments. (R1)
  - Several grammatical errors: “poular” → “popular”, “severly” → “severely”, “occuring” → “occurring”. (R4)
- **Computational complexity**: The method involves a matrix inverse, leading to high complexity. Authors should report complexity in FLOPs or wall-clock time and discuss scalability to larger datasets. (R3)
- **Hyper-parameter sensitivity**: Concern over the number of hyper-parameters and their impact on reproducibility; the model may contain many hyper-parameters. (R4)
- **Unanswered questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - How do learnable filters as polynomials in resolvents achieve the effects of up-projection and down-projection operators? (R2)
  - How would ResolvNet perform in terms of accuracy and computational time on larger datasets? (R2)
  - Why do the reported results fail to beat SOTA baselines (like GCNII) in Table 1? (R4)
