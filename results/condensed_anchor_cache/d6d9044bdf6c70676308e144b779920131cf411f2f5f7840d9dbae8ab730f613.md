## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces FormulaReasoning, a dataset of 5,420 reasoning questions from Chinese junior high school physics examinations, each requiring explicit use of one of 272 formulas. The dataset construction involves multi-step collection, filtering, annotation, and formula normalization. Experiments benchmark LLMs (7B to 100B+ parameters) under zero-shot, few-shot, retrieval-augmented, and supervised fine-tuning methods. The authors claim current LLMs substantially underperform human high-school students (84% vs. 92%) and highlight the dataset’s challenging, unresolved nature.

### Strengths
- Novel dataset filling a clear gap: benchmarks for formula-based numerical reasoning that go beyond simple arithmetic and test domain-specific knowledge application (Reviewer 1, 5).
- Dataset construction process is rigorous, well-documented, and carefully designed: collection, annotation, normalization, formula database built via symbolic, LLM-based, and manual filtering (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 2; Reviewer 3).
- First dataset (to our knowledge) with extensive categorization of the mathematical formulas used in every problem (Reviewer 2).
- Overall dataset size (5,420) is large enough to reliably detect small improvements in accuracy, despite small test split (Reviewer 2).
- Comprehensive evaluations covering a wide range of model types and sizes, with solid baselines for the benchmark (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 3).
- Paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Dataset is not challenging for strong modern LLMs.** Reviewer 2 tested o1‑preview on the first 20 problems (avoiding cherry‑picking) and obtained 19/20; the single error involved a unit mismatch possibly due to dataset ambiguity (figure missing, inconsistent unit in ground truth). Reviewer 2 strongly disagrees with the paper’s characterization of “the challenging nature of FormulaReasoning as an unresolved dataset”—better prompting and cleaning may yield nearly 100%. This contrasts with Reviewer 1’s score of 5, who found the dataset a valuable challenge (_explicit minority contrast_). (Reviewer 2; also Reviewer 4 asks to test o1).
- **Limited novelty compared to existing benchmarks.** Datasets such as GPQA and MMLU already include questions requiring formula usage. The main contribution—explicit formula annotations—does not clearly aid evaluation because most formulas are mainstream and likely appear in LLM training data. The paper lacks a comparison to GPQA diamond for formula-based reasoning. (Reviewer 4)
- **Unclear motivation and added value of formula annotations.** The paper claims formulas guide complex numerical reasoning, but the link is not demonstrated. Annotated formulas are valuable for reducing hallucinations and improving interpretability in scientific domains, but the paper does not articulate this for the broader community. The metric (Section 4.3) only measures final answer accuracy, not whether formulas are correctly identified or used—so why annotate formulas? (Reviewer 3)
- **Weak error analysis.** Failure analysis is brief; only two example failures from GPT-3.5 are shown, not the top‑performing model. Reviewer 4 asks for more examples including strongest model failures and a breakdown (calculation vs. formula mistakes). Reviewer 1 also requests deeper examination of mistake types. (Reviewer 1, 4)
- **Formula Retriever design questionable.** The idea that question representation will be similar to the correct formula representation is problematic—e.g., z = x/y and z = x*y are easily confused unless special tokens handle it. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limited domain and size.** Dataset covers only physics (5,420 samples, 272 formulas). For use as a retrieval database or testbed, expanding to more domains and larger size would enhance applicability. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing evaluations of strongest models.** The paper lacks evaluations of o1‑preview, o1‑mini, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, and Llama-3-405b. Reviewer 4 asks to test o1. (Reviewer 2, 4)
- **Several methodological questions not adequately addressed:** (i) What is the use of the unified formula database; what if formulas are not merged? (Reviewer 1) (ii) How does performance vary with number of variables in the formula (e.g., z=x+y vs. z=a*b+c/d)? (Reviewer 1) (iii) What is the default use case of this dataset in the next year? (Reviewer 2) (iv) Language and expansion plans: Figure 1 is in English but dataset is Chinese; any future expansion? (Reviewer 3)
