## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces ARQ, a mixed-precision quantization framework that uses reinforcement learning to optimize both accuracy and certified robustness (average certified radius) under resource constraints, leveraging randomized smoothing. Reviewers agree that the motivation (robustness-aware quantization) is strong and the experimental setup covers ResNet-20, ResNet-50, MobileNetV2 on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, showing that ARQ often matches or exceeds full-precision performance with only ~1.5% instructions. However, there is significant disagreement: Reviewer 1 (score 8) finds the work novel and thorough; Reviewers 2 and 3 (scores 3) judge the method as not novel, too complex, and insufficiently validated; Reviewer 4 (score 5) sees novelty in the optimization objective but finds baselines weak and comparisons incomplete. There is consensus that experiments are limited and many relevant competitors are missing.

### Strengths
- Novel use of average certified radius (ACR) as an optimization objective, simultaneously targeting accuracy and robustness (R1, R4).
- Strong motivation: existing quantization techniques degrade robustness on adversarial inputs; ARQ directly addresses this gap (R4).
- Well-explained background on randomized smoothing and certification (R4).
- ARQ consistently outperforms compared baselines (e.g., NIPQ) in accuracy and ACR across CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, often matching or exceeding FP32 performance with significantly reduced operations (R1, R3).
- Comprehensive experimental validation including ablation studies and comparative analysis (R1); paper is easy to read (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Incomplete baselines and comparisons:**  
  - No comparison with other robustness-aware mixed-precision quantization methods (e.g., those listed in Table5) (R3).  
  - Missing comparisons with advanced fixed- or mixed-precision quantization algorithms such as LSQ, LSQ+, HAWQ, SDQ, EMQ, OMPQ (R2, R3, R4).  
  - Current comparisons focus on a single baseline (NIPO) and do not cover methods that achieve higher accuracy with similar or lower bit-widths (R4).  
- **Limited scope of experiments:**  
  - Only evaluated on image classification tasks (CIFAR-10, ImageNet); no validation on detection, segmentation, VQA, or other tasks (R2, R3).  
  - Tested only on CNN architectures (ResNet, MobileNet); not on ViT, GPT-2, EfficientNet, or other modern architectures (R2, R4).  
  - Only two datasets; more challenging datasets (e.g., COCO2017, VOC) needed (R2).  
- **Methodological issues:**  
  - The method is not novel and too complex; techniques appear incremental (R2, R3).  
  - Lack of clear definition of “robustness”; is it against adversarial attacks? What adversarial method (PGD, FGSM, etc.)? (R2).  
  - The paper claims traditional quantization only focuses on accuracy, but MPQ inherently trades off accuracy vs. complexity; the two-objective view (accuracy + robustness) misses complexity as an objective (R2).  
  - Algorithm description is poor: terms `FullRobustCertify` and `IncrementalRobustCertify` are not clearly defined, harming reproducibility (R4).  
  - Only BitOps used as resource constraint; model size and other hardware considerations are ignored (R3).  
- **Empirical limitations:**  
  - ARQ requires significantly longer search time than other methods (Table2); impact of search time on final accuracy is not analyzed (R3).  
  - Sensitivity to hyperparameter choices; performance may require additional tuning (R2).  
  - Relies heavily on quality of original DNN training and Gaussian augmentation (R2).  
- **Missing practical details:**  
  - No analysis of memory consumption per layer type and per precision level (R1).  
  - No discussion of hardware implementation for mixed-precision inference (memory access patterns, data layout, transitioning between precision levels) (R1).  
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms:**  
  - How would ARQ adapt to sequence models/transformers and their attention mechanisms? (R1)  
  - Could RL be extended to dynamically adjust precision during inference based on input? (R1)  
  - What modifications to incremental randomized smoothing are needed for other certification methods? (R1)  
  - Why use RL instead of simpler optimization? (R2)
