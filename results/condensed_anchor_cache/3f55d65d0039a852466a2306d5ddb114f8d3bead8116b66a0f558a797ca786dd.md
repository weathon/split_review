## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Composer, a hierarchical world model that uses hypernetworks to generate lower-level transition functions conditioned on a high-level abstract state. The method aims to decompose long-horizon dynamics into simpler components, with state abstractions learned via a combination of prediction losses and contrastive learning. The model is tested on gridworld and Habitat 2.0 navigation tasks, with additional experiments on transferring between rooms and subgoal planning. All three reviewers identify significant gaps that prevent acceptance: the experiments lack baselines, the writing and formalization are incomplete, and the core contributions (abstraction, compositionality) are not convincingly demonstrated.

### Strengths

- The paper is clearly written and motivates the need for abstracted world models (R1). The general question of learning useful state abstractions for hierarchical transition functions is very relevant (R3).
- The use of hypernetworks to generate lower-level dynamics conditioned on a high-level abstraction is novel and an interesting approach (R1, R2).
- The method is tested in a realistic environment (Habitat 2.0), and good visualizations of the latent representations are provided (R2).
- Analysis of the method on Habitat and insights into learned abstractions (e.g., contrastive loss vs. vanilla) are valuable (R2).
- Reviewer 1 additionally highlights the ablative experiments in gridworld as a strength, though this is a minor point compared to the weaknesses.

### Weaknesses

- **No fair baselines or comparisons**: The experiments do not compare Composer against standard world models or model-free methods such as DreamerV3, TD-MPC2, DD-PPO (Habitat baseline), or THICK world-models (R1, R2, R3). Without such comparisons, the claimed benefits of the approach are unsubstantiated (R1, R2). Reviewer 1 specifically suggests comparing against DD-PPO, DreamerV3, TD-MPC2; Reviewer 2 mentions Dreamer, TDMPC, THICK; Reviewer 3 emphasizes that related work (e.g., Gumbsch et al., 2021) should be included and compared to.
- **Insufficient formalization and unclear writing**: The paper contains unfinished text (e.g., line 142: "Mention gridworld being top down POMDP. FIX") (R1, R3) and missing glue sentences (R2). Several equations are unexplained or poorly referenced: (1) hypernetwork H is not defined until later; (4) defines e but e is never used; (8) introduces s_T without proper definition; the high-level time period T is never described; the lambda regularizer is ambiguous (R3). Symbols are reused inconsistently (e.g., τ for time scale, temperature, and inner iterations; K used in caption but τ in text) (R2). The paper does not explain how the high-level timescale is updated or when inference occurs (R2).
- **Contrastive loss treated as an afterthought but actually essential**: The method relies on a contrastive loss (Eq. 11) to prevent collapse of the abstract representation, yet the paper presents it as only needed for scaling to complex environments (R1). The contrastive loss appears late in the method section (page 5) and is presented as a late addition rather than a core component, making the method seem less robust (R1, R3). The argument that humans also learn from labels is weak (R3). An ablation removing contrastive loss exists, but it is not identified as such and no other ablations are performed (R3).
- **Tasks do not clearly require abstraction**: The navigation tasks used (gridworld, Habitat) have state dynamics that do not fundamentally change between rooms; room abstractions are not necessary for successful navigation (R1). The paper does not clarify why these specific tasks benefit from the proposed abstraction. Language-guided navigation (e.g., "go pick up the toothbrush") would naturally require abstraction, but such tasks are not used (R1). Without a clear demonstration that the environment benefits from abstraction, the motivation for the method is weak (R1).
- **Claim of compositionality is not supported**: The name "Composer" implies compositional structure, but only trivial evidence of compositionality is shown (gridworld merging) (R2). The architecture has no particular bias toward creating compositional structures, and much stronger empirical evidence would be needed to support that claim (R2).
- **Preliminary/incomplete results**: Many statements in the results sections refer to ongoing or future work (e.g., subgoal learning, higher-level transition dynamics, replacing autoencoders with ViT) (R3). Key experiments (Section 4.4, 4.5) are not properly introduced in the methods; the corresponding figure (Fig. S10) is relegated to the appendix despite being central to the Habitat results (R3). The paper does not present a complete body of results (R3). For example, in Section 4.4 subgoal learning is left for future work, but in Section 4.5 there are 8 subgoals whose origin is unexplained (R3).
- **Missing related work section**: The paper has no related work section, which is essential to contextualize the contribution (R3). Relevant works in hierarchical RL, world models, and neuroscience-inspired AI (e.g., Gumbsch et al., 2021) are not discussed or compared (R3).
- **Neuroscience motivation is forced**: The neuroscience framing in the introduction does not add insight to the method and can be removed (R1). The paper references a "theory of the mammalian cortex" as if it were a single, established theory, which it is not (R3).
- **No action incorporation into higher-level model**: The higher-level transition function does not condition on actions, which limits its planning utility (R2).
- **Figure 8 missing expected comparison**: Figure 8 should show a comparison between the case with and without the high-level model (R2). The caption for Figure 9(a) should explain why episodic rewards decrease before the goal changes (R3).
- **Minor issues**: "Unsupervised" should be "self-supervised" (R3); code snippets promised in the appendix are not found (R3); equations finishing a sentence lack periods (R3); "kalman" should be "Kalman" (R3); "two … step(s)" (R3).
