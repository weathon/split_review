## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes generating unrestricted adversarial examples via stable diffusion by optimizing null-text embeddings at selected denoising steps, with perceptual loss to preserve image quality. Experiments on an ImageNet subset show improved adversarial transferability (12.59–50.3% average improvement over state-of-the-art unrestricted attacks) and better visual fidelity. The method is evaluated on multiple victim models (CNNs, ViTs, defenses). Code will be released.

### Strengths
- The approach of perturbing null-text embeddings of diffusion models to craft adversarial examples is considered novel by some reviewers (R1, R4), though R2 and R1 note that attacking the text modality has been studied before (e.g., [Xu et al. 2024]).
- R3 specifically highlights that this work constructs an adversarial attack in the image reconstruction process based on diffusion models for the first time.
- The generated unrestricted adversarial examples have relatively small modifications in content and look natural (R3, R1).
- Experimental results demonstrate higher transferability and improved image fidelity compared to existing unrestricted attack methods (R1, R2, R4). The method achieves the best Attack Success Rates (ASR) across various defense mechanisms (R4).
- The experiments cover a broader range of diverse victim model structures (R2). The paper is well-written and easy to understand (R2). Exploring adversarial transferability of unrestricted examples is helpful for robustness evaluation (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and prior work**: The claim in the Abstract that “we introduce perceptual loss into the adversarial attack process for the first time” is invalid. Perceptual loss (e.g., SSIM, LPIPS) has been used in adversarial attacks for years (e.g., [Jordan et al. 2019], [Karli et al. 2021]; also PPGD, LPA [ICLR 2021]). Some reviewers consider the overall method to be not particularly novel, as it applies stable diffusion to generate adversarial images (R2). R4 finds the null-text embedding optimization novel, but R1 notes that perturbing text modality is not new and [Xu et al. 2024] should be discussed and compared as a baseline.
- **Missing ablation study**: The inclusion of perceptual loss (γ) has no ablation experiment showing results with γ=0. Its effectiveness is unclear (R2, R4). The question is whether this loss provides a measurable benefit.
- **Missing baseline comparisons**: 
  - Unrestricted attack baselines are missing, including [Xu et al. 2024] (highly transferable diffusion attack) and [Dai et al. 2025/Adviaiff] (ECCV 2024) (R1).
  - Transferable attacks specifically designed for transferability (e.g., MI-FGSM, TI-FGSM, S2I-FGSM) are not compared (R1). The authors are strongly encouraged to include these.
- **Discrepancies in reported results**: Some experimental results differ significantly from original works. For example, in Table 1 the white-box ASR for ACA on RN-50 is reported as 72.10% but the original paper reports 88.3% (R1). The authors should double-check settings and explain the discrepancy.
- **Missing computational cost analysis**: A time and resource comparison (e.g., average inference time per image, GPU memory usage) with existing methods is needed (R1, R3, R4). Diffusion-based attacks incur heavy cost, and reviewers emphasize this is an important factor.
- **Missing no-reference image quality evaluation**: Table 2 mentions “FR” and “NR” in the title but does not actually report NR metrics. Unrestricted adversarial examples rely heavily on no-reference IQA (as in NCF and ACA), so this omission is significant (R3). Also, the terms “FR” and “NR” are not defined or used within the table (R2).
- **Correctness of mathematical formulation**: Equation 2 is inaccurate. Unrestricted adversarial examples do not follow an ℓ_p norm constraint; the key constraint of naturalness (as defined in NCF, ACA) is missing. Simply maximizing cross-entropy is insufficient (R3).
- **Motivation and trade-off**: The paper minimizes content change to avoid detections, but unrestricted adversarial examples only need to be natural, not necessarily close to the original. Minimizing content change may reduce adversarial transferability (as acknowledged in lines 400–405), and this trade-off is not sufficiently justified (R3).
- **Lack of deeper analysis**: The paper does not provide insights into *why* perturbing null-text embeddings reveals adversarial vulnerability, nor does it discuss limitations or failure cases (R1).
