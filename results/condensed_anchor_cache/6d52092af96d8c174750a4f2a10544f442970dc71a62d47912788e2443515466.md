## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses unsupervised taxonomy-adaptive cross-domain semantic segmentation, where source and target domains have different label spaces (e.g., novel, finer-grained, or differently named categories). It proposes DynAlign, a two-stage framework integrating traditional UDA with foundation models (LLM, CLIP, SAM) to bridge both image-level and label-level gaps. The framework maps source categories to target categories via GPT-4, uses SAM for region proposals, and fuses UDA and CLIP predictions to generate pseudo-labels for self-training. Experiments on GTA→IDD and GTA→Mapillary show improvements over prior methods.

### Strengths
1. **Problem significance.** The label-space domain gap in unsupervised domain adaptation is a practical, important, and under-explored problem [R1, R3, R4, R6]. The motivation is strong and the solution is clearly articulated [R6].
2. **Clear writing and presentation.** The paper is well-written with good formal representation, well-organized, and easy to follow [R1, R4, R5, R6].
3. **Innovative and reasonable approach.** The combination of UDA with foundation models (LLM for taxonomy mapping, CLIP for visual-text alignment, SAM for segmentation) is explainable, technically sound, and innovative [R2, R3, R5, R6]. The knowledge fusion strategy is reasonable [R5].
4. **Performance improvements.** The method achieves remarkable performance gains over prior open-vocabulary segmentation methods on the tested benchmarks [R4, R5]. Figure 4 demonstrates that training with generated pseudo-labels not only improves scores but also increases inference efficiency [R5].
5. **Straightforward design.** The method is straightforward with sufficient technical details and reliable techniques [R6]. It uses a natural two-stage pipeline.

### Weaknesses
1. **High complexity and computational cost.** The framework requires inference from at least three models (UDA model, SAM, CLIP) plus GPT-4, leading to high computational and memory demands [R2, R3, R6]. The paper should report and compare memory usage, training time, and model parameters; it currently lacks such analysis [R2, R6]. Practical usability is hindered [R3].
2. **Heuristic dependence and unclear specifications.**  
   * Semantic taxonomy mapping uses GPT-4 followed by human refinement (e.g., prompt tuning, taxonomy refinement), introducing human burden; details of how the “context description set” is generated and the average operation in Equation (2) are not fully specified [R2, R3].  
   * The criteria for label “ambiguity” (lines 1000–1001) are not defined, making reproduction difficult [R3].  
   * The confidence threshold in lines 372–373 lacks parametric analysis [R1].  
3. **Limited experimental scope.**  
   * **Missing baselines.** Only open-vocabulary segmentation methods are compared; the paper should also include UDA methods that can handle label shifts (e.g., taxonomy-adaptive DA) and recent domain-generalized plus open-vocabulary segmentation foundation models (as noted by a reviewer) [R1, R3].  
   * **Traditional UDA setting.** The authors should evaluate on the standard GTA5→Cityscapes scenario (smaller label-space gap) to isolate benefits [R3].  
   * **Reverse label gap.** Only coarse-to-fine (target has more classes) is tested; the method should also be verified when the target taxonomy is coarser (e.g., Mapillary→Cityscapes) to demonstrate generalizability [R3, R4].  
   * **Compatibility.** The claim of "integrating with any UDA model" (line 112) should be backed by tests with at least one other UDA model (e.g., MIC + DynAlign) [R5].  
4. **Contribution depth.** The performance gains largely come from pre-trained foundation models (SAM, CLIP, GPT-4) rather than novel technical contributions; the paper resembles a mapping/alignment technique that has been explored elsewhere and lacks theoretical depth [R4, R6]. The authors should more clearly articulate the specific design benefits beyond simply using foundation models in an unsupervised manner [R6].  
5. **Presentation issues.**  
   * Figure 2 is confusing: the two CLIP modules are not clearly distinguished, and the second CLIP appears to have no output arrow despite its predictions being used as pseudo-labels. The three steps in the figure do not match the claimed two-stage approach; the figure should be redrawn for clarity [R2].  
   * Symbol inconsistency: multi-scale visual feature is denoted \(F_V\) in the text but \(F_g\) in Figure 3 [R2].  
   * The term “multi-scale vision feature” in Section 5.4 should be “multi-scale visual feature” for consistency [R2].  
   * The meaning of “lane-marking 0.6” in Figure 3 is unclear [R2].  
   * Several papers are cited twice in the references (e.g., “Domain aligned clip for few-shot classification” and “Scaling open-vocabulary image segmentation with image-level labels”) [R4].  
6. **Missing experimental details.**  
   * Backbone of CLIP is not specified (e.g., ViT-B-16 vs. ViT-L-14) [R2].  
   * The process of generating the context description set for GPT-4 is not detailed (only briefly in supplementary) [R2, R3].  
   * It is unclear whether the framework relies purely on SAM for pixel grouping, with UDA and CLIP reclassifying each mask; clarification is needed [R3].  
7. **Robustness and failure analysis.**  
   * No tests with alternative foundation models (e.g., MobileSAM, Llama, another VLM) to show flexibility of the fusion approach [R5].  
   * No discussion or examples of failure cases when fusing UDA and foundation model predictions [R5].  
8. **Overemphasis on open-vocabulary comparisons.** The paper devotes much space to comparisons with open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, making it hard to discern contributions to UDA; authors should focus on UDA baselines [R6].  
9. **Minor issues.**  
   * Need justification why the problem is significant but has not been studied (lines 80–81) [R1].  
   * Code release for the benchmark would aid follow-up research [R2].  

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* One reviewer (R1) rated the paper 8, highlighting its strong writing, problem formulation, and meaningful task, while all other reviewers rated it 6. In contrast, reviewers R4 and R6 were more critical of the limited technical contribution and reliance on pre-trained models, and reviewer R3 emphasized the practical complexity and heuristic nature of the method.
