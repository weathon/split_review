## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes PolySketchFormer, which uses polynomial kernel sketching (TensorSketch) to approximate attention with linear complexity in context length and a block-based algorithm for causal masking. Reviewers generally find the idea interesting and the theoretical guarantees valuable. However, there is significant disagreement on the overall contribution and empirical validation. Two reviewers (R1, R2) rate it low (score 3), citing limited novelty, insufficient evaluation, and concerns about approximating softmax with a polynomial. Two reviewers (R3, R4) are moderately positive (scores 6 and 5), praising the insight but noting missing baselines and weak empirical evidence. One reviewer (R5) is strongly positive (score 8), highlighting the originality of the combination and practical significance. Common weaknesses include lack of comparisons with other efficient transformers, insufficient evidence of long-range benefits, clarity issues in the sketching algorithm, and questions about the polynomial approximation's adequacy.

### Strengths

- The core idea of using randomized sketches (TensorSketch) to reduce the exponential dependency on polynomial degree is insightful and novel as a combination (R1: "nice idea, well explained"; R3: "using randomized sketch to reduce O(nh^{p+1}) complexity is insightful"; R5: "clever combination", "originality").
- The procedure for computing $\mathrm{It}(\tilde{Q}\tilde{K}^{T})V$ is contributive and may apply to other linear Transformers (R3).
- Theoretical guarantees on approximation error are provided (R4: "provides guarantees on approximation"; R5: "solid theoretical results").
- Significance of addressing the quadratic bottleneck of attention; the method runs in truly subquadratic time and achieves perplexity comparable to softmax (R5; R3: "experiments verify effectiveness, speed, ability to handle long context").
- The block-based algorithm for causal masking is a practical optimization over Performer's cumulative sum approach (R1: noted; R5: "decent speed-up gains").
- The paper is relevant to a major bottleneck in transformers (R2: "reducing cost to linear is a relevant topic").
- The proposed method is simple and reasonable (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty**: TensorSketch and polynomial kernel sketching have been used before for similar purposes; the blocked cumulative sum is a straightforward optimization of an existing approach (R1: "None of the ideas are particularly novel"). R5 notes that the individual ideas are known but the combination is original.
- **Polynomial approximation concerns**: Using $f(s)=s^4$ behaves very differently from softmax (e.g., large values for opposite tokens as $x \to -\infty$ where $\exp(x) \to 0$), with no analysis of why this works or how to handle non-negativity and upper bounds (R1, R2). The restriction to degrees that are powers of 2 severely limits flexibility in approximating softmax (R3: W1). It is unclear why a single even-degree polynomial can approximate softmax without further justification (R2).
- **Insufficient empirical evaluation**:
    - Only perplexity reported; no tasks testing long-range dependencies (e.g., Long Range Arena) (R1, R4). Perplexity cannot distinguish whether the model effectively uses long-range attention (R1).
    - No large-scale experiments or NLP tasks (e.g., translation, conditional generation) (R2).
    - Few or no ablations on design choices (R2).
    - Missing comparisons with other efficient transformer baselines: Linformer, Nyströmformer (R3: W4); ChordMixer, LongFormer (R4); Hydra Attention, 3rd Order Polynomials (R2).
    - The proposed method at 8k/16k context is slower than baselines at 2k/4k and has higher perplexity; speedup over FlashAttention is not large, raising doubt about practical advantage (R4).
    - Performer appears slower than softmax baseline, contrary to original paper; requires clarification (R4).
    - All experiments cap at 16k context length; what limits further scaling? (R2)
- **Clarity and presentation issues**:
    - The algorithm for computing $A^{\otimes p}S$ using Fast Fourier Transform is not explained; the paper omits how the exponential runtime dependency on $p$ is avoided (R5).
    - Input/output dimensions of SRHT and TensorSRHT are unclear; a concrete example is needed (R3: W2).
    - Many specific technical confusions: Fig. 1 memory claim seems incorrect (4k attention matrix is ~32 MB); "sequential dependence" phrase unclear; double sketch construction on page 5 appears inconsistent with Ahle et al. (2020); parameter $m$ in Theorem 2.2 undefined; Theorem 3.1 is stated without mentioning algorithm and thus vacuous; JL moment properties not defined; runtime complexity of final algorithm not given (R1).
    - Typos and grammatical errors throughout (R1, R3, R5).
- **Missing references**: The work of Han, Avron, Shin on tensor sketch for entrywise transformed matrices is not cited (R1).
- **Proof concerns**: In Theorem 2.2, the constant $C$ may be too large, and its value is unspecified (R5). Theorem 2.2 also has undefined $m$ (R1, R5). The JL-moment derivation in the proof of Theorem 2.5 is not clearly justified (R5).
- **Data leak claim**: The claim that Performer has a data leak needs elaboration and verification (R4).
