## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes fVLM, a fine-grained vision-language model for CT image interpretation that performs anatomy-level contrastive learning between CT scans and radiology reports. It uses TotalSegmentator to segment anatomical structures and an LLM (Qwen) to extract corresponding report descriptions. A dual false-negative reduction approach addresses ambiguous patient-level pairings from healthy and similarly diseased samples. The authors curate MedVL-CT69K, the largest CT dataset to date (69,086 patients, 272,124 scans), and evaluate on 54 diagnosis tasks across 15 anatomies. In zero-shot classification, fVLM achieves 81.3% average AUC, surpassing CLIP (12.9% gain) and supervised methods (8.0% gain), and also outperforms state-of-the-art on CT-RATE and Rad-ChestCT benchmarks (absolute gains of 7.4% and 4.8%).

### Strengths
- **Anatomy-level fine-grained alignment**: Introducing contrastive learning at the anatomical region level (rather than entire images vs reports) is a novel and meaningful approach (R1, R4); the concept is “neat, simple, and straightforward” (R2).
- **Large-scale dataset**: The curated MedVL-CT69K dataset is the largest CT dataset to date and its collection is a significant contribution (R1, R4).
- **False-negative reduction**: The dual false-negative reduction strategy effectively addresses a known shortcoming of fine-grained alignment (R1, R3).
- **Strong zero-shot performance**: The model achieves substantial gains over CLIP and supervised baselines on 54 tasks, and outperforms prior work on two public benchmarks (R2, R3, R4).
- **Well-structured and clear writing**: The paper is well-organized with detailed ablation studies and visualizations; the insights and implementation details are clearly presented (R2, R4).
- **Promising results across tasks**: The approach shows relevance for both diagnosis and report generation (R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Incomplete and unclear experimental comparisons** (multiple reviewers):
  - Table 1 does not include methods from Table 3 (CT-CLIP, CT-VocabFine, CT-LiPro) and vice versa; it is unclear why these comparisons are missing (R1).
  - No explanation of how 2D CLIP methods were adapted to 3D for Table 1 and Table 2 evaluations (R1, R3).
  - Table 2 lacks comparisons to other CT-CLIP approaches (only natural image counterparts are shown) (R1).
  - In Table 3, it is ambiguous whether CT-CLIP models were fine-tuned on CT-RATE using the authors’ in-house data or another protocol; details needed (R3).
  - Comparisons with vision encoders from peer vision-language pre-training works for CT are needed (R2).
- **Limited report generation evaluation**: Only BLEU-4 and diagnostic accuracy are reported; reviewers unanimously request additional metrics: BLEU 1-3, ROUGE-L, METEOR (R2), clinical relevance metrics such as GREEN, RadFact (R1), RadCliQ, RaTESCore (R4). The assessment is considered insufficient for the claimed improvement on report generation (R1, R2, R4).
- **Methodology clarity and reproducibility** (multiple reviewers):
  - Image encoder architecture is vague: how 3D patch embeddings (patch size 16×16×32) are processed through a 2D ViT model is not clarified (R3).
  - No codebase or pre-trained model is released; dataset and weight availability is not stated (R1, R3, R4).
  - HU value clipping between -300 and 400 may not cover all abnormalities (e.g., lung ~ -1000, bone ~ 1000); it is unclear if the same range was used for all contrast phases (non-contrast, arterial, venous, delayed) and for CT-RATE experiments (R3).
- **Missing ablation on masking approach** (R1): No analysis comparing the proposed masking to intuitive baselines (e.g., anatomical cropping); the advantage of masking over cropping is not demonstrated.
- **t-SNE analysis concerns** (R1, R3, R4):
  - Embedding visualization lacks comparison with CT-CLIP to show whether clustering behavior differs (R1).
  - No fixed random seed used for t-SNE, affecting reproducibility (R3).
  - Limited separation between positive and negative cases in the shown clusters (R4).
- **Limited to CT scans with TotalSegmentator classes**: Approach relies on the anatomy segmentation tool and only applies to the 104 anatomical classes it provides (R1). Additionally, attributing sentences mentioning a structure to that structure’s image features can be misleading (e.g., pancreatitis often associated with the entire abdomen, not just the pancreas) (R2).
- **Data and scaling analysis gaps** (R4): The validation set has 36 disease labels while the test set has 54 – no explanation. Limited analysis on long-tailed disease distributions; radar plot does not show positive case numbers or normal/abnormal ratios. The scaling law curve is not analyzed with respect to data distribution (e.g., whether selection favored abnormal patients).
- **Concern about broader relevance** (R2, minority): Given the rapid advancement of open-source segmentation (TotalSegmentator, SAM) and LLM tools, it is unclear whether weakly-supervised vision-language pretraining for CT remains necessary; direct construction of semantic labels from text could turn the problem into fully supervised learning.
- **Reproducibility status**: Although asked by multiple reviewers, no statement is provided on whether the dataset, code, and model weights will be released (R1, R4).
