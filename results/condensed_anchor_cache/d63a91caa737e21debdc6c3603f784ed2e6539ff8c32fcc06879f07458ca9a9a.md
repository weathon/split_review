## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates memorization in video diffusion models (VDMs). It redefines content memorization and motion memorization, proposes corresponding metrics (GSSCD for content, OFS-k for motion), curates a text-prompt dataset prone to triggering memorization, systematically analyzes memorization across several open-source VDMs (including text-conditional and unconditional models), and introduces detection strategies. The study finds that memorization is widespread in VDMs and can arise from both image and video training data. Three reviewers (scores 5, 5, 3) find value in extending memorization analysis to the video domain and in the proposed metrics and detection methods; two reviewers (scores 3, 3) raise concerns about novelty, validation, and the strength of the contributions.

### Strengths
- The investigation addresses a critical and timely concern: memorization in VDMs poses legal and ethical risks as these models become widely used (R1, R2, R3).
- The paper redefines content and motion memorization, achieving better alignment with human perception and providing a more practical conceptual framework for privacy preservation (R1, R3).
- It proposes measuring frame-level memorization (via GSSCD) and motion memorization (via OFS-k), extending beyond the video-level memorization focus of prior work (R2, R3).
- The paper identifies several shortcomings in prior studies investigating memorization in VDMs, offering valuable insights (R2).
- It examines memorization from the image training dataset, which is beneficial and underexplored in the video context (R2).
- The paper proposes practical detection strategies for content and motion memorization, which are crucial for mitigating privacy risks (R1, R3).
- The experimental results are comprehensive and reliable, including systematic analysis across multiple VDMs and datasets (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited justification for new metrics over existing image diffusion model (IDM) metrics**: The paper introduces GSSCD as a frame-level metric but does not sufficiently explain why existing IDM metrics (e.g., SSCD) are inadequate for video memorization assessment (R1, R4). Reviewer 4 notes that GSSCD is a naive extension of SSCD and that such aggregation is already common in video copy detection research (e.g., VSCD competition by Meta AI); the paper does not address known limitations of SSCD in the diffusion model context (R4).
- **Validation is limited**: The new metrics are validated primarily via 1K manual annotations on a single dataset (WebVid-10M). More extensive validation across diverse datasets and automated evaluation methods would strengthen the findings (R1).
- **Missing qualitative comparison with prior work**: The paper repeatedly references Rahman et al. (2024) on motion memorization but fails to provide a direct, detailed comparative analysis. This weakens the argument for the proposed method’s novelty and effectiveness (R1).
- **Disentanglement of content and motion memorization is questionable**: The paper uses optical flow to measure motion memorization, but optical flow is also influenced by content, as illustrated in Figure 2 (first and third examples with similar content). This casts doubt on whether the two types can be cleanly separated (R2).
- **Detection methods do not solve the memorization problem**: The paper detects memorization but does not offer a solution for what to do after detection (e.g., if a user’s generated video is flagged, what action should the system take—discard and regenerate?). This limits practical applicability (R2).
- **Metrics computation needs clarification**: Table 2 reports metric values; specific equations or pseudocode for computing GSSCD and OFS-k would improve reproducibility. Notations such as OFS-k and NMF in Table 1 appear without explanation (R2, R3). Table 2 would also benefit from separating text-conditional (T2V) and unconditional (UV) generations into distinct columns (R1).
- **Paper organization and writing quality**: The paper reads like an experimental report; the contribution section is overly long, and frequent direct comparisons with prior work are presented in lengthy bullet points (R3). Table 1 uses red text and bold fonts without explanation (R3). The paper lacks coherent flow, with sections feeling disconnected (R2, R3). For example, Table 1 (left) is first referenced on page 6 after the reference to Table 1 (right), causing confusion (R2).
- **Figure 3 and Table 2 redundancy**: Figure 3 appears redundant because Table 2 already sufficiently demonstrates that ModelScope’s generations replicate image training data (R1).
- **Use of outdated video diffusion models**: The experiments rely on older VDMs (e.g., ModelScope). The paper should evaluate state-of-the-art models like Open-Sora, CogVideoX, or CogVideo to draw meaningful conclusions; the poor performance of older models prevents generalizable insights (R4). This is echoed by a reviewer question: can the proposed methods be applied to video diffusion transformers such as CogVideoX? (R3)
- **Minority reviewer (R4, score 3, confidence 5) strongly dismisses the contributions**: This reviewer states that the solutions and evaluations are “naive,” that GSSCD is a trivial extension, and that the paper does not contribute significantly to the field. They do not expect a rebuttal. This negative assessment should be weighed against the more positive evaluations from R2 and R3.
- **Missing discussion of limitations and code/dataset availability**: The paper does not discuss its own limitations, such as performance under varied video qualities or motion complexities. Providing access to implementation and datasets would enhance credibility and reproducibility (R1, questions).
