- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 6, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces SE(3)-Hyena, an equivariant long-convolutional operator for modeling global geometric context with sub-quadratic complexity. It claims to match or outperform equivariant self-attention on associative recall and N-body tasks while using less memory. All reviewers find the general direction valuable, but two reviewers (ratings 3) consider the experiments insufficient and the writing unclear; one reviewer (rating 6) finds the core idea elegant but also notes missing baselines and unclear design choices.

### Strengths
- Using a sub-quadratic operator (via FFT) to capture global context in 3D atomistic modeling is a potentially good idea (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The convolution simplification of the cross product to maintain equivariance is elegant (Reviewer 1).
- Applying deep learning to problems involving geometric context and architectural advances is a valuable research direction (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient experiments and missing baselines**  
   - The N-body results lack comparisons to latest reported numbers (e.g., SEGNN with 0.0043, CGENN with 0.0039; Reviewer 1 W3).  
   - Missing state-of-the-art baselines for dynamical system modeling: SEGNN, SAKE, SEGNO, GeoMFormer (Reviewer 2 W2).  
   - No results on standard benchmark datasets such as QM9, MD17, or larger-scale datasets to demonstrate scalability and effectiveness (Reviewers 2, 3 W2/W3).  
   - The RNA dataset lacks baselines (e.g., LEFTNet, MACE, EquiformerV2, SO3krates; Reviewer 1 W5).  
   - The associative recall experiment does not convincingly illustrate contextual learning capabilities: it lacks practical significance and many more equivariant baselines (Reviewer 1 W4).  
   - The memory and speed advantage (e.g., 20k tokens ×3.5 faster, ×175 longer context) is only shown on toy examples; real-world impact is unclear (Reviewer 3: experiments limited; Reviewer 2 asks for larger-scale datasets).

2. **Unclear motivation and design choices**  
   - The use of the cross product is not well motivated; reviewer asks why cross product (Hodge star dual of outer product) is introduced and notes it produces pseudovectors, making the model only SE(3)-equivariant rather than E(3)-equivariant (Reviewer 1 W2).  
   - The architecture only uses type-0 and type-1 vectors and allows only 1 channel for type-1 vectors, which significantly limits applicability to larger datasets (Reviewer 3 W5).  
   - The exact architecture is missing or very hard to understand; better high-level visualization covering all details is needed (Reviewer 3 W4). Specific writing gaps:  
     - Line 19: need one or two sentences about long convolutions to distinguish from message passing.  
     - Line 22: give dataset names early.  
     - Lines 32–38: give one or two sentences about equivariance.  
     - Figure 1: does not show O(N log N) complexity; looks like typical self-attention.  
     - Figure 2: too simplified; should include details of projection, geometric long convolution, gating.  
     - Lines 144–145: equation numbers missing; \(\hat{x}_i\) undefined.  
     - Lines 169–170: typo "To this end,".  
     - Lines 191–192: \(F^H\) undefined.  
     - Lines 237–239: statement about spherical harmonics with type-0/1 vectors being same as Cartesian needs clarification.  
     - Section 3 should be reflected in figures.  
     - Typos: line 144 function \(\Psi\) or hat notation undefined; line 231 \(\alpha_3\) should involve \(\mathbf{r}_1^\top\mathbf{r}_2\); line 242 LaTeX missing underscore; line 817 "hiddent dimension" → "hidden dimension"; logarithmic symbol should be \(\log\) and base usage inconsistent (Reviewer 1 W7).  

3. **Lack of discussion on related work**  
   - The paper should discuss other works using global features for equivariance (e.g., FastEGNN, Neural P³M; Reviewer 1 W1).  
   - No mention of whether the framework can be extended to other common equivariant networks as projection functions (e.g., SchNet, EGNN, MACE, HEGNN; Reviewer 1 W6).

4. **Reproducibility and verification concerns**  
   - Code was not submitted with the review; given unclear parts and unverified experiments, code should be provided during review (Reviewer 3: Reproducibility).  
   - Questions about actual complexity: is memory still O(N²)? If not, explain briefly (Reviewer 3 Q1).  
   - Weighting factor (lines 294–302) depends on token index; how is permutation of tokens handled? (Reviewer 3 Q2).  
   - Line 269: typical message passing (EGNN layer) is still used to encode context; that may be the true memory bottleneck, not the SE(3)-Hyena operator; reviewer notes other equivariant networks would share the same bottleneck (Reviewer 3 Q3).  
   - Figure 5 shows SE(3)-Hyena better than SE(3)-Transformer, but no explanation is given (Reviewer 3: Experiments Q1).  
   - Line 463: why only 2 layers? In such small models, global context may not exist, making comparison potentially unfair (Reviewer 3 Q2).  
   - Line 473–474: can a smaller SE(3)-Transformer be trained to fit on GPU for fairer comparison? (Reviewer 3 Q3).

5. **Minority reviewer contrast**  
   - Reviewer 1 (score 6) finds the cross-product simplification elegant and the general idea naturally motivated, but still lists several missing baselines and suggests improvements. Reviewers 2 and 3 (both score 3) are more critical, emphasizing that experiments are too limited, the architecture is unclear, and the claimed advantages are not convincingly demonstrated beyond toy examples.
