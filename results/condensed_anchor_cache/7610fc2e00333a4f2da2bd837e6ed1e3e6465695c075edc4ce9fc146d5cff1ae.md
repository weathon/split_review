## Merged Review

### Summary
POTEC is a novel two-stage off-policy learning method for contextual bandits with large discrete action spaces. It first clusters actions using a scalar embedding based on averaged marginal rewards, then learns a first-stage policy via a policy-gradient approach to select a cluster, and a second-stage policy via a regression-based approach (pairwise regression) to select a specific action within the chosen cluster. The paper provides a theoretical analysis of bias and variance for the proposed gradient estimator, showing unbiasedness under a local correctness condition and reduced variance via cluster-level importance weighting. Empirical results on synthetic and real-world recommendation datasets demonstrate improved performance over baselines such as IPS-PG and DR-PG, particularly with sparse action observations.

### Strengths
1. **Novel two-stage decomposition** – addresses the challenge of large action spaces in off-policy learning by combining policy-based and value-based methods hierarchically to avoid both bias and variance simultaneously. The intuition and motivation are well-justified.
2. **Rigorous theoretical analysis** – proves unbiasedness of the POTEC gradient estimator under a local correctness condition, provides bias/variance analysis, and shows that cluster-level importance weighting reduces variance compared to standard IPS.
3. **Clear technical presentation** – well-written, easy to follow, with a logical progression from problem formulation to solution. The discussion of related literature and context is good.
4. **Unified framework** – offers a general approach that includes policy- and regression-based methods as special cases, with theoretical guarantees for joint optimization of the two-stage policy.
5. **Comprehensive empirical evaluation** – extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world data demonstrate performance improvements over existing methods.

### Weaknesses
1. **Clustering method details and justification** – The paper does not provide a satisfactory general recipe for choosing the clustering algorithm, distance metric (e.g., k-means, hierarchical), or how action embeddings are initialized before clustering. The choice to cluster based on a scalar embedding of averaged marginal rewards (implicitly from reward estimation) is not clearly justified, and no baseline such as random bucketing is compared. These details are needed for reproducibility.
2. **Selection of the number of clusters** – The paper lacks clear guidance or a principled method for choosing the number of clusters *C* in real-world applications. The trade-offs associated with different cluster sizes (controlling reliance on policy-based vs. regression-based components) are mentioned but not thoroughly analyzed. (Multiple reviewers raised this.)
3. **Dependence on specific logging policy structure** – The logging policy in experiments is explicitly two-stage (Eq. 11), which aligns with POTEC’s structure. It is unclear how the algorithm would perform under more general logging policies that do not explicitly consider clusters or assume a hierarchical reward decomposition (e.g., reward function defined simply as *r(x,a)* rather than *r(x,c) + r(x,a|c)*). The paper also assumes the logging policy *π₀* is known; it does not discuss performance when it must be estimated. (Reviewer 4 is willing to raise score if these issues are addressed. Reviewer 2 similarly questions whether the theory fundamentally requires policy at the first stage and value at the second.)
4. **Condition for full cluster support** – The unbiasedness condition requires full cluster support in the logging policy. The paper does not discuss how to check this condition in practice or what happens if it is violated. (Reviewer 1.)
5. **Regression-based second-stage bias** – The paper acknowledges that regression-based approaches suffer from bias due to model capacity. However, if the second-stage regression is assumed to discriminate well within clusters, it seems this same assumption could make a global supervised learning fit low-error, weakening the argument for the two-stage decomposition. This criticism is not addressed. (Reviewer 2.)
6. **Missing comparisons and experiments** – No direct experimental comparison with the most related prior work (Saito et al., 2023) is provided. The paper also does not experiment with logging policies that are not two-stage, limiting the assessment of generalizability. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4.)
7. **Test data construction details** – The paper does not clearly describe how test data is constructed in either synthetic or real-world experiments (e.g., overlap of contexts with training data, how much overlap). (Reviewers 1, 3.)
8. **Minor technical concern** – Equation (3) may contain a typo: the third variance term on the RHS might need to be *\hat{q}(x, a) s_θ(x, a)*. A derivation in the appendix would help. (Reviewer 2.)
9. **Potential limitations not mentioned** – The paper does not explicitly discuss limitations of POTEC, such as sensitivity to clustering quality, computational overhead, or performance in environments where the two-stage assumption is unnatural. (Reviewer 3.)

*Contrast among reviewers:* While three reviewers rate the paper highly (8), one reviewer (Reviewer 3) gives a 6, noting that clarity could be improved and requesting more detail on clustering choice, comparison with Saito et al., and limitations. Reviewer 4 is also critical of the logging policy assumption but indicates willingness to raise the score if concerns are addressed.
