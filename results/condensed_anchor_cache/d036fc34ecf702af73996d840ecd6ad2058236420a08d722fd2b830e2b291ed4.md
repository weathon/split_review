## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces ToMValley, a benchmark of 1,100 social contexts and 78,100 questions (belief, emotion, intention, action) to evaluate LLMs’ theory of mind (ToM) in realistic settings. All 71 questions per context are fed in a single prompt and answers extracted from the output – also used for CoT evaluation. The benchmark is entirely LLM-generated (GPT-4-Turbo) with human validation on only 5% of the dataset. Ten LLMs are tested; the best lags behind human performance by 11% on average. Analyses highlight “fail in the middle” and multi-hop reasoning difficulties. Reviewers’ scores (3,5,3,3,5) reflect a split between those who see the benchmark as novel and those who find it incremental and methodologically flawed. The merged review captures praise for scope and analyses but serious concerns about the evaluation protocol, validation depth, and novelty relative to prior work.

### Strengths

- The benchmark explicitly models dynamic mental states across multiple scenarios and dependencies among beliefs, emotions, intentions, and actions – a step toward holistic ToM evaluation.
- Question types (understanding, influence, transformation) are intriguing and designed to test different dimensions of reasoning.
- The framework generates diverse social contexts, detailed character profiles, and relationships, covering a broader range of scenarios and settings than previous datasets (e.g., OpenToM, BigToM).
- Dialogue generation uses carefully constructed prompts, and the focus on mental states (especially emotions and psychological aspects) is a distinct contribution compared to earlier work that often mixes objective factors.
- Ten LLMs are evaluated, providing a comprehensive performance comparison; manual verification and a human baseline are included.
- The analysis of whether including/excluding character profiles affects performance, the “fail in the middle” phenomenon, and the models’ limitations in compositional (multi-hop) reasoning are interesting and shed new light on LLM ToM capabilities.
- The paper provides a thorough quality assessment (five criteria on a Likert scale) and reports per-criterion scores; the dataset is large and systematically constructed.
- Some reviewers noted the benchmark is well motivated and addresses a real gap between abstract ToM tests and realistic usage contexts.

### Weaknesses

- **Evaluation protocol is fundamentally flawed**: All 71 questions are presented in a single prompt (Figure 20), and answers are parsed from a single output. This introduces strong ordering effects, makes answers interdependent, and is unsuitable for benchmarking. CoT is also applied to the entire batch, likely explaining why CoT shows no improvement. **More than one reviewer** (1,2) argued that each question should be tested independently.
- **Insufficient human validation**: Only 5% of the dataset was manually verified. Given the complexity of the multi-step generation pipeline and known LLM difficulties with ToM, a far larger fraction (≥33% or ideally 100%) is needed. The reported per-criterion quality (≈90%) is not complemented by a holistic metric showing how many items meet *all* five criteria simultaneously (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited novelty / incremental contribution**: The benchmark largely synthesizes ideas from OpenToM (character profiles, narratives) and BigToM (social settings, dynamic mental states) and uses the same LLM-generation strategy. Claims that prior work lacks dynamic states, interdependent states, and social context are overstated – OpenToM includes character profiles, FanToM has belief evolution over dialogue turns, and MMToM-QA/MuMA-ToM explore conditioned mental states. Several existing benchmarks (EmoBench, Infant Cognition Benchmark, dialogue-focused ToM works) are missing from Table 2. The overall approach is not a radical departure (Reviewers 4,2,5).
- **Circularity concerns**: Since the dataset is generated by an LLM (GPT-4-Turbo), the capability being evaluated (ToM) may also be required for faithful generation. Steps 2 and 3 (deciding mental state sketches and ensuring dialogues are faithful to those dynamics) rely on the LLM’s own ToM abilities, which have not been validated. **Multiple reviewers** (3,5) raised this issue and questioned how answers are selected and how wrong options are guaranteed incorrect.
- **Weak construct validity and unclear definitions**: The benchmark claims to test “process-level evaluation” but never defines the term (Reviewer 5). The question types (understanding, influence, transformation) and categories (belief, emotion, intention, action) are only briefly mentioned without clear justification or definitions. For influence questions on emotion and intention, the specific relationships relied upon are not explained (Reviewers 2,5). It is also not made clear whether social context (profiles, relationships) is essential for arriving at the correct answer or merely adds diversity.
- **Missing ablations and necessary comparisons**: 
  - For influence-type questions (scenario1→scenario2), would performance improve if only two scenarios were provided? The impact of context length is not examined (Reviewer 2).
  - The paper does not test models specifically designed for ToM (Sim-ToM, SymbolicToM, BIP-ALM) nor GPT-4o, which has shown gains on other ToM benchmarks (Reviewer 5).
  - The “fail in the middle” analysis and discussion of compositional reasoning echo findings from prior work; deeper, dataset-specific insights are lacking (Reviewer 4).
- **Ambiguity in questions and answer design**: Some questions (e.g., “What is Angela Hwang’s belief?”) lack sufficient context to determine the exact belief being asked about, making the dataset potentially ambiguous. The method for generating answer options and ensuring only one correct answer is not fully detailed, especially for open-ended “change across all scenarios” questions (Reviewers 3,4).
- **Methodological concerns**:
  - **Myers-Briggs personality types** are used in character profiles despite their lack of scientific validity; alternatives like Big Five / OCEAN would be more sound (Reviewer 3).
  - **Confidence intervals are missing** from all experimental results (Reviewer 3).
  - Interactivity (e.g., conversations) may be more realistic than static inputs; the current direct input approach may not capture real-world ToM use (Reviewer 2).
  - Human evaluation may be biased if all five annotators come from the same lab (Reviewer 5).
- **Presentation issues**: 
  - Abstract and introduction are vague; specific details about what exactly is being tested, data generation, validation, and LLM performance should be stated clearly (Reviewer 3).
  - Figure 3 would be better as a table; many grammar issues remain (Reviewers 1,3).
  - Limitations are buried in the appendix rather than in the main text (Reviewer 3).
  - “Process-level evaluation” paragraph (Section 2.2) lacks coherence (Reviewer 5).
  - Typo: line 250 “toM → ToM”; line 789 “qomplexity → complexity” (Reviewers 3,5).
