## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces ProteiNexus, a pretraining approach for protein representation learning that integrates sequential and structural information. It proposes a structural encoding mechanism to capture fine-grained distance details and spatial residue positioning, using a Transformer encoder with a masked residue prediction and a denoising objective (recovering atom coordinates from noise-injected distance representations). After pretraining on a dataset including native structures and decoys from DeepAccNet, lightweight decoders are fine-tuned for various downstream tasks. The authors report state-of-the-art results on model quality assessment, mutation stability prediction, fold and EC classification, and (antibody) protein design benchmarks. Reviewer 2 rated the paper 5, commending the originality and thoroughness; Reviewers 1 and 3 rated 3, citing novelty concerns and experimental flaws.

### Strengths

- **Relevance and ambition**: Addresses the important problem of learning protein representations that integrate both sequence and structure. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Comprehensive downstream evaluation**: The model is tested on a broad set of tasks (model quality assessment, binding affinity, fold/EC classification, protein design, antibody design), demonstrating competitive or leadership results. (Reviewer 1)
- **Simple yet effective architecture**: The method for combining sequential and structural encoding through a Transformer is seen as elegant and practically effective. (Reviewer 2)
- **Experimental thoroughness**: Extensive experimental results and implementation details are provided, showing careful work. (Reviewer 2)
- **Potential as a foundation model**: The pretrained model could serve as a general-purpose tool in protein engineering and discovery. (Reviewers 2, 3)

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty concerns**: Both Reviewers 1 and 3 questioned the novelty. Reviewer 1 noted that most method description is in the appendix and the paper focuses on benchmarking rather than a novel method. Reviewer 3 stated key components are adapted from prior work without acknowledgment (Transformer design similar to Shan et al. 2022; pretraining objectives resembling Zhang et al. 2023). Reviewer 2 acknowledged that combining sequence and structural information and encoding distance data are not new, and the paper does not clearly articulate how it fundamentally differs from existing methods. (Strong disagreement remains between Reviewer 2’s positive view and the others’ skepticism.)

- **Incomplete related work**: Reviewer 3 pointed out that significant work in protein language models (TAPE, ProtTrans, Ankh, ProGen2) and structure models (ProNet, CDConv) is missing. There is no discussion of how the proposed method connects to or improves upon these. (Reviewer 3)

- **Data leakage in experiments**: This is a critical issue flagged by Reviewers 1 and 3.  
  – Pretraining data includes PDB structures up to May 1, 2023, which covers targets from CASP14 and CASP15, creating a potential overlap with evaluation sets for model quality assessment and protein design.  
  – For protein design, the ablation in Appendix E.3 shows a substantial performance drop when overlapping test structures are removed, indicating leakage.  
  – Reviewer 1 noted homology reduction was done only to 70% sequence identity (standard is 30%), raising additional concerns about contamination of test sets.

- **Missing baselines and unfair comparisons**:  
  – For model quality assessment, Reviewer 3 noted the essential baselines from the Atom3D benchmark (e.g., Ornate, 3DCNN) are absent.  
  – For mutation stability prediction (binding affinity), the traditional physics-based method FlexDDG is not included. Dataset splits are not described, and cross-validation is not performed.  
  – For EC/fold classification, CDConv and ESM-GearNet are missing; the method falls behind leading approaches in these benchmarks.  
  – Reviewer 1 questioned whether compared methods were evaluated on exactly the same train/test splits (many results are taken from other papers without confirmation).

- **Insufficient ablation and analysis**:  
  – Reviewer 1: The ablation study is relegated to the appendix and is never discussed in the main paper; the crucial ablation of fine-tuning only the lightweight decoder vs. fine-tuning the entire model (or using a fixed encoder) is missing.  
  – Reviewer 2 questioned why the decoder architecture is not fixed across tasks, and whether the encoder is fine-tuned or frozen (not clarified).  
  – Reviewer 3 asked about how noise levels are determined and whether data efficiency (performance with varying pretraining sizes) was evaluated.

- **Misleading metrics for design tasks**: Reviewer 3 argued that perplexity and amino acid recovery (aar) focus on local sequence recovery and can inflate performance. The standard in the field is to use structure recovery via AlphaFold2 (AF2) as a more meaningful metric.

- **Methodological and clarity issues**:  
  – The critique of graph-based representations (inability to capture atom details and long-range interactions) lacks supporting evidence; the method itself ignores side-chain atoms and focuses only on backbone (Reviewer 3).  
  – Discretizing coordinates and using one-hot encoding for distance bins discards fine-grained relative distance information (Reviewer 1).  
  – The motivation for using a Transformer over a fully connected graph is unclear (a fully connected graph with attention is mathematically identical).  
  – The claim “groundbreaking” in the introduction is an exaggeration (Reviewer 3).  
  – Specific details are missing: the “distance tokenizer” is not introduced; masking definition (identity vs. coordinates) is ambiguous; how the denoising objective “encourages” recovery is not explained (Reviewer 1).  
  – Reviewer 1 noted minor issues: SARS-CoV2 is incorrectly described as an antibody; references need fixing.
