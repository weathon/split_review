## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper benchmarks post-training quantization (PTQ) for Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLMs (Mixtral-8x7B, DeepSeek-MoE-16B-base) across six tasks, proposing heuristics for bit allocation based on expert usage frequency, outlier scoring, and block importance. The work is the first dedicated benchmark for MoE PTQ and offers practical insights into varying quantization granularity across MoE components. However, multiple reviewers identify significant flaws in experimental design, contradictory conclusions, lack of novelty, poor presentation, and insufficient validation.

### Strengths
- First comprehensive benchmark specifically targeting post-training quantization for MoE architectures, filling a gap in the literature (R2, R5).
- Core insight—that expert usage frequency and other MoE structure properties can guide mixed-precision quantization—is intuitive and practically relevant (R1, R3, R4, R5).
- Well-structured and easy to follow (R1, R3). R4 notes the question-answer format is clear despite being unconventional.
- Empirical evaluation across two representative MoE models (Mixtral-8x7B, DeepSeek-MoE-16B-base) and six tasks supports many claims (R1, R3).
- The proposed enhancements (linear weight outlier scorer, MoE block importance predictor) are novel and reduce the need for repetitive quantization experiments (R1, R3).
- Directly addresses the memory-bound inference bottleneck of MoE LLMs (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Contradictory conclusions on block importance (R2).** Section 4.2 (Q3) asserts first blocks are more important and need higher bits, but Figure 5 in Section 5.3 shows the last blocks have the lowest importance scores, implying highest importance. This directly contradicts the earlier claim. R2 requests reformulation to avoid conflict.
- **Flawed experimental design for layer sensitivity (R2).** In Q2, experts in FFNN layers are randomly selected for quantization, not necessarily the activated ones. This allows the possibility that 2-bit quantized experts were never used while 4-bit experts were idle, invalidating the conclusion that FFNN quantization is less impactful than attention layer quantization. R2 suggests using currently activated experts instead.
- **Q1 conclusion not novel (R2).** The finding that expert usage frequency guides compression is already established in prior work (e.g., Li et al. 2023, Huang et al. 2024) [1][2]. R1 and others considered this well-justified, but R2 notes lack of novelty.
- **Weight-activation quantization results are unreliable and contradictory (R2, R4).** Table 3 shows FP16 is not optimal in most cases, which is unusual; also, accuracies on Winogrande (~50%), HellaSwag (~25%), and PIQA (~50%) are near guessing, making differences meaningless (R4). R2 argues this undermines the claim that weight-only conclusions generalize to weight-activation quantization. R2 requests in-depth analysis or alternative proof.
- **Proposed block importance predictor shows marginal/inconsistent gains (R3).** In Table 5, compared to simple baselines like FIRST and RANDOM, the predictor does not substantially improve MMLU accuracy. R3 suggests the predictor could be strengthened (e.g., via RL or incorporating MSE loss).
- **Missing baselines (R4).** Table 1 lacks original FP16 accuracy and uniform quantization baselines (e.g., NF3, 3-bit GPTQ, SqueezeLLM applied to all experts) to assess the benefit of mixed compression.
- **Limited to FFNN MoE variants (R5).** No experiments on Attention-based MoE architectures, leaving uncertainty about generalizability of the heuristics.
- **Calibration data concerns (R5).** The block importance predictor is trained solely on WikiText, which may not generalize to tasks with different data distributions (e.g., code generation, domain texts). Normalization in Equation (3) for expert usage frequency is not fully specified (e.g., min-max vs. softmax), hindering reproducibility.
- **Lack of hardware feasibility discussion (R5).** Methods are pseudo-quantization; heterogeneous bit allocation across blocks, experts, and layers leads to unstructured mixed precision that complicates deployment on fixed-point hardware (INT8/INT4). Memory access overhead and sparse routing support are not addressed.
- **Predictor overhead not quantified (R5).** Training a predictor for block importance may introduce computational and memory overhead contradicting efficiency goals. Inference-time impact is not discussed.
- **Poor presentation and organization (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5).** Multiple reviewers cite issues: the core insight (mixed compression + MoE) should be emphasized earlier (R4); extended study (Section 5) reads as a potpourri of mini-papers rather than a cohesive method (R4); the paper’s structure puts ablations before main results (R4); reviewer also notes the title is overstated, implying a broader scope than covering sensitivity of MoE components (R3). R2 and R5 also note insufficient analysis of root causes for expert usage patterns, load imbalance, and outlier-task relationships (R1).
