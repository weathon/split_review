## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes HyperAttention, an approximate attention mechanism with near-linear time complexity under assumptions on column/row norms of the attention matrix. The algorithm uses LSH to identify large entries, then fast approximate matrix multiplication via row sampling. Theoretical guarantees are provided and generalizations to other sketching algorithms are made. Empirically, HyperAttention is evaluated on long-context LLM tasks: it makes ChatGLM2 inference 50% faster on 32k context (perplexity 5.6→6.3), and on 131k context with causal masking achieves 5× speedup on a single attention layer. The work is similar in spirit to KDEformer but does not rely on kernel density estimation.

### Strengths
- The proposed algorithm has nearly linear runtime in theory (under plausible assumptions on column norm) and demonstrates significant speedups in practice.
- Provides a new theoretically-grounded approximation algorithm for attention layers, with a proof strategy that clearly associates assumed properties of the attention matrix with guarantees.
- Although based on the same primitive sortLSH as prior work (KDEformer), the actual algorithm is new and generalizes to other sketching algorithms, strengthening connections to the sketching literature.
- The overall proof strategy is clearly described, with high-level intuition and interpretation of proof parameters so readers can follow the main logic.
- The idea of separating into multiple matrices when performing causal masking is a very nice and interesting contribution.
- The algorithm features a modular design that easily accommodates integration with fast low-level implementations like FlashAttention.

### Weaknesses
- **Empirical evaluation lacks alignment with prior work.** HyperAttention is not compared against KDEformer, despite sharing many similarities and claiming to be more practical (no KDE). This makes it difficult to assess practical improvements. Prior work (KDEformer) evaluated on BigGAN, ImageNet Classification, and Long Range Arena, comparing against Reformer, Performer, etc.
- **End-to-end training experiments are missing.** Only forward/backward computation time was evaluated, not full training.
- **Runtime breakdown is not provided.** It is unclear whether the reported speedups (Figure 3(a), 3(b), Figure 4) include the time to generate the mask \(M_h\). Algorithm 1 may not be GPU-friendly; a breakdown of time spent in different procedures would help readers understand where bottlenecks shift. (Reviewer 3 question: “Are the speedups end-to-end?”)
- **A key assumption of the theoretical analysis is not verified experimentally.** The assumption that for any \(i \in [n]\) there exists \(\alpha = n^{o(1)}\) such that \(\|D^{-1}A \cdot e^{(i)}\|_2 \leq \frac{\alpha}{n}\) is not tested on real attention matrices. Verification would strengthen the theory.
- **Choice of layers to replace with HyperAttention feels arbitrary.** The selection of which layers to approximate is not explained; it would be interesting to understand why it works in certain cases and not others.
- **Missing details on hyperparameter \(m\) (Algorithm 2).** The values of \(m\) used in experiments are not enumerated. Is it based on theoretical bounds or a separate hyperparameter? For long sequence results, where accuracy cannot be verified, listing parameters is critical.
- **Comparison with KDEformer’s assumptions is insufficient.** Although HyperAttention makes different (potentially more realistic) assumptions, a detailed discussion of how they relate to KDEformer is missing, which would clarify the theoretical contribution.
- **Presentation of theory could be improved.** Reviewer 1 notes that the theory part could benefit from inline comments in algorithm descriptions and explicit explanation of which steps rely on which assumptions.
- **Minor:** Eq (10) in Section 1.2 likely refers to Eq (1); Eq (5) in Appendix A needs a clearer reference to the result used in its proof.
