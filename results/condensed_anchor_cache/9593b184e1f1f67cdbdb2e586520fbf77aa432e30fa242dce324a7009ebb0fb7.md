## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DynamicsDiffusion, a denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM) applied to molecular dynamics (MD) trajectory generation. The model is trained to predict the total energy per frame; forces are obtained via Jacobian to ensure conservative fields. Inpainting and variation generation are used to sample transitions between states. Experiments are conducted on a 2D double-well Brownian particle system and alanine dipeptide MD data.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important problem: rare event sampling and transition path generation in MD simulations.
- Applying diffusion models to MD trajectory generation is a novel application; the inpainting and variation generation ideas for conditioning on specific frames are interesting and are shown to work.
- The energy-prediction formulation (with forces derived as Jacobian of the total energy) is physically motivated, guaranteeing conservative forces.
- Qualitative results (e.g., sampling the two wells, conditional density spread) are nice.
- The method is technically sound for modeling the distribution of transition paths.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty / prior art**: Several reviewers note that the key idea (using generative modeling, specifically DDPM, for MD simulation) has been done in prior works [1,2,3] (archived ≥6 months, accepted at JCTC, AAAI, ICLR). The paper does not articulate significant modifications to the DDPM framework nor a clear technical distinction from these works.
- **Clarity and presentation are poor**: Many typos, missing parentheses/brackets for citations, missing “Figure”/“Table” references, LaTeX errors (page 18), low-resolution figures (Fig. 8, 11, axes illegible). The trajectory vs. frame notation ($x$, $\boldsymbol{x}_i$, $p(x)$) is confusing; it is unclear whether $x$ denotes a trajectory or a single configuration. Reference style (author names scattered in text) hinders readability. Forward references are missing.
- **Experimental validation is insufficient**:
  - Only two small systems are tested (2D double-well and alanine dipeptide); large systems (e.g., protein dynamics) are needed to support claims.
  - Experiments focus on free energy estimates (FES) derived from the equilibrium distribution, which can also be obtained from standard (frame-level) diffusion models. Dynamical properties that require trajectory generation are not evaluated.
  - The training data already covers the Boltzmann distribution (high enough temperature to sample transitions); correct sampling is not surprising. Rare event sampling capability is not convincingly demonstrated – the alanine dipeptide transition along $\psi$ has a relatively low barrier and can be sampled directly.
  - Inpainting result (Fig. 4c) shows a conditioned point that is not connected to other states; missing explanation.
  - Ablation study results (self-attention, encoder-only) are not statistically significant given the error bars.
  - No evidence of generalizability to longer trajectories or larger systems; the UNet architecture uses a fixed `3N` input vector and no atom type information, meaning the model is molecule-specific and cannot be transferred.
  - Physical validity of generated paths is not sufficiently validated; visualization of example paths or quantitative dynamical metrics are missing.
- **Missing related work**: Several relevant works on learning MD trajectories with diffusion models (arXiv:2305.18046) and rare event sampling with differentiable simulation (arXiv:2301.03480) are omitted. Also missing are PIPS (Holdijk et al. 2022) and ML-based transition path sampling methods that address the same problem.
- **Methodological concerns**:
  - Predicting total energy per frame and summing implies independence between frames, which is invalid for trajectory modeling (breaking issue for some reviewers).
  - Jacobian computation is more costly than direct force/noise prediction; the cost/accuracy trade-off is only benchmarked on the 2D toy system, not on larger systems.
  - The method cannot provide the probability of the sampled rare events.
  - Self-attention implementation details within the UNet are missing.
  - The term “enhanced sampling” is misused (the method does not introduce a bias potential).
  - Conditioning on temperature is shown, but conditioning on other global parameters (e.g., friction) is not validated.
  - The claim of generating an “ensemble of reactive trajectories” is not experimentally validated.
  - The generalization claim about observing a rare transition along $\psi$ in alanine dipeptide is physically unrealistic because the path flows over a region of high energy.
- **Specific unanswered questions** overlap with criticisms:
  - What is the mathematical expression of the energy formulation?
  - What is the performance difference between the two proposed sampling methods?
  - Which part of the results shows that the method promotes symmetry?
  - Table 9 is incorrectly referenced (ablation of self-attention vs. Jacobian table).
  - How is self-attention implemented with the UNet architecture?
  - Why is the left green condition not connected in Fig. 4c?
  - How can the method be used when the probability of rare events is needed?
