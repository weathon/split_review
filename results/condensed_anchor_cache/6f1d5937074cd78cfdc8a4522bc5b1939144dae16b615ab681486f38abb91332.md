## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes the RED QUEEN ATTACK, a multi-turn jailbreak strategy that conceals malicious intent under the guise of preventing harm. Using 40 scenarios across 14 harmful categories, it generates 56k multi-turn data points. Experiments on four LLM families show high vulnerability: 87.62% ASR on GPT-4o and 75.4% on Llama3-70B. Larger models are more susceptible, and multi-turn structure and concealment strategies drive success. The authors also introduce RED QUEEN GUARD, a DPO-based mitigation that reduces ASR below 1% while maintaining benchmark performance. Reviewers find the topic timely and the dataset thorough, but note concerns about harmfulness definitions, missing comparisons with existing multi-turn attacks, and limited contribution of the mitigation. One reviewer (score 5) was more positive, citing the hot topic, clear structure, and dataset value; the other three (score 3) were more critical of novelty, evaluation, and methodological rigor.

### Strengths
- Multi-turn jailbreak attacks are under-studied and practically relevant, addressing a real-world issue (R1, R3).
- The dataset is thorough and carefully designed, with 56k data points, clustering, and manual validation (R1, R3).
- The ablation study is comprehensive, analyzing key factors behind attack success (R2, R4).
- The exploration of RED QUEEN GUARD as a mitigation shows promising directions for safety alignment (R1, R4).
- The paper is well-structured and clearly presented (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing baseline comparisons (all reviewers):** No comparison with existing multi-turn jailbreak attacks (e.g., Crescendo [R3]; DeepInception, CoU, CoA, Chiper-based, ASCII Art attacks [R4]) or defense methods. No jailbreak attack or defense baselines are included in the evaluation. For mitigation, only DPO is explored; SFT on safe responses is not even considered as a primary baseline (R4). Comparisons beyond HH-RLHF, such as adversarial training or safety-specific RL techniques, are needed (R1).
- **Dataset and harmfulness concerns (R1, R2):** The scenarios are highly controlled and experimentative (R1). The definition of harm is unclear—many responses labeled as “harmful” (e.g., Figure 5 showing the LLM listing items like “notes” or “materials” as potential evidence) are vague, common-sense suggestions, not actual malicious guidance. The LLM not refusing does not inherently mean it produced dangerous content; “success” cases often feel overstated (R1). The attack is template-based and requires human annotators to modify prompts, limiting extensibility (R2).
- **Evaluation judgment issues (R2, R4):** The evaluation prompt is too sensitive to determining harmful responses, even for responses unrelated to the task, introducing bias (R2). The study only uses accuracy as a metric; TPR/FPR/F1/AUC are necessary, and the “calibrated prompt” appears overfit to the 100 prompts used (R4).
- **Missing statistical rigor (R4):** Model inference with temperature introduces randomness; no statistical significance analysis is provided.
- **Limited contribution and novelty (R3, R4):** Multi-turn jailbreak attacks already exist (e.g., Crescendo), and the paper does not demonstrate unique insights into vulnerabilities of multi-turn interactions (R3). RED QUEEN GUARD’s contribution is limited—it is essentially a sampled version of the attack dataset supplemented with safe responses; only adversarial training (DPO) is evaluated, and generalization to out-of-distribution attacks (e.g., other multi-turn jailbreaks) is unclear (R4).
- **Insufficient detail on key evidence (R1, R2):** Table 5 and Figure 6 present more persuasive data points, but no examples or details are provided for those (R1). The mitigation section does not clarify whether training and testing sets overlap; it only says training data is sampled from successful jailbreaks (R2).
- **Generalizability questions (R1, R4):** It is unclear how the attack generalizes to wild harmful actions/targets or to weaker/smaller models beyond those tested (R1, R4). Could multi-turn prompts be stacked as a single-turn prompt (with LLM help) and still achieve similar effects? (R4).
