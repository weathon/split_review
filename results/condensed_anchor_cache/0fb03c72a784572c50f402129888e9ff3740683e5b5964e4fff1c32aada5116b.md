## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes “Bridged Transfer,” a two-stage framework that first fine-tunes a pre-trained model on synthetic images from text-to-image generative models and then adapts on real target data. It also introduces dataset style inversion (DSI) to align the style of generated images with the target dataset. The method is evaluated on 10 classification datasets and 5 models, showing up to 30% accuracy gains. Reviewers note systematic experiments and practical potential, but raise concerns about inadequate baselines, data/compute comparisons, missing ablations, and visual clarity.

### Strengths
- **Novel observation**: The paper first documents that naïve mixing of synthetic and real images does not consistently improve transfer learning due to the distribution gap, and validates this across multiple datasets (Reviewer 2, high confidence).
- **Effective method**: The two-stage bridged transfer framework and dataset style inversion strategy yield consistent improvements (up to 30% accuracy) across 10 datasets and 5 architectures (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Detailed ablations**: Ablations on synthetic data volume show clear scaling benefits; experiments on few-shot settings are also included (Reviewer 1). The study of DSI as an efficient alternative to per-class fine-tuning is noted (Reviewer 4).
- **Practical simplicity**: The method is straightforward and could be valuable when only a generative model provides access to the target domain (Reviewer 4).
- **Reproducibility**: Code is provided (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient baselines**: Several critical comparisons are missing.
  - No comparison with the prior work “IS SYNTHETIC DATA FROM GENERATIVE MODELS READY FOR IMAGE RECOGNITION?” using the same generative model, datasets, and their filtering (RF, RG) to isolate the contribution of bridged transfer (Reviewer 1).
  - No baseline of training *from scratch* on the downstream real data to demonstrate the benefit of transfer, nor a baseline that only uses synthetic data for pre-training (without real-data fine-tuning) to justify the two-stage design (Reviewer 3).
  - Vanilla transfer with FC reinitialization is not reported in Tables 2 & 6, making it unclear whether gains stem from bridged transfer or simply from reinit (Reviewer 3).
  - The main baseline (finetuning from ImageNet-1k) uses far less data (1.3M images) than the proposed method, which leverages Stable Diffusion trained on LAION-5B (5B images). The method should be compared against models that use similar data/compute, e.g., OpenCLIP (pretrained on LAION-2/5B) finetuned or zero-shot on the same tasks (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing ablations and clarifications**:
  - No comparison of “mixing” synthetic+real vs. bridged transfer *when style inversion is applied* (Reviewer 3). This would disambiguate the benefit of the two-stage scheme from simple distribution alignment.
  - Inconsistent figures: Fig. 2 shows bridged transfer degrading on DTD and Dogs, while Fig. 3 & Fig. 9 show it performing equal or better on all datasets. The paper does not clarify whether the method was changed (e.g., from Bridged Transfer to Bridged Transfer++) (Reviewer 3).
  - Terminology confusion: Table 3 “Single Template” – does it correspond to “Bridged Transfer + FC Reinit & mixup”? DTD accuracy in Table 2 (72.3±0.3) differs from Table 3 (71.5±0.4) while other datasets match; explanation and network architecture details are missing (Reviewer 1).
  - Circle plots (Fig. 2, Fig. 8) are difficult to interpret; axis scaling and normalization are unclear; bar plots would be more transparent (Reviewer 3).
- **Qualitative and quantitative evidence for style inversion**:
  - No visual examples comparing synthetic images with and without DSI to demonstrate domain gap reduction (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
  - No quantitative measure of distributional alignment (e.g., FID) between synthetic and real datasets (Reviewer 3).
- **Scope and limitations**:
  - The paper does not discuss whether the observations hold beyond transfer learning or beyond text-to-image generative models (Reviewer 2).
  - Benefits in terms of data cost, privacy, and IP are mentioned but not numerically analyzed (Reviewer 2).
  - Experiments focus on natural fine-grained tasks (aircraft, cars, food, pets, etc.); evaluation on specialized domains such as medical or satellite imagery, which are less represented in LAION-X, would strengthen claims of practical utility (Reviewer 4).
  - Dataset contamination is not addressed: test examples from downstream tasks may have been seen by Stable Diffusion / LAION-X during training, an advantage over the ImageNet-only baseline (Reviewer 4).
  - For classes like Boeing 787, LAION-X may already contain many images; it would be insightful to compare using real LAION-X images vs. generating synthetic ones, especially when synthetic volume is limited (e.g., ≤3k images in Fig. 5) (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing details**:
  - Training time and memory cost of DSI (e.g., 20k iterations for Aircraft) should be reported (Reviewer 1).
  - In mixed transfer, the proportion of synthetic vs. real images used is not specified (Reviewer 4).
  - LEEP analysis in Table 1: a model with lower train/test accuracy (as seen in Fig. 1 for bridged pre-training) naturally yields a lower transferability metric; it is unclear whether the difference remains when training loss is matched (Reviewer 4).
