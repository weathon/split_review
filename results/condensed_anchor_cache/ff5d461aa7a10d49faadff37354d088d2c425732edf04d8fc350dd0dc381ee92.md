## Merged Review

### Summary
This work reconstructs dynamic 3D scenes from casually captured monocular videos without known camera poses. It jointly optimizes camera poses (initialized from pairwise relative poses) and a Hexplane‑based deformable Gaussian field, using depth and optical flow priors. The method is evaluated on DyCheck, NVIDIA Dynamic NeRF, MPI Sintel, and DAVIS, achieving superior view synthesis quality and improved camera pose accuracy over previous approaches.

### Strengths
- The problem is clearly motivated and important; reconstructing dynamic scenes from monocular video without camera poses is a nontrivial task that is gaining attention. (R1, R3)
- The proposed pipeline consistently outperforms prior methods on most tested scenes, sometimes even surpassing methods that assume known camera poses. (R1, R3)
- Camera pose estimation using relative initialization with joint optimization is considered novel by some reviewers. (R2) One reviewer finds the overall idea novel and appreciates the use of depth and optical flow priors. (R1)
- Separating dynamic and static objects during optimization reduces artifacts. (R2)
- The paper provides clear diagrams and thorough baseline comparisons, with enough visuals to illustrate the improved render quality. (R3)
- The work explores 3D Gaussian Splatting for dynamic scene reconstruction with no pose prior and demonstrates effectiveness across diverse datasets for both novel‑view synthesis and camera pose estimation. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty** – While one reviewer found the approach novel (R1), three reviewers (R2, R3, R4) describe it as a combination of existing components: pairwise camera pose initialization from DGMarbles/COLMAP‑Free 3DGS, Hexplane representation from 4DGS, reprojection loss from Robust Dynamic Radiance Fields, and depth alignment loss from MoSca. The authors are asked to highlight their distinct contributions. (R2, R3, R4)
- **Lack of justification for Hexplane** – No comparison is provided against a simpler Fourier‑based method (e.g., Splatter‑a‑Video) for modeling Gaussian dynamics; Hexplane introduces additional computation and storage overhead without demonstrated necessity. The relation of the Hexplane module to 4DGS is not discussed. (R2, R3)
- **Questionable claim of “unified” representation** – Static and dynamic Gaussians are optimized separately using mutually exclusive masks and different supervision, contradicting the claim of a unified representation. Reviewers note that 4DGS provides a more truly unified treatment. (R3, R4)
- **Missing ablation of loss regularizations** – The method uses a complex combination of loss terms (reprojection, depth alignment, 2D trajectory, ARAP, etc.), but the paper does not ablate their individual contributions, making it hard to judge the necessity or source of improvement. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Camera pose module details** – The relative pose initialization is very similar to that of DGMarbles and COLMAP‑Free 3DGS; the authors should clarify the difference. Since the module relies on depth priors, why not directly use Dust3R? (R2, R3)
- **Missing discussion of concurrent works** – Splatter‑a‑Video (NeurIPS 2024) and GFlow (arXiv 2024) were publicly available ~4 months before the ICLR deadline but are not discussed or compared. (R2)
- **Insufficient evaluation metrics** – The paper does not quantitatively evaluate camera pose accuracy on the Sintel dataset despite pose estimation being a key contribution. (R2)
- **Incomplete qualitative results** – More qualitative comparisons (e.g., videos) are needed, especially for Figure 4, where the last two columns empirically conflict with the quantitative results in Table 2. (R1, R4)
- **Clarity and writing issues** – The paper is difficult to follow in places; necessary details are missing, such as: how 3D Gaussians are initialized before the Hexplane optimization (R4), the mathematical form of *rotDeform* in Eq. 17 (R4), how static regions are obtained per frame (R4), and the full minimization procedure of Eq. 16 (R4). Inconsistent dataset listing (DAVIS mentioned in text but not in the initial list). (R4) Typos exist (line 080, 138, 69, 137–138). Citation format and notation should be corrected. (R2, R4)
- **Assumes known camera intrinsics** – The method requires given camera intrinsics, which could be a limitation. (R1)
- **Missing citations** – The scale‑invariant loss and ARAP loss lack proper references. (R4)
