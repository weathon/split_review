## Merged Review

### Summary

LaTable is a diffusion-based generative model for tabular data, designed for cross-dataset training, handling mixed categorical/numerical features, leveraging textual metadata (dataset descriptions, feature headers), and achieving column order equivariance. The model is pretrained on a large collection of datasets and evaluated in few-shot generation scenarios, showing scaling laws with model and data size. It outperforms baselines on in-distribution generation and fine-tuning improves out-of-distribution performance, but zero-shot generation is poor. A minority reviewer (rating 5) is more positive than the others (ratings 3) and particularly emphasizes the clear design goal alignment and identification of scaling laws.

### Strengths

- Clear motivation for tabular generative models and well-written related works explaining why diffusion is preferable to LLM approaches.
- Comprehensive experiments covering scalability, cross-dataset training, and impact of textual context (all reviewers).
- Four primary design goals (cross-dataset, mixed-type, textual context, column order equivariance) are clearly outlined and aligned with specific model design choices; one reviewer (more positive) finds this especially strong.
- Identification of scaling laws unique to tabular data, a relatively unexplored area.
- One of the first works to train a generative transformer across many tabular datasets, representing a step toward scaling up tabular models.
- Evaluation includes several ablation studies demonstrating the usefulness of individual components.
- The Limitations and Discussion section is honest and insightful.

### Weaknesses

- **Evaluation setup unclear**: Datasets Cardio and Insurance are not cited (R1); baselines CTGAN, TVAE, ARF, DDPM, GREAT lack clear references (R1, R2).
- **Potential data leakage**: Model is fine-tuned on the test set and then a CatBoost classifier is trained on generated data to predict test set elements; this conflates generation quality with memorization (R1).
- **Limited evaluation metrics**: Only downstream performance (ML efficiency, TSTR) is used; no real-data upper baseline, no Discriminator metric (R1, R2, R4). Additional metrics (low-order/high-order statistics) are requested (R2).
- **Missing evaluations**: No results for multiclass classification or regression tasks (R1, R2). Conditional generation (e.g., classification by conditioning on features) is described but not evaluated (R4). Zero-shot and full-dataset fine-tuning should be compared (R4).
- **Missing comparisons**: Related work does not discuss or compare to TabPFN, Yak et al., Zhu et al., XTab, or GREAT (R4). Baselines such as TABSYN and TabDDPM should be added (R2).
- **Poor accessibility of write-up**: Architecture is hard to understand; notation is overwhelming; input/output and end-to-end training are unclear (R3, R4). Figure 1 does not explain the generation procedure (e.g., where noise is inserted, latent vs. full-space diffusion) (R3, R4). A higher-level description is needed.
- **Presentation issues**: Figure 2 extends beyond page boundaries (R1, R2, R4). Citation format is incorrect (missing parentheses, citep not used) (R1, R4). “Scaling law” mentioned 14 times without citing any scaling law paper (R1). Typos: “transformer‘s” (l.237), \mathbb{R} not normal R, “e.g.” comma missing, LaTable capitalization in Figure 2 caption (R4). Appendix and supplementary materials are missing (R4). Code is not released (R2, R4).
- **Bias and fairness**: Acknowledged but not explored with practical detection or mitigation approaches (R2).
- **Question overlaps**: Clarification on embedding model “WhereIsAI/UAE-Large-V1” (R1). Investigation of impact of number of categories/features on scaling (R2). These are missing experiments or unclear details, incorporated as weaknesses.
