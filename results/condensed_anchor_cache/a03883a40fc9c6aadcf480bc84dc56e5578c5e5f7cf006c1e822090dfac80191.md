## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers found the paper proposes AdaGrad++ and Adam++, parameter-free variants of AdaGrad and Adam derived from the DoG framework (Ivgi et al., 2023), with theoretical convergence guarantees. However, substantial weaknesses are identified regarding novelty, technical rigor, experimental scope, and practical overhead. Two reviewers gave a rating of 3 (weak reject), and one gave a rating of 6 (weak accept) but still raised critical issues.

### Strengths
- The paper is complete in format (R1).
- Parameter-free methods are relevant; achieving optimal convergence without problem-dependent properties is beneficial theoretically and practically (R2).
- The paper tackles this challenge practically and shows some evidence (R2).
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow (R3).
- It extends the DoG framework to Adam and AdaGrad (R3).
- The authors propose theorems for convergence guarantees (R3; but math not carefully checked by that reviewer).

### Weaknesses
**Limited Novelty and Technical Issues**
- The main differences from (Ivgi et al., 2023) are limited; novelty is low (R1). Introducing $\sqrt{d}$ into the learning rate is not well supported intuitively or theoretically (R2).
- Theoretical convergence bounds (Corollary 4.4, Corollary 5.2) require $T \propto \mathcal{O}(d)$, a linear dependence on parameter dimension. This is impractical for high-dimensional problems where $d$ can be in the millions and $T \ll d$ in practice. Previous parameter-free methods (e.g., DoG) do not have this dependence (R2, Q1).
- The convergence rates for AdaGrad++ and Adam++ are similar, yet AdaGrad++ consistently underperforms. This mismatch suggests the bounds are not tight enough to be informative (R2).
- **First theory–practice discrepancy:** Theorem 5.1 requires $\lambda \in (0,1)$, which disables momentum quickly, but the practical success depends on momentum; authors note $\lambda=1$ works better (R2).
- **Second theory–practice discrepancy:** Algorithm 2 uses a max operation over $v_t$ for proof, but the practical implementation eliminates it and works better (R2). Overall, technical proofs are disconnected from practical performance (R2).
- The theoretical convergence analysis does not sufficiently explore tightness of bounds or compare against known rates of other parameter-free adaptive methods (R1).

**Insufficient and Questionable Experimental Evaluation**
- Comparisons with recent algorithms are missing (R1). The experimental results are not convincing (R1).
- Scope is too limited to demonstrate adaptivity: image classification uses only 2 architectures (ResNet*, VGG) and 1 dataset (CIFAR-10); language tasks use only 1 model (GPT-2) and 1 dataset (OpenWebText). Prior work (e.g., DoG) used 5 architectures and 12 datasets for image classification plus broader language tasks (R2).
- The baseline choice of constant learning rate is not ideal; Adam with cosine decay (common practice) outperforms the proposed methods (Table 1) (R2).
- AdaGrad++ is introduced but stated to underperform Adam and is never plotted in image or language tasks; comparison with standard AdaGrad is missing (R3).
- Sophia is mentioned but never compared; based on Sophia's results, it appears to outperform Adam++ on GPT-2 at 50K steps (R3).
- Standard Adam achieves the highest test accuracy in most image classification cases (R3).
- CIFAR-10 is outdated; testing on CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet, and ViT backbones is needed (R3).
- Small-scale experiments cannot support claimed adaptivity (R2).

**Memory, Computational Overhead, and Lack of Practical Discussion**
- The novel update rule requires storing original weights in addition to current weights and moments, causing ~33% memory overhead for Adam (R3). Additional L2 norm computation slows wall-clock time (R3). These overheads are never mentioned or measured (R1, R3).
- No wall-clock time comparison is provided (R3).
- The paper lacks a thorough discussion on practical implications, especially when computational overhead is a concern (R1).
- Minimal performance gains may not justify the added overhead (R3).

**Presentation and Missing Details**
- Plotted figures are too small to discern details (R3).
- Missing comparisons with recently proposed algorithms (R1, R2).
- The authors switch between Adam++ (image) and AdamW++ (language) without clear reasons; also missing settings like AdamW++ (Case 1) and constant learning rate for language tasks (R2, Q2).
