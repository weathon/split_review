## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Wavelet Latent Diffusion (WaLa), a method that encodes 3D shapes (TSDFs) into a compact wavelet-based latent representation via a VQ-VAE and trains conditional/unconditional latent diffusion models on this latent space. The representation compresses a 256³ SDF into a 12³ × 4 latent grid (2,427× compression ratio). Models contain approximately one billion parameters and produce shapes at 256³ resolution in 2–4 seconds. Training uses 10 million shapes from 19 sources. The approach achieves state-of-the-art quantitative and qualitative results on several conditional generation tasks (image, point cloud, sketch, text, etc.). The authors intend to open-source code and weights. Reviewer 1 (score 6) found the combined solution efficient and the results strong, recommending acceptance with minor concerns. Reviewers 2, 3, and 4 (all score 3) rated the paper low due to limited novelty: the wavelet tree and VQ‑VAE are existing components, combined without significant adaptation, and the work largely extends Make‑a‑Shape [1] and latent diffusion [2,3]. Additional weaknesses include missing comparisons, incomplete documentation, and overclaiming of text-to-3D capability.

### Strengths
- **Efficient combined representation:** The wavelet tree → VQ‑VAE pipeline yields a compact latent grid (2,427× compression) suitable for billion-parameter generative models while maintaining shape quality (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- **Large-scale training feasibility:** The generative model can be trained on 10 million shapes using a single H100 GPU (Reviewer 1; authors are asked to confirm and include training time). The billion-parameter models achieve fast inference (2–4 sec) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- **Strong quantitative and qualitative results:** The method outperforms baselines on multiple conditional tasks (single/multi‑view images, point clouds, sketches, etc.) in terms of generation quality and diversity (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4). The experimental section confirms the performance boost (Reviewer 4).
- **Logical extension of prior work:** The combination of wavelet‑tree encoding (from Make‑a‑Shape [1]) with a VQ‑VAE and latent diffusion follows established diffusion‑based scaling approaches (Reviewer 4). The VQ‑VAE’s adaptive sampling strategy improves overall performance (Reviewer 4).
- **Clear presentation:** The paper is well written, with a clear structure and concise visualizations (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty & insufficient adaptation:** The wavelet tree conversion and structure are directly taken from Make‑a‑Shape [1] with no stated modifications (Reviewer 2). The VQ‑VAE is a standard variant with no adaptation for the 3D task (Reviewer 2). Stage 2 is a standard DDPM (essentially stable diffusion [3]) conditioned on wavelet coefficients (Reviewer 2). The entire approach reads as an extension of [1] and existing latent diffusion works [2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9], making the contribution too incremental for ICLR (Reviewer 4). The parameter‑saving advantage of wavelet compression is well‑known (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing justification for wavelet choice:** The paper does not provide a compelling rationale for selecting wavelet encodings over other decompositions (e.g., spherical harmonics) (Reviewer 3). No parameter sensitivity analysis of the wavelet transform’s frequency domain decomposition is given, raising robustness concerns (Reviewer 3).
- **Reconstruction accuracy concerns:** The paper does not thoroughly evaluate how accurately compressed wavelet coefficients reconstruct original shapes. Empirical evaluations of reconstruction fidelity are absent (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing unconditional comparisons:** Quantitative unconditional generation metrics (1‑NNA, MMD, COV, FID from random renders) are omitted. Only conditional tasks are evaluated quantitatively, preventing comparison with existing unconditional 3D generative models (Reviewer 4). Also missing: how much does the latent diffusion model improve over using the VQ‑VAE alone in the unconditional setting? (Reviewer 4)
- **Overclaiming text-to-3D:** The method only performs text‑to‑multiview depth generation followed by depth‑to‑3D reconstruction; it is not a true end‑to‑end text‑to‑3D model (Reviewer 1).
- **No textures:** The method cannot generate realistic textures, whereas several baselines in Table 3 support novel view synthesis with textures (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing training details and resource specifications:**
  - Training resources and time for both VQ‑VAE and latent generative model are not reported (Reviewer 1). The claim of “only 1 H100 GPU for training on 10M shapes” needs confirmation and elaboration (Reviewer 1).
  - The paper states “billion parameters” but does not provide the exact parameter count or a detailed model architecture (Reviewer 4).
  - The evaluation datasets are not well documented: number of samples per dataset, how they relate to the extensive training set (e.g., diversity, category overlap) are missing (Reviewer 4).
- **Section balance:** The VQ‑VAE section is too extensive while the wavelet‑tree section (a key component) is underrepresented (Reviewer 4).
- **Minor presentation issues:**
  - Limitations and broader impact are not discussed (Reviewer 1).
  - Lack of hyperlinks for citations makes reading difficult (Reviewer 1).
  - Typo: Line 130 “WaLaallows” should be “WaLa allows” (Reviewer 1). Spaces missing on page 3 line 130, page 4 line 164, page 4 line 187; a capital letter on page 8 line 425 (Reviewer 4).
  - The anonymous version shows a last update on October 10 (after ICLR deadline) according to Git commit history; authors should provide evidence that results were uploaded before the deadline (Reviewer 1).
- **Unaddressed questions (overlap with criticisms):**
  - Why does the model not support direct text‑to‑latent generation? Is it infeasible or omitted due to time constraints? (Reviewer 1)
  - The inclusion of SMPL and SMIL in the training dataset is confusing; these are parametric models whose training scan data is not publicly available. How do they contribute? (Reviewer 1)
  - Why is inference speed different for depth vs. RGB condition in Table 3, if architectures are the same? (Reviewer 4)
  - Why are depth metrics consistently higher than RGB metrics? (Reviewer 4)
  - How is the ground‑truth TSDF data generated from meshes, and how compute‑intensive is this process? (Reviewer 4)
  - How does the VAE fine‑tuning strategy (10k samples per dataset) help if random sampling from each dataset keeps the overall distribution the same? (Reviewer 4)
