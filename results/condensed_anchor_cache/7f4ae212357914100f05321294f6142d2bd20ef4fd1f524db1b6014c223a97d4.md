## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Hierarchical Corpus Encoder (HCE), a dense retrieval method that organizes documents into a hierarchical tree structure via clustering, then learns embeddings using contrastive training with sibling negatives at each depth. An EM-style algorithm iteratively optimizes the clustering and the encoder. The authors argue that the success of generative retrieval stems from contrastive training among sibling nodes, and HCE transfers this idea to dense encoders. Experiments on zero-shot and supervised settings show HCE outperforms both dense and generative baselines while allowing easy addition/removal of documents.

### Strengths
- **Creative integration of generative and dense retrieval ideas** (R1, R2, R3): HCE leverages a hierarchical structure for efficient storage and retrieval, combining strengths of both paradigms. The insight that generative retrieval’s performance lies in contrastive training between sibling nodes is identified and transferred to dense encoders.
- **Clear writing and logical flow** (R1, R3): The paper is easy to follow and the exposition is clear.
- **Experimental validation across settings** (R1, R3): Extensive experiments demonstrate robust improvements over baselines in both unsupervised zero-shot and supervised settings, with detailed analysis and ablation studies.
- **Flexible indexing** (R2): HCE allows easy addition and removal of documents without retraining, addressing a limitation of generative retrieval.
- **Versatility without external dependencies** (R4): The method combines dense retrieval with implicit hierarchical information derived from embeddings, not relying on externally defined document relationships.
- **Positive minority view**: One reviewer (R1, score 6) found the approach creative and the experiments extensive; this contrasts with other reviewers’ concerns about novelty and baselines.

### Weaknesses
- **Misleading framing and motivation** (R4, R3): The paper is written from a generative retrieval perspective, claiming that generative retrieval’s limitation is the inability to update documents. Yet the proposed HCE is a dense retrieval method, which natively supports document updates and zero-shot retrieval. This logic is invalid – any dense retriever could use the same motivation. (R4 strongly criticizes this; R3 also notes that in dense retrieval, constructing a hierarchical encoding is unnecessary and adding documents may destroy the tree structure.)
- **Insufficient novelty; prior art in hierarchical dense retrieval ignored** (R2, R3, R4):  
  - The idea that generative retrieval’s contrastive learning is important is not novel; it can be referenced from [1] “Generative Retrieval as Multi-Vector Dense Retrieval”. (R2)  
  - Hierarchical clustering and iterative embedding learning have been adopted in previous studies; the contribution is incremental. (R3)  
  - The paper fails to introduce or compare with existing hierarchical dense retrieval methods, e.g., Dense Hierarchical Retrieval (Liu et al., 2021). (R4) This omission makes it unclear how HCE differs or where it offers advantages.  
  - A related work section on hierarchical dense retrieval is completely missing. (R4)
- **Limited baselines and experimental scope** (R2, R4):  
  - Many standard generative retrieval baselines are absent: NOVO, Ultron, LTRGR, GenRRL, RIPOR, ListGR, SE-DSI. (R2)  
  - Dense retrieval baselines are not state-of-the-art; a strong baseline like Fine-Tuning LLaMA for Multi-Stage Text Retrieval [9] should be included. (R2)  
  - Hierarchical dense retrieval methods (e.g., Dense Hierarchical Retrieval) must be added as baselines for a fair comparison. (R4)  
  - Datasets used have relatively small document counts; performance on medium-to-large-scale datasets (millions of documents, as in RIPOR) should be tested. (R2)
- **Computational overhead** (R1): The hierarchical clustering step may introduce additional computational overhead during initial setup, which could be challenging for very large corpora or low-resource environments.
- **Missing details in co-training and convergence** (R3): The “Co-Training of Encoder and Hierarchy” section is not detailed enough; it lacks a clear description of the training process and convergence proof. (R3 requests these be added.)
- **Incorrect or unsubstantiated claims** (R4):  
  - The claim that generative retrieval leads to improved performance in zero-shot retrieval is inaccurate; methods like DSI and NSI require training to achieve effectiveness.  
  - The claim about “better performance in unsupervised settings when adapted to a new corpus without any labeled training data” is unclear which prior work substantiates it.  
  - The statement that the atomic version’s full-document-gradient update fundamentally differs from dense retriever contrastive learning is incorrect; the difference is simply corpus size, not methodology.  
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms** (R1, R4):  
  - Why does generative retrieval get better performance when adapted to a new corpus without labeled data? (R1) – relates to missing justification.  
  - How does HCE handle out-of-vocabulary or new domains in zero-shot settings? (R1) – relates to missing analysis.  
  - Paper should explain “tiered hierarchical negative sample” more clearly in the introduction. (R4)  
  - Please add a related work section focusing on hierarchical dense retrieval. (R4)
