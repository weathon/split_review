## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the Task2Vec diversity coefficient as a metric for measuring the formal diversity of LLM pre-training datasets, and validates it via interpretability experiments and comparisons to theoretical bounds. Reviewers are split: two (scores 6) find the idea meaningful and well-executed, one (score 3) sees limited novelty and unclear practicality, and one (score 1) judges the paper as lacking insight and suffering from methodological flaws. All reviewers agree that the topic of data quality is important.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles an important and understudied problem: defining and measuring data quality for LLMs, beyond scale alone.
- The idea of measuring dataset diversity via the Task2Vec diversity coefficient is clean and the proposed ideal characteristics (increases with dataset concatenation, latent concept number, and vocabulary richness) are well motivated.
- The paper is well-written and rigorous (R1, R2, R4). R2 particularly notes that the demonstration that public datasets have high formal diversity relative to well-motivated lower/upper bounds, plus providing simpler evaluation methods, is valuable.
- The paper builds on existing literature (Task2Vec, Fisher Information Matrix) and brings the diversity coefficient into the LLM data quality discussion (R3 acknowledges this).
- R4 appreciates the novelty of measuring dataset quality in terms of diversity and the justification via ideal characteristics.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and shallow insight** (R1, R3): The work directly applies Task2Vec diversity coefficient [1] and Task2Vec embeddings [2] without offering new methodology or revealing non-obvious findings. R3 states the paper “does not offer new or noteworthy findings.”
- **Unclear connection between diversity and data quality** (R1, R3): The paper does not empirically validate that high diversity implies high quality. R1 and R3 question why diversity (as measured) should be a proxy for quality. R3 specifically asks why Task2Vec is needed over direct token distribution diversity metrics.
- **Model dependence not investigated** (R1, R4): The metric is computed using GPT-2 as a probe model. R1 asks whether diversity estimates transfer to other architectures (encoder-decoder, encoder-only) or model sizes (GPT-3, etc.). R4 notes that the random-initialization experiment in Section 4 uses “Randomly initialized GPT-2 without fine-tuning,” and questions whether different random seeds or resetting only the LM head vs. the whole model would yield different conclusions.
- **Weak baselines and experimental justification** (R4, R3, R1):
  - The lower bound (uniform single-token sequences) is too trivial; a more meaningful baseline would be a narrow-domain dataset (R4).
  - The batch-size experiment in Section 4 shows saturating diversity, but R1 asks how model performance changes with batch size; the paper provides no such correlation.
  - R4 questions the conclusion in Appendix G that “higher diversity leads to higher test performance” because the effect may be driven by increased train-test overlap when concatenating USPTO and PubMed (both are subsets of The Pile), not diversity per se.
- **Unclear or missing methodological details** (R3, R4):
  - R3 raises a critical ambiguity: whether the probe model’s weights are updated after computing Task2Vec embeddings for each batch. If so, the diversity of subsequent batches would be artificially inflated because information from the first batch is already learned. This would affect the validity of results in Section 4.
  - R3 notes unclear definitions in Section 2.1: “(partially) fine-tuning,” “solving the batch,” and the variable \(t\) and its expectation are not explained.
  - R4 is confused about the dimension of the Task2Vec vector, suspecting it equals the number of model parameters, which would be infeasible for large models.
  - R4 requests clarification on the MIX2 setting in Table 1 (0.77 vs 0.23) and why concatenating C4 and Wikitext-103 decreases the diversity coefficient.
- **Computational overhead not acknowledged** (R4): The finetuning step (even if only the final layer) introduces cost; the paper should quantify parameters updated and training time.
- **Insufficient experiments linking diversity to LLM performance** (R2, R4): R2 calls for more experiments across models, datasets, and multiple runs to establish correlation. R4 notes the need for ablations to separate diversity from other factors (e.g., dataset overlap in Appendix G). R1 similarly asks for ablation controlling for optimization effects when varying batch size.
- **Missing related work** (R4): The paper omits recent data-centric methods, e.g., “D4: Improving LLM Pretraining via Document De-Duplication and Diversification” (Meta AI, 2023.08).
- **Lack of analysis on code data** (R4): Given the increasing importance of code pre-training, the paper should provide insights into the diversity of code datasets.
- **Presentation issues** (R2, R3, R4): R2 found Sections 2.1–2.2 hard to follow and suggests more illustrations. R3 points out inconsistent in-text citation style and a typo on page 2 (“…the high diversity … is high”). R4 notes a typo in Section 3.4 (“due” should be “due to”) and the ambiguous caption of Table 1. R4 also requests that the explanation of Task2Vec in the method section be supplemented with Appendix A.
- **Minor reviewer disagreement**: R3 is strongly negative, viewing much of the paper as methodologically flawed and lacking novelty; R1 is moderately negative about practicality and novelty; R2 and R4 are positive but still point to significant missing experiments, clarifications, and baseline weaknesses.
