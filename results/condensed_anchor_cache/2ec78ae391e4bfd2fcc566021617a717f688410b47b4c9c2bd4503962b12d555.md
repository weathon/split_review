## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces the Complex Video Reasoning and Robustness Evaluation Suite (CVRR-ES), a benchmark with 2,400 open-ended QA pairs over 214 videos (shortest 2 seconds) covering 11 real-world video dimensions. It evaluates 9–11 Video-LMMs (including closed- and open-source) and establishes a human baseline. The paper also proposes a training-free Dual-Step Contextual Prompting (DSCP) method to improve reasoning and robustness, using GPT-3.5 as the evaluation LLM. Reviewer scores are 6, 3, 3, 3, with one reviewer significantly more positive about the contribution.

### Strengths
- The topic—video understanding—is important in multimodal research.
- The focus on reasoning over complex videos and robustness to user prompts (rather than general comprehension) addresses underexplored and valuable aspects.
- The benchmark’s 11 evaluation dimensions are challenging and effectively assess model capabilities in complex scenarios.
- The 10 principled instructions in DSCP can significantly enhance model handling of these tasks.
- The human baseline provides a good reference for evaluation.
- The benchmark is human-verified (not fully automatic) and targets Chain-of-Thought reasoning.

### Weaknesses
- **Similarity to existing benchmarks**: The build-up closely resembles MVBench. Figure 1 incorrectly claims MVBench lacks “in the wild” and “contextual dependency” dimensions and reasoning QA. The paper does not clearly distinguish CVRR-ES from MVBench or explain what robustness to user prompts adds beyond existing work. Several recent benchmarks (Video-MME, PerceptionTest, MLVU, LongVideoBench, EgoSchema, TempCompass) are not compared; some have high overlap. No correlation analysis (e.g., r²) is provided to show how different CVRR-ES is from these benchmarks.
- **Limited contribution to the field**: Much work already evaluates reasoning (e.g., MVBench). Given this context, focusing primarily on complex reasoning may have limited impact for ICLR. DSCP is straightforward, basic, and “rough” – a two-stage text prompt that lacks novelty. Prompt engineering has been widely applied in data generation, evaluation, etc., making the introduction and ablation of DSCP seem redundant. The contribution is overstated.
- **Insufficient video quantity and length distribution**: Only 214 videos (shortest 2 seconds; many short clips) are used. It is questionable whether such a small set can robustly assess 11 categories. Current Video-LLM papers (e.g., Tarsier) provide many more videos. The paper does not report the distribution of video lengths across the 11 dimensions, nor explain how balanced representation was ensured.
- **Missing implementation details**: The number of frames input to open-source models is not specified. Intuitively, more frames improve fine-grained understanding needed for complex reasoning. Prompt templates for all closed- and open-source Video-LLMs tested are not provided, affecting reproducibility.
- **Outdated and expensive evaluation**: Using GPT-3.5 as the evaluation LLM appears outdated. Using an LLM judge is expensive, especially at scale.
- **DSCP overemphasized, CVRR-ES underanalyzed**: DSCP discussion occupies a large portion of the experimental analysis, while insights from CVRR-ES – especially human-model comparison – are lacking. More fine-grained visual examples would better serve human-centric AI motivation.
- **Verification of reasoning level**: It is not demonstrated that CVRR-ES requires a “higher” level of reasoning than existing benchmarks. No evidence is provided that DSCP improves performance more on this benchmark than on others. It is also not tested whether a multiple-choice alternative was considered.
- **Figure quality and typos**: Figures are way too small and hard to read. There is a typo in line 520: “there exist few limitations” should be “there exist a few limitations.”
