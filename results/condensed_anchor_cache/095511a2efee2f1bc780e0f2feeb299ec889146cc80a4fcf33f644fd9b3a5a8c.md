## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Neural Collapse inspired OOD detector (NC-OOD) for multi-class classification. It combines the distance to the weight vector of the predicted ID class (pScore, cosine similarity) and the L1 norm of the penultimate feature (l1Score) into NCScore. The method is motivated by the observation that ID features tend to cluster near class weight vectors and that OOD features tend to have smaller norms. Experiments on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet benchmarks show competitive performance, especially under contrastive loss pre-training.

### Strengths
- **Novel perspective**: Introducing Neural Collapse to motivate OOD detection is interesting and novel (R2, R4). The motivation is sound and well explained (R3, R4).
- **Simplicity**: The proposed method is straightforward, simple, and easy to understand (R1, R3, R4).
- **Clarity**: The paper is well written, clearly organized, and easy to follow (R1, R2, R3).
- **Contrastive loss effectiveness**: NC-OOD is particularly effective when the classifier is pre-trained with contrastive loss (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient theoretical justification for the Neural Collapse (NC) assumption**:
    - The method relies on NC at Terminal Phase of Training (TPT), which requires infinite steps and zero training loss—a condition that leads to overfitting and is unrealistic for deployed models (R2). One reviewer (R2) argued that it is unreasonable to assume ID test samples follow the same distribution as training samples under TPT, as the decision boundary becomes exactly determined by training samples and cannot generalize. Fig. 1 only illustrates clustering properties already known, not the correctness of NC in general (non-TPT) models (R2).
    - It is not convincingly shown how NC implies that OOD features will be far from ID weight vectors (R1) or have smaller norms (R1,R2). The empirical observation that OOD features tend to lie closer to the origin is already known from prior work, and the paper does not provide principled or theoretical conditions for this property (R1).
    - The design choice of using cosine similarity (pScore) over L2 distance is not justified; many other score functions are possible given the NC decomposition (R3). The score’s reliance on a known class vector (w_c) at test time is problematic because the label is unknown and OOD classes are beyond the training scope (R4).
    - The connection between the proposed score and existing NC quantification metrics (e.g., CDNV) is not explored (R4).

- **Limited novelty and overlap with existing methods**:
    - The core idea of measuring proximity to class prototypes is similar to KNN+, Mahalanobis distance, and other clustering-based OOD detectors (R1,R2,R4). The advantages over these baselines are not clearly established (R2,R4).
    - The L1-norm component has been previously used in GradNorm (Eq. 9) for OOD detection (R2). No comparative experiments combining KNN+L1-Norm or Mahalanobis+L1-Norm are provided to demonstrate the novelty of the specific combination (R2).

- **Issues with NCScore formulation**:
    - In Eq. (5), NCScore increases with feature norm ‖h‖, which may incorrectly label OOD samples with large norms as ID (R3).
    - The choice of using ‖h‖₁ instead of ‖h‑μ_G‖₁ is not explained; a symmetric version using the centered feature norm seems more natural (R3).

- **Incomplete experimental evaluation**:
    - Missing comparisons with recent OOD detectors: ViM, ASH, NPOS, CIDER (R2).
    - Near-OOD experiments are not included (R2).
    - The claim of computational efficiency is unsupported by empirical results (R4).
    - Improvements with contrastive learning and DenseNet-101 are marginal (R4).
    - No comparison with methods that directly encourage alignment to class prototypes during training (e.g., Ming et al., ICLR 2023) (R1).
    - Table 1 shows Mahalanobis outperforming NC-OOD on LSUN-R and iSUN, but no analysis explains when distance-based scores are better than cosine similarity (R3).

- **Missing details and clarity**:
    - The standard OOD decision rule (how to threshold NCScore) is not formally defined (R4).
    - The notation δ_{c_c’} in NC2 is unclear (R4).
    - The description of how to determine which w_c to use for an unlabeled test sample is missing (R4).
