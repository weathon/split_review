## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates how language models bind entities to their attributes in context (the binding problem). It proposes the *binding ID mechanism*: LMs attach binding ID vectors to entities and attributes in the residual stream. Using causal mediation experiments on synthetic tasks (e.g., city-person, color-shape pairs with 2–3 pairs), the authors show that swapping activation patterns changes binding, that binding vectors are position-independent, additive, lie in a linear subspace, and generalize across tasks. One task (direct binding) suggests an alternative mechanism.

### Strengths
- Novel concept of binding ID vectors as a mechanism for symbolic in-context binding.
- Well-written paper with clear definitions and hypotheses; helpful figures (especially Figure 2 illustrating the substitution scheme).
- Empirical evidence from causal interventions: substitution shows binding changes (mean log probability shifts), position independence holds, additivity of binding vectors is demonstrated, and switching both entity and attribute binding IDs restores correct query answering (a “wow” moment).
- Results span multiple synthetic datasets and model families (Pythia, LLaMA); includes geometry analyses (e.g., Figure 5, Tables 1–2) and generalization across tasks.
- The ability to surgically manipulate model beliefs provides strong, consistent evidence for the hypothesized mechanism.
- The analysis of a *direct binding* alternative in one task is a valuable additional contribution (though explanation in the main text is too brief).

### Weaknesses
**Generality and Scope**
- Generalization beyond synthetic tasks is unclear: all experiments use simple, short contexts (2–3 entity-attribute pairs). Realistic reading comprehension or multi-step reasoning may involve different mechanisms.
- The paper only examines binary relations; it is unclear how the mechanism scales to more than two relations or entities involved in multiple simultaneous bindings.
- Multi-token entities/attributes are not adequately addressed: the factorization claim relies on substituting only the first token, and it is unclear how binding IDs work for spans with internal dependencies.
- Opposite trends between Pythia and LLaMA in Figure 6 (accuracy vs. model size) are not explained. This raises concerns about robustness and whether the mechanism is model-family-specific.

**Experimental Rigor and Evidence**
- Section 3 experiments (Factorization, Position Independence) provide only anecdotal evidence from a few samples; statistical rigor is lacking.
- The claim that binding vectors reside in a linear subspace is not rigorously demonstrated. Experiments (Section 4.2) only show that linear combinations work; non-linear combinations or vectors outside the subspace are not tested.
- The “random vectors have no effect” finding (argued as evidence for special treatment) is mentioned but not emphasized or sufficiently explained (e.g., what happens when random vectors are scaled).
- Questions from multiple reviewers about critical experimental details (these overlap with weaknesses):
    - In Figure 3 and Table 2, which part of the model is substituted and which is frozen? For autoregressive LMs, when and how are activations substituted during decoding?
    - In Figure 4, among what was the mean log probability computed?
    - In Equation (2), what is the sampling distribution (from what did you sample)?
    - How many samples were used for Figure 3? Are the samples used to estimate differences the same as those tested in Table 1?
    - The legend in Figure 4 does not match the graph; the bottom graph shows non‑trivial variation with position, yet the paper calls these changes “small.”
- The method for extracting/calculating binding ID vectors from activations is not clearly described, hindering verification and reproducibility.

**Clarity and Presentation**
- **Disagreement among reviewers:** presentation quality is rated good by R1 (3), poor by R2 (1), fair by R3 and R4 (2). The paper is often difficult to follow due to too many concepts without sufficient explanation.
- Figure 3 appears to show the same image for panels (a) and (b) – likely a mistake.
- Section 4.1 is very dense; a diagram is needed to explain how binding ID \(k\) is represented by \([b_E(k), b_A(k)]\), the \(\Delta\) calculation in Eqn (2), and the stability assumption that \(b_E(k)\) is consistent across contexts for a given \(k\).
- The crucial “subtle point” that the \(k\)-th entity/attribute always has the same binding ID is buried in passing; it needs elaboration because the mechanism seems to require an internal counter or `gensym`-like operation.
- The concept of “confused” LM (when no context is consistent) is introduced but never used later; its inclusion is confusing.
- The alternative *direct binding* mechanism is only explained concisely in the appendix; it should be expanded in the main text.
- Figure 5 (axes, continuous region) is incomprehensible without more detailed explanation.
- It should be explicitly stated whether a word-level or subword tokenizer is used (the direct binding experiment appears to use subword tokens).
- Notation change to \(\alpha\) in Eqn (2) is not justified.
- Several typos: “alternate” → “alternative” (p.1), “of of” → “of” (p.2), “In See” → “See” (p.8).
