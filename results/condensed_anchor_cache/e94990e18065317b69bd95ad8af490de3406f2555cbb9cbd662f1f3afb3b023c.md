## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper presents a framework to acquire pseudo-tactile information (surface textures and geometries) from point clouds of everyday objects and to simulate fingertip contact localization for a dexterous hand (RH8D) in Isaac Sim by replicating real grasping motions. A dataset of point cloud images is made open-source. The method links simulated 3D coordinates, real 3D coordinates, and pseudo-tactile features (normal vectors, grayscale variance). Experiments report high accuracy in contact localization (precision up to 0.001 m) and texture extraction.

### Strengths

- Open-sourced dataset of point cloud images (Reviewer 2).
- Approach to extract pseudo-tactile information from vision and contact locations; topic interesting (Reviewer 1).
- Clear writing, good structure, well‑made informative figures (Reviewer 3).
- Well‑motivated to address hard‑to‑acquire tactile perception using vision for dexterous hands (Reviewer 3).
- Abundant clear explanation for method presentation; simple yet effective approach for point cloud preprocessing and feature extraction (Reviewer 4).
- Thorough experimental setup and real‑world experiments on a dexterous hand for validation; results seem very ideal indicating effectiveness (Reviewer 4).
- Pipeline presented with details; experiments show high accuracy (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses

- Overall contribution is marginal; pseudo‑tactile features are derived using common point cloud processing tools (Reviewers 1, 4). Reviewer 4 notes the texture extraction method overlaps mostly with [1] (Budiyanta et al. 2021).
- Role of simulation is unclear. Reviewer 2 argues it sounds like forward kinematics, not requiring a full simulation; Reviewer 1 questions the motivation of replaying real motion in simulation. The “real‑time linkage” of joints is a simple forward‑kinematics problem (Reviewer 2).
- Details are unclear in several places: repeatedly mentions “Y component of the normal vector” without clearly defining a coordinate frame (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). “Policy fine‑tuning techniques” appear on page 7 without prior mention of a policy (Reviewers 2, 4). “Grayscale variance” and “Y‑component” lack proper definitions at first appearance (Reviewer 3).
- Claim that Intel RealSense has “sub‑mm accuracy” is not supported by manufacturer documentation (Reviewer 2).
- Section 4.2 on localization precision is unclear: ground truth is not defined, how the measurement is performed, and what is compared against ground truth. Table 4 is difficult to understand, with no mention of errors or comparative ground truth. RMSE error is reported without clarity on the quantities being compared (Reviewer 2).
- Poor formatting: main result tables are placed in the appendix and are hard to understand. Tables 4, 5, 6 have poor unit choices (meters for textures likely sub‑cm scale) and numbers given with arbitrary precision disregarding the error rates of the depth camera. Bullet‑point headers have the same font formatting as body text (Reviewer 2).
- Related work Section 2.3 on pseudo‑haptics is disconnected – pseudo‑haptics gives humans touch sensory feedback, whereas “pseudo‑tactile” in this paper analyzes surface tactile properties of objects (Reviewer 2).
- The method simply combines existing techniques (point cloud from RGB‑D camera, simulator for grasping). Instead of quasi‑static grasping, the authors should extend to dynamic manipulation tasks and explore whether extracted features help (Reviewer 3).
- No quantitative or qualitative measurement of the quality of the generated haptic signals (Reviewer 4).
- Experiments lack comparison with previous work (e.g., 3D point cloud keypoint prediction baselines for contact localization). No ablation studies are conducted (e.g., different KDTree radius, normal threshold) (Reviewer 4).
- Depth images from Intel RealSense D435 can be very noisy, biasing normal estimation – no handling discussed. Transparent objects cause catastrophic depth failures – how are accurate point clouds obtained? (Reviewer 4)
- The experiment does not show how robotic grasping is improved; unclear how the combination of vision‑based information and fingertip contact points enhances tactile feedback reliability (Reviewer 1). How are texture feature points used after thresholding? (Reviewer 2) How is the pseudo‑tactile feedback used – is it part of a closed‑loop controller? (Reviewer 1)
- How is ground truth obtained for contact localization? What is the precision/resolution of the ground truth measurement? (Reviewers 2, 4)
- How does “policy fine‑tuning” work when the method is not deep‑learning based? (Reviewer 4)
- Reviewer 3 suggests moving Table 1 and equations (1)–(4) (camera‑to‑world transformation) to the appendix since they are fundamental computer vision techniques.
