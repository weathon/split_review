## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper theoretically and empirically analyzes open-set noise in learning with noisy labels (LNL), where the true label of a noisy sample may not belong to any known category. The authors compare impacts of open-set vs. closed-set noise and different open-set noise modes (easy/hard). They construct two synthetic open-set noisy datasets (CIFAR100‑O, ImageNet‑O) and a novel open-set test set for WebVision. They examine an entropy‑based detection mechanism and propose using OOD detection as an additional evaluation metric. Findings indicate open-set noise has distinct characteristics and a lesser negative effect on model performance than closed-set noise. Two reviewers (scores 6, 5) value the theoretical framework and empirical validation; one reviewer (score 3) considers the theoretical analysis and dataset construction insufficient for a top‑tier conference, lacking practical impact.

### Strengths
- The paper provides a detailed, largely overlooked examination of open-set noise in LNL.
- A robust theoretical framework (e.g., analysis of noise modes, entropy-based detection) is presented and supported by systematic empirical evidence.
- Careful comparison of “easy” vs. “hard” open-set noise scenarios is included, aiding understanding of how different noise types affect models.
- Introducing out-of-distribution (OOD) detection as a complementary evaluation metric to standard accuracy strengthens the assessment of models under open-set noise.
- Practical relevance is highlighted by demonstrating the behavior of existing LNL methods on real-world datasets (e.g., WebVision).
- The paper is easy to follow, with clear conclusions and well‑designed experimental figures (adequate on the constructed datasets).
- Mathematical analysis is adequate and experiments support the theoretical insights.

### Weaknesses
- Only a few existing LNL methods are tested (Appendix E.1), lacking comparisons with robust losses, MixUp, re‑weighting, meta‑learning, or gradient manipulation—limiting comprehensiveness.
- Section 3.3.2 excludes closed-set noise (Cx = 0) entirely; the interplay between open‑set and closed‑set noise under varying ratios is not studied, which is critical for real‑world data where both types co‑occur.
- The claim that “closed-set noise could even improve OOD detection performance” (Figure 2 (c)(d)) is not clearly supported by the plots; one reviewer sees no such improvement.
- The analysis of hard open-set noise (a combination of feature‑dependent and open‑set noise) does not propose or evaluate methods to handle such noise.
- Some conclusions appear naive; e.g., that entropy‑based detection is effective only for “easy” open‑set noise, but entropy‑based methods generally also fail for close‑set feature‑dependent noise—the limitation is not unique.
- The constructed benchmark datasets are very similar to existing LNL benchmarks, limiting their novelty and justifiability as a contribution.
- One reviewer (score 3) argues that theoretical analysis and dataset construction alone are not sufficient for ICLR: the theoretical findings lack clear actionable insights, the dataset’s utility is not justified, and comprehensive experimental validation demonstrating practical value is absent.
- Minor issues: typos (e.g., “the concept of complete noise transition matrix” should be “...a complete noise transition matrix”; “namely fitted case and overfitted case” missing “the”); the list of contributions (six) could be more compact.
- A reviewer questions whether using a single‑layer linear classifier on a frozen pretrained encoder can adequately fit the data distribution (as done in the analysis).
