## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper claims an improved sample complexity of \( O(\epsilon^{-3}) \) for global convergence of actor-critic in discounted RL, using a constant critic step size and decreasing actor step size. Reviewers question the novelty of this result given prior work, the strength of the assumptions (especially i.i.d. sampling from the stationary distribution), the connection to natural policy gradient, and the quality of the presentation.

### Strengths
- The claimed \( O(\epsilon^{-3}) \) sample complexity improves on a previously known \( O(\epsilon^{-4}) \) bound for this type of actor-critic algorithm (Reviewers 2 and 3).
- Using a constant step size for the critic enhances practical applicability (Reviewer 2).
- The convergence methods are considered novel by one reviewer (Reviewer 1), though this view is not shared by others.
- Reviewers 1 and 4 find the writing and organization clear; however, there is strong disagreement from Reviewers 2 and 3 (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty vs. prior work:**
  - Gaur et al. (2024) already proved last-iterate convergence with the same \( O(\epsilon^{-3}) \) complexity for infinite state and action spaces, using a decreasing actor step size and a constant critic step size. This paper’s result is therefore not new (Reviewer 1).
  - The algorithm in Line 3 of Algorithm 1 appears to match the natural policy gradient update (Lemma 15 of Agarwal et al., 2021). Several works [2–5 in Reviewer 4’s list] show geometric convergence and \( O(\epsilon^{-2}) \) sample complexity for natural actor-critic. The authors do not explain the differences or compare to these results (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing related work:** The paper omits relevant bi-level actor-critic literature, e.g., Xu et al. (2020), Chen et al. (2022), Hairi et al. (2022) (Reviewer 2).
- **Poor presentation (disagreement noted):** Reviewers 2 and 3 heavily criticize the writing. Specific issues include:
  - Unclear intuition for “slow change of policy” and “adversarial component”: how “slowness” is defined, relative time scale, and how it leads to decoupling.
  - Unclear sentences between Lines 71–73.
  - Inconsistent notation (e.g., capital \( C \) vs. lowercase \( c \) in Line 660), undefined notations (e.g., \( A_t \) in the algorithm).
  - Mathematical errors (e.g., missing square after first inequality in Line 297, missing coefficient in Line 380).
  - Lemmas relabeled in the appendix (e.g., Lemma 4 in the main body becomes Lemma 9).
  - Numerous typos: Line 66 “n” → “In”; table caption missing “as”; incomplete last paragraph of Section 1; undefined \( a^{*} \) in Line 207; duplicate expression in Line 268; “Lets” → “Let’s” (Line 346); “=” → “-” (Line 380); Equations (4) and (5) identical; missing “is” in Line 419; missing parentheses in Line 463.
  - The reason for calling (Xu 2020) “highly challenging” is not explained (Reviewer 2).
- **Tabular setting vs. function approximation:** The algorithm is tabular (critic update is tabular), yet the comparison is made to results using linear function approximators. This comparison is not meaningful (Reviewer 3).
- **Strong i.i.d. sampling assumption:** The algorithm assumes state-action pairs are sampled i.i.d. from \( d^{\pi_{\theta_k}} \), which requires drawing an entire trajectory each time and is impractical. A real implementation would sample from a single trajectory, making the noise a time‑inhomogeneous Markov chain and complicating the analysis. The i.i.d. assumption greatly simplifies the analysis and should be relaxed for practical relevance (Reviewers 3 and 4).
  - Related question: In Line 293, does “sample uniformly” refer to uniform over the state space \( \mathcal{S} \) or over collected samples so far? (Reviewer 2).
- **Unjustified claim about Assumption 1:** The authors claim that Parts 2 and 3 of Assumption 1 are weaker than Part 1, but no proof or explanation is given. The relationship between these parts and their impact on the analysis should be clarified (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear reasoning about deterministic policy:** The last paragraph on page 7 discusses Assumption 1, item 3, and claims convergence to a deterministic policy leads to better error. The connection to the assumption is not clear, and it is not explained why the policy would become deterministic or why that would improve the error bound (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited scope:** Can the result be extended to infinite state and action spaces? Without such extension, the work is not on par with Gaur et al. (2024) (Reviewer 1).
