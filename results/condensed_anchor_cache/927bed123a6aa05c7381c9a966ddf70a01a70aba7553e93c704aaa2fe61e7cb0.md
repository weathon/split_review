## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a multi-granularity semantic revision method for distilling large language models (LLMs). It introduces three new components: (1) Sequence-level Correction and Re-generation (SCRG) – detects error tokens in student-generated sequences by comparing teacher/student logit disagreement, replaces them with teacher tokens, and regenerates the rest; (2) Token-level Distribution Adaptive Clipping KL (DAC-KL) loss – uses a learnable sub-network to clip outlier teacher logits before applying KL divergence; (3) Span-level Correlation Consistency (SCC) – extracts spans (e.g., noun phrases) via an external chunker and enforces the adjacent-token probability correlation within each span to match teacher’s. Experiments compare against DistiLLM, MiniLLM, GKD on five instruction-following datasets using ROUGE-L (some human evaluation noted by Reviewer 2). Models range from 0.1B to 13B, with OPT showing large gains and other LLMs showing smaller (<1 ROUGE) improvements. One reviewer (R2, score 8) is notably more positive; others (R1,R3,R4, scores 6,5,5) highlight limited gains, missing analyses, and unclear intuitions.

### Strengths
- **Novelty of SCRG**: Addressing the case where a student-generated token falls outside the teacher’s prefix distribution, SCRG is simple, novel, and easily integrated into existing pipelines (R1, R2). Connection to LaSO framework noted (R1).
- **Token-level DAC-KL**: Provides a simple way to handle less expressive students by focusing on semantically dense areas; appreciates its straightforwardness (R2).
- **General framework**: Multi-granular revision (sequence, token, span) creates a comprehensive framework that integrates with both on-policy and off-policy distillation (R3).
- **Extensive experiments**: Wide range of model families (OPT, others), sizes, and datasets; ablation studies on variants; comparisons to multiple baselines (R1,R2,R3,R4).
- **Human evaluations**: Provided by the authors (R2), though R1 argues only ROUGE-L is used – this point is contested (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing evidence for diversity claim**: SCRG is claimed to improve generation diversity, but no empirical support (e.g., distinct n-grams) is given (R1, R3). Reviewer R4 also suggests the main improvement may stem from fixing trivial repetitions in very weak students.
- **SCC complexity and lack of clear intuition**: The span-level objective is hard to understand; why not directly optimize semantic similarity? Its reliance on an external chunker limits generalizability to low-resource languages (R1). No analysis of different correlation measures, chunking methods, or interaction with other components (R4).
- **DAC-KL insufficiently justified**: The learnable clipping sub-network adds complexity without comparison to simpler alternatives like selective distillation (e.g., top‑k logits, top‑5% logits) (R1, R2 question). Reviewers ask: how do predicted thresholds compare to a fixed mean? (R4)
- **Marginal performance gains overall**: Except for OPT, improvements over the best baseline are <1 ROUGE-L. Table 2 indicates most gain comes from DAC-KL alone, with SCC and SCRG contributing little (R1). R3 notes that ExAccErr (Exposure Bias error) is *higher* for the proposed method in Table 4a, contradicting the paper’s narrative; the conclusion that students outperform teachers is not clearly supported.
- **Evaluation metric issues**: Only ROUGE-L is reported (R1). Reviewer R2 states human evaluation was provided; reviewer R4 does not mention it. This discrepancy indicates the evaluation is not sufficiently strong or standardized.
- **Computational cost concerns**: SCRG regeneration increases training time; efficiency is lower than MiniLLM (Table 4b). No comparison under equal computational budget (R3).
- **Unclear motivation for correcting student outputs**: If the student’s output is always corrected to match the teacher’s, the approach is nearly equivalent to using pure teacher-generated samples; a direct comparison with teacher-only distillation is missing (R4).
- **Ablation and missing comparisons**:
    - Ablation does not separate each component fully; e.g., no comparison of DAC-KL vs. simple fixed clipping or top‑k (R4, R2 question).
    - No experiment with extreme teacher–student capacity gap (e.g., 13B teacher vs. 2B student) to see if DAC-KL mitigates performance degradation (R2 question).
    - No scheduled sampling baseline, despite exposure bias being central to the authors’ claims (R4).
- **Optional / minor**: Typo in Fig1(b) (“Studnet-generated”); table captions could be more informative (R1, R2).
