## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Imprecise Bayesian Continual Learning (IBCL) for continual learning under a large number of user-defined task trade-off preferences. IBCL maintains a knowledge base as a convex hull of model parameter distributions (via FGCS updates) and generates preference-addressing models zero-shot via HDR computation. It guarantees Pareto-optimal parameters theoretically. Experiments on the 20NewsGroup dataset (5-10 tasks) and reportedly three other benchmarks show up to 23% average and 15% peak per-task accuracy improvements over baselines with near-zero/positive backward transfer, reducing training overhead from one model per preference to at most three models total. Reviewers strongly disagree on the paper’s contribution (scores: 3, 6, 3, 8).

### Strengths
- **Novel and significant problem setting**: The paper is the first to tackle continual learning with a large number of user preferences over task trade-offs (R4). The idea of zero-shot model generation from a knowledge base without additional training is interesting and potentially widely applicable (R1, R4). Reducing training overhead is an important goal in continual learning (R3).
- **Theoretical guarantees**: IBCL provides a self-contained theoretical framework guaranteeing that generated models are Pareto-optimal (R1, R2, R3). One reviewer found this impressive (R3), though another questioned how Pareto-optimality translates to performance improvement (R1).
- **Efficiency**: The method reduces training overhead from one model per preference to at most three models for all preferences (R1, R3, R4) and requires fewer batch updates at the last task compared to competitors (R1).
- **Positive experimental signs**: Results show effectively no negative backward transfer and convincing ablations (R1, R2, R4). One reviewer found the experimental comparisons comprehensive (R2). Another noted that the method works on standard image classification and NLP tasks (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient and outdated baselines**: Multiple reviewers note that baselines are too old and insufficient to demonstrate state-of-the-art performance (R1, R3). Only GEM and L2P are compared; L2P is a replay-free prompting method, making the comparison unfair since IBCL uses a replay buffer (R1). Recent prompting methods (DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt), replay-based methods (A-GEM), zero-shot models (CLIP), and efficient training papers are absent (R1, R3). One reviewer questions why GEM was selected and whether IBCL can be deployed on standard single-preference CL benchmarks (R4).
- **Limited experimental scope**: Despite claims of scalability, results are shown only for 5/10 tasks on the 20NewsGroup dataset (R1) – a discrepancy with the motivation of handling many tasks/users. Another reviewer acknowledges four benchmarks but still finds the experimental studies limited and only comparing to GEM (R4). The training procedure is not clearly defined, and hyperparameter impact beyond the ablation is unaddressed (R1). One reviewer requests experiments with more tasks (R1) and with recent replay-based models (R3).
- **Paper density and missing intuition**: The paper is too dense, with crucial motivation and details (e.g., separation from MAML/BMAML, reason for Bayesian CL, Assumption 1’s importance, preference formalization, experimental details) moved to the appendix, making the main paper difficult to parse (R4). Terms like HDR and FGCS are not immediately clear (R2). The advantage of the chosen 2-Wasserstein distance over entropy/loss-based selection is not sufficiently highlighted, especially given its computational overhead (R1).
- **Questionable applicability and contrived setup**: The continual learning aspect seems secondary; examples highlight multi-task nature, and limited references are given for the relevance of needing many preferences for a task over time (R4). The setup may be contrived (R4). The motivation for probabilistic Pareto-optimality in continual learning is unclear (R1).
- **Issues with HDR and validation selection**: Poorly performing models can be sampled from HDRs, requiring separate evaluation on a withheld validation set. The rate of such models and the method’s dependence on validation metrics are not elaborated (R4).
- **Unclear buffer growth and fairness**: The claim of sublinear buffer growth is not convincingly shown to be superior to fixed buffer sizes (R1). A fair comparison with fixed-buffer GEM/A-GEM is questioned; the buffer growth rate and performance variation with sublinear growth are not discussed (R1). HDR computation complexity as tasks grow also remains unclear (R1).
- **Unaddressed questions**: Several specific questions remain: why a smaller α (e.g., 0.001) is not chosen (R2); the motivation and definition of Assumption 1 (task similarity) and how diameter r connects to distribution shifts (R4); how IBCL differs from preference-conditioned Pareto models (R2); and the computational complexity of HDR (R1).

*Reviewer contrasts preserved*: One reviewer found the paper well-written and clear (R2) while another found it too dense (R4). One found experimental comparisons comprehensive (R2) while others found them limited (R1, R3, R4). Scores range from 3 (strong rejection) to 8 (strong accept).
