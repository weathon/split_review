## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper tackles the exploration problem in online RLHF by proposing Count-based Online Preference Optimization (COPO). The method adds a count-based exploration bonus to the DPO objective, derived theoretically from a linear reward assumption and an upper confidence bound. A coin-flip counting network (CFN) estimates pseudo-counts for prompt-response pairs. Experiments on Zephyr and Llama-3 models show COPO improves instruction-following and academic benchmarks over SELM and other baselines. The authors provide a regret-bound analysis to motivate the exploration term.

### Strengths
- Tackles an important but under-explored problem in online RLHF: efficient space exploration during iterative preference optimization.
- Solid theoretical foundation: starts from a linear reward assumption, builds a UCB-based regret bound that motivates the exploration term, providing credible motivation for the algorithm.
- Good insight into the limitations of current online RLHF methods, which focus on preference optimization but lack effective exploration.
- Algorithm shows notable improvements after multiple iterations (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 1 also finds it performs better than SELM in several benchmarks).
- Practical implementation of theory: the CFN-based pseudo-count is lightweight and feasible for large LLM state spaces.

### Weaknesses
- Baseline selection is insufficient. Only SELM is compared; other online DPO variants (e.g., [1], [2], [3]) and Best-of-N, PPO should have been included. (Reviewers 1 and 3)
- Improvements appear marginal given the added complexity. For the Llama-3-8B Instruct model (Table 1), there is no notable improvement over SELM, especially considering potential variance from using GPT-4 as the evaluator. (Reviewers 1, 3) One reviewer (3) finds this concerning, while others see notable gains in other settings; this disagreement should be resolved with more trials or error bars.
- The coin-flip counting network (CFN) seems too simplistic for representing modern LLM hidden states. How can one measure/believe CFN works properly? If CFN performance were improved, would final alignment also improve? More convincing visit-count algorithms should be explored. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- COPO is only implemented on the DPO objective. It is not verified whether the exploration idea transfers to other online RLHF algorithms. (Reviewer 2)
- The effect of different scoring reward models (RMs) on COPO’s performance is not studied. A stronger reward/judge model could improve results, but this remains unexplored. (Reviewer 2)
- More benefits of exploration (e.g., robustness to adversarial cases) could be demonstrated. (Reviewer 1)
- Reviewer confidence varies: one reviewer (score 6) is less convinced of the improvement magnitude, while the other two (scores 8) are positive; this suggests the results may be sensitive to evaluation setup.
