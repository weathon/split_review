## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses submodular maximization under noise, where the objective function can only be accessed via i.i.d. noisy queries (unbiased, R-subgaussian). The authors propose Confident Sample (CS), an adaptive sampling strategy inspired by best-arm-identification in multi-armed bandits, which adjusts the sample size based on uncertainty. CS is integrated into existing approximation algorithms for cardinality and matroid constraints (and unconstrained non-monotone) to obtain approximation guarantees close to the standard value oracle setting with improved sample efficiency. Theoretical bounds are compared to prior work (e.g., Singla et al. ’15), and empirical results on real instances are provided.

### Strengths
- The problem is important and well-motivated, with applications in influence maximization, diverse recommendation, and facility location. (Reviewers 1–4)
- The adaptive sampling (CS) dynamically adjusts sample size to uncertainty, yielding substantial improvements in sample efficiency over fixed-precision methods like ExpGreedy. (Reviewer 4)
- Theoretical guarantees on both approximation quality and sample complexity are provided and supported by empirical evaluation on real datasets. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- The algorithm improves running time compared to previous works (e.g., quadratic dependence on threshold parameter ϕ instead of Δ_max). (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Moderate technical contribution.** The CS algorithm is not new; it existed in previous work (e.g., Alg 2 in [Mat]). The remaining algorithms are not new; the main contribution is applying CS to existing algorithms and analyzing the resulting sample complexity. The adaptation is not highly novel, and the proof techniques are elementary. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Unclear significance of the improvement.** It is not clear why dependence on ϕ is better than dependence on Δ_max. Is ϕ always smaller than Δ_max? (Reviewer 1) The theoretical bounds are complex and involve many parameters (R, ϵ, δ, ϕ) making it hard to see how they interact and scale in practice. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Practicality of bounds.** The gap parameter ϕ is typically unknown in advance, making the bounds less practical. Can ϕ be estimated without running the algorithm? (Reviewers 1, 3) In contrast, Δ_max could be estimated from input properties. (Reviewer 1)
- **Lack of tightness results.** Are there lower bounds on sample complexity showing the bounds are tight? (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing details on distribution robustness.** Does CS work well with other distributions beyond Gaussian? (Reviewer 2)
- **Limited scope of application.** Can CS be applied to the Minimum Cost Submodular Cover (MCSC) problem with better theoretical bounds? (Reviewer 2)
- **Scope alignment.** The paper is highly theoretical and may fit better in NeurIPS/ICML than ICLR, which traditionally focuses on deep learning. (Reviewer 4)
- **Reviewer disagreement in overall assessment.** Two reviewers (1 and 4) rated 6, finding the contributions solid and practically relevant. Two reviewers (2 and 3) rated 5, citing limited novelty and elementary analysis. This contrast reflects differing views on the significance and technical depth of the work.
