## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Free-MoE, a tuning-free method to convert a dense non-MoE LLM into an MoE LLM. It uses a Domain-Oriented Weight Purification (DOWP) Algorithm to generate domain-specific sparse weight matrices (experts) from the original dense weights, and a multi-level router to select the optimal experts per input. Experiments on models below 13B show 2–3% gains (up to 6.8%) on MMLU, HumanEval, GSM8K, etc. Reviewers found the idea potentially novel but raised major concerns about clarity, implementation details, evaluation completeness, and efficiency analysis. Reviewer 3 was notably more positive (score 5), while the other three assigned scores of 3.

### Strengths
- The method is interesting and novel: it leverages the inherent MoE structure from pre-trained dense LLMs without adding extra parameters or requiring tuning, potentially stabilizing optimization. (R3)
- Extensive experiments are conducted across multiple models (below 13B) and datasets, including a comprehensive ablation study. (R1, R3)
- Good algorithmic performance on challenging datasets such as MMLU, GSM8K, and MathQA, showing potential for further exploration. (R2)
- Enhances performance on specific tasks while simultaneously reducing inference costs; introduces a new architecture that leverages the MoE concept. (R4)
- (One reviewer was much more positive about the novelty and effectiveness than the others.) (R3 vs. R1, R2, R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and presentation clarity:** The abstract’s logic is hard to follow (e.g., first sentence says MoE reduces computation, second says MoE methods are costly in computation). Terminology is inconsistent (e.g., “domain-specific experts” vs. “Domain-Specific Subnetwork Experts” – are they different?). The paper contains many typos and poor grammar. (R2, R4)
- **Implementation details are missing or confusing:**
  - How many experts are produced per task? What is the total number? (R1, R2)
  - Storing experts requires additional memory – what is the overhead? (R1)
  - Algorithm 1 uses undefined terms: Perf (performance metric or abstract indicator?), alpha scaling (random search or heuristic?), and “patch” (specific structure or just an element?). (R3)
  - The relationship between DOWP and Free-MoE is unclear. (R4)
- **Evaluation gaps:**
  - Performance of experts on tasks outside their target domain is not reported – is the drop acceptable? (R1)
  - Only models below 13B are tested; larger models (e.g., LLaMA3-70B, Qwen2-72B) are ignored. (R2)
  - Generalizability: are experts effective on other datasets that fall into the same primary domain (e.g., “general”, “code”, “math”)? (R4)
- **No hardware performance analysis:** Typical MoE aims to reduce computation, but each expert appears to be an unstructured sparse weight matrix, which is hard to accelerate without special system support. Inference speed and FLOPs are not reported. (R1)
- **Efficiency drawbacks not discussed:** The method maintains indices to store expert-related information across coarse/fine-grained features, potentially degrading efficiency during inference. (R3)
- **Claims are inaccurate:** The method is described as “tuning-free” yet requires training a router. (R4)
- **Why performance improves is not explained:** Using fewer parameters (sparse weights) typically decreases performance; a clear explanation is needed. (R3)
- **Notation and equation issues:**
  - Equation 1: how is the estimation solved regarding datasets and tasks? (R2)
  - Equation 2: how is an embedding matrix converted to an embedding vector? (R2)
  - Equation 4: weight matrix and input activation have different sizes – how can the same index select in both? What does P_{i,j} stand for? (R2)
  - “Domain” and “dataset” are used interchangeably; it is unclear whether n is number of datasets or domains. D_A, S, D_{j_i} are undefined. Y is missing in Line 250. k denotes number of patches in one place and number of clusters elsewhere; in Algorithm 1, k appears in kmeans_clustering without prior definition. X in Sec. 3.1 may be activation embedding or self-attention head; in Sec. 3.2 X represents input tokens. (R4)
- **Figure and pseudo-code inconsistencies:**
  - Fig. 2 Step 1 includes an “XOR” operator before the hidden layer – why? What does “sort by row” mean? Step 2 has a dashed arrow from hidden layer to output, contradicting Fig. 3 where output comes only through DSS-Expert. The pseudo-code (Step 2 of Algorithm 1) takes patches with importance > θ, but Fig. 2 does not align. (R4)
  - In inference mode, Fig. 3 says DSS experts are dynamically formed – does this require iteratively finding the best threshold per token or per batch? (R4)
- **K-means details missing:**
  - Was one token or one batch used per domain? What initialization strategy was used? Should the centroid be cached and reused during inference? (R4)
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms (included as weaknesses):**
  - How does Free-MoE differ from unstructured weight pruning? (R1 – recommended adding discussion in related work.)
  - What is the density of experts on different tasks? Are there findings like math datasets needing denser weights than law datasets in MMLU? (R2)
  - On which model are the reported 2–3% and 1.11% gains based? (R4)
  - How does the work relate to “Interpretability of LLMs Mechanism” in Section 2.2? (R4)
- **Minor issues:**
  - In Line 21, “selects the optimal domain-specific experts of domain-specific experts” repeats “domain-specific experts”. (R2)
  - Typos: Euclidean distance F_T (not F_T); “In passing, the scaling” (not “The”); “These purified experts are dynamically activated” (not “expert in”); reference to Figure number inconsistent. (R4)
