## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper identifies three critical dependencies for long-term time series forecasting (LTSF) — order dependency, semantic dependency, and cross-variate dependency — and adapts the Mamba architecture to capture them. The authors propose SAMBA, which removes non-linear activation functions from vanilla Mamba and introduces a disentangled dependency encoding strategy to separately model cross-time and cross-variate dependencies, supported by theoretical justification. Experiments on nine datasets show improvements over various baselines.

### Strengths
- The overall idea of applying Mamba to multivariate time series forecasting is novel and timely. Investigating structured state-space models (SSMs) for LTSF is interesting.
- The paper provides a clear and comprehensive definition of three key dependencies (order, semantic, cross-variate), and the model architecture is well-designed to address them.
- The introduction of "semantic dependency" is novel to the time series field and contributes to framing the problem.
- The proposed architecture enhances Mamba’s ability to model cross-variate relationships, and experiments demonstrate that SAMBA outperforms previous methods and can be effectively transferred to other models.
- Theoretical properties of the method are provided, strengthening the design rationale.
- The organization of the paper is clear.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient evidence for claimed advantages of Mamba over Transformers and for key design choices**  
  - The evidence that Transformer-based models are unsuitable for modeling order information is insufficient. Tokenization and training method (e.g., causal masking) largely determine order sensitivity; both Mamba and Transformers can leverage these techniques. The success of LLMs shows Transformers can learn precise positional information. The authors should compare Mamba with Transformers using causal masks to establish structural superiority (Reviewer 2).  
  - The definition and boundary of “semantic dependency” remain unclear; it is not well distinguished from order (temporal) dependency, and it is not explained why historical values from other covariates are excluded (Reviewer 4).  
  - The claim that “most approaches struggle to capture reliable dependencies” (lines 014-016) lacks rigor and does not acknowledge existing progress.  
  - The claim that Transformers “struggle with perceiving temporal order due to permutation-invariant self-attention” (lines 052-054) is debatable; being less sensitive to order may actually be beneficial in some cases, and the experiments only show that Transformers are less sensitive to permutation, not that this harms performance.  
  - The claim that “channel-dependent approaches frequently underperform compared to channel-independent” (lines 058-061) is not universally true; works like iTransformer demonstrate benefits of cross-variate modeling.  
- **Insufficient and narrow experimental validation**  
  - The core analyses (Tables 1-3, Figure 1) are conducted only on the small ETTm1 dataset, which is insufficient to draw general conclusions. The nature of the dataset significantly affects model performance. Experiments should be extended to datasets with richer dynamics (e.g., Solar, Exchange, Traffic, PEMS) to validate claims about ordering, patching, and linearity (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).  
  - The permutation test in Table 1 can be interpreted in multiple ways. A counterexample with an MA(1) process shows that better models may be less sensitive to order, undermining the conclusion.  
  - Table 2 investigates patching effectiveness but does not include PatchTST itself, and the impact of patch size is unexplored. The conclusion that patching enhances semantic dependency may be dataset-sensitive and contradicts the idea that patching reduces the need for complex semantic modeling (Reviewer 2).  
  - Table 3 is foundational but lacks comparisons with Patch+MLP, and performance improvements from removing non-linearities are not consistent (e.g., Patch+Mamba shows a performance drop). Theoretical analysis for regularization benefits is needed, along with validation on additional datasets (ECL, Traffic, Weather) (Reviewer 3).  
  - Baseline results appear downgraded: e.g., PatchTST on ETTh1 reports MSE 0.469/MAE 0.454 in this paper, while the original paper reports much lower errors (Reviewer 1).  
  - The efficiency comparison (dataset unspecified, “Mem” unexplained) is unclear and difficult to verify (Reviewer 3).  
- **Lack of unique contributions and innovation**  
  - Beyond studying Mamba for time series, the paper offers few novel ideas: patching, cross-variate/time dependency, and normalization have been extensively explored. Without leveraging Mamba’s core advantage (efficient long-sequence modeling), the necessity of adapting Mamba is questionable (Reviewer 1).  
  - The SAMBA block design closely resembles existing works [1,2]; the only difference is removing the non-linear activation. The paper does not provide a clear, dedicated mechanism to enforce disentanglement of cross-time and cross-variate dependencies – interference likely persists through summation and backpropagation (Reviewers 3, 4). The definition of “disentangled” in the time-series context is missing (Reviewer 3).  
  - The paper should include comparisons with recent LLM-based LTSF methods (e.g., LLM4TS, GPT4TS, CATS) (Reviewer 4).  
- **Misleading title and model name**  
  - The title “Simplified Mamba” is misleading: performance improvements from removing non-linearity are not significant compared to other strategies (e.g., patch tokenization), removal is not fully implemented (non-linearity remains in the gating), and the overall architecture is more complex (bidirectional Mamba along variate dimension) (Reviewer 2).  
- **Experimental methodology issues**  
  - Baseline implementations are confusing: the paper claims results come from the TimesNet GitHub repository but also from iTransformer. The specific source and implementation details for each baseline should be clarified (Reviewer 3).  
  - Tables lack explanation: in Table 1, what do bold values mean? Does “Transformer” refer to a vanilla or SOTA variant? Table 3 should include Patch+MLP (Reviewer 3).  
  - The choice of lookback length T=96 does not exploit Mamba’s advantage for very long sequences, reducing the impact of the study (Reviewer 1).  
- **Minor weaknesses**  
  - Many spelling, formatting, and typographical errors (e.g., “LTSF” vs “LTST”, formatting of Theorem 1, “Solar-Energy)or”, “Exchange ) overall”) require thorough proofreading (Reviewers 2, 3).  
  - The SAMBA block should be directly compared with the original Mamba block in a figure (Reviewer 4).  
  - Model performance may be highly dependent on patching parameters; this sensitivity should be discussed (Reviewer 4).  
- **Disagreement among reviewers**: Scores of 3, 6, 3, 6 indicate a split. The two lower-scoring reviewers (1 and 3) find the contributions weak and the experimental validation insufficient, while the higher-scoring reviewers (2 and 4) acknowledge novelty and solid experiments but still raise several of the above concerns. The overall confidence in the paper's claims is tempered by the narrow scope and inconsistent evidence.
