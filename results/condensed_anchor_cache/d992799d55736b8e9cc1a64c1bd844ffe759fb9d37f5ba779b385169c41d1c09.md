## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes 4DEditPro, a framework for editing 4D scenes from casual monocular videos using text prompts. It introduces Temporally Propagated Editing (TPE) and Spatially Propagated Editing (SPE) modules to improve temporal and spatial consistency, and uses a pose-free 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) approach for reconstruction and editing without requiring camera poses. The method is evaluated on several monocular dynamic scenes and compared with static 3D editing baselines.

### Strengths
- First approach for 4D scene editing from casual monocular videos, eliminating the need for camera pose input.
- Introduces TPE and SPE modules to improve temporal and spatial consistency.
- Paper is well-structured, clearly written with clear formulas and descriptions.
- Quantitative evaluations show better performance over baselines; ablation studies demonstrate component effectiveness.
- Experiments are thorough and designed with sufficient detail for reproducibility (Reviewer 2).
- Well-organized presentation of a pipeline for editing 4D from monocular video using Gaussian Splatting.

### Weaknesses
- **Task definition and novelty concerns**: The reasonability of “4D scene editing from monocular video” is questioned – the conversion from video to 4D scene is closer to reconstruction, and many paper contents (Sec. 3.2) are orthogonal to editing (Reviewer 3). The task degrades to “video editing with 3D consistency”; the model only works on monocular video, reducing spatial consistency challenges (Reviewer 3). The pipeline lacks novel insights, with TPE/SPE seen as integration of minor techniques (Reviewers 2, 4). Several reviewers note the editing results are limited to color, style, and texture adjustments with minimal shape editing (Reviewer 1). Editing is imprecise (e.g., “silver” and “night” edits in Fig. 4 appear unnatural, Reviewer 2).
- **Temporal consistency and view synthesis issues**: Temporal consistency is not maintained – noticeable flickering in supplementary video (e.g., sailing boat 00:26–00:28, Minecraft 00:45–00:47, horse 00:48–00:51, statue 00:52–00:56, Reviewer 1). Synthesized novel views show minimal differentiation from the original video (segments 00:22–00:23, 1:08–1:12, Reviewer 1). The supplementary video mainly demonstrates static view synthesis, despite the method being proposed for 4D editing (Reviewer 1). Novel view pose changes are too subtle, making results closer to video editing than 4D editing (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient and inappropriate comparisons**: The paper only compares with static 3D scene editing methods (e.g., GSEditor-4D), not with video editing models (e.g., TokenFlow, Fatezero, Flatten) which are directly relevant since the task is monocular video editing with 3D consistency (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). The key baseline **Instruct 4D-to-4D** is not compared – its code was released one month before the ICLR deadline and its datasets are public, making a comparison feasible (Reviewer 3). No experiments on standard 4D datasets (DyNeRF, HyperNeRF, Google Immersive) as used by Instruct 4D-to-4D (Reviewers 3, 4). The paper only evaluates on three monocular dynamic scenes; baseline uses at least 8 scenes (DyCheck, DyNeRF, Google Immersive) with up to 300 frames (Reviewer 3). For static scenes (Tanks and Temples, SemanticKITTI), it is unclear why the 4D version of Gaussian Editor was used instead of the 3D version (Reviewer 1). The model requires the user to provide descriptions of both original and edited scenes, demanding more human work than baselines IN2N, GSEditor-4D, and Instruct 4D-to-4D which only need an editing instruction (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 suggests a baseline of video style transfer followed by 4D reconstruction would be more appropriate.
- **Technical and reporting issues**: The process for selecting the reference token lacks detail and its impact is unclear (Reviewer 2). The explanation for setting camera poses using Slerp is unclear; robustness to camera pose variations is not demonstrated (Reviewer 4). In Tab. 1, only the “Average” row is bolded for best numbers; other rows should also be bolded (Reviewer 3). The method’s contributions may be overstated as the editing results focus on color, style, and texture (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 (score 5) is notably more positive, acknowledging thorough experiments and task value, while Reviewers 1, 3, and 4 (score 3) raise fundamental concerns about novelty, comparisons, and evaluation scope.
