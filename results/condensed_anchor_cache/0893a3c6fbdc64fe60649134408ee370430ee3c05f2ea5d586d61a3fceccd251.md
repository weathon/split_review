## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates how data augmentation affects graph contrastive learning (GCL). The authors argue that GCL benefits downstream tasks primarily by separating different classes (reducing negative center similarity) rather than by gathering nodes of the same class. They claim that perfect alignment (maximizing positive pair similarity) helps contrastive loss but harms generalization, so a carefully designed augmentation that avoids perfect alignment is needed. The paper provides theoretical analysis using infimum bounds, information theory, and graph spectrum theory, and proposes two simple augmentation strategies (information-based and spectrum-based) that can be plugged into existing GCL methods. Experiments on several datasets (citation networks) are conducted to support the claims.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a valuable and important research question: understanding the relationship between data augmentation, contrastive loss, and downstream generalization in GCL.
- It provides a novel perspective that challenges the conventional assumption that perfect alignment of positive pairs is always beneficial, and highlights the role of inter-class separation.
- The analysis (though sometimes unclear in writing) is informative, and the authors support their claims with both theoretical reasoning and empirical evidence.
- The proposed augmentation methods are simple, easy to integrate into various GCL frameworks, and are demonstrated to improve performance on most datasets and baselines in the experiments.
- The experiments are extensive and cover multiple state-of-the-art GCL methods and datasets; the paper is generally well-structured and easy to follow (Reviewer 2, others agree on soundness).
- Reviewer 5 acknowledges the theoretical analysis and the empirical evidence that GCL primarily separates classes.

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical argument based on infimum bounds is flawed.** Several theorems (Theorem 2.3, Theorem 2.5, Corollary 3.1) derive lower bounds in the form of infimum. A decrease in a lower bound does not imply a decrease in the actual value; the conclusion that “negative center similarity will be more likely to be lower” or that “better generalization correlates with worse positive center similarity” is not logically supported. This critique is raised by Reviewer 5 (with high confidence, rating 3, score 3) and is a major concern. Other reviewers did not flag this issue, indicating a strong disagreement in assessment. (Reviewer 5, Weaknesses 1,3,4,5)
- **The justification for substituting $1-\delta_{aug}$ for the positive pair similarity is not justified.** The numerator in the NCE loss is a dynamic function of the model and data, and the argument that it can be directly replaced by $1-\delta_{aug}$ is not well supported. (Reviewer 5, Weakness 4)
- **Perfect alignment vs. sweet spot is not analyzed.** The paper states that perfect alignment is poisonous, but does not identify where the optimal trade-off lies (the “sweet point” between intra-class pulling and inter-class pushing). The threshold $\epsilon$ appears heuristic and is not connected to the positive-negative balance. (Reviewer 3, Weakness 1; also implied by Reviewer 4’s concern about trade-off)
- **Empirical improvements are marginal**, and no error bars or statistical significance are reported. Several reviewers request that results be averaged over multiple runs with error bars to confirm stability and reliability. (Reviewer 3 Weakness 2, Reviewer 2 Weakness 2, Reviewer 5 Weakness 6)
- **Missing experimental details:** dataset sizes, train/val/test splits, backbone architecture, and the specific settings for achieving the reported performance (e.g., how does any GCL method approach 100% accuracy on citation networks?). (Reviewer 1 Question 2.3, Reviewer 2 Weakness 2)
- **No discussion of the trade-off between positive and negative center similarity.** Figure 8 shows that information augmentation increases both positive and negative similarity simultaneously. The paper does not analyze whether the benefit from increased positive similarity outweighs the potential harm from increased negative similarity, or whether this leads to misclassification. (Reviewer 4 Weakness 1)
- **The proposed augmentations lack novelty** as they are very similar to existing works (GCS by Wei et al. 2023 for information theory, GAME by Liu et al. 2022 for graph spectrum), yet these baselines are not compared in the experiments. The contribution appears to be more about explaining existing augmentations than introducing new techniques. (Reviewer 1 Weakness 3, Reviewer 2 Weakness 1, Reviewer 5 Weakness 7)
- **No analysis of why different augmentation strategies work better on different datasets.** Table 1 shows that information augmentation is better on some datasets and spectrum augmentation on others; the paper does not explain which dataset characteristics (structural properties, degree distributions) determine the effectiveness. (Reviewer 4 Weakness 2)
- **Label-consistency is harder to guarantee on graphs** than on images (Assumption 2.1), raising doubt about whether the theoretical basis is practically satisfiable. Experiments on images might be more convincing. (Reviewer 1 Weakness 4)
- **Concluding statement about generalization to other domains is unsupported.** The paper claims “it works the same in other fields” without evidence, and graph contrastive learning differs from vision contrastive learning (Guo et al. 2023). (Reviewer 1 Question 1)
- **Figure 1 claims are not robust.** The claim that negative center similarity decreases faster than positive center similarity is not consistently observed across all four datasets; only 2/4 show the expected trend. Moreover, the accuracy in some datasets seems positively correlated with positive similarity, contradicting the main claim. (Reviewer 1 Question 2, Reviewer 5 Weakness 2)
- **Missing related work discussion.** The paper frequently references Wang et al. 2022b and Saunshi et al. 2022 but does not provide an explicit comparison of differences in the main text. (Reviewer 1 Weakness 1)
- **Unclear definitions.** Key terms such as “augmentation overlap,” “perfect alignment,” $\delta_{aug}$, $\omega_y$ in $L_{CE}$, and $M$ in $L_{NCE}$ are not formally defined when first introduced, making the theoretical sections harder to follow. (Reviewer 1 Weakness 2)
- **Minor error:** In Section 2.3, “Theorem 2.5 explains why as the augmentation becomes stronger, negative center similarity decreases” should refer to Theorem 2.3. (Reviewer 1 Question 4)
