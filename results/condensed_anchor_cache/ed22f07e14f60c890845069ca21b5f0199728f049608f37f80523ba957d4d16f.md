## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates normalization techniques in the Mamba architecture, examining the effects of normalization type, position, and combination on performance and training stability. Experiments cover long sequence modeling (LRA ListOps) and image classification (ImageNet-100). The study finds that applying normalization after the SSM (S6) module when used once, and combining different normalization layers before and after the SSM, can improve stability and performance. Practical recommendations are provided. However, all three reviewers note significant limitations in novelty, task diversity, generalizability, and support for key hypotheses.

### Strengths
- Addresses the important challenge of improving training stability in Mamba networks (R1, R2, R3).
- Provides practical insights into normalization layer positioning and combination that can directly benefit practitioners (R1, R2, R3).
- Offers comprehensive empirical results with systematic evaluation of five normalization techniques, two task types, and multiple configurations (R1, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited task validation and small datasets:** Experiments are restricted to two relatively small datasets (long sequence modeling on LRA ListOps, image classification on ImageNet-100). This is insufficient to draw broad conclusions, and the findings appear task-dependent with contradictory results between vision and sequence tasks (R1, R2, R3). The paper should include diverse tasks such as object detection, semantic segmentation, NLP, speech recognition, or reinforcement learning (R2, R3). It is not clear how broadly applicable the insights are for the broader Mamba architecture (R1, R2, R3).
- **Lack of novelty and insufficient discussion of prior work:** The insights are not considered novel (R1). Prior works have extensively explored normalization in related architectures (e.g., Transformers). The paper would benefit from including discussion of works such as Xiong et al. (ICML 2020), Santurkar et al., Bjorck et al., and others (R1). The analysis is not sufficiently different from previous normalization studies (R1).
- **Weak Weight Domination hypothesis and contradictory evidence:** The Weight Domination hypothesis, central to the findings, lacks sufficient supporting evidence (R2, R3). Contradictory results for the BN→None configuration weaken its credibility (R2, R3). The paper does not provide a clear mechanistic explanation for when the hypothesis holds or fails, suggesting other unaddressed factors (R2). More empirical evidence or explanation of contradictions is needed (R3).
- **Contradictory findings without explanation:** Findings contradict across tasks: normalization after the S6 module is beneficial for sequence modeling (Section 4.1) but not for image classification (Section 4.3) (R2, R3). Vision Mamba shows LN is sufficient, contradicting sequence modeling results where LN+RMSN underperforms relative to LN+LN (R2, R3). The paper fails to provide an in-depth analysis or explanation for these discrepancies, undermining the robustness of conclusions (R2, R3).
- **Methodological issues:**  
  *Reproducibility:* Experimental settings are poorly documented; crucial details about accuracy metrics are missing (e.g., Tables 2 and 3 lack specification of the accuracy metric used). Hyperparameters, evaluation metrics, and data preprocessing steps should be provided (R3).  
  *Inconsistent visualization:* Figure 3 has varying x-axes and plotting styles, and subfigures (2), (3), (4) lack clear x-axis labels, hampering interpretation. Standardization and clearer labels are needed (R2, R3).
- **Lack of generalizability and insufficient justification:**  
  The focus on long sequence modeling for normalization position analysis lacks justification; shorter sequence tasks and other data types should also be considered (R2).  
  The conclusions appear highly task-specific (e.g., BN is particularly effective after the Mamba block for vision, Section 4.3) and do not provide general insights that could replace task-specific ablation studies (R2, R3). The work falls short of offering a unified framework or principles for selecting normalization strategies for new tasks (R2).
- **Minor presentation issue:** Figure 1 caption and presentation can be improved for clarity (R1).
- **Contrast in reviewer assessments:** While all reviewers rate the paper 3, one reviewer (R3) assigns contribution 2 (others 1) and higher confidence (4 vs 3), suggesting a slightly more positive view on the paper’s empirical contribution, but still agrees on major weaknesses. Another reviewer (R2) assigns lower soundness (2) compared to the others (3).
