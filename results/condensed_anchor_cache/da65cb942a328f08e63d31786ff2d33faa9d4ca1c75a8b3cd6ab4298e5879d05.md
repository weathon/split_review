## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SFCNN, a CNN architecture that uses stacked 3×3 convolutions instead of large kernels to capture long-range dependencies while maintaining hardware efficiency. It introduces a dual 3×3 depthwise convolution bottleneck and a Global Sigmoid Linear Unit (GSiLU) activation function. Evaluated on ImageNet-1K classification, COCO instance segmentation, and ADE20K semantic segmentation, SFCNN achieves better accuracy-latency trade-offs compared to existing ConvNets and ViTs. Reviewers universally praise the practical motivation and thorough experiments, but all four raise major concerns about novelty. Most reviewers rate the paper as borderline (scores 5,5,5,3), with Reviewer 4 being notably more negative (score 3) due to minor gains and hyperparameter sensitivity.

### Strengths
- **(All reviewers)** Clear practical motivation: addressing hardware unfriendliness of large‑kernel CNNs by stacking small 3×3 kernels for better speed and efficiency; strong industrial relevance (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **(R1, R2, R3, R4)** Thorough experimental validation: main results on ImageNet-1K, COCO, ADE20K; ablations on depth vs. width, activation functions, receptive field; cross‑platform latency tests (GPU, TensorRT, iPhone). Consistency across scales and tasks (R1, R4).
- **(R1, R3)** Good accuracy‑speed trade-off: SFCNN outperforms large‑kernel CNNs and ViTs while being more efficient (R1, R3).
- **(R1)** Scalability shown across different model sizes (R1).
- **(R4)** Paper writing is clear, with well‑organized motivation, architecture description, results, and ablations.

### Weaknesses
- **(R1, R2, R3, R4 – unanimous)** Lack of novelty / originality.
    - Stacking 3×3 convolutions for larger receptive field dates back to VGG (2014) and is used in ResNets, MobileNets, etc. (R2, R3, R4).
    - Depthwise convolutions well explored (Xception, MobileNet) (R2).
    - Dual 3×3 depthwise bottleneck similar to MobileNeXt (ECCV’20) (R3).
    - GSiLU activation is essentially a form of Squeeze‑and‑Excitation / CBAM (R1, R2).
    - Thin‑and‑deep design philosophy already known (R1).
    - No new theoretical insights or empirical findings that go beyond known techniques (R1, R2, R3).
- **(R1, R3, R4)** Minor performance gains vs. large variance in hyperparameters.
    - Gains over SOTA are at most ~0.1% on ImageNet (e.g., +0.1% vs. SwiftFormer) (R4).
    - Architecture hyperparameter search (Table 9) shows large variance (81.3 vs. 82.6 min/max) while the “SOTA gain” is <1% (R4). This suggests the contribution is more in the search than in the architecture itself (R4).
    - R3 questions whether large effective RF is significant when SFCNN’s RF (Fig. 3) is smaller than UniRepLKNet yet accuracy is higher.
- **(R1, R3)** Missing comparisons and analyses.
    - No comparison with recent large‑kernel CNNs (e.g., UniRepLKNet) on downstream tasks (R3, Table 5 uses older methods).
    - Lack of comparisons with emerging architectures like Vision Mamba or RWKV (R1).
    - Missing analysis of trade‑offs: parameter efficiency vs. speed (e.g., MogaNet is more parameter‑efficient but slower) (R1).
    - Missing memory‑compute trade‑off and training vs. inference efficiency analysis (R1).
    - R1 asks: how does stacking multiple depthwise convolutions in one block improve representation capacity?.
- **(R1, R3, R4)** GSiLU motivation and usage unclear.
    - Why should global information be added into activation functions? (R3).
    - Why is original SiLU still used alongside GSiLU? (R3).
    - GSiLU not sufficiently distinguished from prior art (R1, R2).
- **(R1, R3)** Presentation issues.
    - Writing lacks coherence; methodological details scattered (R1).
    - Table formatting inconsistencies (e.g., line thickness) (R1).
    - Table 7 has two captions; Table 8 missing or confused with Table 7 (R3, R4).
- **(R1)** Missing empirical analyses: Grad‑CAM heatmaps, training dynamics, failure cases, limitations, extreme resource constraints. Thin‑and‑deep design not systematic.
- **(R4 – minority negative)** Minor performance gain vs. hyperparameter variance is a core weakness; paper feels like a “revisit” rather than a novel contribution. Suggests that with NAS such architecture search can be automated.
- **(R4)** Question: how does SFCNN perform with ImageNet‑22k pretraining + fine‑tuning? (Not required but would alleviate performance concerns.)
- **(R3)** Macro model architecture differs from large‑kernel CNNs, so the claimed “efficiency advantage” needs direct controlled experiments (R3).
