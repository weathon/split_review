## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an information-theoretic framework to quantify structure in LLM representation spaces, defining measures of regularity, variation, and disentanglement based on entropy estimates. It introduces a “soft entropy” estimator that avoids binning by computing the entropy of average softmax-weighted cosine similarities to uniformly sampled anchor points on the unit sphere. Experiments on BERT and Pythia models examine how these measures evolve during training, vary with model size, and correlate with downstream GLUE performance. The key claimed differentiators from prior work (especially Conklin & Smith 2024) are the soft entropy procedure and the correlation analysis with benchmark tasks. Reviewer 2 (score 5) is notably more positive, emphasizing the value of simple, dataset-agnostic interpretability tools and the potential for predicting downstream accuracy; other reviewers (scores 3, 3, 3) find substantial weaknesses in novelty, clarity, evaluation, and rigor.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles an important problem: characterizing LLM representations with interpretable, quantitative measures (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- The soft entropy estimator is simple and does not require per-dimension binning; it is differentiable, which could enable gradient-based analysis (Reviewer 3; noted as a potential advantage over bin-based methods).
- Predicting downstream task performance from representational structure alone is a neat and potentially useful application (Reviewers 2,4); Reviewer 2 finds this “very interesting” and draws a parallel to the WeightWatcher tool for model weights.
- The approach works with any generic text corpus and does not rely on specific datasets (Reviewer 2).
- Reviewer 1 acknowledges that the confirmation of low intrinsic dimension and structured representation space during pretraining aligns with generally agreed findings.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty / overlap with prior work**: The entropy estimation method, the definitions of regularity, disentanglement, and variation, and the observation of a two-phase structural transition all closely mirror Conklin & Smith (2024) and/or Saxe et al. (2019), Shwartz-Ziv & Tishby (2017). The authors do not adequately clarify the differentiation (Reviewers 1,3). Reviewer 3 notes that the title could better reflect actual differentiation from [CS2024].
- **Insufficient justification of definitions and design choices**: The formulations of variation, regularity, disentanglement, and soft entropy are sketchy and lack mathematical rigor. For example, $Z$ is defined as a set but then $H(Z)$ (entropy of a set) is used, which is a property of probability distributions (Reviewer 3). The explanation of disentanglement via Jensen-Shannon divergence is unclear (Reviewer 3). The connection to linguistic concepts (n-gram features) is not motivated; the mapping studied is between representations and n-grams (n≤3), which is not the symbol-meaning mapping implied by denotational semantics (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 also calls the definitions “quite arbitrary”.
- **Soft entropy estimator lacks theoretical analysis and has known issues**: Equation (5) is underspecified (summation index missing) and the mathematical formalization is unclear (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 4). Reviewer 3 points out that if all $z$’s in $Z$ are identical, the soft entropy would not be near zero (as expected for a degenerate distribution), which may be a problem. Reviewer 4 asks about sensitivity to dimensionality: as dimension increases, vectors become nearly orthogonal, requiring many more anchor points; the paper does not test scaling behavior or provide error analysis. No theoretical motivation or convergence guarantees are given (Reviewer 4).
- **Incomplete evaluation and insufficient ablation**: The observed two-phase transition and compressed dimensionality could stem from model architecture, training data, or loss functions; the authors should ablate, e.g., by training BERT on corrupted text (word-order shuffled or nonsensical tokens) (Reviewer 1). The correlation analysis with GLUE is weak: many tasks show no correlation or negative correlations, and the paper cherry-picks the one positive result without multiple comparison correction (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 2 notes that only correlations are reported, not regressions, to support claims of predicting benchmark performance.
- **Writing and presentation issues**: The paper is colloquial, imprecise, and lacks structure (Reviewers 1,3,4). Examples: “system level” introduced without definition (L85); run-on sentence at L266; non-standard summation notation in Eqs. 2-4 (“very non-standard form” – Reviewer 4); typos (“effect” for “affect” at L034), missing bounding explanation at L172, unclear description of “heads” in Sec. 4.1 (whether they relate to attention heads or arbitrary chops – Reviewer 4 Q5). The introduction is vague, taking too long to get to the point (Reviewer 4).
- **Related work is too short** (Reviewer 2).
- **Training-time experiments show limited insight**: The initial change followed by smooth degradation does not reveal an interesting pattern (Reviewer 2).
- **Minority reviewer contrast**: Reviewer 2 (score 5) is more positive overall, valuing the simplicity and applicability, but still flags the limited related work, lack of regression for prediction claims, and unremarkable training dynamics. The other three reviewers (scores 3,3,3) are considerably more critical, especially on novelty and clarity.
