## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a general stability-analysis framework for zeroth-order optimization algorithms, extending prior work (limited to vanilla 2-point Gaussian estimates with SGD) to convex, strongly convex, and non-convex settings. It provides bounds for ZO-SGD, ZO-GD, and ZO-SVRG using 1-point, 2-point, and coordinate-wise gradient estimates. The analysis shows that coordinate estimation yields tighter generalization bounds. Numerical experiments on real-world data support the theory. There is a clear split in reviewer opinion: reviewers 1 and 2 (score 6) find the contributions somewhat incremental and the experiments insufficient, while reviewers 3 and 4 (score 8) view the framework as a novel and strong theoretical contribution.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized, with clear motivation, extensive literature review, and rigorous theoretical analysis. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- The framework is simple yet powerful; the idea of approximating gradients coordinate-wise is novel and leads to tighter generalization bounds for ZO-SGD, ZO-GD, and ZO-SVRG that match first-order bounds in strongly convex, convex, and non-convex cases. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- The mathematical theory is strict and the empirical results are consistent with the theoretical analysis. (R1)
- The differences between generalization bounds for different estimators are captured and analyzed, making the theory complete and convincing. (R4)
- The technique used is solid and interesting. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing theoretical completeness**: For ZO-SGD, ZO-GD, and ZO-SVRG with 1-point and 2-point estimates, results are only presented for the non-convex setting. General convex and strongly convex results (analogous to those for coordinate-wise estimation) are missing; adding them would make the theoretical contributions more complete. (R1)
- **Incremental contribution**: The primary lemmas (Lemma 3 and 4) build incrementally on prior work by Hardt et al. (2016) and Nikolakakis et al. (2022). (R2) Some reviewers (R3, R4) find the results strong and novel, but others (R2) consider the novelty limited; the paper should better emphasize the new ideas relative to Nikolakakis et al. (2022). (R2, R4)
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation**: The experiments are limited. They should include a comparison of the performance of different zeroth-order estimators (1-point, 2-point, coordinate-wise) to empirically support the favorable theoretical results for coordinate estimation. (R2)
- **Implementation details missing**: It is unclear which zeroth-order estimator was used in the experiments. More implementation details are needed; parenthetical citations would improve readability. (R2)
- **Notation/display issues**: \(\beta\) and \(C\) should be indicated in Table 1; Figure 2(b) appears to label “Generalization error: GD” when it should be “Generalization error: SGD”. (R2)
- **Assumption of smoothness**: The analysis assumes the objective function is smooth, which does not hold for many important problems (e.g., learning ReLU networks). It would be valuable to discuss whether the results can be extended to non-smooth objectives, and whether any generalization bounds exist even for first-order algorithms in that setting. (R3)
- **Unclear empirical behavior**: In Figure 1(b), the generalization bound of ZO-SVRG decreases at the beginning—this behavior is not explained and warrants clarification. (R4)
