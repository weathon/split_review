## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the geometry of reinforcement learning in continuous state and action spaces. It shows that training dynamics of a two-layer neural policy (with GeLU activation) using a semi-gradient actor‑critic algorithm induce a low‑dimensional manifold of attainable states embedded in the high‑dimensional nominal state space. The dimension of this manifold is bounded by approximately \(2 \times \text{action dimension} + 1\) (Theorem 1). The bound is empirically corroborated on four MuJoCo environments and a toy environment with varying dimensionality. A practical application replaces one fully connected layer in the policy/value networks with a sparse representation (manifold‑learning layer) to improve performance in high‑degree‑of‑freedom control tasks.

### Strengths
- **Novel theoretical insight**: The geometric lens linking the dimensionality of the attainable state manifold to the action space dimension is, to the reviewers’ knowledge, the first result of its kind. Theorem 1 formally establishes that the manifold dimension is bounded by \(2d_a + 1\). (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- **Rigorous mathematical analysis**: The paper derives the manifold dimensionality using differential geometry (Lie series, vector fields) and semi‑gradient dynamics. (Reviewer 1)
- **Empirical validation**: Experiments on multiple MuJoCo environments (e.g., HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d, Ant) quantitatively measure the intrinsic dimension of the attainable state set and confirm it lies below the theoretical upper bound. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- **Practical application**: Using a sparse representation (manifold‑learning layer) in policy and value networks improves performance in complex control tasks with very high degrees of freedom. (Reviewer 2,3)
- **Clear organization and writing**: The paper is generally well‑structured and logical, though complexity is noted as a weakness. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and accessibility of the mathematical framework**:  
  - The introduction of manifolds, Lie derivatives, exponential maps, and Lie algebras in Section 2 feels disconnected from the core RL problem; the relevance to the low‑dimensional manifold derivation is not immediately apparent. The main storyline is unclear before page 7. (Reviewer 4; also Reviewer 2 notes the presentation is too dense)  
  - Better intuition and concise explanations after each major step would help a broader audience. (Reviewer 2)  
  - Minor language issues: awkward sentences at L263‑265, caption of Figure 3, L462, and the paragraph under Equation (14) (L475‑478). (Reviewer 1)  
  - Fonts in Figures 2, 3, and 4 are too small to read. (Reviewer 1)

- **Assumptions limiting realism and generality**:  
  - The analysis assumes deterministic transitions and access to an exact value function, which is impractical in dynamic, stochastic environments. (Reviewer 3; also Reviewer 4 questions the continuous‑time MDP assumption)  
  - The mathematical framework is limited to two‑layer neural networks with GeLU activation. The theoretical analysis further relies on a linear approximation, making it unclear how this differs from a single‑layer network. The impact of approximation errors on the derived low‑dimensional manifold is not addressed, and no justification is given for why the linear approximation is sufficient. Extension to deeper architectures and other activation functions is needed to support generalizability. (Reviewers 3 and 4)  
  - The paper studies continuous‑time MDPs theoretically, but the MuJoCo simulations use discrete‑time systems (DDPG, SAC policy gradients). The extent to which discrete‑time simulation can verify the continuous‑time theoretical results is not discussed. (Reviewer 4)

- **Scalability and comparison**:  
  - The computational efficiency of the sparsification layer compared to fully connected layers is not analyzed, especially as the number of states and actions grows. (Reviewer 2)  
  - No comparison with other state representation techniques (e.g., latent variable models in RL) is provided. (Reviewer 2)  
  - The simulation setups (MuJoCo) may not capture the complexity and variability of real‑world tasks with high‑dimensional data and environmental noise. (Reviewer 3)

- **Missing explanations and validation gaps**:  
  - Figure 2: caption states the green line is \(d_s\), but the theoretical upper bound is \(2d_a+1\); the meaning of the red line is not explained. (Reviewers 1 and 2)  
  - Figure 4: legend refers to “Group” without explanation. (Reviewer 1)  
  - Equation (9): what does \(\mathbb{B}_{W_{k \eta}}\) represent? Why does batch data come from an SDE? Which prior continuous‑time policy gradient works use this concept? (Reviewer 4)  
  - Equation (10): does \(G(W)\) contain \(\eta\)? (Reviewer 4)  
  - How does the present work differ from Ben Arous et al. (2022) given similar proof techniques? (Reviewer 4)  
  - Adaptation to stochastic transitions and noisy rewards is not addressed. (Reviewer 3)  
  - The manifold‑learning approach is only tested with two‑layer networks; its effectiveness with deeper architectures remains an open question. (Reviewer 3)

**Minority viewpoint**: One reviewer (score 6, confidence 3) was significantly more critical of the paper, raising the above clarity concerns, questioning the validity of the linear approximation and the gap between theoretical assumptions (continuous‑time, exact value function) and the discrete‑time simulation algorithms used for empirical verification. The same reviewer also questioned the novelty relative to prior work (Ben Arous et al., 2022).
