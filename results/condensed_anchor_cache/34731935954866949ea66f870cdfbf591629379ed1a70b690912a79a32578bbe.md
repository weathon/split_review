## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies ansatz expressivity in quantum neural networks (QNNs), arguing that closeness to a 2-design is an inadequate measure. It introduces channel sensitivity (diamond norm under parameter perturbation) as a metric, derives an upper bound, and provides numerical experiments on hardware-efficient ansatzes (HEAs) with amplitude encoding. The paper concludes that small QNNs are hardly discriminable upon parameter update, suggesting iterative training may not be effective.

### Strengths
- The work takes a fresh perspective on training difficulties in QNNs by focusing on the ansatz, which is a foundational and widely applicable issue (all reviewers).
- The introduction and background are well-written, offering a succinct overview of QNNs for a broader ICLR readership (Reviewer 2).
- The criticism of the 2-design measure is clear and effectively demonstrates its shortcomings (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- The proposed channel sensitivity metric based on the diamond norm is principled and novel, aiming to characterize the action of a unitary on any state (Reviewers 2, 4).
- The numerical study considers several important architecture combinations (e.g., varying layers, parameters, random versus trained perturbations) (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The paper addresses an important open problem: understanding the expressivity-trainability trade-off in QNNs (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing experimental details and robustness analysis**: The paper does not specify the optimizer, hyperparameters, or confidence interval methodology for the numerical experiments. The dependence of results on the choice of optimizer and hyperparameters has not been studied, weakening the validity of the conclusions (Reviewer 3).
- **Overstated conclusions from a loose upper bound**: The derived bound is an upper bound, not an equality. Several key claims (e.g., “channel sensitivity scales with magnitude of changes and number of parameters”; “many parameters are required to distinguish operations”) are stated as if they apply to the actual channel sensitivity, but the experiments show the bound is very loose. The paper needs to clarify that conclusions are based on the bound and discuss the implications of its looseness (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Unclear usefulness of the proposed metric**: It remains unclear what a practitioner can do with the channel distinguishability metric or its upper bound. The bound only says QNNs change gradually under small perturbations, which is already natural for any neural network. Also, the diamond norm assumes a single measurement on the whole state, not multiple measurements or a specific observable, so its translation to practical QNN distinguishability is not justified (Reviewer 2).
- **Computational difficulty and lack of comparison to Haar-based measures**: The diamond norm scales poorly with the number of qubits, raising practical applicability concerns. The paper does not compare the computational scaling or relative advantages of the proposed metric over expressivity measures based on closeness to the full Haar measure (not just t-designs) (Reviewer 4). Some reviewers found the metric principled, but others doubt its practical advantage.
- **Ignores the role of data encoding**: The paper treats expressivity solely as a property of the ansatz, ignoring the crucial role of feature encoding. It is known that the spectral representation of a QNN is directly given by feature encodings (Schuld, 2021). Reviewer 1 also asks what proportion of expressivity issues are due to ansatz vs encoding, and Reviewer 3 asks for dependence on input-encoding (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Lack of analysis of architectural factors**: The paper does not analyze how specific gate types (e.g., single-qubit rotations, CNOTs, parameterized gates), gate arrangement, connectivity patterns, or ansatz depth affect expressivity and trainability. The impact of parameter initialization strategies is also unexplored. Reviewer 1 notes that this limits actionable insights for QML practitioners (Reviewer 1).
- **Sections 3 and 4 are too condensed**: The main technical contributions are squeezed into about one page, with missing precise definitions (e.g., the quantum states in Eq. (4) are undefined in the main text) (Reviewer 2).
- **The claim that “each ansatz is hardly distinguishable from itself” is not clearly supported**: Figure 3 includes the loose upper bounds, making it impossible to see the channel sensitivity alone. The paper should provide a separate plot without the bounds to support this claim (Reviewer 3).
- **Terminology confusion**: The paper uses “expressivity” whereas prior work (Holmes et al, 2021) uses “expressibility”. The relationship between QNN expressivity and classical function approximation is not discussed (Reviewer 3).
- **Non-standard amplitude encoding**: The numerical experiments use amplitude encoding of PCA components, which is not near-term friendly and leads to only pairwise feature interactions (no zeroth, first, or higher-order terms). Encoding PCA components into single-qubit rotations would be more standard (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing analysis of non-entangled circuits**: Reviewer 3 requests a baseline comparison of channel sensitivity for a circuit with single-qubit rotations only (e.g., R_X per qubit) to understand the effect of entanglement.
- **Minor issues**: Figures should be in vector/lossless format; some questions about mathematical details (e.g., factor 1/2 in Eq. (30); use of mean-squared loss for a classification problem; diamond norm scaling with qubits) need clarification (Reviewers 2, 3).
