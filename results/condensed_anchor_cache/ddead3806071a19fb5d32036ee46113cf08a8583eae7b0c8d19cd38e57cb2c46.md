## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents KAR-RAG, a novel RAG framework that improves retrieval and reasoning via knowledge-aware task decomposition. The method uses four components: a knowledge atomizer to extract atomic questions from raw data, a query proposer to generate follow-up questions, an atomic retriever that locates knowledge based on atomic question alignments, and an atomic selector to decide which follow-ups to pose. The pipeline decomposes complex queries, retrieves atomic contexts, and iteratively constructs a rationale. Experiments on multihop reasoning benchmarks show up to 12.6% improvement over the second-best method. All reviewers note the method is clearly presented and achieves competitive results, but raise concerns about cost, motivation clarity, missing baselines, and insufficient ablation analysis.

### Strengths
- **Novel and realistic approach**: Supplementing semantic retrieval with fine-grained, atomic knowledge via a rewrite-and-index mechanism is a novel practice (R2, R4). The knowledge augmentation is achieved through query decomposition and context gathering, avoiding more error-prone KG generation (R1).
- **Clear organization and presentation**: The paper is well-structured, easy to follow, and explains the four components smoothly with good examples (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Consistent and competitive experimental results**: The method shows significant improvements across open-domain and domain-specific benchmarks, especially for multihop reasoning tasks (up to 12.6% over the second-best) (R1, R2, R3, R4). All four reviewers acknowledge the strong results.
- **Practical design choice**: The approach avoids full KG construction, which reduces one source of error injection (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Cost and efficiency not addressed**: The paper provides no analysis of response time, API cost, or overall overhead. Generating atomic questions for each chunk and repeatedly calling GPT-4 throughout the pipeline is highly cost-intensive (R1, R2, R4). One reviewer explicitly asks for cost analysis comparing to baselines (R4). Another questions whether cheaper LLMs (e.g., less expensive models) could be used, and if performance would degrade (R4).
- **Motivation and focus are ambiguous**: The paper claims to address “domain-specific knowledge mining” and “complex logical reasoning,” but does not clearly specify the challenges that make current RAG methods fall short in these settings. Reviewers find the explanation poor, and the method appears to enhance general retrieval rather than targeting domain-specific or logical reasoning issues specifically (R2, R4). One reviewer suggests reframing the motivation around accurate retrieval in general to avoid misunderstanding (R2).
- **Missing comparisons with strong recent baselines**: The work lacks comparisons with more recent RAG baselines that also use query decomposition or iterative retrieval, such as SearChain, ProbTree (R2), and the methods described in arxiv:2404.14464 and arxiv:2406.19215 (R3). Including these would strengthen the evaluation.
- **Insufficient ablation and component analysis**: The effectiveness of each individual component (atomizer, proposer, retriever, selector) is not explored. For example, what happens if knowledge is mingled together rather than presented as atomic simple questions? (R3). The pipeline nature and heavy reliance on GPT-4 make it unclear what the core technical challenges and contributions are (R4).
- **Heavy reliance on a single strong LLM (GPT-4)**: The entire pipeline depends on GPT-4 for atomization, decomposition, selection, and termination. This makes the strategy both expensive and tied to GPT-4’s capabilities. No experiments with cheaper or weaker models are provided, leaving the generalizability in question (R4).
- **Technical contribution unclear**: The approach is presented as a pipeline; the main technical challenges and how they are solved are not explicitly stated (R4).

*Note on reviewer disagreement*: One reviewer (R4, score 3) is notably more critical, emphasizing the poor motivation explanation, the cost issue, and the unclear technical contribution, while the other three (scores 6,6,5) are more positive about the novelty and results but share concerns about missing baselines and cost.
