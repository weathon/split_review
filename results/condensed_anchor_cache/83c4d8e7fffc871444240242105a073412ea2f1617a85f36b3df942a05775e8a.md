## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Dual-Forecaster, a multimodal time series forecasting model that integrates both historical (descriptive) and future (predictive) textual data with numerical time series to improve forecasting accuracy. The model employs three cross-modality alignment techniques: historical text-time series contrastive loss, a history-oriented modality interaction module, and a future-oriented modality interaction module. Evaluations on synthetic and captioned-public datasets (ETTm1, ETTm2) suggest performance improvements. Reviewers generally find the approach novel and the architecture well-explained, but raise significant concerns about information leakage from future text, limited real-world validity, missing baselines, and insufficient robustness testing. One reviewer (score 3) is notably more critical than the others (scores 5,5,5) regarding the task definition, information leakage, and the captioning process.

### Strengths

- Well-organized, clearly written, and easy to follow; figures (architecture, case studies) aid understanding.
- Model architecture is clean, simple, and well-explained; cross-modality alignment techniques (contrastive loss, history-oriented and future-oriented interaction modules) are sound and well-integrated.
- Ablation study provides in-depth insight, especially the alignment heatmap between time series patterns and textual descriptions.
- Experimental results on synthetic data are strong, and ablation studies on the ETTm2 dataset show positive impact of each proposed component.
- The paper correctly identifies the information insufficiency problem in single-modal time series forecasting.
- Novel contribution: integrating both historical and future textual data, which extends existing multimodal time series models.

### Weaknesses

- **Information leakage from future/predictive text.** The core concern is that future textual captions are generated directly from the future time series (ground truth), leading to potential information leakage and unfair comparisons with baseline models. This discrepancy between training/testing and real-world applicability severely limits the model’s practical utility. One reviewer (score 3) states this may cause overestimation of performance. Another notes that the TGTSF paper (Xu et al., 2024) advises using only external text sources to avoid directly using future time series information. The specific question of how future-oriented predictive text is generated—using the forecasting horizon for captioning—is considered a source of severe leakage.
- **Captioning process of public datasets is problematic.** Directly asking GPT to describe numerical time series is unreliable due to GPT’s limited numerical processing ability and tendency for hallucination. The textual descriptions contain numerical values; it is unclear whether this information remains after text embedding (RoBERTa). The captions are derived from the time series itself, risking additional leakage.
- **Missing baseline comparison.** The paper references Xu et al. (2024) but does not compare against it experimentally. Given the conceptual similarity, this is a significant gap in evaluating the model’s novelty and performance.
- **Limited real-world validation.** Evaluations rely heavily on synthetic data with artificially crafted textual descriptions; public datasets (ETTm1, ETTm2) are limited in variety and complexity. Missing evaluations on more challenging real-world datasets such as exchange rates or stock indices, which would better test the model under noisy and volatile conditions. The task definition’s practicality (availability of caption data in real applications) is insufficiently justified.
- **No robustness testing against noisy or misleading textual data.** The model’s performance is not evaluated when text inputs are unreliable, contradictory, or irrelevant—a critical oversight for high-stakes domains like finance. One reviewer asks directly how the model handles wrong information in texts.
- **Insufficient justification for dual textual modality.** The paper does not ablate using only one type of text (historical or future) versus both. The provided ablation only uses the ETTm2 dataset and does not generalize to other datasets. A reviewer suggests combining both texts into a single input to reduce complexity, and asks for evidence that separate modules significantly enhance results.
- **Missing error bounds / standard deviations.** Results are reported without variability measures across multiple runs, making it difficult to assess consistency and robustness.
- **Weak comparison with other multimodal approaches.** The comparison with methods like TIME-LLM is considered weak.
- **Limited scope of tasks.** The model is only evaluated on forecasting; its potential for other multimodal time series tasks (imputation, anomaly detection) is not explored. One reviewer (score 3) suggests the model might be better suited for classification or anomaly detection due to the information leakage issue.
- **Figure readability issues.** Figure 2 (example) is too simple; Figures 3 and 5 are hard to read.
- **Other reviewer questions folded as criticisms:** Why textual data is the most effective supplementary modality? Why use contrastive loss? More ablation including the pre-trained model is needed.
