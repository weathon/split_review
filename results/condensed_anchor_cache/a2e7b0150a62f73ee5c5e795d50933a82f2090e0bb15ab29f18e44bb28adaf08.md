## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper provides the first theoretical analysis of generalization bounds for Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks (KANs). It considers two settings: activation functions represented as linear combinations of basis functions, and activation functions lying in a low-rank Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (specifically induced by the Matern kernel). Generalization gaps and excess risk bounds are derived using covering number arguments, with results for both bounded and unbounded loss functions. Empirical experiments on simulated and real data are conducted to relate network complexity to excess risk.

### Strengths

- **Originality and significance**: The paper is the first to derive generalization bounds for KANs. Given the recent attention on KANs, this theoretical contribution is novel and timely. (Reviewers 1,2,4)

- **Solid theoretical results**: The analysis is rigorous, deriving generalization bounds under clear assumptions (Lipschitzness, boundedness) and showing how network parameters (Lipschitz constants, ℓ₁ norms of coefficient matrices, ranks) affect generalization. (Reviewers 1,5)

- **Coverage of bounded and unbounded loss functions**: The paper provides bounds for both cases, which is helpful for understanding complexity’s impact across different regression-type losses. (Reviewer 3)

- **Presentation and clarity**: The paper is well-organized and well-written overall. (Reviewers 1,4)

### Weaknesses

- **Comparison with MLPs missing**: The paper does not theoretically or empirically compare the derived generalization bounds to those of traditional MLPs (e.g., ReLU networks). It remains unclear whether KANs offer any statistical advantage. (Reviewers 1,2,5)
  - Reviewer 2 specifically asks: *“Based on the obtained theory, is it possible to identify a problem setting where KANs outperform MLPs?”*
  - Reviewer 5 asks: *“Is there a clear advantage on the generalization behavior by theoretical analysis in the paper?”*

- **Limited technical novelty**: The covering number analysis follows existing techniques for fully-connected neural networks (Bartlett et al., 2017), with Proposition 1 similar to Lemma A.7 and Proposition 2 similar to Lemma 3.2 in that work. The paper does not clearly articulate the specific technical challenge introduced by the KAN structure. (Reviewer 5)
  - Reviewer 5 requests: *“It would be nice if the authors included a detailed discussion on the technical challenge in the covering number estimates of the KANs.”*

- **Assumptions may limit practical relevance**: The analysis relies on many strong assumptions (Lipschitzness, boundedness of input, function space, loss). Whether these hold in practice is not sufficiently clarified. Traditional complexity measures like covering numbers are often insufficient to explain generalization in deep networks, and the derived bounds may be vacuous. (Reviewer 3)
  - Reviewer 3 asks: *“Could the authors provide some empirical results on the comparison between the bounds and the actual generalization error? This would help convince me that these bounds are indeed non-vacuous in practice.”*

- **Issues with unbounded loss bounds**: The high-probability guarantee is poor: Theorem 3 has a linear dependence on \(1/\eta\) (instead of logarithmic) and a rate of \(n^{(1-s)/(2s)}\) (e.g., \(n^{-1/4}\) for \(s=2\)), which is much worse than the \(n^{-1/2}\) rate for bounded losses. (Reviewer 5)
  - Question: *“Is it possible to give high-probability generalization bounds for unbounded loss functions?”*

- **Empirical evaluation is insufficient and poorly connected to theory**:
  - The purpose of experiments (Section 3) is unclear: Are they validating Theorem 1? If so, it is not stated whether the KAN activations are linear combinations of basis functions, nor which bases are used. (Reviewer 1)
  - Experimental details are missing: normalization of the complexity measure, motivation for synthetic target functions, network loss values (not just excess loss), and appendix with full setup are not provided. (Reviewer 4)
  - There is no empirical check of bound tightness (e.g., comparing theoretical bounds to observed excess risk). (Reviewers 3,4)
  - For CIFAR-10 and MNIST, features from AlexNet are used instead of raw inputs; the impact of this choice is not discussed. (Reviewer 4)
  - Figure legends are barely readable and appendices are never referenced in the main text. (Reviewer 4)

- **Restriction to Matern kernel (low-rank RKHS)**: Section 2.3 only considers RKHS induced by the Matern kernel, not a general low-rank RKHS. Given its equivalence to a Sobolev space, the section might be better titled “Activation functions on Sobolev space,” but the fundamental limitation of not treating a broader class remains. (Reviewer 1)

- **Boundedness assumption on data degrades bounds**: Assumption 1 assumes uniform boundedness of the dataset; typically this bound scales with \(\sqrt{n}\), which could worsen the derived bounds. (Reviewer 2)

- **Convergence rates insufficiently explained**: The role of the parameter \(s\) in convergence rates (Corollaries 1 and 2) is not clearly explained. Direct comparison with MLP rates is missing. (Reviewer 1)

- **Lack of intuitive discussion**: The third claimed contribution (“how performance is affected by network structure and complexity”) is never discussed in the paper. Technical results lack intuitive interpretation. (Reviewer 4)
  - Reviewer 4 asks: *“Can the authors provide an intuitive explanation for the role played by the value of φ evaluated at 0 (and the constant C)?”*

- **Undefined symbol**: \(\mathcal{F}\) in Proposition 2 is not defined. (Reviewer 5)

- **Minorities/contrasts**: Reviewer 1 is notably more positive (score 8) and finds the results very solid, while Reviewer 4 (score 5) is more critical on experimental quality and bound tightness, and Reviewer 5 (score 6) is more critical on technical novelty. The weaknesses listed above reflect the consensus but preserve these minority emphases.
