## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper addresses the problem that a standard actor-network often fails to maximize the critic (action-value function) due to challenging non-convex, high-dimensional, and non-stationary landscapes. The authors propose a novel reformulation using a sequence of sub-actors (each with its own critic) that progressively prune/restrict the action space, with the final action selected by the primary critic. Experiments are conducted on both continuous and large discrete action space tasks. The problem is interesting and the algorithmic idea is novel, but the paper suffers from insufficient theoretical support, missing comparisons to ensemble baselines, unclear technical justifications for architectural choices (DeepSet, FiLM), experimental weaknesses (outdated baselines, few seeds, questionable statistical significance, computational cost not addressed), and presentation clarity issues that some reviewers found problematic.

### Strengths

- **Novel idea and problem framing.** The problem of the actor failing to maximize the critic is well-motivated and worth studying. The successive actor-critic reformulation for “pruning” actions with low Q-values is interesting and (to the best of the reviewers’ knowledge) novel. [Reviewers 1,2,3,4]

- **Reasonably clear presentation (disagreement).** Some reviewers found the paper well written and well structured (Reviewers 1,4). Others found the writing confusing and lacking clarity (Reviewers 2,3). The core algorithmic idea is conveyed, but important details are missing.

- **Promising empirical results.** The new algorithm achieves better overall performance on multiple benchmark problems, including large discrete action spaces and more discontinuous variants of continuous tasks. The experimental setup appears rigorous to one reviewer. [Reviewers 3,4]

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of theoretical support for core claims.** There is no theoretical proof or formal analysis that the proposed method indeed finds actions with higher action value, and the connection between maximizing the critic and improved sample efficiency is not justified. The validity of the policy gradient formula (e.g., what gradient is being computed and why it maximizes expected return) is questioned. Convergence analysis is missing; the non‑stationarity introduced by actors depending on previous actors raises stability concerns. The potential downside of successively restricting the action space based on the critic (bias, degraded stability, restricted exploration) is not examined theoretically. [Reviewers 1,3,4]

- **Missing comparisons to ensemble methods and relevant baselines.** Several highly relevant prior works on combining value‑based optimization with cross‑entropy, evolutionary search, and ensemble methods are not cited or compared. Specifically: (i) Q‑learning for continuous actions with cross-entropy guided policies; (ii) Greedy Actor‑Critic; (iii) CEM‑RL; (iv) model reference adaptive search; (v) Wire fitting algorithm for high‑dimensional continuous actions. The paper falls partly into the domain of ensemble actor‑critic methods, yet no ensemble‑based baselines are included. Without these comparisons, it is unclear whether the improvement comes from better maximization of the critic or simply from ensemble exploration. [Reviewers 1,2,3]

- **Insufficient explanation of key architectural components (DeepSet, FiLM).** The motivations and rationales for using DeepSet (why it is suitable for summarizing past actions) and FiLM (how it conditions the policy) are not clearly explained. No analysis of design alternatives (e.g., LSTM, Transformer for action summarization, other conditioning methods) is provided. The paper lacks a description of potential advantages and limitations of these choices. [Reviewers 2,3,4]

- **Experimental weaknesses and fairness concerns.**  
  – Baselines are outdated; no state‑of‑the‑art ensemble methods are included.  
  – Only 3 random seeds are used in some experiments (e.g., Figure 4), raising concerns about statistical significance.  
  – Hyperparameter sensitivity is not reported; specific hyperparameters, tuning ranges, and selection criteria are not given.  
  – The modification of the experimental setup may be unfair, as other baselines may not have been designed for the specific problem.  
  – The method is only tested on TD3; its applicability to other actor‑critic algorithms (e.g., SAC) is not demonstrated, despite the authors’ claim of generality.  
  – Appendix experiments show only Easy environments; Hard environments are not included.  
  – Computation cost (updating K times per step) is not analyzed; practical utility is weakened.  
  [Reviewers 1,2,3,4]

- **Potential overestimation and exploration issues.** The paper does not address whether the successive critic maximization leads to action‑value overestimation, which could hurt learning. Furthermore, it is not explained why successively restricting the action space improves exploration rather than restricting it; the distinction between benefit from better maximization vs. benefit from exploration is not resolved. [Reviewers 1,2,3]

- **Clarity and presentation issues (mixed opinions).** Writing is considered confusing by some reviewers, especially in Introduction and Related Work. Unclear statements include: “navigate the action‑value landscape more proficiently”, “distribute the optimization load over to the critic”, “slower than the original inefficiencies of the actor”, and “more tractable” (the latter is not explained from real data). The structure is hard to follow, and large space is devoted to the experimental environment. The English presentation needs improvement. Algorithm details are ambiguous: in Algorithm 1, the meaning of “state s” in lines 8 and 10, the “{” symbol in line 17, and the difference between notations $(s_t|A)$ and $(s_t,A)$ are unclear. Figure 7 lacks a legend. Code abbreviation (FLAIR) differs from paper (SAVO). [Reviewers 2,3,4]

- **Missing technical details and analysis.** The paper lacks an analysis of the impact of the number of successive actors K, or an investigation of alternative architectures. The convergence properties of the multi‑actor update are not discussed. [Reviewers 1,2]
