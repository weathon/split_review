## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes PolyGCL, a graph contrastive learning framework that uses learnable spectral polynomial filters to generate low-pass and high-pass views, aiming to improve performance on heterophilic graphs. All reviewers find the idea novel and interesting. Three reviewers (scores 8,8,8) judge the theoretical analyses and experimental results as strong and the contribution significant. The minority reviewer (score 5) considers the contribution fair, citing insufficient comparison with recent works, limited intuition behind the spectral design, and weak theoretical justification (Theorem 1 restricted to regular graphs). All reviewers note the need for better positioning within the literature, additional ablation studies, and clarification of design choices and optimization details.

### Strengths

- **Novel idea**: Introducing spectral polynomial filters into graph contrastive learning is novel and interesting (all reviewers). Combining low-pass and high-pass views to handle heterophily is well-motivated.
- **Theoretical foundations**: The paper provides multiple theorems, propositions, and corollaries (Reviewers 3,4). Reviewer 3 praises robust theoretical formulations; Reviewer 4 calls them decent.
- **Experimental evaluation**: Extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets with diverse baselines show effectiveness on homophilic and heterophilic graphs (Reviewers 2,3,4). Results are considered strong.
- **Significance**: Addresses a pressing need in graph representation learning, especially for heterophilic graphs (Reviewer 3). Complexity analysis suggests scalability (Reviewer 3).
- **Well-written**: Reviewer 2 notes the paper is well-written; other reviewers also generally positive about presentation.

### Weaknesses

1. **Missing related work and comparisons**: The paper does not adequately position itself within the literature of graph contrastive learning with spectral filters. Missing references include:
   - Liu et al. “Revisiting Graph Contrastive Learning from the Perspective of Graph Spectrum” (NeurIPS 2022), which explores high-frequency information in GCL.
   - Xie et al. “Contrastive Graph Clustering with Adaptive Filter” (ESWA 2023), proposing adaptive filters.
   - Zhang et al. “Localized Contrastive Learning on Graphs” (arXiv 2022), which provides results on heterophilic graphs.
   - Wang et al. “Can Single-Pass Contrastive Learning Work for Both Homophilic and Heterophilic Graph?” (arXiv 2022).
   - HaoChen et al. “Provable Guarantees for Self-Supervised Deep Learning with Spectral Contrastive Loss” (NeurIPS 2021), relevant to theoretical foundations.
   - FAGCN (Bo et al., AAAI 2021), Ghosh et al. (AISTATS 2023), Yang & Mirzasoleiman (arXiv 2023) (Reviewer 1).
   Clear comparison with these spectral-based GCL methods is lacking (Reviewers 1,2). How does PolyGCL differ from the adaptive filter in Xie et al. and the localized method in Zhang et al.?

2. **Insufficient intuition and explanation of spectral design choices**:
   - The choice of Chebyshev polynomials lacks intuitive justification. Why were they chosen over other polynomial bases? (Reviewer 3)
   - The decoupling of low-pass and high-pass filter functions is not thoroughly justified. Potential pitfalls or scenarios where decoupling is not advantageous are not discussed (Reviewer 3).
   - The reparameterization techniques (prefix sum, prefix difference) to enforce non-negativity and decremental filter values lack clarity on why these specific forms were chosen and their impact on robustness and flexibility (Reviewer 3).
   - Reviewer 1 notes that the paper lacks explanation of the intuition behind low-pass and high-pass information in the learning process, and asks for intuition about low and high frequency in the context of the global embedding \( g \). After response, Reviewer 1 still found the comparison against mean representation naive and wanted frequency domain examples in simple settings, not just accuracy numbers after a classification head.

3. **Theoretical limitations and insufficient justification**:
   - Theorem 1 is restricted to regular graphs and binary classification. Reviewer 1 questions how such a limited case can support the stronger claim that high-pass information helps on heterophilic datasets. The authors’ response that “k-regular graph” restricts eigenvalues to [0,2] was noted to be true for all graphs, indicating a loose statement (Reviewer 1 after-response).
   - Section 4.4’s error bound analysis does not explain PolyGCL’s advantage over directly applying GCL on a standard spectral GNN like ChebNet (Reviewer 4). The theoretical contribution does not convincingly separate from prior spectral GCL approaches.

4. **Missing ablation studies and insufficient evaluation of design components**:
   - The effect of using only one filter (only low-pass or only high-pass) is not studied. What is the performance when \(\alpha = 0\) or \(\beta = 0\)? What about fixing \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\)? The paper lacks ablation on the two separate filters (Reviewers 1,4).
   - The choice of random shuffling as perturbation for the contrastive view is not explained. Why use this instead of common perturbations like edge-dropping or subgraph-sampling? (Reviewer 4) Reviewer 1 also asks about the exact GraphCL method used (node/edge-drop or combination) when reporting GraphCL results.
   - No comparison with NT-Xent loss (used in GraphCL) is provided; it is unclear how the proposed linear combination loss compares (Reviewer 1).

5. **Clarity and presentation issues**:
   - The optimization target in Equation (4) does not involve \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) at all, raising questions about how these parameters are optimized. Are they learned with supervision? If so, it seems equivalent to directly optimizing a polynomial filter (Reviewer 4).
   - Several spelling and grammatical errors: “in an self-supervised manner”, “with an self-supervised one”, “Trainging Algorithm”, “We denote Y … be the”, “further justification are discussed”, “claims that maximize”, “optimising … also maximize”, “proves that maximize”, “Shannon diverge” (Reviewer 2).
   - Evaluation lacks comparison with recent works along similar directions (Reviewer 1).

6. **Minority reviewer stronger criticism**: Reviewer 1 (score 5) considers the contribution fair and was not fully satisfied with the response. This reviewer found the theoretical claim weak, the evaluation lacking key baselines, and the intuitions insufficient. After response, Reviewer 1 still found the authors’ justification of the eigenvalue range unsatisfactory and the intuition for low/high-pass naive. The other reviewers (8,8,8) were overall positive but still noted the missing comparisons and need for more explanation.
