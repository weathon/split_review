## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces MARCEL, the first benchmark for molecular conformer ensemble learning, comprising four datasets (Drugs-75K, Kraken, EE, BDE) that cover diverse molecule- and reaction-level properties beyond typical drug-like molecules. The benchmark evaluates the prediction of Boltzmann averages over conformer ensembles. Extensive baselines include 1D, 2D, and 3D graph neural networks, plus two strategies that explicitly incorporate conformer ensembles into 3D models. The paper is well-written and provides thorough comparisons to state-of-the-art methods, demonstrating that learning from accessible conformer spaces can improve performance across tasks and models.

### Strengths
- **Originality and significance:** The benchmark addresses an under-explored problem (molecular dynamic interconversion) and will be highly useful for the community to develop and compare methods for conformer ensemble learning. (R2, R3)
- **Comprehensive baseline evaluations:** Extensive experiments using popular 1D, 2D, and 3D GNN models offer insight into how different representation levels are affected by conformation changes, providing valuable knowledge for future research. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Detailed curation and presentation:** Dataset preparation, experimental settings, and results are clearly elaborated; the writing is excellent and well-organized. (R1, R3)
- **Meticulous comparison to state-of-the-art:** The paper thoroughly benchmarks existing methods and highlights performance gains from conformer ensembles. (R1)
- **Shared resource:** Establishing this benchmark lowers the barrier for researchers to study multiple conformers, which previously required specialized knowledge. (R2)
- **Disagreement note:** One reviewer (R1) sees no weaknesses, while two reviewers (R2, R3) identify specific concerns below.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited practical evidence:** The tasks predict computed Boltzmann averages over conformers, not direct experimental measurements. Thus, the utility of considering multiple conformers for real-world predictions (e.g., experimental properties) is not proven; the benchmark may feel artificial. (R2)
- **Questionable generalizability of ensemble strategies:** The two conformer ensemble learning strategies are tailored to predicting Boltzmann-averaged values, but evidence that they are generally useful for predicting molecular properties is lacking. (R2, Q3)
- **Data originality concerns:** All four datasets are curated from prior primary sources (GEOM-Drugs, Kraken, EE, BDE). It is unclear whether any original information (e.g., new calculations) was generated or only simple curation/redundancy removal was performed. (R2, Q1)
- **Inconsistent conformer generation methods:** Different datasets use different conformer generation tools (Auto3D for Drugs-75K, Q2MM for EE, Open Babel with DFT for Kraken/BDE). The paper does not discuss whether this inconsistency is problematic for fair benchmarking across tasks. (R2, Q2)
- **Missing 3D graph transformer baseline:** At least one modern 3D transformer (e.g., Equiformer) should be included as a baseline. (R3)
- **Missing discussion/baseline of ConfDSS:** The paper omits reference to ConfDSS, an existing conformer ensemble learning module that could be applied or compared. (R3)
- **Lack of clarification on input conformers for Table 2:** It is unclear which specific conformers are used as inputs for the 3D GNN models in Table 2. (R3, question)
- **Ambiguity on BDE task:** Is the BDE property also a Boltzmann-averaged value? The paper does not clearly state this. (R2, Q4)
