## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies the topological expressivity of ReLU neural networks in a binary classification setting, using Betti numbers as a measure. It proves lower and upper bounds showing that the sum of Betti numbers grows polynomially with width (for fixed depth) and exponentially with depth (for fixed width), implying that deep networks are exponentially more powerful than shallow ones in topological simplification. Reviewers disagreed on the significance and practical relevance: one reviewer (rating 8) considered the characterization elegant and well-motivated; another (rating 6, low confidence) found it well-written but lacking experiments; a third (rating 3, high confidence) questioned the practical meaning, novelty, and rigor of the upper bound.

### Strengths
- The research topic is active and timely.
- The paper is well-written with fine language; no evident mathematical errors.
- The theoretical question is well-motivated, addressing empirically observed topological simplification.
- The paper develops an elegant characterization of expressivity via Betti numbers, showing an interesting dependence on depth and width.
- Potential connections to dynamical systems and chaotic behavior (e.g., Li‑Yorke chaos, topological entropy) are mentioned, opening future directions.
- The paper is situated within the framework of using topology to approach neural network expressivity and presents interesting mathematical results.

### Weaknesses
- **Practical relevance**: Real‑world data typically lies near low‑dimensional surfaces, so there may be no relation between Betti numbers of the decision‑boundary sublevel set and those of the data distribution; the zeroth Betti number could be 1 while data Betti numbers are arbitrary large. The paper does not provide real‑world examples or show how the bounds are useful in practice.
- **Meaning of “lower bound”**: The lower bound is achieved only by a specific set of contrived weights; there exist weights (e.g., constant function) with zero Betti numbers, making the notion of “lower bound” ambiguous. It is unclear whether the maximum is over architectures only or also over datasets \(X\).
- **Reachability of weights**: It is not clear whether the constructed weights can be found by gradient‑based optimization or any practical training algorithm (multiple reviewers ask this).
- **Novelty and relation to prior work**: Similar depth‑width tradeoffs were already known via linear‑region counts (Telgarsky, Montúfar et al.) and via dynamical‑system approaches (e.g., [1]–[3] using Sharkovsky’s theorem, topological entropy). The paper’s principal zeroth‑Betti‑number result is an extension of Bianchini and Scarselli (2014) to ReLU activations, which does not constitute a significant conceptual advance.
- **Upper bound proof is insufficient**: Only about half a page is devoted to the upper bound; the sketch provided lacks essential details and is not obvious from standard topological arguments.
- **Clarity of the exponential gap**: It is unclear what is being compared (number of parameters, layers, or another measure) to establish the exponential gap between deep and shallow networks.
- **Difficulty of computing Betti numbers** undermines the practical implications of the theoretical bounds.
- **Missing computational experiments**: The paper would benefit from simple synthetic experiments (e.g., datasets from Naitzat et al. 2020) to estimate the depth/width required to achieve high classification accuracy.
- **Notation and presentation issues**:  
  – “\(\beta_0(F) \in \Omega(M^d \cdot n_L)\)” is unclear; is it meant to be \(\beta_0(F) = \Omega(M^d \cdot n_L)\)?  
  – Grammar error: “ares” → “are” in Section 1.2.1.  
  – Inconsistent use of \(M\): \(M_a,M_b\) are topological spaces, but elsewhere \(M\) is an integer (Section 2, Lemma 4 etc.).  
  – Figure 1 appears problematic: a graph consisting of two points is used to represent a function that folds the interval – some lines may be missing.
- **Missing comparisons/connections**:  
  – Does the construction relate to periodic points/topological entropy in dynamical systems, as in [1]–[3]?  
  – For 1‑dimensional input, do the results recover the exact separation proved by Telgarsky?  
  – The paper should spell out precisely what set the “lower bound” is taken over and whether it depends on the dataset.
- **Manuscript length**: At ~30 pages, the paper may be better suited for a journal rather than a conference.
