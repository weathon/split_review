## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a training-free method, "1Prompt1Story", for consistent text-to-image generation in storytelling. It concatenates an identity prompt and all frame prompts into a single input to the T2I model, exploiting an observed “context consistency” of language models. Two techniques refine generation: Singular-Value Reweighting (SVR, with SVR+ and SVR-) to enhance the current frame prompt and suppress others, and Identity-Preserving Cross-Attention (IPCA) that zeros out key/value projections of frame prompts to enforce identity from the identity prompt and EOT tokens. Experiments compare against training-free and training-required baselines, using quantitative metrics and a user study. The method outperforms existing training-free approaches in identity preservation and text alignment, and also supports ControlNet.

### Strengths
- The work is well written and clearly structured; figures and results are nicely presented. (R2, R3, R4, R5)
- The main observation of inherent context consistency in language models for identity preservation is novel and insightful, potentially having broader implications. (R4, R5)
- The proposed method is training-free, simple, and increases inference-time compute only moderately. It can be easily adapted to different T2I architectures and sampling strategies. (R1, R3, R4)
- The core ideas of Naive Prompt Reweighting (NPR) and SVR (enhancing the target frame and suppressing others) are well analyzed and form a solid method. (R2)
- The ablation study confirms the effectiveness of both SVR and IPCA. (R1)
- Quantitative analysis of context consistency through t-SNE visualizations and text-prompt distance metrics is sound and convincing. (R5)
- The evaluation uses a comprehensive set of metrics and compares against multiple training-required and training-free baselines, including a user study and ControlNet integration. (R3, R5)
- The visual quality of generated outputs is good. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Token length limitation:** The CLIP text encoder (e.g., 77 tokens for SDXL) severely constrains the number of frame prompts that can be concatenated. This limits applicability for longer stories, while other training-free methods do not have this restriction. The paper does not explicitly address or propose solutions to this limit. (R1, R3, R5)
- **Small-scale evaluation:** The benchmark contains only 200 examples, and the user study only 20 participants, which may limit generalizability and robustness. Bootstrapping or additional benchmarks are needed. (R1, R5)
- **Multi-identity generation not shown:** The paper does not report results or improvements for scenarios with multiple distinct identities (e.g., a man and a dog), which is a common requirement in recent works like StoryDiffusion. (R2)
- **Handling of EOT tokens unclear:** The description of how EOT tokens are first expressed and then suppressed (lines 290-322) is confusing. It is unclear why the [EOT] token belongs to both the expressed and suppressed sets, and whether this cancels out. (R1, R5)
- **IPCA mechanism lacks clarity and justification:** 
  - The module zeros out features of all frame prompts (P_i) rather than removing them entirely. The rationale is not explained, and it may affect attention scores due to normalization. (R4)
  - It is unclear how P0 and EOT features alone can enhance identity consistency, especially since their embeddings likely differ across frames due to asymmetric SVR. (R4)
  - The authors claim memory efficiency but do not provide a memory complexity comparison with existing methods. (R4)
- **Applicability questions arising from specific figures and analysis:**
  - In Figure 8 (right), the proposed method shows little visible impact on identity preservation. (R1)
  - In Figure 2, it is not explained why the text embedding for P1 differs between multi-prompt and single-prompt settings (given CLIP causal attention). (R1)
  - The shaded regions in Figure 2 are not defined or explained. (R1)
  - In Figure 3, the NPR result may not be incorrect because background is unspecified in several frame prompts. (R1)
  - The t-SNE visualization in Section 3.1.1 appears to use only a single set of prompts; more details and generality are needed. (R3)
- **Potential effects on visual quality not quantified:** It is unclear whether the proposed method degrades image realism compared to baselines. (R3)
- **Novelty concerns:** The ideas of reweighting (used previously in video generation) and identity-preserving attention (e.g., in Consistory on image modalities) are adapted from prior art rather than fundamentally new. (R2)
- **Suggested improvements for prompt alignment metric:** Current metric may not be optimal; newer metrics like DSG or VQAScore are recommended. (R1)
- **Order sensitivity not explored:** Changing the order of frame prompts (e.g., always placing the target frame last) may affect results. (R1)
- **Question of alternative design:** Could consistency be improved by applying SVR directly to the concatenation of P0 and EOT embeddings instead of using IPCA? (R3)
