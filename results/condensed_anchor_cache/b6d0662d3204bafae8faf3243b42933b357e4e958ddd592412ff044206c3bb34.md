## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies a single-layer linear self-attention (LSA) transformer in an in-context weight prediction task for linear regression. Unlike standard ICL (predicting label \(y = x_q^\top w^*\)), the model is trained to directly predict the task feature \(w^*\) with loss \(\ell = \| \text{LSA}(X,y)_{[:,-1]} - w^* \|^2\). The authors introduce Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting by appending intermediate GD steps \(w_0, w_1, \dots, w_k\) to the input. They prove that without CoT a one-layer linear transformer can only implement a single step of GD and fails to recover the ground-truth weight, while with CoT it learns multi-step GD autoregressively and achieves near-exact recovery. Convergence results are given via gradient flow analysis, and the model is shown to generalize out-of-distribution. Empirical evidence demonstrates performance improvements from CoT. All reviewers agree the paper provides valuable theoretical analysis on CoT, though there are significant concerns about assumptions, practical feasibility, and comparison to related work. One reviewer (score 6) is notably more critical about data generation and fixed hyperparameters, while the other two (scores 8) focus on restrictive scope and lack of novelty relative to looped transformers.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized and provides a solid theoretical analysis of training dynamics in a clearly defined setting.
- It introduces CoT prompting to enhance the expressivity of single-layer linear attention models for ICL, offering a fresh analytical framework beyond the typical computational complexity perspective on CoT.
- The theoretical results (convergence via gradient flow, OOD generalization) are new in the context of CoT, which lacks formal analysis. The paper includes sufficient experiments to validate the theory.
- The approach is innovative and provides valuable insights for future research directions, addressing a gap in understanding CoT mechanisms.

### Weaknesses
- **Training data generation and feasibility (R1 strongly):** The training setup assumes ground-truth GD steps \(w_0, w_1, \dots\) are available. This requires knowing the gradient of the data model and computing \(M\) gradients over an \(n \times d\) dataset per prompt (each with a different \(w^*\)), raising concerns about computational cost and the practical plausibility of generating such data. In standard ICL only input-label pairs are needed.
- **Fixed learning rate \(\eta\):** The loss and model performance are highly dependent on \(\eta\), which is fixed in the analysis. Optimal loss is achieved only at \(\eta = \frac{n}{n+d+1}\). In practice, \(\eta\) is often learned; the paper does not address how a fixed \(\eta\) limits generalization across different tasks or datasets where the optimal rate may vary. This ambiguity affects the definition of global minimization.
- **Dependence on \(n\) and \(k\) relative to \(d\) (R1, R2):** It is unclear how varying \(n\) and \(k\) affect convergence and evaluation loss. The assumption \(n \le d \log^5 d\) appears contrived and driven by proof convenience rather than intuitive justification (e.g., a quadratic number of samples to control evaluation error). The necessity of this bound for specific results (e.g., Corollary 3.1) is not clarified. A more detailed analysis of how these parameters interact would improve practical understanding.
- **Insufficient comparison to multi-layer / looped transformers (R1, R3):** The CoT + one-layer approach is closely related to ICL + multi-layer methods, especially looped transformers where a fixed single-layer transformer is applied \(k\) times (e.g., Ding et al. 2023, also Ahn et al. 2023, Gatmiry et al. 2024). The paper does not adequately discuss this connection or justify why the proposed CoT framework is necessary. Similar results might be achievable with looped transformers, and a detailed comparison would clarify the novelty and relative advantages.
- **Conflation of CoT objective with CoT data presence (R2):** The distinction between training a transformer on a CoT objective vs. having CoT data in a next-token prediction pretraining corpus may not accurately reflect how CoT reasoning emerges in large-scale LLMs. This framing could obscure the relationship to actual CoT prompting.
- **Restrictive scope and assumptions (R2, R3 general; R1 also notes):** The analysis is limited to one-layer linear transformers. The impact of non-linearities and depth in practical LLMs is unclear. Theorem 4.1 relies on several restrictive assumptions (e.g., \(V_{31}\) and \(W_{13}\) share the same eigenvalues, \(\sigma < 1/2\) in Assumption 4.1). While the authors note these appear in prior works, exploring more general settings (even with weaker bounds) would significantly strengthen the paper.
- **Proof technique novelty unclear (R2, R3):** The construction and proof techniques largely adapt existing methodologies. It is not clearly articulated what is genuinely novel in the proofs compared to works like Bai et al. or the looped transformer analyses. A clear statement of unique contributions is needed.
- **Theorem 4.2 is poorly explained and lacks empirical support:** The intuition behind OOD generalization within the specified spectrum bounds is not provided. The term \(L^{\text{eval}}_{\Sigma}\) is not defined. Furthermore, no experiments validate Theorem 4.2, reducing its impact.
- **Notation and clarity issues (R1):** 
  - Dimensions of model output \(f\) and \(w\) seem to misalign in Lines 199 and 209.
  - Whether \(\sigma < 1/2\) in Assumption 4.1 is ambiguous.
  - It is unclear if the loss in Theorem 4.1 corresponds to Eq. (7).
  - The reasoning behind setting \(V_{31}\) and \(W_{13}\) to have the same eigenvalues in Assumption 4.1 is not explained.
  - In Theorem 3.1 it is unclear whether the model predicts \(w^*\) or \(w_1\).
- **Practical disconnect (R3 question as weakness):** The theory suggests more CoT steps improve performance, but in practical applications more steps do not always yield better results. The paper does not reconcile this or explain how its insights could inform the design of effective step-by-step instructions for CoT.
