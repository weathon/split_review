## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the sample complexity of training score-based diffusion models. It proposes a robust measure (the \(1-\delta\) error) for evaluating score estimation, instead of the standard \(L^2\) error. The main result shows that with this new measure, the sample complexity for estimating scores can be polylogarithmic in the Wasserstein-2 accuracy \(\gamma\) (i.e., \(\operatorname{polylog}(m_2/\gamma)\)), whereas \(L^2\) estimation provably requires polynomially many samples in \(1/\gamma\) (explicit counterexample given). The paper then shows that this weaker guarantee suffices for the sampling analysis of Benton et al. 2023, yielding improved overall sample complexity bounds. The approach assumes a finite hypothesis class \(\mathcal{H}\) and that \(\mathcal{H}\) contains good approximations to the true scores; results for neural networks are also briefly discussed (Theorem 3.1).

### Strengths
- **Significant improvement in Wasserstein-2 accuracy dependence:** The sample complexity improves from \(\operatorname{poly}(R/\gamma)\) to \(\operatorname{polylog}(m_2/\gamma)\), where \(R\) is a bound on data norm and \(m_2\) the second moment (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Novel robust measure:** The \(1-\delta\) (quantile) error for score estimation is a new idea that could be broadly useful when analysing score-based diffusion models (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Explicit counterexample:** A concrete example shows that estimating the score in \(L^2\) requires polynomially many samples in \(1/\gamma\), motivating the need for a weaker metric (Reviewer 2).
- **Generalizes prior work:** The results do not require the target distribution to be bounded, thus generalizing Block et al. 2020 (Reviewer 1).
- **Addresses core sample complexity issue:** The paper provides a clear angle that learning the score accurately in \(L^2\) is not necessary, which helps justify the empirical success of diffusion models (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation clarity:** The paper suffers from poor clarity: too many informal results, scattered technical details, and a lack of formal theorems in the main text make it hard to assess rigor (Reviewers 1, 3).    *Minority reviewer (Reviewer 3, score 5) was notably more critical, finding the presentation overly reliant on informal statements and the contributions difficult to isolate.*
- **Insufficient justification for Corollary 1.1:** The authors claim it is not a straightforward consequence of the uniform law of large numbers but do not adequately explain why; a more detailed discussion (with concrete failure examples) is needed (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited scope – TV accuracy remains polynomial:** While the Wasserstein-2 improvement is remarkable, the dependency on TV accuracy is still polynomial, making the overall benefit unclear (Reviewer 2).
- **Polynomial in dimension:** The complexity is polynomial in \(d\), whereas recent works (e.g., “Linear convergence bounds for diffusion models via stochastic localization”) show linear or quadratic dependence; explicit comparison is missing (Reviewer 2).
- **Dependence on hypothesis class \(\lvert\mathcal{H}\rvert\) and approximation assumptions:** The main results rely on \(|\mathcal{H}|\) and on the assumption that \(\mathcal{H}\) contains a good approximation of the true score (Theorem 3.1); tracking more explicit parameters would strengthen the contributions (Reviewer 2).
- **Novelty in proof is limited:** The core idea is to exclude a low-probability region and then apply the existing analysis of Benton et al. 2023; the proof techniques are not highly novel (Reviewer 2).
- **Questionable metric in realistic settings:** The proposed metric (evaluating score near 0) may not reflect practical diffusion models, where \(p_T\) is highly smoothed and the score is already well-estimated in \(L^2\); a reviewer argued the metric might be a “bug, not a feature” and called for more realistic examples to justify it (Reviewer 3).
- **Setting appears artificial:** The theoretical setup feels disconnected from true score-based models, and more justification for the new measure is needed beyond the counterexample (Reviewer 3).
- **Typos and missing definitions** (Reviewer 4):
    - After equation (1): “for Brownian motion \(dB_t\)” should be “for Brownian motion \(B_t\)”.
    - On page 1, \(x_t\) is written as if restricted to \(\mathbb{R}\); process is in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) but \(d\) is not yet defined.
    - \(\operatorname{polylog}(m_2/\epsilon)\) used before \(m_2\) or \(\epsilon\) are defined.
    - Equation (4) should be \(\arg\min\), not \(\min\).
    - “norm bounded by \(R\)” – more standard: “supported in \(B(0,R)\)”.
    - \(m_2\) still undefined before equation (6).
    - “to satisfy” → “to be satisfied”.
    - Lemma 4.1: parameter \(\eta\) depends on \(m\), but \(m\) is defined only in the proof.
    - \(\hat{\mathbb{E}}\) used but not defined.
    - Notation \(s_r, s^*_r, \hat{s}_r, \tilde{s}_r\) in Theorem A.1 is messy.
    - Appendix D title: “Utility Resuts” → “Utility Results”.
- **Unclear statements and missing explanations**:
    - The claim in the introduction about “approximates \(q_0\) up to \(\epsilon\) TV and \(\gamma\) Wasserstein-2 error” is imprecise (confuses the actual guarantee from Benton et al. 2023) (Reviewer 1).
    - The relation between the last sentence on page 3 and Theorem 1.2 is not explained; Theorem 1.2 seems independent of training (Reviewer 1).
    - The order of the polynomial dependence is not given, making it hard to compare with prior work (Reviewer 1).
    - The assumption that \(\mathcal{H}\) is finite is restrictive (Reviewer 1).
    - No intuition is provided for the polylogarithmic dependence in \(\operatorname{polylog}(m_2/\gamma)\) (Reviewers 1, 4).
    - The relationship between the size of \(|\mathcal{H}|\) and the domain size is unclear (Reviewer 4).
    - How the discussion in Section 4 relates to Appendix D of Lee et al. 2022 is not clarified (Reviewer 4).
    - The claim that if \(x\) is bounded by \(R\) then the score is bounded by \(R/\sigma^2\) lacks proof or reference (Reviewer 4).
    - Meaning of “\(r\)-smoothed version” in Theorem A.1 is not defined (Reviewer 4).
    - In Theorem A.1, \(\hat{s}_r\) and \(\tilde{s}_r\) appear to be different but are treated as equal in the proof (Reviewer 4).
