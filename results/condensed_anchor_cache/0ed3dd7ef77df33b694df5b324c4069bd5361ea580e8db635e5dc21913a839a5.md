## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GenCL, a generative framework for name-only continual learning that uses text-to-image models to create training data from class names, avoiding web scraping and manual annotation. Two components are introduced: HIRPG (hierarchical recurrent prompt generation) for diverse prompts and CONAN (complexity-navigating ensembler) for selecting minimal-overlap samples from multiple generators. Evaluations on PACS, DomainNet, and multi-modal visual reasoning (MVCIL) show competitive or superior performance compared to prior methods and even supervised learning in some settings. Reviewers generally find the idea novel and the writing clear, but raise significant concerns about evaluation scope, hyperparameter justification, missing baselines, computational cost, privacy, and scalability. One reviewer (score 6) is notably more positive; the other three (scores 3, 5, 3) are more critical, especially regarding experimental rigor and practical limitations.

### Strengths
- Novel and interesting approach: using generative models to replace labeled data in continual learning, addressing cost, privacy, and web‑scraping issues. (All reviewers)
- HIRPG and CONAN effectively enhance intra‑ and inter‑diversity of generated samples; claimed to be potentially useful beyond continual learning. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- Extensive experiments covering in‑distribution and out‑of‑distribution evaluations, with comparisons against many baselines; results demonstrate robustness and strong performance. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- Paper is well‑written, easy to follow, with clear figures and thorough ablation studies in main text and appendix. (Reviewers 2, 4)

### Weaknesses
- Performance still shows a large gap compared to fully supervised data; one reviewer suggests exploring supplement with 1–2 manually annotated examples. (Reviewer 1)
- Heavily relies on generative models trained on large datasets that likely overlap with test concepts; unclear how GenCL would perform in entirely disjoint domains (e.g., medical). (Reviewer 1)
- CIL evaluation is limited to only 7 classes (PACS); should be scaled to larger benchmarks like CIFAR‑100 and ImageNet‑1K to assess scalability. (Reviewer 2)
- No discussion of computational cost: training time, storage, extra GPU resources for image generation. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- No evaluation on fine-grained datasets (CUB, Flowers, Cars); unclear if the approach handles subtle inter‑class differences. (Reviewer 2)
- Scaling with current text‑to‑image models is difficult; the paper should explicitly discuss limitations and possible solutions. (Reviewer 2)
- The K‑ary tree structure (K = 7, depth = 2) in HIRPG is arbitrary; no analysis of how different K and D affect prompt diversity or task performance, raising concerns about generalizability. (Reviewer 3)
- Claim that overlap between generated prompts from different nodes is rare lacks empirical support; should provide quantitative similarity measures (e.g., cosine similarity of embeddings). (Reviewer 3)
- No hyperparameter analysis for Eq. 4 (temperature τ, truncation ratio L); a sensitivity study and visualization of RMD feature space (e.g., Relative Mahalanobis Distance) are needed. (Reviewer 3)
- Privacy concerns also apply to generative models (memorization of training data); the paper should discuss this and possible mitigations. (Reviewer 4)
- Baselines are insufficient: several synthetic augmentation methods (e.g., [1], [2]) use similar techniques (prompting LLMs, text‑to‑image generation, filtering) and should be compared; also needs justification why name‑only CL is distinct from standard image classification with expanding labels. (Reviewer 4)
- MVCIL setup is questionable because LLM pretraining likely exposed the model to the new concepts, not fully aligned with a continual learning setting. (Reviewer 4)
- Sample efficiency not reported: number of synthetic samples generated and GPU time for generation and training. (Reviewer 4)
- Alternative approach (continual open‑world representation learning [3]) not considered; would the proposed method work in that setting? (Reviewer 4)
- Why are pre‑trained ImageNet‑1k ViT‑Base models used, while other CIL settings exist? (Reviewer 2)
- Table 3 uses a CLIP‑pretrained ResNet‑50, inconsistent with other experiments; why this choice? (Reviewer 2)
- Why are difficult samples preferred in Section 4.3? (Reviewer 2)
- Need to analyze potential biases in generated data and their impact on continual learning. (Reviewer 1)
- Need to clarify if any mechanism selects hard negative examples and how that affects performance. (Reviewer 1)
