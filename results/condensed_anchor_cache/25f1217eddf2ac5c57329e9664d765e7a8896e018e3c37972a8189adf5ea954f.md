## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes L-MBOP-E, a model-based offline planning algorithm that builds on MBOP by (1) learning a dynamics model in a low-dimensional latent state space to reduce compounding errors, and (2) incorporating an “extrinsic policy” alongside the behavior-cloned (BC) policy using Thompson sampling to decide which policy to follow during MPPI-based trajectory optimization. Experiments on D4RL MuJoCo and Deepmind Control tasks compare L-MBOP-E to MBOP and MOPP, reporting significant gains (over 200% in some cases) and improved zero-shot task adaptation.

### Strengths

- Each proposed modification (latent dynamics model and extrinsic policy with Thompson sampling) shows individual benefit, and combining them yields further performance gains (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The idea of using Thompson sampling to choose between the BC policy and an extrinsic policy during planning is original and potentially allows flexible reuse of previously trained policies, extending MBOP-style algorithms (Reviewers 1, 4).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). Reviewer 2 additionally highlights state-of-the-art results on D4RL and DMC benchmarks, reduced model uncertainty, and improved data efficiency from the latent model.

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty of latent dynamics model is overclaimed.** The learning objective and architecture are not fundamentally different from prior latent model-based methods such as PlaNet, Dreamer (v1–v3), and TDMPC. The paper merely replaces Dreamer’s RSSM with an MLP, and Dreamer already supports state inputs. Therefore, the latent model contribution lacks novelty (Reviewers 3, 4). Reviewer 3 further notes that the claimed advantage of latent models (handling high-dimensional pixel observations) is not exploited, as no visual offline RL experiments are conducted – a major omission.
- **Extrinsic policy assumption is unrealistic and poorly justified.** The paper motivates the extrinsic policy as a meta-learned policy or a policy from another task, but the experiments use a policy trained with SAC on the same task until it performs “reasonably well.” It is unclear why such a policy would be available in practice, and the experimental setup does not reflect the intended use case (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). Reviewer 4 flags that if the extrinsic policy is trained online in the same environment, it constitutes cheating in the offline setting; if trained from a different environment, the paper fails to address how model error from unseen actions is avoided.
- **Missing comparisons with relevant offline RL algorithms.** The paper does not compare with several recent offline RL methods – both model-free (e.g., IQL, TD3+BC, ATAC) and model-based methods that explicitly handle model inaccuracy and over-conservatism (e.g., MOPO, MOReL, COMBO, CBOP) (Reviewers 3, 4). For visual offline RL, comparisons with methods like Lu et al., TACO, and Islam et al. are lacking (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 also requests comparison with uncertainty-regularized planning methods (e.g., ensemble-based approaches).
- **Thompson sampling is not properly ablated.** The impact of Thompson sampling on overall performance is not shown. A simple baseline – always sampling N/2 rollouts from each policy then applying MPPI – is not tested (Reviewer 1). No alternative strategies for incorporating the extrinsic policy are explored, and planning hyperparameters (horizon, number of rollouts, latent dimension) are not ablated; it is unclear whether they are tuned per environment or fixed (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Lack of crucial experimental details and analysis.** The paper does not explain how the latent dimensionality is chosen for each environment, whether sensitivity analysis was performed, or whether an adaptive approach could be beneficial (Reviewer 2). Further missing: how the autoencoder is trained (pretrained separately or jointly with the dynamics model?) (Reviewer 2); how the extrinsic policy is initialized (from BC or from scratch?) (Reviewer 2); what causes variance across random seeds (model uncertainty vs. policy optimization?) (Reviewer 2); and the computational complexity / wall-clock runtime of the method (Reviewer 2).
- **No explicit support for claim that latent representation reduces compounding errors.** The paper asserts that low-dimensional latent models yield higher accuracy dynamics models and reduce compounding errors, but provides no experimental results or citations to support this claim (Reviewer 4).
- **Critic training may suffer from over/underestimation.** The value function is learned in latent space without using techniques like CQL to mitigate overestimation for unseen state-action pairs; the paper does not discuss how this issue is handled (Reviewer 4).
- **Zero-shot adaptation claim is not properly contextualized.** The ability of latent world models for zero-shot adaptation is already well-known (e.g., PlaNet), and the paper should compare with PlaNet or Dreamer in such settings; more details on reward modification/tuning for new tasks are needed (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **One reviewer (Reviewer 2) is notably more positive** (score 5) than the other three (scores 3), commending state-of-the-art results, reduced model uncertainty, and improved data efficiency despite these weaknesses.
