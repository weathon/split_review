## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies sparsity-inducing activation functions (shifted ReLU and soft thresholding) for deep neural networks, analyzing their behavior at random initialization using the large-width Gaussian process limit and edge of stability theory. It proves a training instability for these activations, which is resolved by clipping the activation magnitude (proposed CReLU and CST). Numerical experiments on MLPs and CNNs (100-layer networks) on MNIST and CIFAR-10 demonstrate that the clipped activations can be trained with up to 85% fractional sparsity with minor accuracy degradation.

### Strengths
- Introduces two natural classes of sparsity-inducing activations with a tunable parameter τ, and a simple clipping fix (CReLU, CST) that controls both sparsity and stability.
- The theoretical analysis via variance/correlation maps is elegant and novel, providing insight into instability through V'(q*) and V''(q*).
- The theory is validated in practice: training very deep networks with high activation sparsity (up to 85%) while retaining close to full accuracy (Table 2).
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow despite the math-heavy content.
- The contribution is timely and relevant for improving computational efficiency of deep networks; this work can serve as a basis for future sparse neural network research.

### Weaknesses
- **Applicability and depth**: The experiments use 100-layer networks; it is unclear whether the results apply to networks of more reasonable depth. The authors should show experiments for shallower networks and discuss how depth affects behavior. The effect of residual connections on stability is not discussed; clarify whether findings extend to residual networks.
- **Missing experiments in main paper**: Results for soft thresholding (ST) are only in the appendix; they should be in the main text as a core part of the manuscript. A direct comparison between clipped and non-clipped activations (e.g., CReLU vs. shifted ReLU) is missing from the main paper. Table 2 only shows results for CReLU and ReLU with τ=0; experiments with CReLU m=0 and with ReLU at other τ values are needed to verify the claimed failure of non-clipped sparse activations. The higher sparsity regime (s=0.85) in Table 2 needs more exploration and explanation.
- **Explanation gaps**: The criterion on the variance map V_φ for successful training is not clearly stated; the explanation from Figure 2 is hard to parse. The heuristics for choosing the clipping magnitude m (Section 3.1) are unclear; Figure 4 is not sufficiently discussed and its meaning is not well explained. The important concept of edge of stability (EoC) is not motivated and explained enough.
- **Numerical reporting**: The standard deviations in Table 2 show many zeros; either higher precision is needed or an explanation of why they are zero.
- **Computational efficiency**: The main motivation is reducing computational cost, but no evaluation of actual speed or efficiency gains is provided.
- **Missing comparisons**: There is no comparison to existing sparsity-inducing methods; the authors note the lack of related work but could include baselines (e.g., regular ReLU with dropout or other sparsity techniques).
- **Code not provided**; implementation details only in appendix.
- **Presentation issues**: Figure 2 is missing axis labels and legends are not readable on printed paper. Also, the paper does not clarify whether the variance map equations for these nonlinearities are derived for the first time; the novelty claim could be better positioned.
