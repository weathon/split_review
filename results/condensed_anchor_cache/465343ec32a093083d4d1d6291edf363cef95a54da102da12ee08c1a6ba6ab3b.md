## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Memory-Efficient Nash Q-Learning (ME-Nash-QL), a model-free algorithm for two-player zero-sum Markov games (TZMG). The algorithm provably outputs an \(\varepsilon\)-approximate Nash policy with space complexity \(O(SABH)\), sample complexity \(\widetilde{O}(H^4 SAB / \varepsilon^2)\), and computational complexity \(O(T \,\mathrm{poly}(AB))\). It achieves state-of-the-art space and computational complexity, nearly optimal sample complexity, and the best burn-in cost among algorithms with the same sample complexity. The algorithm retains Markov policies (via CCE relaxation) and uses reference-advantage decomposition and early-settlement techniques. Dependence on \(S\) and \(H\) is optimal; dependence on actions is \(O(AB)\) rather than \(O(A+B)\).

### Strengths
- The algorithm enjoys several benign properties jointly: low sample complexity, low space complexity, low computational complexity, low burn-in cost, outputs a Markov policy, and outputs a Nash policy.
- Performs well for long horizons while retaining Markov output and low burn-in cost.
- Achieves state-of-the-art space complexity \(O(SABH)\) and computational complexity, outperforming existing tabular algorithms.
- Achieves nearly optimal sample complexity, matching the best known bounds for TZMG.
- Achieves the best burn-in cost \(O(SAB\,\mathrm{poly}(H))\) compared to prior works (which need at least \(O(S^3 AB\,\mathrm{poly}(H))\) for the same sample complexity).
- Theoretical proofs appear rigorous and the analysis is solid.
- Well-written and easy to follow; algorithm design is clearly explained; related works are covered in detail.
- TZMG is a foundational and critically significant problem in MARL; the results set a new benchmark for further studies.
- Simultaneously achieves state-of-the-art across all considered aspects (sample complexity, memory efficiency, Markov output policy, burn-in cost).

### Weaknesses
**Disagreement across reviewers:** Two reviewers rated the paper 8 and emphasize its strong theoretical contributions and SOTA results; two others rated 6 and raise concerns about technical novelty and the practical limitation of the \(AB\) action dependence.

- The algorithm does not break the curse of multi-agent: dependence on the product \(AB\) rather than sum \(A+B\) is a significant limitation in environments with large action spaces for either or both agents. Even if long-horizon scenarios are the focus, the authors should more thoroughly address this limitation.
- Technical novelty is a major concern. The reference-advantage decomposition technique has already been used in TZMG by Feng et al. (2023) (not cited), achieving regret \(\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{H^2 SAB T})\) that matches the regret bound in this work. The early-settlement design to reduce burn-in cost is also not new in the literature. The core ideas do not seem fundamentally different from existing approaches (Nash-VI, V-learning).
- The algorithm design shares strong similarities with Nash-VI (Liu et al., July 2021) and V-learning (Jin et al., 2022): computing a CCE and taking marginal policies; the learning rate \(\frac{H+1}{H+N}\); the form of bonus terms; the update of lower/upper bounds for Q-functions. The paper does not provide a thorough discussion of the fundamental distinctions or algorithmic nuances, making it hard to assess the unique contributions.
- The biggest improvements are in space complexity and burn-in cost, which are less important metrics compared to sample complexity or time complexity. These improvements come at the cost of a much more complicated algorithm and proof procedure.
- The theoretical findings are limited to TZMG and the CCE setting. The practical relevance of CCE in general-sum games is not always clear, and the connection between CCE and Nash equilibrium in TZMG needs more explicit justification. The paper does not discuss limitations of this choice or explore extensions to broader classes of games.
- The extension to multi-player general-sum games (Theorem 3) is claimed, but no algorithm or proof is provided. It is unclear what the learning target (Nash or CE) is; the extension is not straightforward and needs detailed discussion and references (e.g., Song et al. 2021, Mao & Başar 2021, Daskalakis et al. 2023).
- Auxiliary functions in Algorithm 2 are too nested, making them hard to read. A more modular presentation with clear inputs/outputs is needed. There is also a blank section A.3.1 on page 14.
- The paper does not explain how \(\operatorname{CCE}(\bar{Q}, Q)\) is computed; a detailed introduction to its calculation is needed for self-containment.
- It is not clearly explained whether the space complexity independent of \(T\) is due to outputting a single Markov policy, and whether the CCE relaxation is essential for that space complexity.
- The paper mentions the possibility of achieving \(A+B\) sample complexity in future work, but does not discuss whether Nash V-learning could help achieve this.
