## Merged Review

### Summary
Topo-Diffusion integrates topological data analysis (TDA) via persistent homology into diffusion models for image and point cloud generation. The diffusion process operates in the topological domain, and samples are generated by reconstructing topological features. Theoretical stability guarantees are provided. Evaluations on seven datasets (including MNIST and simple point clouds) claim state-of-the-art performance across all metrics.

### Strengths
- Novelty: First known integration of TDA and diffusion models (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).  
- Theoretical stability guarantees for topological features of generated samples over diffusion timesteps (Reviewers 2, 4).  
- Empirical results show improved performance across all evaluation metrics on both point clouds and real images; Reviewer 2 states state-of-the-art; Reviewer 4 notes successful real-world data demonstration.  
- Key innovation: employing topological degrading and reconstruction within the diffusion process (Reviewer 3).  
- One reviewer (score 8) praised the soundness, theoretical contribution, and clear potential, while three reviewers (score 6) raised more critical concerns.

### Weaknesses
- **Writing clarity**: The paper is not clearly written; it needs a rewrite that explains the forward and backward processes, technical challenges, and the payoff (Reviewer 1).  
- **Missing related work**: The diffusion models related works section omits key references [1,2] (Reviewer 4).  
- **Limited dataset complexity**: Experiments are limited to toy examples (MNIST, simple point clouds); no evaluation on more complex datasets such as ShapeNet (Reviewer 2).  
- **Overclaiming contributions on persistence point transformations**: The paper claims to introduce “birth-lifespan transformation” and “differentiable distribution transformation” (Eq. 2), but these are merely renamings of standard methods (Betti curve, persistent landscape, persistent image). Many existing vectorization techniques (Barcode statistics, algebraic functions, binning approaches, persistent codebooks, etc.) are not cited; proper attribution is required (Reviewer 3, Questions 1–2).  
- **Parameter-free claim is incorrect**: The birth-lifespan transformation still requires discretization/vectorization, introducing parameters (mesh/grid size). In contrast, methods like Barcode statistics or Algebraic functions produce fixed-size vectors without such tuning, making them truly parameter-free (Reviewer 3, Question 2).  
- **Figure 19 inconsistencies**: Barcodes do not match the data. E.g., second simplicial complex (left to right) has 7 0-dim generators but barcode shows 6; third complex has 7 0-dim generators but barcode shows 1; third complex has 2 1-dim generators but barcode shows 3 (Reviewer 3, Question 3).  
- **Technical inaccuracy**: Page 4 states “K denotes the maximum dimension of simplices of x_t”; however, the PD dimension can differ from the highest simplex dimension (Reviewer 3, Question 4).  
- **Citation errors**: Duplicate text such as “Gao et al. Gao et al. propose” and “Liu et al. Liu et al. (2019)” (Reviewer 3, Question 5).  
- **Figures 2 and 3**: Axes are not labeled, reducing clarity (Reviewer 4).  
- **Conclusion incompleteness**: The conclusion should elaborate on what diverse data types are worth exploring with Topo-Diffusion (Reviewer 4).  
- **Missing ELBO explanation**: How is the ELBO-based approximation modified when including persistent homology? The paper should reference related literature (e.g., https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00848) (Reviewer 1).  
- **Geometric limitation**: TDA excels at topological structure but gives limited detailed local geometric information; the model may be most suitable for data with strong topological properties and may underperform when detailed geometry is important (Reviewer 3).
