## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a new non-conformity score for conformal prediction in classification, called Sorted Adaptive Prediction Sets (SAPS). Unlike APS which uses all softmax probabilities, SAPS discards all probability values except the maximum and relies on ranking. The authors show empirically that SAPS reduces prediction set size and improves conditional coverage compared to APS and RAPS on three datasets under different architectures. The theoretical contribution includes a finite-sample coverage guarantee and a dominance result over APS under the condition \(\hat{\pi} = \pi\) and \(\lambda \geq 1 - \frac{1}{K}\). Reviewer opinions are split: Reviewers 1 and 4 are very positive about the paper’s motivation, clarity, and empirical results, while Reviewers 2 and 3 raise significant concerns about the theoretical contribution, the adaptiveness claim, and presentation clarity.

### Strengths
- The paper is easy to read; the idea and solution are clearly articulated (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- The observation that ranking (or order) matters more than the exact probability values is interesting and, to the reviewers’ knowledge, novel (Reviewer 4).
- Empirically shows that using all softmax probabilities is not necessary in APS; SAPS returns more efficient prediction sets than APS and RAPS on three datasets under different network architectures (Reviewer 3).
- Theoretical justification: Proposition 2 shows that under the condition \(\hat{\pi} = \pi\) and \(\lambda \geq 1 - \frac{1}{K}\), SAPS dominates APS in prediction set size (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
- The finite-sample coverage guarantee is maintained (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- A novel metric ESCV is proposed to evaluate performance (Reviewer 2).
- The results are convincing (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
**Theoretical limitations**
- The theoretical contribution is limited. Proposition 1 states a common property of any non-conformity measure (Reviewer 2).
- The condition in Proposition 2 (\(\hat{\pi} = \pi\)) is very difficult to satisfy in practice (Reviewer 2).
- The condition \(\lambda \geq 1 - \frac{1}{K}\) in Proposition 2 conflicts with the experimental practice: \(\lambda\) is searched in the range 0.001 to 0.5, and Fig. 4a shows that set size increases when \(\lambda\) exceeds 0.2 (Reviewer 2).
- There is no theoretical comparison with RAPS, even though RAPS is also a modification of APS and is empirically compared (Reviewer 2).
- The choice of the constant 2 in Eq. (8) (in the term \(o(y,\hat{\pi}(x)) - 2 + u\)) is unexplained; motivation and justification are needed (Reviewer 2).
- The proof for Proposition 1 is unclear: the difference between Proposition 1 and Theorem 2 is not articulated, and the proof does not involve \(\mathcal{C}(x_{n+1}, u_{n+1})\) (Reviewer 3).
- In the proof of Lemma 1, it is unclear whether the assumption \(p_{(k)}\geq \frac{1}{k}\) is intended and where \(\tilde{k}\) is used (Reviewer 3).
- The derivation of the calibrated threshold (just above Eq. (9) on page 13) is not obvious; moreover, the asterisk (*) at the end of that equation is unexplained (Reviewer 1).

**Adaptiveness and practical performance**
- The claimed contribution of higher adaptiveness is not convincing. In the example of Fig. 3(b), both SAPS and RAPS achieve the same coverage, so why should the method require a larger prediction set for difficult observations? RAPS gives more efficient predictions on high-difficulty observations, while SAPS only gives efficient predictions on a shorter interval of difficulty (Reviewer 3).
- The performance gap between SAPS and an ideal conformal predictor based on the true underlying data distribution is not discussed; how does this gap vary with data difficulty? (Reviewer 3).

**Methodology and hyperparameter selection**
- The hyperparameter \(\lambda\) requires an additional data split (e.g., part of the calibration set) and can affect efficiency (Reviewer 4). 
- In experiments, does SAPS use a subset of the calibration set to tune \(\lambda\)? If so, do all baselines use the (same) remaining calibration set? This detail is not clear (Reviewer 4).
- A pseudo-algorithm for calibration and prediction (like the one in RAPS) is missing. The statement “We choose the hyper-parameter that achieves the smallest set size on a validation set” is insufficient because the smallest set size on a validation set cannot secure the desired coverage. The interaction of hyperparameter selection with the coverage guarantee must be detailed (Reviewer 3).

**Presentation and minor issues**
- Citation error: the APS method is referenced as (Romano et al., 2019) but the correct reference may be Romano et al., 2020; the 2019 paper is on conformalized quantile regression (Reviewer 1).
- In the definition of coverage rate (Eq. (2)), it may be \(\mathcal{C}(x_i, y_i, u_i)\) rather than \(\mathcal{C}(x_i, y_i)\) (Reviewer 3).
- The usage of “i.e.” should be checked throughout (Reviewer 3).
- In Section 4.2, “0.5” should be “0.05” (Reviewer 3).
- In the proof of Lemma 1, it is unclear why \(\frac{1}{\lambda} \leq 1 - \frac{1}{K}\) is used when previously the requirement was \(\lambda > 1 - \frac{1}{K}\) (Reviewer 3).
- The proofs are not friendly to read (Reviewer 3).
