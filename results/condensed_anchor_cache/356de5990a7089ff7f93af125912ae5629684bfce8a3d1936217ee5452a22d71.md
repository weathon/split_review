## Merged Review

### Summary
FASP introduces a structured pruning framework for LLMs that couples column removal in one layer with row removal in the preceding layer, uses a column-summed Wanda importance metric, and applies a least-squares restoration to recover pruned weights. Experiments on OPT and LLaMA families show competitive perplexity and zero-shot accuracy with faster pruning (e.g., OPT-125M in 17 seconds, LLaMA-30B in 20 minutes on a single RTX 4090) and a 16% inference speedup at 30% sparsity. Reviewers are divided: one rates it 6, citing strong restoration and real-world relevance; the other four rate it 3–5, citing limited novelty, missing baselines, and inadequate analysis.

### Strengths
- **Clear writing and well-organized presentation.** The paper is easy to follow, with well-structured sections on methodology, experiments, and results (R1, R3, R4).
- **Clever column-row coupling.** Leveraging the inherent mapping between sequential layers to jointly prune rows and columns is a practical and efficient design (R3, R4).
- **Effective weight restoration mechanism.** The least-squares optimization preserves model fidelity and maintains competitive perplexity and accuracy across sparsity levels without retraining, making it suitable for resource-constrained deployment (R2, R3).
- **Practical efficiency.** FASP requires no retraining and achieves fast pruning on consumer hardware (NVIDIA RTX 4090), reducing time and resource investment (R1, R2, R5). Inference speedup of 16% at 30% sparsity is demonstrated (R3, R5).
- **Comprehensive experiments.** The evaluation covers both perplexity and zero-shot accuracy on OPT and LLaMA models, with multiple sparsity levels and comparison to several baselines (R2, R3, R4). FASP often achieves the highest accuracy or lowest perplexity in the reported settings (R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty.** The core ideas (neuron pruning, Wanda-based column importance via simple summation, least-squares restoration) are all directly adapted from prior work (Wanda [Sun et al. 2023], LLM-Pruner [Ma et al. 2023], SparseGPT, etc.). The integration does not constitute a significant methodological advance (R1, R3, R5). Reviewer 5 specifically notes that formulation, importance metric, and restoration are all present in previous works with only minor modifications.
- **Missing key comparisons and baselines.**  
  - Missing comparisons with recent structured pruning methods: DISP-LLM, Pruner-Zero (R1).  
  - Missing comparison with layer pruning (e.g., SLEB [Song et al. 2024]) and semi-structured pruning (e.g., 2:4 sparse with Wanda) to justify the problem definition (latency vs. model size) (R5).  
  - The only inference speedup numbers are reported for FASP alone without comparison to baselines (R3, R5).
- **Outdated model families.** Experiments are limited to OPT and LLaMA-1. Results on recent models (LLaMA-2, LLaMA-3, Vicuna, Mixtral, Qwen) are absent, and it is unclear how FASP generalizes to newer architectures that are harder to prune/quantize (R3, R4, R5). R3 and R5 both call for experiments on Llama-3.
- **Unfair perplexity evaluation and overfitting concern.** The restoration step uses 128 samples from WikiText, which may bias results toward that dataset. FASP achieves lower perplexity than the original model at 10% sparsity (12.42 vs. 12.47), hinting at overfitting. Perplexity results on a held-out corpus like C4 are needed, even when restoration uses WikiText samples (R3).
- **Missing analyses.**  
  - **Memory savings:** No quantitative comparison of post-pruning memory footprint is provided, despite being a key motivation for structured pruning (R2, R1 notes missing, R2 asks explicitly).  
  - **Why not prune WQ/WK?** The decision to leave WQ and WK unpruned is not sufficiently explained or ablated. Reviewers suggest exploring selective pruning of heads or adaptive sparsity within attention (R2, R3, R4).  
  - **Ablation on pruning direction:** The paper applies Wanda scores to determine which columns to prune (based on down/out projections). An ablation study on whether to prune rows instead of columns is missing (R3).  
  - **Intrinsic mechanism analysis:** Lacks intuitive analytical experiments to explain why FASP works or why certain choices are made (R4).  
  - **No code provided:** Reproducibility is questionable; code was not submitted for review (R4, R5).
- **Limited hardware evaluation.** No benchmarks on edge devices, CPUs, or frameworks like Ollama, ONNX, or llama.cpp are included, despite the stated goal of deployment on resource-constrained hardware. Only GPU latency is reported (R2).
- **Writing and presentation issues.**  
  - Figures 1 and 2 are oversimplified, and Figure 2 lacks a color legend (R4, R5).  
  - Figures 3 and 4 have font sizes too small to read (R5).  
  - Table captions are placed below tables (should be above per ICLR guide) (R5).  
  - Tables 1,3,4 show only slight improvements over FLAP in both time and accuracy (R4). Tables 3,5,6 share the same title despite different purposes (R4).  
  - Section 5 (Discussion) is only five lines with no meaningful content (R5).  
  - Imprecise math: "n(1-s)" in line 254 should use floor/ceiling to guarantee an integer (R5).  
  - Method name "FASP" is too generic and not descriptive (R5).  
  - No reproducibility statement (R5).  
- **Disagreement among reviewers:** While one reviewer (R2) strongly praises the restoration mechanism and practical relevance, the majority (R1, R3, R5) conclude the novelty and experimental depth are insufficient. R4 notes only incremental gains over FLAP.
