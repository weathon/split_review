## Merged Review

### Summary

Reviewers rated the paper 5–8, with one reviewer (score 8) finding the method very solid and others (scores 5–6) expressing concerns about novelty, interpretability, comparisons, and clarity. All agree the paper proposes a motion customization method for video diffusion models using two types of learned motion embeddings: a 1D Motion Query-Key Embedding to modulate temporal attention maps (global motion) and a 2D Motion Value Embedding to modulate attention values (local motion). An inference strategy removes spatial dimensions from the QK embedding and applies a differential operation to the V embedding to debias appearance. Experiments show performance against SOTA baselines from CVPR 2024 and ECCV 2024. All reviewers note the clarity of the writing.

### Strengths

- **Method solid and logical**: The motion embeddings are an intuitive way to disentangle spatial from temporal dynamics (R1). The combination of 1D QK embeddings for global motion and 2D V embeddings for local motion is novel and interesting, even though individual ideas (1D exclusion, differencing) appear in prior work (DMT) (R2).
- **Strong empirical results**: Qualitative and quantitative comparisons demonstrate superiority over SOTA baselines (CVPR 2024, ECCV 2024) (R1, R2, R3). Ablation studies validate each component (R2, R3). Supplementary videos show satisfying performance (R2).
- **Novel task and clear presentation**: The paper introduces the task of motion customization and presents it in a well‑written, easy‑to‑follow way (R1, R3, R4). The experiments are comprehensive with a variety of metrics (R4).
- **One reviewer (score 8) particularly emphasized the method’s robustness and intuitive design**, while the other reviewers (scores 6,5,5) acknowledged the contributions but raised concerns.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited qualitative results**: The number of visual examples in the paper and supplementary is very small, raising doubts about generalizability (R1).
- **Missing comparisons**: No comparisons with Tune‑A‑Video (R1). No comparisons with other motion customization methods such as AnimateDiff or MotionMaster, possibly because they require a specific motion module (R1). No comparison with temporal LoRA optimization to show that Eq. (3) is superior to finetuning temporal attention (which hurts the prior) or temporal LoRA (which does not) (R3). The method targets temporal attention in decomposed architectures; it would be compelling to demonstrate performance in a full‑attention architecture like Open‑Sora (R4).
- **Novelty concerns**: The proposed approach is functionally similar to MotionDirector, which uses motion LoRA in the temporal layer by adding weights to the Q, K, V projectors (Wq, Wk, Wv). Adding learnable embeddings to the input features (Eq. 3) is a variant rather than a truly new technique, limiting novelty (R4). The loss calculation either uses the standard denoising loss or that of VMC/MotionDirector; the paper should better argue why optimizing motion embeddings is superior to other options (temporal attention, temporal LoRA) (R3).
- **Insufficient interpretability of embeddings**: No convincing evidence is provided for the claimed interpretability. It is unclear why 1D embeddings are used for QK and 2D for V—the ablation shows effectiveness but does not explain the underlying mechanism or why 1D is superior to 2D for QK (R2). For the V embeddings, it is unclear what spatial patterns are learned and how they contribute to motion (R2). Visualizing the learned QK embeddings (e.g., attention maps) or V embeddings would help connect the representations to motion characteristics (R4).
- **Lack of clarity in method details**:
  - In Eq. (5), the difference operation only debiases frames with j > 1. Why is the first frame (j=1) not debiased, e.g., by differencing with j = L? (R2)
  - In Fig. 4, “excluding spatial dimensions” is ambiguous: does it mean optimizing temporal attention parameters instead of self‑attention? What is being visualized in the middle of Fig. 4? (e.g., attention maps via PCA?) (R3)
  - The term “differential” in Fig. 4 (right) is unclear; if it refers to residuals across frames, a different phrase would be more appropriate (R3).
  - L260–264 requires more explanation: what is the goal (appearance debiasing?), why would it work, and has it been ablated? (R3)
- **Zero‑shot status**: Is the method zero‑shot? Are the motion embeddings pretrained on any videos or learned from the source video only? (R1)
- **Evaluation issues**:
  - For temporal consistency, CLIP is not the optimal metric; referencing V‑bench metrics would increase value (R3).
  - Why does the output video have fewer frames than the source video? (e.g., supplementary teaser: 4.mp4 at 1 second vs. 4_source.mp4 at 2 seconds) (R2)
- **Other missing analyses**: The paper should provide results when 1D QK and 2D V embeddings function independently to demonstrate their separate effects (R4). The dependence on the backbone foundation model is unclear—would Tune‑A‑Video + a strong backbone be equivalent to this method? Are the stated limitations (line 83–92) due to the method or the backbone? (R1)
- **Minor issues**: Line 191: F should be \mathbf{F} (R2). Line 320: references to DMT and VMC should be updated to their correct CVPR 2024 publication years (Yatim et al., 2024; Jeong et al., 2024) (R2).
