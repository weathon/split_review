- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 5, 3, 3, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes L2H, a method to convert logits from pre-trained flat clustering models (TEMI, TURTLE) into a hierarchy by iteratively merging clusters based on logit probability sums. It is simple, computationally efficient (bypasses O(N²)), and reportedly outperforms bespoke deep hierarchical clustering methods on CIFAR-10/100, Food-101, and ImageNet-1K. However, the evaluation is limited in scope, baselines are inadequate, novelty is incremental, and the algorithm description suffers from clarity issues. Two reviewers gave a rating of 5, finding the method effective and simple, while three gave 3, citing restricted experiments, insufficient baselines, and limited contribution.

### Strengths
- The paper is generally well written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1,2,3,4). However, Reviewer 5 notes significant clarity issues (see Weaknesses).
- The method is simple and bypasses the O(N²) complexity of standard agglomerative clustering (Reviewers 2,3). Reviewer 2 highlights this as elegant.
- L2H-TEMI/TURTLE outperforms competing deep hierarchical clustering baselines in both clustering performance and runtime on the evaluated datasets (Reviewers 2,5; Reviewer 3 says experiments properly conducted and outperforms other hierarchical approaches).
- Provides pseudo code of the algorithm (Reviewer 4).
- The work is valuable as a strong baseline for complex hierarchical methods (Reviewer 1).
- The method is generic: it can be applied to any pre-trained clustering model that outputs logits, and also to supervised setups (e.g., ImageNet classifier) as shown in the paper (Reviewers 1,5).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and incremental contribution:** The observation that logits encode class similarity is well known and has been used previously to reconstruct classification hierarchies (Reviewer 4 cites arxiv 2007.06068, ACM 3491102.3501823). The method essentially performs shallow clustering in representation space (Reviewer 3).
- **Restricted experiments and datasets:**
  - Datasets lack hierarchical labels: CIFAR-10 has none, Food-101 has none, CIFAR-100 has only one intermediate level. The method should be benchmarked on larger, truly hierarchical datasets such as iNaturalist, BIOSCAN-5M, or ImageNet with its full hierarchy (Reviewer 1). Since the method trains in 5 minutes on IN-1k, larger datasets are feasible (Reviewer 1).
  - Quantitative ImageNet-1K results are absent from Table 1 (Reviewer 1). This is a missing experiment.
  - Only two pre-trained clustering models (TEMI, TURTLE) are used as backbones, limiting generalizability; these are not the same backbones used for competing baselines (Reviewer 4). The method should also be tested on non-vision tasks (Reviewer 2) and on unsupervised representations (e.g., SimCLR, MaskedAutoEncoders) to assess performance without class-supervised features (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 asks whether logits from standard classification models (e.g., ResNet trained on ImageNet) were considered.
- **Inadequate baselines and unfair comparisons:**
  - L2H benefits from pre-trained models, while competing methods are trained from scratch; the runtime comparison is therefore not fair (Reviewer 2). An ablation study is needed to isolate the algorithm’s contribution from that of the pre-trained backbone (Reviewers 2,3).
  - No baseline is provided that applies agglomerative clustering directly to the logits (or embeddings) from TEMI/TURTLE. Similarly, no comparison is made with alternative methods for obtaining hierarchies from pre-trained embeddings (e.g., Lowe et al. 2024: UMAP >5 dims + agglomerative clustering) (Reviewer 1). Do other methods assume a starting division to K clusters? (Reviewer 2).
  - Reviewer 1 questions: Is the input to the agglomerative clustering baseline raw pixel values? (Q2). Is L2H-TEMI’s flat clustering performance identical to TEMI’s? (Q3). Did the authors really train the auto-encoder for DeepECT from scratch on CIFAR-10/100 in 25 minutes on a single CPU? (Q4, seems implausible).
- **Algorithm and method clarity issues:**
  - Algorithm 1 is poorly written: variable s is used for both step number and score; several variables (g_θ, g^m_θ, K, Λ) are used in the algorithm but never defined there; argmin with ∈ is confusing (possibly for ties); K is not updated in the outer loop, so the inner loop refers to stale cluster indices (Reviewer 1).
  - Terms “clusters” and “groups” are used interchangeably without clear definitions (Reviewer 5). It is unclear whether “clusters” refer to the initial output of the pre-trained model or to intermediate groupings.
  - The process of determining the number of hierarchies (stopping criterion and depth) is not explained (Reviewer 5).
  - The score function s(G) sums the probabilities of samples in G, making it sensitive to cluster size, which may distort similarity (Reviewer 5, line 190).
  - How are logits generated for different datasets? For example, using a 1000-way ImageNet classifier on CIFAR-10 would give 1000 logits; does that mean the finest-level hierarchy has 1000 clusters? This needs clarification (Reviewer 5).
  - Equations should be numbered and referenced in the algorithm table to better illustrate the execution (Reviewer 5).
- **Minor presentation issues:**
  - Acronyms NMI and ARI should be defined in the text (Reviewer 4).
  - Typos and formatting: L311 “Agg” abbreviation never used; “Appendix Appendix” repeated; L730 says aggregation function but equation is score function; “possiblle”; British English “modelled” vs. American elsewhere; “Deepect” misspelled; incomplete references (e.g., Bengio 2014 lacks publication details); arXiv versions cited where peer-reviewed versions exist; missing DOIs/links; mojibake in Nguyen 2024 reference (Reviewer 1).
  - Figure 2: spacing inconsistency (space between woman and otter but not between palm tree and sunflower) for pairs equally far apart (Reviewer 1). “datapoints” should be “data points” (Reviewer 4).
- **Reviewer disagreement:** Two reviewers (2 and 5) gave score 5 and find the method effective and simple, but they still raise concerns about fair comparison, missing ablations, and clarity. Their positive view is reflected in the Strengths; the Weaknesses above include the issues they also identified.
