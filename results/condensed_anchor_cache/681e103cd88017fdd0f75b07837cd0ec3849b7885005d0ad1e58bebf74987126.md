## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses a limitation of DPO/IPO: they treat all preference pairs equally, ignoring relative quality gaps between chosen and rejected responses. The authors propose a self-refinement function that uses a prompt-augmented query (e.g., “Please generate a response with a usefulness rating of 100 out of 100…”) to obtain a quality score from the same LLM, then modifies the DPO/IPO loss by adding a refinement term (with weight λ, gradients not propagated) that reweights pairs based on the estimated quality difference. The approach is tested on Zephyr models and evaluated on MT-Bench, Vicuna Bench, and Open LLM Leaderboard, showing consistent improvements over vanilla DPO/IPO. The method increases computational cost to two forward passes per input.

### Strengths
- Simple modification of DPO/IPO: only a minor change in the input prompt and an added refinement term. (Reviewer 1)
- Novel use of the LLM’s intrinsic knowledge to refine the loss function, contributing to model alignment. (Reviewer 2)
- Extensive experimental results across multiple datasets (MT-Bench, Vicuna Bench, Open LLM Leaderboard) demonstrating effectiveness. (Reviewer 2)
- Includes theoretical analysis that supports the design of the refinement function. (Reviewer 2)
- Well-written and easy to follow. (Reviewer 3)
- Refinement function is compatible with existing optimization frameworks (DPO, IPO), facilitating adoption. (Reviewer 3)
- Performance improvements over traditional DPO and IPO are observed, indicating potential for future RLHF work. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of logical consistency between claimed mechanism and actual implementation.** The abstract and introduction emphasize using the LLM’s intrinsic knowledge for self-refinement, but the method section merely adds an extra prompt and defines a Δ objective–this does not convincingly demonstrate how intrinsic knowledge is leveraged. (Reviewers 1 and 3; Reviewer 1 also notes the presentation is vague until Section 3)
- **Lack of novelty.** Introducing a gap value via a prompted query is not very novel; the Δ objective closely resembles the standard DPO/IPO objective. The approach heavily relies on prompt choice, yet the relative analysis of prompt influence is insufficient. (Reviewers 2 and 3; Reviewer 3 explicitly states “lack of innovation”)
- **Unintuitive prompt design and questionable mechanism.** It is unclear why adding a numeric rating prompt would align with an oracle reward margin; LMs are known to be poor at assigning numerical feedback. Results in Table 4 further show that prompt wording has little effect, raising questions about the source of improvement. (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 2 also argues prompt choice has large influence but analysis is lacking)
- **One reviewer (score 3) strongly suspects DPO baseline is not properly hyperparameter-tuned.** The naive variation of the refinement operator (Table 4) effectively reduces β and still outperforms DPO, suggesting the baseline may have been suboptimal; this casts doubt on the claimed insights. (Reviewer 1, minority more critical reviewer)
- **Insufficient baselines.** Many prior DPO improvements introduce a gap between positive and negative samples; these should be compared. (Reviewer 2)
- **Tested only on Zephyr models.** Generalizability to other large models (e.g., Llama3.1-8b) is not shown. (Reviewer 2; also asked in questions: “Is Sr‑IPO effective on other LLMs?”)
- **Limited analysis of the adaptive β idea.** Could a similar gap-adjustment be applied to the coefficient β (making it adapt based on the preference pair)? The authors do not explore this or provide theoretical justification. (Reviewer 2 question, overlaps with missing analysis)
- **Scope of integration not explored.** The paper does not test whether the self-refinement method can be combined with other RLHF approaches beyond DPO/IPO. (Reviewer 3 question, overlaps with missing experiment)
