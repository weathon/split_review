## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers agree that the paper addresses an important limitation of dataset distillation by scaling trajectory-matching to large synthetic sets. The difficulty-aligned trajectory matching idea is praised as intuitive and insightful, and the experimental results—especially achieving lossless or better-than-full-dataset performance at IPCs of 500 and 1000—are considered significant contributions. However, reviewers diverge on the novelty and the strength of the “lossless” claim. Two reviewers (R2, R4) are very positive (score 8) and highlight the method’s impact, while two others (R1, R3) are more measured (score 6), citing incremental algorithmic advances and concerns about overclaiming. Several specific criticisms and requests for additional experiments are raised.

### Strengths
- **Well-motivated observation:** The paper’s key insight—that early trajectories encode easy patterns and late trajectories encode hard patterns, and that aligning difficulty with synthetic dataset size improves performance—is novel and clearly explained. (R1, R2, R4)
- **First to demonstrate synthetic data outperforming the full real dataset (R1) / first to achieve lossless distillation on three datasets at large IPC (IPC=500,1000) (R4).** (Note: R3 contests the “lossless” label, see Weaknesses.)
- **Solves a concrete problem:** Previous methods degrade to random baseline at large IPC (e.g., 500); the proposed method is the first to maintain and even exceed full-dataset accuracy under these settings. (R4)
- **Extensive experiments and analysis:** The paper includes thorough studies, comparisons, and insights into Matching Training Trajectories (MTT). (R1, R4)
- **Well-written and reproducible:** The paper is clearly structured, implementation details are provided, and the logic is easy to follow. (R2, R4)
- **Strong cross-architecture performance:** The method achieves state-of-the-art results in cross-architecture evaluations (R4); however, R3 notes that performance across architectures remains relatively low (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited algorithmic novelty:** The method is an extension of MTT with manually set bounds for trajectory matching; the core changes are described as incremental (R1, R2 Q1, R3 strength #1 is positive but R1 notes novelty is fair only).  
- **Cross-architecture generalization needs more thorough comparison:** Table 4 only includes simple baselines; reviewers request comparisons with other dataset distillation algorithms (R1) and more extensive cross-architecture experiments (R3). One reviewer (R2) also notes an apparent inconsistency between Tables 2 and 2a regarding performance of complex vs. 3-layer ConvNets, and asks for results across architectures as IPC increases.  
- **Lossless claim is contested:** R3 argues that “lossless” is not formally defined—what metric exactly matches?—and questions why synthetic data can surpass full-dataset accuracy (possibly regularization). R3 considers the claim overclaimed. In contrast, R4 supports the lossless label and reports that the method is the first to achieve it. This disagreement should be resolved with clearer definition and justification.  
- **Downstream tasks not evaluated:** Only image classification is tested; reviewers ask for performance on tasks like object detection (R2 Q3).  
- **Soft label learning details require clarification:**  
  - Table 1 does not indicate which DATM results use label learning and which do not. Could DATM with soft labels harm MTT performance at small IPC, and is the boost at large IPC merely from the label learning? (R4 W1)  
  - The explanation for initializing soft labels with pre-trained logits is confusing, as the claim that “experts are trained with one-hot labels” would suggest a one-hot-like initialization is better; the reasoning needs clarification. (R4 W4)  
- **Training cost information is incomplete:** The paper mentions a 1.05× cost increase for CIFAR-10, but does not specify whether this applies to all settings, nor does it report GPU type, number of GPUs, or training cost for other datasets. (R4 W2, W3)  
- **Missing ablation on trajectory range:** The method only increases the upper bound \(T^{+}\); the reviewers ask whether increasing the lower bound \(T^{-}\) was also tried and what impact it would have. (R4 W5)  
- **Numeric detail needed on resource use:** See above; also R4 asks for explicit reporting in Section 4.1 or appendix.
