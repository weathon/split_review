## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces EvidenceBench, a benchmark for extracting evidence relevant to hypotheses from biomedical papers. The authors propose a scalable annotation pipeline that uses systematic review monographs, LLMs (GPT4-0125, GPT4-o-mini, Claude3-Opus) and human validation to generate sentence-level evidence annotations. They construct EvidenceBench (400+ papers) and EvidenceBench-100k (107,461 fully annotated papers). Experiments evaluate LLMs, embedding models (including fine-tuned E5-v2, Llama3-8B on EvidenceBench-100k), and different prompting strategies. GPT-4o is the best LLM; embedding models underperform due to lack of context awareness. Fine-tuned models improve on result evidence retrieval but trail larger models. Human experts validated study aspects and sentence annotations for a subset. The task and benchmark are useful for systematic review automation.

### Strengths
1. The task of identifying evidence supporting or against a hypothesis is important and practical; the authors evaluate existing approaches on this task.
2. The original EvidenceBench provides a useful resource for benchmarking LLMs for evidence retrieval; fine-tuning on EvidenceBench-100k improves model performance (E5-v2, Llama3-8B) on result evidence retrieval.
3. The paper is well-structured and easy to follow (though some presentation can be improved). Detailed descriptions and clear figures illustrate the problem and pipeline.
4. The pipeline innovatively uses LLMs and existing survey monographs to scale benchmark construction; multiple LLMs (GPT4-0125, GPT4-o-mini, Claude3-Opus) are used at different stages.
5. Comprehensive experiments with both open-source and closed-source LLMs, plus a small language model; human experts validate study aspects generation and sentence annotation, enhancing trustworthiness.
6. Construction costs are reduced (claimed 3 hours vs. >3,000 human hours) using the LLM pipeline, and large-scale datasets are built with modest budget.

### Weaknesses
1. **Writing clarity and justification**: The paper is often vague and hard to read (Reviewer 1). Examples: Introduction paragraph 2’s distinction of three stages is not justified; “faithful to these judgments” is unclear; “scalable” and “sufficient number of examples” are not clearly defined. Several descriptions need revision. A typo: Line 509 “Figure 3” should be “Table 6”.
2. **Pipeline validation is insufficient**: Only 50 papers checked by experts, which is not persuasive enough for overall quality (Reviewer 1). The claim that pipeline reduces construction time from >3,000 human hours to 3 hours is unsubstantiated; no evidence how the 3 hours was computed, and no explanation why different LLMs were used at different creation stages (GPT4-0125, GPT4-o-mini, Claude3-Opus) (Reviewer 3).
3. **Missing ablations and topic analysis**: No ablation study on how topics influence study aspect and hypothesis extraction. Experiments use a randomly selected subset (only 300 data points for LLM evaluation) without considering topic distribution; this is a very small portion of EvidenceBench-100k (Reviewer 3).
4. **Error propagation concerns**: Summary extraction from study monographs is LLM-driven without human inspection; summaries feed into hypothesis recovery and aspect decomposition. Although aspect decomposition is human-checked, summary extraction is not validated, risking error propagation (Reviewer 3).
5. **Confusing concept of “study aspect”**: Unclear how the evaluation considers it—e.g., if a sentence is retrieved for the wrong aspect or multiple aspects (Reviewer 2). Also unclear if sentences classified as counter-evidence for one hypothesis might support an alternative hypothesis; no experiments or human validations address this categorization (Reviewer 3).
6. **Limited realism and scope**: The task provides the hypothesis and a candidate pool—does not consider real-world scenarios of hypothesis paraphrasing sensitivity or retrieval from a large paper pool (e.g., entire PubMed) (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4). Hypotheses are taken from survey monographs, which may differ from naturally occurring hypotheses; no evaluation of suitability for evidence retrieval. What happens for hypotheses with no evidence? (Reviewer 4).
7. **Exclusion of figures and tables**: These may be highly relevant evidence but are excluded from annotation (Reviewer 2).
8. **Lack of scientific insight from experiments**: The paper focuses heavily on benchmark construction but provides few insights or lessons learned from the experimental results (Reviewer 1).
9. **Unclear difference between EvidenceBench and EvidenceBench-100k**: It appears one includes human validation and the other does not, but this is not explicitly stated (Reviewer 2).
10. **No manual analysis of results**: Despite claim of immediate value to scientists (desideratum), no manual analyses are provided to demonstrate how the results benefit scientists (Reviewer 4).
