## Merged Review

### Summary  
The paper proposes Federated Generative Learning (FGL), where clients send prompts (generated by a captioning model) to a server, which uses a generative foundation model (Stable Diffusion) to synthesize training data for global model training. This aims to reduce communication cost and improve privacy. Reviewers generally find the idea novel and the experiments well-executed, but raise significant concerns about the underlying assumptions and evaluation scope. One reviewer (score 6, confidence 3) was more positive, citing the novelty and communication/privacy benefits, while three reviewers (score 5, confidence 4) were more critical, focusing on the limited domain coverage, lack of scalability, missing baselines, and unresolved privacy threats.

### Strengths  
- Novel framework that transmits prompts instead of model parameters/gradients, addressing high communication cost and privacy leakage (all reviewers).  
- Leverages foundation generative models to mitigate client data heterogeneity and client‑shift (R3).  
- Extensive experiments on four datasets (ImageNette, ImageFruit, ImageNet100, DomainNet) with diverse settings, visualizations, and thorough privacy analysis (membership inference attacks, memorization) (R2, R3, R4).  
- Compelling performance: e.g., FGL with one round outperforms FedAvg’s 200 rounds by 12% on skewed ImageNet100; with five rounds exceeds FedAvg by 30% (R4).  
- Well-written and easy to follow (R2).

### Weaknesses  
1. **Dependency on foundation model performance and domain alignment** – FGL’s success hinges on the quality and congruence of the captioning model (BLIPv2) and generative model (Stable Diffusion). Any bias or limitation in these models can produce proxy data that diverges from the private data, especially under high client heterogeneity (R1, R3, R4).  
2. **Limited evaluation domains** – Experiments use only natural image datasets (ImageNet subsets, DomainNet) that are likely well represented in the foundation models’ pre‑training data. The method is not tested on non‑natural, privacy‑sensitive domains such as medical imaging, remote sensing, or fine‑grained classification (CUB‑200, Cars) where foundation models may not cover the distribution (R1, R2, R3). One reviewer questions whether prompts can capture complexity in such domains (R4).  
3. **Impractical client‑side computation** – Running a large visual captioning model (BLIPv2) on each client is often infeasible given the limited memory and compute capacity typical in federated learning (R1).  
4. **Small‑scale experimental setup** – Only 5 clients are used. Scaling to 50–100 clients is necessary to assess real‑world challenges like increased heterogeneity, communication overhead, and client dropout (R3).  
5. **Insufficient baselines** – Only FedAvg is compared; advanced federated learning methods (e.g., addressing client drift, personalization) and other generative FL approaches are missing. This limits the demonstration of FGL’s superiority (R3, R4).  
6. **Counterintuitive outperformance of centralized learning** – Table 2 shows FGL exceeding centralized training; this requires a detailed explanation to rule out experimental artifacts or confounding factors (R3).  
7. **Unclear privacy threat model** – The paper does not clearly define what “client privacy” means. If the data can already be generated by public models, or if prompts themselves leak high‑level information (e.g., a user having many golf photos enables targeted ads), then privacy may not be meaningfully protected. Side‑channel attacks via prompts are not addressed (R2).  
8. **Reverse‑engineering of prompts** – The risk that prompts could be used to reconstruct private data is not analyzed (R4).  
9. **Robustness to data heterogeneity not justified** – The claim of robustness is questionable because heterogeneity can still surface in instance‑level prompts and the generated images (R2).  
10. **Ablation on foundation models needed** – The impact of varying the captioning or generative model is not studied (R1).  
11. **Overstated necessity of class‑level prompts** – Since the server often knows the label space, it could generate class‑conditioned images without prompt transmission; the paper overstates the value of this component (R2).  
12. **Limited to image data** – The framework relies on captioning and generative models specific to images; it is unclear how it would extend to other modalities (time‑series, tabular data) (R3).  
13. **Non‑IID global distribution congruence not addressed** – The method does not evaluate or discuss alignment of the aggregated proxy data with the true global distribution under non‑IID scenarios (R4).
