## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a systematic framework for designing regression losses in GFlowNet training by connecting each loss to a specific divergence measure. It identifies two key properties—zero-forcing (exploitation) and zero-avoiding (exploration)—and proposes three new loss functions (Shifted-Cosh, Linex(1/2), Linex(1)) that go beyond the common squared loss. The losses are evaluated on hyper-grid, bit-sequence generation, and molecule generation benchmarks, showing improvements in convergence speed, diversity, quality, and robustness. The work generalizes existing GFlowNet objectives and provides theoretical insight into how loss functions affect gradient behavior.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- Establishes a novel theoretical connection between regression losses in GFlowNet training and specific f-divergence measures, enabling targeted design of training objectives.
- Derives the impact of loss functions on the gradient and defines zero-forcing (encouraging exploitation) and zero-avoiding (encouraging exploration) as two key properties.
- Proposes three new losses (Shifted-Cosh, Linex(1/2), Linex(1)) that complement the existing squared loss and cover all four combinations of zero-forcing/zero-avoiding.
- Shows that Linex(1) corresponds to KL divergence.
- Extensive experiments on three diverse benchmarks (hyper-grid, bit-sequence, molecule generation) demonstrate practical benefits:
  - Non-zero-forcing losses (Linex(1) and Linex(0.5)) converge faster on hyper-grid.
  - Linex(1) obtains all modes almost always the fastest; Spearman correlation between train and test is highest for Shifted-Cosh on bit-sequence.
  - Linex(1) increases diversity while quadratic and Shifted-Cosh yield higher average rewards on molecule generation.
- The framework is compatible with most existing training algorithms (e.g., off-policy methods like local search and genetic search).
- Valuable categorization of prior GFlowNet works.

### Weaknesses
- Minority reviewer (R1, score 6, confidence 4) is more critical than the other two (R2, R3, score 8, confidence 3): R1 notes limited novelty (similar attempts exist in reinforcement learning and generative models) and lack of direct theoretical comparison with existing GFlowNet training algorithms. (Majority reviewers R2,R3 find the theory insightful.)
- Empirical evaluation is limited to synthetic tasks (hyper-grid, bit-sequence) and a single real-world benchmark (molecule generation); reviewer R2 notes this as a weakness.
- The paper explores only a limited set of loss functions; several reviewers suggest studying more losses from f-divergences (e.g., Reverse-KL, JSD, Hellinger, etc.) for a more comprehensive understanding of divergence properties on training.
- There may be more key properties beyond zero-forcing and zero-avoiding; studying additional losses would clarify them.
- Missing discussion of hybrid/annealing approaches, e.g., using Linex(1) for fast convergence to many modes, then transitioning to Shifted-Cosh for higher rewards around discovered modes.
- No direct comparison with off-policy exploration methods; it is unclear whether the proposed loss functions are orthogonal or complementary to techniques like local search or genetic search (e.g., Hyeonah Kim et al., NeurIPS 2024).
- Lack of analysis on hyperparameter sensitivity and stability in higher-dimensional or more complex tasks; reviewer R1 asks if stability shifts occur and whether adjustments can bolster robustness.
- No theoretical convergence guarantees or analysis of convergence behavior for the new losses.
- Reviewer R1 questions the rationale for omitting other f-divergences and asks for explicit reasons for the limited loss set.
- Missing literature connection with f-GAN, which also uses f-divergence in generative models.
- The paper feels like it only scratches the surface; reviewer R3 suggests adding more loss functions and hybrid-annealing experiments would significantly strengthen the work.
