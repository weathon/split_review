- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 6, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a neuro-symbolic framework that approximates arbitrary monadic conjunctive queries (CQs) on incomplete knowledge graphs by a family of tree-like queries, then uses existing tree-like query processors (e.g., GNN-QE). The approximation is claimed to be complete and optimal. Reviewers generally find the idea interesting and the paper clearly written, but raise major concerns about theoretical novelty, insufficient justification, incomplete definitions, weak experimental validation, and conceptual flaws in handling probabilistic uncertainty. One reviewer (score 6) is notably more positive than the other four (scores 3, 3, 3, 3), praising the work as the first to tackle cyclic queries and calling it intuitive and simple.

### Strengths

- The problem of answering logical queries over incomplete KGs is timely and relevant to ICLR (Reviewer 5).
- The idea of approximating a cyclic CQ by a family of tree-like CQs is interesting and intuitive (Reviewers 2, 3, 5).
- The paper is, for the most part, well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 3, 5).
- It is the first to tackle cyclic queries on incomplete KGs in a neuro-symbolic setting (Reviewer 3).
- Once approximated, the task can be delegated to any state-of-the-art neuro-symbolic query processor (Reviewer 3).
- The approximation is guaranteed to be complete and optimal for a given computational budget (Reviewer 3).
- Some experimental results on FB15K, FB15k-237, and NELL995 are reported to outperform state-of-the-art (Reviewer 4 summary; Reviewer 3 positive).
- The main formal claims appear correct (Reviewer 5). Reviewer 1 accepts the approach works for the certainty case.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited theoretical novelty**: The core technical contribution (C1) – unravelling cyclic queries to tree-shaped ones – is a conventional technique in database theory/knowledge representation and may be considered straightforward observations (Reviewers 2, 5). The notions of containment and homomorphism are essentially substitution as known from logic programming/ILP (Reviewer 2). The completeness property corresponds to clause substitution (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear contribution and scope**: The paper does not clearly define the specific class of conjunctive queries addressed, making it hard to assess scope and limitations (Reviewer 4). The notion of “approximating” a query is not rigorously defined (Reviewer 4). Contribution (C2) – extending Zhu et al.’s method to unanchored queries – is insufficiently elaborated and it is unclear how it generalizes to other methods (Reviewer 5). It is also uncertain whether the extension can handle arbitrary tree-shaped CQs (Reviewer 5). The paper claims a “neuro-symbolic framework” but appears to be a neural network trained to predict results without clear symbolic reasoning integration (Reviewer 4).
- **Critical conceptual flaw in uncertainty handling**: The paper assumes that the probability of a disjunction equals the maximum probability of its components, which is invalid for probabilistic reasoning. For example, the probability that two of an infinite number of friends are coworkers approaches 1, but the proposed treatment would not capture this. The method treats a learned KG as a standard deterministic KG rather than as probabilistic predictions (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing definitions and justification**: Several key concepts are undefined or poorly defined: “MRR of a set” (Mean Reciprocal Rank) – how are sets ranked? What is the ground truth when multiple witnesses exist for a single answer? (Reviewer 1). Spearman rank correlation is used for the total number of answers, but Spearman measures rank difference – why is it appropriate here? (Reviewer 1). Query types (1p/2p/3p/2i/3i/2in/3in/inp/pni/pin) are not explained (Reviewer 1). The claim that “working with vectors implies unary” is false; there is work on higher-order relations (Reviewer 1).
- **Poor experimental validation**: The MRRs on FB15k-237 are particularly low, suggesting the method may not work well (Reviewer 1). The method performs worse, on average, than the baseline on anchored tree-like queries (Reviewer 3). The baseline comparisons are confusing: GNN-QE and its extension are trained on different datasets, so the significance of results is unclear (Reviewer 5). The experiments may include queries involving disjunction and negation, which are not covered by the paper’s technical results (Reviewer 5). Only two benchmark datasets (FB15K, FB15k-237) are considered, which is insufficient to support the claimed contributions (Reviewer 4). The experiments should be extended to include other approaches (Reviewer 2). The contribution of the proposed approach is not clear from the experiments (Reviewer 2).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: The depth parameter \(d\) for unravelling is crucial but no clear methodology for choosing it is provided; the paper relies on empirical fitting that may not generalize (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation issues**: The presentation is poor, preventing understanding of the content (Reviewer 4). Motivation and research question are not clear (Reviewer 4). The paper lacks discussion of related work in statistical relational learning and ILP, and does not discuss the open-world assumption (Reviewer 2). Valid path restrictions seem very restrictive – it is unclear if the method can answer all useful queries on KGs (e.g., with reified relations) (Reviewer 1).
- **Incorrect statements**: Page 3 “y and z are both existentially quantified” is incorrect; they are universally quantified at the scope of the rule and existentially quantified in the scope of the body (Reviewer 1). The claim that “the number of approximations is infinite” is questionable – if the graph is finite, the model is finite, though possibly exponential; the complexity should be given (Reviewer 1).
- **Applicability concerns**: It is not clear how the method handles queries with disjunction and negation (Reviewer 5). It is also unclear whether methods other than Zhu et al. can be easily extended to support CQs (Reviewer 5). Reviewer 4 asks if the method would work for complete KGs, and what happens if a relation is self-reflective.
