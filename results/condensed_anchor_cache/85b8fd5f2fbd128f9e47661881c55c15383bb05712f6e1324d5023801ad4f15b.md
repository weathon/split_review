## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies zeroth-order (ZO) optimization for finite-sum problems, comparing ZO-SGD-Gauss (ZSG), which preprocesses gradients with a Gaussian vector, and ZO-SGD-Coordinate (ZSC), which estimates partial derivatives along coordinate directions. It introduces a “quadratic regularity assumption” on the Hessian matrix to incorporate second-order information into complexity analysis. The authors claim that ZSG outperforms ZSC because its convergence rate scales with the trace of the Hessian (tr(M)) while ZSC’s scales with d times the maximum eigenvalue (dλ_max(M)). Theoretical convergence results are provided for quadratic and more general objectives, supported by experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets.

### Strengths
- Addresses the important problem of providing a separation result between two competing zeroth-order algorithms (Reviewer 1).
- Empirical validation on synthetic and real-world datasets reinforces theoretical claims; experimental results are presented clearly and support the claims about convergence rates and query complexity (Reviewer 2).
- The study of zeroth-order optimization is a trendy and important topic with applications in black-box attacks, reinforcement learning, and fine-tuning language models (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is well-written with clear research motivation; zeroth-order optimization is crucial when privacy is essential or gradient computation is impractical; theoretical findings are interesting (Reviewer 4, who gave a rating of 5 — the only positive outlier among three ratings of 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of rigorous lower bound for ZSC**: The paper claims ZSG is better because it converges with tr(M) while ZSC converges with dλ(M), but it does not provide a lower bound showing ZSC cannot achieve the tr(M) rate. Without such a proof, the comparison is mathematically incomplete. Reviewer 1 notes that the argument relies on the intuition that tr(M) ≪ dλ_max(M) in practice, which is insufficient justification without formal conditions. Reviewer 3 specifically points out that previous works ([Hanzely et al., 2018], [Wang et al., 2024]) give ZSC a rate of tr(M) under the same quadratic regularity assumption, contradicting the paper’s claim.
- **Incremental contribution and limited novelty**: Similar separation results exist in the deterministic setting; extension to the stochastic finite-sum setting is incremental and does not introduce new theoretical tools or insights (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 adds that the rate of zeroth-order methods under similar Hessian-related assumptions has been extensively studied ([Malladi et al., 2023], [Yue et al., 2023], arXiv:2310.09639), including stochastic mini-batch settings, so the novelty and technical challenge of the current results are unclear.
- **Unclear generality beyond quadratic objectives**: For general functions, the factor γ_u/γ_l² in eq. (23) can be uncontrollable compared to the advantage from Hessian skewness; the improvement over ZSC is not clearly demonstrated. The paper should discuss the trade-off and pinpoint cases where improvement is meaningful (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing analysis of stochasticity’s effect**: The analysis lacks a clear explanation of how stochasticity affects the separation result; it mostly extends deterministic results without novel analysis of the stochastic component (Reviewer 1).
- **Writing, presentation, and code availability issues**:
  - The introduction is poorly designed; a table showing results and efficiency comparisons would improve clarity (Reviewer 2).
  - No link to GitHub or another source for the experimental code is provided (Reviewer 2).
  - Multiple grammar mistakes: e.g., line 144 (“smoothness assumption and strong convexity assumption”), lines 162–163 (poor English), Theorem 4.3 and 4.5 should read “let objective be quadratic” and “let x be update” (Reviewer 3).
  - Conditions (2) and (3) need clearer quantifiers (e.g., “exists z” vs. “for all z”) and more discussion of how strong they are compared to L-smoothness and strong convexity, beyond citing [Frangella et al., 2023] (Reviewer 4).
  - Lines 172–173 (why certain parameters are independent of the condition number) require more explanation (Reviewer 4).
- **Technical flaws in convergence analysis**:
  - In Corollary 4.4, the claim that the algorithm does not converge with a fixed stepsize is incorrect; one can choose η = (log T)/T and obtain a rate. Clarify what is meant by “fixed” step (Reviewer 3).
  - In Corollary 4.6, when σ = 0, the complexity should reduce to the deterministic linear rate log(1/ε). Is the current analysis tight? (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing discussion of high-dimensional variance**: The paper does not address limitations of zeroth-order methods in high-dimensional spaces, such as increased variance of gradient estimates that could negate the benefits of improved complexity (Reviewer 1).
- **Questions about the alpha term and unignored factors**: Does ZSC have an “alpha term” similar to the one ignored from ZSG’s complexity? Does that affect the comparison? (Reviewer 4)
