## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviewers acknowledge that AGUVIS presents a pure-vision GUI agent framework with promising performance across multiple platforms, built by curating existing datasets and training Qwen2-VL in two stages (grounding then planning). They find the motivation reasonable and the organized datasets valuable. However, they raise major concerns about insufficient novelty (primarily a data-combination effort with no new datasets or module designs), missing ablation studies that would isolate contributions of the unified action space, dataset combination, and reasoning steps, unfair comparisons against baselines without similar UI pretraining, lack of detail in data generation (which VLM, quality assurance), and absence of qualitative examples or planning trajectory visualizations. Recurring requests include clearer evidence for each design choice (training staging, inner monologue, grounding-packing acceleration, self-plan setting) and fairer experimental controls.

### Strengths
- Extensive evaluations across multiple digital platforms (ScreenSpot, Multimodal Mind2Web, Android Control, etc.).
- Promising performance, often surpassing recent state-of-the-art methods.
- Interesting use of PyAutoGUI to unify the action space across mobile, web, and desktop.
- Reasonable motivation for integrating planning with action grounding, overcoming planning-then-grounding limitations.
- Valuable curated dataset collection with novel augmentation strategies.
- Planned open-source release of data, models, and training recipes.
- Clear writing and easy-to-follow structure.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty**: The main contribution is pre-training a VLM with massive combinations of existing datasets; no new datasets are introduced and no novel module designs are presented. This is computationally intensive and lacks deep insight.
2. **Insufficient detail on data curation**:
   - Which specific VLM was used to generate the inner monologue and low-level reasoning steps (observation descriptions, thoughts, actions)? Accuracy / quality measures for these generated components are absent.
   - The prompt used for generation is not shown.
   - No examples of the Planning & Reasoning Trajectories are provided, making it impossible to assess how they differ from existing trajectories.
3. **Missing ablation studies** that would justify key design choices:
   - No experiment isolating the effect of the dataset combination (Tables 9/10) to show synergistic benefits across domains rather than simply more training data. A comparison of training on combined vs. each domain separately is missing.
   - No ablation comparing the proposed unified PyAutoGUI action space against using specialized action spaces per device type (e.g., web, mobile, desktop).
   - No evidence that the grounding-packing strategy accelerates training (claimed L248) or that robustness assumptions (L250) hold.
   - No experiment demonstrating that the VLM-generated inner monologue (reasoning steps) contributes to performance; the degradation without Stage 2 could be due to more training data, not specifically the reasoning steps.
   - No experiment testing single-stage training versus the two-stage paradigm.
   - No ablation isolating low-level instruction training (e.g., train without low-level instructions and compare on ScreenSpot).
4. **Unfair comparisons**: In Table 1, AGUVIS (fine-tuned on Qwen2-VL, a strong VLM pretrained on massive UI data) is compared to UGround and SeeClick, which use base VLMs without similar UI pretraining. This confounds the effect of the method with the stronger backbone. The paper should compare with models of comparable base capacity and pretraining.
5. **Lack of qualitative analysis and visualizations**:
   - No visual examples showing AGUVIS’s interaction with UI elements or its outputs, making it hard to understand differences from prior methods.
   - The “self-plan” setting on ScreenSpot is unclear: how the model generates plans from instructions, what prompt is used, and why it outperforms the original instructions setting. Analysis of this surprising behavior (possible overfitting? performance after Stage 1 alone?) is needed.
6. **Numeric/implementation gaps**:
   - Table 6 shows little impact of Stage 1 on Mind2Web but Table 1 shows improvement on ScreenSpot – explanation required.
   - Reason for model size choice (Grounder 7B, e2e 70B) is not justified.
   - Training details missing: schedule per stage, how inputs (observations, thoughts, action/observation histories) are organized, truncation strategies, frozen modules, GPU hours.
   - Some inconsistencies: “AGUVIS” vs “AUGVIS” typo; slight performance differences for AGUVIS-7B between Table 2 and Table 6.
7. **Other missing experiments and clarifications**:
   - Performance with only Stage 2 training on offline planning benchmarks (not just ScreenSpot).
   - How AGUVIS generalizes to real-world scenarios beyond evaluated benchmarks (e.g., closing ads).
   - Whether AGUVIS is compatible with other LLMs / base models.
   - Clarify the update of “its belief state b_t” (L123).
   - For Stage 1 grounding-packing, what attention mask is used (causal vs. custom to prevent cross-sample attention)?
   - Why not report Step SR for CogAgent in Table 2?
   - Source of statistics (L152: accessibility tree 6k tokens, HTML 4k tokens).
   - Should there be specific designs when deploying for phone vs. web?

*Disagreement among reviewers:* While Reviewers 2 and 4 are more positive about performance and motivation, Reviewer 3 strongly emphasizes the lack of novelty and the heavy dependence on existing data. All reviewers converge on the need for missing ablations, fairness in comparisons, and more concrete details on data generation and training.
