## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper analyzes generalization error of gradient descent under label noise in a binary classification setting where inputs are split into a label-dependent signal patch and a label-independent noise patch (following prior work by Kou et al. 2023). Using a one-hidden-layer CNN with ReLU activation applied separately to each patch, the authors characterize two-stage dynamics: in Stage I the model fits clean samples while ignoring noisy ones; in Stage II the gradient from noise surpasses that of the signal, leading to overfitting on noisy samples. The results motivate early stopping and sample selection. Experiments on synthetic and real data support the theory.

### Strengths
- Reasonably well-written theory and experiments; the paper is organized and mostly easy to follow.
- Theoretical results are generally sound and match empirical observations.
- Contributes to theoretical understanding of learning with label noise; extends allowable label noise probability (up to 1/2) compared to Kou et al. (2023).
- Supports the common intuitive heuristics of early stopping and outlier identification.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty:** The analysis largely follows the framework of Kou et al. (2023) with modifications to include label noise; it is not clear the extra contribution over prior work (Cao et al., Kou et al.) is significant enough. [R1, R2, R3]
- **Unrealistic setting:** The data distribution assumes inputs are perfectly split into known signal and noise patches, and the model is designed with this knowledge a priori. This is extremely simplistic, effectively reduces to linearly separable data with label noise, and seems artificial. The paper does not justify why such a complex model is needed for this setting. [R1, R3]
- **Technical confusion about dynamics:** The definitions of \(\overline \rho\) and \(\underline \rho\) appear symmetric with flipped signs, yet the paper (lines 230–233) states that \(\overline \rho\) keeps increasing while \(\underline \rho\) is bounded below. This is confusing and potentially an error; the reasoning why \(w_{j,r}\) eventually has only positive components along noise points (i.e., why \(\overline \rho\) becomes larger than \(\underline \rho\)) is not clearly justified. [R1]
- **Insufficient justification of SNR constraint change:** Compared to Kou et al. (2023), the paper imposes a new condition \(n \cdot \text{SNR}^2 = \Theta(1)\). It is unclear whether this merely requires higher signal to counterbalance higher label noise, rather than a genuine extension. A more granular comparison (e.g., tables like Appendix A of Kou et al.) and justification of each change are needed. [R2]
- **Proof differences not well exposed:** The key difference in proofs is handling the new SNR constraint; more exposition on how the new proof differs from Kou et al. (2023) is needed. Additionally, the claim that “signal coefficients are on the same order as noise coefficients” suggests a different setting, not an extension. [R2]
- **Takeaway limited by asymptotic theory:** The main practical takeaway is early stopping, but the theory is asymptotic; further experiments on early stopping and its relation to the two-stage dynamics would be useful. [R2]
- **Experimental presentation issues:** Figures 1 and 2 are hard to read (too many curves); they should be broken up (e.g., separate train/test in Figure 2) or supplemented with more textual exposition. Figure 2 only evaluates one label noise parameter; the phenomena should be verified for a wider range of label noise parameters to match the theory. [R2]
- **Class-conditional noise not fully analyzed:** The paper claims the analysis applies to class-conditional noise, but the results and quantities seem symmetric in \(\tau_+, \tau_-\). The authors should clarify whether this is intended and how such noise leads to different learning behaviors. [R3]
- **Restricted to binary classification:** The analysis only covers binary classification, where noise patterns are simpler (positive only flips to negative, etc.). The paper should discuss whether the framework can be generalized to multi-class classification. [R3]
- **No discussion of sample-dependent noise:** Whether the analysis applies when the noisy label \(\tilde{y}_i\) depends on the sample noise \(\xi\) (sample-dependent noise) is not addressed; a discussion on this direction is needed. [R3]
