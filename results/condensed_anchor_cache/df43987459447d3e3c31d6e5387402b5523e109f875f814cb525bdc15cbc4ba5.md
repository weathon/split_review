## Merged Review

### Summary
All four reviewers agree that DiffStateGrad is a straightforward, plug-and-play module that improves diffusion-based inverse problem solvers by projecting measurement gradients onto a low-rank approximation of the intermediate diffusion state. Three reviewers (R1, R2, R3) rated the paper strongly (8), praising its clarity, intuitive justification, and convincing experimental results across linear and nonlinear inverse problems with latent diffusion models. One reviewer (R4) gave a low score (3), raising fundamental concerns about the novelty relative to MPGD and DDS, the theoretical justification, and the lack of pixel-domain experiments. R4 acknowledges the method is easy to implement and consistently improves performance, but finds the comparisons and theoretical claims insufficiently supported. All reviewers note the need for additional baselines, more thorough comparison with related projection methods, and clarification of experimental details (noise levels, initialization strategies). Computational cost for pixel-space diffusion models is identified as a potential drawback by R1 and R3.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and exposition**: The paper is well written, easy to understand, and contextualized among related work (R1, R2, R3).
- **Plug-and-play nature**: DiffStateGrad can be added to any existing diffusion-based posterior sampler without retraining (R1, R2, R3). R4 also notes it is straightforward to implement and improves performance in all three tested frameworks (PSLD, ReSample, DAPS).
- **Novel idea of projecting onto the manifold of intermediate noise** \(\mathcal{M}_t\) to suppress artifacts, which is intuitive and makes sense (R1, R2, R3). 
- **Robustness improvements**: The module increases robustness to step size and measurement noise, demonstrated across a diverse set of forward models: box inpainting, random inpainting, Gaussian deblur, motion deblur (linear) and phase retrieval, nonlinear deblur, HDR (nonlinear) on ImageNet and FFHQ (R2). R2 and R3 find the experiments convincing and extensive.
- **Negligible additional cost**: The extra computation for SVD and gradient projection is marginal for latent diffusion models (R2, based on Figure 4). R1 and R3, however, express concern that this cost may be prohibitive for pixel-space models (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient comparison with closely related projection methods**: Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R4) stress that DiffStateGrad should be directly compared to MCG, DreamClean, and especially MPGD [1] and DDS [2]. R1 notes DreamClean and MCG also project gradients onto the prior manifold; R2 calls for an in-depth comparison of projecting onto \(\mathcal{M}_t\) vs. \(\mathcal{M}_0\) (MCG) and conditions under which each is superior. R4 argues that guidance on the posterior mean (as in MPGD) and guidance on \(x_t\) are equivalent up to a constant, so the claimed distinction based on manifold may not hold in practice. R4 further states that DDS uses a Krylov subspace method with similar effect. Direct empirical comparisons with MPGD and DDS on the same tasks are needed (R4).  
  *Minority contrast*: R4 (score 3) views this as a critical flaw questioning the theoretical contribution; the other reviewers (R1, R2) raise it as a missing discussion but still rate the paper highly.

- **Lack of pixel-domain baselines and experiments**: R1 notes the absence of pixel diffusion baselines (DDNM, DDRM) and R3 and R4 similarly call for experiments with pixel-space methods (DPS, PiGDM) to demonstrate generalizability. R3 points out inconsistencies: DAPS is compared in pixel space but not latent space (despite DAPS having a latent version), and ImageNet results are only with PSLD (not ReSample or DAPS), with no pixel-space ImageNet experiment. R4 states that most experiments use latent diffusion and more pixel-domain methods are required.  
  *Minority contrast*: R4 deems this lack of generalizability as a major weakness, while R1 and R3 consider it a limitation but not fatal.

- **Computational cost concerns for pixel-space models**: R1 cautions that computing and storing gradients with respect to the diffusion state at each step could become a significant bottleneck for large models or high-resolution images, especially for methods like PSLD that already have high memory demands. R3 explicitly questions whether the SVD at each iteration becomes prohibitive in pixel space. R2 counters that Figure 4 shows negligible cost for latent diffusion, but this does not address pixel-space scenarios.

- **Unclear experimental details**: R2 and R3 note that measurement noise level is not specified for Tables 2 and 3; R2 asks whether noise was added in the [0,1] range (based on line 365) and whether its presence/absence affects DiffStateGrad performance. R2 further asks for robustness experiment results at noise levels < 0.05. R3 also states that the claimed robustness to measurement noise is not clearly demonstrated in the paper (is uncertain where in the paper it appears). These missing details hinder reproducibility and understanding of the method's behavior under varying noise conditions.

- **Limited theoretical justification and loose definitions**: R3 finds the definition of "artifact free images" manifold loose and unclear how it arises in practice. R4 questions whether the theoretical motivation (projecting onto \(\mathcal{M}_t\)) is meaningful because gradient guidance on posterior mean and \(x_t\) differ only by a scale factor, making the proposed distinction less compelling. R4 requests a more compelling argument for why projecting onto \(\mathcal{M}_t\) is beneficial over \(\mathcal{M}_0\) (as in MCG) or no projection, with practical performance analysis rather than relying solely on theoretical intuition.

- **Missing discussion of initialization strategies**: R1 and R2 ask how DiffStateGrad interacts with early initialization techniques (CCDF, Adapt-and-Diffuse) that already improve robustness. R2 speculates that the benefit might be smaller in chaotic low-SNR regimes and asks whether DiffStateGrad could mitigate or complement such strategies. This interaction is not discussed in the paper.

- **Application to methods without approximate guidance**: R3 asks how DiffStateGrad applies to solvers like RePaint that do not use an approximate likelihood gradient—e.g., would one simply project the updated \(x_t\)? The paper does not clarify applicability beyond approximate-guidance-based solvers.

- **Convergence and optimization trajectory analysis**: R2 notes the need for a discussion of how projection onto different manifolds (\(\mathcal{M}_t\) vs. \(\mathcal{M}_0\)) affects optimization trajectory and convergence, and under which conditions one is preferred. R4 similarly questions the practical implications of the projection choice.

[1] MPGD (Mardani et al., 2024 or similar; referred to in R4)
[2] DDS (Kawar et al., 2022 or similar; referred to in R4)
