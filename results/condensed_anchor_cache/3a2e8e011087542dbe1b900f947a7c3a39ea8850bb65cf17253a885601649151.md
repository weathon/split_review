## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces temporally-coupled perturbations as a novel challenge in robust RL and proposes GRAD, a game-theoretic approach that treats the robust RL problem as a partially-observable two-player zero-sum game, using Policy Space Response Oracles (PSRO) to find an approximate equilibrium. Experiments on MuJoCo continuous control tasks show that GRAD achieves higher robustness against both temporally-coupled and standard attacks compared to prior baselines. The idea is seen as relevant for realistic nature attacks, but several reviewers question the novelty given existing game-theoretic robust RL methods and note insufficient distinction from prior work, incomplete related work, and limitations in the problem formulation.

### Strengths
- The paper is easy to follow (Reviewer 1).
- Thorough experiments for state attacks and action attacks, comparing with several baselines (Reviewer 1).
- The concept of temporally-coupled adversarial perturbations seems new (Reviewer 1) and pushes research toward realistic nature attacks (Reviewer 2).
- Formulating the robust RL objective as a zero-sum game and demonstrating the efficacy of game-theoretic RL in this setting is a nice idea worthy of publication (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The work includes experiments with model uncertainty (Reviewer 2 acknowledges that, though notes baseline limitations).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited temporal coupling**: Definition 3.2 only considers perturbation from the last time step (first-order Markovian dependency). This neglects longer-range temporal dependencies as in m-order MDP (Efroni et al. 2022). A more general formulation is needed (Reviewer 1). Also, the motivation for temporally-coupled perturbations lacks clarity; e.g., using the triangle inequality could give a range of \bar{\epsilon} that breaks the temporally-coupled property, rather than just stating “By setting \bar{\epsilon} to a large value, it converges to the non-coupled attack scenario” (Reviewer 3).
- **Novelty and distinction from prior work**: The zero-sum game approach is well-established in robust RL, e.g., Tessler et al. (2019), Pinto et al. (2017, RARL), and works on Robust Adversarial RL, Robust RL as a Stackelberg Game, and Robust MARL with State Uncertainty. The paper does not adequately distinguish its approach from these existing game-theoretic methods, particularly regarding the specific game formulation and algorithm (Reviewers 1, 3). The proposed method is largely an application of PSRO, and it is unclear how PSRO uniquely addresses temporally-coupled perturbations (Reviewer 3). Additionally, the convergence proof for GRAD appears lackluster since PSRO is known to converge to NE in two-player zero-sum games and has recent extensions (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 notes that GRAD shares similar ideas with RARL and should explicitly detail key differences (e.g., inbuilt state-action uncertainty) and provide due references.
- **Missing comparisons and related work**: The paper misses the classic robust MDP setting (transition adversaries) (Iyengar’05) and related baselines (Zhou et al. 2023). Experiments do not include methods that handle transition uncertainties, limiting scope (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 adds that related works [1–10] on model uncertainty are inadequately discussed; while experiments include model uncertainty, baselines are only action-robust algorithms. Either more extensive experiments for model uncertainty or a focused treatment of state-action uncertainty is needed.
- **Counter-intuitive result in Figure 1**: The robust baseline should be more stable under attacks because it considers the worst-case scenario, yet the proposed method appears more robust than a worst-case robust baseline. This requires an explanation of why the baseline is not robust to temporally-coupled adversaries (Reviewer 1).
- **Problem formulation**: The paper lacks a clear mathematical representation of the problem it intends to address (Reviewer 3).
- **Writing and method improvements**: The paper has ample room for improvement in writing, problem formulation, and the method itself (Reviewer 3). The motivation for temporally-coupled perturbations is not convincingly demonstrated (Reviewer 3).

*Note*: Reviewers 1 and 3 find the novelty limited and rate soundness/presentation as fair (score 5, confidence 4), while Reviewer 2 is more positive (score 6, confidence 5, excellent soundness/presentation), indicating disagreement on the level of contribution and quality of the paper.
