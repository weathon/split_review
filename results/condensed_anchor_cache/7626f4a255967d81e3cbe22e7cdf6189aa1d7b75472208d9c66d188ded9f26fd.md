## Merged Review

### Summary

Show-o is a unified transformer combining autoregressive and discrete diffusion modeling to handle multimodal understanding and generation tasks (VQA, text-to-image, inpainting/extrapolation, mixed-modality). It demonstrates competitive or superior performance to specialized models across benchmarks. While one reviewer (score 8, confidence 5) was notably more positive, the other three reviewers (score 6) raised several concerns about novelty, comparisons, unification benefits, and experimental fairness.

### Strengths

- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow, with well-made figures aiding understanding (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The motivation is clear, and the method (combining autoregressive and discrete diffusion) is sensible and innovative (R2, R4).
- Extensive experiments are conducted, with comprehensive comparisons against many existing baselines on numerous benchmarks (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Thorough ablations cover scaling of data, resolution, and visual representations (discrete vs. continuous, different pre-training) (R2).
- Results are strong and competitive on image-text understanding and generation tasks (R3).
- Extension to video and mixed-modality generation demonstrates wider applicability (R3).

### Weaknesses

- Missing comparisons with other unified models: Vila-u, Transfusion, Emu3 (R1). 
- The use of special tokens (e.g., `[mmu]`, `[T2I]`) explicitly reveals task information; it is unclear whether new tokens are needed for every new task, and whether the design supports truly interleaved input/output (R1).
- The model incurs significant training costs, challenging scalability, and potentially slower inference speed (R2, R4). Specific inference speed analysis is missing (R4).
- The benefits of unifying understanding and generation in a single model are unclear, given that Show-o often underperforms specialized unimodal models in both areas (R2). More concretely, continuous representations yield better understanding performance but cannot be used for generation, so unified performance is less satisfactory (R4). 
- Potential synergies/conflicts between objectives (e.g., does unification improve instruction-following or reasoning? Are there conflicts between generation and understanding objectives?) are not addressed (R2).
- For the LLaVA-v1.5-Phi-1.5 baseline, it is unclear if the same pretraining data were used (R2).
- Additional benchmarks are needed: FID on MS-COCO for generation, MMBench/SEED for visual understanding (VQA benchmarks have variance/bias), and GSM8K/MMLU/HumanEval for text-only tasks (R2).
- The paper does not convincingly justify using discrete diffusion for images instead of autoregressive modeling (like Chameleon), and provides no empirical experiment showcasing the claimed advantage of fewer forward evaluations (R3).
- Comparisons are difficult to assess due to different training/evaluation sets across baselines; human evaluation with shared prompts would improve fairness (R3).
- The discrete diffusion modeling is essentially similar to MaskGIT’s masked autoregression; this should be clarified early to reduce learning burden (R4).
- The current framework is not demonstrated on image-to-image tasks like image editing (e.g., using `[Text, Image, Image]` token sequences) (R4).
- For generation and understanding evaluations (Tables 1, 2), classifier-free guidance usage and the effect of varying guidance score are not reported (R3).
- Multi-turn interaction (e.g., continuing text-based QA and image generation in ongoing dialogues) is not supported (R4).
