## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes AFDistill, a distilled model of AlphaFold2 that predicts structural confidence scores (pTM, pLDDT) using an approach based on ProtBERT with discretized 50-bin outputs. AFDistill serves as a fast, differentiable oracle for a structure-consistency (SC) loss when training inverse folding models (GVP, ProteinMPNN, PiFold). Experiments report up to 3% improvement in sequence recovery and up to 45% improvement in diversity while maintaining structural consistency. The method is intended to avoid the high cost of running AlphaFold during training.

### Strengths
- **Novel idea** of distilling AlphaFold into a fast, differentiable model for structural consistency prediction (Reviewers 1, 3).  
- **Well-motivated insight** to distill AlphaFold2 for efficient inference in protein design; integration of structure consistency is important and should intuitively lead to better performance (Reviewer 2, who rated the paper 5, while the other reviewers gave 3).  
- **Comprehensive experiments** covering multiple inverse folding models and metrics (Reviewer 1).  
- **Elevation in recovery rate** is observed, though marginal (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal improvements / unconvincing benefit**: The overall improvements are small, and it is unclear whether using pLDDT as a loss is actually useful (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Gains in diversity do not translate to substantial improvements in recovery or perplexity, and no analysis is provided for why (Reviewer 2). It is also unclear if the increased diversity comes at the cost of stability or functionality (Reviewer 2).
- **No comparison to direct use of AFDB**: The motivation for using a distilled model over simply training with AlphaFold Database (AFDB) structures is not justified; a comparison is missing (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of technical novelty**: AFDistill is based on existing ProtBERT without major architectural modifications; the core idea of using a pre-trained language model to predict structural quality metrics is not novel (Reviewer 2).
- **Unjustified compute cost**: Both the distillation and the extra cost of evaluating/backpropagating the SC loss (`L_sc`) are significant, yet no justification is given (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Conceptual confusion**: Using pLDDT as a loss encourages “sequence stability” (easy-to-predict sequences) rather than true “structural consistency” with the target structure. The two concepts are conflated in the paper (Reviewer 3).
- **Misleading evaluation**: The reported “self-consistency” metric uses pTM from AFDistill (not real TM) and is treated as TM, which is irrelevant to the target structure. The actual TM-score between the predicted structure and the target should be reported (Reviewer 3).
- **Questions about pLDDT/pTM generation**: In AlphaFold2, pLDDT depends on MSA and template quality; it is unclear how these scores are obtained, especially for augmented datasets where the authors ran AF2 themselves (Reviewer 3).
- **Discretized output instead of regression**: AFDistill uses 50 bins; it is unclear why regression was not used (Reviewer 2).
- **Value of α in Eq. 1** and how it is determined is not stated (Reviewer 2).
- **Impact on training resources**: How integration of AFDistill affects training resources for inverse folding models is not addressed (Reviewer 2).
- **Presentation issues**: Figure 1 has crossing lines (CE loss ↔ AlphaFold) that are confusing (Reviewer 2). Experimental results for different models (GVP, ProteinMPNN, PiFold) are in separate tables; a combined table would be clearer (Reviewer 2). The paper needs proofreading (Reviewer 3).
- **Dataset selection**: Different balanced datasets are introduced for training AFDistill, but the main take-away for choosing a dataset for inverse folding is not explained (Reviewer 2).
- **Unexpected result**: The smallest distillation set leads to the largest improvements, which is not justified (Reviewer 3).
