## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper integrates uniform weight quantization and structured pruning to create a lightweight SNN (QP-SNN). It proposes a weight rescaling strategy (ReScaW) for better bit-width utilization and a pruning criterion based on singular values of spatiotemporal spike activities (SVS). Experiments on classification datasets demonstrate state-of-the-art performance and efficiency. Reviewers generally agree the idea is insightful and the experiments show effectiveness, but raise concerns about innovation, missing comparisons, insufficient efficiency metrics, and limited scope of validation.

### Strengths
- Illustrations are thoughtfully crafted; the manuscript is clearly structured and logically consistent.
- Thorough analysis of the weight distribution in SNNs provides strong motivation for the rescaling method.
- The proposed ReScaW and SVS methods are shown to be effective on both static and dynamic datasets.
- The baseline QP-SNN integrating uniform quantization and structured pruning is hardware-efficient and lightweight.
- Theoretical analysis of bit-width utilization and mathematical grounding of the SVS criterion are well presented.
- The paper introduces two novel techniques that address specific limitations in existing quantization and pruning approaches.
- Comprehensive experiments across multiple datasets and architectures, with detailed ablation studies that validate each component.
- Writing is good and easy to read.

### Weaknesses
- ReScaW-based uniform quantization lacks innovation; it is merely a combination of uniform quantization and weight scaling [1].
- SVD-based pruning appears to be a direct application of existing SVD pruning methods [2][3] to SNNs; the specific advantages of applying it in SNNs are not clarified.
- The proposed technologies do not fully utilize unique SNN characteristics such as spike sparsity to reduce synaptic operations or temporal information to maintain accuracy; the techniques could equally be applied to ANNs.
- The selection of the quantization scaling factor γ is not sufficiently rigorous: the choice of 1‑norm mean over other sampling methods is poorly justified, and a more adaptive piecewise scaling approach is not explored.
- The selection of pruning rates appears arbitrary and lacks systematic justification.
- Compatibility issues between quantization and pruning are not adequately discussed: the order of application and joint training strategies may affect performance and convergence; a unified ablation study including different orders is missing.
- Fine-tuning details after pruning are missing (optimizer, learning rate schedule, number of epochs), hindering reproducibility.
- SVD decomposition introduces additional time complexity; the impact on training time should be quantified.
- Experimental comparisons are insufficient: baselines (SEW-ResNet, Spiking ResNet) are from 2021, not the latest SNN models; comparison with uncompressed SNNs (e.g., SEW-ResNet, MS-ResNet) is missing, and accuracy degradation relative to these works is reportedly large.
- The method is only validated on simple classification tasks; no evaluation on complex downstream tasks (e.g., object detection) or other architecture families (e.g., Spiking Transformers) is provided.
- Efficiency metrics are lacking: no robust metrics such as OPs/SOPs, training time, inference speed, or energy consumption are presented.
- Results on saving synaptic operations are missing; the number of synaptic operations should be reported.
- Pruning ratio statistics are not presented in the main text.
- In Table 2, baselines for items A–F are not consistent; a unified ablation study is needed.
- The impact of the proposed methods on network spike sparsity is not addressed.
- Sensitivity to the choice of ε in the SVS-based pruning criterion is not analyzed.
- The computational cost of calculating singular values in the SVS criterion compared to existing pruning criteria is not discussed.
- *Note:* Reviewers 2 and 3 express stronger concerns about the novelty and generalization of the methods, while Reviewers 1 and 4 are more positive about the theoretical grounding and experimental validation.
