## Merged Review

### Summary
The manuscript presents a self-supervised learning pipeline for MEG speech decoding, introducing three pretext tasks (band prediction, phase shift prediction, amplitude scale prediction) to learn representations from heterogeneous, unlabelled MEG recordings. Pretraining on the Cam-CAN MEG dataset is evaluated on downstream speech detection and voice classification binary tasks via finetuning. Results show improved AUROC over linear baselines, no pretraining, and a reimplemented self-supervised baseline (BIOT), with gains scaling as pretraining data increases. The method supports held-out subjects and multiple datasets.

### Strengths
- Presentation is good, writing understandable, figures professional and legible (R1).
- Motivation and main idea are straightforward and clear (R1).
- Results for varying unlabelled dataset sizes are interesting (R1).
- The result is statistically significant and novel in the area of MEG speech decoding (R2).
- Pretext tasks are potentially applicable to other neural recording modalities (e.g., scalp and intracranial EEG) (R2).
- Novelty in the application (decoding speech from MEG) is present (R3).
- The approach is sound (R3).
- Proposed pretraining tasks are validated by ablation studies and motivated with neuroscience insights (R3).
- Scaling results are promising, especially for held-out subjects (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Low practical performance:** The best AUROC achieved on speech detection is ~0.62 (chance 0.5). This does not inspire confidence that MEG can scale to actual word decoding (perceived speech). The scaling slope is ~0.025 AUROC per order of magnitude in hours, implying a prohibitive 10³² hours to reach 90% AUROC if linear. The use of multiple datasets in pretraining does not improve performance on the Armeni data (Table 2), and improvement from Gwilliams data may not be statistically significant (R2). Reviewer 2 is particularly skeptical about the utility of MEG for speech decoding based on these results.
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons:** BIOT failed (chance performance). It is unclear whether the off-the-shelf BIOT was adapted for MEG (R1, R3). A pretrained BIOT model could potentially be applied to a subset of electrodes (R1). Many other self-supervised EEG models exist (Brant, BrainBERT, MMM, BENDR, LaBraM, EEGFormer) that could have been tried instead of BIOT (R2). Comparison to the Large Brain Model for EEG (arxiv 2405.18765) would also be helpful (R1). Additionally, comparison to existing speech decoding results (e.g., Defossez) is missing (R1).
- **Limited technique novelty:** Self-supervised learning for subject-generic neural representations has been studied before (cites [1-5] for EEG/MEG). The proposed pretext tasks share similarities with existing tasks (replace-discriminative learning from [4], frequency phase forecasting from [1]). The work covers a new downstream application, but it does not convincingly show that the proposed tasks are more effective than existing ones. A justification for why a different set of pretraining tasks is necessary is lacking (R3). The band prediction task seems unmotivated: predicting presence/absence of a frequency band does not encourage learning semantic information about neural activity; the ablation shows usefulness but many other plausible tasks could fill the same role (R3).
- **Missing metrics and comparisons:** Reporting further metrics beyond AUROC (e.g., accuracy, precision-recall) is crucial for comparability with other speech decoding works (R1). The manuscript does not directly compare to existing results on the same downstream tasks (e.g., Defossez) (R1). ECoG studies that train single models on multiple subjects’ data exist (Anumanchipalli et al., Nature 2019; Makin et al., Nat. Neuro. 2020) and are relevant (R2).
- **Clarifications needed:** How is phase perturbation applied (frequency domain, all bins? random bands?)? For amplitude scaling, the same question applies (R1). The nature of subject conditioning (concatenated prompt vector? fine-tuning?) is unclear (R1, R3). The downsampling rate from time t to tau (line 203) is not specified (R3). Equation 4 weightings w_i: is anything other than uniform used? (R3). Line 326 suggests BIOT was taken off-the-shelf, but Line 363 suggests it was pretrained on Cam-CAN – this needs clarification (R3). Table 1: is it for speech detection (vs. voicing)? How does voicing detection compare? (R2). Are the differences between band-only and all-tasks pretraining statistically significant, especially for Gwilliams data (N=3)? (R2).
- **Relevant prior work omitted:** [1911.05419] on self-supervised representation learning from EEG with different pretext tasks should be discussed (R1).
