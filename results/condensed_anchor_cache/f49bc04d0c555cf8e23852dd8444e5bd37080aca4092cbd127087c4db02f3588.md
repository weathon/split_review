## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the Foveated Dynamic Transformer (FDT), a vision transformer architecture inspired by the human visual system’s foveation and fixation mechanisms. FDT uses a single-pass strategy with a fixation module to dynamically select informative regions and a foveation module to encode multi-scale information (high-resolution center, low-resolution periphery). The authors claim a 34% reduction in MACs over DeiT, as well as robustness to adversarial attacks and common corruptions, without specific adversarial training.

### Strengths
1. **Novel bio-inspired framework** – The integration of foveation and fixation into a transformer is a promising direction, addressing computational efficiency and adversarial robustness simultaneously. (R1, R2, R3)
2. **Single-pass design** – Unlike recurrent or sequential hard-attention models, FDT processes the image in one pass, making it more practical for real-time applications. (R1, R2)
3. **Promising efficiency gain** – A 34% reduction in multiply-accumulate operations over DeiT is reported, which pushes the boundaries for efficient image classification. (R1, R3)
4. **Broad experimental coverage** – The paper studies robustness to several adversarial attacks (FGSM, PGD, CW, MIGFSM), common corruptions, and response time, touching on relevant problems in deep learning. (R2)
5. **Sections 3.5 and 3.8** are particularly informative. (R2)
6. **Figures and tables** – One reviewer (R3) found the figures and tables clear and well-presented, though two other reviewers (R1, R2) strongly criticized the presentation (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
1. **Poor organization and clarity** (R1, R2)
   - Figures are not referenced in the main text: Figure 1 is never mentioned; Figure 2 contains critical information but is insufficiently explained.
   - Abbreviations (e.g., MHSA, GMACs) are used repeatedly before definition.
   - Key details are missing: the threat model for adversarial attacks is not defined; security levels in Table 1 are unexplained.
   - The connection between biological mechanisms (foveation, saccades) and transformer components lacks depth; a flowchart showing the overall logic and how HVS concepts map to architecture would help. (R2)
   - Equation (8) uses \(x^{i,j}\) but this notation is not annotated in Figure 2 or explained. (R3)

2. **Insufficient justification for adversarial robustness claims** (R2, R3)
   - Only standard attacks (FGSM, PGD, CW) designed for CNNs are tested. Transformer-specific attacks such as Token Gradient Regularization (CVPR 2023) and transferable attacks on image/video transformers (IEEE TIP) are missing. (R2)
   - Stronger transfer attacks like Translation-Invariant (CVPR 2018) and Variance Tuning (CVPR 2021) should be evaluated. (R2)
   - No rationale is given for the chosen set of attacks; a defined threat model is absent. (R2)
   - No visual analysis shows how fixation points change under adversarial perturbations (e.g., heat maps for clean vs. PGD sample), as done in Mukherjee et al. (2024) – referenced as [k]. (R2)
   - Missing comparisons to adversarially trained Vision Transformers (When Adversarial Training Meets ViT, NeurIPS 2022) and robust transformer backbones (Towards Robust Vision Transformer, CVPR 2022). (R2)

3. **Computational efficiency analysis needs strengthening** (R2)
   - The derivation of the 34% MAC reduction over DeiT should be explicitly shown (e.g., for one variant: tiny, small, or base). (R2)
   - Concepts such as anytime inference and budgeted classification (as in Glance and Focus, NeurIPS/PAMI referenced as [a],[b]) are relevant but not discussed. (R2)
   - The computational budget for FDT when evaluated on common corruptions (Table 1) is unclear. (R3)

4. **Inadequate comparisons to related work** (all reviewers)
   - The paper focuses almost exclusively on DeiT. It should compare to:
     - Other HVS-inspired models: Saccader (Elsayed et al., NeurIPS 2019), Liu et al. (ICLR 2023), Schwinn et al. (2022), FoveaTer (referenced as [j]), and works on foveation for robustness (Shah et al., NeurIPS 2024; Reddy et al., NeurIPS 2020; Gant et al., SVRHM 2022; Harrington & Deza, ICLR 2022). (R3)
     - Multi-resolution/pyramidal networks (e.g., Focal Modulation Networks [c], Focal Transformers [d], Swin Transformers [e]). (R1, R2)
     - Other gaze-based or active vision methods (e.g., from GAZE meets ML workshop at NeurIPS; Jindal et al., CVPR 2024; Gupta et al., CVPR 2024). (R1)
   - Why was DeiT chosen as the baseline? Arguments based on parameter count or MACs should be stated. (R2, R3)
   - Extend the method to other vision transformer backbones (e.g., standard ViT) instead of only DeiT. (R2)

5. **Biological plausibility and architectural alignment** (R1, R2, R3)
   - The order of operations contradicts human vision: humans first saccade (scan) then foveate, but FDT does the opposite (foveation before fixation). (R1, R2, R3)
   - The saccadic mechanism usually involves sequential actions tracking regions of interest; FDT selects multiple fixation patches simultaneously, so the ordered scanpath in Figure 9 is unclear (was it manually constructed?). (R3)
   - The combination module processes both fixated and non-fixated tokens together for dimension matching, which may defeat the purpose of the fixation module. (R2)
   - The work resembles a hard-attention ViT more than a full HVS model, because simple multi-layer CNNs do not constitute foveal-peripheral vision. (R3)

6. **Missing details in experiments and setup** (R1, R2, R3)
   - In Table 4, “response time” comparison to HVS is claimed but no human data is shown. (R1)
   - How were samples classified as easy/medium/hard? No standard definition or reference is provided. (R1)
   - Results are reported on ImageNet-100, not the more established ImageNet-1K; rationale is needed. (R2)
   - ImageNet-O is mentioned for common corruptions, but the standard benchmark is ImageNet-C. (R3)
   - Patch size effects and pretraining details (was a pretrained model used given the small dataset?) are not discussed. (R1)
   - Feature inversion is used for explainability but its necessity for prediction is unclear. (R1, R2)

7. **Missing citations and discussion** – The paper omits numerous directly relevant works (see references cited by reviewers [a]–[k] and the list in R3). This weakens the claim of being up-to-date with SOTA. (R2, R3)

8. **Contradictions and disconnects**
   - C&W attack results are much better (higher accuracy) than PGD, even for DeiT, which is unexpected for white-box attacks. (R2)
   - The abstract mentions “metabolic energy” without clarifying its significance. (R2)
   - The fixation module outputs two logits for a binary decision; why not use a single threshold? (R3)
