## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes two variance-reduced extensions of the distributed matrix-stepsize compressed gradient descent (det-CGD) algorithm, called det-MARINA and det-DASHA, aimed at improving communication complexity in federated non-convex optimization. The authors provide convergence guarantees to stationarity (gradient norm squared vanishes at rate \(O(1/k)\)) and present numerical experiments showing improvements over MARINA, DASHA, and det-CGD. While the motivation of combining matrix stepsize with variance reduction is considered interesting and the paper is well-written, several reviewers raised concerns about limited novelty, insufficient theoretical comparisons, practical computational overhead, and modest experimental gains.

### Strengths
- **Interesting idea**: Using matrix stepsizes together with variance-reduction mechanisms (MARINA, DASHA) to improve communication complexity in federated learning (R1, R2, R3, R4). One reviewer found this “interesting” (R1), another “clear and natural” (R3).
- **Addresses variance issue**: The proposed schemes overcome the restrictive convergence neighborhood of det-CGD by controlling the variance introduced by compression (R2, R3). This makes the distributed algorithm more desirable (R2).
- **Theoretical guarantees**: Convergence analysis is provided, establishing iteration/communication complexity improvements (R1, R3, R4). The paper shows that the gradient norm vanishes with rate \(O(1/k)\) (R3).
- **Well-written and clear**: The paper is generally well-written with sufficient background and a clear contribution (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The work essentially combines a known algorithm (det-CGD) with two known variance-reduction methods (MARINA, DASHA). One reviewer (score 3) found the novelty limited and the technical challenges over combining them not clearly highlighted, especially compared to using scalar stepsizes (R2). Another reviewer noted that both MARINA and DASHA already converge to stationarity (unlike det-CGD), so the advantage is less novel (R3).
- **Insufficient theoretical comparison with baselines**:  
  - The convergence rates of det-MARINA and det-DASHA are not directly compared with the original MARINA/DASHA in terms of the same gradient norm metric (R3, Questions (1)-(2)). Corollary 3 claims better iteration complexity than MARINA, but the gradient norms compared may differ, making the claim unclear (R3).  
  - Missing comparisons with classical first-order methods (e.g., distributed GD, SGD, SARAH), especially regarding per-iteration computational cost (R1, R1e). The matrix stepsize iteration is more expensive, making such comparisons necessary (R1).  
  - General rate comparisons with existing literature are missing (R4).
- **Practical issues with matrix stepsize**:  
  - Computing matrices \(\mathbf{W}^{1/2}\), \(\mathbf{L}^{-1}\), and eigenvalues (eq. (6), Section 4.2) may be expensive in practice (R1).  
  - The Lipschitz matrix is impractical; the Lipschitz constant is more accessible and the inclusion of the matrix may not be essential (R3).  
  - The method for estimating \(\mathbf{L}_i\) (via gradient method, Wang et al. 2022) is not summarized, leaving questions about its complexity and design (R1, R1c).
- **Missing theoretical details**:  
  - The relaxation to a linear subspace for Corollary 1 (line 310) lacks an approximation error bound (R1, R1a).  
  - The derivation of the optimal matrix stepsize in equation (5) (line 304) is questioned: minimizing the RHS does not directly imply optimality for the LHS because the LHS also contains \(D\) (R3, Question (1)).  
  - The challenge in combining variance reduction with matrix stepsize (e.g., analysis novelties) is not clearly explained (R2, R4).
- **Modest experimental gains**: The numerical improvements over MARINA and DASHA are relatively modest (R3).
- **Scope**: The paper only considers the nonconvex case, leaving convex analysis as future work (R4).
