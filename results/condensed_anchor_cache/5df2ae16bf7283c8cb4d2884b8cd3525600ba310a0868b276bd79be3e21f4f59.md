## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes MixAttention, an architecture that combines sliding window attention (storing only recent tokens in the KV cache) with KV cache sharing across layers. Four variants are trained and evaluated on a 5B MoE model using RULER (long-context), Gauntlet-evaleons (MMLU, HellaSwag), and standard benchmarks. The main findings are that sharing the KV cache from the first layer throughout the network hurts performance at 32k context, and that a non‑sliding‑window attention layer should be placed at least in the deeper layers. Some MixAttention variants match the performance of standard attention while being significantly cheaper in compute and memory. Reviewers disagree substantially: two rate the paper 1 (strong reject), citing lack of novelty and effort; two rate it 3 (weak accept/marginally positive), acknowledging some empirical value but still pointing to limited new information.

### Strengths
- The idea is simple and clear; the experimental setup is also clear (Reviewer 1).
- The approach requires no custom kernels, making it easier to adapt to architecture changes or different hardware (Reviewer 4).
- Cache sharing across layers has not been extensively ablated; the paper provides additional sample points showing the relationship between sharing approach and performance (Reviewer 3).
- Results are tested on RULER (long‑context) and conventional evals (MMLU, HellaSwag) through the Gauntlet framework, unveiling differences between sharing approaches (Reviewer 3).
- Some cache‑sharing variants perform as well as standard attention while being cheaper in compute and memory (Reviewer 3).
- The combination of sparsifying the token sequence and sharing KV cache seems promising; the experiments from pre‑training to evaluation offer insights about different setup choices (Reviewer 2).
- The experiment setup is reasonably designed (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty across all reviewers**  
  - The paper simply combines two established techniques (sliding window attention and KV cache sharing) without any improvements (Reviewer 1).  
  - This combination was already explicitly described in the Character.AI blog, which the paper cites; the paper offers nothing new beyond the blog (Reviewers 2, 4).  
  - Two reviewers (1,4) state the paper resembles a technical report or blogpost, not innovative research, and that no amount of experiments can make it acceptable due to the absence of novelty (Reviewer 4, soundness 3 but contribution 1).  
  - Reviewer 2 notes that the combination has already been proposed in other work and the new information provided is limited – e.g., the conclusion about deeper layers being more important for long context has been declared by existing studies.

- **Surface‑level analysis and lack of deeper insight**  
  - The paper presents only high‑level phenomena instead of practical methodology or deeper understanding of why certain cache‑sharing approaches perform better or worse (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).  
  - There is no explanation for why the specific configurations were selected (Reviewer 3).  
  - No discussion of trends between performance and degree of cache‑sharing for both standard and sliding window attention (Reviewer 3).  
  - Missing discussion of differences with another related cache‑sharing paper (Brandon et al., 2024) (Reviewer 3).

- **Missing comparisons and ablations**  
  - No Pareto improvement curve: how does the proposed approach compare to a smaller standard MoE model with similar KV‑cache size? A plot with KV‑cache memory on X‑axis and accuracy on Y‑axis is requested (Reviewer 4).  
  - Many new hyper‑parameters (which layers are standard attention, which are sliding window, group size for KV sharing) are pre‑defined; the paper does not explain how to make these decisions wisely for a new model (Reviewer 2).  
  - The experiments are based only on a 5B MoE model, so generalizability is unconvincing (Reviewer 2).

- **Presentation and execution issues**  
  - Results are only shown as bar charts; a table with precise values would be beneficial (Reviewer 1).  
  - Poorly written: there is a typo on line 199 (the word “sequence” used twice), and the paper says to refer to an appendix for certain experiments, but no appendix is included (Reviewer 4).  
  - No optimization was done in SGLang for the sliding window attention baseline, indicating insufficient effort (Reviewer 4).  
  - Paper focuses on comparing variants but does not answer why certain benchmarks (RULER, Gauntlet) were chosen or provide reasoning for caching‑sharing variant selection (Reviewer 3).
