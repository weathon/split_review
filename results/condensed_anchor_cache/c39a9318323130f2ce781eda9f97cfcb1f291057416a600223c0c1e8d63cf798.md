## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a segment-level reward model for RLHF, assigning rewards to semantically complete text segments (short token sequences) as a middle ground between sparse sequence-level (bandit) and overly granular token-level rewards. The method includes dynamic text segmentation via entropy thresholding, segment-level reward learning using the Bradley-Terry loss (Eq. 3, referencing Yang et al., 2023), and novel location-aware reward normalizers that generalize classical bandit normalizers into functions outputting mean/std per position. Segment rewards are further densified by interpolating across tokens. Experiments are conducted on multiple RLHF benchmarks (e.g., RewardBench) and include comprehensive ablation studies. The method achieves competitive performance in both reward modeling accuracy and downstream LM policy.

### Strengths
- **Comprehensive and detailed ablation studies**: Extensive experiments provide sufficient evidence for design choices, including segmentation method, reward normalizer variants, and segment-level vs. token-level comparisons (R1, R2, R3). Ablations are thorough and well-documented.
- **Intuitive motivation and novel MDP formulation**: The segment-level MDP setting is novel to the RLHF community and addresses the limitations of both bandit (sparse rewards) and token-level (semantically meaningless actions) approaches. The use of “segment” instead of “sentence” is unique (R2, R3). The approach mitigates the sparse reward issue, enabling denser feedback with semantic coherence (R4).
- **Dynamic segmentation based on entropy**: Using the model’s predictive distribution entropy to identify semantically coherent segments is an effective technique (R4).
- **Addresses key RLHF challenges**: The method balances the trade-off between dense and sparse rewards, potentially leading to more efficient learning and better policy optimization (R1, R4).
- **Competitive performance on benchmarks**: The segment-level reward model (85.93 on RewardBench) outperforms the bandit baseline (81.11), and downstream LM policy shows improvements over baselines (R1). However, one reviewer (R4) strongly questions whether these results are convincing and sufficient.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The segment-level reward learning relies on the same Bradley-Terry loss as prior work (Eq. 3, Yang et al., 2023). Text segmentation via entropy thresholding has also been explored before. The main contribution appears to be empirical demonstration that segment-level rewards outperform token-level, which may not be a substantial departure from existing ideas (R1). (Contrast: R3 considers the segment-level MDP setting itself novel.)
- **Computational overhead not addressed**: The semantic segmentation procedure increases the computational burden of online RL (e.g., PPO) training. The paper does not sufficiently analyze or quantify this overhead, which could limit practical applicability, especially for large models (R1, R3). The segmentation is also theoretically unguaranteed (R3).
- **Fixed entropy threshold problematic**: The method relies on a fixed entropy threshold for segmentation. The optimal threshold likely varies across datasets and tasks; no systematic approach is provided, requiring extensive hyperparameter tuning for new applications (R1).
- **Training convergence concerns**: All models are trained with PPO for only one epoch. The paper does not show training dynamics or convergence curves. Improvements may stem from faster convergence rather than higher final performance, potentially undermining the experimental analysis (R3).
- **Inconsistent performance (Table 6)**: In many settings, the segment method does not achieve the best performance, raising questions about its effectiveness (R2). For which specific tasks does segment provide a greater advantage over sentence- or token-level methods? The experiments do not clearly highlight its superiority (R2).
- **Missing experiments with larger models and current baselines**: Results are not convincing without testing on larger models (e.g., Llama3-8B) or modern RLHF algorithms (e.g., Phi-DPO). The reviewer (R4) argues that larger models may already solve the challenges the paper addresses, and segment-level is not intuitive for them.
- **Design choices lack intuition or analysis**:
  - The completion portion \( p \) is simply defined as \( t/T \) without explanation of why later actions should have higher \( p \); intuitive or theoretical reasoning is missing (R3).
  - Evenly splitting segment-level rewards to each token (interpolation) is counter-intuitive and somewhat contradicts the main novelty of segment-level MDP. The fact that this decomposition improves performance mitigates the attraction of the segment-level setting (R3).
  - The new reward normalizer (location-aware) is a key contribution, but no corresponding preliminaries are introduced in Section 2.1 (R3).
  - No guidance is given for how to choose \( f(\cdot) \) when different datasets require different functions (R3).
  - It is unclear how a final sequence score is determined from multiple token-level or segment-level rewards on RewardBench (R3).
- **Why does segment-level enhance reward modeling?**: Both segment and bandit models are trained with BT loss (Eq. 3), yet segment achieves better sequence-level accuracy (85.93 vs. 81.11). No explanation is provided for why finer-grained action space improves reward modeling (R1).
- **Impact of pretrained reward model**: The paper does not test whether using different pretrained models as the reward function backbone significantly impacts performance (R1).
- **Section 2 layout confusing**: The structure of Section 2 is unclear, and its relevance to the later experimental setup is not fully understood (R2).
