## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies in-context learning (ICL) for sequential decision-making by training transformers on expert demonstrations from multiple tasks and evaluating on unseen MiniHack and Procgen tasks, showing generalization from a few expert demos without weight updates. The authors provide ablation studies on dataset size, task diversity, stochasticity, and trajectory burstiness. Reviewer ratings: one reviewer (R1) gave a score of 5 (fairly confident), while three reviewers (R2, R3, R4) gave 3 (confident to fairly confident). The minority more positive reviewer found the problem important, the generalization impressive, and the analysis thorough, though still noting clarity and novelty concerns.

### Strengths
- The problem is important and timely: ICL for decision-making is comparatively understudied relative to supervised learning (R1, R2, R4).
- Demonstrates generalization to entirely new tasks in MiniHack and Procgen, which has not been explicitly shown before; the fact that only a handful of training tasks are used makes this impressive (R1, R2).
- Thorough analytical studies and ablation experiments, especially the analysis of failure modes and the effect of number of training tasks (R1, R2, R3).
- Interesting finding: in deterministic training environments the model learns copying behavior; environment stochasticity improves ICL (R2).
- Proposes a new setting: testing on games withheld during training, using expert policies trained on a separate set of games (R3).
- Clear advantage over BC and hashmap baselines (R3).
- Paper is well written and easy to follow (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and exposition of the setting/methodology are lacking.** Specific undressed questions from reviewers:
  - During testing, are the few expert demonstrations all from the same task and same level? How does this work for the hashmap baseline if demos come from different levels? Why train the transformer with sequences from different levels if test context always uses same-level demos? (R1)
  - What does “burstiness” mean when the levels are never varied in the training sequences? (R1)
  - What does BC-1 condition on? Does conditioning on “one demonstration” mean a full expert demo from the same task and level concatenated with the history? (R1)
  - What is the maximal achievable reward in each environment? This would help interpret the final results (R1).
  - How long are the sequences (T)? Why are returns in Figure 4 so low compared to those reported by Cobbe et al. (2020)? (R3)
  - Is the Procgen dataset evaluated on easy or hard difficulty? Are the results in Figure 3 normalized? (R3)
  - In the background, the initial state distribution μ is not defined (R3).

- **Limited novelty / strong similarity to prior work.** The method appears to be a small adaptation (multi-trajectory training) of pre-existing approaches (causal transformers) to new benchmarks, and is very close to Prompt-DT (Xu et al., 2022) and DPT. A more careful dissection and comparison with these papers, as well as with other ICL-RL works (e.g., [3], [4]) and meta/few-shot imitation learning, is needed. (R1, R2, R3, R4) Note: R1, though more positive overall, also flagged this as a weakness.

- **Missing comparisons.** No comparison to other offline methods such as CQL (Kumar et al., 2020) is provided (R3). Claims that meta-RL methods “tend to be difficult to use in practice and require more than a handful of demonstrations or extensive fine-tuning” are not supported by experiments (R2).

- **Insufficient statistical reliability.** Only 3 seeds are used; for such noisy benchmarks this is not enough to reliably estimate mean and variance (R3).

- **Several claims are not sufficiently supported by the experiments:**
  - The claim that “in sequential decision-making it is crucial for the context to contain full trajectories” is not tested against partial demos, non-expert trajectories, or other context structures. (R2)
  - The conclusion that “not copying context actions suggests in-weights learning as opposed to ICL” is unclear—the reasoning equates ICL with action copying, which is not justified. (R2)

- **Trajectory burstiness concept is weakly motivated.** The connection to Chan et al.’s burstiness is tenuous; it is obvious that same-level context is more relevant than cross-level context. Also, what does “trajectory burstiness” mean for the zero-shot model in Fig. 6(a)? (R2, R4)

- **Insights are mostly unsurprising.** Results showing improvements from larger datasets, more task diversity, and bursty contexts are expected (R4). The paper relies on many demonstrations from related tasks, yet the relationship between training and test tasks needs better clarification (R4). (Note: R1, the more positive reviewer, did not raise this concern.)
