## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes Bad-PFL, a novel backdoor attack against personalized federated learning (PFL). Unlike prior attacks that use manually designed triggers (which fail in PFL because backdoors do not persist in personalized models), Bad-PFL uses natural features from the target label as a sample-specific trigger. It employs a dual-component design: a target-feature enhancement mask (δ) generated by a sample-specific generator, and a disruptive noise mask (ξ) to remove ground-truth features. The trigger undergoes mutual reinforcement training with the model to improve durability. Large-scale experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and SVHN show high attack success rates (ASR) against six prior backdoor attacks and several PFL methods (FedAvg, FedProx, Ditto, FedRep, FedBABU, FedRoD), even under state-of-the-art defenses (Krum, Trimmed Mean, Multi-Krum, FoolsGold, RFA, FLAME, FedAvg+Norm). The work highlights vulnerabilities in PFL previously thought resistant to backdoors.

### Strengths

- **Novel trigger design:** Using natural features from the target label as a sample-specific trigger is a significant departure from prior works (e.g., fixed-pattern triggers). The dual-trigger design combining target feature enhancement and disruptive noise improves stealth and effectiveness.
- **Thorough empirical evaluation:** Experiments cover three benchmark datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN), six prior backdoor attacks, six PFL methods, and seven defense mechanisms, demonstrating high ASR in diverse settings.
- **Clear presentation:** The paper is well-organized and easy to follow; it provides a thorough discussion of why existing backdoor attacks fail in PFL (Section 2.2).
- **Insight into PFL vulnerabilities:** Provides a foundation for advancing attack strategies and encourages development of specialized defenses for PFL.
- **High stealth and persistence:** The attack maintains effectiveness even under state-of-the-art defenses and in personalized models.
- **Reinforcement techniques:** The mutual reinforcement training between trigger (generator) and model is a novel contribution.

### Weaknesses

1. **Lack of theoretical and experimental analysis of why Bad-PFL works:** The paper lacks in-depth discussion of how the proposed method overcomes the challenges in Section 2.2. No intuitive or theoretical justification is provided. Missing experimental analysis such as visualizing representation distributions of different classes under clean and backdoored data, or analyzing gradient flow during backdoor training to identify which layers/neurons are most susceptible. The role of the two masks (δ and ξ) is not fully justified; it is unclear why two separate masks are essential rather than a single adaptive mask, and whether they interfere with each other. Table 4 is insufficient; detailed analysis (e.g., visualization of mask interplay, quantitative metrics) is needed.
2. **Missing comparisons with related backdoor attacks on PFL:** The paper does not compare with or discuss:
    - *Lurking in the Shadows: Unveiling Stealthy Backdoor Attacks against Personalized Federated Learning* (arXiv:2406.06207)
    - *BapFL: You can Backdoor Personalized Federated Learning* (ACM TKDD 2024)
    - *IBA* and *Perdoor*, which also use a generator for mask optimization.
3. **Missing defense methods:** The paper does not evaluate against *Simple-Tuning* (clients reinitialize classifiers and retrain on local clean data while fixing the feature encoder; ACM CCS 2023). Also missing post-training defenses like pruning, CRFL (ICML 2021), or unlearning.
4. **Limited evaluation of stealth/detectability:** No quantitative analysis of how detectable the trigger is by modern anomaly/intrusion detection systems that analyze internal representations or activation patterns. The authors are asked to test detectability with existing backdoor detection methods.
5. **Limited model architecture diversity:** Experiments are conducted only on ResNet models. The attack’s effectiveness on transformer-based models is not evaluated; generalizability is questionable.
6. **Degradation under high data heterogeneity:** The method shows performance degradation when data is highly non-i.i.d., which is common in practice. The attack’s success may vary with different levels of heterogeneity; more heterogeneous distributions could reduce ASR and persistence. The impact of the number of clients (which affects the global model each round) is also unexplored.
7. **Single global backdoor target assumption:** The attack assumes a single global backdoor target, but in PFL clients have diverse data distributions and potentially different objectives. A personalized/adaptive backdoor attack may be necessary; effectiveness could drop when the backdoor target conflicts with a client’s local objective. How the attack performs when attackers target different labels or clients have multiple objectives is not addressed.
8. **Computational overhead concerns:** The use of a generator for mask optimization may raise suspicion if malicious training iterations take too long. The paper should discuss computational overhead.
9. **Need for white-box vs. black-box comparison:** As a first study on backdoor attacks in PFL, the paper should compare white-box and black-box settings to justify the three hypotheses on why prior backdoors failed. A white-box attack (full access to model and client data) would provide a strong baseline; the current threat model is not explicitly discussed for colluded vs. non-colluded attacks.
10. **Lack of clarity on specific implementation details:** The intuition for setting the benign mask η by the reversed sign of the input (Line 294) is not explained. The sharp difference in ASR among datasets (e.g., CIFAR-100 vs. SVHN under FedREP in Tables 15 and 16) is not elaborated.
11. **Minority reviewer concerns:** Reviewer 3 rated soundness 2 (lower than others at 3) and raised fundamental methodological concerns about mask justification and missing comparisons. Reviewer 4 rated contribution 2 (lower than others) and questioned whether the traditional backdoor objective is sufficient for PFL, recommending adaptive attacks and analysis of internal dynamics. These contrasting views suggest the paper’s novelty and soundness are not unanimously strong.

**Explicit reviewer disagreements:** All reviewers rated the paper 6, but one reviewer (R3) rated soundness 2 and another (R4) rated contribution 2, indicating that while the overall quality is acceptable, significant methodological and theoretical gaps remain. Reviewer R3 is notably more critical regarding the justification of the dual-mask design and missing related works.
