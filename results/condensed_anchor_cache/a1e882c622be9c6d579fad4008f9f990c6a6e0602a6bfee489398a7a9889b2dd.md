## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper presents a study on compression techniques for Mixture of Experts (MoE) architectures. The authors propose Expert Trimming (removing entire MoE layers via Layer Drop, removing Transformer blocks via Block Drop, or dropping individual experts via Expert Drop) and Expert Slimming (compressing individual experts, e.g., via quantization). On Mixtral-8×7B they report a 6.05× speedup with 77.1% reduced memory while maintaining over 92% of the original performance. The methods are evaluated on several LLM benchmarks. All three reviewers note that the approach is simple and the paper is clearly written, but raise serious concerns about novelty and missing comparisons; one reviewer (score 3) finds the contributions particularly limited.

### Strengths

- **Clear writing and presentation** – All reviewers agree the paper is well-written, with easy‑to‑understand and visually appealing figures (R2, R3).
- **Comprehensive analysis of Expert Trimming** – The paper systematically studies Expert Drop, Layer Drop, and Block Drop, providing instructive analysis of their trade‑offs (R1). The observations on performance vs. efficiency are meaningful (R2).
- **Practical significance** – LLM efficiency is an important deployment topic (R3); the combined results of Expert Trimming + Expert Slimming are reported (R1).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty is a major concern** – The proposed method is a combination of existing techniques (R1). Expert Trimming (Layer Drop and Block Drop) is essentially layer pruning for LLMs and not truly new, as noted with reference [1] “Reducing transformer depth on demand with structured dropout” (R2). Expert Slimming also does not appear novel (R3). Overall contributions are considered limited (R2, R3).
- **Missing comparisons to strong baselines** – Expected SOTA comparisons are not included (R1). Many layer‑pruning baselines exist and should be compared (R3).
- **Ablation study needed: training from scratch** – Line 371 claims >90% performance is maintained; would a similarly compact architecture trained from scratch achieve comparable accuracy? (R2)
- **Ablation study needed: removing MoE vs. Norm layers individually vs. together** – The strategy of simultaneously removing MoE and Norm layers lacks sufficient justification (R2).
- **Marginal improvement over AWQ** – Table 3 shows only a marginal performance gain when combining Expert Trimming + Expert Slimming (quantization) over using only AWQ; the practical significance of this gain is not discussed (R2).
- **No clear conclusion on best expert trimming method** – A more explicit discussion of which technique (Expert Drop, Layer Drop, Block Drop) is best under different trade‑offs (performance, efficiency, ease of implementation) would help readers (R2).
- **Performance discrepancy on DeepSeek MoE undermines conclusion** – One reviewer (R3) points out that Block Drop and Layer Drop perform considerably worse on DeepSeek MoE than on Mixtral, contradicting the authors’ claim that “Block Drop demonstrating both higher performance and greater speedup” (Figure 8, line ? ). Explanation needed.
- **Why does layer‑wise Expert Drop consistently surpass global strategies at high drop percentages?** (Figure 3) – No underlying mechanism is provided (R3).
- **Why was dynamic pruning not considered?** – The proposed pruning is static; dynamic pruning (adjusting layers per token) could be relevant and should be compared (R3).
- **At which training stage is pruning most effective?** – The paper does not discuss whether pruning should be applied during pretraining, SFT, or alignment (R3).
- **Typos** – Issues include: line 304 “Expert Slimmingand”, line 311 “n this Section” and “Expert Trimmingtechniques”, line 43 “tandard MoE”, line 264 “efficiency. - -. efficiency.” (R2, R3).
- **Minor clarification** – In line 292, the notation for importance score uses \(x_l\) and \(y_l\); is this the same as \(x'_l\) and \(y'_l\) used earlier in Layer Drop? (R3)
