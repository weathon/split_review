## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces a visibility prediction network (VPN) that learns to predict the visibility of any 3D point from each input camera, along with a visibility scoring function to characterize rendering reliability. The approach is applied to two post-training tasks: (1) improving rendering quality by skipping low‑visibility near‑range points during volumetric rendering (0.6 dB PSNR gain on a 62‑scene benchmark), and (2) selecting additional training views for re‑training a NeRF. The method is evaluated on a self‑collected dataset and demonstrates quantitative improvements.

### Strengths
- **Important problem and well motivated.** The paper tackles the practical issue of judging a NeRF’s rendering confidence when ground‑truth views are unavailable. (R1, R2, R3) Reviewer confidence varies: R1 and R3 are strongly positive about motivation, while R2 and R4 also acknowledge it but are more reserved about execution.
- **Simple and effective integration.** The VPN distills visibility information from density values into a lightweight network, and the approach is shown to improve rendering quality. (R1, R2, R4) Experimental results on two downstream tasks confirm effectiveness. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Applicability.** The method is designed for ray‑casting based NeRF variants and is demonstrated on the Nerfacto model. (R2)
- **Well written and easy to read.** (R2, R4) R2 rates presentation 4/5; R4 calls it “mostly well‑written.” (R1, R3 also find presentation fair to good.)
- **Up‑to‑date with recent advances.** The paper cites relevant prior work. (R2) (In contrast, R1 notes a missing citation.)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation.** The method is tested only on a self‑collected dataset with no experiments on well‑known NeRF benchmarks (e.g., LLFF, Blender, Mip‑NeRF 360). (R3, R4) Comparisons to other artifact‑reduction or view‑selection methods are missing. (R3, R4) Only the Nerfacto variant is evaluated, limiting generalizability claims. (R4) Free‑viewpoint rendered videos are lacking for qualitative comparison. (R4)
- **Lack of essential ablations and baselines.** For Task 1, the gain relative to distortion loss is not reported. (R3) For Task 2, no performance for random selection, Rule 1, or Rule 2 is provided. (R3) The upper bound using the exact visibility definition (Eq. 2) instead of the predicted one is missing. (R3) Ablation of the FoV grid alone vs. grid+network is not provided; a reviewer notes the grid might be sufficient and asks whether the neural network is necessary. (R2)
- **Efficiency and simplicity concerns.** The use of a dense FoV grid (K‑dimensional) alongside the network leads to a “huge grid” for datasets with hundreds of views, raising feasibility for post‑processing. (R2) Training time compared to original NeRF training is not quantified. (R3) The process requires concurrent training of VPN with NeRF (influence on NeRF training behavior), which is not a pure “post‑training” step. (R4) No details on training procedure or timings are provided. (R2)
- **Clarity lacking in several aspects.** Eq. 2 (visibility definition) needs more explanation or derivation. (R4) It is unclear whether the FoV grid is used only for training or also at inference; if not at inference, the multiplicative term would be missing. (R2) The method for determining visibility and depth thresholds for skipping near‑range points is not explained, nor how it handles scene variability. (R4) The rationale for the view‑selection task is not convincingly motivated. (R4)
- **Missing citations and comparisons with relevant concurrent works.** Prior work on visibility in neural implicit representations (“Neural Rays for Occlusion‑aware Image‑based Rendering”, CVPR 2022) is not cited or discussed. (R1) Also, recent works like BayesRays and active camera placement are highly relevant and should be compared. (R2)
- **Minor quality issues.** Most citations should be parenthesized. (R4) The n‑axis in Figure 2 overlaps, making it hard to read. (R4) Figure 3 lacks PSNR units and is not reader‑friendly. (R4) The introduction misses important citations. (R4) Classifying transparent points in air as low‑visibility is confusing. (R1 – minor issue)
- **Potential holes from averaging visibility.** Eq. 6 trains the network to predict average v(p) across all views; this may lead to holes in rendering, but the authors do not discuss this. (R1)
- **Dataset not released and source unclear.** The paper calls the dataset ObjectScan but does not cite the original ObjectScan dataset; if it is a new dataset, it is not listed as a contribution and release conditions are not stated. (R2)

*Reviewer disagreement:* Two reviewers (R1, R3) are more positive (5/5) and highlight the well‑motivated idea and experimental benefits, while two others (R2, R4) are more critical (3/5) and focus on insufficient evaluation, efficiency overhead, and missing ablations. The merged weaknesses preserve both sets of concerns, with the more positive reviewers also noting minor issues.
