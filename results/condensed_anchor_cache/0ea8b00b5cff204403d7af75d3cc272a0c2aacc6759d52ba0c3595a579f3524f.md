## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper presents a unified framework that generalizes several optimal transport (OT) based adversarial generative models, including OT-loss-based GANs and OT-map-based models (UOTM). The authors analyze the training dynamics of these models—focusing on mode collapse, stability, and the role of the cost function—and propose a scheduled scaling method (UOTM-SD) that gradually shifts from unbalanced OT toward standard OT during training. The work is supported by theoretical results (Theorems 3.1 and 4.1) and empirical evaluations on toy examples and image datasets (CIFAR-10, CelebA-HQ-256), achieving FID scores of 2.51 on CIFAR-10 and 5.99 on CelebA-HQ-256. Reviewers found the framework useful for exposition and comparative insights, but raised concerns about the practical significance, experimental breadth, and clarity of certain claims.

### Strengths

- The paper provides a clear, well-written exposition that unifies existing OT-based adversarial methods, making the comparative analysis easy to follow (Reviewers 2, 4, 5).
- The background knowledge and analysis of existing OT-based methods under the proposed framework are well executed (Reviewer 1).
- Theoretical contributions (Theorems 3.1 and 4.1) consolidate the UOTM method, prove existence/uniqueness, and justify the proposed scheduling approach to address τ‑sensitivity (Reviewers 3, 4).
- The unified view offers interesting insights into the tradeoffs between mode coverage, stability, and perception quality, which may be valuable for future research (Reviewers 2, 5).
- The proposed UOTM-SD scheduling method is shown to improve generative performance and robustness to hyperparameters, achieving best results on CIFAR-10 among OT-based models (except diffusion) and on CelebA-HQ-256 (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- Experiments are well set up to test the hypotheses, with toy experiments clearly illustrating mode collapse and stability differences (Reviewers 2, 5).
- The paper explicitly discusses limitations and future work in the conclusion (Reviewer 4).
- Majority reviewers rated soundness and contribution as 3 (good); one reviewer (R5) gave presentation 4 (excellent).

### Weaknesses

- **Experimental scope and significance**: Experiments are largely limited to low-dimensional toy data and CIFAR-10; the benefits of UOTM-SD are not demonstrated on higher-resolution or more diverse datasets (e.g., FFHQ, CUB, ImageNet) (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). The FID improvements are minor (e.g., 2.51 on CIFAR-10) and lack confidence intervals, making statistical validation weak (Reviewer 2). Because FID is computed on the training set, there is a risk of overfitting (Reviewer 2). A minority reviewer (R1, score 5) stated that the benefit of UOTM-SD is not clearly shown and that the claim of improved robustness to hyperparameters is insufficiently supported.
- **Missing baselines**: The toy experiments (Section 3.2, Figures 5–6) do not include a vanilla GAN or WGAN-GP baseline, making it hard to conclude the advantage of the softplus (SP) function and the scheduling method (Reviewer 2). The experiments also omit comparisons with non-deep-learning OT maps (e.g., An et al. 2019) that provide unique convex solutions (Reviewer 3). One reviewer was surprised that WGAN failed on the first toy problem and asked how many hyperparameter settings were tried and why it failed (Reviewer 5).
- **Scalability and fairness concerns**: The proposed scheduling scheme is a simple idea that has only been shown effective in very low-dimensional settings; its scalability to high-dimensional image domains is questionable (Reviewer 5). The unified framework’s algorithm (Algorithm 1) assumes the latent space and data space have the same dimension (for OT-map models), but GANs typically use a lower-dimensional latent space—this difference in architecture and parametrization may bias the comparisons between OT-loss and OT-map models (Reviewer 2). The Lipschitzness result (Theorem 3.1) is claimed as an advantage, yet WGAN–GP also enforces Lipschitz constraints, so this should be clarified (Reviewer 2).
- **Unified framework’s novelty**: The unified framework is a straightforward generalization of existing adversarial objectives (Equations 4–5) and does not itself provide novel insights or models; UOTM-SD can be derived without this framework (Reviewer 2). This view was echoed by Reviewer 1, who found the analysis good but the overall contribution limited (score 2).
- **Clarity and notation issues**: Several notations are undefined (e.g., Π(μ,ν), D_{ψ_i}) (Reviewer 2). The mapping between g-functions in Algorithm 1 and Ψ_i in Equation 8 is confusing (Reviewer 4). The explanation of WGAN in Section 3.1 incorrectly sets the cost c = 0; the original WGAN uses L1 distance c(x,y)=|x−y| and includes a Lipschitz constraint (Reviewer 3). The same issue appears in the italic sentence after Equation 5 (Reviewer 3). In Algorithm 1, line 3, the symbol X is ambiguous: Y is from real data, z from prior, but T_θ consistently takes x as input while the algorithm shows x and z (Reviewer 1). The difference between Figure 1 and Figure 5 is not explained (Reviewer 1). The color scheme of Figure 5 is hard to read in grayscale (Reviewer 2). Some references miss publication years (Reviewer 2). Differential symbols in integrals should be upright (Reviewer 2). Equations 4 and 5 are presented as objectives but should be framed as optima of objectives (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing ablations and analyses**: The Lipschitz continuity experiments (Figure 6) are only on 2D data; counterparts on image generation are needed (Reviewer 4). The effect of the cost function on mode coverage (page 6) is not straightforwardly explained (Reviewer 1). No qualitative comparisons of UOTM vs. UOTM-SD are provided for CIFAR-10 or toy data (Reviewer 1). The analysis of the framework’s validity on higher-dimensional data (e.g., 128² or 256²) is missing (Reviewer 1).
