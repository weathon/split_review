## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper empirically investigates Bayesian neural networks (BNNs) as surrogate models for Bayesian optimization (BO) in place of standard GPs. The studied inference procedures include HMC, SGHMC, deep ensembles, infinite-width BNNs, linearized Laplace approximations, and deep kernel learning (DKL). Comparisons are performed on synthetic benchmarks, real-world benchmarks, and high-dimensional settings. The main findings are: (i) method ranking is highly problem-dependent, (ii) HMC is the most successful approximate inference for fully stochastic BNNs, (iii) full stochasticity may be unnecessary as DKL is relatively competitive, (iv) deep ensembles perform relatively poorly, and (v) infinite-width BNNs are particularly promising, especially in high dimensions. The experiments also examine the role of network hyperparameters, hybrid models, number of function evaluations, and runtime.

### Strengths
- The methods under comparison are carefully selected to span a wide range of possible BNNs, and experiments are designed to unveil specific insights about relative strengths/weaknesses.
- Synthetic experiments (Figures 1 and 2) nicely illustrate the influence of different factors, and the “Quality of Mean and Uncertainty Estimates” section (mixing mean and uncertainty estimates) is particularly interesting.
- Conclusions such as the competitiveness of DKL and the promising results of infinite-width BNNs in high dimensions are useful for future BO applications.
- The results are of interest to BO researchers and practitioners; the empirical investigation is extensive and carefully planned, supporting many interesting hypotheses (e.g., performance in high dimensions, role of hyperparameters including network architecture).
- The paper sets a gold standard for clarity and writing.
- The topic is interesting and important for the community, and the presentation of theory and experiments is well presented and sound.

### Weaknesses
- There is no discussion of the connection to neural bandits and NTK-based BO, a relevant line of work. Papers such as [1] (Sample-Then-Optimize Batch Neural Thompson Sampling), [2] (Empirical Analysis of Representation Learning and Exploration in Neural Kernel Bandits), and [3] (Kernel Regression with Infinite-Width Neural Networks) share findings (e.g., deep ensembles’ poor performance explained by lack of principled exploration) that could corroborate or contrast with this study.
- The number of trials per experimental setup is only 5, which is too low. This is evident from many heavily overlapping confidence intervals, especially in Figure 6.
- The hyperparameter tuning procedures differ fundamentally between GP and BNN models: GPs optimize hyperparameters per iteration via marginal likelihood, while HMC and other BNNs use an iterated grid search that requires multiple complete BO trials to evaluate a single hyperparameter set. This raises questions about the fairness of comparing a standard GP setup against an (a priori) optimized BNN.
- The paper does not discuss the interaction between the surrogate model and the acquisition function, which is a critical component of BO.
- Although the authors note that runtime is less relevant when function evaluations are expensive, an experiment assuming fast function evaluations is needed to see whether the ranking of methods holds under that scenario.
- The study is performed on a small number of datasets; there is no guarantee that these findings extrapolate to new problems.
- Some insights are interesting but not surprising, and the effective impact is questionable. It is unclear how these findings could lead to state-of-the-art algorithms in drug discovery, active learning, material science, or hyperparameter optimization.
- There is no discussion of the regularization effect on surrogates (e.g., what happens when DKL or BNNs are regularized via useful priors).
- The paper does not compare to more recent methods such as PFN4BO [1] (Müller et al., *PFNs Are Flexible Models for Real-World Bayesian Optimization*), nor does it ablate how DKL performance is affected by regularization approaches ([2]: Lotfi et al. on Bayesian model selection, [3]: Patacchiola et al. on Bayesian meta-learning with deep kernels).
- One reviewer (rating 6) is notably less positive than the others (both rating 8), raising concerns about limited dataset scope, lack of acquisition function analysis, and questionable impact. This reviewer also notes that the paper could be strengthened by discussing the validity of conclusions under alternative interpretations (e.g., GPs with the I-BNN kernel might still be the top performer, undermining the case for non-GP BNNs). 
- Minor: When referring to the appendix, specific subsections (e.g., “Appendix D.2”) should be referenced instead of just “Appendix D”.
