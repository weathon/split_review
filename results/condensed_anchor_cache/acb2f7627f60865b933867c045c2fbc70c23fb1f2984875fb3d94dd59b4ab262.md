## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PaRa (Parameter Rank Reduction), a method for personalizing text-to-image diffusion models by explicitly controlling the rank of model parameters to constrain the generation space into a well-balanced target space. This approach balances personalization (alignment with target images) with text editability (alignment with diverse prompts). PaRa is more parameter-efficient than LoRA (2× fewer learnable parameters) and supports single/multi-subject generation, combination of multiple personalized concepts, and stable single-image editing without noise inversion. Experiments show advantages over existing fine-tuning methods. The idea is creative and the paper is generally well-structured, though some reviewers found the writing needed improvement and comparisons to the latest SOTA are missing.

### Strengths
- Highly innovative idea: controlling the rank of diffusion model parameters to restrict the generation space for personalization (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- Well-structured and easy to understand (Reviewers 1,3,4). Reviewer 2 noted writing should be improved.
- Detailed explanations of the introduced learnable low-rank parameters (Reviewer 1).
- Extensive and well-designed experiments with sufficient data, demonstrating effectiveness in single-subject generation, multi-subject generation, single-image editing, and compatibility with other modules (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- Significant parameter efficiency: PaRa achieves 2× fewer learnable parameters than LoRA while achieving better target image alignment (Reviewers 1,3).
- Effectively combines rank reduction with editability, especially diversity of generated images (Reviewer 2).
- Simple, effective, and flexible fine-tuning method (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- Methodology section lacks an organized overview of the model framework; missing explanation of how the introduced [V] tokens interact with text embeddings; a diagram showing data flow and parameter interactions would make concepts more intuitive (Reviewer 1).
- Claim of state-of-the-art in the introduction (lines 110–111) is not fully supported: no comparison with encoder-based personalization methods, and fine-tuning baselines used (e.g., LoRA) are not the latest – missing comparisons with LyCoris, DiffuseKronA, and methods incorporating more advanced regularization or adaptive learning rates (Reviewers 1,3).
- In Section 3.1, the paper should clarify the scale of training data, fine-tuning time, optimizer, and learning rate schedule for the experiments (Reviewer 1).
- No computational efficiency analysis (FLOPs, memory usage) to support the claim of higher parameter efficiency over other methods (Reviewer 1).
- Image editing results may show loss of fine texture details; a more detailed discussion of the trade-offs between editability and texture preservation is needed (Reviewer 1).
- The foundational differences from LoRA are not clearly explained; it is unclear how the authors arrived at the current solution given both methods use matrix decomposition (Reviewer 2).
- Writing should be improved to highlight contributions and how the solution differs from existing techniques (Reviewer 2).
- SSIM is used for image diversity evaluation, but it focuses on pixel-level differences rather than semantic-level differences (e.g., position difference of the same instance may contribute more to SSIM than an extra accessory) (Reviewer 2).
- Relationship between editability and the optimal rank of B is not addressed; it is an open question whether learning a good low-rank B in a one-shot manner is always possible for reasonable editing (Reviewer 2).
- Robustness of the model to different one-shot training pairs needs investigation (Reviewer 2).
- The paper should provide a failure case analysis and discuss limitations of PaRa (Reviewer 2).
- Hyper-parameters for compared methods (e.g., rank of LoRA, fine-tuning steps) may affect alignment; more clarification is needed on how these are chosen to ensure fair comparison in the experiments (Reviewer 4).
