## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a scalable training method for GNNs using Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) to sketch the input feature matrix and convolution matrix, combined with count‑sketch and LSH, avoiding online updates of sketches. The method (PGNN) reduces memory and time complexity. Theoretical optimality for linearized GNN (SGC) and error bounds are provided. Experiments on five datasets (Cora, Citeseer, ogbn‑arxiv, Reddit, Products) compare against Sketch‑GNN, Graph‑SAINT, VQ‑GNN, and several GNN backbones.

### Strengths
- Enhancing GNN efficiency is an important and timely topic.
- The idea of applying POD for GNN training is novel; it has not been considered in this training regime before.
- Theoretical justification (optimal projection matrix, error bounds) is provided, lending theoretical soundness.
- The method achieves high compression on ogbn‑arxiv and Reddit with competitive accuracy when compared against Sketch‑GNN under less aggressive compression.
- A wide set of classical GNN datasets (Cora, Citeseer, ogbn‑arxiv, Reddit) and baseline comparisons (Sketch‑GNN, Graph‑SAINT, VQ‑GNN) are included.
- One reviewer found the paper easy to understand, though others disagreed on clarity (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and presentation**: The methodology section is hard to follow; moving the pseudocode from the appendix to the main paper would help. The experiment section applies PGNN to different GNN backbones (SGC, GCN, SAGE) on different datasets with different baselines, making comparisons confusing; conclusions per experiment are hard to locate. The paper’s format prevents text selection (reviewer 2). Citations appear as normal text; they should be parenthesized. Appendix A was reported missing (reviewer 2). Figure 3’s y‑axis lacks reference numbers.
- **Experimental insufficiency and scale**: Several reviewers deem the experiments insufficient. The work uses small datasets (Cora, Citeseer); billion‑scale graphs (ogbn‑papers100M, ogbn‑mag240M, igb‑het, igb‑hom) would better demonstrate efficiency claims. Important results are missing—for example, GCN and GAT results on the Products dataset are not shown. The evaluation is focused heavily on the SGC baseline; when compared against nonlinear backbones (Table 6), PGNN does not fare well. No runtime measurements are reported for the LSH overhead, which is non‑trivial.
- **Performance gains are marginal**: The speedup obtained by sacrificing model quality is not great. Table 3 results are within error of Sketch‑GNN, showing no nontrivial improvement. In Table 4, accuracies mostly lag or only minimally improve. The authors should increase the sketch ratio until PGNN outperforms competitors to reveal the trade‑off curve (same issue in Table 6).
- **Novelty concerns**: The approach is essentially a low‑rank projector plus count‑sketches; the update rules closely resemble those of Sketch‑GNN except that POD replaces polynomial tensor sketches for handling nonlinearity. The use of LSH (SimHash) from SLIDE is questionable: SLIDE employs LSH in the *final* layer to correlate dot‑product magnitude with class logits, but here it is applied to intermediate layers, risking the loss of small but important activations. LSH relies on dense Gaussian matrices and even SLIDE acknowledges that learnable projections are necessary; sneaking it in for optimized forward/backward passes is a non‑trivial engineering task that should be timed.
- **Theoretical contribution is weak**: Theorem 1 does not clearly define “optimal projection matrix” (must consult appendix). The result is a close relative of the Eckhart‑Young‑Mirsky theorem; the appendix recycles lemmas from Ding et al. (2022) and the error bound follows routine sketching theory calculations. Overall, the theory is considered weak.
- **Missing comparison**: The method should be compared against GraphBolt’s fully GPU‑enabled GraphSAGE implementation, which is one of the best available for runtime efficiency.
- **Disagreement**: One reviewer (score 5, confidence 3) found the performance improvements marginal but still rated the paper positively, whereas the other three reviewers (score 3, confidence 4–5) considered the experiments, novelty, and theoretical depth insufficient.
