## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes optimizable polynomial relaxations for ReLU activation in neural network verification, ensuring soundness by shifting the polynomial up/down by the maximum soundness violation. Two methods are introduced: α-Poly (full forward propagation of polynomial zonotopes) and PolyCROWN (polynomial relaxation in the first layer, then linear back-substitution). Evaluation is performed on ACAS Xu (45 networks, d=5) and MNIST (3 fully-connected networks with 2/4/6 layers of 256 neurons). The paper compares to α-Neurify and α-CROWN. On ACAS Xu, PolyCROWN obtains bounds 18% tighter on average for properties with index ≥100, but 6% worse on the rest. On MNIST with adversarial patches, improvements increase with depth and are best for patches of 10–40 pixels. No comparison is made to state-of-the-art methods such as β-CROWN, Multi-Neuron relaxations, Branch-and-Bound (BaB), or GCP. The optimization cost is not evaluated, fairness of the empirical comparison is questioned, scalability is limited to small networks and low input dimensions, and presentation issues reduce clarity.

### Strengths
- The topic (certified adversarial robustness) is of high importance (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- The idea is sound and novel – this appears to be the first paper considering optimizable polynomial relaxations for neural network verification (Reviewer 3).
- The approach to ensure soundness by translating the polynomial (adding an upward/downward shift) is simple and elegant, permitting unconstrained optimization of coefficients (Reviewer 4).
- The paper gives a good introduction and explains technical details clearly (Reviewer 1).
- The empirical study shows that optimizing polynomial parameters can improve bound tightness in multiple settings (Reviewers 1, 4).
- On ACAS Xu, PolyCROWN improves over α-CROWN for many properties (18% tighter on index ≥100). On MNIST, tightness improves with depth for adversarial patches (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing key related work and baselines:** No comparison to state-of-the-art verification methods that dominate the VNN-COMP competition, including multi-neuron constraints (Singh et al. NeurIPS 2019, PRIMA, etc.) and Branch-and-Bound methods (β-CROWN, GCP, MN-BaB). The baseline α-CROWN has been superseded (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Scalability is extremely limited:** Tested only on tiny networks (ACAS Xu d=5, MNIST FC with 256 neurons) with at most 25 free input dimensions. Not evaluated on standard benchmarks such as CIFAR10, ImageNet, or ℓ∞ robustness problems where inputs have 784 (MNIST) to 12288 (TinyImageNet) dimensions. The main bottleneck and limitations are not discussed (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Empirical results are mixed and not compelling:** On ACAS Xu, PolyCROWN is worse on half the properties. Improvements on MNIST are small. Even in the favorable forward propagation (α-Poly) setting, the resulting bounds are much looser than those of back-substitution methods (Poly/α-CROWN), casting doubt on practical relevance (Reviewer 4). Optimisable linear approximations often perform better overall (Reviewer 3). No comparison to standard ℓ∞ certification (Reviewer 2).
- **Optimization cost not evaluated:** The paper does not analyze the overhead of optimizing the polynomial coefficients, which must be done per unstable neuron and per input set. It is unclear whether verification times reported include optimization time (Reviewers 1, 3). Per-iteration costs are higher for polynomial propagation (exponential in depth for α-Poly) than for linear methods (linear in depth) (Reviewer 4).
- **Fairness of empirical comparison is questionable:** Baselines (α-Neurify, α-CROWN) were reimplemented; only correctness, not performance, was verified (Reviewer 4). Termination for ACAS Xu uses optimization iteration counts with different thresholds per method, and none for α-Poly, which may bias the comparison due to vastly different per-iteration costs (Reviewer 4). The frequency of optimization steps is not reported. No comparison to the original α-CROWN implementation or to BaB methods is provided (Reviewer 4).
- **Paper is not fully self-contained, and several details are missing:** The use of implicit differentiation (Blondel et al. 2022) after Equation 17 is not explained; the exact gradient equation is not given, and it is unclear why implicit differentiation is more efficient than solving the analytical minimizer (a single division for 2nd-order polynomials) (Reviewers 2, 4). The list of claimed contributions is unclear (Reviewer 2). The background on polynomial zonotopes is deferred to the appendix; it should be in the main body (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation issues:**
  - The abstract overclaims: “polynomial approximations can overcome the loss of precision induced by linear approximations” – the two domains appear incomparable for most linear relaxations (Reviewer 2).
  - Section 2 is unnecessarily large and could be shortened (Reviewer 3).
  - Figure 5 appears to illustrate results solely from prior work, not contributions (Reviewer 4).
  - Figures 1 and 2 use inconsistent axis scaling, which may mislead about comparisons (Reviewer 4).
  - Figure 6 legend is inconsistent (Reviewer 4).
  - Figure 9 would benefit from a logarithmic y-scale (Reviewer 2).
  - Tightness is never formally defined (Reviewer 4); it is unclear whether it is the sum of output bound widths used in Table 1/Figure 3 or something else (Reviewer 2).
  - Equation 1 quantifies over all y (semantically redundant) and the semantics of evaluating a network on a set are undefined (Reviewer 4).
  - Missing closing bracket in the first paragraph of Section 2.1 (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited delta from prior work:** The overall contribution relative to existing optimizable linear relaxations is small (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Several relevant ablation studies and experiments are missing, leaving open questions:** 
  - Do higher-degree monomials yield tighter bounds in practice? (Reviewer 2)
  - Can one train certifiably robust networks using this method? (Reviewer 2)
  - Why is implicit differentiation used (and is it more efficient than the analytical solution)? Does it give different behavior at border extrema? (Reviewer 4)
  - Are there regularization choices? (Reviewer 2)
  - How are the polynomials initialized? (Reviewer 2)
  - What do PolyCROWN-opt and α-CROWN-opt refer to? (Reviewer 2)
  - How were the examples in Table 1 selected? (Reviewer 2)
  - How fast is the reimplementation compared to the original? (Reviewer 2)
  - Can the method handle CIFAR10 networks? (Reviewers 2, 4)
  - How does it compare to complete verification or multi-neuron splitting? (Reviewers 2, 4)
  - How were step caps for ACAS Xu selected? (Reviewer 2)
  - What hardware were experiments run on? (Reviewers 2, 4)
