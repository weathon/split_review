## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents ControlSpeech, a text-to-speech system that combines zero-shot speaker cloning with zero-shot style control using a few seconds of audio prompt and a textual style description. It employs bidirectional attention and mask-based parallel decoding over a discrete decoupling codec space (FACodec) to capture timbre, content, and style representations. To address the many-to-many mapping problem in textual style control, the authors propose the Style Mixture Semantic Density (SMSD) module based on Gaussian mixture density networks and noise perturbation. They contribute the VccmDataset and ControlToolkit. Experimental results show comparable or state-of-the-art performance in style controllability, timbre similarity, audio quality, robustness, and generalizability. Reviewer ratings range from 3 to 8, with one reviewer (R1) very positive, one (R4) negative, and three (R2, R3, R5) moderately positive but with substantive concerns.

### Strengths
- **Simultaneous zero-shot speaker cloning and style control**: The system is the first to integrate both capabilities in a single TTS model, allowing independent control of timbre, content, and style (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **SMSD module**: The Style Mixture Semantic Density module, based on Gaussian mixture density networks and noise perturbation, effectively mitigates the many-to-many mapping issue in textual style control, enhancing both accuracy and diversity (R1, R2, R3, R5).
- **Disentangled representation**: By leveraging a pre-trained decoupling codec (FACodec), ControlSpeech enables flexible and independent manipulation of speech attributes (R1, R2, R3).
- **Comprehensive evaluation and resources**: Extensive experiments cover multiple metrics and scenarios (out-of-domain tests, ablation studies). The creation of VccmDataset and ControlToolkit (including source code, dataset, and replicated baselines) promotes reproducibility (R1, R5).
- **Clear and well-organized presentation**: The paper is easy to follow, with informative figures and a logical structure (R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Overclaiming of novelty**: Multiple reviewers point out that AudioBox (Meta, 2023) already supports simultaneous control of timbre and speaking style via textual prompts, and NaturalSpeech3 supports audio style prompts. The claim that ControlSpeech is the first such system is inaccurate (R4, R5). R2 adds that the innovation is average—the architecture is largely based on existing zero-shot TTS and style control methods.
- **Style control effectiveness not convincingly demonstrated**:  
  - In the demo’s “One timbre with multiple styles” section, samples 4‑6 show no audible difference in style, and samples 7‑9 have style poorly related to the textual description. This undermines the core claim of style controllability (R2, R4).  
  - The system relies on label-generated style descriptions for both training and testing; robustness to free-form, user-provided style prompts is not assessed (R5).
- **Incomplete analysis of disentanglement and information leakage**:  
  - There is no test of whether zero-shot TTS itself possesses inherent style control ability from the speech prompt, and no analysis of how much the final style is influenced by the speech prompt versus the style prompt (R2).  
  - The arrangement of Yp, Ya, and Yc in the codec (Ycodec = concat(Ys, Yc), Ys = concat(Yp, Ya)) and the use of unmasked tokens in mask-based parallel decoding may cause style information from the speech prompt to leak, reducing correlation with the style prompt (R2).  
  - In timbre cloning experiments, ControlSpeech’s timbre similarity is not optimal; in pitch control experiments it is also not optimal. This may indicate that style and timbre are not fully decoupled (R2).  
  - The use of ground-truth wavs as voice prompts in the “Style control for unseen speakers” and “Control of unseen styles” demos risks leaking style information from the prompt, conflating the measured style control (R5).
- **Missing baseline comparisons and metrics**:  
  - The paper should include AudioBox and NaturalSpeech3 in the baseline systems (R4, R5).  
  - In style control tasks, gender accuracy metrics are missing (R5).  
  - No evaluation of age or gender control (as in PromptTTS) is provided (R3).  
  - The claim that each Gaussian in the mixture density networks represents a specific speaking style lacks textual or experimental support (R5).
- **Insufficient experimental validation of independent attribute modification**: Experiments are needed to verify that modifying one attribute (e.g., speech rate) does not affect others (e.g., speaker similarity) (R3). Reviewer 2 also requests experimental explanations of the correlation between synthesized style and style prompt versus prompt audio style.
- **Presentation and formatting issues**:  
  - The Related Work section should be in the main text, not the appendix (R3, R5).  
  - Equations are presented as plain text instead of LaTeX (R3).  
  - Section 3.1 states “the dashed box represents frame-level features” but the text encoder’s output should be phoneme-level (R5).  
  - Section 3.2.2 uses “the aligned text representations” ambiguously—it is unclear whether “text” refers to content prompts or style prompts (R5).  
  - In Figure 1, the input of the content encoder and style encoder within FACodec should be audios, not texts (R5).  
  - In Figure 2(a), the SMSD Module is labeled both as “frozen” and “trainable,” leading to confusion (R5).
- **Dataset scale and robustness**: The VccmDataset is limited in scale compared to datasets used by large-scale TTS systems, which may affect generalizability (R1). The system’s scalability and minimum data requirements for satisfactory performance are not explored (R1).
- **SMSD training details unclear**:  
  - The style extractor used for training SMSD (with L_SMSD) is not described: is it trainable? Does L_SMSD update it? (R2, R5)  
  - The style extractor extracts a global style representation from long speech; it may be insufficient for complex, dynamic style prompts (e.g., “a woman starts to speak softly… then becomes more emotional…”) (R2).  
  - Sampling from Gaussian distributions and noise perturbation increases diversity but may negatively affect the consistency of the synthesized style with the style prompt (R2).  
- **Clarifications needed about FACodec and architecture**:  
  - Does the decoupling come from the codec generator, the codec itself, or another module? (R3)  
  - If global style representations are in ℝᵈ, why use Q-K-V attention modules instead of conditional normalization layers for fusion? (R5)  
  - Can global style representations be derived from audio style prompts for testing? (R5)  
  - How are style prompts obtained for timbre cloning tasks? (R5)  
  - How does ControlSpeech handle contradictions between the style text prompt and the speaker timbre prompt? (R3)  
  - Does FACodec ensure complete decoupling among content, style, and timbre? (R4)

*Minority positive viewpoint (R1):* This reviewer rated the paper 8 and found the novelty, technical depth, and resource contributions strong. They only noted the missing multilingual baselines and limited dataset scale as weaknesses, and did not share the concerns about style control persuasiveness, decoupling, or overclaiming raised by other reviewers.
