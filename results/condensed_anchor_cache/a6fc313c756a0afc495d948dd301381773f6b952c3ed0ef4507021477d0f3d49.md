## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes SlimTTT, a resource-adaptive test-time training framework that uses slimmable neural networks to handle dynamically varying computational budgets during inference. The method introduces Width-enhance Contrastive Learning (WCL), Logits Consistency Regularization (LCR), and Global Feature Alignment (GFA) to maintain representation consistency across subnetworks of different widths. Experiments on ImageNet-C, CIFAR10-C, CIFAR10.1, and CIFAR100-C with ResNet, MobileNet, and ViT backbones show improvements over prior TTT methods under equal inference FLOPs. Reviewers generally appreciate the new problem framing of resource-constrained TTT and the comprehensive experimental setup, but raise substantial concerns about novelty relative to prior slimmable network work, fairness of comparisons, missing dynamic evaluation, and insufficient analysis of training-time efficiency and online adaptation.

### Strengths
- The paper highlights an important, practical challenge of dynamically changing resource constraints during test-time training, and uses slimmable networks to enable adaptive accuracy-efficiency trade-offs without retraining for different devices.
- The proposed integration of WCL, LCR, and GFA is well-motivated and achieves significant performance improvements over prior source-free unsupervised domain adaptation methods (e.g., SHOT).
- The finding that different width sub-networks capture complementary views of the input, which can enhance TTT performance, is interesting and provides new insights.
- The method demonstrates superior performance compared to baselines when evaluated under the same computational budget (FLOPs), with over 70% reduction in inference complexity in some cases.
- The empirical study is detailed, covering four datasets, three backbones (ResNet, MobileNet, ViT), four switchable widths (1.0×, 0.75×, 0.5×, 0.25×), and reports computation cost alongside accuracy for fair comparison. Ablation experiments support each design component.
- The framework considers every part of the image classification pipeline and is comprehensive in its design.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns:** Several reviewers note that the core idea is essentially applying slimmable networks (Yu et al., 2018) to the TTT setting, and that unique challenges of doing so under test-time training (vs. training-time training) are not clearly articulated. Previous works have studied unsupervised slimmable networks and the idea that different sub-networks capture different image features.
- **Fairness of comparisons:**
  - In Table 1, other methods are not tested with ResNet-50 at different widths, making the comparison inequitable.
  - All compared methods should be evaluated under the same setting (online or offline). If baselines like TENT are online while SlimTTT is offline, the comparison is unfair. The paper does not clarify this for all baselines.
  - Comparisons with more resource-efficient online TTA methods such as EATA (ICML 2022) are missing and would strengthen the evaluation.
- **No evaluation with dynamically changing resources:** The paper’s primary motivation is varying inference budgets, yet experiments only test fixed widths. Reviewers request simulations where resource availability fluctuates frequently during test-time training, analyzing performance sensitivity and error accumulation over time.
- **Training cost and efficiency:**
  - The term “efficient” is misleading because SlimTTT focuses on inference efficiency; training costs are higher (multiple forward/backward passes per sample). The paper does not report training time, GPU memory, or convergence rate comparisons for different architectures, making it difficult to assess practical deployability.
  - Table 5 shows training cost comparable to or lower than TTT++ and TTAC, which seems contradictory given the multiple passes required. An explanation is needed.
- **Missing analyses:**
  - The paper only finetunes pre-trained slimmable networks on ImageNet and ImageNet-C; it is unclear how the method transfers to other source datasets or backbones.
  - Some basic assumptions (e.g., that bigger-width networks always contain smaller-width attention information, that alignments to the supernet work for coarse classes with multiple visual patterns) are not justified or tested.
  - There is a paradox between enforcing consistency across subnetworks (to make them focus on similar features) and the ensembling strategy in Section 3.4 (which relies on diverse predictions). The paper does not explain why this apparent contradiction still improves performance.
- **Questions raised (overlapping with criticisms):**
  - Does SlimTTT work well in an online setting, and how does its online performance compare with TENT, EATA, etc.?
  - Why is the training cost in Table 5 comparable or faster than prior TTT methods when the proposed method requires multiple forward/backward passes?
  - For coarse-grained classes (e.g., “vehicle” containing visually dissimilar subclasses), does aligning all subnetworks to the supernet cause loss of instance-level features, and does this affect contrastive learning?
