## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SNOWS, a one-shot post-training pruning framework that optimizes a global reconstruction objective accounting for nonlinear activations deep in the network, solved via Hessian-free second-order optimization (exact Newton descent steps with conjugate gradients without storing the full Hessian). It is designed to adjust weights of any pre-existing sparse mask to better leverage deep feature nonlinearities. Experiments on CIFAR10/100 and Mini-ImageNet show state-of-the-art results on residual networks (ResNet) and Vision Transformers (ViT/B-16, ViT/L-16 up to 304M parameters). Reviewer enthusiasm varies: scores 8,5,8,6,6, with one reviewer (R2) initially rating 3 then raising to 5, citing significant organization concerns, and R5 raising from 5 to 6 after rebuttal. R1 and R3 are most positive (8), R4 and R5 moderately positive (6).

### Strengths
- **Novel and well-motivated global reconstruction objective.** The idea of optimizing a nonlinear objective that accounts for deep feature representations (not just layer-wise least-squares) is novel and interesting. The use of Hessian-free conjugate gradients to solve the resulting optimization is elegant and theoretically sound.
- **Significant improvements over existing one-shot pruning methods.** The method shows clear gains over OBC and layer-wise reconstruction baselines on image classification benchmarks (CIFAR, Mini-ImageNet). Improvements are also demonstrated when applied on top of existing sparse mask selection algorithms for N:M pruning (e.g., MP), highlighting practical utility.
- **Universal applicability to CNNs and ViTs.** The framework is shown to work on both convolutional (ResNet) and Vision Transformer architectures (ViT-B/16, ViT-L/16), and provides new insights for pruning ViT models.
- **Efficiency without storing the full Hessian.** The Hessian-free approach allows exact Newton steps without computing or storing the Hessian, making it feasible for large networks. Code is publicly available.
- **Clear writing and well-articulated technical details.** The formulation, update rule (Newton), and illustrative examples in the Supplementary are clearly explained. Main claims are supported by experiments. (Note: one reviewer [R2] disagrees on clarity, see Weaknesses.)
- **Applicable to any pre-existing sparse mask.** SNOWS can be seamlessly applied on top of prior methods to refine weights, leveraging nonlinearities deep in the network.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with relevant baselines and efficiency metrics.**
  - No comparison with recent (2024) one-shot pruning methods that also use global optimization or second-order information (reviewer cites [1],[2] as examples). For unstructured pruning, the paper should evaluate against [4], which achieves >90% sparsity without dropping clean performance and refutes the paper's definition of one-shot pruning.
  - No latency or inference speed-up comparisons (actual runtime, not just FLOPs) with other pruning methods across architectures (ResNet50, MobileNet, ViT) and sparsity levels. This is critical since the paper's motivation includes speed gain in pruning.
  - No efficiency metrics (runtime, memory usage) for pruning itself, especially compared to other second-order methods. The analysis of how computational cost scales with K (number of joint layers) and network size is missing. Memory requirements for different K values are not reported, which is crucial for resource-constrained devices.
  - Missing ablation: does Fisher matrix-based approximation of the Hessian (e.g., Martens 2020) or adaptive methods (AdaGrad, Adam) work as well as the true Hessian? The emphasis on true Hessian is not experimentally supported.
  - Does retraining after pruning improve performance further? (Question from R1)
- **Unclear motivations and missing clarity in presentation.**
  - The motivation for the improved layer-wise model (Eq. 3) is unclear: why are subsequent operations \(f^{l:l+K}\) necessary? The logic behind Fig. 2 needs more explanation and captions.
  - The paper lacks a Conclusions and Limitations section.
  - The "Experimental setup" (Section 5) is too long and mixes setup with results; better structure recommended (separate results subsection, perhaps per network type).
  - There are two "Experimental setup" parts at the same level as network sections, causing confusion.
  - Inaccuracies: the paper refers to "ImageNet-1k" but actually uses Mini-ImageNet (10k samples vs 1.2M); should replace with MiniImagenet-1k.
  - Typos: "Like retraining approaches" → "Like retraining, approaches" (Section 1); "network parammeters \(\mathbf{W} = (\mathbf{W}^1, ..., \mathbf{W}^1)\)" should be \(\mathbf{W} = (\mathbf{W}^1, ..., \mathbf{W}^L)\) (Section 3).
  - Readability issues: "minimize (7)" should be "minimize Eq. 7"; equations on separate lines should be enumerated (lines 214, 382, 384); Figure 3 (line 472-484) should be a Table; Figure 4 text too small; define MP metric with citation when first used.
- **Conceptual error in defining one-shot pruning.** The introduction defines one-shot pruning as methods that do not require retraining after pruning, but some fine-tuning is often required (references [1],[2],[3] in review). This definitional issue may affect the claimed advantage.
- **Lack of breakdown for K iterations.** The experimental results should explicitly note the number of K-iterations used in each table/figure caption. An ablation on time complexity of K iterations is needed.
- **Reviewer disagreement on presentation and readiness.** One reviewer (R2) found the paper not ready for publication in current form due to organizational issues and conciseness problems, while other reviewers found it clear. The writing in Section 4 (method) can be more concise to leave space for experiment details.
