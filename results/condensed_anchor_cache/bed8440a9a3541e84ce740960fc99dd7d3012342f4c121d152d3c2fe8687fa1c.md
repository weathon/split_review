## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a transfer-based black-box attack that improves adversarial example transferability through model augmentation. The method (GRASP) applies channel pruning to a surrogate model to create multiple pruned sub-models, uses knowledge distillation to recover accuracy, and adds gradient regularization on intermediate feature maps to reduce variance. Training adversarial examples on the ensemble of augmented models is intended to reduce overfitting on redundant channels. Evaluations on a subset of 2000 images from ImageNet across several CNN and Vision Transformer architectures show improvements in untargeted and targeted attack success rates compared to several baselines.

### Strengths
- The paper studies an important and timely topic (transferability of adversarial examples).
- The paper is generally easy to read and follow.
- The method is intuitive: using channel pruning to reduce reliance on redundant channels is a novel idea for increasing attack transferability.
- The combination of channel pruning with knowledge distillation to recover accuracy of pruned models is a reasonable design.
- Regularizing important feature maps to reduce gradient variance is a useful additional component.
- The attack transfers across multiple network architectures, including from CNNs to transformers.
- Experimental results show that the proposed method outperforms baseline attacks on several target models.

### Weaknesses
- **Formatting and presentation issues:** Tables 1 and 2 extend beyond the page margins. Tables 3 and 4 contain a blank row before the first result row. The paper contains numerous grammatical errors (e.g., a period in the middle of sentences, incorrectly capitalized words – line 354 "table" should be "Table"; line 355 has ",,"; line 252 "running" should be "pruning"; the sentence on lines 226–227 is incomplete).
- **Insufficient experimental validation:** Experiments are conducted on only one surrogate model (ResNet50) in Tables 3 and 4. The targeted networks differ between the two tables, making it impossible to fairly compare the claimed superiority. The method should be compared against other ensemble-based augmentation methods (e.g., Tang et al., CVPR 2024; Chen et al., ICCV 2023) to ensure gains are not simply due to ensembling multiple networks. Only one dataset (ImageNet subset) is used, limiting generalizability. More ablation studies are needed to support the claims.
- **Lack of clarity in motivation and underlying assumptions:** The authors state that adversarial examples "overfit" on highly repetitive redundant channels, but provide no experiment or proof that this occurs. It is unclear why an attack would exploit features that are not useful for classification. The claim that increasing the pruning rate degrades transferability (core to the method) is not explained or validated.
- **Missing technical details and incomplete specification:**
    - The network structure of the self-supervision (SS) module is not defined.
    - The self-supervision loss \(L_{ss}\) (lines 295–296) maximizes similarity of all positive and negative pairs, which is inconsistent with standard contrastive learning practice.
    - In Section 4.3, how the intermediate layer for gradient regularization is selected is not specified.
    - The overall loss function includes minimizing \(L_{ce}\), which conflicts with the adversarial goal of maximizing \(L_{ce}\) (crafting adversarial examples). How the five loss terms are jointly optimized and how hyperparameters are tuned is unclear.
    - The hyperparameters used in Section 4.3 are not explicitly given.
    - The choice of the three transformations for contrastive learning is not justified.
- **Weak support for key claims:**
    - The claim that gradient regularization balances channel importance is weakly supported: Figure 5 shows minimal change in attack success rate when regularizing different layers.
    - The use of L2-norm distance for channel pruning (lines 255–257) is noted to be sensitive to outlier noise; its consistency across different surrogate models is not evaluated.
    - No analysis is provided on whether the method's improvements stem from channel pruning specifically, rather than from other pruning methods (kernel, block).
- **Comparison issues:** The proposed GRASP method is not directly comparable to other model augmentation baselines because it incorporates knowledge distillation and gradient regularization that those baselines do not use. The paper does not discuss loss-based transfer methods (e.g., Naseer et al., ICCV 2021; Zhao et al., CVPR 2023) in the related work.
- **Lack of discussion of limitations:** The computational cost due to model augmentation and the trade-off between transferability and number of pruned models are not addressed.
- **Terminology concerns:** Several terms are used subjectively without rigorous definition (e.g., "redundant channels", "denser information"). One reviewer found the paper lacking in novelty, noting that existing studies (Bai et al., ICLR 2021; Borkar et al., CVPR 2020; Tramer et al., ICLR 2018) already analyze channel-wise pruning for robustness and model augmentation for transferability, and the combination presented offers limited new insight. Another reviewer was particularly critical of the lack of validation and insufficient experiments.
