## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper addresses the task of generating vector diagrams from hand-drawn sketches by introducing the SkeTikZ dataset (3,231 pairs of sketches, TikZ codes, and rendered reference images) and the ImgTikZ model, a vision-language model that combines a 6.7B DeepSeek Coder LLM with a SigLIP vision encoder via an adapter (linear layer) and LoRA. Data augmentation (synthetic notebook backgrounds, Gaussian noise, brightness/contrast changes, distortion) and a multi-candidate inference strategy are used to boost performance. ImgTikZ achieves performance comparable to GPT-4o despite its modest size. The dataset is publicly released. Reviewers’ confidence in the findings varies (mean confidence ~4.5/5), and one reviewer (rating 8) is notably more positive about the contribution than the others (ratings 6, 5, 6). All reviewers agree the dataset is valuable, but there are significant concerns about novelty, missing comparisons, clarity, and potential hallucinations.

### Strengths

- The SkeTikZ dataset is a novel and important contribution that fills a critical gap in publicly available data for sketch-to-diagram conversion. It will greatly help the community in query-to-technical diagram generation and serves as a benchmark for evaluating VLMs (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- ImgTikZ, despite its modest size (6.7B LLM + SigLIP encoder), shows performance comparable to much larger models like GPT-4o, highlighting an efficient architecture and effective training strategies (Reviewer 1).
- Data augmentation (notebook backgrounds, noise, brightness/contrast, distortion) and multi-candidate inference substantially improve output quality (Reviewer 1; though Reviewer 3 and 4 consider this contribution minor).
- A human evaluation is adopted to assess generation quality (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is written clearly; the proposed method is simple, intuitive, and without unnecessary forced contributions. The authors also highlight concurrent works, which helps readers understand the literature (Reviewers 2, 4; Reviewer 2 specifically appreciates the mention of Belouadi et al., 2024).
- Extensive experiments are conducted, providing valuable insights into the method’s effectiveness and limitations compared to other VLMs (Reviewer 3).
- The SkeTikZ dataset is fully open-sourced, supporting future research (Reviewers 1, 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Missing comparisons with other open-source VLMs:** No attempt was made to train or compare with other open-source multimodal large models. This undermines the claim that ImgTikZ is efficient relative to the state-of-the-art (Reviewers 1, 3; Reviewer 3 also asks whether competitors were fine-tuned on SkeTikZ).
- **Claim of unexplored task not fully justified:** While the authors state sketch-to-diagram generation has not been explored before, existing works (e.g., Belouadi et al., 2024; also [a][b][c][d]) on TikZ code generation from sketches are not discussed. It is unclear how this work differs from or improves upon Gervais et al., 2024 (LaTeX generation from screenshots) and whether those methods could be applied with minimal modifications (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Dataset creation caveat – hallucination risk:** Sketches are drawn by humans from rendered images, a lossy process that omits or alters details. This introduces a high risk of hallucination in the generated TikZ code, which could be detrimental for technical diagrams (Reviewer 2).
- **Method novelty is limited:** The approach relies on a combination of existing techniques (adapter + LoRA + augmentation + multi-candidate inference) without introducing particularly innovative technical contributions. The data augmentation and multi-candidate inference tricks are considered minor (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Paper clarity – missing architectural details:** The description of the adapter network and LoRA is insufficient; details (e.g., linear layer architecture, lora_r, lora_alpha) only appear in supplementary Table 7. If space is limited, a cross-reference to the table would help. The paper is generally difficult to follow in places (Reviewer 2).
- **Incomplete dataset description:** The dataset section should demonstrate all diagram types and provide per-category statistics (e.g., counts per type) (Reviewer 3).
- **Open questions that address weaknesses:**
    - Why does the method require generating multiple candidates and selecting the best one? Does this stem from limitations of the vision encoder or the code generator? Is there any strategy to improve consistency and accuracy of the generation process? (Reviewer 3)
    - The difference between CSR_avg and CSR_cum is confusing. A concrete example illustrating how these metrics are calculated and why they might differ would help interpretation. (Reviewer 3)
    - Would it be better to add text descriptions as input along with the hand-drawn sketch? (Reviewer 1)
    - Does the SkeTikZ dataset alone (without the augmentation strategies) universally bring performance improvements in future research? Are there simple methods to correct minor errors in the generated vector diagrams? (Reviewer 4)
- **Minor issues:** A typo exists in Table 2 (CSR_ave) vs. Table 3 (CSR_avg). All tables are too large and consume unnecessary space; reducing font size would free space for architecture/training details (Reviewer 2).
