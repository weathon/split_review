## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes solving linear inverse problems by embedding the solution into a higher-dimensional space and jointly learning the embedding matrix/operator and a regularizer for the embedding vector. The approach is motivated by the mountain pass theorem, uses algorithm unrolling of gradient descent, and is tested on a toy 2D optimization problem, image deblurring, and MNIST digit reconstruction from simulated magnetic measurements. The method is compared to simple proximal methods and a diffusion-based model (DPS).

### Strengths

- The paper is generally well-written and includes informative toy examples that illustrate the intuition behind the approach. (Reviewers 2, 4; Reviewer 2 adds "generally well-written" and Reviewer 4 notes "well-written and includes informative toy examples").
- The proposed method is interesting and (somewhat) novel in jointly designing and learning the embedding and the regularizer for the embedding vector. (Reviewer 2, with score 5, finds it "interesting and (somewhat) novel" and contribution "3 good"; Reviewer 4, with score 3, considers it "a minor variation of unrolling methods" and contribution "2 fair" – this disagreement is noted).
- The outcomes achieved using the proposed method surpass those obtained by the diffusion-based model (DPS) in some experiments. (Reviewer 1). *However, Reviewer 4 notes that DPS outperforms the proposed method when the problem is expected to be solvable (e.g., Figure 4, blur kernel size s=9) and that comparison to DPS is unfair because DPS operates in an unsupervised regime.*
- The paper addresses a linear inverse problem, which is relevant across many scientific fields. (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of clear novelty and contribution:** The proposed approach appears to be a minor variation of extensively studied unrolling methods; similar techniques may have been presented elsewhere. The contribution is not clearly articulated. (Reviewers 1, 4; Reviewer 1: "lack of (or at very least ambiguity) in the contribution", contribution "1 poor"; Reviewer 4: "minor variation", contribution "2 fair").
- **Insufficient theoretical grounding:** There is no discussion of theoretical convergence guarantees, conditions for accurate recovery, or whether sparsity/incoherence conditions required for analysis-based recovery (Equations (8),(9)) are met. The proof of Theorem 3 appears to show only that the regularizer ψ decreases through the path, not that the whole loss function (including data misfit) decreases. (Reviewers 1, 4; Reviewer 1: "lack of discussion regarding theoretical convergence guarantees"; Reviewer 4: question about Theorem 3 proof).
- **Weak experimental validation:** 
  - Comparisons are not comprehensive: the method is only compared to two simple algorithms (proximal methods) and DPS, which is unsupervised and thus an unfair comparison. No comparison to a simple linear Wiener filter, fixed sparse dictionary, or other recent supervised techniques. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4; Reviewer 2: "Comparisons aren't comprehensive", "only two simple algorithms"; Reviewer 3: "does this approach even produce better results than a simple linear Wiener filter?"; Reviewer 4: "unfair" comparison to DPS).
  - Experiments are performed at low resolution and primarily on toy (MNIST) datasets; scalability to higher resolutions is not demonstrated. (Reviewer 2: "Validated primarily on toy (MNIST) datas", "low resolution"; questions about scaling).
  - The visual results (Figures 4, 7-10) show that the proposed method's performance is comparable to proximal methods, not clearly better; interpretation is needed. (Reviewer 1). 
  - Figure 6 (recovery without embedding) is unclear: is the same line/measurement used for the same digit class? How are lines chosen and why do they avoid digits? (Reviewer 4). 
  - Figure 11 is unclear whether it shows training error. (Reviewer 4).
  - The duathalon toy example is not entirely convincing; increasing the data fidelity penalty would avoid the second modes pointed to as a limitation of existing methods. (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing details on computational cost and practical limitations:** The paper does not detail computational overhead, floating point operations, or convergence time for each method, making it unclear whether the proposed method is practical. (Reviewers 1, 3; Reviewer 1: "doesn't detail the computational overhead or potential limitations"; Reviewer 3: "do not discuss the amount of computational time").
- **Lack of controlled ablation:** The experiments do not clearly show whether the embedding merely increases the number of parameters in the neural networks (a well-known technique). A comparison with the method *without embedding but keeping the number of learnable parameters fixed* is missing. (Reviewer 4).
- **Inadequate related work and terminology:** The paper lacks proper review of related works, including end-to-end deep learning, unrolled algorithms, plug-and-play priors (citing Venkatakrishnan et al. 2013), and sparse coding methods (LISTA, ALISTA, etc.). Key terms like "embedding", "expressedness", and "coordinate" are vague and undefined. (Reviewers 1, 2; Reviewer 1: "Lack of proper review of the related works", multiple questions about terminology and references; Reviewer 2: "minor misrepresentation of the literature" on plug-and-play priors since 2013).
- **Minor writing/organization issues:** The placement of Figure 1 breaks text flow; axes in Figure 3 should be labeled; typo "obfuscate" should likely be "obviate". (Reviewer 2). Also, the third paragraph on page 1 should cover three prevalent methodologies with proper references. (Reviewer 1). Equations (12) and (13) should be reformulated in matrix-vector form aligning with Equation (1). (Reviewer 1). The statement "makes the estimation ... substantially more difficult" is made without evidence. (Reviewer 2).
- **Questions about specific experimental details:** What does blurring kernel size s=1 mean in Table 2? (Reviewer 1). How important is unrolling for the method? (Reviewer 2). Does learning the regularizer and dictionary simultaneously come with an increased cost? (Reviewer 3). Possibly report Table 2 results in terms of PSNR. (Reviewer 2).
