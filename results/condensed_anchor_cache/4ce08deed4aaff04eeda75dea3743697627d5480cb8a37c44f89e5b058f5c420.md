## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper uses mechanistic interpretability tools (path patching, information flow routes) to compare internal circuits for two morphosyntactic tasks—indirect object identification (IOI) and past tense inflection—across English and Chinese. Experiments cover multilingual models (BLOOM, Qwen2-0.5B-Instruct) and monolingual models (GPT-2, CPM). The main finding is that shared syntactic processes (IOI) use similar circuits across languages, even in independently trained monolingual models, while language-specific processes (English past tense marking) involve overlapping circuits with additional language-specific components (attention heads, FFNs). Reviewers largely agree the work is interesting and well-conducted but raise concerns about the limited scope (two languages, two tasks), incomplete model comparisons, insufficient methodological detail, and unclear practical implications.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and presentation**: The paper is clear, well-written, has high-quality illustrations, and a logical flow (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).  
- **Experimental design and methodology**: Experiments are sound, well-designed, and effectively executed. The use of path patching and information flow routes is appropriate and extensively documented, providing a valuable framework for future research (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).  
- **Novel fine-grained insights**: The work provides concrete, circuit-level evidence for language-agnostic and language-specific processing, going beyond prior representational alignment studies. Demonstrations of specific functional heads (e.g., copy heads) and language-specific FFNs are informative (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).  
- **Timely topic**: The paper addresses a timely problem—understanding how multilingual LMs process different languages internally—and bridges mechanistic interpretability with linguistic analysis (Reviewers 1, 2, 5).  
- **Convincing ablation results**: Ablations (e.g., FFN ablation in Section 4.6, Appendix Figure 11) convincingly support the identification of language-specific components (Reviewer 4).  
- **Detailed cross-linguistic comparison**: The study thoroughly examines circuit overlap and information flow across languages (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited linguistic and task scope**: The study is a case study with only two languages (English, Chinese) and two tasks (IOI, past tense). The IOI task accuracy is near saturation, limiting informativeness. These choices do not capture typological diversity (e.g., richer morphology, different word orders) or other linguistic phenomena (e.g., long-range dependencies, semantics). The paper’s title and introduction make broad claims about multilingual models that are not fully supported by the limited experimental scope. While some authors acknowledge limitations, others recommend adjusting the claims to better reflect the case-study nature (Reviewers 1, 3, 4, 5). There is disagreement: Reviewer 2 finds the authors careful not to overstate, while Reviewers 1 and 5 find the claims too broad.  
- **Chaotic model selection and missing cross-task comparisons**: The models used differ across tasks (BLOOM for IOI, Qwen2 for past tense) without clear rationale for why Qwen2 is not tested on IOI. This inconsistency undermines generalizability. The paper should present results for both models on both tasks or provide a justified selection (Reviewer 5). Relatedly, the natural questions are: Why not include Qwen2 in IOI? Why filter English irregular past tense verbs? (Reviewer 5).  
- **Insufficient methodological detail for replication and evaluation**: The description of head types (e.g., copy heads), circuit identification, and path patching is too high-level. It relies heavily on prior work (Wang et al., 2023) without explaining how the directed graphs in Figures 2-3 are constructed, how the similarity of circuits is quantified across languages, or how information flow directionality is determined. The criteria for ‘start’ and ‘end’ points in path patching and the effect quantification are unclear. Tokenization differences across languages are not addressed. The paper needs concrete metrics for quantifying circuit similarity and a clearer explanation of how the functional roles of attention heads are algorithmically identified (Reviewers 4, 5).  
- **Anecdotal observations lack quantification**: The statement that “many of the heads that are shared between English IOI and control are also shared with Chinese IOI” is anecdotal. A simple overlap percentage with a threshold would strengthen it (Reviewer 4).  
- **Limited practical implications for multilingual training**: It is unclear how the findings (shared circuits, trade-offs) can be applied to improve multilingual model training (e.g., parameter migration, cross-lingual transfer). The paper lacks guidance on translating insights into actionable improvements (Reviewer 3).  
- **Minor presentation issues**: Section 4.5 is dense and could be better organized (e.g., sub-paragraphs, section headings). The term “ablate” should be explicitly defined. Line 297 has a wording error (“This divergence is not able,”). Figure 11 should be moved to the main text (Reviewers 2, 4).
