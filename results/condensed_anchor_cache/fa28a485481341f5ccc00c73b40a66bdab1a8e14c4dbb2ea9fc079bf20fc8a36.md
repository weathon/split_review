## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces a novel concept, "quantization kernel," defined as the set of elements in activations quantized to zero, and argues that reducing this kernel is critical for preserving accuracy in LLM activation quantization. The authors propose CrossQuant, which cross-quantizes activations using both row-wise and column-wise absolute maximum vectors to compute scaling factors, thereby reducing the quantization kernel. Experiments on LLaMA (1/2) and OPT models (6.7B–70B) show perplexity and accuracy improvements or maintenance. Three reviewers rate the paper 3, and one reviewer rates it 5, finding it a stronger contribution.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized and clearly written, with a logical presentation of the problem, methodology, and results.
- The "quantization kernel" concept provides a fresh perspective on the cause of accuracy degradation in activation quantization, moving beyond the well-known outlier-based explanation. This is, to our knowledge, the first demonstration of the influence of zero-valued quantized elements on model performance.
- The concept is supported by rigorous quantitative analysis linking kernel size to accuracy loss (e.g., kernel proportion <19% for OPT, <1% for LLaMA for negligible INT8 degradation).
- CrossQuant is technically sound, straightforward in principle, and requires no additional training while being compatible with various LLM quantization approaches (e.g., AWQ, GPTQ, OmniQuant, SmoothQuant).
- The method effectively reduces the proportion of values quantized to zero, as shown empirically.
- In some experimental settings, CrossQuant achieves near-lossless performance compared to FP16 baselines.
- The method successfully reduces perplexity in quantized LLMs.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons to stronger baselines**: The paper lacks comparison with recent quantization methods that achieve better accuracy than OmniQuant/SmoothQuant, specifically AffineQuant [ICLR2024], QuaRot [NIPS2024], SpinQuant [arXiv2024], and Atom [MLSys2024]. These methods set new benchmarks for W4A4 quantization and were available within the citation window.
- **Outdated or insufficient model coverage**: Experiments focus on LLaMA-1/2 and OPT, but omit more recent models such as LLaMA-3, LLaMA-3.1, and QWEN. OPT is particularly outdated (e.g., MMLU accuracy for OPT is only 25–30%, near random guess); results on newer models would better demonstrate generalizability.
- **Only perplexity and limited accuracy evaluations**: For LLaMA2, only perplexity on C4/Wikipedia is given in Table 2, which does not reflect true LLM performance. Evaluations are too simplistic. Required: MMLU(-pro) 0/5-shot, IFEval, GSM8K, HumanEval, and other datasets as per the LLaMA-3 technical report. The zero-shot/few-shot evaluation is sparse.
- **Insufficient ablation and motivation**: 
  - The choice to use cross-quantization (row and column-wise max) appears abrupt; the paper does not provide clear intuition or reasoning for selecting this specific solution over alternatives.
  - The ablation study on the α parameter is limited to a single model series (only one architecture), failing to show generalizability.
  - There is no analysis explaining why OPT and LLaMA models show quite different quantization kernel proportions (e.g., ~16% vs. <0.1%), which could deepen the paper.
- **Hardware efficiency and inference speed not addressed**: 
  - CrossQuant introduces a column-wise scaling factor, which per SmoothQuant is inadvisable because per-channel/column-wise scaling does not map well to GEMM kernels. Scaling can only be performed along the outer dimensions of matrix multiplication. The paper does not explain how low-precision matrix multiplication (X_int8 * W_int8) and dequantization would work in practice. The dequantization formula after introducing the column-wise factor is not provided, so it is unclear whether acceleration benefits are retained.
  - Using exponent <1 for scale calculation may incur additional delay. No real-system latency/throughput measurements are given. Required comparisons: TTFT, TPOT, throughput (token/second) at different batch sizes (1,2,4,8,16,32) and sequence lengths (64,128,256,512,1024,2048) against baselines (BF16, SmoothQuant, OmniQuant). This is critical for a quantization method claiming efficiency.
  - The reviewer with rating 5 explicitly notes CrossQuant is not hardware-friendly and that a custom kernel would be needed, but no such analysis or latency data is provided to disprove this concern.
- **Specific technical questions left unanswered**:
  - The paper hypothesizes a relation between B_ij and kernel size (L321), but Table 1 shows that when B_ij decreases, the quantization kernel increases – contradicting the hypothesis. This validity issue is not addressed.
  - The term "quantization kernel" may cause confusion with the CUDA programming concept of "kernel"; renaming to "null space" or similar is suggested.
- **Presentation and experimental detail issues**:
  - Spelling errors: "quantization" for "quantization" (L83, L407, L749); "of it" for "of which" (L181).
  - Inconsistency in model name casing (e.g., "llama3-8b" in Table 4 vs. "LLaMA" elsewhere).
  - Missing detailed experimental setup: calibration sets, hardware usage, etc.
