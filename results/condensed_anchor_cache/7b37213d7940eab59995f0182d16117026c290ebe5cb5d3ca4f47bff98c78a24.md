## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a structured matrix method using Vandermonde matrices to generalize Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs) and Spherical FNOs to non-equispaced point distributions. The method directly evaluates exponential sums via batched matrix multiplications, avoiding fast Fourier transforms. Reviewers acknowledge the well-written motivation and comprehensive benchmarks across several PDE problems. However, they disagree on novelty and clarity: R1 and R2 find the gap in the literature properly addressed, while R3 considers the core idea (direct evaluation for few modes) well-known and the presentation insufficient. Weaknesses include missing baselines (standard NUFFT libraries, vanilla FNO/SFNO on their own grids), unclear explanation of how the structured matrix replaces the FFT, misleading terminology (“inverse transform” vs. adjoint), lack of mode counts in experiments, and absent sensitivity analysis for collocation points.

### Strengths
- **Well-written and motivated.** The paper clearly identifies the limitation of FFT-based neural operators to equispaced grids and proposes a practical remedy. (R1, R2)
- **Addresses a gap in the literature.** Formulation of FNOs on arbitrary grids has been discussed but not demonstrated in practice; the paper fills this gap. (R1)
- **Comprehensive benchmarks.** Experiments cover a broad range of models and problem domains, supporting the claim of training speed gains. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Shows efficiency when few modes are used.** The direct evaluation of exponential sums is convincingly faster than interpolation‑based baselines in the low‑mode regime. (R3)
- **Implementation provided.** Code is available for reproducibility. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and contribution overstated.** The fact that direct evaluation of exponential sums is more efficient than a fast transform when few modes are needed is well known (e.g., the “Do I even need a NUFFT?” section of the FINUFFT documentation). The paper does not clearly differentiate its contribution from this standard wisdom. (R3)
- **Missing comparison with standard NUFFT libraries.** Experiments compare against cubic interpolation but not against a mature NUFFT implementation such as FINUFFT, which is a natural baseline for speed and accuracy. (R3)
- **“Inverse Transformations” section is misleading.** What the paper calls the “inverse” is actually the adjoint transform. These are not the same, and the statement that no direct inverse NUFFT exists conflates the adjoint with a true inverse (e.g., a least‑squares solution). (R3)
- **Presentation of the method is unclear.** 
  - The notation in Section 2 is confusing; for general point clouds it seems to reduce to the case with only two sample points. (R3)
  - Figure 1 does not clearly visualize how the Vandermonde‑structured matrix replaces the Fourier transform in the overall neural operator workflow. (R2)
  - It is not obvious how non‑equispaced data is handled in the frequency domain and how that impacts accuracy and efficiency relative to standard FNO. (R2)
- **Missing mode counts in experiments.** The number of modes used in each benchmark is not reported, making it impossible to assess when the method’s complexity advantage (O(mn) with m modes, n points) applies. (R3)
- **Incomplete complexity analysis.** The computational complexity is not stated explicitly in terms of number of sample points and number of modes, nor is the crossover point with fast transforms discussed. (R3)
- **Baselines and metrics should include structure‑grid methods.** 
  - The performance of vanilla FNO/SFNO on their native regular grids should be reported (timing and L2/L1 error) to quantify the cost of handling non‑equispaced data and to provide a meaningful baseline. (R1)
  - The text implies the proposed method outperforms FNO/SFNO on the regular grid, but it actually operates on an irregular grid (requiring interpolation); this should be clarified. (R1)
- **Missing grid‑transfer experiments.** A key advantage of neural operators is training on one grid and evaluating on another; such tests (structured‑grid‑trained operators applied to unstructured grids) are not included. (R1)
- **Sensitivity to collocation points not analyzed.** No analysis is provided of how the choice of sampling points affects quadrature errors and overall accuracy. (R1)
- **“Full grid” baseline may be misconfigured.** If the “Full Grid” baseline uses more modes than the reduced‑mode method, a fair comparison would use the same (small) number of modes and directly evaluate the sums. (R3)
- **Missing relevant references.** Key NUFFT references (Dutt & Rokhlin 1993; Beylkin 1995; Barnett, Magland & af Klinteberg 2019) are absent, which would help frame the contribution. (R3)
- **Reviewer disagreement on presentation.** While R1 and R2 consider the paper well‑written, R3 finds the explanation of the method obscure and the notation inadequate. (R3 vs. R1, R2)
