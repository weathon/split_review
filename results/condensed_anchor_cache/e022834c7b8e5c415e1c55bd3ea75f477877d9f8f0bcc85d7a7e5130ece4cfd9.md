## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Neural Data Transformer 3 (NDT3), a foundation model for motor decoding from intracortical spiking activity, pretrained on 2000 hours of neural and behavioral data from over 30 monkeys and humans across 10 labs. NDT3 uses a causal transformer to tokenize neural and behavioral data. The paper evaluates pretrained NDT3 on eight downstream decoding tasks, showing benefits in some cases but also revealing scaling challenges: increasing data from 200 to 2000 hours requires growing model size to 350M parameters to avoid saturation, and several datasets barely benefit. The authors attribute limited generalization partly to variability in input/output spaces across datasets.

### Strengths
- **Originality and scale**: Extends NDT2 with architectural changes (causal transformer, multimodal tokenization) to enable large-scale pretraining. Represents the largest dataset used for motor decoding pretraining (2000 hrs vs ~100 hrs in prior work like Azabou et al., NeurIPS 2023). (Reviewers 1,4)
- **Fills a gap**: Despite limited novelty in architecture, there is a lack of effective large-scale multimodal models in neuroscience; NDT3 is a valuable effort toward field-wide improvements. (Reviewer 2)
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: Extensive experiments across diverse tasks/subjects, open discussion of failure modes, and analysis of when foundation models are or are not effective. Offers useful insights for the community. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- **Well-motivated**: Building foundation models for neural data has high implications for BCI and neuroscience applications. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited performance gains and practical utility** (Reviewer 3 especially critical): Gains from large-scale pretraining are minimal and task-dependent; e.g., Fig. 3D shows only slight improvement from 1.5–200 hrs to 2000 hrs. Scaling to 2000 hrs requires a 350M-parameter model, making full fine-tuning costly. This raises questions about practicality. Reviewers 1 and 2 also note cross-subject transfer is not promising.
- **Insufficient baselines and missing comparisons** (Reviewers 3,4): NDT3 is only compared to itself, NDT2 (trained from scratch), and a simple linear Wiener filter. Stronger baselines are needed: MLP, RNNs, and other foundation models such as **EIT** (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2022), **POYO** (Azabou et al., NeurIPS 2023), and **Zhang et al. (2024, SEEG)**. Also, pretrained NDT2 weights are available; finetuned NDT2 performance should be in main figures (e.g., Fig. 3D). (Reviewers 3,4)
- **Novelty concerns** (Reviewers 3,4): Differences between NDT3 and NDT2 are not clearly detailed; the smaller 45M NDT3 has comparable capacity to NDT2 models (20M, 72M). The finding that scaling data requires scaling model size and that generalizability is limited is not novel—similar conclusions were already reported for SEEG (Zhang et al., 2024). Reviewer 4 further notes the paper’s primary contribution is demonstrating limitations.
- **Writing and presentation clarity** (Reviewers 1,2,4): The paper is verbose and hard to follow (e.g., joint/sequential tuning details in main text, Fig. 3C–E cluttered, Fig. 3E missing color legend, hard to distinguish colors in Fig. 3C). Several typos and incorrect figure references: L107 should be Fig. 2B; L466: Fig. 5C not 5B; L479: Fig. 5C not 5D; extra parentheses in L392; missing word in L482.
- **Lack of analysis for alternative explanations of scaling difficulties** (Reviewer 2): Beyond dataset variability, other factors may limit scaling: (i) information upper bound in neural data for some tasks; (ii) imbalanced multi-task optimization across behavior modalities/tasks—the model uses MSE for behavior and cross-entropy for neural/reward, and model selection may favor some tasks over others. Reviewers 2 and 1 also question the choice of cross-entropy loss for neural prediction instead of Poisson likelihood, and the use of per-dataset normalization by max absolute value (which may hinder learning for low-magnitude behaviors). (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing justification for architectural change** (Reviewer 4): Why replace the MAE structure of NDT2 with a causal transformer? No head-to-head architectural comparison is provided, so it is unclear if performance differences stem from architecture or data scale.
- **Incomplete evaluation and potential confounds** (Reviewers 1,2,4): 
    - Fig. 3D shows mean \(R^2\) without errorbars; without them it is unclear if scaling gains are statistically significant. (Reviewer 1)
    - Fig. 3A/B broken red lines between epochs 100–200 may indicate training instability (“sudden drop”); explanation is needed. (Reviewer 4)
    - Fig. 5D: Was the test monkey with S1/FEF/MT recordings seen during pretraining? If not, this would be cross-subject transfer; if those recordings came from a subject whose motor-cortex data was in pretraining, it could confound results. (Reviewer 1)
    - Fine-tuning is done jointly over multiple evaluation sessions to reflect real-world practice, but this could obscure scaling effects. A comparison with targeted fine-tuning on single sessions is needed. (Reviewer 2)
    - Model selection criteria are not fully described; a common problem in multi-task training is that some tasks converge earlier, leading to an imbalanced final checkpoint. Task-specific performance across checkpoints should be analyzed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Unclear behavior dimensionality**: Reviewer 4 questions why movement covariates can be up to 10 dimensions (Fig. 2A), which seems high for hand movements. This needs clarification.
