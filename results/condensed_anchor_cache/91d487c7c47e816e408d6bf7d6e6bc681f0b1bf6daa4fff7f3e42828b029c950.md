## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Multi-Scale Mamba (MSM) for multi-task dense prediction, extending MTMamba with task-aware multi-scale scanning. The MSM uses a Multi-Scale Scanning (MS-Scan) mechanism and two modules: Task-Specific Multi-Scale Mamba (TS-MSM) and Cross-Task Multi-Scale Mamba (CT-MSM). Experiments on NYUD-V2 and PASCAL-Context show MSM outperforming prior methods in most cases. The paper is generally well-organized with clear visualizations.

### Strengths
- The proposed method consistently outperforms prior approaches across multiple tasks on NYUD-V2 and PASCAL-Context (Tables 1, 2) [R1, R2, R3, R4].
- Ablation studies verify the effectiveness of the modules [R1].
- Visualizations (qualitative results) are clear and help interpret performance [R1, R2].
- The architecture adapts to diverse granularity demands of different tasks, an important aspect for multi-task dense prediction [R3].
- The MSM framework introduces minimal additional computational cost compared to the original MTMamba (according to the paper) [R3].
- The organizational structure and presentation are clear and reasonable [R1, R4].
- Possibly novel in how low-rank experts (or multi-scale Mamba modules) are used for multiple tasks [R4 – note: this is a minority positive view; other reviewers disagree on novelty].

### Weaknesses
1. **Novelty and contribution** – All reviewers agree the contribution is limited. The idea of using multi-scale features is common in dense prediction; the paper does not introduce a true innovation in the scanning strategy itself [R2, R4]. One reviewer (score 3) finds the contribution quite limited compared to MTMamba: the proposed modules are a straightforward application of Mamba blocks to fuse multi-scale features, which is a standard approach in dense prediction [R1]. Another reviewer states the paper lacks strong motivation for *why* Mamba is needed specifically for multi-task dense prediction rather than merely combining Mamba with MTL [R3].
2. **Computational efficiency** – Several reviewers request a detailed comparison of computational cost, FLOPs, parameters, and inference speed against prior methods (especially Transformer-based models) because the paper claims efficiency as an advantage of Mamba (L049) [R1, R2, R3, R4]. One reviewer notes that the multi-scale scanning likely adds extra cost and should be analyzed [R2]. Additionally, when the number of tasks increases (more than 2), the channel-wise concatenation in CT-MSM leads to very large feature sizes (e.g., 4C+4C+4C for three tasks), raising scaling concerns [R4].
3. **Insufficient comparisons** – The compared methods are relatively outdated (e.g., InvPT, MQTransformer, ATRC) and have lower computational costs; missing more recent methods such as TaskExpert, DiffusionMTL, and other state-of-the-art MTL approaches [R1, R4]. The baseline MTMamba itself is not a contribution of this paper [R1]. Also, the paper uses Swin-Transformer as backbone; the authors should consider using Mamba-based backbones (e.g., Vision Mamba, EfficientVMamba, VMamba) for a fairer comparison within the Mamba family [R4].
4. **Magnitude of improvement** – The gains in Tables 1 and 2 are minimal, raising doubts about the practical effectiveness of the proposed approach [R2].
5. **Missing failure analysis** – All qualitative analyses show positive examples; the authors should provide failure case visualizations and discuss whether multi-scale processing affects local representation [R2]. Limitations of the method should be discussed [R3].
6. **Clarity issues in architecture description** – Reviewer 4 raises confusion: In the TS-MSM module, how are multiple task-specific features F obtained from a single task-generic feature G at the same scale? When G is replicated T times for each task, why can the replicated features be called task-specific? Also, the sizes of features (e.g., F_{in}^s, F_{h}^s, F^s in MFR block) are unclear [R4]. The notation “∘” in Eq. (1) is not explained [R4].
7. **Contradictory STL vs. MTL results** – In Table 3, the single-task (STL) model values are lower than the multi-task baseline, which is inconsistent with typical MTL experience; this needs explanation [R4].
8. **Minor errors** – Typos: “Pineline” → “Pipeline” (L153) [R1]; “the” → “The” (L049) [R1]; “These” lowercase in L101 [R3]; “these” → “These” in the Introduction [R4]; bolded value in the 2nd column of Table 3 is not the best result [R2].
9. **Missing experimental details** – Tables 1 and 2 lack parameters, FLOPs, and Δ_m values; these should be reported [R4]. Code release would benefit the community [R4].
10. **Disagreement note** – One reviewer (score 3) is notably more critical than the others (scores 5,5,5), stating that the comparison methods are poorly selected and that the contribution is not clear beyond multi-scale fusion of Mamba blocks [R1]. The majority view is moderate acceptance, but this reviewer questions the overall contribution and evaluation rigor.
