## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LIDet, a method to detect whether a suspect LLM has been fine-tuned on watermarked outputs from another LLM (potential IP infringement). LIDet uses anchor LLMs to select queries that maximize token entropy and cross-model generalizability, then applies adaptive thresholding to z-score-based watermark detection. Evaluations on multiple watermarking schemes and domains show >90% accuracy in distinguishing infringing from clean models. Reviewers generally find the problem well-motivated, the threat model clear, and the detection of domain-mismatch and green-ratio-mismatch issues insightful, though several stress the need for more comprehensive evaluation, stronger baselines, and robustness against adaptive attacks.

### Strengths
- Clear and precise problem statement and threat model (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- The paper first empirically identifies vulnerabilities in direct watermark detection (domain mismatch, green ratio mismatch, hash key instability) – a well-structured motivation (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- Strong empirical performance: LIDet achieves over 90% accuracy on challenging model sets where direct methods fail (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4, Reviewer 5).
- Evaluation covers multiple watermarking techniques (e.g., green-red list) and data domains (Reviewer 1).
- The proposed query selection strategy based on token entropy and cross-model generalizability is a novel contribution that addresses domain mismatch (Reviewer 5).
- Adaptive thresholding mitigates detection failures from different hash keys (Reviewer 5).
- The paper is well-written with clear logic and organization (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of comparison with state-of-the-art.** The paper does not compare LIDet with the method in [1] (a prior work on detecting watermarks in fine-tuned models). Even though threat models differ, a comparison under i.i.d. detection queries (as considered in [1]) would be informative (Reviewer 1). One reviewer explicitly questions whether LIDet outperforms [1] (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient evaluation of computational cost.** The paper omits analysis of the number of queries and tokens per query needed for detection, including the trade-off between detection accuracy and computational overhead. This is critical for practical deployment (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited model and domain scope.** Evaluations are restricted to two domains (e.g., code and math) without considering mixed-domain scenarios (Reviewer 2). Only Llama2/3 are used as source and anchor models; suspect models are always Mistral-Instruct-7b – no analysis with other base architectures (e.g., Mistral as source/suspect, Llama as suspect, or different model sizes) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4). The effect of suspect model size (number of parameters) on detection performance is not examined (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear anchor model selection and contribution.** There is no ablation on the number or architecture type of anchor LLMs, nor discussion of which anchor model contributes most to query selection (Reviewer 1). The method always uses the same architecture as the source model – it is unclear whether this is necessary (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of adaptive attack analysis.** The stealer is assumed to directly fine-tune on watermarked data. A sophisticated adversary could (1) modify queried texts to remove the watermark before training, or (2) train a model to mimic the watermark’s statistical bias without using any watermarked data, increasing false positives. The feasibility and robustness of LIDet against such mimicry attacks are not addressed (Reviewer 3). One reviewer (score 5) is notably more critical on this point than others, emphasizing a significant gap in threat modeling.
- **Detection reliability concerns.** If the infringing model uses only a small amount of watermarked data, or if fine-tuning alters watermark patterns, the watermark may not be absorbed (Reviewer 5). The paper does not test detection over varying proportions of watermarked training data or different fine-tuning strategies (Reviewer 5).
- **Risk of false positives from natural language patterns or overlapping datasets.** Unwatermarked models may unintentionally exhibit statistical patterns that align with the watermark scheme (Reviewer 5). If two models are trained on overlapping open-source datasets, the false positive rate could increase – this scenario is not explored (Reviewer 5).
- **Missing analysis of watermark impact on model performance.** The paper does not evaluate how watermarking affects output quality (perplexity, BLEU, human evaluation) or whether it introduces biases (Reviewer 5).
- **Insufficient detail on detection results for different anchor models.** Section 4.2 lacks performance breakdown per anchor model, making it hard to assess their individual contributions (Reviewer 1).
