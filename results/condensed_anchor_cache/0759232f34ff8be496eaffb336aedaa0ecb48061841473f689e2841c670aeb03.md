## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents a latent predictive world model that uses slot attention (SAVi) to extract object-centric representations and conditions on language instructions to predict future latent states. These predicted slots are then decoded into actions via an inverse dynamics model. The method is compared against diffusion-based video prediction models (Seer, SuSiE) on a simulated LanguageTable manipulation benchmark. Reviewers note that the paper is well-written and the problem is relevant, but they raise significant concerns about limited evaluation scope, missing baselines, and insufficient justification of design choices. Scores range from 3 to 5, with two reviewers more positive and two more critical.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- Comparison with two diffusion-based baselines (Seer, SuSiE) shows large performance gaps, highlighting computational and sample efficiency advantages.
- Ablation studies are fairly thorough: action decoding strategies, number of past steps, look-ahead horizon, and the effect of language conditioning.
- Demonstrated generalization to unseen tasks (e.g., new block types, unseen combinations).
- Use of SAVi for object-centric features enhances computational efficiency and model accuracy.
- The problem of building a language-guided world model for predictive control is relevant and useful.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation environment.** The only tested setting is the simulated LanguageTable benchmark, which is overly simplistic (four distinct objects: red moon, blue cube, green star, yellow pentagon). This biases results toward object-centric methods and does not demonstrate applicability to complex visual scenes, real-world robot tasks, or environments with variable backgrounds, lighting, or occlusions. Reviewers suggest evaluating on CALVIN, ManiSkill2, or real-world robot datasets (e.g., Bridge).
- **Missing critical baselines.** 
  - No comparison against a non-language-conditioned SlotFormer model (to isolate the contribution of language conditioning).
  - No comparison with state-of-the-art world models such as TD-MPC2 and PWM.
  - No pixel2action baseline (predicting actions directly from images without a world model) to justify the necessity of a world model.
  - No VAE-based variant of the proposed model in Table 1, making it impossible to conclude that slot attention is beneficial over a standard latent encoder.
- **Insufficient justification of design choices and results.**
  - The claim that future state prediction alone suffices for action decoding is undermined by the low accuracy for “instruction + 0 future steps” (2.5%) and the near-zero performance of Seer; this suggests potential issues in baseline implementation or evaluation rigor.
  - The reason language conditioning on the agent itself decreases success rate is not explained.
  - The nature of slot attention not being robust to variable object number is mentioned but not clarified.
- **Missing analysis of failure cases and qualitative comparisons.** 
  - No qualitative failure analysis for why Seer achieves 0% on all tasks.
  - Figure 3 is hard to parse (objects like cube and moon are difficult to distinguish; differences between the method’s predictions and SuSiE’s are not visible).
  - No videos are provided in the supplementary material to illustrate the quality of predictions.
- **Narrow contribution.** The method is a straightforward extension of SlotFormer by adding language-conditioned goal prediction and training on a large demonstration dataset. The object-centric innovation is limited to substituting the encoder with SAVi, without introducing distinct architectural contributions.
- **Missing comparisons to stronger image/video generation models.** The paper compares to instructpix2pix (an image editor) rather than to multi-frame video diffusion models, which would be a more appropriate comparison for the proposed multi-frame prediction capability.
- **Missing related work.** Recommended references include: Focus (object-centric world models for robotics), Dynamic Attribute-factored World Models, Self-Supervised Visual RL with Object-Centric Representations, Entity-Centric RL, Object-Centric Learning for Real-World Videos (VideoSAUR/SOLV), TD-MPC2, PWM.
- **Evaluation scalability concern.** The dataset consists of only four objects and a limited set of language instructions, which does not demonstrate scalability to the diverse language and visual entities found in real-world robotics benchmarks like Bridge or CALVIN.
