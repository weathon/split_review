## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper formalizes data-dependent parameter symmetries in neural networks using Lie algebras and their infinitesimal form, which enables an automated approach to discover symmetries via a learnable group representation model that minimizes a loss enforcing invariance and group axioms. The framework recovers known symmetries and discovers new data-dependent symmetries in small 2- and 3-layer networks with sigmoid and tanh activations. It also proves that symmetries found in smaller subnetworks can extend to larger networks.

### Strengths
- **Problem relevance**: Identifying parameter symmetries is an active and interesting topic for understanding loss landscapes, training dynamics, and generalization (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Novel formalization**: The paper provides a formalization of data-dependent symmetries using Lie algebras, enabling automated discovery of even non-linear symmetries; this is the first work in this direction (Reviewer 1) and the approach is general enough to apply to any architecture (Reviewer 3). The perspective of discovering implicit symmetries induced by the dataset is novel (Reviewer 4).
- **Theoretical contribution**: Theorem 3.1 generalizes the result of Moskalev et al. 2022 (Reviewer 1). The application to deep networks and the layer-wise partitioning of the parameter space is novel (Reviewer 1).
- **Original discovery**: The method uncovers new data-dependent symmetries, which is original and interesting (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation quality**: The paper is generally well-written, clear, and easy to follow; concepts are explained well and motivation is well-established (Reviewers 1 & 3). However, Reviewers 2 and 4 rated Presentation as 1, indicating serious presentation issues (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing citations**: Several relevant prior works are not cited or adequately contextualized. These include ReLU symmetries (Petzka et al. 2020), general symmetries of linear + even activation functions (Martinelli et al. 2023), neuron-splitting symmetries (Fukumizu & Amari 2000), and the complete characterization of parameter-space symmetries under population loss with full-support input distribution (Simsek et al. 2021) (Reviewers 1 & 2). The latter questions whether the paper’s formalization covers a broader class of symmetries than already known.
- **Lack of novelty or triviality of theory**: Theorem 3.1 is essentially a restatement of Noether’s theorem that has appeared many times in deep learning (Reviewer 2). Proposition 4.1 is trivial and obvious; its two examples are almost trivial if not already known (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1, while praising the novelty of the layer-wise partitioning, also gave Contribution 2, indicating limited perceived novelty overall.
- **Weak connection between sections**: The relationship between Sections 3, 4, and 5 is weak; Section 4 does not rely on either Section 3 or Section 5 (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient evaluation of the main algorithm**: The automated method (Section 5) is only illustrative and lacks any serious evaluation; extensive analysis is needed to make this part solid (Reviewer 2). Utility is only demonstrated for very small networks (2- and 3-layer), and it remains unclear whether the method works at a more practical scale (Reviewer 3). The paper underplays the significance of emergent symmetries in larger networks; symmetries induced from smaller networks may not provide a representative picture because new emergent symmetries can appear (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 is strongly suspicious whether the method really works at all, as the paper significantly lacks experimental analysis verifying its effects (Reviewer 4).
- **Poor figure quality and interpretability**: Figures are hard to interpret (Reviewer 1). Figure 2: what activation function is used? Is it ReLU? How should the generator be interpreted? Figure 3 needs labels (which weight is W1, which is W2?), what data is used, and how the generator depends on the data should be shown. The automated method with infinite data (population loss) case is not presented; figures should appear more structured in that regime (Reviewer 1).
- **Undiscussed hyperparameter selection**: The challenge of selecting the appropriate number of generators and their dimension is not discussed. This may pose a serious problem when applying the method to architectures where symmetries are not well understood (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor errors**: Line 297, “L with subscript Lie_deriv” may be a typo meaning “invariance”; line 341, “training” should be “trained” (Reviewer 3).
