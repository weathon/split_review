## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Midpoint Guidance Posterior Sampling (MGPS) for Bayesian inverse problems with diffusion priors. MGPS decomposes the reverse transition into a denoising step to a midpoint state ℓ_k (0 < ℓ_k < k) and a renoising step, thereby introducing a tunable trade-off between the intractable guidance term and the prior transitions. A Gaussian variational approximation with diagonal covariance is used per timestep, and the DPS gradient is computed from the posterior mean of x_{ℓ_k}. The method is validated on Gaussian mixture toy data, image restoration (FFHQ, ImageNet, etc.) and ECG imputation, with strong results especially on nonlinear inverse problems. [R1,R2,R3,R4]

### Strengths

- **Novel midpoint guidance mechanism.** Midpoint guidance provides a principled trade-off between guidance complexity and prior transition complexity, distinguishing MGPS from existing posterior sampling approaches (DPS, etc.). [R1,R2]
- **Well-motivated and mathematically rigorous.** The decomposition of the backward transition and the use of Gaussian variational approximations are theoretically grounded, with additional novel results in Appendix A.3. [R1,R3,R4]
- **Extensive evaluation across diverse tasks.** Experiments cover Gaussian mixture sampling, linear inverse problems (super-resolution, deblurring, inpainting), challenging nonlinear inverse problems (e.g., high dynamic range, phase retrieval) on image datasets, and ECG reconstruction from partial measurements. [R1,R2,R3]
- **Strong empirical performance.** MGPS achieves SOTA LPIPS across nearly all settings, especially for nonlinear inverse problems, and maintains good sample variety. Performance is evaluated across multiple NFE budgets. [R2,R4]
- **Thorough implementation details for competitors.** The paper provides explicit hyperparameter settings and details for baselines, aiding reproducibility. [R3,R4]
- **Good visuals and appendix content.** Figures in the main text and appendix aid understanding. [R3]
- **Good balance between theory and practicality.** The method is principled yet does not require excessive computation compared to other posterior sampling methods. [R4]

### Weaknesses

- **Notation issues and exposition clarity.** The notation is non-standard: the posterior π(x) is written as a marginal rather than conditional on observations y, and g(·) in (2.1) is not conditioned on y. Vector quantities lack boldface, making Section 3 hard to follow. [R2,R3] (A minority reviewer found the paper well-written and straightforward [R4].)
- **Insufficient evaluation on image datasets.** Only 50 randomly selected test points are used for FFHQ and ImageNet, whereas the standard in the field is 1k images. The paper reports only LPIPS; standard metrics (PSNR, SSIM, FID) are missing. Reviewers argue these are necessary for a complete evaluation. [R2,R3]
- **Concerns about baseline implementation fairness.** DPS is run for 300 steps (NFE=300) instead of the standard 1000 steps, potentially narrowing performance gaps. Additionally, reconstruction quality of some baselines in Figures 13 and 15 appears worse than in original publications, raising the possibility of undertuning. The paper lacks a detailed description of hyperparameter search ranges and selection criteria for baselines. [R1,R2]
- **Lack of analysis on the impact of η and midpoint sequence.** The effect of η is only shown for the Gaussian toy example; no systematic study across tasks (image, ECG) of how optimal η or the midpoint schedule ℓ_k vary with data dimensionality, prior complexity, or problem linearity. Theoretical bounds on approximation errors, convergence rate, or KL divergence from the true posterior are not provided. [R1]
- **Derivation clarity needs improvement.** (i) It is unclear why a variational distribution λ^φ is needed for π̂^θ, which is already a product of two Gaussians (DPS guidance and prior transition). The non-linearity of the measurement model (especially for nonlinear problems) and the isotropy vs. diagonal covariance debate are not adequately explained. (ii) Whether setting ℓ_k = k recovers DPS is ambiguous; the relationship to prior methods (e.g., DPS) should be clarified, possibly in an appendix. (iii) The derivation of the upper bound used for variational inference is not explained. [R4]
- **ECG application appears without sufficient motivation.** The relevance and difficulty of the task are not well motivated; the contribution in this domain is hard to appreciate without more context. [R2]
- **Additional missing experiments/comparisons.** No study of an optimal midpoint schedule ℓ_k to reduce NFE while maintaining performance; criteria for trading off performance and computational cost are not suggested. Feasibility of incorporating faster solvers (e.g., DDIM) is not discussed. [R1,R4]
