## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces SweetDreamer, which uses Aligned Geometric Priors (AGP) to address the multi-view inconsistency problem in text-to-3D generation. The method fine-tunes a pre-trained 2D diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) on the Objaverse dataset to predict Canonical Coordinates Maps (CCM) – view-specific coordinate maps of canonically oriented 3D objects – and incorporates camera information. An SDS loss from the CCM is then integrated into text-to-3D pipelines. Reviewers generally agree on the importance of the problem and that the method achieves good qualitative consistency. However, opinions diverge: one reviewer (R1, score 8, confidence 4) finds the results of very high quality and the method novel; another (R2, score 6, confidence 3) rates it positively but notes several experimental shortcomings; two reviewers (R3 and R4, both score 5, confidences 5 and 4) are more critical, citing limited novelty, lack of ablation, and concerns about generalizability and evaluation.

### Strengths
- **High-quality results and strong motivation** (R1, R3). The qualitative results show great geometry consistency compared with other methods (R2). The paper is well-written with a clear presentation and a thoughtful analysis distinguishing geometric inconsistency from appearance inconsistency (R1, R2, R3).
- **The core idea is sound** (R3). Introducing view-conditioned information into 2D foundation models (Stable Diffusion) via fine-tuning to predict canonical coordinate maps is a compelling and intriguing approach (R3, R4). The chosen camera information injection helps convergence (R3).
- **Human evaluation validates the method** (R3). The user study shows SweetDreamer’s superiority over previous methods in alleviating the multi-face problem (R1, R3). Quantitative results report a >85% consistency rate, substantially outperforming prior methods (~30%) (abstract, confirmed by human evaluation in paper).
- **Realistic problem addressed** (R4). The multi-view inconsistency issue is a realistic and important challenge that current diffusion models struggle with (R4).
- **Novelty** – Reviewer R1 found the method novel; reviewers R3 and R4 considered the novelty incremental (see Weaknesses). This contrast is preserved.

### Weaknesses
- **Need for ablation studies** (R3, R4). There are no ablation experiments. Key design choices are unsupported: the claim that the CCM SDS loss avoids overfitting to 3D training data (R3), how camera information is injected (R3, R4), and the choice of CCM representation itself (R1, R2, R3, R4). The contribution of each component is unclear without ablations.
- **Justification of Canonical Coordinates Map (CCM) over other representations** (R1, R2). It is not experimentally shown why CCM is superior to depth maps, normal maps, or point clouds as geometric guidance. R1 specifically requested: “using Objaverse to train a normal/depth map (instead of CCM) generative model and also include camera condition.” R2 noted that CCM is not a new representation and its advantage over depth/normal/point cloud is not demonstrated.
- **Generalizability and data limitations** (R1, R2, R3). The paper uses only a subset of Objaverse (270k out of 800k) because many objects lack canonical orientation (R3). This significant data attrition may constrain the method’s generalizability, especially on diverse or complicated scenarios (R1, R3). R2 asked whether using only a smaller subset with canonical orientation could lead to better results. R1 also raised concern about reduced generalization ability due to Objaverse’s lack of diversity.
- **Experimental evaluation weaknesses** (R2, R4).  
  - Only 80 prompts tested, which is a small sample and results may be noisy (R2).  
  - The definition of “3D consistency” in the quantitative evaluation is vague (e.g., “multiple heads, hands, or legs”) and does not cover objects without those structures (buildings) or twisted but non-duplicate geometry (R2).  
  - No appearance or texture quality metrics reported, even though the method may affect those aspects (R2).  
  - Only inconsistency metrics are reported in Table 1; other metrics (e.g., R-Precision from DreamFusion) are missing (R4).  
  - A missing baseline: directly replacing the original SDS loss with the aligned geometric SDS loss and measuring inconsistencies (R4).  
  - The exact method used in the user study (NeRF-based IF vs. NeRF-based full) is not clarified (R2).
- **Limited novelty and technical contribution** (R3, R4 – note: R1 found novelty strong, so this is a minority contrast). R3 and R4 consider the novelty incremental: most components (Stable Diffusion, loss functions) come from prior work, and the idea of a view-aware SDS loss to tackle the multi-face problem has been proven in Zero-123 and Magic-123 (using RGB SDS loss rather than CCM). R3 states the novelty and technique contribution is limited; R4 says the approach lacks technical novelty.
- **Practical concerns** (R4). The fine-tuning time for Stable Diffusion is not reported, and inference efficiency compared to other methods is not provided (R4).
- **Additional minor queries** (R1). In Fig. 1, the normal map shows many bumps – details of normal rendering are needed (R1). The paper mentions “a significant portion of the 3D objects in canonical orientation and a few misoriented” – more explanation is needed about what “mis-oriented” means, how many objects are misoriented, and whether manually correcting misoriented cases would improve performance (R1). R2 also requested showing the user study interface in the appendix and details on user selection and understanding of “3D consistency” (R2).
