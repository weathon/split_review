## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a camera-pose controllable video generation method for Diffusion Transformer (DiT) models, specifically fine-tuning OpenSora on RealEstate10K. It represents camera motion using Plücker coordinates, applies sparse spatial sampling (downsample factor x40), encodes the motion field via a pre-trained VAE (MagViT-v2, inspired by Tora), and injects the resulting latent into the temporal self-attention layers via adaptive normalization. LoRA is used during fine-tuning to preserve original parameters. Quantitative metrics (FID, FVD, CamMC) are reported for 16- and 72-frame generation, with comparisons to several LDM-based baselines (SVD, AnimateDiff, MotionCtrl). The paper also studies three injection strategies (cross-attention, in-context conditioning, adaptive normalization) and presents ablations on the sparse motion encoding and injection module.

### Strengths
- Novelty of incorporating camera poses into DiT-based video generation via Plücker embeddings, providing a new capability for open-source DiT models (OpenSora). (R1, R2, R4)
- First work to explicitly tackle camera-motion-controlled generation with an open-source DiT backbone. (R2)
- The idea of sparsely sampling the motion field (downsampled by ×40) before VAE encoding is a new design choice. (R2)
- Studies three different strategies for injecting camera pose conditioning (cross-attention, in-context, adaptive normalization) and identifies adaptive normalization as effective, consistent with findings in Tora. (R1, R2)
- Training a VAE (MagViT-v2) to temporally downsample the Plücker embeddings helps resolve the temporal length mismatch between the dense camera maps and the DiT latent features. (R3)
- Quantitative results demonstrate improved motion accuracy and visual quality for 72-frame generation compared to LDM-based baselines. (R2)
- Visualization results show effectiveness on simple camera trajectories (panning, zooming, roundabout). (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and technical contribution**:
  - The sparse motion encoding module differs from the standard pixel-wise Plücker embedding (Eq. 3) only by computing embeddings at sparse locations (Eq. 4); it is unclear how camera *motion* (rather than per-frame pose) is encoded. (R1)
  - The core components (Plücker coordinates, VAE encoding, adaptive normalization) have been introduced in prior work (VD3D, Tora). The main claimed contribution — sparsely sampled motion fields — lacks ablation studies (e.g., factor x1 vs. x40, visual comparisons), a clear motivation, and justification for the heuristic downsample factor. (R2, R3)
  - The purpose of the VAE decoder is not explained; if the encoder compresses the Plücker embedding, the decoder’s role and whether the VAE is necessary for sparse sampling are unclear. (R3)
  - A detailed comparison with MotionCtrl, highlighting architectural and performance differences, is missing. (R4)
- **Clarity and presentation**:
  - Many symbols and modules are undefined or inconsistently presented:
    - Sparse Motion Encoding Module and Temporal Attention Injection Module are not labeled in Fig. 1. (R1)
    - In Eq. (3) and (4), the definition of \(RT\) (line 179) and the dimension of \(F_s\) (line 195, \(\mathbb{R}^{L\times M\times N}\)) are ambiguous. (R1)
    - Fig. 2 variables (\(c_p\), \(c_s\), \(c_l\)) are not defined in the main text; the shape of \(c_l\) is not explained. (R1)
    - Fig. 3 symbols (\(s\), \(p\), \(p_m\)) are not defined. (R1)
    - Fig. 3 appears inaccurate: injection is shown between two temporal attention layers, but only one temporal attention block should exist. (R2)
    - Fig. 4 rows 2 and 4 provide little information. (R3)
    - Terms like “frame pose” and “camera pose” are used without clear definitions or a step-by-step extraction/conversion process from the dataset. (R4)
    - Eq. (3) does not clarify how \(R, K, t\) are obtained from the dataset and whether \(P_{x,y}\) is the Plücker embedding. (R4)
  - Writing issues: typos (“MegVit-v2” instead of MagViT-v2), inconsistency between “OpenSora” and “Open-Sora”. (R3)
  - Implementation details are insufficient: how adaptive normalization and cross-attention are performed relative to the temporal attention layers is not specified. (R2)
  - The first two claimed contributions are essentially identical (both refer to incorporating camera poses into DiT). (R1)
- **Experiments and evaluation**:
  - The model is trained only on 16-frame videos from RealEstate10K, but evaluated on 72-frame generation; the motivation for the 16-frame training choice and the mechanism for extending to 72 frames (e.g., sliding window or other) are not explained. (R2)
  - For 16-frame generation, the method underperforms some baselines; no justification is given. (R2)
  - Only a single dataset (RealEstate10K) is used; evaluation on other datasets (e.g., WebVid, HD-VILA following MotionCtrl) is missing, limiting verification of generalizability. (R4)
  - Visual results are limited to simple camera trajectories (zoom, pan, roundabout); no results for complex trajectories available in RealEstate10K, and no supplementary video comparisons are provided. (R1, R3)
  - No user study is conducted to assess subjective quality. (R2)
  - The calculation of the CamMC metric for models that do not take camera trajectories as input (SVD, AnimateDiff, OpenSora) is not explained. (R3)
  - Ablation studies (Table 2) lack details: no reconstruction loss (L1) for the sparse Plücker embedding, no ablation on the downsample factor (x1 vs. x40), and no analysis of whether object motion degree is degraded when camera control modules are added. (R2, R3)
  - The use of different resolutions for different models raises concerns about whether FID/FVD fairly reflect ranking. (R3)
  - The authors mention “object movement tends to be limited to small-scale motions” without providing a quantitative or qualitative comparison with other methods. (R2)
