## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes incorporating lookahead mechanisms (extra-gradient EG-SAM, optimistic-gradient OG-SAM, and adaptive AO+AM) into Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) to address convergence instability and saddle-point issues. It provides convergence analysis and experiments on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet using ResNet and WideResNet architectures.

### Strengths
- **Clear presentation and literature overview** – well-structured and easy to follow (R1, R2).
- **Theoretical analysis** – convergence rates are provided; the method does not require additional training epochs (R1, R4). R1 states random checks seem fine; R4 notes a solid mathematical background.
- **Consistent empirical improvement** – ~1% accuracy gain over SAM across several architectures and datasets, including noisy settings; standard deviations reported (R1, R4).
- **Combines with existing adaptive policies** to reduce computational burden (R4).
- **ODEs for EG-SAM** show a smaller region of attraction around saddle points, aiding escape (R2 – though with caveats on stepsize choice).
- **New EG and OG variants** are proposed (R3 – acknowledges novelty but considers marginal).

### Weaknesses
**Limited novelty and contribution**  
- The methods are “only small variations of SAM” with incremental changes; the contribution is marginal (R1, R3).  
- The theoretical results follow standard techniques and do not encounter additional difficulties (R1).  
- Multi-step SAM is not new; the proposed variants are novel but offer limited insight (R3).

**Insufficient experimental scope**  
- Fewer architecture comparisons than the original SAM paper (e.g., ResNet-101, ResNet-152 missing) (R1).  
- Only CIFAR+ResNet and one ImageNet+ResNet experiment; no transformers or language models (R3).  
- Important SAM baselines (e.g., gSAM, mSAM) are not compared (R3).  
- Wall‑clock training time and runtime overhead are not reported; EG‑SAM has different computational cost despite same backprop count (R3, R4).

**Missing intuition and justification**  
- Little intuitive explanation for why EG/OG helps escape saddle points; purpose of perturbing ε is not aligned with loss maximization (R1, R4).  
- The behavior of EG‑SAM in discrete SGD settings (e.g., catapult effect) is not demonstrated, only analyzed via ODEs (R4).  

**Theory concerns**  
- _Assumptions are restrictive_: requires gradient‑Lipschitz, function Lipschitz (implied by bounded variance), and a decreasing perturbation radius ρ, which essentially avoids the difficulty of the perturbation. The argument would hold for any feasible perturbation (R2).  
- _Misleading comparisons to EG literature_: different problem settings (convex vs. nonconvex, equilibrium vs. gradient norm metrics) and performance metrics are compared in a way that does not apply to the proposed problem (R2).  
- The ODE analysis in Sec. 4.1 follows directly from prior work (Compagnoni et al. Lemma 4.3); the extrapolation stepsize η should not be fixed (R2).  
- Typos: missing stepsize for ε in Eq. 9/10, missing hat and stepsize in Sec. 3.2, missing index t for ρ in Theorem 4.6 (R2).  
- Notation ∇F can be misleading; number of gradient oracles should be clarified (R2).

**Empirical concerns**  
- The optimal ρ (perturbation radius) is not swept for all methods; the refined grid for the proposed method may explain performance differences (R2).  
- The value of ρ used in experiments is not reported explicitly (R2).  
- Table 1 reports “EG‑SAM 150% SAM” – unclear what this means (R2).  
- It would be instructive to plot ∥∇L(w_{t‑1})∥ throughout training to understand if EG‑SAM effectively changes the effective ρ (R2).

**Presentation issues**  
- Too many abbreviations (EG, OG, AO, AMO) make it hard to follow the key points (R3).

**Questions that overlap with criticisms** (from reviewers, reformulated as weaknesses)  
- Why is OG‑SAM necessary, given that both EG‑SAM and OG‑SAM require the same number of backpropagations per iteration? (R2)  
- How does EG‑SAM compare with SAM using an increasing ρ schedule? (R2)  
- Section 3.3: What justifies directly applying extra‑gradient – is Danskin’s theorem intended? The cited reference does not address the same problem (R2).  
- The loss maximization w.r.t. ε does not intuitively align with performance gains (R4 – [1]).  
- Need demonstration of saddle‑point escape with **SGD** (e.g., Fig. 7 in [2]) rather than only ODE analysis (R4).  
- Need wall‑clock computational time to assess real overhead (R4).  

**Reviewer disagreement**: R2 (score 3) is substantially more critical than R1 (score 6) and R4 (score 5). R1 and R4 find the theory and experiments acceptable despite limitations, while R2 argues the theory is based on too‑strong assumptions and misleading comparisons, and the empirical evaluation is insufficient. R3 (score 5) mirrors R1 on marginal contribution and limited experiments.
