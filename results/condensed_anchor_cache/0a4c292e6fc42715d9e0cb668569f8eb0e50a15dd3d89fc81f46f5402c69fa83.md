## Merged Review

### Summary
Three reviewers rated the paper 8, 6, 6 (one reviewer significantly more positive). The paper proposes Compose and Conquer (CnC), a conditional diffusion model that integrates 3D object placement via depth disentanglement training (DDT) and localized global style control via soft guidance. All reviewers agree the method is novel and effective, with comprehensive evaluations showing superior results over SOTA baselines. However, concerns are raised about the validity of “depth-awareness” given MiDaS scale/shift invariance, lack of generalization to other condition types, missing details on training triplets and soft guidance implementation, and limitations of evaluation metrics.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and figures effectively visualize the concepts (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The proposed depth disentanglement training (DDT) and soft guidance modules are novel and effective, demonstrated by both qualitative and quantitative results (Reviewer 1). This work is the first to leverage disentanglement of salient-object depth and inpainted background depth for training a relative-depth-aware diffusion model (Reviewer 3).
- The method enables compositional control with two depth images, allowing separate conditioning on foreground and background content (Reviewer 2).
- The soft guidance technique is novel for applying global semantics to specific localizations (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 2 notes this is the first use of attention maps to control local image styles.
- The synthetic training data generation strategy (using single-view image datasets) is key to the method’s success and may be of broader interest (Reviewer 2).
- Thorough qualitative and quantitative evaluations outperform several strong baselines in image quality, image-text alignment, and foreground-background disentanglement (Reviewers 1, 2). Adequate experiments support the model’s effectiveness, including control of relative object placement and prevention of concept bleeding (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **“Depth-aware” is misleading.** The depth from MiDaS is scale- and shift-invariant, not metric depth; thus, the exact ordering of objects is not induced by the depth maps (e.g., the cat can appear in front of or behind the cake given the same depth maps). This calls into question what the model actually learns (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited to depth conditions; generalization unclear.** The paper primarily focuses on depth. It is unclear how the architecture would generalize to other condition types (e.g., edges, surface normals). Can the model apply two different conditions simultaneously (e.g., foreground depth, background edges) while maintaining 3D consistency? (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 similarly suggests that other spatial conditions (e.g., segmentation masks) might work equally well and encourages ablation experiments to fully reveal behavior.
- **Missing visualization and analysis of training triplets.** There is no visualization of the prepared image triplets (foreground image, background image, foreground mask). The inpainting process for generating the background image is particularly concerning – artifacts or inconsistencies could negatively impact training. The paper lacks a detailed analysis of inpainting quality and its potential influence on model performance (Reviewer 1).
- **Qualitative results show incomplete depth conditioning.** The prompts for examples in Figure 3 are not provided, making it difficult to assess instruction-following. Across Figures 3, 4, and 5, the final generated images do not fully reflect the foreground depth condition (foreground objects often have a slightly different depth structure than the provided depth map). Furthermore, the background depth map appears largely ignored in qualitative results, raising questions about the effectiveness of background depth conditioning (Reviewer 1).
- **Soft guidance implementation is unclear.** The details of how the mask is applied in soft guidance are confusing: does it occur during similarity computation or after? The specific dimensions and computational steps need elucidation (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 finds the illustration in Figure 2 confusing and prefers color-coding of attention maps.
- **Evaluation metrics are not meaningful for core claims.** The Mean Absolute Error (MAE) in Table 2, used to evaluate depth reconstruction, is not meaningful because MiDaS does not predict metric depth. No qualitative comparison between input and reconstructed depth maps is provided (Reviewer 2). Are there specific metrics to assess the model’s ability to control 3D object placement and localize global semantics? MAE alone does not capture the latter; other metrics or evaluation methods are needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Reconstruction experiments (Figure 6) lack details.** The exact evaluation procedure is unclear from the text (Reviewer 2).
- **No discussion of limitations or societal impacts** is included in the submission (Reviewer 1).
