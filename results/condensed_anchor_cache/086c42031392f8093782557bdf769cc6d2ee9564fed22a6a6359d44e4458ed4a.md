## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes S4MC, a semi-supervised semantic segmentation method that refines pseudo-labels by considering the spatial correlation of neighboring pixels (3×3 grid) rather than filtering each pixel in isolation. It introduces a confidence-based pseudo-label refinement (PLR) module and a Dynamic Partition Adjustment (DPA) module that gradually lowers the threshold during training. Experiments on PASCAL VOC 2012 and Cityscapes show improved mIoU over baselines like CutMix-Seg and FixMatch. Reviewers found the idea conceptually simple and relevant, but raised concerns about experimental clarity, robustness, boundary artifacts, and marginal gains in many scenarios.

### Strengths
- **Originality and novelty**: Using contextual information from neighboring pixels to refine pseudo-labels is a novel approach rarely discussed in prior literature (R1, R2). The pixel-selection and propagation concept is simple yet intriguing (R2). The angle of solving pseudo-labeling through an encompassing unified module may open new perspectives (R4).
- **Combinability and simplicity**: The method can be combined with many existing semi-supervised segmentation techniques (R3). It is conceptually simple and effective, e.g., Table 1 shows a 4 pp mIoU improvement on PASCAL VOC augment with 1/1 training images (R3). The computational overhead is negligible (R1).
- **Quality of experiments**: Experiments are thorough and well-designed, covering all settings, and the appendix provides additional results (R2). Ablative studies justify design decisions (R1).
- **Clarity**: The paper is well-structured, easy to follow, and an interesting read (R1, R2). The method is sound and explained in detail (R1).
- **Significant improvement over baseline** (R2): Despite overall marginal gains, the method achieves notable improvements in some settings (e.g., +4 pp on VOC aug 1/1 as per R3).

**Minority positive contrast**: Reviewer 2 (score 6, confidence 5) is consistently more positive than the other three reviewers (scores 5 each). R2 emphasizes the novelty and thoroughness, and considers the improvement significant, while others note marginal gains and limited robustness.

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal and inconsistent improvements**: The +1.29 mIoU on PASCAL VOC 2012 and +1.01 on Cityscapes stated at the beginning are not consistently backed by numbers in Tables 1 and 3; the method is not robust across all tested scenarios (R1). Most improvements are less than 1% and within error range (R4).
- **Failure cases not addressed**:
    - When the model mispredicts a majority of an object region (e.g., labeling sofa as chair, car as bus), the refinement cannot correct and may propagate errors (R2).
    - At boundary regions, neighboring pixels have similar low confidence; the method is unlikely to make decisive corrections and does not explicitly address boundary ambiguity (R2). Qualitative results show exaggerated artifacts at boundaries despite using neighboring pixels and a decaying distance factor (R4).
- **Confusing ablation studies**: Section 4.3 (Tables 4 & 5) is unclear. The text states experiments used CutMix-Seg, but the numbers do not correspond; Table 5 caption says FixMatch, causing mismatch between text, numbers, and tables (R1). The difference between best numbers in Tables 4a/b and Table 5 needs explanation (R3, Q6).
- **Limited experimental scope**: Only two datasets (PASCAL VOC 2012, Cityscapes) used; more varied and challenging scenarios (e.g., complicated backgrounds, multiple depth facades) are needed to understand limitations (R1, R4). Only two sets of qualitative results are shown (Fig. 3), insufficient to assess segmentation quality; correspondence between qualitative and quantitative is not obvious (R4).
- **Lack of analysis on 3×3 window**: No insight is given why a 3×3 neighborhood is optimal. Performance degradation with larger windows suggests context is not properly used (R1). This is a missing ablation/analysis.
- **Ineffective refinement in later training**: Figure 4-b shows that the refinement becomes ineffective at the end of training; the reason is not explained (R2, Q4). Similarly, the method does not work well for the evaluation set (R2, Q1).
- **Incomplete comparisons**:
    - Comparison with UniMatch is difficult due to different experimental setups; reasons for reproducing UniMatch instead of copying original numbers are not explained (R3, W1).
    - Comparison with CutMix-Seg uses different backbones and segmentation architectures (R3, W2).
    - The source of CutMix-Seg’s experimental performance is not stated (R3, G4).
    - Variance across different subsets of labeled/unlabeled images is not reported (R3, W3).
    - It is not confirmed whether supervised baselines are equal across all approaches in Tables 1–3 (R3, Q2).
- **Missing ablations and analyses**:
    - The improvement due to threshold γ_t applied to union-level confidence vs. using union-level predictions in the loss (Eq. 3) is not decoupled (R3, Q3).
    - An ablation study using k-NN propagation with various pixel selection strategies is suggested (R2).
    - No experiments with ResNet-50 to allow reproduction on modest hardware (R3, Q5).
- **Presentation and clarity issues**:
    - Figure 1 elements are too small and hard to follow (R1).
    - The term "coarse PASCAL" should be corrected to "augmented PASCAL" (R3, G1).
    - The phrase "information gain" should be rephrased as it is often a synonym for KL divergence (R3, G2).
    - Descriptions of related work are incomplete; e.g., the sentence about "unreliable prediction" fails to describe the gist of Wang et al. 2022 (R3, G3).
    - Equation (3) should replace \(\hat{y}\) with \(f_{\theta_t}(x_i^u)\) for clarity (R3, G5).
- **Hardware requirements not reported**: Minimal GPU RAM and GPU days needed for reproduction are missing (R3, Q7).
