## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies MultiFilterANN (MFANN): approximate nearest neighbor search under the constraint that the query’s label set \(S_q\) must be a subset of the retrieved vector’s label set \(S_v\). The authors connect MFANN to the subset query problem (SQ) and prove theoretical guarantees by combining the CIP data structure (Charikar et al., 2002) with LSH or graph-based ANN indexes (Theorems 4.1, 4.2). Empirically, they propose a heuristic algorithm that extends FilteredDiskANN (Gollapudi et al., 2023) with a greedy search penalty using an asymmetric label distance (inspired by NHQ, Wang et al., 2024) and optional query planning (Appendix E). Experiments on real and synthetic datasets claim competitiveness with ACORN and ParlayIVF², especially for two filters. However, reviewers find a major disconnect between the theory (CIP-based) and the practical algorithm (FilteredDiskANN + penalty), raise concerns about the correctness of theoretical claims, and note missing baselines and hyperparameter sensitivity.

### Strengths
- The problem (MFANN) is important in real-world search and recommendation applications where vector labels (e.g., date, price, color) must satisfy subset constraints. (R2, R3, R4)
- The theoretical connection between MFANN and the subset query problem is interesting and novel. The analysis using the CIP data structure is neat. (R2, R3)
- The proposed penalty search (asymmetric label distance) is an intuitive adaptation of NHQ’s symmetric penalty to subset queries and appears to improve performance empirically. (R1, R2)
- The paper offers both theoretical and practical contributions; the lessons (graph-based methods are powerful; query planning is important) are clearly stated. (R3)
- The empirical algorithm is competitive with existing methods in some settings, and the new datasets fill a gap in the literature. (R2, R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical claims may be incorrect.** The CIP data structure (Charikar et al., 2002) does not report *all* points \(x\) such that \(S_q \subseteq S_x\); it reports only a subset of size \(n^\delta\). Consequently, LSH (or graph) may miss the true nearest neighbor, breaking the \((1+\varepsilon)\)-approximation guarantee. The proof of Theorem 4.1 and 4.2 is therefore unsound, and it is unclear how to fix it without increasing time/space complexity. (R3, R4)  
  (Minority reviewer R3 is more positive overall and does not question the theory as strongly in their overall judgment, but still raises this specific flaw.)
- **Theory and practice are disconnected.** The theoretical algorithm (Section 4) uses CIP + LSH/graph, but the practical method (Section 6) is a different approach: FilteredDiskANN with a penalty distance (inherited from NHQ). The “lessons” drawn from theory (e.g., graph power, query planning) are not logically derived from the analysis; graph power is already known from standard ANN, and query planning is an ad‑hoc heuristic unrelated to CIP routing. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Missing baselines and ablations.**
  - FilteredDiskANN and NHQ are not compared, despite the proposed algorithm being a direct variant of them. (R2, R3, R4)
  - The key parameter \(\lambda\) (penalty weight) is not investigated for sensitivity or selection; its setting across datasets is unclear. (R2, R3, R4)
  - Ablation on the penalty search is only performed on a single dataset; ablation on the query planning (Appendix E hybrid method) is missing entirely. (R2)
  - It is unclear whether experimental results (Section 7) come from the graph method (Section 6) or the hybrid approach (Appendix E) that may fall back to brute force or clustering. (R1) If the hybrid method is used, a direct comparison with the same fallback for baselines is needed; otherwise, results are misleading. (R1, R2)
- **Empirical evaluation concerns.**
  - On the Amazon dataset, the proposed method shows no improvement over ACORN; the reasons are not analyzed. (R2)
  - ParlayIVF² appears only once on the Pareto frontier in two experiments – why? (R2)
  - Ablation studies on query correlation and filter selectivity are claimed to be less interesting than ablations on the core algorithmic components. (R2)
  - The claim of “multiple novel datasets” is overstated; the only truly novel dataset is one new real-world set; synthetic datasets are standard. (R2)
- **Relevance and practicality of the setting.** When the number of filters \(|S_q|\) is large, the set of matching database points is likely very small, so brute‑force search may be both simpler and faster than complicated filtered ANN methods. The paper does not compare against brute‑force or discuss when the proposed method is beneficial. (R1)
- **Proof of Theorem 6.1 (dist’ penalty) appears incorrect.** With large \(\lambda\), the second term dominates the fused distance \(dist'\), so the search may not respect the original vector distance. The argument in the appendix is heuristic and does not guarantee retrieval of a point \(x\) with \(S_q \subseteq S_x\) and small vector distance. (R3, R4)
- **Presentation quality is low.**
  - Citations lack parentheses (e.g., “Indyk & Motwani 1998a” should be “(Indyk & Motwani 1998a)”). (R1, R2)
  - The Related Work section contains a long list of 26+ citations with no discussion, only claims without evidence (e.g., “CAPS suffers significantly as m increases” – by how much? “ACORN does not scale well to even simple AND predicates” – by how?). (R1, R3, R4)
  - Theorem 4.1 is missing “for every \(\delta > 0\)” in its statement. (R2)
  - Proofs in appendix are not referenced in lemma statements (e.g., Lemmas 5.1–5.3). (R2)
  - Figures placed side‑by‑side are not of the same size. (R2)
  - The algorithm does not have a distinct name, making it hard to refer to. (R2)
  - A discussion section is absent. (R2)
  - The theoretical results are only for \(\ell_2\) (implied by LSH analysis). (R2)
- **Overly ad‑hoc components.** The penalty search introduces two hyperparameters (\(\lambda, \tau\)) that are non‑trivial to set; the query planning cutoffs are also ad‑hoc with no evidence of shared settings across datasets. (R2)
- **Failure cases analyzed are not insightful.** The provided toy examples show catastrophic failures for prior methods, but ANN search is typically robust in practice; worst‑case analysis here does not yield new understanding. (R2)
