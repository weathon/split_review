## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents a fine-grained local sensitivity analysis of standard dot-product self-attention. The local sensitivity is formulated as the constrained optimization \(\max_{X': \|X'-X\|_F \le \epsilon} \|F(X')-F(X)\|_F\), where \(F\) is the residual self-attention. The gap is split into \(\Delta_1\) and \(\Delta_2\) (Eqs. 7–8). An analytical upper bound \(\xi(x)\) is given for \(\Delta_1\); Lemma 2 bounds the second part. Experiments on ViT with CIFAR‑10 demonstrate nonzero certified robustness \(\epsilon \approx 36/255\). All reviewers note clear derivations and empirical validation, but disagree on contribution: **Reviewer 1 (score 8, weak accept)** finds the analysis insightful and practical; **Reviewers 2 and 3 (score 5 each)** consider the contribution limited, citing easy derivation (e.g., Lipschitz via gradients, Tian et al. 2023) and the neglect of the expressivity–stability trade‑off.

### Strengths
- Proof organization is clear and easy to follow; the authors meticulously describe looseness of naive bounds and strategies to tighten them (R1).
- The local sensitivity analysis is insightful for both adversarial robustness and the broader ML community (R1).
- Experiments on a real-world task (ViT on CIFAR‑10) make the theory practical (R1).
- Captures an important problem – self-attention sensitivity in Transformers – quantifies it, and provides insight for achieving stability; topic is relevant to NLP and CV (R2).
- Theoretical results are clearly derived (R2).
- Experiments are closely related to the theoretical claims (R2, R3).
- Upper bound is derived and numerical validations support its tightness and reasonableness (R3).

### Weaknesses
- Insufficient novelty: the bounds in Section 4 are essentially a Lipschitz constant derivation via the gradient w.r.t. \(X\); similar closed‑form gradients appear in prior work (e.g., Tian et al. “Scan and Snap”, 2023) (R2).
- The paper ignores the expressivity–stability trade‑off: small weight norms reduce sensitivity but also degrade representation ability (extreme case: all weights near zero). No guidance is given on how to balance both (R2).
- Missing cross‑domain evaluation: robustness experiments are only on vision (CIFAR‑10); the analysis is supposed to be general, so NLP tasks (text classification, entailment) should be tested to confirm domain transferability (R1; overlaps with a missing experiment).
- Lacks a concrete demonstration of how Theorem 1 can be used to predict local sensitivity for a given weight set (e.g., ablating each weight in \((W_Q,W_K,W_V,W_O)\) for a fixed input \(X\)) (R1; overlaps with an ablation request).
- Several naive bounds described in the theory (Eq. 9 for \(\Delta_1\) and \(\|W_l^V W_l^O\|(\|X\|+\epsilon)\) for \(\|X' W_l^V W_l^O\|\)) are not evaluated in Figure 1, weakening the claim of conservatism (R1).
- Undefined matrix dimensions: \(W^O\) and \(H\) are not specified (e.g., \(W^O \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\), \(H \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\)) (R3).
- Solving Eq. (10) requires SVD of an \(n\times d\) matrix; computational efficiency is not addressed (R3).
- The bound for the second term in Eq. (14) uses the triangle inequality, but a closed‑form solution may be obtainable because both the objective and constraint are linear (R3).
- Analysis is limited to \(\ell_2\) perturbations; extension to \(\ell_\infty\) perturbations is not discussed, and the techniques may not be directly applicable (R3; overlaps with a missing comparison).
- Minor typo in Eq. 5: \(\|F(X')-F(x)\|\) should be \(\|F(X')-F(X)\|\) (R1).
- Open questions from the reviewers: how to theoretically guarantee both expressivity and stability; whether the bound can be improved when weights follow a specific distribution, or whether it depends only on weight magnitude (R2).
