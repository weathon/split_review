## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MiniPLM, an offline knowledge distillation (KD) framework for pre-training language models that refines the training data distribution using a large teacher model. The core innovation, Difference Sampling, selects instances where the teacher is confident but a small reference model is not, by comparing log-probabilities. Offline teacher inference (once, storing 200MB for 50B tokens vs. 30PB for online methods) avoids training-time overhead, and the method works across model families (e.g., Llama → Mamba). Experiments on student models from 200M to 1.2B parameters show improved downstream performance on 9 tasks, better language modeling, and a 2.2× computation reduction for equivalent performance. The paper includes theoretical justification (Proposition 2.1). One reviewer (score 8) was notably more positive, emphasizing strong theoretical foundations and data efficiency for low-data scenarios; the other four reviewers (score 6) raised several concerns about scope, clarity, and missing analyses.

### Strengths
- **Novel offline KD via Difference Sampling**: MiniPLM introduces an efficient offline method that compares teacher and reference model probabilities to select challenging, high-value training instances. It requires only a single pass of teacher inference (200MB storage for 50B tokens, vs. 30PB for online methods). (All reviewers)
- **Theoretical grounding**: The paper provides a clear theoretical justification through Proposition 2.1, framing KD with reverse KL divergence as reward maximization. (Reviewer 2, 3)
- **Computational efficiency**: MiniPLM achieves 2.2× computation reduction for equivalent downstream performance, and scales to larger models (200M, 500M, 1.2B students). (Reviewer 1, 3)
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Extensive evaluation across 9 downstream tasks, with comparisons to multiple baselines, demonstrating consistent improvements. (Reviewer 1, 2, 4, 5)
- **Cross-family flexibility**: The method supports KD across different model families (e.g., Llama 3.1 teacher to Mamba student), showing generality beyond tokenizer matching. (Reviewer 4)
- **Data efficiency**: Especially beneficial when training data is limited, improving utilization of pre-training corpora. (Reviewer 2, 5)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing teacher performance in main results**: Table 1 does not report the teacher model (1.8B) performance, making it impossible to assess how much of the teacher's capability is transferred to the student. (Reviewer 3)
- **Limited teacher model scale**: All experiments use a 1.8B teacher. No results with larger model gaps (e.g., Llama 3.1 8B to 1B) to demonstrate scalability to modern large teachers. (Reviewer 3, 4)
- **Insufficient ablation of Difference Sampling**: The comparison with SeqKD may be confounded by implementation details – SeqKD uses only 768 tokens for approximation, which could explain its lower performance. A more rigorous ablation isolating the sampling strategy is needed. (Reviewer 3; also Reviewer 4 notes limited exploration of alternative sampling strategies)
- **Computational and resource complexity**: The method requires three models: a large teacher, a reference model trained from scratch on a 50B-token subset, and then the final student. This increases training complexity and memory usage; the paper does not sufficiently discuss training time, GPU hours, or memory overhead. (Reviewer 2, 5)
- **Theoretical concerns about the sampling metric**: The claim that instances with log(p_teacher/p_ref) < 0 are “harmful” ignores the phenomenon of “overthinking” in PLMs (as noted in prior work), where easy samples can be harder for a larger model due to feature distraction. The justification for using log(p_teacher/p_ref) as a sampling metric is not fully provided. (Reviewer 5; also Reviewer 4 asks for theoretical justification)
- **Unclear relationship between p_ref and student**: It is not clearly explained how p_ref (trained from scratch) can mimic the student q_θ, nor why MiniPLM outperforms MiniLLM even though MiniLLM directly uses q_θ’s distribution. (Reviewer 5)
- **Narrow experimental scope**: Experiments are limited to a 1.8B teacher and a few model sizes; no comparison with recent small LM approaches such as MobileLLM in the 200M/500M range. Cross-family experiments with Llama 3.1 and Mamba lack details on model configurations, training data, and setup. (Reviewer 1, 3)
- **Clarity of explanation**: The Difference Sampling method could benefit from more intuitive examples and simpler exposition. (Reviewer 1)
- **Practical limitations not highlighted**: The requirement for teacher probabilities on the entire pre-training corpus may be infeasible for closed-source models (e.g., GPT-4) or resource-constrained environments; this limitation is not discussed explicitly. (Reviewer 1)
- **Reference model selection**: Criteria for choosing the reference model (size, training data amount) are not fully justified; there is no analysis of how this choice affects performance. (Reviewer 4)
- **Lack of finite-sample analysis**: The paper does not analyze performance when the reference model is trained on finite data or when the teacher’s probabilities are estimated from limited samples. (Reviewer 4)
- **Title misalignment**: The title implies KD is used directly during pre-training, but the method actually uses KD only for subset selection; the pre-training itself is standard causal LM. (Reviewer 5)
