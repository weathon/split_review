## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Salvage, a removal-based explainability method for image classification using Shapley-distributions and an informed sampling strategy to address unbalanced feature importance. Evidence shows it outperforms baselines on ImageNette, MURA, WBC, and Pet datasets and can serve as a fully explainable classifier. Reviewers rated 6,6,6,8; the method builds on ViT-Shapley and is architecture-agnostic.

### Strengths
- The issues identified with existing Shapley-based methods (e.g., MSE approximation limitations, imbalance from uniform sampling) are valid and relevant.
- A new concept of Shapley-distributions is introduced for more accurate approximation of classification probability distributions.
- The informed sampling strategy reduces imbalance and facilitates estimation of underrepresented features.
- Comparison methods, datasets, and metrics are comprehensive; Salvage shows clear advantage on some metrics (e.g., surpasses ViT-Shapley, RISE, and other removal-, attention-, and gradient-based methods).
- Method is well-evaluated, architecture agnostic, and can replace the classifier with comparable accuracy.
- The paper is clear and well-explained (reviewer 4); presentation is comprehensive (reviewer 2). The method is not completely novel but improves shortcomings of SOTA (reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Target model not specified**: The paper does not clearly define what the target classification model is (architecture, parameters). Evidence insufficient that the method works across different target models; tests on diverse architectures (not just datasets) are needed (reviewer 1, confidence 4).
- **Missing qualitative comparisons**: Figure 1 lacks baseline methods; absence suggests selective presentation (reviewer 1). All baselines should be included.
- **Incomplete ablation study**: No analysis of which component (distribution learning vs. informative sampling) is more critical. Only ablation on informative sampling is performed; no ablation for Shapley-distribution estimation alone (reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 3 notes "only make ablation study on INFORMATIVE SAMPLING". Question: "How is the experimental performance when using SHAPLEY DISTRIBUTION ESTIMATION alone?" (reviewer 3).
- **No empirical validation of claimed issues**: Limitations of MSE-based approximation and imbalance from uniform sampling are described conceptually but lack concrete experimental support (reviewer 1).
- **Efficiency claim unsubstantiated**: "Informative sampling improves efficiency" lacks theoretical proof or experimental results (e.g., faster convergence vs. uniform sampling) (reviewer 3).
- **Underperformance on PET dataset (RRA metric)**: Salvage underperforms SOTA by ~3 points in RRA on PET, with no analysis or discussion. Characteristics of PET that cause this are not considered (reviewer 3). Question: "Why is informative sampling's improvement less noticeable in Pet dataset?" (reviewer 2).
- **Typographical errors in Table 1**: Numbers like “68,56” should be “68.56” – undermines credibility (reviewer 3).
- **Incomplete table formatting**: Table 2 bottom line not drawn (reviewer 3).
- **Unclear notation and procedures**: Eq. 2 explanation insufficient ("p_w(S) ∝ w_S"); relationship between weight w_S and sampling distribution not defined; sampling procedure lacks rigor (reviewer 3).
- **Limited scope of evaluation**: Tested on few datasets (ImageNette, MURA, WBC, Pet); needs more diverse/complex datasets (medical with different contrasts, satellite, non-image data) and domains (object detection, segmentation) to assess broad applicability (reviewers 2, 3). Question: "Is this method applicable to object detection or segmentation?" (reviewer 3). Question: "How would different neural architecture designs for the explainer model impact performance?" (reviewer 2).
- **More extensive comparisons needed**: Should compare with wider array of contemporary removal-based methods (different masking strategies, iterative removal) to reveal limitations (reviewer 2).
- **Temperature parameter not explored**: Impacts of temperature changes in softmax/sigmoid on distribution sharpness and explanation quality; computational cost of tuning not investigated (reviewer 2).
- **Minority reviewer 4 more positive**: Reviewer 4 scores 8, finds paper clear and well-evaluated, sees "no much concern". Contrast: other reviewers (6) have multiple weaknesses. Reviewer 4 states method is incremental but better than ViT-Shapley. Note this disagreement but preserve both views.
