## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Conditional Adversarial Support Alignment (CASA) to minimize the Conditional Symmetric Support Divergence (CSSD) between source and target feature representations for domain adaptation under label shift. It provides a theoretical target risk bound and empirical results. The paper is generally well-written (R1, R3) and easy to follow (R1), but several reviewers note significant issues regarding novelty, missing related work, theoretical gaps, and insufficient experimental comparisons.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1); evaluation covers several benchmark tasks (R1).
- A conditional variant of SSD (CSSD) and corresponding theoretical analysis are provided (R2, R3).
- A discrepancy optimization model is proposed to address domain adaptation with label shift (R2).
- CASA addresses the drawback of Adversarial Support Alignment (ASA) by considering discriminative features to align conditional distributions, thus mitigating the risk of misalignment from marginal support divergence reduction (R3).
- Theoretical target error bounds are provided (R2, R3).
- Superior experiment results are achieved on multiple benchmarks under label shift (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty is incremental.** The alignment loss (Eq. 10) resembles pairwise alignment loss explored before for cross-domain graph alignment, making the novelty difficult to verify (R1). The major difference from ASA is the conditional alignment via CSSD, but the underlying approach is an incremental combination of multiple loss functions (R1).
- **Missing discussion of closely related literature.** The problem setting is equivalent to generalized target/label shift, where label distribution and conditional distribution change simultaneously. The paper lacks a thorough discussion of methods that address both label and conditional shifts (R2), and omits comparisons with important works on generalized target/label shift [a-f] (R2). Several recent state-of-the-art UDA methods (SHOT, BIWAA, CoVi) are also not discussed or compared (R3). This absence makes it difficult to ascertain the novelty and practical significance of CASA (R2, R3).
- **Theoretical analysis is insufficient.**
  - The generalization bound (Thm. 1) is less compact and not informative; it includes terms such as the joint error on both domains and non-negative constants $\delta, \gamma$ induced by IMD that are not directly controlled by the learning process (R2). In contrast, existing generalized label shift bounds decompose the shift more compactly into label discrepancy and conditional discrepancy without such intractable constants (R2).
  - The paper introduces CSSD (Def. 4) but lacks a rigorous proof that it satisfies the properties of a valid metric (non-negativity, symmetry, triangle inequality) on conditional distributions (R2). This raises concerns about the theoretical validity of the proposed method (R2). The weighting of divergence terms by label probability masses $P(Y=y)$ in the definition is not clearly motivated or justified (R2).
  - The discussion in Remark 3 is insufficient and does not properly state advantages of existing results; related works [d,f] are not discussed or compared (R2).
- **Organization and clarity issues.** While one reviewer found the paper well-written (R1), another noted insufficient justification and intuition for mathematical definitions and theoretical results, e.g., the motivation behind the specific weighting in CSSD is unclear (R2). This lack of clarity makes it hard to understand the underlying principles (R2).
- **Insufficient analysis and visualization of experimental results.** The improvements when $\alpha$ decreases are observed but no insight is provided as to why this happens; more visualization and intuition are needed (R1).
- **Pseudo-label handling is not adequately justified.** The target domain labels are unknown, and the authors use an entropy conditioning technique [1] which is not designed for generating pseudo-labels (R3). The paper does not explain how this method is adapted to mitigate the error accumulation problem typically associated with pseudo-labels (R3).
- **Experimental comparisons are incomplete.** Several related methods addressing generalized target/label shift [a-f] are omitted from the experimental validation, limiting the ability to evaluate the superiority of CASA (R2). Additionally, modern state-of-the-art methods such as SHOT, BIWAA, and CoVi should be compared (R3).
