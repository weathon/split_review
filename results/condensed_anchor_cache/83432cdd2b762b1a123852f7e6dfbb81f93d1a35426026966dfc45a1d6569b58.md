## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents Fox-TTS, a flow-matching Transformer model with a learnable speaker encoder using temporal mean pooling, data augmentation, and an information bottleneck for expressive zero-shot TTS. The authors also introduce Fox-eval, a multi-speaker, multi-style benchmark for expressive scenarios. They claim on-par quality with human recordings and state-of-the-art performance in expressive settings, but only compare with CosyVoice. Reviewers find the experimental evaluation insufficient, the novelty limited, and several key claims unsupported.

### Strengths
- Outperforms the single baseline CosyVoice in pronunciation accuracy (WER) and speaker similarity (SIM). On the DiDiSpeech dataset, Fox-TTS achieves objective metric values close to human recordings.
- Proposes a speaker conditioning approach via adaptive layer norm in a Transformer, which differs from typical in-context prompting and challenges the trend of variable-length speaker embeddings.
- Shows the potential of a learnable fixed-length speaker encoder as an alternative to in-context methods.
- Introduces Fox-eval benchmark for expressive zero-shot TTS.
- Implementation details appear solid.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient baselines and evaluation**: Only compared with CosyVoice. Missing comparisons with numerous recent zero-shot TTS models that use in-context learning or variable-length embeddings, such as VALL-E, VoiceBox, NaturalSpeech 2/3, P-Flow, E2TTS, F5-TTS, Seed-TTS, DiTTo-TTS, OpenVoice, and TorToise. Reviewers urge following the standard evaluation on LibriSpeech Test-Clean (4–10 s sentences) with WER, SIM-O, SIM-R using the same transcription model and speaker embedding model as prior works (e.g., the wav2vec 2.0 and WavLM-based models). Also should measure in different data regimes (e.g., LibriTTS, LibriLight) to separate data size from method effectiveness.
- **Experimental setup differs from prior works**: The fixed-length speaker embedding approach is controversial because variable-length embeddings have become the de facto choice for state-of-the-art zero-shot similarity. Without a controlled comparison using identical data, it is impossible to assess the proposed method relative to the approaches it claims to surpass.
- **Limited novelty and missing ablations**: The model architecture closely resembles Seed-TTS; the main difference is using a learnable speaker encoder, which is not novel. No ablation compares different speaker conditioning methods (e.g., mean pooling vs. learned pooling vs. in-context prompting). The three model variants (Fox-TTS_LM, Fox-TTS_LM+Flow, Fox-TTS_Flow) are introduced but only Fox-TTS_Flow is described, and experiments cover only the speaker-encoder–conditioned variant. Without experiments, conclusions about the variants are premature.
- **Unsupported claims**: The claim that the information bottleneck balances pronunciation stability and voice cloning similarity (line 224–227) lacks any experimental verification. Similarly, the paper asserts that previous prompt-based or speaker-encoder methods “struggle to fully capture acoustic characteristics of the stylized speaker” without evidence; those methods likely underperform due to less stylized training data rather than inherent design.
- **Incomplete training details**: Training data size and language composition are not provided. The automated pipeline for large-scale data ingestion and the Fox-eval dataset are mentioned but details are lacking, and it is unclear whether they will be shared with the community.
- **Misleading characterization of prior literature**: The statement that “there is a lack of comprehensive research on diffusion or flow-matching models for large-scale TTS training” is inaccurate. Prior works include TorToise, VoiceBox, DiTTo-TTS, Seed-TTS, E2-TTS, OpenVoice, and CosyVoice.
- **Missing explanations and citations**: The use of temporal data augmentation in the speaker encoder needs citation to prior works in TTS and voice conversion that used similar techniques. The term “semantic” is used without definition—if it refers to phonemes, that should be stated. The impact of using a logistic normal distribution (presumably in flow matching) on final performance is not discussed.
- **Demo and evaluation limitations**: All demo samples are in Chinese, limiting assessment by non-Chinese speakers. English samples should be added. The Fox-eval benchmark is only used to compare against CosyVoice, so its usefulness for validating the claimed benefits of the proposed method remains unsubstantiated. Reviewers recommend adding WavLM-based SIM evaluation to demonstrate the effectiveness of the speaker encoder over in-context prompting.
