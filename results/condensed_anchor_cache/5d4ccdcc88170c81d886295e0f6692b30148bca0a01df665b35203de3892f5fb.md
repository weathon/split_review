## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes TidalFlow, a source-free UDA framework for time series that integrates temporal and frequency domain features via a dual-stream encoder, hierarchical embedding table (HET), and voting mechanism. The authors claim temporal features offer higher discriminability and frequency features better transferability, achieving ~10% improvement on five benchmarks. Reviewers (scores 5,5,5,6) agreed on the novelty of the idea and the thoroughness of the experiments, but they diverged on the rigor of validation, clarity of methodology, and positioning relative to prior work. One reviewer (score 6) particularly praised the originality and clarity, while others (score 5) emphasized insufficient validation of core claims, incomplete literature review, and missing baselines.

### Strengths
- Novel insight into the complementary roles of temporal (discriminability) and frequency (transferability) domains; moves beyond traditional alignment to class-specific feature extraction (R1, R4).
- The voting mechanism for feature fusion is promising (R1).
- Strong empirical results with ~10% improvement across five benchmark datasets (R1, R3).
- Thorough experiments on five datasets provide sufficient validation (R3).
- Paper is well written, clear, and well organized; problem statement and motivation explicit; framework components (dual-stream encoder, HET, voting) well detailed; visualizations and PCA plots effective (R2, R4).
- Offers a new perspective that opens new avenues for UDA by focusing on feature extraction and integration (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Core claims validation**: The claims that temporal features are more discriminative and frequency features more transferable lack rigorous experimental or theoretical evidence. Specific quantitative measures should be provided (R1). One reviewer noted the preliminary study on the small, imbalanced WISDM dataset is unreliable – accuracy is used instead of F1-score, and 100% accuracy is easily achieved. This undermines the conclusions (R2).
- **Literature review and positioning**: The claim that time-frequency UDA is underexplored is inaccurate; prior works such as FECAM and Adaptive temporal-frequency network are not acknowledged. The paper should clearly articulate how TidalFlow advances beyond these existing approaches (R1). A categorization or summary table grouping related works by application domain (e.g., video, speech, CV, time series) would be beneficial (R3).
- **Evaluation methodology**: The dual-backbone architecture introduces confounding factors. Source-only performance for both backbones is missing, making it impossible to isolate the contribution of the proposed method from architectural advantages (R1). Ablation studies are conducted on only one dataset; they should be extended to multiple datasets to demonstrate consistency (R1).
- **Positioning and problem formulation**: The paper is more suited to source-free UDA than traditional UDA (R2). The problem formulation section never states the objective; it needs to define what is done with the defined datasets (R2, R3).
- **Design clarity and missing details**:
  - How the encoder G is structured, how z_temp and z_freq are obtained, and how the encoder differentiates domain-specific vs. domain-invariant features are unclear (R2). The dimensions of frequency input and each module’s output are not given (R3).
  - Why concatenating z_temp and z_freq helps domain adaptation is not justified (R2).
  - The rationale for using reconstruction in the adaptation process is not explained (R3).
  - The “sg” operator in Eq. 4 is not defined directly after the equation (R2).
  - What e_h is (R2).
  - Why a hierarchical structure would be more effective for generalization (R2).
  - The extraction and adoption of frequency features for adaptation is not clearly described (R2).
- **Feature fusion analysis**: The voting mechanism’s superiority needs better justification. Why does voting outperform individual components? It should be compared with alternative fusion strategies like averaging or summing time and frequency features (R1). Underlying reasons for why the voting mechanism and dissimilarity loss L_D improve performance are not thoroughly discussed (R4).
- **Hyperparameter and computational analysis**:
  - No assessment of computational cost – scalability with number of categories H or embedding dimensionality is unclear; time/memory comparisons with baselines are missing (R4).
  - Sensitivity analysis is limited. Key hyperparameters (embedding table size, number of nearest neighbors in voting, α and β loss weights) are not explored (R4).
  - For reproducibility, a table listing key hyperparameters (learning rates, batch sizes, epochs) for all models, including baselines, should be provided (R3).
- **Training pipeline and aggregation**: It is unclear whether training and adaptation phases are simultaneous or sequential, and how adaptation completion is determined (R3). In Appendix Tables 3-7, the average and standard deviation are calculated across all models for each source-target pair; per-model statistics would facilitate easier comparison between models (R3).
- **Unanswered methodology questions**:
  - Why Fourier transform over alternatives like wavelet transform? Tradeoffs should be discussed (R3).
  - What is the relationship between frequency domain transferability and the learning rate value during the fine-tuning phase? What is the fine-tuning phase? (R2).
- **Presentation issues**: Fonts in figures (e.g., Fig. 1) are too small (R2). Typo in line 175: “until until” (R2, R3). References are incomplete (missing venue) – likely copied from arXiv (R2).
