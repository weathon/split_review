## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces CR-Planner, a framework that combines critic-guided planning with retrieval augmentation to tackle reasoning-heavy tasks like competitive programming, theorem-driven mathematics, and domain retrieval. It uses fine-tuned critic models for sub-goal selection and execution, with training data collected via Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS). Experiments show performance improvements over baselines, with an average gain of 10.06%. However, multiple reviewers raised concerns about computational cost, fairness of comparisons, generalizability, lack of ablations, limited novelty, and unclear technical details.

### Strengths
- **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-written, easy to follow, with clear visualizations that support the narrative (R1, R2, R4).
- **Strong experimental results**: CR-Planner significantly outperforms baseline methods, with an average improvement of 10.06% on challenging domains (R1, R2, R4). One reviewer found this striking (R2).
- **Meaningful problem and motivation**: Solving challenging reasoning problems is a worthwhile endeavor. The motivation to use fine-tuned critics for evaluating planning answers is intuitive, and decomposing planning into Reason, GenQuery, and Retrieve is reasonable (R2, R3).
- **Addresses data scarcity**: Using MCTS for data generation to train critic models is a practical contribution (R3).
- **Addresses compounding errors**: Stepwise critics can guide long plans without compounding errors, especially important when incorporating RAG (R4).
- **Diverse evaluation**: The method is evaluated on multiple challenging domains, demonstrating improvements in reasoning-intensive and retrieval-intensive tasks (R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **High computational cost**: The MCTS data collection and LLM sampling impose a heavy computational load, making the approach costly. The critic essentially distills the MCTS policy from GPT-4, and the extensive offline computations for data collection may not be reflected in comparisons (R1, R2, R3).
- **Fairness of comparisons and generalizability concerns**:  
  - The training prompts for the critic appear identical to test tasks, raising concerns about generalizability. The paper should demonstrate critic performance on different datasets (R1).  
  - The domain-specific tasks used for training critics are not stated; it is assumed that training subsets of the evaluation benchmarks are used, making comparisons unfair (R4).  
  - No comparison with fine-tuning the generating model using the same budget is provided (R4).  
  - Vanilla MCTS performance metrics are missing; the paper should quantify the degradation when using the learned critic approximator (R1).
- **Soundness issues**:  
  - The critic model for action selection is trained to optimize the reward function, which alone does not constitute an actionable policy. Using the reward function rather than a value or action-value function may not yield optimal trajectories (R3).  
  - The mixed action space (continuous temperature sampling for REASON/GENQUERY, top-k for RETRIEVE) is not clearly integrated; it is unclear how the discrete and continuous spaces are combined (R3).  
  - The sub-goal selection critic may be unnecessary because Reason, GenQuery, and Retrieve have a temporal relationship (reasoning first, then query generation, then retrieval) (R2).
- **Missing ablations and experiments**:  
  - No ablation of individual critics (sub-goal execution critics and sub-goal selection critic) is provided (R4).  
  - No experiments showing the impact of MCTS on critic fine-tuning, e.g., sample efficiency (R4).  
  - No experimental validation that decomposing into three stages (Reason, GenQuery, Retrieve) is beneficial; for example, whether fine-tuning a critic for the "Standard" baseline would yield similar gains (R2).
- **Limited novelty and missing related work comparisons**:  
  - The use of MCTS for reasoning is well-established in existing literature. The paper does not compare to or discuss related MCTS-based reasoning approaches such as Q*, Quiet-STaR, or "Monte Carlo Tree Search Boosts Reasoning via Iterative Preference Learning" (R3).  
  - Technical contributions are considered limited by some reviewers (R1, R3).
- **Clarity and detail deficiencies**:  
  - The problem formulation, critic model training methods, pairwise ranking loss, and data collection process for each critic need clearer explanation with comprehensive equations and descriptions (R3).  
  - It is unclear how the approach balances exploration in MCTS, whether data is collected in batches or increments, and the distribution of different data types (R3).  
  - The rationale for using the reward function over value/action-value functions is not explained (R3).
- **Reviewer disagreement**: While one reviewer (R2) rated the paper 6 and found the results striking and motivation intuitive, three other reviewers (R1, R3, R4) gave scores of 3, with significant concerns about fairness, novelty, soundness, and clarity.
