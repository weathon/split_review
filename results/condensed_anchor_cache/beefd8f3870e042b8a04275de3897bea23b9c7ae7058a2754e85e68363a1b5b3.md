## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a post-training structured pruning method that projects node activations to an orthogonal subspace via Gram‑Schmidt orthogonalization with a lower‑triangular transformation matrix. It uses importance‑score (ZCA‑based) pre‑ordering to rank nodes before orthogonalization and sets a global layer‑wise pruning ratio based on cumulative variance. The method is evaluated on VGG (VGG‑11,‑16,‑19) and ResNet‑50 for ImageNet classification, with and without retraining.

### Strengths
- The problem of structured pruning is important and well‑motivated. (Reviewers 1,4)
- The Gram‑Schmidt orthogonalization and importance‑score pre‑ordering are neat and conceptually clear. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- Global calibration of pruning ratios across layers is an attractive property. (Reviewer 1)
- Experiments demonstrate improvement over the compared methods both with and without retraining, and the benefit of the pre‑ordering is shown. (Reviewers 2,3)
- Results on VGG (Table 1) and ResNet‑50 (Table 2) are competitive against the selected baselines. (Reviewers 1,4) (Reviewer 2 notes the improvement is marginal but still positive.)

### Weaknesses
- **VGG results are not representative:** VGG’s first fully‑connected layer (7×7×512 → 4096) contains 102.76 M parameters out of 144 M total; such a massively oversized layer makes the network artificially easy to prune. Thus Table 1 does not indicate general pruning performance. (Reviewer 1)
- **ResNet‑50 results are not state‑of‑the‑art:** SNP‑ZCA is not the best in accuracy (Table 2) and improvements over baselines are marginal. (Reviewers 1,2,4)
- **Lack of architecture diversity:** The method is tested only on VGG and ResNet CNNs for ImageNet. No experiments on transformers, DenseNets, MobileNets, or other architectures. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- **Lack of task diversity:** No validation on language models, LLMs, generative models (GANs, diffusion), or other tasks beyond vision classification. (Reviewers 1,3)
- **Insufficient baselines:** Missing comparisons to low‑rank (SVD‑based) pruning, which is closely related. Also missing recent strong baselines such as "Structural Pruning via Latency‑Saliency Knapsack" (Shen et al., NeurIPS 2022) and "DFPC" (Narshana et al., ICLR 2023). No comparator for weight re‑compensation in no‑retraining experiments. (Reviewers 2,4)
- **Missing standard datasets:** No results on CIFAR‑10/100, a minimal expectation in pruning literature. (Reviewer 4)
- **Inference speed not measured:** Actual inference time improvements are not reported, despite being a stated motivation. (Reviewer 4)
- **Sensitivity and cost not studied:** No analysis of sensitivity to the number of calibration samples (s). The computational cost of the method is unclear. (Reviewer 4)
- **Poor writing and presentation:** The paper is poorly written, imprecise, and difficult to follow in critical sections. Examples: unclear justification for requiring orthogonal subspace (line 141 “least possible impact on dynamics”), confusion about dot product vectors (line 159), incoherent justification for lower‑triangular matrix, ambiguous definition of importance scores (line 204 vs. line 279, diagonal matrix?), and a non‑indicative title. Formatting is unusual and contains typos (e.g., line 206 should say “keep” not “n”). The paper appears rushed. (Reviewers 3,4)
- **Unclear source of improvement:** It is not clear whether the benefit comes from the orthogonalization or the importance ordering. (Reviewer 2) Reviewer 4 explicitly notes that the ZCA re‑ordering scheme does not seem useful.
- **Limited novelty:** The proposed weight re‑compensation is similar to linear‑least‑squares (LLS) and SVD‑based low‑rank pruning; the paper does not clearly distinguish what novel insight is provided. (Reviewers 2,4)
- **Unclear extension to CNNs and grouped importances:** The method’s application to CNNs and the precise computation of grouped/joint importances for ResNets (Appendix F) is not clearly explained. (Reviewer 4, Q2, Q3)
- **Unaddressed applicability to transformers:** The method’s extension to transformers is not discussed, and its behavior on language vs. vision tasks is unexplored. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- **Missing results:** SNP‑SAW results are not reported in Table 2. (Reviewer 4)
