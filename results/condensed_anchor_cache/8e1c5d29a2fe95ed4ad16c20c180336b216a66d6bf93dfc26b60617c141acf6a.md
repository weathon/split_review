## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers appreciated the multi-objective antibody design approach (binding affinity, non-specific binding, self-association, stability), the use of constrained preference optimization with continuous rewards, and the introduction of a structure-aware protein language model. AbNovo shows strong performance on physical metrics and includes many baselines. Reviewer 3 (score 8, increased after rebuttal) was more positive and found points well addressed; Reviewers 1 and 2 (both score 6) were less enthusiastic. All noted issues with evaluation metrics, missing experiments, clarity, and exaggerated claims.

### Strengths
- Considers multiple objectives (non-antigen binding specificity, low self-association) that are essential for real-world developability, not just binding affinity. (R1)
- Does not simply integrate DPO for binding affinity alone; broadens the optimization horizon. (R1)
- Achieves performance on physical metrics that significantly surpasses existing methods. (R2)
- Introduces a structure-aware protein language model and demonstrates its usefulness for antibody design. (R2)
- Provides rigorous theoretical derivations and analysis. (R2, R3)
- Includes a comprehensive set of baselines, showing good performance. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Energy evaluation concerns:**  
  - Rosetta binding energy has a weak correlation (~0.3) with experimentally measured binding affinity, potentially steering optimization in the wrong direction. (R1)  
  - In energy calculations, it is unclear whether the relaxation step optimizes only side-chain conformations or also the backbone. If the latter, the experiments may only show that AbNovo produces better initial structures for Rosetta relaxation. (R2)  
  - Standard deviations for physical energy metrics are not reported. (R2)
- **Exaggerated novelty claims:**  
  - Claim of being “the first deep generative model for multi-objective antibody design” is inaccurate; AbDPO and Pareto Front Training (Faris et al.) also support multi-objective optimization. (R2, R3)  
  - Claim of “bridging the gap between in silico design and practical application” is too strong, as no practical application is demonstrated. (R3)
- **Unfair or incomplete comparisons:**  
  - Comparison with dyMEAN is unfair because dyMEAN does not provide the real FR region structure, unlike AbNovo. It is also unclear how dyMEAN was used to generate 128 antibodies. (R2)  
  - dyMEAN is missing from Figure 4 and AbX from Figure 2, undermining completeness. (R3)  
  - RMSD calculation should be direct (without alignment) since the FR region is fixed and the CDR cannot independently undergo SE(3) transformations. (R2)  
  - The visualization (Figure 2) selects a DiffAb sample that violates constraints, yet Figure 4 shows that for antigen 5NUZ only one DiffAb sample violates constraints, making the choice inadequate. Additionally, the specific DiffAb sample with Rosetta binding energy -2.12 and Evolutionary Plausibility 2.60 is not found in Figure 4. (R3)
- **Missing experimental rigor and analyses:**  
  - Limited antibody optimization experiments; further experiments (e.g., as in [3,4]) needed to highlight antibody optimization. (R1)  
  - No analysis supports the claim of alleviating overfitting due to scarcity of antibody-antigen training data. (R3)  
  - The analysis of the impact of different language models is very short and not well described. (R3)  
  - AAR performance is excessively high; it should be checked whether the protein language model’s training data contains samples similar to the test set (data leakage). (R2)  
  - It is not reported how many amino acids are mutated in the designed antibodies that outperform natural ones in binding energy. (R2)  
  - It is unclear whether optimization of physical properties (stability, self-association) contributes to chemical validity, e.g., peptide bond lengths. (R2)  
  - No explanation is given for why dyMEAN and DiffAb samples in the visualization do not satisfy constraints like Stability and Self-association. (R1)
- **Clarity and presentation issues:**  
  - Important definitions are missing or introduced late (base/reference/policy model, δ and G in Eq. 1, CTMC, time t ∈ [0,1], T^(0:1)). A background section is needed. (R3)  
  - Evaluation metrics (Evolutionary Plausibility, Stability, Self-association, Non-specific Binding) remain unclear even after reading Appendix A.3. (R3)  
  - Intuition for key equations (e.g., Eq. 3) is lacking. (R3)  
  - Interesting results (Table 9, Figure 4) are relegated to the appendix, reducing readability. (R3)  
  - Table captions and text are unclear: “reference” in Table 1, “ESM-2 based” and “Multi-objective” in Table 3 require guessing. (R3)  
  - Typo: “Bolocks” in Figure 3. (R3)
- **Minor weakness:** The task setting is overly simplistic because the binding pose between antibody and antigen is typically unknown, though this is understandable given the goal. (R2)
- **Updated version concern:** AbNovo appears to be an updated version of AbDPO, heavier but showing better performance. (R2)
- **Tradeoff question:** In Table 2, AbNovo (base) sometimes achieves better scores than AbNovo, suggesting a possible tradeoff between constraint fulfillment and AAR/RMSD. (R3)
- **Contradiction in results description:** Section 4.2 states that averaging all constraints into a weighted objective causes a drop in performance, but the corresponding results show improvement in the “All Constraints” metric. (R3)
