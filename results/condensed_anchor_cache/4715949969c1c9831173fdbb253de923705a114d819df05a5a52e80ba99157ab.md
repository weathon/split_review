## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies demonstration-regularized RL, where the learner first performs behavioral cloning on expert demonstrations (using MLE) and then penalizes KL-divergence from the cloned policy during online interaction. It provides theoretical sample complexity bounds for tabular and linear MDPs, showing that using \(N^E\) expert demonstrations reduces sample complexity by a factor of \(1/N^E\). The analysis is extended to RLHF. The paper avoids pessimism injection by using computationally feasible regularization.

### Strengths
- **Thorough theoretical analysis**: The paper provides a comprehensive theoretical study of demonstration-regularized RL in both tabular and linear MDPs (Reviewer 1). The results are solid and cover multiple settings including RLHF (Reviewer 3).
- **Key theoretical contributions**: The authors prove that the KL divergence between the learned policy and the expert policy decreases linearly with the number of demonstrations, and complement this with a lower bound that matches the upper bound in terms of dependence on \(N^E\) (Reviewer 2). This is a strong technical result.
- **Novel and practical settings**: The two hybrid settings introduced—demonstration-regularized RL and demonstration-regularized RLHF—are novel and match practical situations (Reviewer 4). The problem is of particular interest to the theoretical RL community and pushes the frontiers of RL theory (Reviewer 2).
- **Clear benefit of expert demonstrations**: The results show a strong benefit of using expert demonstrations, with sample complexity improvements of order \(\tilde{O}(\mathrm{Poly}/(\epsilon^2 N^E))\) (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Paper organization**: The paper has too many references to the appendix, making it difficult to follow the main arguments and assess significance. Key definitions, proofs, and results should be more accessible in the main body (Reviewer 1). A separate “Related Works” section would improve clarity (Reviewer 3).
- **Algorithmic novelty**: The algorithm appears to be a straightforward combination of imitation learning and regularized RL; the contribution from the algorithm design part seems not significant (Reviewer 3).
- **Assumption on expert optimality and regime of benefit**: 
  - Corollary 3 requires the expert to be \(O(\epsilon)\)-optimal, but given reward knowledge, it should be possible to prove a bound without that assumption (e.g., a pure BPI algorithm needs no expert) (Reviewer 4, Q1).
  - The benefit occurs when \(N^E > H^3SA\), which is close to the typical sample complexity of standard RL; this may be too much in real applications (Reviewer 3).
  - There is an inconsistency: Corollary 3’s bound depends on \(N^E\), but Theorem 6’s bound does not. The authors should clarify why this is the case (Reviewer 1, question).
- **Missing empirical validation**: The paper would benefit from empirical studies to validate the theoretical findings, even though it is a theory paper (Reviewer 2, weakness and question).
- **Technical issues and missing explanations**:
  - The lower bound in Theorem 2 becomes vacuous in the limit \(\gamma \to 0\) (Reviewer 4).
  - It is unclear why the class of linear policies in Section 3.2 is considered not learnable; [1] (Theorem 5) shows it is learnable via behavioral cloning (Reviewer 4).
  - Lemma 11 should be referred to as the standard performance difference lemma (Reviewer 4).
  - The algorithm UCBVI-Ent+ is introduced as a modification of UCBVI-Ent (Tiapkin et al., 2023) with improved sample complexity, but the crucial difference is not explained. Moreover, the settings differ: UCBVI-Ent+ uses reward information while UCBVI-Ent is for maximum entropy exploration, not regularized MDPs (Reviewer 4).
  - The paper does not provide a lower bound for the regularized RL setting (Reviewer 3, question).
- **Clarity of algorithm and notation**:
  - The definitions of \(\pi^{t,(h)}\) and \(\tilde{\pi}^t\) in Algorithm 3 are not given (Reviewer 4, Q4).
  - In the Demonstration-Regularized RLHF setting, the necessity of the coefficients defined in Equation (3) in the bound is unclear (Reviewer 4, Q5).
- **Comparison with baselines**: There is limited discussion on the relationship between LSVI-UCB and UCBVI+ in the pure online learning context, their exploration strategies, how KL-regularization impacts performance, and when one is preferred (Reviewer 1).
