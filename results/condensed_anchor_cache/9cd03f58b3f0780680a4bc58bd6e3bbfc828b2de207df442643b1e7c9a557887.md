## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Value-Incentivized Preference Optimization (VPO), a unified online and offline RLHF method that regularizes the maximum-likelihood reward estimate with the value function, modulated by a sign for optimism or pessimism. VPO directly optimizes the policy via implicit reward modeling, giving a DPO-style pipeline with an added KL penalty derived from closed-form BT model relations. Theoretical regret and PAC bounds are provided. Experiments on text summarization (AlpacaEval 2) and ARC-challenge (offline) compare VPO to DPO and IPO. Reviewers generally acknowledge the theoretical motivation and unified framework, but severely criticize the experimental validation, practical relevance, and missing comparisons.

### Strengths

- **Unified online/offline framework**: VPO addresses a key gap with a single algorithm adjustable via one hyperparameter, applicable to both settings. This is a conceptually clean and practical unification. (R1, R4)
- **Theoretical guarantees**: Strong regret and PAC bounds matching standard RL rates, grounding the optimism/pessimism principle in the RLHF context. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Simple implementation**: The method reduces to adding a KL regularization to DPO, making it easy to implement and computationally efficient. (R1, R2)
- **Clear, well-structured mathematical derivation**: The assumption of prompt-dependent reward shift and resulting tractable objective are clearly stated and connected to RL theory. (R1, R2) The paper is tidy and easy to read. (R2)
- **ARC-challenge evidence**: Offline experiments on ARC show VPO avoids over-optimization relative to DPO and IPO, supporting the pessimism motivation. (R1, R3) – though this strength is contested.
- **Broader methodology**: The approach suggests a general methodology for principled optimism/pessimism under general RL setups beyond LLMs. (R4)

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient experimental evidence for practical superiority** (R2, R3, R1):
    - AlpacaEval 2 results show a small gap (~2 points) in the single-iteration online setting and *underperformance* in multi-iteration settings (Iter 3 VPO worse than Iter 3 DPO). This weakens the claim that VPO consistently outperforms DPO in practical scenarios. (R3)
    - Table 1 reports gains of only ~0.5–1% with no standard deviations, significance tests, or number of runs reported. Without statistical verification, the improvements are not convincing. (R1, R2)
    - Multi-iteration VPO degrading (Iter 3 < Iter 2 < Iter 1) contradicts the intuition of Theorem 1 that more iterations/uncertainty exploitation should improve. (R2)
- **Questionable choice and evaluation of ARC-challenge for offline alignment** (R1):
    - ARC is a knowledge-based multiple-choice benchmark, not a standard alignment evaluation (unlike AlpacaFarm or HH-RLHF). Multiple-choice format raises concern that VPO (and DPO/IPO) simply learn formatting (e.g., avoiding whitespace after parentheses) rather than genuine preference alignment. (R1)
    - Five generation examples provided by the authors at the end of discussion show that incorrect predictions often include whitespace after parentheses after the choices (e.g., "(C) several insects…" instead of "(C)several insects…"). This suggests the model's improvement may be due to format learning, not alignment. (R1)
- **Missing baseline comparisons with recent *PO variants** (R3):
    - The paper does not compare VPO against SimPO, WPO, or RainbowPO, which reportedly achieve much stronger results on AlpacaEval 2. Without these comparisons, it is unclear if VPO offers any advantage over the state of the art or replicates existing performance with a different formulation. (R3)
- **Lack of ablation studies** (R2, R4):
    - No systematic study of the value-based regularization term: e.g., sensitivity to the strength of regularization, choice of value function, or effect of optimism vs. pessimism under different uncertainty levels. (R4) Such studies would ground the theoretical confidence-bound guarantee. (R2)
- **Misleading use of the term "uncertainty"** (R2):
    - In RLHF, “uncertainty” typically refers to noise/ambiguity in preference labels, but the paper focuses on optimization error in the reward model. This mismatch could mislead readers about the primary contribution. (R2)
- **Strong theoretical assumptions limiting real-world relevance** (R4):
    - The theoretical guarantees assume a linear reward function and well-behaved policies; robustness to violations of these assumptions is not discussed. (R4) The sensitivity of results to hyperparameter choices is also not analyzed. (R4)
- **Limited experimental scope and scalability discussion** (R4):
    - Only two tasks (summarization via AlpacaEval, ARC-challenge) are evaluated; more diverse or real-world tasks would strengthen generalizability. (R4)
    - No analysis of computational complexity or scalability to very large models or datasets is provided. (R4)
- **R1 explicitly notes the answer to the question “Is VPO equal to DPO+KL penalty?” is not addressed** (though R4 lists it as a question). The relationship is mathematically clarified in the paper, but the question overlaps with a request for ablation or clarification. (R4, R1)
