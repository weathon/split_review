## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper studies the impact of label noise in pre-training datasets on downstream tasks. Through experiments on synthetic noisy ImageNet-1K and YFCC15M, the authors find that slight noise improves in-domain (ID) transfer performance but consistently harms out-of-domain (OOD) performance. They analyze the feature space using Singular Value Entropy (SVE) and Largest Singular Value Ratio (LSVR) and propose a lightweight black-box tuning method (NMTune) with three regularization terms (based on SVE and LSVR) to mitigate the adverse effects. The method is evaluated on multiple vision and language models (ResNet-50, CLIP, etc.) and includes API-based scenarios, as well as a combination with traditional noisy label learning.

### Strengths

- The problem is practical and significant, yet under-investigated in prior work. The findings on the distinct effects of pre-training noise on ID vs. OOD tasks are novel and intriguing.
- The analysis using SVE and LSVR to understand feature space changes due to noise is interesting, meaningful, and directly motivates the method.
- The proposed regularizations are intuitive, simple, and effective; experiments show they outperform the Cross-Entropy baseline (including LP and MLP structures).
- The empirical study is extensive and comprehensive: it covers both image and image-language tasks, multiple popular backbone architectures (ResNet-50, etc.), ID and OOD datasets from various distributions, and the API case.
- The paper is generally well-organized and easy to follow. The authors also study the combination of noisy model learning with traditional noise label learning, demonstrating relevance when downstream data also has noise.
- Detailed experimental results, setup, and ablations are provided in the appendix.

### Weaknesses

- **Writing and presentation issues**: Several unclear/confusing sentences (e.g., OOD evaluation description for DomainNet – if training on “real” or “sketch”, testing should only be on unseen domains; typo “the pre-trained the feature space”). The authors did not define M and D (samples and latent dimension) clearly when first used in Section 2.3.
- **Figure 3 is hard to interpret**: Many overlapping points per noise level without connecting lines, making it difficult to see trends. The authors should clarify what each point represents (one downstream task?) and how the five noise levels (0%,5%,10%,20%,30%) should be read together. The same issue exists in other figures (cluttered marks). One reviewer (R4) additionally noted that the SVE patterns for ImageNet vs. YFCC15M differ and require more explanation regarding pre-training objectives and data characteristics.
- **Missing explanation of noise injection**: The synthetic noise generation for ImageNet (label corruption distribution) and YFCC15M (text swapping) is not adequately described. Exact mechanisms and parameters (e.g., how is the noise level controlled?) should be provided.
- **Limited discussion of related work**: Prior works using SVD for noisy labels [R1] and singular value spectrum analysis (Chen et al. 2019, Bardes et al. 2022) should be discussed to clarify distinctions and the novelty of the approach. The core techniques (analysis and regularization of singular value spectrum) have been studied before, which may undermine the theoretical contribution.
- **Modest improvements**: The proposed method yields only ~1% improvement over the MLP baseline. One reviewer (R2) questioned whether the improvements are significant enough.
- **Contradiction or lack of clarity in SVE/LSVR behavior**: (a) The paper claims slight noise benefits ID tasks, but this seems contradictory to the existing literature on learning with noisy labels – more insight is needed. (b) SVE and LSVR may be contradictory (e.g., [1.0,0.0] has highest LSVR but lowest entropy); the authors should address this. (c) Figure 5(d) shows the proposed method does not improve LSVR over LP/MLP, and LSVR does not drop significantly with noise – this undermines the claim that the method enhances LSVR.
- **Scalability and generalization**: (a) Questions about whether conclusions hold for stronger backbones beyond ResNet-50. (b) No experiments exploring fine-tuning all layers with the proposed loss; it is unclear if the loss is effective in that setting. (c) The paper does not mention limitations, potential failure cases, or future work.
- **Minority reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (R4, rating=1, confidence=5) was significantly more critical, citing insufficient detail on noise injection and unexplained differences between models, while other reviewers rated the paper 8. This contrast indicates major concerns about reproducibility and analysis rigor.

**Questions overlapping with criticisms/missing experiments**: (From R1) Does the method generalize to self-supervised pre-trained models? When training CLIP, is the text encoder frozen? (From R2) Is there an optimal noise ratio for ID performance? (From R3) On what scale are SVE and LSVR computed – the entire dataset? Are the regularizations computed per batch? (From R4) How do other black-box tuning methods perform on the noisy model learning setting?
