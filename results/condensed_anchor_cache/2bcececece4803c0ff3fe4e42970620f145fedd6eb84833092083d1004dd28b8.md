## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes EEG-DisGCMAE, a graph-based self-supervised learning and knowledge distillation framework for EEG analysis. It integrates Graph Contrastive Pre-training (GCL-PT) and Graph Masked Autoencoder Pre-training (GMAE-PT) in a unified paradigm to leverage unlabeled high-density (HD) EEG data. A Graph Topology Distillation (GTD) loss transfers knowledge from a teacher model trained on HD data to a lightweight student model for low-density (LD) EEG, enabling robust classification with limited labeled data. The framework is evaluated on four classification tasks across two clinical datasets (EMBARC and HBN), demonstrating improvements over existing methods.

### Strengths
- Novel integration of Graph Contrastive Learning and Graph Masked Autoencoders in a unified pre-training framework, with an interpretation of node dropping and masking that synergistically combines generative and contrastive learning.
- Effective Graph Topology Distillation (GTD) loss for spatial knowledge transfer from high-density to low-density EEG, enabling lightweight student models to handle missing electrodes via contrastive distillation.
- Comprehensive experimental validation on two clinical EEG datasets (EMBARC, HBN) across four classification tasks, with extensive comparisons to multiple baselines and ablation studies.
- Clear problem formulation of leveraging unlabeled data and distilling knowledge from rich (high-density) data, with straightforward insights and well-structured presentation.
- Technically sound methodology with solid theoretical foundations.
- Advances portable and affordable EEG diagnostics through lightweight, high-performance student models.

### Weaknesses
- Insufficient validation of key hypotheses: the claims that combining GCL and GMAE improves distillation robustness, and that joint pre-training with mutual contrasting enhances performance, lack adequate empirical and theoretical support. The paper lacks direct ablation comparisons: (a) combined pre-training vs. GCL-only or GMAE-only, each with and without GTD loss; (b) mutual contrasting during pre-training vs. sequential GCL-then-GMAE without mutual contrasting. This makes it unclear whether the benefits stem from the combination or from individual components (Reviewers 1, 3, 5; R1 also questions what “Seq. Comb.” in Table 3 refers to and asks for such ablations).
- The Graph Topology Distillation loss (GTD) is poorly explained and its formulation is confusing. Specifically: (a) the mechanism for selecting positive and negative pairs is not clarified; (b) Equations (11) and (12) contain unclear notation (e.g., double bars before \(P_{ij}\), sum of KL divergences divided by another sum of KL divergences); (c) the relationship between \(\mathcal{L}^{logits}_{Dis}\), \(\mathcal{L}^{CE}_{Dis}\), and GTD loss and how they are combined during batch training is not described; (d) the KL divergence in Eq. (11) appears to be between two score vectors, which are not distributions (Reviewers 2, 4, 6).
- Comparisons are limited: the baselines are primarily pre-2023 graph SSL methods (e.g., GMAE 2022, GPT-GNN 2020) and omit recent graph SSL approaches [1–4] and EEG-specific SSL methods [5, 6] that combine contrastive and generative learning. Additionally, the paper does not compare against non-graph EEG models (CNNs, RNNs) or other transfer learning / knowledge distillation methods (Reviewers 1, 3, 4, 6).
- The method is largely a combination of existing techniques and is considered incremental. Both contrastive learning and knowledge distillation are well-studied in graph learning, and the core idea – pretraining on large data and distilling from rich features – has been applied in similar problems such as EMR representation learning (Reviewers 2, 3, 6).
- Performance gains are marginal, and because medical datasets are typically small, the improvements are not convincing (Reviewer 3).
- Evaluation is limited to similar classification tasks; broader EEG applications (e.g., seizure detection, cognitive state classification) are not tested, making generalizability unclear (Reviewer 4).
- Insufficient evidence for very low-density EEG settings (<16 channels) despite claims of effectiveness for portable and affordable EEG (Reviewer 4).
- Potential data leakage: the same datasets (EMBARC, HBN) are used for both pre-training and downstream fine-tuning (though with different window sizes), which may cause the model to memorize dataset-specific features. Cross-dataset evaluation (e.g., pre-train on HBN, fine-tune on EMBARC) is missing (Reviewer 1).
- Experimental details are lacking: (a) it is not specified whether baseline methods are trained on HD, LD, or both datasets, leading to potentially unfair comparisons; (b) graph construction details (number of nodes, edges, criteria for edge creation) are insufficient for reproducibility; (c) hyperparameter tuning for fine-tuning (learning rates) may not be optimal for each SSL method (Reviewers 2, 5, 1).
- Random dropping of nodes and edges for contrastive augmentation may destroy important topological structure in EEG graphs; the paper does not discuss this risk or provide validation of the augmentation strategy (Reviewer 5).
- Representation alignment between teacher (Graph Transformer) and student (GCN) is problematic due to their different architectures (spatial vs. spectral GNNs); alignment of representation spaces cannot be directly ensured (Reviewer 6).
- Dynamic adjacency matrix during training may cause instability; GCN is a transductive method, making it potentially unsuitable for dynamic adjacency matrices (Reviewer 6).
- Node representation construction uses only per-channel statistics (mean, variance, etc.), losing high-order temporal information; more expressive time-series embeddings (e.g., transformer- or RNN-based) could capture richer features (Reviewer 6).
- Assumptions 1 and 2 lack justification and empirical support: Assumption 2 does not specify how positive and negative pairs are selected; Assumption 1 may suffer from training instability or architectural differences between GCL and GMAE that affect joint pre-training (Reviewers 3, 5).
- The paper does not explain why GTD loss is used only in fine-tuning (not pre-training) and how it differs from self-distillation methods like DINO (Reviewer 2).
- Presentation issues: (a) Figure 1 symbols for “Frozen”/“Tuned” are not visible and “GNN2GNN Distill Loss” in the figure is not defined in the text; (b) Figure 1 plots AUC vs. ACC but does not clearly map the circle markers to model sizes; (c) Figure 3 colors and shapes are not explained; (d) the paper is difficult to follow in places (motivation for GTD loss is unclear); (e) notation is overloaded and a notation table would be helpful (Reviewers 1, 2, 5, 6).
