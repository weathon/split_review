## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes POMDiffuser, a diffusion-based planning framework for POMDPs that incorporates memory mechanisms (RNN, Transformer, SSM) to handle long-horizon planning and sparse rewards. There is strong disagreement among reviewers: one reviewer (score 6) considers the problem significant and the benchmark suite valuable, while the other five (score 3) are critical of novelty, clarity, experimental rigor, and theoretical depth. The majority view is that the contribution is a straightforward application of known techniques without sufficient comparisons or analysis.

### Strengths
- Addresses long-term memory and planning in POMDPs, a significant challenge in RL and decision-making (R2, R5).
- Proposes a heterogeneous, cross-attention-based memory mechanism with claimed complexity O(L log L + H²) (R1).
- Introduces three new POMDP benchmarks: Superimposed-MNIST (memorization), 2D Memory Maze (navigation), Blind Color Matching (robotics, partial observability, sparse rewards) (R1, R2, R5).
- Investigates different memory architectures (RNNs, Transformers, SSMs), providing insights into their trade-offs (R2).
- Compares different configurations of the proposed design with ablations (R1).
- Hyperparameters for each task are explicitly stated (R6).
- The problem of offline RL in partially observable environments is important and relevant to the community (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical analysis**: No convergence guarantees, discussion of limitations, or justification why diffusion models are suitable for long-memory/planning in POMDPs (R2). No analysis of how memory length affects planning horizon or how belief-state representation quality degrades over longer horizons (R2 questions).
- **Experimental evaluation insufficient**: Only self-evaluation; no comparisons to prior methods (e.g., world models, Diffusion Forcing, traditional POMDP planners) (R3, R5). No baselines on existing POMDP benchmarks (e.g., Pocman, Battleship) to assess difficulty or scaling claims (R5). The new benchmarks are not compared to existing environments to justify their novelty or difficulty (R4, R5).
- **Novelty limited**: Primarily applies known sequence models (RNN, Transformer, SSM) to diffusion planners; no new theoretical or algorithmic insights (R1, R4, R5). The claim of addressing transformer issues is not substantiated – the model still uses a transformer encoder for action selection (R4). The contribution appears to reduce to behavior cloning on sequences (R3).
- **Presentation and clarity poor**: Writing is rushed and disconnected, making methodology hard to follow and results difficult to appreciate without cross-referencing (R1). Abstract/introduction contain unclear claims (e.g., “memorize specific environments” not justified, overly broad statements about effectiveness and meta-learning) (R1). Formal problem definition (POMDP, agent dynamics, task objective, reward structure) is missing or unclear (R6). Mathematical details imprecise (e.g., where O(L log L + H²) comes from not explained) (R3). Sections lack introductory sentences and have missing/incomplete citations (R1). The term “UNet” appears without prior introduction (R5).
- **Inverse dynamics claim unjustified**: Claim that predicting actions from adjacent frames in POMDPs is “unreliable” – this is theoretically impossible (no unique mapping), and the proposed solution using transformer encoders is not explained (R6).
- **Insufficient analysis of failures**: The method struggles with complex tasks, but there is no analysis or future work discussion (R2). The large performance gap between SSM and Transformer in Blind Color Matching (0.6956 vs 0.0187) is not analyzed (R2 question).
- **Benchmark innovation questionable**: The new benchmarks do not represent a genuinely novel contribution; they are simple and tasks are poorly described (R4, R6). No comparison to existing MNIST or Maze environments to illustrate differences (R4 question).
- **Missing awareness of related work**: The SSM is presented as novel but is essentially a standard linear time-varying system; stability and conditioning of such systems is classical control theory (R6). Missing reference in “Efficient World Models” (R5).
- **Minor issues**: Citation format errors (e.g., “Tedrake” appears as misformat, L242 missing citation) (R1). Beta symbol missing from equation (L208) (R1). Tables/figures not referenced in main text (R1).
