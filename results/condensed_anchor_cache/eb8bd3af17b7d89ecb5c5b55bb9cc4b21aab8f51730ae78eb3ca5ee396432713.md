## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes PTA-LLM, a knowledge fusion method that reformulates token alignment for LLMs with different vocabularies as an optimal transport (OT) problem, using a soft mapping instead of the hard mapping in prior work like FuseLLM. The OT plan minimizes transport cost based on edit distance between tokens, dynamically pairing tokens from different sequences. The approach is validated by fusing Llama-2, MPT, and OpenLLaMA on multiple zero-shot benchmarks. The paper claims the method is general, stable, and interpretable from a distributional perspective.

### Strengths
- **Addresses a core challenge in knowledge fusion:** Token alignment between heterogeneous LLMs is a key problem, and the paper provides a sound, well-motivated solution. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Novel application of optimal transport:** Framing alignment as OT is an interesting and mathematically principled approach. The ICLR community may appreciate this application. (Reviewer 1)
- **General, stable, and interpretable:** The soft alignment is described as more flexible and adaptable than hard mapping; the distributional perspective offers interpretability. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Consistent performance gains:** The method shows improvements over competing fusion methods across multiple benchmarks, though the gains are modest. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3; note Reviewer 2 and 3 highlight limited significance, and Reviewer 4 notes marginal gains—this contrast is preserved.)
- **Clear presentation:** The paper is easy to read and explains the method clearly. (Reviewer 3)
- **Ablation and analysis:** Experiments include stability analysis and hyperparameter ablations. (Reviewers 1, 3, but note Reviewer 4 criticizes the ablation depth – this contrast is preserved below.)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited improvement and practical significance:** 
  - In Table 1, the average improvement over FuseLLM is less than 0.5 points, and over CLM it is only about 1 point. Given the computational overhead (multiple forward passes for teacher models, Sinkhorn algorithm), the gain may not justify the complexity. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
  - The zero-shot evaluation setup depresses base model scores (e.g., GSM scores are extremely low: Llama-2 scores 0.66, while original Llama-2 paper typically uses 4-8 shots). This raises concerns about the practical impact of the method. (Reviewers 3, 4)
  - Significance testing is missing; multiple runs should be reported to confirm effectiveness. (Reviewer 3)
- **Theoretical and terminological concerns:**
  - The paper moves mass between *logit* spaces but uses terminology “token distribution” and “probability mass,” which is misleading because logits are not normalized probabilities. The normalization differs between source and target spaces. (Reviewer 1)
  - The cost function uses edit distance at the token level, which still relies on “surface level token correspondences,” contrary to the claim of moving beyond such correspondences (line 153). (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient diversity in evaluation:**
  - Only three models are tested, and OpenLLaMA and Llama2 are architecturally similar. The evaluation should include more diverse architectures such as Mistral and Qwen. (Reviewer 4)
  - Only multiple-choice tasks are evaluated; generative tasks are missing, limiting assessment of general capability. (Reviewer 4)
- **Limited hyperparameter exploration:**
  - The ablation experiments (Section 4.5) examine only two values each for Optimal Transport Temperature, Combination Weight, and Token Alignment Window Size. This does not reveal the impact trends or sensitivity. (Reviewer 4)
  - The effect of the CLM loss weighting parameter λ (lambda) is not analyzed. Does it require careful fine-tuning? (Reviewer 3)
- **Stability analysis lacks explanation:**
  - Section 4.3 shows PTA-LLM more stable than FuseLLM on certain tasks, but no analysis of why soft alignment is beneficial for those specific tasks or what characteristics they share. (Reviewer 4)
- **Computational cost not fully discussed:**
  - The Sinkhorn algorithm is time-consuming; the limitations section should appear in the main pages. Scalability to larger corpora than MiniPile is unclear. (Reviewer 2)
- **Low GSM scores and reporting issues:**
  - Llama-2 gets 0.66 on GSM (zero-shot) but the original paper does not report such a low score. What does the EM score stand for? Is it 1.90%? (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Theoretical justification of knowledge fusion vs. CLM:**
  - Why does distilling distributions from multiple models on the same corpus outperform the next-token prediction training objective itself? The paper does not sufficiently motivate this. (Reviewer 3)
- **Writing quality:**
  - The introduction is grandiose, framing the question of building better models as requiring knowledge fusion rather than presenting it as one approach. (Reviewer 1)
- **Typos:**
  - Line 223: "Pt" → "P_t"; Line 274: "Tt" → "T_t"; Line 417: "-17/07%" → "-17.07%"; Line 430: "26.96" → "36.96". (Reviewer 4)
