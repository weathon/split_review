## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Causal Information Prioritization (CIP) to improve sample efficiency in reinforcement learning by leveraging causal relationships between states, actions, and rewards. CIP uses factored MDPs to infer these relationships, employs counterfactual data augmentation to prioritize high-impact state features, and integrates a causality-aware empowerment learning objective to guide exploration. The method is evaluated across 39 tasks in 5 continuous control environments (locomotion and manipulation, including pixel-based and sparse reward settings) and claims consistent outperformance over existing RL methods.

### Strengths
- The integration of causal reasoning with reinforcement learning, particularly combining causal inference with empowerment learning, is novel and creative. (Reviewer 1, 3)
- CIP addresses the important problem of sample inefficiency by focusing on causal relationships, which has potential for broad applicability. (Reviewer 3)
- The approach leverages counterfactual data augmentation and a causality-aware objective to prioritize meaningful features, aligning with a growing area in RL. (Reviewer 1)
- Experiments are comprehensive, covering diverse environments (39 tasks across 5 domains), which is valuable for assessing generalizability. (Reviewer 1, 2)
- The paper is well-organized, presentation is mostly clear, and the motivation is explained with intuitive examples (e.g., robot-arm manipulation). (Reviewer 2, 3)
- Extensive experimental results demonstrate strong performance, especially in challenging environments with sparse rewards and high-dimensional action spaces. (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 1 finds improvements more limited—see weaknesses)

### Weaknesses
- **Task-specific engineering burden**: The framework does not learn causal structure end-to-end but relies on manually provided task structures, limiting scalability and adaptability. Reducing this engineering effort is not addressed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Marginal and inconsistent improvements**: Empirical results (Table 1) show only limited improvement over baselines like ACE and BAC, with some tasks performing on par or below baselines. Gains are not always significant, and the added complexity may not be justified. (Reviewer 1) In contrast, Reviewer 3 finds the results strong and consistently outperforming state-of-the-art. This disagreement highlights that the improvements may be task-dependent. (Reviewers 1 and 3)
- **Missing related work and discussion**: The paper lacks references to object-centric MDPs and related works in object-centric world modeling and MDP homomorphism. Additionally, two relevant causal RL works are omitted: [1] Sun et al. "ACAMDA" (AAAI 2024) and [2] Sun & Wang "Toward Causal-Aware RL" (2022). (Reviewer 1, 2) The limitation section is not a genuine discussion of CIP's limitations. (Reviewer 2)
- **Empowerment computational complexity**: The empowerment objective adds computational overhead. The paper lacks analysis of time complexity (scaling with state/action dimensions), wall-clock time comparisons with and without the objective, and comparison to traditional entropy regularization. (Reviewer 3)
- **Causal discovery assumptions and generalizability**: Reliance on DirectLiNGAM assumes acyclic graphs and linear relationships, which may limit applicability in environments with non-linear dynamics. The paper does not discuss these limitations or test with datasets that violate the assumptions. Alternative causal discovery methods (e.g., non-linear extensions) were not explored. (Reviewer 3)
- **Incomplete empirical comparisons**: Comparisons are limited to SAC, ACE, BAC, etc. Model-based RL approaches or hybrid methods that also leverage causal reasoning or latent dynamics models are missing, which would better contextualize CIP’s sample efficiency and generalization. (Reviewer 3)
- **Statistical significance and stability**: On some tasks, performance shows large variances. The recommended hyper-parameter setups and robustness to different hyper-parameters are not discussed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Offline RL applicability**: Whether CIP is suitable for offline RL or requires online exploration is not clarified. (Reviewer 2)
- **Handling causal discovery inaccuracies**: No mechanism is described to correct or adapt causal models if causal discovery misaligns with environment dynamics during learning. (Reviewer 3)
- **Adaptation to discrete action spaces**: How CIP would extend to discrete action spaces, where causal dependencies may be more rigid, is not addressed. (Reviewer 3)
- **Computational burden and pitfalls**: The paper does not discuss trade-offs, such as computational burden or challenges in highly stochastic environments. (Reviewer 2)
