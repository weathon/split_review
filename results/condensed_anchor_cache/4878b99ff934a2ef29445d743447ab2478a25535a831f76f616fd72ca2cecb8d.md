## Merged Review

### Summary
All four reviewers acknowledge that the paper proposes the Multimodal Academic Cover (MAC) benchmark for evaluating LMMs on academic journal cover understanding (Image2Text) and generation (Text2Image), and introduces the Multimodal Agent Linkage (MAL) method. There is disagreement: two reviewers (scores 5) appreciate the benchmark’s novelty and structure, while the other two (scores 3) find the scope too narrow, the design and evaluation details insufficient, and the proposed method lacking innovation.

### Strengths
- Novel benchmark targeting a specific but under-explored domain: the first evaluation of LMMs on scientific journal cover understanding and generation (all reviewers agree on novelty; R3, R4 explicitly note “first benchmark”).
- The paper is clearly structured and well-written (R2: “clear structure”, “well-written”).
- The benchmark includes many popular models: GPT‑4V, DALL·E 3, Gemini, CogView‑3, GLM‑4V, LLaVA, LLaMA‑adapter, MiniGPT4 (R1). R2 partially agrees but considers the set too small (see Weaknesses).
- Potential usefulness for specific fields like the journal and advertisement industry (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Dataset size and narrow scope:**
  - Only ~5,872 cover images from three journals (Cell, Science, Nature). This is small, risks overfitting, and limits diversity (R1: “5K images, pretty small”; R2: “too small … likely to overfit, diversity difficult to guarantee”).
  - Scope is too narrow: conclusions may not generalize to other science/engineering disciplines or other cover types (magazines, textbooks) (R3, R4). Two reviewers question whether the benchmark can be extended to other types of covers or journals (R3, R4). R4 notes that some journals do not feature article images on covers, making applicability unclear.
- **Missing model comparisons and related work:**
  - Several important MLLMs are omitted: Emu, GILL, DreamLLM, SEED, VILA‑U (R1); Emu2, Flamingo, and others (R2).
  - Related work does not adequately cover prior human‑involved and Large Model‑involved evaluations (R2).
- **Benchmark design and evaluation details are poor:**
  - The scoring/rating mechanism in Image2Text and Text2Image is vague. The prompts mention only a few aspects (e.g., color, relevance). A more fine‑grained, weighted scoring approach is needed (R3).
  - Lack of a quantitative measure of what makes a “good” cover image. For example, some covers simply list titles – are those good or bad? (R4).
  - The dataset distribution is imbalanced: e.g., Table 4 shows biology has many times more issues than other disciplines (R2). No detailed analysis of this imbalance is provided.
- **Human‑expert evaluation concerns:**
  - The reference cover stories are highly specialized and require strong domain knowledge. The paper does not clarify whether human raters meet that criterion. LLMs may generate plausible but logically incorrect/hallucinated stories (R3).
- **Proposed MAL method lacks innovation** (R3). Its contribution relative to existing in‑context learning or retrieval‑augmented approaches is not clearly distinguished.
- **Unexplained performance difference:** Why is GLM‑4V worse than MiniGPT‑4 in Table 3? (R3). This discrepancy is not discussed.
- **Potential training‑data contamination:** Many models may have already seen the cover images in their training data. No attempt is made to check or construct “unseen” test images (R4).
- **Terminology note:** The term “LMM” is uncommon; “MLLM” (multimodal LLM) is more standard (R1).
