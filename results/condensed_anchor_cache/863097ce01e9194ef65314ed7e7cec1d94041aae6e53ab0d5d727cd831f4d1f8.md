## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies in-context learning of transformers for mixture of linear regressions. The authors construct a transformer that implements a gradient-based EM algorithm, derive statistical guarantees (excess risk bounds, sample complexity) for the low and high SNR regimes, and extend the result to K>2 components in the high SNR regime. Simulations are provided. All five reviewers rated the paper 5 (weak reject), with confidence ranging from 2 to 4. The reviewers acknowledge theoretical rigor but raise significant concerns about the contribution, the realism of the setting, missing experiments (especially the claimed EM outperformance), and the lack of evidence that trained transformers actually learn the EM algorithm. The writing and presentation were criticized by several reviewers as unclear or insufficiently intuitive.

### Strengths
- Thorough theoretical analysis showing that a transformer can encode the EM algorithm for mixture of linear regressions, capturing the role of SNR and number of layers (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Prior work (Pathak et al. 2024) required access to true regression weights; this work instead implements EM without that access, and generalizes the EM analysis of Balakrishnan et al. 2017 to multiple steps of gradient ascent per M-step (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Statistical guarantees are provided for a simple pretraining scheme (ERM on ICL instances), deriving generalization bounds based on the number of training prompts (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The result is extended to mixtures with more than two components (Section 4) (Reviewer 2).
- The paper gives convergence results with explicit error rates distinguishing low and high SNR regimes (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is well-written and mostly easy to follow (Reviewer 1); however, other reviewers found the presentation unclear (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Significance of representability results**: Showing that transformers *can* encode EM does not reveal what algorithm they actually learn. Such results can be misleading (e.g., prior work on linear regression showed transformers can implement gradient descent, but learned dynamics are second-order and exponentially faster) (Reviewer 1). This point is echoed: the main component is based on well‑established results from Bai et al. 2024, which reduces novelty (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing EM baseline in experiments**: The abstract claims transformers outperform the EM algorithm, but the simulation section does not include any EM baseline plots (Reviewers 1, 4). Without those experiments, the claim is unsupported. Moreover, the training scheme (Section 2.3) appears to *not* learn EM – transformers do not suffer from the same initialization issues as EM, contradicting the hypothesis that they implement EM (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited / unnatural setting**: (a) The two‑mixture model assumes weights \(w\) and \(-w\) with equal probability, so the optimal prediction is always 0, independent of the input \(x\). This trivializes the problem and is not a good testbed (Reviewers 1, 4). (b) Theorem 2.1 requires prompt length \(n > d\); the overparameterized regime (\(n \ll d\)) is not discussed (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing architectural details**: Theorem 2.1 does not state the number of layers or how the FFN is constructed; such details are critical for understanding the construction (Reviewer 3). The definition of \(\text{read}_\beta\) is given after the theorem statement (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear problem formulation and workflow**: The paper does not provide a clear algorithm box or explanation of how the transformer’s single output relates to the two possible responses in the mixture, nor how the double‑loop EM algorithm is implemented (Reviewer 3). The notation \(y'_i\) in Eq. (3) and the setting of \(y'_{n+1}\) are not explained (Reviewer 3). Writing is not sound enough – theorems and lemmas lack intuitions (Reviewer 5).
- **Prediction error bound justification**: Bounding the MSE as in L172‑L174 appears fishy because the transformer outputs only one value, and if \(\beta\) magnitude grows, the bound may not hold without conditioning on the actual \(\beta\) (Reviewer 3).
- **Overall novelty**: The contribution is mostly derivative, building on Pathak et al. 2024 and Bai et al. 2024 (Reviewers 2, 4). The scope is limited to a very specific setup (Reviewer 5). The paper only shows existence, not how transformers can be trained to those parameters (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation issues**: Notation like \(f_{n,d,\delta}(\dots)\) is hard to parse; big‑O is misused (e.g., \(f \ge O(g)\) should be \(\Omega\)) (Reviewer 2). Appendix B should not be renamed (it contains proof of Theorem 2.2) (Reviewer 2). Line 509 should read \(D = 32, 64, 128\) instead of 34 (Reviewer 2). The number of layers for low SNR is stated as \(O(\sqrt{n/d} \log\log(n/d))\) but it is unclear if this is an upper bound or a true separation (Reviewer 2). References to proof sections are missing for some theorems (Reviewer 2).
