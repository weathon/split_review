## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes the Zipformer encoder for ASR, introducing multiple modifications to the Conformer: a U-Net-like encoder with downsampled middle stacks, reorganized blocks with attention weight reuse, BiasNorm (a modified LayerNorm that retains length information), new activation functions SwooshR/SwooshL, and a new optimizer ScaledAdam. Experiments on LibriSpeech, Aishell-1, and WenetSpeech show strong performance with improved efficiency. All four reviewers note the paper’s interesting novelties and strong aggregate results, but raise concerns about the comprehensiveness of analyses, the fair comparison to baselines, and the generalizability of the many new components.

### Strengths
- Achieves state-of-the-art results on LibriSpeech and other benchmarks (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Experiments span datasets of different scales: hundreds, thousands, and tens of thousands of hours (Reviewer 1).
- Introduces many interesting novelties (Zipformer model, ScaledAdam, BiasNorm, new activations, bypass module, non-linear attention, Eden scheduling) that individually are promising (Reviewer 2).
- Good relative WER improvements along with better memory and FLOPs efficiency (Reviewer 2).
- Detailed ablation studies quantify the usefulness of each modification, and each appears to offer improvement over the Conformer baseline (Reviewer 4).
- Strong ASR baselines are provided (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- Motivation for the specific downsampling/upsampling designs (e.g., averaging with two learnable scalar weights) is not well explained; there is insufficient connection between multi-resolution processing and final performance (Reviewer 1). The paper lacks a clear discussion of why these methods were chosen over common techniques like strided convolutions or pooling (Reviewer 1).
- Results on Aishell-1 are less convincing compared to other conformer variants; the paper should analyze why improvement is smaller on this dataset (possibly because it is small) and whether the architecture is less suitable for smaller datasets (Reviewer 1).
- Too many new components are introduced (Zipformer, ScaledAdam, BiasNorm, activations, etc.); each would benefit from a more thorough, standalone investigation (Reviewer 2). A single ablation experiment per modification is insufficient to truly assess their individual contributions (Reviewer 2).
- Lack of systematic experimental comparison to other conformer variants (e.g., E-Branchformer). The paper should discuss and experimentally compare differences (Reviewer 2).
- The use of a pruned transducer decoder, rather than a more standard transducer or CTC/AED, makes it harder to compare results against literature values (Table 2) (Reviewer 2).
- Only FLOPs are reported for efficiency; actual measured speed on specific hardware would be more informative, as certain operations may parallelize differently (Reviewer 2).
- It is unclear whether the WER improvement comes from the model architecture or from the ScaledAdam optimizer. Would other transformer variants (e.g., Conformer, Squeezeformer) also benefit from ScaledAdam? (Reviewer 3). Table 5 shows Zipformer-M with ScaledAdam achieves 4.79% WER on test-other versus 5.51% with standard Adam – this question is directly relevant (Reviewer 3).
- The generalizability of ScaledAdam beyond this paper’s tasks and models is unclear; it has many ad-hoc modifications compared to Adam, and it is not tested on other deep learning tasks (Reviewer 3). Whether it works for larger models and datasets (>1000 hours) is also an open question (Reviewer 3).
- The modifications to well-tested components, such as removing mean and variance normalization in LayerNorm (BiasNorm), may not generalize to other tasks or domains (Reviewer 3). Similarly, the specific coefficients in the activation functions (e.g., -0.08 in SwooshL, 0.035 in SwooshR) are described as “slightly tuned” – their generalizability is questionable (Reviewer 3).
- Many ablation results show less than 0.2% absolute WER change; this is within the range of variance from checkpoint selection or different training seeds (up to 0.1% per the reviewer’s experience), so the significance of these small changes is unclear (Reviewer 3).
- Missing related work: Wang et al., “Transformer-based Acoustic Modeling for Hybrid Speech Recognition” (ICASSP 2020) and Zhang et al., “Faster, simpler and more accurate hybrid ASR systems using wordpieces” (2020) both report strong LibriSpeech results using standard transformers with CTC; and Wang et al., “Accelerating RNN-T training and inference using CTC guidance” (ICASSP 2023) is relevant as it uses temporal downsampling similar to the Zipformer (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is closed-source, and the implementation is complicated; this raises reproducibility concerns, similar to the original Conformer paper (Reviewer 4). Future work will struggle with direct comparison (Reviewer 4).
