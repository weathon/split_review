## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes HiCS-FL, a client selection method for federated learning on non-IID data. The server estimates each client’s data heterogeneity using the entropy of soft-max outputs (or the last layer's bias), clusters clients by heterogeneity, then hierarchically samples: first select groups with higher heterogeneity, then sample clients within groups based on their weights (batch size). Convergence analysis is provided. Experiments on FMNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100 show faster convergence (1.63x–7.3x speedup) and lower computation overhead compared to existing client sampling methods like pow-d, CS, DivFL, and FedProx.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important and practical problem: client selection under statistical heterogeneity in federated learning.
- The proposed hierarchical client sampling scheme (select groups by heterogeneity, then clients by batch-size weights) is novel.
- Clearly states relationship to prior clustered sampling work (Fraboni et al., 2021) and explains how HiCS-FL differs.
- Provides a convergence analysis for the proposed method.
- Outperforms existing sampling-based baselines on multiple image datasets (FMNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) with significant training speed improvements (1.63x to 7.3x).
- The paper is generally well-written and easy to follow (R2), and the motivation for sophisticated client sampling is clear (R3).
- Novel ideas are quantitatively evaluated on multiple datasets, showing advantages over state-of-the-art methods (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient experimental scope**: Only simple models and datasets (FMNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) are used; test accuracy is too low to demonstrate practical applicability. Experiments with deeper networks (e.g., ResNet) and more challenging datasets are needed (R1). The method is only validated on image classification tasks; it is unclear whether it extends to non-classification tasks (e.g., regression) or non-image tasks (e.g., NLP) because it relies on soft-max outputs for heterogeneity estimation. Evaluation on a task like Shakespeare (next word prediction) from LEAF is requested (R3).
- **Missing evaluation of client inaccessibility**: The abstract mentions limited client participation, but all experiments assume selected clients are always available. Real-world FL often has client dropouts. The paper should include experiments where a fraction of selected clients fail to respond and compare with state-of-the-art methods (R1).
- **Limited and outdated baselines**: Comparison methods are limited (FedProx, pow-d, CS, DivFL). The paper should be compared against recent non-sampling-based methods that also address non-IID data, such as Scaffold, Scaffnew, FedNova, and FedOpt, to justify the need for client sampling and to show relative training speed and overhead (R2, R3). These methods may outperform FedProx (R3).
- **Convergence analysis lacks originality**: The analysis closely resembles prior FL convergence proofs and does not provide unique insights specific to the proposed HiCS-FL method (R2). (Note: R1 considers the analysis a strength; R2 disagrees.)
- **Privacy concerns not addressed**: The method uses client updates to estimate heterogeneity, which may raise privacy issues; the paper does not discuss this (R2).
- **Section organization issues**: The work is generally well-written, but Section 3 mixes background, existing approaches, and the proposed method together, making the novel contributions hard to identify. It should be split into “Preliminaries” and “Proposed Method” (R3).
- **Extension to non-SGD optimizers not validated**: The appendix claims the method can be extended beyond SGD (e.g., Adam), but no experiments are provided. A comparison with methods like FedCor using Adam optimizers would be valuable (R1, question). This overlaps with a criticism of missing experimental validation (R1).
- **Writing issues**: Some parts are confusing; Figure 6 has a y-axis label that is too long (should be explained in caption) and there are typos (R1).
