- Scores: 5, 5, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces fMRI-PTE, an autoencoder-based pretraining framework that transforms fMRI signals into standardized 2D representations for multi-subject brain activity decoding. Reviewers acknowledged the well-motivated goal of building a large-scale foundation model for fMRI and the exploration of visual decoding, but raised substantial concerns about insufficient ablation studies, unclear methodological novelty, missing strong baselines (e.g., MindEye), vague methodology description, lack of statistical rigor, and limited evaluation scope. There was notable disagreement among reviewers: two rated the work as promising (score 5), while two found it below the conference bar (score 3), especially regarding the novelty of the data transformation and the overall contribution.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a well-motivated and important problem: building a large-scale pretrained foundation model for fMRI data, which could benefit brain decoding and related neuroinformatics tasks (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- It leverages large-scale pretraining (UK BioBank) and resting-state fMRI data, potentially capturing intrinsic brain variability and improving downstream performance (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- The transformation of fMRI signals into 2D representations is seen by some as an effective dimensionality reduction that may capture low- and high-frequency signal variations and reduce computational cost (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2). (Note: there is strong disagreement on this point—see Weaknesses.)
- The work explores a cutting-edge application—visual decoding from fMRI—and demonstrates impressive reconstruction results (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- The paper includes multiple baselines and experiments for both reconstruction (upstream) and decoding (downstream) tasks (Reviewer 3).
- The article is generally readable and well-structured (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient ablations and experimental rigor**:  
  - The baseline "LR" does not isolate the impact of the pretrained encoder; systematic ablation of pretraining components (loss functions, encoder architecture) is needed (Reviewer 1).  
  - Each loss term (perceptual, adversarial, reconstruction, commitment) should be ablated individually and in combination; vector quantization should be ablated (e.g., what if features are not quantized) (Reviewer 4).  
  - The choice of Vision Transformer for compressing quantized indices should be compared against simpler architectures (linear layer, MLP) (Reviewer 4).  
  - The data transformation method itself should be ablated with alternative approaches (DiFuMo, Neuromark, Spectral Clustering) (Reviewer 3).  
  - The MAE baseline is confusing and likely sub-optimally trained; its poor reconstruction suggests unfair comparison (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).  
  - Cross-subject decoding pairs (7→1, etc.) are arbitrarily selected; evaluation should include reverse directions and averaged results (Reviewer 2).  
  - Kernel size not specified—with small ROIs (V6A, LO2) large kernels may merge regions (Reviewer 2).  
  - Tables 2–4 lack variability measures (STD, SE, IQR) and statistical significance tests (Reviewer 3).  
  - Performance gains in Table 1 appear marginal and the subscript reference is unclear (Reviewer 4).

- **Methodological novelty and clarity concerns**:  
  - Multiple reviewers consider the "innovative data transformation" to be trivial—using standard mapping tools (e.g., 32k_fs_LR) with no novelty; Reviewer 1 considers it a strength, creating a clear disagreement (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).  
  - The overall methodology is seen as exploiting existing techniques (e.g., VQGAN, transformers) without significant innovation, raising doubts about suitability for top venues (Reviewer 2).  
  - The GPT model appears in Figure 1 but is never detailed in the method; the "image decoder" for downstream tasks is also missing details (Reviewers 2, 4).  
  - The claim of preserving high-frequency signals is confusing and unsupported—fMRI is undersampled and noisy; evaluation using frequency mode reconstruction is needed (Reviewer 3).  
  - Foundation model claims ("universal applicability") are not supported: only one downstream task (visual decoding), no zero-shot experiments, no scaling demonstrations, and no robustness analysis across demographics (Reviewer 3).  
  - The title overstates "brain activity" when results are limited to cortical surface and mainly visual decoding (Reviewer 4).

- **Methodology description and reproducibility**:  
  - The training process lacks detailed equations and procedural explanations; loss function forms and weighting are unspecified (Reviewer 1).  
  - The input to the second-stage encoder (Epsilon_2) and how quantized indices are generated and fed requires clarification with symbols and dimensions (Reviewer 1).  
  - Variables L and C are used ambiguously (batch size, number of tokens, feature dimensions) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).  
  - How a subset of visual ROIs is input to a model expecting the full cortical image is unclear (Reviewer 4).  
  - Preprocessing details (motion correction, alignment) are absent; no appendix or source code provided (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).  
  - "Implemental Details" are placed in the Results section instead of Methods (Reviewer 4).

- **Presentation and language issues**:  
  - Abbreviations are used without definition (e.g., GLM, column "M" in Table 4) (Reviewer 1).  
  - Figure 1 is not self-explanatory: the GPT block is unexplained, "fires" and "iced" symbols need clarification (Reviewer 4).  
  - The text is wordy and insufficiently specific (Reviewer 3).  
  - English phrasing and grammar need improvement; "intricacies" is overused and acronyms are introduced without definition (Reviewer 4).  
  - Brain visualizations lack a color scale and consistent scale across panels (Reviewer 2).
