## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Integration Flow, a neural-network-based approach that replaces numerical ODE integration in flow-based generative models (diffusion, rectified flow, Poisson flow). It learns the trajectory results directly, explicitly incorporating the target state \(x_0\) as an anchor in reverse-time dynamics. The method achieves competitive FID scores on CIFAR-10 with few function evaluations: VE Diffusion 2.63 (one-step), Rectified Flow 3.4, PFGM++ 2.96, and a state-of-the-art 1.71 with 1000 steps.

### Strengths

- Proposes a reasonable scheme for replacing ODE integration that performs relatively well in practice, especially with a small number of function evaluations (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The experiment results are competitive compared with other diffusion models, flow-based methods, and distillation methods (Reviewer 2).
- Provides a unified framework for multiple probability flow-based generative models (Reviewer 3).
- The intuition of explicitly incorporating the target state as an anchor is theoretically well-justified (Reviewer 3).
- Achieves competitive FID scores with fewer generation steps on CIFAR-10 (Reviewer 3).
- The idea is interesting, and the paper is relatively easy to follow/understand (Reviewers 1, 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Significant overlap with prior work (Reviewers 1, 2).** The motivation, formulation (Sec 3.1–3.3 compared to Section 3), and training/sampling algorithms (Algorithms 1, 3 vs Algorithm 1; Algorithms 2, 4 vs Algorithm 2) closely follow “Directly Denoising Diffusion Models” (arXiv 2405.13540) without citation. Both reviewer 1 and reviewer 2 raise serious concerns about potential plagiarism; reviewer 2 gave a rating of 1 (strongly negative) specifically for this reason.

- **Theoretical flaw in convex loss claim (Reviewers 1, 2).** The paper claims that \(E[x_0|I]\) minimizes \(E[d(x_0,a)|I]\) for any convex \(d\). A counterexample: the \(l_1\) loss is convex but its minimizer is the conditional median, not the conditional mean. This undermines the theoretical justification.

- **Training algorithm is not well presented and its convergence is unclear (Reviewer 3; related questions from Reviewer 4).** 
  - The iterative update \(x_0^{(n+1)} \leftarrow f_\theta(x_0^{(n)}, x_t, t)\) using fresh \((x_0,z)\) pairs per loop is not justified. There is no discussion of what \(\lim_{n\to\infty}x_0^{(n)}\) converges to or whether the algorithm converges at all.
  - For one-step sampling (e.g., Rectified Flow: \(x_0 = f_\theta(x_0^{(0)}, z, t=1)\) with \(x_0^{(0)},z\sim N(0,I)\)), the anchor target is ambiguous.
  - Reviewer 4 asks whether \(x_0^{(n+1)}\) is ever used during training in loop \(n\) and questions the coherence of the update when different classes are sampled across loops.

- **Limited empirical evaluation (Reviewer 3; also implied by Reviewer 1’s out-of-distribution question).** Only CIFAR-10 results are reported. No experiments on larger or more diverse datasets (e.g., ImageNet, higher-resolution data), raising questions about the method’s generalizability.

- **Performance on VE diffusion models is below state-of-the-art due to sub-optimal hyperparameters (Reviewer 3; acknowledged by the authors).**

- **Missing comparisons and connections to related work (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).**
  - Comparisons to Consistency Models, “ODE coarse-graining” ideas, and BOOT are absent (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
  - The claim of being the first *unified* ODE generative model is questionable given prior work such as Stochastic Interpolant and BOOT (Reviewer 4).

- **Lack of reliability/error analysis (Reviewer 1).** The paper does not discuss what errors users might encounter, how they compare to standard discretization errors, or what happens in out-of-distribution settings.
