## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LACE, a continuous diffusion model for unconditional and conditional layout generation, where layouts are sets of rectangles with class labels on a canvas. The key novelty is applying continuous diffusion (instead of prior discrete diffusion or binning) to enable differentiable aesthetic constraint losses (overlap and alignment) during training and post-processing. The model handles multiple tasks (unconditional, class-conditional, refinement) without retraining. Experiments on Rico and PubLayNet show state-of-the-art FID and MaxIoU scores. The code and model checkpoints are promised but not provided with the submission.

### Strengths
- **Novel continuous diffusion formulation**: Applying continuous diffusion directly to geometric and class properties for layout generation is original and opens a new research direction (R1, R2).
- **Unified framework**: The model handles unconditional generation, conditional generation (e.g., given element counts or partial layouts), and refinement/inpainting without retraining (R2, R3). The conditional approach via masked input is clean and effective (R4).
- **State-of-the-art results**: The method achieves new SoTA on the majority of conditional tasks on both Rico and PubLayNet datasets (R2, R3, R4). One reviewer (R2) finds this a strong contribution; others (R1, R3, R4) acknowledge the quantitative superiority but note limitations in evaluation scope.
- **Novel aesthetic constraints**: The differentiable overlap and alignment losses (with time-dependent weight scheduling) are a valuable contribution that can benefit future layout generation research (R2, R3). The alignment loss encourages global alignment of elements (R3).
- **Ablation study**: The paper includes an ablation study highlighting key design decisions (e.g., post-processing, aesthetic constraint use) (R1, R4). However, one reviewer (R4) requests more detailed ablation of sub-constraints.
- **Clarity and presentation**: The paper is well-written, clear, and interesting (R1, R4). Reviewer 2 rates presentation as excellent (4), others as good (3).

**Minority positive viewpoint**: Reviewer 2, who gave the highest score (8), emphasizes the novelty of the continuous state-space formulation and believes the aesthetic constraint schedule independently benefits future research. The other three reviewers gave 6, with lower enthusiasm about contribution (R1, R3 rated contribution 2; R2, R4 rated 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Incomplete reproduction details**: The paper lacks reporting of key training details such as model architecture, noise schedule, loss schedule, and constraint weights. These are needed because continuous diffusion on this state-space is previously unexplored (R2, echoed by R3’s question on time-dependent weight and threshold determination).
- **Code not available**: The authors promise to release code and checkpoints but did not provide them with the submission (R1).
- **Limited qualitative results and human evaluation**: The paper lacks sufficient qualitative comparisons with prior SoTA and does not include a human-rating study. Metrics like FID may not fully reflect human preference (R2). One reviewer (R3) also asks for qualitative results showing diversity.
- **Missing common metrics**: Despite proposing aesthetic constraints (overlap and alignment), the evaluation does not include overlap or alignment indices. Why were these not reported? (R2, R3, R4)
- **Advantage over discrete models with post-processing is unclear**: The paper argues continuous space enables constraint optimization, yet post-processing is still used. It does not analyze the limitations of applying post-processing to discrete diffusion models nor explain why continuous formulation is more effective even with post-processing (R3).
- **Potential misuse of overlap constraint**: The overlap loss pushes elements apart, but in many graphic designs intentional overlap (e.g., text over images) is used. The paper does not clarify the scope of this constraint or discuss when it might be unsuitable (R3).
- **Missing citations**: Several relevant layout generation works [1-3] are not cited or discussed (R3).
- **Limited practical applicability**: The experimental setup is somewhat toy-like (only element types and counts as conditions, no text/image content). Evaluation uses FID only, not human studies, so practical relevance and generalization to real applications (e.g., document or web design) are uncertain (R1, R4).
- **Extended ablation needed**: The ablation study should quantify and visualize the contribution of each sub-constraint (local vs. global alignment, overlap) independently (R4).
- **Lack of limitations discussion**: The paper does not discuss limitations of the proposed method, which would guide real-world application (R4).
