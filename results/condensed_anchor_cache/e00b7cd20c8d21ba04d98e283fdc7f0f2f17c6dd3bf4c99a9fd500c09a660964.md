## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes end-to-end learnable Gaussian Mixture Priors (GMPs) for diffusion-based sampling to replace fixed unimodal Gaussian priors. It aims to address poor exploration, non-linear dynamics, and mode collapse. The method is combined with an iterative model refinement (IMR) strategy that gradually adds components. Experiments on synthetic and real-world benchmarks (logistic regression, Fashion MNIST) show improvements over baselines. Reviewers found the idea novel and well-presented, but raised concerns about oversimplified experiments, scalability, heuristic component selection, unfair baselines, and evidence of mode collapse in generated images.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and impact**: The idea of learning a mixture prior in diffusion models is underexplored and could have significant impact on future work. (R2, R4)
- **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-structured, logically motivates GMPs, provides necessary background, and is easy to follow even with limited prior knowledge. (R1, R2, R4)
- **End-to-end learning**: Enables existing diffusion samplers (which used fixed priors) to benefit from an optimized prior, broadening applicability. (R1)
- **Experimental support**: Adequate comparisons demonstrate overall improvements over existing approaches, with convincing evidence on synthetic and real-world tasks. (R1, R2)
- **Comprehensive related work**: Provides a useful overview of recent developments in diffusion-based sampling. (R1)
- **Interesting ideas**: The iterative refinement strategy and mixture parametrization are novel contributions. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Oversimplified experiments**: The paper only tests logistic regression and a Fashion MNIST target derived from a normalizing flow. These do not demonstrate the claimed benefits (exploration, reduced mode collapse) on complex, high-dimensional tasks like image generation. (R3)
- **Scalability concerns**: Computational cost scales linearly with both number of components \(K\) and diffusion steps \(N\), and memory increases similarly. This could become prohibitive for very high-dimensional problems or large models. (R1, R4)
- **No principled criterion for component count**: The number of mixture components is chosen heuristically. IMR adds components at a fixed iteration interval (500 steps) without theoretical grounding; an ablation study on this criterion is missing. (R1, R3)
- **Contradictory results and mode collapse**: 
  - In Fig. 4, DIS-GMP performs worse than DIS-GP across all sampling metrics (Sinkhorn, ELBO, \(\Delta \log Z\)), contradicting the central claim. (R1)
  - DIS-GMP+IMR improves Sinkhorn distance and qualitative results but yields worse ELBO and \(\Delta \log Z\), suggesting ELBO is unreliable for multimodal targets. (R1)
  - Fig. 4 and Fig. 9 show that generated images are nearly identical, indicating the model mode collapses (especially apparent in Fashion MNIST). (R4)
- **Unfair baseline implementations**: SMC and CRAFT use a different annealing scheme but were given the same number of intermediate distributions as DIS. Since they are cheaper, their budget should be increased for fair comparison. (R4)
- **Inappropriate metrics**: Section 6 relies on ELBO and \(\Delta \log Z\), which are known to be unsuited for detecting mode collapse in multimodal distributions (as per Blessing et al. 2024, cited by the authors). Mode-collapse aware metrics (e.g., EUBO, EMC) are not computed. (R4)
- **Missing comparisons and ablations**:
  - No comparison against MCMC samplers (MALA, HMC) under the same computational budget. (R4)
  - No ablation in a controlled environment (e.g., sampling a Gaussian mixture) to explicitly validate that GMP addresses the claimed issues. (R4)
  - The effect of number of modes vs. diffusion steps on learned dynamics is not investigated; in Fig. 5, EES only marginally improves with more modes, hinting that dynamics may not be simpler. (R2)
  - No analysis of how performance would change if the target were transformed to fit a single Gaussian (given knowledge of support). (R2)
- **Overparameterisation ambiguity**: The paper gives two different interpretations of overparameterisation when the GMM has more components than the target (L479-483 vs. Fig. 9). (R4)
- **Alternative objectives not considered**: The log-variance objective (Nüsken & Richter 2021) could be effective and avoid the reparameterization trick on the prior, but is not used. (R4)
- **Implementation details missing**: The choice of variance for newly added Gaussian components (Eq. 22 specifies mean only) is not discussed. (R3)
- **Limited discussion of limitations**: Potential drawbacks of GMPs compared to other learnable priors are not addressed. (R3)
- **Minor inconsistencies**: 
  - The positioning of challenges C1 and C2 in Section 5 (lines 308–310) may be reversed relative to the Introduction (lines 51–53). (R1)
  - In Eq. 31 of Appendix A.1, the term \((-\frac{2}{\sigma^2})^2\) appears to have a sign inconsistency with Eq. 32. (R1)

*Note: One reviewer (Rating 8) was significantly more positive, emphasizing novelty and impact; the other three reviewers (Ratings 6, 6, 6) were more critical, particularly regarding experimental validation and fairness of baselines.*
