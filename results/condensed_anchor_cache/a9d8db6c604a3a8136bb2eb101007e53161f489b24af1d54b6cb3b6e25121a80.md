## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FreRA, a frequency-domain augmentation method for contrastive learning on time series classification. FreRA automatically separates critical and unimportant frequency components using a trainable vector, then applies Identity Modification to protect global semantics in critical components and Self-adaptive Modification to inject variance into unimportant ones. Experiments on UCR, UEA, and 5 large-scale datasets across classification, anomaly detection, and transfer learning show FreRA outperforming 10 baselines. Reviewers rate 5,5,6,5 (one Reviewer 3 is more positive at 6). While the method is well-motivated and tested, several concerns remain regarding novelty, definitions, ablation, and experimental fairness.

### Strengths
- Well-written, easy to follow, and clear methodology with strong motivation linking frequency-domain properties (global encoding, orthogonal basis, semantic integrity) to contrastive learning.
- Problem is significant; exploring augmentation in the frequency domain for time series is a novel perspective.
- Extensive experiments on diverse datasets (UCR, UEA, 5 large-scale) and tasks (classification, anomaly detection, transfer learning) with 10 baselines; FreRA outperforms most baselines.
- Plug-and-play design that can be integrated with existing contrastive learning frameworks.
- Interesting link between critical frequency components and global semantics, with a comprehensive explanation of the motivation.
- Easy to implement and understand.

### Weaknesses
- **Definitions and justification**: Key terms such as “semantic integrity” and “critical/unimportant frequency components” are not clearly defined. It is unclear how mutual information measures semantic integrity and whether this aligns with human understanding of semantics. Also, why a trainable vector s trained from Equation (7) can automatically select critical components is not justified.
- **Novelty and relation to prior work**: Frequency-based augmentation for time series is not new (e.g., TF-C). The paper oversimplifies existing work by claiming all prior augmentations are vision-inspired. The main novelty is a trainable vector s, which is incremental. The motivation study (Figure 1) focuses on time-domain limitations instead of comparing with existing frequency-based methods, weakening the core contribution.
- **Handling of frequency components**: The method’s treatment of high-frequency components is unclear. The paper should analyze learned importance scores across frequency bands and correlate them with dataset performance. Whether the method focuses only on low frequencies and ignores high frequencies (important for prediction) needs clarification.
- **Simultaneous semantic preservation and variance injection**: How FreRA achieves both goals is not clearly attributed to specific design choices. The self-adaptive modification (scaling unimportant components) appears simple and its motivation is not well explained.
- **Ablation and comparisons**: The ablation study is coarse. Missing variants include: modifying all (or randomly selected) frequency components, modifying unimportant components randomly, and removing all three modules. Comparison with SOTA baselines (SoftCLT, InfoTS) shows no clear advantage on UEA/UCR datasets. Table 1 suggests SoftCLT and FreRA have similar performance; it is unclear if integrating FreRA into softCLT yields gains.
- **Experimental design issues**: The best-performing augmentations from a set of 11 time-domain and 5 frequency-domain methods are selected for comparison; this selection process may be unfair and lacks transparency. Hyper-parameter impact should be in the main paper. For transfer learning, it is unclear why SHAR data was used instead of datasets from Table 1 (e.g., UCIHAR), and performance is lower than reported in the reference (Qian et al., 2022); the reasons for this discrepancy are not discussed.
- **Multivariate time series**: The method does not consider channel dependencies, which are important for multivariate TS. Evaluation on datasets with varying channel dependencies and a discussion on extending the method are missing.
- **Contrastive learning frameworks**: Only InfoNCE results are shown; other CL paradigms (e.g., SimCLR) and a wider range of hyper-parameters within each framework should be explored.
- **Computational complexity**: Only asymptotic complexity is given; practical constant factors (e.g., FFT overhead) and comparisons with time-domain augmentation methods are needed.
- **Anomaly detection evaluation**: Since the paper focuses on classification, the inclusion of anomaly detection evaluation is not justified.
- **Figure readability**: Figure 2 is hard to read; different colors for vector blocks in FreRA are confusing.
