## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates the role of initial random noise (seeds) in compositional failures of text-to-image diffusion models. It proposes mining “reliable seeds” that produce correct compositions, then using those seeds to automatically generate a high-quality training dataset without manual annotation. Fine-tuning the model on this self-generated data improves numerical and spatial compositional accuracy, with relative gains of 29.3%/19.5% for numerical composition and 60.7%/21.1% for spatial composition on Stable Diffusion and PixArt-α, respectively. The approach requires no additional modules or trainable parameters.

### Strengths
- The core idea is easy to follow and the problem is well-designed (Reviewer 1, score 8).
- The finding that certain seeds consistently produce better compositions is interesting and useful to many diffusion users (Reviewer 1).
- No additional modules, layouts, or bounding boxes are needed – the method works with the diffusion model as-is (Reviewer 2).
- Extensive experimentation validates the reliable seed hypothesis, and a broad spectrum of ways to use those seeds to enhance performance is explored (Reviewers 2 and 3).
- The approach is novel and data-efficient: it harnesses seed variability to reduce manual annotation effort (Reviewer 3).
- Quantitative and qualitative evaluations across multiple models (Stable Diffusion, PixArt-α) demonstrate effectiveness (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Proof of seed layout correlation**: Section 3.2’s heatmap analysis is not convincingly proven. It is unclear how “correct”/”incorrect” are determined in Figure 4 (potential bias), which layer is used (output before VAE decoder?), and why the four-coin setup splits exactly into four grid-like regions (Reviewer 1, score 8).  
- **Need for more complex compositional evaluation**: The paper only shows simple, non-overlapping objects. It does not evaluate partially overlapped objects, multi-object mixtures (e.g., “2 airplanes and 4 birds in the sky”), higher object counts (7–8), or boundary/corner spatial relations (Reviewers 1 and 2, scores 8 and 8).  
- **Baseline comparisons are insufficient**: (a) Newer methods like Ranni (Feng et al., CVPR 2024) should be compared instead of older LMD. (b) The comparison in Table 4 is unfair: LMD and Multi Diffusion use 512×512 Stable Diffusion, while this method uses 768×768. Numbers with a 512×512 version of this method are needed (Reviewer 2).  
- **Bias from the correctness judge**: “Correctness” is determined by CogVLM2, which may itself have biases (e.g., preferring non-overlapping layouts). Fine-tuning on self-generated data based on that judge could overfit the model to those biases (Reviewer 1).  
- **No automatic inference-time seed selection**: The paper relies on offline seed mining. There is no method to automatically select reliable seeds for new prompts during inference, making the approach impractical for real-time or dynamic use (Reviewer 3, score 6 – this reviewer is more critical than the two 8-scoring reviewers).  
- **Potential loss of output diversity**: Fine-tuning on a limited set of reliable seeds may cause mode collapse, reducing the range of variations the model can produce (e.g., generating similar images repeatedly) (Reviewer 3).  
- **Unexplored impact on overall image quality**: The paper does not analyze whether fine-tuning for compositional accuracy degrades other quality aspects such as sharpness, color accuracy, or artifacts (Reviewer 3).  
- **Lack of comparison with real-world datasets**: The method assumes self-generated data is superior for fine-tuning, but no empirical comparison with alternative high-quality real-world datasets is provided (Reviewer 3).  
- **Limited generalization to other architectures**: Only Stable Diffusion and PixArt-α are tested. Performance on architectures with different attention mechanisms or sampling strategies is unclear (Reviewer 3).  
- **Clarity on seed definition**: The paper should specify whether “fixing the seed” means fixing the entire input noise or re-sampling from the same seed each time, and why certain seeds exhibit a preference for spatial layouts (as observed in Section 3.3) (Reviewer 1).
