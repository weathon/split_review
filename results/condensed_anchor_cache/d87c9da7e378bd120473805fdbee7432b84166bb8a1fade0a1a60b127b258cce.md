## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Atlas Gaussians, a representation that models 3D shapes as a union of local patches, each decoded into a field of 3D Gaussians via UV-based sampling. A VAE is trained to embed 3D shapes into a compact latent space, then a latent diffusion model (EDM) is used for generation. Reviewers commend the motivation, clear writing, and integration of 3DGS with diffusion. However, concerns are raised about insufficient experimental validation against recent SOTAs (e.g., LN3Diff), unclear disentanglement of geometry/appearance, missing ablations (loss hyperparameters, transformer decoder comparison), and lack of detail in figures/notation. Some reviewers note that the writing is clear overall but needs improvement in method presentation and figure clarity. The novelty is centered on representation and generation of 3D Gaussians, but the latent diffusion component itself is seen as standard.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, well-motivated, and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- Addressing the challenges of VAEs for latent diffusion in 3D is important and timely (Reviewer 1).
- The Atlas Gaussians representation, with disentangled learning of geometry and appearance features, is novel and unique (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Integration of 3DGS with diffusion generation is nicely executed and achieves SOTA results in 3D shape generation (Reviewers 3, 4).
- Using UV-based sampling allows generation of a theoretically infinite number of 3D Gaussians, capturing better details and scalability (Reviewer 3).
- The decomposition into local Gaussian patches enables handling shapes of varying complexity (Reviewer 3).
- Sufficient experimental results, including quantitative and qualitative comparisons, support the claimed contributions (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Release of code enhances transparency and reproducibility (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Contribution clarity and novelty:** The specific novelties beyond recent SOTAs (e.g., LN3Diff) and beyond just generating 3D Gaussians are not clearly supported by the qualitative findings and quantitative results (Reviewer 4). The latent diffusion model itself (EDM) is not a technical novelty; the contribution rests on the data representation (Reviewer 4). It is unclear which component (Atlas Gaussian representation vs. transformer encoder) contributes more significantly to overall performance (Reviewer 1).
- **Disentanglement of geometry and appearance:** The process for learning geometric features in the latent space is vague; the loss function supervises patch centers and appearance, but geometric feature learning remains unclear (Reviewer 1).
- **Experimental validation concerns:** 
  - Qualitative comparisons in Fig. 5 and Fig. 6 raise concerns about whether baselines (e.g., LN3Diff) were trained to their optimal state; in the original papers, structure is better preserved (Reviewer 4). 
  - It is unclear if the authors ran all baselines themselves or copied numbers from papers; reporting mean and std would improve robustness assessment (Reviewer 4). 
  - Inference times in Table 2 are not comparable because GVGEN and LN3Diff experiments were done on different GPU setups (Reviewer 4). 
  - The paper lacks a thorough comparison on inference times, especially with increasing number of 3D Gaussians, to justify efficiency claims (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing ablations:** 
  - Ablation study needed for loss hyperparameters λ_r and λ_KL (Reviewer 2). 
  - Ablation study needed comparing the proposed transformer-based decoder to a vanilla decoder (Reviewer 4). 
  - The uniqueness of the transformer decoder architecture compared to LN3Diff’s transformer decoder is not clarified (Reviewer 4).
- **Optimization complexity:** The loss function involves approximately 10 distinct losses including KL divergence; the optimization process needs more explanation beyond listing losses with weighting parameters (Reviewer 4).
- **Writing and presentation issues:** 
  - The method section is presented in a list-like format, making it difficult to read smoothly (Reviewer 2). 
  - Some notations lack sufficient explanation (e.g., d in L189, L135 Eq4; n in L256, L268; relationship between S, P, I) (Reviewer 2). 
  - Figure 3 lacks detail: the steps from z̄ to z₀ are ambiguous, and the boundary between VAE encoder and decoder is not indicated (Reviewer 2). 
  - The conditioning mechanism C (Section 3.3) is insufficiently described despite some supplementary details (Reviewer 2). 
  - The term “atlas” (intended as in AtlasNet) is not explained, even though it appears in the method name (Reviewer 2).
- **Handling fine details and artifacts:** The approach may struggle with fine details due to Gaussian representation’s blurry effect; it is unclear if increasing the number of Gaussians/patches suffices to address this (Reviewer 3). UV-based sampling could potentially produce artifacts (e.g., inconsistency or distortions) – this is not discussed (Reviewer 3). The framework’s robustness to noisy or sparse data is not evaluated (Reviewer 3).
- **Subjective claims:** The statement “3D-based neural networks learn better 3D features … outperform multi-view based networks” is made without citation and appears subjective (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor:** Some references in the bibliography are irrelevant to the paper’s problem (Reviewer 4).
