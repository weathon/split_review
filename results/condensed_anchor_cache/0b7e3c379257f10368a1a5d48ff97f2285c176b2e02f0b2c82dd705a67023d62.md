## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses instability in off-policy RL with high update-to-data (UTD) ratios, attributing the problem to value function generalization failure on unobserved on-policy actions. The authors propose MAD-TD, which augments real replay buffer data with a small proportion of synthetic data from a learned world model. Empirical results on DeepMind Control (DMC) tasks show competitive performance compared to baseline methods (BRO, TD-MPC, TD-MPC2) without requiring periodic resets. The authors also provide theoretical analysis (matrix decomposition on page 3) linking instability to positive-definite and non-positive-definite terms, though the proof is experimental rather than formal.

### Strengths
1. **Relevant and impactful problem**: The paper tackles a critical challenge in sample-efficient RL—instability under high UTD—which has implications for real-world applications where data is costly.
2. **Motivated combination of insight and analysis**: The authors combine mathematical intuition (identifying value function overestimation on unobserved actions as root cause) with empirical validation, and they provide a balanced presentation of strengths and limitations.
3. **Effective without resets**: MAD-TD achieves stable high-UTD training without relying on periodic parameter resets, a practical advantage for continued learning.
4. **Clear writing and reproducibility**: The paper is well-organized, key ideas are clearly communicated, and the authors provide open-source code.
5. **Competitive results**: MAD-TD demonstrates strong performance on challenging DMC tasks compared to baselines, supporting its viability.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited baseline and benchmark diversity**:
   - Only two baselines (BRO and TD-MPC variants) are compared; a broader set of state-of-the-art methods is absent.
   - Results are validated only on DMC tasks. Reviewers question generalization to other benchmarks such as Meta-World (e.g., Stick-pull, coffee-push/pull, assembly) or MyoSuit, citing that different environments can yield different effects in RL (Nauman et al., ICML 2024).

2. **Missing ablation studies on key design choices**:
   - **Percentage of model-generated data (α)**: No systematic ablation across values (e.g., 1%, 5%, 10%, 25%, 50%) is provided, despite being a critical hyperparameter. Reviewers ask for plots of performance vs. α on representative tasks.
   - **World model accuracy**: No analysis of sensitivity to model fidelity. How does performance degrade when the world model is less accurate (especially early in training)? Reviewers suggest a self-adaptive mechanism that adjusts model data proportion over time.
   - **Influence of architecture changes relative to TD-MPC2**: MAD-TD is based on TD-MPC2 but modifies several components; the impact of these individual changes is not isolated or explained.

3. **Weakness of theoretical guarantees**: The main “proof” is experimental; the theoretical analysis (matrix decomposition on page 3) does not prove that MAD-TD prevents instability—it only suggests conditions under which the approximation error term is minor (small γ or dominant first term). No formal guarantees are offered. The discussion below the unnumbered equation is insufficiently clarified.

4. **Limited originality**: The idea of combining model-generated data with real data has been used in prior work (e.g., Lu et al., NeurIPS 2024, "Synthetic Experience Replay"), and model-based augmentation is common in offline RL (e.g., model-Bellman inconsistency, RAMBO). While the paper applies this to high-UTD off-policy RL, more profound insights—such as how model error affects performance or when to trust generated data—are missing.

5. **Minor presentation issues**: 
   - Equations are not numbered (multiple reviewers note this).
   - Typos: In the first equation of Section 3.1 (definition of L(θ)), there is an extra ")". In the last equation of Section 3.1, the summation symbol is missing the upper limit "n".
   - The submission uses an incorrect formatting template (ICLR 2024 instead of the venue's required format).

6. **Inconclusive results on action-repeat (AR) usage**: Reviewer 3 asks whether there is an advantage to using AR=2 over AR=1, and suggests extending Figure 5 to include AR=1 at 2 million time steps. The paper does not clarify this.

7. **Reliance on model fidelity**: The method's success hinges on learning a sufficiently accurate world model online, which is acknowledged as a limitation. When model quality is poor, the approach may fail; more insight into when/why it fails (if at all) is needed.
