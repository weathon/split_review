## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes IPDiff, a diffusion model for pocket-conditioned 3D ligand generation that incorporates protein-ligand interactions into both the forward and reverse processes. A pretrained network (IPNet) predicts binding affinity from protein–ligand graphs and is then used to alter the forward diffusion drift (prior-shifting) and condition the reverse denoising (prior-conditioning). On CrossDocked2020, IPDiff achieves state-of-the-art binding affinities (‑6.42 Avg Vina Score vs. ‑5.67 for prior models) while maintaining proper molecular properties. The idea is novel and well-motivated, and the experimental design is generally sound. However, concerns remain about technical clarity, potential data leakage between the IPNet training set (PDBBind) and the benchmark, the strength of the evaluation metrics, and the extent of novelty relative to prior work on learned noise schedules and self-conditioning.

### Strengths

- **Novelty of prior-shifting and prior-conditioning.** The concept of adapting both the forward and reverse diffusion processes using a learned protein–ligand interaction prior is original for structure-based drug design (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **State-of-the-art quantitative results.** IPDiff achieves the best average Vina Score (‑6.42) on CrossDocked2020 while preserving drug-likeness and synthesizability, clearly outperforming earlier models (Reviewer 1). An ablation study shows the contribution of prior-shifting and prior-conditioning (Reviewer 1).
- **Clear writing and well-structured presentation.** The paper is clearly written, with well-designed experiments and derivations of the forward/backward kernels and training objectives provided in the appendix (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Reproducible experimental setup.** Experiments are sufficiently described and are likely reproducible (Reviewer 2). The ablation on the scalar in the drift correction is informative (Reviewer 2).
- **Strong motivation.** The problem of mismatch between forward and reverse processes in protein-specific diffusion is convincingly motivated (Reviewer 2, 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Clarity and technical rigor (Reviewers 1, 2).**  
  - The paper cites Satorras et al. as an SE(3) equivariant network, but it is actually an E(n) GNN; chirality is important for proteins and this discrepancy is not addressed.  
  - IPNet uses message passing only over neighboring nodes, unlike the all-pairs message passing in E(n) GNN, and the authors do not prove that their architecture is SE(3) equivariant.  
  - Section 4.2.1 does not explain how the deformation field \(S\) is trained to avoid producing impossible molecular geometries; positions for every atom can lead to arbitrary invalid deformations.  
  - The manuscript contains minor typos throughout (Reviewer 2).

- **Missing details about IPNet training and graph construction (Reviewers 1, 3).**  
  - It is not specified whether receptor and ligand graphs are built using atom connectivity or distance thresholds.  
  - The objective for training IPNet is not clearly stated (e.g., mean squared error or something else? What is the range of the binding affinity label?).  
  - The method requires supervised learning on PDBbind, making it more complex and harder to scale; this limitation should be discussed in the main paper (Reviewer 3).

- **Potential data leakage between PDBBind and the CrossDocked2020 test set (Reviewers 2, 3).**  
  - IPNet is trained on PDBBind and then used as a feature extractor for IPDiff. If PDBBind contains samples identical or very similar to the CrossDocked test set, the reported gains could partly stem from information leakage. The authors do not discuss this possibility.  
  - Reviewer 2 asks explicitly: Were the poses used in testing/evaluation of IPDiff excluded from the training set for IPNet? What are the differences in chemical diversity (scaffolds, flexibility) between the two datasets?  
  - The percentage of novel (not seen during IPNet training) molecules generated by IPDiff is not reported (Reviewer 2).

- **Generalization to unseen targets and edge cases (Reviewer 2, 4).**  
  - Performance on molecules with a large number of rotatable bonds is not investigated.  
  - How well does the model generalize to completely new pockets and protein targets?  
  - Evaluation is limited to CrossDocked2020; testing on additional benchmarks would strengthen the claims (Reviewer 4).  
  - Reviewer 1 notes that using the pocket (holo) structure for diffusion is counterintuitive: for an unknown ligand the pocket is an ensemble, and only the apo structure is available. The paper does not address this limitation.

- **Insufficient evaluation of generated molecule quality (Reviewer 3).**  
  - The metrics used (Vina Score, QED, SA) are known to have limitations. Recent tools such as PoseCheck and PoseBusters offer more rigorous assessments of binding pose quality, protein–ligand interaction patterns, and structural validity. The authors should report results on these additional metrics.  
  - Because the model explicitly uses protein–ligand interactions, it would be natural to evaluate the quality of interactions in the generated poses (e.g., using PoseCheck interaction metrics).  
  - Reviewer 3 further notes that the large gap between IPDiff and baselines on standard metrics may indicate that those metrics are not very informative.

- **Novelty relative to prior work on learned diffusions and self-conditioning (Reviewer 4).**  
  - Prior work has learned the diffusion noise schedule (e.g., learned beta parameters). It is unclear whether the gains of prior-shifting come from adapting the forward process or simply from a better-tuned noise schedule; a direct comparison is missing.  
  - The prior-conditioning component is similar to the self-conditioning approach in “Analog Bits”, and the paper does not clearly explain how it differs or improves upon that method.  
  - Reviewer 4 also asks whether prior-shifting could be applied in class-conditional image generation, but this is a curiosity rather than a required experiment.

- **Missing analysis of approximation error during generation (Reviewer 3).**  
  - Between Equations (10) and (11), the model must approximate \(\mathbf{X}_0^M\) and \(\mathbf{V}_0^M\) (since the ground truth is unavailable). The paper does not analyze how much performance is lost due to this approximation, nor does it propose an ablation to quantify it.

- **Computational efficiency (Reviewer 2).**  
  - IPDiff generates molecules in ~31 s per molecule. A comparison of this wall‑clock time with the time needed by the docking oracle on similar-sized structures is not provided, leaving it unclear whether the method is faster or slower than redocking a known molecule.

- **Contrasting views among reviewers.**  
  - Reviewer 2 (score 8, confidence 4) found the paper clear, well-designed, and reproducible, with only minor issues.  
  - Reviewer 3 (score 5, confidence 3) was more critical, raising concerns about data leakage, metric informativeness, and missing analyses.  
  - Reviewers 1 and 4 (scores 6, confidence 3) had mixed opinions, praising the idea but flagging the clarity and technical concerns above.
