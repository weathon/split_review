## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a variant of multiple-play stochastic bandits with prioritized resource sharing (MSB-PRS), where each arm has a stochastic number of capacities, each play has a priority weight, and capacity is allocated to higher priority plays first. Instance-independent and instance-dependent regret lower bounds of Ω(α1 σ √(KM T)) and Ω(α1 σ² (MK/Δ) ln T) are proved. An algorithm MSB-PRS-OffOpt is designed to locate the optimal play allocation policy with O(M³K³) complexity. Using this as a subroutine, an approximate UCB algorithm (MSB-PRS-ApUCB) is proposed, with instance-independent and instance-dependent upper bounds matching the lower bounds up to K√(ln KT) and α₁K factors. Simulations validate the approach against baselines from the literature.

### Strengths
- **Novel problem formulation.** The introduction of prioritized resource sharing with stochastic arm capacities and differentiated priorities is novel, extending the MP-MAB framework to practical resource allocation settings (e.g., LLM and edge intelligence). (R1, R3, R4)
- **Theoretical characterization.** Both instance-independent and instance-dependent lower bounds are provided, and the proposed algorithm achieves regret upper bounds that closely match these lower bounds (up to acceptable factors). (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Algorithm design.** The MSB-PRS-ApUCB algorithm is thoughtfully motivated and achieves sublinear regret while maintaining low per‑round computational complexity. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Experimental validation.** Synthetic experiments assess the performance of the proposed algorithm compared to baseline algorithms; some reviewers find the experiments provide a good assessment. (R1, R3, R4) *However, Reviewer 2 considers the experiments overly simplistic and lacking thorough description/comparison (see Weaknesses).*

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and readability.** Section 3 is cluttered with endless notation, making the model barely readable. A high‑level overview paragraph and an illustrative example/visual representation are needed. (R1, R2) *Reviewer 3 finds the paper well‑structured and clear, but this is a minority view.*
- **Lack of concrete motivation for LLM/edge applications.** The paper only gives a few vague lines in the introduction (e.g., “reasoning tasks and LLM instances modelled as plays and arms … priority quantified by price, membership hierarchy”) without explaining how the model applies. A dedicated subsection or practical examples are required. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Potential error in concentration bound.** Lemma 5.4 appears incorrect; the authors should use Lemma 9 from [Maillard et al., 2017] instead of Lemma 10. This affects the inequalities in lines 977, 1006, and 1053. (R3)
- **Unclear contribution of stochastic capacity.** Existing work already assumes unknown deterministic arm capacity. It is not clear whether stochastic capacity makes the problem harder (due to randomness) or easier (given known observations), and whether the learning component adds technical novelty beyond standard UCB matching. (R4) *See also Reviewer 3’s remark that the learning component is fairly standard except for the nonlinear combinatorial structure.* (R3)
- **Oracle justification insufficient.** The oracle for optimal matching depends on maximum weight matching from existing work, but no theoretical guarantee is provided that this oracle is optimal for the MSB-PRS setting. Further justification is needed. (R4)
- **Gaps in comparison to prior work.** A table comparing the results of this work with previous findings would clarify the contribution. (R2)
- **Incomplete description of baselines.** The baselines OnlinActPrf and OnlinActPrf‑v are not described in Section 6 or Appendix B; it is unclear how they were adapted to the MSB-PRS setting and whether the comparison is fair given the different problem formulations. (R1)
- **Unclear explanation of an algorithmic step.** The statement around Eqn. 6 (“Exact‑UCB may attain the max value at different selections of μ, P for different action values especially when the confidence band fails”) is unclear and needs better justification. (R1)
- **Questions on reward definition.** The reward in line 190 is scaled by the priority parameter; a practical example clarifying this definition would be helpful. (R3)
- **Limited experiments lacking thoroughness.** The experimental setups are not described in sufficient detail; the comparison with other studies is minimal, leaving room for improvement. (R2) *Reviewer 3, however, considers the synthetic experiments a good assessment; this disagreement is noted.*
- **Lower bound analysis lacks technical novelty.** (R4)
