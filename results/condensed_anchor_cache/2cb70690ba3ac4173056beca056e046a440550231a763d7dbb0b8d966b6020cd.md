## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper tackles unsupervised knowledge distillation (without ground-truth labels) by introducing CoSS, a loss composed of sample-wise cosine similarity (\(L_{co}\)) and a dimension-wise space similarity (\(L_{ss}\)). Unlike prior methods that rely on large feature queues (e.g., 100k+ samples) or contrastive objectives, CoSS operates on a single mini-batch (typically size 256). The method is evaluated on image classification (e.g., ResNet-18, EfficientNet-b0, ViT) on ImageNet-1K and other datasets, as well as image/video instance segmentation. The authors claim state-of-the-art efficiency and competitive performance. Reviewers acknowledge the simplicity and extensive experimental scope, but raise substantial concerns about novelty, theoretical justification, baseline comparisons, and reproducibility.

### Strengths
- **Simplicity and efficiency**: The CoSS method is simple, does not require importance sampling, large feature queues, or contrastive objectives (R2, R3, R4). It reduces GPU memory and training time compared to methods with large queues (R4).
- **Well-organized presentation**: The paper is easy to follow, with a clear logical progression and concise mathematical formulation of the space similarity loss (R1, R2, R4).
- **Extensive experimental evaluation**: Many experiments across different tasks (classification, segmentation, video) and architectures (CNN, ViT) demonstrate the method’s applicability (R1, R3, R5). Qualitative assessments (Figs 3–5) help illustrate learned representations (R5).
- **Robustness**: Students trained with CoSS show strong transferability and robustness to input distribution shifts (R4).
- **Originality in unsupervised setting**: Few works explore pure unsupervised knowledge distillation without labels (R5). However, one reviewer (R4) strongly contests the novelty, noting that a similar inter/intra-relation loss on logits was already proposed in [1]; the difference of applying it to feature vectors is minor and insufficiently justified.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and contribution limited**: The core idea of combining inter-sample (cosine) and intra-sample (dimension-wise) similarity losses closely resembles the inter/intra-relation loss in [1] (applied to logits). The paper does not sufficiently differentiate its contribution or discuss why moving from logits to features is non-trivial (R4). A direct comparison with [1] is missing (R4). Another reviewer (R3) questions why the method discards negative samples, which is standard in contrastive learning—this is not justified.
- **Weak theoretical grounding for space similarity**: The authors argue that the feature manifold is locally Euclidean and treat it as a topological manifold, using a homeomorphism argument to justify cosine normalization. Reviewers note this assumption is strong and unsupported by references or empirical evidence (R3, R1). The discussion does not compare the d-dimensional Euclidean manifold against the hypersphere induced by L2 normalization in prior work, nor does it address sensitivity to feature vector scale (R1). More rigorous justification is needed (R3).
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons and missing state-of-the-art methods**:  
  - Comparisons are lacking against the baseline of plain cosine similarity (only \(L_{co}\)) on the same feature space; without this, the additive benefit of \(L_{ss}\) is unclear (R2).  
  - Missing comparison with SMD [2], a recent self-supervised distillation method (R4).  
  - On large datasets (ImageNet-1K, Table 1), CoSS underperforms some existing methods, raising concerns about its effectiveness (R4).  
  - The paper does not compare with the method of [1] (R4).  
  - The performance on CNN→CNN distillation is worse than on ViT, yet no analysis or explanation is provided (R2).  
- **Lack of ablation studies and hyperparameter analysis**:  
  - No ablation isolating \(L_{ss}\) and \(L_{co}\) individually (R4).  
  - No study on the impact of batch size on space similarity (R4).  
  - The hyperparameter \(\lambda\) (Eq. 5) is sensitive to architectures and datasets; the paper does not discuss how to choose it without costly tuning (R2, R4).  
  - The effect of adding negative samples to the loss is not explored (R3).  
- **Missing details on baselines and computational claims**:  
  - Baseline methods (e.g., CRD, SEED, BINGO+) lack descriptions of architecture, training details, and hyperparameter tuning. It is unclear how supervised baselines are adapted to the unsupervised setting (R5).  
  - The claim of higher efficiency is not supported by measured GPU memory or time comparisons; the paper only provides GPU memory for one setting, without a clear definition of how it was measured (R2, R5).  
  - Details on ViT training (e.g., trained from scratch?) are missing (R4).  
- **Reproducibility concerns**: Appendix A.1 is not detailed enough (teacher training, additional layers, baseline hyperparameters) and does not cover all experiments (R5).  
- **Clarity issues**:  
  - Notation is inconsistent; e.g., \(\hat{A}_t\) (Eq. 3) vs. \(\widehat{A_t^T}\) (Eq. 4), \(\lambda\) undefined before use (R5).  
  - kNN evaluation: choice of \(k\) is not justified; dependence on \(k\) undermines the claim of parameter-free evaluation (R5).  
  - Many typos and grammatical errors (R5 lists multiple specific examples).  
  - Section 4 (topological argument) needs expanded discussion to be convincing (R3).  
  - The dimensions in Equations 3 and 4 are unclear: \(A_I\) and \(A_{II}\) are not properly defined (R4).  
- **Missing investigations**:  
  - Connection between batch normalization and CoSS is not explored (R1).  
  - The potential relation to optimal transport (Sinkhorn function) is not discussed (R1).  
    - *Incorporations of these missing investigations are requested as questions but overlap with criticisms of insufficient analysis.*
