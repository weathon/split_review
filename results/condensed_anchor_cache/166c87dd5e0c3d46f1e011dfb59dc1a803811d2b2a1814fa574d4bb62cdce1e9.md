## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents a systematic empirical study of transformer ICL generalization along three axes: inter-problem, intra-problem, and intra-task. Using synthetic function fitting (sinusoids and their additive/multiplicative/compositional combinations), API calling, and translation, the authors find that transformers lack inter-problem generalization but excel in intra-task and intra-problem generalization, and that training data diversity (mixing tasks) enhances generalization. The reviewers acknowledge the well-designed formulation, comprehensive experiments, and practical insights. However, concerns include: limited representativeness of synthetic tasks, overclaimed novelty, lack of mechanistic understanding, missing baselines (non-transformer), insufficient nuance in the claim about no inter-problem generalization, missing experimental details/ablation studies, and limited applicability to complex NLP tasks. One reviewer (score 8) is markedly more positive, emphasizing the systematic approach and actionable guidance; the other two (scores 6) raise stronger methodological and novelty concerns.

### Strengths
1. **Systematic problem formulation**: The three-dimensional framework (inter-problem, intra-problem, intra-task) is well-defined and the experimental protocol is clearly scoped and adaptable across domains. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
2. **Comprehensive analysis and extended experiments**: Extensive experiments on synthetic data and real-world tasks (API calling, translation) strongly support the conclusions, with smart experiment designs (e.g., tool-calling setup). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
3. **Validation on real data and LLMs**: Results for compositional tasks hold on Llama-3 (Sec. 3.4) and ToolLlama (Sec. 4), verifying that synthetic findings generalize. (Reviewer 3)
4. **Actionable practical insight**: The finding that training data diversity (mixing tasks) enhances ICL generalization provides clear guidance for data design. (Reviewers 1, 2)
5. **Relevant problem**: Understanding when ICL generalizes is an important open question for the community. (Reviewers 1, 3)

### Weaknesses
1. **Representativeness of synthetic functions**: It is unclear if the chosen basis functions (sinusoids, Eq. 2) and their combinations (Eq. 3) are representative of realistic ICL tasks; conclusions may not extend to other function classes (e.g., polynomials). (Reviewer 1) The paper does not explore how the model handles ambiguity, context-dependence, or long-range dependencies common in natural language. (Reviewer 2)
2. **Lack of mechanistic understanding**: The paper does not sufficiently explore the mechanisms behind intra-problem generalization—e.g., how attention weights and internal representations evolve, or what limits exist (how many functions can be combined before performance degrades, which combinations are harder). (Reviewer 2) No explanation is given for why ComFuncLearner generalizes to convex combinations of very different frequencies (e.g., sin(42x)+cos(27x)) despite training on simpler ones. (Reviewer 3)
3. **Overclaimed lack of inter-problem generalization**: The paper claims transformers “lack inter-problem generalization with ICL” (line 19), yet Fig. 2 left and Fig. 3 left show that transformers do generalize to y‑axis shifted sinusoids (e.g., sin(x)–0.5). A more nuanced analysis is needed, including explicit discussion of the bias term added to base functions. (Reviewer 3)
4. **Novelty of diversity finding is limited**: That task diversity aids generalization is a well-known principle from large-scale LM training and robustness research. The paper does not distinguish diversity within a problem from diversity across distinct problems. (Reviewer 3)
5. **Missing baselines**: No non-transformer baseline (e.g., linear regression, MLP) is included, so it is unclear whether the observed behaviors are architecture-specific. (Reviewer 1)
6. **Limited applicability to complex NLP**: The simple tasks (function fitting, tool calling) may not reflect natural language reasoning; Llama-3 struggles with function fitting despite extensive training, highlighting domain shift challenges. (Reviewers 2, 3) Findings on convex and product combinations (Secs. 3.1, 3.2) are not validated in real-world experiments. (Reviewer 3) Results for the mixed translation task (Sec. 4.2) are missing. (Reviewer 3)
7. **Missing experimental details and code**:
   - Range of φ in Eq. 2 is not provided. (Reviewer 3)
   - How test functions are generated in Fig. 2 (e.g., f(x)=sin(x)–0.5) does not match the defined function classes and may affect results. (Reviewer 3)
   - Meaning of superscripts (e.g., (0)) in Eq. 2 is unexplained. (Reviewer 3)
   - Code is not provided. (Reviewer 3)
   - Choice of Llama-3 in Sec. 3.4 is inconsistent with Llama-2 used in Sec. 4. (Reviewer 3)
   - Red numbers in Table 4 are unclear (the number for Baseline+ICL_s under Average_s is not directly computable from the table). (Reviewer 3)
8. **Questions that overlap with missing experiments/ablation**:
   - Why add a third class to balance data for Baseline instead of sampling more from the four base function classes? (Reviewer 1)
   - Was training data size controlled when adding combination data (to ensure fair comparison)? (Reviewer 2) Was a reverse inter- and intra-problem generalization setup explored to see if the model can learn directly from combination data? (Reviewer 2) Does the order of data points (e.g., curriculum learning) affect ComFuncLearner’s performance? (Reviewer 2) Why was ℱ₅⁽⁰⁾ chosen for the Baseline? (Reviewer 2)
   - Do the results for convex combinations hold when training ComFuncLearner on ℱ₁⁽⁰⁾+ℱ₂⁽⁰⁾ instead of ℱ₁⁽⁰⁾+ℱ₃⁽⁰⁾? (Reviewer 3)
   - How are synonyms/paraphrases handled in the translation experiments—why use BLEU instead of BertScore? (Reviewer 3)
   - Were GPT-2 models trained with permutation invariance? (Reviewer 3)
9. **Presentation issues**: Figures and tables would be clearer if they wrote out function names (e.g., f(x)=sin(x)) instead of abstract notation. (Reviewer 3) The paragraph in lines 263–265 addresses two separate ideas and should be split. (Reviewer 3) The description of ComFuncLearner model variants should appear already in Sec. 3.2, not only later. (Reviewer 3)
