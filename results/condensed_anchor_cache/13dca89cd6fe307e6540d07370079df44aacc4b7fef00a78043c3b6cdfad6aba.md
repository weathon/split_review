## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers generally agree that Classifier Score Distillation (CSD) is a simple and conceptually novel approach that replaces the noise-prediction term in SDS with the classifier-free-guidance term, achieving strong qualitative results on text-to-3D generation, texture synthesis, and editing. Two reviewers (R1,R2) rate it highly (8) praising its simplicity, effectiveness, and integration ease; one reviewer (R3, score 6) and one (R4, score 5) raise significant concerns about evaluation fairness, missing comparisons, reproducibility, and lack of ablation. All reviewers note that the paper is well written and the idea is easy to understand.

### Strengths
- **Simple yet effective formulation**: CSD is easy to reimplement and requires no fine-tuning of diffusion models, unlike ProlificDreamer/VSD, avoiding training instability and inefficiency (R1, R4).
- **Novel perspective**: The reinterpretation of classifier-free guidance as an implicit classifier provides new insights into score distillation (R2).
- **General and integrable**: CSD can be seamlessly integrated into any existing score distillation pipeline for text-to-3D, image editing, or other tasks (R2).
- **Thorough evaluation compared to SDS**: Qualitative and quantitative results (CLIP R-Precision over 81 prompts) show improvement over SDS and several baselines (R1, R2). Promising results are shown across shape generation, texture synthesis, and shape editing (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **In-depth discussion of SDS**: Section 3 provides a clear mathematical motivation linking SDS to CFG (R3).
- **Relationship to Delta Denoising Score** is noted, connecting CSD to image-editing literature (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient quantitative comparisons**:
  - Table 2 for text-guided 3D generation includes only two baselines; strong methods like Fantasia3D and ProlificDreamer are missing (R3, R4). 
  - Text-guided texture synthesis lacks a strong baseline [A] and quantitative metrics such as CLIP score or CLIP R-Precision—only a user study is reported (R3).
  - Computational cost is given only for ProlificDreamer and CSD; a breakdown for all methods is needed (R3).
  - Evaluation uses only CLIP ViT-B/32; DreamFusion originally uses three CLIP models (B/32, B/16, L/14). Reporting more models would strengthen robustness (R3).
- **Unfair comparison due to different pretrained models**: CSD uses two different text-to-image models (DeepFloyd-IF stage-I for coarse stage, Stable Diffusion 2.1 for fine stage), while baselines like ProlificDreamer use only Stable Diffusion. This design choice is not ablated, making direct comparisons questionable (R3).
- **Reproducibility concerns**:
  - One reviewer (R4) attempted reimplementation in ThreeStudio using the provided details but could not reproduce the claimed quality; results were similar to SDS, not VSD. The Janus problem was very serious in their experiments. R4 strongly requests code release to verify results. (This concern is not echoed by R1/R2.)
- **Lack of ablation studies**:
  - No hyperparameter ablation is provided (R1). 
  - No ablation on the two-stage model choice (DeepFloyd-IF vs. SD) (R3). 
  - The effect of negative prompts is not ablated; R4 asks how well CSD performs without them (Eq. 7 only). 
  - No experiments compare CSD to standard SDS with very large CFG weights (e.g., 200, 500, 1000) to show that the difference is not merely a CFG increase (R2).
- **Potential downsides of high CFG inheriting from SDS**: CSD may reduce diversity, produce less realistic images, and oversaturate colors, as is typical with high CFG weights. These issues are not discussed or evaluated (R2). 
- **Prompt selection and metrics**:
  - Selection of 81 prompts from DreamFusion’s website is not justified; R3 asks for screening criteria. R4 asks why not use all 415 prompts from the DreamFusion repo (R3, R4). 
  - R2 notes that Figure 2a uses different scales for noise-prediction and CFG-norm, which is misleading. Figure 2b should include CSD and explore higher CFG values to match DreamFusion’s w=100 (R2).
- **Missing discussion of related work**: The connection to Collaborative Score Distillation (which subtracts noise from an InstructPix2Pix model) is not discussed; R1 asks how CSD relates and whether Collaborative could be improved with CSD.
- **Missing details**: R4 asks what specific negative prompts were used in experiments. R2 asks the CFG weights used for baseline methods (DreamFusion default w=100, ProlificDreamer default w=7.5 in ThreeStudio).
