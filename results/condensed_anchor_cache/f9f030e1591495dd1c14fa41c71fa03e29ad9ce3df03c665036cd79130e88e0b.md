## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces SOLOS, a context compression method for training and deploying long-context LLMs with limited resources. It segments input, appends special tokens per segment, uses two LLMs (encoder and decoder) with a projector, and applies LoRA fine-tuning. Training employs incremental computation on the decoder side and reservoir sampling for sparse optimization, enabling training on sequences up to 100K tokens with 8× RTX3090. The approach is evaluated primarily on LLaMA2-7B and LongBench, and compared with Activation Beacon and ICAE on auto-encoding tasks.

### Strengths
- The problem of reducing computational overhead for long-context LLMs is important (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Performance in Figure 7 (auto-encoding reconstruction) is very impressive; recovering original text in natural language is rare for token-level compression methods (Reviewer 1).
- The combination of incremental computation and reservoir sampling for sparse optimization is considered novel by one reviewer (Reviewer 2). However, another reviewer notes that chunk processing with special tokens is well-explored (Reviewer 1).
- SOLOS matches or exceeds mainstream long-context LLMs while requiring significantly less computational resources (Reviewer 2).
- Efficient architecture: fixed compression rate with intra-layer encoder-decoder, shared parameters adapted via LoRA (Reviewer 4).
- Memory-efficient training through recomputation and reservoir sampling to maintain fixed memory usage (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation and baselines:**
  - Only one comparative compression method (Activation Beacon) is evaluated; other baselines are full-attention long-context LLMs. Missing comparisons with gist, ultragist, TOVA, DMC, MInference, SnapKV, and LongT5 (Reviewers 1, 3). Also lacks comparison with ICAE on tasks beyond auto-encoding, and Activation Beacon is not evaluated on auto-encoding tasks (Reviewer 4).
  - Evaluation is mostly on LLaMA2-7B and LongBench; generalizability to larger or more recent models is unclear (Reviewers 1, 2). Requested results on RULER and InfiniteBench (Reviewer 1, also Reviewer 2 asks for RULER).
  - Only full-attention is compared for memory and speed; no efficiency comparison with other context-compression methods (Reviewers 1, 4). SOLOS appears significantly slower than regular LLMs under 4K context length (Reviewer 2).
- **Conceptual and novelty concerns:**
  - The idea of chunk processing with special tokens (per-segment representations) is well-explored (activation beacon, gist, ultragist); this limits novelty. The more complicated design (two LLMs, projector) may not be worthwhile, and its advantages are not clearly demonstrated (Reviewer 1).
  - The reservoir pattern is very similar to heavy-hitter in H2O, but no discussion is provided (Reviewer 3).
  - Distilling context into special tokens has already been proposed in LongT5; no discussion or comparison (Reviewer 3).
- **Training and scaling issues:**
  - Extending to 100K with compression ratio 32 leads to increased perplexity compared to ratio 8, suggesting SOLOS may not fully leverage the 100K context (Reviewer 2).
  - The paper’s position is vague: it is presented as a training method, but the core mechanisms (prefill compression + LoRA) are not unique and are underexplored compared to dedicated long-context training works (e.g., “How to Train Long-Context Language Models”) (Reviewer 1).
- **Technical and implementation concerns:**
  - Reservoir sampling may be hard to make compatible with Flash Attention, which is necessary in long-context scenarios; the authors should explain how it is implemented in practice (Reviewer 3).
  - Many long-context tasks (e.g., multi-turn conversation) are sensitive to token eviction compression; since questions are unpredictable upfront, compression may lose important information (Reviewer 3).
- **Methodological clarity and missing details:**
  - The paper has significant delivery issues, including shallow capture of related work (e.g., mention of architectural similarity with activation beacon without details) and insufficient explanation of how reservoir sampling is adapted for LLMs and how it solves the “cache recover” problem of pure random sampling (Reviewer 1).
  - Needle test setting is not clearly stated; it is very sensitive to the prompt (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 asks: what is the background filler for the needle test? If it is repetition of the same sentence, please consider noisy background or RULER.
  - Did you truncate LongBench inputs for different baselines? (Reviewer 1)
  - Why is LoRA not applied to \(W_k\) in the encoder? (Reviewer 4)
  - The citation for Activation Beacon appears to be incorrect (Reviewer 4).
- **Disagreement among reviewers:** One reviewer finds the combination of incremental computation and reservoir sampling novel (Reviewer 2, score 6), while another sees the overall approach as incremental and lacking novelty (Reviewer 1, score 3). The positive performance in Figure 7 is acknowledged by the most critical reviewer, but the experimental coverage is deemed insufficient by all.
