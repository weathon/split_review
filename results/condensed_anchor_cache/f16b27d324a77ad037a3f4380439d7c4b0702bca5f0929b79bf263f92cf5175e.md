## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces VoiceNoNG, a speech editing model that combines VoiceBox (flow-matching) with VoiceCraft (neural codec language model) concepts. It replaces mel-spectrograms with pre‑quantization features from the Descript Audio Codec (DAC) and adds a cross‑entropy loss. The goal is to reduce the hallucinations (high WER) seen in autoregressive NCLM models while retaining high speech quality. Evaluations on RealEdit report lower WER and better quality than baselines. The authors also propose a deepfake detector that distinguishes edited, real, and synthesized speech. Reviewers disagree on novelty and rigor: one reviewer (score 5) finds the VQ‑module investigation and deepfake detector novel, while three reviewers (scores 3) judge the contributions minimal, the writing unpolished, and the evaluation flawed.

### Strengths
- Well‑written paper with a clear problem statement and strong motivation (R4).
- Compares against VoiceCraft and VoiceBox; ablation study shows the effectiveness of the proposed modifications (R1, R4).
- Replacing mel with DAC features is rational and helpful for speech editing; DAC’s information compression is beneficial (R2).
- Investigates the impact of the vector quantization module; findings may extend to other speech generation tasks (R3).
- Develops a deepfake detector for a new challenging practical scenario (real vs. edited vs. synthesized speech), which has practical value (R2, R3, R4).
- (Reviewer 3, score 5, is notably more positive: he emphasizes that the model achieves lower WER and enhanced quality, and that the VQ module investigation and deepfake detector are novel contributions.)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and overclaimed contributions** – Pre‑quantization feature prediction is a variant of Latent Diffusion and similar to NaturalSpeech 2 (R1, R2). Merely replacing mel features with DAC features does not constitute a fundamentally new idea (R4). The added cross‑entropy loss was already used in NaturalSpeech 2 (R1). Data selection (GigaSpeech) is an engineering choice, not a scientific contribution (R2). The model only merges the codec with VoiceBox, not truly combining the advantages of VoiceCraft and VoiceBox (R3). Overall minimal original idea (R4). (Reviewer 3 dissents, considering the VQ investigation and deepfake detector novel.)
- **Unsupported hallucination claim and flawed motivation** – The claim that NCLM’s higher WER is caused by attention errors lacks extensive experiments; other factors (sampling, codebook size) are not ruled out (R3 Q1). The assertion that VoiceBox’s poor performance on background audio is due to HiFi‑GAN and mel is questionable; HiFi‑GAN with mel can generate diverse sounds including music (R1). The real drawback may be training data, not the VoiceBox architecture (R2). No comparison with a HiFi‑GAN trained on noisy speech for VoiceBox is provided (R3 Q4). The hallucination argument relies on WER, which is not a strong metric because ASR models have an implicit language model that can correct mispronunciations (R4). WER differences between VoiceCraft and the proposed method are minimal except on the YouTube subset (R4).
- **Evaluation concerns** – WER evaluation may be dominated by the vocoder/codec decoder rather than the editing model, since the entire utterance is reconstructed through that decoder (R1). SI‑SDR differences across models are very small and likely not perceivable by human listeners (R4). MOS evaluation is unreliable: only 3 ratings per sample for 10 samples per model, falling short of ITU‑T P.800 recommendations (R4). Experiments on TTS are missing; the method should be validated on both TTS and speech editing (R2). It is unclear whether the reported VoiceBox and HiFi‑GAN were trained on GigaSpeech before comparison (R3 Q4). In Table 1, VoiceCraft (330M) performs best on the Spotify column, which contradicts the paper’s narrative (R3 Q3). The claim in line 199 about reproducing VoiceBox results needs clarification: was VoiceBox retrained from open‑source code, or were results taken from the VoiceBox paper? (R3 Q2)
- **Writing and presentation issues** – Structure is imbalanced: first three pages are background/related work, while the proposed method is described in one brief paragraph (R1). Rough writing, lack of a model diagram or flowchart (R2). Poor organization: drawbacks of VoiceBox and VoiceCraft are placed in the proposed method section, and the ablation description is inside the WER metric section (R2). The introduction is overly simplistic with popular‑science explanations (R3). Vague statements, e.g., “diversity makes RealEdit more challenging” without explicit comparison to other datasets (R4). Missing important references: Seed‑TTS, UniCATS, E1 TTS (R1). Overall the paper appears unpolished or unfinished (R2).
- **Deepfake detector** – It is an extension of prior work by adding a “synthesized” class; its novelty is limited (R4). The practical value is acknowledged (R2), but the contribution relative to existing detection methods is not well differentiated.
