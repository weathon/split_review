## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes D²PO (Decay-based Direct Preference Optimization), which introduces a temporal decay parameter γ to mitigate the length bias of DPO by dynamically weighting token contributions, prioritizing earlier tokens. The method is tested on AlpacaEval 2, Arena-Hard, and MT-Bench, outperforming vanilla DPO (5.9–8.8 points on AlpacaEval 2, 3.3–9.7 on Arena-Hard) and baselines SimPO and SamPO. Four reviewers scored the paper 8, 6, 5, 6 (accept overall).

### Strengths
- Clear motivation: Figure 1 shows length bias in DPO; Figure 3 demonstrates the need for temporal decay (Reviewer 1). The overall direction of token-level reward/credit assignment for RLHF is promising (Reviewer 4).
- Comprehensive, well-written paper with thorough ablations, including Figure 2 and Table 5 in the appendix, and clear comparisons with DPO, SimPO, SamPO (Reviewer 1, 4). Extensive controlled experiments across a broad spectrum of baselines (DPO, SimPO, KTO) (Reviewer 3).
- Innovative temporal decay mechanism that dynamically weights token importance and effectively addresses alignment biases (Reviewer 2). Links temporal decay concepts to value learning within RL frameworks (Reviewer 3).
- Flexible design: works in both reference-based and reference-free settings (Reviewer 2).
- Significant gains on benchmarks (Reviewer 2, 3) – e.g., 5.9–8.8 points on AlpacaEval 2 and 3.3–9.7 on Arena-Hard (from abstract, noted by all reviewers).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty / prior work omission:** The training loss (Eq. 5) is (almost) identical to Eq. (8) and Eq. (21) in prior work [1] (Reviewer 4). The idea that earlier tokens are more important for alignment has been discussed in [2] (Section 3, Appendix F.3) (Reviewer 4). The derivation in 3.3 is a straightforward extension of DPO using discounted return instead of return (Reviewer 1). Authors should provide adequate citation and discussion; otherwise contribution is unjustified (Reviewer 4). Additionally, L253–254 lack citation for maximum entropy RL (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing evaluation on reasoning / general ability benchmarks:** The results rely almost exclusively on instruction-following datasets judged by GPT-4, raising concerns about generalizability and potential evaluation bias (Reviewer 2, 3). No evaluation on OpenLLM benchmark (e.g., MMLU, GSM8K) to verify whether improved RLHF comes at the cost of general LM ability (Reviewer 4). One reviewer explicitly requests testing on IFEval or GSM8K (Reviewer 3, citing [3] on style control). Another reviewer asks whether D²PO’s performance would hold on logical reasoning or math datasets (Reviewer 2).
- **Sensitivity to γ parameter:** Performance depends heavily on γ; requiring extensive fine-tuning across tasks reduces practicality (Reviewer 2). No exploration of adaptive or task-specific γ (Reviewer 2). The paper does not discuss how γ interacts with learning rate, optimization stability, or convergence (Reviewer 1).
- **Over-emphasis on early tokens / potential degradation of later token quality:** The decay may over-emphasize early tokens, harming tasks requiring long-range dependencies or complex reasoning/planning where later tokens correct or refine earlier decisions (Reviewer 1). The assumption that earlier tokens are more important may not hold for certain tasks or datasets (Reviewer 1’s question).
- **Reliance on automated benchmarks:** Automated benchmarks are susceptible to manipulation or “hacking” (Reviewer 3, citing [1]). Human evaluation is necessary to validate the results (Reviewer 3). The gain may come from length and style control rather than alignment improvements (Reviewer 3, citing [3]).
- **Need for comparison with length-control methods:** Since length control significantly increases win rate, further comparison with works focusing on length control (e.g., [2] in Reviewer 3’s list) is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Theoretical gaps:** No formal motivation under a token MDP setting; no exploration of how decay rate impacts optimization landscape or convergence (Reviewer 1). The derivation does not justify how the partition function cancels at the token level (original DPO assumes a bandit reward), which is a fundamental mismatch (Reviewer 4, question 2).
- **Minor technical issues:** L211–212 “accuracy” is undefined (Reviewer 4). Eq. (11) should use π_θ rather than p_θ (Reviewer 4). L78–79: why is “more recent feedback potentially outdated”? (Reviewer 4).
