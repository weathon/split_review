## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes ChebyNet, a neural architecture that uses Chebyshev polynomial connections (element-wise multiplication of Chebyshev polynomials of the input with feature maps) to explicitly model relationships between non‑adjacent layers. The polynomials are defined recursively (Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind) and applied after downsampling to align dimensions. The method is evaluated on function approximation, MNIST image generation (UNet‑diffusion), dynamical systems, semantic segmentation (ACDC, BraTS19), and image classification (CIFAR‑10/100). The work is presented as a new paradigm that can be integrated into existing architectures.

### Strengths

1. **Novelty** – Applying Chebyshev polynomial connections as Hadamard products between features and polynomial transformations is a novel architectural idea. (Reviewers 1, 4, 5)
2. **Versatility and adaptability** – The method can be seamlessly integrated into multiple architectures (UNet, HNN, ResNet, MobileNet) with minimal implementation complexity. (Reviewers 2, 5)
3. **Experimental breadth** – The method is tested on a wide range of tasks: numerical function approximation, image generation, dynamical systems, segmentation, and classification. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
4. **Performance gains** – Experiments show that using high‑order Chebyshev polynomials (orders 1–9) often improves precision over plain networks and often outperforms ordinary polynomial connections (PolyNet). (Reviewer 1)  Table 6 (appendix) shows superior approximation of elementary functions (sign, tanh) compared to MLP and Poly‑MLP. (Reviewer 2)
5. **Presentation** – Some reviewers found the paper well‑crafted and clearly illustrating concepts and results. (Reviewer 3)  The promising direction for network structural design is recognized. (Reviewer 3, 4)

### Weaknesses

1. **Lack of code and reproducibility** – No anonymous code is provided, which is necessary for an experimental paper. Implementation details are insufficient: how Chebyshev connections are applied to complex architectures (UNet, ResNet, MobileNet) is not explained. No pseudocode, no optimizer details (except mentioned for one experiment), no learning rate, weight decay, data augmentation, or architecture structure. (Reviewers 1, 4, 5)  Writing issues exist (e.g., “Implimentation” as section title). (Reviewer 4)
2. **Unclear motivation for Chebyshev polynomials** – The rationale for using element‑wise multiplication (rather than additive form, weighted sum, or simpler residual/dense connections) is not clearly justified. Why Chebyshev specifically? The interpolation property (minimizing Runge phenomenon) is mentioned but not connected to architectural choices or numerical stability. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)  One reviewer questions whether Eq. 4 actually produces a polynomial sequence; if not, the benefit might come from extra learned connections rather than polynomial structure. (Reviewer 5)
3. **Insufficient baseline comparisons** – Comparisons are mostly against basic versions of popular models (plain networks, PolyNet). More sophisticated connectivity strategies (DenseNet, pyramid connections, attention‑based inter‑layer interactions) are not compared, making it unclear whether Chebyshev connections offer advantages over existing inter‑layer interaction methods. (Reviewers 2, 5)
4. **Scalability to larger datasets** – Evaluation is limited to small‑scale datasets (MNIST, CIFAR, small segmentation datasets). The paper should demonstrate scalability on larger benchmarks like ImageNet for classification, and for generative modeling, at least CIFAR‑10 and CelebA. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
5. **Efficiency claims not validated** – The paper claims improved efficiency (fewer parameters, reduced computational overhead), but no experiments on training time, inference time, or memory usage are provided. Additional polynomial connections likely increase memory and I/O overhead (similar to DenseNet trade‑offs). (Reviewers 2, 3, 5)
6. **Lack of significance/error bars** – Performance improvements are often small, and no error bars or statistical significance tests are reported. It is unclear if gains are robust to randomness. (Reviewer 1)
7. **Parameter count fairness** – No discussion on whether parameter counts were kept constant across comparisons. Without this, it is unclear if improvements are due to ChebyNet or extra parameters. (Reviewer 4)
8. **Clarity of figures** – Figure 1 is unclear: it suggests direct input‑output polynomial connections, while the text indicates they are applied within layers. (Reviewer 2)  Figure 2 plots “Loss vs Order” for numerical function approximation, but the meaning of “Order” for an MLP is not explained; also the MLP failing to fit a quadratic function is not discussed. (Reviewer 4)
9. **Generative modeling results weak** – FID on MNIST is high for both Cheby‑UNet and baseline UNet. Qualitative samples appear worse than simple public UNet‑based diffusion implementations. The paper should report log‑likelihood (a more direct measure of function fitting) and use more challenging datasets (e.g., CIFAR‑10). (Reviewer 4)
10. **Order selection principle** – Figure 5 shows negative correlations for low orders, but high‑order polynomials also achieve good performance (Table 5). There is no principled guidance for choosing polynomial order. (Reviewer 1)  In Table 5, some values higher than baseline are missing bold highlighting. (Reviewer 1)
11. **Robustness and overfitting** – Modeling relationships between layers increases model capacity and may lead to overfitting; robustness is not discussed. (Reviewer 3)
12. **Disagreement on presentation quality** – One reviewer found the paper “well‑crafted” (Reviewer 3), while others found the methodology “far from clear” and lacking crucial implementation details (Reviewers 4, 5). This contrast highlights the need for clearer exposition.

*Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms are integrated above: “Why not additive?” (Reviewer 1), “How are connections integrated into UNet/ResNet?” (Reviewer 5), “What justifies calling it polynomial‑based?” (Reviewer 5), “Why Chebyshev over simpler connections?” (Reviewer 2), “What is the role of Order in MLP?” (Reviewer 4), “What is the impact on memory/time?” (Reviewers 2,3,5), “Could you provide more detailed ablation on where/how polynomials are applied?” (Reviewer 4).*
