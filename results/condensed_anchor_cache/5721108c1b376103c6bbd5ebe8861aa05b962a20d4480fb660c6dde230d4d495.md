## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes training agents with video instructions (instead of text) and a dual-pathway attention mechanism (semantic + visual) on the GROOT architecture, using online RL (PPO) in Minecraft. Scores: 3, 3, 3, 5, 3 (avg. 3.4). Decision: Reject. Reviewer 4 (score 5) is notably more positive; all other reviewers rate it 3 or below.

### Strengths
- **Importance of zero-shot generalization in online RL**: Addresses a critical challenge—generalization to unseen tasks without task-specific training (Reviewers 2, 5). This goal is important and under-explored for RL-trained agents (Reviewer 5).
- **Dual-pathway architecture (semantic + visual)**: Introduces a novel attention layer to give the action decoder direct access to low-level visual details, mitigating encoder-decoder overfitting to a “task vocabulary” in the latent space during online finetuning (Reviewers 1, 5). This is a sensible architectural modification (Reviewer 5).
- **Strong quantitative results**: Outperforms baselines on three Minecraft task categories (success rates: 69.1%, 16.0%, 67.7%) (Reviewer 2). Evaluation uses both success and accuracy metrics, providing a solid and fine-grained setup (Reviewer 4).
- **Reasonable idea**: Using video instructions is a reasonable approach since giving examples of desired behavior can be easier than specifying a reward function (Reviewer 3). Working on Minecraft is appreciated as a challenging environment (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear presentation and missing methodological details**: The paper is very confusing, with many critical details omitted (Reviewers 3, 5). Specifically:
    - The reward function used during RL training is never defined (Reviewers 3, 5). Is it based on similarity between observation embeddings and video instruction embeddings, analogous to MineCLIP? (Reviewer 3)
    - No pseudocode or detailed diagram of the proposed algorithm (Reviewer 3). Figure 2 appears to show a baseline, not the proposed model (Reviewer 3).
    - The demonstration data used is not described: how many trajectories/frames? Are textual instructions also available for baselines? (Reviewer 3)
    - The goal-specific reward function $r^g_t$ in Section 2.1 is not defined (Reviewer 3).
    - Section 3.2 is redundant, merely stating standard PPO updates (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing error bars**: No error bars or variance reported for any plots or tables; given the high variance of online RL algorithms, these are required to assess the validity of results (Reviewers 3, 5). How many runs were performed? (Reviewer 5)
- **Ablation study clarity issues**:
    - The settings for “with Attention” vs. “No Attention” in the intention-aware attention ablation are unclear; “No Attention” might be equivalent to the base GROOT model (Reviewer 2).
    - The ablation on the base policy compares the dual-pathway architecture to STEVE1 and STEVEv, which is not a fair comparison since multiple factors differ (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing controlled experiments**:
    - No ablation study on the KL term “between the current policy and the original policy” to validate its effectiveness (Reviewer 2).
    - The effect of online RL vs. imitation learning for the proposed architecture is not investigated (Reviewer 4).
- **Premise and real-world relevance questioned**: Providing a video prompt gives a step-by-step demonstration of actions, which fundamentally limits relevance to open-endedness—agents are not discovering goals autonomously (Reviewer 1). The value of text instructions is their ease of user input; this convenience cannot be ignored (Reviewer 5). Authors must argue how video prompts could be obtained without human intervention, e.g., via multi-agent observation (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited contribution compared to GROOT**: Video instruction already explored in GROOT; the problem of “latent z encoding actions” is specific to GROOT architecture, limiting applicability (Reviewer 4). Experiments focus on short-horizon tasks, while GROOT showed results on long-horizon tasks (e.g., obtain diamond) (Reviewer 4).
- **Feasibility overhead**: At inference, instruction videos must be provided for every task (possibly different per entity), introducing significant overhead compared to simple text instructions (Reviewer 4). How are unseen task instruction videos defined? (Reviewer 4)
- **Poor presentation and many editorial mistakes**:
    - Grammar errors, typos (e.g., “Generalizaiton” in Table 1, “Preliminary” instead of “Preliminaries”), duplicate text (lines 351–353 identical to 358–360), placeholder captions (Figure 3 caption says “Draft”) (Reviewers 3, 4, 5).
    - Figure numbering misaligned with text (e.g., Figure 4.3 → Figure 5, Figure 7 → Figure 6) (Reviewer 4).
    - Inconsistent citation formatting (e.g., no space before in-line citations) (Reviewer 3, 5).
    - Overall writing quality significantly hinders readability (Reviewers 1, 4).
