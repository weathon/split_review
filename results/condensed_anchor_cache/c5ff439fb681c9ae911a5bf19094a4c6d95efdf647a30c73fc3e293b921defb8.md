## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Light-DDG, a lightweight structure-aware Transformer for predicting binding free energy changes (ΔΔG) upon mutation in protein complexes, with a focus on antibody optimization. It achieves 89.7× inference acceleration and 15.45% performance gains over prior state-of-the-art (Prompt-DDG) via two techniques: (1) knowledge distillation from Prompt-DDG, and (2) supervised pre-training on an augmented dataset (SKEMPI-Aug, ~640k samples) derived from SKEMPI v2.0. The authors also introduce a Mutation Explainer using iterative Shapley value estimation to identify key mutation sites, and a Uni-Anti framework for fast antibody optimization without generative models, demonstrated on a SARS-CoV-2 case study. Reviews are mixed: two reviewers rated 8, one rated 6, one rated 5.

### Strengths
- Generated and released an augmented SKEMPI-v2 dataset (SKEMPI-Aug) using predictions from a prior SOTA model for supervised pre-training (Reviewer 1, 4).
- Achieves 89.7× inference acceleration over Prompt-DDG while maintaining or improving predictive accuracy, demonstrating effective knowledge distillation and data augmentation (Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Simplified architecture enables interpretability via explainability results (Figure 6, Reviewer 2).
- Demonstrates utility of an efficient ΔΔG predictor for antibody optimization and screening without requiring generative modeling (Reviewer 1, 4).
- Comprehensive benchmark on ΔΔG prediction with extensive ablations on teacher model, data augmentation, knowledge distillation, and structural information (Reviewer 1).
- Results beat current SOTA by a significant margin; experiments are comprehensive and convincing with real-world case studies (Reviewer 3).
- Introduces an iterative Shapley value estimation algorithm (coarse-to-fine) for identifying key mutation sites (Reviewer 4).
- Uni-Anti framework enables rapid antibody optimization via fast screening, offering a practical solution for antibody design (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation on antibody design/optimization tasks**: Only a single SARS-CoV-2 case study is presented; more comprehensive assessment needed (Reviewer 1).
- **No discussion of combining with generative modeling**: The paper does not explore how the prior-based design could be integrated with generative approaches to improve antibody optimization (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient explanation of low correlation values**: The range of correlation values in benchmarks is not well explained; it is unclear why the bar is low and why predicting ΔΔG is so impactful for antibody effectiveness (Reviewer 1, 3).
- **Dataset and baseline comparison issues**: Baselines in Table 2 use different pre-training datasets (e.g., PDB-REDO, AFDB, SKEMPI v2.0), making performance comparisons uninterpretable. There is no evidence that all baselines were trained on equivalent data (Reviewer 2).
- **Data leakage concerns**: Prompt-DDG, used as teacher and for augmentation, was itself trained on SKEMPI v2.0, creating potential circular dependency. The “cross-augmentation” scheme is insufficiently explained; even with different folds, the same architecture and training objective introduce similar biases, potentially leaking test set information into training (Reviewer 2).
- **Inadequate baseline comparisons for mutation search**: The mutation search only screens 10,000 random candidates; it should be compared against established optimization algorithms (CMA-ES, gradient-guided Gibbs sampling, evolutionary algorithms). The strong performance of random search in Table 6 suggests these methods could be effective and need comparison (Reviewer 2).
- **Overstatement of “unified framework”**: What is demonstrated is an application of knowledge distillation and data augmentation; the integration of Shapley values and mutation search is a standard combination, not a novel framework (Reviewer 2).
- **E(3) invariance of output not guaranteed**: While input features are E(3)-invariant, the paper does not discuss whether the Transformer output maintains E(3) invariance, which is crucial for physical consistency of predicted binding energy changes (Reviewer 4).
- **Iterative Shapley value estimation lacks mathematical guarantees**: No convergence properties or error bounds are provided for the iterative approximation of Shapley values. The heuristic nature raises concerns about robustness and generalizability in complex mutation landscapes (Reviewer 4).
- **Potential inaccuracies in pseudo-labels**: Prompt-DDG may produce erroneous pseudo-labels for augmentation; no methods are considered to address this (Reviewer 4).
- **Potential overlap with original SKEMPI v2.0**: Augmented samples may be highly similar to SKEMPI v2.0; no filtering step is mentioned to prevent this (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing comparisons**: Transfer from single to multiple site mutations in ΔΔG prediction is not examined (Reviewer 1). Comparison with dWJS (Discrete Walk-Jump Sampling) for antibody optimization is not provided (Reviewer 4).
- **Why Light-DDG significantly outperforms its teacher (Prompt-DDG)**: The paper does not clearly explain the components that cause such large improvement (15.45% gains despite being trained from Prompt-DDG) (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor issues**: Title uses ΔΔG without definition, hindering readability (Reviewer 3). Potential reference error to SAbDab dataset on p9 needs verification (Reviewer 4).
