## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies whether editing model weights can delete sensitive information from LLMs. It proposes a threat model where extraction succeeds if the answer is among B candidates, introduces white-box attacks (using hidden states) and a black-box rephrasing attack, and evaluates defenses on ROME/MEMIT with GPT-J. Results show edited models still leak information (38% white-box, 29% black-box), and no defense universally succeeds.

### Strengths
- Important and challenging problem: deleting sensitive information directly from model weights, highly relevant to real-world deployment. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Novel framing of model editing for data removal, unique in literature. (R1)
- Broad scope: presents multiple attacks and defenses with thorough evaluation on Counterfact and zsRE datasets. (R1, R4)
- Well-motivated threat model (B candidates) recognizing that B=1 is insufficient. (R3)
- Technical contributions (attacks and defenses) are insightful, well motivated, and clearly described. (R3)
- Code and reproducibility provided. (R4)
- Elaborates on security issues from hidden states leaking sensitive information. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of experiments and comparisons**: Missing combinations (attacks under a budget, combined defenses) to draw actionable conclusions. (R1) No comparison to prior approximate unlearning approaches despite the ASR metric matching recovery of a single token label given a specific prompt. (R1)
- **Insufficient analysis of results**: Findings are somewhat expected because editing techniques are heuristics without formal guarantees; a defense designed for one attack naturally fails against others. (R3) [Minority: R3 rates contribution 2, lower than other reviewers (3), finding the paper less novel.] Technical differences between ROME and MEMIT are not explained to clarify result differences. (R3) Results for attacking MEMIT without defenses (equivalent of Figure 4) are missing, weakening the claim that editing fails. (R3) No analysis of why data-augmentation defenses fail: interaction between defense paraphrasing and attack paraphrasing is unexplored; need semantic similarity analysis and discussion of adversarial paraphrases bypassing the defense. (R2)
- **Clarity issues**: Unclear how defense methods (e.g., Head Projection) are combined with ROME/MEMIT – optimization objectives need clearer formulation. (R4) Head Projection loss function undefined: what are D_answer (distribution?) and D_k (single value?). (R4) Distinction between password attempts and parallel extraction scenarios is confusing – attacks generate candidates in parallel, not sequentially using prior information. (R1) The rephrasing attack uses the ground-truth prompt; can it work without it? (R1) Intuition for why hidden states leak sensitive information is not discussed. (R2) Only single-token answers studied; multi-token extraction not addressed. (R4) Term "sensitive" includes toxic info, risking confusion with privacy-specific definitions. (R3) Section 4.2 needs bold subheadings for attack names. (R4)
- **Questions implying missing ablations/experiments**: Can fine-tuning be combined with Head Projection defense for stronger protection? (R2) Is model editing with formal guarantees technically possible; what are promising future directions? (R3)
