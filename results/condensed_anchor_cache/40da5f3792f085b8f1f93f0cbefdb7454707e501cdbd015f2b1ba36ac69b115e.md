## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes conditional entropy reduction (RE-based iterations) to improve the denoising steps of diffusion models, deriving gradient estimation-based iterations that reduce conditional entropy in score-integral estimation. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet show FID improvements over DDIM and DPM-Solver. Reviewers find the concept interesting but raise serious concerns about theoretical justification, presentation clarity, empirical scope, and practical applicability. One reviewer (score 3) strongly disputes the theoretical foundations; other reviewers (scores 5,6) are more positive but identify important missing experiments and weak connections between theory and practice.

### Strengths
- The concept of using conditional entropy reduction as a framework to enhance denoising efficiency is novel and interesting (Reviewer 1, 3, 4).
- The paper provides a rigorous theoretical derivation of RE-based iterations and demonstrates FID improvements on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet compared to DDIM and DPM-Solver across single and multi-step scenarios (Reviewer 1, 3, 4).
- The proposed iteration scheme is empirically useful, showing improved performance over existing ODE-based samplers (Reviewer 2, 3).
- The paper is generally well-written and clear, with systematic introduction of key concepts (Reviewer 1, 4). Note: Reviewer 2 and 3 strongly disagree on presentation quality (see weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
**Theoretical justification and assumptions**
- The assumption that \(x_t\) conditioned on \(x_{t+1}\) is Gaussian (Eq 3.9) is unjustified. Only when also conditioned on \(x_0\) is the distribution Gaussian. This invalidates the entropy derivation using the formula for Gaussian random variables (Reviewer 2).
- The claim that estimated noise at different time steps is mutually independent is hand-waving; correlation likely exists due to the sequential nature of denoising solvers (Reviewer 2).
- The motivation for reducing conditional entropy as an objective for improving sample quality is not rigorously justified. It is unclear why minimizing this specific quantity directly translates to perceptual quality; other objectives may be more relevant (Reviewer 1, 3). Uncertainty reduction may be a natural consequence of denoising rather than a cause (Reviewer 3).
- The analysis of the reversed flow via properties of the continuous ODE is not rigorous for the discrete discretized process (Reviewer 2).
- Many formal statements are not clear or explicit; claims are not well justified (Reviewer 2). One reviewer (Reviewer 2) rates soundness as 1 and strongly disputes the theoretical foundations.

**Presentation and clarity**
- The writing is poor; key concepts (e.g., conditional entropy) are not defined or introduced abruptly (Reviewer 2, 3).
- The paper contains too much dense theoretical derivation and proof, hindering readability. It is suggested to move proofs to the appendix and highlight main findings (Reviewer 3, 4). The mathematical proofs, especially for Propositions 3.1 and 3.2, are dense and may be inaccessible to readers without advanced calculus and information theory backgrounds (Reviewer 4).
- The connection between the theoretical framework and practical implementation is unclear. No step-by-step explanation, pseudocode, or high-level integration steps are provided (Reviewer 3, 4).
- The experiments section and conclusion lack sufficient discussion (Reviewer 2).

**Empirical validation**
- The method shows significant FID degradation when NFE reduces from 20 to 5, raising doubts about its effectiveness for few-step sampling. Comparison with distillation methods (e.g., Consistency Models, LCM) is missing (Reviewer 1).
- Only image data (CIFAR-10, ImageNet) are tested. Validation on other generative tasks (text-to-image, audio, video) is lacking (Reviewer 4). The method's adaptability to different diffusion model architectures is unclear (Reviewer 4).
- Visual results are embarrassedly absent. The few visual comparisons provided indicate that the proposed method may destroy the deterministic nature of ODE samplers (e.g., differing structures from DDIM) (Reviewer 3).
- The impact of different classifier-free guidance (CFG) scales on performance is not explored, especially high CFG values where instability may occur (Reviewer 1).
- Computational overhead (memory, runtime, scalability) introduced by RE-based iterations is not discussed (Reviewer 4).
- Beyond FID, other metrics (perceptual quality, content accuracy) are not evaluated (Reviewer 4).
- No baseline or threshold levels for conditional entropy are established for typical datasets (Reviewer 4).
- The method's applicability to image editing via inversion is not tested (Reviewer 1).
- The absolute values of conditional entropy and their correlation with output quality are not explained; the direct influence of entropy reduction on denoising quality at each step is not detailed (Reviewer 4).
