## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper investigates consumer neuroscience by using EEG signals from 16 participants (aged 20-29) to classify preferences for functional food products. A model combining Principal Component Analysis (PCA) with a Deep Convolutional Neural Network (DCNN) is applied to EEG data, with low beta and low gamma frequency bands, as well as attention and meditation levels, identified as key decision-making factors. Two reviewers (1 and 2, score 1, confidence 5) find the manuscript severely flawed and incomprehensible, recommending outright rejection. Two other reviewers (3 and 4, scores 3, confidences 2 and 4) see some potential but highlight significant missing analyses and validation. Overall, the submission lacks methodological rigor, clear writing, and comparative evaluation.

### Strengths

- **Interesting and promising application** (Reviewers 2, 3): Using EEG for consumer preference assessment in functional foods aligns with current interests in personalized nutrition and neuromarketing.
- **Clear logic and well-structured framework** (Reviewer 4): The model integration of PCA and DCNN is presented with a clear logical flow.
- **Practical implications** (Reviewer 3): Findings could be useful for direct marketing and product development, especially for healthier food products.
- **Strong performance metrics** (Reviewer 4): The application results demonstrate the model’s predictive capability.
- **Methodological choice** (Reviewer 3): Combining PCA with a DCNN for decision-making analysis on beta and gamma bands is considered reasonable.

*Note: Reviewers 1 and 2 found no strengths; Reviewer 1 explicitly stated the submission does not meet academic standards. This contrast indicates significant disagreement among reviewers regarding even basic merit.*

### Weaknesses

- **Incomprehensible writing and lack of clarity** (Reviewers 1, 2): The manuscript is very difficult to follow, even for a practitioner in EEG and BCI. The introduction is a single paragraph lacking motivation and structure; sections 2.2-2.3.4 read like a general EEG tutorial. Conceptual linkage between topics is poor.
- **Fundamental misunderstanding of EEG** (Reviewer 1): Analysis of Figure 2 shows confusion between samples and seconds and misinterpretation of EEG amplitude values, indicating lack of basic EEG knowledge.
- **Conflation of EEG and BCI terminology** (Reviewer 1): The methodology section mixes terms incorrectly, further indicating limited expertise.
- **Naive and simplistic methodology** (Reviewers 1, 4): The approach relies on a naive application of PCA, with no more advanced machine learning methods tested. The overall framework (EEG acquisition + DCNN) is described as simple and lacking innovation.
- **No comparative analysis** (Reviewers 3, 4): The paper does not compare the proposed method to existing neuromarketing models or traditional machine learning techniques. It is unclear whether the approach outperforms simpler alternatives.
- **Small sample size and limited generalizability** (Reviewer 3): Only 16 participants tested; findings may not generalize to larger or more diverse populations.
- **Lack of reproduction details** (Reviewers 1, 3, 4): Key hyperparameters for PCA and the DCNN architecture are not provided. No data augmentation strategies are mentioned. An entire hyperparameter table is needed for reproducibility.
- **Insufficient consideration of consumer preferences** (Reviewer 2): Only sensory/taste aspects are considered; important factors like texture, aroma, visual appeal, and emotional response are ignored, limiting industry relevance.
- **Overemphasis on beta and gamma bands** (Reviewer 3): Other EEG frequency bands (e.g., alpha, theta) may be relevant but were not examined or justified as irrelevant.
- **No related work section** (Reviewer 2): The manuscript lacks a review of existing studies on the topic, undermining scholarly depth.
- **Missing validation and methodological gaps** (Reviewer 4): Figures 7-9 show confusion matrices and a single ROC curve but contain no context on how data was split into training, validation, and testing phases; captions refer to “efficiency” without explanation. The paper does not provide sufficient validation of the results.
- **Poor table presentation** (Reviewer 4): Tables appear as distorted screenshots, affecting readability.
- **No proofreading** (Reviewer 1): The manuscript was not reviewed by colleagues to improve comprehensibility for outsiders.
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms** (all reviewers):
  - Why only naive PCA, and why not evaluate more advanced machine learning methods? (Reviewer 1)
  - What are the DCNN hyperparameters and data augmentation strategies? (Reviewer 3)
  - Why restrict analysis to beta and gamma bands? Were alpha/theta considered and found irrelevant? (Reviewer 3)
  - How does the proposed framework compare to existing neuromarketing models in accuracy and interpretability? (Reviewer 4)
  - Was the model validated on larger or different datasets? (Reviewer 3)
  - What are the specific innovations distinguishing this work? (Reviewer 4)
