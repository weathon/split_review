## Merged Review

### Summary
QuickDrop proposes integrating dataset distillation (DD) into federated learning (FL) to enable efficient federated unlearning (FU). Each client generates a compact synthetic dataset during FL training by reusing gradient updates. Unlearning is performed via stochastic gradient ascent on these distilled datasets, drastically reducing computation. Evaluations on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and SVHN with five baselines report unlearning speedups of 463× over full retraining and 65–218× over prior FU methods. The approach supports class-level, client-level, and multiple unlearning requests, as well as relearning.

### Strengths
- The approach significantly reduces unlearning time (463× vs. retraining, 65–218× vs. FU baselines) by leveraging dataset distillation and gradient ascent (R1, R2, R4).
- Dataset distillation is integrated into the regular FL training loop by reusing gradients, incurring minimal extra overhead (R2, R4).
- Scalability is demonstrated with 100 clients (R1).
- The framework is general and applicable to sample-wise, class-wise, and client-wise unlearning (R3).
- The paper is clearly written and well organized (R3).
- The idea is intuitive and effective (R4).
- Experiments are comprehensive (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental contribution**: The core technical contributions are limited. Gradient ascent + fine-tuning for unlearning has been introduced in prior work [1,2]. Dataset distillation is a direct application of the existing algorithm by Zhao et al. (2021), and its integration with FL is a standard adaptation to the FedAvg setting (Sec. 3.2 largely reviews Zhao et al., Sec. 3.3 is short). The approach resembles using representative data selection [3] and does not introduce a novel algorithmic framework for FU (R1, R3, R4).
- **Accuracy trade-off**: Sacrificing accuracy for efficiency may not be acceptable in practice. Figure 4 shows that fine-tuning on the original dataset is needed to recover accuracy, which may require all clients to collaborate—a potentially costly step (R1, R4). The method’s accuracy is lower than that of more expensive baselines (R4).
- **Missing baselines**: Only one FU baseline (PU-MP, which is CNN-specific) is compared. The paper should include representative methods from different categories: efficient retraining [4,5], influence functions [6], gradient ascent + regularization [7,8] ([8] is compared but others not), rollback gradient + knowledge distillation [1], and scaling [9]. The current comparison is insufficient to demonstrate superiority (R3).
- **Missing experiments**: Sample-wise unlearning is not evaluated, only class-wise and client-wise. Sample-wise would better show granularity and broader applicability (R3). The effect of non-IID data on QuickDrop is not discussed (R2).
- **Missing analysis**: Communication overhead of exchanging distilled datasets is not addressed, raising doubts about practical usefulness (R2). The impact of distillation hyperparameters is unclear (R2).
- **Presentation**: Too much space is spent explaining gradient-matching dataset distillation, which is not a novel contribution (R1). The method is simple and lacks theoretical justification (R4).
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 6) was more positive, noting the approach is effective and experiments comprehensive, but still considered it incremental. The other three reviewers (score 3) were more critical, emphasizing insufficient novelty, missing baselines, and the accuracy–efficiency trade-off.
