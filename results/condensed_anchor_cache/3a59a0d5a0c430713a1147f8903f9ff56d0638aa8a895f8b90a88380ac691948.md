## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PCLaSt, a state representation learning method that encodes reachability so that nearby and reachable states cluster together in latent space. It first learns a latent representation via multi-step inverse dynamics (to remove exogenous noise), then transforms it using time-contrastive learning (justified via Brownian motion) to impose a metric structure. This representation enables hierarchical planning: k-means clustering, graph of cluster connectivity, and Dijkstra’s algorithm. The system is tested on 2D maze navigation, 3D Sawyer reaching, and MuJoCo environments with exogenous noise. Experiments cover online/offline RL and reward-free settings, showing improved performance over ACRO and other baselines. The paper is well-written and the idea is intuitive.

### Strengths
- Clever combination of multiple latent spaces and losses to support hierarchical planning; tackles the important problem of learning representations that are both minimal and have a metric structure. (R2, R4)
- Idea of embedding reachability into state representations via contrastive learning is intuitive and straightforward. (R1)
- Use of Brownian motion to generate training samples for reachability constraints is interesting. (R3)
- Experiments span diverse domains (2D/3D navigation, MuJoCo) and multiple learning scenarios (online/offline RL, planning), demonstrating flexibility. (R2, R3)
- Visualizations (Figure 1, Figure 4) clearly show improved conformance between latent space and true dynamics. (R1, R3)
- The imposed metric structure enables hierarchical planning based on L2 distances, which is shown to improve performance. (R4)
- The method works both for deep RL and traditional path planning (Table 1). (R3)
- Only method (to the reviewers’ knowledge) that combines inverse dynamics with metric learning for this purpose. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons and ablations:**  
  *Only ACRO is compared in Maze/Sawyer experiments; HOMER and DRIML are excluded from goal-conditioned RL tasks (only seen in exogenous noise experiments). (R3)*  
  *Need comparisons to the basic building blocks (IDM, CL, FDM alone) to justify the added complexity. (R4)*  
  *Also missing comparison of the proposed loss function to earlier time-contrastive losses (Wang et al. 2015, Nair et al. 2022) – the paper claims novelty but the contrastive loss is very similar to prior work. (R2)*  
  *A detailed ablation of the five components (FDM, IDM, contrastive learner, hierarchical planner, MPC) is needed to show each is necessary. (R4)*  
  *Using hierarchical planning with other latent representations would clarify its benefit. (R3)*

- **Scalability and generalizability concerns:**  
  *It is unclear whether the method scales to high-dimensional C-spaces (e.g., robotic manipulation with many objects); the paper should discuss what problem families it can handle. (R1)*  
  *Experiments rely on exploratory data; scaling to environments with narrow passages is questionable. (R1)*  
  *Performance on the Sawyer-Reach task does not clearly demonstrate advantage; more challenging environments are needed. (R1, R4)*  
  *The main benefit (planning) is only shown in 2D mazes; demonstrating it on more practical environments (e.g., goal-conditioned MuJoCo) would strengthen claims. (R4)*

- **Assumptions and theoretical issues:**  
  *The planner assumes all latent states in the same cluster are pairwise reachable, which may fail due to approximation errors; this issue is not discussed. (R1)*  
  *Reachability is not necessarily symmetric (e.g., systems with momentum), but the contrastive loss minimizes symmetric L2 distance; no theoretical or intuitive justification is given for why this mismatch would not hinder planning. (R3)*  
  *The noise‑filtering effects are mostly due to ACRO, so why does PCLaSt outperform ACRO so much in Figure 6? This is not explained. (R3)*  
  *Proposition 1 contains a typo (P_k(z′|z′, y=1) should likely be P_k(z′|z, y=1)). (R2)*

- **Method justification and clarity:**  
  *The overall system is highly complicated with five moving parts; it is unclear why the encoders must be trained sequentially (ϕ first, then frozen for ψ) rather than jointly with a single multi‑objective loss. (R2, R4)*  
  *Interplay between the hierarchical planner and the low-level MPC planner is not fully justified, especially in reward‑free settings. (R4)*  
  *Symbols x, z, s are used for different abstraction levels without clear separation; Figure 2 is overwhelming with many symbols. (R1)*  
  *Section 3.3 omits the description of how s_t and s_{t+k} are generated based on y for the contrastive loss. (R3)*  
  *The paper lacks focus on the key contribution (loss function vs. system as a whole), making the flow less coherent. (R2, minor)*  
  *ACRO’s performance on MuJoCo with exogenous noise appears worse than originally reported (Islam et al., 2023); this gap needs explanation, and a more comprehensive evaluation (e.g., 12 datasets × n distractor configurations) is required. (R4)*  
  *Minor: ICLR 2023 format used; typo in Appendix D (small ∥). (R4)*

- **Reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (R3, score 3) was significantly more critical, questioning the novelty relative to prior contrastive losses, the role of ACRO vs. PCLaSt, and missing comparisons. The other three reviewers (scores 5) were more positive overall, though still noted many of the same weaknesses.
