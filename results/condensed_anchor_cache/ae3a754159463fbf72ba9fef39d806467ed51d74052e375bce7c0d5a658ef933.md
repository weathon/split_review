## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes the Binary Alignment Network (BiAN) to address domain adaptation (DA) in object counting. Unlike traditional DA methods that treat density changes as task-irrelevant and align domain-invariant features, BiAN segments objects of interest and aligns object-specific feature distributions across domains while preserving density information. It introduces conditional alignment and a Condition-Consistent Mechanism (CM). The paper provides a theoretical analysis of the lower bound on joint decision error and conducts experiments on multiple counting datasets. (Reviewers note the idea is novel for counting, but presentation and experimental validity are questioned.)

### Strengths
- Novel focus on task-relevant density variations in cross-domain counting, shifting from the typical domain-invariant alignment (Reviewers 1,2,3,5). This addresses a gap in existing DA methods for counting tasks (R1,R3,R5).
- Introduction of conditional alignment and conditional consistency mechanisms that explicitly handle object density changes (R1,R3).
- Strong theoretical analysis demonstrating that BiAN achieves a lower bound on joint decision error, providing rigorous support for the method (R1,R5). This conditional alignment is shown to enhance domain adaptability (R5).
- Extensive experiments across multiple datasets and two counting tasks, with significant performance improvements over prior methods (R1,R3,R4,R5). The method outperforms comparison methods on several benchmarks (R4,R5). (However, Reviewer 4 questions the validity of these results – see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear segmentation implementation:** It is not explained whether segmentation uses a pre-trained model or is trained within BiAN, what architecture and loss function are used, and how segmentation accuracy affects counting performance (R1,R4). The method relies on a regressor F for segmenting object/background; the specific model and training details are missing (R4).
- **Confusing symbols and notation:** The description in Sec. 3.2 and 3.3 is hard to follow; symbols are not properly defined when first introduced (R1,R2,R5). Examples: the same symbol F is used for regressor, feature extractor, and features (R5); the distinction between g and F in lines 199–200 is unclear (R5); the meaning of G in Eq. (1) and its implementation is not explained (R2); the subscript correctness of \(z_{\text{inv}}\) and \(z_{\text{var}}\) in line 181 is uncertain (R2); the relationship between \(x_i\) and conditional subsets \(x_{ij}^c\) (including the role of j) is confusing (R2); the symbols \(\hat{c}_s, \hat{c}_s^f, \hat{c}_s^b\) in Eq. (6) are not defined (R2); the condition set \(\mathcal{C}\) is not described (R2).
- **Missing training pipeline:** No overall description of the training process is given; loss functions are not explained clearly (R2). Specifically, it is unclear why the counting loss is divided by the alignment loss using reversed NLL in Eqs. (6)–(7), and why the last two denominator items in Eq. (6) are zero while they are \(y_t^d\) in Eq. (7) (R5).
- **Unreliable experimental results:**
  - In Table 1, the wrong best values are bolded – for SD→SR MAE the proposed method is **not** the best but is marked bold, and for SN→FH MSE the best value is incorrect (R1,R3,R4). The proposed method performs worse than others by a large margin on SN→FH (R4).
  - In Table 1 (SD→SR), the MSE (21.6) is significantly smaller than the MAE (34.6), which is inconsistent with all other experiments; this raises concerns about validity (R4). (R4 asks why this occurs.)
  - The DA results (e.g., BiAN MAE 42.3 on Shanghai Tech→UCF-QNRF) are **much better** than fully supervised state-of-the-art methods (STEERER: 54.5, PET: 49.34) – this does not make sense for a DA method and suggests potential data leakage or unfair comparisons (R2). Implementation details are needed to resolve this (R2). Reviewer 4 also notes the overall reliability of results is questionable.
- **Lack of novelty:** Reviewer 4 argues the method merely splits images into background and foreground before separate processing, which is a common technique. Reviewer 5 also notes that object-aware conditional alignment and segmentation using labels y_i have been used in works [1],[2],[3] (e.g., Decoupled adaptation for cross-domain object detection, Undoing the Damage of Label Shift). Thus innovation is limited (R4,R5).
- **Missing analyses and ablations:**
  - No analysis relating the gain from BiAN to the extent of density change between source and target domains. How should the density change be measured? (R1,R3)
  - The ablation study is insufficient; it should compare conditional alignment versus style alignment (as sketched in Fig. 1) to demonstrate the necessity of conditional alignment (R5).
  - No results are reported for adaptation from Shanghai Tech Part A/B to UCF-QNRF (R2).
- **Presentation issues:**
  - Missing symbols and legends in figures (e.g., Fig. 1 does not label purple/green features as source/target domains) (R1,R3). This hinders understanding.
  - Section 2.2 should state the relationship/difference between the proposed method and prior works to contextualize the novelty (R1,R3).
  - Duplicate phrases in L372–373 (“As to the evaluation metrics…” and “Following the previous research works”) (R1,R3).
  - The paper uses a smaller margin to fit within the page limit, which could lead to desk rejection (R5).
