- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 6, 6, 5, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary

Reviewers generally praised the paper’s introduction of a novel, biologically important problem—conditioning protein generative models on dynamical properties—and its clear exposition. The proposed combination of classifier guidance with a differentiable NMA loss was seen as sensible and the initial results as promising. However, there was substantial concern that the empirical validation is too weak to convincingly support the claims. Two reviewers (R1, R2) rated the paper 6, while two (R3, R4) rated 5; the more critical reviewers highlighted limited evaluation, reliance on qualitative inspection, and a lack of rigorous independent validation. Concerns were also raised about methodological novelty (guidance framework is standard; NMA implementation is a straightforward port) and about clarity in describing what the model actually generates and how key parameters were chosen.

### Strengths

- **Novel problem formulation.** The paper introduces a previously unaddressed task—conditioning protein backbone generation on desired flexibility or dynamical properties (e.g., the lowest normal mode). This is biologically important and may open new directions in protein design. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Clear and well-written presentation.** The background on diffusion models and normal mode analysis is thorough, and the method is described in a way that several reviewers found easy to follow and reproducible given the details. (R1, R4)
- **Sensible loss function.** Transferring NMA to a differentiable, invariant loss for guidance is an interesting extension of existing invariant losses and is well motivated. (R2, R3)
- **Promising initial results.** The conditional samples achieve lower NMA loss values than unconditional ones, and self-consistency metrics (scTM) suggest the generated backbones are designable. The basic premise of the approach is demonstrated. (R1, R4)

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient empirical validation.** Most evaluation relies on the same NMA loss that is used for guidance, so it is unsurprising that guidance reduces it. Several reviewers (R2, R3, R4) called for independent, more stringent tests, such as:
  - Molecular dynamics simulations to verify that the sampled structures exhibit the desired dynamics. (R2, R4)
  - Experimental characterization of designed sequences (expression, dynamics). (R4)
  - Quantitative metrics beyond visual inspection, e.g., for the strain targets in Figure 3 (R1, R3) and for the “blow‑up” phenomenon. (R1, R3)
- **Limited scope of validation.** Only 27 conditional samples are presented (R4). Testing on additional pre‑trained diffusion models would strengthen claims of generality (R2). The evaluation is largely qualitative or based on heuristic thresholds; for example, the filtering step in Section 5.1 removes samples with mean chain distance outside [3.75, 3.85] but the fraction filtered is never reported (R3).
- **Methodological novelty is modest.** The approach is effectively standard classifier guidance (Ho et al., 2020) combined with a new loss; Section 3.1 recapitulates established material (R4). The NMA component is described as a straightforward port of numpy code to PyTorch (R4). One reviewer (R2, after rebuttal) concurred with the critique that both the method and evaluation are lacking.
- **Clarity of the modeling task.** It is not explicitly stated what the generative model outputs: is it only Cα backbone coordinates? Does it produce both sequence and structure? At what stage are sequences added? (R1) The description of the protein generation pipeline is ambiguous (e.g., “sample novel proteins using,” “with ESMFold (Lin et al., 2022) design for each”). (R1)
- **Missing details and undefined variables.** Several key terms are introduced without explanation, impeding understanding:
  - Variable *z* in Equation (4) is not defined (R1).
  - Equation (6) appears to be missing an *x* on the right-hand side (R1).
  - The “interaction constants matrix” *K* in NMA is not described when first mentioned (R1).
  - “Mean chain distance” is not defined; the reference value of 3.8 Å suggests Cα–Cα distance, but this should be stated as part of the model definition (R1, R3).
  - The expected displacement matrix *v(x)* is not explicitly attributed to the NMA analysis (R1).
- **Questionable parameter choices.** Guidance scale values are not explained; they may be overfit to the evaluation proteins (R4). Guidance is turned on only in the middle of sampling, which is non‑standard and not justified (R4). The loss in Equation (8) has no weighting factor (temperature), raising the question of why the scale should be 1 (R1).
- **Figures are difficult to interpret.** Figure 1: the bottom row is labeled “corresponding proteins synthesised with Genie,” but it is unclear what “corresponding” means—are they conditioned similarly? Arrows are not to scale, reducing informativeness (R1, R3). Figure 2: lacks a calibrated baseline (e.g., from MD or from another system) to indicate what “good” or “bad” look like (R3). Figure 3: the reviewer is unsure what to look for or what a bad sample would be (R3).
- **Practical relevance not fully addressed.** If dynamics information is available, one could simply perform NMA or coarse‑grained MD to generate an ensemble and then use inverse folding to design sequences. The advantages of conditioning a diffusion model on dynamics over this alternative are not discussed (R1).
