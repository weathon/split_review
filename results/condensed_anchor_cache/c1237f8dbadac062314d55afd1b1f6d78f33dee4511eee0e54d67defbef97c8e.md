## Merged Review

### Summary

Three reviewers rated the paper 5, 3, 3 (all confidence 3). One reviewer (R1, score 5) was substantially more positive, praising the large-scale dataset and effective OT algorithm. The other two reviewers (R2, R3, scores 3) acknowledged the significance and clarity of the presentation but raised major concerns about methodological novelty, evaluation fairness, missing details, and writing quality. There is consensus on the value of the Mouse-RGC dataset and the inclusion of ablation studies, but strong disagreement on the overall contribution and strength of validation.

### Strengths

* **New dataset contribution:** Mouse-RGC, a large-scale mouse retinal ganglion cell dataset with annotations for nine time stages and ~30,000 gene expressions, is a valuable resource for the community. (R1, R3)
* **Unifying framework:** TAROT combines three components (MAE representation learning, regularized OT with biological priors, B-spline continuous trajectory) that are often treated independently, allowing each step to be optimized for the subsequent task. (R2) *(Note: R3 argues that the individual components are not novel; see Weaknesses.)*
* **Ablation studies:** Ablations are provided to understand the effect of individual components on overall performance. (R2, R3)
* **Clarity and organization:** The paper is well-structured and easy to follow. (R1, R3) *(Note: R2 finds writing quality poor; see Weaknesses.)*
* **Experimental breadth:** The evaluation uses multiple datasets and baselines, quantitative metrics, and biological analysis. (R2)

### Weaknesses

* **Methodological novelty overstated:** Using optimal transport for trajectory inference is not new (WOT, Schiebinger et al. 2019; MOSCOT, Klein et al. 2023) and continuous trajectories via splines have been used before (e.g., Monocle, Trapnell et al.). The paper’s wording implies these are novel contributions, yet the actual novelty (MAE for cell representations and the specific cost regularizers) is not clearly delineated. (R3) R2 notes the *combination* is original, but the individual components need proper attribution.
* **Insufficient comparison for representation learning:** The MAE is not compared against existing single-cell-specific tools (e.g., scVI, scANVI) that already incorporate biological priors; the ablation study also omits such baselines, limiting support for the claim of “superior cell representation.” (R2) No justification is given for why MAE should work better than autoencoders or PCA for gene expression data, and the relation to the vision MAE (CVPR 2022) is not cited. (R1)
* **Inadequate discussion of prior OT methods:** The paper does not describe how TAROT differs from WOT and MOSCOT, which also use regularized OT. MOSCOT in particular already incorporates proliferation/apoptosis priors. The evaluation setting for the MOSCOT baseline is unclear. (R2)
* **Biological priors under-specified:** The construction of the “Developmental” and “Gene expression” regularizers is not detailed—how the gene list is derived, how regularization enters the cost matrix, and the precise form of $\mathcal{D}^{\text{dev}}$, $\mathcal{D}^{\text{fuc}}$, and $\text{corr}()$ are missing. (R1, R2)
* **Clustering choices unclear and unvalidated:**
  * It is unclear whether the clusters $\mathcal{C}_1^{(t)},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{k_t}^{(t)}$ are computed per time stage or globally. (R1)
  * $k=50$ for the nearest-neighbor graph is justified only as “experience of biological sciences”; Seurat typically uses 20. The Louvain resolution parameter is not reported. Clusters should be validated with marker gene expression before being used as ground truth. (R3)
* **Flawed quantitative evaluation metrics:** GPT-G and GPT-L evaluate agreement with prior knowledge that is explicitly built into TAROT’s cost function, making the metrics biased toward TAROT. More objective ground truths (e.g., simulated data, known trajectories as in Fig. 2 of Klein et al.) are needed. (R3)
* **Qualitative evaluation gaps:**
  * In Fig. 7, TAROT captures non-monotonic gene expression patterns, yet the authors frame this as a positive while also assuming monotonic variation—a contradiction. (R3)
  * Monocle-3 and moscot are omitted from the GSEA comparison (Fig. 8). (R3)
  * No discussion of whether the specific pathways found are biologically meaningful. (R3)
  * The simulated gene knockout experiments lack comparison with other methods. (R3)
* **Entropy regularization parameter $\lambda$ not ablated or reported:** Despite acknowledging its importance and presenting a search procedure (Algorithm 1), the paper does not show the chosen $\lambda$ value or an ablation over it. (R2)
* **Writing quality:** The manuscript contains many grammatical errors, typos, and misuse of terminology; comprehensive proofreading is required. (R2)
* **Data and code availability:** The provided code should include the Mouse-RGC dataset files so the community can use them. (R1, R2)
* **Missing/inconsistent dataset reporting:** The introduction claims validation on two public datasets, but only Mouse-CCC appears in Table 1; the naming alternates between Mouse-MCC and Mouse-CCC. (R3)
* **Pseudo-time interpretation not clarified:** How “pseudo time” is defined and how the transport plan $\mathcal{T}$ is used in the B-spline interpolation are not explained. (R1)
