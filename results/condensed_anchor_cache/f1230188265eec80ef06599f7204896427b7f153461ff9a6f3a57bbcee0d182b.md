## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a human-AI framework for generating difficult math questions. It extracts core “skills” from the MATH dataset, then prompts an LLM to create questions that require combining two randomly paired skills. A multi-turn pipeline uses LLMs to solve and filter candidate questions, after which human annotators modify or verify correctness. This yields the MATH² dataset (210 challenging questions). On MATH², model performance is roughly the square of their MATH accuracy, and MATH² questions serve as more effective in-context examples for MATH. All reviewers note the framework’s potential value for the community as existing benchmarks saturate.

### Strengths
- **Clear motivation and well-structured presentation.** The paper is easy to follow (R2, R3, R4). The motivation to generate difficult questions that models themselves struggle with is timely, given saturated benchmarks (R2, R4).
- **Promising AI-assisted generation pipeline.** The framework leverages LLM metacognition to extract skills and combine them, with human oversight to ensure correctness. This approach is extendable to other structured reasoning domains (R1, R3).
- **MATH² is a genuinely more challenging benchmark.** All tested models show significantly lower performance on MATH² than on MATH, supporting that the questions are harder (R2, R3, R4). The square relationship between MATH and MATH² performance is an interesting empirical finding (R1, R2).
- **Useful in-context learning examples.** MATH² questions improve model performance on MATH when used as in-context examples (R2, R3).
- **Comprehensive evaluation across multiple models** (R2). The paper tests several strong LLMs and notes consistent trends.

### Weaknesses
- **Heavy reliance on human annotation limits scalability.** 66% of LLM-filtered questions need further human editing, and the final dataset is only 210 questions (R2). The human re‑validation step (Stage 5) is described without details on expert qualifications or whether humans solve the question from scratch before comparing (R1, R3). Extending the pipeline to “scalable oversight” scenarios is therefore questionable (R1, R4).
- **Small dataset size and limited skill coverage.** With only 210 questions and the authors admitting in Line 412 that not all skill combinations are covered, the dataset may not be representative (R2). The filtering rates per pipeline stage are not reported (R1, R2), and the fraction of questions that are directly generated vs. human‑modified (139 modified) is not analyzed for performance differences (R2, question 3).
- **“Out-of-distribution” claim is insufficiently justified.** If MATH² questions merely combine two MATH problems, solving both correctly would yield squared accuracy, which is not an out‑of‑distribution scenario. The paper does not verify whether the generated questions truly require integrating two distinct skills beyond simple concatenation (R4; also raised by R1 regarding skill faithfulness and the possibility of more than two skills). This is a primary concern for one reviewer (R4, score 6) and a missing evaluation for another (R1).
- **Square-law explanation relies on unverified assumptions.** The interpretation that performance on MATH² scales as the square of MATH assumes skills are orthogonal and similarly difficult. Further evidence is needed (R1). [Minority view: only R1 raises this.]
- **Missing analysis of question discarding during generation.** If all LLM solution attempts produce unique answers, the question is discarded – this may remove valid extremely hard questions (R2, question 1). No human is involved in the solution‑attempt phase, so some valid but very difficult questions could be filtered out (R3).
- **Resource efficiency not addressed.** The framework involves many API calls across multiple LLM stages (skill extraction, generation, solving, filtering). An analysis of computational/financial cost per question is missing (R2, R3).
- **Clarity issues in presentation.** Figure 5 is not interpretable (R1). Stage 5 (human re-validation) lacks procedural detail (R3). The paper would benefit from a concrete, step‑by‑step example of a generated question moving through the pipeline (R3).
- **Missing references and minor typos.** Lines 75–76 and 322–323 lack expected citations (R2). Line 319 has a typo: “creators” (R1).
- **Missing ablations and comparisons.** How does the choice of base LLM affect generation quality (R3)? Can models solve questions they generated better than those from other models (R3)? What is human expert performance on MATH² (R3)? Estimated time to generate one question (R3)?
- **Figures are unreadable in some details.** Reviewer 1 specifically cannot decode information in Figure 5.
