## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes SWAP (Structure-aware Planning with Accurate World Model), a framework that enhances LLM reasoning by structuring multi-step reasoning as a graph, using a generator-discriminator architecture for world modeling, and introducing diversity-based modeling (DBM) with contrastive ranking (CR) to improve action/state diversity and discrimination. SWAP is evaluated on math (GSM8K, MATH), logical reasoning, and coding (HumanEval) benchmarks using Llama3-8B as backbone, with prompts and ablation studies presented. Reviewers note that the overall framework is described and some results exceed baseline methods, but they raise significant concerns about clarity, novelty, comparison to prior work, baseline selection, and evaluation validity.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-structured and generally easy to follow (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4), with detailed prompts and task examples (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).  
- The idea of representing multi-step reasoning as a graph is interesting and novel (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4); it helps avoid local minima in reasoning and enhances verifiability over Chain-of-Thought (Reviewer 4).  
- The normalization metrics (Eq. 2 & 4) for Diversity-Based Modeling are novel and interesting (Reviewer 3).  
- Ablation studies show the performance gain of each design choice, confirming the effectiveness of individual components (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).  
- The method is shown effective on diverse tasks (math, coding, logical reasoning) with Llama3-8b as backbone (Reviewer 3).  

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and presentation issues**: Several reviewers note that the writing lacks clarity in key parts. Reviewer 3 states the paper lacks clarity, especially regarding the structured search algorithm (Section 4.1) and the tree search process; Figure 2 shows a linear step-by-step process but fails to illustrate the tree search of Figure 4; pseudo code is recommended. Reviewer 4 points out that many annotations are used before definition (e.g., $P_{ori}(z; T_{s_{t-1}})$; "learned distribution"; $P_{sem}$; inconsistent notation: $M_{wm-G}$ vs $M_{wm}$; $M_{\pi_D}$ and $M_{wm-D}$ in Eqs. 8–9 unexplained). In contrast, Reviewer 1 finds the writing easy to follow, indicating disagreement on presentation clarity.  
- **Insufficient comparison with related work and baselines**:  
  - The related work section is thin; the paper should more thoroughly compare with existing reward modeling and process supervision literature, e.g., Math-Shepherd and [1] (Reviewer 2).  
  - The framework is very similar to Tree-of-Thought (TOT), RAP, and [1]; RAP also uses world model tree search, and [1] uses discriminator-aided tree search. The main differences are not clearly articulated (Reviewer 3).  
  - Baseline methods only include inference-time techniques; how do results compare to methods that incorporate fine-tuning (e.g., GPT-4 generating training data for fine-tuning a base model)? (Reviewer 1)  
  - Process supervision is claimed as a contribution but missing comparison with other process supervision methods (Reviewer 4).  
  - Lack of comparison with diversity-seeking methods from reinforcement learning designed to enhance diversity, such as [1] and [2] (Reviewer 4).  
  - Table 1 lists many redundant backbone models not directly comparable with SWAP+Llama3-8B; better to fix backbone and add more comparisons (Reviewer 3).  
- **Validity of reported benchmark numbers**: Llama3-8B reported numbers are significantly lower than official Llama report (e.g., HumanEval zero-shot 62.2, MATH 4-shot-CoT 30.0, GSM8K 8-shot-CoT 79.6), casting doubt on evaluation validity (Reviewer 2).  
- **Novelty concerns**: Numerous studies already prompt GPT-4 with roles like analysis, reflection, planning. The novelty of SWAP beyond existing approaches is unclear (Reviewer 1).  
- **Missing analysis and ablations**:  
  - Little discussion on the advantage of a graph (with node connectivities) over sequential step listing; authors should provide ablation on this and an example of the graph produced (Reviewer 2).  
  - Figure 3 uses LoRA tuning for generation diversity but only post-hoc adjustment methods are proposed; explanation needed (Reviewer 3).  
  - No confidence intervals for results; robustness to initialization and randomness unclear (Reviewer 4).  
- **Unclear or unsupported claims**:  
  - Semantic similarity probability calculation is not explained; the current normalization method (mapping negative values to zero) seems suboptimal (Reviewer 1, also asked by Reviewer 4).  
  - Fine-tuning Llama3-8B on SWAP shows inferior or comparable results to CoT (Table 1), which is counterintuitive; no explanation provided (Reviewer 4).  
  - Why is the approach considered a "world model"? Connection to reinforcement learning and reward modeling (e.g., discriminator vs reward model) not clarified (Reviewer 1).  
- **Missing experiments**:  
  - Can the method improve math-specialized (e.g., Qwen Math) or code-specialized (e.g., Deepseek Coder) models? (Reviewer 2)  
  - What is the efficiency of SWAP tree search compared to TOT and RAP? Discriminator pruning may offer an advantage (Reviewer 3).  
  - Training cost of SWAP vs SFT should be provided (Reviewer 4).
