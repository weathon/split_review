- Scores: 5, 8, 6, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SuSIE, which uses a pre-trained image-editing diffusion model (InstructPix2Pix) as a high-level planner to generate intermediate subgoal images, and a low-level goal-conditioned policy to reach those subgoals. Evaluations on the CALVIN benchmark and real-world tasks show strong generalization to novel objects and environments. Reviewers find the idea simple and well-motivated, but raise concerns about insufficient quantitative analysis of subgoal quality, limited real-world evaluation, separate training of modules without feedback, strong assumptions, and reproducibility issues. Scores range from 5 to 8, indicating mixed opinions on contribution and soundness.

### Strengths
- **Novel approach**: Leveraging a pre-trained image-editing diffusion model (InstructPix2Pix) as a high-level planner to propose subgoals, combined with a low-level goal-conditioned policy, is straightforward and properly motivated. Reviewer 2 (score 8) particularly highlights that framing high-level planning as image editing and focusing on short-term future is appealing.
- **Well-written and easy to follow** (all reviewers).
- **Strong experimental scope**: Evaluation includes both simulation (CALVIN benchmark) and real-world tasks, with comparisons to strong baselines including RT-2-X, MOO, MCIL, and others.
- **Zero-shot generalization**: The method successfully solves real-world tasks involving novel objects, distractors, and environments, demonstrating robust generalization abilities.
- **Decoupling of subgoal generation and low-level control** is shown to be effective; the paper provides explanations for cases where the method underperforms.
- **Experimental design** appropriately targets zero-shot generalization with varied difficulty levels (scenes A, B, C; seen/unseen objects; increasing clutter).
- **Use of Internet-scale pre-training** via InstructPix2Pix is a promising direction for injecting semantic knowledge into robotic control.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient quantitative analysis of subgoal quality** (Reviewer 2, major). The claim that InstructPix2Pix pre-training improves subgoal plausibility and downstream policy performance is only supported qualitatively (Figure 3). There is no controlled experiment that compares policies using subgoals from the pre-trained vs. a non-pre-trained model. The paper also lacks quantitative verification of physical plausibility and whether the low-level policy is robust to implausible subgoals. Reviewer 4 notes that a more thorough comparison with video prediction models (for temporal understanding and coherent subgoals) is missing.
- **Subgoal training procedure is unclear** (Reviewer 3). In Section 4.1, variable `k` is used for two contexts, the distribution `q` is undefined, and the optimization for generating intuitive subgoals (e.g., pre-grasp poses) is not explained.
- **Limited real-world evaluation and analysis** (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Only three scenes are used, which may not capture full complexity. RT-2-X outperforms SuSIE on scene C (Table 2) but not on scenes A and B; the paper does not provide a sufficient analysis of this discrepancy in terms of object types, environment complexities, or task requirements. Qualitative claims of higher precision (Figure 4) need more quantitative backing. One reviewer (score 5) argues that many tasks (e.g., placing a crayon in a bowl) are easily solvable using pre-trained perception and traditional planning, and that the real-world evaluation is too narrow to assess generalization.
- **Separate training of diffusion model and low-level policy** (Reviewers 1, 3). The planner is unaware of the low-level policy’s capabilities, leading to subgoals that may be physically unrealizable or too challenging. There is no feedback loop, error detection, or recovery mechanism. The low-level policy is also unaware of the semantic context of subgoals, which may cause suboptimal actions. The method assumes subgoal predictions are always feasible, which is a strong assumption for dynamic environments.
- **Strong assumptions limit applicability** (Reviewers 1, 3). The gripper must always be visible (no occlusions). InstructPix2Pix cannot handle viewpoint changes. The model infers only from visual information and cannot infer physical properties (mass, friction, stability), limiting tasks requiring such understanding.
- **Incomplete comparison with baselines** (Reviewers 3, 4). In CALVIN, the performance difference from other language-conditioned policies (MCIL, GCBC, etc.) is not large; a deeper analysis of relative strengths/weaknesses is needed. The paper omits a higher-performing result from Ge et al. (2023) (Table 2 of that paper reports 72% on 1-step chain vs. SuSIE 75%, and higher on longer chains), which weakens the comparison. Why scene C is easier than scene B for multiple methods (including SuSIE) is not discussed.
- **Reproducibility and presentation issues** (Reviewers 2, 3). The action space is not described in the main paper. Low-level policy implementation details are vaguely referenced to Walke et al. (2023) without sufficient context. Dataset information should appear earlier and more comprehensively. Minor error: “Table 5.3” appears in Section 5.3 and A2.2.2 instead of “Table 1”.
- **Missing ablation studies and experiments** (Reviewers 3, 4). The effect of the goal-sampling interval `K` on performance is not evaluated. No experiment compares using low-dimensional image embeddings as subgoals instead of raw images, which would help isolate the contribution of the image-editing model. The role of the conditional policy (Section 4.2) when encountering a novel subgoal not present in the training dataset is not analyzed. It is unclear whether the robot dataset consists only of successful trajectories; if not, the model could generate subgoals representing failure states (e.g., dropping an object), and this is not discussed.
