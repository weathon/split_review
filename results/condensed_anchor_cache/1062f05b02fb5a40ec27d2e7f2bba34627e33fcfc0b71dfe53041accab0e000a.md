## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper studies dynamic pricing of two complementary items (e.g., flight ticket and luggage) under a minimum-sales constraint. It formulates the problem as a constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) and proposes a primal-dual online learning algorithm (PD-DP). An empirical evaluation is performed on semi-real-world synthetic data. However, all three reviewers identify significant shortcomings: the paper lacks theoretical guarantees, provides no meaningful comparison to existing baselines (including the closely related work of Stradi et al. 2024a,b), and does not clearly distinguish its contributions from prior art.

### Strengths

- The problem of dynamically pricing complementary items with a sales constraint is interesting and practically impactful (all reviewers).
- Using the online CMDP framework for this problem appears to be novel (Reviewer 2).
- The paper provides a clear CMDP formulation for the two-item case and designs an online primal-dual optimization algorithm (Reviewers 1, 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of theoretical guarantees.** No regret or constraint violation bounds are provided, nor is there any discussion of the difficulty or possibility of deriving such guarantees. (All reviewers; see also questions by Reviewers 1 and 2).
- **Insufficient distinction from prior work.** The PD-DP algorithm is heavily inspired by Stradi et al. (2024a, 2024b), combining their techniques (e.g., occupancy measure and Lagrangian variable initialization from 2024b, bandit feedback handling and optimistic biased estimator from 2024a, confidence set update from 2024b, OMD instead of OGD). The paper does not clearly explain what is new or how it differs from these works. (Reviewer 2; also mentioned by Reviewer 1 asking for a comparison).
- **Inadequate empirical evaluation.** The only baseline comparison is with an unconstrained UCB algorithm, placed in the appendix. No comparison is made with any online CMDP algorithm that handles bandit feedback and constraints, such as Stradi et al. (2024a). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3; see also question by Reviewer 2).
- **Limited scope and questionable extensibility.** Only two items are modeled (one primary, one complementary). The paper claims easy extension to more items (Remark 1), but given the complexity of the two-item case, this claim seems unjustified. Additionally, the model does not capture dynamic pricing that depends on remaining inventory (e.g., number of remaining flight tickets), which is a critical aspect in revenue management. (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 2 also implicitly questions the generality).
- **Presentation issues.** (Reviewer 2) The mathematical formulation (Section 2.1) could benefit from better paragraphing. The difficulty parameter *d* is defined only in the appendix but should appear in the main body. The baseline comparison should be moved from the appendix to the main body. A conclusion is missing (could be added by trimming Subsections 4.1–4.3). Consider showing averaged regret and constraint violation (R_T/T, V_T/T) to better visualize convergence.
