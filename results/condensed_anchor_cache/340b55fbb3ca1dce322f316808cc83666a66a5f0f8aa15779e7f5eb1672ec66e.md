## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LMGJOINT, a memory-efficient PLM-nested GCN for link prediction (LP) on text-attributed graphs (TAGs), with a residual textual proximity connection and cache embedding training strategy. It also introduces eight TAG datasets and benchmarks existing LP and PLM-based methods. Reviewers are split: two rate 6 (positive), two rate 3 (negative). The positive reviewers highlight the novel joint framework, comprehensive benchmark, and strong empirical results. The negative reviewers point to poor writing, missing datasets and baselines, unsubstantiated efficiency claims, lack of edge text, and several technical confusions. All reviewers agree on the need for better writing, ablation studies, and broader domain evaluation.

### Strengths
1. The joint PLM+GCN framework for LP on TAGs is novel and addresses an underexplored gap. The proposed LMGJOINT with residual textual proximity and cache embedding strategy reduces computational cost and shows strong performance gains (e.g., up to 19.75% MRR improvement) over prior SOTA. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
2. The paper provides a benchmark of eight TAG datasets (including well-established ones like Cora and PubMed) with a unified experimental setup, enabling fair comparisons between graph-based and PLM-based methods. (Reviewers 2,3,4)
3. Extensive experiments across multiple datasets and model configurations demonstrate consistent effectiveness and scalability with minimal hyperparameter tuning. (Reviewers 1,3)
4. The paper is well-structured and provides a clear overview of related work, methodology, and results (Reviewer 4). Note: other reviewers strongly disagree on clarity (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
1. **Writing quality, clarity, and typos.** The paper suffers from significant presentation issues. Typos include: Figure 1 caption “$H_C$” should be “$H^{C}$” (or “$H_{c}$”); “$Hij$” should be “$H_{ij}$”; line 869 “$2^10$” should be “$2^{10}$”; line 144 reference error in “TAG_Benchmark (Yan et al., 2023)”; Figure 2 caption missing period; line 216 “Equ” should be “Eq.”. The structure is disorganized; related datasets (Section 3) and related benchmarks (Section 2) could be merged. (Reviewers 1,3,4)
2. **Reproducibility and missing implementation details.** No datasets are provided in the referenced repository. Key details are omitted: how PLMs are fine-tuned (full-parameter vs. parameter-efficient), how β in Eq. (5) is determined, how H^k in Eq. (4) is initialized, and what θ_{G,T} in Eq. (7) is. Inconsistencies exist between Algorithm 1 and Section 4 (e.g., SE-NCN, missing H^K, H_C, H_T, X^G). (Reviewer 1)
3. **Missing baselines and related work.** The benchmark lacks many standard LP methods: Metapath2Vec, ConvE, ComplEx, BUDDY, TAPE, GraphGPT, HL-GNN. Also missing is the TEG-DB dataset (Li et al., 2024) for comparison. Duplicate references [1-4] appear. (Reviewers 1,3)
4. **Edge text not considered.** The datasets and LMGJOINT do not incorporate textual information on edges (e.g., citation context), which is crucial for capturing nuanced relationships in TAGs. (Reviewers 2,4)
5. **Domain limitation of datasets.** The evaluation is restricted to academic citation networks. It is unclear how LMGJOINT generalizes to diverse real-world domains like social networks or e-commerce. Either the TEG-DB datasets or similar should be tested. (Reviewers 2,4)
6. **Efficiency claims unsubstantiated.** The paper claims parameter and memory efficiency, but the experiments do not compare training/inference time or memory usage against baselines. Using PLMs under 500M parameters is not challenging. (Reviewers 1,3)
7. **Ablation and component isolation insufficient.** The impact of fine-tuning PLMs vs. frozen embeddings is not isolated. The relative contributions of the residual connection, cache strategy, and combination of embeddings are not disentangled. (Reviewers 2,3)
8. **Unusual and unexplained results.** In Table 2, FT-PLM-MLP outperforms FT-PLM-GCN, contradicting most prior work—this is not discussed. Caption of Table 2 claims boxed results are best, which is not the case. (Reviewer 1)
9. **Residual connection concept is confusing.** Adding textual proximity to GCN output is not a standard residual connection, and there is no experimental evidence that it mitigates over-smoothing (the GNN used has at most 3 layers, not deep). (Reviewer 1)
10. **Additional statistics are not justified.** The paper lists dataset statistics as a contribution but does not explain how they assist LP or why they matter. (Reviewer 1)
11. **Theoretical proofs have oversimplified assumptions.** The assumptions in Theorems A.1 and A.2 appear too simplistic for real TAGs. (Reviewer 1)
12. **Benchmarking setup is ambiguous.** Sections 5.1 and 5.2 should clearly list all compared baselines. Implementation details for LMGJOINT (e.g., chosen GCN and PLM) are insufficient to ensure a healthy benchmark. (Reviewer 3)
