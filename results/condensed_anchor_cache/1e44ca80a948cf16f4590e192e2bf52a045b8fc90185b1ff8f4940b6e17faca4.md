## Merged Review

### Summary
All four reviewers recommend acceptance with scores of 8 each. They find the problem important and the proposed EntiGraph algorithm well-motivated, with convincing experimental results on the QuALITY reading comprehension dataset. The method shows clear improvements over baselines in closed-book QA, instruction following, and, to a lesser extent, open-book RAG settings. The theoretical model provides intuition for the log-linear scaling. However, reviewers raise several concerns: the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (QuALITY), the method relies on a closed-source model (GPT-4) without ablation on generation model quality, there is no intrinsic evaluation of the generated data (fluency, factuality), and the applicability to other domains (legal, medical, math) is unexplored. One reviewer (R2) considers the theoretical model (Section 6) not useful; others (R1, R3) find it insightful. R3 gives the lowest soundness score (2) due to concerns about generalizability and lack of hallucination analysis. R4 raises a distillation concern. All agree the paper is clear and well-written.

### Strengths
- The problem (data inefficiency of pretraining on sparse domain-specific corpora) is important.
- The EntiGraph method, which extracts entities from source documents and generates diverse text connecting them, is well-motivated and demonstrates clear performance improvements.
- Experiments include relevant natural baselines (direct pretraining, paraphrased continued pretraining) and achieve better scaling than simple paraphrasing.
- The closed-book results (Figure 3) show that EntiGraph leads to far more salient claims per false claim than all other models, including GPT-4 and Llama 3 8B trained on the original corpus.
- The method also improves open-book RAG performance, though the benefit is less substantial.
- The theoretical model (Section 6) explains the log-linear scaling pattern and provides insight into how synthetic data augmentation enables more data-efficient learning (R1, R3 find this valuable; R2 and R4 are more critical).
- The paper is clear and easy to read.
- The paper includes comprehensive evaluations across closed-book QA, instruction following, and open-book settings.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited dataset scope:** All experiments rely solely on the QuALITY dataset. It is unclear how well the method generalizes to other domain-specific corpora with different entity densities and relational structures, such as technical reports, legal documents, medical texts, creative writing, or mathematical corpora. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Dependence on closed-source model:** The synthetic data generation relies on GPT-4, making the work non-reproducible. An ablation study on the impact of the generation model's capabilities (e.g., using open-source models) is missing. The practical feasibility is limited for users without access to such models. (R2, R3)
- **Lack of intrinsic evaluation of generated data:** No quantitative metrics are provided for the quality of the generated text—e.g., perplexity, BLEU score against a reference corpus, or human evaluation of fluency and factuality. Without these, it is difficult to assess the generated data independently of downstream task performance. (R2)
- **Hallucination risk not empirically examined:** Although grounding in the original corpus is intended to mitigate hallucinations, the paper does not provide a quantitative analysis of errors introduced during generation, such as factual inaccuracies, logical inconsistencies, or semantic drift. (R3)
- **Insufficient comparisons to other augmentation methods:** The paper compares only with “Rephrase CPT” and direct pretraining. Missing comparisons with back-translation, paraphrasing, adversarial training, reinforcement learning, or other synthetic generation techniques. (R3)
- **Theoretical model (Section 6) considered not useful by one reviewer:** R2 states the section is “unnecessarily mathematical, based on simplistic assumptions and does not bring useful insights (For many continuously increasing lines, there anyway exists a mixture-of-exponential that fit it).” R4 suggests a simulation might suffice. (R2, R4; contrast with R1 and R3 who find it insightful)
- **Distillation concern:** R4 questions whether the improvement partly reflects distillation of GPT-4’s knowledge into Llama 3 8B, possibly injecting new information not in the source corpus, and calls for analysis of whether the generated text is rephrasing or adds new facts. (R4)
- **Modest RAG gains:** The benefit of the approach largely vanishes in the open-book RAG setting. R4 asks whether the additional training is worth the modest improvement. (R4)
- **Errors in generated data:** R4 asks how often GPT-4t made errors in producing the training data and what filtering was applied. (R4)
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** Sensitivity of performance to entity extraction and relation analysis hyperparameters (e.g., entity subset size k) is not explored. R1 asks how performance changes with the size of entity subsets (pairs vs. triplets vs. larger k). R3 asks about sensitivity to hyperparameters for entity extraction. (R1, R3)
- **Lack of comparison to manually curated data:** R3 asks how the synthetic corpus compares to a manually curated dataset in terms of quality and downstream impact. (R3)
- **Unclear applicability to ambiguous/domain-specific entities:** R3 asks whether EntiGraph works effectively in domains with ambiguous entities (e.g., medical/legal texts). (R3)
- **Significance of closed-book experiments not explicitly stated early:** R4 points out that the fact that closed-book experiments show the method’s value is not stated until the Limitations section. (R4)
- **Need for examples of generated text:** R4 requests concrete examples of the generated text to ground the presentation; only prompts are given. (R4)
