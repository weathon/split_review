## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Diffusion Q-Sampling (DQS), an actor-critic method that uses a diffusion model (via iterated Denoising Energy Matching, iDEM) to sample actions from a Boltzmann distribution defined by the soft Q-function. The goal is to capture multimodal behaviors in continuous control. Reviewer scores are split: one reviewer (R1) gave 6, three (R2,R3,R4) gave 3. R1 finds the approach novel and effective on maze and DM Control tasks. R2, R3, and R4 raise major concerns about theoretical validity, novelty, insufficient baselines, missing ablations, and clarity of comparisons. R4 identifies fundamental flaws in the theoretical proofs (Lemma 1 and Theorem 1) due to confusion between entropy-regularized and classical Bellman backups.

### Strengths
- **Novel application of iDEM**: The use of iDEM to learn a score function that enables sampling from the Boltzmann distribution of the Q-function is novel (R4). One reviewer (R1) highlights this as a valuable approach for multimodal action learning.
- **Multimodal demonstration**: DQS successfully learns diverse solutions in a goal-reaching maze task, while SAC and QSM concentrate on a single mode (R1,R3,R4). This multimodality is of interest to the RL community (R3).
- **Sample efficiency and exploration**: DQS balances exploration and exploitation by explicitly sampling from the Boltzmann distribution, and outperforms baselines in early training on several DM Control tasks (R1,R4).
- **Clear writing**: The paper is well-written, the method is clearly explained, and the experiments appear reproducible (R3,R4).
- **Originality** (R4) and significance for settings where compute is not a bottleneck and multimodal policies are desired (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical flaws (major)**: Lemma 1 is false and its proof invalid (R4). The lemma claims that a Boltzmann policy w.r.t. any Q-function has a dominating action-value function; R4 provides a counterexample using the Bellman optimality operator. The proof error: in Eq. (10) the expectation over log(π_old) is taken under π_old in line 1-2, but under π_new in line 3, turning a difference of entropies into a non-negative KL (which is not justified). Theorem 1 relies on that same faulty argument (R4). The authors fail to distinguish between the entropy-regularized setting (where Boltzmann is optimal) and the classical setting (where the optimal policy is deterministic). Without entropy regularization in the Bellman backup, the Boltzmann policy can be strictly worse (R4). R4 suggests removing the theoretical results or replacing the backup with an entropy-regularized one.
- **Lack of novelty (multiple reviewers)**: The method is seen as a straightforward integration of diffusion (iDEM) into SAC, lacking innovation (R2). The proofs in 4.1 are essentially the same as those in SAC and do not rely on the diffusion model (R3). One reviewer (R4) found the specific application novel, but the majority disagree.
- **Insufficient baselines and experiments**: Only SAC and QSM are compared; missing many relevant diffusion RL methods: Q-weighted Variational Policy Optimization (arXiv 2405.16173, R2), Policy Representation via Diffusion Probability Model (arXiv 2305.13122, R2,R3), Diffusion Actor-Critic with Entropy Regulator (Wang et al., NeurIPS24, R3), and PPO (R3). No experiments on harder MuJoCo tasks (Humanoid, Ant) (R3). Custom maze environment is not standard and lacks quantified data – results are shown as abstract images rather than numbers or tables (R2). R3 remains skeptical about QSM results: with tricks (e.g., random sampling to update Q-gradient across full action space), QSM might match DQS.
- **Missing ablation studies**: No ablation on temperature sensitivity, number of Monte Carlo samples (K) and its compute cost, or isolation of contributions (R1,R3). R1 asks why no ablation was done.
- **Temperature tuning**: DQS requires manual temperature tuning (unlike SAC) because likelihood computation under the diffusion model is expensive (R1).
- **Related work incomplete**: Online diffusion RL literature is poorly covered; references lack summaries of approaches and their flaws (R3). Missing citation for offline diffusion RL at L071 (R4). Missing recent online diffusion RL work (R3).
- **Clarity issues in claims vs. QSM**: The paper says QSM’s “exact form of the policy is unspecified,” but Eq. (21) of the original QSM paper defines a Boltzmann distribution (R3). The same criticism applies to DQS – both train score functions to approximate the Boltzmann distribution (R4). The claimed advantage over QSM is unclear (R3,R4).
- **Minor technical errors**: Missing tildes in Eq. (4) expectations (R3,R4); Eq. (4) has incorrect parentheses (R3); subscript in Eq. (5) should be capital K (R4); Q-function expression missing discount factor placement (R3); formulas need sequence numbers (R3). More explanation needed for how reverse SDE (3) generates samples (R4).
- **Runtime and reproducibility**: No runtime comparison with SAC (R4). Question whether code will be released (R4).
- **Long-term performance**: R1 questions if DQS maintains advantages at late training stages (e.g., 1M iterations) and suggests running longer experiments.
- **Narrow test environments**: DQS only shown on a custom maze and 8 DM Control tasks; unclear if advantages generalize (R2,R3).
