## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Distributed Speculative Inference (DSI), which uses Speculation Parallelism to overlap drafter and target model execution, making SI non-blocking and provably faster than both SI and non-SI for any drafter. Reviewers find the idea novel, well-written, and backed by theory, but raise major concerns about the evaluation being purely simulation-based, the lack of multi-GPU or real distributed implementation, the omission of communication and synchronization overheads, the additional compute/memory cost (especially KV cache), and the limited practical readiness. One reviewer (score 3) is much more critical of the evaluation strength than the others (scores 6, 6, 5), while all agree that real-system validation is missing.

### Strengths
- The proposed speculation parallelism is novel and interesting, effectively solving the blocking problem of traditional SI by overlapping target and drafter execution, and generalizing SI to work with multiple drafters in parallel. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The method is backed by rigorous theoretical analysis and proof that DSI is faster in expectation than both SI and non-SI. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- Empirical results (simulations) show DSI achieves 1.29–1.92× speedup over SI in various settings. (Reviewer 1)
- Source code for the experiments is provided. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Evaluation is simulation-based, not real implementation.** All experiments were run on a single machine with delays (`WAIT` commands) mimicking LLM forward passes, not actual LLM inference. This makes it unclear whether gains will hold in real distributed settings. (Reviewers 1, 4) Reviewer 2 emphasizes that “evaluations are very weak” and “evaluating distributed evaluation on a single machine doesn’t make sense.” Reviewer 4 notes that a full implementation is not straightforward because LLM inference is complicated and the multi-thread system may not be completely decoupled.
- **Missing multi-GPU/distributed evaluation.** Practical concerns such as communication latency, GPU-to-GPU data transfer (including KV-cache movement), multithreading overhead, and context switching are ignored or not discussed. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3) Reviewer 2 asks: “What kind of communications are involved in distributed speculative parallelism and how does that affect latency?” Reviewer 3 asks: “How does the data communication affect the approach? The KV-cache also seems to move between GPUs. How about synchronization issues?”
- **Additional compute/memory cost not fully considered.** DSI requires more compute and memory (multiple KV caches) than SI, increasing with the number and size of drafters and with input context length. A fair assessment should account for these extra resources, not just latency reduction. (Reviewers 1, 3) Reviewer 3 also raises energy consumption concerns and notes that an energy analysis compared to SI is missing.
- **Lack of ablation and parameter details.** The paper does not report the lookahead values used for each row of Table 2, nor the rationale for their choice. (Reviewer 1) Reviewer 1 also questions the rationale behind the acceptance rate formula (p. 8 line 410) and why an estimate is used instead of independently simulating acceptance/rejection for each drafter–target pair.
- **Limited model scale and missing experiments with multiple drafters.** The largest target model tested is 13B; it is unclear how DSI performs on larger models (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 explicitly asks for simulation results with multiple drafters, since that is a claimed advantage of DSI.
- **Practical deployment challenges not addressed.** The method needs multiple GPUs to host several target models, which can cause deployment and synchronization issues; managing KV caches across GPUs each running at its own pace is non-trivial and not implemented. (Reviewers 3, 4) Reviewer 4 also questions whether DSI can handle batch sizes larger than 1, noting that batch inference is already a challenge for SI.
- **Minor writing issues.** A long paragraph (Lines 422–487) should be split; Eq. (3) is referenced on Line 128 but located in the appendix; the indices f₁ and f₂ on Line 303 should likely be fⱼ and fₘ; on Line 931 “upper bound” should probably be “lower bound.” (Reviewer 4) Reviewer 2 additionally states that the evaluation writing is confusing.
