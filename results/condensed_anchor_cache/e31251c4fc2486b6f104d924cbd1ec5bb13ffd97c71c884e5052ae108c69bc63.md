## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a deep reinforcement learning architecture with a semantic clustering module that combines feature dimensionality reduction (FDRNet) and a vector quantizer (VQ, analogous to VQ-VAE/VQ-GAN) for online clustering of state representations. The goal is to reveal semantic clustering properties, improve interpretability, and provide analytical tools for understanding policy hierarchies and feature space organization. Experiments on the Procgen benchmark validate the module’s effectiveness, with visualizations (Figures 2, 3) and a table (Table 1) showing cluster semantics.

### Strengths
- The problem of understanding DRL decision-making via semantic clustering is clearly defined (L53–58). (R2)
- The proposed FDRNet is simple, and the use of a vector quantizer for online clustering is a reasonable approach. (R2)
- The semantic clustering module is novel and provides valuable insights into the internal structure of DRL, improving interpretability by revealing how inputs are organized semantically. (R1, R3, R4)
- Extensive experimental validation confirms the module’s ability to uncover meaningful semantic clustering and potential for guiding model improvements. (R1, R2, R4; R4 calls the validation robust and showing good performance)
- Visualizations (e.g., Figure 2 comparing FDR+VQ to t-SNE; Figure 3 and Table 1 illustrating cluster semantics) clearly demonstrate the benefits of the proposed method. (R2, R3)
- The paper is well-structured and written clearly. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Clustering mechanism and Euclidean distance sensitivity**: The method uses Euclidean distance to measure similarity for clustering, but in RL, minor state variations can lead to large changes in optimal decisions. It is unclear how the feature reduction specifically mitigates this issue. (R1)
- **Determination of K (number of clusters)**: The process for choosing K in the semantic clustering module / VQ is not explained. No experiments validate the choice of K or analyze its impact on performance and interpretability. (R4)
- **Integration and training pipeline details**:  
  - “Online training” (L221) is ambiguous: does it refer to concurrent training of the clustering module with the DRL model or a separate schedule? (R1)  
  - The addition of the VQ code (k) to the state feature (Figure 1) is not sufficiently described. Details are needed on how the code is expanded into a vector of matching dimensionality, whether a learnable projection or fixed mapping is used, and what effects this integration has on the DRL policy. (R2)  
  - Missing mathematical formulas to explain the overall algorithm, e.g., whether the VQ embedding e_k is used in the DRL training. (R3)
- **Baselines and comparisons**:  
  - No baseline section is included in the manuscript. (R3)  
  - The paper lacks a comprehensive comparison with existing methods for semantic clustering and DRL interpretability (Section 4). (R4)  
  - The claim (Section 3.2) that the FDR method “more effectively captures nonlinear structure and preserves local neighborhood relationships compared to methods like PCA and UMAP” is not grounded by a source or detailed mathematical comparison. In particular, UMAP is also designed for nonlinear reduction and local preservation, so this claim needs justification. (R2)
- **Interpretability evaluation**: The enhancement of interpretability is not formally evaluated. For instance, does the method provide images or sentences as explanations of strategy selection to users? (R3)
- **Importance and rationale for clustering state features**: The paper does not explain why clustering state features benefits DRL interpretability or how it correlates with policy behavior. (R3) Relatedly, it is unclear why the method does not directly use the DRL output (action probabilities or value functions) for clustering, which might provide a more direct measure of policy similarity. (R1)
- **Background and motivation gaps**:  
  - The paragraph on Vector Quantized VAEs in Section 3’s Background feels out of place; its relevance and context are missing. (R2)  
  - The statement that “directly clustering high-dimensional features is challenging” (L147/148) needs grounding (e.g., a citation) despite being intuitive. (R2)  
  - “Instability of t-SNE” (L150/151) should be elaborated, including sensitivity to hyperparameters (e.g., perplexity), variability across runs, and computational cost that makes it unsuitable for online clustering. (R2)
- **Note on reviewer disagreement**: While reviewers 2 and 4 gave higher scores (6) and praised the idea and validation, reviewers 1 and 3 (score 5) raised more fundamental concerns about clarity, justification, and missing baselines. In particular, reviewer 3 gave low soundness/presentation scores and flagged the absence of mathematical details and interpretability evaluation.
