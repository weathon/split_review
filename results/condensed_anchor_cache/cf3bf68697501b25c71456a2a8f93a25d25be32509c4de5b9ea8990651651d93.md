## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Fun3D, a physics-driven text-to-3D shape generation method that integrates solid mechanics (stress, strain) into the generative process. It first generates an initial 3D mesh using a 3D diffusion model (Shape-E) and then optimizes it through a data-driven differentiable physics layer trained with finite element method (FEM) data. An alternating training strategy balances geometry constraints and physics constraints. The goal is to produce shapes that are both visually plausible and physically functional. Experiments compare against Diffusion-SDF and demonstrate improvements in load-bearing capacity. Two reviewers (R2, R3) rated the paper 5, finding the approach novel and results impressive; two reviewers (R1, R4) rated it 3, citing limited scope, weak comparisons, and insufficient evaluation rigor.

### Strengths
- **Novel integration of physics constraints with text-to-3D generation:** The paper addresses the underexplored problem of ensuring physical functionality (solid mechanics) in generated shapes, moving beyond purely visual/geometric plausibility (R1, R2, R3).
- **Data-driven differentiable physics layer:** Allows end-to-end training where shape geometry and physics are optimized simultaneously; trained on FEM data for accurate mechanical property simulation (R1, R3).
- **Alternating training strategy:** Efficiently balances geometry and physics optimization, producing robust shapes suitable for engineering design and virtual prototyping (R1).
- **Impressive qualitative and quantitative results:** Compared to Diffusion-SDF, Fun3D generates shapes that are both visually appealing and more physically viable, as shown in examples (R2).
- **Well-written and clear presentation:** Complex ideas (e.g., differentiable FEM) are explained with clarity and precision (R2).
- **Timely and important topic:** The work is relevant to real-world applications where generative models are used to produce solid objects (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited baselines and comparisons:** The paper only compares to Diffusion-SDF, omitting recent advances such as SDFusion (CVPR 2023), LucidDreamer (CVPR 2024), and other methods with physics-informed components. Missing comparisons to autoencoder-based frameworks (Craftsman, Clay) that may already have desirable physical properties (R1, R2, R3). This makes it difficult to assess the relative effectiveness of Fun3D.
- **Evaluation metric bias:** Physical strength is evaluated using FEM, which is also a core component of Fun3D’s training. This creates a circular dependency that may inflate perceived performance. Additional unbiased metrics (e.g., load capacity under bending/torsion, material uniformity, real-world physical tests like 3D printing) are needed (R3, R4).
- **Narrow physical scope:** The approach focuses on stress and strain via FEM, which does not capture the full complexity of real-world physical interactions (e.g., soft vs. rigid parts, internal bone/muscle structures in animals). The framework may not be general (R1, R3).
- **Positioning as generative but functioning as post-processing:** The pipeline separates initial generation (Shape-E) from physics optimization; the losses (design, geometry constraint, volume regularization) do not directly integrate generation and physics during training of the generative model. The claim of collaborative learning is not convincingly demonstrated (R4).
- **Insufficient evaluation details and cherry-picking concerns:** The quantitative evaluation (Table 1) does not specify how many shapes are used, how they are generated, or how a fair, unbiased set was ensured. Without this information, cherry-picking could greatly influence results (R4).
- **Overall quality of generated shapes is average:** The visual and physical quality of generated models is not impressive or clearly superior; they do not seem practically useful in the current stage (R1, R4).
- **Recommendation to use better initial mesh generation:** Instead of Shape-E, initial meshes should come from SOTA multi-view diffusion and reconstruction models (e.g., InstantMesh) for better performance and versatility (R2).
- **Need for broader physical evaluation:** Only compression tests are used; other loading conditions (bending, torsion) and material distribution analysis should be included (R2, R3).
- **Unclear conversion from SDF to density field:** The paper does not describe the parameters or transition speed of converting SDF to a density field relative to object scale, which is a minor technical omission (R4).
- **Applicability to objects with mixed soft/rigid parts:** For animals (Figure 6), strength depends on internal bone and muscle structure, not just external geometry. The paper does not justify how Fun3D’s physics optimization can improve such objects (R3).
- **Conflict with artist/architect design intent:** If a designer intentionally wants a shape that defies FEM analysis (e.g., for artistic reasons), the method does not provide a mechanism to balance physical strength against design constraints (R3).
