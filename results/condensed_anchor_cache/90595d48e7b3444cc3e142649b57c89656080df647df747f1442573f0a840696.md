## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DINO-WM, a world model that learns visual dynamics in latent space using frozen DINOv2 patch features and a transformer transition model trained via teacher forcing on offline trajectory data. It enables zero-shot planning via model predictive control with cross-entropy method by optimizing action sequences to match goal features. The method is evaluated on maze navigation (PointMaze), tabletop pushing (PushT), rope manipulation, and granular manipulation, outperforming DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 and demonstrating generalization to unseen configurations.

### Strengths
- Effectively leverages pre-trained DINOv2 features to learn a reconstruction-free latent world model from offline data, enabling task-agnostic planning without reward modeling or inverse dynamics.
- Demonstrates zero-shot planning across diverse tasks (PointMaze, PushT, Rope, Granular Manipulation) and generalizes to unseen environment configurations.
- Outperforms state-of-the-art model-based baselines (DreamerV3, TD-MPC2) and produces more plausible trajectories than video prediction method AVDC.
- Provides analysis comparing DINOv2 with other pre-trained backbones (e.g., R3M) for world modeling.
- Approach is simple, scalable, and uses only offline trajectory data, making it a potential candidate for robot foundation model training.
- The rope and granular manipulation environments demonstrate handling of difficult transition dynamics.

### Weaknesses
- Novelty is limited: learning a visual dynamics model in latent space with pre-trained features is not groundbreaking; leveraging existing vision models is not novel. (One reviewer found the approach not novel; another cited limited overall novelty.)
- The comparison with TD-MPC2 omitting reward signals is unfair, as reward is central to TD-MPC2’s optimization; this undermines its performance and the validity of the comparison.
- Insufficient benchmark diversity: missing standard image-based RL benchmarks (DM-Control, Minecraft), more complex tasks requiring accurate physical reasoning (e.g., Humanoid Run from DMControl, Move Chair from ManiSkill2), and robotics benchmarks (MetaWorld, RoboMimic, MimicGen, RLBench, FrankaKitchen, ManiSkill) that would better assess the world model’s ability to capture robot-object dynamics and long-horizon tasks. The TD-MPC2 baseline paper includes MetaWorld, DMControl, ManiSkill, and MyoSuite; a subset should be added for fair comparison.
- Lack of Q-learning may hinder handling long-horizon tasks and scalability to challenging environments.
- Missing comparisons and citations to recent diffusion- and transformer-based planning methods: AVDC, Chain-of-Thought Predictive Control, Diffusion World Model, and Genie. Section 4.2 implies quantitative comparison with diffusion methods but does not provide it; authors should either add such comparisons (at least for one task) or discuss why they are not possible.
- The claim about learning from “only passive data” is contradicted because DINO-WM training always requires action metadata; the text should be softened to acknowledge that both passive and action-conditioned data are used.
- The generalization experiments in section 4.5 test unseen configurations of environments seen during training, not entirely new environments as the text (L 450-451) implies; this description should be fixed.
- Training details are missing: what datasets were used, how trajectories were collected, and the scale of those datasets are not reported in the main paper or appendix.
- Adding a scaling laws analysis (evaluation performance vs. training dataset size) for at least one environment would strengthen the paper.
- A major assumption is that the offline dataset sufficiently covers the environment’s state-action space; random action datasets may not work for complex sequential tasks (e.g., RoboMimic, FrankaKitchen), highlighting a chicken-and-egg problem: an expert dataset is needed to train an accurate world model, but an accurate world model is needed to plan expert behavior.
- The model architecture description (Section 3.1.2) is confusing: it states the ViT is used as the transition model, but a decoder-only transformer is more accurate. The causal masking design (treating patch vectors of one observation as a whole unit) should be ablated – compare planning success rate with and without the per-image causal mask.
- The baseline comparison would be fairer if DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2 were also equipped with a DINOv2 image encoder; the impact of this change on planning success rate should be measured.
- Missing citation to Embed to Control (arXiv:1506.07365), which also investigates offline world model learning without rewards.
- Inference speed is not reported, which is important for tasks requiring reactive control.
- Several design decisions are not explored: whether the decoder could propagate gradients by first predicting the next state, decoding it, and comparing to the original image; whether rolling out trajectories N steps improves long-term consistency; and whether AVDC is fine-tuned on the authors’ datasets. These missing experiments/ablations weaken the analysis.
