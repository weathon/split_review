## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes EFMARL, an extension of the epigraph form optimization method to multi-agent safe RL under the zero constraint violation setting. It uses centralized training and distributed execution with graph neural networks to model policies. Experiments are performed in Multi-Particle Environments and Safe Multi-agent MuJoCo. Reviewers found the formulation novel and the work relevant, but raised substantial concerns about theoretical correctness, empirical thoroughness, and clarity.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1).
- Related work is well investigated (R1).
- Novel epigraph formulation for MARL; the extension of the epigraph form from single-agent to multi-agent is non‑trivial (R2).
- Good comparison to existing theoretical approaches, highlighting where current methods fail to handle constraint satisfaction and safety in MARL (R2).
- The proposed formulation is an interesting and valid alternative to the Constrained MDP formulation (R2).
- Graph neural networks are used to model distributed policies to improve coordination performance (R3).
- Extensive simulations are performed on standard MARL benchmarks (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical issues:**
    - Proposition 1 is wrong: neither \(h(x^k)\) nor \(V(x^{k+1}, z^{k+1}; \pi)\) is a function of \(u^k\). The proposition also seems unrelated to the algorithm (R3).
    - Equation (6) is not the correct Lagrangian for problem (3). The authors need to provide the correct Lagrangian form and ensure accurate comparison with Lagrangian methods; can the proposed method reduce computational complexity? (R3)
    - The proof of Theorem 1 is very hard to follow: both \(V^h\) and \(V^l\) are functions of \(x\) but this property is neglected. Formula (16) should be rewritten. Why is \(\varepsilon\) positive (line 846)? In the proof of Lemma 1, what happens if \(\varepsilon - \eta < \zeta < \varepsilon\)? Too many redundant notations exist (R3).
    - Unclear what global safety guarantees the epigraph‑based solution provides. The decentralized approach primarily ensures “constraint satisfaction” rather than task completion—agents may stay safe without achieving the global objective (R2).
    - Lack of convergence analysis (R1).

- **Insufficient empirical evaluation and baselines:**
    - Missing task‑completion metrics (mean reward or success rate). Only cost and safety rate are reported; these do not reveal whether agents actually complete the intended tasks. Without such measures the experiments do not fully validate the method’s ability to balance constraint satisfaction with task completion (R2). For example, a CMDP‑based policy that takes no action could match zero violation but not complete the task (R2).
    - Comparison with existing Safe MARL approaches (e.g., works [A,B,C,D]) is missing. A brief discussion and empirical comparison against one or two of these methods is needed (R2).
    - Comparison with Lagrangian‑based methods is insufficient. The performance of Lagrangian methods depends on parameter settings; fine‑tuning those parameters could produce better results (R1). R3 also requires a more accurate comparison with the Lagrangian method.
    - Scalability limitations of using Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) as the backbone for \(z\)-conditioned policies are not discussed. GNNs may introduce computational and structural limitations in larger multi‑agent scenarios (R2). Specific computational challenges arising from centralized training with an increasing number of agents need to be addressed (R1).

- **Clarity and writing issues:**
    - The paper is poorly written and very hard to follow (R3).
    - Inconsistency in formulation: Equation (2) defines cumulative cost with \(l(x^k, \pi(x^k))\), while Equation (3) uses a cost function \(J(x)\) without clear connection (R2).
    - Equation (7) contains \(k \geq \tau\), but \(\tau\) is never defined or discussed (R2).
    - Line 235 refers to “total value function,” which should be “total cost value function”; using “value function” in an RL context is misleading (R2).
    - Homogeneous vs. heterogeneous agents? Section 3.1 suggests homogeneity (shared observation space), but Section 4.2 uses an advantage decomposition method that implies heterogeneity (R3).
    - In the policy and value function update section, the notation should reflect per‑agent policies and constraint‑value functions (e.g., \(\pi_{\theta^i}\), \(V_{\psi^i}^h\)). The authors should specify whether MAPPO or HAPPO is used and incorporate the neural‑network update formulas in the appendix (R3).
    - How is the sampling interval of \(z\) determined? What happens if the interval is too large or too small? (R3)
    - Why is \(z\) updated during the policy and value function update process (lines 295‑296)? (R3)
    - Explain how to obtain the optimal \(z\) once the updates for both the policy and the value function are completed (R3).
    - It is confusing that the outer problem is solved during distributed execution; this could be done during centralized training, and sharing the scalar \(z_i\) over an all‑to‑all or centralized network would not increase communication cost (R3).
    - The Lagrangian multiplier \(\lambda\) may also decrease depending on various Lagrangian version configurations (R1).
