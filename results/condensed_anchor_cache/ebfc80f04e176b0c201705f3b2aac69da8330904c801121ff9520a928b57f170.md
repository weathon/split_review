## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a structural multi-view clustering network via heterogeneous random walks, constructing a heterogeneous graph with sample and view nodes, and using a random walk strategy to learn view consensus and high-order structural information. Experiments on five datasets show competitive performance.

### Strengths
- The investigated multi-view clustering problem is significant and of interest to the community.
- The idea of constructing a heterogeneous graph is interesting and well-defined; introducing sample nodes among views as a bridge for multi-view interaction and the random walk strategy align well with learning the heterogeneous graph.
- A unified random walk process is a good strategy for aligning common information among multiple views for clustering.
- The paper is well written and easy to read (per one reviewer, score 6, confidence 3).
- Experiments are comprehensive with multiple methods, ablation studies, and parameter analysis, organized around five research questions.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and similarity to DIVIDE (2024 CVPR):** Two reviewers (scores 3, confidence 5 and 4) note the paper’s contribution is incremental and bears substantial similarities to the DIVIDE method. The random walk-based proximity measure, model architecture, Siamese encoders, cross-view projectors, and loss functions largely follow from DIVIDE. The main difference is the adoption of heterogeneous random walks instead of per-view transition matrices. The novelty of this work is unclear.
- **Insufficient demonstration of heterogeneous graph advantage:** The advantage of the proposed heterogeneous graph (the main claimed contribution) is not clearly demonstrated. Only one ablation study (Table 3) is provided, which is not sufficient. The authors should deeply analyze and discuss why and how it works (e.g., why a heterogeneous graph is preferable to directly learning among views).
- **Presentation gaps and incomplete methodology description:**
  - The transition matrix \(M\) is defined twice (row-wise normalization and Eq. (10)); it should be formally defined at first appearance.
  - The variable \(T\) is not clearly defined (what is its value range?).
  - In Figure 2, “Momentum encoder” modules and “Moving average” procedures are never addressed in the text.
  - The paper does not explain how final clustering results are acquired.
- **Missing theoretical and convergence analysis:**
  - No theoretical support is given for the expected performance improvement.
  - No convergence proof or guarantee for the algorithm (one reviewer, score 6, also raised this).
  - Computational complexity is not provided for Algorithm 1 or other methods; the scalability of the pairwise similarity matrices, multi-hop transition matrix, and cross-view projections is a concern (asked by two reviewers).
- **Missing broader discussion and comparisons:**
  - The formulation of heterogeneous random walk is, in essence, the average of proximity graph transition matrices from all views—a technique already studied in “Spectral Clustering and Transductive Learning with Multiple Views” (Zhou et al., 2017). This should be discussed.
  - A broader discussion on why the proposed method performs better than others, clarifying connections and differences with existing methods, is needed.
  - More recent multi-view clustering works should be compared (e.g., Dong et al., 2023, on UCI-Digit).
  - Experiments could be strengthened by including more datasets; the average number of views (2.8) is rather small.
- **Result discrepancies:** On the Scene15 dataset, the reported ACC/NMI/ARI (0.431, 0.435, 0.259) are much lower than those reported in the DIVIDE paper (0.491, 0.487, 0.316). On the Prokaryotic dataset, ACCMVC’s results are also much lower than in their original paper.
- **Question about edge pruning:** Is the edge pruning technique in Section 4.7 effective for improving efficiency? Empirical clustering efficiency against baselines is not reported.

*Note: One reviewer (score 6, confidence 3) was more positive, praising the problem value and writing, but also raised the missing complexity, theoretical support, convergence, and broader discussion points listed above. The other two reviewers (score 3, confidence 5 and 4) were more critical, especially on novelty and insufficient demonstration of the main contribution.*
