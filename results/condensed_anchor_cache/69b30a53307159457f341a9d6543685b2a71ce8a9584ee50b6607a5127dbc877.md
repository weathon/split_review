## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Graph-Guided sub-Goal representation Generation RL (G4RL), a graph encoder-decoder architecture that evaluates unseen states in Goal-conditioned Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (GCHRL). The encoder-decoder is trained on state graphs generated during exploration, and produces high- and low-level intrinsic rewards to enhance existing GCHRL methods. Empirical results on AntMaze and AntGather show performance improvements over HIRO and HRAC in both dense and sparse reward settings.

### Strengths

- **Innovative graph encoder-decoder**: The introduction of a graph encoder-decoder provides a new perspective for GCHRL, enabling online construction of state graphs and potentially more effective subgoal representations.
- **Generalizability / integrability**: G4RL can be incorporated into any existing GCHRL algorithm, making it versatile across different methods.
- **Clear motivation and problem focus**: The paper addresses an important problem (integrating graphs with GCHRL) and provides good motivation for its relevance.
- **Clear writing and presentation**: The paper is clearly written and easy to follow; the proposed method is explained effectively.
- **Empirical improvement on baseline tasks**: G4RL improves upon HIRO and HRAC on the AntMaze and AntGather tasks.
- **Originality**: The G4RL approach is presented with a degree of originality, and the presentation is clear.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited empirical scope**: The method is only tested on two environments (AntMaze, AntGather) and compared only to pre-2020 methods (HIRO, HRAC). Recent subgoal representation learning methods (e.g., LESSON, HESS, HLPS, Hilbert representations) are missing, limiting assessment of relative advantages.
- **Insufficient ablation and hyperparameter analysis**: Key parameters ($\epsilon_d$, $\alpha_h$, $\alpha_l$, $N$, number of timesteps $K$ for high-level goals) lack comprehensive sensitivity analysis. How does $\epsilon$ depend on the environment and state-space features? How does $K$ vary across tasks? What impact do these parameters have on algorithm performance? The experimental results do not adequately support the claimed significance of these parameters.
- **Method does not learn subgoal representations**: Despite the title, G4RL predicts state affinity rather than learning subgoal representations; it lacks strong positioning within the subgoal representation learning literature (only one relevant work cited).
- **Strong similarity to HRAC raises concerns**:
  1. G4RL constructs graphs by hard-thresholding distances in state feature space (making it feature- and hyperparameter-dependent), whereas HRAC uses K-step affinity along trajectories.
  2. HRAC applies a contrastive loss to regularize the latent subgoal space; how does G4RL regularize representation learning in the latent space? 
  3. The rationale for combining G4RL with HRAC (HRAC-G4RL) is unclear – does G4RL require HRAC’s regularization?
- **World model limitation**: The learned world model (graph encoder-decoder) is influenced by the current policy distribution and may not accurately reflect the true underlying world model.
- **Modest performance gain vs added complexity**: The increased complexity introduced by the graph encoder-decoder does not yield substantial performance improvements over existing HRAC, raising concerns about practical implementation and debugging costs.
- **Lack of comparison with other representation methods**: No quantitative or qualitative demonstration that the graph encoder-decoder effectively extracts spatial information or that subgoal spatial distance representation is superior to alternatives (e.g., Hilbert representations from [Park et al., 2024]).
- **Limited experimental rigor**: Some results show methods performing similarly; running more seeds or increasing task difficulty could better separate method performance. The number of seeds and how they were chosen is not specified.
- **Implementation details missing**: How is the state representation function $\phi$ implemented (neural network, dimensionality reduction)? Specific details are needed.
- **Minority reviewer is more positive**: One reviewer (score 5) noted the approach has originality and clear presentation, and indicated willingness to increase the score if the above weaknesses are addressed. All other reviewers gave score 3.
