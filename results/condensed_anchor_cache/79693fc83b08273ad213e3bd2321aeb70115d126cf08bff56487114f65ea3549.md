## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes CoT3DRef, an interpretable 3D visual grounding framework that formulates the task as sequence-to-sequence (Seq2Seq) prediction of a chain of anchors before the final target, mimicking human perception. To supervise the intermediate steps, the authors generate pseudo-labels using a grammar parser, GPT-3.5 for logical ordering, and rule-based box matching. The framework can be integrated into existing architectures and demonstrates consistent performance gains on Nr3D, Sr3D, and ScanRefer benchmarks, along with notable data efficiency—on Sr3D, matching SOTA with only 10% of the training data.

### Strengths
- **Technical novelty**: The chain-of-thoughts (CoT) approach introduces a new paradigm for 3D visual grounding, predicting a sequence of objects (anchors) leading to the target, which adds interpretability and aligns with human reasoning.
- **Data curation**: The pseudo-label generation pipeline (grammar parser, GPT-3.5, rule-based box matching) enables CoT training without human annotation, making the system scalable.
- **Performance gains**: The method improves multiple baselines (at least four reported) and achieves state-of-the-art results across three benchmarks (Nr3D, Sr3D, ScanRefer), with comprehensive experiments and ablation studies.
- **Data efficiency**: On Sr3D, the framework matches SOTA performance using only 10% of the training data, a significant advantage for low-data settings.
- **Applicability**: CoT3DRef can be easily integrated into various existing architectures, showing versatility.

### Weaknesses
- **Reliance on foundation models**: A reviewer notes that the core novelty comes from leveraging GPT-3.5 and other foundation models, making this a system-level contribution rather than a principled one (Reviewer 1). This reviewer sees no major weaknesses beyond this, while others raise more technical concerns.
- **Insufficient comparison with PhraseRefer**: The idea of attending to all mentioned objects (anchors) was previously proposed in PhraseRefer (using human annotations). The paper does not give enough introduction to PhraseRefer, making Table 4 hard to interpret; numbers for some baselines (e.g., MVT with PhraseRefer) appear missing. A clearer comparison with this related line of work is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Pseudo-label generation concerns** (Reviewer 4):
  - The FIND function in Algorithm 1 is not detailed (e.g., how relations between objects are known).
  - Algorithm 1 receives object proposals P, likely from a 3D object detector requiring extra training; this could bottleneck anchor localization.
  - The elaborate pseudo-label generation process makes the framework *not* data-efficient in data preparation, even though it is data-efficient in 3D grounding training.
- **Interpretability limitations**: GPT-3.5 extracts logical order from the utterance, but the logical relations between objects are not explicitly modeled; instead they are learned implicitly in the Seq2Seq network, limiting interpretability (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing failure case analysis**: The paper claims interpretability helps identify failure cases, but no detailed analysis or examples are provided (Reviewer 2). This overlaps with a question: *“Can you provide a detailed analysis or examples of the identification of failure cases?”*
- **Missing broader impact discussion**: The paper does not discuss broader impacts or ethical considerations for applications like autonomous driving and robotics (Reviewer 2).
- **Questions about label assignment and path generation** (Reviewer 3):
  - In Sec.3.3, how are objects matched to the closest ScanNet class? If a finer-grain set like ScanNet200 is used, does it provide richer semantics, and is the same semantic level used across all experiments?
  - If auxiliary data (PhraseRefer/ScanEnts GT anchor labels) is available, does the method still require GPT for the path? Performance in that case is not reported.
  - The MVT baseline improves notably with more data (Table 4 vs. 5). Could the chain-of-objects knowledge be learned implicitly from large enough datasets, diminishing the advantage of explicit supervision?
