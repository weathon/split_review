## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper reports three key observations in heterogeneous federated learning: (1) client performance correlates positively with layer similarity across clients, (2) shallow layers have higher similarity than deep layers, and (3) smoother gradient distributions correspond to higher layer similarity. Based on these, the authors propose InCo Aggregation, which uses internal cross-layer gradients (mixing shallow and deep layer gradients within the server model) to improve deep-layer similarity without extra client communication. The method is validated on multiple homogeneous FL methods (FedAvg, FedProx, FedNova, Scaffold, MOON) and heterogeneous settings, showing improved accuracy. Three reviewers assign a score of 8 and one reviewer assigns a 5, indicating a minority concern about the scale of experiments and narrative clarity.

### Strengths
- The discovered observations about layer similarity, the shallow–deep gradient pattern, and the connection to gradient smoothness are interesting and valuable for future FL research.
- The paper combines experimental scientific discovery with system engineering – using observations to drive a practical aggregation method that consistently improves accuracy.
- Writing is well organized and clear; visualizations (e.g., Figures 4 and 6) effectively convey model splitting and gradient divergence.
- The proposed InCo Aggregation is versatile, shown to work with several homogeneous FL methods and datasets, and experiments are sufficient in breadth.
- The paper presents clear diagrams and a clear overall narrative.

### Weaknesses
- The reasons for the observed relationships (why higher layer similarity and smoother gradients lead to better accuracy) are not well explained; Section 5.4 makes only a mostly unsuccessful attempt.  
  – Reviewer 1: lacking depth of understanding.  
  – Reviewer 3: the manuscript does not adequately explain how cross-layer gradient adjustments ameliorate the effects of model heterogeneity.
- Section 3.3, which is central to the improvement, is too short and lacks justification for the specific design choices (e.g., the form of expression (1) and the inequality constraint on gopt vs. g0).  
  – Reviewer 1 asks: why not a cost that increases with decreasing dot product? Is there a fundamental justification for expression (1), or might other formulations work as well or better?
- The primary experiments use a small model (three convolutional layers), raising concerns about generalizability to larger, more complex architectures.  
  – Reviewer 3 notes this explicitly and asks whether the approach can be applied to complex models (e.g., ResNets, Transformers).  
  – Reviewer 2 also asks whether similarity patterns hold for Vision Transformers and how that would impact InCo Aggregation.
- “System level heterogeneity” is mentioned early but only loosely defined; readers must infer that it refers to model and data heterogeneity.  
  – Reviewer 2 suggests defining it earlier and explicitly connecting to how the method addresses those axes.  
  – Reviewer 3 notes a lack of clear problem definition and notations before the method is introduced, hindering comprehension.
- There is no explicit definition of which layers count as "deep" vs. "shallow"; the “cross-environment similarity” section implies a per-stage distinction, but it is not formally stated.  
  – Reviewer 2 asks for an explicit definition and whether the pattern carries over to ViTs.
- Experimental details for several figures are missing: the specific layer indices/types for gradient distribution plots (Figs. 2, 3), the exact setups for Figs. 1–3, and the behavior of Fig. 3 across multiple trials (to account for SGD noise).  
  – Reviewer 2 also requests gradient plots for Stage 2 layers and standard deviation values for InCo-based methods.
- The paper does not discuss the scope of applicability: where InCo Aggregation is not suitable (e.g., when models are from completely different families or weight matrices do not align).  
  – Reviewer 2 emphasizes this as critical for practical use.  
  – Reviewer 4 raises the specific issue of merging cross-layer gradients when layer-wise gradient sizes differ across models.
- The CKA similarity metric is important to the paper but is not explained in sufficient detail.  
  – Reviewer 4: need more details on CKA.  
  – Reviewer 3: the manuscript lacks background on the CAK similarity metric and a clear narrative linking previous works to the proposed method.
- Figure 6 is unclear; indicating the positions of client and global optima would better convey the optimization landscape.  
  – Reviewer 3: current visualization does not effectively communicate how the method navigates the space compared to standard federated learning.
- Lack of analysis of computational and communication overhead, which could be significant in large-scale FL.  
  – Reviewer 4 asks for a more detailed comparison with state-of-the-art methods (e.g., FedROLEX, ScaleFL) in terms of performance, communication, and computational complexity.  
  – Reviewer 2 asks for a breakdown of how experimental settings differ from the original works cited in Table 3.
- Additional missing analyses: whether stronger correlations with gradient smoothness exist (e.g., peak density, variance, skewness, kurtosis) – Reviewer 2; relationship between findings and residual connections in ResNets – Reviewer 1.
