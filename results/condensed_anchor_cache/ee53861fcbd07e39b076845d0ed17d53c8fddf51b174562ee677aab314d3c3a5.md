## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of non-contrastive self‑supervised learning (e.g., BYOL, SimSiam) from the L2 loss studied by Tian et al. (2021) to the cosine loss, which includes feature normalization. Under assumptions such as isotropic Gaussian inputs, linear projection/prediction layers, and the thermodynamic (infinite‑width) limit, the authors show that the cosine loss induces sixth‑order eigenmode dynamics (vs. third‑order for L2 loss), and that a stable equilibrium can dynamically emerge even when only collapsed solutions exist under the L2 loss. The analysis reveals three regimes (acute, stable, recovery) depending on the norms of the weight matrices. Numerical simulations on linear networks support the theory.

### Strengths

- **Rigorous technical analysis** that is clear and easy to follow (R1, R2, R3).
- **Important gap addressed**: the role of feature normalization in non‑contrastive SSL, which was underexplored theoretically (R4).
- **Novel dynamic behavior**: the sixth‑order dynamics lead to a stable equilibrium that emerges even from initially collapsed solutions, showing that feature normalization robustly prevents collapse (R4).
- **Proof of norm concentration**: demonstrates that under proper initialization, feature norms concentrate around a constant, which justifies the subsequent analysis (R3).
- **Assumptions well justified** by the thermodynamic limit and verification via numerical simulations (R2, R4).
- **Numerical simulations validate theoretical predictions** on linear networks (R2, R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Stringent and potentially contradictory assumptions**:
  - The analysis assumes feature norms are nearly equal (high‑dimensional limit), which reviewers argue contradicts the goal of studying feature normalization (R1). The same assumption (isotropic Gaussian inputs, infinite width, and constant norms in Section 5) is considered too stringent and far from realistic settings (R3). A mixture of isotropic Gaussians is suggested as a more plausible alternative (R3).
  - Some reviewers find the assumptions reasonable (R2), but others stress that they severely limit the practical relevance (R1, R3). The increasing error between \(W\) and \(W^\top\) and the decrease of \(N_\phi\) and \(N_\psi\) in the experiments are not fully validated (R3).

- **Missing comparisons and experiments**:
  - **Prior work**: The paper does not cite Halvagal et al. (2023) *Implicit variance regularization in non‑contrastive SSL*, whose eigenmode dynamics appear extremely similar. A comparison and discussion of implicit variance regularization is necessary (R2).
  - **Real‑world networks**: Numerical experiments are limited to linear networks; a similar figure for a real architecture (e.g., ResNet, or at least the linear projection head) would confirm the theory’s applicability (R2). The dynamics in more complex scenarios (real datasets, non‑linear networks) are not investigated (R4).
  - **Relation to BarlowTwins / VICReg**: The authors mention these methods, but how their behavior relates to the feature‑normalization analysis is not clarified (R4).

- **Incomplete explanation and analysis**:
  - The notions of “sixth‑order” and “third‑order dynamics” are introduced without a clear definition or intuition; their significance for convergence and stability is not explained (R1).
  - The three regimes in Section 5.2 appear to be derived solely by visually categorizing the experimental plot (Figure 2), which is a weak justification. A detailed equilibrium analysis (at least in the appendix) is needed (R2).
  - In the stable regime, the equilibrium point \(p^+\) is a saddle point, so norms will increase as eigenmodes grow—raising the question of whether acute and stable regimes might alternate (R2).
  - Figure 6c is hard to read: it is unclear whether the intervals are theoretical, what the theoretical saddle‑point value is, and how representative a single eigenmode is of the entire spectrum (R2).

- **Limited novelty and insight**:
  - The paper does not convincingly articulate the gap it fills beyond prior work (R1). The existing conclusions do not provide substantial new insight into non‑contrastive learning dynamics (R3). The analysis could focus on proving convergence rather than simplifying to closed‑form dynamics (R3).

- **Minor technical issues**: 
  - “Folklore says” is an unusual citation phrasing (R2).
  - Assumption 2: \(\Sigma = I\) seems superfluous after \(D = N(0, I)\) (R2).
  - After Lemma 3: \(\Phi x'\) is not defined (R2).
  - Equation (5): \(\hat H\) is not defined (R2).
  - After Equation (9): “unite learning rate” should be “unit learning rate” (R2).
