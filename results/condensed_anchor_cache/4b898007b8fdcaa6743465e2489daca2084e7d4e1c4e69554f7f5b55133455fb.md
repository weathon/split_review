## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an Improved MLP (IMLP) module for vision transformers, introducing the arbitrary GeLU (AGeLU) activation function and integrating multiple non-linear instances to augment non-linearity, thus reducing hidden dimensions. The module also includes a spatial enhancement part using depthwise convolution. Theoretical analysis frames MLP as a non-linearity generator. Experiments on ImageNet-1K with several ViT variants show reduced FLOPs and parameters while maintaining accuracy.

### Strengths
- The idea is simple and easily applicable to any vision transformer, irrespective of self-attention modifications (Reviewer 1).
- Theoretical analysis delving into the math of the MLP module is provided, establishing MLP as a non-linearity generator and offering insights for parameter selection through bounds derivation (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The non-linearity augmentation approach spans both channel (via AGeLU and concatenation) and spatial dimensions (via depthwise convolution), offering a comprehensive design (Reviewer 3).
- Empirical validation is compelling and extensive for some reviewers, with a variety of architectures (isotopic and stage-wise) and tasks (classification, segmentation, detection) demonstrating effectiveness in reducing complexity without accuracy loss (Reviewers 3, 4). Reviewer 4 explicitly highlights validation across diverse models.
- The paper is well-written, clear, and thought-provoking (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
**Performance and Contribution Concerns**
- Performance gains are marginal or non-existent in many cases. The proposed activation fails to improve larger models like Deit_B and LVT-R4, and degrades performance on Swin-B and Poolformer-M48 (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 notes no solid improvement from AGeLU in Table 3.
- The primary source of improvement appears to come from the depthwise convolution component and knowledge distillation (KD), not from the AGeLU activation itself. Table 2 indicates gains are largely due to KD and spatial enhancement; the contribution of AGeLU alone is not isolated (Reviewers 1, 4). The reliance on KD requires a teacher model, meaning training budgets are not preserved equally (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 4 additionally requests an ablation where spatial enhancement is placed after GeLU (as ‘a’ in Figure 4) to isolate the effect of enhanced non-linearity in parameter reduction.
- The method introduces hardware-unfriendly operations (depthwise convolution) but provides no actual latencies or throughput measurements; FLOPs and parameters are not sufficient for real-world efficiency evaluation (Reviewer 2).
- The performance drop when adding GeLU with four times the number of channels (Table 3) is surprising and unexplained. Since the fc layer is linear, doubling the output should not cause such degradation; further clarification and analysis are needed (Reviewer 4).

**Theoretical and Ablation Gaps**
- The theory lacks justification for why AGeLU with concatenation is necessary; the rationale for splitting into two nonlinear functions (AGeLU and AGeLU') is not addressed (Reviewer 1).
- The ablation study is incomplete. Specifically, it does not explore varying the number of divisions in Equation (5) (e.g., four parts instead of two) or using different activation functions in each division. Such experiments would illuminate the impact on learning and efficiency (Reviewer 3).
- There is no comparison with other non-linear blocks such as the bottleneck in ResNet or linear bottleneck in MobileNetV2, which would contextualize the IMLP module's advantages in parameter efficiency and performance (Reviewer 3).

**Missing Comparisons and Experimental Scope**
- Missing comparisons with simple activation functions like SoftPlus, ELU, ReLU6, Swish, and other recent activation works (Mish, Padé, Smooth Maximum Unit) (Reviewer 1).
- The proposed IMLP module has only been tested on older models (ViT, Swin, PoolFormer); validation on more recent high-accuracy models like iFormer is needed to show versatility and efficacy in current vision transformer landscapes (Reviewer 3). This contrasts with Reviewer 4's praise for diverse validation; the set is still limited relative to state-of-the-art timelines.
- The downstream tasks reported in the Appendix are limited, with only a few backbones (Reviewer 1).

**Design and Implementation Ambiguities**
- It is unclear whether the kernel size for depthwise convolution is consistently set to 5 for large-scale models (DeiT-B, Swin-B). No explanation or ablation on kernel size impact is provided (Reviewer 4).
- The need for an additional shortcut after depthwise convolution and why BatchNorm should follow it is not explained (Reviewer 1).
- The proposed variant is not exclusive to vision transformers; it could also be applied in architectures like ConvNeXt, which share similar building blocks (Reviewer 1).

**Contrasting Views on Strengths**
- While some reviewers found the theoretical analysis solid and insightful (Reviewers 1 acknowledged it, Reviewer 3 praised it as solid), Reviewer 1 noted that theory does not justify the concatenation operation.
- While Reviewer 3 found the empirical validation compelling across many architectures, Reviewer 3 themselves pointed out the need for validation on more recent models; Reviewers 1 and 2 found the performance improvements insufficient or unrelated to the core contribution.
