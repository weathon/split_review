## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a dropout method to mitigate overfitting in bilevel training tasks. It formulates a stochastic bilevel optimization model incorporating the distribution of dropout masks, analyzes how the dropout rate affects the hypergradient, and provides convergence guarantees (to an 𝜖-stationary point of the dropout bilevel objective). Empirically, the method is tested on a data cleaning problem, showing improved test accuracy and stability. Reviewers agree the idea is novel but raise concerns about limited experimental scope, comparison with early stopping, and the depth of theoretical contributions relative to existing work.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and relevance:** Incorporating dropout into bilevel optimization to combat overfitting is a new idea in the bilevel community and has been tested effective (R1, R2, R3, R4). Overfitting has not been fully investigated in this area (R4). The paper paves the way for adapting other state-of-the-art bilevel methods to account for dropout (R3).
- **Theoretical analysis:** The analysis of dropout by modeling the mask distribution is considered thorough and novel (R2). The paper provides convergence guarantees and investigates how the dropout rate influences the hypergradient (R1, R3). The theoretical analysis is detailed (R1). R3 praises the study of convergence properties.
- **Empirical demonstration:** Empirical tests on data cleaning problems demonstrate efficacy in reducing overfitting, with results showing improved test accuracy and stability (R2, R3).
- **Presentation:** The paper is generally well-written and easy to follow (R1). R3 rated presentation excellent (4/4). R2 and R3 found the paper solid and novel overall.

### Weaknesses
- **Experimental scope limited to data cleaning:** Multiple reviewers note that the empirical evaluation is restricted to data cleaning problems (R1, R3, R4). The paper would benefit from experiments on other bilevel optimization tasks (e.g., hyperparameter optimization, meta-learning) to demonstrate broader applicability (R1, R3, R4). R4 specifically states the paper “looks like it studies data cleaning problem rather than the bilevel problem” and requests discussion and experiments on other settings.
- **Lack of comparison with early stopping:** R1 and R4 point out that the results figures suggest early stopping could resolve overfitting, even without dropout, and that test accuracy without dropout is sometimes higher. R4 explicitly requests an experimental comparison between dropout and early stopping to demonstrate the necessity of dropout.
- **Dataset simplicity:** R3 notes that using MNIST, a small and simplistic dataset, may not showcase the method’s potential or limitations in real-world complex scenarios.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis missing:** R3 observes a lack of comprehensive exploration of sensitivity to hyperparameters, especially the dropout rate. More analysis on how the dropout rate affects convergence speed and final performance is needed.
- **Theoretical contributions questioned:**
    - R1 and R4 consider the theoretical analysis not particularly challenging in the current context. R1 contrasts with [2] (Ji et al., ICML 2021), which provides a more comprehensive framework and convergence rate guarantees for stochastic bilevel optimization, and suggests the paper should address the extra challenges in such literature.
    - R4 argues that Theorem 2 shows dropout rate only influences the constant term of the upper bound, not the convergence rate, and does not demonstrate improvement even for the constant term. Likewise, Lemma 1 (variance term) has the same issue. R4 expects the theorem to provide inspiration about dropout rate selection.
    - R2 notes that the convergence analysis is built on a stationarity measure of the dropout bilevel objective, and suggests drawing connections to the original bilevel objective’s stationarity measure to justify the relaxation error and guarantee convergence for the original objective. This would strengthen the analysis.
- **Insufficient motivation for dropout in bilevel:** R2 asks why avoiding overfitting is particularly important in bilevel optimization—does the bilevel structure exacerbate overfitting? The motivation could be explained more.
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms (placed here):**
    - R1 asks for distinguishing factors from prior work [1] (Bae & Grosse, Delta-STN, NeurIPS 2020), beyond theoretical analysis.
    - R1 asks about the relationship between the objective after dropout and the original objective.
    - R3 asks how the method might be applied to other machine learning tasks beyond data cleaning.
    - R4 asks for discussion of technical difficulties in applying dropout analysis to bilevel optimization (to help understand the theoretical contribution).
- **Minor presentation issue:** R1 notes figures should be accompanied by brief captions. R4 gave presentation score 2/4.
- **Reviewer disagreement:** While R2 and R3 (scores 8) consider the theoretical analysis novel and thorough, R1 and R4 (scores 6) raise concerns about its depth relative to existing work and its practical insight. This contrast is retained to reflect the split among reviewers.
