## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces SOAP (ShampoO with Adam in the Preconditioner’s eigenbasis), a simpler and computationally efficient optimizer that improves and stabilizes Shampoo. The authors establish a formal connection showing Shampoo (with the ½ power) is equivalent to running Adafactor in Shampoo’s eigenbasis. SOAP continually updates the running average of the second moment (like Adam) but in the slowly changing eigenbasis of Shampoo’s preconditioner, introducing only one additional hyperparameter (preconditioning frequency) compared to Adam. Empirically, on language model pre-training with 360M and 660M parameter models, SOAP reduces iterations by over 40% and wall-clock time by over 35% vs. AdamW, and by approximately 20% vs. Shampoo in the large batch regime. The paper also provides hyperparameter sweeps in the appendix and analyzes memory consumption.

### Strengths

*   **Well-written and well-organized:** The paper is exceptionally clear and easy to follow, with excellent summaries of prior work and context (R1, R2, R4).
*   **Novel theoretical connection:** Draws a new connection between Shampoo and Adafactor, showing Shampoo is running Adafactor in its eigenbasis, which has not been done before (R2, R3, R4).
*   **Significant practical relevance:** Has strong implications for large language model training (R1). The method is directly applicable to LLM pre-training, a very relevant setting (R4).
*   **Thorough experiments:** Includes detailed experiments on batch size scaling and efficiency; provides memory consumption analysis in the appendix (R2, R4). Hyperparameter sweeps are included, avoiding common pitfalls (R4).
*   **Promising results:** SOAP outperforms both AdamW and Shampoo on relevant metrics with convincing improvements (R3, R4). Baselines are appropriate (direct comparison with Shampoo and AdamW) (R4).
*   **Code provided:** Authors release code for reproducibility (R2).
*   **Good naming and humor:** The progression (Shampoo, Rinse, Lather, SOAP) is noted positively (R3, R4).
*   **Acknowledgement of limitations:** The paper appropriately acknowledges areas left to future work (R4).

### Weaknesses

*   **Limited scale of experiments:** Evaluation is confined to 360M and 660M parameter language models. It remains unclear whether SOAP scales effectively to larger models (e.g., 1B, 7B parameters) or to fine-tuning settings (R1, R2). One reviewer (R2) notes this is the main weakness, acknowledged by the authors.
*   **Lack of task diversity:** Performance is only assessed on language modeling pre-training. Its generalization to other domains such as vision tasks (e.g., image classification) or graph tasks (e.g., node prediction) is unknown (R1). Likewise, performance with emerging architectures like Linear Recurrent Models (e.g., Mamba) is not explored (R2).
*   **Missing baseline comparisons:**
    *   Adafactor is not included as a baseline, yet SOAP is motivated by the Shampoo–Adafactor connection. This makes it difficult to isolate SOAP’s specific contribution (R1).
    *   GaLore is not included as a baseline. Reviewers question how SOAP differs from running GaLore in “full” mode with Adam/Adafactor, and whether using power iterations to approximate SVD in GaLore (as suggested by [Liang et al., 2024]) would work; this should be compared (R3).
*   **Insufficient analysis of why SOAP outperforms:** The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the optimization dynamics or convergence properties that explain why SOAP outperforms both Shampoo and Adam (R1). There is no clear rationale for the observed improvement (R1).
*   **Missing downstream evaluations:** Evaluations on checkpoint quality (e.g., perplexity downstream tasks) are not provided for the different optimizers (R3).
*   **Unclear explanation of frequency degradation:** The paper notes that computing Shampoo’s eigendecomposition less frequently degrades performance, but does not explain why higher frequency leads to worse results (R3). Intuitively more frequent updates should give a better eigenspace approximation (R3).
*   **Contribution overlap concerns:** One reviewer (R3) finds the contribution beyond the algorithmic insight unclear, suggesting the practical implementation may be a special case of GaLore or merely a difference in the number of SVD iterations (R3).
*   **Minor presentation issues:** A typo on line 350 (`n -> In`) (R2); using φ instead of ℒ for loss is unusual (R4).
*   **Disagreement on overall severity:** One reviewer (R4) found no substantial weaknesses, rating the paper 8/8/8 with high soundness and presentation, while other reviewers (R1–R3) raised the concerns listed above, with scores 5, 6, 6 and lower soundness/presentation ratings. This contrast in overall assessment is noted.
