## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Seq-VCR, an adaptation of variance-covariance regularization (VICReg/VCReg) for sequence models, to prevent representation collapse in intermediate transformer layers. Combined with dummy pause tokens, the method improves arithmetic reasoning on multi-digit multiplication, arithmetic expressions, and longest increasing subsequence tasks. Results show that on 5×5 integer multiplication, Seq-VCR with pause tokens achieves 99.5% exact match accuracy, outperforming same-size models (0%) and GPT-4 with 5-shot CoT (44%). The paper argues that preventing representation collapse is key to enhancing reasoning without explicit CoT supervision.

### Strengths

- The adaptation of VICReg to transformers (Seq-VCR) is novel and original; the application of variance-covariance regularization to language models is, to the best of reviewers’ knowledge, new (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Experimental results on arithmetic reasoning are compelling and show large improvements, especially on tasks where LLMs typically fail (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- The paper provides detailed analysis of the regularization effects, including entropy charts, learning dynamics, and digit-by-digit accuracy on multiplication tasks (Reviewer 1).
- The method is computationally attractive compared to chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, as it does not require generating explicit intermediate tokens (Reviewer 2).
- The identification of representation collapse as a limiting factor in transformer reasoning is interesting and could be high-impact (Reviewers 3, 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Evidence for representation collapse is insufficient.** Only two entropy charts are provided (Figure 2), covering narrow tasks. On one chart (Figure 2a) the collapse appears minimal, and on the other (Figure 2b) adding pause tokens increases collapse rather than decreasing it. A broader study over more tasks and models is needed to substantiate the claim (Reviewer 3). The experiment was only done on GPT-2; it is unclear whether this phenomenon occurs in larger models like LLaMA 3/3.1 (Reviewer 4; also echoed by Reviewer 3's suggestion to study general language modeling tasks).
- **Generalization to other tasks is not tested.** The method is only evaluated on arithmetic and LIS tasks. It is unclear whether Seq-VCR degrades performance on general language modeling or reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MMLU, translation). If it does, the technique’s applicability is limited (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). One reviewer notes that without this ablation, it is impossible to judge whether the result is a high-impact advance or a trivial one (Reviewer 3).
- **Writing and clarity are poor.** The paper is described as hastily and sloppily written, difficult to follow in places. Crucial replication details are missing (e.g., promised Appendix A was not provided). Many design choices are unexplained (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 1 also notes lack of contextualization on pause tokens).
- **Pause token integration is underspecified.** The paper does not describe where and how pause tokens are added to the input. The number of pause tokens is task-specific and haphazard; it is unclear how to choose this number, whether it correlates with task complexity, and whether the method generalizes beyond the tested setups (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Equation (3) attribution and implementation details are unclear.** The loss is lifted almost verbatim from VICReg (Bardes 2021) and VCReg (Zhu 2023) without crystal clear citation. It is also unclear whether the covariance term should have a 1/(d-1) factor, and the definition of the covariance matrix (to distinguish it from the cross-correlation matrix of Barlow Twins) is not provided. Hyperparameters λ₁ and λ₂ are introduced but not stated. The batch size for computing the covariance is not reported. The projection layer f_proj is not described in terms of training (Reviewer 3).
- **Loss is applied only to the final layer, not intermediate layers.** The paper argues that collapse occurs in intermediate layers, yet the Seq-VCR loss is only applied to the final layer (line 225). It is not clear why it is not applied to intermediate layers where entropy is measured (Reviewer 3).
- **Comparison with CoT is incomplete.** The paper does not provide clear evidence that Seq-VCR scales better with model size than CoT (Figure 8 hints but does not address this). Speedup vs CoT is not quantified (e.g., wall-clock time as done in Deng 2024). CoT is not included in Figures 7 and 8, making direct comparison harder (Reviewer 2).
- **Fine-tuning vs training from scratch is not justified.** For GPT-2 on multiplication, the authors fine-tune a pre-trained model (which has no multiplication ability), but for minGPT they train from scratch. The rationale for this choice is not explained (Reviewer 3).
- **Potential negative impact on other tasks is not discussed.** Adding Seq-VCR loss after the next-token prediction loss may degrade performance on other tasks (e.g., math reasoning, general MMLU). The authors should discuss this limitation (Reviewer 4).

While Reviewer 1 is highly positive (rating 8) and finds the technique novel with only minor issues, other reviewers (especially Reviewer 3, rating 6) express significant concerns about the evidence, clarity, and replicability. Reviewer 4 (rating 5) also questions generalizability. These contrasts indicate that the paper’s contributions are considered promising but insufficiently validated in its current form.
