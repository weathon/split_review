## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces FreshQA, a dynamic QA benchmark of 600 questions designed to test LLMs on fast-changing knowledge and false-premise debunking, and FreshPrompt, a few-shot prompting method that augments LLMs with search engine results. Through extensive human evaluation under two scoring modes (STRICT and RELAXED), the authors show that current LLMs struggle on these questions and that FreshPrompt improves performance.

### Strengths
- Tackles a critical limitation of LLMs: reliance on outdated or erroneous knowledge.
- Provides a high-quality dynamic benchmark (FreshQA) with 600 questions, categorized into four types (never-changing, slow-changing, fast-changing, false-premise), covering a wide range of topics, with a commitment to future updates. The benchmark includes useful taxonomy (knowledge dates, multi-hop, false premise) and is a valuable tool for assessing LLM factuality.
- Thorough evaluation procedure with two modes (STRICT and RELAXED) to measure both correctness and hallucination.
- Well-written and clearly motivated.
- Good empirical results: FreshPrompt outperforms Perplexity.AI and Self-Ask on GPT-3.5 and GPT-4, with detailed ablation studies examining the impact of number and order of retrieved evidences and answer verbosity.
- One reviewer (score 8) was particularly positive about the benchmark’s novelty and the method’s strong performance, while other reviewers (scores 5 and 6) were more critical of novelty and evaluation approach.

### Weaknesses
- The evolving nature of FreshQA may pose challenges for researchers seeking consistent benchmarks across different time periods.
- The FreshQA dataset bears similarities to existing benchmarks (RealTimeQA, TimeQA), which somewhat dilutes its novelty. Similarly, the proposed FreshPrompt method is not entirely groundbreaking given prior work on internet-augmented LLMs and REPLUG. The overall findings (LLMs struggle on multi-hop and fast-changing knowledge; retrieval helps) are not particularly unexpected (minor severity per Reviewer 2).
- **Major weakness**: The evaluation relies exclusively on human annotation; no automatic evaluation metric is provided. The number of annotators per system is not specified; only inter-annotator agreement on a subset of 100 questions is reported. This limits broad usability. Using multiple-choice questions (like MMLU) could have facilitated automatic evaluation and reduced annotator variance.
- The retrieval methodology is tailored to Google Search, which may limit generalizability (minor).
- Experiments are limited to T5, PaLM, and GPT series; additional evaluations on other popular LLMs such as LLaMA and Falcon would strengthen the benchmark baselines.
- Minor presentation issue: best results in Table 1 should be bolded for better readability.
