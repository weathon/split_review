## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LiFT, a Bayesian meta-learning method for parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) across multiple related tasks. It introduces a hierarchical Bayesian model where task-specific LoRA parameters are governed by higher-level latent variables representing shared knowledge. Posterior inference is performed via a novel SGLD-Gibbs sampling algorithm, and an online EM algorithm maintains a Gaussian mixture representation of the posterior during sampling. Experiments on NLP and vision tasks show improved performance over baselines.

### Strengths
- The idea is novel and interesting.
- Empirical evaluation is extensive and convincing; results are state-of-the-art.
- The paper is well written and easy to follow, with clear intuitive explanations for most mathematical components.
- Two creative contributions: (1) the hierarchical Bayesian model for PEFT parameters; (2) the combination of Gibbs sampling with SGLD for memory-efficient posterior inference.
- The method can re-use pre-fine-tuned models, which is valuable for knowledge reuse.
- The sampling method is efficient; one reviewer suggested that an explicit efficiency gain experiment would further strengthen the contribution.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical justification for SGLD-Gibbs convergence**: Only an empirical toy example in Appendix A is provided. There is no proof that the asynchronous update scheme maintains the desired stationary distribution. The expectations of equations 9–11 should be shown to match those of 7–8 in the limit (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient ablation studies**:
  - No ablation for the J-term update (appearing in equation 11); a comparison to a naive version without J-term is missing (Reviewer 1).
  - For the Online-EM GMM posterior approximation, only different numbers of GMM components are compared; the natural baseline of a single Gaussian is omitted. If the GMM is not provably necessary, the value of the Online-EM contribution is questionable (Reviewer 1).
  - Sensitivity to hierarchical variances σ² and β² is not studied; only one set of values is reported (Reviewers 2, 4).
  - No comparison to a non-hierarchical model to verify that the hierarchical structure is responsible for gains (Reviewer 2).
  - For MAML-based baselines, a single inner loop is used; this may limit performance. A comparison with 5 inner loops (or an estimate of the required resources) is needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing critical related works and baselines**:
  - Meta-learning with modulation: CAVIA, CNAPs, FiLM-modulated MAML, LoRA-modulated MAML, etc. are not cited or compared (Reviewer 3).
  - More recent meta-learning methods (warped gradient descent, bootstrapped meta-learning, sparse interpolated experts) are absent (Reviewer 3).
  - Other relevant meta-learning approaches (SNAIL, ProtoNet, MetaQDA) are not included. The current baseline selection is outdated and not representative (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Missing standard deviations in results**: Without measures of variance, it is impossible to assess whether the observed performance gains are statistically significant or due to random fluctuations (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear novelty of Online-EM for GMM**: Existing work on incremental EM and streaming GMMs (e.g., arXiv:2207.14019, DIVA, ScienceDirect) is not referenced. The paper needs to clearly articulate the specific differences and advantages of its proposed online EM algorithm (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Scaling and practical concerns**:
  - Experiments focus on small-scale networks; the main motivation for LoRA is scaling to large models (≥1B parameters), which is not demonstrated (Reviewer 3).
  - It is unclear whether the method scales with the number of trainable parameters or can be applied to full fine-tuning (Reviewer 2).
  - Joint training on all training tasks may be infeasible for large-scale datasets; computational cost is not acknowledged as a limitation (Reviewer 4).
  - Training requires 2000 burn-in and 1000 warmup steps, but this is not translated to actual training time, making practical efficiency hard to judge (Reviewer 2).
- **Concerns about the Bayesian formulation and its necessity**:
  - No analysis shows that the latent variable ϕ actually learns meaningful task-agnostic adaptations. One reviewer (score 6) questioned whether the hierarchical model is responsible for the gains or if they stem from favorable training dynamics. A comparison to a non-hierarchical model could clarify this (Reviewer 2).
  - Choice of GMM for ϕ’s posterior is questionable: a multi-modal distribution may contradict the goal of learning a task-agnostic prior (each mode could specialize to a task, resembling mixture-of-LoRA). No mechanism prevents this (Reviewer 2).
  - Advantages of Bayesian meta-learning over non-Bayesian joint learning are not clear. There is concern that the sophisticated sampling may not scale to large models (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of clarity about method details**:
  - The J-term appears in equation 11 without clear motivation; a better transition between equations 7 and 9 is needed (Reviewer 1).
  - After test-time adaptation, it is unclear whether the output is stochastic (continued SGLD sampling) or deterministic (using statistics). If stochastic, confidence intervals should be provided (Reviewer 2).
  - The difference between warm-up samples and burn-in samples for SGLD-Gibbs is not explained (Reviewer 2).
  - It is unclear which architectures and hyperparameters were used for meta-learning baselines. MAML is notoriously inefficient with large feature extractors; explicit details are needed for fair comparison (Reviewer 4).
  - Figure 4 suggests SGLD converges faster than SGLD-Gibbs at low iterations; the authors should clarify how to interpret this (Reviewer 4).
- **Reporting metrics**: Using a sum of raw metrics may be less robust; reporting the mean of absolute deltas between baseline and candidate algorithm could be more statistically meaningful (Reviewer 1).
- **Code open-sourcing** not confirmed (Reviewer 1).
