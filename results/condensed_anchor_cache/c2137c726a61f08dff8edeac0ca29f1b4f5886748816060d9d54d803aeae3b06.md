## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes integrating adversarial supervision into the training of layout-conditioned diffusion models. It employs a segmentation-based discriminator (from OASIS) to provide pixel-level alignment feedback between the denoised image and the input layout. Additionally, a multistep unrolling strategy recursively predicts clean images over several denoising steps and applies the discriminator across that time window. Experiments on ADE20K and Cityscapes show improved layout faithfulness over baselines such as ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, and FreestyleNet. The method also boosts domain generalization for semantic segmentation by ~12 mIoU points.

### Strengths
- Well-written, well-structured, with intuitive motivations (R1, R2, R3).
- The proposed method is straightforward, simple but effective; it is the first to integrate adversarial supervision in a diffusion-based layout-to-image framework (R1).
- Experimental results are strong: outperforms existing diffusion-based baselines both qualitatively and quantitatively; achieves a large improvement in domain generalization for semantic segmentation (R1, R3).
- Thorough ablation studies covering different L2I models, segmenters, and hyperparameters (R4).
- Adapting a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model for semantic image synthesis is an interesting and challenging direction (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Cherry-picking and failure cases:** The qualitative results appear much better than baselines, but it is unclear if they are cherry-picked (R1). The paper does not discuss failure cases; e.g., Fig. 1 bottom-right shows the top of a truck resembling a building (R3). Non-cherry-picked comparisons and failure analysis are needed (R1, R3).
- **Limited technical novelty and lack of insight:** The adversarial supervision is identical to OASIS (Sushko et al., 2020), limiting novelty (R2). The multistep unrolling is computationally expensive (scales linearly up to 9× longer) while offering only marginal improvements (R2). The combination of diffusion and adversarial methods remains ad-hoc with no theoretical justification (R3). The paper merely describes results without providing insight or analysis into why the method works, which limits its contribution (R4).
- **Underperformance compared to OASIS:** Despite improvements over diffusion baselines, the method still underperforms OASIS (a GAN from 2020) on key metrics (R2).
- **Limited text editability and style transfer:** The motivation to use a strong text-to-image model is flawed because the semantic mask already specifies content, reducing text control to style/color transfer (R2). The model does not perform style transfer well: changing to a snowy scene resamples the whole image; local editing is not possible (e.g., changing “a red van” to “a burning van” affects all objects) (R2). These issues, partially inherited from Stable Diffusion, undermine practical usefulness (R4).
- **Missing ablations and baselines:** Several important comparisons and ablations are absent:
  - A simple baseline combining OASIS with a state-of-the-art style transfer model (R2).
  - Careful tuning of the frozen segmentation network’s loss weight, instead of adversarial training. Note that the frozen UperNet achieves higher mIoU but worse FID (Table 3), leaving it unclear whether the improvement is due to adversarial training or simply updating the segmenter (R3).
  - Training a diffusion model from scratch for simultaneous L2I and T2I using large-scale data (e.g., LAION-5B with predicted segmentation maps) (R3).
  - Clarification of the difference between “ControlNet + Adv Supervision” and multistep unrolling in Table 1 (R1).
  - Analysis of why larger unrolling steps (K>9) are always better, whether all steps contribute similar gradient magnitude, and the effect of omitting early steps (R4).
  - Analysis of why a frozen segmenter yields higher mIoU but higher FID, and whether better mIoU implies better local controllability (R4).
- **Discriminator visualization:** Visualizations or analysis of the discriminator’s outputs in the latent space are needed to verify its behavior (R1).
- **Terminology and existing layout compliance:** The paper mislabels semantic image synthesis (pixel-level masks) as “layout-to-image”, which typically refers to bounding boxes (R2). Additionally, the claim that Stable Diffusion models do not comply well with layout input is contradicted by existing works [3,4] (R2).
