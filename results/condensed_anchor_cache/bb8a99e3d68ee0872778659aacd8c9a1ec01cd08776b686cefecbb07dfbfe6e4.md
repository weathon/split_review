## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper extends Denoising Diffusion Bridge Models (DDBMs) by adopting the DDIM framework to enable fast, training-free sampling for image-to-image translation and restoration. It introduces non-Markovian diffusion bridges on discretized timesteps, leading to DBIMs that can be deterministic or stochastic, support high-order ODE solvers, and preserve diversity via booting noise. The method is validated on edges2handbags, DIODE-Outdoor, and ImageNet inpainting, achieving up to 25× speedup with maintained quality.

### Strengths
- **Fast sampling with no retraining**: DBIM accelerates DDBM up to 25× while preserving quality, using a training-free modification (R1, R4).
- **Theoretical justification**: Clear derivation showing equivalence of training objectives (Proposition 3.2) and the non-Markovian bridge construction; good connection to I2SB, flow matching, and exponential integrators (R1, R2, R3).
- **Deterministic sampler insight**: The finding that the deterministic sampler (η=0) outperforms stochastic variants is novel for DDBMs, opening the door to distillation methods like consistency models (R1). Reviewer 1 (score 8) considers this a strong contribution.
- **Empirical support**: Results on the same setups as DDBM show significant speed-ups with comparable FID, LPIPS, MSE; experiments are well-conducted (R2, R4, R5).
- **No extra training or fine-tuning needed**: Only the sampling procedure changes (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: Reviewer 5 (score 5, confidence 4) argues the work reinvents the wheel: generic frameworks such as Conjugate Integrators (Pandey et al.) and generalized DDIM already enable fast sampling from any diffusion/flow/interpolant, making the DBIM derivation trivial. Reviewer 4 (score 6) notes the close analogy to SinSR (CVPR 2024), which applies the same DDIM idea to a different bridge process for super-resolution. Reviewer 1 (score 8) disagrees, viewing the method as novel and important. The main text does not sufficiently acknowledge these prior frameworks.
- **Missing comparisons**:
  - Comparison with I2SB on inpainting with freeform masks and evaluation at small NFEs (1–20) is missing (R1).
  - Key baselines based on flow matching or stochastic interpolants (e.g., [4][5][6]) are absent from Table 3 (R5).
  - The applicability of DBIM to the pred-x parameterization (motivated by EDM in DDBM) is not discussed (R3).
- **Unfair baselines**: Table 3 includes task-agnostic methods (DDRM, Π-GDM) alongside task-specific DDBM/DBIM, which is misleading because the former require no paired data and can adapt to multiple tasks without retraining (R5).
- **Missing diversity evaluation**: No quantitative diversity score (e.g., CMDE from BBDM) is reported, especially for small η and NFE values (R1).
- **Missing inference time**: Actual wall-clock times comparing DBIM and other methods are not provided (R1).
- **Source code not provided**, hampering reproducibility (R2).
- **Theoretical issues**:
  - Derivation in Appendix C.1 (lines 892–897) appears incorrect: the gradient should be over the full conditional and Eq. (50) is wrong, though the final result is correct (R2).
  - The statement in line 256 that $x_T$ cancels under the choice of $\rho_n$ lacks a proof or reference (R2).
  - Equation (7) notation $q_{tT}$ is confusing; also a typo: should be $\bar{w}_t$ instead of $w_t$ (R1, R3).
- **Booting noise**: The paper introduces booting noise as required by theory, but it is unclear how DDBM (or earlier works) avoids this noise and whether DBIM’s deterministic sampler still involves stochasticity at the initial step (R2, question 4 and 5).
- **Related works**: The “Related works” section in the Appendix is not referenced in the main text; the main text should also cover related methods (R2, R3).
- **Misleading claims in Introduction**: The claim that adapting diffusion models to informative priors “inevitably results in sub-par performance or slow inference” is overstated; task-agnostic methods (e.g., DPS, Π-GDM) trade training for slower sampling but offer scalability without paired data. The authors should discuss trade-offs and recent progress in accelerating task-agnostic methods (R5).
- **Additional reviewer requests**:
  - High-order solver: How much slower are high-order methods (order ≥2) and does quality improve for order 4? (R1)
  - Limitations or failure cases should be discussed (R1).
  - Discuss relevance of consistency diffusion bridge (NeurIPS 2024) and OT-accelerated diffusions (e.g., [1][2]) (R1, R3).
