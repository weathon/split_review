## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ASOR, an algorithm that regularizes policy optimization using “anchor states” (states accessible across all optimal trajectories under dynamics shift) to address the limitation that optimal state distributions may differ under dynamics changes. ASOR is designed as a modular reward augmentation that can be added to existing algorithms (PPO, MAPLE, ESCP). The paper provides theoretical lower bounds and evaluates ASOR on Gridworld, MuJoCo, MetaDrive, D4RL, and a Fall Guys-like game. Reviewers’ opinions are mixed: one is strongly positive (score 8), one moderately supportive (score 5), and two are negative (score 3). The positive reviewer finds the concept intuitive, the theory promising, and the experiments comprehensive. The negative reviewers question the motivation, the practicality of anchor states, the clarity of the writing and methodology, the significance of the empirical gains, and the theoretical contributions.

### Strengths
- **Conceptual intuition and motivation**: The paper identifies a genuine limitation of state-only policy transfer algorithms (the assumption of identical stationary state distributions across dynamics) and introduces anchor states as a targeted way to address it. This weakens the strong assumption and provides a structured regularization objective.
- **Theoretical analysis**: The paper derives performance lower bounds under weaker assumptions than baselines (Reviewer 1). The analysis includes a linear horizon dependency (improvement over quadratic), though some reviewers question its practical sharpness.
- **Modularity and compatibility**: ASOR integrates as a simple reward modification into existing RL algorithms (PPO, MAPLE, ESCP) and shows consistent improvement across both online and offline settings, increasing its potential applicability.
- **Comprehensive empirical validation**: Experiments cover a wide range of environments (Minigrid, D4RL, MetaDrive, a Fall Guys-like game) and dynamics shifts. The method consistently enhances the performance of several SOTA baselines.
- **Identifies a real challenge**: The paper correctly points out the difficulty of cross-domain imitation when expert actions are not realizable by the agent (Reviewer 3).
- **Technically sound methodology**: The proposed algorithmic design appears sound, and the experimental setup includes most commonly used benchmarks (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear problem definition and motivation**: The paper does not clearly define the training/deployment setting (source vs. target, multiple MDPs drawn from a distribution or fixed). The goal of optimization (simultaneous performance across all MDPs vs. generalization to unseen ones) is vague (Reviewer 2). Moreover, it is not empirically demonstrated that violation of similar stationary state distributions is actually critical for performance; without such evidence the motivation is unconvincing (Reviewer 4).
- **Anchor state concept – justification and practicality**: The rationale for focusing only on shared accessible states is not well explained. Why would inaccessible states be detrimental to policy learning and generalization? (Reviewer 4). The core assumption that anchor states exist and can be consistently identified across environments is unrealistic in many real-world settings, because even in-domain optimal trajectories are diverse and unlikely to fully overlap, making the anchor state set too restrictive (Reviewer 3). The paper does not provide a practical strategy for dynamically identifying anchor states in complex environments; the current approach assumes fixed states across all trajectories, which is overly simplistic (Reviewer 3).
- **Methodology – clarity and details**: Sections 3.3 and 3.4 are difficult to follow. The connection between Eq.(2) and Eq.(3) is unclear, the use of Random Network Distillation (RND) for density ratio estimation lacks explanation (loss function, how maximizing an expectation yields a density ratio), and the binary observation state O<sub>t</sub> is not adequately elaborated (Reviewers 1, 2). The description of ESCP is too brief: the latent variable z and its adaptation to different environments are not formally defined, and Algorithm 1 has undefined notation (Reviewer 2). The paper has a large overlap with prior work [1], which affects novelty (Reviewer 4).
- **Anchor state identification – sensitivity and implementation**: The paper does not discuss how sensitive ASOR is to the choice of anchor states or the trade-offs of different identification criteria (Reviewer 1). How are anchor states obtained during training? (Reviewer 2). The use of an arbitrary T<sub>0</sub> in the loss is not justified—how is T<sub>0</sub> chosen, how does it affect results, and does it guarantee that anchor states in T<sub>0</sub> cover all anchor states across different MDPs? If not, Eq.(2) seems inconsistent with the motivation (Reviewer 2).
- **Theoretical results – limited and questionable**: The relationship between Theorems 2 and 4 is not explained, and the reliance on neural distance (typically used in generative models) is unusual for RL theory (Reviewer 4). Theorem 4.4’s proof heavily depends on an assumption from Xu et al. 2020 that the reward function has a linear space structure, which is not stated in the main text (Reviewer 4). The claim of a linear horizon dependency is not supported by evidence that the bounds are sharp or non-vacuous in practice; common RL theory provides regret or sample complexity bounds, and the linear horizon dependency may not be widely accepted without proper contextualization (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited scope**: The paper only considers Hidden-parameter MDPs, which restricts the dynamics shift to a potentially small class where MDPs share similar structures and have large overlap. The reward function is assumed unchanged, yet this limitation is not stated early (Reviewer 4). The method does not address shifts in the reward function (Reviewer 4).
- **Marginal empirical improvement**: The reported gains over baseline methods are small, and it is unclear whether they are statistically significant or practically meaningful, especially given the added complexity of ASOR (Reviewer 3). No notable conclusions can be drawn from the experiments beyond minor improvements (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of ablation on regularization choices**: The rationale for incentivizing divergence on non-anchor states is not clear. Are there scenarios where this penalty is detrimental? More detailed ablations are needed (Reviewer 1).
- **Clarity and writing quality**: The logic of paper is hard to follow; the central issue and its importance are not clearly communicated (Reviewer 4). Statements such as “KL divergence has poor generalization” (Line 318) are imprecise and require careful wording (Reviewer 4). The paper needs significant improvement in presentation and accessibility (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
