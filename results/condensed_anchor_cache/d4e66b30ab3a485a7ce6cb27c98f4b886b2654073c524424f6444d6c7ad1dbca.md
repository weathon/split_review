- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 6, 6, 8

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes OTDF (Optimal Transport Data Filtering) for cross-domain offline RL, where source domain data with dynamics mismatch is leveraged to improve learning in a target domain with limited data. The method uses optimal transport to align and filter source transitions, plus a dataset regularization term to keep the policy within the target domain’s support. Theoretical performance bounds are provided. Experiments across gravity and morphology shifts show OTDF outperforms prior methods (based on IQL) by a large margin. Reviewers rate the paper 6, 6, and 8 (confidence 4, 3, 2 respectively), with the high rating reviewer noting strong design and proof but lower confidence.

### Strengths
- Novel combination of optimal transport (Wasserstein distance) for data filtering with a dataset regularization term, not commonly seen in cross-domain offline RL.
- Solid theoretical foundation: a performance bound analysis that motivates the data filtering and regularization.
- Efficient OT-based filtering: no need to train neural networks for domain classification; directly computes Wasserstein distance.
- Strong empirical results across diverse dynamics shifts (e.g., gravity, morphology) and dataset qualities, often surpassing prior strong baselines (especially those based on IQL) by a large margin.
- Informative proof and well-designed method that can serve as a baseline for future work (Reviewer 3 positive).

### Weaknesses
- Practical implementation still requires a conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE) to estimate the behavior policy, adding computational overhead. The impact of the CVAE on performance and its role in the method are not fully explained (Reviewer 1).
- The claim that OTDF works well with a very small target domain dataset lacks detailed explanation from both theoretical and empirical sides (Reviewer 1, also reflected in Reviewer 2’s question about sub-optimal gap analysis).
- Ablation study is limited: the relative contribution of the OT data filtering versus the dataset regularization is not clearly disentangled. Results in Figure 3 suggest policy regularization has a very large impact, which may be the primary driver of improvement—previous methods like SRPO and IGDF lacked such constraints, possibly explaining OTDF’s advantage (Reviewer 2).
- Comparison with other dynamic model–based or adaptive transfer learning approaches is not comprehensive. Relevant prior works are omitted—e.g., “When to trust your simulator,” “Off-Dynamics RL via Domain Adaptation,” “Return Augmented Decision Transformer for Off-Dynamics RL” (Reviewer 1).
- The experimental setting differs from prior works (DARA, BOSA, IGDF) which used source and target datasets of similar quality (e.g., both medium). OTDF tests settings like source medium + target expert, which may better align source-target overlap. The validity of such mismatched quality settings and their impact on results is not fully justified (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- No results for other offline RL backbones (e.g., CQL, BCQ, BEAR) are provided; only IQL is used as the base algorithm (Reviewer 1).
- No assessment of IQL’s performance when using only source domain data (Reviewer 2).
- The paper does not provide the impact of OT filtering and policy regularization on the performance bound; a sub‑optimal gap analysis is expected (Reviewer 2).
- The variable μ_{t,t'} is not defined in Equation 1 (Reviewer 3).
- Table 1 formatting is too wide (Reviewer 2).
- A request for a more complex kinematic shift to test generalization (Reviewer 3).
- The OT-based cross-domain method has been used in prior work (e.g., “Cross-domain imitation learning via optimal transport”, ICLR 2022); implementation differences need clarification (Reviewer 2).
