## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes neural approximate mirror maps (NAMMs) to enforce soft constraints for diffusion models (DMs). NAMMs learn a forward and inverse mirror map using gradient-of-ICNN parameterization, cycle-consistency, and constraint distance losses, enabling a mirror diffusion model (MDM) to be trained in an unconstrained mirror space and then map samples back to the constraint set. The method is validated on five benchmark problems covering physics-based, geometric, and semantic constraints, and also applied to constrained inverse problems (DPS) and VAEs. Reviewers agree the approach is novel, well-motivated, and shows promising results across diverse constraint types, but they raise concerns about the toy nature of the experiments, lack of theoretical guarantees, limited evaluation consistency, missing comparisons, and insufficient hyperparameter analysis. One reviewer (R3) is notably more positive (score 8) than the other two (scores 6 each).

### Strengths
- The method generalizes mirror maps to non-convex constraints, overcoming the need for analytical mirror maps (R1, R2, R3).
- The cycle-consistency loss tailored for mirror maps and diffusion models is sound (R1).
- Experiments cover diverse constraints: physics-based, geometric, semantic – demonstrating applicability across types that existing methods cannot handle together (R1, R2, R3).
- The approach also improves constraint satisfaction for VAEs and helps diffusion-based inverse-problem solvers (e.g., DPS) reduce constraint violation (R2, R1).
- Comprehensive ablation studies (constraint loss, mirror map parameterization, finetuning) are provided (R1).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R3).
- The motivation – proper constraint satisfaction is critical for many applications in engineering, physics, and vision – is strong (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Toy experiments**: The experiments are primarily toy/small-scale. It is unclear whether the method scales to high dimensions or image domains (e.g., the image settings of Reflected Diffusion Models) (R1). R3 notes the gap between NAMM and the baseline is less significant for the semantic problem, suggesting limited ability to handle complex constraints (non-convex/discontinuous regions) (R3).
- **Lack of theoretical guarantees**: NAMMs have no guarantee of existence or uniqueness of the mirror map for non-convex constraints, making it difficult to predict when the method will succeed or fail (R2). R1 questions the meaning of “unique solution” from the regularization loss, given that the ICNN gradient already ensures reversibility – the regularization’s necessity and interpretation are unclear (R1).
- **Inconsistent evaluation**: The method is validated on all five benchmark problems for main diffusion experiments, but for inverse problems, ablation studies, and VAE experiments it is only evaluated on a subset of problems. This inconsistent coverage makes it hard to assess generalizability across all three constraint types (R2).
- **Finetuning inconsistency**: Finetuning is introduced as an important component, but the text states (Section 4.1) that finetuning is often not necessary (Section 4.3), and ablation/VAB experiments do not use finetuning. The actual contribution and necessity of finetuning are unclear, and no clear guideline for when to apply it is provided (R2).
- **Missing baseline comparison**: Comparisons are only made to an unconstrained DM. The method is not compared with other existing constrained diffusion model techniques (e.g., reflected diffusion models), making it difficult to assess relative performance (R2).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and tuning**: The constraint distance in Fig. 6 is fairly sensitive to \(\sigma_{max}\) and \(\lambda_{constr}\). A systematic way to tune hyperparameters is lacking; sensitivity in higher dimensions or with more complex constraints is not analyzed (R3). R2 also notes that the robustness introduced by noise in the mirror space is claimed but not experimentally supported (R2).
- **Minor presentation issues**: Typo “a la” on line 1097 (R2). Figure 2 is never directly referenced in the text (R3). The citations in Section 2.1 take up a large portion of the paragraph, reducing readability (R3).
