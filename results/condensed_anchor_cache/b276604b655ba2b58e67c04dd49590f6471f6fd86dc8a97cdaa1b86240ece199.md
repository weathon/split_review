## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces an end-to-end framework that generates compact (6–15 dimensions), discrete (int4), interpretable node identifiers (node IDs) via vector quantization of GNN embeddings from multiple layers, under self-supervised or supervised learning. Experiments on 34 datasets across node classification, graph classification, link prediction, and attributed graph clustering show improved speed and memory efficiency with competitive performance. Reviewers broadly find the paper well-written and the method promising, but raise significant concerns about experimental rigor, loss of structural information, joint training complexity, and insufficient interpretability of the discrete codes.

### Strengths
- Well-written paper addressing a pressing topic (graph quantization), easy to follow.
- The proposed framework generates highly compact, discrete node IDs, improving speed and memory efficiency, which is practical for large-scale graphs.
- Comprehensive experiments across 34 diverse datasets and four tasks demonstrate effectiveness and efficiency.
- The overview of the framework (Figure 1) helps readers understand the pipeline.
- The method works well overall, performing at roughly the same level as the original GNN.

### Weaknesses
- **Egregious experimental result reuse and inconsistencies**: When a baseline is recomputed (e.g., GCN on Photos), the result (96%) differs substantially from the same baseline in the Polynormer paper (92%), raising a red flag. The authors’ method should perform within epsilon of the corresponding baseline it quantizes; if so, the method would not be superior. It is unclear whether other baselines were re-run with a similarly careful grid search; without that, the comparisons are not trustworthy. [Reviewer 1]
- **Potential loss of structural information**: Quantizing node embeddings into a single discrete code per layer may oversimplify nuanced node characteristics, losing discriminative power for tasks relying on subtle differences. [Reviewer 2]
- **Joint training complexity**: Joint training of GNN and vector quantization components raises concerns about time complexity and scalability. The paper lacks analysis of computational overhead from backpropagation through the VQ layer and convergence properties. [Reviewer 2]
- **Lack of interpretability discussion**: The paper claims interpretability but does not explore the semantic meaning of discrete codes or how they relate to graph structure/node attributes, nor how they capture nuances compared to continuous embeddings. [Reviewer 3]
- **Vague concepts**:  
  - “Share similar 1-hop structures” (line 71, Figure 1) is not rigorously defined; it is unclear why nodes in cases A&B or C&D are considered to have similar 1-hop structure. [Reviewer 3]  
  - If two nodes have the same second-layer code but different first-layer codes, does that imply “similar 2-hop structures”? How can they have similar 2-hop structures on the basis of totally different 1-hop neighbors? [Reviewer 3]  
  - The role of M in codebook size L×M is not clearly explained. [Reviewer 3]
- **Uselessness of codebook for downstream tasks**: Node IDs are used directly (as discrete indices) in downstream MLP, not the corresponding code vectors. Why are the optimized code vectors not used? The paper does not justify why the discrete indices alone suffice. [Reviewer 3]
- **Potential node ID collisions**: With a limited codebook (e.g., 5^2=25 possible IDs for a 2-layer, codebook-size-5 model), many nodes will share the same Node_ID. The implications of collisions are not discussed. [Reviewer 3]
- **Interpretation of discrete representations**: The paper does not provide an interpretation of what each discrete code means. [Reviewer 3]
