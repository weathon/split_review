## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper investigates the impact of test-time augmentations (TTA) on semantic-shift out-of-distribution (OOD) detection. The authors categorize augmentations into In-Distribution Augmentations (IDA, mild) that do not shift InD features and Out-of-Distribution Augmentations (OODA, aggressive) that do, using LPIPS distances measured on a pretrained model. Based on this, they propose an OOD detection method that computes the k-th nearest neighbor (KNN) distance between the feature of an input image and its mild TTA counterparts, requiring only 25 augmentations per image and no access to the training set. The method is evaluated on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, achieving performance comparable to or better than methods that use the full training set (e.g., 1.2 million images on ImageNet). The paper also reports compatibility with various architectures (ResNet-50, ViT-b-16) and robustness to adversarial examples.

### Strengths

- **Novel analysis of test-time augmentations for OOD detection**: The categorization of augmentations into IDA and OODA based on distribution shift is interesting and offers a new perspective. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4 – though some reviewers considered the novelty limited, see weaknesses)
- **Method does not require retraining or access to additional OOD samples**: The proposed approach is post-hoc and test-time only, which is practical for deployment. (Reviewer 1)
- **Efficiency with small number of augmentations**: Using only 25 mild TTAs, the method achieves superior OOD detection, reducing computational cost compared to methods that rely on large training datasets. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Compatible with various model architectures and robust to adversarial examples**: The paper demonstrates applicability across CNNs and ViTs, and shows robustness to adversarial perturbations. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Clear presentation and organization**: The paper is well-written, with Section 2 clearly explaining the IDA/OODA concept. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Strong empirical results**: Table 4 shows better OOD detection performance than previous methods on standard benchmarks. (Reviewer 4)
- **Focus on the testing phase**: Addressing the impact of test-time augmentation (rather than training-time only) is a valuable direction. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty and missing related work**: Several reviewers noted that data augmentation for OOD/anomaly detection has been explored before. The paper does not adequately discuss or compare with existing augmentation-based methods, e.g., [R1] (augmentation for anomaly detection), [R2] (test-time blurring for OOD), and [R3]. The claimed limitation of InD-independent methods (e.g., MSP, DICE, GradNorm) needing in-distribution access is misleading, as some can also be applied at test time; moreover, DICE and GradNorm are not compared, and ASH (which does not require additional data) outperforms the proposed method in some cases. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing baseline comparisons**: The evaluation lacks comparisons with important post-hoc OOD scoring methods that operate on output probabilities or logits, such as Maximum Softmax Probability (MSP), Energy score, GradNorm, and GEN. The method should be clearly positioned as a post-hoc technique and benchmarked against these. (Reviewer 2)
- **OOD score not specified in Table 1 and Table 2**: It is unclear what score is being reported (e.g., F1-score, AUROC, etc.). The representation needs clarification. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Lack of baselines on non-CNN architectures**: The paper only reports TTA performance on ViT without comparing to other OOD detection methods on the same backbone. A table similar to Appendix Table 13 but for ViT-b-16 and ResNet-50 is needed to assess generalizability and relative performance. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Unclear adaptation to other OOD scores**: The paper does not explore whether the proposed TTA approach can be combined with other OOD scoring methods such as MSP, Energy, or ASH. Evaluating this orthogonality would strengthen the contribution. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Rigorous definition of IDA/OODA**: The identification of IDA and OODA relies on LPIPS changes computed from a pretrained model, but this dependence is not explicitly stated early enough. Also, the stability of IDA across datasets is questionable: the LPIPS ordering differs between CIFAR-10 and ImageNet (e.g., Rotate90 and Invert swap positions), suggesting the categorization may be dataset-dependent. (Reviewer 4)
- **Efficiency concerns for real-time use**: The method requires 25 forward passes per test image, which may limit practical deployment in latency-sensitive settings. (Reviewer 1)
- **Lack of theoretical analysis**: The paper does not provide a theoretical explanation for why mild augmentations are effective for OOD detection while aggressive ones are not. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing algorithm description**: The paper lacks an overall algorithmic description of the proposed method, making the narrative unclear. (Reviewer 3)
- **Layout and reference issues**: There are formatting problems (crowded/blank spaces in lines 40-107), and Figure 1 uses incorrect reference style (should cite KNN and VIM properly). (Reviewer 4)
- **Need for evaluation on harder benchmarks**: Performance on challenging OOD detection setups (e.g., hard OOD, pseudo-correlation benchmarks) is not assessed. (Reviewer 3)
- **No preliminary section**: The paper would benefit from a preliminary section introducing key concepts. (Reviewer 3)

*(Note on disagreement: While Reviewers 1 and 3 considered the novelty limited and the comparisons insufficient, Reviewers 2 and 4 found the core idea novel and the method well-presented, with Reviewer 4 stating they would raise their rating if the weaknesses are addressed.)*
