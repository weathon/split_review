## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MMICL, a vision-language model designed to handle multi-modal in-context learning by processing complex prompts with multiple images and text. It proposes a novel context scheme with image declaration and proxy tokens, and constructs the Multi-modal In-Context Learning (MIC) dataset (5.8M samples) covering diverse vision-language tasks. Experiments show state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on MME and MMBench, and reduced language bias. Reviewers mostly find the problem interesting, the approach novel, and the experimental results strong (scores: 5,6,6,8), but one reviewer (score 3) raises severe concerns about poor writing, missing evaluation details, and unclear methodology. Others note limitations in ablation, architectural justification, few-shot consistency, and freeform evaluation.

### Strengths
- **Enhanced multi-modal understanding**: MMICL effectively handles multiple image inputs and their relationships, improving complex multi-modal prompt understanding (Reviewers 1,3).
- **State-of-the-art zero-shot performance**: Achieves top results on MME (cognition and perception tasks) and MMBench, outperforming earlier models like LLaVA and MiniGPT-4 (Reviewers 1,2,4,5).
- **Reduction in language bias**: Demonstrated reduction of hallucination from textual context (Reviewer 1).
- **Extensive and comprehensive experiments**: Tested on a wide range of general vision-language tasks (Reviewers 2,4).
- **MIC dataset**: Large-scale (5.8M samples) covering image captioning, VQA, video caption, visual reasoning, dialogue, and classification; could be useful for the multimodal community (Reviewers 2,5).
- **Novel context scheme**: Enables unified handling of diverse tasks via prompt engineering, and allows Vision-Language Models to acquire in-context learning ability during instruction tuning (Reviewers 2,3).
- **Interesting research problem**: Targeting multi-modal in-context learning is valuable (Reviewer 4).
- **More instruction tuning data**: Generated via ChatGPT to augment instructions (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing ablation studies**: No controlled experiment comparing interleaved image-text pairs vs. single image-text pairs; unclear if the interleaved structure is the key contributor to performance gains (Reviewers 1,3,5). Also missing ablation on training data selection, synthetic dataset, and model structure (Reviewers 3,5).
- **Insufficient architectural justification**: Lacks detailed comparison to Flamingo, Kosmos, or other paradigms; no ablation on why Q-Former is optimal or why the proposed architecture is better for interleaved processing (Reviewers 1,3).
- **Evaluation limited to fixed-format benchmarks**: Focus on MME and MMBench (multiple-choice); no freeform answer evaluation (e.g., MM-VET with GPT-4 scoring) to demonstrate generative capability (Reviewer 1).
- **Few-shot performance limited and inconsistent**: In Table 4, few-shot improvements are marginal on some datasets (e.g., FLAN-T5-XXL on VizWiz). Significant performance gaps between backbones: e.g., MMICL (Instruct-FLAN-T5-XL) vs. MMICL (Instruct-FLAN-T5-XXL) gap of 34.6 on Flickr30k; InstructBLIP (Flan-XL) 32.08 vs. (Flan-XXL) 15.11 on VizWiz. Perplexingly, larger backbone (XXL) shows less few-shot improvement (e.g., +3.82 vs. +25.13 for XL) (Reviewer 2, also Reviewer 4 notes bigger model not always better).
- **Lack of exploration of interleaved formats**: Unclear whether only the in-context format (Equation 3) boosts in-context learning, or the multiple interconnected images format (Equation 2) also contributes (Reviewer 2).
- **Methodology difficult to follow**: Dataset curation not clearly explained; e.g., how examples like two people quarreling (Figure 3b, 4) were collected – requires action relation between bounding boxes, which may require costly annotation (Reviewer 5). Conversion of datasets into instruction-following formats is vague; how ChatGPT rewrote instructions to describe "key characteristics of each task" is not defined (Reviewer 5).
- **Poor writing quality**: Numerous typos, grammatical mistakes, missing spaces and periods (e.g., “MMICLtypically”, “fall to” for “fail to”, missing period in Figure 2.b, “MMICLin”). Many sentences hard to understand (Reviewer 5).
- **Missing evaluation details**: Few-shot sample selection not described; no variance/std provided for Table 4. ScienceQA-IMG split into image-dependent and text-only groups not explained (no annotation rationale). Flickr30k and VQAv2 are in the training set, yet Table 4 claims zero-shot/few-shot performance on them – unclear how this is valid (Reviewer 5). Licenses for half of datasets labeled “Unknown” (though some like Flickr have well-known licenses) (Reviewer 5). Stage-1 and stage-2 data usage unclear (Reviewer 5). Winoground sample count mismatch: 800 samples but an image score of 44.99 is reported (Reviewer 5).
- **Unclear if performance boost comes from interleaved data or other factors**; key innovation not clearly isolated (Reviewer 5).
- **Complex design with much prompt engineering**: Slightly complex; borderline but not considered a "good" paper by one reviewer (Reviewer 3).
- **Model sizes not included in tables**; missing in comparisons (Reviewer 4).
- **Explicit meaning of MMICL not defined** (Reviewer 4).
- **Additional questions**: (i) Why use only encoder-decoder (T5) and not decoder-only models? (Reviewer 4). (ii) How many visual tokens per image? Does multiple images exceed LLM input context length? (Reviewer 2). (iii) Does the performance boost truly come from interleaved data, or is it due to other factors like training data volume? (Reviewer 5).
