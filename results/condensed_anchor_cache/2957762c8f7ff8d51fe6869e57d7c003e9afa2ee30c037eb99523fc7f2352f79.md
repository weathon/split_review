## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CHEMGUIDE, a method to guide pre-trained unconditional diffusion models for molecular generation using a non-differentiable oracle from quantum chemistry (GFN2-xTB) via zeroth-order gradient approximation (SPSA). The goal is to generate stable molecules by minimizing atomic forces without requiring labeled data for training property predictors. Experiments on QM9 and GEOM-Drugs show improved stability metrics compared to unconditional baselines, and the method is combined with both explicit and implicit guidance.

### Strengths
- The idea of using a non-differentiable oracle (quantum chemistry) to guide diffusion models is interesting, novel, and practically relevant for domains with expensive oracles (reviewers 1, 2, 4). One reviewer noted that derivative-free guidance itself is not new (reviewer 3), but others saw the specific adaptation to diffusion guidance as novel.
- Improvements in stability and force metrics over unconditional baselines are reported (reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- Implementation of zeroth-order optimization in the diffusion context is well-justified (reviewer 1).
- The empirical evaluation is thorough, covering two datasets (QM9 and GEOM-Drugs), multiple molecular properties, different guidance approaches (explicit, implicit, combined), and includes failure case analysis and trade-off discussion (reviewer 4). Another reviewer considered the experimental results weak (reviewer 3).
- The paper is clearly written with well-designed figures (reviewer 4).
- The problem addressed (moving from random generation to efficient optimization with guidance) is important and timely (reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited baselines / insufficient comparisons**: The paper compares only against unconditional generation. Additional baselines should include MolGAN, gradient-based classifiers trained from evaluated samples, evolutionary algorithms, and twisted sequential Monte Carlo methods (reviewers 1, 3). A stronger direct baseline would be to take GeoLDM unconditional samples and perform relaxation using xTB (reviewer 2). A benchmark [5] is suggested for more comprehensive comparison (reviewer 3).
- **Novelty concerns**: The idea of derivative-free guidance is not new; prior work includes evolutionary algorithms for molecular optimization [1], twisted SMC for protein design [2], and similar methods in other domains (reviewer 3). The related work section in the appendix misses these critical citations. The paper's claim of being the first should be removed or qualified.
- **Small empirical improvements**: On QM9 and GEOM-Drugs, the improvement from CHEMGUIDE over the unconditional baseline is modest (reviewer 2). The computational cost is high: sampling 100 molecules with guidance in the last 400 diffusion timesteps takes 6 hours and 18 minutes (reviewer 2). The computational complexity from using GFN2-xTB and bilevel optimization restricts practical usage (reviewer 1).
- **Lack of justification for SPSA and comparison with other zeroth-order methods**: The paper does not discuss alternative approaches such as finite differences, evolution strategies, or their trade-offs in accuracy and computational cost for this specific application (reviewer 4). A short paragraph explaining why SPSA was chosen over standard finite differences would be helpful, along with a discussion of the overhead.
- **Missing gradient quality analysis**: There is no direct comparison of gradients estimated by CHEMGUIDE with those from a differentiable regressor (e.g., cosine similarity across diffusion timesteps), which would help assess reliability (reviewer 4).
- **No explanation for which guidance method works for which property**: The paper observes that noisy and clean guidance are complementary (e.g., α vs Δϵ) but does not explain why or help practitioners choose (reviewer 4).
- **Limited discussion of limitations**: The paper does not discuss challenges of using a non-differentiable oracle, such as difficulty handling certain molecular configurations or diverse chemical spaces (reviewer 1). An adaptive mechanism for the guidance scale is also missing (reviewer 1).
- **Presentation issues**: Sections 3.1 and 3.2 describe prior work and should be in a preliminaries section (reviewer 2). Equation 15 likely has a typo in the numerator: it should read \((\mathcal{F}[z_{x,t}+cU, z_{h,t}] - \mathcal{F}[z_{x,t}-cU, z_{h,t}])/(2c)\) (reviewer 2). Algorithm 1 shows gradient \(g_{t-1}\) depending on the state at time \(t=0\), which appears incorrect since Eq. 15 does not refer to the clean data prediction (reviewer 2). The term "Stability" is unnecessarily capitalized on page 6 (reviewer 4). Adding a table of contents to the appendix would improve navigation (reviewer 4).
- **Code not provided** (reviewer 1).
