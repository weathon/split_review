## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes OSD-DICE, a method to generate an optimal supported dataset for offline RL by learning density ratios via a single minimization objective with squared regularization and a learned transition model, improving upon OptiDICE. The reweighted dataset is then used with behavior regularization methods (BC, CQL) to obtain safe policy improvement. Reviewers agree the problem is important and the theoretical analysis is strong, but they differ on empirical significance and novelty.

### Strengths
- **Theoretical analysis**: Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5, R6) endorse the theoretical contributions. R2 specifically notes that the single minimization objective resolves the instability of traditional primal-dual methods and is theoretically sound. R3 and R5 highlight the strong theoretical backing.
- **Empirical improvements on OptiDICE and CQL**: R3 states that OSD-DICE shows clear improvement over OptiDICE in tabular tasks, and that CQL benefits from OSD-DICE based importance sampling on D4RL. R2 finds the D4RL results demonstrate practical effectiveness and significant improvements. R6 confirms the experiments verify the capability to find a good policy.
- **Motivation and originality**: R1 finds the method well-motivated and the use of DiCE for weighting new. R5 considers the angle of an optimal behavioral dataset interesting and the paper contains original ideas. R4 notes the idea is general and extendable to other offline RL methods. R6 acknowledges the approach is shown to find a well-behaved policy with theoretical proofs.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with relevant baselines**:  
  R1 criticizes the lack of comparison with prior reweighting/optimization works ([1,2,3,4]), with Lee et al. (2021), and with imbalanced datasets from AW/RW. R3 notes that in the importance-sampled RL part, algorithms are not compared against OptiDICE-based algorithms, nor against the method from “Beyond Uniform Sampling” [1]. R4 points out the paper does not compare with SOTA offline RL methods (EDAC, IQL, RORL).  
  *These omissions make it difficult to assess significance and novelty.*
- **Limited performance gains**:  
  R1 observes that improvements in MuJoCo tasks are not significant; only maze2d shows clear gains. R3 questions whether the additional model learning (beyond OptiDICE) is worth the improvement in complex domains, noting that in finite MDPs the model component does not seem to add much.
- **Reproducibility and implementation issues**:  
  R1 reports that baseline scores in Table 1 lack standard deviations, hyperparameter search results are not presented, and baseline hyperparameters may not match the original papers. R1 encourages uploading code and scripts. R1 also notes that OSD-RL requires two additional models but wall-clock time is not compared.
- **Presentation and clarity**:  
  R5 and R6 state the writing needs major improvement. Notations are undefined (e.g., $\mathcal{V}$ in Assumption 1, $\mathcal{D}_m$ on page 4, $d^*_\alpha$ in (4)) (R6). Equation (3) lacks an intuitive explanation (R2, R5). The use of $f$ is ambiguous (function class vs. f-divergence) (R5). R5 also notes improper \citet/\citep usage.
- **Novelty concerns**:  
  R4 states that adding strong convexity regularization is a popular trick and the two designs lack sufficient novelty. R6 questions the novelty of using importance weights in offline RL, as it is already common.
- **Theoretical and conceptual weaknesses**:  
  R6 notes too many assumptions that are hard to verify (Assumption 5, additional assumptions in Thm6), and the sample complexity $O(\epsilon^{-4})$ is worse than the typical $O(\epsilon^{-2})$ in offline RL.  
  R3 finds it counterintuitive that the squared regularization improves OptiDICE in tabular MDPs when the optimal solution is unchanged.  
  R4 questions the claimed bias issue in Bellman error estimation and the reliability of the learned transition model $\hat{P}$.  
  R5 questions how solving Equation (2) can be efficient beyond tabular MDPs, and asks why the added regularization term does not change optimality.  
  R1 requests clarification on the difference between OptiDiCE+SquareReg and the final OSD-DiCE algorithm.

**Reviewer disagreement**: While Reviewer 2 is highly positive about the empirical results and theoretical analysis, other reviewers (R1, R3, R4, R5, R6) are more critical of the limited gains, missing baselines, presentation issues, and novelty.
