## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers acknowledge that NextLocLLM applies LLMs to next-location prediction by replacing location IDs with spatial coordinates, LLM-enhanced POI embeddings, and a KD-tree retrieval module to map coordinate predictions to top-k locations. The work targets cross-city generalization and achieves state-of-the-art results on four datasets in both supervised and zero-shot settings. Three reviewers rate the paper 5, one rates it 6. The more positive reviewer (R2) considers the contributions novel and well-ablate; the more negative reviewer (R4) finds the experimental design unconvincing and the framework reasoning problematic. All agree the paper is clearly written but raise concerns about missing details, insufficient analysis, and some overclaimed novelty.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and structure**: Well-written, easy to follow (R1, R2, R3).
- **Extensive experiments**: Cover four datasets and show strong supervised and zero-shot performance (R1, R3, R4). State-of-the-art results are reported (R4).
- **Key methodological insights**:
  - Using spatial coordinate normalization to enable cross-city generalization, bypassing location-ID inconsistencies (R1, R2).
  - LLM-enhanced POI embedding to capture functional attributes of locations (R1, R2, R3, R4).
  - Predicting coordinates then retrieving top-k locations via KD-tree—a “rather smart idea” that preserves generalization while being compatible with classic location-ID prediction (R2).
- **Ablation study**: R2 finds it sufficient and demonstrates the importance of each module (R2). R4, however, criticizes that the ablation shows only LLM-enhanced POI nearly matches the full model (see Weaknesses).
- **Integration of multiple data sources**: POI, textual descriptions, and trajectory data enrich contextual understanding (R4).
- Tackles the important problem of model transferability across cities (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing experimental details and parameters**:
  - No training hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, optimizer) (R2).
  - Key hyperparameters (e.g., embedding dimension \(d_{llm}\)) not reported (R2).
  - Which LLM tokenizer and token embedding layer are used? (R2).
  - Length of historical and current trajectories not clarified (R1, R2).
  - Handling of noisy points or sub‑hourly time granularity in raw trajectory data is unexplained (R1). The formalization \(TI=\{(d,t)\}\) with \(t\) as hour of day is insufficient for taxi data that often has finer granularity (R4).
  - How frequency of each POI category within a location is calculated is missing (R1).
- **Missing ablations and sensitivity analyses**:
  - No ablation comparing spatial coordinates vs. location IDs (R1). This is needed to justify the core representation choice.
  - The KD‑tree module’s contribution is not isolated in experiments (R4). R3 notes KD‑tree is commonly used in traffic/GNN models and may not be novel.
  - No sensitivity analysis on embedding dimensions, number of LLM layers, or grid size (R2). A question about how grid size affects accuracy was raised (R2).
  - Lack of analysis on why LLaMA2/LLaMA3 underperform GPT2 in the paper’s setting—a counterintuitive result that questions architecture suitability (R4).
- **Insufficient justification of design choices and generalization claims**:
  - The claim that only using LLM-enhanced POI (without other components) achieves the second‑best result in the ablation study undermines the necessity of other modules (R4). Furthermore, combining POI with LoRA leads to worse performance than pure POI, raising doubts about component interaction (R4).
  - Generalization is claimed without explicit road network information, relying only on POI—unclear if zero‑shot ability can be achieved without road graph (R4).
  - No detailed analysis of which modules contribute to zero‑shot performance and why NextLocLLM outperforms other LLM‑based methods (R1, R3). R3 requests an intuitive explanation of why the LLM‑based model works better.
- **Innovation concerns**:
  - The POI embedding step is incremental—similar logic exists in prior work but integrated with LLM representations (R3).
  - Model structure innovation is limited (R3). The KD‑tree retrieval is a standard technique (R3).
  - The labeling of the model as “white‑box” in Figure 1(c) is unsupported (R2).
- **Minor errors**: Equation 9 contains errors that should be corrected (R3).
- **Experimental fairness and completeness**:
  - It is unclear whether baselines like DeepMove receive the same input data (POI category, stay duration) as NextLocLLM in the fully‑supervised setting (R1).
  - No case studies to demonstrate practical applicability (R3).
  - Computational efficiency (training/inference time, resource usage) is not reported or compared with baselines, which is a concern given the LLM backbone and additional POI encoding (R2).
