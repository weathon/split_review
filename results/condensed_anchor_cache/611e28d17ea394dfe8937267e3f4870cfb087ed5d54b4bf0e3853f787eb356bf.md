## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a sentence-level prompt generation approach for composed image retrieval (CIR). Instead of using pseudo-word tokens, it leverages a pretrained V-L model (BLIP-2) to learn a sentence-level prompt that describes both the reference image and the relative caption. This prompt is concatenated with the original query to enable standard text-to-image retrieval. Two loss functions (image-text contrastive and text prompt alignment) are used. Experiments on Fashion-IQ and CIRR show improved top-k recall over 10+ baselines. All reviewers noted the approach is well motivated, sound, and thorough.

### Strengths
- Technically sound, simple, and well-written (R1, R2, R3).
- Comprehensive literature survey and good problem motivation (R2).
- Solid experiments with thorough ablations (e.g., prompt length, loss weights) and comparisons against many baselines (R1, R3).
- Achieves state-of-the-art results on two public CIR benchmarks (R1, R2, R3).
- Code will be open-sourced (R1).
- The idea of generating sentence-level prompts conditioned on both reference image and relative caption is reasonable and enriches expressivity (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty:** A recent paper (arXiv 2310.09291) uses a highly similar methodology (training‑free vs. learned sentence prompts). The paper should contextualize both methods and ideally evaluate them under the same framework to assess the value of the learned prompts (R1). One reviewer (R2) also noted the overall innovation is modest, though open to being convinced otherwise.
- **Experimental setup on CIRR:** Results are reported on a random split of the training set rather than the official hidden test set, which makes numbers not directly comparable to prior work (R1).
- **Interpretability of latent prompts:** The sentence‑level prompts are actually latent vectors from an MLP. It is therefore unclear whether they truly decouple multiple objects/attributes or correctly handle object removal/attribute modification as Figure 1(c) suggests (R3).
- **Training procedure and loss functions are difficult to understand:**
    - It is unclear whether each reference image has an auxiliary text prompt \(p_i'\) (R3).
    - Figure 2(a) appears to involve two separate training stages (ITC loss optimizing \(p\), then prompt alignment loss optimizing \(p_i'\)). During optimization of \(p_i'\) the text encoder is frozen, while during optimization of \(p\) the text encoder is not frozen. This inconsistency makes the prompt alignment loss tricky to understand and raises questions about how stable performance can be achieved (R3).
- **Missing generalization analysis:** The paper does not evaluate how the method performs across different domains or handles domain shifts (R1Q2, R2Q1). It also does not explore whether database image‑caption pairs could be enriched with the generated sentence prompts to further refine search (R1Q1). These are not studied, leaving open questions about applicability.

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* One reviewer (R2, score 8, confidence 5) was highly positive, finding the approach innovative and the contribution good. The other two (R1 and R3, both score 6) considered the novelty limited and rated contribution as fair, with R3 additionally expressing concerns about interpretability and training clarity.
