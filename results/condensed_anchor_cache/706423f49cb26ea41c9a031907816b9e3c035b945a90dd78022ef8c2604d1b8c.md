## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper theoretically proves and empirically validates that binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss can induce neural collapse (intra-class compactness and inter-class distinctiveness) in multi-class classification, similar to cross-entropy (CE) loss, and further argues that BCE’s explicit constraints on decision scores and classifier bias yield advantages over CE. Experiments on MNIST, CIFAR10, and CIFAR100 show BCE outperforms CE. Reviewer ratings diverge: 8 (strong accept), 3 (reject), 6 (weak accept). Reviewer 2 strongly questions the small experimental scale and weak visualization, while Reviewer 3 acknowledges the theoretical contribution but finds the paper hard to follow and the practical advantage of BCE unclear.

### Strengths
1. **First theoretical proof that BCE loss leads to neural collapse** (Reviewers 1, 3). Uses lower bounds for BCE loss and identifies conditions for achieving them, similar to prior neural collapse proofs (Reviewer 3). This broadens the set of loss functions known to induce neural collapse (Reviewer 3).

2. **Detailed comparison of decision-score and boundary updates** between BCE and CE (Reviewer 1). Shows BCE provides consistent updates to decision scores, leading to stronger feature constraints. Explicitly reveals that global minimizers’ biases in the CE case satisfy a particular equation (Reviewer 3).

3. **Experimental validation supports theoretical points** across different models and datasets (Reviewers 1, 2). Results are presented systematically (Reviewer 2). BCE consistently improves classification performance and yields better feature compactness and distinctiveness (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
1. **Small-scale experiments and generalizability concerns** (Reviewer 2, also noted by Reviewer 1). Experiments are limited to MNIST, CIFAR10, CIFAR100. These are small datasets; findings may not generalize to larger, more complex datasets like ImageNet (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1’s question: “For more complex tasks… do you expect BCE to show similar advantages?” and Reviewer 2’s question: “What is the classification performance of BCE loss on medium to large scale datasets? For example, ImageNet?”

2. **Assumption \(d > K\) not generalized** (Reviewer 1). Theorems assume feature dimension \(d\) larger than number of classes \(K\). In large-scale tasks (e.g., face recognition) \(K\) can exceed \(d\), yet BCE still works in practice. The paper should discuss or generalize this assumption and its implications for convergence properties and feature distributions.

3. **Clarity and presentation issues** (Reviewer 3). The paper is hard to follow despite a good proof; it does not clearly demonstrate the advantage of BCE over CE in the insightful parts. The related work section is generic and does not foreground key references (Wen et al. 2022, Zhou et al. 2023) early enough; they should be revisited in light of the paper’s main findings on bias and decision boundaries. Reviewer 2 adds: the contrastive property is important but not thoroughly discussed.

4. **Visualization shortcomings** (Reviewer 2). Figure 4 is not explicit; only a two-category comparison is clear. It uses a 2D projection of high-dimensional features, which may not capture true feature-space structure. No quantitative metrics support claims of improved compactness and distinctiveness. Reviewer 3 also flags Figure 1: features within hyperball radius shown as fully bounded, with two hyperspheres tangent and two not—significance of tangency/non-tangency is unclear.

5. **Practical training factors not explored** (Reviewer 1). No analysis of how weight decay rates (different for classifier weights vs. other parameters) affect convergence to neural collapse. Also, the impact of class weight initialization schemes (uniform vs. class-proportional) on final feature distribution and BCE’s performance gains is not investigated.

6. **Role of classifier bias in BCE not clarified** (Reviewer 3). Papers on neural collapse with CE (Yang et al. 2022; Kim & Kim 2024) show the final classifier geometry of a regular simplex can be fixed at training start by setting biases to zero. Does BCE follow a similar pattern, or do biases play a significant role? Relatedly, why is the bias set to 0,1,2,3,4 etc. for visual identification in Figure 4 (Line 427)?

7. **Bounded vs. unbounded CE not convincingly advantageous** (Reviewer 3). BCE’s bounded nature does not seem to provide a clear advantage over unbounded CE, since features learned with CE are typically normalized to a hypersphere, effectively bounding them.

8. **Interaction with data augmentations not investigated** (Reviewer 2). The paper mentions Mixup and CutMix can improve classification performance, but does not study how these augmentations interact with BCE and CE losses, or whether the observed feature-property benefits hold across different augmentation strategies and parameter settings.

9. **Questions on loss-function connections** (Reviewer 2). “Sigmoid loss and BCE loss are both commonly used loss functions for binary classification tasks, what are the differences and connections between them?” and “Would BCE loss have a theoretical advantage over sigmoid loss for comparison learning tasks?” (These overlap with criticisms that the paper’s comparison scope is narrow.)
