## Merged Review

### Summary
GPUDrive is a GPU-accelerated, multi-agent driving simulator built on the Madrona Game Engine. It achieves >1 million simulation FPS by running thousands of scenarios in parallel on a single GPU. The simulator loads the Waymo Open Motion Dataset, supports multiple observation modalities (e.g., LiDAR), and is released with Python interfaces (JAX/Torch) along with pre-trained goal-conditioned policies. Compared to Nocturne, GPUDrive yields 25–40× training speedup, solving 10 scenarios in under 15 minutes and enabling agents to navigate thousands of scenarios within hours. All core functions (observation, reward, dynamics) are written in C++ and compiled to CUDA. Agent steps per second (ASPS), controllable agent steps per second (CASPS), and scene completion time are used as throughput metrics.

### Strengths
- **Unprecedented simulation throughput:** Exceeds 1 million FPS on a desktop GPU, delivering 25–40× wall-clock training speedup over Nocturne. Trained agents achieve ~95% goal completion in minutes per scene and can navigate thousands of scenarios within hours.
- **Multiple sensor modalities:** Supports LiDAR and other observation types, providing flexibility for various perception stacks.
- **Memory-efficient design:** Uses Bounding Volume Hierarchy (BVH) to prune agent pairs for collision checking and polyline decimation to approximate straight polylines, significantly reducing memory footprint.
- **Scalability with dataset size:** Demonstrates increased amortized sample efficiency when scaling to larger datasets, beneficial when compute is limited.
- **Well-structured, open codebase:** Clean, easy-to-experiment code with pre-trained baselines and extensive analysis of sampling frequency. Released with accessible Pythonic interfaces.
- **Transparency about limitations:** The paper explicitly acknowledges current shortcomings, aiding user adaptation.
- **Real-world data integration:** Seamlessly loads expert trajectories from real-world driving datasets and offers a variety of observation spaces.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited demonstration of simulator features beyond basic map loading.** Only map loading is shown; no experiments on imitation learning, mixing logged agents with RL agents during training, or handling different data formats. (Reviewer 4 emphasizes this as a major gap in practical utility.)
- **Only IPPO is evaluated**, despite the paper targeting mixed-motive settings. This does not validate the simulator for cooperative/competitive scenarios. (Reviewer 4 – minority reviewer is strongly negative on this point.)
- **No support for domain randomization** over weather, road textures, or vehicle models, which are common for generalization in real-world deployment. (Reviewer 4)
- **Absence of a map** (road network) makes it difficult to implement lane-keeping rewards or global navigation tasks. The current goal-reaching task does not fully showcase the simulator’s potential for complex driving scenarios. (Reviewer 3; also raised as a question on how easily map utilities could be added.)
- **C++ core for observation, reward, and dynamics functions** creates a barrier for users not proficient in C++; the paper lacks sufficient API detail on how to modify these without recompiling. (Reviewer 4; Reviewer 1 also prefer a mono-language Python tool.)
- **Only one dataset supported out of the box** (Waymo). The effort required to integrate other datasets is not discussed. (Reviewer 2)
- **No simple IDM (Intelligent Driver Model) agents** provided for basic reactive behavior. (Reviewer 1)
- **No scaling benchmarks beyond one GPU.** Multi-GPU support is not discussed, and no ablation or benchmark for multi-GPU performance is provided. (Reviewer 2; also asked as a question.)
- **Missing ablations and experiments:**
  - Scaling performance with increased number of environments (to identify saturation) and with increased number of agents per environment. (Reviewer 4 questions)
  - Effect of traffic jams on throughput (due to BVH). (Reviewer 3 question)
  - Whether Figure 5 used the CPU-parallel version of Nocturne. (Reviewer 4 question)
- **Specific observations of problematic agent behavior** in videos:
  - Agent 4 in scene_53.mp4 displays jerky motion toward its goal.
  - Agents 1 and 10 in scene_43.mp4 disappear without reaching their goals. (Reviewer 3)
- **Paper states a limitation in optimally utilizing generated samples** for training. (Reviewer 1)
- **Presentation issues:**
  - Figure 2 caption and explanation insufficient. (Reviewer 2)
  - Figure 3 reports RTX 4080 while line 711 states RTX 8000 (inconsistency). (Reviewer 3)
  - Line 308 contains “number valid number” (typo). (Reviewer 3)
  - Missing ethical statement. (Reviewer 1, nit)
  - Center plot in Figure 5 could benefit from log-scale x-axis. (Reviewer 1, nit)
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms:**
  - Were non-ego agents (pedestrians, cyclists) also controlled and what dynamics were used? (Reviewer 1 – unclear behavior modeling.)
  - Can the simulator be updated to efficiently provide map-like utilities for lane-keeping rewards? (Reviewer 3 – mirrors map absence.)
  - Does the simulator support loading multiple policies for individual agents with different sampling rates, and how does that affect efficiency? (Reviewer 3)
  - Could benchmarks address dataset limitations by using another dataset? (Reviewer 2)
  - Why was stable baselines PPO used instead of faster implementations? (Reviewer 2)
  - How does speedup scale with number of agents (fixed environments)? (Reviewer 4)
