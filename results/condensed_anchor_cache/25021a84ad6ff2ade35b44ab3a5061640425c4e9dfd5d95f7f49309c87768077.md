## Merged Review

### Summary
OmniContrast unifies vision and language into pixel space for contrastive learning on image-text interleaved web documents, proposing three retrieval benchmarks (AnyCIR, SeqCIR, CSR). The model shows competitive or superior performance to standard CLIP models on these benchmarks. Reviewers are divided: some praise the novel approach, ablation (Table 1), and cross-modality performance; others question the core motivation of rendering text as pixels, the lack of standard benchmark evaluations, and the strength of comparisons with simpler baselines.

### Strengths
1. **First to explore vision-language contrast on interleaved web documents in CLIP-style.** The method is straightforward and a single unified model saves memory (R2, R1).
2. **Novel and useful approach for transforming interleaved data into pixel space**, enabling a single vision encoder to handle any modality (R3, R1).
3. **Excellent ablation study (Table 1)** demonstrating the necessity of each modality in the pipeline (R4).
4. **Clearly outperforms baseline methods** across different modality settings (R4, R3).
5. **Proposes three new consecutive information retrieval benchmarks** (AnyCIR, SeqCIR, CSR) to evaluate omni-modality understanding (R1, R2). The evaluation design is sound (R2).
6. **Good writing and detailed experiments** (R3, R2).

### Weaknesses
1. **Motivation for unifying all modalities into pixel space is weakly justified and raises several concerns:**
   - The approach is tailored for cases where data is already visually formatted (e.g., screenshots), but many real-world interleaved documents (e.g., rich HTML) keep text and images as separate modalities, requiring re‑rendering into screenshots—which is redundant and introduces resolution limitations (R1).
   - Simple baselines like CLIP-V+T and UniIR-CLIP in Table 6 achieve strong results, suggesting that pixel‑space unification may not be strictly necessary (R1). One reviewer notes that the method may not offer clear advantages over CLIPPO, which already handles all modalities without rendering text (R4).
   - “Simplifying the structure” by removing the text encoder is not an obvious benefit; CLIP’s text encoder is already simple, and the need to remove it in most applications is not demonstrated (R1, rebuttal).
   - Treating text as rendered pixels reduces sentences to a “bag of words,” which is especially problematic for long‑form text where contextual dependencies matter (R1, rebuttal).
   - The term “rendering” is misleading—simple text pasting is not 3D rendering (R4).

2. **Lack of evaluation on standard, widely‑used benchmarks** (e.g., MS‑COCO text-to-image retrieval, Flickr30k, GLUE, VQAv2) as done in CLIPPO (R2, R3). Without these, it is unclear whether OmniContrast generalizes beyond the proposed retrieval tasks; the model may appear to be designed only for CIR (R2). Comparisons on standard benchmarks would strengthen the evaluation (R2).

3. **Baseline comparisons are reproduced by the authors**; no third‑party results are provided, which weakens the credibility of the comparisons (R2).

4. **No comparison or discussion against next-token-prediction VLMs** (e.g., Emu series), which are currently mainstream and can also handle interleaved data. The paper should discuss why OmniContrast is preferable to such models (R2).

5. **Lack of discussion on the necessity and effectiveness of using interleaved data vs. standard image-text pairs** within this pixel‑space framework (R3). A direct comparison would clarify the benefit of the interleaved contrastive objective (R3).

6. **Data augmentation of training data is not well documented**, raising concerns about synthesizing low‑quality training samples (R4).

7. **Figure 2’s images and fonts are extremely small**, making the figure difficult to understand; caption fonts are also too small (R4). The phrase “text is rendered visually” in the abstract is confusing until after reading the full introduction (R4).

8. **The term “omni-modality” feels odd**—the paper effectively addresses vision‑language problems, not truly omni‑modal understanding (R4).

9. **Open questions not addressed:**
   - Why does the evaluation in Section 5.2 use only the vision encoder of CLIP/OpenCLIP (not the full model)? (R2)
   - Does training in this omni‑style make convergence harder/easier? Does adding modalities help or complicate learning? (R4)
   - How does OmniContrast behave when data in one of the four modalities is scarce? (R4)
