## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LLM-GNN, a label-free node classification pipeline for text-attributed graphs that combines LLMs and GNNs. LLMs annotate a small set of selected nodes, then a GNN is trained on these pseudo-labels. The main contributions are difficulty-aware active node selection, confidence-aware annotation, and post-filtering to improve annotation quality. The method is well-motivated and achieves cost-effective results (e.g., 74.9% accuracy on OGBN-PRODUCTS under $1). However, reviewers raised concerns about the limited effectiveness of the proposed components, lack of theoretical grounding, generalizability, and missing analyses.

### Strengths
- The problem of label-free node classification by combining LLMs and GNNs is novel and well-motivated. All reviewers found the direction interesting.
- The three components (difficulty-aware selection, confidence-aware annotation, post-filtering) are clearly described and each supports the overall motivation.
- The approach is cost-effective: high accuracy on the large-scale OGBN-PRODUCTS dataset with annotation costs under $1.
- The paper experiments on diverse datasets and compares with various heuristic baselines, conducting analyses to demonstrate efficacy.
- One reviewer (score 8) considered the contribution excellent and the methodology comprehensive, while the other three (score 6) considered the contribution fair and noted limited technical novelty.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited theoretical grounding:** The three major components are largely heuristic without theoretical analysis. This makes it difficult to understand when the method will perform well or fail. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Difficulty-aware active selection (DA) shows inconsistent effectiveness and sometimes harms performance:** The gain from DA is incremental. In Table 2, adding DA to traditional active selection or to post-filtering (PS) frequently decreases performance (e.g., 50% of cases worse). The trend in Figures 2/12 of decreasing accuracy with distance from cluster centers is weak. The authors only showed improvement with grid search for one specific case; broader tuning is needed. Moreover, DA may bias towards less diverse nodes (closer to cluster centers) that are less informative for GNN training, while penalizing diverse nodes where LLM annotation quality may be lower. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Post-filtering (PS) also underperforms:** PS-DA-methods perform worse than DA-methods in most cases (Table 2). (Reviewer 2)
- **LLM alone can outperform LLM-GNN:** In Table 3, the LLM predictor achieves superior accuracy on some datasets, raising questions about the necessity of the GNN component absent a compelling justification for its added complexity. (Reviewer 2)
- **Generalizability concerns:** The method is designed for text-attributed graphs only but the paper does not always explicitly state this (overclaiming risk). Performance is primarily demonstrated on OGBN-PRODUCTS; broader applicability to other graph types, domains, or varying graph structures/sizes is not thoroughly explored. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Noisy annotations and lack of robustness analysis:** LLM annotations have no ground truth labels, introducing noise. The paper does not investigate robustness to different noise types (random, systematic, label-dependent) or mitigation strategies like label smoothing, robust loss functions, or data augmentation. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing detailed economic comparison:** The paper lacks a cost comparison with traditional supervised learning and other label-free approaches, considering both LLM annotation costs and GNN training computational costs, along with accuracy trade-offs. (Reviewer 4)
- **Insufficient experimental explanations:** Several aspects are unclear. In Table 6, it is questionable whether using labels in the 1-shot example is realistic under the "label-free" setting, and how confidence scores are determined there needs explanation. (Reviewer 1)
- **Figure 4 anomaly:** Some methods show an increase in performance after a budget of 70, then a sharp decline; this may be due to class-imbalance during selection and should be explained. (Reviewer 1)
- **Lack of demonstration on additional datasets:** Besides Figure 5, the paper would benefit from showing on different datasets that LLM-GNN matches GNNs trained on ground truth labels at reduced cost. (Reviewer 1)
- **Initial LLM labels ignore graph structure:** Only node features are used; graph structure is not considered, leaving room for improvement since both features and structure affect labels. (Reviewer 3)
- **Only one LLM tested:** All experiments use GPT-3.5-turbo; the effect of other LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, open-source models) is not explored. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing related works:** Some zero-shot node classification works ([1-2] in review) should be cited. (Reviewer 3)
- **Minor presentation issues:** Detailed description of \(f_{act}(v_i)\) is missing. Full prompt examples in Appendix D are mentioned but not provided. Typos: "Combing" → "combining" (p.7), "modelson" → "models on" (p.3). (Reviewer 2)
- **Unclear impact of confidence scores and pruning:** The usefulness of LLM confidence scores (Table 6) is unclear; showing mean and variance would help. The number of nodes selected in each step and the pruning ratio after PS are not provided, making it hard to assess step efficacy. (Reviewer 2)
