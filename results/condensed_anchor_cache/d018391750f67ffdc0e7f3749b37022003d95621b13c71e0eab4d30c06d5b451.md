## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies the training and generalization of a two-layer neural network after a single gradient descent step, under a structured data model: Gaussian mixtures with finite-rank + identity covariance. In the proportional limit (dimension, width, sample size growing at constant rates), the authors prove that the network is asymptotically equivalent to a conditional Gaussian covariate model, and that the activation can be replaced by a finite-degree polynomial (Hermite decomposition). They analyze how the joint scaling of learning rate and data spread, the alignment of covariance spikes, and cluster weights affect learning outcomes. Extensive simulations, including Fashion-MNIST classification, support the theoretical predictions.

### Strengths
- Proposes a theoretical framework for two-layer neural networks after one gradient step under realistic Gaussian mixture data with structured covariances, moving beyond previous isotropic assumptions.
- Establishes a finite-degree polynomial model as an asymptotically equivalent performance model, simplifying analysis via Hermite decomposition.
- Proves a conditional Gaussian equivalence theorem (cGET) that extends recent universality results to structured covariances.
- Analyzes the joint scaling of learning rate \(\eta\) and data spread \(\|\Sigma\|\), giving an insightful trade-off picture.
- Section 6 provides rich intuition on the effect of different parameters, including alignment between covariance spikes, and is well-documented with simulations.
- The work is well-written, properly situating itself in the recent literature on feature learning after one gradient step (Ba et al., 2022; Dandi et al., 2023a,b; Moniri et al., 2023; Cui et al., 2024).
- Includes compelling experiments on Fashion-MNIST classification; the empirical validation supports claims and highlights the impact of data structure.

### Weaknesses
- **Assumption A.3 (zero-mean Gaussian components) is a critical limitation.**  
  - Because all components are zero-centered, the mixture essentially reduces to a sum of zero-mean Gaussians; thus the data model may not introduce significant challenges beyond the isotropic case, making the analysis a potentially straightforward extension of existing work (e.g., Ba et al., 2022).  
  - It is unclear why zero means are necessary; can the analysis accommodate non-zero means? If non-zero means can be absorbed into the spiked covariance structure, this should be discussed explicitly and quantitatively.  
  - If multi-modality genuinely requires non-zero means, the paper should clarify what effects are not captured by the spiked covariance alone, or provide evidence that they are negligible.

- **Lack of clear comparison with related technical literature.**  
  - The proofs heavily build on Ba et al. (2022) and Dandi et al. (2023a,b)/Hu and Lu (2023), but the main text does not distinguish how Theorem 3 (cGET) differs from the cGET of Dandi et al. (2023b). A summary of which results are used and what is extended is needed.  
  - It is unclear which regime the results cover: Ba et al. (2022) (no feature learning, Gaussian universality), Moniri et al. (2023) (intermediate partial feature learning), or Dandi et al. (2023a)/Cui et al. (2024) (maximal update/critical step size). Lemma 1–Theorem 3 seem to hold across regimes, while Theorem 4 may correspond to the intermediate regime of Moniri et al. This should be explicitly discussed.  
  - Regarding L199–200, (Ba et al., 2022) showed that a large learning rate is *sufficient* to surpass the best linear model, not necessary; an intermediate scaling (Moniri et al., 2023) can lead to partial nonlinear learning.

- **Unclear connection to real-world applications.**  
  - The motivation for extending beyond isotropic Gaussians to this particular zero-mean mixture model is ambiguous. The practical implications for training neural networks are not well-articulated.  
  - Numerical experiments are limited to Fashion-MNIST; larger-scale datasets (e.g., ImageNet) would strengthen the claims.

- **Open technical questions left unaddressed.**  
  - Can one obtain a tight asymptotic expression for the test/train errors (e.g., limiting values), rather than just equivalence?  
  - Can the feature spectrum be characterized in the single-index target case, as in Moniri et al. (2023)? Do the covariance spikes persist or are they altered by the structured data?  
  - The polynomial equivalence: does it hold only for the readout training step, or does it apply to the entire training procedure including the gradient step on the first layer? The current text (e.g., eq. 17) is unclear whether \(\hat{F}\) still refers to the original network weights.  
  - The claim that the polynomial equivalence “simplifies analysis” (L410) is not supported by a specific example; the authors should indicate which part of the analysis becomes more tractable.

- **Minor issues:**  
  - Appendix A, L724–725 is confusing: do the authors mean that each \(f_i^T x\) is Gaussian conditioned on the class?
