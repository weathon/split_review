## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes M2LNet, a multi-stage reconstruction network for lensless imaging that jointly estimates model mismatch error (M²E) and reconstructs high-resolution images. It formulates lensless image reconstruction as a joint Maximum a Posteriori (MAP) problem, introduces an explicit latent-space representation for M²E, and unfolds the MAP estimator with a learned Laplacian Scale Mixture (LSM) prior. All parameters are optimized end-to-end. Experiments on PHlatCam and FinCam datasets (including a new dataset captured by the authors) show that the method outperforms existing state-of-the-art approaches.

### Strengths
- **Novel explicit handling of model mismatch error (M²E):** The paper directly incorporates an explicit latent-space representation of M²E into the reconstruction framework, learning both the mean (feature) and variance (uncertainty). This improves robustness against PSF inaccuracies, a key challenge in lensless imaging (R1, R2, R3).
- **Strong theoretical foundation:** The method builds on a clear joint MAP formulation with a Laplacian Scale Mixture prior, providing a solid mathematical basis (R3, R4).
- **Outperforms existing methods:** The proposed M2LNet achieves noticeably better image quality than current state-of-the-art methods on experimentally captured datasets (R1, R2, R3). The comparison methods are well-chosen (R4).
- **Clean presentation and well-defined variables:** Equations are clearly defined, making the methodology easy to follow (R3, R4). The paper is well-structured (R3).
- **Large dataset and new dataset:** The authors introduce a new captured dataset, and the overall dataset size is large (R1, R4). 
- **Promising combination of unrolling and statistical/learning priors:** Combining classical model-based denoising with deep learning-based denoising within the unrolled architecture is a promising approach (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical contribution relative to prior art:** Joint forward-model calibration and image reconstruction via unrolling are already well-explored in inverse imaging. The paper does not clearly differentiate its explicit M²E modeling from alternative approaches that estimate PSF parameters or perform model adaptation within iterative reconstruction. The use of a latent-space representation for M²E needs stronger justification—specifically, why it is superior to directly estimating PSF parameters and whether the learned latent space is interpretable or relates to physical imaging parameters (R2).
- **Insufficient evaluation on public/real-world datasets:** The method is evaluated on the authors’ proprietary dataset and on a dataset of 2D images displayed on a screen at a fixed distance. There is no evidence of generalization to real-world 3D scenes, webcam captures, or scenes found in earlier lensless imaging papers (PHlatCam, FlatNet). The new dataset is similarly restricted to 2D images on a display. Furthermore, one dataset is claimed to be public but lacks details on scene diversity, noise levels, and PSF variation range, hindering reproducibility and fair comparison (R1, R2).
- **Mathematical derivation issues (multiple reviewers):** 
  - The derivation from Equation (5) to (6) is not clearly justified; the use of Bayes’ rule and the introduction of log p(x) to support proportionality is not explained step-by-step (R1, R4).
  - Splitting the joint MAP problem in (7) into two independent optimization problems (8a, 8b) is not mathematically valid in general; Equation (8b) appears to treat A as a constant when optimizing x, but the term log p(A | x̂) (which depends on x through x̂ = Ax + noise) is then constant only under an unstated assumption (R1, R4).
  - Equation (8b) is also questionable because it optimizes for x but appears to use A* without defining how A* is obtained simultaneously; it has two unknowns but only solves for one (R1).
  - Equation (16) uses an integral to describe a discrete model w_i, which is inconsistent (R4).
- **Missing comparisons with recent state-of-the-art:** Several recent works, such as “Lensless Imaging Based on Dual-Input Physics-Driven Neural Network” (Adv. Photon. Res. 2024) and “DeepLIR” (WACV 2024), are not mentioned or compared. A more comprehensive comparison with all recent SOTA methods is needed (R3).
- **Ablation study confusion:**
  - The difference between “TFR + MSRN + M2ER” and the “Full model” is not clearly explained, despite a significant improvement in PSNR/SSIM (Table 3) (R3).
  - In the loss ablation (Table 3), “Lmse + 1.0 * Lp” yields the best performance, but the paper chooses “0.01 * Lp” for subsequent tests without justification (R3).
  - The color highlighting in Table 1 is confusing (R3).
- **Simple M²E representation design:** The proposed M²ER module is relatively simple, and the overall reconstruction model design is also simple (R3).
- **Mathematical inaccuracies and presentation issues:**
  - The term “biased” is used inappropriately for the estimate Ô (it may be unbiased, just noisy). Similarly, the PSF in Fig. 7 may be a measured PSF, not “biased” (R1).
  - Citations often point to recent papers that mention a result rather than the original source (e.g., citing a 2024 paper for a convolutional lensless camera model) (R1).
  - Phrasing like “mine a variable” is unclear (R1).
  - Line 318 would be more recognizable as FFT-based deconvolution if the inverse were moved inside the parentheses (R1).
- **Unclear assumptions:** The paper assumes M²E follows a Gaussian distribution and pixel values follow a Laplacian distribution, but lacks detailed theoretical justification for these choices (R2).
- **Limited noise model generality:** It is unclear if the method extends to Poisson or negative binomial noise for low-light images (R4).
