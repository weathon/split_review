## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies learning in adversarial Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) where both losses and transitions are chosen adversarially, but the transition kernel is revealed to the agent at the end of each episode. The authors introduce the concept of a smoothed MDP, which aggregates past transitions via a generic function \(f_t\), and define smoothed regret accordingly. They propose Smoothed Online Mirror Descent (SOMD), an algorithm that uses a novel regularization term to achieve sublinear smoothed regret. For full-information feedback, they prove a regret bound of \(\widetilde{\mathcal O}(L^{3/2}\sqrt{TL}+L\overline{C}_f^{\mathsf{P}})\), and for bandit feedback, \(\widetilde{\mathcal O}(L^{3/2}\sqrt{XAT}+L\overline{C}_f^{\mathsf{P}})\), where \(T\) is the number of episodes, \(L\) the horizon, and \(\overline{C}_f^{\mathsf{P}}\) a measure of transition variation. The framework is designed to subsume prior models as special cases.

### Strengths
- The problem is important: it addresses online learning under adversarial rewards and transitions, a core challenge in adversarial MDPs (Reviewers 2, 4).
- The introduced concepts of smoothed MDP and smoothed regret are interesting and unify several previous models; the connections to prior work are well-highlighted (Reviewers 2, 4).
- The SOMD algorithm is flexible: it can incorporate different aggregation functions \(f_t\), which is more general than classical OMD (Reviewer 1). The algorithm is clearly described and easy to follow (Reviewer 4).
- The regret bounds have a nice dependency on both \(T\) and the transition variation measure \(\overline{C}_f^{\mathsf{P}}\) (Reviewer 3).
- The technical development includes a new regret decomposition and a new technique – smoothed OMD – which aids the analysis (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Strong assumption:** The requirement that the transition is revealed at the end of every round is a significant simplification. Since the main difficulty in adversarial MDPs is the unknown and adversarial transition, revealing it substantially reduces the challenge (Reviewers 2, 4). Reviewers 1 and 3 note that this assumption makes the results less surprising.
- **Limited novelty compared to [Jin et al., 2023]:** Multiple reviewers (1, 2, 3) argue that the contribution is incremental relative to [Jin et al., 2023], which established similar regret bounds without the transition-revelation assumption. The interesting regret results in the paper are derived under the average smoothing function (Example 3.2), which leads to almost the same bound as [Jin et al., 2023]. Reviewer 2 questions whether the general Theorem 4.1 can yield sublinear regret with a refined transition error measure beyond that in [Jin et al., 2023]. Reviewer 3 finds the core idea – using revealed transitions to average them – straightforward and unsurprising given the assumption \(\sum_t \|P_t - P\| \le C\).
- **Unclear motivation:** The paper does not provide a clear real-world or theoretical motivation for why the transition would be revealed to the agent at the end of each episode. The abstract’s connection between adversarial MDPs and the revealed transition setting is not directly justified (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 also asks for motivating examples. Reviewer 2 notes that while the paper introduces an additional assumption and a more general framework, it offers limited new learnability results.
- **Choice of smoothing function:** It is unclear how to select the aggregation function \(f_t\) efficiently to minimize regret, even when transitions are revealed. Reviewer 1 raises a definitional issue: if \(f_t\) can depend on the current transition \(P_t\), one could trivially set \(f_t=P_t\) to make \(\overline{C}_f^{\mathsf{P}}=0\), suggesting that \(f_t\) should depend only on past transitions \((P_1,\dots,P_{t-1})\). Reviewer 2 asks whether other smoothing functions (e.g., Example 3.1) could improve the additive transition error term over the average function used in [Jin et al., 2023].
- **Standard techniques:** Beyond the smoothed OMD component, the other techniques (occupancy measures, implicit exploration, the regret translation from smoothed to standard regret) are standard for adversarial MDPs. The smoothed OMD design itself is related to ideas from [Mirror Descent Meets Fixed Share] (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 3 states that the methodology does not appear generalizable to more challenging settings (e.g., imperfect transition feedback or different variation metrics).
- **Lack of technical insight for regularization:** Reviewer 2 requests more explanation for the choice of the smoothed regularization term in Algorithm 1 (line 4) – specifically, why that particular form is needed for the analysis.
- **Disagreement on contribution strength:** Reviewers 1 and 3 assess the contribution as low (scores 3, contribution 2 and 1 respectively) and find the results unsurprising, while Reviewers 2 and 4 are more positive (scores 5, contribution 2 each) and emphasize the value of the new concepts and unified framework. However, even the more positive reviewers note limitations in novelty relative to prior work.
