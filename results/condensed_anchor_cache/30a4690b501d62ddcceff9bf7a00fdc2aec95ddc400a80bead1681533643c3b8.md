## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper claims that curiosity reduces the hypothesis space and provides optimality bounds in contrastive multi-skill reinforcement learning. It presents a theoretical analysis and an algorithm (CB) with experiments in a low-dimensional 2D environment. Reviewer assessments are divergent: Reviewer 3 (score 5, confidence 5) finds the paper easy to follow with both theoretical and empirical support, while Reviewers 1, 2, and 4 (scores 3, 3, 1) find the paper poorly written, with undefined notations, mathematical errors, overclaimed contributions, and insufficient experimental validation. The following strengths and weaknesses are extracted from all reviews.

### Strengths
- The paper provides detailed mathematical proofs and accompanying experiments that strengthen the theoretical assertions (Reviewer 2, confidence 4).
- Both empirical and theoretical evidence are presented to support the claims (Reviewer 3, confidence 5).
- The title is noted as appealing, though not fully representative of the content (Reviewer 4, confidence 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and Presentation**: The abstract and introduction are hard to comprehend; motivation for curiosity is unclear and background on PAC-MDP is missing, making the paper inaccessible (Reviewer 2, confidence 4). The paper is full of errors and inconsistent notations; it is difficult to evaluate the contribution without clear presentation (Reviewer 1, confidence 3). The writing is very poor, preventing understanding of the intended ideas (Reviewer 4, confidence 5). In contrast, Reviewer 3 (confidence 5) found the paper easy to follow.  
- **Undefined and Inconsistent Notations**: Multiple key variables are undefined or inconsistently used:  
  - $T$ denotes trajectories, transition function, and time steps (Reviewer 1).  
  - $C$ is both contrastive space and a variance (Reviewer 1).  
  - $I$ is used for identity matrix and then for information gain without definition (Reviewer 1).  
  - $\theta$ appears without definition in Eq. 1 and as a subscript in $\psi(s_\theta)_\theta$ (Reviewers 1, 4).  
  - $\tilde{\psi}$ is never defined (Reviewer 1).  
  - $\psi(S)$ takes the state space as input, unclear meaning (Reviewer 1).  
  - The variable $s$ is used for both states and skills (Reviewers 1, 2).  
  - $k$ (line 146) and parameter $\theta$ (line 185) are not defined (Reviewer 2).  
  - $S$ is unclear as metric space vs. state space (Reviewers 2, 4).  
  - The set $K$ of skills and set $T$ of trajectories are introduced without definition (Reviewer 4).  
  - $\psi_B$ definition involves $T$ and undefined $s_j$, $s_{j,t}$ (Reviewer 4).  
  - The policy is claimed to be one-dimensional without explanation (Reviewer 2).  
- **Mathematical Errors and Insufficient Justification**:  
  - The proof of Lemma B.1 is poorly written, inaccurate, and confuses sums of random variables with sums of densities; the limit $\sigma\to0$ makes KL diverge, invalidating upper bounds (Reviewer 4).  
  - The choice of Gaussian distribution is not justified (Reviewer 2).  
  - State visitation is assumed to be point distributions, making KL ill-defined (Reviewer 1).  
  - In Section 3.2: standard MDP definition misrepresented (”where $R$ represents the space of possible reward functions” is wrong); transition kernel bounded by 1 is not guaranteed; reward assumption is incorrect; equation for expected return contains errors ($R$ vs $R’$, $\theta$ indexing, missing discount) (Reviewer 4).  
  - The assumption that L2 distance is useful between states is strong and undiscussed (Reviewer 4).  
  - Theorem 4.1: $\hat{J}$, $R_{\text{max}}$, $s_{\theta,t}$ are undefined, making the theorem useless (Reviewer 4).  
  - Eq. (2) is described as maximizing minimum distance between skills but implements sum of distances to mean, and its optimization target is unclear (Reviewer 1).  
  - Theorem 4.2 states results on undefined objects ($I$, $\tilde{\psi}$, indexing), making the statement incomprehensible (Reviewer 4).  
  - The constant $C$ in Theorem 4.1 is unclearly explained (“how could you divide a space?”) (Reviewer 2).  
  - Theorem 4.1 does not help the discussion (Reviewer 1).  
  - Proof of Theorem B.1 lacks justification for inequalities (norm unspecified, use of Hölder without definition of total variation), and jumps from $L_2$ to a generic metric $d$ (Reviewer 4).  
- **Overclaims and Insufficient Evidence**: The paper overclaims contributions: “mathematically proven that curiosity provides bounds to guarantee optimality” – curiosity and optimality are never defined (Reviewer 4). Claims of applicability to real-world scenarios are unsupported; the only experiment is on a 2D environment (Reviewer 4). Claiming “to surpass other prominent algorithms” lacks empirical evidence (Reviewer 4). Claims about curiosity reducing hypothesis space, skills self-categorizing into tree structures, and curiosity being identical to hypothesis space compression are not substantiated (Reviewer 4). Appendix D (“The Ultimate Significance of This Study”) is representative of the overly promotional tone (Reviewer 4).  
- **Experimental Weaknesses**: The experiment is only on a low-dimensional 2D environment, raising doubts about performance in higher dimensions (Reviewer 3, confidence 5). The experimental result is weak and does not convincingly demonstrate exploration of the hypothesis space (Reviewer 3). The algorithm and experiment lack sufficient details for reproducibility (Reviewer 4). The paper mentions “experiments across a variety of MDP environments” but only shows one 2D environment with no rewards, contradicting the MOMDP framing (Reviewer 4).  
- **Missing Comparisons, Ablations, and Analyses**: The difference between the proposed algorithm (CB) and existing methods APT [1] and APS [2] is not clarified (Reviewer 3). Sample complexity of CB is not provided (Reviewer 3). It is requested to evaluate CB on more complex environments, at least MiniGrid level with image observations (Reviewer 3). The undefined notation questions from Reviewers 1 and 2 (e.g., how information gain is computed, the role of $z$ and $\epsilon$ in the policy, the justification for Gaussian mixture state distribution) are also pointed out as criticisms.
