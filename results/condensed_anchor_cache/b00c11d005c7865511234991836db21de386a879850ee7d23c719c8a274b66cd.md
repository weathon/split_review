## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies and theoretically analyzes two major limitations of structured state space models (SSMs): a strong recency bias (exponentially decaying dependence on distant inputs) and over-smoothing (token representations becoming indistinguishable in deep SSMs). The authors provide theoretical derivations and empirical experiments (adversarial attacks, needle-in-a-haystack tasks, scaling experiments) to substantiate these claims. All four reviewers found the paper well-written, the findings important, and the experiments closely linked to the theory.

### Strengths
- The findings are important and open up future directions to address SSM limitations (R1, R4).  
- The adversarial attack section is praiseworthy, using the recency bias finding to demonstrate a practical vulnerability (R1).  
- Both findings are substantiated with theoretical understanding and intuitive derivations (R1).  
- The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and interesting; it provides a clear reference for known SSM pitfalls (R2, R3).  
- Practical examples of failure cases (e.g., needle-in-a-haystack) are valuable (R2).  
- The plots are high-quality and clear, useful for future research (R2).  
- Experiments are extensive and effectively support the theoretical results (R3).  
- The limitations are examined from multiple perspectives (recency, robustness, over-smoothing), with experiments closely linked to theory (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty of exponential memory result**: Two reviewers noted that the exponential decay of memory in SSMs is already established. Reviewer 2 cited [OpenReview; arXiv 2402.01032; arXiv 2312.04927; arXiv 2406.07887]; reviewer 4 cited Wang & Xue (NeurIPS 2023, Theorem 3.13). The finding is considered not novel nor surprising (R2, R4).  
- **Exponential memory does not directly imply performance loss**: Reviewer 2 pointed out that exponential memory alone does not entail perplexity degradation or length generalization issues (citing arXiv 2410.11135; xLSTM 2405.04517). Additionally, ALiBi positional embeddings induce a similar exponential decay.  
- **CIFAR robustness example is weak**: Constructing a 1D model for 2D data is problematic; in 1D data recency bias is actually helpful. The example needs careful framing (R2).  
- **No discussion of mitigation strategies**: The paper identifies problems but does not provide any hints or discussion on how to solve them (R2, R3).  
- **Over-smoothing claim lacks extensive empirical support**: Reviewer 1 finds the empirical validation for over-smoothing insufficient. Reviewer 3 notes that over-smoothing (performance degradation when stacking layers) does not clearly manifest in the experiments of Section 4.1, and asks whether this phenomenon is inherently absent or a limitation of the experimental setup. Reviewer 4 requests additional experiments on the dynamics of A_min and A_max during training to clarify the trade-off mentioned at line 463.  
- **Experimental details missing for Section 4.1**: The setup for the over-smoothing experiments is not fully described, making them hard to assess (R3).  
- **Recency bias experiments limited to few pre-trained models**: Reviewer 4 suggests observing the change across parameter counts, as done in Figure 4, and checking whether recency bias reduces when hidden state dimensions and number of heads are moderately increased.  
- **Missing trade-off analysis**: The paper suggests a trade-off between A_max (recency) and A_min (over-smoothing) at line 463, but it is not trivial whether this is a real challenge since both can hold simultaneously (A_max≈1, A_min≈0). Dynamics of these parameters during training should be examined (R4).  
- **Missing comparison to Transformers**: Reviewer 4 asks whether over-smoothing occurs in Transformers and if similar verification (as in Figures 4 and 5) can be performed for Transformers. Also, there is a query about differences between SSMs and Transformers at shorter context lengths (~1000 vs >10000).  
- **Missing discussion on initialization**: Reviewer 1 asks how initialization schemes of SSMs interfere with the findings (recency bias and over-smoothing).  
- **Missing comparison to RNN vanishing gradient**: Reviewer 3 notes that the recency bias phenomenon appears similar to the vanishing gradient problem in RNNs and requests a discussion comparing the two.  
- **Minor typos**: Equation 4 has incorrect indices (i,j should be s,t); Equations 18 and 19 have reversed inequality signs (R3).
