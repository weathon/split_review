## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes a hierarchical autonomy pipeline for off-road autonomous driving: a low-frequency high-level MPPI planner generates sparse waypoints, which are tracked by a high-frequency low-level RL controller. To address RL exploration challenges, the authors introduce a teacher-student paradigm. The teacher policy is trained with dense planning information from a (presumed) high-frequency MPPI planner, while the student policy learns from visual inputs and sparse planning information. The core algorithmic contribution is Teacher Action Distillation Policy Optimization (TADPO), a reformulation of PPO that uses off-policy teacher trajectories for guidance and on-policy student trajectories for exploration. Experiments in the BeamNG off-road simulator evaluate success rate, completion percentage, and mean speed, reporting that TADPO outperforms several on-policy, off-policy, and imitation learning baselines.

Reviewer opinions were split. Two reviewers (R1, R4, both score 6) found the work innovative and well-motivated. A third reviewer (R3, score 3) saw promise but raised concerns about generalizability and real-world applicability. One reviewer (R2, score 1) was highly critical, questioning the correctness of the MPPI formulation, the sufficiency of the experimental validation, and the novelty relative to existing work.

### Strengths

- **Novel integration of MPPI and RL in a teacher-student framework** to address off-road driving challenges. The hierarchical setup is well-motivated: it combines long-horizon planning (MPPI) with real-time control (RL). *(R1, R2, R4)*
- **Real-time execution capability**: the low-level RL controller operates at high frequency with low latency, making the framework suitable for real-time off-road applications. *(R1, R3, R4)*
- **Effective exploration guidance**: the teacher provides better coverage of the state distribution via demonstrated trajectories, helping the policy gradient method to explore more efficiently than vanilla PPO. *(R1)*
- **Faster, less frequent planning at deployment** because the sparse planner replaces the dense MPPI planner during execution. *(R1)*
- The paper provides a **detailed contrastive comparison** of why existing IL, on-policy, and off-policy methods struggle in diverse off-road terrains, helping to justify the proposed approach. *(R1)*
- One reviewer (R2) acknowledged the **novel idea and setup** of distilling teacher knowledge via PPO, even though that reviewer ultimately rated the paper low due to other issues. *(R2 minority positive)*

### Weaknesses

1. **Incorrect MPPI description.** Equation (7) suggests greedy selection of the most cost-efficient trajectory, whereas standard MPPI uses importance sampling to average over all sampled trajectories. A reviewer (R2) questions whether using importance sampling or cross-entropy planning [4] would yield more stable convergence. *(R2)*

2. **Experimental validation does not isolate TADPO’s contribution.**  
   - In Table 1, only TADPO and “MPPI + Teacher” use the sparse MPPI planner at test time; all other baselines (PPO, SAC, BC, etc.) do not. Thus the results validate the hierarchical tracking formulation, but do not demonstrate whether TADPO’s modified PPO objective is superior to a simple behavior cloning (BC) loss for distilling the teacher. *(R2)*  
   - The reviewer (R2) also notes that the method “SAC + Teacher” (described in Appendix) is described as putting teacher trajectories into the replay buffer, but it is not reported whether this variant was actually included in the final experiments.

3. **Missing baseline comparisons.**  
   - Traditional planning methods (MPPI itself, other MPC algorithms, sampling-based motion planners) are absent, making it hard to assess absolute performance gain. *(R4)*  
   - Important related work is not discussed or compared: RL-driven MPPI that accelerates trajectory sampling with a learned policy [1], learning terminal value functions for MPPI [1,5,6], guided policy search [2], and high-level MPC with low-level RL [3]. *(R2)*  
   - Broader comparisons to end-to-end DRL models would also help clarify strengths/limitations of the hierarchical design. *(R3)*

4. **Generalization and real-world applicability are unaddressed.**  
   - No experiments or ablations examine robustness to domain shifts (e.g., different terrains, lighting, weather). *(R1, R3)*  
   - Real-world challenges (sensor noise, varying soil conditions—mud, sand, gravel, vegetation occlusion) are not modeled. The method appears unsuited for real-world deployment outside simulation. *(R1, R3)*  
   - The dense teacher guidance requires an accurate environment model; this may not be available in dynamic or unknown real scenarios, and the student policy has no explicit recovery mechanism when teacher guidance is suboptimal or absent. *(R3)*

5. **Missing complexity and efficiency analysis.** The paper claims real-time capability but provides no inference times for TADPO, nor a comparison of computational cost (policy evaluation, gradient computation, action selection) against baselines or traditional planners. *(R4)*

6. **Exploration limitations not resolved.** The method does not explicitly address how the student policy recovers from exploration failures or from situations where teacher guidance is unavailable. *(R3)*

7. **Safety not addressed.** The end-to-end DRL controller lacks guarantees for high-risk off-road maneuvers, raising concerns about costly accidents in real applications. *(R3)*

8. **Clarity and presentation issues.**  
   - The dynamics model used by the MPPI planners (sparse and dense) is not specified. *(R2)*  
   - It is unclear whether baselines use the MPPI planner during testing; confusing naming (“MPPI + TADPO” in Figure 1 vs “TADPO” in Table 1). *(R2)*  
   - Notation errors: Equation (1) argmax returns a function, but left side is a probability density. *(R2)*  
   - Figure 5 has scaling issues. *(R2)*  
   - Figures 2, 3, and 5 are not cited in the text. *(R2)*  
   - Appendix A.3.2 incorrectly refers to “teacher policy” instead of “student policy”. *(R2)*  
   - The introduction is lengthy and would benefit from shortening. *(R2)*  
   - Nits: Equation 2 missing variable A_cap; line 704 unformatted bold; line 752 overloading of action space symbols. *(R1)*  
   - Hyperparameters (e.g., GAE) are in the appendix, but the main paper does not clearly state that GAE is used. *(R2)*

9. **Questions that overlap with missing analysis** (included as weaknesses):  
   - Would using importance sampling averaging or Cross-Entropy Planning [4] instead of greedy trajectory selection improve planner stability? *(R2)*  
   - Why was MPPI chosen over other SOTA traditional planning methods for off-road driving? *(R4)*  
   - Could the hierarchical framework be applied to urban autonomous driving? *(R1; not a criticism but noted as an open question)*  
   - Can ablations be provided to study robustness to domain shifts? *(R1)*
