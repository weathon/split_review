## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces FINBENCH, a benchmark designed to evaluate LLMs on complex, knowledge-intensive financial problem-solving with multimodal contexts. The dataset consists of 4,235 graduate-level questions from three classic finance textbooks, assessing five core capabilities: terminology understanding, temporal reasoning, future forecasting, scenario planning, and numerical modeling. Tasks include statement judging, multi-choice QA, and financial calculation. The knowledge bank comprises 3,032 finance terms. Experiments on 18 LLMs show o1 achieves 67.3% accuracy, still 12.5% behind human experts, with particular weaknesses in temporal reasoning, scenario planning, rounding errors, and visual-context interpretation. The authors note a 3% error rate in answer annotations from human validation of 1,000 examples.

### Strengths

- **Originality and significance**: First comprehensive benchmark extending beyond basic numerical extraction to evaluate advanced financial reasoning (terminology, temporal reasoning, forecasting, scenario planning, numerical modeling) across diverse graduate-level finance topics with multimodal context. Sourced from textbooks and supplemented with GPT-4o enhancements. Provides systematic evaluation framework with five capabilities and three tasks, plus a substantial knowledge bank for augmentation analysis. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4; one reviewer with rating 8 was particularly impressed: "innovative contribution," "sets FINBENCH apart" — Reviewer 2)
- **Quality and thoroughness**: Methodology is robust, with human oversight in data collection, augmentation, and validation. Experiments cover 18 recent LLMs, include human expert comparison, and present detailed error analysis identifying specific failure modes (rounding errors, visual-context "blindness"). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Clarity and presentation**: Paper is well-structured, with clear explanations of dataset construction, task classifications, evaluation metrics, and experimental setups. Visual materials (images, tables) are clearly presented and effectively organized. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Value to community**: Benchmark insights into model limitations are valuable for guiding future AI developments in finance and other high-stakes fields. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)

### Weaknesses

- **Reliability of ground truth labels**: The paper reports a ~3% error rate in answer annotations from a human validation sample of 1,000 examples, implying a ~6% uncertainty interval. Human expert accuracy is only 80%. Multiple reviewers (1, 4) strongly recommend full manual verification of all 4,000+ questions by expert evaluators to ensure benchmark quality. Reliance on GPT-4o for re-annotation/validation raises concerns about potential biases and inconsistencies from prompt-engineering techniques. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Lack of comparisons with existing benchmarks**: No direct performance comparisons on related financial/numerical benchmarks (e.g., TAT-QA, FinQA) or financial components of broader benchmarks (e.g., MMLU-Pro, MMMU). This undermines demonstration of FINBENCH’s distinct difficulty and contribution. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Limited domain coverage**: Only three classic finance textbooks are used as source material. Expanding to additional specialized areas or online professional resources would increase diversity and comprehensiveness. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **No dataset or code provided**: Absence of released data/code prevents independent verification and replication. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Insufficient analysis of prompting sensitivity**: LLMs are known to be sensitive to prompting strategies, but no ablation studies on different prompt templates (e.g., Chain-of-Thought vs. direct answering) are conducted. This limits understanding of benchmark robustness. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Knowledge augmentation setup unclear**: Missing implementation details: how many retrieved knowledge pieces are used, the exact prompt construction, and how definitions are extracted (text-only vs. including math/figures). These are not clearly specified in main text or appendix. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing ablation on Chain-of-Thought**: The paper does not report whether CoT reasoning improves or degrades performance on FINBENCH, despite related work suggesting CoT may not help on all reasoning tasks. (Reviewer 1)
- **Applicant qualifications not disclosed**: It is unclear who the annotators/evaluators are (authors, experienced financial professionals?) and their backgrounds, important for assessing label quality. (Reviewer 1)
- **Data leakage prevention not discussed**: No specific measures reported to mitigate risk of test data contamination from LLM training corpora. (Reviewer 3)
- **Benchmark naming conflict**: The name FINBENCH overlaps with existing works (e.g., LDBC Financial Benchmark, FinPT). A rename is suggested to avoid confusion. (Reviewer 1)
- **Terminology issue**: "Finance subject" (e.g., Fig. 3) should be "finance concept" for accuracy. (Reviewer 1)
- **Table caption formatting**: Caption should be placed above the table per ICLR format instructions. (Reviewer 1)
- **No proposed mitigation for identified errors**: The paper identifies rounding errors and visual-context "blindness" as primary failure modes but does not evaluate or propose specific steps to address them (e.g., improving numerical precision, training on intersecting curves). (Reviewer 2’s question, turned into weakness as a missing experiment/ablation request)
- **Inconsistent improvement from knowledge augmentation**: The paper finds knowledge augmentation only consistently helps small open-source models, but does not provide analysis on why this pattern occurs (e.g., model size, retrieval quality, or task type). (Reviewer 2’s question, turned into weakness)
- **Minor formatting and wording concerns**: Use of "finance subject" instead of "finance concept"; table captions placement. (Reviewer 1)
