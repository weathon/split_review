## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper tackles single-domain generalization without needing visual or textual descriptions of the target domain. It leverages the modality-gap property of CLIP: by shifting source visual embeddings using relative differences of textual embeddings (e.g., “a photo of a cat” → “a painting of a cat”), it performs training-free feature-space augmentations to create domain-invariant embeddings. A linear probe is then trained on a mixture of original and augmented source embeddings and applied directly to target image embeddings. Experiments are on CUB and DomainNet. Performance shows minor improvements over the LADS baseline. Reviewer 1 (score 8) finds the work technically sound and well-motivated. Reviewer 2 (score 5) notes the practical setting but has concerns about marginal gains and limited ablations. Reviewer 3 (score 3, unchanged after rebuttal) raises serious concerns about novelty due to strong similarity with CLIP the GAP (CGAP) and the small performance margin over LADS.

### Strengths
- The paper is generally well-written, clear, and easy to follow; definitions and formulas are explicit (R1, R3).
- The motivation is solid, building on recent findings about CLIP’s multimodal embedding space (Liang et al., Zhang et al.) (R1).
- The proposed method is training-free and therefore time-efficient compared to methods like LADS that require training separate augmentation networks (R1, R2).
- The setting is practical: target domain text descriptions are not required; arbitrary crafted unseen domain descriptions suffice (R1, R2).
- The paper tackles the highly challenging task of single-domain generalization (R3).
- The authors are honest about limitations and discuss advantages/disadvantages (R1).

### Weaknesses
**Novelty and Relation to Prior Work:**
- The paper does not cite or discuss CLIP the GAP (CGAP, [1]), which shares the same fundamental observation (different domains of the same object differ by constant vectors in CLIP space) and performs semantic augmentations using relative text embedding differences. Both papers show that precise domain names are unnecessary (CGAP Table 7: “random” augmentations). The current work’s main claimed difference is training-free vs. training-based, but this still makes the contribution incremental and raises a new set of training-free baselines to compare against (R3). The authors acknowledged similarity in the rebuttal but did not update the paper or add discussion of CGAP, which remains a critical omission (R3).
- The theoretical contribution is limited: the proof that z exists in CLIP’s normalized embedding space is trivial (R2). The paper lacks deeper analysis of the geometry or distribution of the CLIP embedding space that enables domain generalization (R1, R2).

**Performance and Experimental Scope:**
- The performance gains over the strongest baseline LADS are marginal: +0.78% on CUB and +0.92% on DomainNet (Table 1); on some datasets the method does not stand out significantly (R2, R3). This minor improvement raises doubts about practical significance, especially given the close resemblance to CGAP (R3).
- The evaluation is limited to CUB and DomainNet; the method should be tested on larger, more complex datasets like WILDS, which contains diverse real-world domain shifts (e.g., background, lighting, viewpoints) (R1).
- Ablation studies are limited: it is unclear why the linear probe is trained on a mixture of original and augmented embeddings rather than only on augmented embeddings (R2, question). The effect of the mixing proportion is not explored (R2). No empirical analysis is provided for when Equation 4 (the condition for domain-invariant augmentation) fails; the authors mention in Figure 2 (right) that cases may exist but do not analyze them. Does the CLIP feature always satisfy Equation 4? When does it fail? (R1, question).  
- The notation “1x” for time in tables is unclear – it is not defined whether this is wall-clock or computational time, nor what the baseline for that relative measure is (R1).

**Methodological Questions:**
- Why can a linear probe trained on domain-invariant features (augmented from source) be directly applied to target features without any further augmentation? (R2, question). The paper does not provide a clear explanation of the inference procedure’s theoretical justification (R2).
- The paper sometimes overemphasizes LADS rather than its own contribution (R3).  
- Minor typo: [LADS reference] “Using language to *extend* to unseen domains” and “Benefiting from … *we* achieve …” (R3).

[1] *CLIP the GAP: A Connection between Domain Generalization and Domain Adaptation*, 2023.
