## Merged Review

### Summary
CityGaussianV2 builds on 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) and CityGaussian to address scalability and geometric accuracy in large‑scale scene reconstruction. It introduces three main contributions: (1) Decomposed‑Gradient‑based Densification (DGD) and depth regression using pre‑trained Depth Anything V2 to eliminate blurry artifacts and accelerate convergence; (2) an Elongation Filter to mitigate Gaussian count explosion; (3) an optimized parallel training pipeline achieving up to 10× compression, 25% training time savings, and 50% memory reduction. The paper also establishes new evaluation benchmarks for large‑scale geometry. Experiments show the method balances visual quality, geometric accuracy, and resource costs.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a critical and challenging problem: large‑scale 3D reconstruction with Gaussian splatting.
- Results are outstanding: faster convergence, more accurate photorealistic reconstruction, and lower memory usage compared to existing baselines (R1). The method scales CityGaussian without degrading visual quality or geometric accuracy (R3).
- Comprehensive and fair evaluation against all relevant SotA methods (R1). Thorough ablation studies analyze the impact of each module (R3, R4).
- Engineering contributions are substantial: the integration of DGD, Elongation Filter, and parallel training forms a thoughtful and complete pipeline (R4).
- The paper is well‑written with clear logic and appropriate terminology (R2, R3, R4). Complex concepts are explained clearly (R2).
- Additional valuable contributions: new evaluation criteria that better capture geometric details (R1); establishment of a large‑scale geometry reconstruction benchmark (R4).
- One reviewer (R3, score 8) is notably more positive, highlighting that the framework effectively addresses critical scalability issues and that the experimental evaluation is complete and supports the claims.

### Weaknesses
- Limited novelty: the method combines existing techniques (2DGS, Depth Anything, CityGaussian) with ad‑hoc modifications; contributions are more engineering than novel theoretical insight (R1, R2, R3, R4). R1 questions whether improvements come from meta‑fine tuning rather than genuine algorithmic advances.
- Lack of theoretical justification: the optimization techniques (DGD, elongation filter) are justified only empirically; no solid reasoning is provided for why they work (R1).
- Robustness to lighting and appearance is missing: the method does not model shadows, specular reflections, or ambient illumination changes common in outdoor scenes; decoupled appearance models (e.g., GOF) could improve adaptability (R2).
- Elongation Filter concerns: no mechanism for handling cases where splitting is necessary; directly limiting splits may cause under‑reconstruction in detailed areas. Table 2 shows the method with Elongation Filter uses more Gaussians than CityGaussian (the baseline), which seems contradictory to the filter's purpose (R2).
- Discrepancy in reported Gaussian counts: the count shown in Figure 3 for the author's method is less than CityGaussian, but Table 2 shows the opposite; this inconsistency raises concerns about reliability (R2).
- Rendering quality is slightly inferior to CityGS across all datasets, even though reconstruction quality is better; the reason is not explained (R4).
- Missing experimental details: baseline hyperparameter tuning process is not described (R1); hardware used for baseline runs is not stated—should confirm same 8 A100 GPUs (R4). The paper should include metrics (Gaussian count, training time, memory) for all compared methods under similar resource constraints to fairly demonstrate advantage (R1, R4).
- Table 2 lacks units for processing time (seconds/minutes/hours) and memory (MB/GB), hindering interpretation (R2).
- Limitations are only briefly mentioned in the appendix; a dedicated section in the main text is needed for balance (R2).
- Reviewer disagreement: while R3 (score 8) is more positive, the other three reviewers (score 6) share the above concerns about novelty and missing details.
- Minor issues: lines 189-190 appear to have R and S symbols flipped (R3). Ground plane estimation procedure (line 352) lacks details on number of points, scene scale, and hardware to achieve claimed 1‑minute runtime (R3). Ablation results show some modules have only minor impact on final performance (R4).
