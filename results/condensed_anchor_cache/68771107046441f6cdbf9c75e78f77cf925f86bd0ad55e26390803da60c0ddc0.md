## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes boosting semi-supervised 2D human pose estimation (SSHPE) via two cores: (1) ranking existing data augmentations by difficulty and combining them into easy-hard pairs (nominating TJOCO and TJCCM as superior), and (2) a multi-path consistency training framework that applies multiple hard augmentations to unlabeled images and optimizes multiple losses within a single network. Reviewers generally acknowledge the empirical effectiveness of the augmentation combinations and the simplicity of the single-network design, with decent improvements over baselines in several settings (COCO, MPII, WEPDTOF-Pose). However, multiple reviewers raise strong concerns about limited novelty, the lack of theoretical grounding for the augmentation ranking, missing comparisons against foundation models and 3D pose estimators, and inconsistent improvements across architectures. Some reviewers also note insufficient ablation studies, potential biases in small-set evaluation, and clarity issues. Several reviewers (R3, R5) are notably more critical, while others (R1, R2, R4) find the work practically useful despite these gaps.

### Strengths

- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The systematic ranking of augmentations for heatmap-based pose estimation is a novel and important empirical contribution; the identification of synergistic effects among augmentations (e.g., TJOCO, TJCCM) is useful.
- The multi-path consistency training in a single network is simple, interpretable, and efficient, and can surpass certain dual-network approaches (Table 3).
- Experimental results show decent improvements over existing SOTA on COCO validation (Table 3) and with ResNet-50/101 backbones (Table 4), and generalization to WEPDTOF-Pose (Table 9).
- The paper provides a comprehensive evaluation of existing data augmentation methods and their combinations for SSHPE.
- Code release supports reproducibility and open science.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty / overclaim**: Several augmentations (Joint Cutout, Joint Cut-Occlude) are already known. Consistency training has been extensively explored in prior work (Xie et al., 2021; Moskvyak et al., 2021; Li & Lee, 2023; Huang et al., 2023). The method is closely aligned with SSPCM without meaningful differentiation in either novelty or accuracy (Tables 3,4). The augmentation combination strategy (e.g., selecting TJOCO, TJCCM) appears to be a hyperparameter search rather than a principled design. A more advanced augmentation generation paradigm (e.g., PoseAug) is suggested as a stronger alternative.
- **Purely empirical augmentation ranking / no theoretical insight**: The ranking of augmentations (e.g., why T_JC is harder than T_CO) is based solely on empirical observation without quantitative difficulty measures or intuitive principles. No theoretical analysis is provided to help rank any new augmentations beyond those tested. Parameters of each augmentation (e.g., JC5, RA20) are not discussed, and their influence on the rank/combination is unclear.
- **Missing critical comparisons**: No evaluation against foundation models in zero-shot setting (e.g., Sapiens) or high-accuracy 3D pose estimators (e.g., BEDLAM with 3D-to-2D projection). This omission raises questions about the necessity of the SSHPE setting. Also missing comparison with unsupervised domain adaptation methods (e.g., UDAPE) in Table 4. Reviewer 3 notes that the authors’ claim that these models “cannot be tested” is unjustified—zero-shot evaluation is feasible and needed.
- **Architecture dependence / inconsistent improvements**: Gains with ResNet backbones are not replicated with HRNet (Table 4), raising concerns about universal applicability. On the COCO test set (Table 5), the method performs similarly to DUAL, yielding only marginal improvement when labeled data is abundant.
- **Insufficient ablations and clarity**: 
  - No ablation isolating the contribution of the multi-path loss alone (R5).
  - No ablation of the two proposed loss functions (L_u and L_S) on labeled vs. unlabeled data (R3).
  - Section 5.3 is not a proper ablation study—baseline and per-module effects not clearly shown (R2).
  - Undefined variables (u, l, N, M) in lines 181–182, and unclear justification for downsampled heatmaps (line 161) (R3).
  - Rationale for choosing exactly TJOCO and TJCCM is not clearly explained (R5).
- **Potential bias in small-set evaluation**: The 1K/5K/10K labeled subsets are randomly selected, but baselines may not be trained on the same subsets; reported improvements may thus be biased (R4).
- **Batch size fairness**: The multi-path consistency loss uses n paired easy-hard images vs. single easy, potentially increasing effective batch size; comparison with other methods that use different batch sizes may be unfair (R2). Also, why not train for 400 epochs in setting S3 (the only difference from SSPCM)? (R4)
- **Practical significance and efficiency**:
  - Dual-network designs still dominate in some settings; the proposed single network cannot outperform them (R1).
  - Combined augmentations increase training time; it is unclear whether this is still lower than stacked networks (R5).
  - The process of selecting the optimal augmentation combination is time-intensive and may require re-evaluation for new augmentations or datasets (R5).
  - On Table 8, the method outperforms only 4 out of 7 joints, not all (R4).
- **Minor issues**: Incorrect citation format (L.47); incorrect punctuation in Eqs. 3 and 4; the division of sections in Table 5 is unclear; and the significance of Fig. 7 given Table 3 is questioned.
