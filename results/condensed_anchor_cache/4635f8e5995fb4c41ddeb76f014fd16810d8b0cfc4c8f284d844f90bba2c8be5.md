## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes PEFTSmoothing, a method that uses parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) to adapt pre-trained vision models to Gaussian noise-augmented data for randomized smoothing (RS) based certified robustness. The authors present a white-box approach (training a trainable layer or using LoRA with cross-entropy loss on noisy inputs) and a black-box approach (using an autoencoder-style coordinator trained with SPSA to denoise inputs before feeding to the frozen base model). They also explore joint fine-tuning for downstream tasks and robustness. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet compare against denoised smoothing (DS) baselines, reporting large gains on CIFAR-10 (98% accuracy vs. SOTA) and ImageNet (61% accuracy, exceeding CNN-based denoisers, comparable to Diffusion-based denoisers). The paper claims efficiency gains by reducing training parameters by 1000× over Diffusion-based denoisers and 10× over CNN-based denoisers. Reviewer scores are 6, 3, 3, 3, with one reviewer much more positive than the other three.

### Strengths
- The fundamental idea of using PEFT to adapt models for randomized smoothing (RS) is interesting and combines advantages of retraining and denoising approaches. (R1, R2)
- The experimental results on CIFAR-10 beat current SOTA denoised smoothing methods by an impressively large margin (98% certified accuracy). (R1)
- Ablation studies and GradCAM experiments help understand the relative advantages of the proposed approach. (R1)
- The paper explores both white-box and black-box extensions, making the method applicable to different scenarios. (R1, R3)
- Experiments are conducted on large vision models (ViT) and show effectiveness on both CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. (R3)
- The paper is well-written and structured; the method is intuitive and easy to follow. (R4)
- Comprehensive experiments are conducted to demonstrate effectiveness. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns (multiple reviewers):** The training objective (Equation 4) is not novel; combining two well-known methods (smoothing and PEFT) does not meet the novelty threshold for ICLR. The white-box and black-box tuning methods are existing work; the paper just applies PEFT to a large model and tests RS performance. There is a very large literature on certified robustness not compared against (see https://sokcertifiedrobustness.github.io/). (R3, R4)
- **Significance concern (R4):** The primary challenge in RS is the extensive inference time required for certification (majority vote over 10,000 samples). Reducing training time via PEFT does not address this critical practical bottleneck, raising questions about real-world impact.
- **Unfair or incomplete experimental comparisons (R1, R4):**
  - In Figure 2, PEFT methods are compared against training-free denoising methods, which is an unfair comparison. (R4)
  - In Table 1, the base models differ: PEFTSmoothing uses ViT-L and ViT-B while DS methods appear to use ResNet architectures. This needs clarification. (R4)
  - On ImageNet, results in the appendix are quite a lot worse compared to the Diffusion-based denoiser. (R1) The authors attribute this to diffusion models being more powerful for high-resolution images; is there a smoother trade-off that can be established? A discussion is needed. (R1)
- **Imprecise writing and unclear claims (R2, R1):**
  - Lines 144–146 claim "Theoretically, Theorem 2.1 ... with Gaussian noised inputs" is a strong claim requiring citation or rigorous/empirical justification. (R2)
  - Lines 150–156 are unclear, e.g., "large-scale vision model ... acquire such potential" – what does "acquire such potential" refer to? (R2)
  - Lines 346–347 claim "this is mainly due ... especially for high resolution images" – needs citation or empirical justification. (R2)
  - The paper assumes significant background on PEFT; given most results are based on LoRA, a detailed explanation of the setup and training of LoRA is needed. Similarly, an overview of the four considered PEFT approaches would help. Figure 1 does not adequately explain the PEFT setting. (R1)
- **Lack of formal certification proof (R2):** It is unclear what the authors mean by "certified." Does PEFT guarantee the conditions of Theorem 2.1? A rigorous guarantee or formal proof is required to claim a method is certified.
- **Equation (6) is poorly written (R2):** The expression should be written in terms of indicator functions; the use of '&' is unclear (product?). The authors state "certifiedCheck returns 1 if Theorem 2.1 is satisfied," but Theorem 2.1 conditions are probabilistic – how are probabilities computed? Specifically, how is $\mathrm{Pr}[F(x+\varepsilon) = c_A]$ computed?
- **Missing baseline comparisons (R3):** Compare against and cite:
  - S. Srinivas et al., Which Models have Perceptually-Aligned Gradients? An Explanation via Off-Manifold Robustness, NeurIPS 2023
  - R. Shao et al., On the Adversarial Robustness of Vision Transformers, TMLR 2022
  - T. Tsiligkaridis et al., Diverse Gaussian Noise Consistency Regularization for Robustness and Uncertainty Calibration, IJCNN 2023
- **Limited dataset scope (R3):** Experiments are on simple datasets; a more thorough study involving domain-specific datasets (e.g., WILDS) is suggested.
- **Empirical vs. certified robustness comparison needed (R3):** How far is empirical robustness from certified robustness in this setting? A comparison is needed. Approaches for gradient masking mitigation have been studied; what are the tradeoffs and computational complexity?
- **Geometry of loss landscape (R3):** How does the geometry of the loss landscape depend upon the robustness properties of the large vision models studied? These are intimately related per the adversarial robustness literature.
- **LoRA outperforming full fine-tuning (R4):** Typically, parameter-efficient methods are close to but do not surpass full fine-tuning. Here LoRA-based tuning outperforms full fine-tuning. A more detailed analysis is needed.
- **Contrast in positivity (R1 vs. others):** Reviewer 1 scored 6 and highlighted the large CIFAR-10 gains and ablation studies as strong points, while three reviewers scored 3 and raised major novelty/significance/unfair comparison concerns. The positive view from R1 is a minority opinion; the majority find the contributions insufficient.
