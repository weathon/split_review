## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper tackles the important but often neglected problem of balancing similarity (relevance) and diversity in vector retrieval for LLMs. It introduces VRSD, a heuristic that uses the cosine similarity between the query vector and the sum vector of the retrieved set as a single optimization criterion, eliminating the need for the tuning parameter λ required by MMR. The authors prove that the underlying combinatorial problem (maximizing that cosine) is NP-complete and propose a greedy algorithm. Evaluations on three datasets compare VRSD against MMR, BM25, and Cosine Similarity, and also include a downstream generative QA (RAG) task. Reviewers agree the problem is important and the heuristic is simple, but raise fundamental concerns about the formulation of diversity, the evaluation methodology (especially the use of sum-vector similarity as both the optimization target and the evaluation metric), insufficient baselines, writing clarity, and missing comparisons with alternative approaches like DPP or α-NDCG. One reviewer (score 5) was significantly more positive, praising the clarity, theoretical discussion, and experimental methodology; the other three (score 3) had serious reservations about validity, evaluation rigor, and presentation.

### Strengths

- **Important and timely problem.** Balancing relevance and diversity is critical for retrieval in general and especially for LLM/RAG applications; prior work like MMR relies on heuristic tuning (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- **Simple and intuitive heuristic.** VRSD offers a clear optimization objective (maximizing sum-vector cosine with the query) that jointly captures similarity and diversity without a tunable λ (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- **Outperforms MMR in experiments.** On three public datasets, VRSD (greedy version) achieves higher sum-vector similarity than MMR with λ∈{0,0.5,1} (Reviewers 1,3).
- **Includes downstream evaluation.** The paper tests VRSD in a RAG-style generative QA task, showing its practical value (Reviewer 3).
- **Revisits a neglected topic.** Few recent works address diversity in retrieval; this paper brings fresh attention (Reviewer 3).
- **Readable paper.** The paper is well-structured and clear (Reviewers 1,2). Reviewer 1 additionally notes good quality in both theoretical discussion and experimental methodology, while Reviewer 2 calls it easy to read.
- **Potential for simpler proxy metrics.** Developing a metric based on sum-vector similarity could simplify practical implementations compared to complex alternatives like DPP (O(N³)) or MMR’s parameter sensitivity (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Fundamental flaw in diversity formulation (Reviewers 2,3,4).** The core claim that maximizing the dot product between the sum vector and the query (∑ d_i · q) enforces diversity is questionable. Since this dot product equals ∑ (d_i · q), it is trivially maximized by making each individual d_i as similar to q as possible – the opposite of diversity. The paper does not provide a proof or intuitive justification for why this objective would promote divergence in alignment. (Reviewer 2: “It’s no wonder that the paper comes out ahead as the measurement of diversity is based on what the paper chooses to optimize.”).
- **Lack of monotonicity in the metric (Reviewer 4).** The sum-vector cosine is not monotonic: adding a new vector can sometimes lower the overall cosine, which contradicts the intuitive expectation that retrieving more high-quality items should not reduce retrieval quality. This is not addressed.
- **Evaluation is circular and lacks ground-truth diversity validation (Reviewers 2,3,4).** Intrinsic evaluation (Section 4.1) treats the sum-vector cosine as the ground-truth measure of retrieval quality. Using that same metric to compare VRSD with MMR is expected to favor VRSD, since MMR optimizes a different objective. No manual evaluation, user study, or established diversity benchmark (e.g., TREC Web track diversity task, Min et al. 2021 “Joint Passage Ranking for Diverse Multi-Answer Retrieval”, Vikraman et al. 2021 “Passage similarity and diversification in non-factoid question answering”) was conducted to verify that VRSD indeed retrieves more diverse sets. Reviewer 2 notes that dismissing human evaluation as “time consuming” is inappropriate. Reviewer 3 also states that comparing cosines alone is “a wrong one” for measuring diversity.
- **Insufficient baselines and hyperparameter tuning.** Only one diversity-aware baseline (MMR) is compared, and MMR’s λ is limited to {0, 0.5, 1} without grid search over 0.1–0.9 (Reviewers 1,4). Alternatives like Determinantal Point Process (DPP), α-NDCG, or other diversity metrics are not mentioned or compared (Reviewer 4). The later experiments (Sections 4.2.2, 4.3) drop MMR entirely and only contrast with BM25 and Cosine Similarity, which do not capture diversity – essentially showing “method that models diversity beats methods that do not” (Reviewer 4).
- **Weak NP-completeness justification (Reviewer 3).** The reduction to the integer k-sum problem, which has a pseudo-polynomial DP solution, does not convincingly prove that the problem has no efficient exact solutions. A stronger argument (e.g., showing why multi-dimensionality prevents DP) is needed.
- **Only one dense retrieval model used in downstream task (Reviewer 3).** The generative QA evaluation relies on a single embedding model; results may not generalize.
- **Writing and presentation issues (Reviewers 1,3,4).** The paper is “VERY hard to read” (Reviewer 3) and omits important details. Examples: The explanation of MMR’s limitations (Section 2.1, Eq. 1) is unclear and the illustrative example does not clarify why λ causes problems (Reviewer 3). The experimental description (L347-349) is confusing, mentioning concatenation of question and answer without explanation (Reviewer 3). The RAG setup is not named as such and described awkwardly (“reconstructed prompts”, missing citation) (Reviewer 3). Typos: “datasets” → “dataset” (L347) (Reviewer 4). Table 3 font too small (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing characterization of search space (Reviewer 1).** The paper does not analyze the distribution of vectors in the datasets to show that they truly represent a challenging search space where the NP-complete problem manifests. Understanding which scenario characteristics benefit most from VRSD is missing.
- **Statistical significance not reported (Reviewer 1).** Some of the improvements in Table 2 are not statistically significant given the confidence intervals.
- **Limited theoretical grounding for sum-vector metric (Reviewer 1,2,4).** Alternative formulations (e.g., minimizing pairwise diversity d_i·d_j while maximizing relevance d_i·q) are not discussed (Reviewer 2). The claim that the sum vector captures both similarity and diversity simultaneously is presented as self-evident (Reviewer 2).
