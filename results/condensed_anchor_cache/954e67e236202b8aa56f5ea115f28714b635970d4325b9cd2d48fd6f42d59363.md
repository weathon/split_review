## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a bilevel optimization framework (Bilevel ZOFO) that combines parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) with zeroth-order (ZO) optimization to fine-tune large language models efficiently. It uses ZO for full-model updates at the upper level and first-order (FO) gradients for PEFT at the lower level. The method is extended to multitask meta-training. The authors provide convergence guarantees. Experiments show Bilevel ZOFO outperforms standalone ZO (MeZO) and PEFT in single-task settings and is competitive with first-order meta-training methods in multitask settings with lower computational demand.

### Strengths
1. **Novel combination of PEFT and ZO**: The bilevel integration is novel and interesting, bridging the gap between parameter-efficient tuning and full-model zeroth-order finetuning.
2. **Strong single-task performance**: Empirical results consistently outperform both standalone ZO (MeZO) and PEFT approaches, as well as full first-order finetuning in some settings.
3. **Multitask extension**: The method is effectively extended to multitask meta-training, allowing simultaneous handling of multiple tasks with reduced computational demand compared to first-order meta-training methods.
4. **Theoretical convergence guarantees**: The paper provides convergence analysis for the bilevel optimization procedure.
5. **Resource efficiency for resource-limited domains**: The multitask capability is highlighted as beneficial in domains like medical AI where large labeled datasets are scarce.

### Weaknesses
1. **Scalability concerns**: Experiments are limited to small-to-medium models (up to 7B parameters). It is unclear if results hold for larger models like OPT-13B (commonly used in ZO literature). Hyperparameters may vary significantly with model size, and MeZO hyperparameters may not be optimal for these small-scale settings (Reviewer 1, confidence 3; Reviewer 2 does not mention; Reviewer 3 no comment; Reviewer 4 confidence 5 – mixed but explicit request by Reviewer 1).
2. **No efficiency comparisons reported**: The paper lacks quantitative evaluations of wall-clock time per step, memory consumption, and convergence speed. All four reviewers independently request such measurements. Without these, it is unclear if the combination actually improves efficiency over MeZO or PEFT, especially since the method still requires backpropagation through the PEFT module, which may store a full computational graph (Reviewer 4). A potential contradiction: Reviewer 3 notes bilevel optimization is known for high computation cost even after ZO approximation.
3. **Motivation clarity and ablation**: The claim that bilevel optimization mitigates sensitivity to hard prompts is not backed by evidence. It is possible that performance gains simply come from having more tunable parameters (PEFT) rather than the interaction. No ablation compares sequential PEFT followed by ZO (i.e., train PEFT first, then ZO full-model) versus the bilevel approach (Reviewer 2).
4. **Counter-intuitive result in Table 3**: Stage 2 multitask performance is significantly lower than Stage 1 single-task results. The authors should perform basic hyperparameter tuning or explain this drop (Reviewer 2).
5. **Technical novelty of the optimization transformation**: The derivation from Eq.3 to Eq.7 follows existing works (e.g., DARTS, HOAG). New technical contributions beyond this transformation are unclear. The reviewer (3) questions whether this transformation is demonstrably better than alternatives.
6. **Limited comparison baselines**: The only ZO baseline is MeZO. There are improved LLM tuning works over MeZO that are not compared or even mentioned (Reviewer 3). Additionally, the novelty is weakened by the existence of MeZO (Reviewer 2).
7. **Motivation for using ZO only partially**: Since PEFT still requires first-order gradients (backpropagation), the memory savings from ZO may be partially negated. Why not use ZO throughout the algorithm, including for the PEFT module gradient? (Reviewer 4). This also ties to the missing efficiency comparison.
8. **Soft prompt tuning not considered**: Despite ZO techniques being explicitly used to tune soft prompts [1], the paper does not explore this alternative (Reviewer 3).
9. **Notational clarity and minor errors**: Mathematical notations should follow convention (e.g., bold vectors). Typo at line 269: “Sso” should be “So” (Reviewer 4). A pipeline figure to illustrate the optimization procedure would improve presentation (Reviewer 3).
10. **Dataset sharing**: In the bilevel setup, data is split into two parts (upper and lower). Can the same tuning dataset be shared? This is not addressed (Reviewer 3).
11. **Minimax saddle point concerns**: Minimax optimization often faces convergence issues due to saddle points, which could cause training to stall (Reviewer 1). The significance of the theoretical convergence analysis for real LLM tuning guidance is questioned (Reviewer 3).

*Note: Reviewer disagreement exists on novelty: Reviewers 1 and 4 consider the combination effective and the contributions solid, while Reviewers 2 and 3 question the novelty relative to MeZO and prior bilevel work.*
