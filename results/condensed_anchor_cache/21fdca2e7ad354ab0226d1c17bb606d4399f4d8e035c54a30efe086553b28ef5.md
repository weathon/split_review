- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 6, 5, 8, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CellPainTR, a transformer architecture with Hyena operators, learned positional encoding, and a source context token for batch correction and representation learning on CellProfiler features from Cell Painting images. It is evaluated on the multi-site JUMP-CP dataset using a multi-stage training process (masked token prediction, then intra- and inter-source supervised contrastive learning). Reviewers are divided: one (score 8) finds the work novel, thorough, and compelling for addressing a critical challenge; three (scores 5,5,6) note marginal improvements over baselines, insufficient novelty, limited evaluation scope, and poor clarity of metrics and methods. All agree that batch correction in image-based profiling is important and that the dataset is well chosen.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important and widely recognized challenge: batch effect correction in large-scale Cell Painting data, which has practical implications for drug discovery and cellular biology. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- Leverages the JUMP-CP dataset, a challenging multi-site benchmark with strong batch effects, making the evaluation realistic. (Reviewer 2)
- Uses a transformer architecture with Hyena operators, which to the best of the authors’ knowledge (and one reviewer’s note) have not been applied to batch correction for CellProfiler features before. (Reviewer 2)
- Includes extensive quantitative evaluation with multiple strong baseline methods (Combat, Harmony, Sphering) and a broad set of metrics (batch separation, biological relevance, mAP). (Reviewer 2)
- Code is publicly released and the dataset is open, supporting reproducibility. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Terminology and biological interpretation are undefined.**  
  “Biological variation,” “biological metrics,” and “biological relevance” are not explained. Reviewers ask what exactly constitutes biological information (e.g., cell cycle, drug mechanism, or just non-batch variation) and how it is measured. (Reviewers 1,4)
- **Metric descriptions are insufficient, especially in the main text.**  
  - mAP: What is the task and dataset? “no rep” is unclear. (Reviewers 1,2)  
  - “Batch Correction metric” (1 – f1) lacks definition of how batch detectability is measured (e.g., KNN vs. classifier, and why kBET is not used). (Reviewer 2)  
  - “Bio Info score” and “Aggregate Scores” are not defined. (Reviewer 2)  
  - Silhouette batch score: direction (higher/lower better) is ambiguous; the table highlights the highest value, but for batch correction low separation should be better. (Reviewer 4)  
  - How controls are used in metrics (with/without controls) is not explained. (Reviewer 2)  
  - Statistical significance of the small overall improvement (0.63→0.64) is not provided. (Reviewer 2)
- **Novelty is contested and prior work is omitted.**  
  - The source context token for batch correction was already published (arxiv.org/pdf/2305.19402, ICML SCIS 2023). (Reviewer 2)  
  - Related work discussion is missing; references [1] and [2] (not provided in manuscript) should be discussed, and expected citations (e.g., for combat, harmony, transformer, Hyena) are misplaced or absent. (Reviewer 4)  
  - No ablation study isolates the contributions of Hyena operators, learned positional encoding, and source context token. (Reviewer 2)
- **Evaluation is limited to one feature extractor (CellProfiler) and one dataset.**
  - Only engineered features are used; no end-to-end image-based training is performed. Reviewers ask why this is not viable and how the method compares with more modern segmentation tools (e.g., CellPose) or alternative feature sets [3]. (Reviewers 1,4)  
  - No evaluation on unseen batches (new source, new imaging modality, different cell types or staining protocols) to test generalizability. (Reviewer 3)
- **Qualitative results (Figure 4) are unconvincing and poorly labeled.**  
  - The UMAP colored by compound does not show clear separation; one reviewer argues it could be interpreted the opposite way. (Reviewers 2,4)  
  - No legend for colors; coloring by MOA (subset of compounds) would be more informative. (Reviewer 2)  
  - Figure 3 color scheme is not explained. (Reviewer 2)
- **Method description is incomplete.**  
  - Key components (linear adapter, feature context embedding, source context token) lack equations and details. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Batch effect correction section (4.2) is convoluted; it does not clarify which specific batch effects are corrected (e.g., evaporation, liquid handling, reagents, imaging errors) or how “biological” is separated from those artifacts. (Reviewer 1)  
  - Masking ratio choice ([0.05,0.4]) is not justified, especially given highly correlated CellProfiler features. (Reviewer 2)  
  - The value of λ in Eq. 7 is always 1, making it redundant unless ablated. (Reviewer 4)  
  - “Nyffler et al. 2020” does not exist (presumably a typo for Nyffeler?). (Reviewer 4)
- **Computational cost is prohibitive without clear benefit.**  
  - Pretraining took two weeks for three epochs; total training is roughly five weeks. (Reviewer 4)  
  - No ablation shows whether pretraining (masked token prediction) is necessary; intermediate models (CellPainTR(1) and (2)) may already achieve comparable performance, making the final training stage questionable. (Reviewer 4)
- **Presentation and formatting issues.**  
  - Figure 2 lacks visualization of the Hyena operator and its mathematical difference from standard attention. (Reviewer 3)  
  - Typos: “bidrectional” (should be bidirectional), missing space in section 3.2, “vari- ation,” comma instead of decimal point in Table 1 (Sphering Sil. batch). (Reviewers 2,3)  
  - Font sizes and color schemes in figures need improvement. (Reviewer 3)  
  - Experimental sections should be split into setup vs. results. (Reviewer 3)  
  - “Cell Painting features” should be “CellProfiler features” to avoid confusion between the imaging technique and the tool. (Reviewer 4)
