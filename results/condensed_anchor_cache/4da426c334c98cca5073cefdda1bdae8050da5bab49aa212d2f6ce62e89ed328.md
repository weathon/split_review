## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviewers generally recognize SynCamMaster as a novel approach to multi‑camera video generation, producing visually impressive and reasonably consistent multi‑view outputs through a plug‑in synchronization module and a hybrid training scheme that compensates for scarce multi‑view video data. Several reviewers note the clear motivation and the potential of the method for applications beyond 4D reconstruction, such as virtual filming. However, there is significant disagreement: one reviewer (R6, score 3, confidence 5) is highly critical of the camera conditioning and data representation, while the remaining reviewers (scores 6‑8) consider the approach effective but raise concerns about the evaluation rigor, novelty, and practical limitations. Common criticisms include the lack of comparison with existing motion‑control methods (e.g., MotionCtrl, Sora), insufficient evaluation of 4D reconstruction and multi‑view consistency, unclear details about the camera encoder and model formulation, and questions about the generalization of game‑engine data to real‑world scenes.

### Strengths
1. **Novel task and promising results** – Multi‑camera synchronized video generation from arbitrary viewpoints is a forward‑looking and under‑explored task. The generated videos show impressive visual quality, reasonable temporal consistency per view, and spatial consistency across diverse camera perspectives, including wide baselines.
2. **Simple yet effective design** – The multi‑view synchronization module is a straightforward plug‑and‑play addition to a pre‑trained text‑to‑video model, and the overall architecture is clearly motivated.
3. **Innovative hybrid training scheme** – The progressive training pipeline that combines multi‑view images, synthetic multi‑view videos (from Unreal Engine), and real‑world monocular videos creatively addresses the scarcity of real multi‑camera video data. This approach is considered clever and effective.
4. **High‑quality visualizations** – Visual results presented in the paper and on the project website are compelling, demonstrating the method’s ability to maintain appearance and geometry across views.
5. **Good presentation** – The paper is well‑written, easy to follow, and provides a clear breakdown of each training stage and its rationale.
6. **Dataset contribution** – The release of the SynCamVideo‑Dataset (rendered multi‑camera videos) is a valuable resource for the community.
7. **Broad application potential** – The method opens up applications beyond 4D reconstruction, such as virtual filming, by enabling dynamic consistency across multiple viewpoints.

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient comparison with related methods** – The paper does not compare with MotionCtrl (a unified motion controller for video generation) nor adequately address the claim that Sora already possesses multi‑camera generation capabilities, which undermines the claim of being the first to pioneer this task. [R1, R4]
2. **Limited evaluation of 4D reconstruction** – Although 4D video reconstruction is highlighted as a key application, no experiments (e.g., lifting to 4D Gaussian splatting) or quantitative metrics are provided to assess how well the generated videos support actual 4D modeling. [R2, R7]
3. **Insufficient evaluation of multi‑view consistency** – The evaluation lacks a quantitative measure of cross‑view consistency; it relies heavily on visual inspection. Suggested improvements include cycle‑consistency checks (render from generated viewpoints and re‑render back to original) or metrics such as PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS on public datasets (e.g., Plenoptic, D‑NeRF). [R2, R5, R7]
4. **Unclear evaluation data and metrics** – The evaluation data are not explained (e.g., dataset composition, prompt selection). For video quality, the use of 100 manually collected text prompts is criticized; VBench is suggested as a more standard alternative. For camera‑control accuracy (TransErr), the estimated poses should be normalized to account for scale mismatch (as done in CameraCtrl), especially since training datasets (DL3DV‑10K vs. Re10K) differ, which may inflate the error. [R2, R5]
5. **Missing details on camera encoder and conditioning** – The encoding of 12‑dimensional extrinsic parameters into the spatial feature dimension is not described. Several reviewers consider direct matrix encoding too coarse and suggest transforming poses into explicit representations such as ray positional encoding. The determination of rotation center and radius for generating rotating viewpoints is also not explained. [R1, R5, R6]
6. **Model formulation and input‑output pipeline lack clarity** – The figure does not clearly indicate the input, which step produces features, or how the loss is calculated. Notation issues (e.g., confusion between φ_t and ψ_t, meaning of ψ_t⁻¹ and ψ_t′) make the paper harder to follow. [R3, R5]
7. **Only one novel view generated per run** – The proposed approach generates a single video at a novel viewpoint each time; view consistency may degrade when generating multiple novel viewpoints sequentially due to accumulated inconsistency. [R3]
8. **Generalization concerns of synthetic training data** – The model is fine‑tuned on Unreal Engine‑rendered videos, raising the risk that it learns features specific to game engine materials and textures rather than true multi‑view dynamics. Real‑world multi‑view videos are not used for training, and no analysis is provided to verify that outputs do not simply resemble rendered scenes. [R6, R7]
9. **Low camera‑control precision** – Table 4 shows that the precision of camera control is low (e.g., rotation angle in Fig. 6(b) appears noticeably less than the specified 90°). COLMAP‑based evaluation of the spatial relationships among generated views is suggested for quantitative verification. [R5, R6]
10. **Ablation study clarity** – In Fig. 6(a) (joint training strategy ablation), it is difficult to judge improvements without ground truth and due to small images. The differences are not as clear as in Fig. 6(b). [R5]
11. **Trivial cross‑attention module** – The multi‑view synchronization module is essentially a cross‑attention layer that lacks explicit 3D information modeling, which limits its ability to enforce geometric consistency. [R6]
12. **Missing analysis on out‑of‑distribution poses** – The model is only trained with translation ranges of 3.5–9 m; no experiments test its robustness to larger translations that are common in real‑world applications. [R7]
13. **Data mixture probabilities are not justified** – The training data mixture probabilities (0.6, 0.2, 0.2) are given without rationale or ablation to show their impact on performance. [R7]
14. **Minor presentation issues** – “M.V. Images” in Tables 1 and 4 is unexplained. Citations for each method should be added in tables. Table 1 entries for I2V and SVD‑XT should be unified if they refer to the same method. The “polar bear” case mentioned in the text is not found in the figures. [R1, R5]
15. **Request for code and dataset release** – Reviewers ask for the release of code and the synthetic dataset to enable reproducibility and benchmarking. [R3, R7]
