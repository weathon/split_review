## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper explores cross-modality prompt transfer, specifically transferring prompts pretrained on text (NLP) to improve few-shot image recognition tasks in vision (CV). The authors verify feasibility by comparing frozen prompt transfer, projection transfer, and a proposed attention transfer method. They decompose the cross-modal gap into modality and task gaps and propose a transferability estimation metric combining both. Extensive experiments on VTAB-1K with 13 source language tasks and 19 target vision tasks show that attention transfer can improve upon existing prompt-based benchmarks, though results are mixed. The paper is generally well-organized and presents a novel perspective, but several reviewers question the effectiveness, experimental controls, and practical advantage over single-modality methods.

### Strengths
- Good motivation and novel idea: cross-modality prompt transfer (language→vision) for data-scarce tasks [R1,R2,R3,R4].
- Well-organized and clearly written; easy to follow for the most part [R1,R2,R3,R4].
- Thorough experiments with many ablations, design choices, and analysis [R1,R2,R3].
- Introduces a systematic decomposition of modality and task gaps for transferability estimation, offering valuable insights [R2,R3].
- Attention transfer is an effective way to reuse and adapt prompts across modalities [R2].
- Results are promising, showing that cross-modal prompt transfer can achieve performance comparable to or surpassing recent prompt-based benchmarks on VTAB-1K [R2,R4].

### Weaknesses
1. **Effectiveness unclear / advantage not convincingly demonstrated**  
   - The claim of boosting data-scarce tasks is not convincingly shown, especially compared to the SPT single-modality baseline [R1].  
   - Prompt transfer superiority over single-modality methods is not established; additional same-modality baselines are missing to quantify cross-modal impact [R2].  
   - Accuracy on the Natural subset of VTAB-1K decreases, making it hard to assess overall effectiveness; more datasets and failure-case analysis needed [R4].  
   - The improvement from the proposed transferability metric (GM&GT) over the baseline is only 3 points in mean Kendall’s coefficient; it is not shown how this translates to actual transfer performance on VTAB-1K [R4].

2. **Experimental controls and confounds**  
   - Projection transfer and attention transfer introduce extra parameters; the effect of source domain knowledge vs. additional parameters is not decomposed (Fig.3). Suggested controls: (a) use random source prompts to match learnable parameter count, (b) gradually increase source training samples to verify improvement trend [R4].  
   - Generalization limited: only one language model used; experiments needed with different LMs and different visual prompt tuning methods (shallow vs. deep) [R4].  
   - No discussion of cases where cross-modal transfer underperforms or fails; approach limitations need further analysis [R2].

3. **Transferability metric overhead and justification**  
   - The proposed metric requires training a universal projector across tasks, adding significant training cost and overhead [R1].  
   - It is unclear why one would want to quantify transferability (Eq.6) given it is a function of hyperparameters, training data, etc.; intuition missing [R3].  
   - The metric’s practical utility is questionable; recommended thresholds for source prompt selection are not provided [R1].

4. **Lack of intuition and missing discussion**  
   - The hypothesis *why* cross-modal prompt transfer should work is not discussed early in the paper; this is a key contribution but relegated to the appendix [R3].  
   - No discussion on what makes prompts transferable, dataset composition where transfer succeeds/fails, or what is being learned in the source prompts [R3].  
   - The relationship to prior work *APrompt* (which also uses attention in prompt tuning) is not clarified [R4].

5. **Presentation and methodological concerns**  
   - The approach appears sensitive to hyperparameters, which could affect reproducibility and performance stability across diverse target environments [R2].  
   - Figures (e.g., visualizing relative performance) are dense and lack clear conclusions in captions; “relative performance” needs definition [R3].  
   - Equation (8) could increase prompt size >100; this scalability issue is not addressed [R3].  
   - Statistical significance of improvements is not reported in the main paper (standard deviations are in appendix); a significance discussion is needed in the main text [R3].  
   - Some passages (L106-107) are unclear from context [R3].  
   - The method may face scalability issues when applied to larger models or complex attention setups, limiting real-world applicability [R2].  
   - Training cost of the transferability metric and its scaling with tasks/modalities is not quantified [R1].

6. **Minority more positive reviewer**  
   - Reviewer 4 (score 8) is more positive than the other three (score 6), but still raises substantial weaknesses about experimental decomposition, missing baselines, and decreasing accuracy on the Natural subset. This contrast is retained.
