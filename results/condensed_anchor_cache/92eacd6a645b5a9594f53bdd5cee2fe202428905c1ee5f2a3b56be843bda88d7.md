## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents MatFormer, a nested Transformer architecture that applies matryoshka representation learning to Feed Forward Network (FFN) blocks. During training, each FFN block is jointly optimized with a few nested smaller FFN blocks, enabling extraction of hundreds of smaller submodels after a single training run without additional optimization. MatFormer is evaluated on decoder-only language models (MatLM) up to 2.6B parameters, encoder-only vision models (MatViT), and demonstrated for speculative decoding. The Mix’n’Match procedure allows combining different granularities across layers to meet deployment constraints.

### Strengths
- **Novel concept:** First work to introduce a nested Transformer architecture for LLMs, allowing extraction of multiple accurate submodels from a single training process without additional training. (Reviewer 1)
- **High flexibility:** Enables exponentially many submodels based on deployment constraints, rather than only a few fixed sizes. (Reviewer 2)
- **Broad experimental scope:** Evaluated across modalities (language and vision), model classes (decoder and encoder), and scales (up to 2.6B parameters). Explores design choices such as reweighting different granularities (Table 6). (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Speculative decoding potential (minority stronger positive view):** One reviewer highlighted the application to speculative decoding as the most compelling part, with clear wins from shared parameters, shared attention cache, and consistency between base and full model. (Reviewer 4, score 8)
- **Presentation:** Paper is well-written and easy to follow. (Reviewers 1, 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Weak baselines:** Lacks comparisons with efficient LLM inference techniques that accelerate inference without fine-tuning (e.g., Dejavu leveraging contextual sparsity [1]), hardware-aware NAS methods that train supernets for elastic Transformers (e.g., [2,3]), model pruning, and distillation. Current baselines are only vanilla Transformers trained from scratch. Multiple reviewers considered this a significant shortcoming. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Training cost and efficiency misrepresented:** The claim of “zero additional cost” is inaccurate because training requires ‘g-1’ extra forward passes per step. The paper does not provide a clear breakdown of computational costs (FLOPs, time per step) compared to training a single model of equivalent size, nor does it specify the hardware used for training experiments. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Selection of mix’n’match models unexplained:** The method for selecting models on the Pareto frontier from hundreds of candidates is not described. Only 9 submodels are evaluated (Figures 2, 4) despite claiming hundreds. A search algorithm is lacking; the naive Mix’n’Match approach does not solve the combinatorial selection problem. The search space is limited to FFN granularity only, which is simpler than typical NAS spaces. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Focus on FFN only:** The nested structure is applied solely to FFN blocks, leaving the attention mechanism unaddressed. Extension to attention heads is mentioned but not illustrated (Figure 1 shows only FFN nesting) and not evaluated, limiting comprehensive Transformer improvement. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Insufficient comparison with prior work:** The paper does not adequately distinguish MatFormer from NetAug [1] (different training objectives and auxiliary network structure), Kusupati et al. 2022 (nesting of parameters vs output representations), and mixture-of-experts (routing mechanisms vs nested sub-networks). A more detailed comparison is needed to clarify novelty. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Effect of granularity parameter g not explored:** The paper fixes g=4 for all experiments but does not analyze the impact of varying g on flexibility, training efficiency, submodel consistency, and performance trade-offs. (Reviewer 2)
- **Speculative decoding explanation lacking:** Section 4.1.1 does not explain how consistency between models translates to latency reductions, leaving the mechanism unclear for readers unfamiliar with prior work. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Questionable claim about FFN latency:** The statement that FFN layers are responsible for the largest latency chunk is questionable without nuanced analysis; Kim et al. (Figures 7, 8) show the breakdown varies significantly with model size, sequence length, and hardware (CPU serving). (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing ablations and efficiency analysis:** No training time or memory comparison to training a single model, nor an ablation of how a standard Transformer performs with Mix’n’Match without the nested training objective. Hardware used for training is not specified. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Limited scale:** Experiments only verify models up to 2.6B parameters; effectiveness on larger models (e.g., 7B+) remains unverified. (Reviewer 2, noted as minor concern)
- **Terminology confusion:** The paper uses MatLM, MatFormer, and MatViT interchangeably, which may cause confusion. (Reviewer 3)
