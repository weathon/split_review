## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Bayesian approach for offline-to-online RL, using posterior sampling to avoid both excessive pessimism and optimism. Theoretical analysis provides a regret bound for linear MDPs. The practical algorithm trains an ensemble of policies with bootstrap masks during offline phase, then samples a policy from the ensemble at each online step to act and update. Experiments on D4RL locomotion tasks show competitive performance against baselines.

### Strengths
- Explicit identification of the finetuning dilemma (sudden performance drop vs. inability to improve) is commendable and provides strong motivation.
- The Bayesian formulation (posterior sampling throughout both phases) is novel and streamlined, avoiding separate pessimism/optimism mechanisms.
- Theoretical analysis (regret bound in linear MDPs) is mostly clear and easy-to-understand; proof-of-concept experiment (Figure 2) improves credibility of the theory.
- The proposed method (BOORL) outperforms previous baselines in D4RL locomotion experiments, and the paper includes a thorough ablation study and extensive comparison with relevant baselines.
- Writing is clear and easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- **Ensemble fairness (minority reviewer):** The algorithm uses an ensemble with bootstrap masks, which is known to improve performance. The paper does not include an ablation comparing against an ensemble version of baseline methods like TD3 or TD3+BC, making the comparison potentially unfair.
- **Algorithm clarity (multiple reviewers):** The mask m sampled from a distribution M is a key component. It is unclear what format M takes, how it is initialized and updated, and how this differs from standard ensemble methods. In the online phase, sampling uniformly from the ensemble (as commonly done) seems to contradict the Bayesian prior interpretation. The theoretical algorithm used to derive the regret bound (Theorem 3.2) is not explicitly stated; the paper should describe how the posterior is updated and how the policy is derived in both offline and online phases. Similarly, implementation details for posterior updates and mutual information computation are missing.
- **Experimental scope (multiple reviewers):** Experiments are limited to less challenging D4RL locomotion tasks (hopper, walker2d, halfcheetah). Results on the more demanding Antmaze tasks are missing, and two baselines (ODT, Off2On) show zero scores there, which is unexplained. The paper is also missing comparisons with additional SOTA offline-to-online baselines: Reincarnating RL (Agarwal et al., NeurIPS’22) and InAC (Xiao et al., ICLR’23).
- **Baseline performance deviation:** The reported PEX results deviate significantly from original published results and the reviewer’s personal experience, suggesting a potential bug or suboptimal parameter tuning that casts doubt on the fairness of comparisons.
- **Theory presentation gaps (multiple reviewers):** 
  - The information ratio Γ_t lacks a formal definition.
  - Figure 2 experimental setting is unclear: what offline algorithm was used for the UCB and LCB variants? A fair comparison for Thompson Sampling would be LCB offline + UCB online. The bandit setting (e.g., arm probabilities) should be stated for reproducibility.
  - Proposition 3.3 connects one-step suboptimality to performance drop, but it is not clear that a single suboptimal step leads to significant overall drop given that UCB has cumulative regret guarantees; the connection needs further justification.
  - Figure 2 shows Thompson Sampling performing better than the theoretical bound; the paper should discuss reasons (e.g., prior choice, approximation errors, theoretical assumptions) for this gap.
  - Comparison with Song et al. (2022) needs more precision: the paper claims a best-of-both-worlds result, but the exact conditions should be stated and contrasted with Xie et al. (2021) and Song et al. The current coverage notion may not be the tightest in linear MDPs; discuss whether tighter coverage (as in [1]) applies.
  - There are minor issues: "information pain" -> "information gain" (line 4 of Section 3.1); indexing in equation 3 appears off; in equation 4 it is unclear whether linear features are h-dependent—clarify the assumption and implications.
- **Regret analysis scope:** The regret bound is for linear MDPs; provide discussion or proof sketch for nonlinear cases.
- **Figure 2 legend:** Overlaps with curves; place at top of both images.
