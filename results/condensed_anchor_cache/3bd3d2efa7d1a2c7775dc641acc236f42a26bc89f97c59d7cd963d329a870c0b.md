- Decision: Accept

## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper theoretically proves that benign overfitting and grokking both occur in two-layer ReLU networks trained by gradient descent on XOR cluster data with a constant fraction of flipped labels. After the first GD step the network achieves 100% training accuracy but near-random test accuracy (harmful overfitting); later it transitions to near-optimal test accuracy while still perfectly fitting noisy labels (grokking and benign overfitting). This is the first theoretical result on benign overfitting for neural-network classification when the data distribution is not linearly separable.

### Strengths

- **First theoretical demonstration of benign overfitting beyond linearly separable data.** The paper proves benign overfitting in a neural network setting where the target function (XOR) is nonlinear, a long‑open challenge. The XOR cluster data is a natural starting point for this analysis. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2)
- **Provides a novel theoretical understanding of grokking.** The analysis reveals that after one large (relative to initialization) gradient step the network behaves as a linear classifier and perfectly overfits to noisy labels; later steps learn non‑linear features, leading to a transition to near‑optimal test accuracy. This is one of the first rigorous theoretical demonstrations of grokking. (Reviewer 1)
- **First work to study the combination of benign overfitting and grokking in neural networks.** The paper unifies both phenomena in a single framework, offering insights into how overfitting and generalization interact. (Reviewer 3)
- **Well‑written and clearly organized.** The proof sketch is well developed and conveys the key theoretical contributions effectively. The paper is logical and easy to follow. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2)
- **Important contribution to understanding generalization.** By explaining both benign overfitting and grokking together, the work advances understanding of how neural networks handle noisy data. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses

- **Counterintuitive assumption on sample size (A1).** In Assumption A1, a larger number of training samples appears on the right‑hand side of an inequality, seemingly implying that more samples hinder performance—contradicting the usual benefit of larger training sets. This requires further clarification or justification of why this specific bound is needed and how it relates to the model or data. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of justification for small initialization scale.** The analysis crucially requires initialization scale to be small compared to the step size to obtain perfect overfitting after the first step. No justification is given for why such small initialization is reasonable, and practical applications often use larger initializations. An empirical investigation into the effect of varying the initialization scale on model behavior and whether grokking still occurs would be highly beneficial. (Reviewer 1)
- **Very high‑dimensional setting limits practical relevance.** Combining Assumptions A1 and A2 yields \(p \ge C^4 n^{5.02}\), an extremely high‑dimensional condition far beyond typical practice. The paper does not adequately discuss this limitation or explore whether similar results can be obtained under less restrictive conditions. (Reviewer 3; this reviewer’s main concern makes them more negative than the others.)
- **Large signal‑to‑noise ratio (large \(\|\mu\|\)) compared to prior work.** Prior works [1–5] consider rotationally invariant inputs with unit norm; here the signal strength \(\|\mu\|\) is large, raising questions about practical applicability. An analysis or experiment exploring how the behavior changes as \(\|\mu\|\) decreases, and whether grokking persists under lower signal‑to‑noise ratios, is needed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Unclear mechanism of overfitting after learning directions \(u_1,u_2\).** The paper states that positive neurons learn \(u_1\) and negative neurons learn \(u_2\), but it is not explained how this leads to perfect overfitting on noisy (flipped) labels. A more detailed discussion of how the network distinguishes correctly labeled from incorrectly labeled data after initial feature learning would clarify this. (Reviewer 2)
- **Obscure role of Lemma 4.6.** The purpose and necessity of Lemma 4.6, and how it connects to the main results (e.g., the approximation in Equation 4.3), are not clearly explained. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing comparison with prior works [1,2].** The paper lacks a comparative discussion of its results and techniques with those of [1] and [2]. (Reviewer 2)
- **Unaddressed architectural variations.** The second layer is fixed; it would be valuable to know how results change if the second layer is also learned, or if the network width is varied. In particular, if the width becomes extremely large, would the findings align with the “lazy training” regime? (Reviewer 2)
- **Open question on test error dynamics between steps 1 and \(n^{0.01}\).** The analysis focuses on the first step and at a later step; the behavior of test error during the intermediate period (between 1 and \(n^{0.01}\) training steps) is not examined. (Reviewer 3)
