## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a video summarization framework that combines multiple VideoLLMs in a Mixture-of-Experts paradigm at inference time, without fine-tuning. It generates textual summaries, selects keyframes via CLIP similarity, and creates visual summaries using text-to-image models. The method is evaluated on several datasets, showing quantitative improvements and ablation studies, and demonstrates downstream applications. However, all reviewers found the technical novelty very weak; most considered it a straightforward ensemble of existing models with little new insight. The experimental comparisons are limited (only against subsets of the method), technical details are missing, and the reported Table 3 result (94.17) appears abnormally high. One reviewer (score 1) found no technical contribution and the paper insufficient for a research venue, while others (scores 3) acknowledged some practical value but with substantial concerns about novelty, depth, and evaluation.

### Strengths
- Inference-time, fine-tuning-free framework: unlike methods requiring extensive fine-tuning, this approach operates at inference time, saving computational resources (R1, R4).  
- Flexibility and upgradability: the system can easily incorporate new VideoLLMs as they become available (R1, R4).  
- Quantitative performance improvements: the method outperforms its own component models (each VideoLLM without MoE) on text summary generation and keyframe prediction (R3).  
- Interesting downstream applications: demonstrated summary video generation via keyframe selection or using text-to-image models (R1, R2). Some applications are useful and may benefit application-oriented tasks (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Weak technical novelty** (agreed by all, strongly by R3)  
   - The filtering strategy is essentially an ensemble that uses similarity matching to reach consensus – a common approach (R1).  
   - Keyframe retrieval using CLIP similarity between image and text is well explored (cites CLIP4Clip [1], Portillo-Quintero [2]) (R1).  
   - The high-level idea of summarizing video with VLM/LLM was explored in Argaw et al. [3], which is not cited (R1).  
   - Visual step generation was explored in GenHowTo [4], also not cited (R1).  
   - “The idea of using multiple LLMs to generate summaries and integrate them looks naïve; one can expect performance gains without interesting insights” (R2).  
   - “Technical novelty is very weak… this work is just a combination of existing fundamental models” (R3).  
   - “No novel technical contributions… results benefit mainly from pre-trained models” (R4).  
   - The claim of “coherent and unbiased summary” is unsupported; experiments do not evaluate these aspects (R2).

2. **Insufficient experimental comparison and evaluation**  
   - Only compared against the subset of the proposed method (each VideoLLM alone); it is apparent that the mixture will outperform a single model (R3).  
   - Table 3 reports a result of 94.17, nearly identical to ground truth, which seems abnormal and needs explanation (R1).  
   - Missing qualitative discussion: only numerical comparisons are given; the paper does not show and analyze generated summaries or failure cases (R3).  
   - Missing evaluation of generated image frames – “How were the generated image frames evaluated?” (R1).  
   - “I would like to see more specific descriptions about why these results are worth sharing with the community” (R2).

3. **Missing technical details and reproducibility**  
   - Key components like “filter outliers” and “cooperate” have no equations or pseudo code; the method is nearly impossible to reproduce (R3).  
   - The first filtering assumption (most LLMs output correct/similar summaries) and the second (visual-textual embedding comparison) may work for simple videos but could fail for rich, diverse videos with multiple events (R4).  
   - The definition of “good summary” is highly dependent on the quality of the selected VideoLLMs (R4).

4. **Paper presentation issues**  
   - Needs proofreading: reference error at line 346 (R1, R3).  
   - Table 3 is out of bounds (R1).  
   - Presentation scores: 2, 2, 1, 3 (average low).

5. **Reliance on multiple VideoLLMs**  
   - The framework requires the availability and compatibility of multiple VideoLLMs, which may not be feasible or efficient in low-resource environments (R1).

6. **Unsubstantiated claims**  
   - The claim that the method produces a “coherent and unbiased summary” is not validated; the experiments do not measure coherency or biasedness (R2).
