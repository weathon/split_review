## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates whether Video Language Models (VLMs) can reliably evaluate other VLMs, and whether aggregating judgments from multiple VLMs (collective thought) improves reliability. Four judging methods are studied: LLM agent debate with reference answers, individual VLM review, collective thought via a strong meta-judge (GPT-4o), and a mixture of judges using weighted Cohen's Kappa. Experiments use the CVRR-ES dataset (2,400 QA pairs, 217 videos, 11 visual dimensions). Key finding: only GPT-4o is a reliable judge; combining it with less reliable models degrades performance, and the mixture-of-judges offers minimal improvement.

### Strengths
- **Novelty of approach**: Applies collective intelligence principles to VLM evaluation, challenging single-model assessments (Reviewer 1).
- **Critical analysis**: Highlights the drawbacks of combining reliable and unreliable judges, showing it can introduce noise and reduce reliability (Reviewer 1).
- **Well-written and easy to follow** (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Detailed experimental results** with performance breakdowns across visual dimensions (e.g., social context, emotional context, object count) in tables and radar charts (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Timely and useful topic** for the community, as many recent works use VLMs as judges (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Methodology lacks detail**: How model reliability was determined, how judgments were aggregated, and which metrics/statistics were used are not sufficiently explained (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear structure and presentation**: Different judging methods are not clearly separated and are mixed across sections; paper is poorly organized (Reviewer 3). Plots (radar charts) often show no real difference across 11 dimensions and lack visual clarity; vector graphics (PDF) should replace JPEG images (Reviewer 3). Typographical errors in VLM names (e.g., inconsistent capitalization and dashes) (Reviewer 4).
- **Single dataset limitation**: Conclusions are based only on CVRR-ES, a dataset that is not commonly used; strong claims (e.g., "collective thought does not help") may be dataset-specific and not generalizable. Testing on additional datasets is needed (Reviewer 3; also raised implicitly by Reviewer 2’s question about best benchmarks).
- **Insufficient contribution for the field**: The area needs evaluation benchmarks or systems, not just another method. The proposed approach does not provide enough new insight to significantly advance VLM evaluation (Reviewer 2). Minor improvements from mixture-of-judges are not compelling (Reviewer 4).
- **Main conclusion not fully convincing**: The claim that VLMs (except GPT-4o) are unreliable judges lacks thorough explanation. The use of “hallucination” as the primary reason is insufficiently justified; alternative explanations (e.g., misunderstanding of content leading to high scores) are not explored (Reviewer 4). More insights or potential solutions for improving VLM judges are needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Choice of metric and implementation unsubstantiated**: Using weighted Cohen’s Kappa for selecting reliable judges and the specific collective-thought pipeline are not properly justified. It is unclear whether the meta-judge processes the original video or only the VLM reviews, and whether real-time selection of reliable judges is feasible without pre-computed scores (Reviewer 4). The possibility that the metric choice is suboptimal, causing minimal gains, is not addressed (Reviewer 4; also implied by Reviewer 1’s request for more statistical details).
