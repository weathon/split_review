## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies tabular average reward temporal difference (TD) learning for policy evaluation in finite state-action spaces with Markovian sampling and additive noise. It proves almost sure convergence to a sample-path-dependent fixed point, relying on a new stochastic approximation result for nonexpansive mappings with Markovian and additive noise, built on stochastic Krasnoselskii-Mann iterations. Reviewers are divided: one finds the result a long-awaited confirmation (score 6), while the other three rate the paper low (1,3,3) due to limited scope, strong assumptions, and questionable novelty.

### Strengths
- Addresses a long-standing open problem: almost sure convergence of tabular average reward TD, which had not been established before. (Reviewer 2)
- Provides a general stochastic approximation result for nonexpansive mappings with Markovian and additive noise, which may be of independent interest. (Reviewer 2)
- The paper is well-written, with clear comparisons to related work. (Reviewer 4)
- The theoretical interest in examining almost sure convergence without value function projection is noted. (Reviewer 1)
- The convergence result, though under restricted assumptions, may provide hints for proving convergence under milder conditions. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited scope and motivation:** The tabular setting is much simpler than the paper suggests. Prior work with linear function approximation (Tsitsiklis & Van Roy 1999) covers most applications of interest, and Zhang et al. (2021) already provides finite-time L2 bounds with a projection step. The paper’s motivation for focusing on asymptotic tabular analysis is not compelling. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3)
- **Additive noise assumption (Assumption 4.5) is strong and not mild:** The assumption that noise is additive is critical for bounding terms like \(\bar{\bar{\epsilon}}^{(1)}_n\). In RL contexts, this assumption is not commonly used and would trivialize many open problems. (Reviewer 4)
- **Convergence to an unbounded set:** Theorem 2 shows convergence to \(\mathcal{V}_{*}\), which is an unbounded set, similar to the “bounded invariant set” limitation the authors criticize in prior work. The connection between the sample path and the resulting fixed point is not clarified. (Reviewer 2)
- **The problem is solvable with simpler tools:** The ODEs (5) and (6) admit explicit solutions using matrix exponentials; stability follows directly from the fact that \(D(P-I_d)\) has kernel \(\{\text{constant vectors}\}\) and is Hurwitz on its complement. The paper’s complex analysis obscures this simplicity. (Reviewer 3)
- **Tabular updates can be represented in linear form:** By choosing \(\Phi\) of size \(|S|-1\) with entries \(1-1/|S|\) and \(-1/|S|\), one obtains a linear algorithm (up to an additive constant) that allows standard ODE analysis for both \(L^2\) and almost sure convergence, yielding a precise description of the path-dependent limit. The authors fail to recognize this. (Reviewer 3)
- **Incorrect use of terminology:** Equation (3) is not an Euler discretization of (5), nor is the equation on line 188 an Euler discretization of (9). Key definitions are missing, e.g., “sample path-dependent convergence” is not formally defined. The vector \(e\) in equation (1) is not defined. (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4)
- **ODE method not cited explicitly; only asymptotic results are provided.** The paper relies on the ODE method without citing it, and only gives asymptotic convergence when the trend is toward non-asymptotic bounds. This limits the contribution’s relevance. (Reviewer 3)
- **Novelty of the SA result is questionable:** Stochastic approximation with Markovian noise is well-studied (e.g., via Poisson equation), and combining it with nonexpansive mappings is not clearly original or significant since both topics have been extensively analyzed. The authors’ claim about “new terms to bound” is not sufficiently supported. (Reviewer 4)
- **Unaddressed questions:** (a) Is the value function in Line 65 different from that in Sutton (2018)? (b) In equation (5), why not cancel \(v(t)\) with the last term of \(h(v(t))\)? (c) What specific references support the claim that prior analysis “failed to move beyond convergence to a bounded invariant set” (Line 149-151)? (d) Can any choice of feature matrix (other than the one in the paper) imply convergence in the tabular setup, contradicting the authors’ claim that the result of Tsitsiklis & Van Roy (1999) cannot be applied? (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4)
