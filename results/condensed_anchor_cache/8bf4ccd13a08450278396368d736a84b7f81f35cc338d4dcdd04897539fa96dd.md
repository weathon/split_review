## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ReForm-Eval, a benchmark that re-formulates 61 existing task-oriented datasets into multiple-choice and specialized text generation formats to evaluate LVLMs. It provides extensive evaluation and analysis of LVLMs. Reviewers note the large scale and practical approach, but have concerns about training data overlap, solidity of insights, novelty, and methodological details.

### Strengths
1. **Large scale and comprehensive evaluation**: ReForm-Eval includes 61 datasets covering visual perception through high-level reasoning and dialog, with over 500,000 evaluation instances—approximately 100× the size of MMBench.
2. **Practical approach**: Reforming existing VQA datasets into multiple-choice format partially alleviates the exact matching problem (prediction vs. reference) that arises when evaluating generative VLMs on free-form text.
3. **Some insightful conclusions**: e.g., FlanT5-based models perform well on multiple-choice tasks, aligning with findings in the NLP domain. (Reviewer 2 considers this a strength; Reviewers 1 and 4 question the novelty/solidity of insights—see Weaknesses.)
4. **Extensive evaluation**: The benchmark provides results for multiple LVLMs using both black-box (generation) and white-box (likelihood) methods, and studies various factors: model backbone, connection module, pre-training data, instruction-tuning data, in-context examples, and instability during evaluation.

### Weaknesses
1. **Unfair comparison due to training data overlap**: ReForm-Eval includes many datasets that are widely used during the training of evaluated LVLMs (e.g., VQA, VQAv2, GQA, OK-VQA, TextVQA, OCR-VQA, Text caps, Flickr30K). This makes it unfair to compare models that were trained on these datasets with those that have not. The benchmark evaluates supervised learning ability rather than zero-shot learning, undermining claims of comprehensive evaluation.
2. **Insights not solid or original**:
   * When analyzing factors like connection modules, visual backbones, and training data, the study does not control for confounds (e.g., language model architecture, training data composition—e.g., LLaMA vs. FlanT5, different vision encoders). Comparisons are therefore unreliable.
   * The grouping of "high-quality data" vs. "without high-quality data" is not rigorous; e.g., Lynx uses data filtered on rules or CLIP, making its classification ambiguous.
   * The conclusion that "more instructional data leads to better performance" is not statistically significant due to high variance in Fig. 4(c).
   * Several findings are not original: the effect of in-context examples and instability of LVLMs were already discussed in MMBench; the multiple-choice formulation and generation/likelihood evaluation were previously used in MMBench and VisualGPTScore.
3. **Negative option generation**: The method for generating distractors (negative options) for visually grounded reasoning and multi-turn dialogue may be unreliable. Using ChatGPT to generate negatives from question and answer alone may not produce reasonable options. There is no ablation study comparing different distractor strategies (random, top-confidence, semantically related, LLM-based hard negatives) or the effect of the number of options on evaluation results.
4. **Metric bias**: Using CIDEr to evaluate visual descriptions penalizes models that produce verbose outputs (e.g., LLaVA) and benefits models tuned on concise caption datasets (e.g., COCO-caption). This introduces a systematic bias.
5. **Missing fine-grained recognition**: Fine-grained recognition, a substantial component of fine-grained perception, is not included in the fine-grained perception tasks.
6. **Methodological gaps**:
   * It is unclear whether the reformulation of datasets is done manually or automatically.
   * No human validation or other validation is provided to demonstrate superiority over existing benchmarks.
   * No guidance on whether to use generation metric, likelihood metric, or both.
   * The sampling strategy from 500k+ instances is not described (uniform vs. probability-based across benchmarks); the effect of dataset size on evaluation results is not studied; it is unclear if a smaller subset could be used.
   * Typo: "MSOCO" on Page 8, Line 1.
   * Fig. 10 shows unequal proportions of 'A' and 'B' in the ground-truth option distribution, raising questions about whether some questions have only one option.
7. **Focus on evaluation rather than reformulation**: The paper spends many pages on evaluation results and insights rather than delving deeper into the reformulation methodology (e.g., how to generate distractors, handling of fine-grained perception). This is a missed opportunity given that conversion is the core contribution.
