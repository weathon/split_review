## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes DynMo, a dynamic load balancing system for distributed training of dynamic LLMs (Mixture of Experts, parameter pruning, layer freezing, dynamic sparse attention, early exit, Mixture of Depths). DynMo addresses workload imbalance among pipeline-parallel workers by redistributing compute workloads and dynamically packing work into fewer workers to release idle GPUs. The authors provide a proof that DynMo achieves maximum reduction in imbalance. DynMo is built on top of Megatron/DeepSpeed and is open-sourced. Evaluations on a large-scale cluster (hundreds of H100 GPUs) show end-to-end speedups up to 1.23× (MoEs), 3.18× (pruning), 2.23× (layer freezing), 4.02× (sparse attention), 4.52× (early exit), and 1.17× (MoDs) compared to static distributed training solutions.

### Strengths
- **Open source and practical implementation**: The proposed method is open-sourced and built on Megatron, with clear instructions and easy-to-follow code. It covers critical practical challenges, such as handling the fact that each GPU can only have one NCCL communicator when reusing idle workers.
- **Novel problem formulation**: The authors identify and formulate the workload imbalance problem in dynamic LLMs, which is relatively unexplored. The formulation and proposed solution are original contributions.
- **Theoretical foundation**: Mathematical proofs that DynMo satisfies maximum reduction in imbalance provide a strong theoretical basis.
- **Broad compatibility**: DynMo is compatible with a variety of dynamic compute and model reduction schemes (MoE, pruning, freezing, sparse attention, early exit, MoDs), suggesting potential wide adoption.
- **Comprehensive evaluation on production-scale hardware**: Extensive experiments are conducted on a large GPU cluster with hundreds of H100 GPUs, demonstrating scalability and effectiveness. The evaluation covers six distinct dynamic model scenarios.
- **Measurable efficiency gains**: The evaluation shows DynMo greatly reduces bubble ratios and improves end-to-end training throughput without harming model accuracy. Production-level cluster usage adds credibility.

### Weaknesses
- **Resource isolation and safety concerns**: It remains unclear how resources are isolated when NCCL communicators are shared with third-party jobs. There is no discussion of how to ensure the main job’s safety if a third-party job intentionally uses the communicator for other purposes.
- **Imbalance detection overhead**: The detection overhead is proportional to profiling frequency and is highly empirical. The claim of “single-digit overhead” is up to a specific job setting and not generally justified.
- **Repacking details missing**: When repacking jobs into fewer workers, it is unclear what is serialized (model states? optimizer states?), whether all workers stop simultaneously to repack, and how NCCL timeouts are handled if some ranks finish faster than others.
- **Straggler handling**: The method for measuring imbalance and handling straggler ranks is not addressed.
- **Scope and automation limitations**: DynMo is only applicable to dynamic LLMs. It cannot automatically detect imbalance; the user must manually configure the imbalance detection frequency before training begins. (One reviewer suggests extending to an automated framework or integrating into existing mainstream frameworks.)
- **Limited technical innovation**: The technical contribution is somewhat restricted, as it relies on applying established load-balancing techniques to a new context rather than introducing entirely new methodologies.
- **Unclear algorithm description**: The second algorithm (iterative decentralized diffusion-based) is not clearly explained. An intuitive example or visual explanation of how the diffusion works is needed. The main body lacks intuition for why the algorithms achieve load balancing; key descriptions are relegated to the appendix.
- **Omission of dynamic baselines**: Megatron and DeepSpeed can support naive dynamic configurations manually. The paper should compare against these as stronger baselines. Additionally, for MoE training, a comparison with MoE-tailored systems like Tutel is expected. One reviewer recommends changing the story to a cluster-level scheduler to better motivate resource reduction.
- **Writing and motivation clarity**: The paper lacks a formal definition of load imbalance and a fundamental analysis of why the six dynamic model examples cause workload imbalance in pipeline parallelism (e.g., how conditional computation or adaptive layer skipping translates to uneven compute loads). More theoretical modeling or variance analysis of execution times is needed.
- **Algorithm justification**: The two algorithms in Section 3.3 are not convincingly shown to achieve optimal load balancing. There is insufficient justification for why they should converge to an optimal solution, especially under varying dynamic execution costs.
- **Re-packing overhead analysis missing**: Re-packing dynamic models to fewer workers requires checkpoint resharding, graph recomputation, and training restart—overhead that can be minutes for LLMs. The paper claims this overhead is negligible without detailed analysis of how these costs are minimized, how the re-packing frequency is determined, and the trade-off between reduced worker count and overhead. An implementation section detailing these system-level challenges is needed for fidelity.
