## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Cybench, a framework for specifying and evaluating LLM agents on 40 professional-level Capture the Flag (CTF) tasks from 4 recent competitions. Each task includes a description, starter files, an automated evaluator, and subtasks for fine-grained evaluation. The authors evaluate 8 frontier models (GPT-4o, o1-preview, Claude 3 Opus, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Mixtral 8x22b, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Llama 3 70B, Llama 3.1 405B) using a single agent scaffold with bash access, structured reasoning, and memory of the last 3 messages. Top models (Claude 3.5 Sonnet, GPT-4o, o1-preview, Claude 3 Opus) solve tasks taking humans up to 11 minutes; the hardest task took humans 24h54m. Only 8 tasks are solved by any model. First-solve-time (FST) is used as an objective difficulty proxy. Safety refusals are rare.

### Strengths
- **Originality of first-solve-time as a difficulty proxy** is new and a significant methodological contribution (R1).  
- **Benchmark difficulty and relevance:** strongest models achieve only 17.5% success with solve times 6–52 minutes, making it a non-saturated evaluation of frontier agent capabilities (R2, R3). The topic of dual-use cybersecurity capabilities is highly timely and important (R1, R3).  
- **Careful task curation:** tasks are recent (minimal training data leakage), span 6 categories, cover wide difficulty range, and are sourced from multiple competitions (R1, R2). Human solve time provides an additional evaluation axis (R3).  
- **Extensibility:** open framework for community contributions; contributions already received (R1).  
- **Correctness guarantees:** inclusion of solution scripts and automated probes ensures tasks are solvable and evaluations are reliable (R1).  
- **Subtasks enable granular evaluation:** partial credit and detailed capability assessment (R1, R3).  
- **Deterministic and easy evaluation** (R3).  
- **Clear presentation:** good explanation of CTF concepts for ML audience (R3), good contrast with prior CTF datasets (R2).  
- **Model evaluation breadth:** results across 8 models align with other capability benchmarks (R3).  
- **Low refusal rate** indicates minimal need for prompt engineering (R1, R2).  

### Weaknesses
- **Limited statistical power:** only 8 tasks are solved by any model, making empirical statements about LLM cybercapabilities thin (R1).  
- **Future test data leakage:** full dataset is released; once model training cutoffs pass the benchmark release date, the benchmark becomes unusable. Withholding a private holdout set is suggested (R1). The same concern is echoed by R2.  
- **Agent scaffolding quality not fully validated:**  
  - No ablation studies demonstrating the impact of scaffolding components (reflection, planning, thought) on performance (R1).  
  - Missing comparison to stronger paradigms like full CLI access (scrolling, history search) as used in concurrent work (R1).  
  - No assessment of whether task-customized scaffolding would significantly improve results (R1, R2).  
  - Web search / tool use not included; in real-world threat scenarios this would be standard (R1).  
  - Memory limited to last 3 messages – a critical limitation for complex multi-step tasks requiring long-term dependencies (R3).  
  - Agent implementation may be a limiting factor; results may underestimate current capabilities (R2, R3).  
- **No formal task selection algorithm:** principled inclusion criteria are missing, raising cherry-picking concerns (R1).  
- **Limited failure analysis:** no investigation into what makes tasks unsolvable (inherent difficulty vs. scaffolding inadequacy), no qualitative analysis of common failure patterns or reasoning vs. execution bottlenecks (R1, R3).  
- **Limited real-world threat scenario discussion** and no discussion of biases or limitations from data sources (R1).  
- **Metrics lack nuance:** no combined metric weighting success by difficulty (e.g., FST-weighted accuracy), no efficiency metrics (number of messages, submission attempts) (R3).  
- **Missing quality controls for community-contributed tasks** (R1).  
- **Unclear observation parsing influence:** whether parsing observations for flags affects scoring is not explained (R1).  
- **FST stability across competitions/contexts** is not examined (R1).  
- **Minor:** Section 4 first paragraph is unclear (R3). Model handles in main text could be moved to appendix (R3). Consider minor problem modifications to further reduce train-test overlap (R3). Consider a third evaluation level (environment without specific flag description) (R3).  

*Note:* Reviewer 3 rated the paper 10 (more positive than Reviewers 1 and 2 at 8), emphasizing the benchmark’s value for measuring hazardous capabilities and its non-saturated nature, but still raised the above weaknesses regarding agent memory, metrics, and qualitative analysis.
