## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a new task: segmenting thin-slice (high-resolution in z-axis) CT scans using only annotations from thick slices, addressing the scarcity of thin-slice annotations. It releases the CQ500-Thin dataset, a benchmark of 15 expertly labeled thin-slice Non-Contrast CT volumes with Intracranial Hemorrhage (ICH). The method, Thin-Thick Adapter (TTA), combines a data alignment technique (DA) that resamples thick slices to thin resolution (with adjustable depth spacing) and 3D Cross Pseudo Supervision (3D-CPS) for unsupervised domain adaptation on unlabeled thin slices. Experiments on CQ500-Thin and ROTEM-Thin show improvements over baselines (SegViT, nnUNet variants) in Dice and IoU.

### Strengths
- **New problem formulation:** The task of segmenting thin slices using only thick annotations is novel, clinically relevant, and underexplored (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Reviewer 4 notes the paper is clearly written with good illustrative figures.
- **New benchmark dataset:** The CQ500-Thin dataset with 15 pixel-level expert annotations on thin slices is released, enabling fair evaluation and future research (Reviewers 1, 2). This reduces annotation burden by allowing clinicians to focus on thicker volumes (Reviewer 1).
- **Strong empirical results:** TTA outperforms several baselines–2D SegViT, nnUNet trained on thick, nnUNet trained on thin, and fine-tuned nnUNet–across two datasets on mean Dice Similarity Coefficient (mDSC) and mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) (Reviewers 1, 2). Ablations justify both the data alignment (DA) and 3D-CPS components (Reviewer 3).
- **Clear presentation:** The paper and its figures (e.g., illustrating the TTA pipeline) are well designed (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Terminology and clinical realism contested:** Reviewer 4 strongly criticizes the introduced "thick / thin" dichotomy, arguing it does not correspond to actual medical acquisition practice. In reality, high z-resolution (thin) is always desired but limited by acquisition constraints; the notion of generating thick from thin is considered invalid by this reviewer. This challenges the paper’s real-world motivation.
- **Limited technical novelty:** The core technique (3D-CPS) is a direct application of existing Cross Pseudo Supervision to the thick-thin setting, with resampling and a straightforward 3D extension. Reviewer 3 notes the lack of novelty (“relative lack of technical novelty”), and Reviewer 4 calls the contributions “marginal.”
- **Dataset size and generalizability concerns:** CQ500-Thin contains only 15 annotations, which may not represent the diversity of ICH pathologies (size, location, etiology). This raises concerns about generalizability to broader clinical contexts (Reviewer 2). Evaluation is confined to CT datasets (CQ500-Thin, ROTEM-Thin); no tests on other modalities or additional public CT datasets are provided (Reviewer 2).
- **Potential loss of fine-grained details:** Training guided by thick annotations may miss subtle boundaries and small lesions that are visible in thin slices (Reviewer 2). The method does not explicitly address how to recover such details.
- **Computational cost:** The TTA module, especially the unsupervised domain adaptation phase, may be resource-heavy, posing a barrier for settings with limited computational resources (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing ablations and justifications:**
  - No ablation varying the weight λ for the cross-supervision loss to show robustness (Reviewer 1).
  - The gradual linear increase of λ is mentioned but not justified in detail (Reviewer 3).
  - No discussion comparing CPS versus alternative semi-supervised approaches like EMA (Reviewer 1).
  - The generation of thin slices from thick (Section 5.1) uses duplication and depth spacing adjustment without interpolating adjacent slices, which seems unnatural at boundaries (Reviewer 3). The paper does not consider interpolation or additional processing.
- **Unaddressed evaluation reliability:** No inter-grader reliability metric is reported for the manually labeled thin slices, which is especially important for fine-grained data (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing experiments on annotation burden reduction:** Reviewer 1 suggests a controlled experiment (e.g., treat existing thin data as “thick”, artificially coarsen it to produce a “thick” dataset, then evaluate the savings in annotation effort) to substantiate the claim of reduced burden. This experiment is not done.
- **Presentation issues:** Reviewer 4 notes that Figure 3 uses acronyms not introduced in the text. Reviewer 1 points out that Eqn (3) defines a term \(L^l_{cps}\) that is not used later, and that Eqs (2) and (4) do not specify which losses (Dice, Cross-Entropy) correspond to \(l_{sup}\) and \(l_{cps}\).
- **Scope of the paper:** Reviewer 1 suggests the work may be more suitable for a “datasets and benchmarks” track rather than a methods paper.
- **Prevalence of thick vs. thin acquisitions:** Reviewer 1 asks for a discussion on which type is more common in clinical practice, as the significance of the contribution depends on whether thick or thin annotations are more prevalent. This is not addressed.
