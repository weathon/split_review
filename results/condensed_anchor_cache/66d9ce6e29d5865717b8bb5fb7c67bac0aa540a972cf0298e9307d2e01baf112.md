## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper tackles the practically important setting of imitation learning from observations (IfO) with a heterogeneous dataset: expert demonstrations without action labels and a separate background (sub-optimal) dataset with actions. It proposes VfO, which learns a state-value function on the mixture dataset—assigning binary (1 expert / 0 background) or discriminator-based pseudo-rewards—and then uses Advantage Weighted Regression (AWR) to extract a policy. The paper introduces SIBench, a self-improvement benchmark that mimics iterative policy improvement with offline data. Experiments on MuJoCo and Robomimic tasks (state-based and image-based) show VfO often outperforms baselines like SMODICE and DILO, sometimes matching AWR with ground-truth reward, but struggles on bimodal data.

### Strengths

- **Important and relevant problem:** The work addresses the practical challenge of learning from mixed-quality, action-free demonstrations, which is a prerequisite for scaling imitation learning, especially in robotics. Several reviewers note the significance of this setting.
- **Novel methodology:** While building on SQIL and ORIL, adapting these value-based imitation learning methods to the action-less domain is a non-trivial modification (Reviewer 1). Using SQIL-style binary rewards for state-value learning is new (Reviewer 3). The algorithm is clean, straightforward, and easy to implement (Reviewers 5, 6).
- **Promising empirical results:** VfO is competitive with offline RL using ground-truth reward from just a few action-free trajectories (Reviewer 4). It outperforms more complex baselines like SMODICE and DILO on the SIBench data, sometimes closely matching AWR-oracle performance (Reviewer 6). Results are shown in both state-based and image-based setups (Reviewer 5).
- **Self-improvement ablations:** The iterative self-improvement experiments (Figures 6, 7) are interesting and show that VfO can improve even from a very underperforming starting policy (Reviewer 2). The SIBench benchmark itself is a useful offline proxy for this setting (Reviewer 6).
- **Honest presentation:** The paper clearly communicates that VfO performs less well on bimodal data (Reviewer 6). The analysis of the VfO-bin objective (maximizing expert state visitation) builds intuition (Reviewer 6).
- **Well written and structured:** The paper is generally easy to follow, with clear motivations and claims (Reviewers 2, 3). Figures are simple (Reviewer 3), though some labels are too small (Reviewer 6).

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient practical motivation and real-world validation:** The paper lacks concrete real-world scenarios where the specific data setting (expert demonstrations without actions, sub-optimal demonstrations with actions) is prevalent. Several reviewers ask for examples (e.g., UMI gripper, autonomous driving, Carla, D3IL benchmark). The absence of real-robot experiments limits the demonstration of practical effectiveness (Reviewer 1). The conclusion mentions “practical evaluation settings” without supporting evidence (Reviewer 1).
- **Novelty concerns:** A minority of reviewers find the contribution incremental, noting that existing work already labels background data with imitation learning rewards and then applies offline RL; VfO is a variation within that framework (Reviewer 5). The core idea of regularizing behavior cloning by penalizing deviations from the expert state distribution remains conceptually similar to SQIL (Reviewer 2). The paper’s contribution would be strengthened by a deeper analysis of challenges introduced by the absence of action data (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing theoretical grounding:** There is no rigorous discussion of why maximizing the discounted probability of being in an expert state is sufficient as an imitation learning objective. The paper does not provide bounds on policy improvement or conditions under which the method can exceed expert performance (Reviewer 3). The claim of matching AWR performance is unsubstantiated by equations or proofs (Reviewer 3).
- **Incomplete analysis of limitations and failure cases:** VfO fails on bimodal tasks, but the paper does not explain why or suggest modifications (Reviewer 4). The assumption that background actions should receive zero reward may discard valuable partial task executions (e.g., pouring water for a coffee-making task) (Reviewer 4). The method risks the agent staying stationary in a small region of expert states rather than learning the full behavior (Reviewer 6). It is unclear what happens when a large portion of the background data has very low returns (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of thorough ablations and sensitivity analyses:** Missing studies on: the ratio of expert to background data (Reviewer 4), the mixing parameter alpha (Reviewer 4), the behavioral cloning weight lambda (Reviewer 6), the AWR temperature (Reviewer 6), and the similarity/overlap between expert and background datasets (Reviewers 4, 5). Also missing: comparisons to SQIL with action labels (to isolate the effect of removing actions) (Reviewer 3), experiments with the value function trained only on expert data (Reviewer 3), and evaluations on more challenging/long-horizon benchmarks (Reviewer 5).
- **Experimental evaluation concerns:**
    - Reported results are from the training set (line 305); evaluation should be from simulated rollouts (Reviewer 1).
    - The plot style using return differences vs. background data is confusing and obscures absolute performance (Reviewer 2). Expert returns should be displayed on plots (Reviewer 2). Absolute performance plots are missing (Reviewer 6).
    - Confidence intervals are not clearly explained (Reviewer 6). The meaning of subplot confidence intervals in Figures 2-5 is unclear (Reviewer 6).
    - In the walker experiment, the large gap between discriminator and binary variants is unexplained (Reviewer 1).
    - The paper claims a novel offline evaluation recipe (SIBench) without clarifying how it differs fundamentally from existing methods (Reviewer 2).
    - In self-improvement experiments (second set), only one relevant baseline and an oracle are compared; more baselines are needed (Reviewer 1).
    - The box-tooth behavior in Figures 6 and 7 is hard to interpret (Reviewer 2). The iteration order is difficult to read (Reviewer 6).
- **Clarity and writing issues:** Several sentences are difficult to read due to approximative language (e.g., “decade of experience,” “struggle to achieve improvement”) (Reviewer 1). There are typos: “demonstration” in line 085, abstract last sentence broken, “VfO-dist” in line 341 (Reviewer 6). The related work section lacks justification for some citations and does not include a self-improvement literature discussion (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing comparisons and baselines:** The paper should compare with a wider variety of reward functions (e.g., Optimal Transport-based rewards) (Reviewer 5). The reasons why SMODICE and DILO underperform are not analyzed (Reviewer 3). Does simple BC improve on background data because it was not trained to convergence? (Reviewer 2)
- **Equations and figures clarity:** Equation 2 is unclear regarding access to the expert policy pi_E(a|s) (Reviewer 3). Figure labels are too small (Reviewer 6).
