## Merged Review

### Summary  
The paper proposes the Recurrent Action Transformer with Memory (RATE), which augments a Decision Transformer with a recurrent memory mechanism (Memory Retention Valve, MRV) to extend the effective context window for offline RL. The memory embedding is updated via multi-head attention after each trajectory segment, and a set of gating mechanisms (“valves”) control information retention. RATE is evaluated on memory-intensive environments (ViZDoom-Two-Colors, T-Maze, Memory Maze, Minigrid-Memory), Atari games, and MuJoCo control tasks.

### Strengths  
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1, R2, R4).  
- The proposed modification to the Decision Transformer yields performance improvements, especially in memory-intensive environments (R1, R3). Results are supported by extensive experiments across diverse environments (R2, R3, R4).  
- The MRV mechanism is considered original and interesting (R2); its application to Decision Transformers is novel (R4). The ablation study on MRV (RQ3) is particularly informative (R4).  
- The paper is well-organized, contains detailed descriptions, and provides a thorough discussion of related work (R3).

### Weaknesses  
- **Novelty and contribution (disagreement):** Two reviewers (R1, R3) view the contribution as incremental: using transformers for offline RL is not new, and there exist many works that extend context length through recurrent states. They argue the novelty is limited. Two other reviewers (R2, R4) find the MRV design original and the application to DT novel, considering it a valuable contribution.  
- **Experimental soundness concerns:**  
  - *T-Maze results are questionable.* Only DT and RATE achieve high scores on a task that requires memorizing a single bit over ~90 steps. Mamba, LSTM, and GRU reportedly score near 0.5 (random), despite known capabilities of memorizing over 16,000 steps. No code for these baselines is found in the “T-Maze_new” directory, raising reproducibility doubts (R1). Additionally, why DMamba under the same context window (K=90) obtains much lower performance than DT is unexplained (R2).  
  - *The environments used (T-Maze, VizDoom) are poor tests of memory*, requiring only one bit of information over short horizons (<100 steps) – insufficient to differentiate memory architectures (R1).  
  - *VizDoom-Two-Colors dataset is biased* towards one color, greatly affecting baseline DT performance. It is unclear whether RATE’s gains are due to the method or the configuration; collecting a balanced dataset would have been a simpler and more informative approach (R4).  
  - *Memory Maze data collection is arbitrary* (R4): the rule for changing the initial sampling timestep is not justified.  
  - *Atari and MuJoCo sampling horizons are much shorter* than the standard (which is often ≥200), raising questions about generalizability to classic benchmark setups (R4).  
  - *RATE is trained without the feed-forward network* (FFN) because it yielded better results (Appendix D), but this design choice is not applied to the baselines, making comparisons potentially unfair. No ablation explores whether omitting the FFN would also benefit other architectures (R4).  
  - It is unclear whether baselines’ hyperparameters were tuned carefully. RATE itself has many environment-specific hyperparameters, leading to a heavy tuning workload (R3).  
- **Missing baselines and comparisons:** The paper does not compare against simpler, non-sequence-modeling approaches (e.g., LSTM with CQL or filtered behavior cloning) that would justify the need for the sequence modeling framework in memory-intensive RL (R4). Also missing is a comparison with ERNIE-Doc, whose code differs minimally from TrXL (R2).  
- **Lack of theoretical and design justification:** There is no theoretical guidance for the RATE/MRV design; the choices appear empirically determined (R2). The motivation for the Memory Retention Valve is not well explained (R3). It is unclear why MRV significantly improves performance; examples or further explanation are needed (R3). The term “cached hidden states” is used repeatedly without a clear definition (R2).  
- **Missing ablations and analyses:** The paper lacks ablations on the capacity of the memory chunk, the length of the segment \(S_n\), the number of transformer layers, and the positional encoding used (especially within the MRV module) (R2). Appendix F discusses correlated ablations but is unclear (R2). Attention maps for causal and cross attention during evaluation are not shown (R2). How RATE handles backpropagation through time (gradient truncation) is not described (R4). The anomalous performance of TrXL-3 compared to other memory-augmented architectures in Figure 4 is not explained (R4).  
- **Data optimality:** It is unclear whether the data collection policies are optimal or near-optimal; there is no investigation of performance with suboptimal data (R4).  
- **Typo:** L148: “embeddingsx” should be “embeddings” (R4).
