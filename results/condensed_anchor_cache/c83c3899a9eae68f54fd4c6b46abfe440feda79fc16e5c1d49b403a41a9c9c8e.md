## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the online learning interpretation of Adam, building on the discounted-to-nonconvex conversion framework. It proposes clip-free FTRL, eliminating the clipping operation present in prior work, and provides last-iterate convergence guarantees for β-FTRL. However, the reviews raise concerns about correctness, novelty, and assumptions. One reviewer (R3, score 6) found the contributions significant, while another (R1, score 5) considered the topic interesting but raised major proof concerns; two reviewers (R2, R4, both score 3) were highly critical of correctness and novelty.

### Strengths
- The topic of understanding Adam through online learning is interesting and relevant to the machine learning community (R1, R2, R4).
- The paper provides a novel way to avoid the clipping/projection operation present in prior β-FTRL analyses by carefully configuring β, making the algorithm closer to the practical Adam implementation (R1, R3).
- A last-iterate convergence guarantee for β-FTRL is provided, with an O(1/√T) rate (R1, R3); one reviewer (R3) considers this a notable contribution.
- The approach is intuitive and the problem is well-motivated (R2).
- Proper credits are given to existing works (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Correctness of Lemma 4.1 and parameter a**: The selection of a seems impossible in general; if a gradient is zero, a must be ∞, which derails the discounted regime, and gradient skipping does not resolve small non-zero gradients (R2). The dependence of a on the algorithm itself introduces circularity, and it is unclear how to find a universal a (R1, R4). The hyperparameter settings are unclear overall (R1).
- **Inconsistency in β treatment**: β_2 and β are treated as constants in the proofs but are time-varying in Lemma 4.1; this discrepancy is not reconciled (R1).
- **Algebraic error in line 698**: The claimed equality \(1-\beta_2^{t-1} = 1/(a(t-1))\) is incorrect; the correct relation involves a \((t-1)\)-th root, not simple exponentiation, which undermines the subsequent analysis (R1).
- **Error in Theorem 4.2 bound**: The bound should have \(\sqrt{1-\beta_2}\) in the numerator instead of \(1-\beta_2\); the proof (lines 867-870) misapplies the definition of \(\tilde{\tilde{v}}\). This mismatch is also visible in the natural setting \(\beta_2=\beta^2\), where the regularizer is off by a factor \(\sqrt{1-\beta}\) (R2).
- **Last-iterate claim not genuine**: The result does not provide a true last-iterate guarantee; the output is still randomly sampled from the trajectory, only with more weight on later iterates (R2, R4).
- **Theorem 5.3 bound does not decay**: The gradient norm bound fails to go to zero as \(T\to\infty\) because the term \(\frac{(1-\beta)^2 T}{(1-\beta^T)}\beta D\text{Regret}\) does not decay when \(\beta_2 \ge 1 - O(1/T)\), preventing convergence to critical points (R2).
- **Insufficient novelty**: Existing works (e.g., Theorem B.2 of Ahn et al. 2024) already analyzed discounted FTRL without projection; the projected version was used only for convenience, making the first contribution incremental (R4).
- **Strong assumptions**: The bounded gradient and bounded domain assumptions (Assumptions 2.1, 2.2) are restrictive; it is unclear if weaker conditions (e.g., growth conditions) could be used (R3).
- **Lack of thorough comparison with prior work**: The paper does not clearly delineate its assumptions and convergence rates relative to established results for Adam in both smooth and non-smooth non-convex settings (R3).
- **Unclear connection to actual Adam**: The paper does not introduce the full Adam algorithm or explicitly derive how clip-free FTRL differs from standard Adam, limiting insights into Adam’s empirical success (R3).
- **Presentation issues**: The writing is hard to follow, especially Section 4 before 4.1 (R1, R4). Arrows are misused (should be \(\implies\) or reversed), and grammar errors (e.g., “it is equal to verify”) appear (R2). The parameter settings and significance for understanding Adam are unclear (R1, R4).
- **Open questions**: Dependence of Theorem 4.2 on \(\eta\)? Does \(v_t\) have an upper bound? (R1) Can the requirements for hyperparameter a be justified in practice? (R4)
