## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies online nonconvex optimization (ONO) with single oracle feedback per time step. It considers three oracle models: deterministic gradient (SGO), stochastic gradient (SSGO), and function value (SVO). The performance metric is local regret on the original (unsmoothed) loss functions (window size 1), in contrast to prior work that used window-smoothed losses. A key finding is that the problem-dependent quantity “function variation over time” \(V_T = \sum_{t=2}^T \sup_x |f_t(x) - f_{t-1}(x)|\) fundamentally determines regret. For SGO and SSGO, the paper derives lower bounds restricted to a family of linear-span algorithms and shows that online gradient descent and online stochastic gradient descent achieve matching upper bounds (and are thus optimal within that family). For SVO, a novel algorithm using a one-point running difference gradient estimator is proposed, achieving regret that matches the state-of-the-art previously requiring two oracle queries. The paper is well-written and the observation about \(V_T\) is considered important.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and significance**: The idea of using function variation \(V_T\) to obtain sublinear regret bounds for local regret with window size 1 is novel and interesting. All previous works focused on a smoothed version of the loss and believed the worst-case regret was linear. This paper is the first to show both sublinear lower and upper bounds, which may inspire future work. [Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4]
- **Thoroughness**: The paper provides a comprehensive analysis of three distinct oracle models (SGO, SSGO, SVO) and gives tight upper and lower bounds for each. For SGO and SSGO, lower bounds establish optimality of classic online gradient algorithms within the linear-span family. [Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3]
- **More accurate metric**: By directly measuring local regret on the original (unsmoothed) losses instead of window-smoothed approximations, the paper gives a more accurate regret measure. The algorithms also use only a single oracle query per iteration, whereas prior smoothing-based methods required multiple queries. [Reviewer 2]
- **Novel gradient estimator**: For SVO, the one-point running difference gradient estimator is a novel contribution. It matches the performance of a two-point estimator (which required two oracles per step) with a single oracle query per iteration. [Reviewer 2]
- **Adaptive bound**: The regret bounds in terms of \(V_T\) are always tighter than the previous vacuous bounds, and \(V_T\) is a natural adaptive measure of problem difficulty, similar to path length in dynamic regret. [Reviewer 2]
- **Presentation**: The paper is well-written, well-motivated, and the three cases are explained with clear proof sketches. The descriptions are easy to follow. [Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4]
- **Positive minority view**: Reviewer 2 (score 8) is significantly more positive than the other reviewers (all scores 6), particularly emphasizing the thoroughness, the novelty of the one-point estimator, and the importance of \(V_T\). [All]

### Weaknesses
- **Over-claiming optimality and restricted lower bounds**: The lower bounds (Theorems 1 and 3) hold only for a restricted family of linear-span algorithms (\(x_{t+1} = x_1 + \sum_{i=1}^t a_{t,i}g_i\)), which does not include popular algorithms like online mirror descent and FTRL. The paper sometimes states “optimal” without qualifying this restriction. Reviewer 4 considers this a serious issue and recommends revising to say “optimal inside the linear-span algorithm family”. [Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4]
- **No matching lower bound for SVO**: There is no lower bound for the SVO setting, so optimality in that case is not proven. [Reviewer 3]
- **Missing discussion of related work on dynamic regret**: The proof for the upper bound shares strong similarities with techniques used in dynamic regret literature that also achieves function variation bounds. The paper does not adequately discuss this connection. [Reviewer 1]
- **Unclear practical implications**: While the theoretical results are interesting, it is not immediately clear how the function variation bound translates into actionable insights for practitioners. The paper would benefit from a more thorough discussion on practical relevance. [Reviewer 1]
- **Confusing statement about global minimum**: The paper’s motivation says “accessing the global minimum of nonconvex functions is typically infeasible”, but regret is a performance metric that is not computed in practice; this justification is unclear. [Reviewer 1]
- **Need for more comparison across oracle models**: The paper lacks detailed discussion on when one would prefer SGO over SSGO over SVO, and the practical trade-offs between them. [Reviewer 3]
- **Questions about extensibility and limitations**: Several reviewers raised questions that highlight missing analyses:
  - Can the lower bound be extended beyond linear-span algorithms to a larger family (e.g., via techniques from Carmon et al. 2021, Arjevani et al. 2022)? If not, what is the main difficulty? [Reviewer 2]
  - Does the analysis assume a specific dimension \(d\) (e.g., \(d \ge \Omega(1+V_T)\)) to guarantee orthogonal gradients? [Reviewer 2]
  - Can the lower bound analysis be extended to sliding windows with fixed length \(w\)? Will \(V_T\) still appear? [Reviewer 3]
  - The one-point running difference estimator achieves constant variance in this nonconvex setting; does it also achieve constant variance in bandit convex optimization (BCO), implying \(O(\sqrt{T})\) regret? If not, what is the key difference? Could it improve Flaxman’s \(O(T^{3/4})\) regret? [Reviewer 4]
  - Does this work offer a strictly superior methodology compared to prior smoothed-function approaches? If not, what are the disadvantages of using original-function local regret? [Reviewer 4]
- **Minor typo**: In Lemma 2, the first two equalities contain an \(d\) outside \(\|\mathbb{E}[\cdot]\|\); this may be a typo. [Reviewer 2]
