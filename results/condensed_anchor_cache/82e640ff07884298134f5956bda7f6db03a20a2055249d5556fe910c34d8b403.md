## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes IMP-Loss and DIMP-Loss, two weighted-loss objectives to improve model training on LLM-generated synthetic text data by aligning synthetic distribution with real-world distribution. The weights combine a **quality checker** (conditional probability from a BERT-base model trained on a small real-world set of 200–400 samples) and a **diversity checker** (for IMP-Loss: inverse probability from a model trained on the synthetic data; for DIMP-Loss: current model’s predicted probability, motivated by online batch selection). Experients on three NLP tasks (Financial Phrasebank, Tweet Irony, MRPC) show that both methods outperform standard cross-entropy, focal loss, and a meta-learning weighting baseline, across scenarios using synthetic data alone, noisy synthetic data, and real data. IMP-Loss slightly outperforms DIMP-Loss as a trade-off for higher cost. The paper also includes experiments with different model sizes (Table 2) and varying percentages of training data (Figure 2). Reviewers generally agree the problem is timely, the derivations are clear, and the empirical improvements are consistent. However, one reviewer (score 6) is more critical, noting limited benchmark scope, missing convergence analysis, and lack of comparison with SOTA on those tasks; the other three (score 8 each) find the work solid but note concerns about novelty relative to prior weighting methods (e.g., SunGen) and scalability to larger LLMs.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and Motivation**: IMP-Loss and DIMP-Loss are novel contributions. The importance-sampling derivation and the use of both quality and diversity are clearly motivated and step-by-step derived.
- **Clear Theoretical Foundation**: Section 3.2 establishes the need for both quality and diversity. The derivations are rigorous and the implementation approximations are efficient.
- **Extensive Empirical Validation**: Consistent performance gains over CE-loss, focal loss, and meta-learning weighting across three NLP tasks (Financial Phrasebank, Tweet Irony, MRPC). Experiments cover:
  - Synthetic data alone, noisy synthetic data (label flips, duplicated entries), and real data.
  - Robustness checks with different model sizes (Table 2) and varying percentages of training data (Figure 2).
  - Ablation studies confirming the importance of both quality and diversity weights.
- **Practical Efficiency**: The method is computationally lighter than meta-learning approaches (e.g., Hu et al.), making it more practical for real-world use. A runtime complexity analysis is provided.
- **Clarity and Structure**: The paper is well-written; each derivation step is clearly explained. The problem formulation is precise, and the distinction between real and synthetic data sources is maintained (though improved notation is suggested).
- **Timeliness**: Addresses a critical problem in synthetic data augmentation—distribution mismatch—which is of high current interest given the proliferation of LLM-generated training data.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited Benchmark Suite**: Only three NLP tasks are tested (Financial Phrasebank, Tweet Irony, MRPC). The paper should include more challenging benchmarks from prior work, e.g., SQuAD, QNLI, AdversarialQA (as in ZEROGEN), where synthetic data performance is far below supervised. It would be interesting to see if the weighting method closes that gap.
- **Unclear Convergence**: Training uses only 5 epochs (Line 1101). It is not shown whether models have converged. The paper should analyze whether the weighting schemes only speed up early learning or also improve final converged performance.
- **Not SOTA on Used Benchmarks**: The paper compares against CE-loss and simple baselines, not SOTA results.
  - MRPC: TinyBERT achieves 86.4% (PapersWithCode), while the paper’s BERT-large results are lower.
  - Financial Phrasebank: DistilBERT achieves 96.7% (PapersWithCode), again higher than reported.
  - A stronger downstream baseline (not just BERT-large) would better demonstrate the method’s value.
- **Scalability to Larger LLMs**: Experiments are restricted to BERT-based models. How would the weighting method scale to large LMs (e.g., Llama, GPT)? Would the cost of forward passes for quality/diversity checking grow prohibitively?
- **Novelty Concerns**: The core idea of weighting synthetic data samples by quality has been explored before, e.g., SunGen (Gao et al., ICLR 2023) which also reweights synthetic data via meta-learning. While the formulation and implementation differ, the conceptual overlap limits novelty. Related work should more thoroughly cite and discuss SunGen, UniGen, FuseGen, Meng et al. (NeurIPS 2022, ICML 2023) etc.
- **Assumption on Small Real-World Data**: The method assumes that a model trained on a small real-world dataset (200–400 samples) can approximate a model trained on the full dataset. The paper does not explore the minimum number of real examples required for this assumption to hold, nor provides guidelines for practitioners.
- **Minority Critical View (Reviewer 1)**: This reviewer rated the paper 6 (others gave 8) and raised several issues: (a) Missing ablation on convergence; (b) Results are not SOTA; (c) Requires more diverse and difficult NLP benchmarks; (d) Questions about the theoretical interpretation of Q(x)~P(x) assumption when synthetic data is high-entropy, and confusion between Q(x,y) and Q(y|x). They also noted that Focal-loss and Hu et al. sometimes underperformed CE-loss, especially with large real data, and asked for explanation.
- **Unverified Diversity/Quality Metrics**: The paper does not empirically verify that the “diversity” and “quality” weights correspond to their intuitive meaning (e.g., do higher quality samples actually receive higher weights?). Reviewer 2 and 3 both pointed this out.
- **Potential Instability in Early Training**: On Tweet Irony (Figure 1), DIMP-Loss starts with worse performance than baselines in the first two epochs. Is this instability a concern?
- **Diversity Checker on Unrelated Data**: What happens if the diversity checker is fed data unrelated to the target domain? SunGen showed that noise includes both label errors and out-of-domain data—could the proposed checker fail in such cases?
- **Notation Clarity**: Some formulas do not clearly distinguish whether (xi, yi) come from real-world P or synthetic Q. For example, in Appendix C it is clear, but main body could be more consistent (e.g., using (x_p', y_p') for real data throughout). Reviewer 4 suggests starting a new paragraph for the contribution to avoid mixing with prior work discussion.
- **Formatting Issues (Reviewer 3)**: Inconsistent use of \citet vs \citep; missing capitalization/spacing (e.g., Line 030, Line 063, Footnote 1). These reduce presentation quality.
