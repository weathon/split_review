## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes the Adaptive Strategy Evolution (ASE) framework for black-box jailbreak attacks on safety-aligned LLMs. It decomposes jailbreak strategies into four modular components (Role, Content Support, Context, Communication Skills) and uses a genetic algorithm (GA) for optimization with a new fitness evaluation. Experimental results on multiple closed-source models show higher jailbreak success rates. Reviews range from 3 to 6, reflecting significant disagreement: some find the method simple and effective with thorough evaluation, while others criticize it as lacking scientific rigor, relying on heuristic designs, and being an engineering exercise.

### Strengths
- Novel adaptation of evolutionary algorithms to jailbreaking, breaking the pipeline into four distinct modular components (Role, Content Support, Context, Communication Skills), increasing flexibility and scope of the strategy space.
- Enhanced fitness evaluation system that overcomes drawbacks of traditional binary or overly complex scoring, providing a more fine-grained assessment.
- Positive experimental results: ASE achieves higher Jailbreak Success Rates (JSR) with fewer queries compared to existing methods, particularly against advanced models like GPT-4o, Claude-3.5, and o1.
- The method is described as simple and effective (reviewer 2), and the evaluation is considered thorough (reviewer 3).
- The paper is well-written and easy to understand according to one reviewer, though others disagree.

### Weaknesses
- **Component-level strategy space is arbitrary and lacks empirical justification.** The choice of Role, Content Support, Context, and Communication Skills is based on intuition and human-like analogies (“perfectly align with how humans are more easily persuaded”) without systematic analysis or evidence that these components genuinely improve jailbreak efficacy. There is no discussion of how components interact, alternative categorizations, or validation criteria. This resembles an engineering design rather than rigorous science.
- **Fitness evaluation relies on heuristic keyword matching.** This approach is brittle, superficial, and may produce false positives/negatives. The paper does not demonstrate advantages over using a reward model to label harmfulness. The four-point scoring with an additional term is described as a basic engineering trick that could be generated by simply prompting an LLM.
- **Lack of analysis of evolutionary dynamics.** Despite claims of “expanding the search space and allowing for precise, efficient strategy refinement,” the paper only reports JSR and query efficiency. There is no evidence of structure or behavior variations within the population, no discussion of how strategies evolve over time, what types emerge, or how components are combined and modified. This makes it difficult to assess the effectiveness of the GA approach.
- **Computational cost and scalability concerns are insufficiently addressed.** The GA and modular decomposition are likely computationally intensive, but no runtime, memory usage, or hardware specifications are provided. No comparison of computational requirements to existing methods is given. The query budget is a critical consideration; ASE may require many queries, and it is unclear how it performs given the same budget as baselines. On open-source models, ASE does not significantly outperform existing approaches, which could be cheaper for large-scale querying.
- **Missing key experimental details and statistical rigor.** Crossover and mutation rates are not reported. As evolutionary algorithms are inherently random, the experiments should be run multiple times with average and standard deviation reported, but these are absent, lowering soundness. Only one run is implied.
- **Claim of “adaptive” is not justified.** There is no specific mechanism that adjusts the strategy or algorithm parameters based on intermediate optimization results; the framework appears to be a normal optimization framework, not adaptive in a dynamic sense.
- **Overqualification of GA.** The population size is small (15) and iteration count is low (5), where gains in JSR become marginal beyond these settings. This suggests simpler optimization techniques (e.g., local search) might suffice. The choice of GA over other optimization methods is not discussed.
- **Poor writing quality and presentation issues.** The introduction contains numerous grammar errors and inappropriate expressions, making it ambiguous and overly complex. Specific issues include: improper citation formatting (Line 074: brackets generated from incorrect commands), grammatical problems (Line 078, Line 085), redundant comma (Line 137), and “algorithm 1” instead of “Algorithm 1” (Line 323). These affect readability.
- **Methodological concerns.** The core optimization uses a basic GA without addressing why this is the optimal design for jailbreaking. The modular decomposition and fitness function are presented as contributions but are seen by some as heuristic or automatically generable. One reviewer notes the method resembles an engineering demo rather than a rigorous scientific contribution.
- **Questions on initial population and convergence.** If the initial population is not well-chosen, ASE could converge to suboptimal solutions, reducing effectiveness. The paper does not investigate sensitivity to initial conditions.
- **No discussion of defense mechanisms.** The paper does not consider how existing defense methods might perform against ASE, nor propose a defense framework.
