## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces JEI-DNN, an early-exit dynamic neural network that augments a frozen backbone (T2T-ViT) with lightweight trainable gates and intermediate classifiers. The gates are optimized via a surrogate binary classification task, and the system uses a bilevel optimization to jointly learn the gating mechanism and inference modules. Evaluated on CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN, the method aims to improve speed-accuracy trade-off and uncertainty characterization via conformal prediction.

### Strengths
- The studied problem of input-dependent early-exiting is of great interest and practical importance.
- The motivation is clear: decoupled training of gates and classifiers causes train-test mismatch and positive bias in predictive probabilities.
- The manuscript offers a principled formulation and a beautiful mathematical treatment of the early-exit task, providing useful insights for practitioners.
- Most design choices are well motivated and backed by practical insights.
- The proposed method achieves a superior speed-accuracy trade-off compared to a wide set of baselines.
- The approach yields better uncertainty characterization; analyzing conformal intervals for early-exiting classifiers and showing better calibration is promising.
- The ablation study in 8.6 clearly demonstrates the merits of learnable gating mechanisms over other design choices.
- Figure 2a and 2b clearly show the early-exiting patterns for the proposed method vs. competing approaches.
- The literature review is comprehensive.
- The method is technically sound.
- Results indicate effectiveness in both accuracy-efficiency tradeoff and calibrated confidence.

### Weaknesses
- **Contribution novelty**: The use of learned early-exiting gates and intermediate classifiers trained jointly with the backbone is common practice. After approximations, the gate loss term reduces to summing losses of independent binary early-exiting classifiers, similar to Han et al. (2022b). Constructing surrogate binary targets for gates is also common (e.g., FrameExit, Ghodrati et al. CVPR 2021). One reviewer (score 6) found the major contribution unclear.
- **Overclaiming contributions**: The paper’s framing implies architectural novelty, but the core contribution is the joint learning procedure. Experiments do not justify claiming a new architecture.
- **Limited backbone evaluation**: Experiments are restricted to the T2T-ViT backbone. The method should be demonstrated on more established early-exit architectures specifically designed for dynamic exiting, such as MSDNet, RANet, and the SOTA Dynamic Perceiver. Evaluation on frozen-backbone CNNs is a limited scenario; performance on end-to-end trainable EE models (jointly training backbone and exits) remains unclear.
- **Lack of ImageNet experiments**: The method is evaluated only on small-scale CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN. ImageNet-1K is a standard benchmark for realistic evaluation, and its absence makes the results less compelling and generalizability unclear. The use of ImageNet-pretrained backbones fine-tuned on small similar-domain datasets (CIFAR) is questioned; direct testing on ImageNet would be more convincing.
- **Comparison fairness**: The accuracy comparison between T2T-ViT (transformer) and MSDNet/RANet (CNN) in Figure 5 may be unfair, as most gain comes from the more powerful backbone, not the early-exiting method. MSDNet shows more robust early-exiting (retaining original accuracy at ~50% compression) than JEI-DNN, which drops rapidly even at 25% compression.
- **Puzzling early-exit patterns**: In Figure 2a, samples do not exit at the first few early-exit layers for JEI-DNN, despite those layers having high accuracy. This raises the question of whether hyperparameters prevent early exiting from these layers and whether the exiting patterns can be better controlled.
- **Design choice justification**: The use of the min operator in Eq. 8 is not fully justified. Why not simple normalization (dividing by sum)? An ablation experiment is needed to validate this choice and the role of the second (1-sum) term.
- **Monotonic exit assumption**: The assumption that a sample exiting at gate layer $l$ should also exit from any later-stage gate contradicts the overthinking phenomenon (Kaya et al. ICML 2019), where later layers can misclassify correctly classified samples. This assumption should be discussed.
- **Missing comparisons**: The paper should compare with or discuss existing methods like zero-time waste (geometric ensemble similar to gating probability) and differentiable branching (trainable exit strategy end-to-end).
- **Presentation clarity**: The method is hard to understand before reading the whole paper. The abstract and contributions section lack explanation of how the method works; there is no visual schema of the model; important design details (e.g., “Gate design”) appear late in the experimental section. A clear diagram, pseudo-code, or reorganization would significantly help.
- **Bilevel formulation rationale**: Why is bilevel optimization needed when techniques like reparameterization or score function estimators could train all blocks simultaneously? The overall training time compared to alternatives is not reported.
- **Terminology misinterpretation**: The paper uses “IC-Only training” for “inference cost-only training,” but the community typically uses “IC-Only” to denote “Intermediate-classifier only training.”
