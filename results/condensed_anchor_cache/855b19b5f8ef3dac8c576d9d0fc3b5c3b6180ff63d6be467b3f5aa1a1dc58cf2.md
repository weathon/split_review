## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a plug-and-play sampling method for diffusion-based super-resolution (SR) by solving diffusion ODEs with an approximately optimal boundary condition (BC) obtained from a reference LR-HR set. Reviewers agree the motivation is intuitive and the method achieves good perceptual quality (LPIPS) with fewer sampling steps. Reviewer 2 (score 8, high confidence) praises mathematical completeness and finds experiments sufficient, including bicubic-SR and real-SR tasks with ablation studies. Reviewers 1 (score 6, moderate confidence) and 3 (score 6, high confidence) are more critical, citing insufficient comparisons with current SOTA, low PSNR / poor fidelity, missing runtime/parameter analysis, and weak visual evidence. Reviewer 1 raised their score after the rebuttal.

### Strengths
- Good visualization results with fewer steps in SR tasks (R1).
- Flexibility: can be applied to multiple diffusion models (R1).
- Paper is well-written (R3).
- Motivation of analyzing BCs of diffusion ODEs is intuitive (R2).
- Analysis is mathematically complete: proposes the concept of optimal BC and proves it is common to different LR images (R2).
- Experiments sufficient to support the theory: tasks on bicubic-SR and real-SR demonstrate assertion; ablation studies show influence of reference set and BC set (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient comparisons**: Methods compared in Table 2 are outdated. Need comparison with state-of-the-art real-world SR methods [1,2] and a contemporary diffusion SR method [3] that also addresses bicubic and real SR (R3). Missing comparison with GAN methods on parameters or runtime (R1).
- **Low fidelity (PSNR)**: PSNR values on multiple test sets are very low; SSIM not reported. Fidelity is not high, indicating poor reconstruction accuracy. The paper should discuss the trade-off between perceptual quality (LPIPS) and fidelity and aim to improve both (R1, R3).
- **Lack of computational cost analysis**: No runtime, parameter count, or memory usage compared with other methods (R1, R3). Calculating the approximately optimal BC appears time-consuming; analysis of time complexity, memory requirements, and scalability is needed (R2).
- **Choice of distance metric M**: The method leverages LPIPS as M. Discussion needed on whether pixel-level metrics like negative PSNR could be used, how the choice affects the optimal BC, and the trade-offs between perceptual and pixel-level metrics (R2).
- **Assumption of well-trained model**: Even a “well-trained” model cannot perfectly fit the real data distribution. The paper should analyze how distribution mismatch impacts the derived optimal BC, robustness to model inaccuracies, and potential overfitting when deriving the optimal BC from a reference set (R2).
- **Justification for uniform distribution claim**: The paper claims \( p_\theta(y | h_\theta(x_T, \phi)) \) is approximately uniform. Need empirical or theoretical justification, and discussion of whether the “blank token” introduces biases (R2).
- **Visual improvement not significant**: Figures in the appendix show subtle visual differences; need more compelling evidence, e.g., zoomed-in comparisons or highlighted regions (R3).
- **Uneven improvement across tasks**: The improvement on StableSR (real-SR) is smaller than on SR3 (bicubic-SR). The reasons for this discrepancy should be discussed (R2).
