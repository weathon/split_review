## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CodeChain, an inference framework that prompts LLMs to generate modular code through iterative self-revisions guided by representative sub-modules extracted and clustered from prior iterations. The approach improves pass@1 by 35% on APPS and 76% on CodeContests relative to direct generation, and works with both closed-source (GPT-3.5, GPT-4) and open-source (WizardCoder) LLMs. The reviewers generally find the idea intuitive and the results solid, though concerns about novelty, evaluation limitations, and certain design choices are shared.

### Strengths
- The CodeChain prompting technique is intuitive, easy to use, and effectively induces modular code generation—an approach that is novel for code tasks.
- The method achieves strong empirical results (e.g., relative pass@1 improvements of 35% on APPS and 76% on CodeContests) and is evaluated on both closed‑source (GPT-3.5, GPT-4) and open‑source (WizardCoder) LLMs.
- Extensive ablation studies are provided, covering clustering strategies (Table 3), embedding choices, revision sampling, number of clusters, model sizes, and program qualities.
- The work addresses a meaningful gap: LLMs tend to produce monolithic code, while modular decomposition improves reliability, reusability, and reduces manual maintenance cost.
- The paper is well‑structured and written clearly, making the approach easy to understand.
- One reviewer (rating 8) was notably more positive about the significance and novelty than the others (ratings 6), highlighting the value of focusing on modularity as a qualitative improvement beyond functional correctness.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns:** Using CoT to generate and select representative demonstrations has been explored before (e.g., [ref]); the contribution is incremental for code generation. The idea of decomposing source code into components is also related to [2]. Self‑revision has been studied in [3,4] and more. A reviewer (rating 8) found the work “simple and effective” but noted the contribution as fair (2).
- **Sub‑module evaluation and filtering:**  
  * The evaluation of modularity/reusability via GPT‑4 Likert scale is not validated against human preference.  
  * The effectiveness of sub‑module generation itself is not directly measured.  
  * Public test cases are used to filter sub‑modules, but it is unclear how to compose tests for unknown sub‑modules generated by the LLM (since the decomposition is unknown a priori).
- **Sensitivity and alternative designs:**  
  * The clustering step (Table 3) adds only a small performance gain; randomly picking modules also does reasonably well. An experiment adding *all* submodules (instead of random selection) was suggested to test whether clustering is needed.  
  * It is unclear how the results vary with different phrasings of the prompt. The authors did not report testing multiple prompt variants or the variance/sensitivity. Could a single‑shot prompt achieve similar improvements?  
  * The choice of K‑means clustering is not justified; density‑based methods like DBSCAN (which do not require a fixed number of clusters) might be more suitable.  
  * Four schemes for deciding the number of clusters were tested, but tasks vary in complexity; sub‑modules that are never used in the final solution may be generated, and their effect is not analyzed.
- **Self‑revision iteration and overfitting:**  
  * Performance drops at the 5th iteration (Figures 5, 7), which hints at limitations of self‑revision prompting. The claim that this is due to overfitting to public test sets is not supported by evidence.  
  * There is no consistent pattern to determine the optimal number of revision rounds.
- **Evaluation scope and missing comparisons:**  
  * Only two benchmarks (APPS, CodeContests) are used; extending to subsets from the bigcode‑evaluation‑harness was suggested.  
  * It is unclear whether CodeChain benefits CodeLlama models (not tested).  
  * The ability to generalize to other domains (e.g., mathematical reasoning) is not established.  
  * In Table 2, GPT‑4 (All: 34.75) outperforms Self‑repair+GPT‑4 with GPT‑4 as feedback source (33.30); the reason is not explained.
- **Writing and documentation:**  
  * References to Appendix sections (e.g., Appendix F) do not specify which figure or prompt is being referred to.  
  * The one‑shot demonstration used in Section 3.2 is not clearly identified in Figure 3 or Appendix F, nor is its construction described.  
  * Y‑axis labels are missing in Figures 6 and 7.
