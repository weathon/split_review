## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents econSG, an open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation method based on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). It addresses two issues: over-reliance on SAM to regularize CLIP without refinement, and multi-view inconsistency from reducing 2D semantic features before 3D fusion. Two contributions are proposed: (1) Confidence-region Guided Regularization (CRR) that mutually refines SAM and CLIP for precise boundaries and completeness; (2) a low-dimensional contextual space built by fusing backprojected multi-view 2D features into 3D then reducing dimensionality, enforcing multi-view consistency and efficiency. Experiments show state-of-the-art results on four benchmarks and highest training efficiency.

### Strengths
- Well-motivated, addressing 2D feature inconsistency and high computational cost of high-dimensional features (R1).
- Extensive benchmarking against current state-of-the-art methods (R1).
- Clear explanation of motivation, methodology, pipeline illustrations (R2).
- Good experimental results (R3).
- Incorporating visual foundation models for multi-modal learning is interesting (R3).
- CRR is a novel mutual refinement strategy between OpenSeg and SAM, achieving precise boundaries and completeness (R4).
- Low-dimensional 3D contextual space integrates 2D features from different views, improving consistency and accuracy (R4).
- Significant reduction in computational complexity; improved training and querying efficiency via pre-trained autoencoder (R4).
- State-of-the-art results on four benchmark datasets (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **MLP compression concern (R1, minority - score 3).** Claim that a 5-layer MLP trained per scene compresses large VLM embeddings to 6 dimensions without information loss appears counterintuitive. The MLP is trained on a limited subset (objects in one scene). This could disrupt original correlations (e.g., 'bird' vs 'chair' embeddings may become undesirably close). Authors need evidence that such distortions do not occur; otherwise the method may fail on open-vocabulary queries.
- **Text query usage contradiction (R1).** The paper states text queries are provided at test time, but line 209 mentions requiring a set of text queries T used during training to supervise the autoencoder (Eq. 1), and lines 304-305 use T in scene optimization for cross-entropy loss. Clarification needed.
- **Multi-scale segmentation claim unvalidated (R1).** Authors claim multi-scale segmentation but provide no experiments or comparisons. Does it require retraining? The design does not incorporate hierarchical semantics. Elaboration on the technical process needed.
- **Low-dimensional contextual space novelty questioned (R2, R3).** Similar idea of mapping high-dimensional features to low-dimensional space exists in LangSplat (Qin et al. 2024). Recent work FastLGS (Ji et al. 2024) proposes directly rendering low-dimensional semantic features. Unique contribution needs more explanation (R3).
- **Quantitative and qualitative experiments not aligned (R2).** Baselines in quantitative tables are absent in qualitative comparisons, reducing reliability. Provide qualitative results that align with quantitative results, and more qualitative results showing 3D consistency.
- **3D consistency from CRR questionable (R2).** Step b: consistency depends heavily on COLMAP depth map accuracy; average pooling may not fully eliminate large discrepancies between views. Step c: if a reprojected point is occluded in most views, majority voting may assign occlusion label instead of correct label. Elaboration on how 3D consistency is ensured and how majority voting is implemented needed.
- **Open-vocabulary experiment settings unclear (R3).** Section 5.1 does not clarify how sampling ensures open-vocabulary setting or which classes are used for training. Table 1 lists only a few classes (e.g., '20 different semantic class categories' in ScanNet). Include open-vocabulary results on more classes.
- **Writing clarity (R3).** Some sentences are overly long (e.g., ‘The parallel rapid developments…’). Typo in section 4.1 text feature notation T.
- **Missing ablation for CRR (R4).** The paper mentions an ablation study on CRR but does not present the corresponding experimental outcomes. This gap harms evaluation of each CRR component's impact.
- **Tendency to ignore small objects (R4).** Figure 2 suggests the method tends to segment large objects and ignores small objects.
- **Related work suggestion (R4).** Since multi-view consistency is repeatedly emphasized, add introductions to related research in related work or introduction.
