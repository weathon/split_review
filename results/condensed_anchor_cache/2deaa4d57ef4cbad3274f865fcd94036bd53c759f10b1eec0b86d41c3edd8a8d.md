## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Representative Guidance (RepG), a method to address incoherence in diffusion model sampling by steering the process along a consistent direction. RepG uses representative vectors from pre-trained self-supervised models as a coherent target, treating sampling as a downstream task. This contrasts with classifier guidance which uses class labels and can sacrifice diversity. RepG is evaluated on ImageNet and shows improvements in image quality and detail preservation, and can be combined with classifier-free guidance. The reviewers generally found the paper clear and the experiments convincing, but raised concerns about dependency on self-supervised model choice, limited validation on high-resolution images, missing baselines, and the need for more detailed explanations.

### Strengths

1. **Originality and Novelty:** The paper reframes the incoherence problem in diffusion sampling as a feature discrepancy alignment issue and introduces a novel solution using representative vectors from self-supervised models rather than one-hot class labels. This leverages richer semantic information and addresses the overemphasis on discriminative features in classifier guidance, thereby improving generative quality and diversity. (R1, R3)
2. **Clarity and Organization:** The paper is clearly written, easy to follow, and covers relevant related work comprehensively. (R2, R3)
3. **Experimental Validation:** Extensive experiments on public datasets (ImageNet) demonstrate that RepG improves image quality with finer details and reduced artifacts while preserving diversity. The approach shows superior performance, especially when combined with classifier-free guidance. (R1, R2, R3)
4. **Plugin Module Design:** RepG is a plug-and-play module that can be easily integrated into existing diffusion frameworks. (R2)

### Weaknesses

1. **Dependency on Self-Supervised Model Quality and Robustness:** RepG’s performance heavily depends on the chosen self-supervised model. MoCo-v2 gives the best results, while other models like SimSiam show only marginal improvement over classifier-free guidance (e.g., FID 1.88 vs. 1.89 on ImageNet 64×64). The paper lacks analysis of why certain self-supervised models perform better, does not explore fine-tuning or adaptation for the diffusion process, and raises concerns about generalizability and robustness across datasets. (R1)
2. **Limited Validation on High-Resolution Images:** Experiments are limited to lower resolutions (e.g., 64×64, 128×128). The paper lacks validation on high-resolution datasets like ImageNet 256×256 or higher, which is critical for assessing RepG’s ability to maintain fine-grained coherence and detail at scale. (R1)
3. **Missing Baselines and Incomplete Comparisons:**
   - Table 1 omits important baselines, such as RepG combined with IDDPM. The comparison with ADM backbones is not comprehensive enough; different ADM variant settings make direct comparison difficult. The authors should simplify to ADM + X comparisons (e.g., RepG vs. PxP vs. others). (R2)
   - Missing visualization results from existing approaches for side-by-side comparison. (R2)
   - Why does MoCo-v3 perform worse than MoCo-v2 in Table 2? (R2)
4. **Need for More Convincing Quantitative and Qualitative Results:** Some examples in Figure 3 are inconsistent or show limited improvement (e.g., a human figure partially removed but body parts remain, making the result more unrealistic; left example in the last row appears to reduce realism). The paper should include more compelling examples, a failure case analysis, and more diverse results to validate claimed improvements in detail and coherence. (R1)
5. **Computational Efficiency Unclear:** While the paper claims RepG is computationally efficient compared to noise-aware classifier guidance, it does not provide comparative metrics on computational savings (including at different resolutions). The overhead from integrating self-supervised models and tuning features at each timestep needs quantification. (R1, R3)
6. **Clarification Needed on Key Methodological Details:**
   - How do representative vectors capture more valuable information for generative tasks than one-hot vectors in classifier guidance? (R3)
   - How does RepG identify "faulty" features for modification? Could key features be mistakenly altered, harming image quality? (R3)
   - Definition 4.1: should \( t_1 \) and \( t_2 \) be consecutive time steps? (R3)
   - Equation 13: typo in denominator superscript of \( r \) – should be \( i \), not \( c \). (R3)
7. **Sensitivity to Hyperparameter \( K \):** The ablation study on the number of representative vectors \( K \) (lines 480–485) shows sensitivity; the paper does not provide guidance on selecting appropriate \( K \) values for different datasets or self-supervised models, nor explain trade-offs. (R1)
8. **Typos:** There are several typos, e.g., Table 1 caption: "ImageNet 256x26" should be "ImageNet 256x256". The manuscript needs careful proofreading. (R2)
