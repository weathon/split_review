## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes AdT-HyGCL, an adaptive temperature-enhanced dual-level hypergraph contrastive learning framework. It addresses limitations in modeling high-order relationships and group-wise collective behaviors in unlabeled hypergraphs. The method introduces a noise-enhanced augmentation module, a dual-level contrast mechanism (node-level and community-level), and an adaptive temperature coefficient to improve discrimination between positive and negative pairs. Empirical experiments on eight benchmark hypergraphs demonstrate effectiveness and robustness, with the method outperforming several baseline models.

### Strengths
- The paper studies an important problem of self-supervised hypergraph representation learning (S1 of Reviewer 5; also noted by Reviewers 3 and 4).
- The paper is well-organized and clearly written (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- The proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmarks, with extensive experiments including ablation and robustness tests (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 2 notes outperformance over baselines).
- The dual-level contrast mechanism captures both individual node behaviors and group-wise collective behaviors, which is a novel focus compared to prior hypergraph contrastive work (Reviewer 1).
- The adaptive temperature coefficient is an innovative design for autonomously tuning representation distances (Reviewer 1).
- The algorithm is simple yet effective (Reviewer 2).
- Ablation study identifies hyperedge removing as the most helpful augmentation, which is intuitive (Reviewer 2).
- Baseline methods selected are well-suited for comparison and evaluation (S2 of Reviewer 5).
- Theoretical justifications are provided and meet requirements (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**:
  - Both multi-level contrast and adaptive temperature have been explored in prior work; the authors did not cite or discuss relevant literature such as [1–4] (Reviewer 3). Specifically, noise-enhanced feature augmentation is well-utilized (e.g., Zhu et al. WWW 2021) (Reviewer 4); community-level contrastive learning has been proposed (e.g., Li et al. WWW 2022) (Reviewer 4); adaptive temperature in pre-trained models including GNNs has been studied (e.g., [3–6] in Reviewer 4, and [1–3] in Reviewer 5).
  - Only one data augmentation method (HyedgeR) is new, and it is intuitive (Reviewer 2).
  - The contribution relative to contrastive learning (versus hypergraph modeling) is unclear (Reviewer 2).
- **Performance issues and errors**:
  - In Table 1, on Citeseer Macro-F1, CHGNN performs better (64.98±1.77 vs. the paper’s reported best) (Reviewer 2). The claimed best results in Tables 1 and 2 contain errors: on Citeseer the best should be 64.98±1.77 and runner-up 64.65±0.85; on House (Nettack) the best should be 59.65±1.62 (Reviewer 5).
  - Overall performance improvement appears limited in Table 1 (Reviewer 3). AdT-HyGCL is not always the best (Reviewer 4).
  - On House and Walmart, most baselines perform poorly, likely due to heterophily (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**:
  - Table 2 is missing several baselines from Table 1, such as CHGNN (Reviewer 3). Robustness experiments use UniGCNII and AllDeepSets but do not include a more effective method like CHGNN; results for CHGNN are requested (Reviewer 1).
  - Common datasets DBLP and Pubmed are missing from experiments (Reviewer 2).
  - Recent hypergraph contrastive learning baselines are not included, e.g., [7][8] cited by Reviewer 4.
  - No comparison with other temperature adjustment methods (e.g., Adap-τ, automatic temperature individualization, temperature as uncertainty) (Reviewer 5).
- **Insufficient theoretical and methodological explanation**:
  - Section 4.2.2 (Community-Level Contrastive Learning) lacks a comprehensive theoretical explanation (Reviewer 1).
  - How the node-level and community-level branches are integrated to obtain final representations is not clearly explained; the balance between the two branches is not theoretically justified (Reviewer 1). The concatenation operator is used inconsistently (sometimes [·:·] sometimes ⊕) (Reviewer 5).
  - The adaptive temperature parameter implementation lacks formulaic evidence or code details (Reviewer 1). The hyperparameter τ_low is critical (performance drops without it) but no guidance on how to determine its value (Reviewer 2).
  - The claim that “temperature descents” during training is not proven for any loss (Reviewer 4). The propositions and proofs should be presented more carefully rather than by examples (Reviewer 4).
  - The loss function (JSD) is not analyzed; it is unclear why AdT-HyGCL is suitable for JSD over NT-Xent (Reviewer 5).
- **Missing ablation and parameter sensitivity**:
  - Parameter sensitivity experiments are missing for τ_low, η, ρ, λ1, and λ2 (Reviewer 4).
- **Motivation issues**:
  - The claim that existing methods have “limitations in capturing higher-order group-wise structures” is not well-defined; higher-order structures (e.g., communities) are already widely studied in graph data analysis (Reviewer 4).
  - The logic that hypergraphs need a dual-level mechanism because they cannot capture high-order relationships within themselves could lead to an infinite regress (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation concerns**:
  - Many technical details are not self-contained; Table 3 should be in the main body (Reviewer 5).
  - The statement “maximize the agreements among positive and negative contrastive pairs” in Preliminaries is unclear; sim(·) is not defined (Reviewer 5).
  - The term “noise-enhanced hypergraph augmentation” is strange; “feature distortion based” is more appropriate (Reviewer 5). Vectors x_i and δ_i are not bolded; η is unclearly defined as a learning rate (Reviewer 5).
  - Typo in Figure 1: “Dual-Leve” instead of “Dual-Level” (Reviewer 2).
  - Contribution list statements are improper (e.g., “Novelty” instead of a specific description) (Reviewer 5).
