## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Foundation Reinforcement Learning (FRL), a framework that integrates three embodied foundation priors—policy prior (distilled from UniPi video generation model plus inverse dynamics), value prior (VIP), and success reward prior (0‑1 success signal)—to accelerate goal‑conditioned RL. The Foundation Actor‑Critic (FAC) algorithm combines these priors, notably using a KL constraint to regularize the policy towards the policy prior and VIP as a potential‑based reward shaping. Experiments on 8 Meta‑World manipulation tasks show FAC achieves 100% success on 7/8 tasks within 200 k frames, outperforming DrQ‑v2 (which requires up to 1 M frames with hand‑designed rewards) and zero‑shot baselines. Ablation studies isolate the contribution of each prior.

### Strengths

- **Novel integration of foundation models**: The framework decouples policy and value priors from different pre‑trained sources (UniPi for policy, VIP for value) and creatively uses the value prior as a potential‑based reward function (Reviewer 4).  
- **Meticulous ablation study**: Ablations in Section 5.3 isolate the effect of each prior component (Reviewers 1, 4).  
- **Impressive sample efficiency**: FAC achieves 100% success on 7/8 tasks in <200 k frames, drastically surpassing DrQ‑v2 (requires >1 M frames with careful dense rewards) (Reviewers 1, 4).  
- **Robustness to noisy priors**: The method tolerates heavy noise and quantization errors in the foundation models (Reviewer 1).  
- **Minimal human intervention**: No human‑specified dense reward or teleoperated demonstrations are needed (Reviewer 1).  
- **Novel use of VIP**: The paper proposes a new way to leverage VIP beyond representation learning (Reviewer 2).  
- **Investigation of foundation models in RL**: The work explores how pre‑trained internet‑scale models can assist RL (Reviewer 3).  

*Note*: One reviewer (score 8) found the framework particularly novel and the ablation study excellent, while the other three reviewers (score 5) were less convinced of the novelty and scope of evaluation.

### Weaknesses

- **Not a new prior form**: The paper does not propose new foundation priors; it directly uses existing models (UniPi for policy, VIP for value) and a standard 0‑1 success signal for reward. The contribution is largely an empirical combination (Reviewers 2, 3).  
- **FAC is a simple extension of DrQ‑v2**: The core modification is adding a KL divergence constraint to a pre‑trained policy prior, a common technique in RL finetuning literature (Reviewer 1).  
- **Reward foundation prior not demonstrated**: All experiments use ground‑truth 0‑1 success reward, not a learned foundation reward prior. Results with a true foundation success reward are missing (Reviewers 1, 3).  
- **Limited experimental scope**: Only 8 tasks in the simple Meta‑World environment; baselines are restricted to DrQ‑v2, R3M, and zero‑shot methods (UniPi, Prior). No comparison with SOTA methods on Meta‑World or with other foundation models (e.g., VIP as a representation baseline).  
- **Ablation results not always convincing**: On button‑press‑topdown, FAC without policy prior performs similarly; on door‑open, FAC without value prior performs similarly. This weakens the claimed importance of each prior (Reviewer 1).  
- **Confounding source of policy prior benefits**: The policy prior is distilled from UniPi video demonstrations that are fine‑tuned on in‑domain Meta‑World data. It is unclear whether gains come from the foundation model or from the small in‑domain finetuning. An ablation disentangling these factors is needed (Reviewer 1).  
- **Missing discussion of limitations**: The paper does not address scaling to more tasks or more complex environments, comparison with language‑conditioned methods like VLMs, or the computational cost of the pipeline (Reviewer 4).  
- **Baseline choice questioned**: Why R3M (which gets 0% on 6/8 tasks) was used instead of VIP as a representation learning baseline is unclear (Reviewer 2).  
- **Uniqueness of FAC**: The method’s novelty beyond plugging in existing models is not articulated; the proposed actor–critic algorithm is essentially vanilla with added priors (Reviewer 3).  
- **Questions from reviewers** that overlap with missing experiments/ablations:  
  - It would be stronger to show benefits of the foundation policy prior *without any in‑domain finetuning* (Reviewer 1).  
  - Extension to multi‑task RL (training a single policy across multiple tasks) is not explored (Reviewer 4).  
  - How does a policy prior distilled from a small in‑domain DrQ‑v2 dataset compare with the UniPi‑derived prior? (Reviewer 1).
