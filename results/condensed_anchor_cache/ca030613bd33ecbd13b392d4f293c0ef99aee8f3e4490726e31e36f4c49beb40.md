## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper presents BenchMol, a multi-modality benchmarking platform for molecular representation learning (MRL), integrating seven molecular modalities (fingerprint, sequence, graph, geometry, image, geometry image, video) and evaluating 23 single-modality methods on existing (MoleculeNet) and newly constructed benchmarks (MBANet, StructNet). One reviewer (R1, score 10) found the work excellent—comprehensive, clearly written, and highly impactful for the field. In strong contrast, other reviewers (R2 score 1, R4 score 3, R5 score 5) criticized the benchmark’s practical value, design choices, and lack of deeper insights; R3 (score 5) acknowledged the effort but raised concerns about superficial findings and missing aspects (e.g., computational cost). The reviews thus reflect a deep divide over the significance and rigor of the contributions.

### Strengths

- **Addresses a critical problem in MRL:** The paper tackles unfair comparisons and the absence of a unified evaluation platform (R1, R2).  
- **Comprehensive and extensive scope:** Covers 7 modalities, 23 methods, and large-scale experiments across three benchmarks (R1, R3, R5). R1 highlights the inclusion of 9 main insights.  
- **Reproducibility utilities and clear presentation:** Provision of splits, code (GitHub), and detailed documentation (R1). R1 and R5 both found the paper well-written and easy to follow.  
- **Novel task and interesting findings from linear probing:** Predicting basic molecular attributes is a new angle; the observation that randomly initialised sequence models perform well in linear probing is particularly intriguing (R1, R2, R5).  
- **Experiments with multiple conformations:** Image modality experiments using multiple conformers provide informative insights beyond the original scope (R1).  
- **Good survey of MRL methods and clear visual design:** The paper gives a useful overview of existing methods; Figures and Tables are crisp and facilitate comprehension (R1, R3).  
- **Evident effort and thorough benchmarking:** Unifying code from many methods and running extensive evaluations (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **MBANet labels are too simple and lack practical value:** Multiple reviewers (R2, R4, R5) point out that predicting atom types, bond types, and basic attributes does not address real-world applications (e.g., binding affinity, toxicity, drug efficacy). R4 asks what chemistry problem the atom distribution prediction task solves. R1 additionally notes that some subtasks (e.g., Ge atom count) are severely skewed (only one nonzero count), making them effectively classification tasks or noise; the paper should acknowledge this limitation and consider removing or converting such subtasks (also applies to Figure S2-b).  
- **Rationale for splitting data by 2D graph patterns is unclear:** R2 and R5 observe that Table 6 shows only minor performance differences across the chosen structural categories (acyclic, complete chain, macrocyclic peptide, etc.), undermining the claimed significance of the split.  
- **Image/video modalities lack justification and computational cost is ignored:** R2, R5, and R3 all question why artificial modalities (generated from 2D/3D structures) are important and which tasks benefit from them. R2 specifically notes the added computational cost is not justified by performance advantages; R3 criticizes the complete absence of computational cost analysis, which is crucial when comparing different modalities (e.g., for virtual screening).  
- **Benchmark evaluates only single-modality methods, missing multi-modal approaches:** R4 points out that the platform is advertised as multi-modality but only compares single-modality methods, omitting multi-modal representation learning methods (e.g., Wang et al., 2024).  
- **No insights into why 3D modality (Uni-Mol) outperforms others on MoleculeNet:** R2 and R5 both request explanations for this finding, which is currently presented without analysis.  
- **Presentation quality issues:** R3 reports problems: “preproc/essing” word-wrapping in Figure 2(b), unclear term “combing,” and lack of precise definitions for image, geometry image, and video. R4 notes inconsistent font sizes in Figure S3 and poor organization (molecular print method described on page 24, other methods on page 6; no hyperlinks to methods in tables). These issues hinder understanding, though R1 and R5 found the paper clear.  
- **Findings are often superficial, lacking deeper insights:** R3 states that observations (e.g., video modality significantly outperforms on MBANet) are reported without explaining *why*. R2 and R5 ask for the underlying reasons behind modality performance differences.  
- **MBANet dataset size is too small (10,000 molecules) and comparison may be unfair:** R3 argues that the video modality sees more conformations (frames), while other modalities could also access multiple conformations; the small size may favor video models.  
- **Dataset access is non-functional:** R4 reports that the OneDrive link in the README is unreachable, preventing reproduction. (R1 claims reproducibility utilities are provided, creating a contradiction.)  
- **Contribution over existing tools is unclear:** R4 questions the novelty of BenchMol given that RDKit and OpenBabel already interconvert molecular modalities.  
- **Research gap in AI4Science not well-defined:** R4 asks what specific chemistry questions BenchMol addresses (e.g., how correct atom distribution prediction benefits chemistry).  
- **Missing check for duplicate SMILES in datasets:** R1 raises a question about detecting duplicate molecules (canonicalization, InChIKeys), which is a potential data-quality issue that should be addressed.  
- **Missing investigation into random sequence model inductive bias:** R1 asks whether performance depends on model size and what mechanism explains the strong inductive bias; this is a missing analysis (also requested by R2 in a related question about representation failures on simple tasks).  
- **No concise guidance on model/modality selection:** R3 requests a clear conclusion on which model or modality to choose for MRL tasks, which is not provided.
