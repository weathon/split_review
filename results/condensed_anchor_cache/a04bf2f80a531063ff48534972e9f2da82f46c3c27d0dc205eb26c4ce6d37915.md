## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ROAD, a single-stage adversarial self-distillation method that uses the student from the last epoch and a concurrently trained natural model as teachers. It aims to improve robustness and natural accuracy while reducing the computational overhead of conventional adversarial distillation. Reviewers acknowledge the method's empirical performance and the relevance of self-distillation, but raise substantial concerns about novelty, insufficient ablations, missing baselines, theoretical justification, and clarity of exposition. One reviewer is notably more critical (score 3), questioning both novelty and experimental rigor; another is more positive (score 6), praising the self-distillation idea but noting the need for more analysis.

### Strengths
- Focuses on adversarial self-distillation, a promising avenue that lowers resource demands compared to standard adversarial distillation. (R1)
- Simple yet effective framework that achieves strong natural accuracy and robustness; results partially reveal ineffectiveness of existing AD methods under this setting. (R1)
- The idea of extending from distillation to self-distillation is natural and expected to work well. (R2)
- The asymmetry in how the robust model and natural model influence each other is interesting. (R2)
- Achieves reasonably good empirical performances across three networks and attacks including AutoAttack. (R2, R3)
- Provides theoretical and experimental analysis on how soft labels mitigate robust overfitting. (R4)
- Paper is well written and methodology is easy to follow. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty insufficient / overlap with prior work:** The use of soft labels has been explored in prior work [1], online training methods in [2], and natural model as teacher in [3]. Self-distillation via previous epoch/EMA models is already established, and online collaborative training (ACT, CAD) is well-known. The incorporation of soft labels and label-smoothing in AT is not novel. (R3, R4)
- **Incomplete ablation studies:** 
    - Need detailed ablation on asymmetry: what if symmetric knowledge transfer (e.g., natural model also uses KL regularization) is used? What if a hyperparameter similar to γ is used to regulate KL for the natural model? (R2)
    - The impact of self-distillation vs. natural model guidance vs. soft-labels is not isolated; Figure 4 suggests self-distillation contributes little, with the main boost coming from the natural model – this is counter-intuitive and not explained. (R2, R3)
    - Ablations needed: removal of each component individually; comparison of using one-hot labels for both self-distillation and natural model guidance; evaluation with different checkpoints for the robust teacher. (R3, R4)
- **Missing baselines:** Lack of comparisons to several relevant baselines including ACT, MAT, IAD, Weight Averaging [5], and AWP [6]. Results in Table 3 do not match the baseline references [1]. Inconsistencies exist with reported RSLAD and SEAT results. (R3)
- **Theoretical justification of gradient norm effect is incomplete:** The proof in Section 3.1.1 shows that soft labels reduce the gradient norm w.r.t. logits when confidence increases, but does not show that this extends to the gradient w.r.t. model weights θ, which is the quantity typically constrained to prevent overconfidence. (R1)
- **Unclear or unsupported claims in Section 3.2:** The statement that “distilling knowledge from the static natural teacher may impair robustness” is not adequately supported by the cited references. (R1)
- **Logical flow and exposition issues:** Section 3 transitions are unclear; the intuition behind Equation 3 and the Robustness Enhancement term is insufficiently explained. (R1)
- **Ambiguity about the “LS” method:** The abbreviation “LS” is never spelled out, making the ablation study on “Effects of utilizing last epoch predictions” difficult to evaluate. (R2)
- **Computational complexity:** ROAD maintains two separate networks, retains an additional copy of the last-epoch model in GPU memory, and requires three extra forward passes per iteration. This cost should be compared to other online distillation methods. (R3)
- **Hyperparameter tuning concerns:** 
    - The sine-increasing schedule for λ (Equation 1) is not thoroughly explored; the effect of a constant λ or linear schedule is unknown. (R1)
    - The λ schedule is based on the hypothesis that robust models have poor natural accuracy early in training – no supporting evidence is provided. (R3)
    - Sensitivity of γ and λ between CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 is questioned; hyperparameters should ideally not be highly dataset-dependent. (R3)
- **Purpose of TRADES-style term:** In Section 3.3, the second term uses KL loss between robust and natural predictions (similar to TRADES). Does this remain necessary when a separate natural model already exists? (R3)
- **Missing details on reliability and label smoothing:** No reliability diagram is provided for RSLAD for comparison, and the differences between various label-smoothing concepts need clarification. (R3)
- **No baseline natural model in tables:** Tables 1 and 2 would benefit from reporting natural model accuracy/reference. (R2)
