## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MixSup, a mixed-grained supervision paradigm for label-efficient LiDAR-based 3D object detection. It leverages cheap coarse cluster-level labels (via three clicks or automated PointSAM) for semantic learning and a few expensive box-level labels for geometry estimation. The method is validated on nuScenes, Waymo Open, and KITTI using various detectors, achieving up to 97.31% of fully supervised performance with only cluster labels and 10% box annotations. The approach is motivated by a pilot study showing that geometry estimation is robust to data size, implying semantics require more data.

### Strengths
- Well-articulated motivation and logical progression; the method is intuitive and efficient.
- The paper is overall well written and easy to follow.
- The approach is simple yet achieves strong performance across multiple datasets (nuScenes, Waymo, KITTI) and detector architectures.
- The exploration of automatic coarse labeling using SAM (PointSAM) is a valuable addition to reduce annotation burden.
- The authors promise open-source code, which will facilitate future research.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with closest works**: The paper does not compare with [Meng et al. 2020;2021], which also use many weak labels and few full labels for 3D detection. Comparisons on labeling cost, performance, and generality across detectors are needed. Also missing comparisons with recent SAM-based LiDAR segmentation methods ([R1] Zhang et al., [R2] Liu et al., [R3] Chen et al.) and with state-of-the-art weakly/semi-supervised 3D detectors.
- **Pilot study insufficient to support main claim**: The pilot study only shows that geometry estimation is robust to data size, but does not directly demonstrate that semantic learning requires massive data. The term “data size” is ambiguous (number of frames vs. object/instance sizes?). Training on region-level point clouds rather than full scenes may remove important context. The motivation may simply reflect the known importance of high recall for AP, not a new insight. Moreover, Table 4 shows MV-JAR achieves close performance with only 10% box annotations, which appears to contradict the claim that massive semantic labels are crucial. An ablation varying the amount of coarse labels for the classification/proposal stage is needed.
- **Labeling cost claims unsubstantiated**: The claimed 14% cost for cluster labeling and the difficulty of making an accurate center click lack supporting user study. There is no comparison between human-annotated and simulated coarse labels. The clicking annotation process is not clearly described (annotator guidance, strategy for different instance types, whether all point clouds are labeled manually). An alternative approach—using a region-based network (e.g., second-stage PointNet) to refine pseudo-boxes from clicks into precise boxes—is not explored or justified.
- **Important implementation details omitted**: The acronym "SAR" is introduced without context (Sec. 4.3). The connected components labeling (CCL) implementation is too succinct. The pre-trained semantic segmentation model used for 2D masks is not specified, raising potential data leakage concerns. No experiments address the impact of camera-LiDAR calibration errors on SAR-derived coarse masks.
- **Design decisions appear heuristic**: Many choices lack rigorous justification or analytical backing. For instance, using the cluster center to substitute the object center for semantic learning may introduce training instability due to noise from clicking or SAM; the authors could consider voxel-to-point interpolation (e.g., PVCNN, SASSD) with point-level supervision.
- **Presentation issues**: The terms “semantic” and “geometry” are used ambiguously and need clearer definitions. Section 4 is confusing: Figure 3 is not referenced in the main text and its caption is not self-contained; the term “assignment” is not well-defined. Adding a limitations and social impact section would strengthen the paper. (Minor: anchor tables such as Table 2, 4, 5, 7, 10 to the top of their pages.)
- **Inconsistent baseline results**: In Table 4, PointContrast and ProposalContrast show negative gains relative to the 10% frames baseline, which is inconsistent with previous literature (GCC-3D). The training protocol for these baselines should be clarified.
- **Missing explanation of annotation settings**: The difference between “10% boxes annotation” and “10% frames annotation” is not explained.
