## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers generally found the paper’s motivation (inspired by phonemic restoration) and the overall framework design (SeCM, SiCM, CCFM) interesting and well-described. The reported performance is state-of-the-art on SDR, PESQ, and STOI metrics, and ablation studies are extensive (R1, R3, R4). However, multiple reviewers raised serious concerns about technical writing clarity, lack of reproducibility (undefined discriminator, missing code), insufficient attribution of prior work (notably the channel-swapping block from DPRNN and time-frequency alternating modules from TF-GridNet/RTFS-Net), outdated baseline comparisons, and a need for additional evaluations (subjective MOS, more metrics, parameter/MAC analysis). While one reviewer (R3) praised the writing and comprehensiveness, others (R1, R2, R4) criticized presentation and reproducibility, and two reviewers (R2, R4) questioned the magnitude of contributions relative to existing methods.

### Strengths
- **Motivation and narrative** – The phonemic-restoration inspiration is sound and clearly explained (R1, R3, R4). The literature review is well-researched and contextualizes the work effectively (R2).
- **State-of-the-art results** – The model achieves SOTA performance on SDR, PESQ, and STOI across several datasets (R1, R3, R4).
- **Comprehensive ablation studies** – Detailed experiments validate the contributions of individual modules (R3, R4). The use of a discriminator to add an additional loss signal is a positive methodological choice (R2).
- **Clear module descriptions** – The roles of SeCM, SiCM, and CCFM are explained in a structured way (R4).
- **Novel focus on frequency-domain visual correlations** – The paper correctly identifies that prior work often overlooks correlations between visual features and frequency dimensions of speech (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Technical writing and clarity**  
  * Multiple notation errors and inconsistencies: line 278 should read \(B \times F_x \times T_x \times C\) (R1, R2, R4); “diverging” repeated at line 283 (R1, R2); \(F_e\) and \(F_p\) in line 301 are not defined (R1, R2); \(F\) function in Eq. 8 is not described (R4); speech feature \(P\) should not be \(F\) and \(T\) (R4).  
  * Equations 6 and 7 lack spacing around ellipses and commas, hindering readability (R1, R2).  
  * Line 302 should read “contains” not “contain” (R1, R2).  
  * Inconsistent use of “SiCM” – earlier refers to a Mamba block, later to entire time/frequency processing modules (R1, R2).  
  * Insufficient spacing between lines 424–425 (R3, R4). Typo: line 269 shows \(CM_2\) instead of \(CM^2\) (R3).  
  * One reviewer (R3) considered the paper well-written; others (R1, R2, R4) rated presentation low (2/5 or 2.5/5 average).

- **SiCM module under-specified**  
  * Inputs \(Q_{in}\) not defined; equations appear to be defined twice (R1, R2).  
  * The choice of bidirectional Mamba is unusual and no ablation experiments are provided to justify it (R1, R2).  
  * Missing citations for the first use of bidirectional Mamba in the speech domain (e.g., Jiang et al. 2024, Li & Chen 2024) (R4).

- **Discriminator not defined** – The discriminator loss is mentioned but its architecture, training procedure, and integration are not described, affecting reproducibility (R1, R2).

- **Lack of proper attribution / possible original contribution overclaim**  
  * The paper states “we introduce a Channel Swapping Block (CSBlock)” but this operation is identical to DPRNN’s dimension-switching approach and has been used in many previous works (R1).  
  * The time-frequency alternating module is common in speech separation (TF-GridNet, RTFS-Net); these works should be cited and their relationship discussed (R4).  
  * The overall pipeline closely resembles RTFS-Net, TF-GridNet, and CTCNet; the paper’s writing style could mislead readers into thinking these are original contributions, which is concerning (R1 – ethics flag).  
  * Missing references for well-known speech enhancement methods (e.g., LA-VocE, Tan & Wang 2019) (R3).  
  * Citation for BiMamba should include [1,2] as recommended (R4).

- **Outdated baseline comparisons**  
  * The compared methods are quite old; modern AVSS/AVSE techniques (e.g., by setting speaker 2 = noise) should be included (R1, R2).  
  * Specific suggestions: LA-VocE (R3), RTFS-Net (R4), AVSEC 2024 methods (R4).  
  * Many new approaches were proposed in 2024 that claim strong performance; the paper does not compare with them (R4).

- **Insufficient evaluation scope**  
  * No subjective listening test (MOS) or demo video to corroborate the objective metric improvements (R3).  
  * Only three objective metrics are reported; additional metrics like MCD (important for frequency-domain claims), ViSQOL (R3), Si-SNRi and SDRi (R2) are recommended.  
  * Comparison of inference time and number of parameters / MACs is missing; given the heavy reliance on pre-trained models, this is critical to fairly compare complexity (R3, R4).

- **Contribution of CCFM vs. pre-trained encoder**  
  * Ablations in Table 6 show that visual information helps, but the performance gain may originate from the large pre-trained encoder (AV-HuBERT) rather than the proposed CCFM (R3).  
  * Further analysis without the pre-trained encoder and visualizations of CCFM intermediate features are needed (R3).  
  * The use of many pre-trained models makes the system complex; a parameter/MAC comparison is necessary to assess efficiency (R4).

- **AF Block justification** – Equation 11’s operation is unconventional for “attention”; motivation/related work and potential alternatives (e.g., cross-attention) should be discussed (R2).

- **How is \(E\) obtained from \(V\), \(PV\), and \(PAV\)?** – The process for extracting \(E\) in the SeCM module is not explained, and using time-domain input adds complexity without justification (R4).

- **Questions overlapping with criticisms**  
  * How can the method be compared with previous works under different SNR conditions when most prior works did not provide such evaluation? – This points to a missing experimental design detail (R1).  
  * Why does the model use ISTFT instead of a vocoder when converting mel-spectrograms to waveforms? – Relevant to methodological clarity (R3).  
  * Request for inference time and parameter count comparison (R3, R4) – already listed above.
