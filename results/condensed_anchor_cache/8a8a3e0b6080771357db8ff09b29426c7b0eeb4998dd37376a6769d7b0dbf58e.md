## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper defines the semi-supervised long-tailed recognition problem – combining long-tailed and semi-supervised learning – and proposes an alternate sampling framework that iteratively decouples feature embedding and classifier learning in three stages: (1) generate pseudo-labels on unlabeled data, (2) train feature embedding with both labeled and pseudo-labeled samples using random sampling and a consistency loss, (3) train classifier with labeled samples using class-balanced sampling. Experiments on CIFAR-LT and ImageNet-LT show improvements, especially on extremely imbalanced data. The setting is considered interesting, and the paper is generally easy to follow. One reviewer (R2) gave a higher rating (5) than the others (3), indicating disagreement on overall quality, but all pointed to significant weaknesses.

### Strengths
- The setting combining long-tailed and semi-supervised learning opens up a new field and is interesting (R1, R3, R4).
- Leveraging unlabeled data to mitigate the negative impact of long-tailed distribution is reasonable (R2).
- The paper is easy to follow; introduction is easy to read (R2, R3, R4).
- Significant accuracy improvements are demonstrated on extremely imbalanced scenarios (R1).
- Many SSL and long-tailed recognition studies are reviewed in Section 2 (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Outdated related work and missing comparisons**: Many existing long-tailed semi-supervised learning (LTSSL) methods are not discussed or compared, including DARP, ABC, DASO, SAW, CReST, CoSSL, Adsh, DebiasPL, L2AC, UDAL (R2, R4), as well as earlier works such as the 2021 arxiv paper 2105.00133 (R1) and the CVPR 2023 paper by Wei & Gan (R3). Supervised long-tailed baselines used (e.g., logit adjustment, parametric contrastive learning) are not state-of-the-art (R2).
- **Insufficient experiments**: Lack of comprehensive evaluation on different imbalance ratios, unlabeled data ratios, and ablation studies on hyper-parameters and sampling strategies (R2). Missing experiments on standard LTSSL benchmarks (CIFAR-100-LT, STL-10-LT) (R4) and representative long-tailed benchmarks (Places-LT, iNaturalist) (R2). Experimental settings are not aligned with other LTSSL studies (R4). Performance for few-shot classes is not strong enough (R3).
- **Limited novelty**: The method is essentially a combination of decoupling and semi-supervised methods. The consistency loss is similar to early SSL (Laine et al., Temporal Ensembling) and alternating sampling is straightforward (R2, R3). No theoretical ground for the algorithm is provided (R4).
- **Heavy reliance on initial accuracy**: The method’s effectiveness depends strongly on the initial model accuracy – performance improvement is limited when initial accuracy is low, and gains are only average on low-imbalance datasets (R1).
- **Inappropriate novelty claim**: The paper claims to be the first to propose semi-supervised long-tailed learning, yet earlier works exist (e.g., arxiv 2021, ABC, DASO). The relationship to these works is not clarified (R1, R3).
- **Code not submitted**, so reproducibility cannot be verified (R4).
