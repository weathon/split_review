## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces TIDE, a triple-inspired decomposition and verification strategy for TableQA. In the decomposition phase, triples (head entity, relation, tail entity) are extracted from the question to generate sub-questions that capture more key information than direct decomposition. In verification, triples derived from answers are compared with those from the initial decomposition to enable straightforward validation, avoiding reliance on LLM self-correction. TIDE is applied on top of two LLM modes (Direct Prompting and Agent) and achieves state-of-the-art results on multiple TableQA datasets.

### Strengths
- The idea of incorporating triples into multiple stages (decomposition and verification) of TableQA is novel and intuitively makes sense as an intermediate representation; it effectively combines insights from question decomposition and LLM-based information extraction.
- The motivation is clear: using triple extraction as a tool to enhance understanding and validation of TableQA questions.
- The experiments are sufficient, comparing TIDE against many TableQA baselines, and the ablation study (Table 3: TIDE vs. TIDE-decomposition vs. TIDE-verification) is critical for identifying the impact of each component.
- TIDE works alongside existing pipeline modes (DP and Agent) and preserves their benefits, showing that the method is complementary.
- The paper successfully demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on challenging TableQA datasets.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited model generality and missing open-source evaluation**: The method is only tested with GPT-3.5. Reviewers question whether the gains are tailored to this specific model. It would strengthen the paper to demonstrate TIDE with other open-source LLMs (e.g., Llama3) and to analyze how model size affects triple extraction, error propagation, and overall performance. It is also unclear whether the triple extraction step could be substituted with non-LLM methods.
- **Clarity and formulation issues**: Several aspects of the pipeline are confusing and insufficiently formalized. There is only one equation (Eq. 1, problem formulation). The paper lacks:
  - Formal descriptions of each sub-module (decomposition, sub-question answering, verification, final answer synthesis) beyond examples.
  - The exact prompts used for triple extraction.
  - A clear explanation of how the final answer is synthesized after the verification stage.
  - A precise explanation of the ablated versions (TIDE vs. TIDE-decomposition vs. TIDE-verification) – what changes in each ablation?
- **Attribution of performance gain**: The direct reasoning baselines (DP and Agent) are not reported as SOTA on both datasets in prior work. It is questionable whether the observed improvement comes from the triple mechanism or simply from combining multiple reasoning steps (decomposition + agent-style verification). The paper needs a more detailed analysis to disentangle the contribution of triples from the added reasoning integration.
- **Applicability and handling of different question types**: The provided example focuses on a COUNT question. It is unclear how the system handles non-numeric questions (e.g., compose-style, multi-hop, comparative) and how triples are unified for each sub-question. Does the approach only work on aggregation-type questions? For mathematical questions (e.g., average age/salary), would sub-questions require triples for every table entry?
- **Lack of case studies and error analysis**: The appendix does not include successful cases compared to other methods, error case analysis, or examples that illustrate the working mechanism of the entire pipeline. Such cases would help explain how TIDE operates in practice.
- **Missing analysis of verification stage details**: The paper mentions that the LLM is prompted to regenerate if verification fails, but does not report how often verification fails, the maximum number of regeneration attempts, or how many triples typically pass/fail. This makes it difficult to assess the verification stage’s effectiveness and potential for infinite loops.
- **Data contamination concern**: Given that GPT models may have seen the tables in pre-training, it is unclear how much of the performance gain is due to the TIDE method versus the model’s prior exposure. The authors should discuss or address this.
- **Impact on efficiency**: Since triple extraction is an additional step, the paper should report the average response time and token usage overhead compared to baselines.
- **Limited contribution**: The paper combines existing ideas (question decomposition, LLM-based information extraction) without presenting a fundamentally new concept. More in-depth analysis is needed to justify the contribution.
