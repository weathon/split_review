## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes the Robust Causal and Anticausal (RoCA) Estimator to determine whether a data generation process is causal (X→Y) or anticausal (Y→X). The method exploits an asymmetric property: under causal relations, the instance distribution P(X) does not contain information about the noisy class-posterior distribution P(Ỹ|X), while under anticausal relations it does. RoCA injects varying levels of instance-dependent label noise, generates pseudo-labels via unsupervised clustering, and measures how the disagreement rate between pseudo-labels and noisy labels changes with noise level—absence of trend indicates causal, presence indicates anticausal. Experiments on multiple causal discovery and image datasets show significant improvement over existing methods.

### Strengths
- **Novelty of approach**: The noise-injection technique to detect causal vs. anticausal direction is original, moving beyond traditional causal discovery algorithms and providing a new direction for high-dimensional causal relationship detection (R1, R2, R3).
- **Effectiveness**: RoCA shows substantial improvement over existing causal discovery baselines on multiple datasets, including both synthetic and real-world noisy data (CIFAR-10N, Clothing1M, etc.) (R1, R2, R3).
- **Theoretical backing**: Formal analysis proves that RoCA can identify causal/anticausal direction; a detailed appendix reviews relevant techniques and datasets (R1, R3).
- **No true labels required**: By generating pseudo-labels through unsupervised clustering (e.g., K-means, SPICE*), RoCA determines direction without relying on true labels, increasing adaptability to diverse application scenarios (R1).
- **Clarity and structure**: The paper is well-organized and explains clustering, disagreement measures, and the asymmetry rationale systematically (R3).
- **Practical relevance**: The method is robust to label noise and applicable to fields (e.g., healthcare, economics, social sciences) where data noise is unavoidable (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of formal identifiability / assumptions on pseudo-labels**: The paper does not provide a clear statement of the conditions under which pseudo-labels generated by clustering accurately represent the true data distribution, nor a precise definition of when RoCA can identify the direction and when it fails. This undermines theoretical support for the pseudo-label generation process (R1, R2). Reviewer 2 notes that if samples of different classes happen to reside in well-defined clusters, pseudo-labels from P(X) could remain predictive of observed labels even after noise injection, potentially invalidating the method’s asymmetry logic. The authors should clarify expected failure scenarios and necessary assumptions (R2).
- **Instance-dependent noise injection is not convincingly instance-dependent**: The noise-generation process creates dependency on X via the l1-norm, but instances with different X can share the same l1-norm, so noise depends only on that statistic, not on semantic content (e.g., a husky mislabeled as wolf). The explanation of why this counts as instance-dependent noise is weak. Moreover, using a vanilla VAE for feature extraction is problematic because standard VAE features are not guaranteed to be semantically meaningful without specific architectural or training choices. A pre-trained backbone may also conflict with VAE’s generative objective (R2).
- **Missing comparison with latest neural causal discovery methods**: The paper compares with several causal discovery baselines but omits recent neural network-based causal discovery algorithms, which should be included (R1).
- **Computational cost**: RoCA involves multiple steps of noise injection and pseudo-label generation, which may scale poorly on large datasets. The authors should discuss model complexity and feasibility in practical applications (R1).
- **Parameter sensitivity**: RoCA’s performance relies heavily on parameters controlling clustering and the threshold for disagreement trends. If not tuned appropriately, performance may degrade, particularly for subtle causal effects or datasets with indistinct noise patterns (R3).
- **Large dataset requirement**: RoCA assumes the dataset is large enough to form meaningful clusters. For limited or highly sparse data, clusters may be unstable/non-representative, affecting causality detection reliability (R3). Reviewer 3 asks about performance in low-sample regimes; this is not addressed.
- **Class imbalance**: If dataset classes are imbalanced, pseudo-labels may skew toward larger classes, potentially affecting direction detection accuracy. The paper does not discuss how to handle imbalance (R1).
- **Stability with different clustering algorithms**: The paper uses K-means and SPICE* but does not analyze whether RoCA’s stability holds if other unsupervised clustering methods are used (R1, R2). Reviewer 2 asks explicitly how clustering choice affects RoCA.
- **Poor performance on specific datasets**: On Pair0071, RoCA does not achieve best results; the authors attribute this to confounders affecting both features and labels but do not analyze why. Similarly, on the Mushroom dataset, RoCA is not top-performing across all methods – no explanation is given (R1).
- **Robustness to confounders**: The paper does not discuss cases with confounding variables. Reviewer 1 asks whether the method can handle confounders and how it could be improved. Reviewer 2 also notes that the validity of the algorithm depends on P(X) not containing predictive information about the surrogate distribution; confounders could break this assumption.
- **Truncation procedure for noise level injection**: The paper uses a truncated distribution (mean 0.2, truncated at 0). If negative values are set to zero, the actual expected noise level is smaller than 0.2, but the paper does not explain how this is accounted for in experiments (R2).
- **Verification on real-world noisy datasets**: For CIFAR-10N (human-annotated label errors) and Clothing1M (web-crawl with text labels), the data generation process appears causal (X→Ỹ), not anticausal. How do the authors determine ground-truth direction for performance evaluation on such datasets? (R2).

Note: Reviewer 1 is more positive (score 8) than Reviewers 2 and 3 (both score 6), but the weaknesses listed above reflect concerns from all reviewers, with the most critical points raised by Reviewer 2.
