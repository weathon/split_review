## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a hybrid representation for novel view synthesis from sparse inputs, combining coordinate-based networks (for global context) with multi-plane encoding (for fine-grained details). It extends TensorRF/HexPlane by adding an MLP with residual connections and a curriculum training scheme that progressively weights the multi-plane features. Experiments on static and dynamic NeRF datasets (e.g., TensoRF, iNGP, FreeNeRF, HexPlane) show improved metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), especially for dynamic scenes with sparse inputs.

### Strengths

- The idea is simple, easy to understand, and the paper is well written with clear explanations (Figures 2 and 3 are visually pleasing and helpful). (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- Related work is well covered and a wide set of baseline methods are used. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- The hybrid design (coordinate network for global context, multi-plane for details) is reasonable from an optimization perspective. (Reviewer 2)
- Sufficient information and code are provided for reproducibility. (Reviewer 2, 4)
- Dense ablation studies are provided in the paper and supplementary materials. (Reviewer 4)
- Results are good and visually pleasing. (Reviewer 3)
- The authors propose effective techniques: residual connections to preserve properties, and a curriculum training scheme for coarse-to-fine learning. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty:** The method is a straightforward combination of existing designs (multi-plane encoding from HexPlane/TensorRF and coordinate-based MLP from original NeRF); the curriculum weighting scheme resembles a training trick. Few new technologies are proposed, and the contribution is mainly technical but still limited. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3) A minority reviewer (Reviewer 4) also notes the paper reads more like a technical report than a technical paper, lacking in-depth discussion or theoretical explanation for why the hybrid works.
- **Missing computational cost analysis:** No training time, model size, parameter count, FPS, or memory usage are reported. This is a major omission because a key contribution of TensorRF/iNGP is fast training (~10 min) and compact models (shallow MLPs). The added coordinate MLP inevitably increases cost, but the paper does not quantify this. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Insufficient discussion of related work:** The “NeRFs in the sparse inputs” section lacks concrete details on which limitations of prior works the proposed method resolves. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing experiments on more realistic data:** For dynamic scenes, the Plenoptic Video dataset (used in HexPlane) is not tested. (Reviewer 1)
- **Unclear figures and equations:** Figure 3 is confusing and inconsistent with Figure 4 (e.g., does the coarse rendering in Fig 3 correspond to an intermediate output? What do “MLP” and “Residual MLP” refer to in Fig 4?). Equation (3) has two conditions that appear overlapped. (Reviewer 2)
- **No quantitative ablation on the curriculum training scheme:** Specifically, the channel-wise weighting function for multi-plane features and the schedule are not ablated; sensitivity to these parameters is unknown. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of theoretical depth:** The key insight (coordinate features for global context, multi-plane for details) is well known from the NTK analysis of positional encoding (e.g., Fourier features enable coarse-to-fine learning). The paper does not engage this literature or provide any theoretical analysis. (Reviewer 4)
- **Questions about coordinate input:** The type of positional encoding used for the coordinate input is not stated. (Reviewer 4)
- **Visual results in supplementary video appear weak:** Improvements over HexPlane on dynamic scenes seem subtle; there are still strong ghost artifacts between frames, and the rendered images are noisy. The video would benefit from side-by-side comparisons. (Reviewers 3, 4)
