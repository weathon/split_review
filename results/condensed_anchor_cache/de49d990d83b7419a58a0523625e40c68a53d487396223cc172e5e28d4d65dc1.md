## Merged Review

### Summary

SV4D introduces a latent video diffusion model for dynamic 3D content generation from a monocular reference video. It unifies video generation and novel view synthesis into a single model using view-attention and frame-attention blocks to ensure spatial and temporal consistency. Generated multi-view videos are used to optimize a dynamic NeRF without SDS. The model is trained on a curated dynamic subset of Objaverse and evaluated on synthetic and real-world datasets. The work demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on novel-view video synthesis and 4D generation tasks.

### Strengths

*   **State-of-the-art performance:** The method achieves strong results on novel-view video synthesis and 4D generation across multiple datasets, with extensive comparisons against prior work (R1, R2, R4). Experiments are well-designed and validate effectiveness (R1).
*   **Unified design:** SV4D jointly handles multi-frame video generation and multi-view synthesis in a single diffusion model, avoiding separate models and ensuring consistency (R1, R4). The view-attention and frame-attention blocks are key design elements for dynamic consistency (R4).
*   **Clarity and organization:** The paper is well-written, clearly structured, and easy to follow (R1, R3).
*   **Valuable data curation and training efforts:** The authors curated a high-quality dynamic 3D object dataset from Objaverse and shared training experience, which benefits the community (R3). Data filtering and model training details have practical value (R3).
*   **Mixed-sampling strategy:** The proposed interleaved sampling of anchor frames and dense sampling of middle frames effectively extends spatial-temporal consistency to longer videos while avoiding memory constraints (R3, described as an interesting and useful approach even if a stopgap).

### Weaknesses

*   **Novelty concerns and similarity to prior work:** The design is closely aligned with EG4D (both use SVD and SV3D priors), raising questions about distinctiveness beyond combining existing components (R1). The use of dynamic NeRF instead of dynamic Gaussian Splatting is claimed to yield better 4D results, but no experimental evidence or metrics are provided to support this claim within the same setup (R1). One reviewer explicitly asks why dynamic NeRF was chosen over dynamic Gaussians (R2).
*   **Lack of ablation studies for core contributions:** No ablation is provided for the dual-attention mechanism (view attention vs. frame attention, multi-view conditioning) or training process (loss functions, optimization strategies) (R3). The only ablation focuses on the mixed-sampling strategy, which is a memory-mitigation technique rather than a core model design (R3). This makes it unclear how non-trivial the results are and what each component contributes (R3).
*   **Multi-view inconsistency in generated videos:** The network design places frame-attention after view-attention, and frame-attention is not multi-view aware. This may cause multi-view inconsistency and is suspected as a cause of blurry 4D reconstructions (R2). The blending of spatial and temporal attention outputs is not clearly illustrated (R2). The model's performance under significant occlusions or rapid viewpoint changes is not analyzed (R4).
*   **Input inconsistency conflict:** The model takes independently generated reference video and multi-view images of the first frame as input. These can conflict (e.g., video showing turning motion vs. multi-view images). No experiments address such conflicts (R2). This introduces a potential error source, especially with complex motion and occlusions (R2).
*   **Insufficient real-world evaluation:** The model is trained only on synthetic data, yet real-world generalization is claimed. Clear visualizations of real-world generated objects are lacking (R2). Evaluation on complex, high-detail objects (e.g., L4GM website examples) is missing (R4). The model's ability to handle objects with intricate textures, thin structures, or highly irregular shapes is not thoroughly assessed (R4).
*   **Supplementary video shortcomings:** Only 12 generated dynamic objects are shown, each rendered from a single fixed viewpoint. This is insufficient to demonstrate multi-view consistency or the diversity and quality of 4D content (R3).
*   **Long video handling limitations:** The claim of handling arbitrarily long videos is questionable. For very long input videos, anchor frames lose temporal coherence and become out-of-distribution for the proposed mixed-sampling scheme; appearance preservation may degrade (R2). This capability may be more theoretical than practically tested (R3).
*   **Inaccurate citations:** Some references in the text (e.g., Wang et al. 2024a, 4Diffusion) are not accurate and should be verified (R2).
*   **Missing dataset scale and details:** The size of the training dataset after filtering is not reported (R2, R3). Given low-quality animated objects in Objaverse, the number of used models and filtering criteria are important missing details (R2).
*   **Minority reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (score 5) is significantly more critical regarding novelty and substantiation of claims, while another (score 8) is overall positive but stresses the need for ablations. The two middle reviewers (scores 6) express moderate concerns about design, evaluation, and generalization.
