## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Kernel Banzhaf, a regression-based estimator for Banzhaf values inspired by KernelSHAP, using leverage score and paired sampling. Theoretical guarantees (Theorem 3.2, Theorem 3.3, Corollary 3.4) provide error bounds, and experiments on eight tabular datasets show improved sample efficiency and noise robustness over MC and MSR estimators. All reviewers acknowledge the algorithm’s simplicity, solid theoretical support, and that it addresses a gap in computing Banzhaf values for arbitrary set functions. However, reviewer enthusiasm varies: Reviewer 3 (score 8) finds no obvious weaknesses, while other reviewers (scores 5–6) raise concerns about novelty, practical impact, computational efficiency, and the lack of theoretical robustness proofs.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and organization**: The paper is well-structured, with main results presented concisely and heavy theory / additional experiments deferred to appendices.
- **Addresses a gap**: Few algorithms exist for computing Banzhaf values of arbitrary set functions; Kernel Banzhaf is a significant step forward.
- **Theoretical foundation**: Theorem 3.2 connects Banzhaf values to linear regression; Theorem 3.3 provides a standard leverage score sampling guarantee; Corollary 3.4 ensures near-optimal objective recovery. Complexity is reduced from $2^n$ to roughly $O(n \log n / \delta)$.
- **Empirical validation**: Experiments across eight datasets confirm superior sample efficiency and noise robustness compared to SOTA estimators (MC, MSR), with evaluation against true Banzhaf values (not just convergence).
- **Simplicity**: The method amounts to sampling subsets and solving a least squares problem, which is straightforward to implement.
- **Insightful discussion on efficiency axiom**: Appendix H argues the efficiency axiom is unnecessary for feature attribution, supporting the usefulness of Banzhaf values.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The core ideas (regression formulation, leverage score sampling) are directly adapted from Shapley value estimation (e.g., KernelSHAP, Musco & Witter) and generic regression. The shift from Shapley to Banzhaf, while technically different, does not represent a major conceptual leap.
- **Unclear practical impact**: The improvement in sample complexity is not demonstrated on realistically large datasets where MC/MSR fail. The paper should show that Kernel Banzhaf recovers feature rankings (overall and top-𝑘), a metric practitioners care about.
- **Missing computational efficiency analysis**: No systematic evaluation of runtime or memory costs; a proper complexity analysis or empirical time/memory benchmark is needed.
- **Lack of theoretical robustness guarantees**: Robustness is only shown empirically (Figure 3: ℓ₂-norm error under varying noise; Figure 4: Kernel Banzhaf’s error remains flat as noise increases). Prior work (Data Banzhaf, Wang & Jia 2023) provided theoretical proof via the Safety Margin; this paper should incorporate noise assumptions into its theoretical guarantees.
- **Narrow scope of applications**: Broader implications (e.g., data valuation, generative AI tasks) are not explored, despite the paper criticizing MSR for being inapplicable to generative AI.
- **Restricted experimental settings**: The “Noisy” setting is overly simple (noise variance unspecified); experiments should be expanded to larger or more diverse datasets (e.g., MNIST, FMNIST, CIFAR-10) and include structured noise (e.g., adversarial perturbations).
- **Unexplained performance gap in classification**: Reviewer 4 notes that MSR should yield comparable sample complexity under classification, yet Kernel Banzhaf outperforms it. Why? Is this general?
- **Unaddressed theoretical connections**: Reviewer 4 asks whether Wang & Jia’s robustness results can be extended to regression by normalizing responses. The paper does not discuss this.
- **Ambiguous claim**: The statement “our analysis is the best possible” should clarify whether it means “tight” or “the best possible estimator” for Banzhaf values.
- **Minor clarity issue**: Line 106 uses ε-approximation in ℓ∞-norm; it should be explicitly defined and checked for consistency with Data Banzhaf.
- **Potential for further variance reduction**: Reviewer 3 suggests mixing Kernel Banzhaf with MC and MSR estimators to lower variance; this is an unexplored question.
