## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes KANG, a graph neural network that integrates Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) by placing spline-based activation functions on edges. It introduces two interpretability mechanisms – feature influence and edge importance – that are derived from the spline weights and gradients. Experiments on Cora, PubMed, CiteSeer, MUTAG, and PROTEINS claim state-of-the-art results on node classification, link prediction, and graph classification. The reviews are polarized: one reviewer (score 5, confidence 4) rates the work as having medium originality, quality, clarity, and significance, and appreciates the interpretability focus. The other four reviewers (score 3, confidences 3–5) raise serious concerns about experimental validity (incorrect data splitting, unreproducible link prediction results, suboptimal baselines), limited novelty (incremental combination of KAN and GNN), insufficient interpretability evaluation (only one dataset and one explainer), and computational cost (actual training time ~10× slower than GCN).

### Strengths

- **Interpretability focus**: The model inherently provides interpretability via feature influence and edge importance, removing the need for post-hoc explainability techniques. Reviewer 3 notes that this distinguishes KANG from other KAN+GNN papers. Reviewer 4 finds this aspect well-explained and interesting. Reviewers 1 and 5 also highlight the value of inherent transparency.
- **Performance claims**: Several reviewers note that the reported results show superior performance on standard benchmarks (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5). However, Reviewer 4 and 5 contest experimental validity – the positive performance view is not unanimous.
- **Clarity and code**: Reviewer 2 finds the core idea easy to grasp and the writing clear. Reviewer 4 states the paper is clear and provides code for reproducibility.
- **Timeliness and context**: The paper introduces KANs to graph learning in a timely manner (Reviewer 3). It includes a brief discussion of other concurrent KAN-based GNNs, which provides helpful context (Reviewer 4).
- **Flexible modelling**: The spline-based activation functions offer increased flexibility for capturing complex nonlinear graph relationships (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses

1. **Experimental validity and reproducibility**  
   - **Node classification data splitting is incorrect**: models are validated on the test set and tested on the validation set – this invalidates the reported node classification scores (Reviewer 4).  
   - **Link prediction results not reproduced**: Reviewer 4 ran the provided code on Cora, PubMed, CiteSeer and obtained results that differ significantly from Table 1 (e.g., GAT 90.5 vs. KANG 87.9 on Cora, GCN 94.2 vs. KANG 85.2 on PubMed). The paper’s claim that KANG outperforms is contradicted by these runs.  
   - **Standard benchmark not used**: The node classification experiments do not employ widely adopted public splits (Reviewer 2).  
   - **GAT baseline under-tuned**: Only a single attention head is used; hyperparameters for the number of heads should be searched (e.g., {2,4,8}) (Reviewer 4).  
   - **Training time calculation flawed**: In the link predictor, average time per epoch is computed as `(end_time - start_time) / n_epochs`, which is invalid when early stopping is used (Reviewer 4).  
   - **Baselines are not recent/competitive**: Comparisons are limited to GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, GIN. More recent methods should be included (Reviewer 5 suggests rLap, UGNN, PANDA for graph classification; GRLC, SEK, UGT for node classification; Reviewer 1 asks for SOTA models on heterophilic datasets, e.g., [2],[3]).  

2. **Insufficient interpretability evaluation**  
   - Only demonstrated on the CORA dataset and only compared with GNNExplainer (Reviewer 3). More datasets and baselines (PGExplainer, EdgeSHAPer) are needed (Reviewers 1, 3).  
   - Claimed consistency with GNNExplainer is not fully convincing: Figure 2 and Figure 9 show mismatches in node importance ranks and values (Reviewer 3).

3. **Limited novelty**  
   - The approach largely borrows from the original KAN paper and applies KAN layers to GNNs without introducing fundamentally new mechanisms or theoretical advancements (Reviewers 1, 2, 5). Reviewer 1 calls it “incremental modification.”  

4. **Scalability and computational cost**  
   - **Scalability experiment is unfair**: The number of parameters is fixed across models, yet models like GCN can achieve optimal performance with fewer parameters (Reviewer 4).  
   - **Actual training time is ~10× slower** than GCN on Cora, PubMed, CiteSeer (NVIDIA RTX A5000), contradicting the claim of only “slightly” increased cost (Reviewer 4).  
   - Time and space complexity are not provided (Reviewer 5).  
   - The good scaling observed is surprising given the known difficulty of training KANs; more explanation is needed (Reviewer 3).

5. **Hyperparameter tuning cost**  
   - KANG introduces three additional hyperparameters (spline grid size, spline degree, aggregation function), making the hyperparameter search 126× slower than a standard GNN (Reviewer 4). This cost is not highlighted.

6. **Motivation and clarity**  
   - The motivation for combining KAN with GNNs is unclear; the paper does not adequately explain how KANG differs from or improves upon attention mechanisms like GAT or graph transformers (Reviewers 1, 5).  
   - The introduction is overly long and lacks clarity (Reviewer 5).  
   - Notations are inconsistent: `m_j^{(l)}` is defined but never used, `N(i)` is undefined, and the aggregation does not include the node’s own feature (Reviewers 1, 3).  
   - Figure 1 is unclear, especially the connection of node x6 to the two dots (Reviewer 3).  
   - Section 2.3.3 is difficult to understand (Reviewer 3).  
   - Several typos: “incluence” for “influence”, missing `b()` in Equation (2) (Reviewers 3, 4).  

7. **Missing analyses**  
   - Performance on heterophilic datasets is not evaluated (Reviewer 1).  
   - The synthetic dataset used in the scalability study (Appendix A.4) is not described (Reviewer 4).  
   - The effect of different aggregation functions (mean, sum, max) on the spline weights and the connection to the Kolmogorov–Arnold representation theorem (which holds only for sum) is not discussed (Reviewer 4).  
   - Model specifications (hidden dimension, total number of parameters) for the reported experiments are not provided (Reviewer 3).
