## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Score Entropy Discrete Diffusion (SEDD) for language modeling, introducing a novel training objective ("score entropy") that generalizes score matching to discrete spaces by estimating ratios of the data distribution. Building on concrete score matching (Meng et al. 2022) and continuous-time discrete diffusion (Campbell et al. 2022), the authors argue that the new loss addresses limitations like infinite KL divergence and provides desirable properties (non-negativity, reflexivity). They derive simplified variants (Q_uniform, Q_mask) for computational efficiency and present sampling strategies including controllable infilling. Experimentally, SEDD models trained on OpenWebText are reported to achieve perplexity competitive with GPT-2-small (within ~15%), reduce perplexity by 25-75% over prior discrete diffusion paradigms, and generate text with 6-8× better generative perplexity than un-annealed GPT-2, while enabling compute-quality tradeoffs. However, reviewers raise substantial concerns about incomplete theoretical proofs, lack of direct comparisons to existing discrete diffusion baselines (especially CSM and Plaid), unfinished experiments (models not converged, missing GPT-2 medium comparison, errors on 1B dataset), and insufficient ablation studies. There is disagreement on theoretical rigor and the strength of experimental evidence.

### Strengths
- **Problem and Motivation:** The paper addresses an important and timely problem—improving discrete diffusion for language modeling—and is well-motivated by limitations of prior work (e.g., infinite KL divergence in Concrete Score Matching). (R2, R4, R5)
- **Novel Loss Function:** The "score entropy" loss is a novel and intuitively reasonable generalization of score matching to discrete spaces, with desirable theoretical properties (non-negativity, reflexivity, scalability). Some reviewers find the theoretical analysis thorough and the derivations clear and enjoyable. (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5; see Weaknesses for disagreement on theoretical rigor)
- **Extensions of Prior Work:** The paper extends Concrete Score Matching (Meng et al. 2022) and the continuous-time discrete diffusion framework (Campbell et al. 2022) by providing an Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) and a denoising variant of the criterion. (R4)
- **Competitive Language Modeling Performance:** SEDD achieves perplexity competitive with GPT-2-small on standard datasets (e.g., reducing perplexity by 25-75% over prior discrete diffusion models) and outperforms GPT-2 in generative perplexity without temperature annealing (6-8× better). The sampling strategy is shown to be Pareto-optimal relative to fixed GPT-2-small when evaluating GPT-2-large perplexity vs. number of network evaluations. (R1, R2, R3, R5)
- **Compute-Quality Tradeoffs and Flexibility:** SEDD can trade compute for quality (e.g., similar quality with 32× fewer network evaluations) and enables controllable infilling and other generation strategies beyond left-to-right prompting. (R1, R5)
- **Clear Connections to Prior Work:** The paper effectively connects its contributions to previous diffusion model literature. (R5)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of Direct Comparison with Existing Discrete Diffusion Baselines:** The experiments do not include comparisons to Concrete Score Matching (CSM) or other discrete diffusion models (e.g., Plaid, earlier D3PM/LDMs), despite the paper claiming improvement over CSM. The GPT-2 comparisons may also be confounded by differences in dataset, training procedure, and evaluation splits. This makes it difficult to isolate the benefits of the proposed method from architectural choices or training details. (R1, R2, R4, R5)
- **Incomplete and Preliminary Experimental Results:** Multiple reviewers note that the reported results are preliminary: the SEDD-small model is "still improving," the SEDD-medium model "has not started converging," and the 1B-word dataset experiment encountered unexpected errors without elaboration. The lack of final, converged metrics raises concerns about the robustness of the claimed performance. (R2, R5; R4 also notes unfinished experiments)
- **Insufficient Ablation Studies:** There is no ablation study disentangling the contribution of the proposed score entropy loss from other design choices, such as the use of rotary embeddings (vs. learned positional embeddings in GPT-2), the simplified Q matrices, or the architecture. Without ablations, it is unclear whether observed gains come from the loss or from other implementation details. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Theoretical Rigor Disagreed Upon:** While Reviewer 3 finds the theoretical development "extremely well justified" and the derivations enjoyable, Reviewer 5 identifies major gaps: Proposition 3.2 is never proven; proofs for Proposition 3.3 and Theorem 3.4 are handwavey, ignore the weights w_{xy}, and are likely incorrect; Theorem 3.6 relies on a sketch that applies an incorrect trick; Theorem 4.2's proof is insufficient. The "Langevin corrector framework" claimed in the introduction does not appear in the paper. This disagreement represents a significant weakness for those unconvinced by the theory. (R3 vs. R5)
- **Missing Comparisons and Controlled Experiments:** The paper lacks a compute-matched comparison to GPT-2-medium (SEDD-medium is compared only to GPT-2-small) and does not control for training compute in comparisons. Plaid 1B (Gulrajani & Hashimoto, 2023) had previously shown strong non-autoregressive performance relative to GPT-2, undermining the claim of being the "first time" non-autoregressive modeling is competitive with autoregressive. (R2, R5)
- **Unclear Notations and Presentation Issues:** Several equations are unclearly specified: the meaning of 'z' in Eq. (7), the interpretation of Q_t(y,x) in Eq. (6) given Q_t is defined as a matrix in Eq. (5), and the use of indices in score functions is confusing. There is also a possible typo in Eq. (9): the last term should be (p(y)/p(x))(log(p(y)/p(x))-1) rather than (p(y)/p(x))log(p(y)/p(x)-1). Citation formatting is inconsistent (citep vs. citet). (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Lack of Quantitative Evaluation for Infilling:** Infilling results are shown only as qualitative examples (Table 2); no quantitative metrics are provided for this task, and the samples are reported as incoherent by one reviewer. (R1, R5)
- **Evaluation Metric Concerns:** Direct comparison of perplexity between autoregressive and diffusion models is questionable due to different definitions. Using GPT-2-large perplexity to evaluate sample quality can be misleading for low-quality samples. The number of network evaluations for GPT-2 in Fig. 2 is not specified, and it is unclear whether Fig. 2 uses SEDD-small or SEDD-medium. (R1, R4, R5)
- **Lack of Latency Analysis:** While SEDD offers compute-quality tradeoffs, autoregressive models can leverage KV-caching, likely making them significantly faster at inference for the same number of network evaluations. This comparison is not discussed. (R2)
- **Incomplete Motivation for the Denoising Scheme:** Section 4's denoising scheme is motivated for speedup, but no detailed experiments or ablation study support this choice. Some discrete diffusion models already sample within ~10 steps, raising questions about the claimed advantage. (R1)
- **Minor Issues:** Too many arXiv references (should cite peer-reviewed versions where available); no open-source code release mentioned; generated samples still somewhat incoherent overall. (R4, R5)
