## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Unsolvable Problem Detection (UPD) as a framework for evaluating LMMs’ ability to withhold answers when faced with unsolvable problems. UPD comprises three tasks: Absent Answer Detection (AAD), Incompatible Answer Set Detection (IASD), and Incompatible Visual Question Detection (IVQD). The authors construct the MM-UPD Bench, a benchmark of 2k questions derived from MMBench, and evaluate several state-of-the-art LMMs. Experiments show that even strong models struggle on UPD. The paper also explores chain-of-thought prompting, self-reflection, and instruction tuning as mitigation strategies.

Two reviewers gave a score of 6 (confidence 4 and 2) and one gave a 5 (confidence 4). The reviewer with score 5 is notably more critical regarding scope, novelty, and dataset diversity.

### Strengths

- The paper defines a novel and important challenge for LMMs: Unsolvable Problem Detection (UPD). The problem is well-motivated and addresses an aspect of trustworthiness overlooked by existing benchmarks.
- The definitions of the three UPD tasks (AAD, IASD, IVQD) and the evaluation criteria are clearly presented, with helpful visualizations of examples and results.
- The MM-UPD Bench provides a systematic evaluation covering diverse settings, testing both open-source and closed-source LMMs. The experiments are comprehensive and detailed, including analyses of where and how models fail (e.g., Section 5.2 offers valuable insights).
- The paper evaluates existing generic approaches to UPD and finds them ineffective, highlighting the challenge. It then proposes practical strategies (CoT, self-reflection, instruction tuning) that show promise.
- The analysis of different solution approaches and their limitations contributes to understanding the difficulty of UPD.

### Weaknesses

- **Narrow definition of UPD**: The framework is restricted to multiple-choice questions, ignoring open-ended multimodal QA scenarios that are prevalent in real-world applications. This limits assessment of a model’s ability to identify unsolvable problems in free-form contexts. One reviewer questions whether the definition differs meaningfully from existing definitions of unsolvable problems.
- **Benchmark diversity and construction**: The MM-UPD Bench relies solely on MMBench (2,095 questions). The exclusion of other datasets like MMMU and MathVista based on low standard accuracy is not fully justified; these could still reveal failure modes. A minority reviewer emphasizes that the limited dataset undermines comprehensiveness. Additionally, the dataset is constructed from existing data, yet no comparative analysis of data size, distribution, or comprehensiveness with other benchmarks is provided.
- **GPT-4 filtering bias and manual curation**: The strategy for removing image-agnostic questions uses text-only GPT-4, risking false exclusions (e.g., questions GPT-4 answers correctly by chance or training bias). 13% of questions were removed, making the reduction in manual effort marginal—raising the question of why not manually filter all questions for greater accuracy. The criteria for manual removals (eliminating ambiguous questions) are not clearly defined or standardized.
- **Skewed question-type distribution**: As shown in Table B (line 1042), the distribution of question types is inconsistent across AAD, IASD, and IVQD. For example, in AAD/IASD the top three types account for 30% of questions, while in IVQD they account for 44%, and six types have no representation in IVQD. This could skew performance evaluations and limit generalizability.
- **Dual Accuracy metric**: The primary metric couples general VQA ability with UPD ability, as a model must answer both standard and UPD questions correctly. A model might correctly refuse an absurd question (UPD) without understanding the standard version, penalizing models that are good at UPD but weaker on standard tasks. A more isolated measure (e.g., success rate conditioned on correct standard answers) would better isolate UPD capability.
- **Missing scenario: Incompatible Question Detection**: The paper does not consider cases where the image and answer set align but the question is incompatible with both (e.g., replacing the original question with an unrelated one). This seems aligned with the proposed challenge structure and should be addressed.
- **UPD-specific training evaluation**: Table 4 reports performance after UPD instruction tuning, but the *Orig* condition remains the same as in Table 1. Re-evaluating the *Orig* condition after fine-tuning would reveal unintended consequences of the training process on original capabilities.
- **Limited novelty of proposed solutions**: While chain-of-thought, self-reflection, and instruction tuning show promise, they are well-established techniques and their application here lacks significant methodological depth. More sophisticated approaches (e.g., adversarial training, advanced reasoning mechanisms) are needed. One reviewer notes the poor performance of existing models is unsurprising given the lack of specific UPD instruction datasets.
- **Error analysis scope**: The error analysis in Section 6.2 is insightful, but it is not extended to open-source MLLMs, missing a comparative opportunity.
- **Presentation**: The design of Table 3 and Table 4 is similar; it is unclear why they are not merged for cleaner comparison.
