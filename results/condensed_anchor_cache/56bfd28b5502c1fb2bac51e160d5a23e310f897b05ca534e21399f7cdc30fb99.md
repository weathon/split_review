## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Minimal Impact ControlNet (MIControlNet) to improve integration of multiple control signals in diffusion models. It addresses conflicts from “silent control signals” (low-frequency regions with no boundary information) via three strategies: (1) rebalancing data distribution using segmentation masks, (2) multi-objective feature injection (MGDA-inspired) to avoid dominance by one control, and (3) a conservativity loss that stabilizes the score function Jacobian matrix asymmetry. A two-stage training process is used. Experiments with multiple control pairs achieve better FID and visual quality, especially in silent regions. The paper also provides theoretical analysis of the conservativity loss.

### Strengths
- Problem analysis is clear and systematic, identifying three key challenges: data bias in silent regions, optimal feature combination ratios, and conditional score function stability.
- The proposed solutions are theoretically grounded, including a proof that the gradient of the conservativity loss equals that of the estimated conservativity loss, with a simplified optimization form.
- Qualitative and quantitative results are comprehensive and convincing, covering various control combinations (e.g., canny+openpose, depth+hed) and including FID comparisons on multiple datasets.
- The method demonstrably improves image quality in silent control areas, adding high-frequency texture where standard ControlNet produces blur.
- MIControlNet achieves the best FID in most multi-control experiments, and the qualitative comparisons (single and multi-condition) show clear improvements.
- Limitations are discussed in the appendix.

### Weaknesses
- Ablation studies are missing: the contributions of each component (data rebalancing, MGDA feature injection, conservativity loss) are not isolated. The only component-wise comparison is between original and balanced feature combination. No ablation shows the impact of Jacobian asymmetry reduction against baselines (e.g., in Figure 4(b), no ControlNet baseline is shown).
- The paper does not test scalability beyond two ControlNets; experiments focus on pairs. The claim that the framework supports more than two ControlNets is not demonstrated.
- The notation in Section 2.2 (e.g., symbols for layers and equations) is confusing and hard to read. For example, line 121: \( f^e_c \) seems to have an unclear subscript (does \(c\) mean layer or channel?). Equations (8) and (9): \(v_1, v_2\) are not defined explicitly, and since \(\lambda_i\) varies across layers, the notation should reflect that.
- The term “conservativity” is not standard and is not clearly defined; further clarification is needed (Reviewer 4 found it confusing).
- The abstract phrase “silent control signals can suppress the generation of textures in related areas” is ambiguous: does “related areas” refer to spatially close regions, texturally similar ones, or something else? This obscures the exact mechanism of suppression.
- The connection between the three identified problems (data bias, optimal combination ratios, conservativity) and the experiments is not directly evident. The main text does not sufficiently emphasize how each problem is resolved by the proposed method or show explicit evidence (e.g., a table of combination coefficients before/after rebalancing).
- The conservativity loss requires second-order derivatives, which is computationally expensive. The paper does not discuss runtime cost or efficiency trade-offs.
- Several required citations are missing, e.g., for the definition of Pareto optimality in multi-objective optimization (lines 164–165).
- The paper lacks a prompt-dependent analysis. Including detailed prompts could influence texture generation; comparing total variance and visual results under detailed, brief, and null text conditions would strengthen the argument that the method is robust to text influence.
- In Figure 1, why does the openpose signal suppress the canny signal (and not the reverse)? The paper does not analyze the direction or cause of the suppression asymmetry.
- Figure 6’s legend is missing information (e.g., line styles or marker descriptions are not labeled).
- The method’s novelty is questioned: segmentation masking and MGDA-based feature injection are straightforward applications of existing techniques; the novelty of the framework may be limited.
- The paper’s organization is difficult to follow; the main text should more clearly show how the method balances coefficients among multiple control signals.
- Reviewers raised several specific questions: (a) Can the approach be extended by separating foreground/background for guided generation? (b) Does the conservativity principle also benefit single-ControlNet applications? (c) How do the two training stages compare in impact? (Figure 4(b) suggests Stage-1 matters more; the authors should discuss this balance.) (d) Is the silence phenomenon only due to low-frequency regions, or could it also occur in other cases?
