## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes non-targeted white-box adversarial attacks on Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by maximizing information entropy (MIE). The method uses PGD optimization to maximize entropy across three objectives: predicted token entropy (Eq 2), attention weights (Eq 3), and normalized hidden layer values (Eq 4), combined with weights λ1=0.8, λ2=0.1, λ3=0.1. Evaluation is on ImageNet, aiming to induce misleading image descriptions. The approach is illustrated in Figure 1, with Algorithm 1 showing the procedure, and examples in Appendix A.

### Strengths
- The topic of VLM robustness is relevant and important; adversarial robustness of large VLMs has received less attention than that of vision-only models, and this exploration is a valuable step (Reviewers 2, 4).
- The paper is well-written and easy to understand (Reviewers 3, 4). Reviewer 4 notes they enjoyed reading it.
- The specific algorithm for untargeted attacks using entropy maximization is novel and differs from prior targeted approaches on VLMs. Reviewer 2 (score 6, the most positive reviewer) states that to their knowledge, using maximum entropy for adversarial attack is novel. Reviewer 4 calls this the first untargeted attack against large VLMs.
- Experimental results show the attack is effective on several open-source VLMs (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficiently articulated contribution over prior work.** Prior work (Carlini 2023, cited in the paper) also generates adversarial perturbations causing completely incorrect image descriptions (e.g., the Mona Lisa example on page 20 of Carlini 2023), but the paper does not clearly state what the precise contribution is over that work or similar approaches. The claimed main difference—untargeted vs. targeted attacks—should have been highlighted in the Introduction. Even accepting that premise, it is unclear (a) why an untargeted attack is important given targeted attacks already exist, and (b) why an untargeted attack is not a special case of a targeted attack where a random target caption is chosen from the desired domain, which would turn it into a targeted attack solvable by prior methods. There is precedent in adversarial ML (e.g., OPT blackbox attacks wraps code around a targeted subroutine to achieve untargeted attacks). An approach such as feeding a random maximum-entropy image to get a random phrase and then using a targeted attack to force that text is not compared. For example, could “a pair of flip flops sitting on a pile of garbage” in Appendix A.1 first row be set as the target caption in Carlini 2023? (Reviewer 1)

- **Lack of comparison to existing VLM attack methods.** The paper only compares against simple Gaussian noise and does not empirically compare against prior VLM attack methods such as [1] and [2] (e.g., Zhao et al., cited in Related Work). The paper’s justification for excluding [2]—that it uses the original caption—is weak, because the clean image caption can serve as the ground-truth caption, making the comparison feasible. (Reviewers 2, 3)

- **Missing ablation studies.** The final method combines three entropy objectives with weights (0.8, 0.1, 0.1), but no ablation shows how each component (Eq 2, 3, or 4) performs individually versus the weighted combination. An ablation study is needed to demonstrate the contribution of each term and the impact of different coefficient settings. (Reviewers 1, 3)

- **Ad-hoc design choice with insufficient rationale.** There are many ways to disrupt network representations (e.g., maximizing the norm of mid-layer features), but the paper does not clarify why maximizing entropy specifically is chosen for the attack. The rationale behind this design choice is not explained. (Reviewer 3)

- **Limited evaluation depth.** The evaluation is basic: only one attack strength is tested, robustness against simple defenses (e.g., robust training) is not examined, and factors influencing attack performance are not analyzed. The current state is described as a simple proof of concept lacking depth. A comprehensive study with multiple parameter settings and analysis of which MIE method is better (and their strengths/weaknesses) is needed. (Reviewer 4)

- **Hyperparameter concern.** The hyperparameters λ1, λ2, λ3 may be selected based on the same validation set used for evaluation. If so, this could lack statistical integrity. (Reviewer 2)

- **Unclear definition of attack success.** As the attack maximizes entropy, the generated captions may become random sequences of words with no semantic coherence. The figures in Appendix A should illustrate this. If captions become random, is the attack considered successful, or should the caption still make general sense to a human? (Reviewer 1)

- **Typographical error.** Below Equation (5), the second instance of λ1 should be λ3. (Reviewer 3)
