## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a framework for building approximately equivariant neural networks for non-compact, non-abelian Lie groups with non-surjective exponential maps, focusing on \(G = \mathrm{GL}^+(n,\mathbb{R})\) and \(G = \mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})\) and their affine extensions \(\mathbb{R}^n \rtimes G\). The key idea is to decompose these groups into subgroups and submanifolds (via semi-direct products) where the Haar measure factorizes and sampling is tractable. Monte Carlo integration with few samples (e.g., 10 for the affine group) is used to approximate the group convolution integral, leveraging random matrix theory for efficient sampling. Experiments on affine-transformed MNIST and homography-transformed images show improvements over prior methods (e.g., MacDonald et al. 2022), including better sample efficiency.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and generality**: The group decomposition approach is novel in the machine learning context (one reviewer calls it “elegant” and “clearly an improvement”); it applies to important non-compact groups (\(\mathrm{GL}^+, \mathrm{SL}\)) that previous Lie algebra methods cannot handle due to non-surjective exponential maps.
- **Mathematical rigor**: The paper is mathematically well-grounded, provides extensive derivations of Haar measures and integration measures, and includes detailed appendices that serve as a valuable resource (noted by multiple reviewers).
- **Sample efficiency**: The Monte Carlo scheme works well with very few samples (e.g., 10 vs. 100 for MacDonald et al.), offering an order-of-magnitude improvement and alleviating memory issues of previous methods.
- **Experimental performance**: Benchmark results on affine and homographic classification tasks compare favorably to strong baselines, demonstrating the practical utility of the method.
- **Clarity of contributions**: The paper is clearly written, with the contributions (handling non-surjective exponential maps, factorizing Haar measure, efficient Monte Carlo sampling) well stated.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing equivariance error analysis**: The paper lacks a systematic analysis of the numerical stability of the Monte Carlo approximation. Multiple reviewers request a curve of equivariance/invariance error as a function of the number of samples, and a comparison to exactly equivariant methods (e.g., those using linear operators, like [1] and [2]). One reviewer asks for a formal bound on the error in terms of sample count, group dimensions, and representation size.
- **Unclear advantage over MacDonald et al. 2022**: The paper does not sufficiently clarify why it outperforms MacDonald et al. (both handle non-surjective exponential maps). The explanation of sample efficiency (10 vs. 100 samples) is ambiguous – is it 10 samples from the full group or 10 from each subgroup? The mechanism for this efficiency gain is not explained. One reviewer notes that experiments should include tests with larger scale changes or n=3 to demonstrate the method’s regime of superiority.
- **Missing related work comparisons**: Important papers on Lie group equivariance are omitted: Finzi et al. 2021 (EMLP, exact equivariance), Bogatskiy et al. 2020 (Lorentz group), Clebsch-Gordon Nets (Kondor et al. 2018), and Lie algebra methods (Dehmamy et al. 2021). The paper should discuss why these are not suitable or why the proposed decomposition is preferable, especially for the case of non-surjective exponential maps. One reviewer also notes that a known AAAI paper (2020) achieved 98.1% on affNIST with 174K parameters, which the current paper does not cite or compare against.
- **Approximate equivariance not highlighted**: The method is only approximately equivariant due to Monte Carlo sampling. This should be stated clearly in the introduction. One reviewer asks for computational tests to quantify the equivariance error.
- **Restricted input/output representations**: The framework appears to only handle the regular and trivial representations of \(G\) or homogeneous spaces, unlike methods that work with any finite-dimensional representation (e.g., Finzi et al., Bogatskiy et al.). One reviewer also questions whether the construction can be extended to other representations, and whether it is universal (i.e., can represent any continuous equivariant function).
- **Mathematical denseness and presentation issues**: Several reviewers find the paper too dense, with excessive re-derivation of standard Lie theory and Haar measure properties, impeding readability. They recommend moving background material to the appendix and streamlining the layer definition derivation. Notation is sometimes confusing: e.g., \(G\) is used for both the affine group and the subgroup \(\mathrm{GL}^+\) in different sections. The paper incorrectly states that \(\mathrm{SO}(n)\) is abelian (true only for \(n=2\)). A list of typos (e.g., “ecompasses”, “approxiamtion”) was provided.
- **Insufficient motivation and limited datasets**: The application of Lie groups with non-surjective exponential maps is not well-motivated; the datasets are synthetic (affine MNIST, homography MNIST). One reviewer asks whether such equivariance arises in practice. Another suggests using SmallNorb for more realistic evaluation.
- **Questions about decomposition assumptions**:
  - For the Haar measure to factorize as \(d\mu_G = d\mu_P d\mu_K\), does \(G=PK\) require a semidirect product or direct product? The paper uses several different decompositions without clarifying the requirements.
  - In the \(\mathrm{SL}(n) \to \mathrm{SPos} \times \mathrm{SO}(n)\) decomposition, is one factor a normal subgroup?
  - Even when the exponential map is not surjective, the product of multiple exponentials can generate any group element – is the decomposition really necessary? Could multi-layer Gconv using \(\exp\) suffice?
- **Sampling domain justification**: Sampling the translation factor from \([-1,1]^n\) (a compact subset) for the integral over \(\mathbb{R}^n\) is not justified. One reviewer asks how this is valid and what error it introduces, especially in deeper layers.
- **Other theoretical concerns**:
  - Non-compact groups have infinite Haar measure – how are the integrals (e.g., eq. 13) well-defined without additional decay assumptions?
  - Does the Lie bracket appear in the construction?
  - Can the theory be applied to a general subgroup \(H \subset \mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{R})\) beyond \(\mathrm{GL}^+\) and \(\mathrm{SL}\)?
- **Minority view (Reviewer 5)**: The paper is rated low (5) due to mathematical denseness, unclear notation, and doubts about the necessity of the decomposition (local diffeomorphism might suffice). This reviewer also finds the experimental verification preliminary. In contrast, other reviewers (1,2,6) give high scores (8) and praise the rigor and sample efficiency. This contrast should be noted: while most reviewers find the contributions solid, one reviewer is significantly more critical about the theoretical framing and practical scope.
