## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PsiDiff, a conditional diffusion model for ligand conformation generation that incorporates both ligand-target interactions and chemical properties. It uses a Target-Ligand Pairwise Graph Encoder (TLPE) and graph prompt learning to implicitly model pairwise interactions between ligand and target protein. The authors claim an 18% improvement in Aligned RMSD over GeoDiff and TankBind on PDBBind2020.

### Strengths
- Novel approach for embedding chemical properties and conditioning information into the diffusion model (Reviewer 1).
- Methodologically interesting design for constructing graph prompt tokens with two distinct insertion patterns (Reviewer 1).
- Seemingly novel way of infusing protein embeddings into ligand embeddings for conformational generation (Reviewer 2).
- Claims improved Aligned RMSD to crystal ligand structures over GeoDiff and TankBind on PDBBind2020 (Reviewer 2).
- Conformation generation is an important task for drug discovery (Reviewer 3).
- Authors provide theoretical details to ensure rotational-translational equivariance/invariance (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and contribution**: The claim that existing methods neglect ligand-target interactions is overstated. The task strongly resembles docking, where pocket information is standard (Reviewer 1). Target information has already been considered in methods like TargetDiff (Reviewer 3). The transition from singleton to pairwise modeling is not as distinctive as claimed (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**: Several recent competitive machine learning methods are omitted, including UniMol, Torsional Diffusion, and DiffDock, the current state-of-the-art in blind docking (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2). For blind docking on PDBBind2020, DiffDock’s multiconformational capabilities should be compared; the paper criticizes models for predicting single binding poses but ignores DiffDock’s ability to sample multiple poses during reverse diffusion (Reviewer 2). Comparison should include success rate at specific RMSD cutoffs (Reviewer 2). A more thorough comparison to DiffDock and other SOTA methods is needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Results credibility**: PsiDiff’s performance heavily depends on force field optimization. Without it, PsiDiff is not competitive, especially in docking tasks (Table 5, 25th percentile) where it lags behind GNINA, GLIDE, and EquiBind/TANKBind. To enable fair comparison, results for other baselines after force field optimization should be presented (Reviewer 1). The metric (aligned RMSD to crystal ligand structures in PDBBind2020) is essentially blind docking, making comparisons without DiffDock unconvincing (Reviewer 2). The claimed 18% improvement is over limited baselines (Reviewer 2).
- **Presentation clarity – Graph Prompt concept**: The relationship of “Graph Prompt” to “prompts” in NLP is not clearly defined or motivated (Reviewer 3). The paper does not explain whether “graph prompt” and “prompt graph” are the same thing (Reviewer 3). The construction of the prompt graph is unclear: how node features are initialized (only stating “number of tokens equals number of down-sampled target graph nodes”) and how the edge set S is built (Reviewer 3). The explanation of how the prompt graph is built and used is hard to follow (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation clarity – Concatenation operation**: In line 212, \( Z = \text{Concat}(F_L, P) \) lacks specification of tensor shapes. It is unclear how ligand features \( F_L \) and prompt graph \( P \) can be concatenated when they may have different numbers of nodes and feature dimensions (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 2 also notes lack of tensor shape information makes implementation unclear).
- **Presentation clarity – Insertion patterns**: Two insertion patterns are used simultaneously without justification. The rationale for using both patterns together and how they complement each other is not explained (Reviewer 3).
- **Related work inadequacies**: The paper criticizes RDKit initialization in DiffDock without explaining why it is problematic (e.g., RDKit may fail to generate chemically valid structures for complex ligands). Simply stating it is problematic is insufficient (Reviewer 2). The last sentence regarding using target information for molecular generation is unclear – does it mean generating novel molecules or generating poses of known ligands? (Reviewer 2). The related work section lacks sufficient detail to contextualize the unique contributions of PsiDiff (Reviewer 2).
- **Reviewer disagreement on presentation**: While most reviewers note clarity issues, Reviewer 3 rates presentation as 1 (poor), finding the central parts of the submission hard to follow. This is a more severe criticism compared to Reviewer 1’s “good” (3) and Reviewer 2’s “fair” (2). The paper does not explain how dMaSIF embeddings were chosen over other possible protein embeddings (Reviewer 2, question not fully answered).
