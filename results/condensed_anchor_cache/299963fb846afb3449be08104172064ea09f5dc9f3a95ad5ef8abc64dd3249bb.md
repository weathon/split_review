## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Sage Deer, a multi-modal, multi-view framework for intelligent driving cockpits designed to provide personalized, context-aware assistance. It integrates RGB, NIR, and depth cameras along with driver state data (physiology, emotion, behavior, body/hand movements, driving decisions). The architecture uses Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), multi-modal fusion, and expert knowledge fusion to tailor responses to individual preferences without extensive fine-tuning. The authors collected multiple datasets and built a large-scale benchmark (AIDE, DMD, etc.) to evaluate perceptual decision-making and super-alignment accuracy. Experimental results demonstrate multitasking effectiveness and adaptability, positioning Sage Deer as a foundation for future ADAS.

### Strengths
- Clear and compelling motivation to combine 3D scene perception with both visual and physiological human signals (R4) and to emphasize individual preferences for enhanced driving experience (R6).
- First unified dataset for MLLMs in intelligent driving cockpit, incorporating multi-modal, multi-view sensor data with QA pairs (R1, R2, R4). Large-scale benchmark using datasets like AIDE and DMD adds rigor (R5).
- Seamless integration of RGB, NIR, and depth cameras from multiple perspectives, enabling comprehensive environmental understanding (R1, R3, R5). Omni-modal processing captures driver physiological, emotional, and behavioral states (R1).
- Tailored application of RAG for the driving domain provides flexible, adaptive responses to evolving user needs without extensive model fine-tuning (R1, R3, R5, R6). Combined with expert knowledge fusion (R6).
- Proposed method shows improved overall performance on the provided dataset (R4).
- The concept of a super-aligned, generalist agent offers a fresh approach, adapting in real-time to individual user preferences (R5).
- Robust, real-time capabilities show substantial practical potential for ADAS (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns:** The approach is essentially a multimodal LLM with RAG, lacking specific design tailored for the target task (R4). Pre-existing approaches like RAG are not novel enough for ICLR (R1, R6). The contribution is not sufficient for a top conference (R1).
- **Missing architecture details:** Choice of LLM, its size, and other specifics are not provided (R3). Figure 3 shows a “Pre-trained Video Encoder” while Section 4.2 says “ImageNet pre-trained ResNet18”; it is unclear if these are the same component and how the encoder handles other modalities; also output token count is missing (R3). Special tokens (<RGB_bos>, etc.) are introduced, but it is not explained whether they are added to the LLM vocabulary, how they are initialized, or how a frozen LLM recognizes them (R3). Training strategies, specific model architectures, and hyperparameters are missing (R4).
- **Missing ablation studies:** No ablation of individual modalities (visual, physiological, behavioral) to verify their effectiveness (R1, R4, R6). No ablation of fusion techniques (attention-based, cross-attention vs. concatenation) (R1). No comparison of ResNet18 with advanced feature extractors (e.g., ResNet50, EfficientNet, ViT, Swin Transformer) (R1, R6). No ablation comparing language-only input to multi-modal input for safety-critical tasks (R1). No ablation to verify the role of RAG (R1). No ablation for Expert Knowledge Fusion (R6). No temporal dependency analysis for tasks like fatigue tracking (R1).
- **Physiological tokenization issues:** The approach assumes direct correlations between physiological signals (heart rate, blood oxygen) and emotions, but individual baselines, environmental conditions, and physical activity can affect signals and cause inaccuracies; no discussion of these factors (R1). The model may miss essential non-verbal cues for real-time safety (R1).
- **Dataset and evaluation concerns:** Dataset construction lacks details on data curation and GPT-4 labeling; the paper repeatedly refers to supplementary materials, but none were submitted (R2). Captions are generated by open-source VLMs – quality measures are not clarified (R4). No ethics statement for the human-subject dataset (R4). The “intelligent driving cockpit super alignment evaluation protocol” is not well-established (R2). Real-world dataset construction rarely captures abnormal behaviors; training on the proposed dataset may not support effective anomaly detection (R4 question).
- **Limited experimental analysis:** Qualitative results are limited to Figure 4, which does not show the full ability of the model (R2). In-depth experimentation is lacking to substantiate claimed benefits of the multimodal approach (R4). Comparison with other recent intelligent driving agents (e.g., DriveGPT, DriveLM) is missing (R6). No analysis of how inputs are integrated to influence real-time driving decisions or limitations in complex scenarios (R5). User interaction management and interpretation of nuanced/less common behaviors are unclear (R5). The choice of maximum sentence length 64 is not justified; no experiments on its impact on performance/computation (R3).
- **Writing and presentation errors:** Numerous typos, incomplete sentences, and formatting errors throughout the paper (R1,R2,R3,R4,R5,R6). Figure 2 has duplicated “Out View Caption” and spelling error “Accurate labele”. Table/figure ordering inconsistent (Table 2 mentioned after Tables 3 and 4) (R3). Appendix section is empty with placeholder text (R4). Citations insufficiently supported (e.g., James-Lange reference without context) (R4). Expert Knowledge Fusion section lacks clarity; pseudocode or flowchart would help (R6).
