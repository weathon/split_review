## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Variable Forward regularization (-kF), which generalizes forward regularization (-F) by introducing a variable \(k\) to modulate the hallucination term, interpolating between -F and ridge regularization (-R). It provides regret bounds for -kF and introduces an adaptive version -kF-Bayes. Experiments in continual learning show performance gains. Reviewers note the idea is simple and interesting, but the paper suffers from serious writing and presentation issues, unclear theoretical statements, and potential technical flaws in the main theorem. One reviewer found no strengths.

### Strengths
- The idea of interpolating between -R and -F via a variable \(k\) is simple and potentially valuable; it has not been proposed before (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The regret bound analysis shows improvement over -F in adversarial settings (Reviewer 1).
- Experimental results on continual learning demonstrate that -kF and -kF-Bayes perform well, with less forgetting and non-replay (Reviewers 1, 4). (Reviewer 1 disclaimed limited CL expertise.)
- Comprehensive experiments contributed to class-incremental continual learning (Reviewer 4).

However, Reviewer 2 found no strengths.

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and presentation**: The paper requires significant revision. There are numerous grammatical errors, undefined notations, and inconsistent mathematical formatting (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). Specific issues:
  - Lemma/Theorem environments are misused: simple definitions (Lemmas 1, 3) are presented as lemmas; algorithms and definitions are not properly labeled (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
  - Notation inconsistencies: 'In' is used instead of '\ln' for natural log (Reviewers 1, 3, 4); matrices are in lowercase; variables like \(\theta^r_t\), \(d\) in Eq. (4) are not defined (Reviewer 4); Eq. (21) uses \(k_{t+1}=k_t\) without explanation; Eq. (22) introduces \(\kappa\) without definition; Eq. (30) has \(\theta \in \theta_{t+1}\) (Reviewer 1).
  - The continual learning setting is mentioned in the abstract but never properly defined or referenced later; the paper appears to use standard online linear regression (Reviewer 3).
  - The term "relative regret" is used without clarification (Reviewer 3).
  - Plots are unclear: caption says "relative regret" but y-axis says "immediate regret"; plots vary only \(\lambda\) and do not compare different values of \(k\), which is the key hyperparameter (Reviewers 1, 3).
  - The paper lacks self-containedness; a preliminary section on -F and -R with precise expressions is missing (Reviewer 1).
- **Technical flaws**:
  - **Theorem 5 appears incorrect**: In the limit \(k \to 0\), the regret bound becomes at best 0 if \(X_m^2 < \lambda\), or possibly negative, which is impossible. The constant may need to be \(k+1\) instead of \(k\). This issue is severe and suggests the theorem statement is flawed (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 2 also notes that Remarks 3 and 4 do not make sense or are circular.
  - **Missing theoretical analysis for -kF-Bayes**: No regret bound is provided for the adaptive version, which undermines the theoretical completeness (Reviewer 1).
  - **Equations (17)–(19) are unclear**: The argument that \(k \in (0,1)\) yields a better bound is not properly explained; the Gaussian assumption on noise/features seems irrelevant (Reviewer 3).
  - **Bayesian update for \(k\) is unmotivated**: No rationale or theoretical justification is given for the update rule (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing related work and comparisons**:
  - Relevant papers on forward regularization (e.g., [1] analyzing high-probability regret of -R and -F) and nonstationary online learning (e.g., [2] on discounted VAW) are not cited (Reviewer 1).
  - The claim that -F fails in practice is based solely on the authors' CL experiments, which may be misleading since prior work shows -F can outperform -R in many scenarios (Reviewer 1, question).
- **Novelty and contribution**: The idea of introducing a variable \(k\) is a straightforward extension of -F, and the regret bound order remains \(O(d \log T)\), not improving the asymptotic order (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 2 found no strength.
- **Other missing details**:
  - No ablation varying \(k\) in experiments; only \(\lambda\) is varied (Reviewer 3).
  - The choice of \(k\) is not explained theoretically; Remark 4 does not provide guidance (Reviewer 4).
  - The transformation from an unconstrained to a constrained problem in Appendix A.2 is not justified (Reviewer 1).
- **Minor issues**:
  - The use of Bregman divergence overcomplicates the exposition; rewriting in terms of Mahalanobis distances would improve readability (Reviewer 1).
  - The regret definitions differ for -R and -F without explanation (Reviewer 1, question).
  - The paper assumes \(\infty\)-norm bounds on the decision space rather than the tighter \(l_2\)-norm bounds (Reviewer 1, question).
