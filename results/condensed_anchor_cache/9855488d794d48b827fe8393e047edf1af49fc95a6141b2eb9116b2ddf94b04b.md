- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 5, 6, 8

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DeepSPF, a learnable backbone for SO(3)-equivariant point cloud processing based on Spherical Patch Fields (SPF) and the Patch Gaussian (PG) Layer. SPF uses spherical Gaussian representations to produce patch-wise equivariant features that combine local and global spatial neighborhoods via resizable patches. The method is evaluated on Scan-to-CAD tasks: point cloud registration, retrieval, and completion. The reviews agree on the core contribution but disagree strongly on clarity and depth of validation.

### Strengths
- Tackles the challenging goal of learning an adaptive spatial receptive field for equivariant features (R1, R3).
- The idea of representing patches with spherical Gaussians and the PG layer is novel and interesting (R2).
- Demonstrates effectiveness across three diverse Scan-to-CAD tasks with clear quantitative improvements over baselines, including up to 17% Top‑1 error reduction in retrieval and 30% Chamfer Distance reduction in completion (R3; R1 also acknowledges the multitask evaluation).
- **Divergence on presentation quality:** Reviewer 3 found the illustrations neat and facilitating understanding, while Reviewer 1 found Figure 1 unclear and difficult to connect with the caption, and Reviewer 2 noted ambiguity in the same figure (red arrow, missing arrow in SPF).
- **Divergence on writing quality:** Reviewer 2 rated presentation as “good” and found the paper easy to read; Reviewer 1 rated presentation as “poor” and said the paper lacks self-containment.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing recent related‑work comparisons:**  
  - In Section 4.4, the rotation‑invariant method “Rotation‑invariant transformer for point cloud matching” should be compared; its omission is a significant oversight given the paper’s claim of rotation invariance.  
  - In Section 4.6, the state‑of‑the‑art completion method “Diverse point cloud completion with Geometry‑Aware Transformers” should be compared. (R2)
- **No per‑class failure analysis:** Table 3 shows weak performance on some object categories, but the authors provide no analysis or discussion of why the method struggles on those classes (R2).
- **Missing equivariance analysis in the main text:** The crucial discussion of how SO(3)‑equivariance is achieved is placed in the Appendix, not in Section 3 (method). Reviewer 3 considers this a core missing part given the paper’s title and abstract. Reviewer 1 similarly notes that no proposition or theorem supporting the equivariance claim is provided in the main text.
- **Unclear presentation of the method:**  
  - Figure 1 is ambiguous: the red arrow is unclear and the lower‑right circle in the SPF part lacks an arrow (R1, R2).  
  - Many equations are insufficiently defined. E.g., what is \(z\) in eq. (3)? In eq. (1) \(p \in \mathbb{R}^3\) but in eq. (2) \(p \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 3}\); the role of parameter \(\nu\) is unclear. Eq. (4) may have a mismatched index (\(l\) vs. \(i\)). (R1, R2, R3)  
  - The introductory claim that “conventional SO(3)‑equivariant methods do not sufficiently investigate the correlation between global and local structures” is not clearly explained: what does “separately” mean and how is this different from translation‑equivariant pooling? (R1)  
  - Section 3 heavily relies on prior work (Vogel 1979, Wang et al. 2018, Salihu & Steinbach 2023) without enough detail; these components should be summarized in the Appendix (R3).  
  - A typo in Section 5: “resizeable” → “resizable” (R2). Missing punctuation between equations (e.g., eq. 9 and 10) and eq. 12 being part of eq. 11 (R3).
- **Missing or incomplete analyses (reviewer questions that overlap with criticism/ablation requests):**  
  - Is the method also equivariant to reflections (i.e., does it cover O(3))? (R3)  
  - Can it generalize to dimensions higher than 3? (R3)  
  - No sensitivity analysis with respect to the number of spheres \(|\nu|\) (R3).  
  - The learnable parameters of SPF are not summarized; the total number of parameters of DeepSPF models and how they compare to baselines is not reported (R3).  
  - Computational complexity and runtime comparisons with baselines are missing; the brief mention in the conclusion is insufficient (R3).
- **Low reviewer confidence:** Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 2 each expressed low confidence (2/4, i.e., “likely did not understand central parts”), while Reviewer 3 was confident (4/4). This disagreement underscores the clarity problems identified above.
