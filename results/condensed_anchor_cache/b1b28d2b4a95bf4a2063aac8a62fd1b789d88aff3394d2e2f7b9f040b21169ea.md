## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper tackles partially observable video prediction where the camera is mounted on a moving platform, so video dynamics are influenced by both scene motion and camera motion. The authors propose three models built on existing frameworks: VG‑LeAP (extends SVG‑lp with a joint image–action latent state), Causal‑LeAP (learns separate image and action latents with a causal dependency), and RAFI (integrates the image–action state into the flow‑matching model RIVER). All models are evaluated on the RoAM dataset (robot camera moving through indoor environments), which contains 45 training videos and 5 test sequences, each with 300k subsequences of 25 frames at 64×64×4 resolution. The task is to generate 10 future frames conditioned on 5 previous frames. Quantitative metrics include FVD, LPIPS, VGG‑16 perceptual distance, and action prediction error.

### Strengths
- The problem of partially observable video prediction with a moving camera is interesting and relevant for autonomous vehicles, drones, and robot manipulators (Reviewers 1 and 4).
- Incorporating robot actions (as a learned latent) into video prediction is a straightforward but useful idea, especially for world models in embodied agents (Reviewers 1 and 3).
- The paper adapts action‑free VAE (SVG‑lp) and flow‑matching (RIVER) formulations to action‑conditioned learning, showing empirical benefit of including action information for video prediction accuracy (Reviewer 2).
- Combining camera motion dynamics with visual dynamics improves both video prediction and action prediction (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is generally well‑presented and easy to follow, with comprehensive supplementary material (Reviewers 1 and 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty and marginal contributions.** The core extension—conditioning existing video prediction models on action signals—is minimal. For VG‑LeAP and Causal‑LeAP, the action is simply added as an additional conditioning variable with recurrent posterior networks; for RAFI, the action state is concatenated with the image latent along the channel dimension. These are standard latent conditioning techniques. Recent works (e.g., Direct‑a‑video, SIGGRAPH 2024; LEGO, ECCV 2024) already handle user‑directed camera movement or textual motion descriptions, and the paper does not compare or distinguish itself from them. The modification of RIVER is particularly trivial and yields only marginal gains, with no demonstration of a powerful action‑conditioned world model (Reviewer 3; similar concerns in Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation and weak metrics.**  
  - Only evaluated on the RoAM dataset, which has limited diversity: indoor corridors, lobby, staircases, and scripted human movements (e.g., walking, sitting). The action space is only 2‑dimensional; evaluations on robot manipulation (7‑DOF) or drone data are missing (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).  
  - The dataset consists of short sequences (25 frames, 1 second at 64×64×4), making it unclear whether the method generalizes to long‑horizon prediction or larger motions (Reviewers 1 and 3).  
  - Metrics (FVD, LPIPS, VGG‑16) measure perceptual/semantic quality but do not directly assess the benefit of incorporating actions (e.g., impact on decision‑making, collision avoidance). The paper claims results on camera motion but the RoAM camera is static in the sense that it is mounted on the robot, which is not a generic moving camera. Also, the assumption that actions $a_t$ cause observed frames $x_t$ (line 148) is specific to robot manipulation with fixed cameras and does not hold for generic videos (Reviewer 2).  
  - Visual quality in qualitative results (Figures 6 and 8) is blurry, and action prediction errors are surprisingly small, raising concerns about overfitting or trivial action values (Reviewers 3 and 4).
- **Missing comparisons and ablation studies.**  
  - No comparison with prior works that model camera motion in video generation (e.g., [1–6] mentioned by Reviewer 4) nor with recent diffusion‑based video prediction architectures (Reviewer 3). Only SVG‑lp, RIVER, and ACPNet (no reference provided) are compared.  
  - No ablated analysis of key components: different latent conditioning strategies, encoding/decoding choices, the role of the causal assumption, or the effect of conditional flow matching in RAFI (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).  
  - The paper does not discuss the relative computational complexities of the three models nor provide guidance on when to use each one (Reviewer 4).
- **Rationale for proposing three distinct models is unclear.** The relationships, advantages, and disadvantages of VG‑LeAP, Causal‑LeAP, and RAFI are not thoroughly explained. It appears that three separate frameworks are introduced without a cohesive motivation for why multiple paradigms are needed (Reviewers 2 and 4).
- **Assumptions limit applicability.** The method assumes a static camera (line 148), which contradicts the paper’s stated goal of handling camera motion. The assumption of a causal link between actions and observed frames also restricts the method to robot manipulation with fixed cameras; action‑free video prediction (e.g., via inverse dynamics) could be a more scalable alternative (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing related works.** The paper revisits VAE‑based video prediction literature but does not discuss flow‑matching video prediction works (which are central to RAFI) in the related works section (Reviewer 2).
- **Minor issues:** latex formatting (`\citep` vs `\cite`) and too‑small fonts in Figures 4, 5, and 7 (Reviewer 3).
