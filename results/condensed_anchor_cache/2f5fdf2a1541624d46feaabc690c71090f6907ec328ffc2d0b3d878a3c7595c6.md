## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Self-Introspective Decoding (SID), a training-free decoding strategy that alleviates hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). SID uses a Context and Text-aware Token Selection (CT²S) strategy to identify and retain only the least important vision tokens after early decoder layers, thereby amplifying vision-and-text association hallucinations which are then subtracted via contrastive decoding. Evaluations on InstructBLIP, Shikra, LLaVA-1.5, and LLaVA-NeXT using CHAIR, POPE, GPT-4 assisted evaluations, MME, and MMBench show reduced hallucinations with minimal additional computation. One reviewer (score 8) was markedly more positive, emphasizing the method’s effectiveness, efficiency, and hyperparameter robustness; the other three reviewers (scores 5,6,6) raised several concerns about missing benchmarks, baselines, interpretability, and marginal gains on certain metrics.

### Strengths
1. **Training-free and efficient**: SID with CT²S is a training-free decoding strategy that alleviates hallucinations without additional computational resources, requiring only minimal extra cost (Reviewers 1, 2).
2. **Novel and well-motivated**: The idea that LVLMs can introspectively assess vision token importance and that preserving only unimportant tokens can amplify fine-grained hallucinations is novel, with clear motivation supported by empirical evidence (Reviewer 3).
3. **Valuable insight**: The paper demonstrates that existing vision-and-text agnostic contrastive decoding methods can introduce uncertainty noise and degrade performance, providing useful guidance for future research (Reviewer 3).
4. **Comprehensive experiments**: Extensive evaluations across multiple LVLMs and metrics (CHAIR, POPE, GPT-4 assisted, MME, MMBench) validate effectiveness; efficiency comparisons further highlight advantages over other contrastive decoding baselines (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
5. **Hyperparameter robustness**: Sensitivity analysis shows the method is robust to different hyperparameter settings (Reviewer 1).
6. **Good presentation and reproducibility**: The paper is well-written, clear, and straightforward; the code is provided to enhance reproducibility (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).

### Weaknesses
*Note: Reviewer 1 (score 8) was more positive overall but still identified limitations; other reviewers (scores 5,6,6) raised additional concerns.*

1. **Missing benchmarks**: The paper does not evaluate on comprehensive benchmarks that test diverse skills beyond hallucination metrics, such as MathVista, MathVision, MM-Vet/v2, LLaVA-Bench, and MMMU (Reviewers 2, 4). Performance on MME and MMBench is marginal, and standard deviations are not reported to account for random seed sensitivity (Reviewer 3).
2. **Missing baselines**: Several important baselines for hallucination mitigation are omitted: Woodpecker (arXiv 2310.16045), LRV (ResearchGate, 2023), LURE (arXiv 2310.00754), and a simple baseline of appending an image description before asking a question (Visual Description Grounding, Ghosh et al., 2024) (Reviewers 2, 4).
3. **Similarities with AVISC**: The proposed CT²S strategy shares conceptual similarities with AVISC (identifying less-important visual tokens for contrastive decoding). A detailed discussion and experimental comparison with AVISC are needed (Reviewer 3).
4. **Marginal gains from token selection**: In Figure 6, pruning *all* vision tokens yields less than a 0.5% performance difference from the optimal setting, questioning the benefit of the top-k selection strategy. The paper should clarify what “aimless hallucinations” means when all vision tokens are removed, e.g., with examples or experiments showing model behavior (Reviewer 3).
5. **Interpretability and applicability**: The selected low-importance tokens lack true semantic meaning (unlike HALC), reducing interpretability. For models with few vision tokens (e.g., InstructBLIP with only 32), preserving only the least important tokens provides a very coarse measure and may not effectively induce vision-and-text association hallucinations (Reviewer 3).
6. **Reliance on pre-trained models**: The method operates on pre-trained LVLMs, so its performance is inherently limited by those base models, and there is no solution to specifically tune SID for different LVLMs (Reviewer 1).
7. **Unexplained constraints and ablations**: Table 1 shows that greedy decoding consistently performs better regardless of the adaptive plausibility constraint (Equation 3); the significance of this constraint is unclear. The paper does not show whether removing the constraint also degrades performance on the open-ended CHAIR benchmark or the comprehensive MME benchmark (Reviewers 1, 3).
8. **Missing ablations on model size and failure modes**: No results are reported for different LVLM sizes (e.g., other than 7B). It is unclear where the proposed approach fails to mitigate hallucinations (Reviewer 2).
9. **Unclear experimental setups**: Figure 5’s experimental setup is not explained; why “ID” (inconsistency decoding?) produces entirely inconsistent responses is unclear (Reviewer 3). The rationale for why adding (boosting target logits) underperforms the proposed method is not clearly justified (Reviewer 3).
10. **Limited scope of hallucination**: The paper addresses only vision-and-text association hallucinations and does not mention or tackle style hallucinations or biases introduced during instruction-tuning (Reviewer 2).
11. **Missing subset breakdown**: Performance on the Existence, Count, Position, and Color subsets of the MME benchmark is not provided (Reviewer 3).
12. **Clarity and minor issues**: Metrics CHAIRi/CHAIRs should be consistently formatted (e.g., CHAIRi/CHAIRs as in tables); Figure 2 contains a typo (“Gnerated”); line 256 has a typo (“validate”); line 910 has a wrong citation format; Table 6 does not specify whether inference time is per instance or per benchmark; Table 1 lacks standard deviations for VCD in the Sampling setting and the reported deviations appear lower than expected (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
13. **Integration with other strategies**: The possibility of integrating CT²S with other hallucination alleviation strategies is not explored (Reviewer 1).
