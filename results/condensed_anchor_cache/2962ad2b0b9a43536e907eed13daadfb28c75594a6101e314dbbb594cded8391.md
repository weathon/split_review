## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes verifying transformers using Matrix Polynomial Zonotopes, a non-convex abstract domain that preserves higher-order nonlinear dependencies. The method is applied to attention, softmax, and layer norm with a tunable precision parameter ρlim. Reviewers split: two rate 3 (confidence 4, 3) and two rate 5 (confidence 3, 4). The positive reviewers find the approach innovative, showing improved precision over zonotopes and IBP on small transformers. The negative reviewers argue the paper misrepresents its scope (“large language models” for shallow networks), has unclear novelty, weak baselines, poor readability, and missing self-containment. All reviewers agree the problem is important and that the set-based extension is a step forward, but disagree on technical contribution strength, experimental rigor, and clarity.

### Strengths
- **Problem importance**: Formal verification of transformers is critical for safety, especially against adversarial attacks (R1, R4). The connection to LLM safety may inspire future work (R2).
- **Theoretical expressivity**: Matrix Polynomial Zonotopes generalize zonotopes and can preserve nonlinear dependencies, leading to tighter enclosures than zonotopes or intervals (R1, R4). Using a non-convex abstract domain is innovative (R4).
- **Adaptive precision tuning**: The single parameter ρlim balances precision and computation time, which is practical for real-world use (R3).
- **Empirical improvements**: Experiments show significantly larger verified volume and tighter enclosures compared to zonotope (Bonaert et al., 2021) and IBP methods (R2, R3, R4). The approach achieves better precision even on the evaluated shallow models (R1, R2).
- **Scalability demonstration**: Positive reviewers note the method scales well relative to zonotope and IBP, with results in Table 2 and Table 3 (R3, R4). R3 states “scalability demonstrated” in strengths.

### Weaknesses
- **Misleading claim about LLMs**: The paper claims to verify “large language models” but experiments use tiny transformers (≤2 blocks, embedding dimensions ≤8, trained on 2187 training examples from a small dataset; Table 3). R2 states this claim is false; R1 questions how hyperparameters compare with real-world LLMs (single-digit embedding dimensions). R3 agrees the method is not feasible for the largest LLMs in practice. Reviewers strongly suggest removing “verifying LLMs” from the title (R1, R2).
- **Lack of self-containment and poor readability**: Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R4) note the paper relies heavily on definitions and results from previous work (e.g., Ladner et al. 2024) without explanation. Key terms like “enclose” function are never defined (R4: “failed to find any information about it, even in the appendix”). R2 points out Definition 2 and Section 3 (enclosures for operations) are presented without explaining what the enclosures look like or what is challenging/new. Proofs in appendices lack clarity to convincingly demonstrate soundness (R4). R1 mentions the paper is “extremely difficult to follow.”
- **Limited baselines and missing comparisons**: Only IBP and zonotopes (Bonaert et al., 2021) are compared. R2 notes missing comparisons with recent transformer verification works (Wei et al., 2023; Shi et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024) that are cited but not evaluated. R4 suggests comparing with branch-and-bound methods (e.g., WFVM’23 paper). R1 adds that current SOTA verifiers (GCP-CROWN, MN-BaB, DeepPoly, PRIMA, DiffPoly) are not discussed.
- **Unclear technical novelty**: R2 and R1 find it unclear what is new and challenging in the derivation of enclosures for attention, softmax, and layer norm. R1 questions the computational complexity of the output enclosure algorithms (e.g., steps in Fig. 2). R4 asks how transformations between matrix polynomial zonotope and polyhedron are handled (non-trivial?). Parameter ρlim is not properly defined or explained (R1).
- **Precision vs. speed trade-off insufficiently addressed**: R1 notes zonotopes are used for speed, not precision; the proposed method with ρlim=2 is 29.23× slower than zonotopes on Model 1, yet many higher-order terms “contribute little” and can be outer-approximated. This raises a question about whether polynomial zonotopes are justified given the speed loss. R1 and R2 question scalability to larger networks.
- **Assumptions on word embeddings**: R3 notes the method assumes similar-meaning embeddings are close in space, which may not hold in nuanced edge cases. No justification given for why l∞-ball perturbations around embeddings capture realistic semantic changes.
- **Limited scope of safety risks**: R3 points out the approach only focuses on adversarial prompt verification near unperturbed inputs; it does not address jailbreaks or other unsafe behaviors. R3 asks whether the method could be extended to detect other forms of unsafe behavior without significant modifications.
- **Soundness concerns**: R4 states that without a definition of “enclose” it is difficult to validate soundness of over-approximations, especially for softmax. Proofs are available but lack clarity.
- **Minor issues**: Missing multiplication symbols in equations (e.g., QK^T vs d_{QK}), typo in (3) (L^{LIN} vs L^L), missing citation at line 503, unclear how verified volume in Table 2 is computed (R4).
