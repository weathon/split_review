## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper identifies length sensitivity in DPO and proposes LD-DPO, which decouples explicit length preference from implicit preferences by decaying probabilities of excessively long response portions. Experiments on Llama2-13B, Llama3-8B, and Qwen2-7B across MT-Bench and AlpacaEval 2 show that LD-DPO reduces generation length by 10–40% while improving or maintaining scores compared to DPO.

### Strengths
- Addresses a popular and widely existing issue: DPO’s length bias in LLM alignment. (All reviewers)
- Motivation is clearly expressed through theoretical analysis connecting length sensitivity to the DPO objective. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- The proposed LD-DPO is simple yet effective in controlling length and improving alignment. (Reviewer 1)
- Good presentation and easy to read. (Reviewer 2)
- Evaluated on multiple base models (Llama2-13B, Llama3-8B, Qwen2-7B) and benchmarks (MT-Bench, AlpacaEval 2), demonstrating generalizability. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- Empirical performance consistently outperforms DPO. (Reviewers 1, 2)

### Weaknesses
1. **Theoretical analysis is insufficiently rigorous.**  
   - The transition from Equation 4 to Equation 5 omits the expectation sign without explanation; the justification for approximating expectation with a single sample is not stated, and implications are not discussed. (Reviewer 1)  
   - The explanation (lines 211–215) linking longer sequences to lower probabilities is vague and lacks mathematical backing; the connection to DPO’s optimization direction is not made explicit. (Reviewer 1)  
   - In Equation 7, taking the absolute value of the ratio of two Jacobians obscures directional gradient information; justification for focusing on magnitude over sign is missing. (Reviewer 1)  
   - The term “probability bias” (line 416) is unclear. (Reviewer 1)

2. **LD-DPO is a heuristic, not a principled solution.**  
   - Despite a well-formulated problem analysis, LD-DPO simply truncates/decays the influence of tokens beyond a public length – a code-level heuristic. (Reviewer 2)  
   - The truncation risks losing valuable information in later tokens that may be crucial for complex reasoning or detailed explanations, making it a crude approximation that may not generalize to tasks requiring longer responses. (Reviewer 2)  
   - Reviewer 3 echoes this: decaying probabilities of excessively long portions inevitably introduces information loss, and the paper admits “additional text can convey more human-like preferences” and that α is a compromise. It is weird that LD-DPO consistently achieves better scores despite this loss; no clear explanation is provided. (Reviewer 3)

3. **Formulation of LD-DPO (Eq. 10) is not rigorous.**  
   - The decomposition of likelihood into \(p^\alpha\) as “human-like preferences” and \(p^{1-\alpha}\) as “verbosity preference” is by definition only; justification for a multiplicative split over an additive one is missing, and the role of α is not well-defined. (Reviewer 2)  
   - The same point is made by Reviewer 1 regarding the unclear effect of α. (Reviewer 1)

4. **Hyperparameter α is highly sensitive and poorly explained.**  
   - Figure 4 shows that different α values lead to varied effects: in AlpacaEval 2, choosing either 1 or 0 yields similar LC-win rates, but in MT-Bench, α=0 (strong desensitization) leads to significantly lower performance compared to DPO. The paper does not explain this discrepancy. (Reviewer 1)  
   - The method’s sensitivity to α raises concerns about practical applicability and the need for careful tuning. (Reviewer 1)  
   - The values of α differ across all models in experiments; no guidance is given for selecting it. Reviewer 3 asks: is there any way to improve this sensitivity? (Reviewer 3)

5. **Redundant symbol definitions hinder readability.**  
   - The definitions of \(\mathcal{X}_1, \mathcal{X}_2, \mathcal{K}_1, \mathcal{K}_2\) are unnecessary and add difficulty to understanding. (Reviewer 3)

6. **Figure 3 is hard to read and interpret.**  
   - Color differentiation within the same subfigure makes lines difficult to distinguish. (Reviewers 1, 3)  
   - The figure itself is somewhat hard to comprehend. (Reviewer 3)

7. **Spelling and grammatical errors.**  
   - e.g., “Length **Desentsitization**” in the paper. (Reviewer 3)

8. **Scope of length bias analysis limited to DPO.**  
   - Analysis only applies to DPO-based methods; RLHF-based methods also increase generation length – is it possible to apply the analysis to RLHF? (Reviewer 3)
