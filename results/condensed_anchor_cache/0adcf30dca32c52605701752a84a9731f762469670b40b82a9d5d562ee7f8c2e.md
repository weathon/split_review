## Merged Review

### Summary
PostEdit introduces a posterior sampling scheme for zero-shot image editing that is both inversion-free and training-free, achieving high efficiency (~1.5‑2 seconds, 18 GB GPU memory). The method derives a Langevin update rule with a measurement term to preserve background consistency. Reviews are mixed: one reviewer assigns an 8 (very positive), while three assign 6 (positive but with significant concerns). All agree the approach is fast and mathematically motivated, but raise issues about clarity, missing baselines, limited evaluation, and insufficient ablation.

### Strengths
- **Inversion‑free and training‑free** design eliminates costly optimization, contributing to very fast runtime (~1.5–2 seconds on A100 GPU, 18 GB memory) [R1, R3].
- **Strong mathematical foundation** using posterior sampling and Langevin dynamics to theoretically address error accumulation [R3].
- **State‑of‑the‑art editing performance** in CLIP similarity compared to existing methods [R2].
- **Well‑written and clear** manuscript overall [R2].
- One reviewer (score 8) particularly emphasizes that the method is **one of the fastest zero‑shot approaches** and successfully maintains background consistency [R1].

### Weaknesses
- **Methodology lacks clarity:**
  - Figure 1 subfigure “Our Posterior Sampling Process” is difficult to interpret and does not clearly highlight the algorithm’s advantages [R1].
  - Figure 2, Step 3, needs more explanation on how Langevin dynamics and the measurement term are incorporated [R3].
  - Symbols in Eq. 16, such as \(\mathcal{A}\), \(m\), \(h\), are not defined [R3]; the gradient symbol is missing before the large brackets in Eq. 16 and Algorithm 1, line 13 [R2].
  - Algorithm details are ambiguous: (a) in Algorithm 1, is \(z_0\) unrelated to the original image since \(z_N\) is pure noise? (b) Are lines 6–9 a regeneration of \(z_0\)? (c) Is alignment with the original image \(z_{in}\) only performed in lines 12–14? (d) The superscript \(N\) on \(z^{(N)}\) in line 15 is unusual (expected \(m\)), and \(z_{\tau_{i-1}}\) disappears in the next loop iteration [R4].
  - How the mask is generated/provided is not explained [R1, R3].
  - Number of denoising steps \(N\) and \(n\) in Algorithm 1 are not specified; this is needed to understand the claimed runtime [R1].

- **Insufficient evaluation and missing experiments:**
  - **Missing baselines:** Comparisons omit state‑of‑the‑art methods such as SPD Inversion [R3], Guide-and-Rescale [R3], and training‑based methods like InstructP2P, SmartEdit, SEED‑X‑Edit [R4].
  - **Limited editing scope:** Experiments only replace a few words in prompts; whether the method supports more extensive editing operations is unclear [R1].
  - **Simplistic test images:** All reconstruction results show single‑object, simple layouts; results on complex images with multiple detailed objects are needed [R3].
  - **No quantitative reconstruction evaluation:** Image restoration experiments provide only qualitative results; metrics such as measurement consistency (applying the forward operator to the output) are missing [R2].
  - **Ablation study lacks quantitative data** (e.g., numerical contribution of posterior sampling vs. inversion) [R3].
  - **No user study** despite subjective nature of image editing quality [R3].
  - **Runtime breakdown is missing:** Given the multiple loops in the algorithm, it is unclear how 1.2–2 seconds is achieved; a time comparison with other training‑free methods is needed [R4].
  - **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** The method involves many hyperparameters (even within the algorithm); a detailed sensitivity analysis and the specific hyperparameter values used per image should be provided to demonstrate robustness [R4].

- **Related work too brief:** The literature review cites too few relevant studies in the rapidly evolving image editing field; additional survey papers should be referenced [R4]. (One reviewer suggests adding citation to SNIPS [1] for linear image restoration [R2].)

- **Reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (score 8) is notably more positive about the method’s novelty and speed, while three reviewers (score 6) share the concerns above [R1 vs. R2,R3,R4].
