## Merged Review

### Summary
TimeCAT proposes a hierarchical context-aware transformer for time series forecasting that dynamically groups fixed-length patches into semantically coherent groups, then uses a Context-Aware Mixing Block to model intra-group, inter-group, and global dependencies. Experiments on several benchmarks show it outperforms prior models.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-motivated and the dynamic grouping mechanism is a novel approach that can capture dependencies between highly related patches while reducing unnecessary computations between less related ones.
- TimeCAT achieves promising accuracy and outperforms prior models on several time-series forecasting benchmarks (Reviewer 1, 3).
- The hierarchical structure (intra-group, inter-group, global) provides a comprehensive framework for multi-scale modeling of both local and global temporal patterns. This is a significant advancement (Reviewer 2, more positive).
- The method substantially reduces computational complexity by applying self-attention within groups rather than across the entire sequence, which is crucial for handling longer sequences and larger datasets (Reviewer 2, more positive).
- The Context-Aware Mixing Block enhances information exchange between patches and groups (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation quality is unsatisfactory.** The method description is overly wordy with many unnecessary and confusing notations. Consistency issues: \(\textbf{X}\) vs \(X\), \(x\) vs \(\tilde{x}\) vs \(\hat{x}\); frequent accents and subscripts/superscripts confuse readers. For example:
  - How are group indices obtained from \(\mathbf{r} \in \mathbb{R}^G\), and how can they be optimized via gradient methods given discretization (ceiling function)? How are gradients for \(\mathbf{W}_g\) and \(\mathbf{b}_g\) computed?
  - What are \(\mathbf{g}'_{n,i}\) in Eq. (3) and \(\tilde{\mathbf{g}}_{n,i}\) in Eq. (7)?
  - Is \(RD\) a single hyperparameter or a product?
  - In Eq. (3), what does \(\mathbf{g}'\) mean? (Reviewer 1, 2)
- **Claims about the dynamic grouping are not well supported.**
  - Experiments show \(G=2\) is sufficient—a very small number that does not demonstrate the necessity of grouping, as groups may still be too coarse. Results for \(G=1\) and larger \(G\) (e.g., 16, 32) are missing (Reviewer 1).
  - The grouping is determined by a single linear transformation of the input matrix, raising doubt about grouping quality.
  - Training becomes unstable without Eq. (12) and (15), but no ablation justifies the gradient detachment technique (Reviewer 1).
  - The order of operations in the context-aware mixing block lacks clear rationale; if \(G\) is as small as 2, full self-attention over all tokens would not be much more expensive, yet the hierarchical design has no clear experimental validation (Reviewer 1).
  - Figure 5’s claimed effectiveness of grouping is unconvincing: no alignment between (a) and (b), t-SNE plots in (c)-(e) show no meaningful pattern; explanation of why distinct clusters of global tokens reflect effective separation is needed (Reviewer 1).
- **Main experiments are limited.** Short-term experiments and additional datasets (e.g., from https://huggingface.co/datasets/Salesforce/lotsa_data) should be considered (Reviewer 2). The paper does not compare TimeCAT with other efficient transformer-based MTS forecasting baselines, which would be needed for a comprehensive efficiency evaluation (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing hyperparameter sensitivity and efficiency details.**
  - The path size \(P\) is crucial for capturing temporal dependencies and complexity, but a parameter sensitivity study on \(P\) and a table analogous to Table 3 for computational savings are absent. A concrete example comparing reduction in parameters, FLOPs, and memory would strengthen the paper (Reviewer 3).
  - The clarity of the context-aware mixing workflow could be improved by detailing the dimensions of all intermediate tensors (Reviewer 3).
- **Other specific technical questions/complaints (overlapping with criticisms).**
  - How is \(\tilde{X}\) downsampled to obtain \(\tilde{X}'\)? (Reviewer 1)
  - How does model performance change as input length increases? (Reviewer 2)
  - Wouldn’t using the downsampled version of \(x\) for group division cause information loss? (Reviewer 2)
  - Time-series forecasting experiments are often sensitive to learning rates and batch sizes; how were these parameters chosen? Provide actual training times for models in Table 2 (Reviewer 1).
  - Spacing between images and title is too large; unnecessary horizontal spaces between equations and sentences (e.g., L222, L226, L301) should be removed (Reviewer 1, 2).
