## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a DLO instance segmentation method combining CLIPSeg and SAM with a prompt encoder and mask quality classifier, achieving 91.21% mIoU on a new dataset. Reviewers disagree on novelty and evaluation depth: two rate it 6 (good, straightforward application), one rates it 3 (poor novelty, missing ablations and baselines).

### Strengths
- Straightforward approach using CLIPSeg and SAM with adapters is technically sound and well-motivated for DLO segmentation where well-labeled data are scarce (R1, R3). Text prompts add flexibility and improve zero-shot generalization (R1, R2).
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on DLO instance segmentation compared to existing methods (R1, R2, R3). Works well on the given data (R1).
- Proposed prompt encoder to obtain point prompts from text via CLIPSeg, and binary classifier for SAM mask quality (R3).

### Weaknesses
- Novelty is limited: the method is essentially an application of existing models (CLIPSeg, SAM) with adapters; a minority reviewer (R3) finds the combination not novel and lacking clear justification for advancing SOTA beyond combining tools. R1 also notes it is “more an application of existing models to a new domain with some modifications than a novel approach.”
- Generalization unclear: evaluation dataset has only 4 colors, all high resolution (1920×1080), cables at similar distance with limited appearance variation. It is unknown how the model performs on “cables in the wild” with diverse colors, twisting, far distances, or large appearance variance between train/test. (R1)
- Text prompts limited: only 3 single-word class labels tested; given CLIPSeg and SAM’s broad textual concept recognition, more complex/detailed prompts should be investigated to see if they improve or reduce quality. (R1)
- Design motivation for components in Section 3.1.2 (MLP, cross-attention, self-attention) is missing; the choice seems arbitrary without discussion of individual roles and contributions to overall performance. (R3)
- Failure cases not analyzed: the method relies on the assumption that properly prompted SAM always provides correct masks; it is unclear how close the proposed method reaches the upper-bound of SAM and CLIPSeg. No analysis of when generated prompts lead to suboptimal masks. (R2)
- Run-time not evaluated in Tables 1 and 2; computational cost of prompt encoder, SAM, CLIPSeg needed to assess practical real-time applicability. (R2)
- Insufficient baselines: only RT-DLO compared; should also compare with DeepLab, Mask R‑CNN, and other strong segmentation models given the use of SAM. (R3)
- No ablation studies: impossible to determine contribution of each module (prompt encoder, mask classifier) to overall performance. (R3)
- Minor issues: broken sentences in Section 2.2 (“However,Struggingle to” and “its prompt encoder with a single capable”) (R1); typo “that have historically have been difficult” (R2). Claim that augmentation improves generalization shows marginal improvement (e.g., 97.01 to 97.71) on some data; explanation needed (R1).
