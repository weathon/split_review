- Scores: 1, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the Texture Transformer Network (TxTN), which integrates three fixed texture layers (GLCM, Laplacian, and VQ) into a Vision Transformer to improve medical image classification. The method is evaluated on two public datasets (breast ultrasound and pneumonia X‑ray) and compared to MobileNet, SWIN, and ViT. Reviewers agree that the core idea (combining classical texture descriptors with a transformer) is interesting and the experimental results show performance gains. However, they raise substantial concerns about technical novelty, insufficient comparison to task‑specific baselines, unclear method description, lack of clinical motivation, and missing references to prior work on combining texture and deep features.

### Strengths
- The idea of introducing explicit texture features into a Vision Transformer is interesting and promising.
- Clear motivation and writing (though with grammatical issues).
- Good performance gain over the selected baselines.
- The paper considers two different body sites (breast, lung) and two modalities (ultrasound, X‑ray), both datasets are relatively large from a biomedical perspective.
- Ablation study of some hyperparameters (e.g., gray level, patch size) and an additional ablation that includes shape features.
- Comparison with three baselines (MobileNet, SWIN, ViT) and demonstration of improved results.

### Weaknesses
- **Technical novelty is lacking.** The texture extraction modules are standard, fixed operations (GLCM, Laplacian, VQ). Several reviewers note that ViTs already learn high‑level information and that CNNs can capture textural features (e.g., [1,2]); the paper does not convincingly argue why ViT + handcrafted textures is a step forward. The proposed module may be seen as a pre‑processing step rather than a novel architectural contribution. (R1: score 1; R2,R3: score 3 for overall paper, but contribution rated lower.)
- **Insufficient comparison with task‑specific methods.** The chosen baselines (MobileNet, SWIN, ViT) are general‑purpose classifiers. The paper should include other algorithms designed for breast or pneumonia classification, such as customized CNN/transformer baselines from recent medical image analysis literature. (R1, R2)
- **Missing references to related work** that combines texture descriptors with deep networks, e.g., local binary pattern networks, texture CNN variants. The original sources for GLCM (Haralick 1973) and Laplacian are not cited; only recent application papers are given. (R3)
- **The method description is unclear and insufficient for reproducibility.**
  - Which parameters in the texture layers are learned? (R3)
  - How is position embedding performed in the “Texture Representation and position embedding” step (Fig. 1)? (R3)
  - Are texture features concatenated back into the transformer stream after being used as input? (Fig. 1 suggests so.) (R3)
  - The VQ pattern description is vague: “1‑ring neighbor” is not defined; why 9 principal components/eigenvalues? (R3)
  - The GLCM description lacks a clear definition of parameters (e.g., gray level, distance, angle); the statement about “keeping at least 6 hits per bar” is ambiguous. (R3)
  - Algorithm Histogram_GPU is presented without explaining its significance or GPU‑specific aspects. (R3)
  - The section on shape features (3.4) is introduced abruptly: how are shape features extracted and merged with texture features? (R3)
- **Clinical motivation is not explained.** Why are textural features particularly relevant for X‑ray and ultrasound images? The paper does not justify this from a clinical or imaging‑physics perspective. (R2)
- **Several technical and presentation issues:**
  - Images are resized to 72×72 (stated in implementation details) but also mentioned as 28×28; please clarify. (R1, R3) R1 questions why such a small size is used – it may harm resolution.
  - Equation (1) defines the Laplacian on ℝ², but it should be a discrete operator on ℕ². The description of the Laplacian’s “maintenance of local topological structures” and “concerns about its global behavior” is unclear. (R3)
  - The paper states that the idea stems from addressing “topological destruction, the loss of geometrical information and the lack of global characteristics” of ViT, but no evidence or analysis is provided to support this claim. (R1)
  - Grammar, punctuation, and phrasing errors are frequent (e.g., spaces missing, words repeated, math environment issues). (R3)
  - The algorithms in the paper could be moved to the appendix for readability. (R3)
- **Missing dataset details:** dimensions of pneumonia images, number of images per class for the breast dataset. (R3)
- **The phrase “small size of image patches” (in relation to gray‑level selection) is ambiguous.** (R3)
- **Reviewer disagreement:** one reviewer (R1) is particularly critical (overall score 1), finding the method lacking in novelty and insufficiently compared; two others (R2,R3) give score 3 but share many of the same concerns, especially about novelty, clarity, and missing baselines. All three agree that the paper needs substantial revision.
