## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the last-iterate convergence of regret-matching\(^+\) (RM\(^+\)) and its variants in two-player zero-sum normal-form games. It numerically demonstrates that simultaneous/alternating RM\(^+\) and predictive RM\(^+\) (PRM\(^+\)) can fail to converge even on a simple \(3\times3\) game. For smooth variants (extragradient RM\(^+\), ExRM\(^+\); smooth predictive RM\(^+\), SPRM\(^+\)), it proves asymptotic last-iterate convergence (without rate) and \(1/\sqrt{t}\) best-iterate convergence. Introducing restarted variants yields linear-rate last-iterate convergence. The analysis relies on operator properties (Minty condition) and metric subregularity.

### Strengths
- The paper is generally well written and clearly presented.
- The problem of last-iterate convergence for RM-family algorithms is fundamental and important in algorithmic game theory.
- The nonconvergence results for RM\(^+\) and PRM\(^+\) are new and interesting, including a numerical example showing that strict Nash equilibrium is necessary for last-iterate convergence (Theorem 1).
- The operators of ExRM\(^+\) and SPRM\(^+\) are shown to be non-monotone but satisfy the Minty condition, enabling structural analysis of limit points.
- Asymptotic last-iterate convergence for ExRM\(^+\) and SPRM\(^+\) is proved, with best-iterate convergence at rate \(O(1/\sqrt{t})\).
- The proposed restarted variants achieve linear-rate last-iterate convergence—a novel and insightful technique that may guide future research.
- To the best of the reviewers' knowledge, this is the second paper to explore convergence properties of RM\(^+\) algorithms, offering valuable insights.
- The results are sound and the contribution is significant (Reviewer 2, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Domain restriction**: The paper only analyzes two-player zero-sum normal-form games. Extension to general convex-concave games or to extensive-form games (EFGs)—where RM\(^+\) is most commonly applied—is unclear and not convincingly motivated. Reviewer 3 emphasizes that results are not obviously extendable to EFGs, making the motivation to study RM\(^+\) in normal-form games insufficient given that convergent algorithms (e.g., EG/OG) already exist there.
- **No-regret property**: ExRM\(^+\) is not a no-regret algorithm, which contradicts the core purpose of regret-matching. Whether SPRM\(^+\) is no-regret is also questioned (Reviewer 3).
- **Dependence on prior work**: The convergence proof for RM\(^+\) (Theorem 1) is largely identical to that in [Meng et al., 2023]; the paper should explicitly acknowledge this dependence rather than claiming it is “incomparable to our matrix game setting” (Reviewer 4).
- **Clarity on “convergence of iterates”**: The distinction between convergence of the iterates and convergence in duality gap is unclear. Previous works used tangent residual to claim last-iterate convergence, whereas here the metric for linear-rate convergence is the duality gap. The conclusion’s claim of “linear-rate convergence in iterates” may therefore be misleading (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing rates**: No asymptotic rate is given for the last-iterate convergence results (Reviewer 1). The convergence for strict NE (Theorem 1) has no rate guarantee (Reviewer 2).
- **Parameter specification**: In Theorems 4 and 7, the step size \(\eta\) must be constant (not a function of \(t\)) to achieve linear convergence (since \(\beta\) must be upper bounded by a constant strictly less than 1). This should be explicitly stated in the theorems (Reviewer 2).
