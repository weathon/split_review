## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper studies natural actor-critic (NAC) with neural network function approximation for the critic and softmax parameterization for the actor. The authors propose NAC2L (Natural Actor-Critic with 2-Layer critic parametrization) and establish a sample complexity of \(\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{4}(1-\gamma)^{4}}\right)\) for global convergence. The analysis allows Markovian data (non-i.i.d. samples) and does not require linear or low-rank MDP structure. The paper extends previous asymptotic NAC–NN results to a non-asymptotic bound, building on NTK analysis and the global convergence framework of Xu et al. (2020a). The four reviewers gave scores 3, 5, 6, 3, indicating substantial disagreement. One reviewer (R3, score 6) recommended publication if concerns are addressed, while the others found the contribution incremental or the correctness questionable.

### Strengths

- **Non-asymptotic sample complexity for NAC under neural network function approximation**: The paper provides a finite-time bound for NAC with a two-layer neural network critic, whereas most prior work only gives asymptotic convergence (R1, R2).  
- **Relaxes i.i.d. assumption**: The analysis works with Markovian (non-i.i.d.) data, using a result from Bertail & Portier (2019) that may be of independent interest (R1, R4).  
- **Solid theoretical study** with well-structured presentation and clear writing (R1, R2).  
- **Problem closely connected to practical RL** with discrete time, finite actions, and continuous state space (R3, R4).  
- **Global convergence result** under reasonable conditions (R3).  
- **Presentation quality**: The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1, R2).

### Weaknesses

- **Missing empirical study**: The paper provides no experiments, implementation, or discussion of empirical implications (R1).  
- **Novelty concerns**: The result is essentially a combination of NTK analysis and the global convergence of NAC from Xu et al. (2020a). With a sufficiently wide network, NTK turns the NN into a kernel method, making the analysis similar to the linear case (R2).  
- **Weaker sample complexity than linear baselines**: The obtained \(\epsilon^{-4}(1-\gamma)^{-4}\) is worse than the \(\epsilon^{-3}(1-\gamma)^{-4}\) achieved in the linear-function-approximation setting. The extra \(\epsilon^{-1}\) factor appears to be sacrificed for NN approximation errors (R2).  
- **Insufficient comparison with related work**:
  - The claim of being the first to provide sample complexity for NAC with NNs is questionable because asymptotic results (Wang et al., 2019; Fu et al., 2020) could be converted to upper bounds, making the contribution incremental (R1).  
  - The paper does not compare with Cayci, He, and Srikant (2022) on entropy-regularized neural natural actor-critic, which also provides finite-time analysis (R4).  
  - The non-i.i.d. contribution overlaps with Xu et al. (2020a) (already cited), which also allows Markovian data (though with linear FA). The difference in analysis technique for Markovian data is not clarified (R4).  
  - Missing references: Agarwal et al. (2021) on policy gradient theory (R1); Jacot et al. (2018) on the neural tangent kernel (R1); LQR actor-critic papers (R3).  
- **Serious technical issues with the main theorem and proof** (R3, R4):
  - **Bellman operator definition (Eq. 3)**: Uses a random reward realization \(r'(s,a)\), making \(T^\pi Q\) a random variable, so \(T^\pi Q^\pi = Q^\pi\) does not hold. For stochastic rewards, \(\varepsilon_{\text{approx}}\) in Assumption 4 could be \(O(1)\) due to reward variance, rendering Theorem 1 meaningless (R3).  
  - **Compatible function approximation assumption**: The paper invokes the compatible function approximation condition (Sutton) before Eq. (8), which is extremely strong for NN parameterization. Sutton noted it may only be satisfied if \(Q\) is linear in the features. A standard policy gradient theorem would suffice (R3).  
  - **Missing assumption on \(\mu_k\)** in Theorem 1 and Lemma 7: The constants in the big-O notation depend on \(\mu_k\), which in turn depends on \(K\), making the bound meaningless. A positive lower bound for \(\mu_k\) (the minimal eigenvalue of the Fisher information matrix) is needed (R3, R4).  
  - **Lemma 6 probabilistic statement unjustified**: In Eq. (50) there is no randomness in the conditional expectation, yet “with high probability” is used. The randomness comes from NN initialization, but the network description (beyond width) is insufficient to apply the referenced theorems (R3).  
  - **Proof of Theorem 1 inconsistencies**:
    - Eq. (56) lacks a factor \(1/(1-\gamma)\) present in Algorithm 1, line 20; the dependence on \((1-\gamma)\) in the theorem needs revision (R3).  
    - Definition of \(\text{err}_k\) (Eqs. 58–60) should use \(A^{\pi_{\lambda_k}}\) instead of \(A_{k,J}\) to be consistent with Eq. (66) (R3).  
  - **Discrepancy between Algorithm 1 and theoretical analysis**: Algorithm 1 uses gradient descent for the critic, but the theory assumes an oracle that finds the global minimizer of Eq. (11). This difference should be explicitly stated; Theorem 1 should not be claimed for the presented algorithm without clarification (R4).  
  - **Lack of dependence on function class complexity**: The error bound does not show how \(\Theta\) (the network parameter space) affects the variance. The Rademacher complexity in Eq. (110) likely depends on \(\Theta\), but this is not reflected in the constants \(C_k\) in Eq. (111) or in the final bound (R4).  
  - **Potential non-decaying term**: If \(\alpha m = \Theta(1/\text{poly}(n,L))\), the second term in Eq. (17) behaves as \(\frac{J}{1-\gamma}\), which is non-decaying (R4).  
- **Missing details**:
  - Architecture of the neural network: Only \(L\) layers and \(m\) neurons per layer are stated in Section 4; no rigorous description of the function class or additional specifications is provided (R3, R4).  
  - Lack of detailed parameterization of the \(Q\)-function and policy with neural networks in the main text (R1, R3).  
  - The paper does not cite NTK literature (e.g., Jacot et al., 2018) despite the analysis lying in the NTK regime (R1).  
  - DQN is used without the full name at first appearance (R3).  
  - The dagger notation in Eq. (7) is not defined (inverse vs. pseudo-inverse) (R3).  
  - The assumption from Xu and Gu (2020) described on page 6 after Eq. (15) could not be found in that reference (R3).  
- **Presentation and correctness concerns** (R3): The paper contains numerous typographical errors, inconsistent notation, and unclear steps in the proof:
  - “\(d^\pi(s_0)\)” should be “\(d^\pi_{s_0}(s)\)” (page 3).  
  - “\(\Lambda\) should be a subset (not element) of \(\mathbb{R}^d\)” (page 4).  
  - Algorithm 1 line 12: “\(Q_k = Q_{\theta_n}\)” likely wrong.  
  - Eq. (15): “\(w^t\)” should be “\(w\)”?  
  - Theorem 1 and Lemma 6/7: many product dots appear as periods.  
  - \(\lambda_K\) should be \(\lambda_k\) in Eqs. (17)–(19) and proof.  
  - “Upper Bounding Error in Actor Step” missing a square in the expression below.  
  - Definition 9: “\(Z\) is a subset of \(\mathbb{R}^n\)” (page 14).  
  - Lemma 2 considers three random variables (page 15).  
  - Eq. (47): “bias” should be “approx”.  
  - Page 16, bottom: \(\theta_k\) should be \(\lambda_k\), “argmin” should be “min”.  
  - Eqs. (57)–(62) lack the second “KL(”.  
  - Notation “\(n.J\)” (e.g., in Algorithm 1 and Theorem 1) should be “\(n\cdot J\)” (R1, R4).  
  - Eq. (64): explanation for \(\log(|\mathcal{A}|)\) is missing (R3).  
- **Unclear statements**:
  - “Non-convexity of the critic can be avoided if we use the natural policy gradient algorithm” is confusing—natural policy gradient is an actor algorithm, not a critic one (R3).  
  - Remark 3 attributes the high-probability bound to non-convexity of the critic optimization, but normally such bounds come from sample randomness. Even with convex reformulation (Mishkin et al., 2022), samples remain random; the deterministic claim is unexplained (R4).  
  - Why does the stationary distribution \(\rho^\pi_\nu\) depend on the initial distribution \(\nu\) when Assumption 2 holds? (R3).
