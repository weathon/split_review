## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LL-VQ-VAE, which replaces the vector quantization layer in VQ-VAE with a learned lattice discretization. The lattice uses a diagonal basis, enabling fast Babai rounding and requiring only O(D) learned parameters. A sparsity-promoting loss term (Eq. 7: –γ‖diag(B)‖₁) forces the lattice to expand, increasing codebook utilization and preventing collapse. Experiments on FFHQ-1024, FashionMNIST, and Celeb‑A show lower reconstruction error, faster training, and high codebook utilization compared to standard VQ-VAE with a fixed codebook size.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an active research area (discrete representation learning) where improvements are useful. (R1)
- The method is described reasonably clearly, with results on three datasets and visualizations of reconstructions. (R1)
- The proposed learnable lattice quantization is an interesting and well‑reasoned extension of VQ-VAE; using a lattice to structure the codebook is a clever idea. (R2, R4)
- The method learns fewer parameters than traditional VQ-VAE, making it computationally efficient; Babai rounding with a diagonal basis is faster than nearest‑neighbor search. (R2)
- The approach achieves lower reconstruction error between input and decoder output while circumventing codebook collapse issues. (R2)
- Experiments across multiple datasets demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed method. (R3)
- Results appear impressive, though some reviewers were not fully familiar with the literature. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing downstream evaluation (generative modeling):** The method is evaluated only on reconstruction error. Since VQ-VAE and VQ‑VAE‑2 are standard for training autoregressive models on discretized latents, and [] (Huh et al., 2023) trains MaskGIT on discretized latents, generative modeling results are needed to judge the discretization’s usefulness. (R1)  
  *[R1 is strongly skeptical: without generative modeling it is hard to tell whether the approach is actually useful.]*
- **Suspicious “size loss” term:** The sparsity term –γ‖diag(B)‖₁ (Eq. 7) is unbounded below; the diagonal entries of B should explode, quantizing everything to 0 and minimizing the loss. The fact that this does not happen makes the results suspect. (R1)  
  *[R1 questions whether the improved reconstructions are simply due to a very fine discretization.]*
- **Codebook size computation unclear:** It is not explained how the effective codebook size (reported as K=1,405) is computed. (R1, R2) The claim that the lattice quantizes to a finite codebook is confusing when the lattice itself can produce infinitely many points. (R2)
- **Missing baselines and comparisons:** The paper does not compare against recent improvements to VQ training, e.g., [] (Huh et al., 2023) which addresses optimization challenges in vector quantized networks. (R1)
- **Insufficient experimental evidence:**  
  – Table 2 presents quantitative reconstruction results for only one dataset (FFHQ‑1024); additional quantitative experiments would clarify pros and cons. (R3)  
  – The claim of resistance to codebook collapse is supported by only one data point (the “no. embeddings” column is close to the desired 512). More empirical trends are needed. (R4)  
  – Training was performed for only 5 epochs, which seems low for image datasets; this raises concerns about convergence and the validity of comparisons. (R1, R4)
- **Writing and presentation need major improvement:**  
  – Several reviewers found the paper confusing, especially the notation, the description of Babai rounding, and the key equations. (R2, R4)  
  – Section 3.2 does not justify why enforcing sparsity in the lattice ensures shared points (R4) nor explain “the combined result of those two effects mentioned above” (R2).  
  – Figure 1 is unclear: the role of D, VQ, z_q, etc., is not explained. (R4)  
  – Figures 3 and 4: it is unclear whether originals are shown on the left and reconstructions on the right (R3); moreover, Figure 4 seems redundant given the reconstructions already in Figure 3. (R3)  
  – For a clearer comparison, reconstructions of the same original image should be shown side‑by‑side for LL‑VQ‑VAE vs. VQ‑VAE. (R3)  
  – Table 3: “init. range” and “sparsity coef.” (does this correspond to γ in Eq. 8?) are not defined. (R3)  
  – Table 2: “No. embeddings/dataset” is ambiguous; for VQ‑VAE (EMA) it is larger than the layer size; “duration” presumably means training time; the source of the training‑time fractions (84/235 and 84/86) is unclear. (R1)
- **Method justification:** Only a diagonal basis is considered; other structured bases (block‑diagonal, Hadamard frames) have shown good quantization performance and could be explored. (R2)
- **Notation and organization issues:** Page 1 incorrectly cites language model papers for continuous data (they are about video generation and BERT masking). (R1) The related work should be moved to the introduction. (R2) Equation 5 uses non‑standard notation for a diagonal matrix. (R1) The B initialization, crucial to the method, is in the appendix and should be in the main paper. (R1) Architectural details in Section 4.1 could be placed in the appendix if space is limited. (R1)
- **Missing reproducibility:** Request for pretrained models and code to reproduce the metrics, which would increase confidence in the results. (R1)
- **Hyperparameters and choices not documented:** The setting for γ in Section 4.1 is not given. (R1) The reconstruction metric is not stated (e.g., Frobenius norm?). (R2) A perceptual metric would be valuable. (R2)
- **Spelling/terminology:** In Table 2, “No. embeddings” should be “Number of embeddings” or similar. (R1)
