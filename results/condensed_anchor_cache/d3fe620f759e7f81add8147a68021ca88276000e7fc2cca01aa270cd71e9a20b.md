## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a folded VAE architecture that splits long ECG signals into smaller folds for encoding and reconstruction, evaluated on two datasets (MESA, MIT-BIH) and for downstream sleep stage classification. Reviews were split: two reviewers rated the paper very low (score 1), highlighting poor presentation and lack of novelty; two rated it moderately low (score 3), acknowledging the interesting problem but criticizing insufficient methodology, missing baselines, and suboptimal results. All reviewers agree the paper has significant issues in clarity, evaluation, and novelty.

### Strengths
- The problem of reconstructing long ECG sequences is interesting and clinically relevant, potentially enabling capture of essential information in a continuous latent space.
- Experiments are conducted on two datasets (MESA and MIT-BIH), demonstrating some generalizability across different signal sources and contexts.
- The application of the model to sleep stage classification is valuable and has relevance for healthcare and future ECG-based monitoring systems.

### Weaknesses
- **Poor presentation and clarity**: The paper contains numerous grammatical errors, missing commas, and unclear technical descriptions. Equations (1-2) are ambiguous (e.g., meaning of summation, merging of 30 8×4 features into 8×120). The relation between the VAE in Section 2.4 and the model in Section 2.8 is not explained. Results are presented as long vectors of numbers instead of structured tables. No related work section is provided, omitting relevant works (e.g., Harvey et al. 2024, Beetz et al. 2022, van der Valk et al. 2023, Matyschik et al.).
- **Lack of novelty**: The core contribution is simply splitting signals into folds; this is conceptually trivial and not compared to any alternative long-sequence encoding methods (e.g., sliding window, hierarchical or multi-scale VAEs, LSTM-based approaches, or a vanilla VAE baseline).
- **No quantitative reconstruction metrics**: Reconstruction quality is assessed only through visual inspection; quantitative metrics such as MSE or SSIM are missing, making evaluation subjective.
- **Suboptimal downstream performance**: Sleep stage classification accuracy (mean 65% across subjects) is lower than other baseline methods, and no attempts to improve temporal information sharing (e.g., recurrent layers in the Parameterizer) are explored.
- **Benefit over sliding window unclear**: The paper does not explain conceptually or empirically why folding is better than simply applying a sliding window to process short sequences and then concatenate reconstructions. No sliding-window baseline is provided.
- **Missing technical justification**: The folding and merging operations are not mathematically justified (e.g., why merging 30 8×4 features into 8×120 is valid, or why averaging by summation over folds is appropriate).
- **Missing comparisons with existing work**: Several relevant works that investigate similar problems (ECG representation learning, VAE-based feature extraction, ECG reconstruction from minimal leads) are not cited or compared, and no empirical baselines are provided.
