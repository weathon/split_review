## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a meta-learning approach (based on MAML) for training a meta-model on a diverse set of imaging inverse problems (denoising, TV recovery, deconvolution, inpainting, super-resolution, MRI reconstruction). The meta-model can be fine-tuned with few steps for new tasks, in both supervised and unsupervised inner-loop settings. Theoretical analysis shows convergence to the Bayes optimal estimator in simplified cases. Reviewers’ scores range from 3 to 5, with Reviewer 4 being more positive overall, while Reviewers 1-3 (score 3) express stronger reservations about novelty and experimental validation.

### Strengths
- The idea of using meta-learning to learn a prior (meta-model) that generalizes across different imaging tasks is interesting and worth considering.
- The method is supported by theoretical analysis: in simplified settings it recovers the Bayes optimal estimator (Reviewers 3, 4).
- Insightful perspective on the relationship between the solution and the kernel space, serving as motivation (Reviewer 3).
- A relatively high diversity of imaging tasks is considered in the experiments (Reviewers 2, 4).
- The proposed model can be generalized to a new imaging task with as few as 1 fine-tuning step (Reviewer 4).
- The paper is well-written and technically sound (Reviewer 3).
- Reviewer 4 (score 5) was more positive overall, while other reviewers (scores 3) had stronger reservations about experimental validation.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited methodological novelty.** The method is essentially a direct application of MAML to image reconstruction, without clearly articulating the unique challenges of inverse problems (ill-posedness, multiple solutions, overfitting to noise patterns/artifacts) and what novel solutions are needed to overcome them (Reviewers 1, 2).  
  - What are the requirements on the measurement operator A across tasks (size, dimensions)? How are different x/y dimensions handled? (Reviewer 2)  
  - In PDNet, which part of the architecture is fine-tuned in the inner optimization? The stability and cost of MAML training are not addressed (Reviewer 2).

- **Insufficient experimental comparison.**  
  - No comparisons to other meta-learning approaches (e.g., MAML itself) or to standard supervised task-specific methods (Reviewer 1).  
  - Missing comparisons to state-of-the-art methods for each task: e.g., BM3D for denoising; EDSR or RCAN for super-resolution; advanced compressed sensing and deep learning methods tailored for MRI reconstruction (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).  
  - Comparisons only between different versions of the proposed method (varying fine-tuning steps) and training from scratch on test data (a weak baseline); even then the benefit is not significant or consistent (Reviewer 2).  
  - Missing comparisons to basic pre-training [e.g., Goodfellow et al. Ch. 8.7.1], ADMM, plug-and-play priors, and unsupervised/self-supervised baselines (Reviewers 3, 4).  
  - For generalization experiments (e.g., MRI with acceleration factor 4), quantitative results are missing; the qualitative results (Fig. 6) show a clear shift from ground truth and appear lower quality than many existing methods (Reviewers 1, 4).

- **Lack of experimental details and reproducibility.**  
  - Number of data samples per training task, size of context vs. query data, and number of fine-tuning samples at test time are not specified (Reviewer 2).  
  - Dataset sources, sizes, preprocessing steps, and transformations are not provided. For super-resolution, it is unclear if standard benchmarks (Set5, Set14, Urban100) are used. For MRI, acquisition protocol, image type (T1-weighted, T2-weighted), and undersampling strategy are absent (Reviewer 3).  
  - Training details (iterations, learning rate schedule, convergence criteria) are missing (Reviewer 2).

- **Unclear motivation and physical interpretation.**  
  - Why does learning a meta-model help? What is the physical meaning of the learned prior, especially when domain gaps are large (e.g., transfer to MRI)? How should training tasks be chosen? (Reviewer 1)

- **Performance concerns.**  
  - The meta-learned model performs worse than task-specific models (Fig. 3), raising doubts about practical utility given the computational overhead of meta-training (Reviewer 4).  
  - In Fig. 2, the similarity between the learned and the analytic solution is not close; an absolute difference map would help (Reviewer 4).

- **Presentation issues.**  
  - Paper structure and writing (abstract, introduction) are confusing; motivation and novelty are hard to grasp (Reviewer 1).  
  - Figure 5 (many overlapping curves) is difficult to read; a table would be better (Reviewer 1).  
  - A framework figure illustrating meta-training and inner-training would be helpful (Reviewer 1).
