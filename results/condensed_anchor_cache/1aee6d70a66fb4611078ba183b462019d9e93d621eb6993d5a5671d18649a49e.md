## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates label poisoning attacks on GNNs, identifying six evaluation pitfalls in prior work. It then introduces a linear surrogate attack (solved via mixed-integer linear program) and a meta attack (using Gumbel-softmax for discrete optimization). Experiments show that existing attacks overestimate effectiveness by ~9% on average, and the proposed attacks are up to ~8% stronger. Reviewers agree the paper is well-written and the pitfall analysis is important, but several question novelty, the connection between pitfalls and attacks, and the validity of certain pitfalls. One reviewer (score 5) finds the paper not well-organized; another (score 6, high confidence) finds arguments unconvincing and novelty limited. The other two (scores 8) praise the contributions and presentation.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, well-structured, and easy to follow. The presentation is coherent and explains intuition behind each step, making it a pleasure to read (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Pushes for higher standards in evaluating label poisoning attacks; identifies vital cracks in existing evaluations and supports claims with extensive experimental results (Reviewers 1, 3). Provides a clear summarization of pitfalls (Reviewer 4).
- The discussion on binary attacks overfitting is interesting and worth further investigation (Reviewer 1).
- The linear surrogate and meta attacks are novel and effective, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines by a large margin, with theoretical backing (Reviewer 3). Formalisation of research questions is provided (Reviewer 4).
- Focus on nuances and accountability helps build trustworthy GNNs (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of direct connection between pitfalls and proposed attacks**: It is unclear whether the new attacks were motivated by the identified pitfalls or are disjoint contributions. Specifically, how hyperparameter tuning pitfall informed attack design is not explained (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **First pitfall (validation set size) is insufficiently justified and contested**: The paper states that using validation sets much larger than training sets (e.g., 500 validation vs 140 training on Cora-ML) is a pitfall, but does not explain why. One reviewer (3) asks for elaboration; another reviewer (4, high confidence 5) argues this partitioning is practical in graph learning and therefore not a pitfall—the claim is unconvincing. A third reviewer (2) similarly finds the pitfall weird, noting that in semi-supervised learning splits are normally unequal and validation sets are kept clean for model selection.
- **Overly broad claims without exhaustive evidence**: The statement that “the entire literature on label poisoning for GNNs is plagued by serious evaluation pitfalls” is a strong assertion not convincingly supported by the presented evidence (Reviewer 4).
- **Ambiguous statements**: For example, “fine-tuning with only 20 configurations can significantly deteriorate the attack performance” is not explained—what configurations? (Reviewer 4)
- **Self-contradiction**: The paper first criticizes previous works for focusing on binary tasks, but then generalizes to multiclass—this appears contradictory (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited novelty**:
  - The linear surrogate attack only uses a linear GCN, ignoring the non-linear nature of most GNNs (Reviewer 4).
  - The meta attack’s effectiveness is limited by the expressive power of the surrogate model in the inner optimization (Reviewer 4).
  - The formulation is a very natural extension of label flips, heavily based on prior work, and feels like a multi-label extension rather than a fundamentally new idea (Reviewer 2).
  - Existing methods could be adapted to the current setting, undercutting the claimed novelty (Reviewer 4).
- **Organization and logic flow**: While most reviewers found the paper well-structured, one reviewer (2, score 5) states the paper is not well-organized: the analysis of evaluation pitfalls does not help understand why the new method is proposed, and the paper lacks a coherent logic flow.
- **Incomplete handling of defense-awareness pitfall (Pitfall 3)**: The paper defers discussion of undefended models to a later paragraph, so the full impact on performance (which is expected to be large) is not shown. Suggestion: provide a figure similar to Fig 2 that includes this pitfall (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing details and clarification requests** (from reviewers):
  - Equation (1): Could the argmax over loss be replaced by error rate without loss of generality? How would that affect the design of the Meta attack? (Reviewer 1)
  - The computation of the poisoned label via \(\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{l} = \mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{b}) \odot \boldsymbol{H} + \mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{1}_{L} - \boldsymbol{b}) \odot \boldsymbol{Y}_{l}\) needs explanation (Reviewer 3).
  - Suggestion to number pitfalls (e.g., P1–P6) to improve readability of figures and text (Reviewer 1).
  - Use larger legends and axis labels in plots (Reviewer 3).
- **Contrasting opinion on binary attack overfitting discussion**: Reviewer 1 finds this discussion interesting and worth investigating further, but no other reviewer comments on it; it is not connected to the main contributions.
