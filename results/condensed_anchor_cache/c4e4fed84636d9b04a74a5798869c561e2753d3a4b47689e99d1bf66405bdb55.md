## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper reformulates the weak dual of the entropic optimal transport (EOT) problem into an optimization objective that resembles training an energy-based model (EBM). This allows leveraging EBM training techniques—in particular MCMC sampling—to compute the optimal EOT coupling. Theoretical generalization bounds are provided (Theorem 4 uses Rademacher complexity), and the method is validated on 2D toy data, standard image datasets, and high-resolution (512×512) unpaired image-to-image translation via a pretrained StyleGAN latent space. The paper emphasizes that the semi-dual form, with only one Schrödinger potential parametrized, still yields a clean approximation of the optimal coupling with bounded gap (Theorem 2) and that the construction naturally extrapolates to generative sampling.

### Strengths
1. **Semi-dual approximation insight**: The paper shows that even when only one potential is parametrized (semi-dual form), constructing a joint distribution via normalized exponential conditionals gives a clean approximation of the optimal EOT coupling with bounds on the approximation gap (Theorem 2). This construction naturally extrapolates to generative sampling. *(Reviewer 1)*  
2. **Principled use of EBMs**: The EOT semi-dual objective admits an exponential form that directly connects to EBMs, enabling the use of well-studied sampling methods and simple gradient forms. *(Reviewer 1)*  
3. **Sufficient experiments and clear illustration**: The experiments cover toy 2D and image domains with adequate discussion of EBM training techniques. Figure 2 effectively demonstrates the method’s efficacy. *(Reviewers 1, 2)*  
4. **Novel connection**: The work uncovers a previously unexplored bridge between EBMs and entropy-regularized OT, opening up new applications for EBMs (e.g., unpaired data-to-data translation). *(Reviewer 3)*

### Weaknesses
1. **Scalability and computational cost of MCMC**: The training relies on MCMC sampling (e.g., Langevin dynamics), which introduces significant computational bottlenecks, especially in high-dimensional spaces. The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of how the number of MCMC steps/samples affects the accuracy of EOT computation, nor does it discuss the computational cost of the sampling procedure itself. This raises concerns about direct applicability to high-dimensional image spaces without a latent space bottleneck. *(Reviewers 1, 3)*  
2. **Dependence on pretrained GAN for high-resolution tasks**: The unpaired image-to-image translation experiments (AFHQ 512×512) use a pretrained StyleGAN and operate in its latent space. This limits applicability when such pretrained models are unavailable or unsuitable; it remains unclear whether the method works directly on pixel space without such an intermediate representation. *(Reviewer 3)*  
3. **Practicality of theoretical bounds**: The bound in Theorem 4 involves the Rademacher complexity of a function class after a $(c,\epsilon)$-transform, but it is not clear how this complexity relates to the original neural network class or how it scales with input dimensionality. The choice of parametric class remains heuristic; an explicit rate (e.g., in terms of dimension) would clarify practical applicability. Additionally, the effect of sampling error on the bound is not considered. *(Reviewer 1)*  
4. **Missing comparisons with other OT solvers**: The unpaired image-to-image translation experiments lack comparisons to other neural OT methods to assess the relative advantages of the proposed EBM-based approach. *(Reviewer 2)*  
5. **Lack of computational complexity analysis**: A full space/time complexity analysis is missing, especially regarding the construction of the loss function which requires significant computation for the MCMC oracle. The $\epsilon$-dependence of computation/estimation/approximation is not characterized. *(Reviewer 1)*  
6. **Insufficient justification of the MLE interpretation and alternative training**: The connection between Equation 18 (maximum likelihood of the EBM in Equation 13) and the EOT objective is not intuitively explained. It is also unclear whether the objective (Equation 17) can be optimized using alternative EBM training techniques such as noise contrastive estimation or score matching. *(Reviewer 3)*  
7. **Presentation**: While overall clear, one reviewer rated the presentation as only 2/3, indicating room for improvement. *(Reviewer 3)*
