## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes an interpretable framework, ForgeryTalker, for image forgery localization that outputs both a mask and a textual explanation of the manipulation. The authors construct the Multi-Modal Tamper Tracing (MMTT) dataset of 128,303 deepfake-manipulated face images paired with manual textual annotations. Two reviewers (ratings 5) commend the dataset scale, the novel interpretability direction, and the well-written presentation. Three reviewers (ratings 3 and 5) raise major concerns about incremental technical contribution, lack of meaningful baselines and ablation, missing localization comparisons, dataset limitations, annotation credibility, and insufficient robustness analysis. The majority consensus is that the work is a step forward but requires significant improvements.

### Strengths
1. The paper is well-written, clearly organized, and the diagrams effectively explain the problem and proposed solution. (Reviewers 1, 4)
2. The MMTT dataset is large-scale (128,303 image-text pairs) and diverse in manipulation types (35% GAN-based inpainting, 36% Diffusion-based, 29% traditional), providing a valuable resource for the forgery localization community. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5)
3. ForgeryTalker is among the first to address interpretability in forgery localization, simultaneously performing localization and generating human-understandable textual explanations. (Reviewers 1, 2, 5)
4. Extensive experiments on multiple metrics (CIDEr, BLEU, METEOR) show that ForgeryTalker outperforms or competes closely with advanced models like InstructBLIP and SCA. (Reviewer 2)
5. The work highlights the under-explored problem of explaining why a region is forged, moving beyond binary masks. (Reviewers 3, 5)

### Weaknesses
1. **Incremental technical contribution**: ForgeryTalker closely follows InstructBLIP, adding only a plug-and-play Forgery Prompter Network and a mask decoder. The overall architecture lacks significant innovation. (Reviewers 2, 3)
2. **Lack of interconnection between interpretation and localization**: There is no bidirectional interaction; the LLM's output does not inform mask prediction, and the two tasks are treated independently. (Reviewers 3, 5)
3. **Insufficient baselines and localization comparisons**: 
   - Only two comparison methods (InstructBLIP, SCA) are shown in Table 2. A fair comparison with state-of-the-art forgery localization methods (e.g., TruFor, IML-ViT, PSCC-Net) is missing. (Reviewers 1, 5)
   - Missing comparisons with classic deepfake detection methods and recent M-LLMs for deepfake detection (e.g., LLaVA). (Reviewers 2, 5)
   - No performance breakdown by manipulation type (GAN, Diffusion, traditional) or image source. (Reviewer 4)
   - In Table 2, the comparisons may not be fair; the version of SCA used is not specified, and there is no analysis of why ForgeryTalker's localization ability is lower than SCA. (Reviewers 1, 4)
4. **Dataset concerns**:
   - Only three generative models used (E4S for face-swapping, two inpainting methods); no forgery editing, reenactment, or other face tampering methods. Generalizability to unseen forgeries is questionable. (Reviewers 1, 3, 5)
   - The dataset is restricted to facial images; the title should reflect this ("face image forgery localization"). (Reviewers 1, 3)
   - Annotation quality: some examples (e.g., "the size of both eyes is different") are not reliable because such differences appear in real faces. There is no user study or mechanism to ensure annotation credibility. (Reviewer 3)
   - No plan to release the dataset publicly is stated. (Reviewer 3)
   - With 30 annotators for 130k images, it is unclear if each annotator reviewed 4,000+ images; the annotation process and quality control need clarification. (Reviewer 4)
5. **Missing robustness and error analysis**:
   - No evaluation of how forgery localization and text generation degrade under common image perturbations (e.g., compression, blur). (Reviewer 1)
   - No user-driven error correction or hallucination handling mechanism for MLLM predictions. (Reviewer 3)
   - The paper does not explore pitfalls or failure cases of the model. (Reviewer 4)
6. **Insufficient annotated examples**: The paper and supplementary materials lack enough samples to demonstrate interpretation quality for different forgery types. (Reviewers 1, 3)
7. **Flaws in methodology details**:
   - "Iterative refine" (L88) is not explained. (Reviewer 1)
   - The Forgery Prompter Network is labeled "Frozen" in Figure 2 but requires training. (Reviewer 5)
   - "Mask encoder" should likely be "mask decoder". (Reviewer 3)
   - IoU is used as a metric in Table 2, but it is not standard for language-task outputs; no reference is provided. (Reviewer 5)
8. **Task relevance**: The tampered regions in deepfake faces are almost always on the face; a model could simply segment the face to achieve high localization, making the task less meaningful. (Reviewer 2)
9. **Heatmap alternative**: The text prompts from the Forgery Prompter Network could be replaced by a heatmap that directly indicates forgery intensity and model confidence. (Reviewer 3)
