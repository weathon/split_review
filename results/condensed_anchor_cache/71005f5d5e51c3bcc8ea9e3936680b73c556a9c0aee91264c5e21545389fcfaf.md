## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates how different types of scaled dot-product self-attention affect sample efficiency in visual deep reinforcement learning. It compares four self-attention modules (SWA, CWRA, CWCA, CWRCA) applied after a couple of CNN layers in the Arcade Learning Environment across 56 games with 5 seeds, using stratified bootstrap confidence intervals. The paper concludes that different attention operations have distinct impacts on different games due to inductive biases and game mechanics. However, the reviews are strongly split: one finds major flaws with the architecture, arbitrary attention selection, and inconclusive observations (score 1/5, confidence 5); another finds the topic interesting but criticizes limited insights and lack of theory (score 3/5); a third rates it more positively, praising the experimental setup and results (score 5/5). The merged review preserves all distinct criticisms and contrasting opinions.

### Strengths
- The motivation to analyze different types of self-attention layers in DRL is good (R1).
- The topic is interesting, connecting self-attention from NLP to DRL (R2, R3).
- The paper evaluates 56 games within the ALE benchmark with 5 seeds, using stratified bootstrap confidence intervals for reliable assessment, which is more extensive than prior work by Manchin et al. (R2).
- Four specific self-attention modules (SWA, CWRA, CWCA, CWRCA) are designed and contrasted, revealing how each module’s inductive biases affect learning in specific environments (R2).
- The paper is brief and straightforward (R2).
- The experimental setup is thorough and well-thought, especially regarding evaluation metrics and state presentations (R3).
- The results are interesting and give new perspective; the reviewer found the explanations of those results satisfactory (R3). (Note: R1 strongly disagrees, calling the observations inconclusive and the architecture limited.)

### Weaknesses
- **Shallow and unusual architecture:** The model uses only a couple of CNN layers with a single self-attention layer, which is extremely limited for visual representation learning. Standard practice (e.g., ViT) uses a series of MHSA and MLP layers interleaved. No concrete conclusions can be drawn from this architecture (R1). R3 also notes that only a single self-attention layer was tested, without varying the number of layers or heads.
- **Arbitrary selection of attention mechanisms:** The compared operations (spatial-wise attention, channel-wise row/column attention, etc.) are not used in any major state-of-the-art models or large vision-language models. The paper should have compared practical components like Performer (linear attention) or Mamba2 (sequential models) to interest the general audience (R1). R2 adds that the types considered (only different channel operation sequences) are far too limited for a fundamental contribution.
- **Inconclusive and poorly developed insights:** Observations from the experiments do not show any major trend (R1). The insights lack theoretical analysis and rely merely on limited observations; e.g., “different attention operations have different impacts on different games” cannot guide the community. More specific conclusions (like “height dimension attention improves performance on vertically moving games”) would be beneficial (R2).
- **Unclear link to sample efficiency:** The paper does not explain how the attention operation relates to sample efficiency. Attention changes network structure, but sample efficiency typically involves selection of transitions from the replay buffer. Further justification is needed (R2).
- **Missing hyper-parameter details and ablations:** No information on hyper-parameter choices or ablation studies regarding network structure (number of layers, heads, etc.) is provided (R3). This is crucial for reproducibility and understanding the impact of attention (R3). R2 also calls for more investigations beyond the channel operation variant.
- **Insufficient related work:** Only three references are cited; a more thorough survey is needed (R2).
- **Specific results contradict claims:**  
  - In Figure 6, methods without attention seem to learn critical environment information effectively, while variants with attention have limited learning and fail to capture essential details. The paper does not explain this (R2).  
  - In Figure 7, the mean and standard deviation of logits for CWRA and CWRCA are the lowest, indicating less exploratory behavior due to uneven logit distribution. This appears to contradict the paper’s conclusions (R2).  
  - The paper does not analyze the causes or conditions under which artifacts occur, nor potential ways to eliminate them (R2).
- **Limited to scaled dot-product:** The paper only investigates scaled dot-product attention; other aspects of attention (e.g., different attention mechanisms beyond dot-product) could affect sample efficiency but are not discussed (R2).
- **No analysis towards multi-head self-attention (MHSA):** The paper focuses on single-head attention without explaining the rationale or whether findings would generalize to MHSA, which is more applicable in NLP and Vision Transformers (R3).
