## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Multi-Fidelity Active Learning with GFlowNets, a method that uses a generative flow-based model to optimize the acquisition function of a multi-fidelity deep-kernel Gaussian process. The aim is to discover diverse, high-scoring candidates in scientific discovery tasks where querying a high-fidelity black-box function is expensive. The algorithm is evaluated on molecular discovery tasks (DNA, antimicrobial peptides, molecules) and outperforms single-fidelity GFlowNet baselines and RL-based alternatives. Reviewers’ scores range from 3 to 8 (mean ~5.5). Reviewer 4 (score 8) finds the work novel and clear; the other three reviewers (scores 3,5,6) raise significant concerns about novelty, weak baselines, limited real-world validation, and insufficient analysis of diversity and surrogate models.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and integration**: Reviewer 4 considers the algorithm a novel combination of multi-fidelity active learning with GFlowNets. Reviewer 1 acknowledges the method is sensible with some novelty, and Reviewer 3 finds it a good combination of active learning and GFlowNets.
- **Empirical results on molecular discovery**: The method discovers diverse, high-scoring candidates with fewer queries than RL-based methods (Reviewer 4) and outperforms its single-fidelity counterpart while maintaining diversity (Reviewer 3). Results are shown on multiple molecular tasks (e.g., drug discovery, materials science).
- **Focus on diverse high-value samples**: The work targets acquiring a diverse set of top candidates rather than a single optimum, which is relevant for scientific screening (Reviewer 3).
- **Reproducibility**: Algorithm steps are given in Algorithm 1, with supplementary details in Appendices A, B, and C. Code is provided as open source (Reviewer 4).
- **Clarity and presentation**: Reviewer 4 finds the paper well-written and easy to follow. Reviewer 1 notes the writing is clear (though focusing on the wrong aspects). Reviewer 2 disagrees (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and incremental contribution**: Reviewer 2 states the extension to multi-fidelity is straightforward from GFlowNet-AL (Jain et al., ICML 2022) and the paper’s novelty is very limited. Reviewer 1 notes the method is essentially multi-fidelity Bayesian optimization with deep kernel GPs, and GFlowNets are the acquisition optimizer, so the main contribution is unclear relative to prior GFlowNet papers [4,6]. The relationship to existing GFlowNet work is not clearly stated.
- **Weak baselines**: Comparisons are only against random search, PPO, and single-fidelity active learning. Missing: other BO methods (e.g., multi-fidelity GPs with standard kernels and EI/cost acquisition), other query synthesis active learning methods (e.g., as in [r1]), and active search / quality-diversity optimization (mentioned in Sec. 2 but never used). No BO baselines are provided. Experiments are simulation-based, not real-world multi-fidelity scenarios.
- **Real-world applicability and generalizability**: Evaluation is limited to molecular discovery tasks with short sequences (e.g., DNA, antimicrobial peptides). Generalizability to longer sequences (e.g., antibodies), other scientific domains (optimization of physical systems, process design), and tasks with different underlying structures is unclear. The paper lacks a real-world application with multiple fidelity levels and varying costs.
- **Insufficient analysis of diversity**: The origin of diversity (surrogate model vs. GFlowNets) is not disentangled. The top-K score/diversity metric is flawed; Reviewer 1 recommends a monotonic diversity metric like #circles (Xie et al. 2022). Reviewer 3 asks whether selected samples could be out-of-distribution despite the top-K and diversity criteria.
- **Surrogate model and acquisition function**: Deep kernel GPs are prone to overfitting (as noted in prior work [11]), but their training is not discussed. The choice of acquisition function and its interaction with the surrogate are not explored. Reviewer 1 asks for results of GP baselines using Matern kernel (toy tasks), string kernel (DNA), and Tanimoto kernel (molecules) combined with EI/cost acquisition.
- **Insufficient problem and method description**: The multi-fidelity problem is not clearly defined (Reviewer 2). Terms like “cost” and “cheap online simulations” are ambiguous (are they validation experiments or query costs? how long do cheap simulations take?). The paper includes excessive introductory text about scientific discovery but lacks detail on the method; GFlowNet and active learning sections are not well integrated (Reviewer 2). Referencing GFlowNet-AL as a preliminary section would clarify novelty.
- **Writing and presentation quality (mixed)**: Reviewer 2 finds the paper poorly written: plots are densely packed, too much basic information, insufficient multi-fidelity description. Reviewer 1 notes the writing is clear but focuses on the wrong aspects (too much context, not enough method). Reviewer 4 disagrees, calling it clear and well-organized.
- **Missing discussion of limitations**: No thorough discussion of when the algorithm might struggle (e.g., very high-dimensional spaces, discontinuous objective functions) or potential future directions (Reviewer 4). Computational cost is mentioned but not analyzed.
- **Other specific questions/concerns**:
  - How does this method differ from a standard BO method that uses a GP model and GFlowNet as acquisition optimizer?
  - What are the results for GP baselines with standard kernels (Matern, string, Tanimoto) and EI/cost?
  - Can the method handle longer sequences (e.g., antibodies)? (Reviewer 2)
  - What is the main challenge addressed, and how is it tackled? (Reviewer 2)
  - Could you provide a real-world example with multiple fidelity levels and varying costs? (Reviewer 2)
  - Why is it useful to have multiple oracles when there is a single expensive objective? (Reviewer 2)
  - The method can be viewed as ensemble modeling – what is the main advantage? (Reviewer 2)
