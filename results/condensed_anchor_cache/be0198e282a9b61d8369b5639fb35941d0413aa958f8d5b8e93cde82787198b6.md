- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 5, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes that the loss barrier in linear mode connectivity (LMC) after re-basin can be explained by the entropy (non-uniformity) of the neuron weight distribution. A theoretical bound is given that upper-bounds the output difference between interpolated networks by a term involving Shannon entropy. Experiments examine entropy and barrier under different initializations, training (which shifts the distribution from uniform to bi-modal), and pruning. Pruning is then used as a method to decrease entropy and improve subsequent re-basin performance. The theory is also applied to other alignment methods such as OTFusion and FedMA.

### Strengths
- The connection between LMC barrier and neuron distribution entropy is novel and worth further exploration (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- The application of pruning to improve model fusion is a promising direction (Reviewer 1,2).
- The paper identifies an important problem: why permutation‑based re‑basin methods (e.g., weight matching, Entezari et al.) sometimes achieve LMC and sometimes fail (e.g., early training) (Reviewer 4).
- Building a link between random matching problems and the loss barrier is a good point (Reviewer 4).
- The phenomenon that pruning can decrease the loss barrier after re‑basining is interesting (Reviewer 4).
- The paper provides both theoretical analysis and practical experiments (Reviewer 3).
- The finding that pruning then fine‑tuning at a higher rate improves re‑basin is an interesting discovery (Reviewer 3).
- One reviewer found the writing clear (Reviewer 3), though others found the paper hard to follow (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
**Theoretical Issues**
- The bound in Theorem 3.1 is on the supremum difference between the network output at interpolated weights and the interpolation of the outputs, not on the loss barrier itself; the link between the two is not given (Reviewers 1,2).
- The proof of Theorem 3.1 does not require permutations – it only bounds the output difference; permutations are only introduced at the end to shrink the bound, making the role of permutations unclear (Reviewers 1,2).
- Lemma 3.2 (called Theorem 3.2 in the text) requires zero‑mean weight distributions; this is not true for trained networks, so the bound may be inapplicable (Reviewers 1,2).
- Theorem 3.3 is stated without proof and requires unexplained polynomial properties of the network (Reviewers 1,2).  
- Overall, Theorem 3.1 is a trivial statement that barrier is bounded by parameter distance; Theorem 3.2 is the core claim, but it directly recycles results from random Euclidean matching problems (as cited by the authors). The theoretical contribution is therefore marginal (Reviewer 4).
- Because model parameters after training are not random, the bound in Theorem 3.2 may be too loose to be useful in practice (Reviewer 4).
- The analysis using entropy is rather trivial in hindsight: that different initializations give higher entropy (Fig. 2) and training shifts from uniform to bi‑modal (Fig. 3) are not deep insights. The theoretical/analytical contribution overall is thin (Reviewer 3).

**Experimental Issues**
- Section 4.2 uses a very toy setup (Polynomial Task, single‑output MLP, only first layer tested). Harder datasets and models are needed to support the claims (Reviewer 4).
- In Section 4.2, only showing the change of weight distribution before and after training does not establish a strong correlation between entropy and loss barrier; more carefully designed experiments are required (Reviewer 4).
- The explanation for why “Only Pruning” and Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH) fail to produce good fused networks is unclear; their failure is not predicted by the theory (Reviewer 4; also Reviewer 1 Q6: “Why does LTH not lead to good fused networks? Different matching algorithms should be able to combine two models generated by LTH.”)
- From Figure 5, the loss does not always first decrease then increase. For MLP on CIFAR‑10, the loss actually increases with higher pruning ratios, contradicting the behavior on other datasets/models; also train and test loss curves are not always consistent (Reviewer 4).
- Entropy values before and after pruning are not presented, making the claimed mechanism hard to evaluate (Reviewer 4).
- The argument that pruning by itself does not decrease the barrier is unconvincing: the barrier is normalized by the losses of the two individual models, and fine‑tuning after matching is known to always improve LMC. The experiment therefore does not directly show the desired result (Reviewers 1,2).
- Section 4.2 uses sigmoid activation, which is known to saturate into two corner cases (as noted in information bottleneck work); the resulting peaks in weight values may not indicate the intended effect (Reviewers 1,2).

**Presentation and Clarity**
- The paper is hard to follow and raises many questions (Reviewers 1,2). Reviewers note inconsistent terminology (*neuron entropy* vs *neural entropy*), mislabeling (Lemma 3.2 called Theorem 3.2), and unexplained assumptions from (Mei et al.) even in the appendix, making the paper not self‑contained (Reviewers 1,2).
- Figure 1 is sloppy: the shaded regions and their justification are unclear (Reviewers 1,2).
- The phrase “making neuron distribution gather around minima” is ambiguous (Reviewers 1,2).
- The claim that the proposed method “makes re‑basin easier” is not supported; the paper instead uses pruning to reduce entropy (Reviewers 1,2).
- Pruning does not integrate smoothly: the introduction presents it as an observation (“we observe that pruning can improve LMC, but what is the mechanism”), while the paper then directly proposes pruning as a method to decrease entropy (Reviewers 1,2).
- **Typo:** VGG11 and VGG16 are used interchangeably in Table 2 and Section 5 (Reviewer 1).
- The use of the barrier definition from Entezari et al. is ambiguous: how is α selected? In later work (Entezari) α=1/2 is used, yet the paper seems to use a different α; the discrepancy is not addressed (Reviewers 1,2).

**Remaining Unanswered Questions (overlapping with criticisms)**
- How does the entropy theory explain the findings in Ainsworth et al. that mapping found after training improves LMC at initialization, and the result of Entezari et al. that for wide networks LMC is possible at initialization? The experiments do not test the same initializations used in those works (Reviewers 1,2).
- For Sec 4.1, if models are randomly initialized, both models should be random guess classifiers and have no loss barrier; the existence of a barrier in that setting contradicts the expectation (Reviewer 4).
- Consider an untrained VGG on MNIST vs a trained VGG: trained models have lower entropy but higher loss barrier; this contradicts the claimed relationship (Reviewer 1, Q2).
- How are bias terms handled? They need to be zero for the theory to hold; if not, how is OTFusion extended (Reviewer 1, Q5)?
- What do the ‘/’ mean in Table 2? Why are models fine‑tuned only up to epoch 30? Base model accuracy should be included (Reviewer 1, Q7).
