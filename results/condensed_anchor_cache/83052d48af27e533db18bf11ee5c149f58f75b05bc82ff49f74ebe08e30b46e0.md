## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes using the CLIP vision-language model for online continual learning. The authors identify that naively tuning CLIP with all previous non-matching class texts as negatives causes asymmetric image-text matching and catastrophic forgetting. They introduce symmetric image-text (SIT) tuning, which removes those asymmetric negative texts (classes not in the current batch) from the contrastive loss. They also propose a new challenging setting, MiD-Blurry, that mixes multiple data distributions to simulate real-world non-stationary streams. Empirical results on several benchmarks show gains over baselines. Two reviewers rated the paper 5 (positive), while three rated it 3 (negative or weak reject), citing concerns about novelty, missing baselines, limited evaluation, and clarity.

### Strengths

- Simple and easy-to-understand idea (R2, R3).
- Significant empirical gain: 10 percentage points improvement over the AIT-CLIP baseline (R1).
- Generally achieves the best results among compared methods (R2, R3).
- Gradient norm and PCA analysis convincingly identifies a systematic bias toward newer classes and demonstrates the effect of asymmetric negative samples, providing intuitive evidence for the cause of forgetting and the effectiveness of SIT (R3, R4, R5).
- Introduces a more challenging online continual learning setting (MiD-Blurry) that simulates real-world non-stationary data streams, addressing limitations of existing benchmarks (R4, R5).
- Comprehensive and diverse datasets are used for evaluation, including zero-shot performance after online learning (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty and insufficient insight.** The SIT strategy is essentially a cross-entropy loss on the current batch, which is a straightforward first approach (R3). Regularizing loss for asymmetric examples has already been explored in prior work (Manco et al., 2022; Zolfaghari et al., 2021) (R5). The paper lacks deep insight into why removing asymmetric contrastive samples reduces bias, relying solely on empirical observation (R1). The problem of larger gradient norms for older classes corresponds to the known recency bias issue, where simpler solutions (e.g., reducing oversampling) exist (R3).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons.** The paper does not compare to several relevant CLIP-based continual learning methods, such as AttriCLIP (CVPR 2022) (R2) and other CLIP-based works that support an online setting (e.g., [1], [2] in R3). For open-world claims, non-VLM methods should also be evaluated (e.g., open-set recognition) (R1). Additional baselines from “Continual Learning in Open-vocabulary Classification with Complementary Memory Systems” (TMLR 2024) and “Anytime Continual Learning for Open Vocabulary Classification” (ECCV 2024) are suggested (R4).
- **Insufficient evaluation and scalability.** The largest datasets used are CUB200, StanfordCars, and Aircraft; the method’s scalability and robustness on larger-scale datasets are not tested (R3). The proposed MiD-Blurry setting still relies on standard, small datasets with high semantic correlation, which may not resemble real-case online CL scenarios (R5). The paper does not validate SIT on other online CL settings beyond the proposed one (R1).
- **Efficiency concerns not addressed.** The paper claims efficiency but provides no measurements of training or inference time (R3). As the number of classes grows, processing all class-label prompted sentences for each batch creates a computational burden that is not analyzed (R5).
- **Presentation and clarity issues.** Key sections are hard to follow: Lines 177–202 confuse the definition of task vs. time step, and the Gaussian probability distribution is not conditioned on t≥τ, making the meaning of a task unclear (R2). Figures are unclear: Figure 1 (right) is confusing (R2, R4), Figure 3 is not clearly explained (R2, R3), and Figure 4 is hard to follow (R4). Details for how plots (Fig. 3, 4, 5) are created are missing (R3).
- **Zero-shot performance degradation on some datasets.** After online learning, zero-shot accuracy on Food101 drops substantially; the paper provides no insight or discussion for this drop (R1, R4). The removal of cross-batch contrastive learning may harm the model’s ability to distinguish images from text labels of other datasets, impairing open-world classification (R4).
- **Class leakage concern.** Using CLIP for continual learning may leak class information from its pretraining stage, as class names could have been seen before (R1). The paper does not address this.
- **Technical inaccuracies.** Equation (3) contains a typo (the numerator should be Z_S/(Z_S+Z_A) for the L_A component) (R1, R2). The claim that existing CL methods are closed-ended because they require a pre-defined classifier dimension is incorrect; incremental classifiers can be added without knowing the total number of classes in advance (R5).
