## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LongMamba, a training-free technique to enhance Mamba's long-context capabilities. The authors categorize hidden state channels into local and global based on receptive field lengths, and hypothesize that exponential decay in global channels causes poor performance on extended sequences. LongMamba expands global channels' receptive fields by filtering out less important tokens (based on delta_t averaging) to maintain similar decay distributions as during training. Experiments on synthetic and real-world tasks (PG-19, passkey/document retrieval, LongBench) show improvements over vanilla Mamba and prior methods.

### Strengths
1. **Insightful discovery and root-cause analysis**: The categorization of Mamba channels into local/global based on receptive field lengths, and the hypothesis that exponential decay in global channels is the root cause of poor long-context performance, are interesting, meaningful, and bring valuable insight to the community.
2. **Simple, training-free context extension**: The proposed modification is simple and surprisingly enables Mamba to generalize to much longer contexts without fine-tuning. This is important for scalable, efficient models.
3. **Strong empirical results**: Extensive benchmarks show LongMamba consistently outperforms vanilla Mamba by a large margin on PG-19 language modeling, passkey/document retrieval, and LongBench.
4. **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with sufficient analysis and visualization (e.g., tidy Figure 2, well-illustrated channel categorization).

### Weaknesses
1. **Novelty concerns relative to DeciMamba**: LongMamba relies heavily on DeciMamba's framework. The token filtering idea based on averaged delta_t was proposed by DeciMamba; LongMamba merely improves by limiting this averaging to global channels. Some reviewers find the approach interesting and effective, but others consider it incremental. [Reviewer 2 Weakness 1, Reviewer 4 Weakness 1; minority is more negative on novelty]
2. **Threshold selection not systematically addressed**: The classification threshold for global vs. local channels (reported as 0-300-600-900-1200) is empirical. It is unclear whether these are percentiles of a fixed model. The lack of a formal tuning approach may limit generalizability across datasets or models. [Reviewer 1 Q4, Reviewer 2 Weakness 2]
3. **Method elaboration for decay distribution adjustment is incomplete**: Several details are unclear: (a) How are \(A'_t\) computed when the update is greater than the threshold (the dual of Eq. 17)? (b) How is \(g(S)\) computed (to satisfy Eq. 16)? (c) Is averaging across global channels performed over all global channels or independently per channel? These ambiguities affect reproducibility. [Reviewer 1 Q2,Q3; Reviewer 2 Weakness 3]
4. **Missing comparisons with transformer-based long-context models**: The core motivation is to address Mamba's poor long-context ability compared to transformers, but no direct comparison (e.g., Pythia, hybrid models) is provided. Including such comparisons—following Mamba's original experiment setting—would help gauge how much LongMamba bridges the gap. [Reviewer 3 Weakness, Reviewer 4 Weakness 2]
5. **Missing evaluation on Ruler benchmark**: Evaluating on standard long-context benchmarks like Ruler would be more convincing. [Reviewer 4 Weakness 3]
6. **Questions about generalization and scalability**: 
   - Is similar exponential decay observed in Mamba2, and does LongMamba improve there? [Reviewer 2 Q2]
   - Would the approach generalize to other SSMs including hybrid architectures? [Reviewer 2 Q3]
   - How do local/global channels behave on larger Mamba models? [Reviewer 3 Q3]
   - Does token filtering bring extra compute or memory cost? [Reviewer 3 Q2]
7. **Open question on further improvement with training**: Since LongMamba still lags behind transformer-based LLMs for long contexts, would allowing training further enhance performance? [Reviewer 4 Weakness 2]
8. **Additional missing experiments and details**:
   - Provide a cumulative graph of receptive fields for each channel, especially comparing training sequence length and a much longer sequence length. [Reviewer 1 Q5]
   - Report performance on specific LongBench tasks: 2wikimqa, TREC, TriviaQA, LCC, RepoBench-P (Reviewer 2 Q4) and the code category (Reviewer 3 Q1).
   - Expand Section 5 details on how \(A'\) are computed. [Reviewer 1]
   - Note that line 333 references Eq. 16, not E 18. [Reviewer 1 Q1]
