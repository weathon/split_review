## Merged Review

### Summary
Two reviewers rated this paper 6 (confidence 3 and 5), one rated it 8 (confidence 4). The higher-rated reviewer particularly emphasized the novelty and thoroughness of the work, while the lower-rated reviewers raised concerns about methodological ambiguities, generalizability, and comparisons with existing work. All reviewers acknowledge that γ-MoD introduces a Mixture-of-Depth adaptation to MLLMs using the ARank metric to identify redundant layers, reduces training and inference time with minor performance degradation, and is validated on multiple benchmarks. There is agreement that the approach is original and clearly motivated.

### Strengths
- Significant reduction in training and inference time by efficiently identifying and replacing redundant layers. For example, with a minor performance drop (-1.5%), training and inference time of LLaVA-HR are reduced by 31.0% and 53.2% respectively.
- The ARank metric is a novel and structured approach to select layers for MoD replacement, addressing the lack of guidance in existing MoD methods. One reviewer highlights that this is the first adaptation of MoD to MLLMs, which face unique multimodal challenges.
- The shared vision-language router and masked routing learning are thoughtfully designed for multimodal alignment; in particular, masked routing learning prevents critical tokens (e.g., question subjects) from being skipped, preserving the model’s ability to generate accurate answers.
- The method maintains the pre-training stage unchanged, contrasting with approaches that require pre-training from scratch (e.g., MoE structures), and is considered a plug-and-play adaptation (though see Weakness 3).
- The paper is clearly motivated and logically structured, with comprehensive theoretical analysis and empirical validations.
- Extensive experiments across multiple MLLM structures (LLaVA series) and benchmark datasets demonstrate generalizability and cost reduction. Analysis shows that γ-MoD leads MLLMs to focus on informative tokens and skip redundant ones. Up to 51.6% computational sparsity is achieved.
- The paper is mostly well-written, with a clear articulation of motivation and solutions.

### Weaknesses
1. **ARank accuracy depends on input sample quality**: The metric’s reliance on a small batch of samples may lead to biased redundancy assessment, especially in datasets with high intra-class variance or outliers, potentially causing suboptimal layer selection (Reviewer 1).
2. **ARank may overlook critical low-rank layers**: Low-rank attention maps might encode compressed but important contextual information, and discarding layers based solely on rank could lose essential information (Reviewer 1).
3. **Plug-and-play claim is misleading**: γ-MoD modifies the instruction tuning process and requires pre-training (e.g., on LCS-558K), so it cannot be directly applied to published instruction-tuned checkpoints. This hinders practical applicability (Reviewer 2; also Question 1). The authors should demonstrate how to apply γ-MoD to existing checkpoints.
4. **Limited generalizability beyond LLaVA structures**: Experiments focus on LLaVA; additional experiments on other MLLMs are needed to fully demonstrate generalizability (Reviewer 2; also Question 2).
5. **Methodological ambiguities** (Reviewer 2, Question 3):
   - The applied value of the pre-defined sparse target α in Equation 4 is unclear, and its relationship to the “routing ratio” and “skip ratio” is not explained.
   - In Equation 4, α is the sum of indicator functions but should be normalized to lie in [0,1]; the current formulation is not properly bounded.
   - Equation 8 places one-hot vectors inside a logarithm, which appears incorrect; the routing objective is hard to understand and requires detailed explanation.
6. **Comparison with simpler token pruning insufficient**: Prior work [1] (ECCV 2024) shows that simply pruning image tokens after early layers reduces FLOPs by >50% without performance drop and without extra training. γ-MoD’s pre-training and instruction tuning modifications may be too costly for acceleration; a detailed comparison and demonstration of exclusive advantages are needed (Reviewer 2; also Question 4).
7. **ARank is a repurposed version of HRank**: The connection to HRank (originally used for attention maps) should be discussed in detail (Reviewer 3).
8. **Insufficient ablation of routing mechanisms**: Only a binary routing (skip or not skip) is explored; more sophisticated strategies (e.g., weighted routing, adaptive thresholds) could make the study more comprehensive (Reviewer 3).
9. **Lacks comparison with other sparsity techniques**: No comparison with pruning or quantization (Reviewer 3).
10. **Table 5 presentation**: The table is difficult to compare; consider rearranging or highlighting optimal results (Reviewer 3).
11. **Mask Mq determination across tasks**: The binary mask Mq assigns zero to question tokens, but the location of question tokens varies per task/dataset; how this mask is designed and fixed for different tasks is not explained (Reviewer 3, Question 1).
12. **Implementation of “All layer” in Table 1**: How this variant is specifically implemented is unclear (Reviewer 3, Question 2).
13. **Sensitivity of ARank to hyperparameters**: The effect of batch size used for ARank estimation is not analyzed (Reviewer 3, Question 3).
14. **Threshold criteria for MoD conversion**: The choice of ARank threshold for deciding which layers to convert is not principled; a more systematic method is needed (Reviewer 3, Question 4).
15. **Quantitative statistics on rank distributions**: Figure 2 shows that layers with Rank < 110 are considered redundant, but more quantitative statistics beyond the figure would strengthen the analysis (Reviewer 2, minor point).
