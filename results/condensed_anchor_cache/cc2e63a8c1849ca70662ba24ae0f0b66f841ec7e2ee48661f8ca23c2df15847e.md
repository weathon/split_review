## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes L-PINN, a Langevin dynamics-based adaptive sampling method for PINNs, addressing limitations of high residual-based methods (instability with increasing model complexity). The authors provide theoretical analysis showing that high residual methods require tighter learning rate bounds and propose balanced sampling via Langevin dynamics. The method is evaluated on multiple PDEs (Burgers', Convection, Allen-Cahn) and compared against existing adaptive sampling approaches, showing improved or comparable relative L2 error and stability across varying model complexities and learning rates. However, several concerns are raised regarding experimental justification, baseline comparisons, computational cost, practical utility, and theoretical assumptions.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow for non-theory parts (R1, R2, R4). It presents a concise overview of the literature (R1).
- The proposed L-PINN method is novel and elegant, using Langevin dynamics to update collocation points instead of resampling (R4). It provides rigorous theoretical analysis establishing clear mathematical connections between sampling strategies and learning rate constraints, with detailed proofs (R2, R3).
- Empirical validation is thorough: experiments span multiple representative PDEs (Burgers', Convection, Allen-Cahn) (R1, R3). The paper carefully compares against various sampling methods and includes comprehensive ablation studies on learning rates and network depth (R3).
- Sample trajectory plots help observe the method's performance (R1). Figures and tables are clear (R2).

### Weaknesses
1. **Unconvincing deep network experiments**: The rationale for testing deep networks is unclear. Baselines achieve similar results with small networks, so using deeper networks for 1D PDEs is unnecessary; these PDEs do not require deep MLPs, and increasing depth introduces training instability (R1, R2). Figure 5 (deep 8-layer) is confusing: performance appears off for L-PINN contrary to the text, and it is unclear whether the plot shows error or the solution itself (R1, R4). The authors should clarify what Figure 5 shows (error or solution) (R4). Moreover, instead of deep MLPs, it might be more meaningful to adopt other architectures like attention or Fourier layers to demonstrate robustness for large models (R2).

2. **Insufficient explanation of the method**: The proposed L-PINN algorithm is not clearly explained; implementation details are lacking, making it difficult to reproduce (R1). The paper should provide a more detailed explanation of how the method works (R1).

3. **Missing computational cost comparison**: The paper lacks a detailed comparison of computational cost between L-PINN and baseline sampling methods (R1, R4). Intuitively, updating points according to the residual gradient seems more computationally intense than cheap resampling (R4). A systematic cost analysis is needed (R1, R4).

4. **Insufficient baseline setup and missing comparisons**: 
   - Hyperparameters of baseline methods (e.g., $k$ and $c$ in RAD) are not described; improper values could fail the methods, raising concern about whether baselines were well-trained (R2).
   - The paper should compare with RAR-D (from [1]) or justify why not (R1).
   - The framing of "Unresolved questions 2" about potential risks of high residual methods is already discussed experimentally by RAD; it is unclear what is new or unsolved by RAD with respect to this question (R2).

5. **Limited sensitivity analysis and practical utility of theory**:
   - The learning rate ablation study covers a limited range; it is unclear how methods perform at even smaller learning rates (R1). In Figure 4b, L-PINN only outperforms at learning rate 0.003, which might be due to randomness; more cases between 0.002 and 0.004 are needed, and standard deviations should be added (R2).
   - The theoretical contribution on learning rate bounds appears not very useful in practice: the steepness changes only slightly in Figures 3a and 4a, so simply picking a smaller learning rate would help convergence of current methods (R4).
   - L-PINN introduces two additional hyperparameters ($\beta$ and $l_L$) that require fine-tuning and highly influence performance (R4). A systematic sensitivity study for the step size $\tau$ is also missing (R4).
   - Theorem 4.1 relies on an asymptotic limit for reaching the collocation sample population. However, Appendix E shows no clear improvement in performance as $l_L$ increases, especially for small $\beta$; this suggests that reaching the collocation sample population is not particularly useful in practice (R4). The authors should explain this discrepancy (R4).

6. **Theoretical assumptions questionable for nonlinear PDEs**:
   - Assumption 3.1 (expressing PDE residuals as linear combinations of feature-mapped vectors) is too strong for nonlinear PDEs, though acceptable for linear PDEs (R3).
   - The paper relies on this assumption but does not clearly define how feature vectors $\phi(x)$ are obtained for nonlinear PDEs. Are they hidden layer outputs? If so, there is a disconnect between the theoretical framework (Assumption 3.2 about heavy-tailed feature vector distributions) and the empirical visualizations in Figure 6; the authors need to provide precise mathematical expressions for the visualized quantities and justify the assumption for nonlinear PDEs (R3).

7. **Overlooked prior work**: The paper does not discuss PirateNet [1], which addresses similar scaling challenges in training deep PINNs via adaptive skip connections; this relevant work should be acknowledged and discussed (R3).

8. **Limited to low-dimensional problems and simple PDE architectures**:
   - The method is only validated on low-dimensional PDEs; scalability to high-dimensional problems is not addressed (R1). Selection of collocation points in higher dimensions is a complex problem, and the paper does not discuss how the method scales with dimensionality (R1).
   - The method is not tested on multiscale systems of PDEs, which pose additional challenges in choosing collocation points (R1). Similarly, applicability to large models that truly need complex architectures (e.g., attention/Fourier layers) is not shown (R2).

9. **Presentation issues**: Section 5.2 experimental results are dense and difficult to follow; supporting details could be moved to the appendix (R3). Several typos: line 88 missing definition of $R_\theta(x)$ (R4); line 105 comma instead of period before "i.e." (R4); line 148 missing period before "Additionally" (R2, R4); line 430: for L-PINN, RAD, R3 and L*, "exact solution" should be "predicted solution" (R4); lines 460-466: results for Burgers' equation with 8 layers for Random-R and R3 should also be in boldface (R4).
