## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces two interpretable pre-trained transformer models (PPG-PT and ECG-PT) based on the GPT framework for heart time-series data (PPG and single-lead ECG). The authors demonstrate that the models develop attention patterns corresponding to physiologically meaningful cardiac cycle features, such as the dicrotic notch in PPG and the P-wave in ECG. Fine-tuning for atrial fibrillation (AF) classification yields AUCs of 0.99 (ECG) and 0.93 (PPG) on the MIMIC Perform AF dataset with 11 minutes of training, and a beat detector achieves an F1 of 98%. Interpretability analyses include aggregate attention maps, token clustering by phase, and head-specific responses.

### Strengths
- **Novel application of generative pre-training to heart time-series** – To the best of the reviewers’ knowledge, this is the first work applying GPT-style next-token prediction to representation learning for PPG and ECG, where contrastive learning has been dominant (Reviewer 2).
- **Rigorous and systematic interpretability analysis** – The attention mechanism analysis is very detailed, providing clear understanding of learned representations and how transformer layers operate on heart time-series data (Reviewers 2, 3). The focus on interpreting next-token generation and downstream classification is logical and well explained (Reviewer 3).
- **Extension of interpretability techniques from CV/NLP to physiological time-series** – Decoding attention heads for explanations, common in other domains, is seen as a contribution when applied to cardiac signals (Reviewer 3).
- **Subject-wise data split** – Avoids data leakage between training and testing (Reviewer 3).
- **Evaluation on two physiological signal types** – PPG and ECG (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
1. **Novelty questioned relative to prior work** – PPG-GPT has been explored across multiple tasks (Chen et al., 2024). It is unclear how the current work differs methodologically (Reviewer 1). A more positive reviewer (Reviewer 2) considered the generative pre-training idea novel, but the discrepancy in assessment should be noted: one reviewer found the novelty questionable, while two others saw it as a contribution.
2. **Insufficient comparisons** – The manuscript does not compare against PPG-GPT (Chen et al., 2024) on the same tasks. Comparison with one baseline per PPG and ECG is only in the appendix, and setups differ (Reviewer 1). Additionally, no comparison with other representation learning methods such as contrastive learning-based CLOCS (Kiyasseh et al., 2021) is provided (Reviewer 2).
3. **Limited downstream tasks** – Only AF classification and beat detection are evaluated. The proposed approach may rely on short-term dependencies (beat-to-beat analysis) and its generalizability to tasks requiring longer temporal context (e.g., sleep staging) is unclear (Reviewer 3). More downstream tasks are needed to assess the learned representations (Reviewer 2).
4. **Lack of performance variability reporting** – Experiments were not run multiple times (e.g., 5 seeds or 5-fold) to report standard deviation/IQR (Reviewer 1).
5. **Missing metrics for AF classification** – Additional metrics (sensitivity, specificity, false-positive/negative rates, F1 score) should be reported (Reviewer 1).
6. **Clinical validation of interpretability claims** – Most examples rely on machine learning community judgment; no clinical experts were involved to validate that attention aligns with physiologically meaningful features, e.g., whether the examples shown guarantee the presence of AF (Reviewer 1).
7. **Datasets not comprehensive** – The CinC2020 dataset is superseded by CinC2021 (more data); larger datasets like CODE-15% exist (Reviewer 2). Clean, high-quality data was used; behavior on noisy data is not explored, despite the availability of high-performance models on clean data (e.g., F1 0.96 with DeepBeat, Torres-Soto et al., 2020) (Reviewer 1).
8. **Clarity of methodology** – Attention maps as an interpretability tool have been explored previously (Zhao et al., 2023); it should be clarified what is methodologically novel (Reviewer 1). The use of `findpeaks` is poorly structured; pseudocode for methodology choices is recommended (Reviewer 1).
9. **Unclear token generation process** – It is not clearly stated whether tokens represent whole 10-second segments or isolated beats, nor how generation continues to produce outputs like in Figure 1 (Reviewer 3).
10. **Minor issues** – Figure 3 caption needs correction (a–d) (Reviewer 3). More references on GPT interpretability (e.g., Creswell et al., 2022; Ben Melech Stan et al., 2024) would give a broader picture (Reviewer 3).
