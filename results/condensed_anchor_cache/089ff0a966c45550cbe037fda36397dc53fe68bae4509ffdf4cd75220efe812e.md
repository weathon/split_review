## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates when chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting reduces LLM performance, drawing inspiration from cognitive psychology tasks where explicit verbal thinking hurts human performance. It identifies three task types (implicit statistical learning, visual recognition, classifying patterns with exceptions) where CoT significantly drops accuracy (e.g., up to 36.3% absolute for o1-preview vs GPT-4o) and three tasks where human constraints do not generalize and CoT helps. The methodology spans multiple state-of-the-art models and includes comparisons with zero-shot prompting.

### Strengths
- Novel and timely contribution to understanding when CoT harms model performance, of high interest to the community.
- Broad experimental results across diverse state-of-the-art models.
- Well-structured and clearly written; logic and framework are detailed.
- Original interdisciplinary approach bridging cognitive psychology and LLM evaluation.
- Categorizes tasks systematically into those benefiting or suffering from CoT, providing practical guidance.
- Highlights important limitations of default CoT usage, urging caution in deployment.
- Framework is sufficiently detailed and explained.

### Weaknesses
- **Post-hoc framing and vagueness of criterion B**: The central hypothesis that tasks satisfy condition (ii) (“human constraints generalize to AI”) feels post-hoc and is difficult to apply in advance. R1 notes that criterion (ii) allows substantial room for interpretation and post-hoc fitting. R2 argues it is arbitrary and can retrospectively explain any result, since LLMs are both similar and different from humans in many respects (e.g., multimodal LLM processes images token-by-token, unlike humans; implicit statistical learning justification is tenuous given that LLMs may retrofit explanations to answers, citing Turpin et al. 2022). R2 strongly recommends either removing this criterion or thoroughly investigating how to apply it to arbitrary tasks, and scaling down claims. (Minority reviewer R2 is significantly more critical of this point.)
- **Insufficient controls and ablation depth**: (multiple reviewers)
  - Lack of distractor controls to normalize token count between CoT and zero-shot conditions; performance drops might be due to extra text rather than verbal reasoning itself. R2 notes pilot experiments with distractors generated noise, undermining mechanistic explanations.
  - No systematic investigation of tasks where CoT hurts LLMs but would not be expected to hurt humans (e.g., text classification, encyclopedic knowledge; Sprague et al. 2024). This undermines the claim that condition A is predictive.
  - Additional prompting variations (e.g., attempts to improve CoT on the three tasks where it hurts) are missing.
  - No ablation studies to understand the effect of different components.
  - Reverse order of answer and explanation not tested (R2 suggestion).
- **Lack of detailed error analysis and example outputs**: (R1, R3, R4) The paper would benefit from representative CoT trajectories showing model failures (e.g., grammar task), error case comparisons between CoT and direct prompting, and real CoT outputs for each task. R4 specifically asks for a case study in the appendix.
- **Missing related work and contextualization**:
  - Inverse scaling (McKenzie et al., 2023) is highly related and should be discussed.
  - Recent works on CoT robustness (e.g., arXiv:2410.05229) are not cited; the paper should distinguish its findings from these.
- **Unclear experimental details and reproduction issues**:
  - Hyperparameter settings (decode strategy, max_new_token) are not reported, hindering reproduction.
  - For Table 6, the poor performance of Llama-3.1-70B under CoT is not explained; R4 suspects token repetition and requests the actual outputs and hyperparameters.
  - Inconsistent terminology: LMMs vs VLMs.
  - Models listed as “open-source” (Anthropic, OpenAI) are actually closed-source (also noted in R1 nit: line 356: open-source -> closed source).
- **Specific task concerns**:
  - The final task (aggregating features) presents statements for only 1 second to humans, which is potentially unfair; a simultaneous display baseline would be relevant.
  - Table 4’s conclusion that “a reasonable level of zero-shot performance is a prerequisite for CoT to reduce performance” is ambiguous; how to define “reasonable level”? R4 points out it doesn’t hold for Claude models.
- **Missing code/replication setup** (R3).
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms** (included as weaknesses):
  - R1’s question: Is there a pattern where CoT hurts when zero-shot is already good? Suggests controlling for baseline performance.
  - R3’s question: Could poor CoT performance in multimodal settings be due to insufficient multimodal model training rather than the paper’s claims?
  - R4’s questions about real outputs, Table 4 definition, and Llama-3.1-70B performance (covered above).
