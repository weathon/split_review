## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates training online RL agents entirely within learned world models derived from uncurated offline data, aiming to avoid conservative penalties typically used in offline RL. The proposed method combines an ensemble of independently trained world models (simple MLPs) with Unsupervised Environment Design (UED) via Prioritized Level Replay (PLR), treating each world model as a different level to create a curriculum. It also explores Domain Randomization (DR) variants. The method is evaluated on vector-based locomotion tasks (Cartpole, Pendulum, Hopper, Halfcheetah) using a curated dataset claimed to have more uniform state/action coverage than D4RL. Results show sample-efficiency gains over CQL and a vanilla single world model baseline. Three reviewers rated the work 5 (positive but with noted limitations) and two rated it 3 (skeptical of empirical evidence and presentation).

### Strengths
- Original combination of ensemble world models and UED (PLR) to enable full-length rollout training inside world models without hand-crafted penalties.
- Training entirely within the world model on full-length rollouts, avoiding online interaction with the real environment.
- No online tuning in the environment; uses held-out world models for hyperparameter tuning.
- Well-designed experiments with clear assumptions, comparisons against baselines (CQL, SAC_N, vanilla world model), and ablation studies on data scale and method components.
- Informative figures, especially Figures 9 and 10, showing how the curated dataset differs from D4RL in state/action coverage.
- Demonstrates sample-efficiency gains over CQL and vanilla world model baseline on the curated dataset.
- Timely and relevant motivation: reducing reliance on task-specific simulators by using offline data to train world models.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited scope and generalizability**:
   - Only tested on simple vector-based locomotion tasks (Cartpole, Pendulum, Hopper, Halfcheetah). No experiments on more complex environments like Ant-Maze, robot arm manipulation, or visual domains (Atari, Procgen, Meta-World). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5)
   - World model is a simple MLP with no history (current state and action as input), far from SOTA world model architectures (RSSM, Transformers, diffusion models). This raises concerns about scalability to partially observable or visual environments, and contradicts the paper’s framing of a “generalist world model.” (Reviewers 3, 4, 5)
   - The term “world models” is misleading; the paper does not handle visual or partially observable data, unlike seminal works (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018). (Reviewer 2)

2. **Insufficient experimental support and comparisons**:
   - Only Hopper is shown in detail; comprehensive results across D4RL tasks are missing. (Reviewer 2)
   - Baselines are limited: only CQL and SAC_N are compared. Missing comparisons with other offline MBRL methods (MOPO, MBPO, Planning with Diffusion, Dreamer, IRIS) and sample-efficient BC methods (ROT, MCNN). (Reviewers 2, 3)
   - Returns are not normalized, making it hard to judge performance relative to random/expert or compare with other papers. (Reviewer 3)
   - In Figures 4-6, DR methods perform well, and PLR methods do not show clear advantages over DR. (Reviewer 4)
   - Across most environments, ensemble methods (PLR, DR) learn faster but reach the same final performance as a single world model. (Reviewer 5)

3. **Method clarity and motivation**:
   - The method section is confusing: Equation (5) and the definition of δ are unclear; how it approximates regret is not explained. Figure 1 is hard to understand; no pseudocode is provided. (Reviewers 4, 5)
   - The paper does not clearly distinguish its proposed method from the six variations tested (PLR, PLR_PVL, DR, DR_STEP, DR_PROB). (Reviewer 5)
   - The motivation for treating each world model as a level in UED is not well justified. It is unclear how varying the quality of offline data translates to a meaningful curriculum for policy learning. (Reviewer 2)
   - The claim that D4RL is “adversarial in data coverage” (line 55) is not justified; D4RL already offers datasets with various coverage types (e.g., medium, medium-replay, medium-expert). The dataset construction method (recording transitions from randomness to expertise) appears similar to D4RL. (Reviewer 4)
   - The paper’s framing is misleading: the problem is offline model-based RL, not “online RL in world models.” Many online MBRL methods (e.g., MBPO, Dreamer, TD-MPC) already train agents entirely with imagined trajectories. (Reviewers 2, 4)

4. **Missing ablations and analysis**:
   - No ablation on the number of world models. The paper uses 100 world models for Hopper, which is excessive and raises computational concerns, but no analysis of diminishing returns is provided. (Reviewers 1, 2, 5)
   - Checkpoint frequency for dataset construction appears uniform (Figure 2), despite claims of heuristic selection; distribution changes little after the agent converges. (Reviewer 4)
   - Additional cost (training and evaluation) of the ensemble is not reported, raising questions about practical applicability. (Reviewer 5)

5. **Writing and presentation issues**:
   - Paper structure is unbalanced: Preliminaries is too long, Methods is too brief. Introduction lacks clear contributions. (Reviewers 2, 4)
   - Numerous typos and citation errors (e.g., line 131: “This setting Furthermore”; line 136: “the policy’s minimizes”; line 305: “Prioritized Level Replay as described in with an”; incorrect citation format in lines 51, 53). (Reviewers 1, 4)
   - Discussion section on dataset distributions appears after Related Work, instead of as motivation. (Reviewer 5)
   - Appendix A.3 is empty. (Reviewer 3)
   - Paper does not use the required ICLR 2025 template (rendering of `\ref` and `\cite` commands is incorrect). (Reviewer 4)

6. **Reviewer disagreement**: Two reviewers (score 3) found the empirical evidence lacking and the presentation poor, stating that the method’s advantages are not clearly demonstrated and the paper overclaims. Three reviewers (score 5) found the work novel and promising but acknowledged significant limitations as listed above.
