## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a mask-based goal-conditioning method for goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (GCRL) in robotic reaching and grasping. It uses text-based directives and a pre-trained detection model (Grounded-DINO) to generate object masks that serve as goal specifications and enable a new reward function based on masked-pixel counts. The method is evaluated in a custom simulation environment with an eye-in-hand MuJoCo setup on a grasp-in-clutter task. All four reviewers rate the paper 3 (borderline). They identify strengths in identifying goal specification challenges and showing performance gains, but major weaknesses in novelty, evaluation rigor, comparisons with existing works, real-world feasibility, and presentation clarity.

### Strengths
- The paper identifies key challenges in goal specification for vision-based robotic manipulation with textual commands: generalizability to novel objects and the need for dense rewards.
- The mask-based goal representation is a novel approach that mitigates the pattern gap of RGB images and improves generalization compared to one-hot vector encodings.
- The mask-augmented reward leads to faster convergence and a high (~90%) grasp success rate for both in- and out-of-distribution objects.
- The authors perform comprehensive experiments that compare various design choices and configurations (e.g., mask types, reward variants).
- The methodology is technically sound and the paper is generally easy-to-read.

### Weaknesses
- The task (vision-based pick-up in clutter) has been extensively studied (e.g., Dex-Net series, DexGraspNet 2.0), yet the paper lacks discussion or comparison with these works, making the contribution appear incremental.
- The claimed “new environment” contribution is not significant; setting up egocentric cameras on existing benchmarks is straightforward.
- Evaluation is naïve: the object set is small, and the results do not demonstrate robustness or real-world deployability. Expanding the object set and testing sensitivity to mask noise (e.g., erosion/dilation) is needed.
- The mask-based goal is time-variant (goal region changes during the rollout), while the compared one-hot and RGB goals remain fixed; this may provide additional spatial information and makes the comparison unfair.
- Performance drops significantly when using inferred masks (Table 2, Grounded-DINO), yet the paper does not analyze the impact of imprecise masks (e.g., different noise levels).
- The paper lacks comparisons with related baselines, including previous reward-shaping techniques and other GCRL methods. The proposed representation should be applied to existing works for objective benchmarking.
- The method relies on ground-truth bounding boxes or a pre-trained detection model (Grounded-DINO), which limits feasibility for real-world applications where precise masks are hard to obtain. Efficiency during rollout (episode time) is also unclear.
- The overall novelty is low: the work repackages existing GCRL techniques without substantial improvements or new theoretical insights, and does not fundamentally advance the field.
- Presentation issues: equation numbering is reused (lines 203–205), Figure 2 lacks detailed explanation, and the introduction should have a dedicated paragraph summarizing contributions. Related work on GCRL and manipulation benchmarks is not fully reviewed.
- The method’s generalizability is questionable: grasping from only 2D vision may harm fine-grained action planning; it is unclear if the approach extends to tasks like pick-and-place. The reason for using rectangular masks instead of precise masks is not justified.
- The analysis of why Grounded-DINO masks produce poor performance is insufficient.
