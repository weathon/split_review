## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a dataset pruning method designed to preserve adversarial robustness. It first theoretically analyzes how existing pruning methods lead to non-smooth loss surfaces, increasing vulnerability to attacks. Two innovations are proposed: (1) a Frequency-Selective Excitation Network (FSE-Net) that dynamically selects important frequency components to smooth the loss surface and reduce storage requirements; (2) a “Joint-entropy” score for selecting stable and informative samples (referred to as JE, JELF when combined with LF). Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-1K using ResNet-18/34 show that the method outperforms state-of-the-art pruning algorithms across various adversarial attacks (AutoAttack, PGD-20, C&W). For example, on CIFAR-10 at 80% pruning, the method achieves 58.19% accuracy under AutoAttack vs. 42.98% for previous methods. On CIFAR-100 at 50% pruning, the JELF variant achieves 22.85% under AutoAttack, compared to 26.61% for full-dataset adversarial training (TDFAT) and 17.92% for CCSFEM.

### Strengths
- **Novel and well-motivated problem:** The paper identifies that current data pruning methods ignore adversarial robustness, providing a new lens for evaluating pruning. (Reviewers 1-5)
- **Frequency-Selective Excitation Network (FSE-Net):** Dynamically selects essential frequency components to smooth the loss surface, enhancing robustness while potentially reducing storage needs, making it suitable for resource-limited devices. (R1, R3)
- **Joint Entropy-based sample selection:** Selects stable and informative samples based on training dynamics, improving both robustness and performance of the pruned model. (R1, R3)
- **Low-cost adversarial robustness:** Achieves significant savings in training data without sacrificing a major amount of robustness compared to full adversarial training, and does not rely on generating adversarial examples during pruning. (R2, R5)
- **Theoretical motivation:** Data selection from the perspective of loss curvature and surface is aligned with the essence of adversarial training. (R3, R4)
- **Breadth of datasets:** Experiments cover CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-1K. (R4)
- **Reduced training cost:** The method reduces training set size and does not depend on a specific adversarial attack, improving efficiency. (R5)

### Weaknesses
- **Assumptions in Lemma 1:** The derivation assumes \(\frac{\partial f_{\theta}(\tilde{x})}{\partial \theta}\) is bounded under adversarial perturbations, which is overly restrictive. The paper does not provide supporting evidence that mechanisms preventing gradient explosion in standard training guarantee boundedness when the input is an adversarial example. A more rigorous mathematical proof is needed. (R1)
- **Missing definitions and unclear formulations:**
  - Joint Entropy is mentioned in Abstract and Introduction but never explicitly defined in the method section. (R1, R3)
  - The “Reward Score” on page 6 is confusing; its relationship to Joint Entropy and the exploration/exploitation phases is unclear. (R3)
  - “High energy” and “low energy” appear first without a precise threshold definition. (R1)
  - Pseudo-code lacks a definition for \(\hat{X}_{i,j}\), making the use of DCT coefficients unclear. (R1)
- **Unclear relationship between components:** The paper does not clearly explain how FSE-Net, Joint Entropy (JE), JELF, and LF interact. For example, is Joint Entropy estimated before/after/by FSE-Net? What is the relationship between FSE-Net and the Target Network? A flowchart or equivalent diagram is needed. (R1, R3)
- **Equation 6 concern:** Derivatives are applied only to correctly classified samples. If correct classifications decrease during updates, incorrect samples cannot guide parameter updates; this may hinder recovery from poor initializations or temporary accuracy drops. (R1)
- **Missing related work comparisons:** Several closely related works are not compared or discussed:
  - “Adversarial Coreset Selection for Efficient Robust Training” (IJCV/ECCV’22)
  - “Less is More: Data Pruning for Faster Adversarial Training” (AAAI-23 workshop SafeAI)
  - “Adversarial training with informed data selection” (EUSIPCO’22)
  - “Efficient Adversarial Contrastive Learning via Robustness-Aware Coreset Selection” (NeurIPS’23)
  - Sabre (spectral decomposition and energy-based filtering) (R2)
  - “Adversarial Weight Perturbation Helps Robust Generalization” (NeurIPS’20) and “Theoretically Principled Trade-off between Robustness and Accuracy” (ICML’19) for evaluating different optimization goals and loss curvature optimization. (R3)
- **Limited evaluation scope:**
  - Only ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10/100 and ResNet-34 on ImageNet-1K are used. The architecture change is not justified. Experiments on MobileNet, ShuffleNet, or EfficientNet (more relevant for resource-constrained environments) are missing. (R4, one reviewer: R4)
  - Transferability of the selected coreset across different architectures (e.g., selected by ResNet-18, trained on ViT) is not evaluated. (R3)
  - Only l∞-norm attacks are tested; evaluations on l2-AA, l1-AA, and sAA (l0-bounded) are missing. (R5)
- **Qualitative and quantitative support lacking:**
  - Figure 1 does not clearly demonstrate that frequency pruning helps the model focus on shape over textural details; selection criteria for examples is missing. (R4)
  - Figure 2 is qualitative; smoothness is not defined quantitatively. Average loss landscapes with standard deviations over multiple runs are needed (see A.4 in [1]). (R4)
  - No averages or standard deviations reported for main results (at least 3-5 runs are expected). (R4)
  - No visualization of images generated by FSE-Net or selected by JE-score, nor analysis of their characteristics. (R5)
- **Incomplete reporting and missing details:**
  - Appendix E lacks detailed structural information (layer configurations, activation functions, parameter counts) for FSE-Net, hindering reproducibility. (R1)
  - Training setup is insufficiently described in the main text; a link to a detailed appendix is expected. (R4)
  - The number of epochs for full dataset vs. coreset training is not clarified. Figure 3 suggests coreset training uses 500 epochs vs. 200 for full data, making “Time per epoch” in Table 2 inadequate; total training time may not decrease. The table title should indicate if epochs differ. (R5)
  - For ImageNet-1K, only 1000 randomly selected validation points are used for AutoAttack evaluation; no justification is given for why 1000 is sufficient. (R4)
  - Magic numbers (ε, learning rate, iterations) are not justified; no hyperparameter tuning process is described. (R4)
- **Skepticism about robustness without adversarial training:** One reviewer (R5) is skeptical that a model trained on carefully selected but not adversarially perturbed data can achieve robustness, and requests analysis of loss landscape flatness in the input and parameter space (as in Stutz et al. 2021 and Liu et al. 2020). Other reviewers find the approach promising but also note that accuracy under attacks is still relatively low compared to full adversarial training. (R5, R2)
- **Storage reduction claim not justified:** FSE-Net selects frequency components but does not reduce the number of training samples or image dimensions; the claim of reduced storage requirements needs quantitative explanation. (R5)
- **Additional minor issues:**
  - Tables 1 and 3 are complex; showing differences from the baseline would be clearer. (R4)
  - Figure 3b and 3c contain black lines that are not explained. (R4)
  - Line 55 has a double full stop and missing appendix section reference. (R4)
  - Line 178 uses “Generation error” without definition (presumably “Generation loss”). (R4)
  - The practicality of the method for larger datasets is questioned, as well as the feasibility of the LF pruning approach at scale. (R2)
  - Adaptive attacks have not been considered. (R2)
