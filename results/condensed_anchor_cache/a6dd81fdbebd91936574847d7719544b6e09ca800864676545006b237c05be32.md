## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies convergence and generalization of gradient methods for deep MLPs with smooth activations (e.g., quadratic) in binary classification. Using algorithmic stability and Hessian analysis along the GD path, the authors derive tighter, width-independent excess-risk bounds that improve over Rademacher-complexity bounds and previous NTK-based results. For noiseless NTK-separable data with margin γ, the test error is bounded as e^{O(L)}/(γ² n) under only poly-logarithmic width. For noisy data, optimal excess risk is achieved under polynomial width. A separate analysis for the XOR distribution with constant-width one-hidden-layer quadratic nets shows that SGD with large step-size η=m achieves perfect test accuracy after ⌈log(d)⌉ iterations with n=O(d) samples. Experiments on MNIST/FashionMNIST with a 2-hidden-layer network validate the bounds.

### Strengths
- **Clearly written and well organized** (R1).
- **Novel, tighter generalization bounds for smooth-activation networks** that are width-independent and improve over prior ReLU-network bounds (O(1/n) vs. O(1/√n)) (R1, R2, R4).
- **Improved over-parameterization requirement**: the NTK regime is shown to hold under far more relaxed conditions (|w*−w₀| ≲ m^{O(1/L)}), implying constant or poly-logarithmic width suffices (R2, R4).
- **Extension to noisy data** with optimal excess risk under mild width conditions (R2).
- **XOR learning with linear sample complexity**: constant-width network with quadratic activation and large step-size η=m achieves perfect test accuracy after ⌈log(d)⌉ iterations using n=O(d) samples (R1, R2). Experiments confirm the logarithmic dependency on d (R1).
- **Overall contribution bridges theory and practice** (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Experimental limitations**: Experiments use only a small 2-hidden-layer network, and expectations over the training set S are estimated with a single realization of S (R1).
- **Novelty concern regarding Hessian bounding technique**: The method to bound the spectral norm of the Hessian by the distance from initialization is not new, limiting the technical novelty (R1). Two other reviewers (R3, R4) also request a clearer explanation of how the Hessian structure is exploited and how it leads to the improved width bounds; they ask for high-level intuition and comparison with prior Hessian-based analyses (R3, R4). *Note: R1 (score 8) considered the technique not new, while R4 (score 6) found the improvement impressive but wanted more detail – a contrast in assessment.*
- **Unclear if improvements are due to smoothness or proof limitations**: The paper does not clarify whether the improved bounds fundamentally require smooth activations or if ReLU networks could ultimately achieve the same rates with better proof techniques (R1).
- **Exponential depth dependence in over-parameterization**: Theorem 2.1 requires width Ω(ρ^{*6L+4}) with ρ = O(log n / γ). This results in dependence Ω((log n/γ)^{6L+4}), which grows exponentially in depth L and makes results impractical for deep networks (R2).
- **Unclear notations and missing details**: 
  - The definition of S in Eq. (5) and what “expectation over S” means are not explained (R2).
  - In Section 2.1.2, the noise generation process is not described (R2).
- **Assumption 1 (NTK separability) implicitly ties width and separability**: The separability condition is defined over random tangent features at initialization; larger width trivially increases the chance of separability, obscuring the true relationship between width and the method’s effectiveness (R2).
- **Counter-intuitive special case for Eq. (5)**: For n=1, Eq. (5) implies that any parameter achieving zero training error yields zero generalization error, which seems unreasonable (R2).
- **Potential technical errors**:
  - In Eq. (6), plugging in Ĥ ≤ 4ρ^{*2}/(ηT) should include an additional log T factor from summing 1/t for t=1,…,T−1 (R2).
  - The proof claims to use Lemma B.3 of Lei & Ying (2020), but that lemma does not appear in the cited paper; moreover, the leave-one-out error there is defined by replacing the i-th sample with another random sample, not deleting it (R2).
- **Table mislabeling**: The table on page 2 is labeled “Table 2” but should be “Table 1” (R3).
- **Disconnect of Subsection 2.2**: The XOR analysis in 2.2 uses a different problem setting (quadratic activation, large step size) and appears disconnected from the NTK-based analysis of earlier sections; the paper does not clarify how the earlier techniques link to the XOR results (R3).
- **Step-size restriction in Theorem 2.4**: The step-size is set equal to the network width m. It is unclear whether it can be arbitrarily large or if there are restrictions (R3).
- **Lack of practical implications**: The paper does not discuss how the non-convexity of the loss is handled in the analysis, nor what the results imply for practitioners (R4).
