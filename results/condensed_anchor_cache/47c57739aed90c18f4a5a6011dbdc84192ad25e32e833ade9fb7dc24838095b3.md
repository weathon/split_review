## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a feature-level instance attribution (FLIA) method that extends TracIn by identifying important features whose perturbation leads to large changes in the instance-level influence (IL) value. The method is evaluated on image classification tasks. Reviewers note that the problem (combining feature and instance attribution) is interesting and well-motivated, and the paper provides mathematical derivation and extensive experiments. However, all three reviewers identify significant weaknesses in clarity, experimental rigor, and novelty, and two of the three explicitly mention a directly relevant prior work (Pezeshkpour et al. 2022) that weakens the claim of being first. The paper lacks direct experimental validation of FLIA’s effectiveness, relying on indirect evidence, and the evaluation metrics (e.g., CDCI) are poorly defined. The writing and presentation have structural issues, inconsistent terminology, and figures referenced out of order.

### Strengths
- The paper investigates an interesting and well-motivated problem that combines feature and instance attribution (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- It discovers that TDIA results can be altered by very small perturbations, and these changes can significantly affect the model’s decision-making process without altering sample confidence, leading to the proposed FLIA method (Reviewer 3).
- The authors provide rigorous mathematical derivation, introduce the calculation process of IL value, and derive the contribution of samples at a fine-grained feature level (Reviewer 3).
- Extensive experiments are conducted across a variety of evaluation metrics and image classification settings (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing directly relevant prior work:** A previously published method [Pezeshkpour et al. 2022] that exactly combines feature and instance attribution is not cited, significantly undermining the paper’s novelty claim (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3 also questions the "first study" claim and requests a clear comparison with existing feature attribution methods; Reviewer 3 asks why existing feature attribution methods fail to achieve fine-grained analysis and recommends a detailed comparison).
- **Unclear definitions and inconsistent terminology:**  
  - The concept of “model confidence” is introduced without explanation but used as core evidence for Argument 1 (Reviewer 2).  
  - The “confidence difference correlation index (CDCI)” is introduced in the next section (supporting Argument 2) without clarity on whether it is the same as the earlier metric; the description (lines 405–407) is particularly unclear (Reviewer 2).  
  - Terminology is inconsistent (e.g., Figure 1 uses “altered” while Argument 1 uses “modified”) (Reviewer 2).
- **Core arguments lack clarity and rigorous definition:** What does it mean for an IL value to be “modified” and why is that desirable? (Reviewer 2). The three core arguments need clearer definition and connection to the core logic (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of direct experimental validation of FLIA:** The validation relies entirely on indirect evidence; the practical applicability of FLIA requires further explanation or experimentation (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 notes it is unclear whether FLIA itself is being evaluated, as experiments consistently refer to “IL values”.
- **Experiment setup and results not convincing:**  
  - Experiment A results are presented without a clear description of the setup (Section 4.2), making results difficult to interpret (Reviewer 1).  
  - Experiment A does not report repeated experiments or significance analysis (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 2 also asks whether other random seeds were tested).  
  - Experiment B uses a self-defined evaluation metric (CDCI) without a clear formula definition (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 2 asks for properties and implementation details).
- **Writing and presentation issues:**  
  - Figures 1 and 2 are referenced in the wrong order (Reviewer 3).  
  - Figure 1 contains a lot of content but lacks detailed explanation (Reviewer 3).  
  - The method section mixes prior work and new contributions, making it unclear what is original (Reviewer 2).  
  - The code is provided but is dense and lacks comments, even in the example notebook (Reviewer 2).  
- **Related work structure:** The categorization of TDIA methods (retraining-based vs. gradient-based) in Sections 2.3 and 2.4 does not clearly align with the initial framework; authors should revise or clarify how those additional methods relate to the outlined framework (Reviewer 3).
- **Minority reviewer note:** Reviewer 2 rates contribution higher (3) than Reviewer 1 (2) and Reviewer 3 (2), and finds the method interesting but with major structural problems, while Reviewer 1 and 3 are more critical of novelty and experimental rigor. However, Reviewer 2 also gives a soundness score of 1, the lowest, due to unclear definitions and missing experimental validation.
