## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviewers acknowledge that Sentinel targets an important gap by modeling both temporal and inter-channel dependencies in multivariate time series, and that the multi-patch attention mechanism offers a fresh perspective by linking patching to multi-head splitting. Code is provided, writing is clear, and ablations are reasonable. However, the reviewers collectively identify major shortcomings: the contributions are incremental, performance does not reach state-of-the-art (CARD consistently outperforms Sentinel despite its added complexity), the multi-patch attention lacks justification and visualizations, and the experiments are incomplete (missing short-term forecasting, parameter sensitivity, recent baselines such as TimeXer and TimeMixer). One reviewer was notably more positive (score 5), praising the presentation and competitiveness, while the other three (score 3) were more critical, especially on novelty and experimental sufficiency.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and clearly presented; code is available for reproducibility.
- The model is designed to capture both temporal and inter-channel dependencies, a crucial aspect of multivariate time series forecasting.
- The multi-patch attention mechanism replaces standard multi-head attention by leveraging the patching process; the connection between multi-head splitting and multi-patch splitting is insightful.
- Ablation studies reasonably demonstrate the contribution of individual components.
- Comparisons with numerous baselines are provided.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The contributions are considered incremental. Multi-patch attention provides only marginal improvement, and separately modeling temporal and channel dimensions is not new. The method largely stitches together existing ideas without a clear unifying innovation. Even the most positive reviewer acknowledges this.
- **Insufficient justification of multi-patch attention**: The paper does not convincingly explain why multi-patch attention outperforms multi-head attention. No theoretical insights or visualizations (e.g., learned attention patterns) are provided to substantiate the design.
- **Performance not state-of-the-art**: Sentinel does not achieve SOTA; it is often second best. CARD clearly outperforms it on numerous datasets, despite Sentinel having added model complexity (e.g., a specialized channel encoder).
- **Missing experiments and comparisons**: Short-term forecasting is not evaluated; parameter sensitivity analysis is lacking; recent baselines such as TimeXer and TimeMixer are omitted. Compared to similar ICLR papers (CARD, iTransformer, PatchTST), the experimental setup is less extensive (fewer datasets and settings).
- **Encoder-decoder design choices not convincingly justified**: The ablation study (Table 3) provides limited insight into why Multi-Patch Channel attention is used in the encoder and Multi-Patch Time in the decoder. The possibility of learning both dependencies simultaneously is not explored.
- **Lack of error bars in Figure 3**: Variability in results is not shown, reducing clarity.
- **Inference limitation from final projection**: The final layer flattens all token embeddings and maps them to the output, implying a separate model must be trained for each forecast horizon.
- **Unclear advantage over competitors, especially CARD**: The paper fails to clearly articulate advantages (e.g., efficiency, performance with limited data) over existing methods.
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 5) was more positive about the writing and competitiveness, but still acknowledged the above limitations. The other three reviewers (score 3) found the contributions and experiments insufficient.
