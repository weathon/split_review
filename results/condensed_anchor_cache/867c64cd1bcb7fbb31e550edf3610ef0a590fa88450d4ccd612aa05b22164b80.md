## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a curriculum-enhanced attention distillation method for semi-supervised node classification. It uses a Local Graph Transformer (LGT) teacher to guide a Global Graph Transformer (GGT) student via layer-to-layer attention maps. The authors introduce in-class/out-of-class concepts, out-of-class entropy, and top‑k pruning to encourage the student to explore beyond the teacher’s attention. A curriculum‑based scheduling dynamically balances in‑class guidance and out‑of‑class exploration over training epochs. Experiments on seven benchmark graph datasets (e.g., Pubmed, Cornell, Texas) show improvements over state‑of‑the‑art methods.

### Strengths
- **Effective and original methodology**: Applying attention‑based knowledge distillation to Graph Transformers is novel. The curriculum distillation framework that gradually shifts from strict teacher guidance to more student exploration is creative and grounded in educational learning principles. (Reviewer 2)
- **Sound motivations and reasonable solutions**: The paper clearly identifies limitations of Graph Transformers in semi‑supervised node classification, and the proposed out‑of‑class entropy and top‑k pruning effectively constrain and enhance the student’s exploration. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4)
- **Strong empirical validation**: The method outperforms both the teacher and student models individually on multiple node classification benchmarks, and demonstrates improved generalization. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4)
- **Well‑written and easy to follow**: The motivations are clearly explained, algorithm descriptions are clear, and figures aid understanding of core concepts like curriculum scheduling. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: The approach combines several well‑known techniques (knowledge distillation, curriculum learning, attention mechanisms), and some reviewers found the overall novelty limited. (Reviewer 3)
- **Complexity and computational overhead**: The method adds multiple components (distillation losses, hyperparameters for curriculum scheduling) and the paper does not analyze the training time or memory overhead compared to directly training the student. The absolute performance gains are sometimes small (~1‑2%), which may not justify the added complexity in some applications. (Reviewer 2)
- **Teacher‑student pair analysis is insufficient**: Only one teacher‑student configuration (LGT → GGT) is studied. There is no systematic exploration of how architectural differences (e.g., number of layers, parameter counts) affect distillation performance. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing comparisons and missing related work**:
    - The paper does not compare with general knowledge distillation methods for classification (e.g., KD, AT, FitNets, DKD, CRD). (Reviewer 4)
    - It lacks comparisons with other Graph Transformer variants (e.g., Graphormer, Nodeformer) and does not justify why those models were not used as teachers or students. (Reviewer 4)
    - The proposed attention distillation is not explored for other graph model families (e.g., GCNs). It is unclear whether the framework generalizes to models without attention mechanisms. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4)
    - Relevant KD studies (e.g., Self‑Regulated Feature Learning, NORM, Shadow KD, DisWOT) are not discussed; including them would better position the paper. (Reviewer 2)
- **Implementation details unclear**:
    - The exact distance metric \(d\) for distillation losses (Eq. 7) is not specified. (Reviewer 1)
    - The averaging of attention coefficients over graph nodes and layers may smooth out important signals. (Reviewer 1)
    - The top‑k pruning threshold \(k\) is not reported, and it is unclear whether it varies across datasets with very different sizes (Pubmed has 19,717 nodes, Cornell and Texas have 183 nodes). (Reviewer 1)
    - The specific architectures of the LGT teacher and GGT student are not fully described (number of layers, hidden dimensions, parameters). It is unclear if the “Graph Transformer” used equals Graphormer or Nodeformer. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4)
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and study**:
    - The impact of curriculum scheduling hyperparameters (e.g., epoch boundaries) is not systematically studied. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3)
    - The loss function has multiple components (e.g., weights \(\gamma\), \(\beta\)); the paper does not analyze how sensitive performance is to variations in these hyperparameters in terms of stability and performance. (Reviewer 3)
    - The ablation study lacks a detailed explanation of why removing the distillation loss term (\(\gamma\)) or using a global uniform distribution for attention weights causes significant performance degradation. (Reviewer 4)
- **Scalability and dataset‑specific behavior**:
    - Scalability to larger datasets is not addressed. (Reviewer 3)
    - No analysis of whether teacher/student behavior differs on datasets with varying homophily levels. (Reviewer 3)

**Minority reviewer contrast**: Reviewer 4 (score 5, confidence 5) was the most critical, raising major concerns about the lack of comparisons to general KD methods and to other Graph Transformers, as well as the insufficient detail on teacher/student architectures. Other reviewers (scores 8, 8, 6) considered the work effective and well‑presented, though Reviewer 3 noted limited novelty and Reviewer 2 requested more thorough analysis of teacher‑student pairs and computational cost.
