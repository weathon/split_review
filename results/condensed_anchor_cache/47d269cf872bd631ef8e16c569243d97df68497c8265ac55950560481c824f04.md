## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces **RenEmb** (or RENEMB), a model that combines a transformer module and a CNN to learn structural embeddings of tabular files. It first applies pattern tokenization (replacing data values with generic placeholders to isolate file structure), then uses a structural transformer and CNN, and is pre-trained on ~1M public data files. The approach is evaluated on three data preparation tasks: dialect detection, row classification, and column type annotation. Reviewers largely agree that the paper is clearly written and that the general problem is of practical importance. However, the majority (Reviewers 2 and 3, scores 3) find the technical contribution modest and the experiments insufficient, while Reviewer 1 (score 5) considers the method feasible but raises concerns about missing comparisons with modern LLMs and about design choices.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and readability**: All three reviewers note the paper is well written and easy to understand.
- **Practical importance and feasibility**: The target problem (embedding file structure for data preparation) is of broad interest and the approach seems usable by other researchers (R1). The model provides unique solutions to specific structural representation problems, such as dialect detection and structural awareness (R2).
- **Competitive performance**: RenEmb achieves results comparable to state-of-the-art baselines across three diverse tasks (dialect detection, row classification, column type annotation), and combining RenEmb with Strudel outperforms prior methods (R2, R3).
- **Novel architecture combination**: The integration of transformers and CNNs to extract structural information is considered a unique aspect (R3). Reviewer 2 also acknowledges that the method exhibits good structural representation abilities.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty and outdatedness**: The use of structural features or human-designed pattern tokenization to encode structured text is not new (R2 cites StructTExt, Li et al. 2021; TableFormer, Nassar et al. 2022). The approach relies on basic modules (transformer + CNN) after a handcrafted tokenization step (R2, R3). Furthermore, baseline methods are from 2019–2021, and no comparison is made with modern general-purpose LLMs such as ChatGPT, LLaMA, GPT-3, or Galactica (R1, R2). Reviewers 2 and 3 argue that testing against LLMs that directly tokenize tables with BPE is a core missing comparison (R2 Q1; R3 weakness 2).
- **Unclear problem definition and motivation**: The paper lacks a formal problem definition tying together the chosen tasks (dialect detection, row classification, column type annotation) and the claimed objective of better representing structural information (R3). The motivation that “previous methods neglected structural information” is questionable because pre-trained language models (BERT, GPT) already learn structural information during pre-training (R3). Reviewer 2 adds that more challenging downstream tasks (e.g., TableQA on WikiSQL, WikiTQ) are needed instead of only the three tasks studied.
- **Pattern tokenization limitations**:
  - It excludes textual content; the model could be enhanced by also using the original text together with the pattern (R1).
  - Patterns are not applicable to multilingual settings: languages without upper/lower case (e.g., Chinese, Korean) break the tokenization (R2).
  - The same pattern can result from structurally different tables (e.g., tables with shared vs. unshared column headings) (R2).
- **Questionable pre-training tasks and missing ablations**:
  - **Structural Masking Modeling**: The model cannot really distinguish the same symbol’s different meanings based on position; it merely learns where to output specific symbols, not the meaning (R3).
  - **Same File Prediction**: The description is unclear—how the logistic regression classifier is obtained and how it contributes to BERT’s training is not elaborated. The task itself may be unreasonable because rows from different files can have identical structures (R3).
  - No ablation studies are provided to isolate the impact of the two pre-training tasks or the CNN module on final performance (R3).
- **Insufficient experimental analysis**:
  - No analytic experiments (e.g., probing or visualization) demonstrate that RenEmb has learned superior structural information compared to baselines (R3).
  - Dialect detection and row classification may not be challenging enough; more table understanding benchmarks (e.g., TableQA, WikiSQL, WikiTQ) are expected (R2).
  - The advantage of RenEmb over simply tokenizing tables with BPE and encoding them with a standard transformer (as done in modern LLMs) is not shown (R2 Q1).
  - The hybrid approach of concatenating RenEmb + Strudel is used but the paper does not explain why a two-stage concatenation is better than a single model that jointly considers structure and content (R1; also related to R2 Q1).
- **Other open questions**:
  - Applicability to complex or irregular tables (not just full rows and full columns) is unclear (R1).
  - Whether the method generalizes across different table formats and languages (multilingual setting) is not addressed (R2 Q2).
