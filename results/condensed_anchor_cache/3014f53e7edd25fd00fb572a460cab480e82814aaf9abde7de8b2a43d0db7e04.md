## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces T-Measure, a new metric for estimating model transferability in zero-shot transfer learning (label-agnostic model selection). The method learns a representation space aligned with each source dataset using contrastive triplets, then computes a transferability score by selecting a subset of source samples similar to the target and aggregating Pointwise V-Information (PVI). Experiments across 4 tasks (classification, emotion recognition, question answering, relation classification) and 11 datasets are presented, comparing T-Measure against existing transferability measures. The method shows average improvements in ranking (Kendall-tau distance) over baselines.

### Strengths
1. **Important problem**: Addresses the practical need for model selection in transfer learning without labeled target data. Multiple reviewers note this is a timely and significant challenge, especially given the proliferation of models in repositories.
2. **Interesting data-centric formulation**: The idea of measuring transferability based on source/target dataset similarity is novel and clearly stated. The paper provides a good explanation of different transfer types (Section 2).
3. **Diverse evaluation setup**: Experiments span multiple NLP tasks (classification, emotion recognition, question answering, response selection, relation classification), moving beyond the classification-only focus of prior work. The method shows consistent benefit over baselines on average.
4. **Clarity of core idea**: The paper is easy to follow in presenting the main concept of comparing source and target data similarity.

One reviewer (score 5) found the contributions and consistent gains across tasks more compelling, while the three other reviewers (scores 3, 3, 3) had significant reservations about practical applicability and rigor.

### Weaknesses
1. **Source data availability assumption**: The method requires access to source training data, which is often unavailable in practice (e.g., pre-trained models released without training datasets, proprietary data, or foundation models). This severely limits real-world applicability. Multiple reviewers flag this as a major concern.
2. **Limited model evaluation**: Only 3 or 6 pre-trained models are evaluated per dataset (Table 3). It is unclear if the metric scales to larger, more diverse pools of models with varying architectures and training procedures. The robustness of the ranking to model diversity is not demonstrated.
3. **Over-simplification of transfer factors**: The measure focuses almost exclusively on data similarity, ignoring other critical factors that influence transferability: model architecture, pre-training objectives (e.g., masked language modeling vs. next sentence prediction), and optimization strategies. No justification is provided for this narrow scope. One reviewer notes the paper essentially evaluates dataset closeness, not full model transferability.
4. **Unrealistic experimental setting**: All experiments assume source and target tasks are identical. This contradicts common practice where a pre-trained model is adapted to a different task (e.g., MLM fine-tuned for classification). Cross-task transfer is not evaluated, limiting the measure's practical utility.
5. **Missing comparisons with domain adaptation literature**: The core idea is akin to estimating dataset distance; the paper should compare with established measures from unsupervised domain adaptation (e.g., A-distance, H-divergence) and multi-source UDA methods. References and comparisons to works in domain adaptation and robust optimization are absent.
6. **Opaque methodological details**:
   - PVI computation is not defined; readers unfamiliar with (Ethayarajh et al., 2021) cannot understand Step 2. No equation for PVI is provided.
   - The use of triplet loss for representation learning is not justified. Alternatives like contrastive loss with multiple negatives are not explored, and sensitivity to representation learning choices is not studied.
   - The initial representation uses Sentence-BERT; the impact of this pre-training on final transferability scores is not discussed. The effect of the pre-training corpus of Sentence-BERT on target domain relatedness is unexamined.
   - The aggregation function over the selected subset (averaging PVI) is presented without justification. Other aggregation strategies are not considered.
   - The transfer method α is not clearly defined (Section 2.2); it is stated that α is identity only in Section 2.4, but general handling is unclear. How α interacts with PVI aggregation is not explained.
   - Hyper-parameter details for self-supervised learning (architecture, optimization) are missing, hindering reproducibility.
   - The distance metric for comparing data distributions is not justified and sensitivity analysis is lacking.
7. **Presentation and rigor issues**:
   - Equations in Sections 2.1 and 2.2 are not numbered, making it hard to distinguish φ* for source models from φ* for representation learning. The difference between the two equations is unclear on first read.
   - Table 3 reports average Kendall-tau distance across datasets, which is not a meaningful statistic. Per-dataset breakdowns are needed to see if gains are consistent. References to competing methods are missing from the table.
   - Results Tables 4 and 6 are in the Appendix, not the main text.
   - Figures 1-4 use vision dataset examples (Fig. 2) and are not well-aligned with the NLP evaluation; they do not effectively illustrate the method.
   - Several incomplete sentences appear (e.g., introduction "While some recent body of work sugguest...", after Figure 2, "6 presents boxplots" missing "Figure").
   - Phrases like "intuitively" weaken scientific rigor; design choices appear arbitrary without justification.
8. **Literature positioning**: The paper claims to focus on model fine-tuning, but the setting (same task, no target labels) is actually unsupervised domain adaptation (or multi-source UDA). Papers cited for fine-tuning (e.g., Bao et al., Nguyen et al., Tran et al.) assume access to target labels, which is a different setup. A thorough literature review is needed to properly place this work.
9. **Contributions not clearly refined**: The "contribution" list in the introduction merely summarizes sections rather than highlighting novel technical contributions.
