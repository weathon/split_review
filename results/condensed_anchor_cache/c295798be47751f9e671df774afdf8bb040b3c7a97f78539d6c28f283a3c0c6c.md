## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Loss-Free Balancing, a load balancing strategy for Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models that dynamically adjusts per-expert biases on routing scores based on recent expert load. Unlike traditional auxiliary-loss methods, this approach does not introduce interference gradients. The method is applied to DeepSeekMoE models (1B and 3B parameters) trained on up to 200B tokens. Results report validation perplexity and a proposed MaxVio metric.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, the method is clearly explained, and visualizations are good (Reviewer 1; but Reviewer 2 notes figures are enlarged to fill space).  
- The approach is simple, easy to test, and cheaper to compute than conventional load-balancing losses (Reviewer 1).  
- Eliminating the auxiliary loss avoids undesirable interference gradients, yielding a cleaner training signal focused on the primary language modeling objective (Reviewer 2).  
- Dynamic bias adjustment ensures balanced expert load without compromising model efficiency (Reviewer 2).  
- The strategy is compatible with expert parallelism, a key feature for scaling MoE models across devices (Reviewer 2).  
- Unlike Expert Choice, Loss-Free Balancing maintains causal constraints, preventing future token leakage (Reviewer 2).  
- The problem and solution are simple and easy to understand (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).  
- There is some originality in viewing load balancing from the perspective of interfering gradients (Reviewer 4).  
- The research problem is interesting and the solution is efficient (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation.** Experiments are confined to two DeepSeekMoE models (1B and 3B) and report only validation perplexity; improvements are marginal (~0.05 difference) and their significance is unclear (Reviewer 1, 2, 4).  
- **Lack of downstream tasks.** No evaluation on benchmarks like MMLU or GLUE to show practical impact (Reviewer 1).  
- **Narrow model diversity.** The method is not tested on other MoE architectures (e.g., Mistral, QWen, LLaMA-MoE, OpenMoE, Mixtral, Switch Transformer) or in computer vision (Reviewers 2, 4).  
- **MaxVio metric is sensitive to outliers.** Because it subtracts mean load from the maximum load, a single batch with unusually high token routing to one expert can disproportionately affect the score, making imbalance appear worse than it is (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 4 also notes a minor definitional error: the $\max_i$ is included in the numerator fraction where $\bar{Load}_i$ also uses index $i$).  
- **Missing analyses of load balancing behavior.** The paper should show the load-balancing loss (without backpropagation) over training steps for both methods (Reviewer 1).  
- **Inference behavior unclear.** It is not explained how the proposed method behaves during inference compared to auxiliary-loss methods that directly affect routing weights (Reviewer 1).  
- **Unclear motivation and intuition.** The underlying intuition and whether auxiliary-loss gradients actually impair model performance is not theoretically or empirically substantiated (Reviewer 3, 4).  
- **Theoretical justification lacking.** No formal analysis supports why the bias adjustment works or how the update amount is determined (Reviewer 3).  
- **Bias initialization and adjustment details are insufficient.** Specifically, in Formula 3 it is unclear whether $b_i$ is influenced by $K$ or independent, and by what amount it is increased or decreased (Reviewer 3).  
- **Claim about batch size scaling.** The statement that load balance consistently improves with batch size is not qualified with an upper bound (Reviewer 3).  
- **Arbitrary choice of 9 MoE layers.** No reason is given for selecting exactly 9 MoE layers for testing (Reviewer 3).  
- **Poor related work survey.** Only 10 papers are cited; many relevant auxiliary-loss methods, MoE improvements, and current MoE-based LLMs are omitted (Reviewer 2).  
- **Unconvincing argument against Expert Choice future token leakage.** The explanation is brief, relies solely on Figure 6, and implicitly assumes an abnormal loss drop must be due to leakage (Reviewer 4).  
- **Figure size issues.** Figures appear enlarged to reach page count, creating a disproportionate layout (Reviewer 2).  
- **Even positive reviewers note limitations.** Reviewer 2 calls the improvement trivial and the related work insufficient; Reviewer 3 asks for theoretical justification and scalability analysis (Reviewers 2, 3).  
- **Minor:** The improvement over baselines is marginal, raising doubts about the practical impact of the method (Reviewers 2, 4).
