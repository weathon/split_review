## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces hierarchical rectified flow (HRF), which extends standard rectified flow (RF) by modeling not just the mean velocity field but the full multi-modal random velocity and acceleration fields. This is achieved by coupling multiple ordinary differential equations (ODEs) and using a training objective equivalent to acceleration matching. The resulting stochastic sampling process allows trajectories to intersect, which is claimed to produce straighter paths and reduce the number of neural function evaluations (NFEs). The method is evaluated on synthetic 1D/2D data, MNIST, and CIFAR-10. The contributions are partly theoretical (Theorems 1 and 2), and the methodology has potential but raises significant practical concerns.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and motivation**: The paper is well-written and easy to follow. The motivation for moving beyond mean velocity to model the full velocity distribution is clear and compelling (all reviewers).
- **Theoretical contributions**: Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 are interesting and provide insights into the conditional distribution of velocities at each point, enabling higher-order stochastic sampling. The theoretical foundations are solid (R3 strongly positive; R2 agrees on solid foundations).
- **Intuitive demonstrations**: Low-dimensional toy experiments (1D, 2D) help illustrate the dynamics and provide an educational understanding of how the model handles multi-modal distributions and complex trajectories (R2 positive; R1 says experimental results partially support theoretical claims).
- **Potential impact**: The approach addresses limitations in existing flow-based generative models and could inspire further research (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Computational inefficiency and scalability** (all reviewers, with R3 being most critical of practical application):
  - The generation framework requires significantly more model calls than standard RF: NFEs multiplied by the number of discretizations in the velocity space (J · L). It is unclear whether the reported NFE includes the steps to sample the velocities; the paper should explicitly report both L and J for each experiment and compare compute time per step with RF (R1, R3).
  - Computational overhead is substantial, especially for high-dimensional data; even with latent models the approach appears resource-intensive for datasets like ImageNet (R1, R2, R3).
  - Scaling to higher depth (D-depth model) requires a substantially larger architecture, and it is unclear whether the “forgetting” problem of the optimal trajectory occurs, necessitating architecture expansion. An ablation on model performance and computational requirements as depth increases is needed (R1).
  - The paper lacks a detailed comparison of training and inference time, parameter counts, memory usage, and convergence behavior against RF and rectified RF across all experiments. Requested: a table comparing HRF and RF models including parameter counts, training time per iteration, and memory usage (R1, R3). For toy settings, more architecture details are needed for reproducibility (R1).
  - The approach is not a diffeomorphism, so density estimation is lost (R3). This is a trade-off that should be discussed.
- **Insufficient empirical validation** (all reviewers, but R3 is most negative on CIFAR-10 results):
  - Experiments are limited to small, low-dimensional image datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10). Given current generative modeling progress, these do not fully demonstrate the method’s potential (R2). Results on CIFAR-10 are “not encouraging” and suggest the model does not scale well (R3). Strongly recommended: test on ImageNet or higher-dimensional complex data, perhaps incorporating latent diffusion architectures (R1, R2, R3). Comparison with state-of-the-art generative models on standard benchmarks is needed (R2).
  - R1 specifically requests a comprehensive comparison between rectified RF and the proposed method, including generation performance and training/inference time, on existing datasets or ideally on ImageNet.
- **Ambiguity in the hierarchical extension** (R2, theoretical/methodological):
  - The transition from the acceleration-based approach (training objective in Eq. 8) to the hierarchical model (Eq. 10) is not thoroughly explained. The conceptual progression and structural specifics of the higher-dimensional source distribution (D-dimensional) are ambiguous.
  - It is unclear how the multiple levels of the hierarchy interact and how velocity and acceleration fields are composed across levels. The paper should more explicitly describe how the source distribution is constructed (dimensionality, interface with the hierarchical model) and how gradients are computed and propagated through the hierarchy during training.
- **Convergence concerns** (R1):
  - Does increasing depth make the model harder to converge during training? Empirical evidence on convergence rates for models with different depths, or a discussion of theoretical bounds, would be helpful.
- **Minor issues** (R1):
  - Typo on line 156: should be \(\pi_0\) (not \(\pi_0\)? original says should be correct). All typos should be corrected.

*Note: R3 (rating 8, confidence 4) is more positive about the theoretical value but equally concerned about practical challenges, especially CIFAR-10 scaling and loss of diffeomorphism. R1 and R2 (both rating 6) are more critical of the method’s practicality and empirical scope. All agree the work would benefit from more thorough computational and experimental comparisons.*
