## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper explores methods to improve LLMs for solving math word problems, motivated by the gap between pass‑at‑one and pass‑at‑N performance. Three fine‑tuning strategies are investigated on the MATH dataset using PaLM 2 models: (1) supervised fine‑tuning to generate step‑by‑step solutions; (2) solution‑cluster re‑ranking, where the LLM ranks candidate solutions clustered by equivalence; (3) multi‑task sequential fine‑tuning that combines generation and evaluation tasks. The work also introduces a new training objective for the sequential fine‑tuning.

### Strengths
- The solution‑cluster re‑ranking approach creatively combines majority voting and re‑ranking in a novel way to improve performance. (R3)
- Experiments (Table 3) show consistent improvement by *RR.Top‑8* compared to *RR.All*, and the paper investigates several re‑ranking strategies to demonstrate the advantages of re‑ranking through top‑*K* clusters. (R2)
- Using re‑ranking + majority voting together is shown to outperform either method alone, with comparisons across different re‑ranking algorithms and model sizes. (R1)
- The multi‑task sequential fine‑tuning (with its new training objective) yields improvement over the MLE fine‑tuning baseline. (R1, R2)
- The paper provides clear motivation for the approach. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty / incremental contribution**: Re‑ranking and majority voting are not new; the combination alone offers limited novelty. The absolute improvements are not significant (Tables 3 and 4), and the biggest gain comes from supervised fine‑tuning (SFT) itself – the paper claims 11.2% gain over the few‑shot baseline, but SFT on PRM800K + MATH already provides a 7.6% improvement (47.6 → 55.2 in Table 2). (R1, R2, R3)  
- **Insufficient experiments and generalizability**:  
  - Only PaLM 2 models are used; results are not reported on other LLMs such as LLaMA / LLaMA‑2 (7B/13B), or compared with ChatGPT, GPT‑4, Claude‑2. (R1, R3)  
  - Only the MATH dataset is used; an additional dataset would be more convincing. (R2)  
  - The choice of *K* = 8 for clustering is not sufficiently justified; more experiments or explanation are needed. (R2)  
  - It is unclear whether the re‑ranking step is combined with the multi‑task sequential model in the final experiment (Section 4.4); results for the full combination are missing. (R1, question)  
  - Ablation questions: What if all three tasks are trained jointly instead of sequentially? What if only two tasks are used? (R1, question)  
- **Deeper insights missing**: The paper lacks scientific analysis, e.g., how sequential fine‑tuning changes model behavior. (R2)  
- **Writing and clarity issues**:  
  - Section 5.1 incorrectly refers to the dataset as “GSM800K” – should be PRM800K. (R1)  
  - In Section 3.2, the handling of unbalanced positive/negative pairs in the pair‑wise loss (Eq. 7‑9) is not explained; also unclear whether gold annotations are still used for re‑ranking. (R2)  
  - Equations 10‑11 appear unused in the experiments – only Eqs. 12‑13 are applied. (R2, question)  
- **Performance gain minimal**: Compared to the SFT baseline, the additional gains from the proposed methods are small. (R1, R2, R3)
