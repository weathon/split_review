## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper addresses causal discovery from two time-slices data, treating the previous time-slice as an auxiliary instrumental variable to identify (non-)descendants of each node. The proposed DHT-CIT algorithm builds a Descendant Hierarchical Topology that yields a more precise topological ordering, reducing the search space and spurious edges. The method is theoretically justified and evaluated on synthetic linear/non‑linear data and one real‑world dataset (PM‑CMR). Reviewer scores are 6, 8, and 5; the minority reviewer (score 5) is more critical of the paper’s positioning, novelty, and evaluation, while the other two are generally positive about the contribution and experiments.

### Strengths

- Proposes a novel algorithm, DHT‑CIT, that uses a previous time‑slice as an auxiliary instrumental variable to learn a unique descendant hierarchical topology from two time‑slices of data (R1, R2).
- Reduces the search space for causal discovery, automatically enforces the acyclicity constraint, and eliminates numerous spurious edges in the learned topology (R1).
- Shows superior and robust performance on both synthetic (including non‑Gaussian) and real‑world data compared to several state‑of‑the‑art baselines (R1, R2).
- Has the potential to serve as a plug‑in module for existing topology‑based methods (e.g., SCORE) to improve the learned DAGs (R2).
- Provides a comprehensive overview of traditional causal discovery and topology‑based methods; paper is well‑organized and easy to follow (R2, R3).
- Figures are helpful for illustrating key concepts (R3).
- Experiments include a broad range of comparison methods and dataset sizes (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Problem positioning and related work:** The paper mixes IID and time‑series settings without a clean definition. Limited discussion of time‑series causal discovery works (e.g., Granger causality, VARLiNGAM, Section 10 of “Elements of Causal Inference”). Many compared baselines (PC, FCI, etc.) are designed for IID data; it is unclear how they are applied to time‑series (R3). A minority reviewer (score 5) stresses that the problem statement needs clarification about the summary graph and the role of time slices.
- **Novelty concerns:** The use of a lagged variable as an instrumental variable is a well‑established technique; the paper does not adequately differentiate its approach from prior methods (R1). Theorem 1 appears to follow directly from the lagged structure and Markov property; comparison with Section 10.3 of “Elements of Causal Inference” and work by Mastakouri et al. is requested (R3).
- **Limited evaluation:**
  - Experiments rely heavily on synthetic linear Gaussian data; only one real‑world dataset (PM‑CMR) is used (R1, R3).
  - No evaluation on datasets with complex non‑linear relationships or non‑Gaussian noise distributions beyond the linear case (R1).
  - Missing comparisons with time‑series methods such as Granger causality and VARLiNGAM (R3).
  - Functional relationships could be more general (e.g., neural networks with random weights) (R3).
- **Assumptions and model restrictiveness:**
  - Equation (1) is more restrictive than a general additive noise model for time series because it separates lagged variables and only adds them additively (R3).
  - Assumption 1 seems flawed: X^t may still depend on X^{t-i-k} given the intermediate lags due to skip connections (R3).
  - Definition 2 is unclear about multiple layers (e.g., X → Y → Z would wrongly imply a direct edge from X to Z) (R3).
  - It is unclear why a time slice is treated as an intervention (R3).
- **Computational efficiency:** Running conditional independence tests for all variables from scratch is time‑consuming; the paper does not compare against directly pruning edges from a complete ordering (e.g., SCORE) to save time (R2).
- **Gaussian assumption:** Unclear whether the method relies on Gaussian models; while non‑Gaussian results are shown, the theoretical justification is ambiguous (R2).
- **Missing practical considerations:**
  - How does the algorithm handle missing or unreliable previous time‑slice data? (R1)
  - How can it be generalized to other domains (social sciences, economics, biology)? (R1)
- **Unclear uniqueness claim:** The paper claims “unique orderings”, but equivalent topological orderings exist for graphs like X → Y ← Z; this needs clarification (R3).
