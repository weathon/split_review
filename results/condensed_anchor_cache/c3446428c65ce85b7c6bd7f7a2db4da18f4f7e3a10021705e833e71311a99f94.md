## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes holographic node representations (HoloGNN) to learn node embeddings that can be reused across tasks of any order (node, link, meta-path). The approach uses a task-agnostic expansion map to produce high-dimensional symmetry-free embeddings, followed by a reduction map that reintroduces task-specific permutation symmetries. Reviewers find the idea novel and interesting, with theoretical analysis and empirical results showing effective transfer within a dataset. However, concerns are raised about the scope of experimental validation (within-dataset only, small graphs), the use of the term "pretraining" for supervised transfer learning, missing comparisons (graph transformers, graph classification), and insufficient justification of certain design choices and hyperparameters.

### Strengths
- The research question is novel and interesting, and the proposed holographic node representation approach is creative. (R1, R2, R4)
- The paper is generally well-written, well-structured, and easy to follow. (R1, R4)
- Theoretical analyses are provided to justify the validity of the method, including a thorough analysis from the perspective of canonical eigenvectors. (R2, R4)
- The finding that task-specific embeddings require different permutation symmetries, and that the source of task-specific information is symmetry distinctive across tasks, is insightful. (R4)
- Experimental results demonstrate that HoloGNN can effectively adapt GNNs trained on one task to other tasks within the same dataset. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Terminology of "pretraining":** The paper frames the approach as "pretraining," but the experiments use supervised labels from a downstream task (e.g., node classification) and adapt to another task. This is more akin to supervised transfer learning; the term "pretraining" is inconsistent with the community’s common usage (unsupervised or self-supervised, as in Hu et al. 2019, 2020). The authors should revise the terminology and compare against existing graph pretraining methods. (R3)
- **Limited experimental scope:**
  - All experiments are within a single dataset (e.g., pre-train on node classification in Cora, then test on link prediction in Cora). There is no evaluation of cross-dataset generalization, which is a significant limitation for a method claiming general-purpose pre-training. (R2)
  - Figure 3 shows that pre-trained HoloGNN can perform worse than HoloGNN trained from scratch. The paper should clarify the specific scenarios where pre-training is beneficial, given this result. (R2)
  - The experiments use small datasets (e.g., Cora with a few thousand nodes). It is unclear whether the benefits of pre-training scale to large, complex graphs. (R3)
  - Results on MovieLens are difficult to understand; more detailed explanations of the experimental setup and outcomes are needed. (R2)
- **Missing tasks and comparisons:**
  - No experiments on graph classification are provided. Reviewers request such experiments to demonstrate the method’s applicability to higher-order tasks. (R1, R2)
  - No comparison with graph transformer models. The paper should discuss how HoloGNN handles positional information versus graph transformers, and why the proposed approach is more suitable for multi-task learning. (R2)
- **Insufficient justification and clarity of design:**
  - Properties (1) and (2) in Section 4.1 lack theoretical proof or justification; they are not self-evident. (R2)
  - The definition of Structural Representation (line 107) is unclear: the meaning of "order r," the subscript S, the permutation notation, and the output format of f need better explanation. The same issue appears in the reduction map formulation (equation 3). (R4)
  - The necessity of the symmetry-breaking technique is not well justified for real-world graphs that already have node features. How important is breaking symmetry to distinguish structurally isomorphic nodes when features are present? (R4)
  - It is counter-intuitive to dismiss all symmetries in the expansion map and then reintroduce them in the reduction map. Why not preserve all symmetries from the start and fuse them without bias? (R4)
- **Complexity analysis missing:** The paper does not provide computational complexity analysis of the sequential and parallel breaking algorithms; it is unclear how the cost scales with graph size and the number of breaking operations. (R2)
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** No sensitivity analysis is given for key hyperparameters T and L in equation (2). (R2)
- **Reproducibility:** The code is not provided, making reproduction difficult given the complexity of the model. (R2)
- **Minor question:** Section 4.1 lacks sufficient details on node-breaking selectors and why they are needed; more elaboration would enhance clarity. (R2)
- **Minor note:** Reviewer 3 is more critical of the pretraining framing, while Reviewers 1 and 4 are more positive (scores 8 vs. 6). This contrast is preserved to indicate reviewer disagreement.
