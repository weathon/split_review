## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces **fractal nodes** to enhance Message Passing Neural Networks (MPNNs) by addressing over-squashing and improving long-range dependency modeling. Each fractal node summarizes information from a subgraph (obtained via METIS) and is connected to all nodes in that subgraph, enabling direct shortcuts for long-distance information flow. The method is compared against graph Transformers and other baselines on graph-level tasks and a synthetic expressivity benchmark (Section 5.2). Reviewers generally agree that the concept is interesting and the paper is well-structured, but concerns are raised about novelty (resemblance to ClusterGCN+VirtualNode), superficial theoretical analysis of over-squashing mitigation, insufficient validation on node-level tasks, reproduction issues, and the need for more comprehensive comparisons and ablation studies. Two reviewers (scoring 6) find the approach novel and significant, while two others (scoring 5) are more critical of its novelty and the depth of analysis.

### Strengths
- The paper is **well-structured and clearly written**, with effective use of figures (especially Figure 1) and a logical flow from motivation to implementation.
- The **concept of fractal nodes is interesting and novel**, offering a fresh perspective on balancing local and global information in MPNNs while maintaining computational efficiency.
- The method is **straightforward and low-complexity**, achieving competitive or better performance compared to graph Transformers on graph-level datasets.
- **Comprehensive experimental validation** covering four different aspects, including synthetic expressivity (Section 5.2) where the proposed method shows **intriguing performance gains** (100 on CSL and SR25), and ablation studies.
- The method **mitigates over-squashing** and provides a **computationally efficient alternative to graph Transformers**, preserving MPNN advantages.
- **Variety of benchmarking experiments** with meticulous descriptions of settings.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical rigor**: No formal analysis of how fractal nodes alleviate over-squashing (e.g., impact on gradient flow, information propagation) or improve expressivity. The connection to renormalization group theory is only heuristic, and the paper would benefit from a more rigorous treatment (R1, R4).
- **Insufficient validation on node-level tasks**: Experiments focus almost entirely on graph-level tasks; missing real-world node classification benchmarks (e.g., ogbn-arxiv). Long-range interactions are a known issue in node-level tasks, and the method’s effectiveness there is unclear (R1, R3).
- **Limited novelty**: The framework resembles a combination of **ClusterGCN and VirtualNode** – METIS clustering (like ClusterGCN) and fractal nodes that connect to all nodes in a cluster (like VirtualNode) (R2). The paper does not convincingly differentiate from these existing ideas.
- **Misapplication of LPF and HPF** (Equations 6,7): The low-pass filter is simply a cluster-wise mean, and the high-pass filter is the residual; these do not correctly capture spectral graph signal components as claimed, indicating a misunderstanding of spectral graph theory (R2).
- **Superficial analysis of properties in Section 4**: The L2 norm distribution experiment does not inherently reveal whether local or global information is captured; claims about a “balanced” approach lack concrete metrics (R2). The explanation of how the norm distribution experiment informs about local/global information requires more detail (R1).
- **Inaccurate characterization of related techniques**: Fractal nodes are **precomputed by METIS** (not dynamically generated during training), unlike graph coarsening methods. Virtual nodes do not always use mean pooling; they commonly share the same hidden space. The paper’s comparison to these methods is misleading (R2).
- **Unclear practical impact of “self-similarity”**: Despite being a core motivation, the paper does not demonstrate how enforcing self-similarity through fractal nodes actually improves performance or differs from arbitrary clustering (R1, R2, R4). METIS optimizes in-cluster connectivity, not self-similarity (R1).
- **Modest performance gains**: On several benchmarks (e.g., MNIST, CIFAR10), the proposed method **only matches or underperforms** compared to existing methods (Graph-ViT/MLP-Mixer, Exphormer, GRIT). Performance improvements are not consistently significant across all datasets (R3). Additionally, GCN+FN_M performs notably worse than GINE/GatedGCN+FN_M (R3).
- **Need for more comparisons**: Missing comparisons with other over-squashing mitigation techniques (e.g., virtual nodes, graph rewiring) and a broader set of baselines (R4).
- **Reproduction issues**: The provided source code does not reproduce the reported results. On a 3090 machine, runtime is **58s per epoch** vs. **5.03s** claimed. Three runs gave accuracies of 0.27, 0.252, 0.2474 vs. the reported 0.2464±0.0014. The number of runs for the best result is unclear (R2).
- **Lack of ablation on subgraph partitioning strategies**: The method relies on METIS, but no experiments explore how different partitioning algorithms affect performance or whether the method is robust to partitioning choices (R4). Optimal criteria for partitioning are not discussed (R1, R4).
- **Missing analysis on fractal node size and number**: The effects of subgraph size (number of nodes per subgraph), the ratio of fractal nodes to total nodes, and a potential performance threshold for increasing C are not studied (R3, R4). The paper states “the higher the number of fractal nodes, C, the better the performance” (Lines 510–513) but does not examine trade-offs or saturation (R3).
- **No message passing between fractal nodes**: Information mixing only occurs in the last layer via an MLP mixer (Equation 8). Why not consider message passing on the coarse-grained graph of fractal nodes? (R1)
- **Notation ambiguity in Equations 2–4**: The subscript \(c\) in \(h^{(\ell)}_{u,c}\) suggests message passing only within subgraph \(G_c\), contradicting the claimed cross-subgraph information flow (R1).
- **Unexplained abrupt performance drop in Figure 5**: Accuracy remains near-constant until \(r=7\) then drops sharply to zero; no explanation is given (R1).
- **Missing discussion of computational overhead and scalability**: No detailed analysis of the cost of creating/updating fractal nodes, memory usage, or runtime scaling with graph size (R4).
- **No discussion of failure cases or limitations**: The paper does not identify scenarios where the method might underperform (R4).
- **Insufficient depth of experimental discussion**: Results are mostly listed rather than analyzed for deeper insights (e.g., why certain backbones benefit more, architectural differences causing performance gaps) (R3).
