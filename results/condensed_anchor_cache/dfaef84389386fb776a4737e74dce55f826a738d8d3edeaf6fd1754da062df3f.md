## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies an expectation shift in Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) for diffusion models and proposes Rectified Classifier-Free Guidance (ReCFG). ReCFG relaxes the standard sum-to-one constraint on the conditional and unconditional score coefficients, aligning the sampling process with diffusion theory. A closed-form solution (using a precomputed lookup table) is derived given the guidance strength, and the method is applied as a post-hoc modification without retraining. Evaluations are performed on a toy example, class-conditioned ImageNet (EDM2), and text-conditioned CC12M (SD3). One reviewer (score 6) finds the contribution novel and well-written; the other three reviewers (scores 3, 5, 3) have significant concerns about experimental scope, practical impact, numerical stability, and diversity trade-offs.

### Strengths
- Rigorous theoretical analysis that identifies and quantifies the expectation shift flaw in CFG, providing a mathematically grounded alternative. (R1, R2)
- The proposed ReCFG is a novel solution to a known issue in conditional diffusion sampling. (R2, R3)
- Post-hoc design allows application to pretrained models without any retraining. (R1)
- The toy data experiment clearly illustrates the method. (R1)
- The paper is well-written and accompanied by intuitive theorems. (R3)
- The analysis of CFG is extensive and thorough. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental scope and practical impact.**  
  The empirical gains on ImageNet (FID, CLIP) and CC12M are reported as small and not statistically significant (e.g., no standard deviations in Tables 1 and 2). (R1, R3)  
  Reviewer 1 states that the improvement is insufficient to demonstrate practical impact on complex tasks like text-to-image generation, and requests a user study or visual comparisons. (R1, R4)  
  Reviewer 4 questions whether the baseline CFG results are persuasive; suggests using well-accepted benchmarks with comparable sampling steps (25 or 256 with DDPM/DDIM). (R4)  
  No visual examples (sample images or sampling process visualizations) are provided. (R1, R2, R4)

- **Computational overhead and practical feasibility.**  
  The method requires solving equation (28) for each time step and precomputing expectation ratios per class/label per step (Table 3). This adds extra computation during sampling or precomputation. (R1, R2, R3)  
  The combination of class and step makes the lookup table cumbersome, and numerical stability is a concern because the coefficient deviations from 1.0 are minute. (R4)  
  An ablation study is requested: comparing class-specific ratios vs. a global average, analyzing rounding effects, and evaluating the impact of fewer sampling steps. (R4)

- **Diversity trade-off.**  
  The toy example (Figure 2) shows that achieving unbiasedness sacrifices diversity (variance becomes much smaller than the ground-truth distribution). (R1, R4)  
  Reviewer 4 asks for a quantification of the trade-off and notes that it is unclear which distribution is closer under KL divergence. (R4)

- **Numerical stability and reliability concerns.**  
  The adjustments to the CFG coefficients are extremely small (close to 1.0), suggesting the violation of the zero-mean assumption may have negligible practical effect. (R4)  
  The paper does not conduct an ablation study with small perturbations to the coefficients to verify robustness. (R4)  
  Relaxing the sum-to-one constraint may increase or decrease the predicted noise norm, potentially collapsing the sampling process; analysis of this effect is missing. (R4)

- **Theoretical presentation and clarity.**  
  The connection between Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 is unclear; Theorem 3, which best illustrates the expectation shift, appears too late in the manuscript. (R3)  
  Figure 1 (expectation ratio vs. sampling steps) is difficult to interpret; a clearer explanation of how the ratio is calculated and separate figures for first vs. last steps are needed. (R4)  
  Reviewer 2 suggests adding more explanation after Theorem 3 and reducing the length of Theorem 2. (R2)

- **No true closed-form expression.**  
  The method provides an estimator rather than an exact closed-form solution. (R3)

- **Expectation ratio close to 1 suggests original CFG may be adequate.**  
  Table 3 shows that the computed expectation ratios are often near 1.0, which might advocate for retaining the standard setting γ_0 = 1 – γ_1. (R3, R4)

- **Theoretical question on expectation shift condition.**  
  Reviewer 2 notes: if Δ_t=0, the proof implies E(x0) = E_{c}(x0), which would mean that whenever unconditional and conditional expectations differ, an expectation shift always exists. (R2)

- **Applicability to other domains (e.g., text-to-3D).**  
  For methods like DreamFusion that use a much larger CFG coefficient, it is unclear whether the analysis extends or whether similar improvements can be made. (R1)
