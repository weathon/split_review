## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Multimodal Class-Incremental Learning (MCIL) benchmark for classification tasks. It selects three multimodal datasets (Oxford Flowers, CUB, DVM-CAR), adapts the Flava vision-language model, and evaluates five continual learning strategies (DualPrompt, L2P, Experience Replay, plus upper/lower bounds). The work aims to standardize evaluation of vision-language models in incremental classification settings.

### Strengths
- Introduces a standardized MCIL benchmark, addressing a gap in multimodal continual learning research (Reviewers 1,2,3,5).
- Demonstrates the benchmark’s relevance by adapting Flava and providing experimental insights into VLM behavior under incremental learning (Reviewer 1).
- Focuses on classification tasks with fixed tasks and domains, enabling controlled comparison of methods (Reviewer 5).
- Provides detailed descriptions of models, metrics, datasets, and experimental settings (Reviewer 4).
- Includes initial experimental results of current continual learning methods on multimodal data (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited contribution and novelty**: The benchmark largely overlaps with existing multimodal CIL works (e.g., CLIP-based incremental learning [1-7 cited by reviewers], AttriCLIP, CoDA-Prompt, etc.). Two reviewers (scores 1) are much more critical, stating the contribution is extremely weak (no new data, no new method, poorly motivated) and the paper appears semi-finished. Others (scores 3) acknowledge some utility but note significant deficiencies (Reviewers 1,2,4,6).
- **Unclear motivation**: Why is incremental learning needed for multimodal classification when current VLMs can classify using a single modality? The proposed setting (removing explicit ground-truth mentions from text) does not fully prevent category leakage—indirect cues remain. The specific use case or scenario for MCIL is not clarified (Reviewers 3,4; echoed by 6).
- **Insufficient and narrow datasets**: Only three small-scale, domain-specific datasets (birds, cars, flowers) are used. Missing larger/generic datasets (e.g., ImageNet1K) and realistic temporal evolution (e.g., CLEAR Benchmark). No justification for why these three are sufficient for a benchmark (Reviewers 1,2,5).
- **Insufficient baselines**: Only L2P, DualPrompt, and Experience Replay are compared. Classic CIL methods (LwF, iCaRL) and recent prompting methods (Coda-Prompt, PromptFusion, OSPC, HiDe-Prompt) are not included. Experiments need expansion to validate the benchmark (Reviewers 1,2).
- **Only one VLM evaluated**: Flava is used exclusively. More models from the taxonomy (CLIP, ALIGN, ViLBERT, BLIP, LLaVA, CogVLM) should be tested to make conclusions robust (Reviewers 2,5).
- **Missing experiment details and analysis**: No information on Flava’s pretraining (from scratch or pretrained), number of runs for standard errors, train/val/test splits per dataset. Missing ablations and quantitative analysis to support claims. Presentation is poor (only 7 pages, insufficient analysis, captions like “Results.”) (Reviewers 3,4,5).
- **Unrealistic incremental setting**: Classes arrive as pure chunks; real-world data is mixed in proportions. The CLEAR benchmark’s temporal evolution is more realistic (Reviewer 5).
- **Confusion about model selection and usage**: Why Flava is chosen over CLIP/ALIGN despite the claim that those do not meet Eq1? How is text input of Flava used in MCIL (Reviewer 1)?
- **Incomplete literature review**: Misses audio-visual and other multimodal incremental learning works; needs comprehensive survey to differentiate from existing CLIP-based CIL and multimodal CIL protocols (Reviewer 1).
- **Minor presentation issues**: Table 1 formatting (UB/LB should be separate, best bolded), Figure 1 caption too simple, line 144 and 152 punctuation errors (Reviewer 1).
