## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces LiveXiv, an evolving multi-modal live benchmark that automatically generates Visual Question-Answering (VQA) pairs from newly published ArXiv papers, using a document parsing pipeline (preprocessing), GPT-4o (generation), and Claude (filtering). The goal is to mitigate test data contamination in LMM evaluations. The authors also propose an efficient evaluation method, inspired by Item Response Theory, that estimates model rankings by evaluating only a subset of models on the latest data, reducing computational cost. Multiple open and proprietary LMMs are benchmarked, and a human study on a small subset shows a minimal performance variance (<2.5%) between automatic and manual annotations.

### Strengths
- **Clear motivation**: Addresses the critical issue of test set contamination in LMM evaluations by proposing a continuously evolving benchmark that draws on newly published scientific papers (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Scalable automatic pipeline**: The benchmark is generated without human involvement, using a structured PDF processor, GPT-4o for VQA generation, and Claude for filtering (R1, R2, R4). Reviewer 2 notes that similar data curation and evaluation methods have been effective in language-only domains and are now extended to multimodal.
- **Efficient evaluation method**: The introduced approach (inspired by Item Response Theory) offers significant computational savings by evaluating only a subset of models to estimate the full ranking (R1, R2, R4).
- **Effective filtering**: The human study confirms that the automatic filtering pipeline removes annotation errors; performance variance between automatic and manual annotations is minimal (<2.5%) (R2; also mentioned in abstract).
- **Comprehensive LMM evaluation**: The benchmark covers a range of state-of-the-art open and proprietary LMMs (R3).
- **Expandability**: The design allows future expansion into new domains (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Reliance on proprietary models for generation and filtering**: Using GPT-4o for question generation and Claude for filtering introduces potential biases (e.g., favoring Claude-Sonnet’s architecture, as seen in its top performance in Table 1) and low reproducibility (R1, R2, R3, R4). Reviewer 1 specifically suggests that Claude’s role in verification may skew results. The reliance on closed-source APIs also makes the benchmark difficult to replicate (R2, R4).
- **Limited diversity of question types and format**: All questions are generated by GPT-4o and are multiple-choice, restricting evaluation to a narrow range of capabilities and potentially missing generative/reasoning aspects (R1, R2). Reviewer 1 and R2 recommend including free-form or long-form generation. Reviewer 3 adds that using only captions and OCR for tables/figures (ignoring broader context) leads to simple structure‑related questions (e.g., “Which column has value X?”) rather than insightful content‑based ones.
- **Scope and domain coverage**: Some reviewers consider the number of samples and domain diversity insufficient for a benchmark paper (R4). The focus on Knowledge‑VQA tasks narrows applicability; data contamination is a broader issue (R4). Reviewer 3 notes that only certain visual types (tables, figures, block diagrams) are used, which may oversimplify scientific visuals.
- **Need for more extensive human validation**: The human study only verified the QA filtering step, not the final model ranking alignment with human perception or with established benchmarks like DocVQA and ChartQA (R1, R2). Reviewer 3 requests a more detailed manual check beyond the brief description (Line 255) to validate the entire LLM‑generated dataset for hallucinations/errors.
- **Novelty concerns**: Several reviewers question the technical innovation, noting that the approach is a straightforward combination of existing techniques: parsing papers, using proprietary APIs for synthetic data generation and filtering, and applying an efficient evaluation method from NLP to VLM (R2, R4). Reviewer 2 asks for clarification regarding innovations compared to prior work on paper QA (e.g., [1] from the review).
- **Robustness of the evaluation method**: The efficiency and robustness of the evaluation model over time, across diverse paper styles/topics, and with potential distribution shifts caused by outdated LMMs is not fully validated (R3, R4).
- **Missing analyses and details**: 
  - The paper does not show how models perform across different domains and categories (Table 3 correlation) (R2).
  - The reason why GPT-4o performs badly in Table 1 despite being the question generator is not addressed (R2).
  - The specific LLM used at Line 235, the test set selection for Table 1, and the exact 5 models selected by the algorithm (Line 458) are unclear (R3).
  - How the system ensures diversity in question types and difficulty levels is not explained (R1, R2).
