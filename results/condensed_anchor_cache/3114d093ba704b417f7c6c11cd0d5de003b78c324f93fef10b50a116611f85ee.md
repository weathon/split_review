## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses single-frame temporal action localization (STAL) by proposing a dual-level prototype guidance (DPG) method with a graph matching random walk (Gm-Rw) algorithm and an adaptive collaborative strategy (ACS) to refine pseudo-labels. Instance-level guidance builds intra-class associations within a video, and video-level guidance uses a memory bank for more discriminative prototypes. Experiments are conducted on THUMOS14, GTEA, BEOID, and ActivityNet1.3. Reviewer opinions are split: two reviewers rate 5, citing novelty and strong results; two rate 3, raising concerns about novelty, missing references, writing quality, and experimental omissions.

### Strengths
1. The dual-level prototype guidance (DPG) – combining instance-level and video-level prototypes via graph matching random walk – introduces a novel perspective for pseudo-label refinement in STAL, addressing insufficient feature discrimination and incomplete feature representation.
2. The adaptive collaborative strategy (ACS) for dynamic optimization is a notable addition.
3. Extensive results on four datasets (THUMOS14, GTEA, BEOID, ActivityNet1.3) show state-of-the-art mAP@IoU, with ablation studies on THUMOS14 confirming the effectiveness of each component.
4. Qualitative results further support the method’s superiority.
5. Baseline comparisons are thorough, and a large body of component-wise ablations is provided.
6. One reviewer found the paper clearly written and easy to comprehend (though this is contested – see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and coverage of literature** (disagreement): Two reviewers consider DPG a good direction and sufficiently novel; one reviewer argues it builds on existing pseudo-label strategies and the Gm-Rw innovation is insufficient for publication. The paper omits key recent references for comparison: HR-PRO (AAAI24), Enhancing Single-frame Supervision (TVCG24), and Stepwise Multi-grained Boundary Detector (ECCV). Additionally, Tables 1 and 3 lack comparisons with advanced fully-supervised TAD methods (e.g., Actionformer, TadTR).
- **Experimental limitations**:
  - On GTEA, mAP@IoU=0.1 and 0.3 are marginally worse than NGPR (bold annotations need correction).
  - Improvement is large on smaller datasets (THUMOS14, BEOID) but small on large-scale ActivityNet1.3; the authors should provide analysis for this discrepancy.
  - Only tested with I3D backbone; experiments with more advanced backbones (e.g., Omnivore, VideoMAE) are requested.
  - Extensibility to open-vocabulary or multi-label video scenarios is questionable, potentially limiting downstream impact.
- **Missing analyses**:
  - No discussion on why sparse annotations cause specific issues, reducing readability.
  - The claim at line 102 that dual-level guidance leads to a “generalized vision framework” (“Flexibility”) is not supported by experimental evidence.
  - Computational cost not addressed: missing runtime, parameter count, and memory consumption comparisons.
  - Lack of ablation for hyperparameters: λ and ω₁ in Eq. (8) on performance, and ω₁ in Eq. (5) specifically.
  - No ablation study for alternative algorithms that could replace Graph Matching in Eqs. (5) and (6).
- **Writing and presentation quality** (disagreement): One reviewer found the paper clear; three others (including the two higher-scoring reviewers) noted substantial issues:
  - Many symbols undefined or confusing: `sort()` in Eq. (1), `M_c` in Eq. (2) unexplained; how `A` in Eq. (4) relates to `A` in Eq. (3) is unclear; `W_D` in Eq. (6) and `W_A` in Eq. (5) are not defined.
  - Equations lack punctuation (e.g., missing periods).
  - Syntax error at line 231 (“Where” should be “where”); errors in Figure 3 description.
  - Spelling and grammar errors; abbreviation “DBP” is introduced without definition.
  - One reviewer described the abstract as unprofessional.
  - Citation format in references is inconsistent and contains inaccuracies (e.g., missing pages, wrong formats such as “Zikang Yu et al....”).
