## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces RetPur, a pre-trained diffusion model that purifies adversarial test datasets for defending targeted attacks in uni-modal (Image-to-Image) and cross-modal (Image-to-Image, Image-to-Text) hash retrieval systems. Adversarial samples are used as conditioning factors to guide image generation. One reviewer (score 6) found the problem novel, the methodology high quality, and the implications significant. Two reviewers (scores 3) considered the contribution limited because the approach closely follows existing diffusion-based purification works (Guided Diffusion Model for Adversarial Purification [1]; Diffusion Models for Adversarial Purification [2]) and is essentially a transfer from image classification to retrieval. All reviewers agree the paper is well-written and the experiments demonstrate improved defense performance.

### Strengths
- Introduces adversarial purification into hash retrieval systems, mitigating targeted attacks in both uni-modal and cross-modal retrieval scenarios.
- Validates effectiveness through extensive experiments across multiple attack types (generative and iterative) and modalities, showing the purified dataset achieves performance close to the original dataset.
- The paper is well-written and easy to understand.
- (Minority reviewer) Addresses a novel problem in image retrieval with high-quality methodology and comparative analysis, holding significant implications for deep learning and image retrieval.

### Weaknesses
- The method’s novelty is below the acceptance threshold: it reuses adversarial examples as guidance (as in [1]) and applies the forward/reverse diffusion process to remove perturbations (as in [2]), transferring a known protection paradigm from classification to retrieval without task-specific adaptations.
- The paper does not provide a specific explanation of the differences between RetPur and the “Guided Diffusion Model for Adversarial Purification” ( [1]), making it unclear what is newly contributed.
- The purification approach is akin to preprocessing the data; clean preprocessed data can obviously be used for various downstream tasks, further reducing novelty.
- Missing ablation experiments: the authors should evaluate the method across more datasets, attack methods, and retrieval models.
- The method is not specific to image retrieval or cross-modal relationships; why not conduct experiments on other tasks (e.g., classification) to demonstrate generalizability?
- Computational overhead and scalability of the plug-and-play diffusion model are not addressed. An evaluation of time and resource costs, along with a discussion of feasibility for real-world large-scale applications, is needed.
- It is unclear whether there are specific adversarial attacks or scenarios where RetPur might not be effective.
- Typos: Figure 1 writes “RerPur” instead of “RetPur”; the Conclusion section uses “RerPur” and “PerPur” incorrectly.
