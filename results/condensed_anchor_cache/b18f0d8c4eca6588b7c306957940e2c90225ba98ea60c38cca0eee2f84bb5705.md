## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a benchmark for multimodal continual learning (CL) using the VGGSound dataset (visual and audio modalities) and introduces a simple baseline method (SAMM) that leverages experience replay and relational structural similarities to integrate multimodal information. The authors provide empirical evidence that multiple modalities can reduce catastrophic forgetting compared to single-modality learning.

### Strengths
- The paper introduces a novel multimodal (visual–audio) CL benchmark based on VGGSound, covering three CL scenarios. If made public, it would provide a standardized evaluation framework for the community. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- Empirical evidence in Section 3 demonstrates the complementary benefits of integrating vision and audio modalities, showing more robust representations that reduce forgetting. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- The paper is well motivated and organized, and easy to follow. (Reviewers 1,3)
- The analysis of how individual modalities exhibit varying robustness to distribution shift offers useful insight. (Reviewer 2, implicitly in strength of empirical evidence)

### Weaknesses
- **Weak baselines and lack of comparison**: The only baseline compared is standard Experience Replay (ER, 2018). More recent unimodal CL methods (e.g., Buzzega et al., NIPS20; Arani et al., PAMI 2022; SS-IL; knowledge distillation with adaptive feature consolidation) and other CL categories (regularization‑based, architecture‑based) are not evaluated. This makes it unclear how the proposed method compares to state‑of‑the‑art approaches. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- **Insufficient reproduction details**: The paper does not provide clear data splits of VGGSound for the continual learning tasks, exact hyperparameters, or other experimental details needed to independently verify the results. (Reviewer 2)
- **Memory storage fairness**: The memory buffer stores the same number of examples for single‑modality and multi‑modality, but multi‑modality data requires more storage per sample. Comparisons should be made under equal storage cost. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limited modality scope**: Experiments are only on visual and audio modalities. The authors should discuss whether the approach applies to other modalities (e.g., language) and explain the advantage over existing vision‑language CL benchmarks such as CLiMB. (Reviewers 1,3)
- **Method novelty questioned**: The proposed SAMM is a straightforward combination of experience replay and existing multimodal learning techniques; it is not clear what unique contribution it makes. (Reviewer 1, with high confidence: 5)
- **Missing analysis of limitations**: The paper does not explore sensitivity to hyperparameters, performance under different task sequences, or pros/cons of the method. Reviewer 2 notes this makes the paper weak both as a dataset proposal (no thorough characterization) and as a method proposal (no clear validation of uniqueness). Contrast: Reviewer 3 considers the benchmark beneficial but also asks for additional comparisons.
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - Why compare only with ER? (Reviewer 2)
  - What is the motivation for using relational structural similarity? (Reviewer 2)
  - Why can the method not be applied to vision‑language tasks? (Reviewer 3)
  - How does this benchmark differ from CLiMB beyond modality? (Reviewer 3)
  - How does it relate to the concurrent work Audio‑Visual Class‑Incremental Learning? (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing visualization**: For the semantic‑aware feature alignment, the authors should provide visualizations (e.g., similar to Figure 4 in “The Modality Focusing Hypothesis”) to demonstrate that the model indeed learns modality‑specific features. (Reviewer 3)
- **Minor issues**:
  - Typo in Figure 4 caption: “leverage leverages” → “leverage”. (Reviewers 2,3)
  - Line 5 of page 5: “models a capture” → “models to capture”. (Reviewer 2)
  - Figure 3 uses colors that are hard to distinguish in grayscale; consider different line styles. (Reviewer 2)
