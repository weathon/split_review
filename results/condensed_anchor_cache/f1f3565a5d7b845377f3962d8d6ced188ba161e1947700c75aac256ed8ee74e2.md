## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes FAITHQA, a benchmark of 18,068 problems for evaluating “intent hallucination” — the phenomenon where an LLM follows instructions but produces factually incorrect output. The benchmark covers query-only and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) setups, and separates hallucinations into minor fabrication (sentence-level errors) and major fabrication (paragraph-level fabrication). The paper also introduces INTENT DECOMPOSE, an automatic evaluation method that breaks a query into weighted intent constraints and computes an importance-weighted score. Various open-source and proprietary LLMs are evaluated on the benchmark.

### Strengths
- The problem of instruction-following overshadowing factual accuracy is well-motivated and important.
- The benchmark is large (18k problems) and each query is paired with fine-grained importance weights.
- The evaluation includes many open-source and proprietary LLMs, providing a broad comparison.
- The idea of decomposing a query into point-wise intent constraints is excellent for fine-grained LLM evaluation.
- The benchmark covers both query-only and RAG setups with varying topics and difficulty levels.

### Weaknesses
- **Paper presentation is very poor, hindering readability.** Multiple reviewers noted that many sections are extremely hard to understand. Numerous typos and formatting issues exist (e.g., line 38 “a instruct”, line 45 missing space, line 138 missing verb, line 229 wrong equation R=P(M|q), incorrect use of quotation marks, and missing spaces before citations).
- **Critical details about the FAITHQA benchmark construction are missing.**  
  – No example is given for any subset (e.g., minor fabrication in the Tech domain, or major fabrication in the Relationship subtask).  
  – “FactQA” (line 276) is introduced without definition or citation.  
  – It is unclear how queries are generated, what external content is used, and how the RAG setup is adapted for major fabrication.  
  – The quality control process is described only as “manual review of each example to ensure that the questions were well-constructed” — this is too vague to assess benchmark quality.
- **Experiment inconsistencies and missing details.**  
  – Accuracies for Mistral-7B on the minor fabrication subset are inconsistent between Table 1 (e.g., Culture domain Acc=0.63) and Table 2 (Culture domain Easy=0.15, Hard=0.09).  
  – The metric “Acc” is listed alongside “Base” in Table 1, but it is not clearly defined, and the reason for the large gap between them is unexplained.  
  – The paper claims human evaluation of INTENT DECOMPOSE (line 113-114), but **no human evaluation is presented**. Without it, the reliability of the automatic results is questionable.
- **Insufficient baselines and analysis.**  
  – Only one baseline method is compared for INTENT DECOMPOSE. More baselines (e.g., FActScore, FLASK) are needed to demonstrate effectiveness.  
  – The paper draws four conclusions in Section 7, but provides no quantitative breakdown of error types — only a few anecdotal examples.  
  – No correlation analysis between human evaluation and automatic evaluation is provided.
- **The proposed method’s contributions are incremental.** Similar fine-grained evaluation frameworks exist (e.g., FActScore [Min et al. 2023], FLASK [Ye et al. 2024]) and are not adequately differentiated.
- **The weight assignment for intents is unexplored.** No section describes how importance labels are determined, and the meaning of these weights is not investigated.
- **Missing related work.** The paper omits relevant RAG evaluation literature (e.g., Chen et al. 2024 on four fundamental generator abilities; Ru et al. 2024 on RAGChecker; Liu et al. 2024 on counterfactual robustness). It does not compare its approach to these works.
- **The premise of the experiment is unconvincing** (noted by one reviewer): the paper implicitly assumes that a lower identification rate of hallucination is better, but this should be validated against human judgments.
- **No verification of the query decomposition step** — it is not shown whether an LLM can reliably decompose queries into the given constraint categories.
- **Questions that reflect missing information:** How is Acc computed? What is the test set size? What are the ratios of each error case in the analysis? Why is only one baseline used? How are the intent weights determined?
