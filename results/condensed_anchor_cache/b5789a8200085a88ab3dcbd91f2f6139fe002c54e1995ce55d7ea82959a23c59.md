## Merged Review

### Summary

This work proposes Agnostic-SAM, which combines Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) with Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning (MAML). The method uses a worst-case perturbation on a training batch (inner loop) and then adjusts the perturbed parameters further using a gradient descent step on a validation batch (outer loop), aiming to find flatter minima that also generalize under distribution shift. The authors provide theoretical motivation via a PAC-Bayes bound, and evaluate on image classification, noisy-label training, transfer learning, and meta-learning benchmarks. Across reviewers, the evaluation is seen as comprehensive (R1, R3) but the novelty, baseline comparisons, and computational cost are major concerns.

### Strengths

- The motivation to combine sharpness-minimization with meta-learning for better generalization makes sense, and the intersection of SAM and MAML is an encouraging direction (R1, R2, R4).
- Agnostic-SAM improves over the reported baselines in most experiments, including supervised learning, label noise, and meta-learning settings (R1, R4).
- The method is extensively tested across a wide range of tasks: image classification (CIFAR, ImageNet), transfer learning, noisy labels, and meta-learning benchmarks (R1, R3).
- The authors found a consistent hyperparameter setting across experiments: \(\rho_1 = 2\rho_2\) (R4).
- Combining ideas from SAM and MAML is a creative approach (R4).

### Weaknesses

1. **Novelty and relationship to prior work**  
   - The method is **eerily similar** to Abbas et al. 2022 (Sharp-MAML) but the paper does not explain the differences or similarities (R1).  
   - In the MAML experiment (Tables 6,7), only the Sharp-MAML-low variant from [9] is compared; the Sharp-MAML-both variant outperforms Agnostic-SAM in three out of four reported cases, and the omission is not justified (R4).  
   - The paper claims to be a “framework,” but it is a simple combination of SAM and MAML without rigorous evaluation to show it is a general scheme; it remains a proof-of-concept (R2).

2. **Motivation and justification**  
   - The main idea and motivation appear random: why specifically combine these two methods? No clear justification is given (R2).  
   - The problem formulation in Equation (3) is not convincingly justified; it is unclear how the proposed algorithm directly aids generalization beyond existing methods (R3, R4).  
   - Theorem 1 is nearly identical to Theorem 1 in the original SAM paper (Foret et al. 2021) with minimal modification, and the connection from that theorem to Equation (3) and the final algorithm is not understandable (R4).  
   - The MAML perspective is unclear because in practice both the “train” and “validation” batches are sampled from the training set, and almost all experiments involve only a single task (R4).

3. **Computational cost and fairness of comparison**  
   - Agnostic-SAM requires **4 gradient computations per step** (two forward-backward on a train batch, two on a validation batch), while SGD uses 1 and SAM uses 2. The reported improvements are marginal, making the method seem not worth the extra cost (R1, R2, R3, R4).  
   - Wall-clock time increases by roughly **27%** (Table 9), yet baselines (SAM, ASAM) are not given additional iterations to account for this. For a fair comparison, SAM/ASAM should be allowed to run for twice the iterations of Agnostic-SAM (as was done in Foret et al. 2021 for SGD) (R3, R4).  
   - The method also requires an additional validation set (or splitting the training set), adding hyperparameter tuning overhead (R2).

4. **Hyperparameter and ablation concerns**  
   - **Momentum**: Table 8 shows that using **no momentum** yields better performance, yet the authors still use momentum in all main experiments, despite the gains being of similar magnitude (R1).  
   - **Perturbation radii**: The choice \(\rho_1 = 2\rho_2\) is used throughout, but it is unclear if this was grid-searched, based on intuition, or derived from the ablation in Appendix A2. Some \(\rho\) values (e.g., \(\rho=0.05\) for SAM in ImageNet transfer learning vs. \(\rho=0.1\) for ImageNet training from scratch) appear arbitrary (R1, R4).  
   - **Reproduction of baselines**: For CIFAR-100 with WRN28-10, the reproduced SAM accuracy (83.0%) is lower than the original SAM paper (83.5%), which would outperform the reported Agnostic-SAM number. This raises fairness concerns (R4).  
   - **Ablation on inner/outer SAM**: No ablation study separates the contribution of SAM in the inner loop vs. the outer loop. Which part benefits more? (R1)

5. **Scope of experiments**  
   - The experiments only compare against a few SAM variants (ASAM, FSAM). Many other SAM improvements are not included (e.g., Liu et al. 2022, Kim et al., Mordido et al., Andriushchenko 2022, Fisher SAM, Sparsified SAM, Efficient SAM) (R4).  
   - The only larger-scale experiment (ImageNet from scratch) uses at most a ResNet-34; no Vision Transformers, no text data, and limited model diversity (R4).  
   - Improvements over baselines are generally **marginal** (R1, R2). Given the extra computational cost, it is questionable whether Agnostic-SAM is the best possible choice for generalization (R2).

6. **Clarity and correctness**  
   - The notation \(\theta^*(\theta)\) is not introduced; the Taylor expansion in Equation (7) is presented as an exact equality (R4).  
   - The paper claims to align perturbed gradients of train and validation batches, but in Section 5.1 it reports alignment of the **unperturbed** train gradient with the **perturbed** validation gradient – inconsistency in what is measured (R4).  
   - The algorithm assumes a held-out validation set, but in practice the training set is used as the validation set, which diverges from the theoretical framing. This is especially problematic for CIFAR-10/100, where training loss converges to zero – a behavior not seen with a true validation set (R3).

7. **Unanswered reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms**  
   - How were the perturbation radii (\(\rho_1=2\rho_2\)) determined? Was it grid-searched? What is the intuition? (R1, R4)  
   - What exactly are the claimed contributions from Theorems 1 and 2? (R2, R4)  
   - In the ImageNet experiments (first set), why are top-1/top-5 accuracies so low? Can the authors provide ResNet-50 results and standard errors? (R2)  
   - What is the exact difference between the two versions of Agnostic-SAM in Table 2 and Table 5? Why does Agnostic-SAM (with ASAM) often underperform Agnostic-SAM (with SAM)? (R2)
