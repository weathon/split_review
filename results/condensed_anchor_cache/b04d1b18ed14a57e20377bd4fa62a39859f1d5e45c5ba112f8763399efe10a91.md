## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces WeatherODE, a physics-driven neural ODE that models weather evolution using an advection equation. It employs a CNN-ViT-CNN sandwich architecture: a wave-equation-informed CNN estimates initial velocity v(t0); a ViT-based neural ODE models velocity dynamics; a 3D CNN learns a time-dependent source term. Experiments on 5.625° ERA5 data (5 variables, 72h lead time) claim large RMSE improvements over baselines (>40% global, >31.8% regional). Reviewer ratings are mixed (1,3,3,5,6). Two reviewers are positive (R3=5, R4=6), two moderate (R1=3, R5=3, with R5 post-response decreasing to reject), one very negative (R2=1).

### Strengths
- The wave-equation-inspired approach for estimating time derivatives of velocity and the CNN-ViT-CNN sandwich architecture for advection and source terms are interesting (R1, R3, R4, R5). The source field addition is considered a major contribution (R5), and the overall ODE-based physics-informed framework is promising for chaotic systems such as weather (R2, R3, R4).
- Strong empirical results: WeatherODE outperforms all baselines by large margins (R1, R3, R5), in some cases even exceeding IFS, which is an outstanding achievement (R5). The experimental setup is robust and well-documented (R3, R4). Ablation studies and convergence speed analysis are provided (R1, R3). The model enables flexible inference for variable lead times (R3).
- The paper is well organized with clear structure and informative figures and tables (R3, R4). The continuous dynamics capability allows time interpolation (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Physical assumptions and validity**: The advection equation is a gross oversimplification; weather dynamics requires compressible fluid dynamics (buoyancy, convection, teleconnections, turbulence), which the model excludes (R2). The assumption of incompressibility ignores key phenomena (e.g., ENSO, hurricanes, general circulation) (R2). The model also ignores Earth's sphericity, coriolis force, and conservation of mass, energy, and momentum; the advection equation alone does not guarantee energy conservation, and neural networks may introduce non-physical sources/sinks (R2). Better approximations (Shallow Water Equations, Quasi-Geostrophic) exist and are more realistic (R2, R4). The wave equation used for initial velocity estimation is poorly motivated and appears incompatible with the advective ODE system; Laplacians are introduced without justification, and the connection between the wave and advection equations is never clearly explained (R3, R5). The derivation of Eq. (5) may not be appropriate for predicting v(t0) (R3). The statement that spatial resolution is 100× denser than temporal is dimensionally inconsistent and unconvincing; (t,x,y) ranges are similar (24,32,64), so no resolution gap (R5, R3). The study uses a 2D equation and ignores vertical velocity, which is critical for tropical predictability and convection; treating vertical velocity inside the source term may be inadequate (R4). Data assimilation errors in ERA5 are non-systematic, so modeling source terms from them could introduce unpredictable errors (R4).

- **Model complexity and practical applicability**: The architecture (CNN+ViT+CNN) is becoming too cumbersome and threatens the computational speed advantage of AI models; running at 5.625° resolution already yields run times comparable to traditional NWP (R4). The model has low practical applicability because ERA5 is not operationally available (R1), the coarse resolution (5.625° versus 0.25°) and few variables (5) are unconvincing for real-world forecasting, and the short lead time (72h) limits utility (R2). Simply using higher-resolution ERA5 data (0.25°) might achieve similar improvements in gradient accuracy without introducing wave-equation complexity (R4). The model cannot capture severe weather or provide meaningful longer-term predictions (R2).

- **Experimental fairness and baselines**: The comparison to ClimODE is unfair: ClimODE uses only 5 variables while WeatherODE uses 48, and ClimODE results are taken as-is from the original paper; an apples-to-apples comparison is needed (R5). ClimaX is a weak baseline (trained on CMIP6, not designed for forecasting); stronger baselines such as SFNO-based FourCastNet or GraphCast should be used (R4, R2). IFS baselines are missing from some evaluations (R2, R5). The achievement of beating IFS is reported too casually without thorough discussion relative to Pangu, GenCast, or GraphCast (R5). Results lack standard deviations (R5). A 0.01–0.02 RMSE difference may not be physically meaningful; case-specific tests (e.g., cyclogenesis) are required (R4). Appendix Table 9 shows ACC for u10 degrading from 0.93 to 0.80 over 36h, and the model is inferior to ClimaX at 3-day lead time (R2).

- **Methodological clarity and presentation**: The v(t0) estimation is unclear: the wave equation is introduced but not connected to the CNN; the link between v(t0) and ∂u/∂t is not explicitly stated (R3, R5). The source model is confusing: Sec. 3.4 describes the source taking as input all states u(t₁..tₙ), which is impossible during an ODE rollout; it appears the source is estimated from future states or added as a correction after the PDE rollout, contradicting Eqs. (1)–(3) if that is the case (R5). The “convergence” argument for choosing CNNs vs. ViTs is vague (convergence of training? rollouts?), and the claim that CNNs are unstable is implausible; the choice should be motivated by physical properties (locality/globality) (R5). The claim that advection dynamics involve long-range dependencies is questionable since PDEs are local (R5). The claim that earlier methods ignore intermediate time points is incorrect—ClimODE and neural ODEs train on all intermediate points; calling this “multi-task learning” is wrong (R5). Notation in Sec 3.1 (t₀, tₙ) is confusing (R3). There is over-citation of irrelevant references (Vaswani et al., Evans 2022) and figures (1, 4) have text that is too small (R4). Computational resource usage and runtime comparisons are missing (R3, R4).

- **Minority disagreement**: One reviewer (R2) rates the work 1, stating that the physical assumptions are fundamentally flawed and the model is of no practical use. Another reviewer (R5) initially positive but after response decreased score to reject, concluding that the wave equation and source term explanations violate the PDE formalism and the paper is not ready for publication. In contrast, two reviewers (R3, R4) rate 5–6, praising the experimental results and hybrid approach despite noting clarity issues.
