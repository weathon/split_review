## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes DEITA (Data-Efficient Instruction Tuning for Alignment), an automatic data selection method for instruction tuning. It scores data samples along three dimensions—complexity (of the instruction), quality (of the response), and diversity—using a learning-based approach (fine-tuned LLaMA-7B on GPT-scored samples from the 20K Alpaca dataset) and then selects a small set (e.g., 6K SFT + 10K DPO) that achieves performance comparable to or better than state-of-the-art models trained on much more data (MT-Bench 7.55, AlpacaEval 90.06% for Mistral-7B+DPO). The paper provides controlled studies to validate the scoring measurements and an efficient, diversity-aware selection algorithm.

### Strengths
- The procedure used to quantify complexity and quality (iteratively improving seed samples with ChatGPT, then having ChatGPT score them, then training a smaller Llama-7B model to predict those scores) is innovative and can be applied to other hard-to-quantify data quality aspects in future work.
- The controlled experiments clearly demonstrate that the proposed method selects important data points (i.e., outperforms random selection) on AlpacaEval and MT-Bench.
- In-depth controlled studies show that the proposed scoring measurements are better than baseline metrics for complexity and quality.
- Fine-tuning LLaMA-7B on 20K Alpaca dataset with GPT scores allows scoring unseen instructions at low cost.
- The diversity-aware selection method is efficient and easy to implement.
- Clear presentation and easy-to-follow writing, with nice tables and illustrations.
- The paper is structurally complete and self-contained, with a reasonable narrative. (Reviewer 3 acknowledges this but also finds the contribution insufficient.)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation scope**: The main evaluation relies solely on AlpacaEval and MT-Bench (GPT-4 based). These are small evaluation sets, and GPT-4 evaluation is known to be biased (Wang et al., 2023; https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04751). The paper does not include benchmarks with ground-truth answers like MMLU or Big-Bench-Hard to verify commonsense knowledge and reasoning. (All three reviewers; Reviewer 2 flags marginal performance improvement: DEITA is worse than Vicuna-13B on AlpacaEval, and human evaluation shows Vicuna performs similarly to DEITA.)
- **Human evaluation is limited**: Only 100 instances from LIMA's test set were used, and no inter-annotator agreement or significance measures are reported. Larger scale evaluation would be needed for reliability. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing baselines**: Recent instruction selection works such as LIMA and Alpagasus are not included in the evaluation. (Reviewer 2)
- **Novelty concerns and overlap with similar work**: The approach and findings are very similar to several recent papers (e.g., Alpagasus, Instruction Mining, InstructionGPT-4). Combining existing metrics (quality, diversity, etc.) in a straightforward way and evaluating using GPTs is not technically challenging or particularly innovative. (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 1 and 2 find the procedure innovative, so there is a disagreement.)
- **Validation of scoring procedure**: ChatGPT is shown multiple evolved samples from the same seed example when scoring complexity (and likely quality). It is unclear whether scores are comparable across different seed sets; if not, this could affect the trained score predictor. The paper would benefit from human scoring to correlate with ChatGPT's judgments. (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient ablation and analysis**: The paper lacks ablation studies, insightful discussions of design choices, or novel findings. No additional results or discussions are provided in the Appendix. (Reviewer 3)
- **Reproducibility**: Code, data, model checkpoints, or data selection tools were not provided during the review phase. (Reviewer 3)
- **Format issue**: The appendix is included within the main paper PDF (20 pages) rather than being cut separately. (Reviewer 3)
- **Potential issues with diversity selection**: The diversity criterion might end up selecting outliers. Is there a way to fix this? (Reviewer 1 question; overlaps with criticism of method)
- **Unaddressed question**: Could a different (smaller) model be used for computing distances instead of the same model being instruction-tuned? (Reviewer 1 question)
- **Surprising human evaluation result**: DEITA-6K loses more to the random selection baseline than to Vicuna (Table 4), which seems to contradict expectations. (Reviewer 1 question; indicates missing analysis)
- **Typo**: “large margine” should be “large margin” in the introduction. (Reviewer 2)
- **Unjustified design choice**: Complexity is measured from the instruction, quality from the response—what is the intuition behind this split? (Reviewer 2 question; suggests missing justification)
