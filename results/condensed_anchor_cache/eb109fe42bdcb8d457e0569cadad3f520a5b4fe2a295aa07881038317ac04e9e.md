## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces SONAR, a framework and benchmark for detecting AI-generated audio, constructed from 2274 samples from 9 TTS platforms (e.g., OpenAI, PromptTTS2). It evaluates traditional and foundation-model-based detectors using EER, AUROC, and Accuracy, studying generalization and few-shot fine-tuning. Reviewers note the dataset and systematic evaluation as valuable, but raise major concerns about dataset size, diversity, missing forgery types, incomplete related-work comparisons, and limited novelty.

### Strengths
- First large-scale evaluation dataset incorporating audio samples from nine different sources, including cutting-edge TTS providers and state-of-the-art models (e.g., VALL-E, NaturalSpeech 3, XTTS, WhisperSpeech).
- Comprehensive experiments evaluating generalization capabilities of multiple detection methods (traditional and foundation models) using multidimensional metrics (accuracy, AUROC, EER).
- Demonstrates the potential of few-shot fine-tuning to improve detection performance, suitable for customized or personalized detection systems.
- Covers a wide range of audio synthesis techniques, enhancing real-world robustness.
- The work is a meaningful supplement to existing speech forgery detection research, with clear explanations and acknowledgment of limitations.
- Open about study limitations and future research directions.

### Weaknesses
- **Related works inadequately addressed**: Prior benchmarks like VoiceWukong (34 synthesis platforms) and works evaluating detectors on state-of-the-art generation (VALL-E, NaturalSpeech 3) are not cited. ASVspoof 2019/2021 are not used for training/testing despite being standard. The paper should clarify SONAR’s unique advantages over these established benchmarks (e.g., VoiceWukong, MLAAD).
- **Dataset too small and imbalanced**: Only 2274 fake samples from 9 generation methods, vs. existing datasets with >10,000 samples. Samples per method vary dramatically (e.g., 25 for PromptTTS2 vs. 600 for OpenAI), which significantly affects detection performance. Total sample size is limited for robust generalization conclusions.
- **Missing forgery types**: Focuses only on TTS forgery; does not cover voice conversion, voice splicing, or synthetic acoustic scenes. AudioGen data (synthetic acoustic scenes) is included without clear relevance to speech detection. If the goal is to explore fake speech vs. synthetic sounds, separate subsets with more generation methods are needed.
- **Lack of diversity and real-world variability**: Dataset has restricted range of languages, voices, speech content, and lacks details on speakers. Variables like speech encoding compression, format, background noise, room impulse response, and audio length are not considered, which could impact detection and generalization.
- **Conclusions are well-established**: Findings (foundation models improve performance, larger model size or more fine-tuning data helps) are already known from prior work. Due to small dataset size, per-subset results may not fully reflect broader performance.
- **Missing documentation and interpretability**: No quality assessment of generated spoofed examples (e.g., naturalness, artifacts). No analysis of which specific speech attributes cause model failures. The paper would benefit from interpretability methods like LIME or SHAP.
- **Preprocessing unclear**: Figure 1 shows data preprocessing at evaluation, but specific steps (sample rate, format, normalization) for ensuring dataset consistency are not explained.
- **Training/testing setup not standard**: Common protocol (train on ASVspoof 2019 LA, test on others) is not followed; no test results on ASVspoof are provided. The impact of training data augmentation strategies and loss functions on model performance is not considered.
- **Minor counting discrepancy**: In line 125, 6 speakers × 600 prompts = 3600 audios, but the number is stated as 600.
- **Limited innovation**: Several reviews and comparative studies on forgery speech detection already exist; the primary contribution is dataset construction and systematic testing, but methodological novelty is low.
- **Suggested supplementation**: Experiments should be extended to the MLAAD dataset (59 TTS models, multiple languages) to improve credibility.
