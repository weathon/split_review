- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 5, 3, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Gradient Inversion Transcript (GIT), a generative-model-based gradient inversion attack that assumes the adversary knows only the victim model’s architecture (not its parameters) and has access to some training data (without labels) to pre-train a proxy model. GIT adaptively constructs a generative network whose architecture mirrors the target model, and operates “offline” once client gradients are obtained. Experiments on CIFAR-10 with LeNet and ResNet show lower MSE compared to baseline methods. Reviewers found the problem meaningful and the adaptive architecture interesting, but raised major concerns about the practicality of the assumptions, the limited experimental scope, the lack of comparison with state-of-the-art attacks, and the theoretical depth.

### Strengths

- **Gradient inversion attacks are a crucial way to investigate the privacy of federated learning.** (Reviewer 4)
- **The adaptive architecture of the generative model** – mirroring the target model’s structure – allows more effective exploitation of gradient information than fixed-architecture generative methods. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **The attempt to relax assumptions** (no need for model weights or repeated queries) and find a proxy model is interesting. (Reviewer 1)
- **The training approach formalized in Algorithm 1 is novel.** (Reviewer 4)
- **The method includes some theoretical analysis of backpropagation**, providing a stronger justification compared to purely heuristic approaches. (Reviewer 3)
- **The paper demonstrates effectiveness in the setting of noisy gradients.** (Reviewer 4)
- **The attack scenario considered** – adversary with only shared gradients, no labels, and no ability to query the model – is practical in real-world constraints. (Reviewer 3)

One reviewer (Reviewer 4, score 6) was more positive, highlighting the novelty of Algorithm 1 and good readability, while another (Reviewer 3, score 3) was more negative, stressing overfitting issues and limited theoretical contribution.

### Weaknesses

1. **Impractical or poorly motivated threat model.**  
   - **Access to substantial training data (up to 10,000 images, 20% of CIFAR-10) violates the privacy assumption of FL.** Why would an attacker have this data but not the model weights? The paper does not justify this scenario or ablate the effect of the number of available data points. (Reviewers 1, 4)  
   - **Access to data but not labels is counterintuitive** – typical supervised training requires labels; the setting is not standard in FL attacks and needs justification. (Reviewer 1)  
   - **The claim of “no access to model parameters” is contradicted by the reliance on per-client gradients;** the server in FL typically sees only aggregated gradients. The method should be tested with aggregated gradients, or the assumption should be clarified. (Reviewer 1)  
   - **It is unclear where input-gradient pairs (needed for training the proxy model) would come from in practice.** (Reviewer 4)  
   - Reviewers 4 and 1 note that several existing attacks (e.g., SPEAR, DAGER) achieve exact reconstruction without any prior training data, making GIT’s data requirement a *stronger* assumption, not a weaker one. (Reviewer 4)

2. **Limited experimental scope.**  
   - Experiments are conducted **only on CIFAR-10 with only two architectures** (LeNet and ResNet). Generalizability to other datasets (e.g., ImageNet, medical images) and architectures (e.g., transformers, deeper networks) is not shown. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)  
   - **Only small batch sizes (up to 4) and small images (32×32) are considered.** Scalability to larger batches and higher resolutions is not demonstrated. (Reviewer 1, 4)  
   - **No results on out-of-distribution data or hold-out datasets** are provided to assess generalization. (Reviewer 1)  
   - **Acknowledged overfitting problems are not addressed.** (Reviewer 3)

3. **Insufficient baseline comparison.**  
   - Baselines operate under **different assumptions** (e.g., access to model parameters or repeated queries), making a fair comparison difficult. (Reviewer 1)  
   - **UNet, a well-established generative baseline for gradient inversion, is not included** as a baseline. (Reviewer 2)  
   - **No reconstructed images from baselines are shown** in the paper. (Reviewer 2)  
   - The paper lacks comparison with state-of-the-art methods that do not require training data (e.g., SPEAR, DAGER). (Reviewer 3, 4)

4. **Limited theoretical contribution.**  
   - The core equation (Eq. 4) is a **straightforward extension of gradient back-propagation**; the theoretical novelty is marginal. (Reviewer 2)  
   - The paper uses **pseudo-inverse for non-invertible layers without providing error bounds** or analyzing how approximation errors propagate through the reconstruction. (Reviewer 3)  
   - When MLPs are used to replace unstable approximations, **the practical advantage over simply training a larger MLP directly from gradients to inputs is unclear.** (Reviewer 3)

5. **Missing implementation details and analysis.**  
   - **The size of the fixed MLP layers used for fairness comparison is not given.** (Reviewer 2)  
   - **No analysis of the learned neural network architecture** – how similar is it to the target model? What metric can measure the effectiveness of the optimized structure? (Reviewer 2)  
   - **No analysis of the distance between the learned proxy weights and the victim model weights.** (Reviewer 1)

6. **Evaluation metrics are insufficient.**  
   - Only **MSE** is reported, which does not directly reflect visual quality. Perceptual loss, inception score, or other perception-based metrics should be included. (Reviewer 1)

7. **The term “offline training” is misleading.**  
   - The method still requires clients to produce gradients, which are inherently generated during online FL training. The exact meaning of “offline” and how it differs from standard online optimization is not clarified. (Reviewer 1)

8. **Terminological and presentation issues.**  
   - “Threat model” is misused (in L48) to refer to the attacker model, overloading the term. (Reviewer 4)  
   - Incomplete sentence after “federated learning (FL)” (L33). (Reviewer 4)  
   - Typos: “Since both σ and σ” (L194), missing braces around g_i (L245). (Reviewer 4)  
   - Table 3 should bold the best reported number. (Reviewer 4)

9. **Unanswered questions that overlap with criticisms.**  
   - How does GIT generalize to **federated averaging** (not just per-client gradients)? (Reviewer 4)  
   - Can it handle architectures beyond linear layers and skip connections (e.g., **transformer architectures**)? (Reviewer 4)  
   - Given the number of input-gradient pairs available, could one instead **reconstruct model weights** via gradient-matching? (Reviewer 4)  
   - Why is **UNet not considered** as a baseline? (Reviewer 2)
