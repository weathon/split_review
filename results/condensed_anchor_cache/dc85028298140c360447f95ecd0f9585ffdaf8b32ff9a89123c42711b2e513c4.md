## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a method to detect unauthorized data usage in text-to-image diffusion models by implanting injected memorization via stealthy image warping functions that are nearly imperceptible to humans but can be memorized by the model. Detection is performed by analyzing whether generated images exhibit the warping artifact. Experiments on Stable Diffusion and VQ Diffusion with LoRA, DreamBooth, and standard training demonstrate high detection accuracy over four datasets. Code is provided.

### Strengths
1. **Problem importance and timeliness** – Addresses a critical and pressing concern regarding unauthorized data usage and copyright protection for artists in the context of text-to-image diffusion models. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
2. **Novel connection to memorization** – Links unauthorized data usage detection to the memorization properties of diffusion models, which is a novel and interesting direction; the design is reasonable. (Reviewers 3, 4)
3. **Soundness and clarity of the approach** – The solution is solid, easy to follow, and the motivation is clear. (Reviewers 2, 4)
4. **Presentation quality** – Well-written, problem clearly illustrated with visual examples, easy-to-follow. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
5. **Experimental breadth** – Evaluated on state-of-the-art models (Stable Diffusion, VQ Diffusion) and training/personalization techniques (LoRA, DreamBooth, standard training), showing good robustness across different diffusion models. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
6. **High detection performance** – Achieves 100% detection accuracy under various settings with nearly unnoticeable perturbations; comparisons to existing methods are well-discussed. (Reviewer 4)
7. **Practical black-box access** – Only requires black-box access to the examined model, making the method practical for real-world use. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
1. **Missing robustness evaluation** – No analysis of robustness to common image transformations (compression, blurring, smoothing, sharpening, etc.) and no discussion of potential adversarial countermeasures that could defeat the detection. (Reviewers 1, 3)
2. **Insufficient quantitative evaluation of warping imperceptibility** – The paper lacks quantitative metrics (e.g., PSNR, SSIM, residual) to compare original and coated images, making it difficult to assess the actual distortion introduced by the warping and its impact on image quality or the training process. (Reviewers 2, 3)
3. **Lack of justification for key design choices** – 
   - The advantage of “element-level” memorization over “sample-level” memorization is not logically or experimentally justified. (Reviewer 3)
   - The motivation for two types of injected memorization and the necessity of the trigger function are not well explained. (Reviewer 3)
   - The choice of image warping over other possible operators (e.g., additive noise, watermarking) is not discussed, nor is the reversibility of the warping addressed (Reviewer 2 notes coated images may be permanently damaged). (Reviewer 2)
4. **Missing evaluation with Textual Inversion** – Textual Inversion is a common personalization technique for diffusion models, but the proposed method’s performance under that scenario is not tested. This is a significant gap given the prevalence of such techniques. (Reviewer 4)
5. **No discussion of computational cost** – The paper does not provide a breakdown of time cost for the image coating process, signal classifier training, and detection algorithm, which would be helpful for assessing scalability and practical applicability. (Reviewer 4)
6. **Potential flaw regarding coating rate** – The value of the coating rate used in Section 4.2 is not reported. It is unclear how a model trained on a subset of the data that does not include the protected portion could still be detected with 100% accuracy; if the subset selection does not overlap with the coated images, the detection mechanism should not work. (Reviewer 3)
7. **Typos and grammar errors** – E.g., on page 7: “we assume the subsets provide by different data sources” should be “we assume the subsets provided by different data sources.” (Reviewer 2)
8. **Insufficient evaluation scale** – Fewer than 20 models are constructed, which is far from enough to demonstrate the effectiveness of the approach. (Reviewer 3)
9. **Missing summary table for symbols** – Adding a table clarifying all symbols used would improve clarity. (Reviewer 4)

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* Three reviewers rated the paper 6, while one reviewer (Reviewer 4) rated it 8. Reviewer 4 was notably more positive about the novelty, performance, and practicality, whereas Reviewers 2 and 3 were more critical of missing justifications, insufficient evaluation, and lack of robustness analysis. Reviewer 2’s confidence was low (2), indicating less certainty in their assessment, and they noted several typos and missing metrics.
