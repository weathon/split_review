## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper provides a theoretical analysis of three topological descriptors (Euler characteristic, persistent homology, Laplacian spectrum) for enhancing graph neural networks, focusing on expressivity, stability, and computational cost. It introduces the novel descriptor RePHINE^{Spec}, proves it is strictly more expressive, analyzes weaker variants for computational savings, proposes stability metrics for RePHINE, and explores filtrations on molecular graphs and graph products. The work aims to guide the principled selection of topological descriptors.

### Strengths
- Comprehensive theoretical treatment with rigorous proofs: the paper proves the equivalence of EC and max EC diagrams, establishes stability bounds for RePHINE, and characterizes filtrations on graph products (Reviewer 2).
- Covers a range of topological descriptors (EC, PH, Laplacian spectrum) and provides a detailed comparison of expressivity, stability, and computational expenses (Reviewers 1, 5).
- Introduces useful variants (e.g., RePHINE^{Spec}) that maintain or improve expressivity while reducing computational costs (Reviewer 2).
- Addresses a significant problem: providing a comprehensive guide for which topological descriptor to use on different data/tasks, which would help the TDA in ML community (Reviewer 3).
- Outlines the challenges of topological methods in computational settings, especially in deep learning and GNNs (Reviewer 4).
- Considers applications in molecular graphs, offering insights relevant to chemistry and drug discovery (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of focus and cohesion**: The paper presents multiple, incremental findings loosely grouped under a single theme without a clear unifying narrative. Each topic (expressivity, stability, product of graphs, death-time filtrations) requires deeper individual analysis; the results appear largely unrelated (Reviewer 5). The flow is lacking from Section 4 onward; the motivation for product of graphs and death-time filtrations is unclear and not connected to the main analysis (Reviewers 3, 5). The purpose of Section 7 is not understood (Reviewer 3). Section 5’s applicability is not clearly motivated (Reviewer 3).
- **Expressivity analysis is incomplete from a machine learning perspective**: Expressivity of topological descriptors is difficult to define without specifying the space of filtration functions. Without this specification, the results lack meaning and are not comparable to GNN expressivity; the paper needs to explicitly define the space of filtration functions and justify its relevance (Reviewer 5). Some comparisons (e.g., EC less expressive than PH) are obvious from definitions and not surprising (Reviewer 4). Theorem 2 appears redundant given Theorem 1 (Reviewer 3).
- **Stability results are incremental and limited**: The stability result for RePHINE is a straightforward extension of the stability of persistence diagrams (Cohen-Steiner 2006) and offers limited novel contribution (Reviewers 3, 5). The paper should more clearly articulate the specific challenges in extending existing results to RePHINE and highlight technical hurdles and novel insights (Reviewer 5). The stability of RePHINE^{Spec} and EC is not characterized, and the analysis should be more thorough (e.g., considering different metrics for distance between persistence diagrams) (Reviewers 3, 5). Practical examples where stability significantly impacts outcomes are missing (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing empirical validation**: The paper is heavily theoretical and lacks experimental validation of the proposed methods, particularly RePHINE^{Spec} (Reviewers 2, 5). Since this is an ML venue, experiments demonstrating the utility of the approaches on molecular graphs (or other tasks) would illustrate practical implications (Reviewer 5). There is no evidence that the improved expressivity of RePHINE^{Spec} translates to better performance on real-world tasks (Reviewer 2).
- **Computational cost analysis is insufficient**: The computational cost for RePHINE^{Spec}, especially on large graphs or in real-time applications, is not addressed (Reviewer 1). The analysis of computational complexity remains abstract, lacking concrete details on how the additional spectral information impacts runtime and memory usage in practice (Reviewer 2). The paper does not discuss limitations for very large graphs where topological descriptors can become prohibitively expensive (Reviewer 2). Missing sensitivity analysis of descriptors to parameter variations (e.g., filtration type or depth) (Reviewer 1).
- **Clarity and presentation issues**: The definitions and theoretical explanations are dense and very difficult to follow for readers with limited background (Reviewer 1). The paper is not well written; it lacks a traditional bullet point list clearly summarizing all contributions (Reviewer 4). There is confusion about whether persistent or static versions of topological descriptors are studied; the presentation is not clear (Reviewer 4). Specific issues: “theoretical foundations of TDs remain rather elusive” is incorrect—all TDs have rich theory; should say expressivity analysis is elusive (Reviewer 3). Definition 1 has a typo: f_e(c,c’) should be symmetric (Reviewer 3). Definition 8: meaning of h1 ~ h2 is unclear (Reviewer 3). Line 491: “Bond Coloring Representation” should be “Bond Counting Representation”? (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing analyses and comparisons**: The paper does not address interpretability of topological methods, which may be related to expressivity (Reviewer 4). It would have been stronger as a rigorous computational survey rather than presenting new contributions that some reviewers view as straightforward extensions (Reviewer 4). The paper does not consider other types of filtrations beyond color-based ones (Reviewer 1). The discussion does not include the Hodge Laplacian for static descriptors (Reviewer 4). The framework does not handle dynamic or temporal graphs (Reviewer 2). The motivation for studying graph products is not connected to machine learning; it is unclear how they arise in real-world datasets or improve GNN performance (Reviewer 5). The relevance of death-time filtrations is not justified, and their advantages over birth-time filtrations are not clarified (Reviewer 5). The criteria used to select specific filtrations for molecular graphs are not explained (Reviewer 1).
- **Minority disagreement**: While some reviewers (1, 2) found the theoretical contributions significant and the paper well-structured, others (3, 4, 5) found the novelty incremental, the presentation lacking, and the contributions not convincing. Reviewers also disagree on the overall direction: Reviewer 4 suggests the paper would be stronger as a survey, while Reviewers 3 and 5 recommend narrowing the scope to fewer topics with deeper analysis. The paper needs significant revisions according to Reviewer 3.
