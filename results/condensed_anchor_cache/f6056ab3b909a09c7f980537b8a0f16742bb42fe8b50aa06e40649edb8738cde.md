- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 6, 8, 8, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper converts regression into a classification problem by discretizing the output space into bins, then applies conformal prediction for classification to obtain potentially non-contiguous prediction sets. The method uses a softmax output with an ordinal-aware loss and entropy regularization to preserve ordering. Experiments on several benchmarks show competitive efficiency compared to CQR, CHR, and other baselines. Reviewers disagree on contribution magnitude: three find the idea interesting and experiments convincing (ratings 6,8,8), while one (rating 3) considers the core contribution a new conditional density estimator with limited novelty and insufficient baselines.

### Strengths

- **Interesting and novel approach to CP for regression.** The regression-as-classification view allows prediction sets that are unions of intervals (non-compact), which is difficult to achieve with standard CP regression methods (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- **Method is simple and applicable in many settings.** The pipeline (discretize, classify with ordinal loss, smooth, conformalize) is straightforward to implement and follow (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- **Experiments are convincing enough to show practical interest.** The method often outperforms or matches CQR, CHR, and density-based baselines on heteroscedastic, multimodal, and skewed data (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- **Objective function is a good alternative for optimizing interval efficiency.** Unlike direct density estimation, the loss explicitly targets prediction interval quality (Reviewer 3).
- **Related work is well referenced,** including the broader regression-as-classification framework (Reviewer 1).
- **Limitations are discussed** (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Missing comparison to a direct regression baseline.** One reviewer criticizes the absence of a baseline where a standard regressor (same architecture) is trained and then conformalized (e.g., using “optimal prediction sets” from Angelopoulos et al., 2020). This comparison is needed to justify the complexity of the R2C method (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient comparison to other conditional density estimators.** The proposed method is essentially a new nonparametric density estimator, yet baselines are limited to KDE and quantile regression. Missing comparisons include mean-variance neural networks, conditional transformation models, normalizing flows, Gaussian mixtures, and histogram-based methods (trees, nearest neighbors). A reviewer (rating 3) argues that without these, it is unclear where the method stands in the broader density estimation landscape (Reviewer 4).
- **No analysis of conditional coverage guarantees.** The experiments use only marginal coverage; for heteroscedastic regression, conditional coverage (e.g., via Mondrian CP or normalized scores) should be evaluated (Reviewer 4).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity is not addressed.** The number of bins $K$ and entropy regularization strength $\tau$ are chosen without clear guidance or sensitivity analysis. The performance likely depends heavily on these values (Reviewers 3,4).
- **Smoothness penalty novelty is unclear.** The interpolation (linear, to go from discrete $q_\theta$ to continuous $\bar{q}_\theta$) and the entropy regularization are not clearly distinguished from prior work (Reviewers 1,3).
- **Method may be unsuitable for unimodal regression** due to too many degrees of freedom (Reviewer 4, rating 3).
- **Writing issues hinder clarity.** Section 3 contains verbose motivational comments that delay the algorithm; Table 1 is hard to read: acronyms not defined, bracket notation unclear, “length” and “coverage” not defined in the paper (Reviewer 1). Conversely, other reviewers found the paper well written (Reviewers 2,3,4).
- **Missing error bars in many results.** Table 1 and other experiments lack standard deviations or confidence intervals, making statistical significance hard to assess. Reviewer 1 notes an anomalously large gap where CHR underperforms CQR. Reviewer 4 questions near-zero standard deviations in Table 3 (Reviewers 1,4).
- **No positioning relative to ordinal conformal prediction.** The method is very close to ordinal regression, but the paper does not discuss or compare with ordinal CP work (e.g., Xu et al. 2023, Lu et al. 2022). The choice of modal values (softmax probabilities) over median/mean for conformity scores is also not justified (Reviewer 2).
- **Dependence on interpolation and histogram smoothness is a limitation.** The histogram approximation is inherently non-smooth; interpolation is needed for good scores, whereas other methods (e.g., Gaussian mixtures, normalizing flows) do not have this issue (Reviewer 4).
- **No analysis of how often prediction sets are non-intervals.** The paper claims this as a key advantage but does not provide empirical statistics on set shapes (Reviewer 2).
- **No full coverage curve plots.** Only fixed coverage (e.g., 90%) is shown; a wider range (90–99%) would better illustrate the coverage-efficiency trade-off (Reviewer 2).
- **Minority reviewer (rating 3) finds contributions overstated.** This reviewer argues the main contribution is an application of Stewart et al., with no CP innovation, and that the experimental scope is too limited (Reviewer 4). Other reviewers (1,2,3) saw more novelty.
