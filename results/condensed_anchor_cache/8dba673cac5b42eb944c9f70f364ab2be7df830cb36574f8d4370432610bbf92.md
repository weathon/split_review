## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SAGE, an evaluation framework for sparse autoencoders (SAEs) that automates cross-section discovery, uses supervised feature-dictionary reconstruction, and introduces a projection-based reconstruction via residual stream SAEs. The goal is to scale SAE evaluations to large models and tasks lacking ground-truth features. The topic is important and relevant. However, all four reviewers find the presentation severely lacking, with significant confusion about the method’s novelty, motivation, and implementation. Two reviewers (R1, R4) are more positive (scores 5), finding the reconstruction method sound and the cross-section automation a step forward; the other two (R2, R3) are more critical (scores 3), questioning the clarity, justification, and novelty. The paper is rejected but could be improved with major revisions in writing, exposition, and experimental justification.

### Strengths
- The problem of evaluating SAEs for interpretability in large language models is important and highly relevant to the ICLR community (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- SAEs and the indirect object identification (IOI) circuit are promising directions for interpretability (R1).
- Automated identification of cross-sections is a step forward; previous work (Makelov et al.) relied on prior knowledge, limiting applicability. This alleviates that limitation (R2).
- The proposed projection-based reconstruction method appears sound (R1).
- The overall attempt to scale evaluations to larger models (Pythia 70M, GPT-2 Small, Gemma-2-2B) is a worthwhile direction (R2, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Poor presentation and readability.** All four reviewers (R1, R2, R3, R4) note that the paper is very difficult to read. It assumes the reader is deeply familiar with prior work (Wang et al. 2023 on IOI circuits, Makelov et al. on supervised SAE evaluation) and quickly throws out jargon without explanation (R1, R2, R3). R3 states that after multiple reads they still cannot answer “what is done, why it is done, and how it is done.” R4 finds the presentation too abstract and entangled with background/related work. Terminology such as “large frontier model,” “linearly represented features,” “manual experimentation,” “sublayer activations,” and “residual stream SAE” is left undefined (R3). Figure legends are too small (R3, R4). R2 suggests some content (e.g., Appendix A.5) implies the paper might be better suited for a journal due to page constraints.
- **Unclear method details and justification.**
  - *Supervised dictionary extraction:* It is unclear how the supervised dictionary feature vectors \(u\) are extracted. Makelov et al. used MSE estimation; this paper uses a weighting scheme (Eq. 5 and 6) but does not explain the extraction process itself (R2).
  - *Projection-based reconstruction with residual streams:* The motivation is not justified. If the goal is to evaluate SAEs, the cost of training SAEs should not be an issue; why is training overhead suddenly a concern? The projection-based reconstruction’s role and necessity are unclear (R2).
  - *Connection between interpretability and SAEs:* The second paragraph of the introduction fails to clarify how interpretability of LLMs relates to SAEs (R3). Similarly, the role of circuit discovery in evaluating SAEs is not explained (R3).
  - *Novelty:* The abstract claims both an evaluation framework and a “novel reconstruction method,” creating a contradiction—is the paper about evaluation or model improvement? (R3). What exactly is new compared to Makelov et al.? The cross-section discovery is the main novelty, yet its accuracy is not evaluated or discussed (R2). R3 finds the novelty difficult to assess due to poor presentation.
- **Philosophical concern (minority reviewer).** Reviewer 4 (score 5) raises a fundamental issue: generating pseudo-ground-truth features via supervised dictionary learning may defy the purpose of SAE evaluation, which is to isolate interpretable components. This critique suggests the approach may be circular or misaligned with the goal.
- **Experimental results hard to read.** R4 notes that the results are presented in a chart-style with small font, making them difficult to interpret.
- **Cross-section discovery accuracy.** R2 questions how one can assess whether the automated cross-section discovery is actually good. This is a missing evaluation component.
- **Abstract and validation unclear.** R3 points out the abstract states validation on “novel tasks on 3 datasets” but leaves the conclusion unspecified. The validation’s outcome is not stated.
- **Minor issue.** On line 264, “x_corr” is likely a typo for “x_clean” (R2).
