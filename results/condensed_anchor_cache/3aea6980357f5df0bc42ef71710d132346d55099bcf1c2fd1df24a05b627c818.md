## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces SHIKI, a self-supervised heuristic pipeline that first trains an MLP on non-graphical (tabular) data to generate weak labels with confidence scores, then uses these to create a synthetic graph (by connecting confident nodes) and finally trains a GNN (GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT) on that graph for node classification. The authors provide a theoretical analysis on a Gaussian mixture / stochastic block model and evaluate on one synthetic dataset and two real-world datasets (entity matching, textual review classification). Three reviewers gave a rating of 3 (soundness 1–2, presentation 1–2, contribution 1–2); one reviewer (Reviewer 3) gave a rating of 5 with higher marks on presentation and contribution.

### Strengths

- **Problem and direction**: The idea of using GNNs to enhance tabular/edge‑less learning is a promising and meaningful research direction (Reviewers 1, 4). The problem is interesting and potentially impactful for domains where data lacks inherent graph structure (Reviewer 3).
- **Originality of the edge‑creation pipeline**: One reviewer (rating 5) highlights that the learning‑based pipeline for edge creation (as opposed to relying solely on prior knowledge) is an original contribution that addresses a gap in prior work (Reviewer 3). The heuristic approach specifically enables GNN application to new, non‑graphical domains (Reviewer 2).
- **Theoretical justification**: The paper provides some theoretical justification (theorem, analysis) to support the arguments, which is considered a positive aspect by Reviewer 3. Reviewer 2 notes that the theoretical analysis exists but its connection to the pipeline is unclear (this is placed in Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses

- **Poor writing, presentation, and organization** (all reviewers): The paper is unclear and difficult to follow in terms of contributions, theory, method, and experimental setup (Reviewer 1). Writing lacks conciseness and clarity: e.g., Table 2 and Table 3 mix main results with ablation studies in a confusing format, with multiple values per entry (mean MLP accuracy, mean improvement, improvement std, number of edges) making interpretation hard (Reviewers 2, 3). Section 2’s visualization (presumably theoretical) does not clearly reflect the general case for classification (Reviewer 4). Line 266 states “We conclude this section with some results on the improvement that can be gained by using SHIKI” – this information is misplaced in the pipeline section instead of experiments (Reviewer 3). There is a typo: “huristic” instead of “heuristic” in a section title (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient and unconvincing experimental evaluation** (all reviewers):
  - Only two real‑world datasets (entity matching, textual review) and one synthetic dataset are used; this narrow selection fails to demonstrate generalizability across domains (Reviewer 1, 4).
  - Missing baselines: The paper compares only with a basic KNN baseline; stronger tabular baselines such as XGBoost and TabTransformer should be included (Reviewer 1). The baseline results of a sole MLP (without graph construction) are not reported in Tables 2 and 3, which is necessary to show the benefit of the full pipeline (Reviewer 2).
  - The evaluation is not sound: the two real‑world datasets are uncommon, and the theoretical analysis is tailored to those datasets (using LLM embeddings) rather than considering more general scenarios (Reviewer 4).
- **Edge‑construction method is simplistic and lacks innovation** (Reviewers 1, 3): Relying solely on MLP‑generated weak labels and confidence scores to create edges can produce unreliable connections, especially on noisy or complex data (Reviewer 1). The approach is not compared to more sophisticated edge‑construction methods; e.g., [Zhao et al. 2023, WSDM] and other graph structure learning techniques (Reviewer 2). The pipeline is overly complicated (MLP→edges→GNN) – why not just use the MLP itself? This complexity also limits scalability and computational efficiency (Reviewer 3).
- **Theoretical analysis has several issues** (Reviewers 2, 4):
  - The notation and motivation for Theorem 1 are not well introduced; the correlation with the proposed pipeline is hard to see (Reviewer 2).
  - The XOR GMM theoretical model is not motivated; it appears chosen to match the specific real‑world datasets (LLM embeddings), lacking generalizability to other tabular data (Reviewer 4).
  - The analysis relies heavily on frameworks from other papers; the proposed MLP confidence‑based selection lacks theoretical support. Intuitively, trusting the confidence of a weaker model is not sound; it probably requires strong features (e.g., LLM embeddings) (Reviewer 4).
  - Chapter 3 contains symbol confusion, e.g., line 212 defines “improvement in terms of expectation” unclearly (Reviewer 4). Line 256 states “want p and q different” – but the underlying generative model is fixed for a dataset; it is unclear how one can choose p and q (Reviewer 4). Table 1’s graphical model is not a GCN (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing technical details**: Key aspects such as edge creation are omitted and instead the reader is directed to references that explore different problems (Reviewer 4). The introduction is too brief and fails to motivate the problem and give a concise solution overview (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing related work**: Several highly relevant works are omitted from the related work section, e.g., Errica (NeurIPS 2024) on class distributions induced by nearest neighbor graphs for tabular data, and Zeng et al. (2023) on mixture of weak & strong experts on graphs (Reviewer 4). Problem 2 is placed in the main context but is for future work and not discussed, which is out of place (Reviewer 2).
- **Contribution is incremental** (Reviewer 3): The work primarily builds on previous research and only modifies the edge‑creation part using a learning pipeline, making the overall contribution incremental.
