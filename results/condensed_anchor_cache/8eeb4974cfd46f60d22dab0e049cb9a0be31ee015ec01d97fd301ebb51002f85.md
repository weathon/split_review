## Merged Review

### Summary
HiBO is a hierarchical Bayesian optimization algorithm that uses a search-tree-based global navigator to adaptively partition the search space and a local BO optimizer (specifically TuRBO) that leverages partitioning information to guide acquisition. Evaluations on high-dimensional synthetic benchmarks and a DBMS configuration tuning task show improvements over several baselines. The concept is straightforward and the paper is well-written, but reviews raise concerns about limited novelty, missing related work, unclear motivation and limitations, insufficient analysis of computational costs and hyperparameters, and reliance on a single local optimizer.

### Strengths
- The problem of optimizing black-box functions in high-dimensional spaces is well-motivated and practically important (R1, R3, R4, R5).
- The core idea of adaptive search space partitioning to guide local BO is simple, intuitive, and sound (R1, R2, R4, R5).
- The adaptive partitioning strategy, especially using consecutive successes/failures to determine tree depth, is considered novel in the context of exploration/exploitation (R2, R5).
- Promising empirical results show HiBO outperforms state-of-the-art methods across several synthetic benchmarks and a real DBMS tuning task (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The paper is well-organized, easy to follow, and includes clear explanatory figures (R1, R3).
- The methods used (TuRBO, GP models, UCB acquisition) are appropriate for the problem (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing related work:** The paper ignores prior work on "Hierarchical Bayesian Optimization Algorithm" by Pelikan & Goldberg (2006), which introduced the same topic earlier (R2). It also overlooks the most recent DBMS knob tuning works by Zhang et al. (R2). The rationale for inclusion and exclusion of related works is not justified (R2).
- **Limited novelty and motivation:** Despite dedicating significant space to partitioning integration, many techniques are well-known, limiting originality (R4). The motivation for introducing structural information and the criteria for optimally utilizing it are unclear (R4). Several observations rely on heuristics and empirical evidence rather than theoretical grounding (R5). Rule 1 and Rule 3 lack sufficient insight (R5).
- **Theoretical analysis lacking:** No theoretical results are provided despite the relatively simple approach (R1, R3).
- **Limited scope of experimentation:**
  - Only TuRBO is used as the local optimizer; it is unclear whether other local optimizers would also benefit from HiBO (R1).
  - The empirical study could better identify settings where HiBO struggles (R1, R3, R4). For example, DBMS tuning may have a small number of important knobs, which might be especially suitable for BO (R5).
  - Few real-world experiments make it difficult to support claims about black-box functions (R4). There are no experiments on other applications beyond DBMS.
  - Performance improvements across different dimensions need specification (R4).
- **Computational costs and scalability unclear:**
  - The required computational costs and scalability are not discussed (R3).
  - Using a binary search tree for space partitioning may cause excessive computational complexity for large-scale data; mitigation is not addressed (R4).
  - Appendix C Table 3 is oversimplified: reporting percentages is not effective; the optim execution time increased 1.5× from depth 1 to HiBO, yet the paper claims minimal increase within 2% (R5). The table should also include S-PITR scores to support the adaptive tree claim (R5).
- **Hyperparameter tuning unclear:** It is not specified which hyperparameters (e.g., C_p in line 263) are required or how they are automatically chosen/tuned (R3).
- **Unaddressed design choices:**
  - Why a binary search tree instead of partitioning into multiple clusters per level (e.g., R-tree)? Does the tree need to be balanced? (R5)
  - How is the optimal balance between the global navigator and local optimizer determined in practice? (R4)
- **Missing experimental details:**
  - The workload type in sysbench (read-only, write-only, mixed) is not specified (R5).
  - Minor notation issues: line 54 "that integrate" should be "that integrates"; line 84 x and X undefined; line 264 the set for index p is unclear; line 316 which sets belong to i, j, j’ (R3).
  - References are inconsistent in casing (R3).
- **Reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (R2, score 8) is much more positive, emphasizing novelty and well-argued proposal, while another (R4, score 5) finds motivation unclear and novelty limited. The paper's weaknesses are noted by all reviewers, but with varying severity.
