## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies memorization in generative diffusion models for manifold-supported data using statistical physics. It analyzes the eigenvalue spectrum of the Jacobian of the score function, focusing on the emergence of spectral gaps that indicate loss of dimensions due to memorization. The authors characterize critical times and dataset sizes where memorization occurs, and propose that subspaces with higher variance are lost first. Theoretical results on linear manifolds are presented, along with experiments on synthetic data and image datasets (MNIST, CIFAR10, CelebA).

### Strengths
- Novel investigation of geometric memorization in diffusion models, providing insights into the memorization–generalization transition (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- Rigorous and insightful analysis of the empirical score using Boltzmann averaging; the observation that subspaces with larger variance exhibit earlier condensation time and are more vulnerable to memorization is a notable contribution (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- Good visualizations (Figures 1 and 3) aid understanding; Figure 5 clearly shows the spectral gap opening as sample size increases and across time steps, offering a new perspective on the phase transition (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- Sections 5.2 and 5.3 are well-written; the effective sample size going from N to 1 as t decreases quantitatively explains memorization and highlights the curse of dimensionality (Reviewer 2).
- Connections to statistical physics (e.g., random energy models) are impressive and merit broader communication within the ML community (Reviewer 3).
- The observed losing-dimensions phenomenon opens interesting research questions (Reviewer 1).
- **Note on reviewer contrast:** Two reviewers were positive (scores 6 and 5) and recommended acceptance with improvements, while two reviewers were negative (score 3) and found major issues with clarity, theoretical validation, and connection to practice.

### Weaknesses
1. **Unclear contributions and insufficient theoretical validation**  
   - The paper does not clearly state its main contributions; it is ambiguous whether the focus is on the difference between empirical and population score or on the Jacobian spectrum, and the connection between these is not established (Reviewer 2).  
   - Several results (e.g., equation (9) from another paper) are mentioned but not reused, making the big picture unclear (Reviewer 2).  
   - Theoretical claims are not adequately supported: the claim that “subspaces of higher variance are lost first” is not explicitly shown (Reviewer 2).  
   - The theory predicts x‑dependence of condensation times, but all numerical experiments are on Gaussian data (where the Jacobian is input‑independent) or show only the average Jacobian (Reviewer 2).  
   - The statement that experimental curves from the empirical score are consistent with theory is disputed; Figure 4 shows qualitatively different behavior across panels, and the theory essentially only predicts spectral gaps that could be derived from linear algebra (Reviewer 2).  
   - The paper appears to be largely a technical extension of prior work (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18491) and offers limited new insight; memorized diffusion models are impractical in real‑world applications (Reviewer 4).

2. **Problematic manifold definition and tangent space interpretation**  
   - The manifold is defined as points with zero score (Eq. 3), which is highly restricted and only holds near t=0 in practice; it is unclear whether Jacobians in natural image experiments are computed at points satisfying this condition (Reviewer 1).  
   - Without this special assumption, singular vectors with zero singular values no longer represent the tangent space; how the tangent space interpretation applies to models trained on natural images is not clarified (Reviewer 1).  
   - In the Gaussian case, the manifold M_t corresponds to a single point (the mean), which cannot illustrate any geometrical properties of the diffusion process (Reviewer 2).  
   - The formal explanation of section 5.1 needs improvement: higher‑ and low‑dimensional subspaces should be explicitly defined in terms of F (Reviewer 3).

3. **Gap between idealized empirical score and practical learnability**  
   - The analysis relies on the empirical score function (Boltzmann average of training points), but recent work (Zeno et al., 2024) shows that denoisers in practice cannot learn such a function; thus the theoretical results may not apply to the experiments on natural images (Reviewer 1).  
   - Reviewer 2 disagrees that experimental curves (Figure 4) are consistent with theory; the theory only predicts gaps from linear algebra rather than from memorization dynamics.  
   - Experiments are limited to linear manifolds or 2D spheres; non‑linear manifolds in practice are not addressed. A requested experiment is to perform the same analysis as Figure 5 but with a high‑dimensional sphere embedding (Reviewer 3).

4. **Inaccurate description of “losing dimensions”**  
   - The singular vectors of the Jacobian change significantly with dataset size and time (as shown in the authors’ notebook: https://github.com/LabForComputationalVision/memorization_generalization_in_diffusion_models/blob/main/notebooks/Demo_UNet_CelebA80x80.ipynb). Therefore “losing dimension” is not accurate because the subspaces themselves change; the current theory cannot explain this change (Reviewer 1).  
   - In Figure 6, the estimated dimensionality increases with dataset size, which is counterintuitive for memorization—one might expect memorization to be characterized by a collapse of dimensionality (Reviewer 2).

5. **Missing comparisons and inconsistencies with prior work**  
   - The paper should compare its approach more directly to Stanczuk’s method for intrinsic dimension estimation, explaining key differences (Reviewer 1).  
   - The manifold dimension estimates in Figure 6 (e.g., ~100 for MNIST, ~3000 for CelebA) appear inconsistent with existing results (e.g., https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08894) which report lower dimensions (Reviewer 4).  
   - The paper should discuss Kamkari et al. and Tempczyk et al., who performed similar experiments on multivariate normal distributions with specific covariance eigenspectra (Reviewer 3).

6. **Missing derivations and potential errors in the text**  
   - Several equations require explicit derivation or appear inconsistent: the derivation of Eq. (8) is not provided and does not match Eq. (14) in the supplementary material (Reviewer 3); the derivation of Eq. (19) is missing, especially for non‑diagonal F (Reviewer 3).  
   - The definition of w_mu appears to be p(y^mu|x) = p(x|y^mu) / sum p(x|y^nu) (Reviewer 2).  
   - The energy in Eq. (12) may have the wrong sign (Reviewer 2).  
   - Line 321 says “small values of t” where “large” might be intended (Reviewer 2).  
   - The analysis focuses on bias‑free networks (Eq. 4), which may limit its generality (Reviewer 1).

7. **Clarity and presentation issues**  
   - The paper is too dense, with ideas such as spectral gap, critical time, active samples, generalization during sampling, and geometric memorization interwoven; prioritization of key results would improve clarity (Reviewer 4).  
   - For an ML audience, a primer on random energy models would be helpful; the exposition in Appendix C is difficult to follow (Reviewer 3).  
   - Figure 1 is not referenced in the text and should be incorporated into the explanation of latent manifolds (Reviewer 3).  
   - There are several typos (Reviewer 2). A sequential colormap is suggested for Figures 5 and 6 for better readability (Reviewer 2).

8. **Questions about generalization and experimental details**  
   - The relationship between the spectral gap opening and the conventional notion of generalization (generation of novel samples) is not clearly explained; if the spectral gap opens at a particular t, does that mean the model is in the generalization regime? (Reviewer 4)  
   - For the natural image experiments (Figure 6), it is unclear what happens when t is increased (Reviewer 3).  
   - The motivation behind the “smoothed” Jacobian is not explained (Reviewer 2).  
   - The meaning of the sentence about curvature (lines 219–222) is unclear (Reviewer 3).
