## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces two synthetic datasets, SHAPES and CHARS, to study out-of-distribution (OOD) detection under controlled, fine-grained attribute shifts (color, size, position, rotation) across three OOD scenarios. The authors evaluate 13 OOD detection methods with 10 architectures, analyzing how individual attribute shifts and image corruptions affect OOD scores. Reviewers note the importance of OOD generalization but strongly disagree on the usefulness and validity of the proposed benchmarks. One reviewer (score 5) finds the paper well-written and the experiments extensive; the other three (scores 3, 3, 1) are highly critical of the synthetic simplicity, limited realism, lack of background research, and unclear contribution.

### Strengths
- OOD problem/generalization ability is an important topic (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is generally well-written and easy to understand (Reviewer 2).
- The authors evaluate a broad range of OOD detection methods with different architectures; experiments are extensive (Reviewer 2).
- Originality: Controlled attribute-focused OOD detection is a unique angle, permitting precise examination of specific visual properties on model robustness (Reviewer 3).
- Quality: Thorough experimentation covering multiple architectures and OOD methods offers a comprehensive view (Reviewer 3).
- Clarity: Paper structure and flow are accessible; visual results are well-organized (Reviewer 3).
- Significance: Focus on attribute-level OOD sensitivity highlights limitations that are often overlooked (Reviewer 3).
- The paper proposes two datasets (Reviewer 4, minimal strength).

### Weaknesses
- **Synthetic simplicity and lack of ecological validity (all reviewers):** The proposed datasets are too simple and not realistic (only geometric primitives and alphanumeric characters) compared to real-world images. Reviewers 1, 2, and 3 agree that results cannot generalize to real-world OOD detection in high-stakes fields like autonomous driving or healthcare. Reviewer 1 specifically notes that far more complex OOD datasets exist (SVHN; ObjectNet, *https://objectnet.dev/*) and questions why these toy datasets are useful. Reviewer 2 states that creating a more realistic dataset with precise attribute control over general objects/scenes would be more meaningful.
- **Limited attribute shifts (Reviewer 2):** Only two attribute shifts are considered (color and class) when many others exist (e.g., shape, size). A minority reviewer (score 5) explicitly asks what happens if more attributes are evaluated.
- **Inadequate related works and missing methods (Reviewer 3):** The related work section lacks coverage of recent OOD detection benchmarks, notably missing Zhao et al. 2022 (*Ood-cv: A benchmark for robustness to out-of-distribution shifts of individual nuisances in natural images*) which introduce benchmarks closer to real-world data. The method part also lacks some state-of-the-art methods, e.g., Li et al. 2023 (*Rethinking out-of-distribution (ood) detection: Masked image modeling is all you need*).
- **Lack of rigorous theoretical analysis and sufficient background research (Reviewer 4):** The paper reads more like a course assignment than an academic submission for ICLR. Reviewer 4 gives score 1, very negative.
- **Unclear contribution to real-world applications (Reviewer 3, echoed by Reviewers 1 and 2):** The paper does not clarify how these synthetic datasets advance understanding of OOD detection in practice. Reviewer 3 asks whether findings will be validated on more complex real-world datasets.
- **Unnecessary formalism for trivial concept (Reviewer 1):** Lines 126–137 explain a trivial concept with a large block of equations, which is not needed.
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms:** Reviewers explicitly raise the following concerns, treated as weaknesses: (a) Reviewer 2 questions the actual usefulness of toy datasets and limited attribute shifts; (b) Reviewer 3 asks for clarification on how synthetic datasets contribute to real-world OOD detection and whether validation on real-world data is planned; (c) Reviewer 1 asks whether the concept in lines 126–137 requires such extensive equations.
