## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Denoising Diffusion Bridge Models (DDBMs), a generative framework based on diffusion bridges that interpolate between two paired distributions via Doob’s h‑transform. DDBMs learn the score of the bridge conditioned on the endpoint \(x_T\) and map between arbitrary distributions by solving a reverse SDE or probability flow ODE. The formulation unifies score‑based diffusion models and OT‑Flow‑Matching, and reuses many existing design choices. Empirical results are presented on image‑to‑image translation tasks (pixel and latent space) with strong quantitative performance, and on unconditional image generation (noise‑to‑data) achieving competitive FID scores. The theoretical derivation, loss formulation, and adaptation of network parameterizations are detailed.

### Strengths
- **Novel formulation**: DDBMs introduce a principled diffusion‑bridge perspective for mapping between arbitrary paired distributions, enabling tasks like image‑to‑image translation in a way that standard diffusion models cannot handle directly. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Well‑written and structured**: The paper is clear, easy to follow, and carefully explains the mathematical connections to score matching and flow matching. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Solid theoretical grounding**: The derivation of the reverse SDE via Doob’s h‑transform, the training objective, and the unification with diffusion models and flow matching are sound. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Strong empirical performance**: Extensive experiments on image‑to‑image translation tasks show significant improvements over baselines; the method also achieves competitive FIDs for unconditional generation. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Adaptation of diffusion techniques**: The paper effectively adapts network parameterizations, sampling strategies, and signal‑to‑noise ratio considerations from state‑of‑the‑art diffusion models to the bridge setting. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Thorough experimental evaluation**: The ablation study and the analysis of function‑call efficiency are useful and support the claims. (Reviewer 2)
- **Important problem**: The work addresses a critical limitation of diffusion models—their restriction to Gaussian‑to‑data mapping—and opens avenues for more general conditional generation. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient related work discussion** (strong criticism from Reviewers 2 and 4):  
  *The paper overlooks and does not properly distinguish from several closely related lines of work:*  
  – ODE‑based generative models that connect arbitrary densities (e.g., [1,2,3] – specific references missing) and that do not require a score; the paper’s claim that ODE methods “tend to severely underperform when compared to diffusion models” is unsubstantiated and should be demonstrated experimentally.  
  – Bridge‑matching methods, including Heng et al. (2021), which derives a similar h‑transform equation (Eq. 6 appears to coincide with Heng et al. Section 2.1), and BBDM (Li et al. 2022), which is essentially a discrete‑time version of DDBMs.  
  – Works on the influence of the coupling \(p(x_0,x_1)\) and on using both ODE and SDE for arbitrary densities with the score [4,5,6,7].  
  – The characterization of “OT‑Flow‑Matching” is inaccurate: Liu et al. (2023) do not solve optimal transport, and “VE (OT) bridges” are never defined.  
  *The paper misrepresents prior work:*  
  – The statement that Somnath et al. (2023) requires “sampling an entire SDE trajectory for computation” is incorrect.  
  – Citing [3] for the Schrödinger‑bridge interpretation of diffusion models is misleading; more relevant references exist.  
  *These omissions inflate the apparent contribution and must be addressed with a thorough re‑evaluation of the related work section.* (Reviewers 2, 4 strongly; reviewer disagreements exists as Reviewers 1 and 3 found the presentation clear but did not flag these omissions.)

- **Missing comparisons and experiments**:  
  – No comparison with ControlNet for image‑to‑image translation, despite ControlNet being the dominant solution in that domain. (Reviewers 1, 3)  
  – No comparison with ODE‑based methods (e.g., flow matching) to support the claim that they underperform. (Reviewer 2)  
  – All experiments use only the independent coupling \(p(x_0)p(x_1)\); no results with more interesting, structured couplings that could demonstrate the method’s advantages. (Reviewer 2)  
  – Lack of large‑scale generation experiments (e.g., DDBM with Stable Diffusion). (Reviewer 1)  
  – The ability to generate diverse outputs from the same input (e.g., multiple plausible images from one edge map) is not analyzed. (Reviewer 1)

- **Lack of justification for design choices**:  
  – The weighting factor in Equation (13) is not shown to preserve an exact/unbiased transport; it may introduce bias. (Reviewer 2)  
  – It is unclear whether the sampling strategy and integrator were fixed across all comparison methods; different samplers could confound conclusions about method quality. (Reviewer 2)

- **Presentation and clarity issues**:  
  – Acronyms (e.g., VE, OT, SB) are not always defined. (Reviewer 2)  
  – The advantage of DDBMs over flow matching—which also models flows between arbitrary distributions—is not clearly explained, despite the paper’s claim of generalization. (Reviewers 3, 4)  
  – The relationship to bridge matching and conditional bridge matching is insufficiently discussed; many aspects are shared. (Reviewer 4)

- **Misconceptions about optimal transport**:  
  The paper refers to “VE (OT)” without defining what optimal transport notion is used; OT‑Flow‑Matching is mischaracterized as solving OT. (Reviewer 2)

- **Novelty varies across reviewers**: While Reviewers 1 and 3 highlight strong novelty, Reviewers 2 and 4 argue that DDBMs are essentially a conditional diffusion/bridge‑matching model with limited novelty given the existing literature (see related‑work issues above). (Reviewers 2, 4 are more critical; this contrast should be preserved.)

- **Generalization claim is questionable**: Because the score is conditioned on \(x_T\), the resulting backward process is non‑Markovian; thus DDBMs do not subsume rectified flow, iterative Markovian fitting, or Schrödinger bridge methods as claimed. (Reviewer 4)
