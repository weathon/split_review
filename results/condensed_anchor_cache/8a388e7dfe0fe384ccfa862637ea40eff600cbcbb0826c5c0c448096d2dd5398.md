## Merged Review

### Summary

Four reviewers evaluated this paper, which proposes viewing message-passing GNNs as noisy communication channels and uses entropy maximization (via Shannon’s theorem) to prescribe network width and depth. Scores are 3, 5, 1, 3. Reviewer 2 (score 5, confidence 1) explicitly states they are not an expert and defers to others; Reviewer 3 (score 1, confidence 4) and Reviewer 4 (score 3, confidence 4) share a nearly identical, strongly critical weakness list. Reviewer 1 (score 3, confidence 4) also raises fundamental flaws. All agree the problem is important and the information‑theory perspective is original, but the approach is not convincingly validated theoretically or empirically.

### Strengths

- The paper aims at an important and unsolved problem: prescribing GNN model capacity (widths and depths) before training.
- The information‑theoretic perspective (maximum entropy, Shannon’s theorem, channel capacity) is original and novel.
- The necessary theoretical background (entropy, communication channels) is well explained, making the paper easy to follow.
- The overall idea and analysis are considered sound by some reviewers (e.g., Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Critical assumption not justified:** The central claim that maximum entropy corresponds to optimal information transmission in GNNs (Lines 159–160, 173–174) is neither justified nor validated. This assumption underlies Theorem 1 and most of the paper’s conclusions. **(R3, R4 – W1; echoed by R1 Question 5)**
- **Contradiction in depth analysis:** Equation (16) shows that adding depth yields diminishing but non‑negative returns (all summands positive for w≥2). The paper’s conclusion (Line 259–260) that “its negative effects always surpass its merits” contradicts the equation itself, which suggests depth is at least non‑detrimental. **(R3, R4 – W2)**
- **Flaws in the core optimization (Eq. 18):**  
  - It optimizes only widths, not depth *L*, so the stated goal of choosing both width and depth is not addressed.  
  - The termination condition depends on a new constant ζ, whose meaning in the information‑theoretic framework and choice method are unclear.  
  - Whether a solution always exists is not discussed (R1 Question 4). **(R3, R4 – W3; R1 Question 4)**
- **Unfair / impractical experimental comparison:** The proposed C3E models require very large widths (>1000) and total parameters exceeding 1 million, while plain/deep baselines have ~20,000 parameters. No justification or computational‑cost constraint is given. This makes accuracy comparisons invalid. **(R1 – major weakness 2 & 3)**
- **Experimental results contradict the theory:**  
  - The paper admits (L355–356, L372–374) that C3E variants can suffer from over‑fitting and that higher entropy does not guarantee better performance, undermining the applicability of the maximum‑entropy principle. **(R3, R4 – W4)**  
  - The claim that Figure 1 shows “U‑shaped and V‑shaped patterns” is not convincing; in several cases (e.g., GAT on AmazonComputers) accuracy simply degrades with increased capacity. **(R3, R4 – W5)**  
  - The interval description [ϕ0 − log n, ϕ0 − 2log n] is unclear because the x‑axis is ϕ0 − log n. **(R3, R4 – W6)**  
  - The interpretation of entropy reduction as “diverse features collapsed” is not uniquely justified; an alternative explanation is that classification finishes early. **(R3, R4 – W7)**  
  - The empirical verification of Theorem 1 (Eq. 19) using mean/variance is confounded because learned GNNs likely deviate from the zero‑mean assumptions of the theorem. **(R3, R4 – W8)**
- **Inapplicability to many GNN families:** The key assumption C<sub>l</sub> = A (Line 152) is valid only for GCN. The framework is applied to GAT and GDC, whose propagation mechanisms (attention, diffusion) are fundamentally different and not captured. The paper explicitly excludes other architectures “to avoid biased results,” but this limits the evaluation and does not justify why GAT/GDC are acceptable while GCNII/GIN/transformers are not. **(R1 – major weakness 1; R2 – “not all GNNs can be explained by Eq. 7”; R3/R4 – W9)**
- **Insufficient and weak experimental evaluation:**  
  - Only five small semi‑supervised node‑classification datasets (Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed, etc.) are used, despite known issues with these splits (e.g., GCN can achieve higher accuracy than reported [6][7]).  
  - No comparisons on larger, more rigorous benchmarks (LRGB, OGB, TUDatasets) are provided.  
  - Important backbones like GCNII, GIN, or graph transformers are not included. **(R2)**  
  - The time cost of solving the nonlinear programming problem is not reported; scalability to large graphs is unclear. **(R2)**
- **Lack of novelty:** The derivation in Equation (8) leading to oversmoothing is a known result. The final conclusion that GNNs behave like noisy channels restates known phenomena without providing new insight. Similar ideas already appear in [3][4][5] (not discussed). **(R2)**
- **Poor related‑work discussion:** The paper does not clearly differentiate its approach from existing literature, omits relevant works (Di Giovanni et al., [3][4][5]), and does not explain the benefit of the new perspective. **(R1 minor; R2)**
- **Presentation issues:**  
  - Figures 1–4 have subfigures that are too small and hard to read; some figures cause file‑rendering problems. **(R1 formal a; R2)**  
  - Typos and English errors: “Let's” → “Let us”; missing word in L359–360. **(R2)**  
  - Equations need fixes: missing integral domain in Eq. 1, confusing index notation in Eq. 4, Eq. 19 identical to earlier equations (9, 10) causing confusion. **(R1 minor)**  
  - Information‑theory basics in Sec. 4 could be moved to Sec. 3. **(R1 minor)**
- **Unaddressed reviewer questions (overlap with criticisms):**  
  - Why was the activation function removed in Eq. 8? (affects interpretation) **(R1 Q1)**  
  - Derivation of Eq. 11 second term is unclear. **(R1 Q2)**  
  - What does “L=2” below the sum in Eq. 18 mean? **(R1 Q3)**  
  - How are hyperparameters for plain/deep models chosen (Appendix E.4)? **(R4 Q1)**  
  - What is meant by “graph mining” in the abstract? **(R3 Q)**
