## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates how soft-label regularization techniques (label smoothing, Mixup, CutMix) improve calibration and robustness, offering a representation-space explanation via two metrics: root mean square (RMS) of features and cosine similarity to class centers. Reviewers found the topic important and the visualizations exceptionally clear, but raised substantial concerns about novelty, experimental depth, and the strength of the claimed connections. The analysis is seen as well-motivated and easy to follow, yet several key claims are either trivial or insufficiently supported. One reviewer was notably more critical, scoring the paper low due to lack of theoretical analysis, narrow scope (only linear heads), and weak experimental correlations.

### Strengths
- The topic—understanding why soft-label regularization improves model performance—is laudable and important for scientific progress.
- The analysis is properly motivated and easy to follow, with a clear logical flow from motivation to findings.
- Visualizations (2D/3D representations of the feature space) are exceptionally high-quality and intuitively convey the analysis.
- Multiple model architectures are examined (ResNet50, MobileNetV2, Swin-T, DeiT-T), lending some breadth to the empirical observations.
- The finding that the representation space forms a cone-shaped structure when using a linear head is interesting and offers a new perspective on soft-label approaches.
- The introduction of RMS and cosine similarity as novel metrics provides a concrete lens for analyzing feature distributions.
- The manuscript is well-written and thoughtfully structured; figures are well-designed and help convey complex information.
- The work opens avenues for further investigation into the fundamental reasons behind the effectiveness of soft labels.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient robustness experiments:** Only a single set of robustness experiments is carried out; prior works study robustness more thoroughly (e.g., natural corruptions, black-box attacks). The claim about improved robustness is under-supported.
- **Calibration claim is misleading:** The paper states that regularization improves calibration, but the evidence (Figure 5 a–d) shows it primarily reduces confidence. The baseline is overconfident, so reducing confidence initially improves calibration, but Mixup and CutMix reduce confidence too much, leading to underconfident, poorly calibrated models. The claim should be revised to “reduces model confidence” rather than “improves calibration.”
- **Cone-shaped structure is not novel:** This is a well-known consequence of linear classifiers trained with cross-entropy loss, and has been noted in prior work (e.g., Luo et al. on person re-identification, and contrastive learning methods like VICReg). The paper does not adequately acknowledge this prior art.
- **Weak connection between cone shape and proposed metrics:** Section 3 spends considerable effort describing the cone-shaped structure, but the relationship to the later-used metrics (RMS, cosine similarity) is not clearly articulated. The paper lacks a coherent explanation of how the cone shape relates to the chosen evaluation criteria.
- **Limited scope of analysis:** The analysis focuses exclusively on linear classification heads. Many applications use non-linear classifiers (e.g., MLP) or no classifier at all (e.g., self-supervised learning), which narrows the applicability of the findings.
- **Absence of theoretical analysis:** While label smoothing, Mixup, and CutMix all use soft labels, the paper provides no theoretical insights linking the proposed metrics to these methods, which would strengthen the conclusions.
- **No proposed methodology:** Given that RMS and cosine similarity are identified as key factors, the paper could propose new methods based on these observations, but it remains purely analytical.
- **Weak experimental correlations:** The connections between RMS, cosine similarity, and attack success rates are unclear, undermining the conclusions drawn from the experimental data.
- **Lack of clear, non-trivial insights:** The main conclusion—“feature vectors with smaller magnitudes improve calibration and robustness”—is a well-established idea in machine learning (e.g., compact feature design, PCA). The paper would benefit from deeper insights or concrete suggestions for future work.
- **Missing comparisons to prior work on robustness of CNNs vs. Vision Transformers:** The paper claims that higher Transformer robustness might be partly explained by regularization, but prior works (e.g., Pinto et al.) present a much more thorough examination of CNN vs. Transformer robustness.
- **Missing comparison to prior work on clustering and robustness:** The finding that tighter clustering around class centers improves robustness has been analyzed in contrastive learning contexts (e.g., Gupta et al.), which is not discussed.
- **Unaddressed question about cone-shape formation:** One reviewer asks for a detailed explanation of why the representation space remains cone-shaped when the first misclassified step is large; this gap reflects insufficient theoretical justification.
