## Merged Review

### Summary
MolGene-E is a deep generative framework for inverse molecular design conditioned on single-cell transcriptomics. It combines a denoising autoencoder (harmonizing L1000toRNAseq and Sciplex-3 datasets via cycleGAN and DCA), a SELFIES-based variational autoencoder for molecules, a CLIP-like contrastive alignment between gene expression and molecular embeddings, and an MLP prior that maps expression embeddings to chemical embeddings. The task is framed as generating molecules that restore a diseased cell to a healthy state. Experiments on the MCF7 cell line claim superior performance over MolGAN and GxVAE in validity, uniqueness, novelty, diversity, and synthesizability. Reviewer scores range from 1 to 8, reflecting strong disagreement: one reviewer found the method interesting and well-executed, while others raised substantial concerns about novelty, validation, generalizability, and missing ablations.

### Strengths
- The problem of designing drugs based on gene expression (phenotype-based drug discovery) has biological and pharmaceutical value (R1, R3, R5).
- MolGene-E may generate molecules more similar to ground-truth test molecules and shows quantitative improvements over baselines across multiple generation metrics (R2, R3, R4).
- Using SELFIES guarantees 100% validity of generated molecules (R2, R3).
- The approach addresses the limitation of the one-drug-one-target paradigm by leveraging comprehensive gene expression profiles (R3).
- The paper is clearly written, with informative figures that aid understanding (R3, R4, R5).
- The contrastive learning application for inverse molecule design is innovative (R4).
- The formulation of the inverse problem as restoring the unperturbed state is interesting and relevant (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited generalizability**: All experiments are conducted only on the MCF7 cell line without justification (R3, R4). The model requires retraining for each cell line and depends on paired data, limiting broader applicability (R4). Validation is only on a single study, not cross-study settings (R5). The train-test split may also allow protein complex members (e.g., A and B) to appear in different splits, contaminating evaluation (R1).
- **Insufficient validation**: The only validation is similarity to known perturbagens; docking or binding affinity prediction is needed to confirm that generated molecules actually bind the intended targets (R1). The claim that molecules are “hit-like” is not justified (R2).
- **Missing ablation studies**: The architecture combines multiple components (denoising VAE, SELFIES VAE, CLIP alignment, MLP prior) without demonstrating the necessity of each (R2, R3, R4, R5). Specific unanswered questions: Why learn a gene encoder prior to CLIP instead of aligning directly (R2)? Why introduce an MLP to map z^gene→z^chem when CLIP should align them (R2)? How does the prior compare to a simple KNN search in the CLIP space (R4)? Would fine-tuning the frozen encoder after alignment improve performance (R5)?
- **Identical metrics in Tables 1 and 2**: MolGene-E results are exactly the same across datasets, which appears to be a data entry error and requires clarification (R2, R3).
- **Metrics concerns**: Using SELFIES trivially yields perfect validity; novelty and uniqueness are less relevant because one can sample more molecules and discard duplicates (R3). The authors recommend using Jaccard distance between generated and true molecules for better interpretability (R5). The differences in Tanimoto similarity distributions in Figs 2 and 4 are not quantified or tested for significance (R2).
- **Dataset and preprocessing issues**: The L1000toRNAseq and Sciplex-3 datasets are heavily processed with trained models (cycleGAN, DCA), which may introduce bias; the impact is not analyzed (R2). The filtering criteria (24-hour infection, 10 µM) appear arbitrary (R3). Overlap between molecules in the training and test sets across datasets is not reported (R4). There is no analysis of how the model handles high variability across replicates in single-cell data (R4, R5).
- **Architecture complexity unjustified**: The harmonization and multi-step training add complexity without ablation showing benefit (R3). Simpler alternatives should be considered (R3). Parameter-tuning steps and reproducibility details are missing (R5).
- **Technical novelty limited**: The model introduces little new technical innovation for the machine learning community; it may be more suitable for a bioinformatics journal (R1). Biological terms (e.g., “nuclear hashing”, difference between single-cell and bulk) need explanation (R1).
- **Unanswered methodological questions**: How is the MLP network trained after imputation of Sciplex-3 (R4)? How are the L1000toRNA datasets split (randomly or by molecular structure) (R4)? How does the model generalize to molecules structurally distinct from the training set (R4)? Were baseline results (MolGAN, GxVAE) obtained from retrained models or as reported (R2)? What plans exist to correct noise from cell variability (R5)? A relevant recent paper should be cited [Fradkin et al., 2024] (R2).
