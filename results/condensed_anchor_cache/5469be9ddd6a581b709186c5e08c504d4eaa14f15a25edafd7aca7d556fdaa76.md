## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper (BootRec) interprets self-supervised MRI reconstruction with secondary undersampling as bootstrap estimation. It models repeated re-undersampling as a virtual sample set, uses the sampling distribution for bootstrapping, and proposes using the bootstrapped error as a loss function to train the reconstruction model and estimate uncertainty without labeled data. Experiments on public MRI datasets show promising quantitative metrics and correlations between estimated and true errors.

### Strengths
- **Innovative conceptual link** (R1, R3, R4): Viewing secondary undersampling as bootstrapping is novel and inspiring. R3 finds it “very interesting,” R1 “innovative,” R4 “inspiring” and considers the equivalence between sample-set bootstrapping and re-undersampling a key insight.
- **First unsupervised/self-supervised uncertainty quantification for MRI** (R2): According to R2, this is the first method to quantify MRI uncertainty in an unsupervised/self-supervised manner from undersampled data. R3 also notes that estimating reconstruction error is practically valuable for trustworthiness.
- **Performance gains against strong baselines** (R2): The method outperforms SSDU and other zero-shot approaches in PSNR/SSIM (Figure 7). R1 says performance is “commendable”; R3 notes observable correlations between estimated and true reconstruction errors.
- **Training loss consistency** (R1): The loss trajectory during self-supervised training aligns closely with the self-supervised MSE (Figure 6).
- **Algorithm presentation** (R2): R2 finds the algorithm well-written and delivered. (Note: this is contradicted by other reviewers’ evaluation of the overall paper presentation; see weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and clarity (major; R1, R2, R3; R4 disagrees)**:
  - Excessive equations and notations overshadow the core idea; multiple summations and products in single equations (e.g., loss function) are hard to parse (R1).
  - Confusing notation: two different ‘U’s in Eq. 6 are ambiguous and visually similar (R1).
  - Unexplained figures: e.g., Figure 4 lacks axis labels and detailed caption (R1).
  - Prior works insufficiently explained: readers are assumed familiar with Yaman 2022 (zero-shot learning) and secondary undersampling; Sec. 2 should introduce the basis [1,2] (R1, R3).
  - Key concepts are arbitrarily named and left unexplained (e.g., first paragraph Sec. 3.2, paragraph under Eq. 8, start of Sec. 4.3) (R3).
  - Abstract and introduction do not clearly convey the key arguments and take-home message (R3).
  - The sloppy writing does not meet ICLR standards (R3). Reviewer 4, however, considered the paper well written, indicating a clear disagreement on this point.
- **Missing comparison with existing uncertainty quantification methods (critical; R2, R3)**:
  - No comparison with sampling-based approaches [Uncertainty Quantification in Deep MRI Reconstruction] or direct residual error estimation [Rigorous Uncertainty Estimation for MRI Reconstruction] (R2).
  - Should compare against methods that explicitly model uncertainty (e.g., Bayesian neural networks, aleatoric/epistemic separation). Missing prior work [3] on aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties for deep learning MRI reconstruction (R3); unlike BootRec, Bayesian methods separate uncertainty types (R3).
  - How to quantitatively evaluate the quality of the uncertainty estimates is not addressed (R2).
- **Lack of visual results and detailed error evaluation** (R2, R3):
  - Only a PSNR/SSIM plot (Fig. 7) is given; no visual reconstructions or error maps to inspect details (R2). Fig. 5 provides little information (R2).
  - Sec. 5.2 lacks quantitative evaluation of the error estimation quality (R3).
  - R3 further notes no significant theoretical/methodological breakthroughs beyond existing secondary-undersampling MRI reconstruction [1,2].
- **Unclear purpose and utility of the uncertainty estimate** (R1, R2):
  - The paper does not clarify why the variance from bootstrap resampling is important, especially compared to uncertainty for raw measurement reconstruction (R1).
  - The practical value of estimating MSE is unclear: how can the uncertainty be used for downstream diagnosis or clinical decision-making? (R2). The link between estimated MSE and actual reconstruction uncertainty is not articulated (R2).
- **Theoretical foundation claimed but not substantiated** (R1, R3):
  - The paper relies heavily on equations without deep theoretical analysis; the claim of a “theoretical foundation” is not fully justified (R1).
  - Despite the interesting bootstrapping interpretation, there is no major theoretical/methodological advance beyond existing secondary-undersampling approaches [1,2]; secondary undersampling is not the only viable approach for unsupervised/zero-shot MRI reconstruction and error estimation (R3).
- **Missing comparison with state-of-the-art deep learning MRI reconstruction** (R4):
  - The paper should compare with more recent, performant methods (e.g., self-supervised learning framework [1], deep learning approach [2], including vision transformers) even if they are not zero-shot; testing performance can still be benchmarked (R4).
- **Lack of discussion on loss differentiability** (R4):
  - The aggregation function \(h\) is used in the MSE loss for training; its differentiability and impact on gradient flow, training stability, and convergence are not discussed (R4).
- **Unrealistic assumption of independent Bernoulli sampling** (R3):
  - Modeling the undersampling masks as independent Bernoulli variables ignores physical constraints of the gradient system, which make real sampling patterns non-independent. This challenges the core assumption of the method (R3).
- **Additional missing discussions** (R4, implied):
  - Whether the uncertainty arises primarily from the bootstrap resampling operation or other sources is not analyzed (R4 question).
  - The paper does not discuss whether the method generalizes to other image reconstruction tasks outside MRI (e.g., natural images) (R4 question).
