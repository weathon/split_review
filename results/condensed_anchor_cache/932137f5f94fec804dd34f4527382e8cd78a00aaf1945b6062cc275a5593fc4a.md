## Merged Review

### Summary
SineNet introduces a sequential multi-stage U-Net (multiple "waves" of small U-Nets) to mitigate the temporal misalignment in skip connections when applying U-Nets to time-dependent PDEs. The method is motivated by the insight that U-Net features do not evolve across layers for dynamical systems, and the architecture progressively refines high-resolution features through multiple stages. SineNet is evaluated on incompressible/compressible Navier-Stokes and shallow water equations, demonstrating competitive or superior rollout performance compared to baselines (U-Net variants, Factorized FNO, Dilated ResNet). The paper is well-organized and the idea is simple yet effective. Reviewer opinions range from 6 (three reviewers) to 8 (one reviewer). The higher-score reviewer finds the architecture novel and experimental results strong; the lower-score reviewers raise concerns about limitations, missing baselines/ablations, and fairness of comparisons.

### Strengths
- **Original observation**: The spatial misalignment between U-Net hierarchies for temporal PDE modeling is an elemental finding (R1, R4). The visualization in Figure 1 is comprehensive and helps understanding (R1, R4).
- **Architecture design**: The idea is simple, well-motivated, and thought-through (R2, R4). The adaptive δ_k per wave to model different velocity scales is a neat property (R1).
- **Well-supported claims**: All claims are backed by appropriate visualizations and experiments (R1). Ablations are wide-ranging and convincing, in particular Figure 3 showing monotonic improvement with more waves (R2, R4). The code is clean and easy to follow (R4).
- **Performance**: SineNet outperforms strong baselines in rollout experiments on multiple PDE benchmarks (R1, R2, R3, R4). The method solves a common problem in applying U-Nets directly to dynamical systems (R3).
- **Thorough analysis**: Runtime analysis and memory footprint for all models are provided (R1, Table 4 in Appendix C). Related work is exhaustive and adequately cited (R1).
- **Potential impact**: The approach has large potential impact on time-series forecasting with U-Nets and related spatiotemporal dynamics (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear limitations and failure modes** (R1, R3, R4):
  - No discussion of when SineNet would fail (e.g., highly chaotic or turbulent dynamics) (R1).
  - Performance for varying temporal complexity and scaling with spatial resolution / long time horizons is not addressed (R1).
  - Cannot handle irregular grids or different grid sizes; only fixed grid resolutions, limiting applicability (R3, R4). Reviewer 4 suggests evaluating on super-resolution (different resolution than training) to demonstrate flexibility.
  - Autoregressive forecasting only at multiples of Δt, not at any arbitrary time (R3, minor).
- **Missing or incomplete experiments** (R1, R2, R3):
  - More baselines needed: DeepONet, MP-PDE (cited but not implemented), Neural ODE (R3).
  - Evolution of the error over rollout time is not shown; only results at a fixed number of steps are reported (R3).
  - No ablation on the number of historical time steps used as input (R3).
  - Missing ablation: replace each V_k with a standard U-Net using equations (2) and (3) (R2) and provide visualization similar to Figure 5.
  - No test with a very high number of rollout steps to assess stability (R3).
  - No test with more waves than shown in Figure 3 (to see if error eventually plateaus) (R3).
  - No experiments with noise injection for rollout stability (R3, with reference to Stachenfeld et al. 2021).
  - No experiments with different boundary conditions (e.g., non-zero Dirichlet/Neumann) to test zero-padding effectiveness (R3).
- **Metric and justification for misalignment** (R2):
  - The misalignment metric is not clearly introduced; the loss a_{t+Δl} in Appendix A.1 appears to test robustness against noise, not directly measure misalignment. Thus it is unclear if the performance improvement comes from reduced misalignment.
- **Parameter count fairness** (R1):
  - Dil-ResNet has only 4.2M parameters compared to 35M for SineNet, raising questions about fairness of the comparison. A breakdown of parameter counts per layer and trade-off analysis is needed.
- **Memory and storage efficiency** (R4):
  - SineNet’s memory footprint increase with number of waves is not discussed. Standard U-Nets are storage-efficient because high resolution is used only in first/last block; it is unclear if P and Q networks preserve this. Quantitative analysis is requested.
- **Limited novelty compared to earlier stacked U-Nets** (R3):
  - Stacked U-Nets have been used for other tasks; the implementation differs only in minor points, making this a marginal improvement over previous work.
- **Minor issues**:
  - Typo in Section 3.3: "contruct" (R2).
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (R4, score 8) found the experiments strong and ablations convincing, while R3 (score 6) considered experiments not extensive and the improvement marginal. R1 (score 6) raised fairness concerns about the Dil-ResNet comparison. R2 (score 6) questioned the misalignment metric and requested additional ablation.
