## Merged Review

### Summary
Llamole is presented as a multimodal large language model (MLLM) for inverse molecular design that integrates a base LLM with pre-trained graph modules—a Graph Diffusion Transformer and a Graph Neural Network—to enable autoregressive generation of interleaved text and graphs, addressing the difficulty of coherent molecular structure generation in LLMs. It incorporates A* search with LLM-derived cost functions for retrosynthetic planning. The authors created new benchmarking datasets (MolQA, MolPair) and fine-tuning guidelines. Experiments show Llamole outperforms 14 adapted LLMs across 12 metrics for controllable molecular design and retrosynthetic planning. Reviewers generally praise the novelty and performance, though some note that existing MLLMs for molecules already exist and that the key innovation is the interleaved text-graph generation rather than the first MLLM. Concerns include figure clarity, theoretical justification, scalability, baseline comprehensiveness, and the need for comparisons on established datasets.

### Strengths
- **Novel multimodal integration for interleaved text-graph generation**: The combination of a base LLM with a Graph Diffusion Transformer and GNN to handle both text and graph data in an autoregressive manner is innovative. Reviewers 1 and 4 argue it is the first multimodal LLM for molecular design, while Reviewer 2 notes existing MLLMs but emphasizes that the ability to generate interleaved text and graphs is the key departure. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Strong empirical performance**: Llamole achieves significant improvements over 14 adapted LLMs across 12 metrics on the newly introduced controllable molecule generation and retrosynthetic planning benchmarks. Experiments are described as “particularly compelling” for controllable generation. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Practical integration of retrosynthetic planning**: The use of A* search with LLM-based cost functions is a practical and effective approach to ensure synthesizability of designed molecules. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **New datasets (MolQA, MolPair)**: The curated datasets enable complex molecule-text evaluations and are valuable resources for future research if made open-source. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Clear writing and comprehensive prior review**: The paper is well-motivated and clearly written, with a thorough review of prior work on LLMs for molecule synthesis according to Reviewers 1 and 4. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Code provided**: Source code is available, facilitating reproduction and extension. (Reviewer 4)
- **Ablation studies**: The utility of different model components is validated through ablation experiments. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Baseline and comparison limitations**:
  - Baselines are mainly LLMs with ICL or SFT; the only comparable method is GraphGA. The paper should compare with existing inverse molecular design methods (e.g., Weiss et al., Nature Computational Science 2023) and retrosynthesis planning tools (e.g., BioNavi, GNN-Retro). (Reviewer 1)
  - Existing MLLM methods tailored for molecule-text tasks (scalable to 7B parameters) are not included in the introduction, preliminaries, or experiments. Their absence makes it difficult to assess the true novelty and effectiveness. (Reviewer 2)
  - No comparison with recent multimodal approaches that bridge graph and text (e.g., graph attention networks, message passing neural networks). (Reviewer 3)
- **Dataset choice narrows comparisons**: Experiments are conducted primarily on the new datasets (MolQA, MolPair). Additional comparisons on established benchmarks like MoleculeNet or USPTO retrosynthesis datasets would provide better evaluation against previous approaches. (Reviewer 2) *One reviewer (Reviewer 4) described the experiments as “extensive” and including popular benchmarks, indicating disagreement on the scope.*
- **Theoretical justification is weak**: The paper lacks a deep theoretical explanation of why Llamole works effectively. In particular, it does not clarify how the latent spaces of the graph diffusion transformer, GNN, and LLM are aligned, nor how information flow is optimized for multi-conditional generation. A more rigorous information-theoretic analysis would be beneficial. (Reviewer 3)
- **Scalability concerns not addressed**: The computational cost, memory usage, training time, and energy consumption during both training and inference are not discussed. It is unclear how performance scales with the size of the molecular database or the complexity of target molecules. Potential architectural bottlenecks are not identified. (Reviewer 3)
  - Additionally, computational cost and inference time compared to GraphGA are not reported. (Reviewer 1)
- **Figure clarity**: Figure 3 (framework) is hard to understand due to tight layout and restricted color use; rearranging and using a more varied color scheme would improve clarity. (Reviewer 2)
- **Question about input format flexibility**: Can the model handle any random question format, or is the format specified in Figure 2 predetermined? (Reviewer 4)
- **Minimum information requirement**: What is the minimum information needed by Llamole to generate an appropriate molecular structure? (Reviewer 4)
- **Qualitative matching**: Are there qualitative results showing how well the generated material matches the information requested in the text? (Reviewer 4)
- **Generalization to unseen property prediction datasets**: Can the model handle unseen datasets for property prediction in drugs and materials? Insights are requested. (Reviewer 4)
- **Scope of tasks**: The paper should explore performance on a wider range of molecular design tasks, including those involving more complex chemical reactions and molecular structures. (Reviewer 3)
