## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a data-driven Knockoff Sampling (KS) approach for high-dimensional action selection in deep reinforcement learning. It adapts model-X knockoffs by introducing an action mask for policy and value function, sample splitting to reduce temporal dependency, and majority voting to integrate split results. The method aims to select minimal sufficient actions while controlling the False Discovery Rate (FDR), and integrates seamlessly into online RL. Experiments in MuJoCo environments (augmented with redundant action dimensions) and a sepsis treatment allocation task show that KS effectively identifies relevant actions and achieves performance close to using only the true relevant actions.

### Strengths
- Addresses the important challenge of high-dimensional action selection in deep RL, which has practical relevance for robotics, healthcare, and other fields (all reviewers).
- Creative adaptation of Knockoff Sampling to the RL setting, adding action masking, sample splitting, and majority voting to handle temporal dependency (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- Sound theoretical guarantees: KS is proven to control the False Discovery Rate even with dependent data, under the assumptions of stationary environments and independent action dimensions (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The method significantly improves performance over using the full action space on tasks with augmented irrelevant actions, and achieves reward comparable to learning with only the actual relevant actions (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- Presentation is overall clear and well-organized, with detailed explanation of the knockoff sampling adaptation and the action-masking mechanism (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 3 found it clear; Reviewer 4 noted missing details but rated presentation 3).
- One reviewer (score 8) highlighted the KS method as a significant contribution expected to advance the field of high-dimensional action selection; another reviewer (score 3) found the novelty limited (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Unrealistic problem setting**: The paper assumes action dimensions are either fully relevant or completely independent of the environment; in real tasks (e.g., humanoid control) all action dimensions influence state transitions. The experiments add artificially meaningless dimensions, which makes the selection task trivially easy. Several reviewers question the practical relevance. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Overly simplistic assumptions**: The method assumes actions follow a multivariate Gaussian distribution with independent dimensions—a strong simplification not typical in real RL. The extension to correlated actions is neither theoretically developed nor empirically tested. (Reviewer 1)
- **Stationary environment assumption**: The theoretical guarantees and experiments assume a stationary environment, limiting applicability in dynamic settings. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Limited novelty**: One reviewer (score 3) argues the algorithm merely combines knockoff sampling with an off-the-shelf RL algorithm, seeing no substantial algorithmic novelty. Another reviewer (score 8) considers it a significant contribution. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Insufficient empirical evaluation**:
  - No comparison with established baselines for high-dimensional action spaces (e.g., Growing Action Spaces, Action Elimination) in the MuJoCo experiments—only full-action-space and true-action baselines are used. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
  - FDR control validated only in idealized synthetic settings; no evaluation in more complex environments with action dependencies or non-linear dynamics. (Reviewer 1)
  - Missing ablation on the machine learning predictor (e.g., LASSO) used to estimate reward and next state; given non-linear dynamics, the impact of using a linear model is undiscussed. (Reviewer 4)
  - The Treatment Allocation task lacks details: it is referred to as a dataset, but an online algorithm (PPO) is applied; the rationale for selecting comparison algorithms (Lattice, gSDE) is not given. (Reviewer 2)
  - Missing comparison of FDR between KS, true action, all action, and a naive variable selection (VS) method in MuJoCo. (Reviewer 2)
  - The number of times the action subset is recomputed (\(T_{vs}\)) is not reported; its impact on performance is unexamined. (Reviewer 4)
  - The choice of the anti-symmetric function \(f\) used in experiments is not revealed. (Reviewer 4)
  - Figure 2 (left) legend incorrectly labels PPO instead of SAC. (Reviewer 4)
  - In Figure 2, a variable selection method is included but not cited or explained. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing explanations and incomplete proofs**:
  - The motivating example in Figure 1 lacks details (environment, number of seeds). (Reviewer 4)
  - Definition of \(\tau_\alpha\) in Equation 2 may be undefined if the set is empty; conditions for proper definition are missing. (Reviewer 4)
  - No justification for assuming trajectories are independent “without loss of generality” (Line 344). (Reviewer 4)
  - The flip-sign property and its necessity in knockoff-type methods is not explained (Line 358). (Reviewer 4)
  - Proof of Theorem 4.3 is incomplete, referring readers to another work without full derivation; the paper cannot be accepted without it. (Reviewer 4)
  - Section 4 ends abruptly; the relevance of Theorem 4.3 for the method is not explained. (Reviewer 4)
  - Baselines introduced in Line 425 (Lattice, gSDE) are not explained. (Reviewer 4)
  - Analysis of Figure 5 is insufficient; the reader is left with little information. (Reviewer 4)
- **Impact of masking on learning dynamics**: The effect of action pruning on exploration behavior and convergence stability is not analyzed. Sudden changes to the action space may destabilize training. (Reviewer 1)
- **Static action mask**: The mask does not adapt based on state, limiting flexibility compared to state-dependent methods like Action Elimination. (Reviewer 1)
- **Sufficient action set definition is too strict**: In realistic settings with high-dimensional actions, many dimensions may influence state without being necessary for task completion. A more flexible definition would be beneficial. (Reviewer 2)
- **Time efficiency not demonstrated**: Doubling action sampling and looping over the replay buffer (Algorithm 2) incur additional time; no clock-time comparison is provided. (Reviewer 4)
- **Action distributions in Figure C.1 look alike**; the authors should comment. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing details in notation and algorithms**:
  - \(\rho_\pi(s_t)\) and \(\rho_\pi(s_t,a_t)\) are undefined; \(s_t, a_t\) not defined before Definition 2.1. (Reviewer 4)
  - In Algorithm 2, index \(j\) is used both for action dimensions and for trajectories; suggest distinct letters. (Reviewer 4)
  - Figure 2 (left) legend shows PPO instead of SAC. (Reviewer 4)
  - Line 332 references “Section 4” instead of “Section 2.3”; “equation” should be capitalized. (Reviewer 4)
  - Suggest explaining intentions of Section 4 before diving in. (Reviewer 4)
  - Figures are small; font should be increased; Figure 3 has wasted space. (Reviewer 4)
- **Unaddressed questions that overlap with criticisms** (converted to weaknesses):
  - Request for experiments with partially correlated redundant actions to better simulate real-world weak influences. (Reviewer 1, Question 1)
  - Request for empirical validation of FDR control in more complex RL environments with action dependencies. (Reviewer 1, Question 2)
  - Request for comparison with baselines (Growing Action Spaces, Action Elimination) and explanation why only “true actions” baseline was used in MuJoCo. (Reviewer 1, Question 3)
  - Request for discussion or adaptation to state-dependent masking. (Reviewer 1, Question 4)
  - Request for adaptation to non-stationary environments. (Reviewer 1, Question 5)
  - Request for analysis of masking impact on exploration and convergence, tracking metrics like action diversity, visit counts, variance in policy updates. (Reviewer 1, Question 6)
  - Request for discussion on using LASSO (linear) for non-linear reward/transition dynamics. (Reviewer 4, Question 1)
  - Request for value of \(T_{vs}\) and its impact on performance. (Reviewer 4, Question 2)
  - Request for choice of anti-symmetric function \(f\). (Reviewer 4, Question 3)
  - Request for comment on action distributions in Figure C.1 looking alike. (Reviewer 4, Question 4)
  - Request for clock-time plots instead of gradient-step counts to fairly compare efficiency. (Reviewer 4, Weakness 1d)
