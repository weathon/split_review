## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces the Long Question Coreference Adaptation (LQCA) method to improve LLM performance on long-context QA and summarization by preprocessing documents with coreference resolution. It uses a BERT-based model to resolve coreferences within sub-documents, computes mention distances, defines representative mentions, and replaces ambiguous pronouns with disambiguated entities before feeding the context to the LLM. Experiments on LongBench and other datasets show improvements, particularly on OpenAI-o1-mini and GPT-4o, and gains over methods like RAGs.

### Strengths
- The method is simple and directly addresses mention ambiguity in long contexts, using a BERT-based coreference model with a statistical approach to combine sub‑document coref results via distance metrics (R1).
- Empirical results show consistent gains across models and datasets, including comparisons with RAGs, with analysis quantifying the improvements (R1, R2).
- The paper is well‑written and the proposed approach is easy to follow (R2).
- The coreference preprocessing idea is intuitive and could potentially be applied during LLM pretraining or fine‑tuning (R2).
- The work tackles an important problem: LLMs’ difficulty with long contexts (R3).
- LQCA is a novel combination of coreference resolution with long‑context QA (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Coreference error impact**: It is unclear how errors in coreference resolution affect end performance. A suggested experiment is to inject noise into coreference clusters to measure this impact (R1, R2, R3 all raise this).
- **Lack of coreference accuracy evaluation**: No empirical evaluation of the coreference algorithm itself is provided (only downstream results). The accuracy of the mention merging computation is unknown; manual evaluation on shorter documents could help quantify the degree of noise and how much better results would be with perfect coreference (R2).
- **Limited technical contribution**: One reviewer (score 6) finds the coreference merging idea interesting but notes that without evaluation of coreference accuracy, it is hard to assess technical novelty (R2). Another reviewer (score 6) notes insufficient comparison with alternative state‑of‑the‑art long‑context understanding methods (R3).
- **Missing details and clarity**:  
  - Figure 2 caption is not informative; it is unclear what is being shown (R2).  
  - No concrete example of the method in Section 3.2 is provided (R2).  
  - Prompts used for QA and summarization tasks are not presented (should be in appendix) (R2).  
  - Maverick is not described in sufficient detail for ILCR readers (R2).  
  - The “Avg” column in Table 1 is not defined; it is unclear whether it averages across Rouge, Accuracy, and F1 (R2).  
- **Missing experiments and analyses**:  
  - Results with fine‑tuning (e.g., LoRA) are not explored (R2).  
  - Computational complexity of the coreference preprocessing step is not reported, which is crucial for scalability and practical applicability (R2, R3).  
  - RAG and RECOMP are not incompatible with LQCA; it would be useful to test coreference on retrieved segments (R2).  
  - Analysis of why performance improves is lacking—e.g., whether improvement stems from more accurate named entity identification (R2).  
  - LQCA uses zero‑shot coreference, but existing work (e.g., LREC 2024) shows LLMs are not strong at coreference; providing examples might improve results (R2).  
- **Handling ambiguous references**: The paper does not sufficiently discuss how LQCA deals with highly ambiguous contexts where multiple coreference interpretations are possible (R3).
- **Disagreement among reviewers**: While one reviewer (score 8, confidence 3) emphasizes the method’s simplicity and gains, other reviewers (scores 6, confidence 4 and 3) find the technical contribution limited and the evaluation insufficient, particularly regarding coreference accuracy and comparisons with broader baselines.
