## Merged Review

### Summary
RetFormer enhances image recognition (long-tailed classification, learning with noisy labels) by integrating a multimodal knowledge base and a retrieval cross-fusion module to establish robust image-text relationships. The method stores world knowledge externally and uses retrieval to incorporate relevant information into the model’s decision process, aiming to overcome limitations of large model parameters and real-world data distribution issues. While the idea is interesting to some reviewers, a majority find the motivation unclear, the comparisons unfair or insufficient, the novelty limited, and the writing lacking in clarity and detail. One reviewer (score 5) strongly supports the work; others (scores 1,3,3,3,3) raise significant concerns.

### Strengths
- The use of a retrieval-augmented strategy for classification is a meaningful direction that can improve interpretability and leverage external knowledge (R1, R3, R6).
- The paper is easy to follow for some reviewers (R3, R4), with clear formulation and some well-conducted experiments (R3).
- The method achieves state-of-the-art results on several benchmarks (R2, R4, R6) and the ablation study nicely describes hyperparameters like K-means clusters (R6).
- The motivation of using a knowledge base to establish multimodal sample relationships is interesting and reasonable (R4; minority positive view).
- The experiments and analyses covering different scenarios (CLIP, retrieval) are relatively comprehensive (R5).
- The effectiveness of introducing retrieval augmentation is evident (R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and clarity issues** – The paper is very poorly written; important information is missing (R1). Terms and symbols are unexplained (e.g., D= I,T in Line 198; what is “h” in Eq.3; CLIP is introduced without prior mention; P and D in Line 224; “retrieval-augmented into the field of vision recognition” Line 73-74 is vague; Line 81-83 is unclear) (R6). Several sections lack clear definitions and examples (R6). Figure ordering does not match the text description (R6). One reviewer (R4) disagrees, calling the paper well-written – but the majority find it unclear.
- **Unclear or contradictory motivation** – The paper lists multiple motivations (model size, long-tail, noisy labels, efficiency) without showing how the method addresses each specifically (R1, R2, R6). It is not clear why retrieval-augmented classification solves long-tailed or noisy label problems (R1, R2, R4, R6). If the claim is efficiency, no complexity or inference time comparison is given (GFLOPs, inference time per query) (R6). Using two image encoders and a 1.4B image-text pair knowledge base (DataComp subset) contradicts the claim of parameter/model size efficiency (R1, R6). Results suggest that pretraining on ImageNet outperforms DataComp-1B pretraining, undermining the retrieval advantage (R6). The “chicken-or-egg” problem: a reliable knowledge base is critical, but if a good retrieval model already exists, then classification may be unnecessary (R3).
- **Insufficient and unfair comparisons** – The experiments compare RetFormer with methods that do not use multimodal retrieval augmentation, making the comparison unfair (R2). Many relevant works are not cited or compared: RA-CLIP (Xie et al., CVPR2023), Learning Customized Visual Models with Retrieval-Augmented Knowledge (Liu et al., CVPR2023) (R2); MAM (Iscen et al., 2023) (results for many-shot on ImageNet-LT are 85.4, not 80.6 as reported) (R6); REVEAL (CVPR2022), Fine-grained Late-interaction Multi-modal Retrieval (NeurIPS2023), Retrieval-Augmented Multimodal Language Modeling (ICML2023) (R5); Long-Tail Learning with Foundation Model (ICML2024), Scenic (CVPR’22), Sample Prior Guided Robust Model Learning, Label-Retrieval-Augmented Diffusion Models (NeurIPS’24) (R6). Missing comparisons to vanilla data augmentation (rotation, mixup, re-caption by LLMs) as alternative ways to model sample relationships (R3). For long-tailed learning, results on Places-LT and iNaturalist2018 should also be reported (R4).
- **Fairness of experimental setting** – The method uses a CLIP pretrained image encoder to initialize its own image encoder, which gives an unfair advantage over compared baselines (R1). Builds a knowledge base using the downstream dataset directly (Line 255-258), which raises generalization concerns (R3). Fig. 4 shows large performance impact from the knowledge base, questioning practical usability (R3).
- **Limited novelty** – Retrieval augmentation for multimodal recognition is already well-explored (e.g., MAM, RA-CLIP); the basic idea of RetFormer is very similar to existing works (R2, R4). The authors do not clearly differentiate their contribution from prior work (R1, R2).
- **Noise robustness concerns** – Figure 1 shows a noisy label case (misspelled “Lable”) but the method fuses that information into image features via cross-attention; how is the model prevented from being influenced by noisy semantics? (R5). The retrieval number k=32 can cause significant GPU memory overhead; the improvement from k=16 to 32 in Fig. 4 is not significant, so clustering or prototype-based methods may be better (R5).
- **Missing evaluations and ablations** – The method is only validated on long-tailed and noisy label tasks separately; it is not tested on combined long-tailed noisy label learning (R4, citing [a] ICCV2023). Why not evaluate on large-scale datasets like ImageNet (which has about 7% noisy labels)? (R1). It is unclear what specific part of the methodology provides robustness to long-tail and noise; could the method simply be applied to normal classification? (R6). Would fine-tuning CLIP on the target small dataset be more effective and efficient? (R1). The paper does not quantify the time and spatial complexity in Tables 1-4 (R3).
- **Fixed retrieval samples** – Lines 288-289 mention encoder parameters are frozen and retrieval results can be reused; does this mean retrieval is offline and samples (kNN) are fixed per query? If so, why not directly add the retrieved data to pretraining? (R5). The decision boundary changes before and after retrieval augmentation should be visualized to understand whether improvement comes from extra data or cross-attention (R5).
- **Outdated references** – The vast majority of cited works are from 2023 or earlier, missing several relevant 2024 publications (R6).
