## Merged Review

### Summary

This work proposes Token-Aware Inference-Time Intervention (TA-ITI) to address sentence-level ITI’s coarse granularity and fixed intervention strength. TA-ITI comprises Mutual Information-Guided Token-level Graph Aggregation (MIG) for richer token representation via a graph propagation step, and Misalignment-aware Adaptive Token-level Intervention (MAI) that adjusts intervention strength per token using representation misalignment and prediction uncertainty. Experiments on TruthfulQA, RealToxicityPrompts, and StereoSet show performance gains (e.g., 25.8% improvement on truthfulness primary metric). Reviewers note that the token-level motivation is plausible and the experimental results are strong, but they raise substantial concerns about incremental novelty, unclear implementation choices, missing intuitive justification, additional inference cost, generalization to unseen datasets, and hyperparameter sensitivity.

### Strengths

- The general motivation for moving from sentence-level to token-level intervention is reasonable and addresses a recognized limitation of prior ITI methods (R1, R2, R3, R4).  
- The paper is well-written and well-motivated (R1).  
- Extensive experiments are conducted on truthfulness (TruthfulQA), harmlessness (RealToxicityPrompts), and fairness (StereoSet), with proper ablation studies and analysis (R1, R2, R4). The method not only improves scores but also generates fluent outputs (R1).  
- MIG and MAI components are shown to be effective through overall performance and ablation experiments (R2, R4).  
- The added inference computational cost is acceptable relative to the obtained alignment gains (R1, R2).  
- Technically sound – the experiments demonstrate clear performance improvements across benchmarks (R5).  
- The work innovatively combines MI-guided token-level graph aggregation with adaptive token-level intervention (R4).  

### Weaknesses

- **Originality and incremental contribution**: The work is an extension of a previous ITI method ([1]) from sentence to token level; the mutual information-based propagation and adaptive intervention are common techniques in graph representation learning and inference-time methods, respectively (R2, R3, R5). The core intervention mechanism remains additive adjustment using a single direction vector, without exploring more complex or adaptive intervention paradigms (R4).  
- **Lack of clear motivation and intuitive explanation for key design choices**:  
  - Why is graph aggregation needed at all? The last token already integrates sentence information via self-attention; the paper does not explain how MIG overcomes limitations of self-attention for capturing global alignment information (R3, R4).  
  - The entropy calculation in lines 209-210 uses discretized bins; the choice seems arbitrary and alternative methods are not discussed (R1, R3).  
  - The graph propagation module lacks strong motivation and theoretical justification (R3).  
- **Missing details and unclear notation**:  
  - How are the bins set up when computing entropy? (R1)  
  - In Formula 4: what is the exact implementation of P and what role does label y play? (R4)  
  - In Formula 7: what is W_LM and how is it used? The paper should clarify that W_LM is the language modeling head and explain its role in uncertainty quantification (R4).  
  - The training process for the universal alignment probe $\widetilde{P}$ and the role of y in the binary classification task require more explanation (R4).  
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Two hyperparameters alpha and beta need tuning, and performance is non-optimal when beta < 0.4. If these behaviors do not hold across tasks, applicability is hindered (R1).  
- **Generalizability concerns**: MIG and MAI rely on supervised trained misalignment probes and misalignment estimators, which limits transfer to unseen test data (R2, R5). The construction of the token-level misalignment dataset (how is a token deemed prone to misalignment?) is insufficiently detailed (R2). It is unclear whether the intervention direction and Misalignment Estimator are derived from the same dataset as the test sets; performance gains may be less surprising if both come from the same dataset, and generalization to other benchmarks (e.g., HaluEval) is not shown (R5).  
- **Additional inference cost**: The method requires recalculating token representations multiple times via graph aggregation and training/evaluating a Misalignment Estimator, incurring extra computational resources (R5).  
- **Potential over-refusal**: The improvement on RealToxicityPrompts may be due to excessive refusal; the paper does not investigate whether ITI can avoid over-refusal. Including experiments on XSTest would help address this (R5).  
- **Background insufficient**: The related work and preliminaries sections should provide more background on editing-based inference-time intervention, rather than just citing previous works (R2).  
- **Stationarity of graph propagation**: Would the entropy propagation method reach a stationary distribution after multiple rounds? (R1)  
- **Adaptive direction not explored**: Only the intervention strength is token-adaptive; the direction remains fixed. Trying token-adaptive directions is not attempted (R4).  
- **Normalization**: It is unclear whether vector normalization is applied at any step (MI-guided aggregation, direction extraction, intervention) and what impact it has (R4).  

**Reviewer disagreement**: Reviewer 1 is substantially more positive (score 8) and finds the cost acceptable and the hyperparameter issue manageable, while Reviewers 3 and 5 (score 5) are significantly more critical, emphasizing the incremental nature and lack of strong justification for design choices. Reviewers 2 and 4 (score 6) are moderately positive but note limitations in originality, explanation, and generalization.
