## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper presents UC-NeRF, a method for novel view synthesis in under-calibrated multi-camera systems (specifically automotive setups, though the title does not reflect this). The approach addresses color inconsistency via layer-based color correction (separate sky/foreground affine matrices), expands training views through virtual warping using MVS depth and geometric consistency checks, and refines camera poses with spatiotemporally constrained optimization. Experiments on Waymo and NuScenes datasets show state-of-the-art results against Mip-NeRF and Zip-NeRF. Reviewers generally find the problem relevant and the method sound, with solid ablations, but raise concerns about novelty, clarity of notation and training procedure, limited baseline comparisons, and reliance on segmentation/MVS preprocessing.

### Strengths

- The problem (under-calibrated multi-camera systems in autonomous driving) is relevant and interesting.
- The proposed method is technically sound and effective. The layer-based color correction addresses color inconsistencies across cameras, and virtual warping expands training view diversity.
- Experimental results are promising and achieve state-of-the-art performance; the ablation study convincingly shows each module’s contribution.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (reviewer 3), though reviewer 5 found the introduction unclear.
- References are adequate; the experiment design (especially ablation) is solid.

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty concerns.**  
  - The first two contributions (layer-based color correction and virtual warping) are considered somewhat trivial. They are constructed from existing tools (pretrained segmentation, MVS, geometric consistency) and act as preprocessing. It is not clear why classical multi-view color correction (e.g., from SfM) or existing NeRF methods (RAWNeRF, block-NeRF) cannot handle the same issue.  
  - The spatiotemporally constrained pose refinement is similar to BARF (ICCV 2021) and BAD-NeRF (CVPR 2023). The novelty over these works is not explicitly justified.  
  - Pose refinement is straightforward: decomposing into ego-pose and relative transformation is naive since the rig’s relative poses can be obtained by calibration. The pose refinement relies on keypoint correspondences, which are prone to outliers from repetitive structures and moving objects.  
  - The whole pipeline is verbose; the three modules are stitched together with few connections. It is unclear whether UC-NeRF can be trained end-to-end.  
- **Notation and missing definitions.**  
  - In Eq. (5): **b** and **d** are not defined (multiple reviewers).  
  - In Eq. (6): *d_v* and *d_o* are not defined.  
  - In Eq. (7): it is unclear whether the relative transformation Δ**T**_k is optimized. The formulation assumes fixed relative pose across time, but mechanical vibrations during driving change those poses—this is not handled.  
  - The final training loss composition is missing: e.g., ℒ = ℒ_pho + λ₁ℒ_reg + λ₂ℒ_rpj? Are there per-loss weights?  
- **Clarity of training strategy and modules.**  
  - Section 3.5 (overall training) needs more explanation: does training proceed by first converging with Eq. (4), then computing color correction matrices, then applying them to virtual views, then re-training with virtual views included? The flow is ambiguous.  
  - Section 4.4 is considered redundant; the space could be used to detail Section 3.5.  
  - The introduction is difficult to follow: it is not clear why virtual warping is needed and what distinguishes multi-camera NeRF from single-camera NeRF.  
- **Issues with virtual warping and sky segmentation.**  
  - Virtual views contain holes due to low confidence or occlusion (Fig. 9). How does the sky segmentation (Yin 2022) perform on these images? What is the mask value in missing regions, and how does that affect Eq. (3)?  
  - The virtual warping step relies on an MVS method to generate dense depth maps; such methods may not generalize well to street views and may require further pretraining.  
  - The time cost of filtering inaccurate depths via geometric consistency check is not reported.  
- **Dependence on sky segmentation and limited weather conditions.**  
  - The accuracy of color correction depends heavily on sky segmentation, but all shown cases contain clean skies and sunny weather. Performance under diverse weather (rain, fog, night) is not evaluated, though the method targets outdoor autonomous driving.  
- **Limited experimental comparisons and missing baselines.**  
  - Only Mip-NeRF and Zip-NeRF are compared. Missing comparisons to other multi-camera NeRFs such as block-NeRF, MC-NeRF, NeRF-MS.  
  - Efficiency comparisons (runtime or resource usage) are not provided.  
  - Code is not released (reviewer 4 notes that even a promise to share upon acceptance would help reproducibility). If Zip-NeRF was reimplemented, the source of that reimplementation should be cited.  
- **Specific technical questions that overlap with criticisms.**  
  - Regarding Eq. (7): would it be better to directly model a 4×4 transformation between cameras across time, instead of propagating fixed relative poses from time 0? (Reviewer 1)  
  - Has the author considered DBARF (CVPR 2023) or FlowCam (NeurIPS 2023), which jointly optimize consecutive camera poses and NeRF?  
  - How does the spatiotemporally constrained pose refinement differ from standard bundle adjustment?  
  - In Fig. 5, the proposed result appears hazier than Zip-NeRF in the road region where sky meets ground; what is the cause?  
- **Minor presentation issues.**  
  - The title is misleading because it omits the automotive setting; sky and foreground layers are assumed as necessary input, so the method is not generic for all under-calibrated cameras. Please correct.
