## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers agree the paper addresses a relevant network tomography problem and shows promising empirical improvements over existing baselines (R1, R3, R4). They highlight the effective integration of connectivity, sparsity, and triangle inequality constraints (R2, R3). Reviewer 3 is most positive, emphasizing the path-centric GNN framework, the combination of structural and performance constraints, and thorough experimental evaluation across latency, bandwidth, and loss rate. However, collectively the reviewers identify significant concerns: limited novelty of graph learning and path aggregation (R1), missing baselines (bilevel optimization, SEAL, path neural networks, matrix completion methods in social network scenarios) (R1, R4), lack of theoretical support (optimality guarantees, convergence, complexity analysis) (R1, R2), and insufficient experiments (topology reconstruction only at error rate 0.2, need higher missing rates) (R4). Additional criticisms include embedding method restrictions, pooling information loss, failure to handle dynamics or path sequentiality, poor generalization, unclear path set construction without known topology, practical probing overhead, and inapplicability of triangle inequality under load balancing (R2, R3, R4). After the rebuttal, Reviewer 1 partially raised their score but noted the paper must cite path neural networks, adjust related work, and include it as a baseline.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a relevant and important network tomography problem (R1, R3, R4).
- DeepNT effectively integrates connectivity, sparsity, and triangle inequality constraints, enabling robust inference of network topology and path performance metrics under incomplete data (R2, R3, R4).
- The path-centric GNN provides a new theoretical framework for network tomography, focusing on path-level performance rather than node or graph-level (R3).
- Experimental results demonstrate clear improvements over existing network tomography baselines across multiple performance metrics (latency, bandwidth, packet loss rate) and diverse datasets (real-world and synthetic), using multiple evaluation metrics (R1, R3, R4).
- The method shows particular robustness on large-scale and complex network structures (R3).
- Multiple classical baselines are considered in the experiments (R1).
- Ideas are clearly expressed and the paper presents interesting findings (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty:** Graph learning in GNNs already extensively studied; the proposed path aggregation layer does not seem significantly novel (R1). After the rebuttal, the paper now uses expressiveness proof from the path neural networks paper, so related work needs adjustment and that paper must be cited and included as a baseline (R1).
- **Missing baselines:** No comparison with bilevel optimization graph learning approaches, SEAL subgraph aggregation, or path neural networks (R1). In social network scenarios, DeepNT should be compared to matrix completion methods rather than only network performance tomography baselines (R4). The originally considered baselines were not sufficiently competitive (R1 post-rebuttal).
- **Insufficient theoretical support:** No guarantees that predicted pairwise metrics are near optimal given enough observations (R1). The paper lacks theoretical analysis of overall training complexity, convergence of the alternating optimization, and computational trade-offs for different network sizes, embedding types, and constraints (R2). Memory requirements for storing and processing large adjacency matrices are not discussed (R2).
- **Limited embedding methods:** Only DeepWalk-inspired random walks and SVD-based HOPE embeddings are used; more complex embeddings (graph attention, spectral) are overlooked (R2). The approach does not consider incorporating node features, nor does it analyze how the path sampling strategy (e.g., biased random walks) impacts embedding quality and overall performance (R2).
- **Pooling information loss:** Pooling may lose critical information on long paths or when node features vary significantly (e.g., bottleneck nodes with highly variable latency). A single pooling operation may average out important variations, limiting flexibility and accuracy. An adaptive pooling mechanism is needed (R3).
- **Lack of dynamic adaptability:** The model relies on static graph structures and pre-learned features; it cannot quickly adapt to real-time network changes such as dynamic addition/removal of nodes/edges, traffic surges (flash crowd example), or congestion changes (R3).
- **Ignoring path sequentiality:** The model does not consider the order of nodes along a path, which can affect performance predictions in location-specific congestion scenarios (R3).
- **Generalization concerns:** Training uses only a portion of graph information; the model may perform poorly on network configurations or conditions not represented in training data (e.g., different geographical regions) (R3).
- **Path set construction unclear:** How is the set of all possible paths $P_{uv}$ defined without a known adjacency matrix? (R4). Probing overhead and the feasibility of controlling probes to traverse specific routing paths (source routing) are not addressed (R4).
- **Triangle inequality may not hold:** In practice, delay between $<u,v>$ may not always be less than $<u,z> + <z,v>$ due to load balancing policies; it is even less applicable in social networks (R4).
- **Topology reconstruction experiments insufficient:** Only tested at a topological error rate of 0.2; experiments with higher missing rates or block missing patterns are needed to validate effectiveness (R4).
