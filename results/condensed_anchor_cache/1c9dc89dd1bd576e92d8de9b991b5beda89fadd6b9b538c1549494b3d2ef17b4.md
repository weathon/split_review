## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Chebyshev polynomial-based self-attention layer (CheAtt) to replace the standard self-attention in Transformers for tabular data, addressing the oversmoothing problem. Inspired by graph signal processing and the PageRank algorithm, the method uses finite-degree Chebyshev polynomials to approximate a graph filter. Experiments on three base models (TabTransformer, MET, SAINT) show performance improvements on downstream classification and regression tasks with moderate computational overhead.

### Strengths
- **Novel idea**: Introduces polynomial-based self-attention to tabular data, effectively mitigating oversmoothing (Reviewers 1,3).
- **Principled design**: Uses matrix polynomials inspired by graph signal processing and the PageRank algorithm, with Chebyshev polynomials to stabilize coefficient training (Reviewer 1).
- **Clear motivation and reasonable solution**: Well-written paper, motivation is clear, and the proposed solution is reasonable (Reviewers 2,3).
- **Experimental validation**: Experiments are thorough and convincing; CheAtt improves base models significantly on classification and regression, outperforming state-of-the-art methods (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- **Computational efficiency advantage**: The polynomial layer’s inherent computational cost is mitigated by the less-token nature of tabular data, and the approach is compatible with current accelerating techniques (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient theoretical support**: The paper claims that self-attention can be viewed as a graph filter, but does not cite enough literature or provide theorems to fully justify this. The proof that the self-attention matrix satisfies the three properties of a transition matrix required for PageRank convergence is only a rough qualitative analysis. Specifically, while softmax ensures non-negativity, it is not rigorously shown that row-sum to one is always satisfied given numerical instability and the dynamic nature of attention weights. If the self-attention matrix does not meet these requirements, the polynomial approximation may fail (Reviewer 1).
- **Unreliable experimental results due to high variance**: Some results have excessively large standard deviations (e.g., 70.9±13.90 in Table 2, 58.1±24.32 in Table 3). These error margins raise doubts about which method is truly best and undermine the fairness of comparisons. The reported means may not reflect typical performance (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear applicability of CheAtt**: The paper states that CheAtt’s effect depends on the “quality of the base model,” yet this term is undefined. CheAtt shows strong improvements in TabTransformer and MET but almost none in SAINT, which has the best original performance among the three. This suggests CheAtt may not be widely applicable; further analysis is needed to understand when and why it works (Reviewer 1).
- **Inadequate computational complexity analysis**: (i) Table 5 reports times in milliseconds, but it is unclear whether these are inference times only. If so, they ignore the additional matrix multiplications required during training. (ii) Only absolute time differences are given; relative time increase is more meaningful—e.g., on the Phishing dataset, the extra time is up to 40% of the original, a significant overhead. (iii) A suspicious result shows MET+CheAtt reducing time on Phishing (from 2.7538 to 2.4625); this may be a clerical error or needs explanation (Reviewer 1). (iv) The paper lacks a detailed theoretical complexity analysis of the proposed layer (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited comparison with other approaches**: The paper does not thoroughly compare CheAtt to other recent methods for addressing oversmoothing in Transformer-based tabular models. It is unclear whether existing techniques exist for this task and how CheAtt stacks up against them (Reviewers 2,3).
- **Scalability concerns**: Experiments use small datasets; it is unclear how the layer would scale to larger datasets or more complex models. Further testing on larger data is recommended (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation details**: Tables 2, 3, 4, and 5 lack up/down arrows to indicate relative performance direction, which would improve readability (Reviewer 3).
- **More details on tabular data task**: The paper should provide additional background on the specific challenges and prior work in tabular data representation (Reviewer 2).

*Note: While Reviewers 2 and 3 were generally positive about the paper’s contributions and validation, Reviewer 1 raised significant concerns about theoretical grounding, experimental reliability, and applicability, indicating substantial disagreement on the soundness of the work.*
