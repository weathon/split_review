## Merged Review

### Summary

UniEdit proposes a tuning-free framework for text-guided video motion and appearance editing using a pre-trained text-to-video (T2V) diffusion model within an inversion-then-generation paradigm. It introduces auxiliary motion-reference and reconstruction branches that inject features into temporal and spatial self-attention layers respectively, enabling motion editing while preserving content. The method is demonstrated on multiple T2V models and outperforms prior zero-shot methods on CLIP score, Frame Quality, and Temporal Quality metrics. However, reviewers raise major concerns about limited novelty (building on widely-used spatial-temporal attention manipulation and auxiliary branches), missing strong baselines (ReVideo, COVE, AnyV2V, I2VEdit, FLATTEN), only 16-frame outputs, a small user study (10 participants), noticeable failures on large motion and inconsistency (e.g., cat→dog head turn), and overclaiming. One reviewer finds the work a significant unified framework (score 8), while another judges it insufficient (score 1). The paper is in a competitive field and would benefit from broader comparisons, longer videos, and clearer scope.

### Strengths

- Unified tuning-free framework handling both motion and appearance editing, leveraging the insight that temporal self-attention encodes inter-frame dependency and spatial self-attention intra-frame dependency.
- Zero-shot editing capability: no fine-tuning of the base T2V model for each video or task, avoiding computational cost.
- Technically sound design of attention replacement for appearance, structure, and motion editing.
- Good clarity in writing, easy to follow the flow of ideas; well-motivated problem.
- Editing results shown on multiple T2V models, not limited to a single architecture.
- Video quality on the project page is excellent; experimental results are easy to understand.
- Improvements over methods like TokenFlow and Rerender in CLIP score, Frame Quality, and Temporal Quality (though margins are modest, e.g., 1-2%).
- Emphasis on temporal consistency via reconstruction branch feature injection into spatial self-attention layers and mask-guided coordination.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty:** The core techniques (auxiliary branches, feature injection via self-attention, spatial/temporal self-attention manipulation) are widely explored in existing video editing works including Video-P2P, Tune-A-Video, Zero-Shot Video Editing, FateZero, Edit-A-Video, FLATTEN, I2VEdit, SIFA. The contribution feels incremental rather than a significant breakthrough. (Multiple reviewers)
- **Missing critical baselines:** ReVideo (NeurIPS 2024) focuses on both motion and content control and should be directly compared. Also missing COVE (NeurIPS 2024), AnyV2V, I2VEdit, FLATTEN. Including these would reveal strengths/weaknesses in tasks like object replacement, style transfer, and motion modification. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Only 16 frames:** All results are 16-frame clips, too short to demonstrate practical utility for long video editing. Real-world applications require longer sequences. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Small user study:** Only 10 participants, leading to highly subjective results. Including this in the main performance table could confuse readers; either more participants or a clear caveat near the table is needed. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Overclaiming:** Claim "pioneering leap" is unsupported given the incremental nature. Also claim in L217 "better tackle source video with large dynamics" but most examples have small motion magnitudes and failures appear on large motion (e.g., cat→dog head turn shows temporal inconsistency during the dog’s head turn, noticeable in Fig G.1). (Reviewers 4, 3)
- **Inconsistency in motion editing:** The cat→dog example and Fig.4 bottom-right case (motion editing changing background lawn) show unintended edits, indicating lack of precise spatial isolation. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Dependence on architecture:** Claim that UniEdit is not limited to specific models is inaccurate; it depends on separate spatial and temporal attention design, incompatible with full 3D attention DiT-based generators (increasingly effective). (Reviewer 3)
- **Inversion-generation paradigm limits real-world videos:** Likely performs better on generated videos but struggles with out-of-domain real videos due to imperfect inversion, raising questions about robustness. (Reviewer 3)
- **Ambiguity in edit scope:** Not explicitly stated which types of motion/appearance edits are feasible, what prompts may fail (complex scenes, multiple simultaneous edits), and known failure cases. The inherent ambiguity of language makes success not guaranteed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Minor reviewer disagreements:** While reviewer 2 scores 8 and finds the integration novel, reviewer 4 scores 1 and considers the approach not up-to-date. Reviewer 2 acknowledges novelty in integration but notes the techniques are not entirely new.
- **Missing objective metrics:** Evaluation relies heavily on CLIP scores and user preferences; additional metrics like Flow Warping Error (Ewarp) or Frame Variance Metric for Motion Detection (FVMD) would strengthen robustness. (Reviewer 2)
- **Sensitivity analysis could be more detailed:** Analysis of injection steps and blend layers/steps shows trade-offs (stylization vs. fidelity) but needs clearer guidelines for adjustment. (Reviewer 2)
- **Ablation question:** Why does content preservation improve textual alignment significantly in Table 2? (Reviewer 3)
- **Success rate:** Different methods have their own generation success rates; how to measure this for fair comparison beyond visual examples, and how to know if this tuning-free method really works compared to large-scale trained methods? (Reviewer 3)
- **Formatting note:** Consider indicating in table captions that higher values are better. (Reviewer 3)
