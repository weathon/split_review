## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper tackles the problem that EEG models trained on fixed datasets fail to generalize to the continuous stream of new subjects in real-world scenarios. It proposes BrainUICL, an unsupervised continual learning framework that uses a Dynamic Confident Buffer (DCB) to selectively replay past knowledge and a Cross Epoch Alignment (CEA) method to align model states over time. The approach is evaluated on three EEG tasks (sleep, emotion, motor imagery) and shows improved plasticity and stability.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important and practically relevant problem: inter-subject variability in EEG, and the need for models that adapt to new individuals without forgetting.
- The method is well-motivated and fits the problem setting.
- Outperforms existing methods on the evaluated datasets.
- The approach is well benchmarked with comparisons to relevant SOTA methods, ablation studies, and analysis of computational costs (R3).
- Technically detailed with appendices; presented with fair clarity (R3).
- The research question (unsupervised continual learning for EEG) is interesting (R4).
- Reviewer 2 acknowledges the attempt to use continual learning to manage individual differences.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited dataset coverage**: The paper should test on a wider range of EEG datasets (e.g., DEAP, high gamma) (R1). Many popular EEG datasets (not specified) are absent (R2). The chosen datasets (sleep, emotion, motor imagery) are **not clinical**, despite the abstract claiming clinical relevance (R2). Multiple runs with random subject splits and averaged accuracy are needed given heterogeneity (R4).
- **Large performance gap**: Prior work on these datasets achieves >90% accuracy via supervised/transfer learning, while this study reports ~40% accuracy. The paper does not explain what factors account for this gap (R2). **Minority strong view (R2)**: This raises fundamental doubts about the approach’s effectiveness.
- **Memory and buffer details missing**: No detailed analysis of memory cost for DCB and CEA (R1). Need to specify: total number of samples stored per individual, how target domain samples are replaced in memory, and a comparison with standard memory sampling (e.g., reservoir sampling) and recent advanced methods for EEG inter-subject variability (R4).
- **Order of individuals**: How are individuals ordered during CL (by ID or attribute)? Could different orders affect performance substantially (R1)? The paper states “five same input orders” but it is unclear if orders are shared across methods or shuffled randomly; the averaging procedure for ACC and MF1 across incremental individuals and iterations is not clearly described (R3).
- **Missing related works**: Several recent works on continual learning for EEG are not cited ([1][2][3] in R1’s review) (R1). The related review is limited; individual differences are a well-known challenge addressed by prior supervised/transfer learning with robust features (R2). The novelty of “Unsupervised Individual Continual Learning (UICL)” as a concept is unclear – how is it different from combining unsupervised domain adaptation and continual learning, apart from defining an individual as a domain (R3)?
- **Presentation of method**: Figures (e.g., Fig. 2, 3, 5) need better labeling and more detailed explanation (R1, R3). The method section should explicitly mark the three stages (pseudo-label generation, model update, storage update) in Figure 2 for easier flow (R3). A modular formulation of related works (e.g., regularization vs. memory-based CL, classic vs. continual EEG decoding) is recommended (R1).
- **Pseudo-label generation and SSL threshold**: The mechanism for generating confidence values for pseudo-labels and deciding the threshold is unclear (R3). How is the threshold determined under inter-subject data heterogeneity? Could the previous model’s bias toward earlier subjects cause pseudo-label inaccuracies (R4)? In section 3.3.2, when the paper says it “utilize[s] the real labeled samples for replay rather than the previously preserved pseudo-labeled samples”, does this mean it uses ground-truth labels for those selected samples (R3)?
- **Cross Epoch Alignment (CEA) and KL penalty**: The role of CEA in managing within- and across-subject variations needs a more detailed explanation (R2). The KL-based penalty is applied only every second epoch – why not every epoch? Is there a specific parameter controlling its influence (R4)? The motivation for CEA is similar to EwC (R3).
- **Algorithm 1 inconsistency**: It mentions both *M_g* and *M_{i-1}*, but while using DCB and CEA, *M_g* is not used; instead *M_{i-1}* is used. Also the text mentions using CPC for domain adaptation – these need clarification (R3).
- **Data preparation details missing**: Epoch lengths, overlaps, CNN block sizes are not reported – these choices significantly affect evaluation and explainability (R3).
- **Stability definition ambiguity**: “Stability” as defined (generalization to unseen individuals) may mislead; how well does the model retain performance on the initial M0 training set (R3)?
- **Plasticity evaluation split**: How is plasticity of the incremental set evaluated? Is there a specific incremental train/test split (R4)?
- **Ablation results puzzling**: In Table 4 and Figure 5, base performance (AAA and AAF1) does not decline as individuals are added. Does the base model have replay? Needs clarification (R3).
