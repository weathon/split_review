## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Exact Orthogonal Initialization (EOI), a sparse orthogonal initialization scheme using random Givens rotations to achieve exact (not approximate) orthogonality at arbitrary densities. The method targets static sparse training, aiming to improve gradient signal propagation. Experiments on MLPs and CNNs (up to 1000 layers) and on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and mini-ImageNet are presented, with results compared to approximate sparse orthogonal initializations and existing static sparse training methods.

### Strengths

- The paper is well-written, clearly structured, and easy to follow (Reviewers 1,2,3).  
- The main idea (using Givens rotations for exact orthogonal sparse initialization) is straightforward, mathematically grounded, and addresses an important issue in sparse training (Reviewers 2,3).  
- The expected density formulation makes the method practical and appealing (Reviewer 3).  
- The experimental section positions EOI well relative to existing methods (Reviewer 3). The method is simple and has clear potential impact (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient comparisons and unclear advantage**: The experiments lack extensive comparisons with similar algorithms, and EOI’s advantage is not convincingly demonstrated (Reviewer 1). For example, Figure 3 raises questions about whether the AI method could replace EOI; innovation is unclear (Reviewer 1).  
- **Figure 5 interpretation**: It is unclear whether the time curves show that EOI significantly underperforms SAO as matrix size and density increase (Reviewer 1).  
- **Table 3 formatting**: The meaning of bold and underlined entries in Table 3 is not explained (Reviewer 1).  
- **Missing critical details for readability**: The paper is not fully self-contained. For instance, the SAO method is not explained sufficiently for a new reader (Reviewer 2). The indices \(ijkk\) on page 4 are unclear (Reviewers 1,2). A diagram showing how the orthogonal matrix \(H\) is embedded in a convolutional kernel of shape \(B \times C_{in} \times C_{out} \times H \times W\) would help (Reviewer 2). Delta orthogonalization assumes cyclic consistency for convolutional layers, but this assumption is not discussed (Reviewer 2).  
- **Clarity on sparse training setting**: It is unclear whether pruned connections (not updated during backward pass) are still used in the forward pass (Reviewer 2). The masks in GraSP/Synflow are adaptive over iterations, contradicting the static setting introduced by the authors (Reviewer 2).  
- **Construction of EOI for convolutional kernels**: The use of random entries in the mask to achieve desired density is not explained in detail. Is EOI guaranteed to be exactly orthogonal in this case as well? (Reviewer 2).  
- **Theory vs. empiricism**: Most results are empirical; the paper would benefit from theoretical analysis of Dynamical Isometry in the proposed setting. Currently, both the theory and the numerical aspects are incomplete (Reviewer 2).  
- **Table 1 ambiguity**: It is unclear which numbers belong to MNIST and which to CIFAR-10 (Reviewer 2).  
- **Parameter counts and fair comparison**: The paper should show exact parameter counts for each model to indicate what 10% of parameters means. A fair comparison would then use models of the same total size but different width/depth configurations (Reviewer 2).  
- **Limited experimental scope**: Only favourable experimental settings appear to be chosen. The authors are encouraged to consider the full ImageNet benchmark or a multi-task/meta-learning setting (Reviewer 2).  
- **Missing references**: Relevant prior work is omitted, e.g., Sun et al. (low-degree term first), Thukur et al. (incremental parameter selection), Larsson et al. (FractalNet), and Shulgin et al. (independent subnetwork training) (Reviewer 2).  
- **Link between sparse training and sparse initialization**: The connection is not clearly established. It is well known that a small subnet contributes most within a dense network, but this contribution differs across settings (e.g., multi-tasking, meta-learning). The claim that post-pruning (adaptive during training) performance is better than sparse-initialization-based training needs support (Reviewer 2).  
- **Role of exact orthogonality**: Orthogonal initialization (sparse or not) ensures signal propagation, not generalization. It is not clear how crucial *exact* orthogonality is unless the network is regularized (Reviewer 2).  
- **Missing analysis of sparsity–performance trade-off**: The reader would benefit from understanding the price (in performance) for different sparsity levels on mini-ImageNet (Reviewer 3). Additionally, are some rotation angles better than others? Can one guide the Givens dimensions towards better sparsity patterns? (Reviewer 3).  

*Contrast among reviewers*: While Reviewer 3 is slightly more positive (rating 6) and finds the positioning and practicality strong, Reviewers 1 and 2 (ratings 5) are more critical of the experimental comparisons, clarity, and theoretical depth.
