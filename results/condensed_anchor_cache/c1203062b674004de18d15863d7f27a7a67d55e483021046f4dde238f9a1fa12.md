## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an iterative red teaming framework that uses a smaller model (Llama3.1-8B) integrated with opponent modeling to generate adversarial prompts for testing LLM safety. It employs three strategies: direct prompting, mutation rules (sentence rearrangement, style transfer), and persuasion techniques (from 40 PAP techniques). The training includes three loss objectives: opponent loss (generate responses as opponent), topic-aware attack loss, and refine attack loss. The method achieves 74.95% attack success rate (ASR) on Llama2-7b-Chat and 69.10% on Llama3-8b-Instruct. The paper also provides parameter-level analysis suggesting red teaming abilities are densely embedded in parameters, unlike sparse safety alignment features.

### Strengths
- The main idea of iterative training with opponent modeling for small models (Llama3.1-8B) is novel and interesting. Experimental results (Table 3) clearly show that more iterations lead to higher ASR, that the trained model can surpass the ASR of the dataset used to train the generator, and that refinement further improves ASR (Reviewer 4).
- The framework effectively distills red teaming capabilities from larger models to smaller, more practical models (Reviewer 3), addressing the cost-inefficiency of relying on large models for jailbreak generation (Reviewer 4).
- The opponent modeling approach (modeling the target’s responses) is a new contribution to training attack modules, and results support its value for future red-teaming research (Reviewer 2).
- The parameter-level insights showing that red-teaming abilities are densely distributed across model parameters, distinct from sparsity in safety alignment features, are valuable and enhance understanding of safety-critical neurons (Reviewers 1 and 3).
- The framework preserves prompt diversity, addressing the common problem of repetitive adversarial prompts (Reviewer 1). (Note: but Reviewer 3 notes diversity collapse persists to some degree.)
- The motivation is clear: existing jailbreaking using LLMs is cost-inefficient; this work enables small models to generate red-teaming prompts (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with strong baselines**: The paper lacks comparison with state-of-the-art jailbreaking strategies such as GCG, TAP, PAIR, and AutoDAN (Reviewer 4). Additionally, relevant red-teaming baselines that provide better ASR both with and without opponent knowledge are not compared: rainbow teaming [R1], ruby teaming [R2], ferret [R3], wildteaming [R4], and EasyJailbreak [R5] (Reviewer 2). Without these, it is unclear how the proposed approach advances the field (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient ablation and understanding of loss objectives**: The training process is complex with multiple stages, and the effect of each loss objective is unclear (Reviewer 4). Specifically, Table 2 shows that using response as context does not significantly improve ASR, making the complex loss objectives seem unnecessary (Reviewer 4). Detailed ablation studies are requested (Reviewer 4). The methodology explanation lacks necessary conceptual and mathematical clarity, with insufficient formulas and step-by-step explanations, making reproduction difficult (Reviewer 1).
- **Diversity collapse persists**: Although opponent modeling helps, the problem of limited prompt diversity during training is not fully resolved (Reviewer 3). (Contrast: Reviewer 1 notes diversity is preserved, but this weakness from Reviewer 3 indicates the issue is not completely solved.)
- **Computational cost**: The approach requires multiple iterations and large-scale model training in the data generation phase, which could limit practical applicability in resource-constrained settings (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of analysis across model families**: The paper only evaluates on Llama2-7b-Chat and Llama3-8b-Instruct. Results across different families (e.g., Mistral, GPT, Claude) are missing (Reviewer 2).
- **Weak motivation and positioning relative to existing literature**: The three strategies (direct prompting, mutation, persuasion) exist in various forms in the jailbreak literature, so the paper should better highlight its contributions and significance (Reviewer 2).
- **Long-term effectiveness unanswered**: Whether language models might develop resistance to these attack strategies over time through further safety alignment training is not addressed (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient background and conceptual clarity**: The paper lacks a detailed introduction to red teaming, and some core concepts (e.g., blue-team, red-team in this context) are not well-defined (Reviewers 1 and 2). Specific issues: line 107 should define blue-team and red-team LLM; line 286 has an incomplete reference (“See in Section”); Equation (1) and Algorithm 1 are poorly explained (Reviewers 2 and 4).
- **Section 3.4 appears irrelevant**: One reviewer suggests moving Section 3.4 to the appendix and replacing it with more experimental results such as ablations and comparisons (Reviewer 4).

Note: Reviewers 1 and 2 gave lower scores (3) and were more critical, citing insufficient comparisons and methodological gaps. Reviewers 3 and 4 gave higher scores (6 and 5) and acknowledged the novelty of iterative training with opponent modeling, but still raised significant concerns about missing baselines and ablations. This contrast indicates that while the core idea is appreciated, the paper’s execution and validation are considered inadequate by the majority.
