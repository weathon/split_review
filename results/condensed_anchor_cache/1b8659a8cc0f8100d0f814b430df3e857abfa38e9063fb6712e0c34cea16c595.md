## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes PRM, a photometric stereo based large reconstruction model (LRM) that reconstructs high-quality meshes with fine-grained local details from sparse multi-view images. Unlike prior LRMs that rely on fixed lighting and simple materials during training, PRM renders photometric stereo images with varied materials (metallic, roughness) and lighting (environment maps) on the fly using a real-time physically-based rendering (PBR) pipeline with split-sum approximation. The model uses an explicit mesh representation with differentiable PBR, enabling supervision from diffuse and specular color maps. Evaluated on GSO and Omni3D, PRM outperforms baselines like InstantMesh. All reviewers note that the approach yields convincing results (especially Reviewer 2 at score 8), but concerns are raised about the necessity of online rendering, missing stronger baselines (e.g., Mesh-LRM), incomplete ablation studies, and insufficient clarification of key technical details.

### Strengths
- **Novel integration of photometric stereo into LRMs.** The paper improves upon the LRM line by incorporating PBR insights—training with sampled complex materials and lighting conditions—which enhances geometry estimation and generalization to diverse appearances (R1, R3).
- **Efficient online rendering via split-sum approximation.** Enables on-the-fly view synthesis and ground truth generation in a large parameter space of materials and lighting, significantly reducing data generation overhead (R1, R2, R3).
- **Explicit mesh representation with differentiable PBR.** Allows incorporation of additional priors such as depth and normal maps, and supports supervision from specular color for better geometry optimization (R1, R2).
- **Extensive evaluation and ablation.** Standard benchmarks (GSO, Omni3D) show large improvements over InstantMesh and other baselines; ablation studies validate the PBR-related losses and robustness to number of views and field of view (R1, R3). Reviewer 2 finds the quantitative results “very convincing” and the model’s ability to handle a wide variety of shapes, materials, and small numbers of input views impressive (R2, strength).
- **Practical applications.** Demonstrates relighting and material editing; handles specular surfaces well (R3).
- **Focus on training data diversity.** Unlike prior work, the method uses varied materials and lighting during training, an often overlooked factor affecting performance (R4 strength). Reviewer 2 notes that the model generates “fine geometric detail” and handles “a very wide variety of shapes, materials, lighting” (R2 strength).  
  *Note: Reviewer 2 (score 8) is substantially more positive than the other reviewers (scores 6, 6, 5), especially regarding the overall convincingness and performance.*

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation for online rendering unconvincing.** Reviewer 4 argues that offline rendering with random sampling of finite material/lighting combinations would be sufficient, and would use standard engines yielding better image quality than the split-sum approximation. The paper does not adequately justify why online rendering is strictly necessary (R4).
- **Missing comparisons with stronger baselines.** The paper does not compare with Mesh-LRM, which had an online demo available before the submission deadline (R4). Other baselines like InstantMesh are included but stronger recent models are omitted (R4).
- **Incomplete ablation studies.** Ablation results are only qualitative on 1–2 selected objects; quantitative tables are missing for the ablation conditions (R4). Reviewer 3 also notes that some failure cases (e.g., with lacking depth information) are not analyzed thoroughly (R3).
- **Clarification of PBR parameter estimation is needed.** It is unclear whether the model estimates only albedo, or also roughness and metallic maps. If only albedo is predicted, implications for downstream applications requiring full material properties are significant (R1). Moreover, sampled metallic/roughness are applied globally—if the original CAD model has spatially-varying (SV) maps, this strategy may diminish generation quality for real images with complex SV materials. The paper should justify why it does not predict ground truth BRDF (roughness, metallic) from models that already have PBR materials (R1).
- **Clarity and writing issues.** Several passages are vague or incorrect:  
  - “for what purposes?” (L015) (R1)  
  - PBR acronym not defined at first use (L020) (R1)  
  - The claim that previous methods depend on “fixed and simple lighting” is inaccurate because they also use PBR materials and envmap lighting (L050, L235) (R1)  
  - “functionalities” should be “downstream applications” (L186) (R1)  
  - “a richer set of equations” (L283) is unclear (R1)  
  - The split-sum approximation section (Section 3) is too brief; its necessity and assumptions are hard to follow (R2).
- **Computational considerations insufficiently discussed.** The overhead of online rendering compared to simpler offline or non-PBR approaches is not quantified (R3). Hyperparameter tuning is not discussed, and given the high training cost, it is unclear which hyperparameters matter most (R2 question, R3).  
  *Question from R2: How were hyperparameters tuned and which ones matter more?*
- **Material/lighting consistency probability threshold appears arbitrary.** The 50% probability for enforcing material/lighting consistency across views is not justified (R3 question).  
  *Question from R3: What is the rationale behind the 50% threshold?*
- **Sensitivity to multi-view diffusion model quality.** Results seem sensitive to the quality of the input multi-view diffusion model; failure cases with poor depth or lighting cues are not analyzed (R3).
- **Limited evaluation on complex lighting and materials.** Figure 9 shows only one qualitative scene; comparisons with baselines under complex lighting/material conditions are missing, and quantitative results are needed (R1).
- **Handling of real-world backgrounds.** It is unclear whether the model can process non-segmented real objects with natural backgrounds, or if it would reconstruct the background as part of the geometry (R2 question).  
  *Question from R2: Can the model run on multi-view images of non-segmented real objects with natural backgrounds? Will it reconstruct the background or ignore it?*
- **Ground truth rendering details not clarified.** It is ambiguous whether the split-sum approximation is used for both estimated and ground truth images, and whether ground truth images are rendered on-the-fly per batch. The computational implications of online ground truth rendering need clarification (R1).
- **Reviewer 4 (score 5) is notably more negative**, especially regarding the necessity of online rendering, missing strong baseline comparisons, and incomplete ablation. These concerns are not shared by Reviewer 2 (score 8), who finds the results “very convincing” despite the high training cost.
