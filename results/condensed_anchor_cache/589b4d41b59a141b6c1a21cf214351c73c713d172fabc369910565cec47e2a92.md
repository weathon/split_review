## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a theoretical analysis of masked autoencoders (MAE) via the information bottleneck (IB) principle. It proves that optimizing latent features to balance relevant and irrelevant information is key to improving MAE. Based on this, MI-MAE introduces two extra losses: maximize mutual information between latent features from orthogonal masks (retain relevant info) and minimize mutual information between latent features and input (remove irrelevant info). Experiments on ImageNet classification, COCO detection, and ADE20K segmentation show improvements over MAE, notably achieving comparable accuracy with 4× fewer pre-training epochs (400 vs. 1600).

### Strengths
- **Originality and theoretical framing**: This is the first paper to study MAE under information bottleneck, re-interpreting MAE as minimizing a Lagrangian that includes a simplest effective description and distortion. It provides detailed proofs linking MAE to IB and deriving how balancing relevant/irrelevant information can improve performance. (Reviewers 1,2,4)
- **Practical method derived from theory**: The two mutual-information-based losses (maximizing MI between latents of different masked views, minimizing MI between latents and input) are directly supported by the theoretical analysis and lead to a simple architecture. (Reviewers 1,3,4)
- **Empirical effectiveness across tasks**: MI-MAE outperforms MAE on image classification (ImageNet-1K: +0.5% fine-tuning with 400-epoch training vs. 1600-epoch MAE), object detection (COCO), and semantic segmentation (ADE20K). It also generalizes to other masked image modeling methods (e.g., SimMIM). Ablations over mask generation strategies, loss functions, and loss weights confirm the contribution of each component. (Reviewers 1,3,4)
- **Training efficiency**: MI-MAE achieves better results than the standard MAE while using 4× fewer pre-training epochs (400 vs. 1600). (Reviewer 4 – also noted by Reviewer 3 and implied by Reviewer 2’s numbers)
- **New insight for the community**: Interpreting MAE from an IB viewpoint provides useful understanding for developing more powerful self-supervised models. (Reviewer 2, supported by others)

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical clarity gaps**:
  - The explanation of why MAE can only find a suboptimal effective description (after Eq. 3) is missing. Specific constraints or formal proofs that prevent MAE from reaching the IB optimum are needed. (Reviewer 2)
  - The step from the bias term *r* (Eq. 4) to improving MAE is unclear. The exact effect of *r* on the upper bound, and how improving that bound benefits the original IB objective (Eq. 3), requires mathematical derivation. (Reviewer 2)
  - There is no clear theoretical link showing how the proposed losses directly improve Eq. (3). For example, why maximizing MI between latent features and *ζ* reduces *I(ẑ; X·m | r)* and how that affects the IB formulation, needs direct proofs or reasoning. (Reviewer 2)
  - Proofs for Eq. (12) and Eq. (14) are missing; the theorems used to derive these error bounds should be stated. (Reviewer 2)
- **Empirical improvements not substantial**: The performance gains, especially on ImageNet fine-tuning, are marginal. The assumptions underlying the method may be too idealized for significant practical gains. (Reviewers 1,2) Reviewer 2 adds that the empirical improvement is “unfortunately not substantial.”
- **Lack of thorough comparison with existing latent-space methods**: The paper does not adequately differentiate MI-MAE from VAEs, GANs, or diffusion models that also manipulate latent space (e.g., isolation, constraints). The practical implementation and unique aspects of MI-MAE relative to these methods are not clearly articulated. More baselines and case studies are needed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient ablation of the multi-mask setup**: For a fair comparison with MAE pre-trained for 400 epochs, the paper should evaluate (a) standard MAE, (b) MAE with 4 masks per image but without the information losses, and (c) full MI-MAE (with 4 masks and losses) to isolate the effect of the information losses versus the extra data augmentation. (Reviewer 4)
- **Unexplained behavior on linear probing / 1% fine-tuning**: MI-MAE shows larger gains on linear probing and 1% fine-tuning than on full fine-tuning (Table 1). The paper explains this only briefly; a deeper explanation from the information bottleneck perspective is needed, especially since this is not typical. (Reviewer 4)
- **Increased training complexity and parameter sensitivity**:
  - The two added losses introduce three weighting parameters (*λ₁, λ₂, λ₃*) that are empirically determined. The process of tuning these weights is not well-defined, and sensitivity across datasets/tasks is unclear. (Reviewer 3)
  - The minimization of MI between latents and input uses an approximation network (simple MLP) that may not capture the true complexity of the mutual information, introducing approximation error without analysis. MINE is known for high variance; alternative estimators should be considered. (Reviewers 2,3)
  - The method assumes the model can effectively minimize information distortion in intermediate layers without over-compressing relevant information, but provides no theoretical or empirical evidence to support this assumption. (Reviewer 3)
- **Lack of robustness analysis**: The information bottleneck principle often increases robustness, but MI-MAE is not tested on ImageNet-A/C or similar challenging validation sets. (Reviewer 3)
- **Notation inconsistency**: In Figure 1, the two proposed losses are denoted as *l_max_mi* and *l_min_mi* instead of following the format *ℒ_rec*—standardization is recommended. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minority reviewer note**: Reviewer 2 (score 8) is more positive about the originality and significance of the work than the other reviewers (all score 6). However, Reviewer 2 also raises the clarity and derivation issues listed above and agrees the empirical gains are not substantial.
