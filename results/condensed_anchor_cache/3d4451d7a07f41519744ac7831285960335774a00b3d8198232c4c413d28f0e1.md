## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Graph Filter-based Self-Attention (GFSA) to address the oversmoothing problem in Transformers. The authors reinterpret self-attention as a graph filter from a graph signal processing (GSP) perspective and redesign it using a trinomial-based graph filter that incorporates higher-order neighborhood information. Experiments across computer vision, natural language processing, graph-level tasks, speech recognition, and code classification are presented. The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and ambitious in its experimental scope. However, there is sharp disagreement among reviewers: two reviewers gave low ratings (1, 3) and raised fundamental concerns about theoretical grounding and experimental consistency, while two reviewers gave moderate ratings (5, 6) and found the idea promising and the experiments extensive.

### Strengths
- The problem of oversmoothing in Transformers is clearly stated and the motivation for a graph-filter approach is conceptually appealing (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Extensive experiments across diverse domains – vision (DeiT-S, etc.), NLP (BERT, RoBERTa), graph transformers, speech recognition, and code classification – demonstrate consistent performance improvements (R1, R3, R4).
- The paper includes empirical analyses of frequency response and cosine similarity across layers (Fig. 2a-b) to support the claim that GFSA mitigates oversmoothing (R1).
- Runtime and FLOPs are measured and presented in detail (R1).
- The presentation is clear, well-structured, and easy to follow, with generous supplementary material (R1, R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient theoretical foundation:** The paper does not provide a rigorous theory explaining why Transformers suffer from oversmoothing or how GFSA resolves it. The argument that oversmoothing occurs because self-attention is a weighted average is considered weak and vague (R2). The explanation of how the added term (Ā² − Ā) alleviates oversmoothing is unsupported; it is unclear why self-attention, which already allows all tokens to attend to each other, needs 2‑hop minus 1‑hop information (R2, R3 Q1).
- **Questionable derivations:** The first-order Taylor approximation in Eq. 5 is presented without justification, and Theorem 3.1 provides an error bound that depends on ∥Ā² − Ā∥, which itself is unbounded and renders the bound meaningless (R2).
- **Inappropriate use of graph signal processing:** The graph shift operator in directed graph convolution must be diagonalizable, but the attention matrix in GFSA does not necessarily satisfy this condition. Unless diagonalizability is proven, applying GSP theory as the theoretical basis is inappropriate (R4).
- **Experimental inconsistencies:** The claimed improvements are not consistently reflected in the figures and tables. For example, the 3.3% improvement for image classification and the 1.63% improvement for DeiT‑S + GFSA over DeiT‑S are not evident in Fig. 1 and Table 3; similarly the 6.23% improvement in natural language understanding is not observable when comparing Fig. 1 and Table 5 (R2). Only Table 5 provides uncertainty estimates; other experiments lack such reporting, and the use of fixed seeds does not fully address this concern (R1, R2).
- **Increased computational cost:** GFSA introduces additional computation and memory overhead due to the calculation of A² (or its approximation), which exacerbates the already high O(N²) complexity of standard self-attention. The paper does not adequately address the practical implications for long sequences or resource-constrained environments (R3, R4). Furthermore, only training time overhead is discussed; inference time comparisons are missing (R3 Q2).
- **Missing implementation details and ablations:** It is unclear whether the learnable coefficients w₀, w₁, wₖ share across layers/heads, how they relate to the residual connection in standard self-attention, and whether any constraints (e.g., positivity) are imposed. The integration of GFSA with pre-trained large language models – training from scratch versus fine-tuning – is not clarified (R3 Q1, Q3).
- **Clarity issues:** Several minor writing problems exist: missing capitalization of “Equation,” possible typo “U” instead of “V” after Eq. 3, unclear phrase “w_K is learned to negative value,” and an overly strong claim that ML computations are “doubling every few months” (R1).
- **Missed references:** The paper does not cite relevant prior works such as Graph Attention Networks (Velickovic et al., ICLR 2018), Diffusion Improves Graph Learning (Klicpera et al., NeurIPS 2019), and Multi-hop Attention Graph Neural Networks (Wang et al., IJCAI 2021) (R3).
- **Minor formatting suggestions:** Table 3 column width could be increased, “#Layers” should be plural, and section 4.4 title (“Graph Transformer”) should be plural to match the content (R1).
