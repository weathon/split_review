## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes incorporating ties into the Bradley-Terry (BT) model for LLM preference alignment, deriving the Tie-rank Oriented BT (TOBT) model and the TODO algorithm (a ternary extension of DPO). TODO uses a ternary ranking including "prefer," "tie," and "disprefer." Experiments on Mistral-7B and Llama 3-8B, fine-tuned on 20k instances from UltraFeedback with varying tie ratios, show TODO outperforming DPO in preference modeling (in-distribution and out-of-distribution) and on MT-Bench, Piqa, ARC-c, and MMLU. The paper provides derivations and analysis of the tie parameter α. One reviewer (score 8) is substantially more positive, finding the results convincing and the idea well-motivated; other reviewers (scores 5,5,6) raise concerns about baselines, theoretical justification, generalizability, and practical improvements.

### Strengths

- Novel extension of the Bradley-Terry model to explicitly incorporate ties via the TOBT model, enabling more nuanced preference representation.
- Well-motivated rationale: ties are common in human preference elicitation and become more frequent as models improve; binary models discard this information.
- The paper is clearly written; the derivation from BT to TOBT and the TODO loss is easy to follow and supported by theoretical grounding.
- Experiments are well-structured, with detailed hyperparameter settings (Appendix A.9) and evaluation on in-distribution and out-of-distribution preference datasets, plus downstream benchmarks (Piqa, ARC-c, MMLU, MT-Bench).
- Results provide solid evidence that TODO consistently outperforms DPO, even in binary (no-tie) settings.
- Raises awareness of modeling ties in preference optimization, a direction that has been underexplored in recent LLM alignment work.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited baselines**: Only DPO is compared. Important related methods are missing: KTO, IPO, RPO, PPO, SimPO, ODPO. Without these, it is hard to assess TODO’s relative advantages. Some reviewers specifically request comparison to KTO (which avoids pairwise data) and to methods that also modify the BT model (e.g., ODPO). The paper claims TODO is complementary to prior approaches but does not empirically demonstrate this.
- **Lack of comparison to prior generalized BT models**: The work does not cite or discuss earlier extensions of BT to multi-class or tie-aware settings (e.g., Rao & Kupper, 1967; Stern, 1992; and related works). A thorough discussion of how TODO differs from these prior formulations and why a new derivation is needed is missing.
- **Theoretical justification for the tie parameter α is weak**: The choice of α appears arbitrary. Appendix A.3 argues it balances loss values, but there is no sensitivity study showing how different α-values affect gradients, training stability, or final performance. A deeper probabilistic comparison between TOBT and original BT is absent.
- **Limited datasets and generalizability**: All experiments use the UltraFeedback dataset (with only 20k training instances). The paper does not test on other preference datasets (e.g., different languages, domains, or tasks where tie patterns may differ). Larger-scale experiments (full UltraFeedback) and a second dataset are needed to verify generalizability.
- **Marginal improvements on reasoning tasks**: Gains on Piqa, ARC-c, MMLU are small (Tables 2 and 3), raising questions about the practical significance of TODO for reasoning-heavy tasks. One reviewer specifically notes that the benefits may not be universal across all LLM tasks.
- **No treatment of annotation noise**: Ties can arise from annotator uncertainty, ambiguity, or inconsistency. The method treats ties as equally informative as clear preferences, potentially incorporating noise. The paper does not discuss weighting ties by annotator confidence, modeling uncertainty, or data-quality strategies.
- **Missing ablation on tie-data ratio and dataset size**: The paper does not investigate how the natural or chosen tie ratio affects performance, nor does it compare TODO vs. DPO when ties are simply filtered out (a question raised by a reviewer). Practitioners lack guidance on optimal tie ratio for a given task.
- **Unsupported claim about robustness to noise**: The paper states TODO exhibits better robustness against noise in binary preference data, but no experiment with artificially added noise is conducted. Ties are not necessarily noise, so this claim is not empirically justified (as noted by one reviewer).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity not explored**: While hyperparameters are detailed (Appendix A.9), there is no analysis of how variation in these parameters changes results.
- **Potential trade-offs**: The impact of incorporating ties on computational complexity or convergence is not discussed. Questions remain about integrating TODO with RLHF or reward model training (only DPO is used as the base, and the method is not decoupled from DPO).
