## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces HyCoCLIP, a hyperbolic vision-language model that leverages hierarchical compositions of images, object bounding boxes, and their textual descriptions via contrastive and entailment losses. The automatically generated object-level supervision is used to train on 20.5M image-text pairs from GRIT. Evaluations on zero-shot classification, retrieval, detection, and scene understanding show consistent improvements over CLIP and the hyperbolic model MERU.

### Strengths
- The central idea is clever and novel: using the hierarchical nature of nouns in captions (obtained via an automatic procedure with GLIP grounding) as supervision for a hyperbolic model.
- The method is simple, elegant, and easily applied to large-scale pretraining; the procedure to automatically generate paired image and text boxes is straightforward.
- The paper is well-organized and clearly written; the motivation and method are easy to follow and well-illustrated.
- Extensive experiments demonstrate strong empirical performance across multiple tasks (zero-shot classification, retrieval, detection, scene understanding) and achieve state-of-the-art results among contrastive-pretrained models.
- Table 1 shows that adding the same image-text box data to standard CLIP does not improve performance, whereas the proposed hierarchical losses yield significant gains, highlighting the effectiveness of the compositional learning approach.

### Weaknesses
- The work is incremental: hyperbolic embeddings for hierarchies have been explored before, and the paper builds on established ideas. A more thorough discussion of how the proposed contrastive and entailment losses differ from standard hyperbolic contrastive losses and specifically enforce hierarchy is needed.
- Missing comparisons: the paper should compare with recently proposed vision-language models (beyond CLIP and MERU), other hyperbolic models that capture hierarchical relationships, and pretrained Euclidean VLMs known to represent hierarchies [citation needed].
- Hyperparameter sensitivity: the choice of hyperbolic space parameters (e.g., curvature) should be explored to assess robustness.
- The suggested controlled experiment is missing: split GRIT into two 10M subsets and compare (1) CLIP on 10M pairs, (2) CLIP on 10M pairs + boxes, (3) HyCoCLIP on 10M pairs + boxes, (4) CLIP on 20M pairs – to isolate whether the boxes provide new information or the losses alone matter.
- Results on other datasets (e.g., RedCaps) are not provided.
- Qualitative results are limited: there is no qualitative comparison to CLIP or MERU to show whether HyCoCLIP’s embedding space represents hierarchies in a more qualitatively satisfying way.
- Potential bias: using object boxes as supervision may bias the model toward nouns and concrete concepts, possibly at the expense of attributes, dynamic actions (verbs), etc.
- Some presentation details: in Tables 1/2, bolding the best overall performance across different backbones (ViT-S/16, ViT-B/16) is less informative than bolding the best per backbone group.
- Experimental design: the batch size of 768 was used due to memory limitations; gradient accumulation could have simulated larger batch sizes, which are known to affect contrastive learning.
- Several details about the automatic grounding procedure are unclear: how were abstract nouns filtered? Are the grounded nouns open-vocabulary or from a fixed list? What is the accuracy of the GLIP-based grounding?
