## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies stream-based and pool-based active learning with neural network approximations, building on bandit-based approaches (Wang et al. 2021). It proposes two algorithms (NeurOnAL-S and NeurOnAL-P) that use exploitation and exploration neural networks taking the original instance as input and outputting predicted probabilities for all K classes simultaneously, avoiding transformation to DK-long vectors. This makes inference K times faster and reduces input dimensionality. Theoretical guarantees are provided using the neural tangent kernel (NTK) framework, showing slower error-growth rate concerning K and improvement over Wang et al. by up to O(md) (network width m, input dimension d). Experiments on UCI datasets and FashionMNIST compare NeurOnAL-S/P against I-NeurAL, ALPS, BADGE, ALBL, CoreSet, DynamicAL, consistently outperforming in test accuracy and running time. Reviewer 2 rated the paper higher (8) than the others (6), praising the substantial theoretical and empirical improvements. Other reviewers found the contribution fair and had concerns about novelty, theoretical scope, and experimental design.

### Strengths
- Intuitive and theoretically sound. (R1)
- Introduces exploitation/exploration networks that take original instance as input and output probabilities for K classes, eliminating the need to transform into DK vectors. (R1)
- Provides theoretical performance guarantees with slower error-growth rate as K increases. (R1)
- Achieves optimal active learning rate under different noise conditions. (R1)
- Theoretical bounds improve over Wang et al. 2021 by a factor of O(md) (network width m, input dimension d). (R2)
- Empirical wins on increased test accuracy and decreased running time for both stream and pool settings, with significant improvement over multiple prior approaches (I-NeurAL, ALPS, BADGE, ALBL, CoreSet, DynamicAL). (R2)
- The idea of reducing dimensionality to d is novel compared to the literature starting from [10]. (R3)
- Proofs appear concrete. (R3)
- Experiments are extensive and detailed, validating performance and computational efficiency. (R3)
- The paper is generally well-written and easy to follow. (R3, R4)
- The algorithms are of interest; the input is more coherent and performance is strong. (R3)
- Studies an important and interesting problem. (R4)
- The algorithms have some novelty. (R4)
- Checked parts of the proof and they appear correct. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- The core theoretical difference from existing methods that transform instances into DK vectors is not clearly explained. (R1)
- Demonstrated only with a specific MLP structure; no discussion of generality to arbitrary network structures, nor how bounds would change. (R2)
- Bounds are in terms of quantities (S and L_H) that are difficult for practitioners to quantify or trade off to select network architecture. (R2)
- It is not stated which subsets of data were used for hyperparameter tuning; there is a risk that hyperparameters were tuned to give good test performance. (R2)
- For the pool-based setting, why cumulative population regret is chosen over simple regret is not justified; pool-based learning typically cares only about the last model. (R2)
- The exploration network uses gradients of the exploitation network as inputs; there is no theoretical explanation (beyond intuition) justifying why this helps fit the noise. (R2)
- The statement in Section 5, \(h(x_t)[k] = \langle \nabla f(x_t, \theta^*)[k], \theta^* - \theta_1 \rangle\), is not clearly an assumption or a guarantee; it is unclear if the same \(\theta^*\) exists for all \(t\) and \(k\). (R2)
- The main originality appears to be the theoretical proofs rather than the network structures, but the proofs heavily depend on NTK techniques. (R3)
- Experiments were conducted only five times, which may impact reliability; more runs would be appreciated. (R3; the authors cite computational constraints as a possible reason)
- Theoretical results are only for the Tsybakov noise parameter \(\alpha = 0\), making comparisons to existing literature less direct (except indirectly with [48] as \(\alpha \to \infty\)). (R3; a reviewer also asked what difficulty prevents analyzing other \(\alpha\) settings and what influence different \(\alpha\) would have)
- A minor concern: the paper could better explain how its decision to observe a label differs from the bandit-based algorithms. (R4)
