## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Non-Stationary Natural Actor-Critic (NS-NAC), the first model-free policy gradient algorithm for infinite-horizon average-reward RL in non-stationary MDPs (time-varying rewards and transitions with variation budget Δ_T). It derives a dynamic regret bound of \(\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(|\mathcal{S}|^{1/2}|\mathcal{A}|^{1/2}\Delta_T^{1/9}T^{8/9})\). Reviewers acknowledge the theoretical step (regret analysis, Lyapunov methods, auxiliary Markov chain) but raise serious concerns: a fundamental technical error that likely invalidates the analysis (Assumption 2 fails in tabular setting), suboptimal regret even in stationary case (\(T^{8/9}\) vs. known \(\sqrt{T}\)), questionable regret definition, strong impractical assumptions, limited novelty of the algorithm (standard NAC with a two‑timescale schedule), and weak experiments. One reviewer (score 8) is significantly more positive about the theoretical contribution, despite identifying the same critical flaw.

### Strengths
- **First policy‑gradient method for non‑stationary average‑reward RL.** This fills an important gap; no previous model‑free policy‑based algorithm had a theoretical guarantee in this setting. (R1, R2, R4, R5, R6, R7)
- **Theoretical analysis is non‑trivial.** Extends the convergence analysis of natural actor‑critic (Wu et al. 2020, Khodadadian et al. 2022) to non‑stationary environments, using Lyapunov functions and an auxiliary Markov chain to handle time‑varying dynamics. (R2, R3, R5, R7)
- **Regret bound has a better Δ_T dependence than existing value‑based methods** in Table 1 (e.g., Δ_T^{1/9} vs. Δ_T^{1/3}). (R2)
- **Ablation study and proof sketches** provide empirical support and clarity for the main theoretical claims. (R3, R5)
- **Entropy regularization** is shown to remove the strong eigenvalue assumption (Assumption 2). (R6)
- **Well‑written and clear**, with a comprehensive literature overview and intuitive explanations. (R3, R4, R5, R7)

### Weaknesses
- **Critical technical error: Assumption 2 cannot be satisfied in the tabular setting.** In the tabular critic, the matrix \(\bar{A}\) always has row sums zero (by construction of \(A(O_t)\) and linearity of expectation), so it is singular and cannot have largest eigenvalue \(-\lambda\) with \(\lambda>0\). This invalidates the existence/uniqueness of the solution that the analysis relies on. (R3)  
  *Minority view:* One reviewer (score 8) considers the paper a significant theoretical contribution despite this error, but still recommends rejection because of it. (R3)
- **Regret bound is suboptimal even in the stationary case (\(\Delta_T=0\)).** The bound is \(\tilde{O}(T^{8/9})\), far worse than the known \(\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})\) for stationary average‑reward RL. When \(\Delta_T\) is linear in \(T\), the regret becomes linear. (R1, R2, R5, R7)  
  The dependence on \(\Delta_T\) is \(\Delta_T^{1/9}\), which is smaller than the lower bound \(\Delta_T^{1/3}\) (Theorem 2); the paper does not explain this discrepancy. (R5)
- **Regret definition is questionable.** The optimal policy \(\pi_t^*\) is defined with respect to the stationary distribution of environment \(M_t\), but at time \(t\) the agent is not necessarily in that distribution. The expected reward under \(\pi_t^*\) could be lower than what another policy would achieve, making the regret potentially negative and not a true measure of performance loss. (R1, R4)  
  Relatedly, the paper assumes that the optimal policy of the non‑stationary MDP is equivalent to the per‑time optimal policies \(\pi_t^*\); this equivalence does not hold in general. (R4)
- **Strong, impractical assumptions.**
  - *Uniform ergodicity* (Assumption 1) is not justified and essentially removes the need for exploration, weakening the contribution compared to value‑based methods that explicitly address exploration. (R1)
  - *Assumption 2* (largest eigenvalue negative) is known not to hold in any average‑reward MDP because the all‑one vector can be added to any solution. (R7) The paper’s claim that the projection step “ensures a unique solution” is incorrect; projection ensures boundedness, not existence/unicity. (R7) The entropy regularization approach is unnecessary if one adopts the subspace argument of Zhang et al. 2021. (R7)
  - No examples or verification are given for Assumption 1. (R4)
- **Algorithmic novelty is weak.** NS‑NAC is essentially the standard natural actor‑critic (Khodadadian et al. 2022) with a two‑timescale learning rate schedule derived from the perturbation budget. The “new algorithm” is more accurately a traditional NAC with a specialized schedule for non‑stationary environments. (R3, R5, R7) The paper does not clarify what other differences exist beyond the learning rate schedule. (R3)
- **Dependence on unknown variation budgets.** The learning rates \(\beta_t,\gamma_t\) and the perturbation budget \(\epsilon\) depend on \(\Delta_{R,T},\Delta_{P,T}\) (or \(\Delta_T\)), which are assumed known in advance but are typically unavailable in practice. No practical guidance is given on how to choose them without this knowledge. (R2, R4, R5)
- **Experimental evaluation is weak.**
  - The synthetic environments are simple and the results are only “comparative” (R2), not convincingly superior. (R2)
  - The switching times between MDPs are uniformly distributed over \(T\), which is a best‑case scenario because the fixed learning rate schedule can be tuned to those predictable switches. Experiments with random switching times are missing. (R3)
  - Uncertainty measures are inconsistent (shaded regions vs. error bars). (R3)
  - Figure 1(c) does not show the advantage predicted by Table 1 for large \(\Delta_T\). (R2)
- **Limited to tabular setting, no function approximation.** While function‑approximation analyses for AC and NAC are mature, the paper does not discuss how to extend NS‑NAC to large or continuous state spaces, severely limiting practical applicability. (R1)
- **Technical concerns and missing clarifications.**
  - Algorithm 1’s input includes an exploration parameter that is never used in the pseudocode (likely intended for \(\epsilon\)-greedy selection). (R2, R3)
  - The Bellman equation for the value function should index the policy \(\pi\) by \(t\) to reflect non‑stationarity. (R3)
  - The entropy regularization section uses the same notation for un‑regularized and regularized rewards, causing confusion. (R6)
  - The parameter configuration in Theorem 1 is not clearly optimal; e.g., \(N\) should be chosen so that \(N/\beta\) and \(\Delta_T T/N\) have the same order, but the current configuration does not satisfy this. (R6)
- **Inferior regret compared to value‑based state‑of‑the‑art.** The bound is much worse in the \(T\) dependence than the best value‑based algorithms (e.g., Mao et al. 2024, which achieves \(\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})\) regret). The paper does not explain why the same order cannot be attained—whether the gap stems from Q‑learning vs. policy gradient, active restarting vs. passive forgetting, or episodic vs. infinite‑horizon. (R7)
- **Limited technical innovation in the analysis.** The most challenging component—handling samples from a time‑varying Markov chain—is standard when only policies change; handling changes in \(P\) by bounding TV distance via the shift in \(P\) is a straightforward extension. The authors should clearly state what the key technical challenge is and which novel analytical techniques address it. (R7)
- **The remark that “\(N\) is an artifact of the proof” should be moved to the main paper** after Theorem 1. If the environment change frequency is roughly known, there may be a better way to choose \(N\) to improve the bound. (R7)
- **Typo:** “lest” in line 241. (R5)
- **Missing question from reviewer 3:** Is there a regret result that depends independently on \(\Delta_{R,T}\) and \(\Delta_{P,T}\) rather than just their sum \(\Delta_T\)? (R3)
