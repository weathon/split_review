## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper examines seven recently proposed concept erasure methods for text-to-image models (Stable Diffusion 1.4). It demonstrates that all seven methods fail to fully excise targeted concepts by introducing a “Concept Inversion” (CI) attack: learning special word embeddings (based on Textual Inversion) that can retrieve the supposedly erased concepts without altering model weights. The results call into question the robustness of post hoc concept erasure and suggest they amount to input filtering rather than true removal.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an important and under-explored problem: the failure cases of concept erasure methods in text-to-image generation (R1, R5).
- It is clearly written, well-structured, and easy to follow (R2, R3, R4, R5). Many experiments are conducted, covering both qualitative examples and quantitative metrics, and subjective evaluations from volunteers are included (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5).
- The authors provide thorough background, clear explanations of each of the seven erasure methods, and adaptive CI attacks tailored to each (R4). The inclusion of preliminary experiments on Text Inversion to show it does not introduce additional knowledge is appreciated (R3).
- Detailed appendix materials (e.g., Appendix Figure 9, Appendix B.5) and the failure explanation for SLD-Max add depth (R1, R4). Code is also provided (R2).
- The motivation and storyline are novel; existing work rarely explores whether erasures are truly effective (R3, R5). Diverse experimental settings covering four attack scenarios make the results convincing (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Only one model version is tested:** All experiments use Stable Diffusion 1.4. The authors do not justify this choice or discuss whether results would generalize to later versions (e.g., SD 2.x, SDXL) (R1). This limits the evaluation’s scope.
- **The paper does not propose fixes or detailed mitigation strategies** for the broken erasure methods; a deeper discussion on how to make erasures robust is missing (R1).
- **White-box evaluation of black-box methods:** One reviewer (R2) notes that the paper evaluates black-box erasure methods (e.g., Kumari et al. [1]) using a white-box CI attack—yet those methods already acknowledged their limitation in a white-box setting. It is unclear why an adversary with full white-box access (able to add tokens, read weights) would attack a model’s black-box erasure instead of directly altering the weights (R2, R3, R4).
- **Strong and unrealistic assumptions:** The CI attack assumes the adversary can add new tokens to the model’s dictionary (Assumptions 1 and 2 in PRELIMINARIES). This is unrealistic for typical MLaaS platforms (e.g., Stability AI, DALL-E 3) that do not accept text‑embedding inputs (R2, R4). The proposed TRANSFERABILITY method in Section 4.4 also relies on modifying the internal dictionary, which is infeasible in black-box deployments (R4). Assumption 5 (adversary with computational resources cannot alter weights) is inconsistent with a white-box setting (R2); a more practical adversarial model would be helpful.
- **Limited novelty of the attack method:** The CI approach is a straightforward application of Textual Inversion (Gal et al., 2023). It lacks algorithmic innovation; a more sophisticated attack (e.g., adversarial training, latent space manipulation) would strengthen the contribution (R5).
- **Evaluation scale and metrics are insufficient:** The paper shows only a limited number of image examples, not large‑scale dataset evaluations (R5). The absence of quantitative metrics (e.g., over many concepts) makes it difficult to assess generalizability and practical significance (R5). A more rigorous evaluation on diverse concepts and modalities is needed.
- **Missing comparisons and clarifications from the text:** The learned concept placeholder embedding is not compared to the embedding of the original concept name (R3). Section 4.4 is confusing: it is unclear how “remapping the concept in token space” works when the text encoder and original tokens are not updated; the left panel of Figure 7 does not clearly demonstrate editability as claimed; and the caption of Figure 7 has a typo inconsistent with the figure ordering (R3). These issues reduce clarity.
