## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces an end-to-end smooth (C<sup>∞</sup>) implicit neural representation (INR) framework for real-time rendering of surfaces. It learns a multiscale signed distance function (SDF) from oriented and colored point clouds using nested neighborhoods, a coarse-to-fine hierarchy, and residual training. Rendering is performed via multiscale sphere tracing without mesh extraction. Attributes (normals, colors) are mapped from a higher-detail level to the rendered surface via orthogonal projection. A GEMM-based normal computation is also proposed. Results are shown on classic synthetic objects (Armadillo, Buddha, Bunny) and compared to IDF and NGLOD. Applications include surface morphing and accelerating marching cubes.

### Strengths
* The multiscale SDF representation and learning framework (loss formulation, nested neighborhoods, attribute mapping) are novel, sound, and well-grounded (Reviewer 1).
* Nested implicit functions provide a natural way to achieve multiple scales of detail; mapping attributes from a finer implicit than the rendered surface increases detail while keeping sphere-tracing time short (Reviewer 2).
* The method generalizes sphere tracing to multiscale, enabling real-time rendering of textured 3D models without surface extraction, and is several times more efficient than the state of the art (Reviewer 3).
* High-fidelity textures/colors are rendered; visual results look good (Reviewers 2, 3).
* The GEMM-based normal computation is faster than PyTorch autograd (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
* The multi-level network approach accelerates sphere tracing (Reviewer 4).
* Orthogonal projection effectively transfers visual details (normals, colors) to coarser representations (Reviewer 4).
* Useful applications are demonstrated, including surface morphing and speeding up marching cubes (Reviewer 2).
* The paper discusses many aspects of surface implicit rendering (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
* **Motivation and purpose unclear (Reviewer 4, echoed by Reviewer 1):**  
  - The abstract motivation for discarding meshes is not fully convincing. Extracting meshes is not real-time, but learning SDFs is also not real-time; a comparison to explicit meshing in terms of rendering quality and performance is missing (Reviewers 1, 4).  
  - The claim that meshes are “not continuous” is misleading; the authors should clarify that they refer to differentiability, not continuity (Reviewer 4).  
  - The motivation to “fully use the smoothness of INRs” is not quantified or justified. Ironically, the method’s limitation is a “lack of sharp features.” Specific applications requiring high-order derivatives of the surface should be provided (Reviewer 4).  
  - A minority reviewer (score 3) found the overall purpose and practical use cases (e.g., real-time rendering vs. mesh rendering, handling of noisy real data) not clearly defined, and the contributions insufficiently novel or well-explained (Reviewer 4).

* **Writing and presentation issues (all reviewers):**  
  - The paper is generally well-written but could be clearer; Section 3.1 (Overview) and Section 3.5 need better explanation. The abstract and introduction use jargon (e.g., “localised SDF training based on nested neighborhoods”) without upfront definitions (Reviewers 1, 4).  
  - The layout is busy; references to figures and tables in the text are missing (Reviewer 2). Tables (e.g., Table 5) are misplaced (Reviewer 4).  
  - Delayed acronym definitions: GEMM is used on line 53 but defined on line 80 (Reviewer 4); “Sphere Tracing (ST)” should be spelled out earlier (Reviewer 2).  
  - Terminology: “textures” should be “colors” to avoid confusion with traditional texture mapping (Reviewer 2).  
  - Figures 2 and 3 have inconsistencies (e.g., q1 not on S1; “using too large d1 == d2” should be “too large d1 or d2”), and Figures 7 and 9 are confusing (e.g., missing shaded surfaces in Fig. 7, left vs. right side of Armadillo in Fig. 9) (Reviewer 2).  
  - Line 153: “compute it first intersection point q2” should be fixed to “compute its S2 intersection point q2” (Reviewer 2).

* **Technical issues and limitations (Reviewers 3, 4):**  
  - Using multiple neural SDFs increases training time and storage space (linearly with the number of residual levels). The mesh extraction process becomes computationally expensive. Residual SDFs can be unstable, causing oscillations or artifacts, especially in high-curvature regions (Reviewer 3).  
  - The parameters θ<sub>i</sub> are sensitive: too small leads to premature termination of sphere tracing (inaccurate intersections), too large increases computational cost. Global θ<sub>i</sub> may not be optimal; location-varying θ<sub>i</sub> could adapt but adds overhead (Reviewer 3).  
  - Geometric accuracy of the rendered surface is not sufficiently evaluated (Reviewer 3).  
  - The method is limited to smooth surfaces and cannot represent sharp edges, which is a practical limitation for many real-world objects (Reviewer 1).

* **Novelty and comparisons (Reviewers 1, 4):**  
  - Relevant prior work, such as Deep Marching Tetrahedra (Shen et al.) and follow-ups, is not discussed or compared (Reviewer 1).  
  - The acceleration of marching cubes (Sec. 4.5) is already established in existing literature (e.g., BACON, Lindell et al.) and should be acknowledged as not novel (Reviewer 4).  
  - Sec. 4.5 presents interpolation between genus 0 and genus 1 shapes as a demonstration of differentiability, but this is pure interpolation, not driven by gradient descent; the claim should be corrected (Reviewer 4).

* **Experimental scope (all reviewers):**  
  - Experiments are limited to a small number of simple synthetic objects (Armadillo, Buddha, Bunny). No experiments on complex real-world datasets (e.g., ShapeNet, Objaverse) or real noisy RGB point clouds (Reviewers 1, 4).  
  - Applicability to image-based surface representation learning (e.g., VolSDF) is not explored (Reviewer 1).  
  - The paper does not demonstrate robustness to noise or varying point densities (Reviewer 4).

* **GEMM normal computation (Reviewer 4):**  
  - The motivation for the GEMM-based acceleration is unclear: how much would frame rate drop without it? The explanation of why it is faster than PyTorch autograd (fewer FLOP? fused operations?) is missing. This aspect is under-explained and seems arbitrary without quantifying the bottleneck.

* **Minor issues:**  
  - Notation in Section 4: “(N, d) mean MLP with d hidden layers” is confusing because of the added +1 (Reviewer 2).  
  - A reference to “Local Deep Implicit Functions” (Genova et al.) could be added as an example of using multiple smaller implicit representations (Reviewer 2).
