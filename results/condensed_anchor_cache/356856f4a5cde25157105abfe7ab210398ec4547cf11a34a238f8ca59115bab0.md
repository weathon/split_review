## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes an uncertainty-aware in-context learning framework that fine-tunes LLMs to adaptively adjust responses (correct or refuse) based on uncertainty. Experiments on the SciQ dataset show improvements over the base model and its fine-tuned variant. The authors conclude that logit output values partially reflect uncertainty and that finetuning on a calibration dataset improves accuracy.

### Strengths

- The problem of improving LLM reliability is important.
- The proposed method is simple, interesting, and easy to understand.
- The paper presents evidence for two conclusions: (1) logit output values partially reflect inherent uncertainty; (2) fine-tuning on the calibration dataset improves response accuracy.
- Incorporating uncertainty yields significant improvements compared to the base LLM and its fine-tuned variant (Reviewer 2 is more positive than Reviewer 1 on this point; Reviewer 1 notes only within-framework comparisons).
- The framework's effectiveness is validated on the SciQ dataset.

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of comparison to other uncertainty estimation methods / baselines:** The evaluation only compares variants of the proposed framework (e.g., different uncertainty score thresholds or range settings). It does not compare against existing uncertainty estimation methods (e.g., [Xiong et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2022] cited by R1, or methods from Kuhn et al. and Quach et al. cited by R3) or state-of-the-art methods on the SciQ dataset. This limits assessment of the framework's relative performance.
- **Only one dataset (SciQ) used:** The generalization of the approach is unclear; experiments should be extended to additional datasets (R3).
- **Unclear discrepancy between fine-tuning and inference:** The model never sees the uncertainty score during supervised fine-tuning (SFT) but is expected to utilize it at inference. This discrepancy is not intuitive and is not supported by empirical results (R1).
- **Incorrect claim about multi-inference uncertainty estimation:** The statement that "multi-inference-based uncertainty estimation methods need to get the answer set before calculating the uncertainty" is wrong. Methods like semantic uncertainty [Kuhn et al., 2023] do not require the gold answer set (R1).
- **Calibration dataset construction unclear:** The procedure for creating the calibration dataset (Section 2.3.1) is insufficiently explained. The choice of uncertainty ranges (1–10, 1–100, 1–1000) appears arbitrary and lacks empirical or theoretical justification (R3).
- **Granularity of uncertainty scores not discussed:** The impact of uncertainty score granularity on performance is mentioned but not presented in the final paper (R2).
- **Missing references:** Several relevant works on LLM uncertainty are omitted, e.g., Lin et al. (2022), Kuhn et al. (2023), Quach et al. (2023) (R3). [R1 also notes missing comparison works: [1],[2] but those are listed in the review as baselines.]
- **Presentation issues:** The paper is difficult to read and would benefit from additional revision (R2). Typos and grammatical mistakes exist, e.g., "We will elaborated this part" (R3). Abbreviations are used before definition, e.g., "SFT" in Figure 1 (R2).
- **Insufficient experiment details:** The only baseline is vanilla fine-tuning; comparison with methods like those in [1] is missing (R3).
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms/missing details:** 
  - Why is the temperature set to 0.001 in Section 3.1? (R3)
  - Could additional insights be provided on why the calibration dataset is constructed that way? (R3; overlaps with unclear construction)
  - Would it be possible to expand the scope of datasets? (R3; overlaps with single-dataset limitation)
