## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LumiSculpt, a plug-and-play lighting control module for text-to-video (T2V) generation, built on a DiT backbone. It uses a synthetic dataset, LumiHuman (65 identities, frontal illuminations, Unreal Engine), and a dual-branch framework with a disentanglement loss to control lighting intensity, direction, and trajectory. Reviewers agree the task is challenging and the dataset has potential, but raise major concerns about limited novelty, synthetic data generalization, missing video-specific design, insufficient evaluation, and clarity of contributions.

### Strengths
- Precise lighting control (intensity, position, trajectory) for portrait video generation, with impressive cast shadows (R1, R3).
- Plug-and-play module design allows flexible combination with other foundation models (R1).
- LumiHuman dataset construction is valuable: placing point lights close together enables composing frames into videos; the pipeline could benefit the community (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Novel model design aspects: lighting embedding and a style-transfer-based disentanglement loss in latent space to control lighting while preserving appearance (R2).
- Results provide interesting insights about the internal lighting prior of recent diffusion models (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Dataset limitations and generalization** (R1, R2, R3, R4):
  - Synthetic data from only 65 identities with frontal-only lighting; diversity and real-world generalization are questionable – no evaluation on real-world portraits or scenes (R1, R3, R4).
  - No comparison showing that real light-stage datasets cannot provide sufficient supervision (R3).
  - Realism suffers: results appear fake, with unrealistic facial texture and oversaturation; only white/black lighting (no colored lighting) demonstrated (R4). Over-exposure in some generated videos raises robustness concerns (R1).
- **Novelty and model design** (R2, R3, R4):
  - Key difference from ControlNet is unclear beyond being a video version; the dual-branch design and its effectiveness are not well justified (R3, R4).
  - The disentanglement loss is poorly motivated: latents contain geometry, material, and lighting features, not just appearance (R3).
  - No specific video-oriented design – the algorithm merely extends image lighting control to video; temporal lighting consistency via 3D attention is mentioned but not explained (R2, R3).
  - Lighting intensity control mechanism is not clearly described (R4).
  - Main contribution is ambiguous: title/abstract emphasize LumiSculpt, but introduction highlights LumiHuman as the key contribution (R3).
- **Evaluation and baselines** (R1, R3, R4):
  - Generated videos lack motion dynamics; results are nearly static – no demonstration of complex real-world scenarios with dynamic lighting and motion (R1, R3).
  - Insufficient baseline comparisons: since videos are static, T2I lighting methods (e.g., LightIt) should be compared; IC-Light shows much better photorealism (R3, R4). Why does LumiSculpt output look similar to IC-Light but not to the base model Open-Sora? (R3)
  - Lack of diversity in generated samples under same conditioning (R3).
  - Lighting trajectory inconsistency in Fig. 1 (light moves from bottom to top right instead of purely upwards as depicted) (R3).
- **Presentation and clarity** (R2, R3, R4):
  - Notation in pipeline figure is unclear (e.g., “?” parameters) (R3).
  - Typos: L.204 “using a unreal engine”, L.290 “an naive” (R3).
  - Lighting representation (flat shading map) is imprecise (R3).
  - Title is too broad – the work focuses only on human portraits (R3).
  - Caption augmentation description is unclear: is it just replacing captions or also modifying backgrounds? How are lighting captions generated? No lighting-related captions shown in results (R2, R3, R4).
  - Uncertainty about dataset being open-sourced, which would affect reproducibility (R2, R4).
- **Questions overlapping with criticisms**:
  - How does the synthetic training data affect real-world generalization? (R1, R2)
  - Why oversaturation? (R3)
  - Are augmented captions just used as noise to avoid overfitting? (R3)

Note: Reviewer 2 is more positive (rating 6, confidence 4) and explicitly values the model design and dataset pipeline, while Reviewer 4 is most negative (rating 3, confidence 5) and sees the model as essentially ControlNet and the results as far from photorealistic. This contrast is preserved.
