## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper tackles the computational bottleneck of spectral clustering—eigendecomposition of an \(n \times n\) matrix—by proposing D-SPEC, a distribution-based spectral clustering method. D-SPEC constructs an \(n \times k\) bipartite graph between data points and \(k\) distributions, enabling clustering via eigendecomposition of a \(k \times k\) matrix. The method includes theoretical guarantees (graph information preservation, noise tolerance bound) and experiments on synthetic and real datasets to demonstrate effectiveness and scalability. Four reviewers gave ratings of 6, 5, 6, and 1; the low-rated reviewer (R4) raised strong concerns about motivation and experimental validity.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- The proposed D-SPEC is interesting: it transitions from a traditional point-based to a distribution-based perspective, enhancing effectiveness.
- Efficiency: constructs an \(n \times k\) bipartite graph (smaller than \(n \times p\) in sampling-based methods), enabling \(k \times k\) eigendecomposition—a notable shift from standard approaches.
- Theoretical studies provide proofs of graph information preservation and a noise tolerance bound, demonstrating robustness.
- Extensive experiments on synthetic and real datasets (including large-scale ones) show effectiveness and scalability; the approach has potential for large-scale applications.
- Explanations of the bipartite graph structure and eigendecomposition process are well-structured.

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and computational paradox:** One reviewer (R4, rating 1) strongly questions the motivation, noting that many graph-based clustering methods use a sparse \(W\) (e.g., 10-NN), making eigendecomposition cheap. R1 adds that computing the full \(n \times n\) similarity matrix \(S\) requires \(O(n^2)\) time, negating efficiency gains; if the method instead samples \(p\) points to construct \(S\), it degenerates to common landmark-based approaches. Thus the claimed efficiency advantage is questionable.
- **Lack of clear differentiation from landmark-based methods:** D-SPEC shows strong similarities with landmark-based or Nyström methods. R2 states the innovation is limited to “a better method to construct an \(n \times k\) bipartite graph,” and R3 notes a missing clear differentiation. Direct comparisons of noise tolerance with landmark methods and the possibility of a hybrid method are missing (R3).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and threshold determination:** The method is sensitive to hyperparameters (e.g., \(\tau\) and \(\psi\)). How \(\tau\) influences the number of landmarks/anchors and clustering performance is unclear (R1, R3). The threshold parameter for bipartite graph construction lacks clear guidance, and sensitivity analyses are insufficient (R3, R4). Parameter selection (e.g., best ranges in Table 5) is not explained (R4).
- **Theoretical issues:** Theorem 3.2 contains a mistake: the eigenvector corresponding to the zero eigenvalue is not as defined in Line 661; the proof is not rigorous because the optimal eigendecomposition solution is not unique, and it is not guaranteed that the obtained eigenvector matches the theorem’s statement (R1). Theoretical sections are occasionally verbose with redundant content (R3).
- **Clarity of symbols, figures, and notation:** The paper uses unclear symbols and inconsistent notation. In Eq.(1) the indices \(i\) and \(j\) have different value ranges elsewhere; pseudo-code step 4 introduces an undefined variable \(V\) (R1, R2). Figure 1’s leftmost graph (example with 3 subgraphs) lacks annotation; Figure 3 is unclear; Figure 4 omits size information for the five large-scale datasets and does not clearly show efficiency (R2). The title uses “\(k \times k\)” before \(k\) is defined (R4).
- **Experimental limitations and unconvincing results:**
  - Results on MNIST and CoverType are not convincing; D-SPEC performs poorly on CoverType (R4). Why it fails on that dataset is unanswered.
  - Large-scale experiments: dataset sizes are not reported for the five datasets in Figure 4, and the figure does not demonstrate efficiency (R2).
  - Noise tolerance validation is limited to simple noisy graphs; comparisons with landmark or Nyström methods are absent (R3).
  - Method is not tested on non-spherical or poorly-separated clusters (R3).
  - No performance analysis for highly connected graphs (R3).
  - Empirical comparisons of computation time vs. traditional spectral clustering on large datasets are missing (R3).

One minority reviewer (R4, rating 1, confidence 5) finds the method’s motivation flawed and the experimental evidence unconvincing, while other reviewers (ratings 5, 6, 6) see potential but raise the above weaknesses.
