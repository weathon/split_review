## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SASS, a framework for generating instruction-tuning data using only open-source models. It flips input–output pairs from existing datasets to train an instruction generator, then employs an extract-then-generate strategy on an unlabeled corpus (mini‑pile) to produce additional instructions, followed by perplexity‑based filtering. The resulting data is used to fine‑tune LLaMA‑7B. Evaluation on the LongForm benchmark and via GPT‑4 shows improvements over baselines including GPT‑generated (Alpaca) and human‑curated data. Ablations also test BLOOMZ and Baichuan‑7B. Reviewers note the timely motivation (IP issues of closed‑source models) and the interesting inversion of input–output pairs, but raise substantial concerns about novelty, evaluation depth, missing baselines and ablations, and insufficient discussion of closely related work.

### Strengths
- **Good problem and motivation**: The work addresses the timely issue of generating instruction data without relying on closed‑source API models, which is relevant to IP and reproducibility concerns. (R1, R2)
- **Interesting method**: Flipping input–output pairs from existing datasets to create training data for an instruction generator is a novel twist. The extract‑then‑generate strategy (organizing generation at three levels) is well structured. (R1)
- **Perplexity as filtering**: Showing that perplexity can replace GPT‑based cleaning for filtering generated instructions is a useful finding. (R1)
- **Simple and effective**: The method is straightforward and achieves performance that often surpasses both GPT‑generated and human‑curated instruction data on LongForm. (R2)
- **Ablations**: The ablation studies help understand the effect of filtering unsuitable instructions. (R2)
- **Preliminary investigation**: The paper begins with a preliminary study that motivates the choice of instruction generation over self‑training, which helps readers follow the reasoning. (R4)
- **Focus on data curation**: The topic of instruction‑tuning dataset curation is interesting and timely for the community. (R4)

*Note: Reviewer 2 (score 5) is notably more positive than the other reviewers (all score 3). While R2 acknowledges weaknesses (see below), they consider the method simple and well‑motivated, and see the experimental results as showing superiority over many baselines.*

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: The core ideas (instruction generation from outputs, self‑alignment) are not new. The paper largely applies existing paradigms (instruction→output, output→instruction) from prior work on GPT‑series models to open‑source LLMs, rather than proposing a fundamentally new paradigm. (R2, R3) Self‑alignment has already been demonstrated effective in RLAIF and Constitutional AI; the paper only introduces a different prompt. (R3)
- **Missing comparison with direct use of open chat models**: An important baseline is missing: using models like LLaMA‑2‑70B‑chat (or even 13B‑chat) directly with Self‑Instruct to generate instruction data, which could obviate the need for training an instruction generator. (R2)
- **Insufficient evaluation scope**:
    - Experiments are only on LLaMA‑7B (and briefly BLOOMZ/Baichuan‑7B). Results at larger scales (13B, 33B, LLaMA‑2‑7B) are needed to demonstrate scalability. (R3)
    - No human evaluation is provided. The paper should examine the quality of the generated instructions from multiple dimensions (e.g., diversity, alignment) and show qualitative examples comparing with GPT‑4 generations. (R1, R4)
    - The performance on LongForm is not overwhelmingly significant compared to baselines. (R4) Also, improvements are reported on automatic metrics only; results on the Alpaca‑Eval leaderboard and OpenLLM leaderboard are missing, though these are widely used for instruction‑following ability. (R3, R4)
    - The reported METEOR score for the LongForm baseline (Koksal et al., 2023) in Table 1 is 20.27, but the original paper reports 24.8 (Table 2 in that paper). This discrepancy needs explanation. (R1)
    - DYNASAUR is missing from Table 1 (the performance of this baseline should be reported). (R1, R2)
- **Incomplete related work discussion**: Several highly relevant works are not discussed or compared:
    - Self‑Alignment with Instruction Backtranslation (Li et al., 2023) – also uses output‑to‑instruction generation and self‑alignment. (R1, R3, R4)
    - Instruction Mining (Cao et al., 2023), InstructionGPT‑4 (Wei et al., 2023), AlpaGasus (Chen et al., 2023) for data filtering. (R3)
    - Learning Instructions with Unlabeled Data for Zero‑Shot Cross‑Task Generalization (EMNLP 2022). (R1)
    The paper fails to position its contribution relative to these, making the novelty unclear. (R4)
- **Missing implementation details**:
    - Details on approximations used when constructing the LIMA training dataset (since LIMA does not release its full dataset). (R1)
    - Hyperparameter settings (e.g., learning rate, decay schedule) for training with LIMA and other baselines. (R1)
    - Experiment details for Section 5 are insufficient. (R4)
- **Preliminary study (Section 3) not rigorous**:
    - Why does adding more Dolly data (1000→5000→14 K) lead to worse performance? Why does combining LIMA with Dolly yield even worse results? The conclusion that “it is easier to generate appropriate instructions for the given response” is not convincingly justified. (R1, R4)
    - Figure 1 axes are not monotonically increasing; the x‑axis jumps from Dolly 14 K to LIMA 1000 to Dolly 14 K + LIMA 1000, making trends hard to follow. (R1)
    - Results for self‑training on Dolly 1000 and Dolly 5000 are omitted without explanation. (R1)
- **Writing and organization**:
    - The term “origin document” is used without clear definition. Standardizing terminology would help. (R1)
    - “Tight alignment” is ambiguous (page 2, paragraph 1). (R1)
    - The preliminary study could logically be moved after the method section, as some method details are needed to understand it. (R1)
- **Clarification needed**:
    - How exactly does the “extract‑then‑generate” method work? What are “keywords” and “random sentence” – is it just extracting one sentence as the response? (R4)
    - How is Dolly used for training the LLM Extractor? What are the input and output? (R4)
