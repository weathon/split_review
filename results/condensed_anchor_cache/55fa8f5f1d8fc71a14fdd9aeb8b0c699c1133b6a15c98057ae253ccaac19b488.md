## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper theoretically analyzes geometric tempering for Langevin dynamics, proving both upper and lower bounds under functional inequalities. The positive results provide convergence guarantees for continuous- and discrete-time tempered Langevin, and yield closed-form optimal tempering schedules for certain proposal–target pairs. The negative results demonstrate that geometric tempering can worsen functional inequalities and lead to exponential convergence, even when the proposal and target are both well-conditioned, challenging the conventional wisdom that tempering helps sampling from multi-modal distributions.

### Strengths
- First convergence analysis for geometric tempering under functional inequalities (Poincaré/log-Sobolev).
- Positive result (Theorem 3) is thorough, covering all reasonable regimes about as well as one could hope for.
- Negative examples are intuitive and insightful: they concretely show why tempering can fail (emergence of multimodality, poor log-Sobolev constants).
- Lower bounds demonstrate exponential dependence on the distance between modes, and even unimodal targets can suffer from poor convergence.
- Findings are surprising and significant for both theory and practice, challenging a long-standing intuition.
- Paper addresses a natural, technically difficult question; the optimization of upper bounds w.r.t. the tempering schedule is of theoretical interest.
- Presentation is very clear and flows nicely; all technical claims appear correct.
- Inclusion of more concrete lower bounds is insightful.

### Weaknesses
- The related work section would benefit from better structure (e.g., paragraph titles).
- The lower bound examples are in dimension one; it is not intuitive why the distance between modes would grow in a fixed dimension. Can the bounds be extended to high-dimensional settings (e.g., where the distance scales with √dimension)?
- In Corollary 5, the KL-term vanishes only if λ₀=0, which conflicts with the continuous schedule requirement.
- If all λᵢ are close to 1, the algorithm is effectively vanilla Langevin; why does exponential convergence appear?
- The analysis assumes dissipativity of the proposal and target measures; why is this needed when vanilla Langevin requires only log-Sobolev and smoothness? Could convergence be shown under weak Poincaré inequalities as in Mousavi-Hosseini et al. (COLT 2023)?
- It would be useful to discuss how to choose optimal proposal distributions ν when only partial information about π is known.
- The definition μ ∝ ν^{1-λ}π^λ differs from many prior works that use π^λ (implicitly taking ν as uniform). Could the authors clarify the reason for this form? This may be the main source of the negative results.
- The negative results dominate; the paper should provide more positive examples where tempering improves over vanilla Langevin, ideally with comparative bounds for Propositions 6 and 7. The current presentation leaves the impression that tempering is rarely beneficial, which may not reflect practice.
- The analysis could explore further areas where tempering has provable benefit, especially for multimodal distributions, perhaps considering barrier height between modes.
- Citation: Durmus & Moulines (2019) is cited for the Langevin rate in the strongly convex + smooth setting; earlier works exist.
- In practice, is tempering observed to improve over vanilla Langevin? If so, why?
- Would the conclusions and analysis techniques apply if ν were taken to be the Lebesgue measure instead of a probability measure?
- Is the proposal ν always taken to be Gaussian in practice? Could it be multi-modal?
- Is the rate 1/(2α_π t) in Proposition 7 classical? Please provide a reference.
