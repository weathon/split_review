## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DIV-SE and IDIV-SE, prompting strategies that generate diverse prompts (via personas and approaches) and ensemble the outputs, rather than relying on decoding diversity. DIV-SE uses multiple inference calls with different prompts; IDIV-SE merges these into a single call. The method selects optimal persona/approach combinations using a held-out set, then augments few-shot demonstrations accordingly. The authors report improved performance–cost trade-offs over self-consistency on AQUA-RAT, Blocksworld, GSM8K, and CommonsenseQA, with especially large gains on Blocksworld (exceeding prior SOTA by ≥29.6 points on 4/5 blocks). Ablations examine error propagation and aggregation strategies.

### Strengths
- **Inference cost optimization**: IDIV-SE reduces multiple inference calls to one, improving the performance–cost Pareto frontier over self-consistency. (R1, R2)
- **Novel prompting direction**: Using prompt diversity (not just decoding diversity) is a novel take; the method requires little prompt engineering and applies to many tasks. (R1, R2)  
  *Note: One reviewer (R3) finds novelty very limited due to prior work (arXiv:2206.02336); another (R1) calls it novel. This contrast is preserved.*
- **Ablation on error propagation** is interesting and informative. (R1)
- **Well written and clear** (R1, R3) – though another reviewer (R2) describes the methodology section as poorly structured and the paper rushed (see Weaknesses).
- Outperforms self-consistency on cost/accuracy trade-off on most datasets (except CommonsenseQA). (R2)
- **Large gains on Blocksworld** (planning benchmarks), with clear performance advantage even at low cost. (R1, from abstract)
- Ablation on aggregation strategies beyond majority vote. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Requires a held-out set** to select persona/approach combinations, limiting practical applicability; CoT and self-consistency work out-of-the-box without such tuning. (R1, R2)
- **Mixed results** – method works well on Blocksworld and AQUA-RAT but less so on GSM8K and CommonsenseQA; this uneven benefit is not explained. (R1, R3)
- **No quality assessment of generated demonstrations** – the synthetically augmented few-shot examples are not evaluated for correctness, diversity, or validity; automatic generation may introduce errors that propagate. (R1, R2)
- **Limited evaluation** – missing common reasoning benchmarks (AddSub, MultiArith, ASDiv, SVAMP, CSQA, StrategyQA, ARC, Letter, Coinflip), making comparison with prior self-consistency work harder. (R1)
- **Unfair comparison in Figures 4 & 5** – comparing DIV-SE‑1..5 against SC‑1 only; self-consistency should also be shown with multiple reasoning paths for a fair cost/accuracy trade-off. (R1)
- **Missing experiments** – should be extended to smaller models (e.g., Llama 2 7B) and to compare pre-trained vs. chat/RLHF variants. (R1)
- **Novelty / citation issues**: Prior work (arXiv:2206.02336) already uses diverse prompts for diverse reasoning paths, and Batch Prompting (cite) introduced merging multiple inference calls into one; these need to be cited and the contribution claims toned down. (R2, R3; R1 disagrees on novelty – see Strengths)
- **Context length constraints** – IDIV-SE likely faces significant limits due to packing many examples in one prompt. (R3)
- **Persona ablation missing** – unclear whether the persona component increases diversity; an ablation (persona vs. approach only) is needed. (R3)
- **Methodology presentation**: Section 2 lacks clear high-level structure; the paper seems rushed (page 5 badly formatted, missing figure references like Fig 6 & 8). (R2)
- **Few-shot generation unclear** – are exemplars created automatically or manually? If automatic, errors need validation; using a validation set makes the cost comparison with self-consistency unfair. (R2)
- **“Independently formulates new approaches”** (Section 2.2) may be hallucination; ablation on whether to allow new vs. only pre-specified approaches is needed. (R2)
- **Figure clarity** – labels and color codes missing in Figures 1 and 3, making them hard to read. (R3)
- **Inconsistencies**: Blockworld 3 results differ between Figure 1 and Figure 3; inference cost for DIV-SE‑3/5 is lower in Figure 3, which is not explained. (R3)
- **Number of trials** not reported. (R3)
