## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper presents SAT-LDM, a watermarking method for latent diffusion models that addresses the train-test distribution mismatch by training the watermark modules on images generated by the model itself, using an empty prompt. The method includes a theoretical generalization bound and claims improved image quality and robustness. Reviewers acknowledge the theoretical contribution and the importance of the observed mismatch, but raise significant concerns about experimental setup, motivation, missing ablations, and insufficient explanation of the claimed mechanism.

### Strengths

- Theoretical generalization guarantee is provided, which is novel compared to prior works (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The observation that there is a mismatch between training (external dataset) and testing (generated images) distributions is crucial and can inform future research (Reviewer 4).
- The theoretical section is informative (Reviewer 4).
- The method does not require additional external training data (Reviewer 1).
- The writing is clear and easy to follow (Reviewer 1).
- The paper focuses on a significant topic—copyright protection for AI-generated images (Reviewer 2).
- Experimental results show that watermarked images have better quality than competitors, with robustness on par with state-of-the-art methods (Reviewers 1, 3).

### Weaknesses

- The motivation is unclear: comparing watermarked to unwatermarked images generated from the same prompt is irrelevant for diffusion-native watermarking, as users would not see both; such a comparison would only be valid for post-watermarking methods (Reviewer 2).
- The claim that watermarking performance degrades across image styles is questionable; style diversity is largely dictated by the diffusion model’s training data, not the watermarking process (Reviewer 2).
- Figure 2 is likely incorrect: if the method follows FSW, message embedding should be fused into the UNet-decoder, not the VAE decoder. If the figure is correct (frozen VAE decoder), training only the message processor and extractor risks extracting false-positive messages from non-watermarked images; the paper does not include a false positive rate (FPR) analysis (Reviewer 2).
- The claim of “no external data” is misleading; the message components still require training data, just like existing methods (Reviewer 2).
- Limited novelty: the approach mainly replicates FSW’s structure and uses the spatial transformer from StegaStamp (Reviewer 2); Reviewer 1 also notes limited novelty.
- Lacks critical ablations and comparisons: no investigation of the impact of using different pre-trained diffusion models; the method should be evaluated under strong attacks such as diffusion-based attacks (WAVES was suggested as a reference) (Reviewer 2).
- The assumption of Lipschitz continuity of the loss function is strong; it is unclear how to verify it in practice or estimate the Lipschitz constant K, which could lead to an unsatisfactorily large upper bound (Reviewer 3).
- Experimental setting uses only 1,000 AI-generated prompts, which is far too small and may introduce bias; the authors should provide results using selected prompts from LAION-400M (not used during training) to ensure the reported advantage is not solely due to prompt distribution shift (Reviewer 4).
- The explanation of why the method works is insufficient: the demo experiment in Sec. 5.3 (Fig. 3) shows that the distance between no-prompt and GPT-prompt latents is smaller than between LAION-prompt and GPT-prompt latents, but this does not prove that the unconditional distribution is close to the conditional distribution. It is widely believed that these distributions differ significantly, which is why classifier/classifier-free guidance is used (Reviewer 4).
- The actual mechanism may be that the watermark modules adapt to the negative output of the unconditional generation when guidance scale > 1, rather than reflecting the generated distribution as claimed. Results with guidance scale = 1 are needed to verify this (Reviewer 4).
- No ablation study separates the contributions of the two claimed improvements: (1) shifting training data from an external dataset to generated data, and (2) the structural update in Sec. 4.2. The observed gains could come entirely from the structural update (Reviewer 4).
- More visualization results across different image styles would help demonstrate generalization (Reviewer 1).
- The writing could be simplified to better emphasize the intuition, with the formal theory introduced later (Reviewer 4).
- Minor: LaTeX should use ``" for double quotes (Reviewer 4).
