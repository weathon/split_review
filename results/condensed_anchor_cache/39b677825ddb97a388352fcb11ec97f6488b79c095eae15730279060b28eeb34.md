## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces a framework to adapt compressed image latents for use with Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), aiming to reduce transmission and computational overhead. It proposes a lightweight transform-neck and a surrogate loss to align compressed latents with the MLLM’s visual encoder (CLIP) without backpropagating through the full MLLM. The framework is claimed to be generic across neural image codecs and MLLMs. Experiments on captioning and VQA tasks show rate-accuracy trade-offs with lower complexity than reconstruction baselines.

### Strengths
- First work specifically targeting neural image coding for MLLMs (R4).  
- Lightweight transform-neck avoids full image decoding, reducing computation (R3, R4).  
- Surrogate loss enables training via the visual encoder only, avoiding backprop through billion-scale MLLMs (R3, R4).  
- Framework is generic and applicable to various neural codecs and MLLMs sharing the same visual encoder (R1, R3, R4).  
- Papers is easy to follow and well-organized (R1, R2).  
- Topic addresses practical value of reducing visual input cost for MLLMs (R1).  
- Extensive experiments on different codecs and MLLMs show improved rate-accuracy and less complexity (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation vs. existing MLLM acceleration:** The paper does not clearly differentiate from token pruning/merging methods (e.g., crossget) that also reduce MLLM costs; it should discuss unique contributions (R1).  
- **Motivation for image compression in MLLMs challenged:** MLLMs typically use CLIP with 224×224 resolution, which incurs negligible transmission overhead compared to MLLM inference time. High-resolution approaches often crop images into sub-images and can outperform compressed inputs. The paper does not explain advantages over using more compact image encoders (R4).  
- **Lack of MLLM-specific design:** The method is a general compression approach with no unique elements tailored to multimodal understanding; the core mechanism remains agnostic to MLLM nuances (R2).  
- **No training cost comparison:** The paper does not quantify reduction in training time/compute compared to methods that incorporate MLLMs directly, making it hard to assess true advantage (R2).  
- **Surrogate loss novelty is weak:** The loss is a simple combination of MSE and cross-entropy, common in other domains. No ablation studies on different loss compositions are provided to justify the design (R2).  
- **Limited to CLIP vision encoder:** Only CLIP is used for alignment; many advanced MLLMs now use Intern-ViT, SGLIP, or other encoders. Experiments with diverse encoders are missing, limiting generalizability (R2, R3).  
- **Inconsistent performance across benchmarks:** The method performs close to uncompressed latents on image captioning but shows significant degradation on the POPE benchmark; no insight is given (R1).  
- **Loss function design in Phase 1 not justified:** Motivation for using different losses at various stages across epochs is unclear, and no corresponding ablation supports this choice (R2).  
- **Evaluation inconsistency:** Different MLLMs are used for different tasks instead of running multiple MLLMs consistently on each task, which would clarify generalization (R2).  
- **No comparison with other image compression methods** outside the MLLM context to contextualize broader performance (R3).  
- **Requires task-specific retraining:** The transform-neck and codec need retraining for different downstream tasks, raising questions about practical necessity (R4).  
- **Minority stronger positivity noted:** Reviewer 3 rated contribution as 3 and found the method generic and effective, while other reviewers (R2, R4) were more critical of novelty and motivation (R2 soundness 2, R4 soundness 2).
