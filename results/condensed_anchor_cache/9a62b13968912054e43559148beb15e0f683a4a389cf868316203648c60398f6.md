## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a task-oriented knowledge transfer approach to leverage vision foundation models (VFMs) for training small task-specific models with limited labeled data. It uses three stages: fine-tuning the VFM on the target task, distilling knowledge from the fine-tuned VFM to a smaller model via an unlabeled transfer set, and fine-tuning the small model with labeled target data. A task-agnostic alternative distills from the frozen VFM. Experiments on three target tasks with limited labels show that task-oriented transfer outperforms task-agnostic distillation, web-scale CLIP pretraining, and supervised ImageNet-1k pretraining (by 1–10.5%, 2–21%, and 2–14% respectively). The paper highlights the importance of transfer set distribution and proposes a retrieval-based method to curate task-related transfer sets.

### Strengths
- **Interesting investigation of non-in-domain transfer sets and retrieval-based curation** (R1, R3, R4): The study of how transfer set distribution affects performance and the proposal of a retrieval approach to curate task-related transfer sets are seen as valuable contributions, especially for domains not well-covered by web data. R1 notes this is the most interesting aspect of the paper.
- **Simple, clean, and well-written experiments** (R2, R4): The paper presents comprehensive comparisons to baselines (task-agnostic distillation, CLIP pretraining, ImageNet pretraining) and includes ablation studies on transfer set distribution. Experiments cover multiple target tasks and imbalanced datasets.
- **Clear empirical demonstration of performance gains** (R2, R3, R4): The proposed approach beats strong baselines (ImageNet pretraining, web-scale CLIP pretraining) across multiple settings, using two VFMs (DINOV2, CLIP) and two mobile architectures (MobileViT-v2, MobileNet-v2). R3 notes these results support beneficial insights for the community.
- **Practical significance** (R3, R4): The work enables leveraging large VFMs for specialized small models in low-data regimes, which is relevant for resource-constrained deployment. R3 considers the contribution good despite low technical novelty.

**Note: All four reviewers acknowledge these strengths, but R1 (score 3) is significantly more critical and finds only the investigation of non-in-domain data interesting. R1 considers the paper lacking novelty and having problematic baselines (see Weaknesses). R3 acknowledges the method is simple but views the contribution as positive.**

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty / obvious findings** (R1, R2, R3 acknowledges): R1 states the approach is essentially knowledge distillation, the most straightforward setup, and that the finding that in-domain transfer sets are better when sufficient in amount is expected (citing prior open-set SSL work). R2 says most findings are obvious because the transfer set is designed to be similar to the target task. R3 agrees the technical novelty is low but still finds the work useful.  
- **Transfer set curation is heuristic and insufficiently explored** (R1, R2, R4):  
  - R1: The retrieval-based selection is not thorough enough to convince that it works across different target tasks and domains (medical, satellite, clipart were not tested). No strategy for selecting data from multiple domains is proposed.  
  - R2: The transfer set creation process is mostly heuristic and is a critical factor for performance.  
  - R4: The retrieval approach is simple (basic feature similarity); more sophisticated methods (active learning, core-set selection, adversarial filtering, clustering-based diversity, explicit informativeness optimization) are not explored. The impact of the size of the retrieved set on final performance is not studied.  
- **Lack of analysis on transfer set size** (R2, R4): The paper does not investigate how different transfer set sizes affect distillation effectiveness. R4 specifically highlights that this is a crucial factor left unexplored.  
- **No sensitivity analysis on choice of VFM** (R2): Experiments only use two VFMs (DINOV2, CLIP). It is unclear if the gains are consistent across other foundation models or if certain models are more suitable.  
- **Problematic baselines and unfair comparisons** (R1):  
  - R1: Task-agnostic distillation does not include the fine-tuning step, which requires significantly more compute. It is unclear if task-agnostic distillation could outperform the proposed method given the same compute budget (e.g., by distilling an ensemble).  
  - R1: The CLIP-pretrain baseline uses an internal dataset with unknown distribution/statistics; it is impossible to tell if its weaker performance is due to contrastive pretraining itself or data quality.  
  - R2: It is unclear whether the target models (e.g., MobileViT-v2) are pre-trained. If they are, the comparison between a “normal” pre-trained model (IM-Pretrain, CLIP-Pretrain) and one that undergoes additional task-oriented/agnostic training may be misleading.  
- **Scalability and computational concerns** (R1): The approach relies on fine-tuning the VFM, which can be computationally prohibitive for extremely large models, limiting practical applicability. The paper only considers inference compute constraints, not training compute.  
- **Missing details and clarifications** (R1, R4):  
  - R1: The loss function for the “CLIP-Pretrain” baseline (Section 3.1) is said to be similar to CLIP but the difference is not clarified.  
  - R1: The process of distilling patch features (important for segmentation) is described in only a few sentences; an expanded explanation with equations or a figure is needed.  
  - R1: Distilling from patch tokens of CLIP was not tried.  
  - R4: The paper lacks detail on how the VFMs are fine-tuned (e.g., which parts are updated, learning rates, etc.).  
- **Narrow scope of considered scenarios** (R1): The paper does not consider the case where multiple pre-trained models are available (a more realistic setup given the current landscape).  
- **Additional baseline suggestion** (R1): Self-supervised pretraining approaches like DINO should be included as baselines.  
- **Missing discussion on inherited biases** (R4): The paper mentions that target models may inherit VFM biases but does not discuss how to address this.  
- **Figure and supplementary issues** (R2, R3):  
  - R2: From Figure 5 (left), using a random transfer set yields performance similar to CLIP/ImageNet pretraining, which needs clarification.  
  - R3: In Fig 2(a), the meaning of three lines for DINOV2 - MobileViT-V2 - CC3M is unclear. The supplementary material is nearly identical to the main paper with broken references.  

**Minority critical view (R1, score 3):** R1 is significantly more negative than the other reviewers, arguing that the paper lacks novelty (most straightforward approach), the baselines are unfair and poorly justified, and the investigation of transfer set selection is incomplete. R1 recommends rejection, while R2, R3, and R4 rate the paper at 5–6 and see useful contributions despite weaknesses.
