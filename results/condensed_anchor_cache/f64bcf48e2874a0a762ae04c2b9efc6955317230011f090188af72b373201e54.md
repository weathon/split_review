## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SuperCorrect, a two-stage framework to improve reasoning and self-correction of small LLMs using a large teacher model. Stage 1 extracts hierarchical thought templates (generalized + step-level) from the teacher for better reasoning. Stage 2 uses cross-model collaborative DPO with teacher correction traces to enhance the student’s self-correction ability. Experiments on MATH and GSM8K show state-of-the-art results among 7B models (e.g., SuperCorrect-7B surpasses DeepSeekMath-7B by 7.8%/5.3% on MATH/GSM8K). One reviewer is strongly positive (score 8), while the other three are moderately positive (score 6) with several reservations.

### Strengths
- **Clear writing and presentation:** The paper is well‑structured, easy to follow even for readers not familiar with self‑correction concepts, and includes helpful preliminary explanations (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Significant performance gains:** The method achieves new SOTA among 7B models, with consistent improvements in both accuracy and stability across benchmarks. The open‑sourced 10K SFT data and 1K preference data are appreciated (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Step‑level supervision via cross‑model DPO:** Using the teacher’s step‑level correction traces provides more fine‑grained supervision than solution‑level training, and the distillation approach is simple and potentially applicable to many tasks (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Innovation and bottleneck breaking:** Combining hierarchical thought templates with cross‑model DPO is considered an innovative way to teach the student model to locate and rectify specific errors, breaking its reasoning bottleneck (Reviewer 3).
- **Open‑source data release:** The release of high‑quality SFT and preference alignment data benefits the community (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of teacher‑generated content evaluation:** The paper does not evaluate the quality of teacher‑generated corrections, logic flaws, or error detection. The analysis \(a_i\) and correction \(c_i\) (Line 333) may be teacher‑generated, but no assessment of their correctness or bias is provided. Concerns about propagation of teacher biases are raised (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Near‑identity to Step‑DPO:** The cross‑model collaborative DPO appears nearly identical to Step‑DPO (Lai et al., 2024); the only stated difference is that here \(a_i\) and \(c_i\) are annotated/generated by humans/teacher rather than sampled from the policy model. The paper does not clarify the core novelty or whether this is parallel work (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing step‑wise distillation verification:** No quantitative analysis of whether the student after cross‑model DPO agrees with the teacher at the step level (e.g., ratio of steps predicted correct/wrong by both). Such an inspection would confirm that self‑correction ability is truly distilled (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient ablation of hierarchical thought templates:** The gains from HSFT alone (vs. SFT) may stem from different prompt formatting, not the hierarchical structure. Controlled experiments are needed, e.g., moving the <Generalized> statement, removing it, or adding it to a CoT baseline. Also, the motivation for why hierarchical reasoning is specifically crucial for error correction (rather than general reasoning) is not explained (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Missing results in Table 2:** Table 2 only shows HSFT results for Qwen, not for DeepSeek or Llama, yet the paper claims HSFT gains across all models. This data should be provided (Reviewer 2).
- **Inconsistent baseline for Qwen2.5‑Math:** The reported accuracy of Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct in Table 1 appears inconsistent with the original paper (Yang et al., 2024a), raising fairness and reliability concerns (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited evaluation scope:** The method is not inherently math‑specific, but it is only evaluated on MATH and GSM8K. Generality to other reasoning tasks is not demonstrated (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of logical connection between stages:** The paper does not explain why hierarchical thought templates (Stage 1) are necessary for cross‑model DPO (Stage 2); a standard CoT might suffice if the goal is only to generate a thought process for DPO training (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear novelty over SFT on corrected data:** The performance gains from cross‑model DPO could be due simply to having more corrected training data. The paper does not ablate whether DPO is the key factor versus SFT on the same corrected data (Reviewer 4).
- **Scalability concerns:** The framework is only demonstrated on 7B models; no discussion of scaling to larger models or the computational cost of generating correction traces for larger LMs (Reviewer 3).
- **Quality criteria for correction traces:** The paper lacks detail on how correction trace quality is determined, and how to handle niche domains or biased teacher outputs (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Training data parity for baselines:** It is unclear whether the training data (types and volumes) for baseline models (Qwen2.5‑Math‑7B‑Instruct, DeepSeekMath‑7B‑Instruct, Llama3.1‑8B‑Instruct) are the same as those used for SuperCorrect (Reviewer 4).
- **Teacher model used for data generation:** The identity of the teacher LLM used to generate the high‑quality dataset is not specified; measures to handle incorrect teacher‑generated data are not detailed (Reviewer 4).
- **Two‑stage training stability:** No discussion of potential stability issues or convergence challenges in the two‑stage training process (Reviewer 3).
