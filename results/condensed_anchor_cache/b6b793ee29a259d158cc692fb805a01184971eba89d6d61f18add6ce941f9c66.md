## Merged Review

### Summary
Scores ranged from 6 to 8, with one reviewer (score 8) being notably more positive about the thoroughness and adaptability of the system. All reviewers agreed that the integration of proprioception, kinesthesia, force haptics, and muscle activation for video generation simulation is novel and addresses gaps in fine-grained motor control. The method shows improvements over UniSim across MSE, PSNR, LPIPS, and FVD, with ablation studies validating design choices. However, multiple reviewers raised concerns about limited generalization (single dataset ActionSense, evaluation only on subject 5 at 64×64 resolution), missing cross-domain and cross-subject validation, lack of comparisons to other multimodal fusion methods (Mutex, LanguageBind, ImageBind), and vague explanations of core mechanisms. One reviewer noted counterintuitive ablation results where removing modalities caused minimal performance drop, questioning the necessity of multisensory inputs.

### Strengths
- The work is well-motivated: integrating a comprehensive set of interoceptive signals (proprioception, kinesthesia, force haptics, muscle activation) into generative video simulation is novel and addresses a significant gap in fine-grained control.
- The proposed pipeline (cross-attention, channel-wise softmax, relaxed hyperplane interaction) effectively aligns modalities in a shared representation space while preserving unique information.
- Clear quantitative improvements over the previous state-of-the-art (UniSim) are demonstrated across four metrics (MSE, PSNR, LPIPS, FVD) with consistent gains over different prediction time spans.
- Extensive ablation studies (Table 2, Table 3, and others) confirm the effectiveness of using multiple modalities and validate different design choices; interesting correlations (e.g., hand force and muscle EMG) are highlighted.
- Visualizations in the main paper and appendix enhance understanding; figures illustrate performance across different sensory configurations.
- The problem is well-formulated and the approach is presented with sufficient detail for reproducibility (one reviewer particularly emphasized the thoroughness and adaptability of the system).
- Downstream applications (policy optimization, planning) are demonstrated, showing practical potential for robotics, especially in culinary or surgical scenarios requiring precise control.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited generalization due to single dataset and setting:** The model is trained and tested only on ActionSense, a kitchen-based dataset with a limited range of tasks and objects. All testing used a single subject (subject 5) from that dataset, risking overfitting to specific motion patterns. No evaluation is performed on out-of-domain data (e.g., EpicKitchen) or with different backgrounds, tools, or objects (e.g., different vegetables/fruits). This limits the method’s applicability to varied real-world robotic manipulation scenarios. *Reviewers requested cross-dataset and cross-subject validation.*
- **Low resolution and lack of real-time feasibility:** Experiments are conducted at 64×64 resolution, which is too low for precise robotic control (e.g., finger positions, object shape changes). No analysis of computational efficiency, latency, memory usage, or scalability to higher resolutions is provided. The chosen metrics (MSE, PSNR, LPIPS, FVD) do not assess real-time execution capability on hardware-constrained systems, which is critical for practical deployment.
- **Missing comparisons to other multimodal fusion baselines:** The paper compares performance variations within its own framework but does not compare against established multimodal alignment methods such as Mutex, LanguageBind, or ImageBind. This makes it difficult to isolate the unique benefits of the proposed design choices. *Reviewer 2 also requested a qualitative breakdown of where the model outperforms or falls short relative to these baselines.*
- **Incomplete experiments and insufficient ablations:**
  - In Table 3, the “Ours with sentence” result is missing, which would assess the impact of sentence-level information.
  - Table 2 shows minimal performance drop when some sensory modalities are removed at test time (e.g., hand-force only achieves high LPIPS; hand/body pose can be removed with minimal impact). This counterintuitive result challenges the necessity of multisensory input and requires further investigation. *A comprehensive ablation of all modality combinations is missing.*
  - No analysis of model stability over extended simulations (temporal drift beyond reported spans) is provided.
- **Vague or unclear explanations of core mechanisms:**
  - Terms and subscripts in Section 2 (e.g., \(z_{t,m,j}\)) are not defined, and the variable is not used elsewhere.
  - The geometric interpretation of the relaxed hyperplane interaction is confusing (“two space partitioned by a hyperplane”).
  - The loss term \(L_{VDM}\) is referenced before its definition.
  - The policy optimization section (downstream task) lacks clarity: the difference between the baseline policy and the policy using the simulator is not articulated.
  - Figures lack arrows/labels to clarify data flow; text in illustrations is too small.
- **Inability to predict multimodal outputs:** The model conditions on multisensory data to generate video but does not predict proprioceptive, kinesthetic, or haptic outputs. Adding such a capability would significantly extend the contribution and enable applications requiring accurate multisensory feedback. *Reviewer 3 specifically requested this extension.*
- **Dataset task specification missing:** The paper does not list how many tasks are in ActionSense or what they are.
- **Minor presentation issues:**
  - Typo in Section 3.1: “Phrasse” should be “Phrase”.
  - Best results in Fig 3, Fig 6, and Table 1 are not highlighted; LPIPS is not explained in figure captions.
  - Figure 15 labels may be swapped (“Top right should be Failure cases and Top left additional qualitative results”).
