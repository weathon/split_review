## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces CamTrol, a training-free method for camera control in off-the-shelf video diffusion models. It uses a two-stage pipeline: first, a single image is projected into 3D point cloud space via depth estimation, and novel views are rendered along a camera trajectory (with inpainting for holes). Second, these rendered images are noised and used as layout priors via inversion to guide the video generator. The method does not fine-tune the base model, but includes a depth coefficient optimization step. Experiments claim superior camera alignment and perceptual quality compared to MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl, and AnimateDiff. Most reviewers (4 of 5) rate the paper 6; one reviewer rates 5, expressing stronger concerns about result quality and efficiency.

### Strengths
- **Training-free and easy to integrate**: Requires no supervised fine-tuning or camera-annotated datasets; can be plugged into most pretrained video diffusion models (e.g., SVD, CogVideoX). (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- **Clear writing and presentation**: Two-stage framework is well-structured; observation that camera movement can be seen as latent layout rearrangement is clearly motivated. Ablation studies are comprehensive and visualization demonstrates effectiveness of each component. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- **Outperforms prior methods**: Outperforms competing methods (MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl) in both perceptual quality (FVD, FID) and motion alignment (CamMC) in quantitative and qualitative experiments, especially on complex camera movements. (Reviewer 1)
- **Motion strength control**: Because a metric depth estimator is used, the method can control motion strength with metric scale, whereas prior methods lack such scale knowledge. (Reviewer 5)

### Weaknesses
- **Complex and fragile pipeline**: The full cascade (depth estimation → point cloud lifting → rendering → inpainting → depth coefficient optimization → video generation) is vulnerable to error propagation. Small depth estimation errors lead to scene distortions; poor inpainting produces 2D inconsistencies and artifacts, especially for large holes or large camera motions. The integration of components is not seamless, reducing generation efficiency and temporal consistency. (Reviewers 1,4,5)
- **t_0 / inversion timestep selection is heuristic**: The best timestep t_0 (which controls fidelity–diversity trade-off) is highly input-dependent and no systematic selection rule or heuristic is provided, limiting practicality. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- **Inpainting model (single-image) causes temporal inconsistency**: The inpainting model operates on individual frames, leading to flickering, inconsistent content, and difficulty generating reasonable new content across frames. Additionally, noisy latents carry semantic information that may interfere with generating novel dynamic content, making the method potentially unsuitable for long videos. (Reviewers 1,3)
- **Missing quantitative baselines and comparisons**:
  - Table 1 lacks a vanilla SVD baseline (FVD, FID, IS, CLIP-SIM) to assess the pipeline’s impact on base video generator quality. (Reviewers 1,3)
  - Base video generation models for MotionCtrl and CameraCtrl in Table 1 are not specified; if they are not SVD, the comparison is unfair. (Reviewers 1,3)
  - No quantitative ablation for depth coefficient optimization; Table 2 shows negligible metric differences, indicating it changes nearly nothing. (Reviewer 5)
  - No quantitative results on complex camera trajectories to verify consistent camera movement across different inputs. (Reviewer 1)
- **Performance on ground-truth cameras**: ParticleSfM’s accuracy on ground-truth videos is unreported; lower bounds for motion accuracy metrics in Table 1 are unknown. (Reviewer 1)
- **Scene inconsistency and inaccurate camera control**: In demo videos, the scene often changes when the camera moves (e.g., “Tilt Down” changes top of scene), and the camera motion does not match the specified trajectory (e.g., “Pan Left” also moves forward) – the method does not guarantee a rigid scene. (Reviewer 5)
- **Computational overhead not analyzed**: The optimization-based approach (especially depth coefficient optimization) requires significant per-inference computation; no analysis of optimization time, memory usage, or comparison to fine-tuned methods (which have zero inference overhead). This makes the method potentially impractical compared to trained camera-control modules. (Reviewer 5)
- **Limited novelty and unclarified “training-free” claim**: The pipeline combines point cloud reconstruction and inversion, similar to prior work like Infinite Nature. The depth coefficient optimization (L190) is itself a training-like step, albeit without fine-tuning the base model, and it can be empirical and time-consuming. (Reviewer 2)
- **No explicit design for dynamics**: The method does not encourage video motion beyond what the base model inherently provides; no mechanism to control or enhance dynamics/temporal coherence. (Reviewer 2)
- **Text-to-video capability unclear**: Figure 1 and the method always assume an input image; no discussion or experiments on generating videos from only a text prompt. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing qualitative and qualitative comparisons with relevant works**: No comparison with AnimateDiff (qualitative), CamCo, Vd3d, or CVD (Collaborative Video Diffusion). The demo website lacks samples from latest base models (e.g., CogVideoX) despite mention in the paper. (Reviewers 3,4)
- **Generalizability unsubstantiated**: The paper claims fine-tuned methods (e.g., on RealEstate10K) suffer from generalizability issues but provides no experiments or visuals demonstrating that CamTrol works better on out-of-distribution cameras. (Reviewer 5)
- **Writing quality declines in later sections**: Grammar issues make the method section hard to read. (Reviewer 5)
