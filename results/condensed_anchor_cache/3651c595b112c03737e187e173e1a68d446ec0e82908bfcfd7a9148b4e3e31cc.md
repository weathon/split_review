## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes BAdd, which injects bias-capturing features into the final layer of a neural network during training to learn bias-neutral representations. The method divides the training loss between bias-aligned and bias-conflicting samples, and uses explicit bias attributes to prevent loss spikes. It is evaluated on seven benchmarks, including single- and multi-attribute ones, and reports accuracy gains (e.g., +27.5% on FB-Biased-MNIST, +5.5% on CelebA). Reviewers find the problem important and the idea interesting, but raise concerns about the reliance on known bias labels, similarity to prior work (FLAC), insufficient analysis of computational cost and scalability, limited ablation studies, and lack of validation on real-world unknown biases or on other data modalities. Reviewer 3 is notably more positive (score 8) than the others (scores 5, 6, 5), praising the clarity and importance of the problem, while the minority reviewers highlight missing experiments and practical limitations.

### Strengths
- The paper clearly describes the bias mitigation problem and the proposed BAdd method (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The investigated problem—mitigating bias in deep learning models—is important and timely (Reviewer 3).
- BAdd effectively reduces bias across multiple benchmarks, showing consistent improvements in various bias settings (Reviewer 2). The approach achieves significant accuracy gains on challenging multi-attribute benchmarks: +27.5% on FB-Biased-MNIST and +5.5% on CelebA (as reported in the paper; supported by experiments).
- The method is architecture-agnostic and can be easily integrated into different deep learning models without extensive modifications (Reviewer 4).
- Activation maps and GradCam visualizations help demonstrate how BAdd shifts the model’s focus away from bias-inducing features (Reviewer 4).
- The core idea of injecting bias features to mitigate loss spikes is interesting and supported by various experiments (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Dependency on bias labels/classifier:** The method requires either explicit bias attribute labels or a pre-existing bias classifier. This limits applicability where such labels are unavailable or unreliable, and performance is intrinsically tied to the quality of the bias classifier, with inaccuracies propagating to the debiased model (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Reviewer 1 notes the claim of suitability for real-world scenarios is contradicted by the need to know bias attributes.
- **Similarity to prior work (FLAC [1]):** The idea of bias injection to mitigate bias has been used in FLAC. The paper does not clearly differentiate BAdd from FLAC (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Assumption of bias representability:** The method assumes biases can be captured explicitly; this may not hold for complex, subtle, or unknown biases (Reviewer 4). It is unclear whether the approach can handle biases embedded in social contexts or implicit factors (Reviewer 4). Additionally, there is no evidence or theoretical justification for extending the method to other data modalities such as text (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited computational complexity analysis:** The paper does not discuss computational overhead, memory usage, or training time. This is a concern for large-scale models or datasets and crucial for practical deployment (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Insufficient ablation studies:** The ablation studies are limited. More extensive ablations are needed to validate the individual contributions of each component, e.g., varying batch size (Reviewer 3) and providing guidance on tuning the fine-tuning hyperparameter (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of training details:** It is not explained how the bias classifier’s gradients are incorporated into the main model’s training. The interaction between components and the loss functions/optimization strategies are unclear (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 asks whether forcing the loss to zero overcorrects the bias injection module, leading to incorrect features, and whether a large ratio of bias-conflicting to bias-aligned samples (B_c >> B_a) harms learning of diverse features.
- **Limited evaluation on real-world biases:** While evaluations include CelebA, further analysis on datasets with complex, less-defined biases is needed to demonstrate real-world applicability (Reviewer 4). The claim that BAdd is easily applicable to any network architecture and any CV dataset is unproven; experiments with different architectures (e.g., ViT) and balanced datasets (e.g., ImageNet) are missing (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor:** Tense inconsistency at line 728—“were” should be “are” (Reviewer 2).
