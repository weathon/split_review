## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates when Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets) correctly learn a target distribution. It shows that impact of detailed-balance violation on a state is proportional to total flow through that state, leading to a weighted detailed-balance (WDB) loss. It demonstrates that GNN-parameterized GFlowNets are limited by 1-WL expressivity (incapable of sampling from distributions that require distinguishing 1-WL–indistinguishable nodes on graphs other than trees), and proposes Look-Ahead GFlowNets (LA-GFlowNets) to overcome this. Finally, it introduces Flow Consistency in Subgraphs (FCS), a tractable metric approximating total variation distance, with PAC guarantees and empirical validation.

### Strengths
- The core question (when GFlowNets learn the right distribution) is well-defined and timely; the paper provides theoretical analysis together with empirical validation.
- Clear breakdown of contributions (Table 1) and logical, comprehensive evaluation.
- The WDB loss design (non-uniform weighting of transition terms) is grounded in the theoretical observation that imbalance impact scales with flow.
- LA-GFlowNets are motivated by the representational limits of 1-WL GNNs; the synthesis is confirmed experimentally.
- FCS is a new, computationally efficient metric with the potential to standardize GFlowNet evaluation; equivalence to TV distance and PAC guarantees are provided, with detailed comparison to traditional metrics.
- The paper is dense and comprehensive; most theoretical findings are supported by experimental evidence.

### Weaknesses
- **Figure readability**:
  - All figures have too-small legends, captions, and ticks (R1, R2).
  - Figure 5 lacks a legend (R1); state graphs in Figure 4 are blended and confusing (R2).
  - The “i.d.d.” versus “wrt” inconsistency appears throughout the text (R2).
- **Unexplained/insufficiently detailed design choices**:
  - The weighting function for WDB loss (choice of γ) appears heuristic; no detailed discussion of how to optimize these weights or how they vary across tasks (R1; echoed by R1’s question on alternative weighting functions).
  - The definition of “more powerful” in Theorem 4 is not clarified (R3).
- **Missing details in experimental methodology**:
  - Figure 2 – unclear if results are averaged over multiple runs; if so, number of runs and variation (e.g., initialization) not reported (R2).
  - Figure 3 – shaded area (standard deviation or interquartile range?) and its computation not explained (R2).
  - Figure 7 – why FL-GFlowNets has such large variance compared to other methods (R3).
- **Incomplete exposition of concepts**:
  - The sampling distribution and target distribution are not clearly defined early on (e.g., the symbol for the sampling distribution appears only in line 143, π is introduced as target only in line 202); the flow is a function on trajectories, not just states (R2, errata).
  - Background notation is complicated; a figure to illustrate the model would help (R3).
  - Some expressions are informal (e.g., “Figure 7 and Table 2 teach us three facts”) (R1).
- **Limited scope of experiments**:
  - Experiments focus on controlled scenarios, limiting insight into real-world suitability (R1).
- **Missing comparisons/ablation**:
  - No equivalent of Figure 2 using the WDB loss to visually compare its impact on transitions against the standard DB loss (R2’s question; overlaps with criticism on heuristic design).
  - The claim about efficient computation of p_T for some benchmark tasks (line 370) – clarification needed on whether arbitrary marginal distributions can be efficiently computed (R2’s question; overlaps with missing experiment scope).
- **Generalizability unclear**:
  - Whether FCS generalizes to other applications (e.g., NLP, molecule generation) is not discussed (R1’s question; overlaps with limited experimental scope).
