## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Re-Debias, a framework combining a Residual-Energy score and a Debias estimator to address long-tailed image classification. The authors identify two issues with Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM): the Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) loss over-relies on softmax, leading to poor distinction when correct/incorrect probabilities are similar, and the ERM naive estimator is biased toward head classes. The Residual-Energy score (which excludes the target class logit, Eq9) is claimed to provide a more sensitive reflection of prediction quality. The Debias estimator applies causal inference (Missing Not At Random, MNAR) for unbiased aggregation. Experiments on iNaturalist18, ImageNet-LT, CIFAR10/100-LT, both training from scratch and fine-tuning Vision Transformer (ViT), show improvements over state-of-the-art, sometimes with faster convergence (R1). A supporting theorem for unbiasedness of the Debias estimator is provided (R4). The paper lacks a conclusion section (R5).

### Strengths

- **Novelty and relevance**: The method is interesting and addresses often overlooked shortcomings of existing long-tailed learning methods (R1). However, one reviewer found insufficient innovation and uncompelling motivation (R3), and another noted only marginal improvement (~1% in Table2) that reduces perceived significance (R5).

- **Experimental thoroughness**: Extensive experiments across multiple datasets (iNaturalist18, ImageNet-LT, CIFAR10/100-LT), backbone architectures, and numerous baselines, including ablation studies (R2, R3, R4, R5). Results consistently outperform baselines, often by a substantial margin after only a few epochs (R1). Yet R5 specifically highlights that the improvement in Table2 is only about 1%, questioning significance.

- **Clarity of exposition**: The paper is generally well written, clearly structured, and easy to follow (R2, R4). The discursive approach from problem to proposal is appreciated (R2). However, R1, R4, and R5 note significant editorial issues and confusing statements (see Weaknesses). R5 finds writing challenging for readers not well-versed in the field.

- **Theoretical support**: The paper includes theorems demonstrating the unbiasedness of the Debias estimator (R4). One reviewer questions whether efficiency and consistency are also discussed (R4).

- **Evaluation completeness**: The evaluation section contains all experiments needed to understand method strength, and ablation studies are included (R2). R3 agrees experiments are extensive.

### Weaknesses

- **Editorial and typographical issues**: The manuscript has many typos that hinder readability and understanding (R1, R2, R4, R5). Examples include “tipycal” (line 269), “Finnally” (line 348), “traning from scratch” (line 373), and stray characters near line 250 (R2). R5 notes writing challenges for non-experts.

- **Unclear/contradictory notation and definitions**:

  - Sign in Equation 6: adjusted logits are given as \( g_y(x) = f_y(x) + \log(C \cdot \pi_y) \), but the \(\arg\max\) is over \(\exp(f_y(x) - \log(C\cdot \pi_y))\). Unclear whether it should be \(+\) or \(–\) (R1).

  - Equation 9 for Residual-Energy score: \( E(x, \bar{y}) = -\log \sum_{y \neq 1}^C e^{f_y(x)} \) appears to always exclude the first class, not the ground-truth class \(y\). The intended meaning of \(\bar{y}\) is not clarified (R1, R2). R2 also notes a likely typo in summation index \(j \ne 1\).

  - The approximation \( p(y_i|x_i;\theta) \propto e^{f_{y_i}(x_i)} \) appears without sufficient justification (R1).

- **Insufficient motivation for Residual-Energy score over existing energy scores**:

  - No experimental comparison or theoretical justification is given for choosing the Residual-Energy score (which removes the target class logit) over the original energy score that includes all logits (R4).

  - The original energy score includes a temperature parameter \(T\); setting \(T=1\) is neither justified empirically nor theoretically (R4).

  - The claim that the energy score provides more sensitive changes than softmax (Section 3.2) relies solely on a two-sample illustration (Figure 2) rather than a theoretically robust proof (R4). R3 also finds the two-sample motivation unconvincing and requests more examples.

  - The paper does not clearly differentiate its Residual-Energy score from other energy-based scores (e.g., Liu et al. NIPS2020, Lin et al. CVPR2021, Peng et al. ICLR2024) (R4).

- **Incomplete or insufficient analysis in experiments**:

  - Hyper-parameter analysis in Appendix A.4 is just a wall of numbers without interpretation (R4).

  - In Appendix Table 2, the result for ViT-B/32 with 200 epochs is missing, and no explanation is provided (R4).

  - The motivation figure (Figure 1) shows performance differences with Sacrificial Accuracy axis ranging only from -1% to 1%, making observed differences appear trivial (R5). Similarly, the marginal ~1% improvement in Table 2 weakens persuasiveness (R5).

- **Lack of theoretical depth**:

  - There is insufficient theoretical explanation of why Re-Debias works; experiments show it outperforms baselines but the reasons are not clearly reasoned (R3).

  - The connection to causal inference (MNAR) is insufficiently explained (R5). The phrase “only a subset of data is observable” is unclear.

  - The Residual-Energy score description (“lower values indicate larger errors, higher values more accurate predictions”) does not clarify how it differs from standard model calibration techniques (R5).

- **Missing or insufficiently addressed reviewer questions** (overlap with criticisms/ablation requests):

  - R2 asks for support on the claim that incorporating the new energy function into NLL is suboptimal.

  - R2 asks how \(\log(C\cdot \pi_j)\) is estimated; what happens when the training set ratio does not reflect the test set or in real-world problems where class priors are unknown.

  - R2 asks how the \(k\) components \(f\) in Eq10 are implemented.

  - R5 requests a step-by-step explanation of the computation of SR (Segregation Rate) in Figure 1.

  - R5 asks for additional explanation of how the method addresses both individual and aggregate evaluation challenges, with concrete examples or theoretical backing.

- **Missing conclusion section**: The paper lacks a conclusion (R5).
