## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes AdE, an adaptive hypergraph expansion method that first uses a Global Simulation Network (GSi‑Net) to select two representative nodes per hyperedge, then connects remaining nodes inside the hyperedge to those representatives, and finally applies a distance‑aware kernel to dynamically compute edge weights. The weighted graph is fed into a GNN for node classification. Reviewers generally agree the problem is meaningful and the experimental coverage (five benchmark datasets, multiple expansion methods and hypergraph learning baselines) is extensive. However, there is substantial disagreement: one reviewer (score 3, confidence 5) finds the performance improvements marginal, the theoretical propositions trivial, and the design choices poorly justified, while another reviewer (score 8, confidence 5) considers the model design novel and rational with strong empirical evidence. Two other reviewers (scores 6 and 5) are moderately positive, raising concerns about the pairwise‑weight nature of the expansion, missing state‑of‑the‑art comparisons, homophily assumptions, and scalability.

### Strengths
- The problem of adaptively expanding hypergraphs into weighted graphs is timely and addresses real limitations of fixed‑weight expansions (information loss and redundancy). (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- The two‑component design – GSi‑Net for representative node selection and the distance‑aware kernel for edge weight calculation – is novel and rational, with theoretical justification (Propositions 1–4) and ablation studies confirming each module’s contribution. (R2, R4; R3 also notes the ablation study is helpful)
- Comprehensive experiments across five benchmark datasets (Cora‑CA, DBLP, Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed) including comparison with multiple expansion methods (Table 1) and hypergraph learning methods (Table 2), plus embedding visualization and complexity analysis. (R1, R2)
- The equivalence of AdE to weighted clique expansion in 3‑uniform hypergraphs (Proposition 4) provides a theoretical guarantee of expressiveness. (R4)
- The paper is well written and easy to follow. (R2, R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal performance gains:** The numerical results in both Table 1 and Table 2 show only very small improvements over the best baseline. For example, on Cora‑CA the gain is smaller than 1 standard deviation in both tables, and on Cora the gain is also within 1 std. (R1, R4) *Note: Reviewers 2 and 3 consider the method to outperform baselines, but they do not quantify the margin.*
- **Number of representative nodes:** The choice of always two representative nodes per hyperedge (regardless of hyperedge size) lacks justification. Why not three or an adaptive number? This essentially approximates any hyperedge’s internal connectivity with a “barbell‑shaped” subgraph, which seems unintuitive. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Selection criterion for representative nodes:** The use of the sum of scaled attribute features (S) to pick representative nodes is not motivated. Why would a large (or small) sum of scaled attributes be relevant for representing the hyperedge? (R1)
- **Theoretical depth of propositions:** Propositions 1–4 are straightforward or trivial. The distance metric in Eq. (3) is obviously non‑negative, commutative, and assigns higher values to similar attribute pairs. Proposition 4 is trivially true because AdE connects every pair in a 3‑node hyperedge. (R1; R2 considers the theoretical proof a strength, so there is reviewer disagreement on this point.)
- **Ambiguity in Proposition 4:** The claim that AdE is equivalent to weighted clique expansion in 3‑uniform hypergraphs is confusing because AdE dynamically learns edge weights, whereas standard weighted clique expansion uses fixed weights. Clarification is needed. (R2)
- **Missing comparisons with state‑of‑the‑art expansion methods:** The paper does not compare with node‑degree preserving hypergraph projection [1] (R3). Moreover, recent hypergraph contrastive learning models reportedly outperform You are AllSet (AllDeepSet), but such methods are not compared (R4). Clique/star‑based expansions are not representative of the current state of the art. (R3)
- **Homophily assumption unverified:** Using node features to compute edge weights assumes an underlying homophily that is not well captured by the original hyperedges. The paper does not justify that such homophily exists or that the projected graph will capture it better. (R3)
- **Limitation of pairwise weights for high‑order relations:** Even with dynamic edge weights, the expansion still generates only pairwise relations. For example, in a 4‑node hyperedge {a,b,c,d}, the method cannot distinguish between a scenario where {a,b,c} form a strong triple and {a,b} and {b,c} form strong pairs but {a,c} does not. This is a fundamental limitation of clique‑based expansions. (R4)
- **Scalability concern:** The method reconstructs the graph and finds the maximum distance within each hyperedge each time. Time complexity will increase significantly when the average hyperedge size is large. (R4)
- **Dataset details incomplete:** Lack of information about dataset format (e.g., whether hyperedges have features, how hypergraphs differ from their graph counterparts, size, degree distribution, average hyperedge size). (R2, R3)
- **Limited task scope:** The method is evaluated only on node classification. Applicability to other tasks such as clustering or hyperedge prediction is not explored. (R3)
- **Two‑stage training:** The loss is applied separately to GSi‑Net and the kernel function, indicating the model is not end‑to‑end. This contrasts with unified approaches. (R4)
- **Minor typo:** “in a 3‑unifirm hyperedge” at the end of Section 4. (R1)
