## Merged Review

### Summary
GeoRDe is an RNA inverse folding method that predicts sequences from backbone structures by combining a geometric graph neural network (GVP), an RNAformer module (inspired by AlphaFold2's EvoFormer), secondary structure labels, and RNA language model features. The method aims to learn geometric constraints of RNA molecules. Experiments on training data, CASP15, and RNA-Puzzle datasets show substantially higher nucleotide recovery than existing baselines. However, reviewers raise major concerns about limited algorithmic novelty (the approach closely reuses protein design components), poor presentation quality (citation errors, inconsistent notation, AI-generated-sounding text, lack of appendix), insufficient evaluation rigor (missing dataset details, no standard deviations, cherry-picked structure examples, unclear baseline sources), and weak biological interpretation.

### Strengths
- Tackles an important and challenging problem: RNA inverse folding, which is structurally and data-limited (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- Achieves significantly higher nucleotide recovery compared to current SOTA baselines on multiple datasets (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 5; Reviewer 2 notes the pipeline might work better with more care).
- Interesting chimeric architecture that integrates geometric graph networks (GVP), triangular attention from AlphaFold2, and RNA language model features (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 5).
- Identifies a limitation of existing graph-based structure encoders for RNA design, which is an inspiring observation for the field (Reviewer 5).
- Conducts extensive experiments with cross-comparison on multiple datasets (CASP15, RNA-Puzzle) and includes ablation studies (Reviewer 5; Reviewer 4 questions the rigor of these experiments).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and originality:** The method is a minor modification of gRNAde with additional features (secondary structure labels, language model embeddings) (Reviewer 1). Most techniques are directly transferred from protein design: GVP, language models, and the SeqFormer module which is a one-to-one copy of AlphaFold2's EvoFormer (Algorithm 6) applied to nucleotides (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). Incorporating secondary structure is not new (already done in RDesign) (Reviewer 5). The algorithmic contribution is marginal (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4, Reviewer 5).
- **Poor presentation and writing quality:**
  - Citation style is inconsistent and often misplaced (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4, Reviewer 5).
  - Math equations are not compiled correctly; notation varies (e.g., sequence length denoted as "N" then "seq_len") (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
  - Inconsistent naming: the module is introduced as "RNAformer" but later called "SeqFormer" (Reviewer 2).
  - Mixed writing styles: pseudocode in text (Section 3.1, 3.4.2) without clear mathematical description; "message(...) -> update" is unclear (Reviewer 4).
  - Parts of the introduction (e.g., line 60-70) and other phrases (line 161-162) feel AI-generated (Reviewer 2).
  - Figure 1 appears late and is sparsely referenced (Reviewer 4).
  - No appendix is provided, lacking detailed explanations (Reviewer 3).
  - Evaluation metrics are presented before the methodology, which is unusual (Reviewer 2).
- **Study design and scientific rigor issues:**
  - Choice of backbone atoms (C4', C1', N1/N9) is not justified and departs from the canonical RNA backbone definition (C3'-C5' phosphate bond used in RNA-TM score) (Reviewer 4).
  - The 12 Å nearest-neighbor cutoff is not motivated or ablated (Reviewer 4).
  - Self-consistency RMSD calculation: it appears to compute RMSD between forward-folded predicted sequence and the ground truth structure, ignoring the forward folding model's bias; should be RMSD/TM-score between forward-folded ground truth sequence and predicted sequence (Reviewer 4).
  - Datasets lack detailed description: no size, no train/test split explanation (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
  - Source of baseline results is unclear: were PIFOLD and other models retrained on the same data? (Reviewer 4). For PiFold, a protein design method, its strong performance on RNA is suspicious—did the authors replace its structural feature extraction? (Reviewer 5).
  - Results tables (Tables 1-4) report no standard deviations or confidence intervals, making significance assessment impossible (Reviewer 4).
  - CASP15 evaluation: overlap between CASP15 targets and training data is not discussed; only results from gRNAde training are shown, not from both gRNAde and RDesign datasets (Reviewer 4).
  - Tertiary structure prediction section (Section 4.3) appears to be cherry-picking: only three cases are shown, with no statistical results; selection criteria are not given, and "low RMSD" is vague (Reviewer 4).
  - Conclusion and claims are overblown (e.g., "significant advancements", "powerful tool") without corresponding evidence (Reviewer 4).
- **Evaluation metrics are limited:** Almost exclusively nucleotide recovery is reported; self-consistency RMSD (scRMSD) is mentioned in methodology but not reported for all samples in Sections 4.1 and 4.2 (Reviewer 3). There is no assessment of whether predicted sequences fold into the target structure across the full test sets (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- **Biological interpretation lacking:** The results section reads as pure benchmarking, with no biological insights or hypotheses generated from the model (Reviewer 2).
- **Ablation study shows negligible LLM contribution:** The language model feature improves recovery only marginally; reviewers question its usefulness, especially on RNA aptamers without sequence evolution (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). No strategy to better leverage the LLM is proposed (Reviewer 3).
- **Questions raised as missing experiments/analysis:**
  - Why not consider multiple RNA structures, given RNA flexibility? (Reviewer 1)
  - Why not use generative models (diffusion/flow-matching) or iterative refinement for sequence generation? (Reviewer 3)
  - What other tasks can GeoRDe perform beyond inverse folding? (Reviewer 3)
  - Why are pair_transition and triangle_attention used in SeqFormer? How do they function specifically for RNA? (Reviewer 5)
  - The main claimed novelty (resolving geometric constraints) should be discussed in a dedicated subsection to clarify (Reviewer 5).
- **Reviewer disagreement:** Reviewers 2 and 4 rate the paper very low (1/5), citing fundamental issues in presentation, rigor, and originality; they question even the validity of the reported results. In contrast, Reviewer 1 rates 5/5 and Reviewer 5 rates 3/5, praising the empirical performance and the identification of a constraint in graph encoders. This split suggests that the strengths do not convincingly outweigh the weaknesses for the lower-scoring reviewers.
