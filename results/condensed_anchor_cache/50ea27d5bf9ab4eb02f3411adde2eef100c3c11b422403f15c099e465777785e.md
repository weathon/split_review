## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper tackles the robust fairness problem—the amplified accuracy disparity between classes during adversarial training. The authors empirically observe that under adversarial attacks, predictions for the worst class are biased towards classes similar to the worst class, not towards easy classes. They theoretically and empirically show that robust fairness deteriorates as the distance between classes decreases. To address this, they propose Distance-Aware Fair Adversarial Training (DAFA), which assigns distinct loss weights and adversarial margins to each class based on class similarity (measured using the model’s output prediction probability). The method encourages a robustness trade-off among similar classes. Experiments on three datasets validate that DAFA maintains average robust accuracy while significantly improving worst robust accuracy. The method is agnostic and is empirically validated by combining it with TRADES and PGD.

### Strengths
- Clear motivation: Figures 1–3 illustrate what similar and dissimilar classes are and how they affect class-wise performance.
- Empirical verification that misclassified samples from the worst class are biased towards similar classes, supported by theoretical analysis (e.g., robust errors monotonically decrease with class gap, as shown in Section 4.1).
- Novel method that assigns distinct loss weights and adversarial margins based on class similarity, going beyond existing work (e.g., CFA) by considering the interaction between classes.
- Method is agnostic and validated by combining with TRADES and PGD frameworks.
- Experiments conducted on three widely used datasets.
- Claims are supported by both theoretical and empirical analysis.
- Paper is easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- The paper rambles until page 6; the introduction is verbose and could be more concise.
- Class similarity is measured using class prediction probability rather than embedding similarity, which is confusing and not justified.
- The meaning of red and blue arrows in Figure 1 is not explained.
- Proofreading needed; sentences such as “Allocating a larger adversarial margin to the hard class than to adjacent classes has the effect of pushing the DB away from the hard class’s center by an amount corresponding to the difference in their adversarial margins.” should be simplified.
- The update rule for \(\mathcal{W}\) may produce negative values (e.g., when a class consistently remains the best), leading to negative robust radii (\(\mathcal{W}_y \epsilon\)), which is unreasonable. The authors do not address this or its implications.
- The update process for \(\mathcal{W}\) appears time-inefficient for datasets with many classes; time cost compared to other baselines is not reported.
- Missing analysis of the theoretical and empirical range of \(\mathcal{W}\).
- No comparison of weight curves between the proposed method and baselines; it is unclear how they differ.
- **Major concern**: Unfair comparison with baselines due to different experiment settings. For example, CFA uses a Fairness-Aware Weight-Average (FAWA) strategy that requires more epochs and a different learning rate schedule (decay at 100th and 150th epoch in 200 epochs). The authors’ settings likely underperform CFA. Original settings for all baselines should be used.
- The research thread of robust fairness is presented confusingly; the main thread focuses on adjusting objective functions in AT, not long-tailed classification. The introduction should be revised accordingly.
- Some theorems appear very close to prior work [1,3]; it should be clarified which are re-deductions and which are new contributions.
- There is a mismatch between motivation and method: the schedule in equations (6)–(9) still focuses on easy vs. hard classes even though class-wise similarity is involved. Theoretical analysis on how these configurations improve class-wise robustness is missing.
- The definition of class-wise distance as the average distance to all other classes may be less relevant than distance to the closest class; rationale for this choice is not provided.
- Missing sensitivity analysis for trade-off hyperparameter \(\beta\).
- Experiments are limited to TRADES; more adversarial training frameworks should be tested.
- Several baselines are missing, e.g., Group-DRO [1].
