## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper develops a science-constrained learning (SCL) framework for solving PDEs with neural networks. It shows that finding a weak solution of a PDE is equivalent to a constrained learning problem with worst-case losses, connecting to ideas from adversarial robustness. The framework integrates structural constraints (e.g., symmetries) and observational knowledge. A practical algorithm is proposed to solve the resulting constrained problem, aiming to reduce sensitivity to hyperparameters (e.g., loss weights, collocation points) and computational cost. The approach is evaluated on a few small PDE problems against neural network baselines; no comparisons to conventional numerical solvers (FEM) are made.

### Strengths
- Creative adaptation of adversarial robustness results to neural PDE solvers, allowing more formal reasoning about the PINN setup and connecting directly to the weak form solution.
- The proposed SCL framework directly addresses the hyperparameter sensitivity issue in PINNs by framing the problem as constrained learning with worst-case losses, avoiding extensive tuning of loss weights.
- The paper is mostly well written and formatted; the development of SCL is clearly described.
- The algorithm is easy to follow, and the mathematical proofs help explain the benefits of the method.
- The work tackles important and practical problems in physics-informed learning: reducing computational costs and avoiding hyperparameter tuning.
- Experimental results show that the proposed formulation outperforms neural network baselines in some cases.
- The paper provides ways to incorporate structural knowledge, observational knowledge, and science-constrained learning.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of comparison to conventional PDE solvers:** No comparisons to traditional methods (FEM, FDM) are performed. The paper only compares against other neural networks on tiny problems, making it unclear whether the method is practically useful for solving PDEs. A recent review (McGreivy & Hakim, Nature Machine Intelligence, 2024) criticizes such weak baselines. Reviewer 1 strongly emphasizes this as a fundamental weakness; Reviewer 2 does not comment on this; Reviewers 3 and 4 do not raise it.
- **Guarantee undermined by sampling:** The theoretical guarantee relies on sampling from a distribution ψ_α, which becomes difficult exactly when the error bound is tight. Using Metropolis–Hastings sampling throws away the guarantee unless uniform ergodicity of the Markov chain is proven. This parallels the original adversarial robustness result where sampling near worst-case inputs is as hard as finding them. (Reviewer 1; also implied by Reviewer 4’s question on convergence conditions.)
- **Novelty concerns:** Algorithm 1 can be seen as a variant of DNN training with learnable loss weights, common in multi-task learning, lacking novelty. The reviewer has difficulty distinguishing the contribution from the work of Paris Perdikaris and his group (e.g., Daw et al., arXiv:2207.02338; Wang et al., arXiv:2203.07404; SIAM paper on constrained learning). Broader literature search and comparisons against adaptive sampling methods by Perdikaris and others are missing. (Reviewers 3 and 4; Reviewer 4 also calls it a “rephrasing of PDE BVPs into a constraint problem solved straightforwardly”.)
- **Missing error bars and reproducibility:** The dependence on random seeds is unknown; error bars should be added. (Reviewer 4)
- **Paper readability:** The paper is difficult to follow; information is not always presented in relevant paragraphs and sections, making it challenging to discern key ideas from supplementary notes. Background could be shortened to give the new technique more room. (Reviewers 2 and 4)
- **Claims not fully justified or clarified:**
  - The claim that SCL addresses sensitivity to the number of training points is not clearly explained; either a proof or a statement that this is an empirical observation is needed. (Reviewer 2)
  - The key claim that the method does not require extensive tuning of coefficients (compared to penalty methods) and guarantees generalization individually for each constraint needs elaboration and references. (Reviewer 4)
  - Algorithm 1 still includes hyperparameters η_p and η_d; training heuristics are still necessary, weakening the claim of no extensive tuning. (Reviewer 4)
  - The statement that causality arises naturally by solving (SCL′)(M) without manual tuning is not explained. (Reviewer 4)
- **Convergence analysis missing:** Under what conditions does Algorithm 1 converge? References such as Chamon et al., 2023 and Elenter et al., 2024 could be valuable. (Reviewer 4)
- **Specific missing comparisons/explanations requested:**
  - How does Algorithm 1’s update schedule compare to that in Paris Perdikaris’ SIAM paper (Section 2.4) and the loss-weighting approach of arXiv:2007.04542? (Reviewer 3)
  - What was used to compute ground truth solutions, how long did that take relative to the compared methods? A Pareto curve of compute time vs. accuracy (including FEM with different discretizations) is needed. (Reviewer 1)
  - The reduction in conditioning number claimed in Appendix B needs clarification. (Reviewer 2)
- **Minor:** (Lines 1730, 1733, etc.) “Fig” should be “Fig.” (Reviewer 4)
