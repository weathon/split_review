## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents a 1.9 billion-parameter ViT-G/8 MAE trained on over 8 billion microscopy image crops from a curated dataset (Phenoprints-16M) and a manually curated 40-class Anax dataset. The authors explore data curation strategies and propose selecting intermediate transformer block representations via linear probing for biologically relevant tasks. Compared to a smaller ViT-L/8 MAE, the larger model achieves a 60% improvement in linear separability of genetic perturbations and the best overall performance on biological recall and replicate consistency benchmarks. Reviewers note substantial effort but disagree on novelty, evaluation rigor, and reproducibility.

### Strengths
- Cell microscopy analysis is an important problem with large impact on computational pathology and bioinformatics research (R1, R4).
- The curated dataset (Phenoprints-16M) uses biologically informed filtering (statistical significance vs. negative controls) which may be a worthwhile contribution (R1, R2, R4). The manually curated 40-class Anax dataset (containing 348–384 genes) provides a useful proxy task for linear probing and evaluation (R2).
- The authors performed careful tuning of model architectural and training details (R1) and the required effort to produce the dataset and train the large-scale model was substantial (R3).
- The paper is very well written and provides clear explanations of experiments, dataset curation, and visual aids that enhance understanding (R3, R4). The figures are thoughtfully designed and illustrate core concepts effectively (R4).
- The exploration of data curation, proxy linear probing tasks for evaluation during training, and selection of optimal intermediate layer representations for downstream tasks are important aspects of scaling foundation models for microscopy (R2).
- The evidence that mid-level representations from ViT models (both pretrained on natural or microscopy images) can outperform deeper layers for biologically meaningful representations is valuable for the field, as it reduces inference cost and potential fine-tuning (R3). Though noted as not novel in other domains, this demonstration is beneficial for cell microscopy (R3).
- The new ViT-G/8 MAE model outperforms previous SOTA (ViT-L/8 MAE) on multiple benchmarks (R2, R3). The results are convincing to some extent (R4).
- A minority reviewer (R3, rating 8) is substantially more positive overall, praising the quality of the writing, the value of the mid-level representation finding, and the substantial effort, despite agreeing on some novelty concerns.

### Weaknesses
- The methodological contribution is limited. The paper applies MAE without comparing to more effective SSL algorithms like iBOT or DINOv2 on microscopy data (R1, R3). There is no comparison of MAE vs. DINO-ViT trained on microscopy images to determine whether improvements come from the training method or just the data (R2, R3). Additional experiments with DINO-ViT trained on microscopy are needed to validate the generality of the proposed data curation and block selection strategies (R2).
- The performance boost over the much smaller MAE-L/8 trained on RPI-93M is very marginal for several metrics, especially biological recall across all four gene network databases in Table 2 (R1, R2). Gains are not substantial enough to justify the increased computational cost of the larger model (R2). A detailed analysis of specific gene sets where the larger model performs better is necessary (R2).
- Lack of ablation studies. The paper does not explore the impact of different masking ratios during pre-training, varying patch size, or whether improvements come from increased capacity vs. the proposed data curation or block selection strategies (R2). No experimental comparison of different data curation strategies on microscopy (e.g., statistical significance filtering vs. magnitude-based filtering vs. unfiltered dataset) is provided (R2).
- The selection of optimal block for a specific downstream task based on balanced accuracy on linear classification is a standard practice, not a novel contribution (R2). More sophisticated methods (e.g., learned attention mechanism, multi-objective optimization) could have been explored (R2).
- The biological recall metrics for trimmed vs. untrimmed versions of the MAE models trained on microscopy images are mostly the same, which is counterintuitive to the claim that intermediate block representations are more useful (R2). This suggests the trimming strategy may not be as effective as claimed or the metric is not sensitive enough (R2). Other evaluation metrics (e.g., kNN, fine-tuning) should be used to better capture the impact (R2, R3). Evaluations on downstream tasks using a varying number of blocks via fine-tuning would be useful, even if limited to a select number of layers (R3).
- There are serious concerns about reproducibility and public availability. It is not explicitly stated whether the ViT-G/8 model weights, the Anax dataset, or the Phenoprints-16M dataset will be made publicly available (R1, R2, R3, R4). Metrics on private datasets are insufficient for evaluating scientific contribution (R2). The quality control and dataset curation steps in Appendix A are too general; much more detail is needed for reproducibility (R4).
- Evaluation protocol concerns: Models are compared with different hyperparameters, raising questions about the validity of direct comparisons (R4). The benchmarks (especially Table 2) lack confidence intervals/error bars, making it impossible to assess statistical significance (R1, R4). Ideally, the authors would train models with the same hyperparameters to confirm differences (R4). Additionally, the evaluation should include a phenotypic screening benchmark with scaffold-based splits for chemical perturbations and an independent dataset to assess batch effects (e.g., mode of action prediction via linear probing) (R4).
- The bolding in Table 2 is inconsistent (R3).
- The performance of CA-MAE (worse than other baselines) is not discussed or explained (R2). The authors should provide an analysis of why CA-MAE underperforms.
- Figure 5 compares the ViT-G/8 MAE model against DINO-V2 G/14 pretrained on natural images, which is an unnecessary comparison given that Table 2 already shows pretraining on microscopy is critical (R3). A fairer comparison would be against MAE-L/8 and RPI-93M (R3).
- The paper claims importance of data quality, but these issues (imbalance, undersampling, overall integrity) are general computer vision concerns, not specific to microscopy (R4). The novelty is extremely limited because the microscopy-specific issue of batch effects is not sufficiently addressed (R4).
