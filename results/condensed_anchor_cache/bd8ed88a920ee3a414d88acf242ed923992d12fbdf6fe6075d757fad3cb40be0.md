## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper applies Rectified Flow (Liu et al., ICLR 2023) to Stable Diffusion (SD) to create a one-step text-to-image generator (InstaFlow). The reflow procedure straightens ODE trajectories, improving the noise–image coupling and enabling effective distillation. A Stacked U-Net architecture (two concatenated U-Nets) is introduced. InstaFlow achieves FID 23.3 on MS COCO 2017-5k (vs. 37.2 for progressive distillation) and FID 13.1 on MS COCO 2014-30k in 0.09 seconds, outperforming StyleGAN-T (13.9 in 0.1s). Training costs 199 A100 GPU days. The paper is clearly written, well-organized, and presents thorough experiments with careful comparisons. The results are captivating and practically important for the community.

### Strengths
- **Impressive results**: One‑step generation with reasonable quality in ~0.09 seconds; FID 23.3 on COCO 2017 and 13.1 on COCO 2014, surpassing Progressive Distillation (37.2) and StyleGAN-T (13.9 in 0.1s). Training only 199 A100 GPU days.
- **Clear writing**: Provides a self-contained introduction to rectified flows; the paper is extremely well-structured and easy to follow.
- **Thorough evaluation**: Detailed ablation studies, multiple settings, and fair comparisons. Experiments convincingly support the method’s motivation.
- **Practical impact**: Making conditional diffusion work with one or very few steps is a major step forward; the resource‑performance tradeoff is attractive.
- **Architecture novelty**: Stacked U‑Net design is interesting; the paper shows it helps achieve the reported results.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty** (minority R1/R2): The pipeline is a direct application of the existing Rectified Flow framework to the coupling learned by SD. Parts were introduced previously; this is an incremental extension, more a technical exercise than an independent contribution.
- **“1‑step” definition** (R1): The one‑step model actually uses two concatenated U‑Nets, making it feel closer to a 2‑step process.
- **High‑frequency detail loss** (R1): Without the SDXL refined model, results show noticeable degradation in high‑frequency details.
- **Stacked U‑Net justification missing** (R2): No ablation study comparing the stacked structure against simply increasing the depth or number of channels of a conventional U‑Net. It is unclear whether gains are due to the architecture or just more parameters.
- **Missing comparison with cascaded models** (R2): The evaluation only covers latent models; a comparison with open cascaded models (e.g., DeepFloyd IF) is needed to assess true state‑of‑the‑art.
- **Artifacts and quality trade‑off** (R3): Generated images contain distortions (faces, fine‑grained textures, complex object boundaries) inherited from the 2‑Rectified Flow teacher; the one‑step distillation amplifies some artifacts.
- **Storage overhead** (R4): Creating the large (noise, image) pair dataset for reflow/distillation imposes additional storage requirements.
- **Distillation vs. reflow distinction unclear** (R4): The intrinsic difference between the two processes is not sufficiently clarified—distillation aims at zero‑timestep ODE target, while reflow applies to all timesteps.
- **Noise scheduler conflict** (R4): SD uses a normal diffusion scheduler, but reflow requires a linear scheduler; it is unclear how this mismatch is handled and what time embedding is used with the SD UNet.
- **Prediction target choice** (R4): The paper predicts x₁−x₀ instead of x₁−xₜ; the rationale for this choice is not explained.
- **Training pipeline details not justified** (R2): The multi‑step training (Appendix D) changes batch size (step 2) instead of tuning the learning rate; switches from L2 to LPIPS objective (step 4) rather than using a combination from the start. The switching points are not motivated.
- **Missing comparison with DDIM inversion** (R1): It would be informative to see results with very few steps (≈2) using methods like DDIM inversion in a flow context.
