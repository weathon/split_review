## Merged Review

### Summary
EVO-RDesign augments RDesign by injecting evolutionary priors from a pretrained RNA language model (RNA-FM) via adapter modules (weighted token embedding, structural adapter, attention bias guidance). It achieves 3.5% higher sequence recovery on RNAsolo and 5.1%/4.1% on RNA-Puzzles/Rfam over RDesign, with zero-shot generalization. The paper is clearly written but incremental; most reviewers see limited methodological novelty and important unresolved concerns about evaluation, data leakage, and missing baselines.

### Strengths
- Performance improvement: EVO-RDesign outperforms RDesign across multiple benchmarks (3.5% on RNAsolo, 5.1% on RNA-Puzzles, 4.1% on Rfam) and shows zero-shot generalization.
- The idea of using evolutionary information from RNA language models for structure-based design is interesting and relevant.
- Experiments consider various sequence lengths, providing a comprehensive view (Reviewer 4); datasets are processed for sequence and structure similarity (Reviewer 3).
- Evaluation on two benchmark datasets (RNAsolo, RNA-Puzzles) with comparison to baselines (Reviewer 2).
- The approach enriches the less-explored field of RNA design, potentially aiding biotechnologies like CRISPR (Reviewer 4).
- Minority reviewer (Reviewer 4, score 5) finds the experiment solid and the paper easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: Main contribution is a module that conditions a pretrained foundation model on structural features from RDesign; the core building blocks (RNA-FM, GNN) are existing (Reviewers 1,3,4). “This is yet another RDesign with an adapter” (Reviewer 1); “the key information is just retained from two pre-trained models” (Reviewer 4).
- **Data leakage / contamination risk**: As noted in gRNAde, the dataset may have information leakage, which “essentially puts a question mark on all the results” (Reviewer 1). RNA-FM trained on raw sequences may cause information leakage if used at the output layer; e.g., the model could bias toward sequences seen in pretraining (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient evaluation metrics**: Only sequence recovery is reported in most sections, which fails to capture structural quality (Reviewer 1). Even the structure recovery (Figure 3) yields poor average TM-scores (~0.2–0.3) (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 suggests AUROC/AUPRC and more quantitative in-silico analysis (e.g., what RNA types/lengths perform better).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**: Only RDesign is compared as the main deep learning competitor; gRNAde is cited but not included in evaluation (Reviewers 1,3). No comparison with other structure encoders or RNA language models (Reviewer 2). External datasets like Rfam are already used but more could be tested (Reviewer 4).
- **Technical details insufficient for reproducibility**: Structural adapter and attention bias guidance not fully explained; “it is hard to follow” (Reviewer 2). Specifics needed: K in K-nearest neighbors (Line 202–203), justification for choosing RDesign and RNA-FM, what FFN and MPNN are in Fig. 2, the four nucleotide types (Reviewer 2). The transformer layer in Equation 5 needs more discussion: “Why does the RNA structural featurizer have to go through a transformer layer …?” (Reviewer 4).
- **Questions overlap with criticisms**: 
    - Why train on sequence recovery at all? For design, diversity is important; a measure of diversity should be provided (Reviewer 3).
    - How does the inclusion of the LLM aid design of RNA aptamers? (Reviewer 1).
    - How does EVO-RDesign handle large-scale RNA structures? What is the mean sequence size? (Reviewer 2).
    - What exactly are the “evolutionary priors” used? Give examples (Reviewer 2). The concept does not appear in Fig. 2 (Reviewer 2).
    - Why no code release? (Reviewer 3).
    - Is in-silico folding (RhoFold) supported by quantitative analysis? Provide conclusions from that experiment (Reviewer 2,4).
    - Future wet-lab validation plan? (Reviewer 2).
    - Can the model give reliable outputs when the input structure is not in RNA-FM’s training set? (Reviewer 4).
- **No biological insights** from the result section; “except for a benchmark which is quite boring” (Reviewer 1).
- **Minority contrast**: Reviewer 4 (score 5) is more positive overall but still raises the same key weaknesses (information leakage, lack of novelty, missing details from previous reviews) and notes the paper “seems less significant to the RNA design field.”
