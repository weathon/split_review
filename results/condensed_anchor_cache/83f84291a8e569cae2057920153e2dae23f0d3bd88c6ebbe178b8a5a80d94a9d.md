## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes ExVideo, a post-tuning methodology to extend the temporal length of existing video diffusion models (specifically Stable Video Diffusion) with reduced training cost. It designs extension strategies for 3D convolution, temporal attention, and positional embedding. The resulting model, ExSVD, generates up to 5× the original number of frames (25 → 128) using only 1.5k GPU hours on 40k videos. The writing is clear, visual results are pleasing, and the approach is compatible with existing model architectures.

### Strengths

1. The writing is excellent—clean, clear, concise, and easy to understand. (Reviewer 1, 2, 3)
2. The method requires significantly fewer computational resources than training from scratch (8 A100 GPUs for one week; 1.5k GPU hours). (Reviewer 1)
3. Visual results are of good quality and the approach is easy to use/applicable to existing models. (Reviewer 3, 4)
4. The paper presents a well-thought-out methodology with clear description of extension strategies for different temporal architectures. (Reviewer 2)
5. The work addresses a critical challenge in video synthesis (generating longer, high-quality videos) and has potential impact on content creation, entertainment, etc. (Reviewer 2)
6. Reviewers note that the paper claims originality in extending video synthesis model capabilities and includes both automatic metrics and human evaluations. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses

1. **Limited novelty.** The proposed additions (an identity 3D convolution layer, trainable positional embedding) are commonly used techniques. Adding learnable parameters alone is not a contribution without theoretical/experimental justification of necessity. (Reviewer 1: “technique novelty is limited”; Reviewer 3: “no algorithm-level contribution, practical settings should be in appendix”; Reviewer 4: “simply adding learnable parameters cannot be a contribution”)
2. **Unclear motivation and insufficient comparison.** The motivation for adding an additional 3D convolution is not justified. What is the problem with directly fine-tuning the original model on 128 frames? Does it save memory or converge faster? Please provide numerical comparisons. (Reviewer 3) The paper does not compare with other long video generation models, especially on frame consistency over extended durations. (Reviewer 2) Experiments do not highlight the claimed extension ability – e.g., how existing methods fail when generation length increases (latter-timestep collapse). In Fig.3, baselines already show good temporal coherency; the main visible difference is text-alignment, which may come from the stronger baseline SVD, not extension. (Reviewer 4)
3. **Incomplete experiments and evaluation concerns.**
   - User study only compares with SVD (25 frames) vs ExSVD (128 frames). Users can easily tell the difference in length; this is not a fair comparison. (Reviewer 1)
   - VBench evaluation: How many frames does ExSVD generate? Other methods (SVD, Kling) generate short videos – are these metrics sensitive to frame count? (Reviewer 1)
   - Table 2 only boldens the best result in the total score column; more bold labels needed. (Reviewer 1)
   - Quantitative results are not strong enough: using 8 A100 GPUs for a week is not clearly cost-efficient; improvement is marginal. (Reviewer 4)
4. **Missing ablation study.** No ablation of the proposed components (e.g., initialization scheme for identity 3D convolution, effect of switching positional embeddings). (Reviewer 2, 3, 4)
5. **Lack of implementation details.** The “Identity 3D Convolution” and “Trainable Positional Embedding” need detailed illustrations with formulas and diagrams. (Reviewer 3) The exact training and inference algorithms are not clearly outlined. (Reviewer 2)
6. **Generalization to other models is not demonstrated.** Evaluation is only on Stable Video Diffusion; more evidence needed. (Reviewer 2)
7. **Paper layout issues:** Figures 3 and 4 are placed at the end, not visually pleasing. (Reviewer 3)
8. **Scalability and limitations:** How does ExVideo perform as model complexity/number of parameters scales? The paper mentions ExSVD struggles with accurately synthesizing human portraits – please elaborate on nature and potential solutions. (Reviewer 2)
9. **Dataset diversity:** Are there plans to train on other datasets to evaluate robustness? (Reviewer 2)
