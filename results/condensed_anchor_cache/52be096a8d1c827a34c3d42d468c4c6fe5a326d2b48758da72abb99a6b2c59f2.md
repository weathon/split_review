## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MoMoK, a mixture-of-modality knowledge experts framework for MMKG completion, using relation-guided experts, joint decision, and expert information disentanglement. Experiments on four MMKG benchmarks show significant improvements over baselines, with robustness in complex scenarios. The paper is well-motivated but has limitations in novelty, experimental scope, and clarity of certain components.

### Strengths
- **Novel design for relation-aware modality experts**: The relation-guided modal knowledge expert network adaptively aggregates multi-view embeddings based on relational context, addressing a gap in prior works.
- **Effective disentanglement via mutual information minimization**: The ExID module reduces redundancy between modalities, enhancing expert independence and performance.
- **Strong empirical performance**: MoMoK significantly outperforms recent baselines on four public MMKG benchmarks, including complex scenarios with noise, sparsity, and missing modalities.
- **Thorough experimental analysis**: Ablation studies, case studies, and visualizations validate each module's contribution, and five well-defined RQs structure the evaluation.
- **Robustness in challenging conditions**: The model shows resilience to noise, data sparsity, and missing modalities, as demonstrated in complex environment tests.
- **Well-written and self-contained**: The motivation and algorithm are clearly presented, with detailed theoretical proofs and sufficient experimental evidence.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing metrics and incomplete reporting**: Table 2 only shows Hit@1 and MRR for MKG-W and MKG-Y; include Hit@3 and Hit@10 for MoMoK, AdaMF, and MMRNS to fully assess gains. Also, parameter counts for all baseline models should be provided for fair complexity comparison.
- **Missing evaluation on standard MMKG datasets**: The paper omits classic datasets such as WN9-IMG, FB-IMG, WN18-IMG, FB15K-237-IMG, and datasets used in baselines (FB-15K, WN18, WN9), limiting generalizability.
- **Unclear experimental details**: Several important implementation aspects are not clarified: (a) which modalities are used for each dataset and how multiple images per entity are handled; (b) whether VGG and BERT are consistently used as encoders across all datasets; (c) how raw structural embeddings are obtained; (d) the setting of number of experts \(K\) in main experiments; (e) the exact definition and impact of relation-aware temperature \(\mathcal{E}_r\) in GFN computation; (f) the operation for \(\mathcal{V}_{m,i}^{e} = \mathcal{W}_{m,i}(e_m)\) (simple multiplication?) and the shapes of learnable matrices; (g) whether GPU memory and time in Table 4 refer to training or inference, and whether time is per sample or per epoch.
- **Inadequate ablation scope**: Ablation experiments are limited to MKG-W and DB15K; results on MKG-Y and KVC16K are missing. Additionally, components like adaptive fusion, noise \(\delta_{r}\), and relational \(\epsilon_{m}\) show minimal individual impact in some settings and need more detailed analysis. The effect of using only the 'J' modality score (instead of sum) is also unexplored.
- **Limited novelty relative to prior work**: The claim that prior work lacks relational context is weakened by existing methods like MoSE that also consider relational context. The paper does not sufficiently discuss differences from MoSE and IMF in the related work.
- **Concerns about expert network design**: The expert networks rely on raw embeddings, which may compress information and limit the diversity captured within each modality. The paper does not address how to ensure sufficient diversity or mitigate information loss.
- **Insufficient explanation for disentanglement loss**: The use of conditional probabilities of different entities in formula (8) for mutual information minimization is not adequately justified. The improvement of about 1 point from CLUB loss with a very small weight (\(\lambda=10^{-4}\)) is not well explained.
- **Missing discussion on LLM context**: The paper lacks a discussion on the importance of KG completion in the era of LLMs and how traditional embedding-based approaches compare to LLM-based approaches. Integration with a unified multimodal LLM representation is not explored; a performance comparison with such an approach is missing.
- **Notation and presentation issues**: Formula (1) uses ambiguous notation \(\mathcal{E}_h\); equation (3) seems to miss relation \(r\) in modality-specific scores; fonts in Figures 3 and 4 are inconsistent; the paper has a typo "constrative" for "contrastive" and an incorrect marking of the second-best method in Table 2 (MKG-W MRR: MMRNS 35.03, not IMF 34.50).
- **Specific performance anomaly**: The paper does not adequately explain why MoMoK achieves lower MRR than AdaMF on MKG-Y.
- **Insufficient detail on Section 5.4**: The construction of training datasets and the evaluation protocol for complex environment experiments need more careful description.
