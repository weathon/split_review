## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DynaCL, an unsupervised contrastive learning framework for time series that defines positive pairs as temporally adjacent steps and uses N-pair loss to dynamically treat all batch samples as positives/negatives. An extended version DynaCL-M adds feature prediction and dynamic margins. Experiments on multiple public datasets show competitive clustering and classification performance. The paper also highlights that high clustering metrics (Davies-Bouldin Index, Silhouette Score) do not guarantee useful downstream representations. Reviewers generally agree the problem is well-motivated but raise concerns about limited novelty, missing baselines and datasets, insufficient related work, and weak experimental evaluation.

### Strengths
- Clear motivation, well-structured presentation, and consistent notation.
- Insightful analysis contrasting clustering metrics with downstream task performance, revealing that high clustering scores do not guarantee useful representations.
- The dynamic margin in DynaCL-M is a valuable addition, allowing adaptive separation based on temporal proximity.
- One reviewer (R2) noted fairly good novelty, though others found it marginal (see Weaknesses).
- Comparison against state-of-the-art baselines on clustering quality and event classification is uncommon but meaningful in the time series representation domain; one reviewer (R3) found experiments relatively comprehensive, while another (R4) considered them weak.
- Experiments are detailed across different setups (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty & related work**: The MP-Xent loss closely resembles existing approaches like SoftCLT/soft temporal contrastive learning, raising novelty concerns. The innovation is modest; the addition of positive samples can be seen as a special case of prior work [2]. Related works are insufficient; missing discussions of SoftCLT (ICLR 2024), Dynamic Bad Pair Mining (ICLR 2024), and other recent methods. References are not up-to-date. The specific contributions over numerous existing contrastive learning methods for time series are unclear.
- **Motivation clarity**: There is a logical issue: the paper claims positive/negative pair construction is the main focus, but contrastive learning has three key components (pairs, encoder, loss), weakening the motivation. The opening claim about “understanding events in time series” lacks explanation of how modeling moments (the approach) helps understand events. The statement about “complicated statistical approaches for sampling positives” is not well-supported by current references.
- **Experimental evaluation**: Only three datasets are used; should include the 125 UCR, 29 UEA benchmarks, UCIHAR, and other domains. Missing important baselines: SoftCLT, TimeDRL, InfoMin, SimMTM, and others. Missing tasks: semi-supervised learning, transfer learning, forecasting, and sequential prediction tasks. Experiments are not comprehensive enough; the evaluation is considered weak by some reviewers.
- **Clarity & details**: Confusion about instance-wise vs. temporal contrastive learning in formula (3); the paper emphasizes temporal but the formulation suggests both, requiring clearer differentiation (especially versus SoftCLT). In Section 4.5, it is unclear whether all self-supervised models use the same backbone architecture; consistency is needed for fair comparison. The extended DynaCL-M fails to perform consistently well across the two downstream tasks even with additional augmentations, which lacks explanation.
