## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Sparse Matrix Tuning (SMT), a parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) method for large language models. SMT uses a gradient-based criterion to identify the most significant submatrices of the model's weight matrices and updates only those while freezing the rest. This reduces computational and memory costs relative to full fine-tuning while achieving competitive or superior performance compared to existing PEFT methods such as LoRA, DoRA, and SpIEL. Experiments across multiple model sizes and datasets show SMT outperforms these baselines and does not suffer from the performance saturation observed with other methods. The paper also provides analyses indicating that attention layers, especially the value vector, are more critical for fine-tuning.

### Strengths
- The problem addressed (bridging the gap between PEFT and full fine-tuning while reducing cost) is important, and the evaluation results are positive across multiple model sizes and datasets (Reviewers 1, 5).
- SMT inherits the performance benefits of prior sparse tuning methods while addressing their shortcomings in computational and memory efficiency (Reviewer 3).
- The paper provides an insightful comparison of activation-awareness vs. gradient-awareness, selecting the gradient-based approach as more effective for identifying meaningful submatrices (Reviewer 2).
- SMT offers an improved strategy for identifying impactful sub-networks, potentially boosting fine-tuning efficacy by focusing on parameters that most influence downstream performance (Reviewer 2).
- Analysis reveals that LoRA, DoRA, and SpIEL suffer from performance saturation as tunable parameters scale, whereas SMT maintains consistent performance without this limitation (Reviewer 3).
- The gradient-based parameter selection enables analysis of critical parameter distributions within LLMs: results indicate that the attention module, particularly the value (V) vector, requires more intensive fine-tuning. This observation provides valuable insights and could inform future studies in PEFT (Reviewers 3, 4).
- The paper provides extensive analyses, including insightful ablations in Sec. 5 and the appendix (Reviewer 5).
- The sparse matrix tuning method appears reasonable for improving downstream task performance, and the two findings (attention layers and value matrix being more influential) may inspire new insights in the field (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Gap between claimed contribution and actual contribution**: The paper claims SMT aims to "close the performance gap between PEFT and full fine-tuning," but the primary focus appears to be on improving computational/storage efficiency of existing sparse tuning methods rather than introducing fundamentally new fine-tuning strategies. The paper does not clearly articulate how SMT achieves performance gains beyond what is already attainable through existing sparse fine-tuning techniques, which also focus on efficiency. The claim of bridging the gap with full fine-tuning lacks theoretical justification (Reviewers 1, 3; also noted as a novelty concern by Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of theoretical support**: The claim that SMT can close the performance gap with full fine-tuning is not backed by theoretical explanation. The paper discusses implementation and efficiency improvements without explaining why fine-tuning a sparse matrix should lead to performance comparable to full fine-tuning (e.g., how it preserves essential parameter interactions or what relevant properties of sparse representations justify this potential). The paper does not delve into mathematical properties of the selected submatrices, such as spectral characteristics or ability to capture the underlying data manifold (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**: The paper lacks comparison with other sparse tuning methods (e.g., delta tuning, diff pruning approaches, and random masking, such as those in https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02596 and https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.931.pdf). It also does not compare with recent methods that aim to bridge the PEFT/FT gap, such as LoRA-GA [1] and LoRA-Pro [2]. Additionally, no comparison is made with other gradient-based parameter selection methods (Reviewers 1, 3, 5).
- **Insufficient detail on submatrix selection and significance metrics**: The paper lacks necessary conceptual explanations and mathematical formulations for submatrix selection and the significance metric, which could hinder reproducibility and a deeper understanding of SMT's implementation (Reviewer 2).
- **Warm-up stage dependence**: SMT requires a warm-up stage to identify the salient submatrix, and the number of warm-up iterations depends on the specific downstream task and dataset size. This complicates usage and limits generalization. It is unclear whether the warm-up phase can be avoided by reusing sub-block selection from a different (multi-task) experiment (Reviewers 4, 5).
- **Model reuse limitation**: Unlike LoRA, SMT updates a subset of parameters of the original model and changes the original weights, so the pretrained model cannot be directly reused for other downstream tasks (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of analysis on catastrophic forgetting**: The paper does not address whether SMT offers better resistance to catastrophic forgetting compared to other PEFT methods, leaving questions about its stability and robustness in long-term, iterative fine-tuning scenarios (Reviewer 2).
- **Unaddressed practical questions about block size and mask generalization**: The motivation for choosing 256 x 256 submatrix blocks is not explicitly justified; the trade-off between performance and efficiency for smaller (down to 1x1) or larger block sizes is not investigated. The paper does not discuss how the mask (selected submatrix) generalizes across tasks (Reviewer 5; also raised by Reviewer 1).
- **Questionable speed-up and cost claims**: The reported speed-ups in Table 1 (e.g., ~14x for LoRA) seem large compared to typical LoRA speed-ups (2-4x); the paper should cite work reporting similar speed-ups. The claim that gradient step computation cost is z% cheaper is questionable because full gradients may be needed in earlier layers to correctly propagate to the sparse matrix; same concern applies to activation costs in the forward pass (Reviewer 5).
- **Missing full fine-tuning results**: Full fine-tuning results are missing for some models in Table 2 (only provided for LLaMA-7B and LLaMA2-7B), which weakens the comparison with full fine-tuning (Reviewers 3, 5).
- **Presentation issues**: The paper contains missing articles, incorrect pluralization, tense inconsistencies within paragraphs, and excessive bold/italic formatting that can make text appear cluttered. Figure 1 could be made more visually clear. Tables (e.g., Table 3) could be better presented (Reviewer 1; also noted by Reviewer 3). Terminology inconsistency: "commonsense reasoning datasets" in Table 3 vs "COMMONSENSE" in Table 4 should use the same term (Reviewer 1).
