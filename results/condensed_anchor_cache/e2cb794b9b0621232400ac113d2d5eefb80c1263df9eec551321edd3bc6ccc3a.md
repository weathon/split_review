## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a meta-learning framework (MAML) combined with a graph neural network (GNN) backbone to learn a unified Hamiltonian representation that generalizes across different physical systems (e.g., mass-spring, two-body, etc.). The meta-training step enables few-shot adaptation to unseen systems with fewer data points compared to randomly initialized and pre-trained baselines. The authors analyze learned representations using centered kernel alignment (CKA) to argue that meta-trained models capture more task-specific features. The paper is well written and the motivation is clear. However, reviewers raise concerns about the fairness and breadth of baselines, the lack of investigation into limitations of meta-learning in this context, and insufficient evidence for true generalization.

### Strengths
- The paper is easy to follow; motivation for cross-domain generalization is clearly stated (R1, R2, R4).
- The work targets a novel and significant problem: learning Hamiltonian representations that generalize across different physical laws, unlike prior system-specific models (R1, R3, R4).
- Experiments show clear superiority of the meta-trained model over pre-trained and randomly initialized baselines across multiple metrics and adaptation data amounts (R1, R2, R3).
- Robustness is tested with different numbers of K-shot support set points (R1).
- CKA analysis provides insight into why meta-learning yields better adaptation (R1).
- Implementation details and task curation are described in detail for reproducibility (R1).
- The paper defines a new task (cross-domain generalization for Hamiltonians) and is well structured (R4).
- Qualitative and quantitative results demonstrate better adaptation to new systems (R3).

### Weaknesses
- The main contribution appears to be a straightforward application of off-the-shelf MAML; no discussion of challenges in applying meta-learning to Hamiltonian dynamics or why optimization-based meta-learning is preferred over other representation learning approaches (e.g., Domain Generalization, other meta-learning families) (R1).
- Insufficient baselines: comparisons with existing domain generalization and meta-learning methods (e.g., CoDA (Kirchmeyer et al. 2022), iMODE (Li et al. 2023), other few-shot learning or physics-informed NN approaches) are missing (R1, R3, R4). 
- The comparison with the pre-trained baseline is unfair because the pre-trained model is not fine-tuned on the K-shot support set; this should be done for a fair evaluation (R1).
- No visual comparison of predicted trajectories or energy against ground truth (e.g., phase space plots) is provided, weakening the qualitative conclusions (R1).
- The limitations of meta-learning for (Hamiltonian) dynamics are not investigated. For instance, what happens when meta-training is done on only conservative systems and then adaptation is attempted on dissipative systems? Such failure cases would strengthen the claims (R2). More generally, cases where meta-learning is not beneficial should be revealed to make claims falsifiable (R2).
- Generalization claims are weak because experiments always consider a single held-out system. An ablation on the number of systems used during meta-training is needed, and ideally the model should be tested on all held-out systems simultaneously (R4). Minority reviewer R4 is more critical, rating contribution 2 and soundness 2, and finds the generalization evidence unconvincing.
- Only the performance of MAML (a well-known method) is reported in a particular setting; the paper would benefit from more careful, bounded claims including cases where meta-learning fails (R2).
- The interpretation of CKA in Figure 4 appears contradictory: the authors claim a lower CKA value indicates more similar representations, but typically a low CKA suggests different representations. This needs clarification or the analysis should be reconsidered (R3).
- The framework is not suitable for larger systems; the authors should discuss possible extensions or refinements to meta-learning to handle scaling (R4).
