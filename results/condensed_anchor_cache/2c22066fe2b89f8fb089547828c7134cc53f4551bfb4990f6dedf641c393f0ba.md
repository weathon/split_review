## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes UniCoTT, a unified teacher-student framework for distilling structured chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning from large language models (LLMs) into small language models (SLMs). It extends traditional linear CoT by considering diverse reasoning structures (chains, trees, graphs). The teacher LLM is iteratively prompted to generate multiple explanations with correct answers, which are unified into a graph-structured representation called UniCoT. The student (e.g., BERT, RoBERTa) is trained with three objectives: standard cross-entropy, node-level supervised contrastive loss, and structural consistency loss. Experiments on multiple QA and NLU datasets show significant improvements over baselines. One reviewer (score 8) was particularly positive about the innovation and clarity, while another (score 5) was more critical of the practical limitations and narrow evaluation scope. The remaining two reviewers (scores 6 and 6) appreciated the technical soundness but raised concerns about generalizability and clarity of the construction process.

### Strengths

- The writing is clear and well-structured, with a well-defined motivation.
- The distillation framework is innovative, especially the use of a graph structure to represent diverse CoT pathways and the corresponding training objectives (structural consistency + contrastive learning).
- Extensive experimental evaluation across multiple benchmark datasets (factual reasoning, multiple-choice QA, NLU) with ablation studies and analyses of design choices.
- The consideration of tree and graph reasoning structures, beyond simple chains, better captures complex reasoning and is a significant advancement over existing methods.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited student model scope:** The framework is evaluated only on encoder-only models (BERT, RoBERTa), which are not representative of typical SLMs (e.g., 2B-3B decoder-based models). Distillation into decoder-based models and testing on complex reasoning tasks is missing. (R1, R2)  
- **Lack of computational complexity and scalability analysis:** The iterative construction of UniCoT may incur significant time and resource costs for large datasets; the paper does not analyze this or how the method scales with reasoning complexity (e.g., many steps or interconnected concepts). (R2, R4)  
- **Dependence on external LLM APIs:** UniCoT construction relies on API access to LLMs, which may be costly or restricted. Alternative approaches (e.g., open-source models, in-house generation) are not discussed. (R2)  
- **Limited task generalizability:** Experiments are confined to multiple-choice QA and NLU tasks. It is unclear whether the node-based representation and structural consistency loss transfer to generation tasks (summarization, translation, code generation) or tasks without discrete answers and well-defined reasoning structures. (R2, R3)  
- **Clarity and transparency of UniCoT construction:** The iterative construction process is difficult to understand from the main paper; statistics on the constructed graphs (average number of nodes, edges) are missing, making it hard to assess robustness, potential bias, and computational overhead. (R3)  
- **Hallucination mitigation not demonstrated:** The paper lists “hallucinations” as a motivation, but it does not explicitly show how UniCoTT alleviates hallucination issues compared to baselines. (R3)  
- **Hyperparameter tuning unclear:** The choice of α=0.5 and β=0.2 in the loss function is not justified (intuitive trial vs. grid search). (R3)  
- **Annotation efficiency not studied:** The paper does not investigate how many CoT annotations from the teacher are sufficient for effective distillation. (R3)  
- **Missing comparison of compute budgets:** While UniCoTT adds complexity, reviewer notes that baselines (e.g., self-consistency) also use more compute to generate higher-quality distillation data; a fair comparison of total computational cost is not provided. (R4)
