## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes UFGM, the first unsupervised federated graph matching algorithm. It uses graphlet feature extraction (with an approximate enumeration method) to generate pseudo matched node pairs for pseudo supervised learning, preserving privacy. A separate trust region algorithm splits model optimization between client and server with encrypted embeddings. Theoretical analysis covers graphlet estimation error, Hessian approximation (using two weak quasi-Newton conditions), and convergence. Experiments on real network pairs show UFGM outperforms all federated graph learning (FGL) baselines and achieves close or better results than centralized graph matching.

### Strengths
- **Novel and well-motivated problem.** The first work to explore federated learning for unsupervised graph matching, which is important for applications like social network alignment and financial crime detection (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). The observation that supervised methods may compromise privacy is valid (Reviewer 4).
- **Practical and reasonable design.** Using graphlet features to generate pseudo training data helps maintain privacy while leveraging supervised learning (Reviewers 2, 4). The separate trust region algorithm splits optimizations between client and server so pseudo matchings are not exposed to clients (Reviewer 4). Acceleration techniques (MCMC sampling, quasi-Newton Hessian approximation) reduce cost (Reviewer 4).
- **Extensive theoretical analysis.** Novel analysis of graphlet estimation error (demonstrates quality while reducing cost) and convergence analysis of the separate trust region with Hessian approximation error (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). The theoretical results guarantee effectiveness in the unsupervised setting (Reviewer 2).
- **Promising experimental results.** Outperforms all federated baselines in all tests, and achieves close or better performance than centralized graph matching (Reviewer 2, 3, 4). The paper evaluates both centralized and federated variants (Reviewer 3). Parameter analysis is also done (Reviewer 4).
- **Clear organization.** The paper states its design rationale clearly and is easy to follow (Reviewers 3, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear challenges and technical advancement.** The challenges and applications of federated training for unsupervised graph matching are not clearly articulated (Reviewer 1). Traditional graph matching with encrypted aggregation on the server side could solve the problem, but such encryption may incur huge computation and communication cost (Reviewer 1). The technical advancement over such a baseline is unclear (Reviewer 1). The paper claims to be the first federated graph matching algorithm, but should more clearly emphasize innovation compared to peer works (Reviewer 3).
- **Unreasonable algorithm comparison.** Comparison methods include federated models designed for supervised training; comparing them with unsupervised training is unfair (Reviewer 1).
- **Critical assumption about graphlet similarity.** The method assumes nodes with similar graphlet degree vectors across networks are the same node. The paper provides no quantitative analysis of how often this holds in real-world networks, nor discusses the impact of structural changes (e.g., an author changing research focus) on pseudo-label accuracy (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of ablation/analysis on key components:**
    - No systematic study on the effect of the number of pseudo-labels on final performance (tradeoff between quantity and noise) (Reviewer 4).
    - No experiments evaluating the effectiveness of MCMC sampling parameters on graphlet degree vector quality (Reviewer 4).
    - No analysis of how the quasi-Newton Hessian approximation affects convergence speed and accuracy compared to exact Hessian (Reviewer 4).
- **Need for comparison with simpler alternatives.** A split-learning approach (client maintains encoder, server trains matching unit with pseudo labels) could achieve the goal of the separate trust region algorithm without requiring Hessian computation. The paper does not justify why the separate trust region and Hessian are necessary, nor compare with a split learning baseline (Reviewer 4).
- **Concerns about encrypted embeddings and distance preservation.** The loss is computed on encrypted embeddings $\hat{v}=Kv$ where $K$ is a non-singular matrix. A general non-singular matrix does not preserve distances, so how does the loss function work? Orthogonal matrices would preserve distance; the paper does not address this (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing centralized baseline comparison.** The paper does not explicitly compare the centralized variant of UFGM to other state-of-the-art centralized graph matching methods, which would help quantify the cost of privacy preservation (Reviewer 2). The performance gap between federated and centralized versions of the proposed method is not quantified (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited scalability and generalization.** Experiments are on small datasets; performance on large-scale datasets is unknown (Reviewer 3). The method is specifically designed for federated graph matching; generalizing to other federated learning tasks (e.g., image or text classification) is hard due to reliance on graphlet features and the trust region algorithm (Reviewer 2).
- **Presentation issues.** Figures and legends have small font, decreasing readability (Reviewers 2, 3).
