## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a two-stage offline MARL algorithm (OMSD) that first uses a diffusion model to learn the joint behavior policy (score function via score matching), then applies offline RL with divergence-based behavior regularization (a multiagent variant of BRPO, reverse KL). To ease optimization, agents act sequentially, and the gradient of the log conditional behavior policy is extracted from the diffusion model. The method is evaluated on HalfCheetah-v2 from MAMuJoCo, where it performs on-par with or below baselines. Reviewer 1 is not in the MARL field; Reviewer 2 notes the approach follows the BRPO framework; Reviewer 3 emphasizes the goal of learning multimodal policies.

### Strengths
- The use of diffusion models to explore data distributions in MARL is interesting (Reviewer 2).
- The motivation is clear, with a nice flow between problems with prior approaches (independent learning, IGM) and OMSD (Reviewer 3).
- As far as known, this is the first offline MARL approach able to learn multimodal policies, making the problem important (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited and insufficient experimental evaluation.** Only HalfCheetah-v2 from MAMuJoCo is tested; other MAMuJoCo tasks (Ant, Humanoid) and standard offline MARL benchmarks (MPE, SMACv1, SMACv2) are omitted (Reviewers 2, 3). Results are mixed – on the Random dataset performance is significantly lower than baselines (Reviewer 3). Post-rebuttal, Reviewer 2 remained unconvinced because the requested SMAC evaluations were not provided.
- **Missing relevant baselines.** Comparisons are not provided against OMIGA (Reviewer 2), MADiff, AlberDICE (Reviewer 3), OMEGA, or standard BC (Reviewer 2, post-rebuttal). This weakens the contribution.
- **Methodology is incremental and lacks depth.** Sections up to 3.1 repeat well-known formulations from the BRPO framework (Reviewer 2). The core idea of decomposing the score function lacks clear motivation and insight; the section on score decomposition is too brief, giving the impression of an early-stage method (Reviewer 2).
- **Doubts about whether OMSD actually learns multimodal policies.** No correlation mechanism is provided at test time; it is unclear whether the method can solve the XOR Game (mentioned in the paper). Figure 2 is hard to interpret and does not clearly demonstrate learning of optimal multimodal policies (Reviewer 3). Relatedly, Reviewer 3 asks which part of the algorithm allows learning multimodal policies without relying on an IGO assumption (used by FOP and AlberDICE).
- **Motivation for sequential decomposition and diffusion models is tenuous.** Reviewer 1 (confidence 2) finds the justification via coordinate descent and multiagent Transformer weak: if the target is sequential, why use diffusion instead of an auto-regressive model? It is also unclear how the conditional score \(\nabla_{a_i} \log \mu(a_i \mid s, a_{<i})\) is obtained – would training multiple diffusion models or a joint diffusion model with arbitrary ordering be required, and at what computational cost? reviewer 3 specifically asks why diffusion is better than MLPs with sequential action selection.
- **Gradient calculation errors** (Reviewer 1). From Equation (14) to (15) the gradient w.r.t. the entropy \(H(\pi_i)\) is ignored; the same issue appears in the proof of Proposition 2 (page 18). On page 19, when computing the gradient of \(\theta_1\) w.r.t. \(\pi(a_1|s) \log \pi(a_1|s)\), the first \(\pi(a_1|s)\) is incorrectly treated as a constant. These errors raise concerns about the correctness of the optimization.
- **Figure 1 caption appears contradictory** (Reviewer 1). As dataset quality decreases (Expert to Random), the distribution becomes more uniform (appearing uni-modal for Random) rather than multi-modal, contradicting the caption.
- **Missing algorithm details and reproducibility.** It is not clearly stated where the diffusion models are used (only for learning the behavior policy or also for the target policy) (Reviewer 3). No open-source code or hyperparameter details are provided (Reviewer 3). Notation issues: SSD is used but undefined (Line 322, Table 1); confusion between \(x\) and \(i\) for agent indices (Lines 113-118) (Reviewer 3).
