## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes OneFit, a self-supervised garment simulation method that uses a function-based representation (PolyFit) to achieve mesh-agnostic and garment-agnostic deformation. Instead of training a separate model per garment mesh, OneFit divides the garment mesh into patches, approximates each patch with a fourth-order Taylor polynomial, conditions on body pose, and learns patch deformations via a physics-guided loss. The authors claim that training a single model on a few garments generalizes to unseen garment types and resolutions.

Reviewers 1, 2, and 3 raise major concerns: the paper misses several prior works (GarSim WACV 2023, GenSim CVPR 2023, LayersNet ICCV 2023) that already propose unified garment simulation across different garment types, topologies, and mesh resolutions; the physics dynamics are inaccurate (garments appear tight-fitting and lack realistic fabric lag, especially for loose garments); comparisons with baselines are insufficient (no simultaneous qualitative comparisons in video, no quantitative comparisons to ground truth or SOTA on metrics like geometric invariances, physical accuracy, or collisions); training time claims for HOOD are questionable and comparisons are unfair; the physics loss formulation (Equation 9) is invalid; crucial variable definitions (k, ε) are missing; and the supplementary video shows stiff, unrealistic deformations with collision artifacts. Reviewer 4, on the other hand, finds the patch-based representation and polynomial approximation a promising innovation but notes missing ablation studies on polynomial order and patch resolution, and unclear positional encoding strategy. Overall, the majority of reviewers consider the paper incomplete and lacking sufficient evidence for its claims.

### Strengths
- The use of patches (via Voronoi diagram) to represent garment meshes, combined with polynomial approximation using a fourth-order Taylor expansion, is a novel and interesting approach that can capture curvature and fine wrinkle details, enabling flexible, topology‑agnostic modeling.
- The design is agnostic to garment topology and can handle different mesh resolutions, and the training and inference speed is faster than some prior methods.
- The presentation of the concept is clear, with structured explanations and visual diagrams that effectively convey how the PolyFit module supports localized, function‑based learning.

### Weaknesses
1. **Missing critical related works and claims of novelty are incorrect.**  
   - GarSim (WACV 2023) already proposed a unified garment simulator that simultaneously learns deformations of multiple garment types (tops, skirts, etc.) with varying topologies and fabrics, conditioned on body shape, pose, and motion, and can be trained/tested on arbitrary resolution meshes. GenSim (CVPR 2023) also proposed a generic method for unsupervised 3D garment simulation that handles multiple garment types, sizes, topologies, and body shapes/poses/resolutions. Both methods are based on graph neural networks (contradicting claims in lines 115–120) and predict fabric‑aware deformation, which OneFit lacks.  
   - LayersNet (ICCV 2023) also adopts patches with a Transformer‑based model, is topology‑agnostic, and is close to the settings in this paper. The claim that this is the first method to use patches for garment simulation (L513) is false; the authors should discuss or compare with LayersNet using similar training loss.  
   - The paper therefore has very limited novelty and the two major claims (train on one garment → generalize to other types; handle various mesh resolutions) are incorrect given prior art.

2. **Physics-based dynamics are inaccurate and animation results are poor.**  
   - The model produces garments that appear “tight‑fitting” and too closely follow body motion (a common issue in LBS‑based methods), lacking realistic fabric lag and flow – especially evident for loose garments like dresses and skirts in the supplementary video.  
   - Supplementary video timestamp 0.36 shows heavy collisions with the body without post‑processing, indicating collision loss is not working properly.  
   - Timestamp 1.06 onwards shows stiff, unrealistic movement; the skirt does not flow freely but rigidly follows the body.  
   - The deformations in the supplementary tend to be rigid, with fixed wrinkles at the end of the dress.  
   - The physics loss in Equation 9 is invalid: in SNUG and HOOD the loss is equivalent to an optimization problem defined by potential energy and external forces, but Equation 9 is incomplete and violates that optimization.  
   - The proposed method cannot handle different materials of garments.

3. **Insufficient and unfair comparisons with baselines.**  
   - Only limited qualitative results are shown (Figure 7 shows a single pose). For garment animation, at least sequences demonstrating dynamics should be provided, and baselines should be compared simultaneously (the supplementary video compares only one garment sequence with one baseline at a time).  
   - No quantitative comparisons to baselines are given. Table 4 reports only OneFit’s own errors; no SOTA methods (SNUG, HOOD, GarSim, GenSim) are included.  
   - Training time for HOOD (L502) is stated as 10 hours, but the original paper reports 26 hours on a single GPU. OneFit’s 8 hours is measured on 4 GPUs, making the comparison unfair. The authors should report HOOD training time on 4 GPUs for a proper comparison.  
   - HOOD originally solves collision artifacts through collision loss, but OneFit requires extra post‑processing on unseen garments, suggesting limited generalization and robustness.

4. **Missing quantitative evaluation and ablation studies.**  
   - The paper lacks quantitative comparisons to ground truth sequences, especially for dynamic accuracy. Metrics such as geometric invariances, physical accuracy, and external/self collisions should be evaluated and compared against other methods and ground truth.  
   - No ablation study on the order of the polynomial (Taylor expansion) is provided. Different orders could affect the level of detail captured (e.g., wrinkles, curvature).  
   - The impact of patch resolution (number and size of patches) on model accuracy and computational efficiency is not explored. Smaller patches simplify the polynomial approximation but may lose detail; an analysis of the trade‑off is needed.  
   - The approach to positional encoding is unclear: is it applied globally across all garment types or localized per garment type with unique indices? This is important for assessing generalization across diverse garment types.

5. **Unclear definitions and presentation issues.**  
   - Variables in equations are not defined: what is \(k\) in Equations 2,3,4,5? What does \(\epsilon\) mean in Table 1? These are hard to find.  
   - The claim that HOOD needs post‑processing to remove collisions (L363) is incorrect; the original HOOD paper solves collisions via the loss function.
