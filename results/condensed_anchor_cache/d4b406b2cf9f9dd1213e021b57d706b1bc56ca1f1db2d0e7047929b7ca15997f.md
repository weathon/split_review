## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates whether declarative statements in training data can systematically influence LLMs' generalization behavior during a domain shift. The authors fine-tune GPT-3 and Llama 2 on two toy datasets (weather prediction, teacher gender prediction) and find a small but statistically significant effect that declarative knowledge steers predictions away from the pattern implied by the rest of the training data. The effect persists even when no token overlap exists between the descriptions and test-time generations.

### Strengths
- The research problem—understanding how declarative statements in training data affect inference-time behavior—is important and timely for improving LLM safety (Reviewer 3).
- The design of using constructed toy examples to measure the counterfactual effect of adding descriptions to a finetuning set is ingenious (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is well written and easy to understand (Reviewer 1). *(Reviewer 2 disagrees, see Weaknesses.)*
- Experiments are well-designed and carefully analyzed (Reviewer 3). *(Reviewers 2 and 4 disagree, see Weaknesses.)*
- The experiments focus on two specific domains: weather prediction and teacher gender prediction (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Conceptual clarity and definitional rigor are lacking.** Several reviewers raised concerns: the term “generalization” is used broadly to mean “prediction without overfitting” rather than the standard statistical view; “declarative information” is vague; and the paper uses the word “AGI” without proper caution (Reviewer 4). These issues weaken the framing and reproducibility of the claims.
- **The phenomenon’s significance and novelty are questionable.** The effect of declarative statements is very subtle, making the practical importance uncertain (Reviewer 1). Moreover, it is expected that both demonstrations and descriptions influence model predictions; the community would benefit more from explaining *why* demonstrations have a stronger impact than descriptions, which the paper does not address (Reviewer 2).
- **The experimental design has multiple methodological concerns.**
    1. The toy tasks (only a few hundred training examples per domain) may not reflect real-world scenarios; the model could simply be overfitting the small finetuning set (Reviewer 3). When an LLM has strong capability on many tasks, it is unclear whether a single declarative statement still has the same effect (Reviewer 3).
    2. The tasks themselves are artificial: weather prediction based on time and teacher gender prediction based on name are not convincing proxies for generalization (Reviewer 4). The definition of “generalization shift” is overly narrow (linear interpolation or distribution matching) (Reviewer 3).
    3. Controlling the occurrence frequency of common tokens like “rain” and “sun” is challenging because such tokens are prevalent in the pre-trained corpus, making it hard to avoid raw bias (Reviewer 2).
    4. The training data may contain potentially conflicting declarative knowledge descriptions, a situation not explored in the paper (Reviewer 1).
    5. The datasets are not diverse enough, which could affect the significance of the conclusions (Reviewer 1). Adding a more realistic dataset (e.g., pre-training or instruction-tuning data) would help measure the effect on real-world tasks (Reviewer 3).
- **Writing and analysis quality are inconsistent.** While Reviewer 1 found the paper well written, Reviewer 2 stated the writing needs improvement and the experiment analysis is inadequate. The experimental rigor is insufficient—for example, the paper does not examine whether the number of descriptions in the training data directly influences prediction results (Reviewer 2).
- **The motivating example is confusing.** Using the weather report for a city in 2050 as an example of conflict between descriptions and demonstrations is problematic because the correct answer is inherently undeterminable; different people would reason differently (Reviewer 1).
- **Many other issues remain unaddressed.** Reviewer 4 noted that “a lot of work is needed before publication” without specifying all problems, indicating that the paper is not yet ready.
