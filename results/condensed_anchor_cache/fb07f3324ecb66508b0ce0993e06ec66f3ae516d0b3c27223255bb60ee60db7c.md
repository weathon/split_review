## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper investigates factors influencing the performance of logit-based LLM ensembles (performance gap, vocabulary size, response style) and concludes that model compatibility is essential. It proposes a determine-then-ensemble strategy: first select compatible models using criteria of response style consistency and performance alignment within 10%, then apply a novel ensembling method called UNITE (Union Top-k Ensembling). UNITE combines only the union of the top-k tokens from each model, avoiding full vocabulary alignment and reducing computational overhead. Extensive experiments on multiple benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, TriviaQA, etc.) show that UNITE outperforms existing ensembling methods (LLM-Blender, DeePen, GAC) while significantly reducing latency and token manipulation.

### Strengths

- Identifies and empirically tests three key factors affecting ensemble performance: performance discrepancy, vocabulary size differences (found to be less important in English), and response style differences. Provides concrete empirical conclusions about what matters for ensembling.
- Proposes UNITE, a novel top-k union ensembling approach that dramatically drops computational overhead by using top-k tokens from each model instead of the full vocabulary, obviating the need for full vocabulary alignment.
- UNITE significantly outperforms baselines (LLM-Blender, DeePen, GAC) in average accuracy across six popular question-answering, reasoning, and knowledge benchmarks.
- The paper is clear and well written. Figures and tables present clear latency comparisons against existing methods.
- The model selection strategy provides practical guidelines (e.g., response style consistency, performance alignment within 10%) for choosing compatible models in real-world applications.
- The determine-then-ensemble framework is generalizable, making findings applicable to efficient LLM collaboration.
- The paper includes good analysis and preliminary experiments that motivate the top-k reduction.

### Weaknesses

- **Task-specific analysis lacking:** The paper does not deeply explore why certain tasks (e.g., MMLU) see greater improvements. A breakdown of MMLU sub-tasks (knowledge-based vs. reasoning-based questions, subject areas) is missing. Such analysis could reveal the underlying mechanisms of ensemble success.
- **Model selection scalability and rigor:** The model selection process relies on manual inspection of response styles and a 10% performance alignment criterion, which is not scalable and lacks theoretical justification. A more automated metric (e.g., embedding-space similarity, agreement level) and a principled statistical approach are needed.
- **Benchmark diversity:** The evaluation focuses on question-answering and reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, TriviaQA). Including more varied tasks (creative writing, code generation, complex dialogue, multilingual tasks) would strengthen claims of general applicability.
- **Vocabulary size analysis limited to English:** Conclusion II on vocabulary size differences is restricted to English-language tasks. Multilingual scenarios, where tokenization is less consistent across models, could alter conclusions.
- **Response style handling is ad hoc:** The proposed solution to response style differences (limiting longer responses to 2x the length of shorter ones) lacks theoretical justification. This approach may truncate crucial information and limits practicality. A more robust solution addressing the underlying stylistic variation is needed.
- **Efficiency comparison missing:** One reviewer notes that the experiments should have included a direct efficiency comparison with previous approaches. Another requests a detailed breakdown of memory and latency per method, including average and worst-case per-token latency, to clarify computational trade-offs.
- **Theoretical concern: top-k should not outperform full vocabulary:** A reviewer questions why top-k outperforms full vocabulary on every dataset, which seems logically impossible (since full vocabulary includes more distribution information). Another reviewer asks for theoretical support or intuition for this counterintuitive result. A third reviewer asks why increasing k beyond 10 does not improve performance. [Minority reviewer (score 6) also questions the balance between accuracy and computational efficiency at a probabilistic level.]
- **Lack of analysis on failure cases:** There is no analysis of settings or benchmarks where the top-k token alignment fails to improve over base models. This limits understanding of the method's limitations.
- **Impact of base model performance difference:** The effect of base model performance disparity on UNITE's effectiveness is not analyzed.
- **Figures and tables:** Captions are not sufficiently self-contained (e.g., Figures 4, 5, 6). Font sizes are very small, hindering readability.
- **Optimal k determination:** It is unclear whether the optimal k varies across tasks/domains and whether there is a principled way to choose k.
- **Handling models with different response styles:** A reviewer asks how UNITE handles markedly different response styles in practice and whether a preprocessing step to standardize formats could help. [This overlaps with the ad hoc truncation criticism above.]
