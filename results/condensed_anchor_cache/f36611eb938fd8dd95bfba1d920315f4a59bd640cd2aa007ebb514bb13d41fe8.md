## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces M4U, a multilingual multimodal benchmark of 8,931 multiple-choice questions covering 64 disciplines across 16 subfields (Science, Engineering, Healthcare) in Chinese, English, and German. The dataset is collected from college exams, online videos, and textbooks to reduce data leakage risk. Evaluation of 21 LMMs and LLMs shows GPT-4o achieves only 47.6% average accuracy. Reviewers agree the dataset is large and addresses data leakage, but disagree on the significance of its contributions and methodological rigor. While some (R1, R3) find the benchmark well-motivated, well-written, and a useful resource, others (R2, R4) argue that the contributions are exaggerated, the multilingual and “challenging” claims are not convincingly supported, and the construction lacks necessary detail and quality control.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1, R3).
- The curation process is designed to avoid data contamination, addressing high leakage risk in existing benchmarks (R1, R2).
- M4U is a large multimodal reasoning dataset reflecting university-level knowledge across many disciplines (R1, R2, R4).
- The benchmark provides a thorough assessment and analysis of many LMMs/LLMs, including analysis of failure causes (R3, R4).
- It is a potentially useful resource for evaluating multilingual performance of LMMs (R1, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Language coverage is limited**: only Chinese, English, and German are included (R1). Chinese and German are not low-resource languages; the necessity of this specific multilingual focus is not clearly justified (R4).
- **Multilingual construction is weak and unverified**: The English and German versions are created by translating text only, without ensuring translation accuracy or involving native speakers (R2). Language-specific characteristics (e.g., cultural differences, healthcare guidelines) are not considered; for STEM, this reduces to evaluating translation ability rather than genuine multilingual reasoning (R1).
- **Images contain untranslated Chinese text** across all language versions, which is unreasonable for German/English evaluation. Most visual encoders are not trained on Chinese text-image pairs, significantly limiting model reasoning and potentially causing inaccurate assessment (R2). Separate OCR-only evaluation is needed to diagnose whether failures stem from multilingual OCR (R1).
- **Claim of “challenging” lacks human performance baseline**: no data on human expert accuracy is provided to define difficulty, undermining the “challenging” assertion (R2).
- **Benchmark’s general applicability is limited**: it requires extensive subject-specific knowledge, making reasoning skills secondary. The title and introduction should be reorganized to clarify the intended scenario (e.g., expert AGI, educational robots, medical applications) (R4). Compared to existing benchmarks, M4U does not present a significant difference in design (R3).
- **Evaluation method is simple and may not fully reflect multilingual understanding and reasoning**: additional approaches (e.g., translating questions to English, incorporating OCR results into prompts) are needed to probe models’ capabilities more comprehensively (R3, R4).
- **Top failure barriers are perceptual errors and lack of knowledge** (over 69% in Figure 4), suggesting the main limitations stem from image encoding and subject knowledge, not reasoning. Describing the benchmark as a test of reasoning may be misleading; examples of reasoning-error failures should be provided (R4).
- **Data processing lacks rigor and missing information**: vague language on annotators (“over 10 college and graduate students”) is insufficient for a scientific paper (R2). Details are missing on: proportions of sources (videos, PDFs, textbooks) (R2, R4); translation accuracy verification (R2); question accuracy cross-checking and consistency rate (R2); annotator selection, annotation standards, quality control procedures, and label consistency measures (R4). The process for extracting questions from textbooks and formulating answer options should be documented (R4).
- **Limited novelty and contribution**: the paper introduces a dataset and evaluates models but offers no novel approach in design, construction, or evaluation methods, nor does it attempt to improve poorly performing models (R4).
