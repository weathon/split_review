## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes UET (Uncertainty Estimation-Discriminative Knowledge Extraction-Knowledge Transfer), a feature-based distillation paradigm that uses Monte Carlo dropout to estimate teacher knowledge uncertainty for object detection. UET integrates with existing KD methods, achieving 44.1% mAP on COCO with GFL and ResNet50 (surpassing baseline by 3.9%), and is extended to classification and semantic segmentation.

### Strengths
- Motivation is convincing: multiple teachers can provide more diverse and informative supervision to the student (R1).
- The idea is extremely simple and works okay in practice; a simple combination (KD+MC-dropout) that outperforms more complex approaches is a strength (R2).
- The paper is relatively well written; the method is reproducible from the description (R2).
- UET can be seamlessly integrated with existing KD methods and achieves new state-of-the-art results with FGD on COCO (R1).
- Comprehensive evaluation across multiple detectors: GFL, Faster-RCNN, RetinaNet, FCOS, with ResNet101 teacher and ResNet50 student, plus smaller backbones (Res18/Res34) in appendix (R2).
- Successfully extended to classification and semantic segmentation tasks (R1).
- Novelty: introducing uncertainty estimation into knowledge distillation for object detection is novel (R3).
- UET achieves state-of-the-art performance on COCO (R3).

### Weaknesses
- The idea is similar to prior AKD; novelty is weakened. The major difference (including the original teacher) is not ablated: no study of w/ and w/o the original teacher. Direct comparison with AKD is limited (only Table 3) (R1).
- Results in Table 1 and 2 are obtained with FGD, a strong approach; comparison with other SOTA KD methods may be unfair (R1).
- Generalization not fully evaluated: expected to embed UET across more detectors and KD methods (R1).
- Student’s knowledge uncertainty “S” in Table 6 is not defined in the Methods section (R1).
- Writing in the Method section (Eq. 2-3) is obfuscated; the paper should be proud of its simplicity rather than making it look complex (R2).
- The claim “Why rely on a single teacher’s deterministic knowledge…” is well-known; the paper should cite prior multi-teacher works (Son et al. ICCV 2021, Wu et al. ACL 2021, Pham et al. WACV 2023, Liu et al. Neurocomputing 2020) (R2).
- Result inconsistencies with original papers:  
  - Table 1: CrossKD reproduced at 43.6 vs. original CrossKD 43.9; UET 44.1 vs. original CrossKD 43.9 gives only +0.2 mAP marginal improvement (R2).  
  - Table 2 (FCOS): FGD reproduced at 42.1 vs. original FGD 42.7; UET 42.9 vs. original FGD 42.7 shows minimal improvement (R2).
- No comparisons with Transformer-based detectors (e.g., VkD, Vidt) (R2).
- Lack of calibration analysis; since the method reduces noise passed to student, it should show that detectors are more reliable (R2).
- Timing performance unclear: how can k forward passes (5,10,15) not increase training time? Also unclear if MC-dropout is applied at student inference and how that affects timing (R2).
- Monte Carlo dropout requires multiple sampling passes, which may slow training (R3).
- LD+UET only improves by 0.2 mAP over LD alone (Tab. 7); why do logit-based KD methods benefit less from UET? (R3)
- Could the student also generate uncertainty features and directly imitate feature uncertainty? (R3, as suggestion for missing experiment)
- Comparison between AKD and UET does not show significant difference; more discussion needed (R3).

*Note: Reviewer 1 (score 5) is more critical of novelty and result fairness; Reviewer 2 (score 6) emphasizes the simple idea as a strength but points out result inconsistencies and missing analyses; Reviewer 3 (score 6) finds the idea novel but notes key ablated performance and training overhead.*
