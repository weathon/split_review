## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper tackles hidden confounding in offline RL under nonidentifiable settings. It introduces delphic uncertainty—the variance of Q-values across world models compatible with the observed data—distinguishes it from epistemic and aleatoric uncertainties, and proposes a pessimistic offline RL algorithm that penalizes actions with high delphic uncertainty. The method is evaluated on a sepsis management benchmark and electronic health records, outperforming baselines like CQL and BC under strong confounding. Three reviewers rated the paper 8 (strong accept), one rated it 6 (weak accept). The lower-rated reviewer found the probability setup unclear; the higher-rated reviewers praised the motivation and results but raised concerns about estimator bias, state-action vs. trajectory-level uncertainty, limited baseline comparisons, and ambiguity in defining compatible worlds and the confounding measure Γ.

### Strengths
- Addresses the important, understudied problem of hidden confounding in offline RL.
- Novel concept of delphic uncertainty is insightful and well-motivated (R2, R4).
- Clear theoretical decomposition of total uncertainty into delphic, epistemic, and aleatoric components (R1, R2, R4).
- Practical, intuitive algorithm: learns multiple compatible worlds, then applies pessimism based on estimated delphic uncertainty (R1, R4).
- Evaluation on real/semi-real medical datasets (sepsis management, EHR) adds practical relevance (R1, R3).
- Expert evaluation supports the method’s efficacy (R1).
- Presentation is excellent with helpful illustrations; writing is clear and easy to follow (R2, R4).
- Minimal problem setting (contextual MDP) isolates confounding and makes approach clear (R1).
- Empirical results show significant improvement over CQL and BC when confounding is large (R1).
- Overall soundness rated 3–4 (good to excellent) across reviewers.
- Contribution rated 3–4 (good to excellent) across reviewers.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited baselines and environments**: Only compared against CQL, BC; missing comparisons with partial identification methods (e.g., those discussed in related work) and with POMDP-based approaches that could identify the confounder online within an episode (R1). The reviewer asks whether such extension is possible and whether the method would still be superior.
- **Not end-to-end**: The method is split into learning multiple worlds and then using them for pessimism, as opposed to an integrated formulation like CQL—this is noted as a minor concern (R1).
- **Estimator bias for delphic uncertainty**: The practical estimator \(\mathbb{Var}_w(Q^\pi_w(s,a))\) may not be unbiased because the individual particle estimates \(Q_{\theta_w}^\pi\) themselves contain epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty that propagates into \(Q^\pi_w\) and then into the delphic uncertainty estimate (R2). The paper does not justify this point.
- **State-action vs. trajectory-level uncertainty**: Evaluating delphic uncertainty at the state-action level may miss correlations between different \((s,a)\) pairs and may not align with optimizing total return; a trajectory-level assessment might be more appropriate (R2).
- **Assumption of known behavior policy**: The method relies on importance sampling with the known behavior policy \(\pi_b\). If \(\pi_b\) is unavailable, the estimator becomes biased. The reviewer asks whether other unbiased estimators (e.g., from Shi et al., 2022) could be incorporated (R2).
- **Probability setup unclear** (R3): 
  - Notation \(P_w \mapsto \Delta W\) is nonstandard; likely meant \(P_w \in \Delta W\).
  - The introduction of a stochastic value model \(Q_{\theta_w}\) after Definition 4.1 is confusing—the value function of a policy should be a deterministic function; source of randomness not explained.
  - Theorem 4.2 involves two expectation measures (\(E_w\) and \(E_{\theta_w}\)) without clarifying their relationship or how they arise from the underlying probability model.
- **Criteria for compatible worlds ambiguous** (R4): Any model that maximizes ELBO seems to yield a compatible world, raising concerns about identifiability and sensitivity to the choice of prior \(p(z)\) and model architecture. Robustness across different modeling choices is not assessed.
- **Behavior policy is context-aware**: Enforcing similarity to the behavior policy may inadvertently encourage mimicking a potentially suboptimal policy, and the method’s ability to distinguish beneficial context-awareness from confounding bias is unclear (R4).
- **Counterintuitive result on policy discrepancy**: The paper reports that the learned policy differs significantly from the behavior policy, which seems to contradict the expectation that penalizing high delphic uncertainty would produce a more conservative, behavior-mimicking policy. This discrepancy needs explanation (R4).
- **Counterfactual \(Q^\pi_w\) estimation via importance sampling is not clearly described**: Specific form of importance weights, how they are computed from estimated confounder distributions, and the exact connection to Appendix C are missing (R4).
- **Confounding strength measure \(\Gamma\) not well justified**: Figure 7 shows that the preference between BC and the RL policy remains near 50%, raising questions about practical significance. Sensitivity analysis to different \(\Gamma\) values is needed (R4).
- **Additional question from R1**: Clarify whether the \(\max\) in the definition of \(\Gamma(s,a)\) is taken over \(z, z'\) only or also over \(s,a\); if not, how is \(\Gamma(s,a)\) summarized for the whole environment?
