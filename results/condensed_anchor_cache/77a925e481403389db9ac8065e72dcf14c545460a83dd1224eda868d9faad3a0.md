## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents HShare, a hierarchical framework that shares critical KV cache token indices across queries, layers, and heads to reduce computational overhead in LLM decoding. It exploits observed similarities in token criticality across neighboring queries, layers, and heads. A greedy algorithm dynamically determines optimal layer- and head-level sharing configurations during decoding. Experiments on LLaMA2-7b, LLaMA3-70b, and Mistral-7b show that HShare maintains accuracy while providing up to 8.6× speedup in self-attention and 2.7× improvement in end-to-end throughput.

### Strengths
- Addresses a critical and practical problem (KV cache management in long-context LLMs) that is of high interest to the community (R1, R5).
- Novel hierarchical sharing approach across heads, layers, and queries, motivated by systematic analysis of similarities in KV cache token criticality (R2, R3).
- Well-designed technique that reduces computational overhead without compromising accuracy; clear motivation with microbenchmarks and examples (R1, R3, R4).
- Comprehensive evaluation: wide variety of datasets, reasonable number of models (LLaMA2-7b, LLaMA3-70b, Mistral-7b), four baseline frameworks, and assessment of both accuracy and compute performance (latency, throughput) (R4, R5). Includes a fair ablation study (R4).
- Maintains accuracy while delivering substantial speedups (up to 8.6× self-attention, 2.7× throughput), which is significant for practical deployment (R1, R3, R5).
- Well-organized paper with adequate discussion of existing methods (R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Baseline comparison for speed is insufficient**: only FlashAttention is compared for throughput; missing comparisons with other query-aware dynamic sparsity methods (e.g., H2O, Quest, MInference, DoubleSparse) that also reduce computation via sparsity (R1, R2). This makes it difficult to isolate HShare’s specific contribution (R2). One reviewer notes the speed gain could be partly attributed to the underlying approximate attention method (DoubleSparse) rather than HShare’s sharing mechanism (R2).
- **Accuracy evaluation limited**: experiments use only 6 of 16 commonly used English datasets from Longbench, which may not be sufficient for solid conclusions (R2). Performance gaps between baselines like H2O and Quest differ from prior work, suggesting possible dataset selection bias (R2). Results across all datasets should be reported (R2).
- **Missing baseline of original unmodified model**: the performance of the original model without any sparsity or sharing is not included, making it difficult to assess the true impact of the proposed method (R4).
- **Speedup analysis lacks direct decoding evidence**: theoretical complexity analysis alone is insufficient to demonstrate that HShare significantly reduces overhead, because KV cache selection may account for only a small portion of total decoding time (R2). Direct decoding speed measurements and a trade-off study between accuracy and speed are needed (R2, R5). The impact of prefill-phase overhead on very short sequences is not fully quantified (R3).
- **Hierarchy and sharing order unclear**: the paper does not discuss the order in which head, layer, and query dimensions are applied, nor dependencies or priority among them (R4). The claim of a hierarchy contradicts the statement that the levels are independent in memory benefits; the relationship between sharing decisions at different levels is not explained (R4).
- **Language and presentation issues are significant**: many grammatical errors, typos, missing whitespaces, incorrect figure references (e.g., “Fig. 1(c) and Fig. 1(d)” should be 1(e) and 1(f); “Fig. 1(e) and Fig. 1(f)” similarly wrong), inconsistent axis labels (Figure 6: TriviaQA uses “Sparsity Level” instead of “Sharing ratio”), and problematic averaging of different metrics (similarity, F1, Rouge) in Table 3 (R4). Minor issues include missing spaces on page 6 line 270, Table 3 averaging distinct score types, and reference formatting problems (R4).
- **Limited generalization**: sharing configurations are tested only on a few model architectures (LLaMA2-7b, LLaMA3-70b, Mistral-7b); broader applicability to models with different attention mechanisms, varying numbers of layers/heads, or tasks requiring highly dynamic attention (e.g., document summarization with varying context lengths) is not explored (R3).
- **Equations 4 and 5 add little value** and could be removed (R4).
- **Missing analysis on accuracy-efficiency tradeoff**: a figure showing the trade-off between accuracy and efficiency would be informative (R5). The sharing ratio may need to be more finely tuned and could depend on dataset and task (R5).
- **Unaddressed questions that overlap with weaknesses**:
  - Can HShare effectively support transformer variants such as convolutional or graph neural networks? A brief discussion of potential adaptations would help (R3).
  - What is the suspected reason for the performance gain shown in Figure 6? (R5)
  - Does the sharing ratio depend on the dataset and task? (R5)
