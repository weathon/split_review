## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces an ego-centric 4D dataset for error recognition in procedure videos (kitchen recipes). The dataset comprises 384 recordings (94.5 hours) from 8 subjects in 10 kitchens performing 24 recipes, with additional modalities (depth, IMU, camera trajectories, hand joints). Annotations include 5.3K step annotations and 10K fine-grained action annotations for 20% of the data. Three tasks are formulated: error recognition (binary classification and early prediction), multi-step localization (temporal action localization), and procedure learning. Baselines show Omnivore as the best backbone across tasks, with similar performance for procedure learning methods (Dwibedi et al. 2019; Bansal, Siddhant et al. 2022). Reviewers question the dataset's novelty compared to Assembly101 (Table 1), note that error videos are scripted/instructed rather than realistic, that the depth modality is not used in baselines, that baselines provide limited insights, that binary classification is questionable for fine-grained error detection, that early prediction AUC (Table 3) is surprisingly similar to full recognition (Table 2), that timing/temperature errors may not require ML, and that procedure learning is vaguely defined.

### Strengths
- The paper dedicates effort to building a new benchmark for procedural video understanding; any dataset construction is a positive community contribution. [R1]
- The paper is well presented with clear motivation, simple language, and a clear data collection process. [R2, R3]
- Proposed error types (technique, measurement, etc.) test models’ fine-grained understanding of activities. [R2]
- The work is constructive towards both coarse-grained and fine-grained error understanding, a relatively new field. [R2]
- Extensive fine-grained annotations (10K actions, 5.3K steps) will benefit future work. [R2]
- Higher ratio of error to normal videos compared to Assembly101 (though this claim is contested in weaknesses). [R2]
- Baseline methods are provided for error recognition and multi-step localization. [R3]

### Weaknesses
- **Dataset novelty and comparison to Assembly101**: The additional benefit over existing datasets, especially Assembly101, is unclear. Table 1 shows the proposed dataset is small/medium in number of videos, hours, or tasks; the main differentiator is the depth channel, but its utility is not validated as baselines only use RGB. [R1, R2, R3] The claim of higher error-to-normal ratio needs validation. [R2] Error videos are captured under three scenarios (Sec 3.1.2): the first two are scripted, the third is instructed; these produce intentional errors that may not look realistic, whereas unintentional mistakes are more relevant in practice. [R1] Post-rebuttal, reviewers remain unconvinced the dataset is significantly more useful than Assembly101. [R2]
- **Modalities collected but not used or validated**: The sensor data (depth, camera trajectory, hand joints) are not employed in any baseline, despite being claimed as an advantage. It is unclear whether depth is necessary for error detection or could be derived from existing models. [R1, R2, R3]
- **Baselines provide limited insights**: For error recognition (Table 2) and early error prediction (Table 3), the main finding is that Omnivore is the best backbone—this is unsurprising and does not reveal dataset-specific insights. [R1] Early prediction AUC scores are surprisingly similar to full recognition, despite early prediction being presumably harder. [R3] For multi-step localization (supervised TAL using ActionFormer), the same Omnivore-best finding holds. [R1] For procedure learning, two methods (Dwibedi et al. 2019; Bansal, Siddhant et al. 2022) yield similar performance, no real insights are observed, and details are missing (e.g., lambda value in Table 5 not reported). [R1, R2] Overall, the experiments do not demonstrate why the new error videos matter or how the dataset advances understanding. [R1, R2]
- **Binary classification for error detection is questionable**: Coarse-grained binary classification (correct vs. error) may not assess models’ understanding of fine-grained errors like technique errors. [R2] Timing errors (duration of an activity) and temperature errors may not require machine learning; simple sensor readings or heuristics could suffice. [R2]
- **Procedure learning section is vague and incomplete**: The task is very vaguely defined, seems like an afterthought, and needs more description of methods M1 (Dwibedi et al., 2019) and M2 (Bansal, Siddhant et al., 2022). [R2] Implementation details (e.g., lambda in Table 5) are missing. [R2]
- **Subjectivity and severity of errors not addressed**: Errors vary in gravity (e.g., using tablespoon vs. teaspoon), but no weighted scores, level of deviation per error type or timestamp, or measure of recipe difficulty are provided. [R2]
- **Writing clarity issues**: In Section 4.2, it is unclear whether supervised TAL is trained task-agnostically or task-specifically (i.e., per recipe). [R1] The meaning of "refer to 16" in Section 4.2 is ambiguous. [R1]
- **Missing analyses**: Distribution of error categories is only shown in supplementary Figures 16-17, not the main paper. [R3] Per-error-type baseline results are not provided to gauge difficulty. [R2] Best-performing models in Tables 2-3 and Figure 4 are not highlighted. [R3]
