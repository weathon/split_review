## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies task-specific subnetworks in billion-parameter LLMs (LLaMA-2-7B/13B, Gemma-7B/9B) by pruning weights. It uses a weight importance score from Sun et al. (2024) – absolute weight value × norm of input features – then sorts weights into groups and masks entire groups if removal improves in-domain validation performance. The authors claim that (1) weight activations are task-specific, (2) pruning improves in-domain accuracy, (3) pruned models lose cross-domain generalization, and (4) this reveals that generalization arises from a multiplicity of domain-specific subnetworks. Experiments cover MMLU (medicine, philosophy, etc.), GSM8K, and HumanEval. One reviewer (score 6) found the analysis detailed and the build-up compelling, while four other reviewers (scores 3, 3, 3, 5) rated it lower due to novelty and experimental weaknesses.

### Strengths
- Weight pruning and identifying subnetworks without full retraining is an important and active area for large models, with potential for efficient deployment and personalization.
- The paper studies multiple large models (LLaMA-2-7B, LLaMA-2-13B, Gemma-7B, Gemma-2-9B), suggesting some generality.
- The evaluation covers diverse domains and tasks (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval) and includes cross-domain evaluation (Table 2).
- The analysis of weight distributions across layers and models to justify the scoring method and identify patterns is detailed; there is a compelling build-up from observations to the concrete probing algorithm.
- The method is simple and easy to reproduce.
- (Minority, R3 only) The subnetworks outperforming the full model in-domain (while degrading out-of-domain) is a meaningful observation, and the study of “weight overlap” across domains could be interesting if better presented.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty compared to prior work.** Domain-specific parameter behavior in LLMs has been frequently observed (e.g., Wang et al. 2024 use Wanda, a very similar technique; Panigrahi et al. ICML 2023; Chang et al. EMNLP 2024). The paper does not clearly articulate what new insight it provides beyond validating this point, especially for large models. One reviewer (R5) states novelty is “limited for ICLR”; another (R2) notes the stated motivation (comparing large vs. small models) is not answered.
- **No meaningful baselines.** The results are only reported for the proposed method. Comparisons are missing against: (i) the exact method of Sun et al. (2024) from which the weight scoring is borrowed (suggested: report 50% sparsity on MMLU-5 shot with LLaMA-2-7B/13B, see Sun et al. Table 21); (ii) gradient-based search methods in a FLOP-equalized manner; (iii) random pruning; (iv) other structured or unstructured pruning baselines. Without baselines, it is impossible to gauge effectiveness.
- **Missing critical experiments and ablations.**
    - No experiment with topic-agnostic grouping (e.g., a single grouping for the whole MMLU) to show that cross-domain overlap is not an artifact of domain-based grouping.
    - Ablation of the grouping design: why group weights instead of individual weight pruning? The claim of “refined and efficient elimination” is not supported by experiment.
    - Ablation of sequential layer order in Algorithm 1 – does iterating layers in reverse or random order change the discovered subnetwork? (R3, R4)
    - L1 vs. L2 norm in the scoring scheme; no analysis of alternative importance definitions.
    - No investigation on smaller models to establish whether pruning insights transfer from small to large scale, as claimed.
    - No tests on LLaMA-3; one reviewer (R5) raises that LLaMA-3 degrades more under pruning, suggesting subnetworks become less distinct with more pretraining.
- **Algorithmic efficiency and resource claims not supported.** Algorithm 1 requires G evaluations on the validation set and G×L forward passes on the training set (significant FLOP); this is not compared to gradient-based alternatives. The paper claims computational benefits of sparsification (L.364–368) but provides no throughput, latency, or memory footprint measurements, nor compatibility with quantization.
- **Figures and presentation are unclear.** Many figures lack axis labels, legends, or captions that explain how to read them (e.g., Figures 2, 3, 4, 5 – is 0–5% the highest-score group? Does outer circle mean 100% overlap?). Different layers and models appear in different plots with minimal explanation; the analysis focuses on describing what is shown rather than extracting insights. Figure 6 omits 10 groups without justification. Figure 7 group numbering (#1, #2, …) is inconsistent with earlier notation (0–10%, 10–20%). The “overlap” plots (Figure 2, 3) show counter-intuitive results (e.g., Philosophy is close to Anatomy but far from Moral/Medicine) with no comment.
- **Poor writing and typos.** Several typos (e.g., “Phychology”), ambiguous phrasing (Line 165: “pruned model always performs lower than the full model” contradicts an earlier statement), and missing domain descriptions (e.g., medicine, philosophy should be identified as MMLU subsets in Section 3).
- **Credibility issue when pruned model outperforms full model.** Some results show the pruned subnetwork outperforming the full model (Tables 1, 2), which is unexpected for a pruning method that removes weights. This conflicts with the claim that pruning only removes unimportant weights; it suggests the method is searching over masks on a validation set, not simply removing low-importance groups. The reviewers question whether the calibration and test sets are disjoint and whether the improvement is statistically significant.
- **Lack of concrete actionable suggestions.** The paper does not discuss how the observations (task-specific subnetworks, limited cross-domain performance) could be used to improve model design, training, or deployment beyond the pruning scheme itself.
- **Specific technical concerns.**
    - Equation (1) has unclear indexing (should the weight matrix be C_out × C_in?). (Reviewer 4)
    - Equation (6) assumes linearity; the role of activations in FFNs is not accounted for.
    - The algorithm masks whole groups for all matrices per layer; it is not specified whether layernorm, embedding layers, or final linear layer are excluded.
    - The selection of groups to prune is based on validation performance; the optimal group may differ on test data – this is not discussed.
