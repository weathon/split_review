- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 5, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Learning to Optimize (L2O) framework to adaptively select penalty parameters for the Majorized Proximal Augmented Lagrangian Method (MPALM), a convergent multi-block ADMM-type algorithm. The method uses supervised learning with unrolling and a regression loss. It is applied to the Lasso problem and the discrete optimal transport problem, showing improved empirical performance over baselines including MPALM with fixed penalty parameters. Reviews are split: two reviewers (3,3) find the method impractical and lacking novelty, while two (5,5) consider it a simple yet effective extension of L2O, albeit with noted weaknesses.

### Strengths
- The studied problem (learning parameters for multi-block ADMM-type methods) is interesting and addresses a gap in L2O research (R1, R2).
- The paper is easy to follow (R1, R4), provides a comprehensive summary of related work and MPALM (R3), and explains problems in a reader-friendly way (R4).
- The proposed framework is simple yet effective (R2, R4). It learns penalty parameters for MPALM and shows improved performance over commonly used algorithms and MPALM with various fixed penalty parameters on both Lasso and optimal transport problems (R1, R3, R4).
- Several recent baselines and scenarios (distributional shifts) are considered, implying improvements in accuracy and robustness (R4).
- Originality of learning parameters specifically for the MPALM algorithm (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Practicality and scalability (R1, R4):** The reliance on exact solutions \(x^*\) for the empirical risk minimization (ERM) training is a major bottleneck. Obtaining exact solutions is expensive or intractable for complex high-dimensional problems, and the paper does not address the computational scalability or provide complexity/time/memory analysis for real-world applications (R1, R4).
- **Small-scale experiments (R1):** Datasets used are too small and synthetic. Experiments on larger, high-dimensional real-world datasets are needed to convincingly show practical utility. The two problems (Lasso, optimal transport) are insufficient to generalize to more challenging scenarios (R1).
- **Novelty limited (R3, R4):** Only parameters are learned; no novel algorithm, problem, or method of learning parameters is introduced. The framework is not clearly differentiated from the MPALM in [1] (Liang Chen et al., 2021); the paper should clarify differences and provide an example solvable by this algorithm but not by MPALM (R4).
- **Missing comparisons (R3):** For LASSO, FISTA (an accelerated method) is a strong baseline but not compared.
- **Lack of theoretical contributions (R2, R4):** The main text includes Theorem 1 and Theorem 2, but neither is proven in the manuscript. It is unclear if these are intended as theoretical contributions of this work (R2). No convergence analysis or convergence rate for the proposed learned algorithm is provided; while asymptotic convergence of MPALM is discussed, non-asymptotic convergence is not (R2). Convergence with learned parameters under Assumption 3 (which depends on \(\sigma\)) is not proven—is this a clear/easy result? (R3). The paper lacks strong theoretical guarantees (R4).
- **Writing and presentation issues:**
  - The method is introduced only on page 7; too much space is devoted to background. Background could be moved to the appendix to allow a more comprehensive method presentation (R1, R2).
  - MPALM and LMPALM are never explicitly defined in the main text (R3, R4). The abbreviation LMPALM first appears in the Results section without introduction (R4). The Results section does not clearly explain why LMPALM improves results (R4).
  - Theorem 1 lacks a reference or proof (R1, R3).
  - Figures 1 and 2 have too small font and are not visually appealing (R2).
  - Typographical/grammatical errors: “with slightly abuse of notation” (R3); indexing with \(i\) in (ERM) (R3); “\(g(w) := \mu \| x \|_1\)” (mixing \(w\) and \(x\)) (R3); “the Sinkhorn’s algorithm” (R3).
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms (all):**
  - Are Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 theoretical contributions of this work? If not, why are they presented as main text theorems? (R2)
  - Could bilevel optimization be employed to optimize hyperparameters instead? (R2)
  - What is the definition of \(\{\sigma_j\}\)? Does it denote \(\{\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_J\}\) with \(J = \lfloor K/K_0 \rfloor + 1\)? (R3)
  - Under Assumption 3, does convergence hold for learned algorithms? (R3)
  - “If back-propagation fails in practice, stochastic gradient based optimizers are no longer applicable… we may rely on grid search…” – Which cases is this true for? (R3)
  - Please clarify differences with the algorithm in [1] (R4).
- **Minor reviewer contrast:** Reviewers 1 and 2 found the method impractical and saw little novelty (scores 3,3), while Reviewers 3 and 4 were more positive (5,5) but acknowledged many of the same weaknesses (limited novelty, missing theoretical analysis, writing issues). The positive reviewers still note that the method is simple and lacks strong theoretical grounding.
