## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies the stability-plasticity dilemma in continual learning from an architectural perspective, claiming that under equal parameter constraints, deeper networks exhibit better plasticity while wider networks exhibit better stability. Based on this, the authors propose Dual-Arch, a plug-in framework that uses two specialized networks (a deeper, thinner plastic network and a wider, shallower stable network) with knowledge distillation to combine their strengths. Experiments on CIFAR-100 and other datasets show improved performance over several CL baselines with up to 87% parameter reduction. Reviewers generally find the idea intuitive and the results promising, but raise concerns about the generality of the main claim, over-parameterization of the experimental setup, insufficient hyper-parameter analysis, and limited architectural validation.

### Strengths
- The observation that deeper networks favor plasticity and wider networks favor stability under fixed parameter count is novel and interesting (R1, R4).
- The dual-architecture concept is simple, intuitive, and effective, providing a fresh architectural perspective rather than focusing solely on weight optimization (R1, R3, R5).
- The paper is well-written, clearly structured, and easy to follow (R1, R4, R5).
- Additional experiments including ablation studies and stability-plasticity analysis make a strong case for the method (R2).
- Related work is comprehensive, covering past work on forgetting, architecture, and complementary learning systems (R5).
- Code is provided for reproducibility (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Generality of the main claim**: The finding that deep→plasticity, wide→stability is tested only with iCaRL, a method with distinctive features (knowledge distillation, prototype-based classification) that may obscure the direct architectural impact. Using a simpler algorithm like experience replay would provide clearer evidence (R1, R5). It is also unclear if this trade-off generalizes to other architectures (e.g., other ResNet variants, transformers) (R2, R3). The paper should discuss why iCaRL was chosen (R5). Additionally, experiments are limited to CIFAR-100 (mostly 10/20-task) with at most 20 tasks; more realistic settings with many more tasks are needed (R1). Effectiveness in other CL settings (e.g., domain-incremental) is not explored (R1, R5).
- **Static view of stability/plasticity**: Plasticity and stability are not static properties of an architecture; they depend on training duration and other factors, which weakens the argument for a purely architectural trade-off (R2).
- **Over-parameterization concerns**: Most experiments use ResNet-18 on CIFAR-100, which may be over-parameterized. Figure 3a shows very limited performance drop with parameter reduction, suggesting that ResNet-18 is already over-parameterized for this task, raising questions about the validity of the claimed parameter efficiency improvements (R2).
- **Marginal gains and need for stronger baselines**: In Table 2 vs Table 3, ResNet18 without Dual-Arch is comparable or worse than using only the stable network (“Sta-Net”). Sta-Net appears a more reasonable baseline, especially since prior work already showed wider/shallower nets have superior CL performance (R2).

  The performance difference in Table 3 between Dual-Arch and two stable networks is minimal (<1%), and reported mean/std suggest differences between sta-net architectures and between sta-net and pla-net are less than 1%. This small margin undermines conclusiveness and necessitates further experiments on additional datasets (R4). Reviewer 4 finds the results not sufficiently justified, while other reviewers are more positive but agree that more thorough analysis is needed.

- **Hyper-parameter tuning and transparency**: There is insufficient transparency on hyper-parameter tuning, especially for α and β. Prior work emphasizes the impact of tuning in CL, and the paper should discuss assumptions and setup. Additional experiments (provided during rebuttal) show that varying α impacts performance, requiring a more thorough analysis rather than relying on values from prior work. Confidence intervals are missing; without error bars, the two-architecture approach is hard to fully justify (R4).

- **Missing comparisons with related work**: The paper should compare with methods that use distillation or network expansion (e.g., DER, MEMO, expansion-based approaches) which share similarities with Dual-Arch (adding parameters). These comparisons are essential to clarify unique contributions and position the work with respect to prior works (R4).

- **Compute cost not reported**: Dual-Arch is resource-intensive: the stable network must wait for the plastic network to complete learning, doubling runtime. The paper does not report runtime or FLOP counts, which would help assess practicality (R5).

- **Lack of mathematical details**: The methods section lacks precise formulations (e.g., definition of continual learning, specific loss function formulas) (R3).

- **Notable typos**: Tables 1 and 4 captions have “ANN” instead of “AAN” (R1). Section 5.3 has a typo in “ABALATION STUDY” (R5).

- **Specific criticisms and missing experiments/ablations**:
  - The main claim’s empirical evaluation could be more thorough (R1).
  - The ablation study in Table 3 does not include a Pla-Net→Sta-Net combination (R3). How are the stability and plasticity architectures trained? How is the loss modified from Eq. (2)? (R4)
  - Table 1 is hard to interpret: it lacks total parameter count despite referencing it, and both depth and width vary simultaneously, making it hard to isolate effects (R4).
  - The paper should report forgetting in Table 2, as reducing forgetting is a key focus (R4).
  - The model appears to rely on the class-incremental setup. How does it perform predictions within a task? What if task boundaries are not available? A truly continual learning model should be ready to make predictions at any stage (R5).
  - Did the authors consider periodically resetting the plastic learner’s weights to improve plasticity? (R5, citing prior work).
  - The stable learner alone is used for prediction; yet it exhibits plasticity similar to or even greater than the plastic learner (Fig 4c). This is counterintuitive and needs explanation; one possibility is that the stable learner benefits from distilled knowledge. A fair comparison would be to give the stable learner twice the training epochs to control total training (R2).
  - How is the testing workflow implemented? How is the replay mechanism implemented? (R3)
  - For knowledge distillation, do the authors use the current dataset? (R4)
  - The paper does not sufficiently emphasize the limitations of previous methods and the advantages of its own approach, e.g., designing the framework based on structural characteristics (R3).
  - Section 3 results largely align with existing findings (e.g., “Wide Neural Networks Forget Less Catastrophically”), so they do not add much to existing knowledge (R5).
  - The broader implications of the results on other architectures remain unclear and need further explanation (R4).
