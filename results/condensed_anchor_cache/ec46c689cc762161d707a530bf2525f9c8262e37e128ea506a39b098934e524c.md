- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 5, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Vicinal Estimation (VE) for conditional GANs to improve generalization by augmenting conditional samples with an auxiliary distribution. It provides theoretical bounds on the 2-Wasserstein distance that are independent of the dimension of Y and depend only on the dimension of X, using a 1-layer neural network discriminator based on Barron space and early stopping. Experiments on a synthetic dataset show improvement over a baseline cGAN. However, reviewers found the theoretical exposition unclear, the mathematical rigor incomplete, and the experimental validation limited.

### Strengths

1. **Novel combination of vicinal estimation with conditional GANs** (R1, R2, R4). The idea of augmenting conditional samples via an auxiliary distribution is interesting (R4).
2. **Precise theoretical guarantees** are provided, including a bound on the generalization error that does not depend on the dimension of the output Y (R1, R2, R3). The bound is \(N^{-1/(6+2d_X)}\) (R3).
3. **The synthetic data experiment is clearly explained and illustrative** (R1).
4. **Creative integration of a 1-layer discriminator in Barron space and early stopping** to achieve dimension-free rates (R2).
5. **Addresses an important problem** in conditional density estimation (R3).

### Weaknesses

1. **Clarity of theoretical exposition (multiple reviewers).**
   - Section 4 notation is unnecessarily complicated; the simplicity of conditional expectation and variance is obscured (R1).
   - The sets of generators and discriminators in the min-max problem are not clearly specified; it is unclear over which parameters the optimization is performed (R3). For example, the functional space of \(D(y,x)\) in Eq. (5) and its relation to the 1-Wasserstein distance is not explained (R2). Similar confusion exists for \(\tilde D_\alpha\) in Eq. (14) and its Lipschitz property (R2).
   - The definition of \(\tilde p_{X,Y}(y|x')\) in Eq. (19) is not clear (R3).
   - The distribution \(p(x')\) in Eq. (20) is ambiguous: is it the empirical distribution as in Eq. (7)? This confusion persists across Theorems 2 and 3 (R2).
   - Theorem 1 presentation does not highlight the scaling relationship; e.g., expanding \(\|\tilde q^L\|_\infty = L^{-d_x} \|\tilde q^1\|_\infty\) would improve intuition (R1).
   - Section 5 is very dense and hard to read; the results are technical and not easily verifiable (R3). A journal submission with more space for explanation is suggested (R3).
   - Overall, the connection between the proposed method and the Wasserstein-2 error bound is not clearly articulated (R2, R4). The mechanism by which vicinal estimation addresses the curse of dimensionality is not intuitive (R4).

2. **Mathematical rigor issues.**
   - The statement in Eq. (1) about \(p\) is not universally true; a counterexample like a point mass shows it is false (R1).
   - The definition of VE conditional density via Eq. (9) assumes absolute continuity with respect to Lebesgue measure, but the empirical distribution \(p_{X,Y}\) is not absolutely continuous, making the definition not mathematically rigorous (R3).
   - Questions about needed conditions: Does Theorem 3 require the kernel to be characteristic? What are the exact conditions for Theorem 5, and why does \(d_y\) not appear in its statement? (R2)
   - The derivation of the optimal scale of \(\|\tilde q\|_\infty\) after Eq. (33) is not explained (R2).
   - The role of the RKHS regularization in the loss \(V'\) (Eq. 18) is unclear; what would happen without it? (R2)

3. **Insufficient experimental validation.**
   - Only a synthetic experiment is performed; real-world data and applications are missing (R1, R4). This leaves a gap in demonstrating practical utility (R1).
   - No comparison to other methods beyond the baseline cGAN (implied).

4. **Concerns about novelty and significance of the theoretical contribution.**
   - The "early stopping" component is hardly discussed and appears to be a direct adaptation from Yang & E (2021), not a novel contribution (R1). The title might be misleading (R1).
   - One reviewer (R3) argues that the dimension-free rate may be a consequence of the restricted discriminator set (Barron space), not necessarily due to vicinal estimation. For example, using an RKHS with bounded kernel already gives a dimension-independent rate of \(N^{-1/2}\). Thus, the theoretical results do not show that VE outperforms vanilla cGAN (R3). This point is disputed by other reviewers who found the theory a strength (R2).
   - Another reviewer (R4) found the explanation of how vicinal estimation provides "sufficiently many conditional samples" unclear and the connection to the curse of dimensionality not explicit.

5. **Minor issues.**
   - Using \(X\) for labels and \(Y\) for images conflicts with common ML notation (e.g., classification); a clarifying sentence would help (R1).
   - Wording: “a VE vanishes the conditional information” should be “reduces” (R1).
   - Eq. (25) uses \(y\) but \(y\) does not appear on the right-hand side (R2).
   - The relationship between vicinal estimation and importance sampling was questioned (R4).

### Reviewer Disagreement

There is notable disagreement among reviewers. Two reviewers (R1, R2) rated the paper higher (5) and found the theoretical guarantees and novel combination compelling, despite clarity issues. Two reviewers (R3, R4) rated the paper lower (3), citing significant weaknesses in clarity, rigor, and the ability to assess whether the theoretical results truly demonstrate the value of vicinal estimation over existing methods. R3 specifically doubts that the dimension-free bound is attributable to VE rather than the discriminator choice, while R4 found the core idea insufficiently explained. The minority more positive view (R1, R2) emphasizes the potential of the combination and the theoretical derivation.
