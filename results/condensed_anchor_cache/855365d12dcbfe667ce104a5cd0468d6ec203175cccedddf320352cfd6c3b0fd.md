## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes HIDI, a conditional diffusion model-based subgoal generator with a Gaussian Process (GP) prior to address non-stationarity in off-policy hierarchical reinforcement learning (HRL). It uses adaptive inducing states for sparse GP-based subgoal generation and an exploration strategy based on predictive uncertainty. Experiments on continuous control benchmarks show improvements over prior HRL methods.

### Strengths
- The problem of subgoal selection is fundamental in HRL, and the paper tackles a relevant challenge of non-stationarity in off-policy training.
- The combination of diffusion models and GP priors for subgoal generation is novel (reviewers 1,3,4). One reviewer (5) finds the idea sensible and empirical results supportive of the claim that diffusion models fit subgoal distributions well and GP makes learning easier.
- Thorough and detailed ablation study that identifies the effectiveness of individual components (reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- Comprehensive comparison with relevant baselines across multiple environments, including GP-based and count-based exploration methods (reviewers 1,2,3).
- Good use of intrinsic reward for low-level policy learning (reviewer 2).
- Related work provides a concise survey of HRL, subgoal generation, and GP techniques (reviewer 2).
- Use of confidence intervals with a reasonable number of independent runs (reviewer 3).
- Method is extendable and can be combined with other RL algorithms (reviewer 4).
- Sufficient details about computational complexity and reproducibility for the proposed algorithm (reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of clarity on how diffusion models address non-stationarity.** Multiple reviewers (1,2,3,4) find the paper does not provide reasoning, examples, or proof that the diffusion objective aligns the high-level policy with optimal relabeled subgoals. The connection to the original off-policy problem is not established (reviewer 2). The diffusion model is presented without motivating how it mitigates the changing low-level policy (reviewer 1).
- **GP prior benefits oversold.** The claimed benefits of the GP beyond uncertainty quantification (regularization, robustness, sample efficiency) are not convincingly shown. Reviewer 2 notes that Figure 3 shows ablating the GP does not cause significant performance drop, and the claim that GP reduces training data for the diffusion model was never tested. Reviewer 3 finds these claims unconvincing.
- **Exploration strategy appears redundant and poorly justified.** The exploration based on the GP predictive mean (μ*) is not well motivated (reviewers 2,3,4). Multiple reviewers observe that HIDI and HIDI-A (without exploration) have similar performance in Figure 3, suggesting exploration adds little benefit. Reviewer 2 questions how the GP mean is "promising" for exploration.
- **Sample efficiency claims not directly measured.** Reviewers 1 and 2 point out that no metric (e.g., number of samples to reach a target performance) is used to quantify sample efficiency; only performance plots are shown. The comparison with HESS on exploration is also unclear (reviewer 2).
- **Ablation studies limited to AntMaze environment.** Reviewers 2 and 3 note that ablation claims (e.g., "How do various design choices impact performance?") are only validated on AntMaze; reviewer 2 clarifies they are for AntMaze W. The claim that "increasing N is better" is unconvincing as N=3 slightly outperforms N=5 in one case (reviewer 3).
- **Sparse reward definition questioned.** Both reviewers 2 and 3 note that -1 per step is not truly sparse, as it still provides informative signal for subgoal learning via intrinsic reward. A sparser setting (0 everywhere, +1 at goal) would be a more challenging test.
- **Limited environments and scalability concerns.** Reviewers 4 and 5 note that evaluation is limited to a few environments; advantages are not apparent in stochastic Ant Maze (reviewer 4). Reviewer 5 raises a concern about scaling to high-dimensional state spaces (e.g., image observations) since exploration is built on state-space uncertainty. No discussion of GP scalability despite inducing points (reviewer 4).
- **Reproducibility and implementation details insufficient.** Hyperparameter ranges and tuning for baselines are not provided (reviewers 1,2). Several implementation details are unclear: how the "pseudo dataset" D̄ and "imaginary subgoals" ḡ are obtained, how inducing states s̄ are chosen, why the covariance is fixed in diffusion, and how the sparse reward threshold is set (reviewers 2,3,4).
- **Figure/formatting issues.** Figure 5 and Figure 2 show different HESS performance (0.2 vs 0.6 success rate) in Reacher (reviewer 1). Figure 3 lacks specification of shaded regions and number of seeds (reviewer 1). Equations have formatting errors (reviewers 2,3). Environments and Empirical Analyses subsections are incorrectly placed under Related Works (reviewers 1,2).
