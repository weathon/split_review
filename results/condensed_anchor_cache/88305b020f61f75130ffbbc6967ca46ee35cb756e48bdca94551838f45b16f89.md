## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MultiModal Variable Step Forecasting (MM-VSF), a foundation model pretraining framework for geoscience that forecasts spectral satellite imagery from past imagery and weather data, aiming to capture causal relationships between modalities. The authors evaluate on crop mapping (using CDL labels) and missing image prediction, comparing against single-modality and MAE variants.

### Strengths
- The idea of using forecasting of satellite imagery conditioned on weather data as a pretraining task is novel and underexplored in the geoscience foundation model community. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- The authors apply variable-step prediction—a common technique in time series forecasting—to the multimodal pretraining setting, showing performance improvements on two downstream tasks. (Reviewer 2)
- The framework’s ability to generalize across years and handle missing data offers practical utility in real-world remote sensing applications. (Reviewer 4)
- The attempt to combine physics-driven weather data with observation-based remote sensing data is valuable and interesting. (Reviewer 1)
- The paper is generally well-structured (Reviewer 4); writing is satisfactory overall, though there are significant drawbacks (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient baselines and missing SOTA comparisons.** The experimental section is essentially an ablation study: the proposed MM-VSF is only compared to ablation variants (single-modality, MAE-based). No comparisons are made with established remote sensing foundation models such as SkySense (CVPR 2024), SatMAE++ (CVPR 2024), DeCUR (ECCV 2024), MAE-based or contrastive-learning-based models, or general pretrained ViTs. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)  
  *Reviewer 1* especially notes that even pretrained ViT models without fine-tuning achieve commendable performance on RS tasks and should be included. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limited downstream tasks and generalization experiments.** Only two quantitative experiments (crop mapping, missing image prediction) are provided. The model’s generalization ability is not tested on more diverse tasks (e.g., urban semantic segmentation mapping) or on data from different satellites. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)  
  *Reviewer 3* recommends standard benchmarks like BigEarthNet, fMoW, DIOR.
- **Weak or absent analysis of causal relationships.** The causal relationship between weather and imagery is claimed as a major contribution but is only briefly mentioned in the abstract/introduction. No experiments (e.g., cases showing weather influence, ablation isolating weather contribution, quantitative causality metrics, mathematical induction) are provided. (Reviewers 2, 3)  
  *Reviewer 2* suggests presenting cases or ablations to demonstrate how weather affects predictions.
- **Lack of fine-grained ablation studies.** The architecture uses multiple components (BiLSTM for weather, ViT for imagery, causal Transformer for fusion) but no ablation justifies these choices (e.g., ViT vs. SwinViT, LSTM vs. GRU, different masking ratios/strategies, unidirectional vs. bidirectional processing of weather). (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)  
  *Reviewer 2* specifically asks why causal encoding is applied to fused embeddings while BiLSTM is used for weather data, and whether the method affects image regions outside restored areas (mosaic artifacts in Fig. 6).
- **Fundamental assumption may be flawed.** Forecasting spectral imagery solely from weather and past imagery overlooks human activities (e.g., farmers choosing not to plant, urban construction independent of weather). This questions the suitability of the pretraining task for a wide range of remote sensing tasks. (Reviewer 3)  
  *Reviewer 1* adds that claims about the temporal flexible architecture (line 093) and richer embeddings (line 096) lack theoretical support and experimental validation. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limited qualitative analysis and suboptimal visual results.** The visual results (e.g., Fig. 6, Fig. 8) are of limited utility; in Fig. 8, the MM-MAE baseline appears visually superior to SM-MAE, and SM-VSF results are blurred; Figures 6 and 8 look very similar. No detailed analysis of challenging cases or visualization of learned representations is provided. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Unclear methodology and training details.** The data format for weather information is ambiguous (e.g., why shape is 1×1?), and it is unclear how BiLSTM processes it. The overall methodology lacks formalization (input/output description, data flow pipeline). Hyperparameters (epochs, batch size), fine-tuning procedures, and architecture selection rationale (ViT over SwinViT, LSTM over GRU) are not clearly presented. (Reviewers 1, 3)  
  *Reviewer 3* suggests providing a formal description of input and output data.
- **Incomplete related work.** Important works are missing from the discussion, including multimodal RS models that use satellite and weather data (Ravirathinam et al. SDM 2024; Nedungadi et al. MMEarth arXiv 2024), weather forecasting models (GraphCast Science 2023, Fuxi Nature Comms 2024), and contrastive learning methods (DeCUR ECCV 2024, DINO-MM IGRASS 2022). (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Writing and presentation issues.** The paper lacks academic rigor, with informal expressions, grammatical errors, improper punctuation, formatting issues (e.g., using $\times$ instead of x), typos, and unclear text in Figures 1 and 2. A thorough revision is recommended. (Reviewer 1, 4)
- **Missing details on data handling.** CDL labels are known for noisy annotations; no label correction steps are mentioned. (Reviewer 3)  
  *Reviewer 4* asks how variable-step forecasting handles significant time gaps in satellite imagery.
- **Contrastive learning not addressed.** The potential of contrastive learning for RS foundation models is not discussed or compared. (Reviewers 3, 4)

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (Reviewer 1, rating 1) is strongly critical, finding the paper lacking in experimental/theoretical support, experimental design, and presentation; others (Reviewers 2 and 4, rating 5) acknowledge the novelty and practical utility but also point out significant shortcomings including insufficient experiments, missing baselines, and lack of ablations. Reviewer 3 (rating 3) considers the approach novel but criticizes the flawed assumption, insufficient analysis of causality, and need for more thorough evaluation.
