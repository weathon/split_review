## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes UniTS, a hybrid framework for long-term multivariate time series forecasting that combines CNN for local feature extraction (LFE) with MLP or Transformer for global feature extraction (GFE). It also standardizes parameters like lookback length to address unfair comparisons in prior work. Experiments on eight benchmark datasets (ILI, electricity, weather, traffic, etc.) show MSE/MAE improvements, especially at long horizons (e.g., prediction length 60 over transformers, 720 over PatchTST). The code is open source.

### Strengths
1. **Effective hybrid design**: Leverages complementary strengths of CNN (local) and MLP/Transformer (global) for temporal modeling.
2. **Strong empirical performance**: Outperforms baselines across diverse datasets (ILI, electricity, weather, traffic) in MSE/MAE, particularly at long forecasting horizons (up to 60 vs. transformers, up to 720 vs. PatchTST) (Reviewer 2).
3. **Extensive ablation studies**: Analyzes the contribution of each module (LFE, GFE, attention, positional encoding, instance normalization, decomposition, etc.) and demonstrates the necessity of the multiscale hybrid approach (Reviewers 2, 3).
4. **Open-source code and reproducibility**: Code provided and experiments are fully reproducible (Reviewer 3).
5. **Addresses unfair comparison**: Highlights the issue of inconsistent lookback window usage and attempts to standardize parameters (Reviewer 1).
6. **Thorough hyperparameter analysis**: Compares different parameter search methods and the impact of various hyperparameter choices on predictive performance (Reviewer 3).
7. **Well-written paper**: Clear presentation with convincing experimental setup (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
1. **Unclear presentation of components**: Key notations are not explained: meanings of \( H^{l,N} \) and \( H^{g,N} \) in Figure 1, how LFE and GFE operate after Patching, the variable \( j \) and the Decompose operation in the first equation of Section 3.2.3, and \( V_i \) is used before being defined (Reviewer 1).
2. **Missing baseline comparisons**: The paper does not compare against RLinear and RMLP (Li et al. 2023), which outperform PatchTST on some datasets (Reviewer 1). It also omits Spacetimeformer (Grigsby et al. 2023, prediction lengths up to 672) and NBeats, both SOTA for long-range multivariate forecasting and the former featuring a local+global architecture (Reviewer 2).
3. **Potentially unfair baseline evaluation**: UniTS uses its own hyperparameter tuning (learning rate, hidden size, etc.), but baselines are not similarly tuned, raising a potential fairness issue (Reviewer 1). Furthermore, PatchTST/64 is compared using only lookback 512, while PatchTST’s performance varies with lookback; the best result across lookbacks should be used (Reviewer 1). This contradicts the paper’s own call for standardized parameters.
4. **Limited novelty in architecture**: LFE and GFE components are largely adapted from existing approaches; the combination of existing high-performing modules does not introduce truly novel elements (Reviewer 2: “not very novel”; Reviewer 3: “lacks innovativeness” — Reviewer 3 was notably more critical on this point).
5. **Weak justification for module choices**: The paper does not provide a rigorous rationale for selecting the specific modules (e.g., why these over alternative well-established time series forecasting modules), nor does it explore or compare alternatives (Reviewer 3).
6. **No complexity analysis**: Computational complexity, especially from hyperparameter selection, is not analyzed (Reviewer 1).
7. **Unanswered interpretability question**: The ablation shows attention and positional encoding are useful, yet PatchTST (which uses only linear layers) outperforms attention-based models like Autoformer/Fedformer; no explanation is provided (Reviewer 2).
8. **Typos**: “four primary categories” should be “three” (Page 1); “overaTidell” typo in Table 1 caption (Page 6) (Reviewer 1).
