## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces CLEAR-VAE, a weakly supervised framework that disentangles content and style in the latent space of a Variational Autoencoder using a Pair Switching (PS) anti-contrastive regularization. Content labels serve as weak supervision: a contrastive loss (adapted from SNN) encourages content features to be similar within the same label, and an anti-contrastive loss (label flipping) encourages style features to be ambiguous with respect to the label. Experiments on styled-MNIST, CelebA, and Amazon Product Reviews demonstrate qualitative swapping/interpolation (Fig. 3, 4, Appx. A.6) and quantitative improvements in generalization to unseen content-style combinations over a baseline (ML-VAE) and a standard β-VAE.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and significance**: Pair Switching is a new anti-contrastive regularization that directly addresses limitations of previous disentanglement approaches by encouraging independent style distributions across content labels. (R1,R2,R3)
- **Problem significance**: Disentangling content and style to improve generalizability and mitigate biases is important for robust representations and fairness, e.g., in healthcare. (R1,R2,R3,R5)
- **Elegance of anti-contrastive regularization**: Flipping labels in the contrastive loss avoids negative terms in the total loss, simplifying optimization compared to directly minimizing −L_SNN^{(s)}. (R2)
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: Qualitative results (swapping, interpolation) are impressive and clearly show disentanglement. Quantitative setups are well-defined and demonstrate improved generalization. (R1,R2,R3,R4,R5)
- **Clear intuition**: The problem is clearly motivated with the healthcare example, and the theoretical foundations (ELBO, Mutual Information Gap, classifier-free disentanglement metrics) are soundly presented. (R4,R5)
- **Thorough experimental analysis**: Includes swapping, interpolation, and generalizability experiments on unseen content-style combinations. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited comparisons with recent methods**: The paper omits many relevant baselines: weakly-supervised disentanglement approaches (e.g., Group VAE, SW-VAE [ICLR'22], Weakly Supervised Disentangled Generative Causal Representation Learning [JMLR'22]), content-style identification works (Self-Supervised Learning with Data Augmentations Provably Isolates Content from Style, Multi-View Causal Representation Learning with Partial Observability), contrastive learning methods (Supervised Contrastive Learning by Khosla et al.), and other VAE extensions (cc-VAE [ICLR'21], Contrastive VAE [Abid & Zou 2019], separating common from salient patterns [Louiset et al. ICLR'24]). This weakens the contribution and positioning. (R1,R2,R3,R4,R5)
- **Pair Switching not compared with standard mutual information minimization losses** such as Total Correlation or kernel Joint Entropy Minimization. (R4)
- **Mathematical errors and unclear writing**:
  - Equation 1 should be the negative ELBO. (R2,R5)
  - Inconsistent notation: L_SNN^{(s)} and L_SNN^{(s)} used interchangeably. (R2)
  - Equation 9 uses z* without annotation. (R2,R5)
  - Missing closing parentheses in Eqs. 4,5,6; Eq. 8 writes c instead of s. (R5)
  - L155, L194, L215, L253: many unexplained terms. (R5)
  - L184: cannot reasonably assume the posterior factorizes since z_c and z_s are dependent given x. (R5)
  - L204: unclear what the norm of two comma-separated terms is. (R5)
  - L236: claim that a negative value would complicate minimization lacks explanation or experiment validation. (R2,R5)
  - L248: unclear why the model would reach an equilibrium. (R5)
  - L358: unclear why contrastive regularization is applied only to EOS token latent representation while KL applies to all contextualized embeddings. (R2)
  - Multiple typographical errors (L40, L147, L201, L218, L247) and incoherent statements (L63, L149-L151). (R5)
- **Hyperparameter analysis insufficient**: More depth needed on the impact of temperature and similarity metrics in the contrastive loss, especially on complex datasets. (R1)
- **Limited datasets and scenarios**: Experiments use only three relatively simplified datasets (styled-MNIST, CelebA, Amazon Reviews). Testing on more complex real-world datasets (e.g., medical imaging) with higher-dimensional style variations is needed. (R3,R4) No healthcare application experiment despite motivation. (R4)
- **Motivation question**: If content labels are available and only content is needed for downstream tasks, why train a VAE rather than a supervised classifier? (R3,R5) Why use an autoencoder when only the encoder is needed for the downstream task? (R5)
- **Assumption of complete and reliable content labels**: The method relies on content labels as weak supervision; label noise or ambiguities could limit performance. (R1) How to handle partially observed style attributes in practice (e.g., in healthcare)? Could it be adapted into a semi-supervised framework? (R4) Experiment with only a subset of content labels available would be informative. (R3)
- **Discrepancy in performance trends across datasets**: As K (number of classes in training content-style combinations) increases, CLEAR-VAE's advantage over baseline diminishes on styled-MNIST but amplifies on CelebA; explanation needed. (R2)
- **Clarification needed**: In L233, does "ambiguous about the supervised label" mean minimizing I(z_s; y) or maximizing gMIG(y)? (R2)
- **Terminology and conceptual issues**: Difference between group and class? What is the Jeffrey divergence? Why the naming "anti-contrastive"? (R5)
- **VAEs are not state-of-the-art** for generation or representation learning compared to diffusion models or pure contrastive methods, limiting practical impact. (R4)
- **Medical domain example questionable**: Differences directly caused by sensitive attributes should not be removed from predictions in a fair decision-making context. (R5)
- **Minority reviewer disagreement**: While R1 and R2 (score 5) are more positive on novelty and evaluation, R3/4/5 (score 3) consider the literature review weak, the motivation unclear, and the experiments insufficiently challenging. R5 (confidence 5) is most critical, pointing out many writing/math errors and missing comparisons. (All reviewers)
