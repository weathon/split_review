## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a memory module (DT-Mem) that stores, blends, and retrieves transition embeddings inside a Decision Transformer (GPT-2) for offline multi-task RL. Experiments on Atari games and Meta-world object manipulation show improvements in pre-training, generalization, and fine-tuning with LoRA.

### Strengths
- The general motivation—enhancing memorization via an internal memory module to improve multi-task RL—is sound and well-motivated (R2, R4).
- Experiments are sufficient and demonstrate clear performance gains over baselines including RMDT (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1, R2).
- The architecture diagram (Figure 2) and step-by-step explanation of training/inference are clear (R3).
- Evaluation includes well-designed questions that test the paper’s hypotheses (R3).
- Comparison against multiple sizes of MDT (Figures 3, 4) effectively shows that parameter-based memorization is less efficient than the proposed learnable memory (R3).
- Additional Appendix results (especially Figure 10) provide helpful insight (R3).
- One reviewer (R3) found the rebuttal convincing and increased their score, noting that the connection between motivation and method was clarified.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and relation to prior work** (R1, R2, R4): The memory mechanism (content-based addressing, add/erase vectors) is heavily based on NTM/DNC. The paper does not clearly differentiate from earlier memory-augmented models such as Neural Episodic Control, long‑range attention Transformers, or retrieval‑based RL methods. More explicit comparison with these lines of work is needed (R2, R4). Only RMDT is used as a memory-based baseline, which is insufficient; other memory-based Transformers adapted to offline RL should be tested (R4).  
- **Insufficient detail on the memory module** (R1, R2, R3, R4):  
  - How read and write operations interact with the Transformer layers is not explained in enough detail (R1).  
  - Memory initialization is not specified: is it re-initialized per episode (as in NTM) or kept throughout training? This affects both novelty and reproducibility (R3, R1, R2).  
  - The influence of retrieved memory on action generation is unclear (R1).  
  - Figure 2 is misleading: the memory module appears to be stacked as a Transformer layer rather than a separate component (R2); the term “retrieved memory” is confusing and should be “retrieved experiences” or similar (R2).  
  - An algorithmic description of memory read/write within a trajectory is missing (R4).  
  - The importance of Step 1 in the memory procedure is not ablated (R4).  
- **Motivation and connection to results** (R3, R4): The paper argues that memorization in parameters leads to forgetting, but it does not explicitly demonstrate how the proposed memory reduces forgetting or how reduced forgetting directly causes better generalization on unseen tasks. The link between the motivation and the observed performance improvements remains unclear (R3). No ablation or visualization proves that the memory (rather than LoRA or representation tricks) is the cause of the improvements (R4).  
- **Lack of ablation studies and memory analysis** (R1, R2, R3, R4):  
  - No ablation on LoRA; because the model is small (50M parameters), LoRA may be unnecessary and could harm performance (R2, R4).  
  - No ablation on memory adding/erasing or on Step 1 (R4).  
  - No analysis of memory capacity limits, catastrophic forgetting, or the impact of noisy/irrelevant stored information (R1, R2).  
  - No visualization showing that meaningful experiences are stored and retrieved (R4).  
  - It is unclear whether prompting (instead of fine-tuning) can be used with DT-Mem (R2).  
  - Could MDT also be fine-tuned with LoRA (R3)?  
- **Writing and clarity issues** (R2, R3, R4):  
  - The paper refers to “Large Language Model based decision making agents” when the tokens are game-specific; “transformer” or “decoder-only transformer” would be more precise (R2, R3).  
  - Section 3.1 assumes familiarity with offline RL; a brief introduction and listing of standard baselines (DT, MGDT, RMDT, HDT) is needed (R2, R4).  
  - Figure 5 shows abbreviations NFT and FT without explanation, and no results for NFT are plotted (R3).  
  - Line 243: “reduce this value as close to zero as possible” contradicts the objective of maximizing cumulative reward; the phrasing should be corrected (R3).  
  - Line 297: typo “we generat” should be “we generate” (R3).  
  - Related work omits many memory-based Transformer papers (R4).  
- **Additional requested comparisons and experiments** (R2, R3, R4):  
  - Test an appending-style external memory DT and retrieval-based RL baselines to highlight the advantages of the proposed internal memory (R2, R3, R4).  
  - Evaluate on tasks with long-term dependencies (e.g., MemoryMaze) where memory should be especially beneficial (R3).  
  - Provide clarification on the motivation for computing strength $\beta$ via attention, and whether $w$ in lines 202, 215, and 232 is the same (R4).  
- **Minor contrast**: While one reviewer (R3) became more positive after rebuttal, the concerns above remain from multiple other reviewers, and the paper still lacks several critical analyses and comparisons.
