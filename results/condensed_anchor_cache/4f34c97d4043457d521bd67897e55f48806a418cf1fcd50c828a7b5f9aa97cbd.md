## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Subtracted Importance Sampled Scores (SISS), a family of loss functions that integrate importance sampling to enable data unlearning in diffusion models. SISS balances fine-tuning on retained data (naive deletion) and gradient ascent on the set to unlearn. Two variations are tested: SISS with λ=0.5 using importance sampling, and SISS (no IS) without importance sampling. Experiments on CelebA-HQ, MNIST, and Stable Diffusion show promising results, with SISS achieving Pareto optimality and mitigating memorization on ~90% of prompts tested. Reviewers generally find the work novel, well-written, and experimentally sound, but raise concerns about computational cost justification, hyperparameter analysis, theoretical grounding, and clarity of certain design choices. One reviewer is significantly more positive (rating 8), one more negative (rating 3), indicating substantive disagreement on core motivations and method necessity.

### Strengths
- Novel application of importance sampling within a loss function for data unlearning, not previously explored in literature.
- Simple method with light mathematical derivations, easy to follow.
- Well-written and clearly structured, effectively motivating the problem and approach.
- Promising numerical performance: significant improvements over reported baselines across standard benchmarks (CelebA-HQ, MNIST, Stable Diffusion).
- Comprehensive experiments including ablations, covering multiple datasets and evaluation metrics.
- Uses standard benchmarks and well-designed experiments, supporting effectiveness and efficiency claims.

### Weaknesses
- **Computational cost analysis is insufficient.** The paper lacks a detailed breakdown of operations in forward/backward passes, number of gradient calculations, and memory requirements. The cost of computing importance weights and its impact on runtime should be quantified. A summary comparison table with other methods is needed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Inconsistency in fine-tuning steps across experiments.** Table 1 uses 60 steps while Table 2 uses various steps, undermining comparability of results. Results for Table 2 with the same step count across all methods should be reported. (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient hyperparameter exploration for λ.** Only λ=0 and λ=1 are explicitly tested; behavior for λ between 0–0.5 and 0.5–1 is not analyzed, leaving the method’s sensitivity to this hyperparameter unclear. Furthermore, λ=1 is missing from Fig. 3 and λ=0 from Fig. 6(a), complicating interpretation. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **The necessity of importance sampling (IS) is unclear.** SISS (no IS) shows similar or better performance than SISS with IS and λ=0.5 across all experiments, and the computational overhead of IS occurs only during training. This raises the question of whether the defensive mixture distribution is needed at all. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Core motivation for sampling from the unlearning set A is not justified.** The paper does not explain why involving samples from A is beneficial for data unlearning, which is the foundational motivation for SISS. An alternative simpler objective is suggested that also resembles Eq. (4) but requires only one forward pass. (Reviewer 4)
- **Choice of defensive mixture distribution may be problematic.** Sampling from \( q_\lambda(m_t | x, a) \), a mixture of two normals, produces interpolated noisy images that may not be suitable for the denoising objective. Fig. 2 hints at an overlay issue, indicating potential innate problems with the objective. (Reviewer 4)
- **Theoretical justifications are insufficient.** While light derivations are appreciated, the paper could be strengthened by connecting to traditional machine unlearning tools and discussing difficulties unique to the generative setting. (Reviewer 2)
- **Ablation studies are incomplete.** The effect of the “superfactor” s in Eq. (8) on unlearning performance is not tested. (Reviewer 2)
- **Limitations of synthetic data and k-means clustering for Stable Diffusion are not discussed.** A qualitative analysis of similarity (beyond clustering) would strengthen the evaluation. (Reviewer 3)
- **No discussion of why SISS surpasses gold-standard retraining on MNIST T-Shirt (Fig. 4b).** A brief explanation of this surprising result is needed. (Reviewer 3)
- **Concern about training from scratch despite claims:** Eq. (4) involves an expectation over the full training distribution \( p_X \), which seems to require retraining; the paper should clarify how this is avoided. (Reviewer 2)
- **Minor formatting issue:** Page 8 contains excessive white space that could be used or eliminated. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing details:** How the importance sampling ratio is calculated, and why 6 samples were chosen for the CelebA removal set, are not explained. (Reviewer 4)
- **Alternative simpler objective not empirically compared:** The suggested objective \((1 - \delta_{x\in A})L(\theta, x) - \lambda (\delta_{x\in A})L(\theta, x)\) should be tested against SISS to demonstrate advantage. (Reviewer 4)
