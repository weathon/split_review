## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces a multimodal few-shot 3D point cloud segmentation setup that leverages free textual labels and 2D images. The proposed MM-FSS model uses a shared backbone with two heads (for intermodal and unimodal features) and a pretrained text encoder. It includes a Multimodal Correlation Fusion (MCF) module, a Multimodal Semantic Fusion (MSF) module that refines correlations with text guidance, and a test-time adaptive cross-modal calibration (TACC) technique. Experiments on S3DIS and ScanNet show strong performance improvements over unimodal baselines.

### Strengths

- The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and each design choice is clearly motivated.
- The multimodal few-shot setup is novel, cost-free (no added annotation effort), and consistent with standard few-shot tasks – leveraging free modalities provides valuable insights.
- The model design is novel and justified: MCF exploits cross-modal visual correlations, MSF incorporates textual semantics, and TACC is a parameter-free test-time calibration.
- Performance gains are substantial; experiments are thorough with detailed ablations and visualizations that validate the design.
- The use of 2D images is efficient: 2D features are learned during pretraining, so 2D inputs are not required at inference.
- The work is significant for reducing the annotation burden in 3D segmentation by combining text and images.

### Weaknesses

- **Clarity and presentation issues**: (a) Two paragraphs (lines 235–240 and 259–266) redundantly describe the same training steps and should be merged. (b) The integration of 2D image modality is insufficiently detailed – it is unclear how 2D features are extracted, aligned with 3D point clouds, and used (direct augmentation vs. indirect via loss/attention). (c) The regions in Figure 3 need more explanation. (d) A typo exists in line 110 (“ii” should be “iii”).
- **MSF module explanation**: Reviewer 2 requests more explanation of how the MSF module’s design achieves correlation improvement. The mechanism of text-aware semantic guidance refining multimodal correlations is not fully clarified.
- **Lack of discussion on using multimodal features for few-shot**: While multimodal 3D segmentation has been explored in fully supervised and open-vocab settings, it is unclear why prior few-shot methods only used unimodal information. The challenges/difficulties specific to few-shot should be discussed.
- **Ambiguity of “cost-free”**: The term “cost-free” is ambiguous (e.g., could include training cost) and may be unnecessary.
- **Missing ablation isolating each modality’s contribution**: Existing ablations evaluate components (MCF, MSF, TACC) but do not isolate the individual contributions of the 2D image modality and the text modality (e.g., text-only, 2D-only, both). Also lacking discussion of scenarios where one modality is more beneficial than the other.
- **Missing computational complexity comparison**: There is no comparison of computational complexity or efficiency with other methods.
- **Limited dataset scale and scalability concerns**: Existing FS-PCS datasets are small. The paper should acknowledge this limitation and discuss potential challenges, computational resource requirements, and needed modifications for scaling to larger, more complex datasets.
- **Unanswered methodological details**: (a) How are the viewpoints for the RGB images fed into the VLM determined? (b) Could textual or other information be incorporated into the support set for further gains? This exploration is missing. (c) The selection of support samples appears random; theoretical guidance for support selection would strengthen the work.
