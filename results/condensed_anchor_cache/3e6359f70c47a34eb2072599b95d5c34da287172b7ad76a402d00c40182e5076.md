## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper extends conformal prediction for survival analysis from type-I right-censored data to general right-censored data, where either the survival or censoring time is observed but not both. It proposes doubly robust calibration methods (focused and fused calibration) to construct lower predictive bounds (LPBs) with finite-sample distribution-free guarantees, building on Gui et al. (2024). Experiments include synthetic datasets (lognormal/exponential distributions) and one real-world dataset (TCGA, n=1044). Reviewer opinions are split: Reviewers 2 and 3 consider the work well-motivated with solid theoretical support and practical value, while Reviewers 1 and 4 find the contribution incremental over Gui et al. (2024) and the experimental evaluation lacking comparisons and depth.

### Strengths
- Extends conformal prediction to general right-censored data, a setting not covered by prior works that assume known censoring times (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- Provides doubly robust calibration with finite-sample, distribution-free guarantees for lower predictive bounds (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- Well-written and well-motivated; the paper clearly explains the challenges and progresses from a naive method to focused and fused calibration, each addressing limitations of the previous (Reviewer 2).
- Strong theoretical support with theorems guaranteeing validity under certain conditions (Reviewers 2,3).
- Includes both simulated and real-world data experiments to demonstrate practical utility (Reviewers 1,2,3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of comparisons with existing methods**: The paper only compares against a simple naive calibration baseline. No comparisons to other survival analysis methods (e.g., weighted Cox regression, Qin et al., Meixide et al., Qi et al.) are provided, making it difficult to assess the practical value of the theoretical guarantees (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- **Simplistic synthetic experiments**: Synthetic datasets use only lognormal and exponential distributions, which do not reflect real-world complexities such as non-parametric censoring, competing risks, time-varying covariates, or non-proportional hazards. In Setting 6, the proposed methods underperform the naive method, raising robustness concerns, and the data may not adequately represent a general right-censoring setting (Reviewers 2,3,4).
- **Insufficient real-world evaluation**: Only one small dataset (TCGA, n=1044) is used. The evaluation lacks proper calibration metrics (e.g., distribution calibration from Haider et al.) and does not report coverage results or test on other real datasets (e.g., those from Candes et al. 2023, Gui et al. 2024) (Reviewer 2,4). The proposed focused and fused LPBs are not significantly tighter than the naive LPB on this data (Reviewer 4).
- **Incremental contribution and unclear presentation**: The method builds heavily on Gui et al. (2024); the only new component is the miscoverage estimator. Many sections rely on the prior work and are unclear without referencing it (e.g., the role of PAC-type LPBs) (Reviewer 4). The motivation for general right-censoring over type-I is not convincingly argued (Reviewer 4).
- **Theoretical gaps and inconsistencies**: The theoretical analysis only provides a lower bound on coverage, and the claim (line 294-295) that inaccurate estimation of \(\hat{s}_\tau\) does not affect validity appears to contradict Theorem 3.1, which requires accurate weights (Reviewer 2). Sensitivity of results to hyperparameters (weight estimation, conditional quantile estimation) is not investigated, and computational complexity of the fused calibration is only superficially discussed (Reviewer 1). Proof notation contains errors (e.g., Lemma B.1, Section B.2) (Reviewer 4).
- **Practical challenges with double robustness and IPW**: Achieving either accurate censoring model or outcome model is difficult in practice; the paper does not analyze sensitivity to misspecification. Inverse probability weighting is known to have high variance and sensitivity to high censoring rates; the paper does not report censoring rates or assess performance across varying rates, nor does it discuss strategies to handle extreme weights (Reviewers 2,4). Reviewers also ask about the impact of weight estimation error and how to evaluate weight accuracy (Reviewer 1,4).
- **Waste of information and inconsistent performance**: Focused and fused calibration use only a subset of the calibration data (uncensored or a threshold-based selection), which may waste information, especially under high censoring (Reviewer 4). The methods are not consistently better than the naive method across synthetic settings (e.g., Settings 4 and 6) (Reviewers 3,4).
