## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviewers find TIMBA to combine existing techniques (bidirectional Mamba with S6, GNN, CFEM, NEM, diffusion) for multivariate time series imputation, achieving good empirical performance on three spatio-temporal datasets (PEMS-Bay, etc.). However, they raise significant concerns about limited novelty, unclear motivation for each component, insufficient ablation studies and baseline comparisons, marginal performance gains given standard deviations, and lack of analysis on non-spatio-temporal data. One reviewer (R1, rating 5) deemed the paper clear and technically sound with improved performance, while three reviewers (R2–R4, rating 3) considered the contribution incremental and the architecture over-designed.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow (R1).
- By and large the proposed method is technically sound (R1).
- Explores interesting technical combinations: bidirectional Mamba with S6 for bidirectional context, GNN for spatial relationships between variables, and sensitivity to varying missing rates (R2).
- Addresses a relevant problem—integrating State-Space Models into time series imputation (R4).
- Empirical results are competitive, often outperforming or matching state-of-the-art on benchmark datasets, with limited but consistent improvements (R1, R3, R4).
- Includes ablation study (TIMBA vs. TIMBA-Uni), sensitivity analysis to missing rates, and downstream task evaluation (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The primary contribution is replacing S4 with S6 (another SSM variant) or substituting Transformer layers with bidirectional Mamba in an existing diffusion architecture (PriSTI). This is incremental and of little new insight (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Unclear motivation and insufficient rationale for components**: The paper combines multiple techniques (SSM, GNN, CFEM, NEM, diffusion) without theoretical or empirical justification for each choice. Why are SSMs better than Transformers, RNNs, or TCNs for sequences of at most 36 time steps? The necessity of GNN-based representation for temporal data, CFEM, and NEM is not explained (R2, R3, R4).
- **Incomplete ablation studies**: Only the effect of bidirectionality (TIMBA vs. TIMBA-Uni) is ablated. Missing ablations: S6 vs. no S6, unidirectional vs. bidirectional Mamba, replacement of Mamba with other sequence models (RNNs, TCNs), and independent contribution of GNN, CFEM, and NEM. Without these, the benefit of each component is unknown (R2, R3, R4).
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons**: The paper compares only two diffusion-based models (CSDI, PriSTI) and many traditional ML methods. Missing comparisons to other diffusion imputation models (SSSD, diffusion with RNNs), attention-based architectures (SAITS, SPIN, ImputeFormer, SADI), and recent 2024 works. The omission makes it unclear if TIMBA outperforms state-of-the-art (R2, R3, R4).
- **Results discrepancies and marginal significance**: Some results (e.g., PriSTI) differ from original publications without explanation. Performance improvements are often small relative to reported standard deviations; on PEMS-Bay, CSDI with only diffusion slightly outperforms TIMBA, raising overfitting concerns (R3, R4). One reviewer (R4) suspects better hyperparameter selection for baselines could close the gap.
- **Limited training epochs**: Most experiments use only 50 training epochs, which may be insufficient for convergence of generative models (R1).
- **MAR vs. MCAR ambiguity**: The limitations section claims MAR, but the paper does not clarify how the probability of missingness is modeled or whether experiments actually use MCAR (R1).
- **No non-spatio-temporal datasets**: Experiments focus on spatio-temporal datasets; clinical healthcare or other non-spatio-temporal time series are not evaluated, limiting generalizability and mismatching the paper’s title (R3).
- **Formatting issues**: Some tables are out of range, hurting readability (R3).
- **Missing references**: Relevant works (SPIN, SAITS, recent 2024 imputation papers) are not cited or discussed (R3, R4).
