## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper explores the use of large language models (LLMs) on single‑cell transcriptomics data, distinguishing them from single‑cell foundation models (e.g., scGPT). Ten pre‑trained models encode text representations of single‑cell data (“cell sentences”); Ember‑V1 outperforms other general‑purpose LLMs, while scGPT outperforms the generalist class as a whole. Including potential cell types in the prompt improves LLM cell‑classification performance. Interpretability (integrated gradients, LIME) identifies marker genes among top‑importance features. Obfuscating gene names, expression frequencies, or both in cell sentences still yields above‑random performance, suggesting that structural/syntactic information is used. Finally, scMPT joins Ember‑V1 (frozen) and scGPT (frozen) via trainable MLP layers and shows stronger, more consistent cell‑classification performance than either component alone, frequently lying between GPT‑4o and scGPT. The paper is aware of recent literature but is considered a surface‑level bundle of investigations; technical novelty is weak, and the claimed biological insights are not deeply validated.

### Strengths
- Comprehensive comparison across multiple text encoders for single‑cell cell‑type annotation, with awareness of recent literature and sufficient replication detail (though replicates are missing in most experiments).
- The stated goal—elucidating what pre‑trained biological knowledge LLMs leverage in single‑cell analysis—is interesting and potentially useful.
- scMPT consistently outperforms standalone scGPT and individual LLMs by combining complementary insights from text and gene expression.
- The model effectively identifies biologically relevant marker genes, adding transparency to cell‑type predictions.
- scMPT’s architecture keeps both encoders frozen, reducing computational cost while improving accuracy.
- The finding that Ember‑V1 leverages both gene semantics and syntactical similarity of cell sentences is interesting.
- The hybrid approach is simple but effective, and the performance on cell‑type annotation is good.
- Part of the discussion about biomarkers is interesting.

### Weaknesses
- The study lacks depth on each topic; it feels like a bundle of surface‑level investigations rather than a focused, depth‑first study.
- The technique contribution is weak: scMPT simply concatenates the outputs of scGPT and an LLM encoder (like GenePT) through a dense layer, analogous to early NLP ensemble approaches.
- The paper largely confirms known knowledge (e.g., marker genes are crucial for cell‑type classification) without introducing new biological or practical insights.
- The highlighted markers from interpretability experiments are not followed by further analysis that could provide biological or practical insights.
- scMPT’s performance mostly lies between GPT‑4o and scGPT, which is not surprising given the straightforward ensemble.
- The biological factors actually driving LLM performance improvements are not clearly defined; the interpretability and ablation studies are not definitive.
- The study only uses “cell sentences” as the textual representation, potentially missing other informative representations.
- Figure 1 implies all information comes from scRNA data; non‑biological factors (e.g., syntax) may contribute to performance, questioning the biological specificity of embeddings.
- A detailed analysis of biological tasks beyond cell‑type annotation is lacking—e.g., gene regulatory networks, aging, cross‑species analysis, novel cell‑type discovery, which are standard in comparable sc‑foundation‑model papers.
- Only a few datasets are used; robust analysis across multiple species, tissue types, aging, development, or a large‑scale dataset is absent.
- The related work criticizes the ensembling approach in GenePT (aggregating predictions) but does not benchmark that strategy (should be included in Table 6).
- In the ablation study, a hash function was used to obscure gene names; it is unclear why this was chosen over random strings, and hashed names may carry permuted information.
- No ablation study on the information loss incurred when converting scRNA formats to text formats has been conducted.
- The paper does not cite or compare with other top‑performing single‑cell foundation models (e.g., CellPLM, scFoundation, GeneCompass, scBERT) to see if scMPT can be combined with them for higher performance.
- Several tables exceed page margins (P6, P7, P8, P9, P19).
