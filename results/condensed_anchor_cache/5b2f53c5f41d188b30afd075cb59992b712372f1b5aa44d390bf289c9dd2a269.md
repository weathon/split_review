## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper attempts to prove that multi-neuron convex relaxations can compute exact bounds for general ReLU networks, overcoming the expressivity limitation of single-neuron relaxations. The authors propose two relaxations—$M_3$ and $M_{d_{in}+3}^o$—and build on Hanin’s construction to claim that any piecewise linear function has a ReLU representation whose output can be exactly bounded by these relaxations. The motivating max-function example is included. However, multiple reviewers identify a critical flaw in Lemma 3 (ReLU networks map polytopes to polytopes) and its use in Theorem 2, which undermines the main results. The lack of precision in definitions and proofs, missing experimental evaluation, and absence of complexity analysis further weaken the paper. One reviewer strongly rejects (score 1) due to the unsupported main claim; two others rate 5 but have major concerns; one rates 5 with reservations about presentation and implementation.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a theoretically important question: whether convex relaxations can provide exact bounds for general ReLU networks, which is of interest to the neural network robustness/verification community. (R1, R3)
- It presents a novel attempt to establish full expressivity of ReLU networks under multi-neuron relaxations for the first time. (R2)
- The motivating example of the max function in $\mathbb{R}^2$ is clearly illustrated, demonstrating the failure of single-neuron relaxation and the potential of multi-neuron relaxation. (R1, R3)
- The connection between stable/unstable neurons and multi-neuron relaxation (Lemmas 7 and 8) is considered novel and clearly presented. (R3)
- The paper is generally well written with sufficient motivation; the importance of the problem is convincingly argued. (R1, R3)
- The case study on max functions suggests that multi-neuron relaxation could improve guarantees, motivating further research. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Critical flaw in Lemma 3 and Theorem 2:** Lemma 3 claims that ReLU neural networks transform polytopes into polytopes, but a simple counterexample shows otherwise: $P=[-1,1]$, $f(x)=(x,\max\{x,0\})$ yields the graph of the ReLU, which is not convex and thus not a polytope (R4). Another counterexample: let $L=k=d_{in}=d_{out}=2$, $X=\{(x,y) \mid -2 \leq x+y \leq 2,\ -4\leq x-y \leq 4\}$, $W_1=W_2=I$, so $f(x)=\rho(x)$ (affine layer at the end). Then $f(X)$ is non-convex (R3). This flaw invalidates the proofs of the main results for $M_3$ and $M_{d_{in}+3}^o$ (R3, R4). A reviewer (R3) also notes a flaw in the induction step of Theorem 2: the base case $L=1$ should have a ReLU activation, not just an affine layer, and the case $L=2$ is not properly covered. (R3) These issues make the paper’s main results unsupported. (R4)
- **Lack of precision in definitions:** The definition of $M_k^o$ is unclear: it says $C(x,\rho(x))$ is in the form $\{C(\rho(x_I)) \mid I \subseteq [d], |I| \leq k\}$ and “only one index set $I$ is allowed per layer,” which appears contradictory (R4). The relaxations depend on the chosen set $I$ of output neurons, but this dependence is not reflected in the notation (R4). Missing justifications in proofs, e.g., the second equality in the last equation of Theorem 2’s proof is not justified (R4). The term “completeness” is used interchangeably with “full expressivity” without clear definition (R2). Notation confusion: $X_L$ and $\mathbb{X}_L$, $Y_L$ and $\mathbb{Y}_L$ are used interchangeably without distinction (R2). The symbol $\mathbb{M}_\infty$ is used without physical explanation (R2).
- **Poor presentation and organization:** Definitions such as “stable” and “unstable” neurons appear only in Section 6 (page 6), but are mentioned frequently earlier (R1, R2). Basic lemmas like Lemma 1 are referenced before being introduced (R2). The paper would benefit from a preliminaries section that clearly defines stability, bounded stability, etc., and places Lemma 1, Lemma 7, and Lemma 8 before the main theorems (R2, R3). Proofs are not written clearly and are hard to follow (R2).
- **No experimental evaluation:** The paper provides no experiments. An evaluation comparing single-neuron and multi-neuron relaxations on various architectures (different widths and depths) to demonstrate that computed bounds are exact would greatly strengthen the paper (R2). Without experiments, the practical impact is unclear (R1).
- **Lack of implementable algorithms and complexity analysis:** The paper does not describe how to compute the proposed relaxations in practice, nor does it provide time complexity in terms of network size and parameter $k$ (R1, R4). The computational complexity of determining stable/unstable neurons is also not discussed (R4). The paper references branch-and-bound verifiers like $\alpha,\beta$-CROWN but gives no concrete context on how the relaxation advances state-of-the-art (R1). The motivation for avoiding BaB is appealing but insufficiently supported (R1).
- **Practicality concerns:** The construction from Hanin (Lemma 1) yields networks whose depth depends on the number of piecewise linear regions, which can be exponential in input dimension. This may make the resulting networks too deep for practical certified training (R3). The claim that the result is applicable to practical certification (Section 8) is therefore questionable (R3).
- **Missing details in proofs (reviewer questions that overlap with criticism):** 
  - The base case of Theorem 2 and Theorem 6 should use $f(\mathbf{x}) = \rho(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b})$ rather than just $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{b}$ (R2).
  - In Lemma 7, the term “effective” should be clarified; “active” may be better, or a definition should be given (R2). The relationship between $l_1$, $l_2$, and $l$ combined is unclear (R2).
  - Does Theorem 2 imply Lemma 3 (since $\mathbb{M}_k^o(f,X)$ is convex)? (R3)
  - The proof of Lemma 8 uses $X_L$ and $\mathbb{X}_L$ interchangeably; what is the difference? (R2)
  - Could Lemma 3 be replaced by a weaker statement to salvage parts of the results? (R4)
