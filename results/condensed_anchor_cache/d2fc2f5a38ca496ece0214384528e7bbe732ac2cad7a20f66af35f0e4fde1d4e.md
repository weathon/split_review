## Merged Review

### Summary
OmniQuant introduces two learnable components for post-training quantization of LLMs: Learnable Weight Clipping (LWC) optimizes clipping thresholds, and Learnable Equivalent Transformation (LET) shifts activation outliers to weights via learnable scaling/shifting. Calibration uses block-wise error minimization with 128 samples on a single GPU. Experiments cover weight-only (W2A16, W3A16, W4A16) and weight-activation (W4A4, W6A6) quantization on LLaMA-1/2, OPT, Falcon, and instruction-tuned models, evaluating perplexity, common sense reasoning, and latency.

### Strengths
- **LWC is simple yet effective**: The learnable clipping formulation (Eq. 2 with sigmoid over min/max factors) is cleaner than PACT (only positive activations) and LSQ (multiple optimization goals), applies to weights, activations, and negative values, and avoids step-size co-optimization. One reviewer (score 8) considers LWC the paper’s biggest contribution.
- **LET improves activation quantization**: By making the equivalent transformation (channel-wise scaling and shifting) learnable, it outperforms the pre-defined migration strength in SmoothQuant and avoids grid search like AWQ.
- **Wide applicability**: Framework handles both weight-only and weight-activation quantization; the learned parameters fuse into weights, adding no runtime overhead.
- **Efficient calibration**: Block-wise optimization runs on a single A100-40G GPU in 1–16 hours for LLaMA‑2 7B–70B.
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Evaluated on multiple model families (LLaMA‑1/2, OPT, Falcon), several bit-widths, and latency benchmarks; ablation studies analyze each component.
- **Clear presentation**: Well‑structured, good figures, easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with key baselines** (multiple reviewers):
  - **Outlier Suppression+ (OS+)**: LET is conceptually similar to OS+ which already learns channel-wise scaling via output optimization without a pre‑defined strength. Several reviewers request direct experimental comparison, a detailed theoretical/empirical distinction, and discussion of why OmniQuant’s design is preferable.
  - **SpQR and SqueezeLLM**: For weight‑only quantization (e.g., LLaMA‑1 7B/13B at 3‑bit and 4‑bit) these methods achieve lower wiki/c4 perplexity than reported by OmniQuant. Missing performance numbers and discussion.
  - **GPTQ**: Latency benchmark (Table 5) lacks comparison with GPTQ and AWQ. Reviewer asks: “How does OmniQuant runtime efficiency compare to GPTQ and AWQ?”
  - **PACT and LSQ**: Despite claiming advantages (generality, simplicity), no experiments compare LWC directly with these methods. One reviewer (score 8) explicitly requests this evaluation.
- **Novelty concerns** (three reviewers):
  - The overall framework combines two existing ideas (weight clipping + equivalent transformation) without substantial new insight. LET is seen as a minor extension of OS+ / SmoothQuant; the learnable scaling idea is not new—OS+ already proposed learned scaling without gradient.
  - Combined training of LWC and LET is not well motivated; the paper does not explain how they interact or whether alternating training would improve convergence.
- **Insufficient explanation of LWC**:
  - Motivation for the specific formulation (Eq. 2) over prior clipping methods is unclear. Reviewer asks: “How does Eq. 2 solve the optimization difficulty (concept given in 3.2) of previous techniques?” More discussion needed.
  - LWC advantages over PACT and LSQ are not articulated, especially when weights are frequently altered by LET.
  - No visualization of the learned γ and β parameters across layers (requested by multiple reviewers).
- **Missing ablations and analyses**:
  - LET is applied to all linear layers except the second FFN layer—reviewer questions: “Do all instances of LET actively contribute? Investigate individual and cumulative impact per linear layer.”
  - Sensitivity to initialization: Scale initialized from SmoothQuant, shift from OS+. Would performance degrade if scale=1 and shift=0? (Reviewer asks for experiments under asymmetric cases like W4A8, W4A6, W8A4 where SmoothQuant behaves poorly.)
  - Overfitting risk: Optimization on only 128 samples with backward propagation; generalization validation is needed.
  - Softmax is kept in full‑precision—reviewer asks: “What are the implications of quantizing Softmax output to 8‑bit?”
  - Figure 1(a) lacks quantitative numbers for time and performance.
- **Limited task evaluation**:
  - Missing complex reasoning benchmarks (MMLU) and instruction‑tuned model benchmarks beyond GPT‑4 evaluation (reviewer suggests MT‑Bench, AlpacaEval for models like Vicuna‑v1.5).
  - GPT‑4 evaluation is considered ad‑hoc; more standard benchmarks should be used.
- **Other requested comparisons**:
  - Reviewer mentions a recent outlier‑decomposition method (arxiv 2310.08041) that could strengthen validation.
- **Minority disagreement on novelty**: One reviewer (score 8) views LWC as a clear contribution, praising its generality over prior work; the other four reviewers are more critical of novelty.
