## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces INS-MMBench, the first benchmark designed to evaluate LVLMs in the insurance domain. It covers four insurance types (auto, property, health, agricultural) and comprises 8,856 multiple-choice questions across 12 meta‑tasks and 22 fundamental tasks. The authors evaluate multiple LVLMs, provide error analysis, and attempt to address gaps with prompt engineering. Reviewers acknowledge the pioneering nature and practical motivation, but raise concerns about task depth, format limitations, bias, and missing complex reasoning.

### Strengths
- **First comprehensive benchmark for insurance domain**: The paper fills a clear gap by creating the first systematic benchmark for evaluating LVLMs in insurance, covering four representative insurance types relevant to real-world applications (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Systematic and hierarchical task definition**: The bottom‑up methodology ensures tasks are aligned with practical insurance stages such as underwriting and claims processing. The benchmark is well‑organized, clearly explained, and the task structure is a strong contribution (R1, R3, R4).
- **Extensive evaluation and error analysis**: The work evaluates multiple closed‑ and open‑source LVLMs, provides thorough error analysis categorizing failure types, and explores prompt engineering to mitigate issues. This gives actionable insights into current model limitations and the narrowing performance gap between open‑ and closed‑source models (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Practical motivation**: The focus on core insurance processes (underwriting, claims, fraud detection) makes the benchmark immediately relevant to the industry and could drive automation and efficiency improvements (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Multiple‑choice format limitations**: The exclusive use of multiple‑choice questions prevents evaluation of open‑ended reasoning and interpretability. Models may rely on pattern matching rather than genuine understanding, and they cannot be asked to provide justifications—a critical requirement in insurance applications (R1, R3).
- **Lack of insurance‑specific depth and reasoning**: Many tasks are basic or general vision tasks (e.g., license plate recognition, vehicle damage detection) rather than capturing specialized insurance reasoning. The benchmark lacks multi‑step reasoning, risk assessment, cross‑referencing multiple data sources (images, repair estimates, policy details), temporal reasoning (e.g., analyzing claim patterns over time), and tasks requiring contextual decision‑making. One reviewer (R2) specifically notes a misalignment between the claimed focus on insurance stages and the actual tasks, which feel more like a general vision task set. Several reviewers request clarification on how tasks were selected to ensure they reflect real‑world insurance challenges rather than general VQA (R2, R3, R4).
- **Potential bias and lack of mitigation discussion**: The benchmark is primarily based on U.S. insurance law and data, introducing regional bias that limits global applicability. The paper does not discuss potential biases in data sources or any mitigation strategies (R1, R4).
- **Static benchmark and data leakage concerns**: The benchmark is static with no dynamic update mechanism, making it vulnerable to data leakage and reducing its long‑term utility as the insurance domain evolves (R1).
- **Limited novelty of some conclusions**: Findings such as “performance of closed‑source LVLMs varies by training data size and methods” are too general and well‑known, offering little domain‑specific insight. The paper could benefit from deeper analysis of factors driving the open‑ vs closed‑source performance gap (R2, R3 question).
- **Reproducibility issues**: The GitHub repository has not been updated in four months and contains only a readme and a few diagrams, limiting reproducibility and further assessment (R2).
- **Small human baseline**: The human baseline experiments involve only 3 graduate students specialized in insurance, which is a small sample and may not accurately represent real‑world expertise (R4).
- **Potential error in Table 1**: The labeling of “OmniMedVQA” as domain‑specific for math and “Mathvista” as domain‑specific for medical appears to be switched and should be corrected (R3).

*Note:* Reviewer 2 (score 3) is substantially more critical than the others, particularly regarding the misalignment of tasks with insurance stages and the limited novelty of the analysis. Reviewers 3 and 4 (score 6) are more positive but still highlight significant shortcomings in task depth and reasoning requirements.
