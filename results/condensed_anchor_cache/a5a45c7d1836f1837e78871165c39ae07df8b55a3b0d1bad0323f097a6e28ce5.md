## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes structured diffusion models (mixDDPM, mixSGM) that replace the standard Gaussian prior with a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) prior to incorporate data structure information. A training procedure accommodating the GMM prior is developed, and a new metric, Reverse Effort, quantifies efficiency. Experiments on synthetic data, EMNIST, CIFAR-10, and MNIST show improved generation performance (e.g., FID) over DDPM and SGM at the same number of sampling steps.

### Strengths
- Well-organized paper with clear, logical progression (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).  
- Intuitive and reasonable to use a GMM prior to capture clustering information; performance improvements align with this intuition (Reviewer 1).  
- Idea simple and appealing: modeling each cluster separately, nicely formalized in Proposition 1 (Reviewer 4).  
- Practical implementations discussed, e.g., data-driven clustering to select mixture centers (Reviewer 2).  
- Introduction of the Reverse Effort metric provides a fresh perspective on model efficiency (Reviewer 3).  
- Experiments on basic datasets (e.g., MNIST) show improvements in training steps, and simple computational algorithms are provided (Reviewer 2).  

### Weaknesses

**Innovation and Prior Art**
- Similar ideas have been proposed in Guo et al. (2023, NeurIP) and Zach et al. (2023, SSVM). There is extensive existing work using non‑Gaussian or more general priors (Reviewer 2).  
- Theoretically, moving from Gaussian to GMM is equivalent to adding one layer in a hierarchical model, limiting novelty (Reviewer 2).

**Algorithm and Model Structure**
- The algorithm is described poorly: at first reading it looks complicated, but it is essentially just clustering the data and then running separate diffusion models per cluster (Reviewer 4).  
- The forward and reverse processes depend on pre‑determined cluster labels (based on initial centroids), making the entire process roughly equivalent to independent diffusion processes within each cluster. There is no interaction between the Gaussian components during diffusion, preventing a truly unified representation; this significantly downgrades the innovation level (Reviewer 3).  
- In Algorithm 2, the cluster index *j* is missing in the noise model ϵ_θ – why? (Reviewer 3, question).  
- The requirement to first cluster the data is a weakness: obtaining a good clustering is a difficult problem, and a poor clustering can hurt final performance (Reviewer 4).

**Choice of K and GMM Parameters**
- No thorough analysis of the impact of varying K: does mixDDPM simply degrade to DDPM when K=1? Does an excessively large K introduce unnecessary computational overhead? No discussion of convergence rate, sample quality, diminishing returns, or guidelines for selecting K per dataset/task (Reviewer 1).  
- No discussion of the challenges in determining the optimal number of mixture components or of GMM limitations (e.g., oversimplifying complex distributions) (Reviewer 3).  
- The impact of the initialization of the centroids c_j on the final generated distribution is not explored (Reviewer 3, question).  
- If the number of clusters is large, the prior can become ill‑posed and computationally expensive; the method already restricts dimensionality and number of clusters (Reviewer 2).

**Computational Overhead and Trade‑Offs**
- No analysis of storage and computational overhead for training: memory footprint, time complexity of training and sampling, or whether the added complexity leads to overfitting (Reviewer 1).  
- No discussion of the trade‑off between multiple Gaussian components and computational cost (Reviewer 1). *Note: Reviewer 4 (score 3) is more negative on the practical significance, questioning why effort reduction is important in practice and noting no theoretical support for improvement in visual quality.*

**Reverse Effort Metric**
- Practical interpretation and relevance of the Reverse Effort metric are not well explained; no visualizations or step‑by‑step examples showing how it correlates with sample quality (Reviewer 3).  
- The limitations of this metric are not discussed; it is unclear if it is applicable to different data types or diffusion models (Reviewer 3).  
- Is the Reverse Effort defined in eqns (14) and (15) the OT‑distance? (Reviewer 3, question).  
- The paper argues for lower effort, but it is not clear why in practice we would care about this effort measure (Reviewer 4).

**Experiments: Scale, Comparisons, and Quality**
- Experiments are too small in scale; only basic datasets (EMNIST, CIFAR‑10, MNIST) are used; the method should be tested on more complex and higher‑resolution data (e.g., LSUN) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).  
- Only original DDPM and SGM are compared; no comparisons with other improved diffusion models. The reported FID scores are far higher than state‑of‑the‑art on CIFAR‑10, suggesting lack of training (Reviewer 2).  
- The numerical experiments show better FID with fixed steps but the results are inferior to previously published DDPM results on the same datasets (Reviewer 4).  
- There is no theoretical support for the improvement in visual quality; the connection between effort and sample quality is not established (Reviewer 4).  
- No study of robustness to different hyperparameter settings or noise levels (Reviewer 1).

**Typos**
- Typos exist, e.g., missing space in Section 4.2: “x0is” should be “x0 is” (Reviewer 1).
