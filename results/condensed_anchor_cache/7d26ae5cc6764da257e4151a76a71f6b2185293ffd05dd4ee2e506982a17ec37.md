## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper identifies that Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) perform worse on VQA questions about small objects, even though their attention maps often localize the correct region. Through intervention studies (human cropping), the authors show this is a perception, not localization, limitation. Based on these findings, they propose training-free automatic cropping methods that use the MLLM’s own attention/gradient maps to crop the image, then pass the crop alongside the original image. Experiments on two MLLMs and seven VQA benchmarks show improved accuracy, especially on detail-sensitive datasets.

### Strengths

- The paper studies an important and underexplored limitation: MLLMs’ lack of sensitivity to small visual details, and provides an original insight that the model often attends correctly even when answering incorrectly (reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The intervention study (human cropping) causally validates that the problem is due to perception, not localization (reviewer 1).
- The proposed automatic cropping methods are intuitive, training-free, and leverage the MLLM’s internal knowledge (attention/gradient maps) to improve perception (reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Empirical results (Table 2) are convincing for detail-sensitive datasets (e.g., TextVQA) and show significant improvement over the base MLLM on two models and seven benchmarks (reviewers 2, 3). One reviewer (reviewer 1) notes that the improvement is limited (up to ~4% even compared to no-crop) and that the gain on general VQA datasets (large visual concepts) is less substantial (reviewer 3).
- The paper is well-written and clear (reviewer 2; reviewer 1 gave presentation 2).

### Weaknesses

- **Baselines insufficient**: The evaluation uses only a no-crop baseline; there is no comparison with other methods that address similar problems, such as V* (reviewer 1). A simple random-crop baseline (cropping a random location, half the size of the original) is missing – it would help demonstrate that the MLLM’s accurate localization is what makes the crops informative (reviewer 2).
- **Limited improvement magnitude**: The gain is only ~4% on average across benchmarks, which one reviewer (reviewer 1) considers modest. The improvement on general VQA datasets (large visual concepts) is not as significant as on small-object datasets (reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparison with traditional grounding/detection**: Why use VisCrop instead of running an off-the-shelf grounding/object detection algorithm to crop relevant regions? (reviewer 1)
- **Single-region assumption**: The method assumes there is a single relevant location in the image; it may fail for questions about spatial relationships between multiple objects (reviewer 2). Similarly, how does it handle cases where the visual subject spans multiple regions of interest? (reviewer 1)
- **Related work context**: The drawback of MLLMs for small objects may have been noted before (e.g., training with higher-resolution patches, cited in lines 287–288). The paper should better discuss prior work addressing small-object perception in MLLMs/VQA (reviewer 2).
- **Attention calculation details**: It is unclear whether the method of calculating attention (averaging across layers/heads) is standard or appropriate given that later-layer tokens are highly transformed. Comparison with GradCam for transformers (mentioned in related work) would strengthen the analysis (reviewer 2).
- **Analysis of when attention is correct despite wrong answer**: More intuition is needed. For example, in the “car” question in Fig. 2, proper localization seems to imply correct perception – why does the model still answer incorrectly? (reviewer 2)
- **Computational latency**: Adding the cropped image introduces extra visual tokens and latency (Table 4). Suggestion: use a method like Matryoshka Query Transformer (NeurIPS 24) to reduce visual tokens from the re-scaled crop (reviewer 3).
- **Alternative image presentation**: Could the cropped object be added to the original image (e.g., overlaying or replacing a region) instead of concatenating two separate images, potentially saving computation? (reviewer 3)
- **Human-crop realism**: The human-crop intervention uses a very tight crop around the ground-truth object. A more realistic experiment would crop to match the average size ratio of medium/large splits and measure whether accuracy matches those splits (reviewer 2).
- **Model architecture not explored**: The role of model architecture in perception limitations (e.g., patch size, resolution) is not examined (reviewer 1).
- **Minor**: Clarify what “input image resolution” means in line 356 (patch size vs. image resolution). Explain the oscillatory behavior in Fig. 3 (reviewer 2).

**Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (reviewer 1, score 5) is notably more negative, citing weak baselines, limited improvement, and missing exploration of architecture. The other two reviewers found the contribution solid and methods convincing (scores 8 each).
