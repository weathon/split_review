## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper revisits the classifier re-training paradigm in long-tailed recognition, proposing two metrics—Logits Magnitude and Regularized Standard Deviation—to analyze classifier efficacy. Based on these metrics, the authors develop a simple label over-smoothing method (LORT) that assigns a probability slightly above 1/K to the true class and slightly below 1/K to others. The method achieves state-of-the-art performance on CIFAR100-LT, ImageNet-LT, and iNaturalist2018. Reviewers generally find the method simple and effective, but have mixed views on clarity of presentation, novelty, and mathematical rigor (scores: 6, 5, 6, 8). One reviewer (score 8) found no significant methodological drawbacks, while another (score 5) raised several substantial concerns about the metrics, derivations, and novelty.

### Strengths
- Proposes two new metrics (Logits Magnitude and Regularized Standard Deviation) that reveal shortcomings of previous metrics (e.g., Weight Norm ignores feature magnitude) and correlate with the performance of various existing methods.
- The label over-smoothing technique is simple, interesting, and backed by both theoretical and empirical results; it can be used as a plug-in to improve existing methods.
- The proposed method outperforms most compared methods across multiple long-tailed benchmarks (CIFAR100-LT, ImageNet-LT, iNaturalist2018); ablation studies are well-done.
- The paper has a clear structure, starting from problem definition and motivation to implementation.
- Over-smoothing effectively reduces overfitting, and the comprehensive theoretical and experimental validation supports its effectiveness.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity of figures and notation:** Figure 1 is hard to follow (red/blue lines, why they obey Gaussian distributions, unclear meanings) and Figure 2 is not initially clear (what are true/non-true samples; how mean logits are computed; the definition of LoMa as the difference between these means is not well motivated). The paper needs more explanations.
- **Missing baselines and comparisons:** The authors should include the Cosine classifier (simple and effective, directly mitigates feature magnitude impact) as a baseline. Some recent works are not compared: RIDE, BCL, NCL, GML. Also recommended to compare with the latest state-of-the-art methods and discuss related works [2,3] (references unspecified). References are outdated: among 50 cited papers, none from 2024, only 3 from 2023, and 4 from 2022. Format of references is inconsistent (e.g., missing source for “Peifeng et al., 2023”, URL only for some, inconsistent source naming).
- **Mathematical derivations and claims:** 
  - Proposition 1 claims the conclusion remains valid for all cross-entropy-based methods, but deep learning objectives are non-convex; how is this justified?
  - Proposition 2 is argued to show weight norm is unreliable, but the claim is that “arbitrary lengths” render it meaningless; however, it is relative weight norm that matters, so Proposition 2 may be irrelevant.
  - Equation (4) derivation from s' to s is not clearly shown.
  - Equation (9): the expectation decomposition looks artificial; the assumption that terms are independent is not justified, and the approximation is not rigorously derived.
  - The assumption on line 313-314 (“Consider random perturbations Δ_i that independently affect each class z_i and are proportional to the standard deviation of their logits”) lacks clarification—why proportional to σ and how are these perturbations introduced by training factors?
- **Connection between metrics and proposed method:** While the authors claim LoMa and Regularized Standard Deviation are hard to optimize directly, they do not propose a direct optimization approach. The label smoothing technique does not clearly fit into the story of achieving uniform LoMa; it is unclear how modifying the training objective addresses LoMa or RSD optimization. Section 3.1 (deep dive into LoMa) feels rambling, lacks concrete takeaways, and has unclear connection to the proposed method.
- **Novelty of label smoothing:** The proposed (over)label smoothing is a minor variant of prior work; the novelty of using a higher smoothing factor is insufficiently justified, and its connection to the long-tailed problem is not clearly established.
- **Presentation issues:** Transitions between sections could be improved. The point of Definition 2 (if the key idea is already contained in Δ and σ) is unclear—should it refer to R_i? A typo (missing parenthesis in line 107) should be fixed.
