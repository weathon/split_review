## Merged Review

### Summary

All four reviewers describe TopoGaussian as a pipeline that infers the internal topology of opaque objects from photos and videos by combining Gaussian Splatting with a particle-based differentiable simulator. The approach is mesh-free, supports three topology representations (particle, neural implicit surface, quadratic surface), and is validated on both synthetic and real-world tasks, producing 3D-printable outputs. One reviewer (score 8) is notably more positive, praising the novelty and holistic pipeline, while the other three (scores 6) are positive but raise several methodological and evaluative concerns.

### Strengths

1. **Novel problem and approach.** The task of inferring internal topology from visual observations is novel for modern 3D vision, and the combination of Gaussian Splatting with physics-based optimization is a creative solution. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
2. **Mesh-free and flexible pipeline.** Avoiding mesh processing eliminates tedious filling/fixing steps. The pipeline offers three flexible topology representations and integrates rigid and soft body simulation within a single differentiable framework. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
3. **Impressive quantitative efficiency and quality.** The method reports a 5.26× speedup and 2.33× improvement in boundary reconstruction quality over mesh-based baselines like PGSR and Gaussian Surfels. Outputs are smoother and directly 3D-printable. (Reviewer 3; also noted by Reviewer 2 as a strength)
4. **Real-world validation.** The pipeline is demonstrated on multiple real-world tasks with 3D-printed prototypes, showing practical feasibility. (Reviewers 3, 4)
5. **Well-structured presentation.** The overall pipeline overview and design choices are clearly communicated. (Reviewer 2; contrasting with Reviewer 1’s concern on writing)

### Weaknesses

1. **Missing ground truth for internal structure.** None of the evaluations directly compare the inferred internal topology to a ground truth, even in synthetic data where such GT is available. Only rendering results and optimization loss are used, which does not verify structural correctness. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
2. **Overfitting to a single motion.** The pipeline optimizes the interior structure using only one motion video. This may produce a plausible structure that matches the observed motion but fails to generalize to unseen motions, and the true structure cannot be confirmed. Multiple motions or a test set on new motions would strengthen validation. (Reviewer 3; also noted by Reviewer 1 on real-world applicability)
3. **Insufficient comparisons and ablation.** The paper compares only with mesh-based baselines (PGSR, Gaussian Surfels) on optimization loss, time, and smoothness. The proposed method does not achieve the lowest loss in several Figure 3 samples. It is unclear whether the reported speed and quality gains come from the Gaussian Splatting representation itself or from the proposed pipeline components. Ablation studies on each stage (filling quality, representation choice, simulation parameters) are missing. (Reviewers 2, 4)
4. **Lack of technical details for reproducibility.** The coupling between the particle-based simulation and the volumetric representation is inadequately described. How are particle positions and velocities translated into a continuous indicator function or density field? What interpolation or smoothing is used? The decision variable integration into the point cloud needs clarification. These details are essential for assessing novelty and reproducing the method. (Reviewers 1, 2)
5. **Unclear real-world applicability.** In many practical settings (engineering, medical), exact internal topology is required, and the method offers no accuracy guarantees. The pipeline is also limited to single-object, dual-material compositions, which restricts use with complex heterogeneous materials. (Reviewers 1, 3)
6. **Ambiguity of the solution and metrics.** The problem may have multiple plausible internal structures consistent with a single motion, raising questions about uniqueness and how to determine reasonableness. The relationship between optimization loss and reconstruction quality is not defined; it is unclear why similar loss leads to significantly better reconstruction quality. Compatibility of mesh-based baselines with the particle-based pipeline is also not explained. (Reviewers 2, 4)
7. **Writing and presentation issues.** The paper is convoluted, packs many ideas, and misses required specifics in the appendix. There are typos (e.g., “point clout” line 175) and inconsistent notation (e.g., “point-cloud” vs. “point clout” line 234). Key terms such as “reconstruction quality” and “Smoothness” require clear definitions. (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 4)
