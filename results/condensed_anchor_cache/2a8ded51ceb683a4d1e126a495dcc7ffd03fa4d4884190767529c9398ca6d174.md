## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces WebArena, a realistic and reproducible web environment for language-guided autonomous agents, hosting fully functional websites from four domains (e-commerce, social forums, collaborative software development, content management) along with tools and knowledge bases. A benchmark of 812 diverse, long-horizon tasks is released, evaluating functional correctness. Baseline agents (GPT-4, etc.) achieve only 14.41% success rate vs. 78.24% human performance. Code, data, and environment are publicly available.

### Strengths
- **Originality and impact**: One reviewer (score 8) was delighted by the work’s significance, noting it bridges a major gap and lays a foundation for industrial web agents. It stands out among agent benchmarks seen over the last six months. Another reviewer (score 5) considered the contribution insufficiently novel for ICLR, so there is disagreement on this point.
- **Quality and real‑world relevance**: The environment is praised for being highly realistic, complicated, and challenging. The benchmark construction is solid and robust (confirmed by personal setup by one reviewer). The choice of four common domains and genuine data is well‑motivated and backed by a user study.
- **Clarity**: The paper is well written, accessible, and has a coherent logical flow.
- **Preliminary evaluation**: The first evaluation of GPT‑based agents provides useful insights, even though the agents are limited to standard variants.
- **Code and documentation**: Code is usable, documented, and made available, making contributions clear.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing evaluation with latest intelligent agents**: The benchmark is tested only with standard GPT variants. One reviewer strongly notes the absence of modern reasoning and planning methods (e.g., Tree of Thought, Reflection architecture, iterative refinement strategies). Another reviewer similarly laments that “standard GPT variants only” are used, without pointing to stronger alternatives.
- **Insufficient agent implementation details**: Very little detail about how the agent was implemented and tested appears in the main paper; understanding the evaluation requires consulting the appendix.
- **API call methodology and ablation studies**: The presentation of API calls as web pages may not align with current paradigms. The paper lacks ablation studies on the effectiveness of tools for “human‑like task‑solving” (e.g., performance with/without tool websites, different levels of tool access).
- **HTML vs. Accessibility Tree not justified**: No empirical validation supports the choice of Accessibility Tree over HTML. One reviewer argues that HTML may be more natural for LLMs and could help hierarchy understanding; the choice should be tested and presented as a core contribution.
- **Gold trajectories missing**: The absence of optimal action sequences (“gold trajectories”) is a notable gap; they would provide a performance ceiling and enable efficiency analysis for future research.
- **Evaluator demographics and potential bias**: Human performance (78.24%) was measured using only CS graduate students, who may not represent average users. There is risk of evaluator bias if dataset creators participated. One reviewer and colleagues could not replicate such high accuracy in their own case studies.
- **Related work advantage not clear**: The claimed advantage of functional correctness over AndroidEnv is not explained. The paper notes a lack of diverse/complex tasks in prior benchmarks, yet new tasks can be defined in WebArena itself, making the comparison ambiguous.
- **POMDP model not argued**: The choice of a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process to model tasks is not justified in the paper.
- **Reproducibility and closed‑source concerns**: The evaluation relies on closed‑source LLMs (GPT‑4, Text‑Bison) and uses GPT‑4 for success evaluation. This impedes reproducibility and limits use (commercial tool, not guaranteed 100% correct, evaluation results may be unconvincing).
- **Human success rate analysis needed**: The human success rate is only 78% on tasks reported as realistic everyday tasks. One reviewer requests analysis of why humans fail and whether tasks are unrealistically difficult for agents. An explicit analysis of GPT‑4’s evaluation reliability is also missing.
- **Comparison with existing benchmarks**: The paper does not discuss whether the benchmark tasks could be integrated into or compared with other existing web‑agent benchmarks, nor does it establish whether the tasks are on par or superior in difficulty.
- **Lack of technical novelty (minority opinion)**: One reviewer (score 5) considered the work lacking technical novelty for ICLR, despite acknowledging the value of the resource. This contrasts with the high originality rating from another reviewer.
