## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces CounterFactual Concept Bottleneck Models (CF-CBMs), which extend standard CBMs to answer “What?”, “How?”, and “Why not?” questions within Pearl’s causal ladder. The method uses a variational approach to model concepts as latent confounders and generates counterfactual concept-label pairs. Experiments on dSprites, MNIST add, and CUB show it achieves comparable classification accuracy to black-box models and baselines while providing interpretable, concept-based counterfactuals and simpler explanations (fewer important concepts).

### Strengths
- Tackles the extremely challenging problem of counterfactual reasoning with deep learning; meaningful progress would have high impact. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- Extends CBMs to counterfactual sampling, enabling a single model to address all three levels of Pearl’s ladder of causation. (Reviewer 1)
- Structured training objective allows test-time manipulations that shed light on how predictions are made. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- Method is straightforward, intuitive, and well organized; figures are well described. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- Enhances interpretability and interactability of standard CBMs, potentially valuable for real-life settings like medical decision-making. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- Tested against many baselines across multiple metrics. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Concept label reliance**: Method requires handcrafted concepts, which are difficult and expensive to collect in realistic settings; predictive performance depends on how well these concepts describe input features. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Hyperparameter complexity**: The training objective (Eq. 1) introduces many hyperparameters (e.g., λ_i in Table 6). No discussion of tuning procedure, sensitivity to values, or whether separate systems would be easier to tune. Validation set use is not reported. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Missing medical dataset despite medical example**: The paper uses x-ray images as a working example (Figures 1, 2) but never experiments on any medical dataset (e.g., OAI x-ray from Koh et al. 2020, or MIMIC-CXR). The x-ray images are not cited and their license is unclear. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Limited dataset complexity**: The three datasets (dSprites, MNIST add, CUB) are relatively simple. More diverse and complex real-life datasets (e.g., CIFAR-100, MIMIC-CXR) would strengthen claims and demonstrate broader applicability. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of qualitative counterfactual visualizations**: No visual comparisons of counterfactuals against baselines are provided for the datasets used (only Figure 1 for x-ray). Quantitative results alone are insufficient to demonstrate interpretability improvements; evaluation with human end-users is missing. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Unclear SCM design and justification**: Ignoring \(p(x|z)\) in the optimization (L152) is not sufficiently motivated; sampling counterfactual concept \(c'\) should also require sampling new covariates \(x'\) (Eqn. 1, L123). Conditioning of \(z'\) on both \(c\) and \(y\) (L156) is not explained, nor whether \(c\) suffices to know \(y\). (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Slightly lower predictive performance**: On average, CF-CBMs show a slight decrease in accuracy compared to baselines; stating the method is “on par” is overstated. (Reviewer 4)
- **OOD generalization and novelty concerns**: It is unclear whether the method is meaningfully different from structured generative/discriminative modeling with interpretable features. Performance on OOD concept pairings (e.g., unrealistic bird combinations) is not examined. (Reviewer 1)
- **New black-box components hurt interpretability**: The counterfactual generator introduces additional black-box elements, potentially reducing the overall interpretability of the model. (Reviewer 3)
- **Sparsity explanation unclear**: Equation 5 is said to give sparser explanations, but it is not explained how moving the posterior of latent \(z\) toward the prior translates to sparsity in concept space \(c\). (Reviewer 3)
- **Confounding factors intuition missing**: The mechanism by which CF-CBMs are less prone to confounding is not explained; an ablation study is needed to underscore these findings. (Reviewer 4)
- **Training details and code**: It is unclear whether all components are trained jointly or some parts frozen. Code is not provided (an anonymized link is requested). (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Missing related work citations**: Early work on VAEs for causal inference (e.g., Louizos et al., NeurIPS 2017) and Neural Concept Binder (Stammer et al., NeurIPS 2024) are not acknowledged. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Disagreement among reviewers**: One reviewer (rating 5) expressed concerns about novelty, grounding, and the complexity of the objective, while another (rating 8) considered the contribution clear and important. (Reviewers 1 vs. 4)
