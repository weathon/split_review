## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Correlation Mode Decomposition (CMD), a method that clusters neural network parameters into groups (“modes”) exhibiting synchronized temporal dynamics. CMD represents each mode by a reference trajectory, with other parameters expressed as affine transformations of it. Variants include post-hoc CMD, Online CMD (running concurrently with training without storing histories), and Embedded CMD (reducing trainable parameters). The method is applied to ResNets and Transformers on CIFAR‑10 and to federated learning to lower communication overhead. Reviewers strongly disagree on the paper’s overall quality (scores: 8, 3, 5, 1).

### Strengths
- **Novel observation and algorithm**: The discovery that parameters exhibit intrinsic correlations over time and the development of CMD to exploit this are widely seen as creative and impactful. The idea of clustering parameters based on correlated dynamics is novel. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Clear logical flow**: The paper is well-structured, progressing from post‑hoc CMD to Online CMD to Embedded CMD, with good motivation and exposition. (Reviewer 1, 2, 4)
- **Impressive empirical results**: CMD outperforms standard full training (SGD/GD) on CIFAR‑10 in test accuracy, and achieves state‑of‑the‑art results among low‑dimensional training methods (beating DMD etc.). One reviewer specifically highlights that Online/Embedded CMD surpassing full SGD is “somewhat contradictory” and surprising. (Reviewer 1, 3, 4)
- **Extension to federated learning**: The application to communication‑efficient distributed training is novel—claimed as the first use of dynamical modeling to reduce communication overhead—and the initial results are promising. (Reviewer 1, 3, 4)
- **Rich supporting materials**: The appendices contain pseudo‑code, clarifying remarks, and extra experimental details. (Reviewer 4)
- **Fit with related work**: The hypothesis of correlated modes aligns well with existing Koopman‑operator and DMD literature. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Imperfect correlations and hypothesis validity**: Figure 1 (Left & Middle) shows that many correlations between parameter trajectories are less than 1.0, violating the hypothesis that trajectories are perfectly correlated (and hence expressible as affine transformations). The paper does not analyse how performance degrades when correlations deviate from 1.0. (Reviewer 3)
- **Unclear or missing experimental details**:
    - Figure 1 (Right) has no y‑axis label, making the plotted values uninterpretable. (Reviewer 3)
    - Figure 2 (Left) is shown over too few epochs; extending the x‑axis would better demonstrate long‑term effectiveness. A visual discrepancy (blur) appears on the right side of the middle plot, requiring clarification. (Reviewer 2)
    - Figure 2 (Left) test accuracy of CMD is consistently higher than CMD training accuracy—a puzzling inversion that suggests a potential experimental issue. Moreover, the plotted accuracies appear inconsistent with Table 1. (Reviewer 3)
    - Table 2 acronyms (Mom, SCH) are not defined. (Reviewer 4)
- **Insufficient comparisons**:
    - The main comparison for CMD is limited to P‑BFGS and DMD. Comparisons with other relevant methods (e.g., pruning, other low‑dimensional training approaches) are missing. (Reviewer 2)
    - In the federated learning experiments, only two baselines (APF, A‑APF) are considered; more recent state‑of‑the‑art FL methods should be included. (Reviewer 1)
- **Lack of theoretical support**: Hypothesis 1 is presented without any theoretical justification, and the paper does not provide evidence that reference trajectories can be selected once and never updated (as claimed). (Reviewer 3)
- **Overclaiming**: The statement that “Eq. (2) accurately represents the dynamics using a notably small set of modes” is not fully supported given the imperfect correlations and the lack of clear explanation of DMD (used as a comparison). (Reviewer 3)
- **Limited scalability and generalizability**: Experiments are confined to CIFAR‑10; it is unclear whether CMD would work on larger, more complex datasets (e.g., ImageNet). The use of a single representative per mode may oversimplify dynamics and limit expressive power in more challenging tasks. (Reviewer 2, 3)
- **Federated learning motivation unclear**: The rationale for applying CMD in FL is not well explained; the explanation in Section 4 feels rushed, and Eq. 16 is confusing. (Reviewer 2, 4)
- **Presentation and writing consistency**: One reviewer notes the paper is well‑written and easy to follow, while another suggests improving writing quality (e.g., citation formatting using \citet or \citep) and clarifying the notation in Section 3.3 (Online CMD). (Reviewer 1, 2, 4)
- **Terminology clarity**: The concept of “modes” is not sufficiently defined for readers unfamiliar with the topic. (Reviewer 2)
- **Conflicting rating–text discrepancy**: One reviewer (Rating 1) states “I found no major weaknesses” and highly praises the paper, yet assigns the lowest possible score. This contrast makes the overall evaluation difficult to interpret. (Reviewer 4)
- **Potential experimental bug or leakage**: In Table 1, CMD surpasses full training (GD) even in training accuracy, which is unexpected for a method designed to approximate GD. If CMD improves training accuracy, the paper’s regularisation explanation is insufficient, raising the possibility of a bug. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing DMD description**: The paper highlights CMD vs. DMD in Figure 1 but never explains DMD, making it impossible to assess relative merits. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing ablation**: The claim that reference trajectories do not require updates after selection is not backed by experimental results in the paper. (Reviewer 3)
