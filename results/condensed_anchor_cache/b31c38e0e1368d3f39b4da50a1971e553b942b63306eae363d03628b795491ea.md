## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes IntersectionZoo, a benchmark suite for multi-agent contextual reinforcement learning (MA-CRL) via cooperative eco-driving at signalized intersections. It is built on data-informed simulations of 16,334 signalized intersections from 10 major US cities, modeled in the SUMO traffic simulator. The benchmark provides one million data-driven traffic scenarios with configurable contexts (e.g., vehicle throughput, speed limit, temperature, adoption rate, human driver models, peak/off-peak patterns). The authors benchmark PPO, DDPG, MAPPO, and GCRL, finding that these popular algorithms struggle to generalize even in-distribution.

### Strengths
- The work targets a real-world, impactful application (reducing vehicular emissions) and resonates with bridging RL research and practical deployment (R1, R2, R4).  
- The scenario database is large and grounded in realistic context distributions derived from transportation department literature (R1, R4).  
- The benchmark models factors affecting exhaust emissions (temperature, road conditions, travel demand) and captures real-world characteristics like partial observability and multiple competing objectives (R4).  
- Open-source code with good documentation is provided, facilitating community use (R1, R3).  
- The problem formulation is clear and detailed, and the literature review is extensive (R3).  
- Significant data collection and processing effort offers value to the transportation research community (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Experimental analysis is insufficient and needs considerable improvement.** Only four algorithms are evaluated (PPO, DDPG, MAPPO, GCRL). PPO and DDPG are not specialized for multi-agent RL (they use independent agents); only two multi-agent methods (MAPPO and GCRL) are tested, and only on in-distribution generalization. This makes the conclusion that “popular multi-agent RL algorithms struggle to generalize” hard to justify (R1, R2, R3). The authors should justify the algorithm selection with a review of latest MARL methods (R3).  
- **Missing training performance.** Without showing that MARL agents can overfit training scenarios and exceed human-like driving performance, it is unclear whether the poor test performance is due to poor generalization or an ill-designed MDP (reward, observation, task difficulty) (R1).  
- **No baseline definition for key statements.** In “Both DDPG and PPO fail to systematically generalize; baseline performs better in almost all cases” (Line 497), what “baseline” refers to is unclear (R3).  
- **No comparison with other benchmarks** (e.g., those listed in Table 1) to show how results differ and what advantage IntersectionZoo offers (R4).  
- **No validation of simulation fidelity** – experimental results do not demonstrate that the simulated vehicular exhaust emissions accurately reflect real-world emissions (R4).  
- **Writing and presentation issues.** Figures are hard to interpret: “total count” is unexplained, benefit percentage units unclear (0.5% or 50%?), and captions of Figure 5 and Figure 6 appear copy-pasted. More visualizations (e.g., map topologies, vehicle distributions, video) would help readers (R1). A typo exists in Appendix Table 3: “evaluation” should be “training” (R1).  
- **Missing analysis of contextual factors.** Adoption rate is fixed at 1/3; experiments to identify which factors (e.g., temperature, adoption rate) most affect performance are absent (R1). Including an IDM (Intelligent Driver Model) rule-based baseline would help verify that MARL can at least outperform it during training (R1).  
- **Benchmark scope is narrow for the broader MARL community.** While valuable for eco-driving, the task has fixed formulation—continuous action space (acceleration), specific reward, single-intersection scale, predefined driver models, no significant uncertainty—making it questionable whether insights transfer to other MARL domains. One reviewer noted the benchmark would have greater impact if it focused on contributing novel algorithms for the application rather than positioning as a general MA-CRL benchmark (R2). (Contrast: other reviewers found the benchmark beneficial for CRL research; this weakness reflects a minority but persistent view.)  
- **Need for additional algorithm-driven metrics and qualitative analysis.** Metrics like sample efficiency, training stability, and qualitative policy visualizations are missing (R2).  
- **Potential data processing concern:** OSM data provides traffic light locations but not which lane each controls; how this is resolved in IntersectionZoo is not explained (R1).  
- **Page limit issue:** The Reproducibility Statement appears on page 11, beyond the main content limit (R3).
