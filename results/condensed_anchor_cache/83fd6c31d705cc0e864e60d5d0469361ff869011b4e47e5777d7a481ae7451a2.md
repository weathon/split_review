## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the problem of minimizing the maximum of \(N\) convex, \(L\)-Lipschitz functions, i.e., \(\min_x \max_{i\in[N]} f_i(x)\) given access to a first-order oracle. The best classical algorithm (Carmon et al., 2021) requires \(O(N\epsilon^{-2/3}+\epsilon^{-8/3})\) queries and is optimal in the low-accuracy regime \(\epsilon\ge 1/\sqrt{N}\). The authors develop a quantum algorithm with complexity \(\tilde{O}(\sqrt{N}\epsilon^{-5/3}+\epsilon^{-8/3})\) using a zeroth-order quantum oracle (which can be used to obtain gradients) and prove a lower bound of \(\tilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{N}\epsilon^{-2/3})\), showing the dependence on \(N\) is optimal up to polylog factors. The main idea is to modify a classical algorithm based on a regularized ball optimization oracle (BROO) by replacing a Gibbs‑sampling step with a faster quantum Gibbs‑sampling subroutine, which yields the quadratic improvement in \(N\).

### Strengths
- **Progress on a fundamental problem.** The paper studies a natural and well‑studied optimization task, and showing a quantum speedup for minimizing the maximal loss is interesting and timely. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- **Near-optimal dependence on \(N\).** The algorithm’s query complexity is \(\tilde{O}(\sqrt{N}\epsilon^{-5/3}+\epsilon^{-8/3})\) and the lower bound \(\tilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{N}\epsilon^{-2/3})\) proves optimality in \(N\) up to polylog factors. (Reviewers 1,4)
- **Clean and informative results.** The quantum speedup is clearly demonstrated, and the techniques (quantum Gibbs sampling to accelerate a gradient‑estimation step) are appropriately explained. (Reviewer 2)
- **Well written (majority opinion).** Two reviewers found the presentation good and the paper generally well written. (Reviewers 2,3,4; Reviewer 1 found the English occasionally awkward – see below.)

### Weaknesses
- **Gap between upper and lower bounds.** The upper bound \(\tilde{O}(\sqrt{N}\epsilon^{-5/3}+\epsilon^{-8/3})\) and the lower bound \(\tilde{\Omega}(\sqrt{N}\epsilon^{-2/3})\) do not match in the dependence on \(\epsilon\). This is the major unsatisfactory point. (Reviewers 1,2; Reviewer 4 notes this gap but does not consider it a significant weakness.)
- **Quantum bound not competitive with classical methods in \(\epsilon\).** Classical first‑order algorithms can achieve \(\epsilon^{-2}\) dependence, which is significantly better than the \(\epsilon^{-5/3}\) and \(\epsilon^{-8/3}\) terms in the quantum bound. Since the speedup is only in \(N\), the practical relevance is questionable; classical methods already handle \(N\) well in many regimes. (Reviewer 2)
- **Limited novelty.** The algorithm works by taking an existing classical algorithm and replacing one step with a known quantum subroutine (quantum Gibbs sampling). The core ideas are expected, and the contribution is incremental. (Reviewers 2,3; Reviewer 4 found the results novel and interesting – this disagreement is noted.)
- **Insufficient explanation of Algorithm 2.** The quantum sampling algorithm is not fully explained. Specifically, the procedure finds the \(K\) largest elements of the distribution, sets the probability of the remaining elements equal to the \(K\)-th element, and then presumably applies rejection sampling. This step is critical but unclear. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing discussion of the \(\epsilon\)-gap reason.** The paper does not explain why the upper and lower bounds differ in \(\epsilon\), nor does it compare with classical first‑order results. Is the inferior \(\epsilon\)-dependence due to using a zeroth‑order oracle instead of first‑order? A clear discussion is needed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Open‑problem statement before Contributions.** The sentence “However, as far as we know, …” (last sentence before Contributions) should be verified to ensure the claimed open problem is indeed open. (Reviewer 2)
- **Presentation issues.** The English is occasionally awkward. Specific formatting problems: (1) p.1: “Nesterov (2018) showed …” should be phrased as “it is known that …” (2) p.4: “named ket” is unnecessary and adds no information; (3) Algorithm 1, line 5: unclear subscript; (4) p.8: “given in Ref. Carmon et al. (2021)” – remove “Ref.” (Reviewer 1)
- **Request for broader applicability.** The techniques might be generalizable; a meta‑theorem describing for which class of optimization problems a quadratic quantum speedup can be obtained would strengthen the paper. (Reviewer 4)
