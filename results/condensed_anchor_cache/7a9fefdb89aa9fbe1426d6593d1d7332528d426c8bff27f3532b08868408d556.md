## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Recurrent Context Compression (RCC), a method to extend the context window of LLMs by segmenting input, encoding each segment, and passing hidden states from the last token of each segment to a decoder. It also proposes instruction reconstruction to mitigate performance degradation when both instruction and context are compressed. Evaluated on text reconstruction (BLEU4 ~0.95 at 32x compression), passkey retrieval (nearly 100% accuracy at 1M tokens), and LongBench QA tasks, RCC shows competitive performance with non-compressed methods while saving storage.

### Strengths
- The problem of extending context length is well-motivated and important; the method aims to address training/inference challenges and achieves better memory usage than some prior work. (R1, R2)
- The work is original—introducing a decoder in this manner is novel to the best of the reviewers’ knowledge. (R2)  
  *Minority contrast:* R4 notes similar ideas exist in Compressive Transformers and Dodo (layer-level compression, last-token reuse), and short-to-long training was used in Longformer. The claim of straightforwardness is also noted (R3).
- The training paradigm is carefully designed: instruction-aware reconstruction mitigates instruction-following degradation, and short-to-long training saves computational overhead. (R4)
- RCC achieves a high compression ratio (up to 32x) vs. ICAE and preserves most context information across reconstruction and downstream tasks. (R4)  
  *Minority contrast:* R3 notes the architecture uses small models (pythia-1.4b, mamba-1.4b) and requests results on larger LLaMA-3/Mistral families.
- Strong PassKey results at 1M input tokens in supervised settings suggest the architecture may pass synthetic stress tests. (R1, R3 acknowledges substantial performance improvement claims)

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient baselines and comparisons:**  
  - Limited comparisons on LongBench (only QA subsets, only Pythia with/without fine-tuning). (R1)  
  - PassKey comparisons lack related long-context methods such as AutoCompressor, LLoCO, Unlimiformer, and StreamingLLM. (R1)  
  - Not compared with other KV cache compression methods like MInference, Razorattention, InfLLM, KIVI, or Recurrent Memory Transformer (RMT). (R3, R4)  
  - Only one baseline (infini-transformers) used in many experiments. (R4)  
- **Evaluation gaps and inconclusive experiments:**  
  - Short context performance: 2-point degradation on tasks within the pretraining context length is significant and needs further study. (R2)  
  - Text reconstruction BLEU score of 0.95 is not strong; Dodo achieves 0.98 at 20x compression with LLaMA. (R4)  
  - No evaluation on out-of-domain tasks (e.g., LongBench summarization, few-shot) or zero-shot passkey retrieval. (R1, R3)  
  - Claim of “context-instruction confusion” lacks direct experimental support. (R3)  
- **Architecture and efficiency concerns:**  
  - The approach roughly doubles the number of parameters, impacting both training and inference. (R2)  
  - RCC requires fine-tuning; it is unclear how memory/time efficiency compares to other prompt compression methods given this overhead. (R3)  
  - Major computational overhead of transformer decoding may come from non-quadratic terms; wall-clock time comparison between RCC and standard decoding is missing. (R4)  
  - Ablation study on varying segment length in the encoder is missing. (R3)  
- **Unclear or incomplete presentation:**  
  - Table 1: meaning of three numbers per cell (e.g., “96/98/94”) is not explained. (R3)  
  - Table 2: unclear if each cell is the average of 4 subtasks per context length column. (R3)  
  - Question about evaluation of LongBench at 0-2k: are only samples in that range used, or is truncation applied? (R1)  
- **Instruction reconstruction overhead:**  
  - Question/instruction tokens are first reconstructed from encoder hidden states, then input to decoder; time/space complexity may be worse than directly prepending them to decoder inputs. (R1)  
  - RCC-Ins-Human (without compressing instruction) sometimes performs better; the advantage of compressing instructions is not convincingly shown. (R4)  
- **Novelty concerns (minority):**  
  R4: Incorporating hidden representations from each layer is reminiscent of Compressive Transformers and Dodo; short-to-long training was used in Longformer. (R4)  
  R3: Idea is straightforward but experiments are incomplete.
