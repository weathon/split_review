## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Selective Knowledge Distillation (SelKD), a method for transferring only a subset of a teacher model's knowledge to a student, framed via Inverse Optimal Transport (IOT). The authors present closed-set SelKD (student learns only selected categories) and open-set SelKD (student can reject non-selected categories). Experiments on CIFAR and Tiny ImageNet with ResNet backbones demonstrate performance gains over standard KD methods. The work addresses a practical challenge for resource-constrained environments, but reviewers noted several limitations in experimentation, clarity, and analysis.

### Strengths
- **Novel IOT perspective**: Reformulating KD as a bi-level optimization via IOT is innovative and opens new research directions for selective knowledge transfer.
- **Practical relevance**: The ability to transfer only task-specific knowledge is well-motivated for edge-device deployment and resource-constrained settings, reducing redundant learning.
- **Open-set extension**: Allowing the student to handle unknown categories adds real-world applicability.
- **Clear motivation and structure**: The paper is well-written, with a clear presentation of the problem and related work, despite complex OT concepts.
- **Empirical validation**: Experiments on CIFAR and Tiny ImageNet show SelKD outperforms traditional KD baselines on targeted tasks with lower computational cost.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited experimental scope**: Results are only on small datasets (CIFAR, Tiny ImageNet) and small backbones (ResNet50). Experiments with larger architectures (e.g., ViT), more diverse datasets, and recent KD methods (2023–2024) are needed to demonstrate scalability and generalizability. (R2, R4)
2. **Lack of efficiency analysis**: No quantitative comparison of computational cost (training/inference time, memory footprint) between SelKD and vanilla KD, especially for OT plan computation. This is critical for resource-constrained claims. (R1, R3, R4)
3. **Notation and clarity gaps**: Key symbols (e.g., \(z\) in Eq. 2, \(\tilde{P}\) in Eq. 7) are not introduced. The inner/outer optimization in Eq. 8 lacks detail—it is unclear whether KL divergence or cross-entropy is minimized. Algorithm 1 assumes familiarity with classical OT methods, reducing accessibility. (R3)
4. **Subtask definition and composition**: How subtasks are determined is unspecified. It is unclear whether SelKD remains robust when categories within a subtask are highly dissimilar. The impact of subtask composition on performance should be discussed. (R1)
5. **Motivation for selective distillation**: The advantage over a single general student model is not clearly justified. If vanilla KD already performs well across all tasks, training multiple task-specific students may incur higher costs without clear gains. Empirical evidence showing limitations of vanilla KD in transferring task-specific knowledge (especially under resource constraints) is missing. (R1)
6. **Practical deployment scenario**: The paper does not clarify whether the teacher is trained offline or on-device. Simultaneously training both models on a resource-constrained edge device is impractical. This should be explicitly stated. (R1)
7. **Lack of theoretical guarantees**: No convergence or optimality analysis for the IOT formulation limits understanding of method behavior and reliability. (R2)
8. **Dependence on label information**: SelKD relies on explicit class labels, making extension to semi-supervised or unsupervised settings difficult. The open-set formulation also lacks guidance for dynamically changing categories (addition/removal over time). (R2)
9. **Parameter sensitivity**: No analysis of random seed variance or sensitivity to the entropic regularization parameter in OT. These hyperparameters can significantly impact OT-based methods, and stability should be shown. (R3, R4)
10. **Insufficient implementation details**: The construction of the cost matrix (multiplicative form in Eq. 11) and the partial transport mechanism are not adequately explained. Derivation or code for reproducibility is missing. (R1, R4)
11. **Feature-level transport unexplored**: The paper mentions feature-level transport as a possibility but does not investigate it, missing an opportunity to demonstrate OT’s advantage for representation learning. (R3)
12. **Missing baselines**: Multi-task KD studies [1–3] should be included for fair comparison, as SelKD trains multiple task-specific students. (R1)
