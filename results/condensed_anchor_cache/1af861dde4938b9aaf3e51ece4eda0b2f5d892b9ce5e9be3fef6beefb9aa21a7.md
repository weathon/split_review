## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes CGACell, a crossover operator for binary genetic algorithms inspired by cellular automata (CA). Offspring are generated by arranging parent chromosomes in a grid and applying CA rules (specified for elementary CA and 2D CA with von Neumann and Moore neighborhoods). The operator is evaluated by optimizing the number of neighbors (K) in a KNN classifier on the Yale face database (165 images) for image classification. The authors compare CGACell-GA against KNN, PCA, and KMeans. Both reviewers note the idea is novel, but the paper suffers from significant weaknesses in motivation, comparison, and experimental rigor.

### Strengths
- The crossover operator based on cellular automata is a novel idea for genetic algorithms with binary chromosomes (both reviewers). One reviewer found the concept “intriguing”; the other highlighted it as a novel contribution.
- The paper demonstrates the operator’s application to facial image classification and reports high correctness percentages on the Yale face database (Reviewer 2; noted as a positive result).
- Specific forms of the operator for 1D and 2D CA cases (including Moore and von Neumann neighborhoods) are described and exemplified (both reviewers acknowledge the description).

### Weaknesses
- **Tone and presentation**: The paper reads like a textbook chapter or blog post rather than a conference article, lacking conciseness and appropriate focus (Reviewer 1). Presentation is insufficient: concepts are hard to comprehend, acronyms are not defined before use, symbols and equations are poorly explained, and the detailed process (e.g., how the CA rules are applied in high-dimensional conditions) is unclear (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing motivation and context**: The authors never state the motivation or insight behind using CA for crossover, nor do they contrast their method with previous works combining CA and GAs. The exact contribution relative to related work is not specified (Reviewer 1). An ablation study isolating the effect of the CA operator is absent, leaving a gap between results and conclusions (Reviewer 2).
- **Inadequate experimental comparisons**:
  - The proposed operator is compared only against KNN, PCA, and KMeans—clustering/dimensionality reduction methods that are not relevant for evaluating a GA crossover. No comparison is made with standard crossover operators (e.g., single-point, multi-point, uniform) or a hill-climbing baseline within the same GA framework (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 2 also notes missing comparisons to classifiers like SVM, K-tree, MLP).
  - Only one small dataset is used (Yale face database, 165 images), which is insufficient to verify performance (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 1 calls the experimental results “unintelligible”).
  - The authors do not describe how the database was partitioned (train/test split) or give details of the compared methods’ configurations (Reviewer 2).
- **Experimental design flaws**: One reviewer notes that the entire experimental design is inconsistent—optimizing K for KNN and then comparing against clustering/dimensionality reduction methods does not make sense (Reviewer 1). Both reviewers agree that the experiments fail to demonstrate the advantage of the CA-based crossover.
- **Scope concerns**: The paper primarily deals with an evolutionary computation technique; one reviewer argues it falls outside the scope of ICLR (Reviewer 1).
- **Disagreement between reviewers**: One reviewer was highly critical (score 1; Contribution 1), describing the draft as “way below the quality required for ICLR” and finding the presentation poor (1) and soundness fair (2). The other reviewer was less harsh but still rated it borderline (score 3; Contribution 2) and identified the same core issues with soundness (2) and presentation (2). Both agree the contributions and experimental validation are weak.
