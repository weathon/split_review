## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes OCCAM, a framework that optimally assigns image classifiers to instances within a cost budget using an asymptotically unbiased nearest-neighbor accuracy estimator and integer linear programming. All reviewers find the problem practical and the results promising, with one reviewer being notably more positive (score 8). However, multiple reviewers identify critical weaknesses: the method’s own costs (sample collection, nearest neighbor search, ILP solving) are ignored; experiments use only 7 classifiers with a large cost gap, limiting baselines; hyperparameter λ selection is ad hoc; asymptotic guarantees may not hold in practice; and related work (algorithm selection, mixture-of-experts routing) is insufficiently discussed.

### Strengths
- The problem of maximizing accuracy under inference cost constraints is timely and relevant, especially as classifier costs grow.
- OCCAM provides statistical guarantees (asymptotically unbiased accuracy estimator) with weak assumptions, and the assignment is solved via ILP.
- The accuracy estimator is theoretically justified under Lipschitz continuity and well-separatedness.
- Experiments show up to 40% cost reduction with little or no accuracy drop, outperforming baselines like Frugal-MCT and single best across various cost levels.
- The method is well-motivated and considered sound by all reviewers; one reviewer (score 8) specifically highlights its practical relevance and strong theoretical/empirical support.

### Weaknesses
- **Ignored extra costs of the method itself:** The problem formulation does not account for the cost of computing nearest neighbors, solving the ILP, or acquiring the precomputed accuracy samples. Without showing that the total cost (including these steps) is less than the inference savings, the method may be impractical. Time complexity and empirical running times for the ILP solver and nearest neighbor search (sample size up to 40000) are not reported (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Limited scope of experiments and baseline issues:** Only 7 pretrained classifiers are used, with a large cost gap between SwinV2-S and SwinV2-T and no intermediate-cost models. Consequently, the "single best" baseline at 10% and 20% cost reduction in Table 2 uses the same model, making it weak. Experiments with more classifiers (e.g., >100, as in model selection literature) are missing (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Unclear hyperparameter λ selection:** λ is set to 100 for ImageNet-1K and 5 for other datasets, a large range with no principled tuning method. Sensitivity to λ is not analyzed, raising robustness concerns (Reviewer 3).
- **Asymptotic guarantees may break in practice:** Lemma 4.5 and 4.6 are asymptotic; the embedding space depends on the training set, and the data may not be well-separated on a validation set. If accuracy samples come from training data, estimates can be biased; if from a separate validation set, a large representative set is needed. The i.i.d. assumption may not hold (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing upper-bound baseline:** A strong baseline solving the ILP with true classification likelihoods (rather than estimated) would show the achievable performance ceiling and isolate approximation error (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient positioning relative to related work:** The paper does not adequately discuss connections to mixture-of-experts routing, per-instance algorithm selection (Rice 76), or online model selection; e.g., no reference to the classic algorithm selection framework (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Unexplained behavior of Random baseline:** In Tables 2 and 4, the Random results are identical for all cost reduction levels, which is not explained (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing justification for K=40:** The reason for choosing K=40 as the maximal number of neighbors is not provided (Reviewer 1).
- **Minor:** Typo on p.1 line 37: "On the other (hand)" (Reviewer 4).
