## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DuMBO, a decentralized no-regret Bayesian optimization algorithm for high-dimensional functions with an additive structure. It relaxes the restrictive assumption on the Maximum Factor Size (MFS) while still providing asymptotic optimality. DuMBO uses ADMM to maximize the acquisition function and is designed to work in a message-passing decentralized setting. Experiments on synthetic and real-world problems demonstrate competitive performance against existing BO algorithms, especially when factors are high-dimensional. Reviewers find the idea interesting and the algorithm asymptotically optimal, but express concerns about experimental completeness, missing baselines, presentation clarity, and theoretical novelty relative to prior work.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written overall (R1, R3), though some presentation issues are noted (R4).
- The use of ADMM to maximize the acquisition function is novel compared to existing works (R1).
- The algorithm can be implemented in a decentralized manner, which is practically useful (R3).
- DuMBO is provably asymptotically optimal with a regret bound (R2, R3).
- The paper explores the trade-off between model complexity and acquisition function maximization guarantees, contributing to theoretical understanding (R2).
- Numerical experiments illustrate the practicability of the method and include state-of-the-art baselines; DuMBO performs competitively and is particularly effective when the objective function comprises many high-dimensional factors (R2, R4).
- One reviewer (R3) found the experiments exhaustive.
- Explicit descriptions of the optimization of the acquisition function via ADMM make the algorithm feasible to use (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing related work / baselines.**  
  - The paper does not discuss several approaches that avoid assumptions on additive structure (R1: [1],[2],[3]).  
  - A very related paper, “Are Random Decompositions All We Need in High-Dimensional Bayesian Optimisation?” [4], is neither cited nor compared (R1).  
  - Trust-region based high-dimensional methods are missing, e.g., “Learning Search Space Partition for Black-box Optimization using Monte Carlo Tree Search” (Wang et al.) (R2).  
  - Some methods mentioned in the text are not included in the experiments (R2).  
  - A more detailed comparison with Add-GP-UCB [3] is needed, as the decomposition approach is similar (R4).

- **Insufficient and questionable experimental evaluation.**  
  - The baseline TurBO is not up-to-date (R2).  
  - Performance improvement over baselines is not significant (R2); e.g., in Figure 1c DuMBO is close to TurBO, raising doubts about whether it outperforms on higher-dimensional tasks (R2).  
  - Standard deviations are missing in Table 2 (R2).  
  - Ablation studies on hyperparameters (especially sensitivity to \(\bar d\) and the inferred decomposition) are missing (R2, R4).  
  - The time horizon is only about 100 steps; in Figure 1a some methods’ costs are still decaying (R2).  
  - The algorithm’s sensitivity to the inferred decomposition is not studied; it is unclear how many rounds are needed to generate initial data for decomposition inference (R4).  
  - While one reviewer (R3) called the experiments exhaustive, other reviewers found them insufficient, lacking proper baselines, ablation, and statistical reporting (R1, R2, R4).

- **Presentation and clarity issues.**  
  - The decomposition of the objective function into factors is not explicitly explained in Section 3.1; Figure 2 from the supplements would aid understanding and could be moved to the main text (R4).  
  - The assumption that the acquisition function is a “restricted prox-regular function that satisfies the Kurdyka–Lojasiewicz condition” is not formally defined (R4).  
  - The method description is unclear on why ADMM can solve the high-dimensional optimization when \(\overline{d}\) is high, and whether it can guarantee finding the arg max for a non-convex acquisition function (R1).  
  - Equation (8) appears to have a typo (R1).  
  - The paper does not discuss known lower bounds for the problem or compare the algorithm to them (R3).  
  - The authors should provide a proof sketch of the main theoretical result and highlight the main contributions and technical challenges (R3).

- **Limited theoretical novelty.**  
  - Similar results to Theorem 3.4 have been derived in [1] (R4).  
  - The regret analysis is similar to that in [2] without significant novelty (R4).  
  - It is unclear what assumptions (e.g., Lipschitz continuity) are needed to guarantee convergence (R1).  
  - The conditions under which the acquisition function satisfies Assumption 5.1 should be discussed (R4).
