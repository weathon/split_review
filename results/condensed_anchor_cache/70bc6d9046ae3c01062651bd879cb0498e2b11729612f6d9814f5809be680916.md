## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes the labelled pseudo Neural Tangent Kernel (lpNTK) that incorporates label information into the NTK to study sample interactions during supervised learning. It shows asymptotic convergence to the empirical NTK (eNTK), and uses vector angles to categorize samples into interchangeable, unrelated, and contradictory types. The authors apply lpNTK to analyze learning difficulty, forgetting events, and data pruning, with experiments on MNIST and CIFAR-10. The paper also demonstrates improved generalization via pruning and de-biasing using lpNTK.

### Strengths

- The main conceptual contribution—incorporating label information into the NTK—is novel and interesting (R2, R3). The sign approximation is sensible (R2).
- The paper provides useful theoretical tools for analyzing learning dynamics, learning difficulty, and forgetting events based on NTK (R1, R3).
- The application of lpNTK to data pruning is reasonable, convincing, and shows simultaneous utility improvements, opening connections to selective prediction (R1, R3).
- The paper is well-structured, with intuitive hypotheses, clear questions, and reasonable experiments that validate the utility of lpNTK’s mapping functions (R1, R2).
- Related work is sufficiently discussed (R1, R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Definitions and theoretical clarity**  
  – “Interaction” and “learning difficulty” need mathematically clearer definitions (R1).  
  – The explanation for why the magnitude of the error term is omitted (only sign used, Eq. 2) is incomplete; magnitude could be useful for ranking, especially in early training (R3).  
  – In Section 2.1, the notation \(\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^K\) is misleading (it is a function, not a vector) and the simplex should be \(\Delta^{K-1}\) (R1).

- **Theorem 1 and its interpretation**  
  – The paper claims lpNTK “asymptotically converges” to eNTK, yet the central theme is the difference label information makes. The theorem should clarify in what sense the gap is “not significantly different” and under what conditions the difference becomes practically relevant; a more precise bound is needed (R2).

- **Lack of baselines and comparisons**  
  – No comparison with the influence function for measuring sample similarity; this would validate uniqueness of lpNTK and require analysis of computational cost and type of information provided (R2).  
  – The categorization of samples into three types mirrors the Data-IQ framework (Seedat et al., 2022); a direct comparison is needed to highlight distinct contributions, e.g., whether lpNTK offers a more direct measure of sample interaction while Data-IQ focuses on individual difficulty (R2).  
  – Table 1 results on forgetting events are not impressive, especially on MNIST; gains are marginal and it is unclear if lpNTK provides a substantial advantage over simpler methods (R3).

- **Contradictory samples and limitations**  
  – The paper states “contradictory samples are rare” but does not explain why; this likely reflects the clean MNIST/CIFAR-10 datasets, limiting practical implications (R1).  
  – No discussion of limitations: computational cost scales quadratically with number of samples and the square of parameter dimension, and the NTK approximation assumption is not always valid (R1, R2). A formal computational complexity analysis (dependence on sample size, dimensionality, parameter count) is missing (R2).

- **Missing experiments and analyses**  
  – Why is the similarity matrix \(\mathbf{K}\) fixed? A pair of examples can be similar or dissimilar at different training stages; how to obtain the best validation model in data pruning applications? (R1)  
  – Additional visualizations of the lpNTK (e.g., kernel matrix or spectral properties over training) could reveal more about its dynamics (R3).  
  – No visualization of pruned samples: are they noisy, ambiguous, or difficult? Such analysis would strengthen the paper (R3).  
  – Connections to forgetting/machine unlearning (Thudi et al., 2022) are not explored (R3).

- **Presentation**  
  – Formatting artifacts: very little vspace throughout, especially on pages 7–9; author(s) should reduce dangling words rather than resort to formatting tricks (R3).

- **Reviewer disagreement note**  
  One reviewer (score 8) was more positive about the novelty and interest of the work, while other reviewers (scores 6) raised substantial concerns about theoretical clarity, lack of baselines, and missing complexity analysis.
