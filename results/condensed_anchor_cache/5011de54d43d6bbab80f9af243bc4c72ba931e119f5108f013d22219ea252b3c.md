## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a framework for distributed momentum SGD (mSGD) that integrates momentum steps into existing distributed SGD algorithms (e.g., D-PSGD and EASGD). It provides theoretical results on last-iterate convergence (convergence of gradient norm and function value) under convex objectives with decaying learning rates, and analyzes the effect of momentum on early-stage acceleration. Experiments vary the momentum parameter and averaging frequency for one algorithm. Reviewers agree the problem of last-iterate convergence is practically relevant, but they differ on novelty, assumption strength, and experimental validation. One reviewer (score 5) found the problem important and the analyses detailed; the other three (scores 3) were more critical.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses last-iterate convergence, which is a stronger guarantee than time-average convergence and more relevant to practice (all reviewers).
- It extends existing results by fixing issues in prior work (paragraph after Theorem 2.1) (Reviewer 4).
- Demonstrates that incorporating a momentum term can accelerate the algorithm’s convergence rate during early stages (Reviewers 1, 2).
- One reviewer (score 5) particularly emphasized the importance of the problem and valued the detailed convergence analyses and characterization of momentum effects (Reviewer 2).
- The motivation is solid: many related works only establish average-gradient convergence (Reviewer 3).
- Mathematical results appear correct (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and significance of results are questioned**:
  - The framework is essentially an extension of existing algorithms by adding momentum steps, lacking sufficient novelty; the last-iterate results and early-stage acceleration observation are traceable to Jin et al. (2022b) and appear as a straightforward modification (Reviewer 1). Theorem 2.1 provides only asymptotic convergence without rates; Theorem 2.2’s rates are almost useless due to strong assumptions, and the results are not significant for a top ML conference (Reviewer 3). The distributed aspect is hardly investigated; the asymptotic results treat D-PSGD and EASGD identically, essentially behaving as mini-batch SGD (Reviewer 4). The paper suggests higher momentum is always better with no upper bound (Reviewer 4).

- **Assumptions are overly restrictive or unverifiable**:
  - Assumption 2.1(b) (bounded gradient) excludes quadratic and strongly convex functions (Reviewer 3). Even the milder gradient-similarity condition is not assumed, unlike in methods like SCAFFOLD (Reviewer 2).
  - Assumption 2.4 (bounded averaged iterate) cannot be checked before running the algorithm and should be a consequence of analysis, not an assumption (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). It is very strong; Reviewer 2 asks how to relax it, and Reviewer 4 calls for removal or proof.
  - Assumption 2.3 requires convexity with a unique optimizer, but the bounded gradient assumption prevents strong convexity, making it unclear what functions satisfy both (Reviewer 3).
  - Bounded stochastic gradients are strong; Reviewer 2 asks what challenges arise in extending the analysis under relaxed conditions. Reviewer 4 notes the combination of Lipschitzness and bounded gradients is strong.

- **Limited theoretical scope and rigor**:
  - Analysis is restricted to convex functions, limiting generalizability (Reviewer 1). For convex functions with a unique solution, it is odd to only consider gradient convergence rather than iterate/objective convergence; notation θ vs x is inconsistent (Reviewer 3). Lemma A.2 may require convexity; its validity for non-convex functions like sin x is questioned (Reviewer 2). Theorem 2.3’s definition of τ^{a_0} is unclear and likely contains a typo (Reviewer 3). Theorem 2.2 does not lead to linear speedup (Reviewer 1). The second term in Theorem 2.2 is useless since m ≥ 1 (Reviewer 4). Results are coarse; non-asymptotic guarantees are missing (Reviewer 4). The impact of the mixing matrix W is not discussed (Reviewer 4).

- **Experiments are weak or insufficient**:
  - Only one algorithm is tested under different momentum parameters α; no baselines are provided (Reviewer 1). The framework subsumes three special cases, but experiments cover only one; the paper should either include experiments for the others or explain the omission (Reviewer 2). The experiments have no added value because the algorithms are already studied and the setup is classical; the point of the paper is last-iterate convergence, not comparing momentum values, so the experiments do not validate the core theory (Reviewers 3, 4). It is unclear whether the assumptions of the theorems are satisfied in the experimental setup (Reviewer 3). Figures and legends are very small and hard to read (Reviewers 3, 4).

- **Presentation and clarity issues**:
  - Numerous typographical errors, unclear formulations, and incorrect citations (e.g., “equation equation 7”, “low bound condition”) (Reviewers 1, 4). Definitions of convergence are not rigorous (Reviewer 4). Writing is confusing, with terms appearing without definition (Reviewer 2). For Theorem 2.3, it is unclear whether x_n^{(i)} or \bar{x}_n is used, what V_0 is, and whether “bounded” means lower or upper bounded (Reviewers 2, 3). Inconsistent notation (θ vs x in Section 2.4) (Reviewer 3).

- **Missing literature**: The literature review misses relevant works on decentralized SGD with multiple local updates (Li et al., 2019; Ge & Chang, 2023) (Reviewer 2).

- **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms** (included above where applicable; additional explicit concerns):
  - How does the mixing matrix W impact results? (Reviewer 4)
  - Can Assumption 2.4 be lifted? (Reviewers 2, 4)
  - What tricks enable the last-iterate analysis? (Reviewer 2)
  - In Theorem 2.3, momentum=1 would suggest instant convergence, which is implausible; the O() notation likely hides dependence on a_0 and should be clarified (Reviewer 4).
  - Why does Theorem 2.2 not give linear speedup? (Reviewer 1)
