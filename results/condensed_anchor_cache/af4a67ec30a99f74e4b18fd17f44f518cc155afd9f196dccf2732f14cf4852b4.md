## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a continuous autoregressive speech model that eschews discrete quantization (e.g., RVQ) in favor of continuous latent representations. It uses a VAE with a Gaussian Mixture prior (GMM-VAE) for feature extraction and an autoregressive Gaussian Mixture Language Model (GMM-LM) with a stochastic monotonic alignment mechanism for TTS. The model consistently outperforms baselines like VALL-E, StyleTTS-2, and HierSpeech++ with only 10.3% of VALL-E’s parameters, achieving lower WER and higher MOS. Reviewers find the idea novel, the formulation clear, and the results impressive given the parameter efficiency. However, concerns are raised about the ablation of the monotonic alignment contribution, missing modeling details, and some aspects of the experimental design. Reviewer 2 is more positive (score 8), while Reviewers 1 and 3 are moderately positive (score 6) but more critical about specific weaknesses.

### Strengths

- The use of GMM-VAE to regularize the latent distribution (serving a role similar to discretization) and GMM-LM for autoregressive modeling is novel and interesting.
- The formulation is clear, straightforward, and easy to follow, with good pointers for reproducibility.
- The model achieves significantly lower WER and higher MOS than VALL-E using only 10.3% of VALL-E’s parameters, a notable efficiency gain.
- The method enables probabilistic sampling, which is beneficial for TTS applications.
- The paper provides a thorough discussion of related work and motivation.
- It can inspire research on continuous variational approaches in speech synthesis, which has been dominated by discrete-based methods.
- Comparative results against multiple strong baselines (VALL-E, StyleTTS-2, HierSpeech++) are presented.

### Weaknesses

- **Missing ablation on monotonic alignment**: Reviewer 3 identifies a key issue: the main experiments compare the full method (with monotonic alignment) against baselines that do not enforce monotonic alignment. The results in Appendix A.1 show that the version without monotonic alignment (Cross Att.) has higher WER than all baselines, suggesting the performance gains may primarily stem from the alignment mechanism rather than the GMM-VAE/GMM-LM novelty. An ablation study separating these contributions is recommended, ideally added to Table 2.
- **Counterintuitive VAE result**: Reviewer 1 notes that increasing the number of Gaussian mixtures in GMM-VAE (e.g., from 3 to 6) leads to worse reconstruction, whereas a 6‑mixture GMM should subsume the capacity of a 3‑mixture GMM. This result requires explanation.
- **Missing modeling details**:
  - The frame rate of the GMM-VAE features is not specified; it is crucial because it affects the type of information captured (Reviewer 1). It is unclear if it aligns with the mel spectrogram hop length (240 ms) described in Section 5.2.
  - Section 3.3 is unclear: how is the energy function \(e_{ij}\) calculated, and does it relate to cross‑attention weights in the transformer? (Reviewers 1 and 3).
  - The specs (e.g., parameter sizes) of baseline methods StyleTTS-2 and HierSpeech++ are not given, making comparisons less informative (Reviewer 3).
- **Validity of GMM assumption for autoregressive distribution**: Reviewer 3 questions whether the GMM regularization in the VAE latent space implies that the autoregressive conditional distribution \(p(h_t | h_{<t})\) is also a mixture of Gaussians. The logical connection is not justified.
- **Why few Gaussian mixtures suffice?** Reviewer 1 asks why the model needs only a small number of Gaussian mixtures (e.g., 3) compared to many VQ codes in RVQ, and what the mixture components capture.
- **Minor motivation issue**: Reviewer 2 notes that the paper positions discrete quantization as a necessity, but there are many prior continuous reconstruction approaches in speech modeling. The VQ approach also has practical advantages (easy parallelization with text encoding, storage/I/O efficiency) that are not addressed.
- **Sensitivity to random seeding**: Reviewer 2 asks about robustness of Monte‑Carlo estimation to random seeding; no analysis is provided.
- **Generalization to noisier data**: Reviewer 2 asks about performance on datasets beyond clean LibriSpeech; robustness is not evaluated.
- **Notation inconsistency**: Reviewer 1 points out that “GMM-VAE” vs. “VAE-GMM” should be consistent.
- **Limited prior work discussion**: Reviewer 1 notes insufficient discussion of prior Gaussian mixture VAE work (e.g., “Deep Unsupervised Clustering with Gaussian Mixture Variational Autoencoders”).
