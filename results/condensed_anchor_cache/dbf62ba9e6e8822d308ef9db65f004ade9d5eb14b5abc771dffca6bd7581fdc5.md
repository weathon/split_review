## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MINERVA, a supervised feature selection method using neural estimation of mutual information between features and targets. It introduces feature weights with sparse regularization to select feature subsets as an ensemble. Experiments are performed on synthetic datasets, showing the method can select important features. However, the paper has significant shortcomings in theoretical justification, experimental validation, and clarity.

### Strengths
- Mutual information is employed as a learning criterion for supervised feature selection (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The method outperforms existing techniques on two synthetic datasets (Reviewer 2).
- The use of synthetic data for feature selection experiments is appropriate for estimating algorithm performance (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of real-world validation**: Experiments are limited to two synthetic datasets. Multiple reviewers emphasize that this casts doubt on the method's applicability to real datasets, and the absence of experiments on real data is a major weakness (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Reviewer 1 notes that for real data, evaluation depends on downstream tasks, and Table 3 lacks details (e.g., why different numbers of features are compared, in-sample/out-sample ratio). Reviewer 2 mentions several uncontrolled factors (sample size, hyperparameters c1, c2, network architecture, unexplainable noise). Reviewer 3 points out that extra pages could have been used for real experiments.
- **Theoretical concerns about the mutual information estimator**: After introducing the constraints in Equation (5), it is unclear whether the optimized function remains a valid mutual information estimator. This requires deeper theoretical exploration (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 adds that the regularization on p (Eq. 5) uses an l1 norm on scaled-invariant p, leading to unbounded p, and a square loss term to prevent divergence, but the benefit over l1 directly on p is unclear, and the setting of constant a is not justified. Reviewer 3 makes a more fundamental criticism: the loss function never takes into account the correlation between X and Y; consequently, a reviewer with high confidence (5) argues the trained network will provide a constant output independent of the data, rendering the method ineffective.
- **Missing experimental details and incomplete comparisons**: The experimental section lacks important details, such as the number of samples, dimensionality, data generation process, number of random runs (Reviewer 1). The training procedure is not described (Reviewer 3). The comparisons omit recent filter methods (e.g., Inf-FS, ILFS) and other feature selection methods like CAE and E2E-FS (Reviewer 3). The results in Table 3 are difficult to evaluate without proper context, such as why different numbers of features are compared across methods and the in-sample/out-sample ratio (Reviewer 1).
- **Undefined mathematical terms and notation**: Several terms are not properly defined: the metric h(X_i, Y) (Reviewer 1); φ in Algorithm 1 (Reviewers 1, 2); p is used before definition, S is never defined (Reviewer 3). Additionally, in Equation (5) the target norm a is replaced by √d in the algorithm, but d is never defined (Reviewer 3). The definition of p and its scaling is confusing (Reviewer 1).
- **Algorithmic and optimization issues**: The gradient descent stop criterion is ambiguous: the text mentions stopping when estimated MI becomes smaller than the non-selection model, while Algorithm 1 says termination upon convergence. It is unclear which gradient descent method is used. If stochastic gradient descent, one step may not be sufficient for the selection step to proceed (Reviewer 1). Conventional gradient descent rarely sets entries of p exactly to zero, so selecting features via non-null entries may be impractical (Reviewer 1).
- **Writing and presentation quality**: The paper writing needs improvement; some sentences appear unfinished and there is a lack of coherence (Reviewer 3). The introduction omits embedded methods as a class of feature selection (Reviewer 3). Presentation and soundness are rated fair-to-poor by all reviewers (Reviewers 1, 2: 2/5; Reviewer 3: 1/5).
