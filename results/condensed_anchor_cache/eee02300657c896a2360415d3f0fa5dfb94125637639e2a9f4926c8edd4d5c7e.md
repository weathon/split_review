## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Adversarial Contrastive Decoding (ACD), which generates two opposite soft prompts (a Safeguarding Prompt SP and an Adversarial Prompt AP) via Opposite Prompt Optimization (OPO). These prompts are used during decoding to contrast safe and harmful logits, aiming to improve safety without retraining the LLM. ACD requires only a lightweight prompt tuning on a small anchor dataset. Experiments on multiple models and benchmarks (including AlpacaEval and TruthfulQA) show ACD reduces harmfulness and jailbreak attack success rates compared to other decoding-time baselines. Overall opinion is mixed: Reviewers 1 and 2 (scores 3) raise significant clarity and evaluation concerns, while Reviewers 3 and 4 (scores 5) find the method creative and effective but note limitations in scope and analysis.

### Strengths
- The use of soft prompts (SP and AP) optimized for contrastive decoding is novel and creative.
- ACD achieves safety alignment without extensive retraining or additional models, offering a lightweight and resource-friendly alternative.
- Experimental results demonstrate that ACD reduces harmfulness and jailbreak attack success across several models (e.g., LLaMA, Vicuna) and datasets.
- The method performs well compared to other decoding-time baselines, indicating robustness in security-sensitive contexts.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and terminology issues** (Reviewer 1): Key terms in Figure 2 (`D_HR`, `D_S`, `D_HA`) and Equation 5 (`logit_S`, `logit_A`) are undefined. In the Baselines section, `nID` and `oID` are ambiguous, and “prompt” is not clarified as soft or hard. These make the method difficult to follow.
- **Missing analysis of anchor dataset** (Reviewer 1): The abstract claims “lightweight prompt tuning on a relatively small anchor dataset,” but the paper never discusses the dataset size, which is critical for understanding practicality.
- **Missing baseline: fine-tuning on the anchor dataset** (Reviewer 1): A strong and simple baseline would be fine-tuning the LLM on the same anchor data; this comparison is absent. No reason for the omission is given.
- **Missing efficiency analysis** (Reviewers 1, 2): ACD requires two forward passes per query (SP and AP), increasing decoding time and resource usage. The paper does not analyze this cost, even in a Limitations section.
- **Incomplete jailbreak evaluation** (Reviewers 2, 4): Only direct attacks are evaluated; the method should also be tested against stronger jailbreak methods such as GCG, PAIR, and AutoDAN.
- **Insufficient evaluation of general capabilities** (Reviewer 2): Only AlpacaEval and TruthfulQA are used. Critical reasoning and understanding tasks (e.g., GSM8K, SQuAD, XSum) are omitted, leaving a gap in assessing whether ACD preserves generation ability.
- **Lack of analysis on the contrastive coefficient α** (Reviewer 2): Figure 5 shows α’s effect on harmfulness, but its impact on general capabilities is not discussed. Since both safe and unsafe logits may contribute to general performance (especially for safety-unrelated queries), this omission undermines the explanation of why the method works.
- **Limited depth and generality of prompt-based method** (Reviewer 3): ACD’s reliance on prompt-based contrastive decoding rather than deeper alignment mechanisms may limit its ability to generalize across varied contexts.
- **Complexity of dual prompts and parameter settings** (Reviewer 3): The dual-prompts and α parameter introduce extra complexity that may hinder practical scalability for larger models or diverse use cases.
- **Related work gap** (Reviewer 3): Other decoding-time safety methods exist (e.g., perplexity detection, self-defense, intention analysis [1-3]) but are not discussed or compared.
- **Lack of real-world applications** (Reviewers 3, 4): The study relies on benchmark tests; diverse real-world settings are not explored, raising concerns about practical robustness.
- **Potential bias from anchor dataset quality** (Reviewer 4): The manually generated anchor dataset may not cover a broad range of harmful content, constraining ACD’s effectiveness.
- **Unfair evaluation of Harmlessness Rate (HLR)** (Reviewer 4): Using ChatGPT to evaluate HLR risks high false positives; a combined rule‑based and LLM judgment approach might be more reliable.
- **Presentation inconsistency** (Reviewer 1): Table captions should be placed above tables for consistency.
