## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a practical backdoor attack scenario called data-constrained attacks, where attackers lack full access to the victim’s training data. It defines three constraint variants (number, class, domain) and identifies entanglement of benign and poisoning features as the cause of poor performance for existing attacks (BadNets, Blended) under these constraints. To address this, the authors propose CLIP-based techniques: clean feature suppression (CLIP-CFE) and poisoning feature augmentation (CLIP-UAP, CLIP-CFA). Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-50 show >100% improvement in attack success rate while preserving benign accuracy and visual stealthiness (PSNR, SSIM).

### Strengths
- Addresses a highly practical, real-world attack scenario (data-constrained), reflecting environments where victims collect data from multiple sources.
- Provides a clear taxonomy of data-constrained attacks (number, class, domain constraints).
- Identifies through analysis and experiments that existing attacks fail due to feature entanglement – an important and insightful contribution.
- The use of CLIP for clean feature erasing and poisoning feature augmentation is novel (Reviewers 1, 3, 4) and consistently outperforms baseline triggers across constraints, architectures (ResNet-18, ResNet-34, VGG-16, MobileNet-V2), and datasets.
- Attacks remain stealthy (high SSIM/PSNR) and do not impact benign accuracy.
- Comprehensive evaluations showing effectiveness across three constraint types and multiple architectures.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and theoretical depth**: One reviewer (R2, rating 3, high confidence 5) strongly questions the novelty, arguing that the core idea of using adversarial examples to suppress clean features and augment poisoning features has already been exploited in prior work (Zhao et al., 2020; Turner et al., 2019). The only difference is the use of CLIP. This reviewer requests a clear differentiation from those references. Multiple reviewers also note the lack of theoretical analysis for the observed performance decline of existing attacks (R2) and for the effect of erasing benign features compared to using out-of-domain samples (R4).
- **Limited experimental scope**:
  - **Datasets**: The method is only evaluated on natural-scenery datasets (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-50). Performance on domains drastically different from CLIP’s training data (e.g., medical scans, satellite imagery) is not guaranteed. Fine-grained datasets (CUB-200-2011, Stanford-Cars, Oxford-Flowers) are also absent (R3).
  - **Architectures**: Target models are relatively small (ResNets, VGG-16, MobileNet-V2). Evaluating on larger, more advanced architectures (e.g., ViT) is needed to claim real-world applicability (R3).
  - **Defenses**: No evaluation against backdoor detection/defense methods. Reviewer 3 notes that Neural Cleanse (Wang et al., 2019) or feature-based defenses (Min et al., 2023) may be particularly effective against this attack. Reviewer 4 adds that the optimized noise for erasing benign features might be unlearned, and adaptive defenses should be considered.
  - **Alternative feature extractors**: Only CLIP is used; comparison with other proxy feature extractors is missing (R4).
- **Computational and practical costs**:
  - Runtime information (e.g., number of gradient updates, memory footprint) for the CLIP optimization process is not provided (R1, R3). Reviewer 3 notes high resource requirements could limit real-world feasibility.
  - The interaction of constraints (e.g., limited samples across few classes from one domain) is not explored (R1), leaving a gap in understanding real-world attack combinations.
- **Stealthiness and clarity**:
  - Stealthiness metrics rely solely on pixel-level measures (PSNR, SSIM); perceptual visualizations and defense resilience analysis are absent (R1).
  - Several expressions are ambiguous: the reason and effect of operating within CLIP’s feature space, and the analysis of erasing benign features versus semantic-agnostic out-of-domain samples (R4).
  - Minor: fonts in figures are too small; page 8 has a typo (“VGG-16 datasets” should be “models”) (R3).
- **Real-world evidence**: Reviewer 4 requests more empirical evidence regarding the occurrence of data-constrained backdoor attacks in real-world scenarios.
