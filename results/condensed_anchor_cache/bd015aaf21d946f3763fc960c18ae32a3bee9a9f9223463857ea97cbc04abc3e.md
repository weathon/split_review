## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents a multi-task transformer architecture (POYO+) for neural decoding from calcium imaging data in the mouse visual cortex. Trained on the Allen Institute Brain Observatory dataset (100,000+ neurons, 6 visual areas), the model demonstrates that multi-session, multi-region, and multi-task training improves decoding performance and enables transfer across brain regions and cell types. The model’s latent representations automatically cluster by region and cell type even without explicit labels. The work is seen as an important step toward a foundation model for neural data.

### Strengths
- **Novel multi-task transformer architecture for neural decoding**: The paper introduces a flexible multi-task decoder that can handle a variety of decoding tasks (e.g., running speed, pupil dilation, stimulus identity) from calcium responses (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Demonstration of transfer learning across regions and cell types**: The model successfully transfers between distinct visual cortex areas and between excitatory/inhibitory Cre-lines, showing that heterogeneous neural circuits share common information (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Interpretability analysis**: The latent representations of the model, trained purely on response-task pairs, automatically distinguish brain regions and cell types without any explicit supervision, providing scientific insight into neural circuit organization (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Thorough experimental validation**: The experiments cover a wide range of realistic transfer scenarios (cross-session, cross-region, cross-dataset), and the results are quantitatively backed (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Importance for NeuroAI**: The work addresses scaling and foundation models for neural data, a topic of great interest in the field (Reviewer 2).
- **Rarity of large-scale calcium decoding**: Scaling decoding models to calcium imaging data is relatively uncommon and could have practical benefits (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited region coverage**: The dataset only covers the mouse visual cortex (VISp, VISl, etc.), not functionally distinct brain areas. Reviewer 1 notes that other papers use “multi-region” to mean whole-brain coverage (citing [1], [2]). Claims of handling “diverse brain regions” are weakened by this limitation. Reviewer 1 and 4 suggest testing on datasets with broader spatial coverage (e.g., OpenScope) to validate generalizability.
- **Only one dataset used**: The study relies exclusively on the Allen Institute Brain Observatory dataset. Reviewer 1 and 4 argue that additional experiments on a second dataset (e.g., with different species or conditions) would strengthen claims of scaling benefits and transfer learning.
- **Comparable performance of POYO+ vs. POYO despite overhead**: Figure 3 shows that POYO+ and POYO yield similar performance, yet POYO+ requires training 12 task-specific variants. Reviewer 1 asks for discussion of this finding.
- **Limited comparison with state-of-the-art methods**: The comparison with CEBRA is only shown for natural movie frame decoding (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 notes that more established neural decoding frameworks should be included for a robust benchmark.
- **Architectural details and loss weighting are vague**: The multi-task decoder’s design (number of attention blocks/heads, unique design choices compared to standard multi-task transformers) is not clearly described (Reviewer 3). The loss weighting method (“determined based on the scale of the loss from an initial model”) is not well-defined—does it use an untrained model? (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Missing/explained results and inconsistencies**: Figure 3C reports only 9 cases for single-task POYO variants, but the text mentions 12 variants. The missing 3 cases are not explained (Reviewer 3). The claim that a model trained on inhibitory Cre-lines performs well on the drifting gratings task is not supported by results on other tasks (Reviewer 3). Terminology “POYOplus” and “POYO+” are used inconsistently; there are minor typos (e.g., “from various depths...”).
- **Low decoding accuracies**: The absolute performance levels are relatively low, raising questions about the model’s practical utility for neuroscience research (Reviewer 3).
- **No standard deviation/error reported**: Reviewer 2 asks for s.e.m. or standard deviation across trials/sessions/animals in all performance figures (Figure 3, Tables 1-3) to assess variability.
- **Data-hungriness not assessed**: It is unclear whether POYO+ would beat single-session baselines in a single-session setting, or how much data is needed for the benefits to emerge (Reviewer 2).
- **Overfitting risk**: With a complex model and a dataset covering only six brain regions (one species), Reviewer 4 questions steps taken to mitigate overfitting. Reviewer 4 also notes that the feature selection process is not explained.
- **Limited discussion of limitations**: Reviewer 4 notes that the paper does not adequately address noise, computational demands, or other potential shortcomings of the model.
- **Minor structural concerns**: Figure 1’s caption does not clearly distinguish query tokens from output tokens; the model’s autoregressive vs. non-autoregressive prediction and causal attention masking are not clarified (Reviewer 2). The authors do not introduce “POYO+” in the main text (Reviewer 2).
- **Minority reviewer more critical**: Reviewer 4 (score 6) is notably less positive than the other three (all score 8), citing limited comparisons, generalizability concerns, and sparse discussion of limitations. This contrast should be preserved.
