## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Min-K%++, a method for pre-training data detection in LLMs that interprets training samples as local maxima under maximum likelihood training. It uses normalized token probabilities (subtracting mean and dividing by standard deviation across preceding tokens) instead of raw log-likelihoods. Experiments on WikiMIA and MIMIR benchmarks across 10 models show SOTA performance on WikiMIA (6.2–10.5% AUROC improvement over runner-up) and competitive results on MIMIR without needing reference models.

### Strengths
1. Clear theoretical motivation linking MLE to local maxima detection (gradient conditions: ∂logp/∂x_i = 0, second derivative < 0), providing a more principled foundation than prior heuristic methods.
2. Simple yet effective method (Min-K%++) that achieves new SOTA on WikiMIA and performs on par with reference-based methods on MIMIR.
3. Comprehensive evaluation across 2 benchmarks (WikiMIA, MIMIR), 5 model families (10 models), and 6 baselines, demonstrating consistent improvements.
4. Well-written paper with helpful visualizations (Figures 2 and 3) explaining the core idea and contrast with Min-K%.
5. Addresses important problem (data detection for copyright, contamination) with practical implications.

### Weaknesses
- Novelty is somewhat incremental; Min-K%++ builds on the existing Min-K% methodology without introducing a transformative approach (Reviewer 1).
- The theoretical connection between the gradient conditions in Sec. 4.1 (∂logp/∂x_i=0, second derivative <0) and the proposed normalized score (log p(x_t|x<sub>&lt;t</sub>) − μ) / σ for token selection is not clearly established. The link from the ISM object's hidden dimension to token probability is missing, and the argument that training data forms a local maximum is insufficiently connected to the method. A more direct observation (e.g., final layer token hidden states) would be more convincing (Reviewer 2).
- Missing comparison with recent state-of-the-art methods beyond Min-K, notably ReCall (reports nearly 20% improvement over Min-K on WikiMIA). This omission weakens the claim of SOTA (Reviewer 2). Also missing discussion of DC-PDD paper that found Min-K%++ underperforms Min-K on PatentMIA benchmark (Reviewer 4).
- Justification for effectiveness on rare or difficult-to-learn texts (lines 256–263) is not sufficiently strong. Distinguishing untrained from rare samples remains challenging, and more empirical validation is needed (Reviewer 2).
- The MIMIR benchmark results show minimal differences among methods, and reference-based methods do not show a clear advantage. This requires further discussion and explanation (Reviewer 2).
- Conceptual concerns about nuanced false positives/negatives for subportions of text, rare vs. untrained sequences, and rare tokens; the method may not effectively capture these (Reviewer 2).
- No information on computational resources required, making it difficult to assess feasibility for real-time or large-scale applications (Reviewer 1).
- Missing evaluation on additional benchmarks, such as using new open-source LLMs with released training data (Reviewer 3). Broader language task comparison is also lacking (Reviewer 1).
- Insufficient clarification on why WikiMIA and MIMIR are the right datasets for evaluation, especially for recent models whose training data may not be fully covered (Reviewer 3). Also, the creation of paraphrased inputs for WikiMIA and verification of accuracy is not elaborated (Reviewer 3).
- The citation for autoregressive LLM maximum log-likelihood training (Wang et al. 2022) may be incorrect (Reviewer 3). Should also discuss findings from arxiv.org/pdf/2212.04037 (Reviewer 3).
- The focus on WikiMIA and LLaMA-13B for certain experiments (line 460) is unexplained; additional model/benchmark combinations would strengthen generalizability (Reviewer 3).
- Missing references to impactful 2024 works in related works: 'A Careful Examination of LLM Performance on Grade School Arithmetic' and 'Benchmarking Benchmark Leakage in LLMs' (Reviewer 4).
