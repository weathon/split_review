## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a hierarchical generative modeling approach for efficient neural architecture search (NAS) using zero-cost (ZC) proxies. The method consists of two levels: a conditional continuous normalizing flow (CCNF) for micro-cell design and a transformer-based sequence generator (fine-tuned GPT-Neo-125M) for macro-architecture generation. Task-agnostic pretraining leverages a metric space of graphs and their ZC similarity. The approach is evaluated on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet, with reported competitive performance relative to other low-cost NAS methods. Reviewers' scores range from 3 to 6, with generally positive assessments of motivation and clarity, but significant concerns about the claimed reduction of human prior, generality, experimental design, and reliance on ZC proxies.

### Strengths
- Well-motivated: The goal of reducing dependency on manually designed search spaces is a fundamental and practically important challenge for NAS (R1, R3, R4).
- Clear presentation: The paper is well-written, technically sound, and explains each step of the pipeline along with its limitations (R1, R2, R4).
- Novel hierarchical generative modeling: The two-level hierarchy (CCNF for cells, transformer for macro) is original and interesting for exploring large, expressive search spaces (R1).
- Effective use of zero-cost proxies: Using ZC proxies for task-agnostic pretraining avoids expensive supernet training and makes the search computationally feasible (R1, R2, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Reliance on zero-cost proxies (R1, R3, R4):** The method’s effectiveness is directly tied to the reliability and generalization of ZC proxies. Their performance varies across architectures and tasks, and the paper lacks a thorough analysis of sensitivity to the choice of proxy (e.g., ablation of different strong proxies) and diversity of tasks. One reviewer (R3) argues this reliance limits adaptability to novel domains where such proxies may not be valid.
- **Limited experimental scope and fairness (R1, R3, R4):** Comparisons with methods like OFA are on different search spaces, making it unclear whether gains come from the search space design or the NAS approach. Reviewers recommend comparisons with black-box NAS methods (e.g., regularized evolution, hierarchical NAS) on exactly the GraphNet space. Experiments are confined to standard benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet), which the community may have collectively overfit. One reviewer (R3) states that the experiment design is a poor fit for the paper’s stated goal; showing a practical case in a less-studied domain would strengthen the work. Another (R4) suggests testing on a different modality (e.g., NLP) and recommends reporting confidence intervals from multiple runs (3–5 random initializations).
- **Human prior still present (R2, R3):** Despite claiming to reduce dependency on manually designed search spaces, the method still relies on human prior: the search space is manually defined (Page 4), reference designs are used, clustering parameters and the choice of zero-cost metrics (e.g., FLOPs) are design decisions. One reviewer (R3) argues that this undermines the claimed improvement in applicability to less-studied domains.
- **Search cost accounting and efficiency (R1, R2):** The reported search cost does not include the pretraining cost of GPT-Neo-125M, which should ideally be added. One reviewer (R2) questions the method’s generality and time-consumption, as it requires pretraining, fine-tuning, and may be task- or search-space specific. Another (R4) asks why the evolutionary search for macro architectures is a major bottleneck despite using ZC proxies and whether it was parallelized.
- **Novelty concerns (R3):** One reviewer (R3) notes that the idea of using a hierarchical design to reduce the search space is not novel.
- **Missing comparisons and ablations (R1, R4):** Requested ablations include: the contribution of each phase (cell vs. macro search); different ZC proxy choices for macro generation; and insights into which architectural designs are most impactful. Also asked: extension to transformer-based search spaces (e.g., AutoFormer, HAT).
- **Technical clarification needed (R4):** How is the number of GMM components estimated? Why is the ES cost a bottleneck when using ZC proxies?
- **Reproducibility (R1, R4):** Code was not submitted; the authors are encouraged to release it to foster reproducibility.
