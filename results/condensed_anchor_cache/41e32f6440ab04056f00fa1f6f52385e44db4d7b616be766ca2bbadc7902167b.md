## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes control variate estimators to reduce the Monte Carlo variance when estimating the sliced Wasserstein distance. The control variates are constructed by fitting Gaussian approximations to the one-dimensional projections and using closed-form Wasserstein-2 distances or bounds thereof. Experiments on images, point clouds, gradient flows, and generative modeling show variance reduction compared to standard SW with the same computational cost. Reviewers found the approach original and practically relevant but raised several theoretical and comparative concerns.

### Strengths
- Novel and original approach bridging variance reduction and sliced Wasserstein estimation; addresses a previously unstudied aspect (the MC estimation side). (all reviewers)
- Construction of control variates via Gaussian approximations and closed-form Wasserstein-2 distance is highly non‑trivial and useful. (Reviewer 1 emphasized this more strongly; others also noted originality and practicality.)
- Lower and upper bounds on the expectation of the Wasserstein-2 distance between fitted Gaussians (Proposition 3) are valid. (Reviewer 1)
- Proposed estimators have the same computational complexity as standard SW (both time and memory). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- Extensions to other SW variants and gradient computation are noted and interesting. (Reviewer 1)
- Extensive experiments on three applications (images, point‑cloud gradient flows, generative modeling) convincingly demonstrate practical variance reduction. (all reviewers)
- Code is provided and experimental protocols are well explained, ensuring reproducibility. (Reviewer 3)
- Paper is generally well presented and easy to follow. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4; Reviewer 1 noted minor writing issues, see below.)

### Weaknesses
- **KL divergence in Proposition 1**: Minimizing KL between a discrete distribution and a Gaussian is not well‑defined (discrete vs. continuous). The authors should revise, possibly by using moment‑matching instead. (Reviewer 2)
- **Justification of upper/lower bounds and the role of γ**: The design of the bounds and their correlation properties is insufficiently justified. It is unclear why the expectation of σ₁σ₂ cannot simply be estimated by MC, and why the regression estimator with γ is preferable to a difference estimator (γ=1). (Reviewers 2 & 3)
- **Treatment of the final estimator**: The resulting estimator’s unbiasedness, variance (which is a further estimate of the control‑variate estimator), and the quality of the estimated optimal coefficient \(\hat{\gamma}^*\) are not properly discussed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Non‑negativity of \(Z(\theta,\mu,\nu)\)**: Whether this random variable can take negative values is not addressed; negative values could affect the variance‑reduction strategy and interpretation of results. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing comparisons with other SW variants**: The paper benchmarks only against standard SW, but methods like max‑sliced SW, distributional SW, and projected Wasserstein also improve sampling. Theoretical and experimental comparisons are needed. (Reviewer 4)
- **Convergence rate discussion**: The convergence rate of the control‑variate estimator relative to the Gaussian‑approximation SWD (Nadjahi et al., NeurIPS 2021) is not discussed. The known rate \(O(d^{-1/8})\) raises practical concerns in high dimensions. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing application to two‑sample testing**: One reviewer noted that SW can be used for non‑parametric two‑sample testing; the proposed variance reduction could benefit this task, but it is not explored. (Reviewer 1)
- **Writing and presentation issues**: (i) Section 3.1, paragraph 2 (motivation for control variates) is poorly written and should follow the exposition of Shapiro et al. (2021). (ii) Section 3.2 should omit the detailed derivation of the closed‑form Gaussian Wasserstein‑2 distance, presenting only the final expression. (iii) The description for computing SW between continuous distributions (below Proposition 1) is brief and confusing; it should be moved to an appendix. (Reviewer 1)
- **Minor typos and undefined notation**: Extra \(F_\nu\) (p. 2); extra term \(\gamma^2\text{Var}[C(\theta)]\) in \(f'(\gamma)\) (p. 4); missing space after “i.e.” (p. 5); sign error in derivative of \(f(m_1,\sigma^2)\) (p. 12); undefined notation \([[\cdot]]\) (p. 13); \(W(\theta)\) not defined after eq. (7) (Reviewer 4). (Reviewers 3 & 4)

*Contrast note*: Reviewer 1 was markedly more positive, praising the non‑trivial construction and the validity of the bounds, whereas other reviewers questioned the theoretical grounding (KL, role of γ, missing comparisons) and called for more thorough justification.
