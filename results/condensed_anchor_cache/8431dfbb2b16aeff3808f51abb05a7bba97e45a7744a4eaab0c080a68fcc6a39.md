## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents SOLD, a model-based RL method that learns object-centric latent dynamics from pixels by extending Slot Attention (SAVi) to a control setting. It uses a slot-aggregated transformer with register tokens and ALiBi for dynamics and behavior, building on DreamerV3. Evaluations are done on a custom multi-object Fetch-based suite (reach, push, pick-and-place with color variants), DM-Control (4 tasks), and Meta-World (8 tasks). The authors report performance gains over DreamerV3 and provide visualizations of slot decomposition. The paper received mixed reviews: two reviewers rated 5 (positive) and two rated 3 (negative). The positive reviewers find the idea sound and the results promising; the negative reviewers raise major concerns about limited evaluation, missing analyses, and unclear contribution.

### Strengths
- The core idea of learning object-centric latent dynamics for model-based RL is sound and well-motivated, and extending Slot Attention to a control setting is a natural step (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The paper is well-written and organized, with clear equations, system diagrams, and informative visualizations of slot attention patterns and object reconstructions that demonstrate how the model identifies moving parts (R1, R2, R4).
- A custom multi-object task suite (Gym Fetch-based) is designed to isolate the benefits of object-centric reasoning; on these tasks SOLD outperforms DreamerV3 (R1). Performance gains over DreamerV3 are also reported on DM-Control and Meta-World across 12 simulation tasks (R4).
- The method shows promising adaptation to unseen state distributions and provides structured representations that could aid relational reasoning and manipulation (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation scope and missing baselines**: The method is tested predominantly on a custom environment with simple geometric shapes, which is detached from realistic clutter and texture (R1, R4). Systematic RL comparisons on Meta-World and DM-Control are absent (R1), and broader benchmarks (Atari, ProcGen, Minecraft, ManiSkill2, IsaacGym, Robosuite) are needed to assess versatility (R2, R4). Tasks requiring articulation skills (open, close, pull, push) are not included (R3). Competitive baselines like TD-MPC and TD-MPC2 are missing (R3).
- **Unclear contribution and insufficient ablation**: R1 considers the contribution limited (mainly adding actions to OCVP and placing it in an RL loop) unless accompanied by more systematic studies or hardware experiments. The paper does not ablate the slot attention module (e.g., replacing it with another transformer-based model) or test alternative aggregation methods (R2). The difference between “Ours w/o OCE” and DreamerV3 is not clearly explained; multiple tricks (register tokens, ALiBi, EMA, spatial bins, imagination) are introduced, but it is unclear which are already present in DreamerV3, raising fairness concerns (R4, high confidence). The effect of removing the pre-training stage of the world model is not studied (R4). The choice of slot dimension is not justified; visualizations suggest few slots suffice (R2).
- **Lack of world model quality and efficiency analysis**: No quantitative comparison is provided for computational cost (inference latency) or prediction accuracy (prediction error, reconstruction loss) against DreamerV3 (R1, R4). R4, with high confidence, notes that it is unclear whether the slot attention and transformer increase rollout time despite any sample efficiency gains. Inference latency is not reported (R1).
- **No analysis of how object-centric representations benefit RL**: There is no insight into whether the structured latent space improves the accuracy of value estimation or policy learning (R4). Sample efficiency in Meta-World and DM-Control is not reported (R1). Learning curves and detailed return scores are missing (R2, R3). Whether fine-tuning SAVi (as done in SOLD) actually benefits RL training is not demonstrated (R3).
- **Object discovery concerns**: Figure 6 shows that objects are not discovered until the manipulator interacts with them (around t=3), suggesting a possible chicken-and-egg problem (R4). It is unclear whether DreamerV3 or a CNN-based world model could already discover target objects (e.g., via Grad-CAM) (R4). The influence of action and reward labels on the quality of SAVi representations is not examined (R4).
- **No discussion of robustness or multi-camera input**: The paper lacks analysis under scene disturbances (background changes, object textures, variation in number of objects) and does not address multi-camera input (R1, R4). The ability to handle complex geometries, textures, lighting, and many interacting objects is not tested (R4).
- **No real-world or hardware experiments** to demonstrate practical applicability (R1).
