## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes RainbowPO, a unified framework that extracts and combines seven key components from over ten direct preference optimization (DPO) variants. The authors analyze existing xPO methods, identify mathematically orthogonal components (length normalization, link function, home advantage, mixing reference policy, rejection sampling, contextual scaling, SFT loss), and show through experiments that length normalization, contextual scaling, and rejection sampling contribute most to performance gains. RainbowPO, which integrates these components with a novel mixing reference policy, outperforms existing DPO variants on AlpacaEval. The work aims to provide a structured evaluation pipeline and insights for practitioners.

### Strengths
- Provides a systematic and comprehensive meta-analysis of DPO variants, offering the first side-by-side evaluation under the same setting. This structured framework is valuable given the large and growing number of xPO methods.
- Thorough experimental ablation studies examine the contribution of each component individually, yielding concrete findings (length normalization, policy mixing, contextual scaling, rejection sampling improve performance) and solid empirical evidence.
- The proposed RainbowPO unifies effective components into a single objective, achieving superior performance over baselines; the mixed reference policy mechanism is a notable novelty.
- The paper promises publicly available implementation, which would be a significant contribution to the community.
- The study offers clarity and insights that help researchers and practitioners understand which components matter and how to combine them.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation scope**: The experimental benchmark is restricted to AlpacaEval, which may not provide sufficient evidence to validate findings or generalizability to other tasks. The paper assumes background knowledge about AlpacaEval without clear explanation.
- **Lack of systematic/theoretical justification**: The advantages of key components and the necessity/sufficiency of the seven identified components are explained only empirically. There is no deeper analysis of interactions between components or formal reasoning for their effectiveness. The identification process lacks transparency (e.g., Are these components necessary and sufficient? Could there be interactions?).
- **Writing and presentation issues**:  
  - The paper is poorly written with missing explanations, logical gaps, and inconsistent notation (e.g., y_l vs. y^l, π_α vs. π_ref^α).  
  - Acronyms (IPO, KTO, LC) are never defined; "warm-up adjustment" is introduced without prior explanation.  
  - The organization forces frequent forward references to experimental results, making the background and method sections hard to follow.  
  - Table 4 uses counterintuitive color coding (red for better, green for worse).  
- **Missing experimental details and ablations**:  
  - The base model used for win rate evaluation is not specified.  
  - Results are reported only for 3 epochs; it is unknown whether they would change with longer training.  
  - No explanation is given for why rejection sampling does not perform well with length normalization.  
  - "LC WR" in Table 4 is not defined in the caption or footnote.  
- **Missing comparisons and scope limitations**:  
  - Several relevant DPO variants are not considered (e.g., Robust DPO, approaches that remove the reward model entirely, chain-of-thought policy learning).  
  - The paper only measures win rate; metrics like BERTScore are not reported.  
- **Increased complexity**: RainbowPO introduces significant additional complexity compared to baseline methods, raising questions about practicality and scalability—the benefits may not justify the complexity.
- **Unclear methodology details**:  
  - How to obtain the policy π_γ in practice (Equation 1) is not explained.  
  - The purpose of raising Equation 12 (a lower bound) and whether minimizing it has any implication is unclear.  
  - The use of link functions and how the "good" policy π_γ is obtained for mixing are not elaborated.
