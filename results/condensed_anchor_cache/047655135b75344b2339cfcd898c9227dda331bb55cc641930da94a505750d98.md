## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates the effectiveness of synthetic training data for improving visual grounding in vision-and-language models. It proposes SynGround, a pipeline that uses LLMs, object detectors, and image generation models to generate synthetic image-text-box triplets. Experiments on ALBEF and BLIP show improvements in pointing game accuracy on RefCOCO+ and Flickr30k, and comparisons are made with real and web-crawled data. One reviewer (score 5, confidence 4) found the analysis thorough; three others (score 3, confidence 3) found baselines weak, gains limited, and missing comparisons.

### Strengths
- Systematic exploration: The paper thoroughly experiments with different strategies for generating synthetic image-text pairs and image-text-box triplets, covering varying degrees of reliance on real data and providing comprehensive ablations that highlight the importance of each pipeline component.
- Pipeline design: SynGround offers a structured, multi-component pipeline (LLM captioning, object detection, image generation) that is clearly defined and can be replicated.
- Empirical improvement: SynGround improves pointing game accuracy of ALBEF by 4.81% and BLIP by 17.11% absolute percentage points on RefCOCO+ and Flickr30k baselines, showing that synthetic data can boost localization.
- Outperformance over web-crawled data: Synthetic data (SynGround) outperforms web-crawled data in the comparisons, indicating the potential for more tailored and effective training datasets.

### Weaknesses
- **Weak baselines and use of older models**: All reviewers note that ALBEF and BLIP are older, smaller models (pre-2022). The paper does not evaluate SynGround on more recent state-of-the-art models (e.g., OFA, LLaVA, Phi3.5, or models from the PapersWithCode leaderboard for RefCOCO). One reviewer (score 3) specifically asks: *“What about applying the method to a more recent model (such as OFA)? One concern is that ALBEF is a much smaller model (BERT based LM), while the pipeline used to generate synthetic data leverages larger and more capable models such as LLaVA. I would be more convinced if the authors can apply their approach to improve a similar sized model.”* Another reviewer (score 5) notes: *“Could you extend the method to more recent models such as LLaVA, Phi3.5, etc so that it is more likely to be a general conclusion?”* The minority reviewer (score 5) is open to raising score if addressed, but the majority view is that the weak baselines undermine the generalizability of the findings.
- **Limited and marginal performance gains**: Multiple reviewers (scores 3) report that the absolute improvements are modest. Table 1 shows an average 0.36 point improvement over the baseline, and fine-tuning on existing real data gives 0.96 improvement—better than SynGround. Table 3 shows synthetic data combined with real data yields only marginal gains. Figure 5 shows improvement within error bounds, suggesting scaling may not be beneficial. One reviewer (score 3) states: *“Even with the synthetic data, the relative improvements are worse than using real data (Table 2) and only marginally better when combined with real data (Table 3).”* Another reviewer (score 3) adds: *“The gains over the baselines are not really substantial enough at the moment to warrant running this (rather convoluted) synthetic generation pipeline.”* A fourth reviewer (score 3) requests error bars or statistical significance tests.
- **Missing comparisons with existing synthetic/semi-synthetic data and SOTA baselines**: The paper omits comparisons to GRIT (from Ferret, a ground-and-refer instruction-tuning dataset), Shrika, and Ferret directly on RefCOCO+ and Flickr30k. One reviewer (score 3) notes: *“It's not clear how SynGround is differing from previous synthetic visual grounding data, and how it surpasses previous data generation approaches.”* Another reviewer (score 3) mentions: *“The main tables lack important SOTA baselines, for example, Shrika and Ferret on RefCOCO+ and Flickr, which are a lot better than the model fine-tuned on SynGround on RefCOCO+, and similar on Flickr.”* A different reviewer (score 3) asks: *“Another common semi-synthetic pipeline is to re-caption images (e.g., see Sec 7.1.1 of The Llama 3 Herd of Models). How would such a recaptioning approach fare on the CC experiments in Sec. 3.9?”*
- **Clarity and organization**: Two reviewers (score 3) found the paper difficult to read and confusingly structured. One suggests: *“Figure 1 could be replaced with an overview of the full SynGround pipeline, including captioning, bounding box generation, image generation components, as well as the training objectives detailed in Sec 3.1.”* Another requests: *“Consolidating related experiments (like the BLIP experiments) into fewer tables would enhance readability. The paper would benefit from focusing on the key findings, such as the comparison with web-crawled data, earlier in the presentation.”* The motivation and reasoning behind each experiment should be more clearly articulated.
- **Lack of scaling analysis and limitations**: One reviewer (score 3) notes: *“In Figure 5, the improvement with introducing synthetic data also seems marginal, and within the error bounds of using less data (which does not bode well for scaling).”* Another (score 3) states: *“While the paper mentions the potential for infinite data generation, it does not discuss or analyze potential limitations or saturation points in scaling up the use of synthetic data.”*
- **Additional unanswered questions** (overlapping with criticisms): *“The selection of base vision and language models. Why not applied to more recent SOTAs? Is SynGround data benefiting recent SOTAs in visual grounding also?”* *“How does SynGround compare with existing visual grounding data collected from public source with bounding boxes synthesized as well?”* *“There are several versions of Llava (1, 1.5, 1.6) as well as different model sizes (7B, 13B, 34B). Which one is being used in this paper?”* *“Could you elaborate on the computational resources required for generating and utilizing the synthetic data, especially in the context of scaling up to larger datasets?”* *“Could you discuss the potential impact of biases present in the source data (e.g., caption descriptions) on the generated synthetic data and downstream visual grounding performance?”*
