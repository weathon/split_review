## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CircuitFusion, a multimodal and implementation-aware circuit encoder that fuses hardware code, structural graph, and functionality summary into general representations for downstream digital IC design tasks. Pre-training uses register-based circuit splitting, three self-supervised tasks (masked modeling, contrastive learning, mixup summary matching), and implementation-aware alignment. A retrieval-augmented inference method is introduced for fine-tuning and zero-shot. Evaluations on five tasks show consistent outperformance over task-specific SOTA methods. Reviewers rated the paper 6,6,6,8 (one reviewer markedly more positive). The paper is generally well-structured and novel in its multimodal fusion for circuit representation, but several concerns about technical novelty, evaluation fairness, dataset size, downstream applicability, and code readability were raised.

### Strengths
* Novel first multimodal fused and implementation-aware circuit encoder using three modalities (HDL code, structural graph, functionality summary) for agile digital IC design, distinguishing it from prior LLM-based or graph-only methods. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
* Clear elaboration of encoding and fusion techniques, with illustrated methodology making the paper easy to follow. (R1, R2)
* Comprehensive evaluation across multiple tasks (five) compared with many SOTA methods; ablation studies validate the necessity of each modality and the proposed components (register splitting, retrieval-augmented inference, pre-training tasks). (R1, R2, R4)
* The approach explicitly overcomes unique hardware properties (P1–P4: parallel execution, functional equivalent transformation, multiple design stages, circuit reusability). (R2)
* Register-based circuit splitting enables fine-grained encoding with low computational complexity and scalability. (R4)
* Retrieval-augmented inference effectively integrates external knowledge, improving zero-shot and few-shot learning. (R4)
* Capable of both few-shot and zero-shot inference, demonstrating generalizability. (R3)
* One reviewer (score 8) particularly highlighted the pre-training techniques (masked modeling, contrastive learning, mixup summary matching) and sufficient experimental validation. (R4)

### Weaknesses
* **Technical novelty questioned**: A related work, RTLRewriter (ICCAD 2024), employs multimodal fusion, graph partitioning, and retrieval-augmented inference with similar techniques, reducing the perceived novelty. (R4)
* **Unfair comparisons**: Reported results are not based on the same number of model parameters across baselines. (R1)
* **Limited dataset**: Only 41 RTL designs are used; the dataset may not represent the full design space. Effectiveness on a wider range is not demonstrated. (R2)
* **Summary-centric fusion lacks justification**: No quantitative support is provided for why summary-centric fusion is chosen over alternatives; it may underrepresent structural/code details important for timing, power, and area. More experiments or comparisons with other modality-centric fusion (e.g., graph-centric or code-centric) are needed. (R1, R4; also R4 Q3)
* **Zero-shot inference reliability**: Simply averaging performance metrics of retrieved circuits is unreliable; Table 3 shows prediction error increases with more retrievals. Building a VectorStore with quality metrics also contradicts the concept of zero-shot. (R4; R4 Q2)
* **Pre-training details missing**: How the netlist encoder is pre-trained is not explained. (R1)
* **Questions about labeling and timing**: It is unclear how data are labeled for fine-tuning (R1) and how long pre-training and fine-tuning take (R1). How are correlation coefficient (R) and MAPE in Table 2 obtained—single or multiple inferences? (R1)
* **Downstream application vague**: The paper does not adequately explain how to adjust RTL design based on performance predictions, weakening the connection to “agile chip design.” Prior works (e.g., RTLRewriter) use encoded RTL for code reconstruction or fine-grained synthesis optimization. (R4)
* **Selection of representative netlists unclear**: One RTL can be synthesized into many gate-level netlists with different configurations; the selection process to represent true performance and guide optimization is not described. (R4 Q1)
* **Multiple clock handling**: The method’s ability to handle sequential logic with multiple clocks is not addressed. (R2)
* **Purpose mismatch**: Most multimodal encoders (e.g., CLIP) are used as controllers for generation, not as evaluators; applying them as evaluators may not fit the intended purpose. (R3) The authors are asked to provide examples of multimodal models successfully used for evaluation in hardware/software domains and to elaborate advantages over unimodal methods. (R3 Q)
* **Scalability argument weak**: Deep learning scales naturally with data, but large datasets are unavailable due to IP issues; achieving excellent results with limited data is more important than claiming scalability to large nonexistent datasets. (R3)
* **Source code lacks readability**: Uploaded code is hard to run; files mentioned in README (data_bench, pretrain_model, pos, etc.) do not exist. (R4)
