## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MODEM, a conditional diffusion model for unsupervised anomaly detection in non-stationary time series. It combines a coarse-to-fine diffusion paradigm with a frequency-enhanced decomposable network to leverage cross-resolution correlations. The model embeds multi-resolution information into the forward process and uses low-resolution recovery to guide high-resolution reverse trajectories. A frequency decomposition module extracts time-invariant and time-variant components. Experiments on five real-world datasets show state-of-the-art performance, and additional experiments on forecasting and imputation are provided.

### Strengths
- The paper is well written, mathematically sound, and clearly formulates subcomponents of the method. The exposition of research motivation and the analysis of differences between low- and high-resolution time series are clear. Figures and supplementary details are relevant and informative.
- To the best of the reviewers’ knowledge, this is the first work to introduce a multi-granularity mechanism into time series anomaly detection. The same applies to the contextualized forward and reverse conditional diffusion processes. The proposed method is novel in utilizing multiple resolution information to improve anomaly detection.
- The experimental evaluation is extensive and broad. MODEM consistently outperforms recent, relevant competitor methods on standard quality metrics (e.g., F1-score) across diverse real-world and non-stationary datasets. Additional experiments on multivariate forecasting and imputation are also conducted, though relatively weak.
- A series of ablation experiments test the effect of each important choice and show that MODEM outperforms reasonable alternatives. The ablation study includes varying the resolution parameter \(R\) (Figure 4).
- One reviewer rates the paper highly (score 8) and finds the method well-developed and the contributions strong, while the other three reviewers rate it 6, noting some concerns combined with overall positive assessment.

### Weaknesses
- **Hyperparameter tuning:** There is no objective method for setting parameters based solely on training data. The authors mention empirical tuning with Bayesian selection but provide no further details. An analysis of sensitivity to variations of individual parameter values (keeping others fixed) is missing, raising concerns about generalizability to other datasets.
- **Low resolution parameter:** The resolution parameter \(R\) is set to only 4 in experiments, which covers a narrow range of resolutions. This seems contrary to the claim that the method leverages correlations across various resolution scales. The ablation shows improvement for SMD when increasing \(R\) from 1 to 4; does this generalize to other datasets? For datasets with many short-lasting anomalies (spanning only a few time points), anomalies may be captured only in high-resolution data, making the utility of low-resolution data questionable.
- **Precision-recall trade-off:** MODEM achieves the best F1-score due to higher recall, but its precision values are comparatively lower than other methods (Table 3). This trade-off needs clarification for practical scenarios. The authors should explain this observation.
- **Computational cost:** There is no comparison of computational cost (e.g., number of parameters, GFLOPs) with existing models. Extending the diffusion model into a multi-resolution paradigm leads to an \(R\)-fold increase in sampling steps; furthermore, the FFT process and multi-resolution design raise concerns about training time. ADD is a metric for responsiveness, not computational efficiency. Efficiency comparisons (diffusion and non-diffusion methods) are of great interest, especially for real-time anomaly detection.
- **Novelty concerns:** One reviewer notes that the novelty is modest overall: the main body stacks existing works (framework ← CONTEXTDIFF; Top-K iFFT ← ETSFormer; network ← ModernTCN and D³R; multi-resolution ← MG-TSD), though applying algorithms from different tasks to a new task (anomaly detection) is a valid contribution. Another reviewer (score 8) considers the novelty strong.
- **Missing citations and attribution:** The content of Section 3.3 with theoretical derivation is very similar to CONTEXTDIFF [1]; the difference is just “coarse-resolution sequence ← text (condition)” and “high-resolution sequence ← image (target)”. The citation [1] appears only once in an inconspicuous place and is completely omitted in the theoretical results in Appendix A. The authors should emphasize contributions of prior work when necessary.
- **Missing related work:** Important related works are not discussed, including density-based methods (e.g., [1,2]) and other diffusion-based approaches such as conditional diffusion models [1] and diffusion models for time series [5,6].
- **Clarification needed on multi-resolution benefit:** The statement that multi-resolution facilitates learning of non-stationary time series (Section 3.2) needs further explanation of how this facilitation occurs.
- **Code not provided:** No code is available, making proper assessment of experimental results and method difficult.
- **Details on specific components:** More details on the pooling operation in the MRD sampler are needed. For TSDiff (used as a baseline?), the authors should give more details of modifications for univariate time series generation.
- **Figure 1:** Is the time series in Figure 1 real-world data? If so, which domain does it belong to, and do similar challenges appear across other domains?
