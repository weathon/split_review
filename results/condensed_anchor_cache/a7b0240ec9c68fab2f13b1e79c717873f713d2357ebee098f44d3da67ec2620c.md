## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes MR-SDS, a switching nonlinear state-space model for multi-region neural dynamics. It extends existing linear switching models (e.g., MR-SLDS) by using multiple nonlinear dynamical systems per region, with a Transformer-based amortized inference. The model includes directed interactions between regions and accounts for sensory inputs. Validation on three simulations and application to two real multi-region datasets (mesoscope and widefield calcium imaging) show improved held-out and co-smoothing performance over baselines. Reviewers generally appreciate the technical contribution, clear mathematical exposition, and simulation experiments, but raise significant concerns about interpretability, hyperparameter sensitivity, insufficient baseline comparisons, clarity of figures and text, and limited insight from real-data applications. One reviewer is more critical (score 5) citing numerical issues, limited complexity of simulations, and lack of clear benefits; the other four gave scores of 6.

### Strengths

- **Technical novelty:** extends existing linear switching multi-region models (MR-SLDS) to nonlinear dynamics, using multiple nonlinear DS per region with a Transformer encoder for inference. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Clear mathematical exposition and intuitive schematic** (Figure 1) for the generative process. (R3, R5)
- **Three simulation experiments** demonstrate recovery of latent trajectories, vector fields, and cross-region communication, even with model mismatch (multi-region RNN). (R2)
- **Outperforms existing methods** (LDS, SLDS, rSLDS, PCA) on real datasets (mesoscope, widefield) in held-out and co-smoothing tests. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Useful addition of quantifying messages** between brain regions. (R3)
- **Captures nonlinear evolution** of latent process and switching dynamics. (R1, R5)
- Generally well-written introductory and method sections. (R2)

### Weaknesses

- **Interpretability not demonstrated:** MR-SDS uses a Transformer, which may be even less interpretable than simpler models; the paper claims interpretability but does not show it, and cross-region communication recovery is inaccurate (e.g., Figure 2D). (R1)
- **Hyperparameter selection not robustly addressed:** number of switching states and latent dimensions are critical; simulations used ground-truth number of states; no sensitivity analysis to incorrect numbers; real data lacks ground truth, risking over- or under-fitting and different interpretations. (R2, R3, R5)
- **Real data applications provide limited neuroscientific insight:** flow fields appear overly simplistic (e.g., Figure 3B shows only linear 1D flows); interpretation of inferred dynamics is unclear; plots lack explanation (e.g., main occupied states, trajectory highlights, axis labels missing in Figures 3D, 4B/C). (R2, R5)
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons in key experiments:** Section 4.2 (multi-region decision-making) lacks comparisons with LDS, SLDS, rSLDS; Section 4.3 lacks visualization to support claims; comparisons on real data only evaluate prediction/reconstruction, not whether MR-SDS recovers more meaningful latent dynamics (e.g., nonlinearity that rSLDS misses). (R4, R5)
- **Table 1 reports no standard deviation/variance**, making it difficult to assess significance of performance differences. (R4, R5)
- **Numerous textual errors, figure mismatches, and formatting issues:** leftover note “MAKE SURE to say here that we also include in appendix A1…” (Section 4.1, last sentence); wrong figure references (page 6 line 2 should be Figure 2c, not 2a; page 8 “Figure 5 shows a macro…” should be Figure 3); missing legends (Figure 2B, Figure 3D); unresolved terms (e.g., “rho” in Figure 2; “accum”, “msgs”); low-resolution Figure 5B; missing panel label (Figure 5C); inconsistent figure formatting; text too small. (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5)
- **Simulation experiment 4.1 uses forward Euler integration**, leading to significant numerical instability and unrealistic trajectories (Figure 2B); only one state switch allowed within a narrow window (40–60), limiting complexity and generalizability. (R5)
- **Assumption that cross-region communication occurs in latent space** is not justified; brain communicates via spikes (observational level). (R2)
- **Lack of guidance on choosing hyperparameters:** why is using fewer latent dimensions (e.g., MR-SDS 3 vs mr-rSLDS 10) beneficial? Computational load or biological plausibility? (R2, R3)
- **No comparison of SLDS/rSLDS on the Lotka-Volterra simulation** to test whether linear models can capture similar nonlinear dynamics despite linearity. (R4, R5)
- **Choice of Transformer over simpler RNNs for inference is not justified**; could be computationally heavy without clear benefit. (R4)
- **Discussion is weak**, consisting of a series of unjustified statements. (R3)
- **Model cannot discern the number of interacting brain regions**; regions are pre-specified. (R3)
- **A minority reviewer (score 5) finds overall benefits not clearly demonstrated**, citing poor numerical methods, insufficient validation, and no significant advantage in prediction or latent estimation; other four reviewers are more positive but still note substantial weaknesses. (R5 contrast)
