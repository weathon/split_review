## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper studies the convergence of gradient descent (GD) for overparameterized two-layer linear neural networks trained with a generic loss function, relaxing assumptions on infinitesimal step size, infinite width, and specialized initialization. The analysis relies on local versions of the Polyak–Łojasiewicz (PL) condition and the Descent Lemma, where global constants are replaced by iterate-dependent versions. By bounding these local constants using initialization, current loss, and properties of the non-overparameterized model, the paper derives a linear convergence rate and proposes an adaptive step-size scheme. The work builds directly on Xu et al. (2023), which established linear convergence for the quadratic loss using constants \(c_1, c_2\) to bound singular values along the GD trajectory.

### Strengths

- **Rigorous analysis of convergence conditions**: The paper thoroughly studies convergence under relaxed assumptions on step size, width, and initialization, providing a detailed theoretical understanding (R2, R3).
- **Adaptive step-size scheme**: Based on the derived convergence analysis, the paper proposes an adaptive step size that accelerates convergence compared to a fixed step size, and it shows numerically that the step size can even increase during training (R1, R2, R4).
- **Clearly written and well-structured**: The paper is well-organized and presents its ideas in a clear manner (R1, R4).
- **First work (apart from Xu et al. 2023) to study this setting**: It considers finite step size, finite width, and “general” initialization (still requiring imbalance) without relying on infinitesimal step size, infinite width, or special initialization (R3).
- **Cleans up and offers improved analysis** of the prior result (R1).
- **Interesting theoretical result**: The analysis is sound, useful, and includes a good discussion (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty and incremental contribution**: Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R3, R4) independently noted that the contribution is incremental relative to prior work, especially Xu et al. (2023) and Arora et al.  
  – The convergence rate in Theorem 3.2 is qualitatively similar to that in [1] (Xu et al.), depending on margin and imbalance parameters (R2).  
  – The proof structure is extremely similar to Xu et al.; the novelty feels thin-sliced and would benefit from a clear statement of what is new in the proofs (R3).  
  – The prior work [1] already contains an adaptive step-size scheme, but the paper presents it as having only fixed step sizes. A detailed comparison of the two adaptive schemes and an explanation of how the current method overcomes the limitations of [1]’s reliance on constants \(c_1, c_2\) is needed (R1).  
  – The paper should clarify whether Lemma 3.1 is the central novel contribution and how it enables a wider choice of step sizes compared to [1] (R1, R3).  
  – Many results applying the PL inequality to neural networks already exist (e.g., Nguyen & Mondelli, 2020), which is not cited (R4).  
  – The significance of refining an already proven linear convergence rate (e.g., with adaptive step sizes) is not sufficiently justified; the paper should discuss practical implications or theoretical insights beyond a faster linear rate (R1).

- **Dependence on initialization through \(\alpha_1 > 0\)**: The requirement \(\alpha_1 > 0\) in Theorem 3.2 is not thoroughly discussed and may exclude some initializations. It is not clear how often this condition holds in practice (e.g., for standard Glorot & Bengio initialization), and it does not appear to be a purely technical requirement (R2, R3, R4). The analysis also still requires imbalance in initialization (R3). Related questions: what is the probability that \(\alpha_1 > 0\) fails? How could one estimate \(\eta_{\max}\) and how small can it be? (R4).

- **Limited generalizability to deep linear networks**: The paper does not discuss how to extend the analysis to networks with more than two layers. It is unclear how the local PL and smoothness constants would be bounded in the deep case or how the structure of \(\mathcal{T}\) would change (R2). This leaves a significant gap, and the authors do not address whether the analysis can be extended to deep nonlinear networks (R2).

- **No experimental validation for the main linear convergence result**: The paper contains no experiments that empirically validate the proven linear convergence rate. The numerical experiments focus on step-size choice, but the core theoretical claim is not demonstrated (R4).

- **Presentation issues**:  
  – Figure 1 (diagram of related work) is considered weird and inappropriate, e.g., placing NTK in the “finite step size” category (R4).  
  – Several typos and grammatical errors (listed by R3): “where Descent lemma is” should be “where the Descent Lemma is”; “for arbitrary non-convex functions that is” → “for an arbitrary non-convex function that is”; missing articles (“satisfies μ-PL condition” → “satisfies the μ-PL condition”); “via chain rule:” → “via the chain rule:”; “we show that as” → “we showed”; extra “the” in “if the \(\lim_{t \to \infty}\)”; “too larger” is incorrect; “but not too much \(\eta_t \leq 1/K_t\)” is poorly phrased (R3).  
  – Missing citations: Karimi et al. (2016) for the PL condition revival, and Nguyen & Mondelli (2020) for PL results in neural networks (R3, R4).

- **Reviewer disagreement**: Reviewers were split on the paper’s overall contribution. Two reviewers (R3, R4) rated the paper 6, finding the theoretical result interesting and sound, while two others (R1, R2) gave lower scores (5 and 3), citing limited novelty and significance.
