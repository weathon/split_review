## Merged Review

### Summary

LaVIT unifies vision and language pretraining by converting images into discrete tokens via a learned visual codebook with dynamic token length, then training an LLM with a unified autoregressive objective. The paper claims state-of-the-art results on multimodal understanding and generation tasks, but while some reviewers found the approach effective and well-presented, others raised concerns about novelty, fairness of comparisons, insufficient ablation, and reliance on Stable Diffusion for generation.

### Strengths

- The proposed method achieves strong performance on several vision-language tasks (e.g., VQA, image captioning) and significantly outperforms previous baselines.  
- The ablation studies support the contribution of each component (codebook, token selector, token merger). The paper is well-organized and clearly describes the dynamic discrete visual tokenization approach.  
- The strategy of treating images and text under a unified generative learning paradigm is interesting and effective for improving the inefficiency of conventional vision-text fusion.  
- Converting images into discrete tokens with dynamic length and visualizing those tokens helps readers understand the semantic content carried by each token.  
- The method can generate high-quality images from text prompts, though this relies on Stable Diffusion.  
- The paper can be considered primary research for further improvement in this direction.

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty concerns (raised strongly by Reviewer 3):** The key ideas of end-to-end LLM training for vision-language have already been explored in Emu [MR1], and converting visual information into discrete tokens appears in prior work [MR2–MR5]. The decoder strategy using Stable Diffusion is also identical to Emu’s; a reference to that work and justification are needed.  
- **Unfair comparisons vs. baselines:** The reported gains over BLIP-2 may stem from using additional English text corpus and further fine-tuning the LLM, not necessarily from the proposed tokenization. A controlled experiment (e.g., training BLIP-2 with the same extra data and fine-tuning strategy) is required to isolate the method’s contribution.  
- **Generation dependence on Stable Diffusion:** It is unclear how much of the good generation results is due to the discrete labels from the model versus the strong Stable Diffusion decoder. The paper should decouple and verify the contribution of the learned tokens.  
- **Missing ablation experiments:**  
  - Compare text generation performance when using merged continuous embeddings vs. quantized visual tokens (to measure information loss from quantization).  
  - Show the performance difference with and without the token merger (to demonstrate its necessity and added complexity).  
  - In Table 3, using a larger fixed number of tokens yields worse results—explanation is needed.  
  - The ablation results differ across Tables 3, 6, and 7 without clear justification; authors should state the number of training steps, batch size, and hyperparameters per ablation for reproducibility.  
- **Questionable claim of high-level semantic encoding:** The visualization of codebook entries (Figure 5) appears to group patches by low-level patterns (color, texture) rather than semantics. Additional image-level visualizations (e.g., as in [4]) are needed to support the claim.  
- **Token merger and codebook concerns:** How much additional complexity does the merger introduce? Are different codebooks needed for different domains (animation, painting, natural landscape)? Why not use a simple attention map from a pre-trained model instead of the merger?  
- **Writing structure issues (noted by Reviewer 4):** The method section is confusing; the tokenizer learning stage is described in a way that suggests end-to-end training, but it is actually a two-stage process. This should be restructured for clarity.  
- **In-context learning not evaluated:** The paper claims the model can achieve in-context learning, but no experiments support this.  
- **Missing explanations:**  
  - Why does inequitable treatment of vision and language harm multimodal learning? (The claim is not justified.)  
  - For the causal attention used on image tokens, what is the ordering? Since images have no natural sequence, the use of causal attention is not convincing.  
  - Why are unselected tokens still used as keys and values (via the merger) rather than simply discarded, given they are claimed to be redundant?  
  - What is the function of the second half of Equation 4?  
  - More details about the language-vision pre-training stage are required.  
- **Computational cost and efficiency:** Training a visual codebook from scratch is computationally costly; the paper should investigate using existing pre-trained codebooks (e.g., VQ-VAE [1], BEIT [3]) for initialization or direct use, analyzing the trade-offs in cost and downstream performance. Additionally, what is the computational (speed) improvement during inference from using dynamic token length?  
- **Typos:**  
  - Figure 1(a) caption: “Adater” → “Adapter” (noted by both R1 and R2).  
  - Equation (2): the symbols for calculating K and V should be X_d (not X).  
  - Section 3: “the Large language model” → “the large language model”.  
  - Section 3.1 “Token Selector”: “the binary decision M” → “the binary decision mask M”.  
  - Equation 3: missing subscript “2” in the norm (should be \(\|\hat{x}_i - c_j\|_2\)).  
  - Section 4.4: “the token predictor” → “the token selector”.
