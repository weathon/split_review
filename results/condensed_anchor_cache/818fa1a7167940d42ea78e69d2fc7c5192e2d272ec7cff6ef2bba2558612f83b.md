## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes ProAdvPrompter, a two-stage adversarial prompting method to generate suffixes that jailbreak LLMs. The Exploration stage uses loss feedback to guide a prompter (based on AdvPrompter's search strategy with beam search) to generate harmful suffixes. The Exploitation stage iteratively fine-tunes the prompter using high-quality adversarial suffixes (with a filtering mechanism). The method achieves high attack success rates (ASR) on Llama2-Chat-7B and Llama3-Chat-8B on AdvBench, with low perplexity and reduced training time compared to baselines. Ablation studies evaluate key components. Two reviewers rated the paper highly (6,6), one moderately (5), and one lower (3), with primary disagreements on novelty and contribution depth.

### Strengths

- The method achieves very high attack success rates (99.68% on Llama2-Chat-7B, 97.12% on Llama3-Chat-8B after 10 trials on AdvBench) with low perplexity, showing strong effectiveness. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- The Exploration and Exploitation stages form an interesting and effective idea; the paper clearly demonstrates the principles and has well-motivated improvement strategies over prior work. [R3, R4]
- The paper is well-written and clearly presented, with a detailed algorithm description. [R2, R4]
- The selected baselines (e.g., AdvPrompter, AutoDan, GCG) are recent and relevant. [R2]
- The method reduces training time by 20% on Llama3-Instruct-8B and generates more generalized adversarial suffixes with resilience against perplexity defense. [R3, R4]
- The paper includes ablation studies evaluating key components (prompt template and filtering mechanism). [R3]

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty and contribution concerns**: The first Exploration stage is methodologically identical to AdvPrompter's search strategy (Paulus et al., 2024) with beam search; the second Exploitation stage, while more original, amounts to dataset generation followed by fine-tuning, which is a conventional approach. One reviewer found the overall contribution low, arguing it is essentially an application of ensemble learning. Another reviewer noted that the two-stage pipeline lacks significant technique contribution and there is no investigation of different fine-tuning methods (e.g., LoRA vs SFT). [R1, R2]
- **Missing intuition and ablation for Exploitation stage**: The paper does not explain why the Exploitation stage significantly improves ASR, nor does it provide an ablation study isolating its contribution. One reviewer explicitly requested this. [R1]
- **Incomplete experimental reporting**:  
    - Only one dataset (AdvBench) is used; additional datasets should be included. [R2]  
    - Table 2 (comparison with baselines) omits training and generation times, though these are claimed as evaluation metrics. [R2]  
    - Figure 4 only shows AdvPrompter's training time; training times for AutoDan and GCG are missing, while generation times are shown. [R2]  
    - The "template alone" baseline in Figure 2 is unexplained until late in the paper. [R1]  
    - Numerical results for AdvPrompter in Figure 2: the reported Mistral ASR (98.1%) does not align with the original AdvPrompter paper, while Vicuna-7B and Vicuna-13B values appear quoted. [R1]
- **Missing cross-model and cross-dataset transferability**: No experiments on closed/black-box LLMs (transfer-attacking), despite their practical importance. Transferability of attack prompts across datasets or target LLMs is not evaluated. [R3, R4]
- **Parameter sensitivity concerns**: The method has many adjustable parameters (gamma, k, T1, T2), and it is unclear whether the method is sensitive to them; exploring all settings requires significant effort. [R3]
- **Training cost and overfitting risk**: The method requires fine-tuning the adversarial prompter at each stage, leading to training cost concerns. The two-stage fine-tuning may cause overfitting on the training data, and it is unclear if the high ASR holds on safety questions from other domains. [R4]
- **Inconsistency in universality claim**: Line 122 specifies the discussion as universal, but the examples in Table 4 are not universal. The term "Adaptive to input" in Table 1 needs clarification. [R1]
- **Minor technical errors**: Line 135 hyperlink refers to Equation 2 but should be Equation 1. Algorithm 1 input has repetitive description of "the initial parameter theta_0". [R1]
- **Unaddressed questions on adversarial prompter**: It is unknown whether using an adversarial prompter without safety alignment would produce better results, and how much the quality of the adversarial prompter (prompt LLM) affects ASR (i.e., would a bad prompt LLM yield poor ASR). [R4]
- **Unclear rationale for time reduction**: One reviewer does not understand why using more methods (two stages) would take less time than one method. The paper's claim of 20% training time reduction requires clearer explanation. [R2]
- **Replication burden**: The pipeline appears lengthy with many steps, which may pose significant difficulties for replication. [R3]

*Note: One reviewer was substantially more critical overall (score 3), focusing on lack of innovation and missing experimental details, while the other three reviewers rated the paper higher (scores 5, 6, 6) but still raised significant concerns about novelty (R1), missing transfer experiments (R3), and training cost/overfitting (R4). The disagreements center on the significance of the two-stage contribution and the completeness of the evaluation.*
