## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Sampling One Then Accepting (STA-1), an unbiased watermark for large language models that is robust and does not require white-box access at detection, and an extension STA-M. It also addresses the trade-off between watermark strength and text quality, particularly in low-entropy scenarios, and provides theoretical guarantees. However, several reviewers find the contributions unclear, the experimental results not strongly supporting the claims, and the method showing no significant improvement over existing unbiased watermarks. Evaluation metrics and the necessity of the low-entropy scenario are also questioned.

### Strengths
1. **Clarity and originality** (Reviewer 1): The article is written clearly and logically. This is the first work to explore watermarking for order-agnostic LMs, which have practical significance in diverse real-world applications. *(Note: These comments appear to refer to a different method [PATTERN-MARK] and may be misaligned with the paper's actual focus on STA-1/STA-M.)*
2. **Theoretical analysis and guarantees** (Reviewer 2): The paper theoretically analyzes the problem of balancing text quality and detection performance in existing unbiased watermarks under low-entropy scenarios. It provides theoretical proofs of STA's unbiasedness and guarantees regarding Type II error.
3. **Novelty of STA-1 and STA-M** (Reviewer 3): The proposed STA-1 watermark is unbiased, robust, and requires no white-box model access during detection. STA-M extends this to enhance watermark strength with low text quality shifts.
4. **Interesting low-entropy setting and approach** (Reviewer 4): Exploring the low-entropy generation setting is interesting. Using the entropy of the token distribution to govern watermark embedding is a good idea (though existing methods like Kirchenbauer et al. already do this implicitly). Evaluating watermarked text quality through the risk of unsatisfying outputs is an interesting and natural fit for reasoning/QA tasks.

### Weaknesses
1. **Unclear contribution and narrative** (Reviewers 2, 4):  
   - The paper lacks clarity in distinguishing its main contributions, leading to a confusing narrative (Reviewer 2).  
   - Writing is problematic; for example, Hu et al. (see its appendix) already have a model-agnostic detection method for unbiased watermarks, so the first problem identified in the abstract (requiring white-box access) would be void (Reviewer 4).  
   - The low-entropy scenario example (e.g., model repeating prompt) is not common in real life; the rationale for adding watermarks in such cases is unsatisfying (Reviewer 4).

2. **Experimental results do not support the claims** (Reviewers 2, 3):  
   - The key claim that unbiased watermarks outperform traditional red-green watermarks in low-entropy scenarios is not supported. Tables 1 and 2 show no clear distinction in text quality between red-green and unbiased watermarks across high and low entropy (Reviewer 2).  
   - There is no significant difference in text quality between STA-1 and other unbiased methods on HumanEval and C4 datasets; this is expected as all are distortion-free (Reviewer 3).  
   - STA is not significantly superior to other unbiased watermarks (Reviewer 2).  
   - On the C4 dataset, STA-1 shows no improvement in detection performance over current unbiased baselines, and STA-M is outperformed by KGW in both text quality and watermark strength (Reviewer 3).  
   - AUC scores for HumanEval are too low, making suspect that true positive rates are very small, questioning the usefulness of all methods for low-entropy datasets (Reviewer 3).

3. **Inadequate evaluation and metrics** (Reviewers 2, 3):  
   - The evaluation of text quality is insufficient, especially in low-entropy scenarios, as it relies solely on code generation tasks (HumanEval) (Reviewer 2).  
   - AUC is not an ideal metric; direct results such as true positive rates at very low false positive rates (e.g., 0.1%, 0.01%, 0.001%) should be provided (Reviewer 3).  
   - The presentation of results is confusing, particularly with multiple best and second-best results highlighted (Reviewer 2).

4. **Missing comparisons and ablations** (Reviewers 1, 3, 4):  
   - The advantages of this method compared to traditional modification-based watermarking (generate content first, then embed) are not discussed (Reviewer 1).  
   - Extension to multi-bit watermarking, which is more applicable in real-world scenarios, is not addressed (Reviewer 1).  
   - Results for Dipmark at lower values of α (e.g., 0.1 and 0.2) in Table 3 are requested; Dipmark also involves a trade-off and, with lower α, may perform better than STA-1 while retaining unbiased property (Reviewer 3).  
   - The need to discuss very low entropy scenarios is questioned; is it rational to add watermarks when the model is prompted to repeat the prompt? (Reviewer 4).
