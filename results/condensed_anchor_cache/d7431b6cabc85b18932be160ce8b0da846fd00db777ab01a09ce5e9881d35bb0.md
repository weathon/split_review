## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DRAMA, a model-based reinforcement learning agent that uses the Mamba-2 state space model as the dynamics core of its world model, replacing RNNs or transformers. The authors argue that Mamba-2 provides linear memory/computation complexity while capturing long-term dependencies. They also introduce dynamic frequency-based sampling (DFS) to improve early-stage training. Evaluated on Atari100k, DRAMA achieves performance comparable to state-of-the-art methods (IRIS, TWM, DreamerV3) using only a 7 million parameter world model that can be trained on a laptop. Four reviewers rated the paper (scores: 6, 6, 8, 6), finding the idea novel and computationally efficient, but raising concerns about unclear attribution of performance gains, insufficient evidence for long-term dependency claims, missing ablations, presentation clarity, and limited comparisons (e.g., with R2I). One reviewer (score 8) was more positive overall but still identified key weaknesses.

### Strengths
- **Novel application of Mamba-2 to world models in MB-RL.** Reviewers agree that using SSMs (specifically Mamba-2) as the dynamics model is a creative and sensible way to overcome the limitations of RNNs (vanishing gradients, poor long-term dependencies) and transformers (quadratic complexity). (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **State-of-the-art performance with significantly fewer parameters (7M) on Atari100k** compared to baselines like DreamerV3. The lightweight architecture promises practical accessibility and efficiency; the abstract claims trainability on a standard laptop. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Inclusion of ablation studies** comparing Mamba-1 vs Mamba-2 and evaluating the effect of DFS, which strengthens the empirical claims. (R1; R2 notes questions about these ablations)
- **Proposal of DFS sampling** to mitigate early suboptimality from an imperfect world model; it is a simple yet potentially effective add-on. (R1, R3)
- **Practical impact for resource-constrained environments** – the small model size and efficient architecture could make MB-RL more accessible. (R1)

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear contribution of Mamba vs DFS.** The paper does not convincingly isolate how much of the performance stems from the Mamba architecture versus the DFS sampling method. Reviewers request ablations that replace Mamba with an RNN or transformer (while keeping DFS) or replace DFS with uniform sampling. (R1, R2, R3) One reviewer (R2) specifically asks to compare “Drama with uniform sampling” or “baselines with DFS” to separate the two factors.
- **Insufficient evidence for capturing long-term dependencies.** The paper repeatedly claims Mamba-2 handles long sequences well, but provides no direct experiments or metrics (e.g., tasks requiring long-term memory, effective memory length analysis) to support this. It remains unclear what “long” means in practice. (R1) Reviewer 1 questions if the authors have considered such tasks.
- **Limited comparison with scaled models and missing baselines.** The most prominent baseline DreamerV3 is much larger; the paper does not show how DRAMA performs when scaled up to match DreamerV3’s parameter count (even on a subset of games). If hardware constraints prevented this, they should be discussed. (R1) Additionally, the method R2I (Samsami et al., ICLR’24) also uses SSMs for world models and is not compared – one reviewer (R2) argues it should be included as a baseline.
- **Marginal performance gains across the full Atari100k suite.** DRAMA is comparable to SOTA but does not significantly outperform on many games. The paper would be strengthened by demonstrating scenarios where DRAMA clearly excels. (R1)
- **DFS method needs better explanation and ablation.** The sampling mechanism is not clearly contrasted against alternative sampling strategies, and its impact is not isolated in an ablation that covers more games (beyond the current results). (R1, R2, R3)
- **Missing hyperparameter sensitivity analysis.** The paper does not explore sensitivity to model size, sequence length, learning rates, or imagination context length. One reviewer (R1) finds this makes the work “relatively incomplete.” Reviewer 4 specifically questions why the imagination context length is fixed to 8 and requests experiments varying it (with time-complexity and performance analysis).
- **Paper clarity and notation issues.** Several reviewers (R2, R3, R4) note that the exposition is hard to follow in places. Specific problems:
  - Section 2.3 (behavior learning) is unclear: the sampling of “b starting points” from the replay buffer batch is not justified; why not simply sample b trajectories? (R2)
  - The variable $h_t$ appears in behavior policy learning but the deterministic hidden state is defined as $d_t$ in Equation 5. (R2)
  - Only the last hidden state of the imagination sequence is used for policy learning; motivation is missing (Dreamer uses the whole sequence). (R2)
  - Notation inconsistencies: terminal flag is $e_t$ in Equation 3 but $t_i$ in Figure 1; time index changes between $t$ and $i$ across sections. (R2)
  - The paragraph on lines 99–105 mixes general world model description with specific design choices, which could mislead readers about what is standard. (R3)
  - Minor typesetting errors: “Employs” appears twice (line 132); hat notation incorrect ($\hat{r_t}$ should be $\hat{r}_t$); line 218 variables not in math mode; “auto-generative” may be a typo for “autoregressive” (line 238); line 164 should read “tracks the number of *times*”; incorrect $A$ (lines 148,154,169) and $T$ (line 177). (R2, R3)
  - Figures have small text that is difficult to read without zooming. (R3)
- **Lack of wall-clock time measurements.** The abstract claims trainability on a laptop, but no exact training or inference times are provided. Specific runtime metrics would substantiate this claim. (R3)
- **Missing evaluation on additional benchmarks.** The paper only uses Atari100k. Reviewers (R3, R4) suggest including the DeepMind Control Suite (though R3 acknowledges this may be challenging) or providing qualitative examples (e.g., why DRAMA struggles on Breakout, with generated frames) to better understand the model’s behavior.
- **Potential reward underestimation by the behavior model** – reviewer 2 asks whether this was tested, especially with limited data.
- **Several design choices lack justification.** Reviewer 4 asks: What is the value of $h$? How exactly does the Mamba-based imagination process differ from the transformer-based one? One reviewer (R3) questions whether the decoder could instead use the Mamba output $d$ rather than $z$. These point to missing details in the methodology.
- **Minor presentation suggestion:** Highlight the highest scores in Table 1 for quick reference. (R3)
