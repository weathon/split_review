## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes TraPNet, a probabilistic model that aggregates the joint distribution of potential trajectories to predict city-scale road volume. It integrates current observations, historical data, and road network information using multi-view attention, and makes single-step predictions to reduce computational time. Experiments on Boston and Jinan road networks show strong performance against two baselines, especially with only 20% observations. However, the paper suffers from an unclear problem definition (trajectory recovery vs traffic volume prediction), insufficient baselines and metrics, lack of scalability and efficiency analysis, missing implementation details, and contradictions in the ablation study. Reviewer 1 (score 5) found the method reasonable and results good, while reviewers 2-4 (score 3 each) raised substantial concerns about clarity, comparisons, and evaluation.

### Strengths
- The micro-level probabilistic approach is innovative and intriguing, breaking down aggregated volume into individual vehicle movements (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
- The model demonstrates strong tolerance to missing data, maintaining advantage with only 20% observation ratio (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3 S2, abstract).
- The design balances performance and efficiency, with single-step prediction reducing computational time (Reviewer 2 S2, abstract).
- The method comprehensively uses current observations, historical data, and road network information (Reviewer 2 S2).
- Reviewer 1 states that the paper is easy to follow and the method is reasonable, with good experimental results against baselines.

### Weaknesses
- **Ambiguous problem definition**: The paper conflates trajectory interpolation/generation with traffic volume prediction. It is unclear whether the task is future volume prediction or trajectory recovery. The notation \(T\) for \(Y\) is not specified as past or future (Reviewer 3 W1). Baselines Cam-Traj-Rec and Traj2Traj address trajectory generation, not volume prediction (Reviewer 2 W3, Reviewer 3 W1). No comparison is made with any existing traffic flow prediction methods (Reviewer 4 W1).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**: Only two trajectory-related baselines are used. More recent trajectory generation methods (e.g., MobilityGPT, TS-TrajGen) should be included as they produce similar trajectory maps (Reviewer 2 W3). The method should be compared with state-of-the-art traffic flow or spatiotemporal graph prediction methods (Reviewer 4 W1). Reviewer 1 also notes the need for more recent baselines.
- **Insufficient evaluation metrics**: Only MAE is reported. RMSE and MAPE should be added for a comprehensive evaluation (Reviewer 3 W4). Since the method is probabilistic, evaluations should assess prediction uncertainty and confidence (Reviewer 4 W2, Reviewer 3 Q7). The current MAE-based evaluation does not validate the claimed uncertainty handling (Reviewer 4 W2).
- **Lack of computational efficiency and scalability analysis**: The model requires simulating each vehicle's appearance probability, raising concerns about computational cost (Reviewer 4 W4). No comparisons of training/inference time or memory usage are provided (Reviewer 3 Q5). The trajectory representation repeats nodes (e.g., node 2 four times to indicate four timesteps on edge (2,3)), causing substantial overhead and memory usage (Reviewer 3 W3). Performance on larger road networks (10k+ nodes) is not tested; the Boston dataset uses generated shortest-path data, which may not reflect real-world conditions (Reviewer 2 W4).
- **Unclear model details and justifications**: The transition from Equation 6 to Equation 7 in the loss function is not clearly explained (Reviewer 3 W5). The rationale for aggregating trajectory probabilities to estimate road volume is not theoretically or practically justified (Reviewer 3 Q4). The use of Multi-Query Attention for adjacency and observation tokens but Multi-Head Attention for historical data is not motivated (Reviewer 3 Q6). The calculation of adjacency tables and alignment is not clearly defined (Reviewer 4 W5).
- **Ablation study contradictions**: Multi-head attention (8) yields MAE 0.538 vs multi-query attention 0.667, and configuration (6) with BVLC embedding shape yields MAE 0.451 vs default 0.667. These results contradict the paper’s claim that the default setting “has little effect on performance” (Reviewer 3 W6).
- **Redundancy and clarity issues**: The related work section contains redundancy (e.g., first two paragraphs and Section 2.1) (Reviewer 2 W1). The description of how the method differs from checkpoint-based methods is confusing; checkpoint-based methods appear to be a subset of deep learning methods, and it is unclear why they are considered “more applicable to real-world scenarios” (Reviewer 2 W2, Reviewer 3 W2). The paper states challenges (inherent uncertainty, sparse observations) but does not clearly demonstrate how the proposed method addresses them (Reviewer 4 W2, W3). Reviewer 3 rates presentation 1, Reviewer 4 rates presentation 1.
- **No explicit uncertainty evaluation**: Despite being a probabilistic model, there are no illustrations or results showing confidence in trajectory predictions (Reviewer 3 Q7).

*Note: Reviewer 1 (score 5) found the paper easy to follow and the method sound, but the majority of reviewers identified substantial weaknesses in clarity, baselines, metrics, and evaluation.*
