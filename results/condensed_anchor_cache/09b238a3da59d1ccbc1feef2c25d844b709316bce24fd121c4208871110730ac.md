- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 5, 6, 8, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies variance-dependent regret bounds for contextual bandits with function approximation under only a realizability assumption on the mean reward function. The function class is assumed to have a finite eluder dimension. The authors propose two algorithms based on an uncertainty-filtered multi-scale least squares procedure. Algorithm 1 handles the case of an unknown but constant noise variance across time steps, achieving a regret bound that matches the linear bandit setting. Algorithm 2 addresses the more general setting where the noise variance can change over time, obtaining a regret bound scaling with the square root of the cumulative noise variance but with an additional factor of the eluder dimension (and a \(\sqrt{d}\) gap compared to the linear case). This work is the first to provide variance-aware regret guarantees for contextual bandits with general function approximation without requiring knowledge of the conditional variance.

### Strengths
- **First variance-aware regret bounds for general function approximation.** The paper is the first to develop algorithms with variance-dependent regret bounds for contextual bandits with function approximation under only a realizability assumption (Reviewers 2, 4). This improves upon prior work that often assumed stronger conditions on the measurement noise.
- **Novel algorithmic technique: uncertainty-filtered multi-scale least squares.** The method of using multiple layers of confidence sets, where each layer uses only data points with sufficiently small uncertainty, is novel and may be of independent interest (Reviewers 1, 4). The analysis of the variance and the truncated loss introduces new technical elements (Reviewer 1).
- **Regret bounds for the homogeneous (constant unknown variance) case are near-optimal.** When reduced to the linear function class, the regret bound for Algorithm 1 matches the known near-optimal variance-dependent bounds for linear bandits (Reviewers 3, 4). Specifically, the regret scales as \(\widetilde{O}\big(d_e \sqrt{\log(|\mathcal{F}|)\sum_t \sigma_t^2} + d_e \log(|\mathcal{F}|)\big)\) for eluder dimension \(d_e\).
- **Handles both known and unknown variance, and both constant and changing variance.** The paper provides two algorithms with distinct guarantees: one for uniform but unknown variance (Algorithm 1) and one for time-varying unknown variance (Algorithm 2). The latter is particularly practical as it adapts to the actual noise level at each round (Reviewer 2).
- **Proofs appear correct.** The technical derivations are rigorous and the claims are formally justified (Reviewer 3).
- **Practical relevance.** Solving for varying variance over time can yield tighter regret bounds in many real-world scenarios where the noise level fluctuates (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation is often difficult to follow.** Several reviewers found the paper poorly organized or lacking intuitive explanations (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Specific issues include:
  - Theorems and corollaries are presented without sufficient commentary on the meaning of key terms (e.g., \(\mathcal{G}_t'(\tau_i)\), \(\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_t'\)), and it is unclear whether the results assume \(\sigma_t=\sigma\) or not (Reviewer 1).
  - There is a mismatch between the text and the appendix: Line 264 references "Proposition 3.2" and states its proof is in Appendix C, but Appendix C describes an existing algorithm with a Hoeffding-type confidence set, not a Bernstein-type algorithm (Reviewer 4).
  - Some terms are used without definition: "event \(E\)" (Line 279) and "Algorithm 3" (Line 299) are not defined (Reviewer 4).
  - Typos: Line 163: \(\mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{X}\) should be \(\mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{A}\); Algorithm 1 line 5: \(f_t^{\tau}\) should be \(f_t^{\tau_i}\); Eq. (5): \(\widehat{\sigma}_t\) should be \(\widehat{\sigma}_t^2\) (Reviewer 1).
  - In contrast, one reviewer found the paper well-written (Reviewer 3), but the majority noted presentation problems.
- **No empirical evaluation.** The paper contains no experiments or simulations on synthetic or real-world data, leaving the practical behavior of the algorithms untested (Reviewer 2). This makes it hard to assess how the regret scales with eluder dimension, time horizon, or approximate realizability in practice.
- **Insufficient comparison with prior work.** The paper does not directly compare its regret bounds to existing results in the literature, making it difficult to assess the contribution (Reviewers 2, 4). In particular:
  - The claim (Line 171) that no second-order bounds for contextual bandits exist appears to contradict prior work (e.g., Zhao et al., 2022), which gave such bounds for the linear case (Reviewer 4, Question 3).
  - No explicit comparison is provided between the bounds of Algorithm 1 or Algorithm 2 and previous algorithms for general function approximation (e.g., Hoeffding-type bounds) (Reviewer 4).
  - The technical relationship to the work of Zhao et al. (2023) for linear bandits is not clearly explained; the paper could better highlight the new challenges and key differences when moving to general function classes (Reviewer 3).
- **Gap to linear bandit rates in the changing-variance setting.** For the case of time-varying noise variance, the regret bound of Algorithm 2 has an extra \(\sqrt{d}\) factor compared to the known optimal bound for linear bandits (Reviewers 3, 4). The authors do not discuss why this gap arises or whether it is fundamental. Reviewer 3 suggests highlighting the obstacle to removing this gap.
- **Practical concerns about algorithm efficiency.** The algorithm relies on computing the intersection of confidence sets and performing optimization over the intersected set. This may be intractable when the confidence sets are not convex (Reviewer 4). The paper does not address computational complexity.
- **Motivation for the changing-variance setting is unclear.** While the uniform variance setting has clear applications (e.g., linear mixture MDPs as in Zhao et al., 2023), the case where variance changes arbitrarily at each step seems artificial and lacks motivating examples (Reviewer 3).
- **Estimation of time-varying variance is not adequately justified.** The paper does not explain how it obtains sufficient observations to estimate \(\sigma_t^2\) when the variance is unknown and potentially changing at each step. With limited data, it is unclear how accurate estimates can be obtained, especially if \(\sigma_t\) can increase or decrease arbitrarily (Reviewer 1). This is a core challenge that the paper should address.
- **Missing discussion of weighting versus non-weighting approaches.** Previous second-order bounds for homogeneous variance often use weighted estimators (e.g., uncertainty weighting or variance weighting). The proposed algorithm uses no weighting; it would be interesting to explain how this is possible and whether it offers advantages (Reviewer 4, Question 1).
- **Minor questions and clarifications needed.**
  - Should \(\sigma_t\) be considered fixed or is it allowed to be a random variable that depends on the selected action? (Reviewer 4, Question 2)
  - Is the regret bound for the changed variance setting tight? (Reviewer 3, Question 1)
  - A brief discussion of related work on variance-aware bounds for dueling bandits (e.g., [3]) would be helpful (Reviewer 4, Question 4).
