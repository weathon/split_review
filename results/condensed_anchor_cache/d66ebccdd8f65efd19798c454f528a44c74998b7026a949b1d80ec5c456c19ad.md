## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes L-LoRA, a method that partially linearizes only the adapter modules (via first-order Taylor expansion) and applies task arithmetic over the linearized adapters for multi-task model fusion. The approach aims to improve weight disentanglement and enable more effective fusion compared to standard LoRA and prior full-linearization methods. Experiments are conducted on CLIP (vision-language) and Flan-T5 (language) models across multiple tasks.

### Strengths
1. **Clear motivation and writing.** The paper is well structured, motivations are clear, and results are (mostly) well presented (Reviewers 2, 4). Reviewer 4 notes the logical structure makes comprehension easy.

2. **Interesting and novel idea.** The idea of performing partial linearization on LoRA modules is interesting and nicely presented (Reviewer 2). The method is a new linearized LoRA variant (Reviewer 3). Code is provided (Reviewer 3).

3. **Comprehensive experiments covering both vision and language domains.** Experiments are conducted on both vision-language (CLIP) and language (Flan-T5) tasks (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Visualization in Figure 6 on disentanglement error substantiates efficacy (Reviewer 4). Results on CLIP-ViT-B-16 (Figure 5a) are a compelling argument for the central hypothesis (Reviewer 2).

4. **Significant performance improvement under multi-task fusion.** Compared with standard LoRA, the proposed method achieves significant performance gains, especially as the number of tasks increases (Reviewer 1; Figure 5a).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty.** The method is essentially adapting the linearized full-fine-tuning approach of [1] by replacing full fine-tuning with LoRA. The efficiency gain is obvious since LoRA is more parameter-efficient than full fine-tuning (Reviewer 3). The connection between the derivation in Appendix A and the central hypothesis that partial linearization of a subset of parameters improves weight disentanglement is not fully justified (Reviewer 2). Specifically, the derivation shows the change in linearized model output is proportional to the gradient of the loss at initialization, but does not immediately explain why linearizing only a subset (LoRA) would inherently lead to better disentanglement than linearizing all or none (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 2 also notes that the scaling-law discussion in Section 4.2 is not clearly connected to the method.

2. **Inconsistent performance across modalities.** On NLP tasks (Flan-T5-Base), L-LoRA not only performs worse than full fine-tuning but also worse on average than standard LoRA (Reviewers 2, 3). This performance drop compared to LoRA on language tasks is particularly concerning and raises questions about general applicability across modalities (Reviewer 2). The single-task fine-tuning results (Figure 7) show a large performance drop for L-LoRA compared to LoRA on many datasets (Reviewer 3). Combined with only a slight increase on merged cases (especially in NLP), the advantages of L-LoRA over standard LoRA are unclear (Reviewers 2, 3).

3. **Missing evaluations.** Vision experiments only cover high-level tasks (image classification); mid-level and low-level tasks (e.g., semantic segmentation) are missing (Reviewer 1). It is also unclear if the method works on image segmentation foundational models like SAM (Reviewer 1 question). Experiments on larger-scale models are absent, diminishing the significance of the proposal (Reviewer 4). The authors should provide per-dataset results (not just averages) for Table 1, as encouraged in the appendix (Reviewer 3 question).

4. **Unclear or questionable definitions and explanations.** The definition of the task vector for LoRA (as change of LoRA parameters \(\phi_i - \phi_0\) rather than the change of \(\theta_0\) induced by \(A_iB_i\)) seems unreasonable; the meaning of \(\phi_0\) (shared initialization? for each task?) is ambiguous (Reviewer 3). The remark on page 8 that higher cosine similarity implies greater redundancy and leads to more destructive task interference is claimed to be opposite to observations in multi-task training (e.g., GradNorm favors similar tasks) and requires clarification (Reviewer 2 question).

5. **Questions about outperforming full fine-tuning.** Table 1 shows L-LoRA outperforming full fine-tuning in some fusion settings; possible explanations are needed (Reviewer 1 question). It is also not demonstrated whether the method works on state-of-the-art models beyond the tested architectures (Reviewer 4 question).

*Note: There is disagreement among reviewers: Reviewers 1 and 4 are more positive (rating 8), highlighting clear structure and significant improvements, while Reviewers 2 and 3 are more critical (rating 6), raising doubts about novelty, cross-modal consistency, and theoretical justification.*
