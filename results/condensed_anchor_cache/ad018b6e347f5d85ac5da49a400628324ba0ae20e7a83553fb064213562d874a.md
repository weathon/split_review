## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper analyzes gradient descent dynamics of deep linear networks (deep matrix factorization) in the Edge of Stability (EOS) regime. Under assumptions of initialization in the singular vector stationary set and strictly balanced singular values across layers, it proves that top singular values oscillate in a 2-period orbit, confining loss oscillations to a low-dimensional subspace whose dimension is determined by the learning rate. The paper claims this explains phenomena in deep nonlinear networks, such as oscillations in top features and absence of EOS in shallow/simple tasks. Experiments include simulations on deep linear networks and a LoRA fine-tuning experiment on language models.

### Strengths
- The EOS oscillation theorems (Theorem 1 and Theorem 2) for deep linear networks are novel and rigorously proven, extending previous analyses from scalar networks. The proofs are clear and the exact characterization of Hessian eigenvalues at convergence is a valuable technical contribution. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- The theoretical results directly map to simulations (Figs 2, 3, 6), providing empirical validation of oscillations in top subspaces. (Reviewer 2)
- The paper explains several previously unexplained observations: why shallow models/simple tasks may not exhibit EOS, why oscillations occur in top features, and how network architecture matters (deep vs diagonal linear networks). (Reviewer 4)
- The work is theoretically rigorous and experimentally thorough overall. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Overstatement about deep nonlinear networks:** The abstract and introduction claim the analysis explains key phenomena in deep nonlinear networks, which is exaggerated. The paper misinterprets prior work on linear–nonlinear similarity (e.g., Ahn et al. 2023); deep linear networks have a strict separation from nonlinear networks in function approximation, and the analysis does not account for non-convex landscapes, saddle points, or stochasticity. (Reviewers 1, 2) Reviewer 2 considers this a major weakness; Reviewer 1 calls it “overstated.”
- **Assumptions are strong and impractical:**
  - The α-scaled initialization (Eq. 3) and the singular vector stationary set are very specific and not used in practice. It is unclear whether Lemma 1 and Lemma 2 hold for any initialization in that set or only for the specific one. (Reviewer 1)
  - The “strict balanced state” assumption (singular values exactly matching across layers) is strong; Lemma 2 only shows the balancing gap is non-increasing, not that it converges to zero. A non-increasing sequence lower bounded by zero does not guarantee convergence (e.g., 0.5 + 1/t). Thus the assumption is not theoretically justified. (Reviewers 3, 4)
  - The paper does not discuss sensitivity to deviations from these assumptions or provide sufficient justification beyond Figure 4. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **Model does not capture non-monotonic decreasing loss in EOS:** A hallmark of EOS is that training loss oscillates but decreases over time. In this model, weights oscillate in a 2-period orbit, causing loss to oscillate indefinitely without decreasing. This limits relevance to realistic settings. (Reviewer 3)
- **LoRA experiment is not well-supported:**
  - The LoRA initialization (one matrix zero, one random) does not match the α-scaled initialization studied theoretically. (Reviewer 2)
  - The claimed “catapult” behavior is not clearly visible in Figures 7 and 8; oscillations occur even at low learning rates (e.g., η = 10⁻⁵, 10⁻⁶), and evidence linking catapults to better generalization is insufficient. (Reviewer 3)
  - The connection to the main theory is unclear; viewing adaptations as individual low-rank matrix factorization problems lacks mathematical justification. (Reviewer 4)
  - The observation that catapults occur for ranks r = 4, 8 but not r = 1, 2 is not visually evident from Figure 7. (Reviewer 4)
- **Clarity issues in Theorem 1 and r-subspace extension:**
  - The notation ρ_i(t) is ambiguous: it only says ρ_i(t) ∈ {ρ₁, ρ₂} but does not specify how it depends on t (e.g., alternating with parity). (Reviewer 4)
  - The claim that oscillations in two or more subspaces is a trivial extension of Theorem 1 is unclear; the condition λ₂ ≥ K > λ₃ is not obviously derived from Theorem 1. (Reviewer 4)
  - The index in the second term of Theorem 1 appears to use σ_{*,i} when it should be σ_{*,j}. (Reviewer 4)
- **Other specific criticisms:**
  - The period of the fixed orbit is always 2; the paper does not discuss whether other periodicities can occur. (Reviewer 1)
  - The claim that “self-stabilization does not occur in DLNs” is too broad; self-stabilization is a broader concept and the threshold may not be exactly 2/η. (Reviewer 4)
  - The claim that catapults cause faster training loss decrease is questionable; large learning rates may decrease loss quickly regardless of catapults. (Reviewer 4)
  - The natural experiment of verifying how step size depends on depth L (Line 188) is not conducted. (Reviewer 2)
  - Writing issues: missing related work section in main text (Reviewer 2), Definition 2 introduced abruptly (Reviewer 2), typos (Reviewer 1: (15)–(16) should be Ẏ_l?; Reviewer 3: unbold subscript, wrong citation, duplicate references; Reviewer 4: spacing errors L29–30, “give a …” in L415).
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms (converted to weaknesses):**
  - Why is the period always 2? Can other periodicities occur? (Reviewer 1)
  - How does behavior change if initialization is outside the singular vector invariant set, or if oscillations begin before strict balance is achieved? (Reviewer 3)
  - Can the paper provide quantitative estimates of σ_{*,1} for Figure 10 to support the low-complexity learning claim? (Reviewer 4)
  - Why adapt all weights in LoRA instead of only attention as in the original LoRA paper? (Reviewer 2)
  - What is the Pearson correlation in Figure 1? (Reviewer 2)

*Reviewers disagreed on the overall significance: Reviewers 1 and 3 (score 8) found the theoretical contributions strong and well-supported, while Reviewers 2 and 4 (score 6) considered the assumptions too restrictive and the applicability to nonlinear networks overstated.*
