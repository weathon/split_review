## Merged Review

### Summary

Reviewers gave scores of 3, 6, 3, 3, indicating a split assessment: one reviewer (score 6) found the model novel and clearly presented, while the other three (score 3) raised substantial concerns about evaluation depth, motivation, and clarity. All reviewers agree the paper proposes Tri-Tense Former, a transformer with three tense-specific attention modules and contrastive learning, and that it is clearly written with good visual elements. However, weaknesses dominate: insufficient and outdated baselines, limited datasets, missing validation of the missing-data claim, lack of computational cost analysis, and several unclear or unsupported design choices.

### Strengths

- The paper is well-structured and clearly explains the methodology using formulas, figures, and descriptions, making it easy to follow (all reviewers).
- The ablation study convincingly demonstrates the individual contribution of each of the three attention components and the contrastive learning module (Reviewers 2 and 3).
- The model is evaluated on two real-world benchmark datasets (METR-LA and PEMS-BAY) and achieves strong forecasting performance (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- The Dodger Stadium case study illustrates the behavior of the present-perfect and present attention mechanisms (Reviewer 4).
- One reviewer (score 6) particularly highlighted the novelty of the three tense-specific attention modules and the effective use of real datasets and ablation studies (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient and outdated baselines**: The paper lacks comparisons with recent state-of-the-art models, especially those that also capture cross-time-slot spatio-temporal dependencies (e.g., DSTAGNN, D2STGNN) and other strong baselines from after 2023 (e.g., STID, PDformer) (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Without these, it is unclear whether the tri-tense design offers a meaningful advantage over existing approaches.
- **Limited dataset coverage**: Only two datasets (METR-LA, PEMS-BAY) are used; additional benchmarks such as PEMS03, PEMS04, or others are needed to demonstrate generalization (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Missing-data handling is not validated**: The paper claims contrastive learning improves robustness to missing data, but experiments do not show missing data ratios, do not compare against any imputation baseline (e.g., linear imputation), and do not explain why missing data is a barrier for forecasting (Reviewers 3, 4). The specific “inconsistencies” in traffic data that contrastive learning addresses are not clarified (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear design choices and lack of motivation**:
    - Why use three separate attention modules? The authors should justify this design using traffic flow characteristics (Reviewer 3).
    - Why apply contrastive learning to traffic forecasting? Is it domain-motivated or a general technique? (Reviewer 4).
    - Present and Present-Perfect attentions compute interactions over all nodes without using an adjacency matrix (either predefined or learnable). This may introduce excessive irrelevant information from geographically distant or functionally unrelated nodes (Reviewer 2). The rationale for avoiding adjacency matrices and using spatial embeddings instead needs empirical justification (Reviewer 4).
    - The case study contradicts the model’s own attention values: Road 16 (outgoing from Dodger Stadium) shows higher attention than Road 48 (leading toward the stadium), which contradicts the description in the paper (Reviewer 2).
    - Present-Perfect attention’s use of past time steps is ambiguous: how many past steps are considered (only t-1 or more)? (Reviewer 4).
- **Computational cost not reported**: Adding three attention mechanisms likely increases cost, but no runtime or parameter comparisons are provided (Reviewer 4).
- **Writing and presentation issues**:
    - On page 4, line 195, the meanings of x and y conflict with the surrounding text (Reviewer 2).
    - In Section A.6 (page 15), two paragraphs are repeated (Reviewer 2).
    - Second-best results in tables are not underlined (Reviewer 2).
    - Using different shapes to represent nodes in a graph suggests heterogeneity when the graph is homogeneous, causing confusion (Reviewer 1).
    - Figure 2 contains unexplained “?” symbols (Reviewer 4).
    - Terminology such as “future attention” and “present-perfect attention” is not intuitive and could be clearer (Reviewer 4).
- **Related work misalignment**: The related work section discusses traffic data imputation, but the contrastive learning method aims to improve representation quality, not imputation. If imputation is relevant, missing data ratios should be clarified (Reviewer 4). The related work also lacks a review of mainline traffic forecasting (Reviewer 3).
- **Key components relegated to appendix**: Important parts like the tri-tense attention module and negative filtering are detailed only in the appendix, not in the main paper (Reviewer 4).
- **Case study should compare with SOTA**: Showing only model predictions vs. ground truth is insufficient; comparisons with other methods are needed to interpret the quality of the attention weights (Reviewer 1).
