## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a novel framework for analyzing transformers through the lens of Markov chains. It studies why a single-layer transformer struggles to learn the ground-truth bigram distribution when trained on first-order Markov data, often converging to a bad unigram minimum. The theoretical analysis characterizes the loss landscape, showing the existence of global minima (bigram) and bad local minima (unigram) contingent on data properties and model architecture. Specifically, with binary states and transition matrix P = (1‑p, p; q, 1‑q), a bad local minimum arises when p+q > 1 and weight tying is used; without weight tying, such minima become saddle points. Experiments validate these findings, and results are extended to multi-state Markov chains and multi-layer transformers. Several open problems are outlined. The problem setup is interesting and concise, the presentation is clear, and the theoretical and empirical work is solid. However, several reviewers raised concerns about the role of the attention mechanism, the sufficiency of existence arguments, and the simplicity of the data model.

### Strengths
- The problem setup is interesting, concise, and well-motivated.
- The presentation is particularly clear and comfortable to follow.
- The theoretical analysis is solid, novel, and informative, characterizing the roles of Markov switching probabilities (p+q > 1) and weight tying in the emergence of bad local minima.
- The experimental results are well-designed and provide extensive support for the theoretical insights, validating the conditions under which bad minima appear.
- The framework establishes a new, principled approach to studying transformers on Markov data, which is worth investigating further.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing role of the attention mechanism:** The analysis does not include the attention layer; the Markov task does not require attention, and the theoretical treatment essentially treats the transformer as a complex MLP with a potentially redundant attention layer. This raises the question of whether a transformer architecture is necessary for the task. The attention patterns are not presented, and a comparison with pure MLPs on the same task is requested. *[Reviewer 1]*
- **Existence arguments only:** The results primarily show that a bad local minimum exists when p+q > 1, but they do not rule out the possibility that only global minima exist when p+q < 1. This does not fully support the empirical observation that the transformer converges to a bad minimum in one regime and the global minimum in the other. The implicit bias would benefit from additional theoretical support, such as analysis of learning dynamics. *[Reviewer 3]*
- **Data model is overly simplified:** The set of words/states is too toyish, which raises expectations for a more rigorous analysis that fully characterizes the learned solution rather than relying on existence arguments alone. *[Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4]*
- **Concern about the proof sketch of Theorem 2:** In the proof (line 363), setting **e** = **a** = 0 implies that under the uni‑embedding, **xₙ** depends solely on position encoding and lacks information from the state xₙ. This setup could lead to problematic solutions for the model that are independent of the effects of weight tying, needing clarification. *[Reviewer 3]*
- **Missing explicit assumptions:** The paper does not list the assumptions used in the theoretical analysis. The authors should clarify which assumptions are employed. *[Reviewer 4]*
- **Comparison with related work:** The difference from the concurrent work [1] (Training Dynamics of Multi-Head Softmax Attention for In-Context Learning, COLT 2024) should be explained. *[Reviewer 4]*
- **Reviewer disagreement on severity:** One reviewer (Reviewer 2, score 10) found no major weaknesses, while others (especially Reviewer 3, score 5) raised substantive concerns about the depth and completeness of the theoretical characterization.
