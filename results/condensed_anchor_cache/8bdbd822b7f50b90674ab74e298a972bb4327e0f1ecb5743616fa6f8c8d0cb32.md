## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DADA, a general time series anomaly detector with Adaptive Bottlenecks (AdaBN) and Dual Adversarial Decoders, pre-trained on multi-domain data for zero-shot anomaly detection on target datasets. The adaptive bottleneck dynamically selects bottleneck sizes for different data; dual adversarial decoders amplify decision boundaries between normal and abnormal patterns. Experiments on nine datasets show competitive or superior zero-shot results compared to dataset-specific models.

### Strengths
- The task of zero-shot time series anomaly detection is challenging and practically significant; DADA provides a unified end-to-end solution with impressive zero-shot capability.
- The writing is clear, logical, and well-organized; figures and tables are beautiful and explanatory.
- Adaptive Bottlenecks address the flexible reconstruction requirements of multi-domain data, enabling dynamic bottleneck size selection.
- Dual Adversarial Decoders enhance distinction between normal and abnormal patterns, improving general anomaly detection.
- Extensive experiments on nine datasets from different domains with ablation studies demonstrating necessity of each module (AdaBN, adversarial training, dual decoders) and superiority over multiple SOTA methods.
- The method shows good results even compared to models trained specifically for each dataset.

### Weaknesses
- **Adaptive Bottleneck (AdaBN) novelty and effectiveness (Reviewers 1, 2):** The adaptive router formula lacks innovation and is similar to the routing function in PATHFORMER (Reviewer 1). More critically, AdaBN is nearly identical to a standard sparsely-gated Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) (Shazeer et al. 2017) except for dimension differences; the authors exaggerate its design and do not cite or compare to MoE in the main text (Reviewer 2). Ablation (Table 3) shows that removing AdaBN yields the second highest performance, close to the full model, and Appendix Table 10 shows MoE 256 completely outperforms AdaBN, suggesting AdaBN's actual effect is much smaller than claimed (Reviewer 2). This contradicts the claim that AdaBN is a key innovation.
- **Lack of explanation for reconstruction process and formulas (Reviewer 1):** The reconstruction function Recon(·) is not described in detail; the paper lacks clear explanations for some formulas, causing confusion.
- **Missing analysis of complementary mask modeling (Reviewers 2, 4):** The paper does not analyze the effect of complementary mask rate (e.g., 30%/70% split) on anomaly detection F1-score. The specific choice is not justified; no investigation into how different mask ratios affect reconstruction quality and downstream performance (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 2 also notes the lack of further research on complementary mask modeling despite it being a key method component.
- **Insufficient robustness analysis under anomaly contamination (Reviewer 3):** Anomaly injection assumes all original samples (including possible anomalies) are normal; the paper should test performance under varying levels of anomaly contamination in training samples to evaluate robustness, especially for real-world datasets.
- **Optimization details and loss curves missing (Reviewer 3):** Dual optimization losses need visualization of convergence (loss curves) to understand their interaction and stability; the optimization algorithm (e.g., Adam, SGD) and hyperparameters are not detailed.
- **Small fine-tuning improvement and lack of analysis (Reviewer 4):** Fine-tuning improves performance by only ~1% on MSL and SMD (Figure 4); the paper does not justify this small gain given computational overhead, and lacks details on fine-tuning epochs, learning rate, etc.
- **Labeling mistakes in tables (Reviewer 4):** In Table 8, the R_AUC_PR for DADA on PSM (47.40) is incorrectly bolded when ModernTCN achieves 47.48; in Table 2, the F1 column for CICIDS is missing underline marking.
- **Missing comparison of computation cost and inference time (Reviewers 4, 5):** The paper does not report inference time or computation overhead of AdaBN and Dual Adversarial Decoders compared to baselines, nor compare with other pre-trained models on inference speed.
- **Question on encoder distinction of masked vs non-masked zero values (Reviewer 4):** Since masked portions are set to 0, but original data may also contain zeros, how does the encoder differentiate?
- **Lack of dataset selection criteria and preprocessing details (Reviewer 5):** The difference between pretrain and evaluation datasets is not listed; selection criteria for pretrain datasets are not given. Data preprocessing steps are not specified.
- **Missing details on anomaly injection implementation (Reviewer 3):** More details needed on how anomaly injection is implemented in the supervised setting, including types of anomalies.
- **Missing adaptation details for baselines (Reviewer 3):** How are baselines, which are typically used in standard form, adapted for zero-shot evaluation on multi-domain pre-training?
- **Missing visualization details and comparison (Reviewer 2):** Visualization analysis does not state which dataset is used, lacks original data visualization, and lacks comparison with other algorithms, reducing credibility.
- **Request for open-sourcing (Reviewer 2):** The paper does not provide generation method/script for Monash+ dataset or a directly usable dataset; current explanations are insufficient.
