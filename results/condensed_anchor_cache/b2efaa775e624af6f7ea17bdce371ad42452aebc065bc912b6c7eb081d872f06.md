## Merged Review

### Summary
Anti-Reference proposes adding adversarial noise to images to protect against reference-based generation (customization and human-centric driving methods). It uses either a DiT-based Noise Encoder (fine-tuned) or PGD optimization with a unified loss function. The method is tested on SDv1.5-based models and some commercial APIs, showing reduced computational time compared to per-image PGD. However, evaluations are limited to SDv1.5, and practical robustness is insufficiently analyzed. Reviewers disagree: two rate it 6 (positive on visual effects and practicality), while two rate it 3 (critical of novelty, black-box claims, and experimental rigor).

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a timely and significant issue: protecting personal images from unauthorized AI generation, with ethical relevance.
- The use of a DiT-based Noise Encoder as a meta noise generator avoids per-image optimization, improving efficiency (validated in related adversarial LLM work, e.g., AdvPrompter).
- The method achieves impressive visual disruption effects, demonstrating effectiveness in privacy defense.
- Anti-Reference shows practical applicability against some commercial APIs (e.g., success in black-box settings).
- The proposed framework is straightforward and easy to implement.
- The paper is generally well-written and clearly presented with visual examples.
- The combination of adversarial noise with a DiT-based encoder is unique and leverages transformer scalability.
- Compared to traditional PGD, Anti-Reference significantly reduces computational time, enhancing real-time feasibility.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited model scope**: Anti-Reference is primarily tested on Stable Diffusion 1.5, which is now deprecated. It is unclear if the method transfers to newer architectures like Stable Diffusion XL or autoregressive models (this is acknowledged by authors as a limitation). Missing experiments on non-SD models or autoregressive models.
2. **Overstatement of black-box transferability**: The term "black-box" is overused. The black-box methods tested share the same SDv1.5 architecture; it is well-known that adversarial examples transfer better to similar architectures. The paper lacks evaluation on reference methods that are not based on SDv1.5 (preferably non-Stable Diffusion models). This weakens claims of universality.
3. **Insufficient real-world robustness analysis**: The only practical transformation tested is JPEG compression (details missing). Real-world scenarios involve resizing, strong JPEG compression, Gaussian blurring, diffusion purification (e.g., null-text inversion), and social media transforms. Current experiments are insufficient to support the claim that the method is real-world-ready.
4. **Gap between optimization objective and proposed losses**: The optimization problem (increase distance between generated images) does not directly align with the defined losses. The paper should theoretically bridge this gap or empirically report MSE between generated images to demonstrate increased distance. Without this, protection may be just a search rather than optimization.
5. **Missing details and reproducibility**: Weights for each individual loss (α, β, γ) are not provided. Hyperparameters for PGD are not discussed. Equations are not numbered, and Figure 2 has a confusing placement of I_adv. These omissions hinder reproducibility.
6. **Weak quantitative results and questionable qualitative presentation**: Quantitative results (Figure 4, based on Appendix Tables 2–5) show methods performing similarly, with no clear evidence that Anti-Reference outperforms existing methods. This raises concerns about cherry-picking qualitative results. The authors should either explain the discrepancy or demonstrate clear quantitative superiority.
7. **Limited technical novelty and analysis**: The loss functions and optimization methods are standard, following prior works (e.g., Liang et al. ICML 2024, Van Le et al. CVPR 2023). The paper lacks an in-depth technical explanation of why existing methods fail and how the proposed approach addresses universality and transferability. No ablation is done on the three individual losses to analyze their impact. No evidence supports why a DiT-based encoder is preferred over ViT or CNNs. The analysis feels shallow and not insightful for the community.
8. **Missing datasets for intended application**: The method is claimed to protect copyrighted artwork, yet no dataset in this domain is included in experiments.
9. **Neglect of related defense mechanisms**: The paper does not discuss defenses that target generative models might employ, such as adversarial training or adversarial detection. Additionally, related works on adversarial examples for privacy protection in standard image generation models are not cited (e.g., restricted black-box attacks, universal adversarial watermark, IDGuard).
10. **Risk of trivial solutions in noise encoder**: With a fixed reference network and no adversarial training, the noise generator may settle into trivial solutions, compromising robustness. The suitability of joint optimization with the reference network is not explored.
11. **Minority positive reviewer contrast**: While two reviewers find the method impressive and practical (scores 6), two others (scores 3) strongly criticize the experimental rigor, novelty, and claims. The positive reviewers also note the limitation to SDv1.5 and suggest further exploration (e.g., Reviewer 4 asks for ablation on white-box vs black-box gap; Reviewer 1 asks about autoregressive models). This disagreement suggests the method's claims are not yet fully substantiated.
12. **Additional requests from reviewers**: More visualization for natural items instead of humans; analysis of the gap between white-box and black-box attacks and how the method shrinks that gap; technical details of the Noise Encoder's architecture modifications to adapt DiT; potential limitations of relying on specific proxy models for black-box transferability beyond the tested APIs.
