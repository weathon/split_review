## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ConsisGAD for graph anomaly detection under limited supervision, combining a homophily-aware GNN backbone with consistency training and a learnable data augmentation mechanism. It leverages the variance in homophily distribution between normal and anomalous nodes to design a simplified backbone, and uses unlabeled data via consistency training with controlled noise from the learnable augmentation. Experiments on multiple benchmark datasets (including a private one) generally show superior performance over state-of-the-art baselines. The code is provided.

### Strengths
1. **Learnable data augmentation mechanism** – The approach is intuitively effective, as it can be finely tuned through optimization of two core metrics: label consistency and data distribution. This iterative optimization enhances both the GNN and augmentation modules (Reviewer 1). The proposed label consistency and distribution diversity concepts are particularly novel (Reviewer 2).  
2. **Effective homophily-aware backbone** – The simple, edge-level homophily aggregation is straightforward yet powerful for anomaly detection (Reviewer 2). Figure 3’s node clusters (well-separated for different edge relations) provide compelling visual evidence for leveraging homophily distribution distinctions (Reviewer 1).  
3. **Clear writing and thorough evaluation** – The paper is well-structured, coherent, and accessible (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Experiments are comprehensive, with extensive baselines and metrics, strongly supporting the proposed model’s effectiveness (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). There are no obvious grammatical errors (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Quality of pseudo-labels for “high-quality” unlabeled nodes** – The model selects “high-quality nodes” (V<sub>hq</sub>) from unlabeled data for consistency training, but the paper does not analyze the actual accuracy of these pseudo-labels, nor the impact of node selection on training stability and final performance. Errors may propagate and hurt convergence. The definition of V<sub>hq</sub> and V<sub>un</sub> in Section 2 is missing (Reviewers 1 and 3).  
2. **Hyperparameter sensitivity** – Key hyperparameters α (consistency loss weight) and ξ (drop ratio) are given fixed values, but no sensitivity analysis is provided. For example, how does α affect the balance between labeled and unlabeled data, and how does ξ influence augmentation diversity? (Reviewer 1).  
3. **Limited scope of evaluation – need for broader comparisons and analysis**  
   - **Comparison with other augmentation methods** – The paper does not compare the proposed learnable augmentation against other graph augmentation techniques (e.g., random dropping, attribute masking) (Reviewer 3).  
   - **Complexity analysis** – No computational complexity or runtime analysis is provided (Reviewer 3).  
4. **Applicability to heterophilous graphs and generic node classification** – The homophily-aware aggregation is designed for anomaly detection (where anomalous nodes often have low homophily), but it remains unclear how well it would perform on graphs with varying heterophily levels or on standard multi-class node classification tasks (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 explicitly doubts whether the aggregation is effective for abnormal nodes given their low homophily distribution.  
5. **Missing related work discussion** – The paper should cite and discuss relevant prior works on learnable data augmentation (e.g., [a] and [b]) to clarify the novel contributions (Reviewer 2).  
6. **Potential benefits of using labeled data for consistency training** – The method currently applies consistency training only on unlabeled data, but labeled data (with ground-truth labels) might also provide useful signals. This possibility is not explored (Reviewer 2).  

*Note:* Reviewer 3 (rating 5, minority) is notably more critical of the homophily-aware aggregation’s effectiveness for abnormal nodes, whereas Reviewers 1 and 2 (both rating 8) consider the backbone well-justified. All reviewers agree that the writing and experimental breadth are strengths.
