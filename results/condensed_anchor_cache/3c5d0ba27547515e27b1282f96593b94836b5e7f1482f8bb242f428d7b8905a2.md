## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FedHP, a federated learning framework with a hardware-conditioned prompt network for snapshot compressive imaging (SCI). The goal is to coordinate reconstruction models across different hardware (coded apertures) without sharing data. The prompt network aligns measurements to each hardware configuration to mitigate client drift from data heterogeneity. Experiments on simulated and real multi-hardware SCI datasets are presented.

### Strengths
- The problem is interesting and practical: leveraging federated learning to enable cross-hardware cooperation while preserving data privacy, addressing an underexplored issue in SCI (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The hardware prompt network is a novel and efficient solution for mitigating data heterogeneity in federated SCI, enabling co-optimization of multiple hardware and a global model (Reviewers 2, 3). The idea of automating alignment without per-hardware fine-tuning is appealing (Reviewer 4).
- A multi-hardware dataset was collected and built, which could broadly benefit the SCI community (Reviewer 2). Both simulation and real-world datasets are used, enhancing practical contribution (Reviewer 1).
- Extensive experiments are provided, including quantitative and qualitative evaluations on multiple settings, comparison with several state-of-the-art federated learning baselines, and detailed ablation studies (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). The paper is overall well-written and the motivation is clearly presented (Reviewers 1, 4).

### Weaknesses
- The technical contribution to computational imaging beyond SCI is limited; applicability to lensless imaging, LiDAR, or CT reconstruction is unclear (Reviewer 1). The core FL algorithm (FedAvg) is well-established, and the hardware prompt is a relatively straightforward adaptation, so the contribution to the deep learning community appears marginal (Reviewer 1).
- The method may not be necessary: training a separate model from scratch for each new measurement model is already practical (a few hours/days of training) and automatically addresses privacy constraints. The crucial need for the proposed approach is not well-justified (Reviewer 4).
- The robustness of the hardware prompt to distribution shifts (e.g., very new coded apertures) is not sufficiently analyzed. No theoretical or intuitive discussion is given on why the method handles codec pattern shifts, and no evidence is provided that the prompt module indeed corrects for such shifts (Reviewer 1). The prompt network itself could introduce instability when faced with out-of-distribution measurement models (Reviewer 4).
- The experiments lack scalability analysis: only a few clients are used; performance with 10 or 20 clients (more representative of real FL) is not explored (Reviewer 3). The method is only tested on one hyperspectral dataset; generalizability to other datasets with different spectral ranges, resolutions, or noise levels is unclear (Reviewer 3).
- The comparison with FedGST is questionable: GST is a centralized method, and the adaptation to the federated setting is not explained. Potential biases or limitations in that comparison are not clarified (Reviewer 3). In Figure 1, joint training (which should serve as an upper bound) is included without clarifying whether it is evaluated on the same measurement models as used in training; this makes the comparison unfair (Reviewer 4).
- Quantitative results do not clearly show FedHP outperforming FedAvg: in Table 2, FedHP (SSIM 0.8481) is highlighted as best, but FedAvg (0.8496) is actually higher (Reviewer 4). There is also inconsistency between Table 1 and Figure 3: the visual quality difference in Fig. 3 appears larger than the ~0.14 dB PSNR gap reported, suggesting metrics may not capture true quality (Reviewer 4).
- The deep unfolding network is only briefly mentioned at the end; it is not included in all results, limiting the evaluation (Reviewer 4). The reason for using adaptors instead of directly updating the full model is not explained (Reviewer 2). The choice of C=3 in experiments lacks justification (Reviewer 2). In Equation (9), alternative ways to impose the prompt (e.g., concatenation) are not discussed (Reviewer 2).
- Ablation on the hardware prompt itself is insufficient: no analysis of what the prompts look like for different hardware, whether they differ, or whether the prompt network is just an attention block (Reviewer 2). The difference between FedAvg and FedHP in Figure 1 is hard to discern visually (Reviewer 1). The dataset split for centralized learning vs. federated learning is not clearly explained, and there are typos (e.g., Fig. 3 caption) (Reviewer 3).
