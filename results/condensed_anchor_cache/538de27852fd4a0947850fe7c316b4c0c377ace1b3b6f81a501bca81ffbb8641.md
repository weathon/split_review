## Merged Review

### Summary
Five reviewers rate the paper 3/5 (moderate significance) with a mix of moderate to high confidence (2–5). They acknowledge the proposed RL-based macro placement method (HeatMask) that jointly optimizes wirelength and peak temperature, and the creation of a new open-source macro thermal placement benchmark. However, all reviewers find the technical contributions limited—primarily adding a thermal reward term to an existing RL method (MaskPlace)—and see major gaps in comparisons, experimental detail, benchmark analysis, reproducibility, and alignment with real chip design flows. Concerns about novelty, missing baselines, limited scope (2D only, standard cells omitted), insufficient ablation, and presentation quality are unanimous.

### Strengths
- **Problem and dataset contribution:** Addresses thermal-aware macro placement, an important but understudied aspect in chip design (Reviewers 1,2,3,5). Creates the first open-source macro thermal placement benchmark with power information, lowering barriers for future research (Reviewer 1,3,5).
- **RL approach with explicit thermal reward:** Uses PPO and an FEA thermal solver to guide placement toward lower peak temperatures; the HeatMask component is noted as a useful mechanism (Reviewer 2,4,5).  
- **Comprehensive experiments on RISC-V designs:** Experiments span multiple chip designs (e.g., HwaChaRocket), showing significant reduction in maximum temperature for large-scale chips (Reviewer 1,3,4).
- **Use of industry-standard tools:** Integrates third-party FEA thermal solver, grounding the RL learning process in realistic thermal simulations (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty:** The core contribution is adding a thermal term to the reward function of MaskPlace; no new RL algorithm, network architecture, or optimization method is introduced. Several reviewers consider this insufficient for a conference paper (Reviewer 1,3,4,5).
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons:** Only MaskPlace is compared (which is not designed for thermal optimization). Missing comparisons with thermal-aware methods such as TAP-2.5D, chiplet placement methods, AutoDMP (gradient-based macro placement), and ICCAD 2022 Contest / ChipBench benchmarks (Reviewer 1,3,4,5).  
- **No evaluation of full chip performance:** The work focuses solely on macro placement, ignoring standard cells, timing, area, power-performance-area (PPA), and post-global-placement wirelength. It is unclear whether macro-level thermal optimization translates to overall chip improvement (Reviewer 1,3,4,5).
- **Lack of ablation studies and hyperparameter analysis:** No ablation on reward components, network architecture choices, or hyperparameter derivation. Justification for using PPO (vs. other RL algorithms) is missing (Reviewer 2,3).
- **Computational efficiency not reported:** The reward calculation requires an FEA thermal solver call per iteration, which is likely expensive. No runtime comparison, hardware profiles, or simulation times are provided. Training on subsets of macros for large benchmarks raises questions about selection criteria and generalization (Reviewer 2,3,4).
- **Poor reproducibility:** No supplementary material, code, or benchmark data provided at submission time (though authors promise future release). Hardware and simulation settings are omitted (Reviewer 2,3,5).
- **Inconsistent experimental results:** In Table 2, the thermal-aware method incurs a 30% HPWL penalty on HwaChaRocket, and sometimes does not outperform the MaskPlace baseline even on temperature. The claim that shorter wirelength correlates with better temperature (Figure 1(d)) appears to contradict text lines 37–39 (Reviewer 4,5).
- **Unaddressed technical details:** Thermal simulation description lacks clarity on the FEA process, dimensionality reduction, HSIF, and how power distribution maps to floorplans. Benchmark analysis does not show how designs behave under different physical design solutions or how they differ from existing benchmarks (OpenROAD, ICCAD contests) (Reviewer 3,4).
- **Presentation quality issues:** Numerous typos (e.g., “Prak” → “Peak” in Figure 1, “pe” in Line 108–109, “T^e” in Line 306, “IMB” instead of “IBM” in Figure 1(c)), incorrect citation format throughout Section 1 (Reviewer 1,3,5).
- **Scope and generalizability concerns:** Only 2D placements are considered, where thermal issues are generally less critical than in 2.5D/3D stacking. Benchmarks are generated using SMIC 55nm; generalization to other technology nodes is not explored (Reviewer 1,3,5).
- **Missing justification for dataset novelty:** Established benchmarks (e.g., OpenROAD) already support RTL-to-GDS flows with power evaluations. The paper does not articulate what new capabilities the proposed benchmark adds beyond existing ones (Reviewer 5).
