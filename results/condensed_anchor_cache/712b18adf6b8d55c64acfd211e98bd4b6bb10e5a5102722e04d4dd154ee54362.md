## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces ViCo, a plug-and-play method for personalized text-to-image generation that uses an image attention module (cross-attention with multi-resolution reference image features) and an attention-derived object mask to condition the diffusion process. It requires training only ~6% of the U-Net parameters and no fine-tuning of the original diffusion model. The reviewers generally acknowledge efficiency and favorable results but express concerns about novelty, experimental scope, identity preservation, and missing comparisons. Scores: 6, 5, 5, 6 – reflecting disagreement between positive (Reviewers 1,4) and more critical (Reviewers 2,3) assessments.

### Strengths
- **Parameter efficiency and training speed**: The method learns relatively few parameters (only extra cross-attention layers) and achieves very low training time cost, while still effectively incorporating visual information from the reference image. Results are better than vanilla DreamBooth and Textual Inversion, with far less training time. (Reviewer 1, 2)
- **Preservation of original model capability**: Because only the introduced attention modules are fine-tuned (diffusion U-Net frozen), the pre-trained model’s original generation capability is maintained. This is considered an advantage over methods that tune part of the U-Net. (Reviewer 2)
- **Automatic mask generation without external annotations**: The method obtains an object mask at no extra cost from the cross-attention map and binarizes it with Otsu thresholding, simplifying foreground isolation and reducing background distraction. No heavy preprocessing or mask annotations are needed, making implementation easy. (Reviewer 4; also noted as transparent by Reviewer 1)
- **Good ablation and analysis**: The paper provides thorough ablation studies and is information-rich, aiding reproducibility. (Reviewer 1)
- **Flexibility and deployment ease**: The plug-and-play design avoids any diffusion model fine-tuning, making deployment flexible and scalable. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: The core idea of using cross-attention blocks to condition generation on a reference image has been explored in many prior works (e.g., [i, ii, iii, iv, v, vi, vii]), both for warping and identity preservation. The contribution of applying this specifically to LDMs for personalization is considered incremental and insufficiently novel. (Reviewer 3)  
  *Contrast: Reviewer 4 finds the mechanism innovative, but two reviewers (2 and 3) specifically question novelty.*
- **Limited experimental evaluation**:
  - Only 20 unique concepts from Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, and Custom Diffusion are used; the authors should have included the full DreamBench dataset or a union of all testing samples. (Reviewer 2, major concern)
  - The low T_CLIP score suggests unsatisfactory edit-ability; more qualitative results with complicated style changes are needed. (Reviewer 2)
  - No comparison or discussion with encoder-based methods that require even less fine-tuning time or are tuning-free (though needing pre-training). (Reviewer 2)
- **Identity preservation and generation quality**: Despite efficient training, the preservation of the reference object’s fine details is imperfect. Examples: in Figure 1, the Batman toy’s body pose changes; in Figure 4, the cat statue’s face, text on a can, and drawing on a clock are altered. This suggests the cross-attention layers may not be fully trained. (Reviewer 3)  
  *Reviewer 4 similarly notes a potential trade-off between computational efficiency and high-fidelity detail capture.*
- **Missing related work**: An important baseline, OFT (Wu et al.) is missing; OFT fine-tunes fewer parameters than LoRA while maintaining model stability even after many iterations. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of multi-image and multi-object capability**: The method only works with a single reference image during inference and apparently only with a single primary object per image. It cannot leverage multiple reference images for more robust generation. (Reviewer 1)
- **Weakness of attention-based masking**: The improvement gained from the mask component (Table 4) is not significant, questioning its added value. (Reviewer 1)
- **Otsu thresholding concerns**: Using Otsu for mask binarization may increase time overhead per sampling step, and its robustness across different image types and lighting conditions is not discussed. Suboptimal masks could introduce artifacts. (Reviewer 4)
- **Unclear training procedure and cross-attention placement**: The training procedure is not fully clear (e.g., simultaneous learning of cross-attention layers and placeholder embedding). It is also unclear why cross-attention blocks are only added to the U-Net decoder; experiments with encoder+decoder placement (as in prior work) should be reported. (Reviewer 3)
- **Stability and ablation requests**:
  - Could using a ground-truth mask (e.g., from SAM) or forcing attention map alignment with the mask improve performance? (Reviewer 2, question)
  - Does the model degenerate after many training iterations (like DreamBooth)? Results with varying numbers of training steps (especially very large) should be shown, similar to OFT’s stability analysis. (Reviewer 2)
  - Would training the cross-attention layers on a larger dataset with many objects (each with ≥2 images) and only optimizing the placeholder embedding per object improve identity preservation? (Reviewer 3, question)
