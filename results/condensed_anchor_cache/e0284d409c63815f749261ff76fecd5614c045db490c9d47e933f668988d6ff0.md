## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents GroupBind, a novel molecular docking framework that simultaneously considers multiple ligands docking to the same protein pocket. It introduces an interaction layer for the group of ligands and a triangle attention module for embedding protein-ligand and group-ligand pairs. Integrated with a diffusion-based docking model, the method sets new state-of-the-art performance on the PDBBind blind docking benchmark.

### Strengths
- The writing and organization are very clear (Reviewer 1).
- The core idea of leveraging the binding poses of multiple ligands targeting the same protein is intriguing, biologically relevant, and well-motivated (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- The approach is particularly plausible during lead optimization, where known ligands for the target often exist; using that information to improve docking could aid discovery of better ligands (Reviewer 4).
- The triangle attention message-passing across ligands provides a general template for tasks where outputs are graphs and a consistency bias is known (Reviewer 4).
- The method does not require accessory ligands to have known structures at inference time, which is a realistic scenario (Reviewer 4).
- Informative ablation studies are performed (Reviewer 4).
- Incorporating augmented ligands empirically improves docking performance (Reviewer 2).
- Proposes a new framework for jointly docking multiple ligands to a protein (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Overstated novelty:** The contribution statement is inappropriate. The concept of using multiple similar molecules to enhance docking has been described before—e.g., ComBind (Paggi et al., 2021; cited in the paper). The paper should be qualified as the first *end-to-end deep neural network* approach using this insight (Reviewer 4). Direct comparison to ComBind or OpenComBind is missing (Reviewer 4, Question).
- **DiffDock baseline inconsistency:** The reported DiffDock success rate of 32.4% (40 samples, Table 1) is considerably lower than previously published results (38.2% in [4], 36.0% in [5]) (Reviewer 2). The authors should clarify the parameters and settings used for DiffDock to ensure fair comparison (Reviewers 1, 2). Bold text annotations in Table 1 may be inaccurate (Reviewer 1).
- **Unfair comparison between settings:** GroupBind-Ref, which uses prior knowledge of the binding pocket, is directly compared against blind docking methods like DiffDock. This is unfair because DiffDock performs truly blind docking; GroupBind-Ref is site-specific. The manuscript should clearly distinguish these settings and evaluate GroupBind in a truly blind scenario (Reviewer 2).
- **Assumption of similar poses not justified:** The model assumes all ligands binding to the same pocket share similar poses, but Figure 1 only depicts structurally similar ligands with similar dockings. In contrast, [3] (Figure 4) shows structurally distinct ligands adopting distinct poses. A statistical comparison against MCS docking assumptions and an analysis of performance when group ligands have low structural similarity is needed (Reviewer 2). More statistical data on how many pockets in PDBBind actually have multiple similar ligands (Reviewer 1). The impact of ligand similarity within a group should be explicitly evaluated (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Missing evaluation on relevant benchmarks and metrics:** The evaluation should include additional baselines like FABind [5] and FABind+ [6] (source code available) (Reviewer 2). Test on PoseBuster v1 and v2 to assess physical plausibility (Reviewers 2, 3). Metrics beyond success rate (e.g., RMSD, interaction fingerprints) should be reported (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited augmentation scope:** Only ligands from PDBBind are used as augmentation ligands. Since structures are not needed at inference, many more ligands from databases could have been used, potentially enlarging groups. Replicating the ComBind evaluation framework would both provide predefined groups and allow direct comparison to a conventional approach (Reviewer 4).
- **Retrieval failure at inference:** The paper does not report how many test-set entries can actually retrieve similar ligands from the database. If no similar ligands are retrieved, the model likely becomes ineffective (Reviewer 1). Redundancy removal on test-set proteins that are duplicated or highly similar to training/validation sets (e.g., using MMseqs) is needed to properly evaluate generalizability (Reviewer 3).
- **Figure and table clarity issues:**
  - Figures 4, 5, 6: Captions lack key details. For Figures 4 and 6, it is unclear whether the reported success rate is Top-1 or Top-40. The acronyms SG, AG, NG are not defined in the captions (Reviewers 2, 3). Figure 5's purpose and its reference in the text are unclear (Reviewer 3).
  - Figure 7 includes Figures 3–6 as sub-figures; they should be presented explicitly as panels (e.g., 7a, 7b, 7c, 7d) (Reviewer 2).
  - Overloading of "k" to mean two different things in Fig 2 is confusing (Reviewer 4).
  - Table 2 shows Top-10 results but this is only stated in the main text; the table header should mirror Table 1. "Med." is not defined (presumably median RMSD) (Reviewer 4).
- **Writing and presentation issues:**
  - “Section” is used when “Figure” is meant in multiple places (Reviewers 2, 4).
  - Typo: “beyound” (line 101) should be “beyond” (Reviewer 2). Other typos like “ifself” (Reviewer 4).
  - The term “ligand ground graph” is not formally defined; “noisy group ligands” (line 214) is unclear (Reviewer 2).
  - In Equations (4) and (5), “z” is overloaded: the text states these two separate values will be stacked, but the equations say they are summed (Reviewer 4).
  - Line 457: the percentage 36.3% appears twice, causing confusion (Reviewer 3).
  - “and since the C-C bond length is about 1.5Å” appears without clear context (Reviewer 4).
