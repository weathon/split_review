## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Dual Temporal Research Analysis (DTRA), a task unifying historical research summarization with future trend forecasting. To address DTRA, the authors propose HorizonAI, a framework inspired by Dual-System Theory. System 1 uses PaperTKG, a temporal knowledge graph built via the Paper2Graph algorithm, to capture historical research patterns. System 2 leverages LLMs with Chain-of-Thought reasoning for analytical summarization and prediction. The framework is evaluated on ResBench, a new benchmark comprising 20 research topics and 210 key AI papers (though one reviewer notes only 9 data points, all centered on LLMs from 2024). HorizonAI reportedly outperforms AutoSurvey on historical summarization (18.99% improvement) and GPT-4o on future prediction (10.37% improvement).

### Strengths

- **Important integrative task.** Addressing the need to combine historical synthesis with future predictions is valuable, especially in fast-evolving fields like AI. *[R1]*
- **Dual-system methodology.** The cognitively inspired two-system workflow (temporal graph + LLM reasoning) is theoretically sound and yields strong empirical results. *[R2]*
- **Novel task definition.** DTRA uniquely merges retrospective analysis and forecasting, which prior works treat separately. *[R3]*
- **Benchmark contribution.** ResBench provides a structured, public evaluation platform for scientific trend analysis. *[R1, R3]*
- **Reasonable graph-based approach.** Using temporal knowledge graphs to track literature evolution is a sensible design choice. *[R4]*
- **Clarity.** The paper is well-structured and easy to follow. *[R4]*
- *Minority-positive note:* One reviewer (score 5) found the contributions substantial and the benchmark rigorous, in contrast to the other three reviewers (score 3) who raised significant concerns. *[R1 vs. R2/R3/R4]*

### Weaknesses

- **Limited domain scope and dataset size.** All topics and papers focus on AI / LLM research, with only 9–20 topics and 210 papers. This restricts generalizability to other fields (e.g., biology, chemistry). The dataset feels more like a case study than a comprehensive benchmark, and the reliance on arXiv HTML sources (preprints) introduces a temporal and peer-review bias. *[R1, R2, R3, R4]*
- **Insufficient baselines.** Historical completeness is evaluated only against AutoSurvey; no comparisons with TF-IDF, graph-based retrieval, or other summarization methods. Future prediction is compared only to GPT-4o, without including relevant prior works (e.g., Si et al. 2024, Baek et al. 2024, and other cited references [1-4]). The baseline model for prediction is not clearly described. *[R2, R3, R4]*
- **Lack of ablation and validation of design.** No ablation study quantifies the contribution of the dual-system design (e.g., performance without PaperTKG or without LLM reasoning). The claim that LLMs with historical context generate more reliable predictions remains unvalidated. *[R2, R4]*
- **Unreliable evaluation metrics.** Predictive reliability relies entirely on LLM-generated scores (semantic similarity, innovation, feasibility, temporal consistency) without human expert verification. The scoring ranges and weightings are not justified, making the metric opaque and potentially inflated. “Text readability” is mentioned as an evaluation area but never measured. *[R1, R3, R4]*
- **Limited novelty and unclear significance.** The main contribution—inferring future trends from a knowledge graph of past papers—is not clearly distinct from prior work (e.g., Wang et al. 2024 on survey generation, Si et al. 2024 on idea generation). The real-world benefits of this prediction task (e.g., what problems it solves) are not articulated. The comparison with pre-LLM methods for the same task is missing. *[R1, R3]*
- **Lack of experimental detail.** The definitions of “citation overlap” and “key citation overlap” are not provided. The specific LLM used as a baseline in Table 3 is unidentified. Given the variety of LLMs, comparisons with additional models are needed. *[R3, R4]*
- **Disconnect between the two tasks.** Historical summarization and future prediction are treated as independent tasks; how the historical retrieval component directly improves predictive accuracy is unexplored. This raises questions about the overall coherence of the framework. *[R4]*
- **Generalizability not demonstrated.** All topics in the dataset are LLM-related; whether HorizonAI works for non-AI scientific domains (e.g., chemistry, biology) is untested. *[R2]*
- *Minority-positive note:* One reviewer (score 5) found the benchmark rigorous and the results convincing, while the other three reviewers considered the evaluation weak and the dataset insufficient. *[R1 vs. R2/R3/R4]*
