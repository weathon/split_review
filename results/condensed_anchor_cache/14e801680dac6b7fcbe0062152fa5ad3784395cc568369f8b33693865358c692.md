## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Water-Probe, an algorithm to detect whether an LLM is watermarked by crafting prompts that exploit consistent biases under the same watermark key, and Water-Bag, a strategy that merges multiple keys to improve imperceptibility. It is the first study on the imperceptibility of watermarked LLMs.

### Strengths
- First work to investigate the imperceptibility of watermarked LLMs.
- Well organized and written; easy to follow.
- Experiments are conducted across different LLMs, sampling methods, and temperature settings; conclusions are clear.
- Low false positive rate for non-watermarked LLMs strengthens reliability.
- Water-Bag offers a practical solution to improve watermark imperceptibility.
- Identifies watermarks in a black-box setting.
- Reasonable to use repeated sampling to detect whether an LLM is watermarked.
- Prompts are designed to reveal the connection between generated text and watermark keys in a black-box setting.
- Evaluation is comprehensive, testing many different watermarking methods; experimental results show the proposed method works.
- High accuracy and robustness across various LLMs, watermarking methods, and generation settings.

### Weaknesses
- The threat model should be further described, especially regarding the prior knowledge assumptions of the detector.
- Symbol reuse: In Section 3 and Section 4, the symbol $P$ represents both the model distribution in $P_M$ and the prompt in $P_1, P_2, ..., P_N$, which is confusing. Too many $P$ across lines 222–227.
- In Figure 2 (line 289), ‘KTH’ is undefined; it is unclear whether it refers to Exp-Edit or ITS-Edit.
- The method for enhancing key pseudorandomness in the Water-Bag Strategy (“set of master keys and their inversions”) lacks theoretical support. “Inversions” is ambiguous: does it mean bit flips or keys satisfying equation (12)? If the former, the claim of enhanced pseudorandomness has no basis; if the latter, the paper does not explain how to derive the inversion key space that satisfies equation (12), and it seems that satisfying (12) does not directly relate to the pseudorandomness of $K^*_j$. The experiments in Table 2 only test defense against the proposed attacks, not the pseudorandomness of the key sequences using suites like NIST SP 800-22, which is inconsistent with the authors’ claims.
- Concept confusion: The manuscript misleads watermarking and fingerprinting; the proposed method should be categorized as fingerprinting rather than watermarking. It discusses identifying LLM output consistency but never addresses watermark injection or detection steps.
- Lack of contribution: The method merely defines concepts and samples prompts to see similarity; it does not provide novel identification beyond that.
- Limited scope: Water-Probe may be limited to certain watermarking schemes; the paper does not sufficiently test against a wider range of techniques, especially more sophisticated or less predictable ones.
- The proof in Appendix B uses Sim(0, 0) for the no-watermark case, which is confusing because cosine similarity requires non-zero vectors. Moreover, if distances of the elements are examined directly, they are approximately zero, suggesting high similarity rather than low similarity.
- The selection of prompts appears empirical; the paper could be strengthened by providing guidance on generating prompts that produce similar output distributions.
- The paper does not clearly explain how the proposed method differentiates between watermarked and non-watermarked LLMs, despite asserting that this is the goal. A specific experiment or analysis addressing this is missing.
- The identification key sampling method (steps 1–3) lacks detail; the authors only discuss the object of identification key sampling, not the sampling procedure itself.
- Missing experiments on closed-source models like ChatGPT (though the method is black-box and should be achievable); also missing discussion of the difficulty of black-box detection on real-world LLM services.
- **Minority opinion (Reviewer 3, score 6):** This reviewer is more critical, citing concept confusion, lack of contribution, and limited scope as major weaknesses. Other reviewers gave scores of 8 and assessed the contribution higher.
