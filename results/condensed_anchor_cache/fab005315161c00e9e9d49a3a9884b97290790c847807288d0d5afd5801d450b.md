## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Diffusion Trajectory-guided Policy (DTP), an imitation learning framework that first trains a diffusion model to generate 2D key-point trajectories (gripper motion) from an initial observation and language instruction, then trains a policy (fine-tuned from GR-1) conditioned on these trajectories to predict actions. The two-stage approach is evaluated on the CALVIN simulated benchmark and outperforms included baselines by an average of 25% in success rate. The method is aimed at long-horizon language-conditioned manipulation tasks and combines diffusion-based generation with VLM pre-training.

### Strengths
- Compelling core idea: using a diffusion model to generate task-relevant 2D particle trajectories to guide policy learning (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). One reviewer (Rating 6) found the idea particularly compelling and the method's design effective at producing particle trajectories.
- Well-written paper (Reviewer 1).
- Compelling results on CALVIN benchmark compared to included baselines; performance was state-of-the-art at time of submission (Reviewers 1, 2). Achieves 25% higher success rate over baselines (Reviewer 3).
- Several informative ablations: removing GR-1 video pre-training, limiting data for the policy only, and scaling data for the trajectory predictor (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Addresses an important problem (long-horizon language-conditioned imitation learning) with a combination of powerful techniques (diffusion + VLM pre-training) (Reviewer 2).
- Active research area; community likely to find this interesting (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation not clearly justified**: The paper does not adequately explain why conditioning on predicted 2D key-point trajectories is preferable to end-to-end action chunking. If the motivation is planning, action chunking (predicting multiple future actions) is a simpler baseline that should be compared (Reviewers 1, 2). The stated rationale ("reduce feature disparity between vision-language and action spaces") is vague. The method works without GR-1 pre-training, so a different explanation is needed when that initialization is absent (Reviewer 2).
- **Novelty concerns and similarity to prior work**: The method is very similar to ATM and RT-Trajectory. Stated differences (e.g., linear vs. particle trajectories, single-point vs. multi-point prediction) are not meaningful or explained. Why is predicting only the gripper trajectory beneficial when object movements could also be useful? ATM does not require a calibrated camera and includes more thorough evaluation (real robot, training on human videos) (Reviewer 2). Many recent works have incorporated diffusion into long-horizon manipulation, reducing novelty (Reviewer 3). The related work should also discuss chain-of-thought VLAs and visual prompting with VLMs (Reviewer 2).
- **Incomplete comparisons to SOTA**: The paper underperforms compared to other SOTA methods on CALVIN such as RoboUniView [3] and MDT [4]; these should be included (Reviewer 1).
- **Evaluation limited to simulation**: All experiments are on the CALVIN benchmark only. There are no real-world experiments or even visualizations of generated trajectories on real images. This severely limits confidence in practical applicability (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). The minority reviewer (Rating 6) also noted this as a weakness.
- **Data assumptions and data-scarcity inconsistency**: The Diffusion Trajectory Model requires robot global states (proprioception) to map the trajectory, which prevents leveraging additional data such as human or non-robot videos. A more general approach to obtaining 2D trajectories without proprioception would be more interesting (Reviewer 1). The introduction claims robot data is scarce, yet DTM relies on robot data for training—this inconsistency should be addressed and claims made more carefully (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing analysis of generated trajectories**: No experiments evaluate the quality of the generated trajectories (e.g., similarity to ground-truth trajectories). It is also unclear how robust the policy is to noise or inaccuracies in the generated trajectories (Reviewer 4). The paper does not discuss how overlapping or self-intersecting trajectories (which may occur in long-horizon tasks with multi-stage atomic skills) are handled (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation and clarity issues**:
  - The introduction does not state that the method predicts 2D key-point trajectories, so the reader may assume the method does video prediction (Reviewer 2).
  - It is not clear whether the video prediction loss is used when fine-tuning GR-1 (Reviewer 2).
  - It is not clear from the method section whether the approach uses only the architecture from GR-1 or the pre-trained model itself (though this becomes clear in experiments) (Reviewer 2).
  - The term "diffusion trajectory" should be replaced with "key-point trajectory" since diffusion describes the generation process, not the type of trajectory (Reviewer 2).
  - The inference procedure (at evaluation time) is never described in the method section. It is assumed that at test time the DTM generates the key-point trajectory to condition the policy, while ground-truth trajectories are used during training (Reviewer 2).
  - Section 4.4 ("Diffusion Trajectory Prompt") should be titled "Re-planning" and should cite model predictive control or receding horizon control (Reviewer 2). The term "Strategic Prompting" in that section is unclear (Reviewer 2).
  - Reviewer 1 asks: In the 10%ABCD→D task, is the DTM also trained on the limited 10% dataset? (This overlaps with missing experimental detail).
  - Reviewer 1 asks: Why is the wrist camera necessary? How would DTP perform with only a static camera? (Missing ablation).
