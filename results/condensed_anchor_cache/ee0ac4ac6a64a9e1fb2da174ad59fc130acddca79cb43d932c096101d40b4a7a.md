## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper systematically investigates how Large Language and Vision Models (LLVMs), specifically the LLaVA family, process visual information across five properties: permutation invariance, robustness, math reasoning, alignment preserving, and importance. Using 10 evaluation benchmarks, the study finds that LLVMs process images in a global manner (order of visual patches can be permuted), sometimes solve math problems without fully perceiving numerical details, lose some original perceptual capabilities after cross-modal alignment, and that lower layers (<25%) are crucial for visual understanding. The authors suggest future directions for building better LLVMs and constructing more challenging benchmarks.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an important and timely problem (how vision is perceived in LLVMs) of high interest to the multimodal community.
- The analysis covers multiple diverse aspects (permutation invariance, robustness, math reasoning, alignment, importance) and uses a comprehensive set of 10 benchmarks.
- Some observations are insightful and have the potential to inform the development of new architectures, benchmarks, and better balance between complex reasoning and basic perception.
- The appendix contains a detailed list of benchmark datasets.
- Several reviewers (scores 6,6) consider the empirical study valuable and the question profound for future MLLM development.

### Weaknesses
**Overly broad and under-evidenced conclusions**
- The claims are often too general for the experiments that support them. For example, the permutation invariance conclusion is based solely on VQA tasks, which cannot support a sweeping statement about overall visual processing capabilities.  
- Many conclusions lack rigorous backing; some observations appear cherry-picked (e.g., results given on a single model and sample while the conclusion is stated generally – Figure 1, Section 3.7).  
- The paper makes approximately 10 major claims without sufficient context about their limitations, application scenarios, or when they break down. Two reviewers (scores 5,5) highlight that the evidence is insufficient and that broad generalizations make it hard for others to build on the results; two other reviewers (scores 6,6) also note generalization concerns to other model families and a weak link between findings and future directions.

**Unclear or unjustified experimental choices**
- Rationale for focusing only on the LLaVA family (continuous vision representation) is not explained, and the potential limitations in generalizing to other LVLM architectures (e.g., those with discrete vision representation) are not discussed.  
- Dataset selection for each analysis (e.g., MMVet for localization, MMStar for layer importance) is not justified, raising questions about whether results are model-, dataset-, or sample-specific.  
- The grouping and neighbor definition used to quantify local information in patches (Section 3.3) is unclear; the impact of this choice is not assessed.  
- The method for evaluating cross-modal alignment loss (Table 3) is not clearly explained: details of the prompt are missing, and the approach may be unfair compared to a contrastive method (prompt optimization affects even zero-shot CLIP). Alternative log-likelihood comparisons (e.g., “This is an image of a [class label]”) are suggested.  
- In Table 2, the setting for measuring the frequency of outputting “1” (synthetic vs. original) is ambiguous; the paper should also report “no” frequency and precision/recall for yes/no questions.  
- The section on shared world concepts is hard to follow: what metric measures alignment, why DOCCI was chosen, and how alignment is measured with long captions given CLIP’s 77‑token context.  
- The local scores in Figure 8 and Figure 1: is the average taken over the whole dataset or just one example? This should be clarified.  
- The importance score method for Figure 9 is not explained (noised input or weights? proportion?).  
- The 1k subset used in Table 3 is not described.

**Weak connection between findings and future directions**
- The empirical findings are loosely linked to the suggested future directions. For instance, the authors propose “deeply consider innovative model architectures” for better cross-modal alignment, but it is unclear how the presented analysis motivates or entails any specific architectural enhancement.  

**Expected findings and unclear takeaways**
- Some findings (e.g., robustness to occlusion due to robustness of the image encoder) are largely expected, yet the paper does not clarify what new insight they provide or how they should guide future work.  

**Minor presentation issues**
- Figure 5’s text is too small to read effectively.  

**Missing analyses**
- The analysis is limited to models with continuous vision representations; an investigation of discrete vision representations would be interesting and would strengthen generality.  
- For several claims, extending experiments to multiple datasets (or explaining why that is infeasible) is recommended to support generalizability.
