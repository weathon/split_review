## Merged Review

### Summary

The reviewers find the paper attempts a unified view of delta parameter editing but suffers from fundamental theoretical flaws, trivial or erroneous derivations, limited novelty, and questionable experiment setups. Reviewer 1 is strongly critical (score 1, confidence 4), while Reviewers 2 and 3 are moderately critical (score 3, confidence 5 and 4, respectively). Common concerns include a severe technical flaw in the loss difference formulation (using W_POST instead of W_PRE), invalid assumptions in the Riemann sum approximation, lack of theoretical justification for sign of ΔL, contradictions in numerical results, incremental contributions over existing work like EXPO, unclear logic in extensions of DARE and BitDelta, and insufficient experimental validation (e.g., EXPO not tested on proper benchmarks). Reviewer 1 additionally finds the math entirely trivial and the experiments merely confirming prior work, while Reviewers 2 and 3 acknowledge the unified perspective as interesting but deem the execution insufficient.

### Strengths

- The paper is clearly written. (Reviewer 1)
- Presents a novel unified view of model merging methods that is lacking so far. (Reviewer 2)
- The proposed Riemann sum approximation of loss difference based analysis is interesting and gives insights for future work on theoretical understanding of model merging. (Reviewer 2)
- Delta parameter editing is an important topic in LLM efficiency. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses

1. **Severe technical flaw in the main theoretical framework** (Reviewer 2): Equation (1) analyzes ΔL = L(W_POST + ΔW̃) – L(W_POST), but all discussed methods (DARE, TIES-Merging, BitDelta) apply the edited delta to W_PRE, not W_POST. The correct analysis should be on terms like L(W_PRE+ΔW̃) – L(W_POST) or L(W_PRE+ΔW̃) – L(W_PRE). This invalidates claims about downstream performance of existing methods.

2. **Riemann sum derivation is mathematically trivial and relies on unrealistic assumptions** (Reviewers 1 and 3):
   - The claim in Section 4.1 that “it is straightforward to deduce” Equation (5) is wrong because it requires ΔW_ij and ΔL_ij to be uniformly distributed, which is generally false. Consequently, the conclusion that ΔL = 0 regardless of pruning probability p (even p=0.999) is not justified.
   - The experiment showing ΔL ≈ 1e‑5 contradicts the theoretical prediction of zero.
   - Adding hyperparameter k in Section 4.2 does not affect the triviality of the proof. (Reviewer 1)

3. **Inconsistent reasoning for BitDelta vs. DARE** (Reviewer 3): When proving Equation (5) for DARE the authors assume uniformity of ΔW_ij and ΔL_ij, but for BitDelta they claim Equation (9) is not zero due to the interaction between sign(ΔW_ij) and ΔL_ij. If uniformity also held for BitDelta, ΔL=0 would follow, making the conclusion that BitDelta performs worse than DARE questionable.

4. **Numerical contradiction between sections** (Reviewer 1): Section 4 derives ΔL=0 but reports ΔL ≈ 1e‑5; Section 6 derives ΔL non‑zero but reports absolute ΔL ≈ 1e‑6—an order of magnitude smaller than the “zero” case. This suggests the derivation in Section 4 is empirically false.

5. **Limited and incremental contributions** (Reviewers 2 and 3):
   - Generalizations (e.g., multiplying DARE by a magnitude hyperparameter k) are trivial and lack clear motivation from the proposed theorem. (Reviewers 2, 3)
   - The analysis of EXPO closely mirrors the first-order Taylor expansion already in the EXPO paper (Eq. 2). EXPO already established that success depends on a positive correlation between the gradient and the delta parameter (Section 2.2 of EXPO). The current paper builds on known results without substantial novelty. (Reviewer 3)
   - Claims about EXPO’s limitations may misrepresent EXPO’s stance on extrapolation, weakening the argument. (Reviewer 3)

6. **Unreasonable experiment setup for EXPO** (Reviewer 2): The authors claim that the relative effectiveness of interpolation/extrapolation depends on the dataset, but EXPO is designed for alignment to enhance instruction-following ability. It should be evaluated on benchmarks like AlpacaEval 2.0 as in the original EXPO paper.

7. **Unclear logic in DARE and BitDelta extension sections** (Reviewer 3):
   - The introduction of k in DARE lacks a clear connection to the theorem in Section 4.1; Section 4.3 does not provide the expected motivation.
   - The claim that DARE overlooks delta loss is inaccurate because original DARE analysis considers the input x, and delta loss under linear approximation is proportional to x.
   - Similarly, the motivation for adding noise to mean magnitude in BitDelta lacks connection to the theorem in Section 5.1. Original BitDelta already demonstrates that calibrating scaling factors can improve performance, so the claim that BitDelta overlooks this limits novelty.

8. **Poor presentation and questionable statistical significance** (Reviewer 2):
   - Figure 3 would be clearer without pre‑train model performance to highlight comparisons between varying k values.
   - Bar plots (Figures 1, 4, 7) show very small absolute differences, raising concerns about the statistical significance of claimed trends.

9. **No theoretical implication for sign of ΔL** (Reviewer 1): Sections 5 and 6 derive ΔL > 0 and ΔL < 0, respectively, without providing theoretical reasoning for why the sign is positive or negative. The experiments merely reproduce empirical observations already reported in BitDelta and EXPO.
