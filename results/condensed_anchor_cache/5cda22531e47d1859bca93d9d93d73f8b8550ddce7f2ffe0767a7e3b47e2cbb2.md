## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper analyzes pre-trained attention patterns for infrared semantic segmentation. It benchmarks various pre-training methods on infrared segmentation, identifies three attention patterns (local, hybrid, global), and finds that the hybrid pattern (attending to both nearby and foreground elements) is crucial while texture bias harms thermal generalization. Based on these insights, the authors propose UNIP: a hybrid-attention distillation target (NMI-HAD) that distills layers with a hybrid pattern, a large-scale mixed dataset (InfMix) for pre-training, and a last-layer feature pyramid (LL-FPN) for fine-tuning. Experiments show up to 13.5% mIoU improvement over other pre-training methods on three infrared segmentation benchmarks, and UNIP-S matches MAE-L with 1/10 cost. The work also claims potential in other modalities (RGB, depth).

### Strengths
- Writing is easy to follow; ideas are well supported by detailed experimental results.
- Pre-training method is important for semantic segmentation; the paper provides a deep analysis and benchmark of vision foundation models on infrared segmentation.
- Introduction of NMI to identify attention patterns for distillation is a useful contribution to the community.
- Extensive and comprehensive experiments demonstrate that the proposed approach significantly surpasses state-of-the-art infrared or RGB segmentation methods.
- The work is well-motivated, clearly written, and convincing; all observations and hypotheses are proved by empirical or numerical evidence, making the paper complete and sound.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty of local-global attention insufficiently clarified**: The idea of combining local and global attention (e.g., Swin Transformer) is not new. The paper needs a direct comparison with attention patterns in RGB segmentation datasets (e.g., ADE20k) to clarify why the difference in attention patterns is more important for infrared than for RGB.
- **Distinction between “local-global” and “global” patterns is unclear**: The “global” patterns are described as attention collapse. If only a single distillation target is used, the last layer may not be optimal (previous works have noted this). When multiple hierarchical features are used, the conclusion may differ.
- **Missing ablation on distillation combining features and attention**: The paper states (line 475) that “excessive constraints on features may intensify distillation shift.” An ablation comparing distillation that combines features and attention against each method individually is needed to support this claim.
- **Layer-wise vs. head-wise distillation unexplored**: The proposed NMI-HAD is layer-wise. It would be informative to compare performance with head-wise distillation to gain deeper insight into the distillation strategy.
- **Unclear underlying motivation**: The paper sometimes emphasizes infrared-specific performance and sometimes claims versatility. However, the approach is not specific to infrared, yet experiments on more general RGB datasets and performance comparisons with related knowledge distillation works are missing. The main contribution may be limited to a method for choosing which layer to distill.
- **Rationale for using MAE teacher despite MIM texture bias**: The paper argues that MIM focuses on texture and is unsuitable for infrared, but uses an MAE-based model as the teacher for distillation. This apparent contradiction needs explanation.
- **Comparison with domain-adaptive distillation methods needed**: The paper should compare with existing distillation strategies used in domain-adaptive methods to better position its contribution.
- **Missing cross-domain experimental settings**: Additional experiments are suggested, e.g., training on RGB images then fine-tuning on infrared, or joint training on both datasets, to further demonstrate effectiveness.
- **Title appropriate**: The title “UNIP: Rethinking Pre-trained Attention Patterns for Semantic Segmentation” is misleading because the work focuses on infrared segmentation. The word “infrared” should appear in the title (this concern is echoed by multiple reviewers; one reviewer also considers the work focused on small models).
- **Minor issues**: Abbreviations (e.g., SSL in Sec. 2.1) should be defined with full names and citations upon first use. In Fig. 8, letters of ViT are not aligned – this should be fixed.

Note: One reviewer (score 8) found no major weaknesses and considered the work well-motivated, clearly written, and convincing, with only minor presentation issues. Other reviewers (scores 5 and 6) raised the substantial concerns above, particularly regarding novelty, motivation, experimental comparisons, and the need for clearer differentiation from prior work.
