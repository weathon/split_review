## Merged Review

### Summary
The four reviewers (scores 6, 6, 6, 8) recognize ComaDICE as an extension of the DICE paradigm to offline cooperative MARL, using f-divergence-based stationary distribution regularization and value function decomposition. Reviewers 1, 2 (after rebuttal), and 3 find the idea novel, and Reviewer 4 emphasizes strong experimental results. However, all reviewers raise concerns about novelty relative to prior work (AlberDICE, OptiDICE, OMIGA), the justification for value decomposition, missing baselines and toy evaluations, theoretical exposition quality, and writing clarity. Reviewer 2 initially rated the paper as reject but upgraded to 6 after the rebuttal, yet still notes that the main contributions remain scattered and require a rewrite. Reviewer 4 gave the highest score (8), but still flags insufficient comparison with AlberDICE. Reviewer 3 and Reviewer 2 both criticize mathematical presentation and notation.

### Strengths
- **Effective distribution correction for multi-agent settings**: ComaDICE extends stationary distribution correction to MARL via f-divergence alignment between behavior and target policies, handling distributional shifts unique to multi-agent interactions (Reviewer 1).
- **Theoretical foundation**: The paper provides convergence and stability proofs through f-divergence correction (Reviewer 1).
- **Stable value decomposition**: The decomposition of global values into convex local objectives enhances training stability and aligns local agent optimization with the global objective (Reviewer 1).
- **Potential to learn optimal decentralized value functions without restrictive IGM assumption**: A very optimistic reading suggests the decomposition implicitly searches for optimal factorized policies, offering a scalable and principled offline MARL algorithm. Reviewer 2 notes this is more potential than a current strength, but after the rebuttal the authors clarified that ComaDICE approximates a global optimum as opposed to Nash policies (AlberDICE), and outperforms all baselines including AlberDICE and OptiDICE in all environments (Reviewer 2, post-rebuttal).
- **Scalability**: ComaDICE avoids the alternating optimization required by AlberDICE, making it more scalable and simpler (Reviewer 2, post-rebuttal).
- **Comprehensive experimental evaluation**: The paper evaluates on an extensive set of benchmarks including SMAC-v2 and MaMuJoCo, showing superior performance relative to baselines (Reviewers 3, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient novelty and positioning relative to prior work**: 
  - The derivation is based on OptiDICE (Lee et al. 2021) but this is not explicitly mentioned, which is misleading. Proposition 4.1 appears equivalent to Proposition 1 of OptiDICE. (Reviewer 2)
  - AlberDICE (Matsunaga et al. 2023) already extends OptiDICE to offline MARL; the final algorithm also closely resembles OMIGA (Wang et al. 2023) (Reviewer 2). 
  - The paper does not adequately discuss the theoretical or empirical advantages of using value decomposition over the resampling approach in AlberDICE. The AlberDICE paper shows a simple multi-agent task where value decomposition leads to substantial performance loss. (Reviewer 4)
  - The discussion of DICE and its goals in Related Work and Preliminaries is not sufficiently in-depth to locate the innovations relative to existing work. (Reviewer 3)
  - Lack of comparison on coordination problems (XOR game, Bridge) from AlberDICE; it is unclear whether ComaDICE can solve them. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing baselines and empirical gaps**:
  - AlberDICE, OptiDICE, and CFCQL are mentioned in Related Work but not included as baselines in experiments. (Reviewers 2, 4)
  - After rebuttal, the authors added these baselines and the XOR game, but the original draft lacked these comparisons. (Reviewer 2)
  - SMACv1 is discussed in Sections 6.1 and 6.3 but experimental results for SMACv1 are not in the main manuscript; these should be relocated or incorporated. (Reviewer 4)
- **Value decomposition and mixing network issues**:
  - The theoretical analysis relies on non-negative weights and convex activations in the mixing network for stability, which limits representational capacity. This is intensified by using a single-layer linear mixing network in practice. (Reviewer 1)
  - The ablation study shows performance decreases with a more complex network; the paper does not explain why, nor whether more data or model improvements could change results. (Reviewer 1, question converted to weakness)
  - Experimental results for mixing networks contradict mainstream findings (single-layer outperforms double-layers), hinting at a possible fundamental flaw in applying DICE to MARL, but the paper lacks in-depth discussion. (Reviewer 3)
  - The purpose of learning individual Q-functions via value decomposition is unclear; the advantage could be computed from the learned state-value function ν and run weighted behavior cloning (as in Appendix C of AlberDICE). (Reviewer 2)
- **Theoretical and presentation quality**:
  - The proof section skips key steps, has confusingly organized derivation and notation, and lacks clear interpretation between Lagrange functions and the offline RL problem formulation. (Reviewers 2, 3)
  - The contribution emphasizes value decomposition and claims equivalence of the local policy product to the globally optimal policy, but is repetitive and superficial; it does not explicitly define local subtasks, their corresponding policies, nor provide a complete proof. (Reviewer 3)
  - The formulation section lacks professional mathematical structure; derivations appear draft-like. (Reviewer 3)
  - Notation inconsistencies: occupancy measure \( \rho^{\pi_{\text{tot}}} \) is defined over \( \mathcal{O} \times \mathcal{A} \) in Equations (1) and (2) then shifts to \( \mathcal{S} \times \mathcal{A} \) from Equation (3). Also \( \mu_{tot} \) is not bold, inconsistent with \( \pmb{\pi}^{tot} \); formatting issues (bolding of “s.t.” and \( \sum \) in Eq. (3)); equations lack punctuation. (Reviewer 3)
  - The paper is generally not well-written; the narrative is scattered. (Reviewer 2) Reviewer 2 recommends a rewrite highlighting that ComaDICE pushes beyond IGM and AlberDICE. (Reviewer 2, post-rebuttal)
- **Problem scope and assumptions**:
  - It is not clear what main problem ComaDICE solves beyond what AlberDICE/OMIGA address; OOD actions were already addressed. (Reviewer 2)
  - The paper fails to explore the common sparse reward scenario in offline RL, and the analysis of behavior policy quality requirements is insufficient. (Reviewer 3)
  - Notation for states and observations is confusingly mixed (e.g., using \( s \) and \( o \) interchangeably). It is unclear whether the setting assumes that \( s \) can be sufficiently represented from \( o \); this assumption should be clarified in the POMDP description. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Computational cost**: ComaDICE incurs significant cost due to f-divergence-based adjustments for each agent’s local policy, reducing efficiency in environments with many agents. (Reviewer 1)
- **Other questions turned into weaknesses**:
  - Does Theorem 4.2 have a purpose if the original loss is also concave? Does \( \mathcal{\tilde L} \) still find the global optimum with mixing functions? (Reviewer 2)
  - Is the Individual Global Max (IGM) assumption required for the mixing network? Does ComaDICE assume the underlying optimal Q function satisfies IGM? (Reviewer 2)
  - What is the main algorithmic difference from OMIGA? (Reviewer 2)
  - Figure 2 lacks information on which specific task was selected for each benchmark (e.g., 5_vs_5 or 10_vs_10 in protoss, or expert/medium data quality). (Reviewer 4)
