## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper analyzes LLM reasoning via 3-SAT with phase transitions, testing models on CNF and a natural language menu prompt. It finds LLMs fail robustly, especially on harder instances, and that using an external solver helps. Reviewers generally acknowledge the approach as novel but view the results as unsurprising and the contribution limited; one reviewer (R3, score 6) is more positive about the potential of the approach for broader benchmarks, while others (R1, R2, R4, scores 5) find the contribution insufficient for a top conference.

### Strengths
- **Clear problem and well-studied benchmark:** The paper defines reasoning using a canonical NP-complete problem (3‑SAT) with a well-known phase transition, moving beyond toy examples (R1, R2, R3).
- **Thorough related work coverage** situating the paper in the landscape of both positive and negative LLM reasoning results (R1, R2).
- **Principled dataset construction:** The evaluation set uses strong random sampling for 3‑SAT, ensuring distributional validity and allowing detailed analysis grounded in previous complexity work (R1).
- **Analysis beyond accuracy:** The study examines performance across the phase transition region, satisfiability ratio, and other difficulty proxies, strengthening the conclusions (R1).
- **Demonstrated external solver integration:** Shows that combining an LLM (as translator) with a SAT solver can improve performance (R1, R3).
- **Multiple models tested** (R1).
- **Novel empirical findings via statistical complexity:** New observation that LLMs struggle even on “simple” instances (≤10 variables) and often do not exceed random baselines (R4).
- **Potential for broader reasoning benchmarks** (R3).
- **Secondary result:** LLMs are capable of translating textual 3‑SAT instances into a format suitable for SAT solvers (R3).
- **Clear presentation** of claims and methods (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Natural language prompt (SAT‑MENU) is problematic** (R1). The prompt produces absurd assignments (e.g., nachos orderable despite being disliked by all) because it is a syntactic sugar of the CNF, not a natural reasoning scenario. The paper does not justify it as a proxy for real‑world constraint satisfaction. The goal (average‑case reasoning vs. demonstrating a failure domain) needs clarification.
- **Incorrect complexity class claims** (R1). The paper states that logical reasoning, planning, and constraint satisfaction reduce to 3‑SAT (lines 90‑91, 151). In fact, first‑order logic is undecidable and planning (STRIPS plan existence) is PSPACE‑complete; only limited forms reduce to 3‑SAT.
- **Misrepresentation of the LLM‑Modulo framework** (R1). The paper claims to implement the LLM‑Modulo approach (Kambhampati 2024a) but actually uses the LLM only as a translator to a SAT solver, not as a generator with external critics/verifiers. This reduces the experiment to noisy translation analysis, not reasoning. (R4 also notes that translation success is not surprising.)
- **Results are not surprising given prior work** (R2, R4). Theoretical and empirical studies (Dziri 2023, Peng 2024) already show that transformers struggle with constraint reasoning beyond certain depths or expressiveness. The 3‑SAT failure is expected. (R2 adds that LLMs are not expressive enough for 3‑SAT.)
- **Limited contribution** (R2, R4). The main result is a statistical analysis of accuracies on random 3‑SAT, which, while novel, does not suffice for a top conference (R2). Even on easy instances (α→0) GPT‑4 accuracy is only ~70% (R4). A natural extension to simpler CSPs (2‑SAT, Horn, 2‑HORN‑SAT) is missing, which would better clarify reasoning abilities (R4). (R3 also notes the lack of discussion on extending to lower complexity classes.)
- **Lack of discussion on NP‑completeness vs. prior positive reasoning results** (R3). The paper claims to counter previous work showing LLM reasoning, but that work addressed simpler problems. The role of NP‑complete problems in the discourse is not addressed.
- **Outdated models** (R3). Tested models (GPT‑4 Turbo, Gemini 1.0, Llama 2) are outdated for the current submission cycle.
- **Citation and clarity issues** (R1, R4):
  - Contradictory stance on emergent abilities (line 39 vs. line 83) (R1).
  - Irrelevant citation of Dziri 2023 for architectural limits of transformers (R1).
  - Incorrect citation of Olausson 2024, Liu 2023, and Lightman 2024 as “verifiers”; they use solvers or process supervision (R1).
  - Formatting error on line 340 (citet vs. citep) (R1).
  - Final paragraph of conclusion makes claims unrelated to the paper’s body (R1).
  - Inconsistency between left parts of Figure 3 and Figure 4 for GPT‑4 accuracy (R4).
  - Minor: modern SAT solvers use CDCL, not basic DPLL (R4).
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms** (converted):
  - Figure 3 shows GPT‑4 accuracy near the critical point that is worse than random. Is this consistent/predictable? (R1)
  - Have the authors examined the CoT reasoning steps or compared to a noisy reasoner (e.g., DPLL with epsilon failure)? (R1)
  - Why are instances annotated with number of satisfying models rather than just SAT/UNSAT? (R3)
  - Consider extending beyond 3‑SAT to other combinatorial problems with phase transitions (R3).
