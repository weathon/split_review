## Merged Review

### Summary
InstructRAG teaches LMs to explicitly denoise retrieved documents by generating self-synthesized rationales that explain how the ground-truth answer is derived. These rationales are used as demonstrations for in-context learning (ICL) or as supervised fine-tuning (SFT) data. The method requires no additional human supervision, enables easier verification of predictions, and consistently improves generation accuracy across five knowledge-intensive benchmarks (average 8.3% relative improvement over best baseline). Extensive analysis shows it scales with more retrieved documents and generalizes well to out-of-domain datasets.

### Strengths
- Simple yet effective denoising approach: LMs explicitly generate rationales that justify the final answer, making the process interpretable and verifiable (R1, R5).
- Requires no additional supervision beyond the original task data, leveraging existing data to reduce annotation cost (R1, R3).
- Applicable to both ICL and SFT settings, demonstrated consistently (R1, R4).
- Thorough experimental validation across five datasets (short-form, multi-hop, long-form QA) with clear figures and tables (R1, R2, R5).
- Ablation studies confirm that providing true answers and retrieved documents is crucial for inference; example-based reasoning only benefits ICL, not FT – a valuable insight for future work (R4).
- Out-of-distribution experiments show InstructRAG generalizes well to unseen tasks (R4).
- The design is well-founded: rationale generation mitigates noise, and the authors report a high consistency ratio (98% on average across benchmarks) as a sanity check that rationales are reliable (R5).

### Weaknesses
- Insufficient detail on the rationale generation process: how is denoising of wrong rationales performed? What quality assurance steps ensure generated rationales are correct and free from noise? (R1, R2, R3). Quantitative measurement of noise in extracted knowledge and its impact on performance is missing (R3).
- Lack of intrinsic quality assessment for the generated rationales beyond final task accuracy; no human study to verify whether rationales align with useful, meaningful content or simply reinforce dataset biases (R2, R3).
- Additional computation cost from generating rationales before answer prediction is not analyzed; the paper should discuss this overhead and whether improving retrieval models might be more efficient (R1, R5).
- No comparative analysis with other explicit denoising techniques; the paper should position INSTRUCTRAG against alternative methods for handling noisy retrieval (R2, R3).
- Limited backbone diversity: experiments only use LLaMA-3-Instruct; testing across other models (e.g., LLaMA-2, Qwen, Mistral) is needed to assess generalizability and whether success is tied to strong LM capability (R3, R4).
- The method may lack novelty – it primarily adapts a classical pipeline (instruction tuning + ICL + knowledge extraction) to RAG rather than introducing RAG-specific techniques (minority view from R3, score 5).
- All datasets are based on wiki-style content; evaluation on more diverse, domain-specific RAG benchmarks (e.g., FreshQA, BRIGHT) is needed to confirm real-world applicability, and challenges for such datasets should be discussed (R4).
- Experimental settings are unclear: were all training samples used for rationale generation? Why different retrievers (Contriever, DPR, GTR, BM25) across datasets? Performance differences among retrievers are not reported; why does 2WikiMultiHopQA require 10 retrieved documents while others only 5? (R4).
- Robustness issue: if a complete answer spans multiple documents, each containing only part, the rationale-based filtering might discard useful documents and cause hallucination; more detailed analysis or experiments are needed (R4).
- When the answer is not explicitly stated in any retrieved document but can be inferred, can the rationale generator still correctly reason and identify relevant content? No corresponding ablation or case study is provided (R4).
- Presence and concentration of noise in the document collections used in experiments are not described; this is crucial for understanding robustness (R2, R3).
