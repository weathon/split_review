## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper explores uncertainty in neural networks for a meta-learning bandit news recommendation problem, using autoregressive generative modeling to predict future rewards and then applying Thompson sampling. It provides a regret bound linking online performance to sequence model training loss and validates the approach on synthetic and semi-realistic news recommendation tasks, including fine-tuning a pretrained language model on article headlines.

### Strengths
- Novel approach to Bayesian uncertainty quantification via autoregressive generative modeling, offering a scalable alternative to explicit posterior approximations.
- Application to news recommendation demonstrates potential for real-world decision-making under uncertainty.
- Formal regret bound directly connects decision-making performance to the sequence model’s training loss, solidifying the theoretical basis.
- The idea of incorporating sequential models into decision-making is sensible; pre-training on historical data allows the autoregressive model to predict missing rewards, benefiting downstream algorithms.
- Multiple theoretical analyses support the efficiency of the proposed modules.
- Experiments on both synthetic and real-world settings show improvements over baselines (PS Neural Linear, PS Beta-Bernoulli).
- Tackles the important issue of exploration in general neural networks, with broad real-world applications (recommendation, education, LLM generation).
- Effectively bridges sequence modeling and Bayesian inference, introducing "generative Thompson sampling" with strong theoretical justification, clarifying pre-training as empirical Bayes.
- Proposed method is easy to implement, and experimental results demonstrate effectiveness in reducing regret and providing valid confidence intervals.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient novelty and related-work coverage:** The paper lacks thorough discussion of highly relevant existing work, including (a) generative modeling for posterior approximations (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016) and recommendation (Da Tsai & Lin, 2022); (b) connections between autoregressive generative modeling and approximate Bayesian inference (Hollmann et al., "TabPFN", 2022; Müller et al., "Transformers Can Do Bayesian Inference", ICLR); (c) meta-learning with autoregressive generative networks in classification (Bonet et al., "HyperFast", AAAI 2024), Bayesian Optimization (Müller et al., "PFNs4BO", ICML 2023), and sequential decision-making (Lee et al., "Supervised pretraining can learn in-context reinforcement learning", NeurIPS 2024). Additionally, the proposed approach is very similar to reward models used in offline bandit/RL literature (e.g., https://arxiv.org/pdf/1103.4601); the authors should provide a more detailed comparison and explicitly highlight novel contributions. One reviewer found the contribution low (score 2) and soundness low (score 2), while others rated them 3.

- **Lack of clarity in problem formulation and methodology:**
    - The motivating news recommendation scenario assumes no overlap in action sets across time steps, but the formal problem definition and Algorithm 1 do not enforce this constraint (articles are drawn IID, not precluding overlap between historical and evaluation sets). The algorithm’s behavior when encountering previously seen actions online is not addressed.
    - The role of different users in Equation 2 and Algorithm 1 is unclear; it appears to estimate a population-level mean reward per action (over all users), making the formulation a multi-armed bandit rather than a contextual bandit. The authors should clarify whether their problem is MAB or contextual bandit, as the current setup seems insufficient for personalized recommendation.
    - The claim that a language foundation model improves uncertainty quantification is imprecise. The pre-trained sequential model is not a foundation model, and the fine-tuned language model provides strong representations but uncertainty estimates come from the sequence model’s predictions, not the language model’s inherent uncertainty. This distinction needs clarification.

- **Weak experimental evaluation:**
    - Baselines are limited to basic models (PS Neural Linear, PS Beta-Bernoulli). Comparisons against state-of-the-art contextual bandit algorithms with neural networks and uncertainty quantification (e.g., variational inference, Bayesian deep learning) are needed to convincingly demonstrate advantage.
    - Thompson sampling at inference relies heavily on generated missing reward values. If the sequence model’s predictions are inaccurate or biased, errors propagate, potentially leading to suboptimal decisions. The paper should include a sensitivity analysis of the method to sequence model accuracy and discuss strategies to mitigate inaccurate predictions.

- **Computational cost concerns:** The method’s complexity scales linearly with action set size, overlap between new and historical actions, and time horizon T. This can be prohibitive for large action spaces typical in recommendation systems. The authors should discuss scaling, memory usage, runtime, and potential mitigation strategies.

- **Unanswered questions relevant to weaknesses:**
    - Are the proposed methods used to address the delayed feedback issue (missing outcomes) rather than building a recommendation policy? Clarification needed.
    - Please clarify the differences between this approach and existing works, highlighting the specific novelty.
