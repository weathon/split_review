## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces Constrained Exploitability Descent (CED), a model-free offline reinforcement learning algorithm for finding mixed-strategy Nash equilibria in two-player zero-sum Markov games. CED combines exploitability descent with policy constraints from offline RL to handle distributional shift. The authors provide theoretical analysis showing that under a uniform coverage assumption, CED converges to a stationary point that satisfies the necessary condition for a mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium, without requiring generalized gradient computation. Experiments in matrix games, a tree-form game, and an infinite-horizon soccer game demonstrate that CED achieves lower NashConv compared to an existing pessimism-based method and can improve the behavior policy even under non-uniform coverage. One reviewer (score 3, high confidence) is more critical of the theoretical novelty and rigor, while the other two (scores 6) are more positive about the contribution.

### Strengths

- **Novel algorithm design**: CED is a novel model-free offline RL algorithm that merges exploitability descent with policy constraints, eliminating the need for generalized gradient computation (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Theoretical guarantees**: Provides last-iterate convergence to a stationary point under uniform coverage in deterministic two-player zero-sum Markov games; the stationary point meets the necessary condition for a mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Empirical validation**: Experiments across matrix games, a tree-form game, and an infinite-horizon soccer game show that CED attains lower NashConv than a pessimism-based baseline and can improve the behavior policy even under non-uniform coverage (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 2 notes that the numerical results support the proposed theorems.

### Weaknesses

- **Uniform coverage assumption**: The convergence proof relies on a strong uniform coverage assumption that may not hold in real-world offline datasets. The paper does not analyze performance under non-uniform or sparse coverage, nor does it provide mechanisms to mitigate violations. This is a significant limitation (Reviewer 1, high severity; Reviewer 3; also noted by Reviewer 2 as part of assumption concerns). No sensitivity analysis or ablation for coverage conditions is given (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited theoretical novelty and rigor**: Reviewer 2 (high confidence) finds the theoretical novelty limited. Specific issues include:
  - Theorem 1 is only asymptotic, contrasting with the non-asymptotic results provided in the direct previous work [1].
  - Theorem 1's proof assumes 1/ϵ → 0, but experiments use ϵ values that are small (1, 0.1, 1e-3, 1e-4), not near 0.
  - Theorem 2 assumes the stationary point lies in the interior of the feasibility set, which requires the Markov game to have a Nash equilibrium with full support. The paper claims previous work [1] made the same assumption, but Reviewer 2 could not find it referenced.
  - The convergence to Nash equilibrium argument (lines 386-387) uses a chain of approximations (Q^{μ,ν̂} ≈ Q^{μβ,ν̂} ≈ Q^{μ̂,ν̂}) without providing a rigorous error bound, e.g., ||μβ − μ̂|| (Reviewer 2).
  - There is an error on line 203: the regularizing term should be +ϵD, not negative (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of comparison to other methods**: The experiments compare CED only to Exploitability Descent (ED) and one other pessimism-based method. Missing comparisons to other offline RL algorithms for adversarial settings, such as robust optimization or distributionally robust methods, or algorithms handling partial observability or non-stationary opponents (Reviewer 3). This limits the assessment of CED's relative performance.
- **Simplistic experimental environments**: The games tested (matrix games, tree-form, soccer) are relatively simple. There is no evaluation on high-dimensional state/action spaces, longer horizons, or more complex multi-agent interactions, raising uncertainty about scalability (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 1 additionally notes the paper provides no analysis of computational complexity, memory requirements, or cost of policy constraint optimization and exploitability descent steps.
- **Missing experimental details**: Behavior policies are not provided in the numerical results (Reviewer 2). This hinders reproducibility.
- **Technical issues in proofs**: Lemma 4 lacks justification for why (μ̄(s,a) + p̂s_a) stays in the probability simplex, and the last line of its proof omits an explanation for the disappearance of the y²/|A| term (Reviewer 2). These details affect the soundness of the theoretical analysis.

Note: The above weaknesses reflect reviewer criticisms. Reviewer 2 (score 3, high confidence) is notably more critical than Reviewers 1 and 3 (scores 6), especially regarding theoretical novelty and rigor. All reviewers agree on the need for a more thorough coverage analysis, broader comparisons, and scalability studies.
