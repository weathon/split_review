## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes BOtied, a multi-objective Bayesian optimization (MOBO) acquisition function based on the highest multivariate rank, which is tied to the extreme quantile of the joint cumulative distribution function (CDF). The authors introduce a Pareto-compliant CDF indicator, implement it efficiently using copulas, and argue that this approach scales to many objectives while maintaining desirable invariance properties. Experiments on synthetic and real-world benchmarks are reported.

### Strengths
- Multi-objective optimization is an interesting and important topic in Bayesian optimization (R1).
- The idea of using multivariate ranking to drive acquisition is interesting (R1).
- Improving the computational efficiency of acquisition functions is a key issue in MOBO (R2).
- Incorporating domain knowledge and modeling dependency structures among objectives appears novel (R2).
- Experimental results on some benchmarks are seemingly good (R3; note: R1 and R2 disagree – see Weaknesses).
- Introducing copula-based computations into multi-objective BO is a novel contribution (R3).
- The method description is detailed (R4; note: R3 finds description unclear – see Weaknesses).
- The paper provides a good summary of relationships with the state of the art (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and presentation** need improvement: figures are too small (R1, R4), there are typos (e.g., Theorem 2 “there exist” should be “there exists”, Section 3.4 “Aas (2016) propose” should be “Aas (2016) proposes”, page 7 “being the whether the”) (R1, R4), and symbol definitions are missing (e.g., \(\hat{f}\) in eq. (3.8) appears without explanation; the experimental section later clarifies it is the posterior mean, but this is not clear in the methodology) (R3).
- **Experimental results are not fully convincing**:
  - In Table I, BOtied underperforms NParEGO on HV for DTLZ(M=6) and DTLZ(M=8), and even achieves lower HV than Random for DTLZ(M=8) (R2).
  - Only 5 repetitions are performed, variances are not reported, and this number is insufficient to validate the algorithm (R1).
  - The speed advantage claimed in the motivation is not supported: Figure 5 shows that ParEGO requires similar computational time to BOtied, and some results are comparable to other algorithms (R1, R2).
  - Most test problems are toy examples with simple Pareto fronts (e.g., DTLZ), and only discrete inputs are considered; the adaptation to continuous problems (Branin-Currin, DTLZ) is not clearly discussed (R4).
  - Progress curves over iterations would be preferable to fixed snapshots (R4).
- **Methodological gaps and lack of clarity**:
  - The distribution of the CDF is not explicitly written in the methodology section; the appendix reveals that a multi-task GP is used, yet typical MOBO employs independent GPs. The paper does not justify why modeling correlation among objectives is important, and independent GPs often perform as well or better without careful tuning of task correlations (R3).
  - There is insufficient description of how high-dimensional CDFs are estimated with copulas (R2).
  - Algorithm 1 appears to assume a discrete candidate set, but many test problems (Branin-Currin, DTLZ) are continuous; the adaptation is not clearly explained (R4).
- **Conceptual concerns**:
  - The rationale behind the CDF-based criterion is unclear. In the special case of independent Gaussian objectives, the CDF simplifies to a product of one-dimensional CDFs, which intensely seeks a single “direction” in output space. However, the Pareto frontier can cover a variety of directions, making the proposed acquisition potentially unsuitable for exploring the entire frontier – this issue persists even with a correlated model (R3).
  - The CDF indicator does not discriminate between sets that share the same best element, but MOO aims to identify a solution set, not a single best point (R2 question).
  - The paper argues that the HV indicator is sensitive to monotonic transformations, but it is the HV for a set that matters; the claimed drawback may not be problematic (R2 question).
- **Inconsistencies and questionable design choices**:
  - There is an inconsistency between the HV and CDF data in Figure 4 and Table 1 for BC(M=2) and DTLZ(M=4) (R2 question).
  - BOtied v1 is always worse than BOtied v2; the need to include v1 is not justified (R1 question).
  - A reviewer questions the use of ranking versus absolute metric values: absolute values can sometimes be important (R1 question).
- **Reviewer disagreement**: While R3 and R4 find the method and experiments positive overall, R1 and R2 rate the paper lower (scores 3 and 3) due to weak experimental support, insufficient validation, and unclear advantages. R3 rates the paper 5 but expresses significant concerns about clarity and rationale; R4 rates it 6 but notes mostly toy examples and discrete inputs.
