## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a prototype-oriented fast refinement model for few-shot industrial anomaly detection (IAD). It formulates prototype refinement as a nested optimization problem between transport probability for anomaly suppression and transform matrix for characteristic transfer, solved via an EM-based algorithm using the Sinkhorn algorithm (E-step) and gradient descent (M-step). The model integrates with PatchCore and WinCLIP and is evaluated on MVTec, ViSA, and MPDD. Reviewers acknowledge the novelty and practical value of few-shot IAD and the significant performance improvements on existing methods, but raise concerns about unclear motivation and explanation of the optimization, insufficient experimental comparisons and analysis (including missing baselines like GraphCore), limited justification for the name “Fast,” lack of analysis on whether the method actually refines prototypes or merely selects them, presentation and notation issues, and missing experiments on extensibility and larger datasets.

### Strengths
- Few-shot anomaly detection is practically valuable and has high impact in IAD.
- The approach of formulating prototype refinement as a nested optimization problem and solving via optimal transport is innovative and novel.
- The method shows significant performance improvements on PatchCore and WinCLIP across different shot settings on MVTec, ViSA, and MPDD.
- The model is designed to integrate with existing methods (PatchCore, WinCLIP), demonstrating generality and potential for widespread adoption.
- Extensive evaluations are carried out on three industrial anomaly detection datasets.
- The efficiency of the proposed method is demonstrated.
- The overall organization and writing are well done.

### Weaknesses
- The motivation is not clear; there is insufficient explanation of why optimal transport (OT) is used in the EM algorithm and how the two terms in Eq. (3) differ (distance between transformed prototypes and query features vs. OT distance); reviewers note overlapping objectives and request a thorough discussion on the difference and necessity of the OT regularization.
- The prototype transformation appears to act more like a prototype selector than a refinement: Eq. (7) minimizes the difference between the query feature and selected prototypes, and prototypes do not benefit from seeing multiple query samples. Analysis of the distribution of \(W^*\) (e.g., whether it tends to be one-hot) is missing to validate the “selector” hypothesis.
- In the EM algorithm, using gradient descent for \(W\) is inefficient; since OT is independent of \(W\), Eq. (7) is a simple linear regression problem with a closed-form solution. Evaluating the closed-form solution is requested.
- The method requires solving Eq. (7) for every query sample, adding overhead; the term “Fast” in the title is not justified because the method is based on PatchCore and does not improve inference speed over PatchCore or FastRecon (which directly computes a closed solution for feature reconstruction).
- Experimental comparisons are incomplete: GraphCore (ICLR23) achieves significantly higher P-AUROC for MVTec 4-shot and I-AUROC for MPDD 4-shot than the paper’s best results and should be included in Table 1.
- Analysis of experimental results needs strengthening: the improvement varies significantly across datasets (larger improvement on WinCLIP in MVTec but larger on PatchCore in VisA) without theoretical explanation. Directly showing the increment in Table 1 and calculating the average improvement would better reflect the method’s value.
- Relative changes on MPDD are noticeable, possibly due to the small dataset size (458 test images) and randomness; conducting experiments on a larger dataset such as Real-IAD (CVPR2024) is recommended. (Reviewer 3 noted after rebuttal that concerns were addressed and the author will add experiment results.)
- Further experiments on optimal transport are needed; OT is effective but the paper does not provide additional ablation on it.
- The limited performance improvement of WinCLIP+ over WinCLIP lacks explanation, and the first setting in Table 2 (w/o \(W^*\), \(T^*\)) appears to be naive WinCLIP but yields lower performance than the original WinCLIP in Table 1 (2-shot), especially in Image-level AUROC; this discrepancy needs clarification.
- Applicability to methods beyond PatchCore and WinCLIP is not demonstrated; the authors should provide experiments or explanations on extensibility and transferability.
- The paper has structural issues, notably the absence of a conclusion section.
- Presentation and notation issues: inconsistency in font for “l” in Formula (1) (lines 144–146) causes confusion; notation \(M \in \mathbb{R}^{\alpha \times k \times h \times s}\) uses undefined \(s\) (line 149); the overall presentation is difficult to follow.
- Recommendations for reproducibility and qualitative validation: provide code or reliable PyTorch pseudocode; in Figure 3, simultaneously display qualitative results of PatchCore+ to enhance reliability.
