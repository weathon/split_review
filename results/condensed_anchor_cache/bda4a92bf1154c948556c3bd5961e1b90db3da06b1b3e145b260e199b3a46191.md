## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces ScImage, a benchmark for evaluating multimodal LLMs on scientific text-to-image generation. It assesses spatial, numeric, and attribute comprehension (and their combinations) using 404 queries in 101 templates across 4 languages (English, German, Farsi, Chinese). Five models are tested: GPT‑4o, Llama, AutomaTikZ, Dall‑E, and StableDiffusion, with two output modes (code‑based: Python/TikZ; direct raster). Eleven scientists evaluate on correctness, relevance, and scientific accuracy. GPT‑4o outperforms others, but all models struggle with complex prompts. The authors provide detailed query construction, example outputs, and human‑agreement analysis. Limitations noted by reviewers include reliance on human evaluation, small dataset size, lack of automated metrics, insufficient depth in result analysis, and imprecise technical categorization.

### Strengths
1. **Originality and gap‑filling** (R1, R2): ScImage is a new benchmark specifically for scientific image generation, a critical area lacking systematic evaluations. It goes beyond prior work (e.g., charts/bar graphs) by including 2D/3D shapes, charts, graph theory, matrices, tables, and more.  
2. **Comprehensive task scope** (R1, R2, R3): The benchmark covers a wide variety of image types and evaluates three key dimensions (spatial, numeric, attribute binding). It also assesses multiple textual languages and coding languages/packages, making the analysis thorough.  
3. **Human evaluation quality** (R1, R2, R3): Evaluation by 11 scientists ensures high-quality assessment. Agreement analysis is provided to confirm reliability.  
4. **Detailed documentation** (R1, R3): The query construction process, templates, and example queries/outputs are clearly presented, facilitating benchmark use and extension.  
5. **Clarity and structure** (R1): The paper is clearly structured, with illustrative figures and examples.  
6. **Significance** (R1): The benchmark has potential to foster progress in scientific image generation for research, education, and communication, with emphasis on spatial accuracy and numeric precision.

### Weaknesses
1. **Dataset construction concerns** (R1, R2): The dataset is generated solely from templates and lacks ground truth. With only ~400 examples (vs. tens of thousands in related datasets), representativeness and reliability are questionable. The authors should justify the small size, show diversity analysis, and consider evaluating a subset with domain experts to ensure prompt quality. Real‑world scientific data could provide a more robust basis. Additionally, the rationale for selecting the “most commonly encountered” taxonomy types is unclear.  
2. **Dependence on human evaluation** (R1, R2, R3): The benchmark exclusively uses human assessment, which limits scalability and reproducibility. Reviewers note that hiring professional evaluators for >400 instances is expensive. The justification that automated metrics (e.g., CLIP Score, FID Score, or the MMVP example) are unreliable is insufficient—a single bad case does not prove general inadequacy. The authors should either develop automated metrics with high correlation to human judgments, provide a crowdsourcing platform, or adopt LLM‑as‑a‑judge approaches. R3 suggests image‑image similarity with an oracle image as a potential alternative.  
3. **Lack of depth in result analysis** (R1): The results section reports scores but lacks explanation of observed phenomena (e.g., why certain models fail, underlying problems, and directions for improvement). Deeper analysis would enhance the paper’s impact and guide future work. Specific questions: clarify reasoning behind evaluation results and how they contribute to significance/applicability.  
4. **Related work and comparison gaps** (R2): The paper does not thoroughly review related work on scientific image interpretation (e.g., [1,2,3]) and the distinction from ChartMimic is unclear. Table 8 (appendix) should be moved to the main body to clarify how this study compares to prior work.  
5. **Limited model coverage** (R2, R3): Only five models are tested; more multimodal LLMs (especially open‑source ones) should be evaluated to increase the benchmark’s utility. R2 asks: is evaluating only these models sufficient?  
6. **Missing domain‑specific scientific knowledge** (R2): The benchmark focuses on spatial/numeric/attribute features, but scientific images in biology, chemistry, physics, and materials science carry nuanced domain‑specific information that should also be assessed for a complete evaluation.  
7. **Imprecise technical categorization** (R2): Multimodal models and Stable Diffusion are collectively labeled as LLMs, which is imprecise and lacks clarity.  
8. **Editing errors** (R1): Figure 7 has a typo: “Llame” should be “Llama.”  
9. **Evaluation guideline suggestions** (R1, R1’s questions): Reviewers recommend using the same prompt to generate images for different scores (rather than different prompts per score) to improve reliability. Also, the paper should elaborate on the specific limitations of automated metrics for scientific graphs, not just cite one case.  
10. **Scalability for other models** (R2, R3): Because the benchmark relies on human evaluation, it is difficult for other researchers to test new models without incurring high cost or needing evaluators. A cost‑effective alternative or automated pipeline is needed.
