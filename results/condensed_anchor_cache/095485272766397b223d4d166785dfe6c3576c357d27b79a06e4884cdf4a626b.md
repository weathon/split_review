## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies GNN performance on the contextual stochastic block model (cSBM) and related models, both theoretically and empirically. Theoretically, it provides accuracy formulae for one-layer GCNs (Theorem 1) and two-layer linear GCNs (Theorem 3), and shows that under certain conditions linear classifiers can match the best GNN. It also lower-bounds expected log-likelihood of a two-layer GCN with ReLU by that without ReLU. Empirically, it benchmarks four GNN architectures (GCN, GAT, SAGE, Graph Transformer) on synthetic and real datasets, examining effects of heavy-tailed degrees, heterophily, noisy edges/features, and higher-order structures. The paper concludes that GNNs are more suited to simpler models like cSBM and struggle with noisy data and higher-order structures.

### Strengths
- Highly motivated problem and important area of research. [R1,R3]
- Extensive experiments covering a wide range of synthetic and real-world datasets, which could inspire future work. [R1,R2,R3]
- Theoretical analysis uses interesting techniques (e.g., symmetry) and appears correct and self-contained. [R2,R3]
- The conclusion that linear GNNs are sufficient for optimal classification under cSBM is interesting and corroborated by other literature. [R2]
- Empirical discussion on GNN performance across different data regimes is inspiring and thought-provoking, showing GNN volatility to higher-order structures. [R2,R4]
- Paper is well-written and clear according to one reviewer (R4), though others disagree (see Weaknesses).
- Accuracy analysis reveals when GCNs perform better and why they achieve optimality on cSBM. [R4]

### Weaknesses
- **Limited theoretical contribution and overlap with prior work:** The theoretical results are limited to 1–2 layer GCNs, focus on single-node accuracy, and assume fixed (untrained) GCNs. [R1,R3,R4] Stronger results already exist in the literature: Lemma 1 of Wu et al. (ICLR 2023) establishes Theorem 1, and Appendix K1 of the same paper shows the ineffectiveness of nonlinearities (ReLU) that may give rise to Theorem 2. [R2,R3] The analysis does not extend to deeper GNNs or provide insight into architectural choices like activation functions or normalization layers. [R4]
- **Narrow scope of assumptions:** The theory is highly tied to specific assumptions (e.g., two classes only, symmetric distributions, cSBM setting). Generalizability beyond these is unclear. [R1,R2,R4] The definition of “maximum accuracy” is not clearly stated. [R2] The results assume means of opposite sign (only justified for two classes). [R2] The analysis does not consider multi-class datasets or complex feature distributions. [R4]
- **Presentation and notation issues:** Extensive revision is required to make contributions clear. [R1] Section 4.2 is poorly written and lacks flow. [R1] Notation is problematic: the definition of σ (page 4) is missing and appears to be both a function and a constant. [R1] n_in and n_out depend on vertex i but notation does not reflect that. [R2] Missing transpose/dot in inner product (page 3, Theorem 1). [R2] Confusion whether W is a row or column vector; dimension mismatch for z_i. [R3] The notation y[X] is unclear; remarks about “extremely dense” graphs are ambiguous (SBM graphs are naturally dense). [R2] Theorem 3’s linear model still includes a nonlinear sigma. [R2] Equation 7 derivative is zero when n_in = n_out, which may correspond to phase transition regimes not addressed; the scope of “we make no claims” is unclear. [R2] Specifying “i” as a fixed a priori node is needed. [R3]
- **Insufficient architecturally-aware empirical analysis:** The paper does not sufficiently explain how different GNN architectures (GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT) respond to varying graph properties; it is unclear whether performance differences arise from specific architectural choices (aggregation function, attention mechanism). [R4] The empirical study is mostly restricted to 2-class cSBM, limiting generalizability to real-world scenarios. [R4] There is a gap between theoretical and empirical results; the relationship is not fully explored. [R4]
- **Missing experiments and ablation:** A reviewer asks for more explanation about the impact of higher-order structure when one-hop structure is already homophilic; the paper does not ensure higher-order impact is completely erased, raising doubt about the usefulness of higher-order GNNs. [R4] The physical meaning of Theorem 3 is not explained. [R4] The paper lacks experiments on multi-class datasets or datasets with more complex structure. [R4] No exploration of the interaction between different feature distributions and graph structure. [R4]
- **Minority positive reviewer acknowledges limitations:** Reviewer 1 (score 5) praises the theorems but still notes they are “nice but unfortunately not enough and limited to particular assumptions” and requires extensive rewriting. After the rebuttal, R1 increased the score but the fundamental limitations remain. [R1]
