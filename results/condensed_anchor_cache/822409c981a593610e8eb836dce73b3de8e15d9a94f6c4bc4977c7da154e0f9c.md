## Merged Review

### Summary
All three reviewers (scores 6, 6, 6) see value in this paper’s study of optimization conflicts in long-tailed learning. The paper proposes PLOT, a Pareto deep long-tailed recognition method built on multi-objective optimization (MOO) that uses temporal decoupling, a variability collapse loss, and sharpness-aware minimization. Experimental results on several benchmarks show improvement over baseline methods. Reviewers agree the problem is interesting and the approach is reasonable, but they raise concerns about technical novelty, the strength of the empirical evidence, missing analyses, and clarity issues. Reviewer 3 is markedly more critical (soundness 2, contribution 2) than the other two (soundness and contribution 3 each). Reviewer 1 has the highest confidence (5); reviewers 2 and 3 are confident but not absolutely certain (4).

### Strengths
- The long-tailed recognition problem is fundamental and deserves further study.
- The paper is well organized and easy to follow (all reviewers).
- The approach of leveraging multi-objective optimization (MOO) to address optimization conflicts is reasonable and novel, and it is tailored to the challenges of long-tailed recognition.
- The empirical demonstration of gradient conflicts among categories is an important observation.
- The proposed method does not introduce additional computational cost (Reviewer 3).
- The source code is released for reproduction (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing state-of-the-art comparisons**: The paper lacks comprehensive performance comparisons with leading methods such as BBN, SADE, and PaCo, making it difficult to judge the method’s overall impact and general applicability (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient literature coverage**: The references do not thoroughly discuss some classical long-tailed recognition methods (Reviewer 1).
- **Presentation issues**: The conclusion should be written in the past tense and include future work (Reviewer 1). The paper also needs careful proofreading (Reviewer 1).
- **Figure 1 ambiguity**: It is unclear how Figure 1 is computed (e.g., are the values cosine similarities between mean gradients of different classes?). The connection between “gradient conflicts” and “gradient similarities” is not explained (Reviewer 2).
- **Gradient magnitude ignored**: The analysis only considers gradient directions; the impact of L2 norms of gradients is not considered (Reviewer 2).
- **Choice of MOO stage**: The paper applies MOO during early representation learning but does not justify how to select the applied stage (Reviewer 2).
- **Dataset version**: iNaturalist should be explicitly labeled as “iNaturalist 2018” (Reviewer 2).
- **Concurrent related work**: A similar work that also studies long-tailed learning with multi-objective optimization (“Long-Tailed Learning as Multi-Objective Optimization”, arXiv:2310.20490) is not discussed or compared (Reviewer 2, suggestion).
- **Ad-hoc sharpness-aware minimization (SAM)**: SAM is introduced without a strong connection to the MOO framework; it could be integrated into almost any long-tailed baseline, making the contribution feel ad‑hoc (Reviewer 3).
- **Weak technical novelty**: The method largely adopts CAGrad with a slight scheduling modification (temporal decoupling), directly applies SAM, and adds a variability collapse loss that is merely a regularization on the standard deviation of the loss (Reviewer 3).
- **Variable and selective performance**: Effectiveness varies significantly across baselines (Tables 1‑2). Experiments are only reported for cRT + Mixup and MiSLAS on ImageNet‑LT, Places‑LT, and iNaturalist, casting doubt on the results’ confidence (Reviewer 3).
- **Table 1 contradicts core claim**: MOO underperforms in nearly half the cases (24/54) compared to baseline methods, which contradicts the claim that MOO is beneficial; yet Table 4 shows the most favorable improvement (cRT + Mixup), making the overall results less convincing (Reviewer 3).
- **Theoretical issues (Theorem 4.1)**: The bound is on weighted intra‑class loss variability ($w_KM$). It is unclear whether uniformly constraining intra‑class variability is optimal. Moreover, neural collapse theory shows that minimizing the cross‑entropy loss already leads to intra‑class collapse at optimality, so an explicit constraint seems unnecessary (Reviewer 3).
- **Lacking justification for CAGrad**: The paper does not discuss alternative MOO methods (MGDA, EPO, CAGrad). The choice of CAGrad is performance‑driven, with no explanation of its inherent compatibility with long‑tailed learning (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing multi‑label experimental validation**: The multi‑class problem is treated as multiple binary tasks, but no empirical study on imbalanced multi‑label classification is provided; such an analysis would strengthen generalizability claims (Reviewer 3).
- **No per‑group analysis**: The paper does not provide disjoint classification performance for many/medium/few categories (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing results**: Some logit‑adjustment results are missing from Table 2 (Reviewer 3).
- **No evidence that gradient conflicts are resolved**: There is no direct proof or analysis showing that the proposed method actually solves the gradient conflict issue depicted in Figure 1 (Reviewer 3).
- **Figure 8 inconclusive**: It is unclear whether the proposed method surpasses the baseline LDAM‑DRW in gradient similarities, as the proposed method shows a similar trend in the second stage (Reviewer 3).
- **Suggested qualitative analysis**: Providing t‑SNE visualizations of the embedding space would help validate the method’s effectiveness (Reviewer 3).
