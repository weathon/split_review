## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the role of partial online state information (OSI) in learning partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs). It first establishes a hardness result (Theorem 1) showing that without full OSI, exponential sample complexity is required in the worst case, using a novel combinatorial lock construction. To counter this, the paper identifies two tractable subclasses of POMDPs under additional structural assumptions (independent sub-states and a revealing condition) and proposes algorithms achieving \(\sqrt{K}\) regret (with \(K\) the number of episodes). The algorithms employ a novel strategic querying mechanism (e.g., exponential weight updates) and are distinct from prior OMLE/MOPS-based approaches. The paper provides both upper and lower bounds for these tractable classes, with analyses relying on new techniques for joint OSI query and action control.

### Strengths
- The topic is highly relevant: identifying tractable subclasses of POMDPs with realistic partial side information is an important problem in theoretical RL, and the proposed framework (querying part of the state before acting) naturally complements existing works on hindsight OSI.
- The story is complete: motivating examples, formal framework, a pessimistic lower bound motivating structural assumptions, and two algorithmic solutions with regret guarantees.
- The hardness result (Theorem 1) uses a clever combinatorial lock construction that meaningfully demonstrates that partial OSI (even with active querying) does not break worst-case intractability.
- The algorithmic designs (Algorithms 1–3) are new and distinct from popular OMLE/MOPS-based methods, exploiting observable operator structure with fresh ideas to handle partial side information; the exponential-weight-based query strategy for substates is particularly promising.
- The motivation for the two problem classes is clear, supported by empirical works where the structures naturally arise.
- The techniques for both the lower-bound construction and the pessimistic-optimistic exploration strategy for query and execution policies are appreciated.
- The setting is common in real-world problems, and the motivating examples (e.g., autonomous driving) help ground the model.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and notation issues across the paper.** Multiple reviewers noted poor writing quality with numerous typographical errors and inconsistent notation that hinder comprehension. Examples include: confusion between \(r_h\) (reward function) and \(r_h^k\) (realized reward); inconsistent use of \(\hat{\Phi}_h\) vs. \(\hat{\Phi}_h^k\); unclear definition of \(V^{\pi^k}\) (expectation seems to rely on \(\pi_q^k\) not \(\pi_p^k\)); ambiguous regret notation \(Reg^{\pi}(K)\) lacking dependence on executed policy sequence; and the statement of Theorem 1 lacking a precise formal definition of “with only partial online state information.” These issues led one reviewer (score 5) to question the ability to validate the paper’s accuracy and call for comprehensive revision; others (score 6) found the story complete but also cited clarity as a concern.
- **Modeling discrepancy with prior OSI work.** The paper adopts a *before-action* query model, whereas prior work on hindsight OSI typically assumes state information is revealed *after* the action. The motivating examples (e.g., autonomous driving) suggest post-action revelation, yet the model operates differently. The paper does not sufficiently discuss this mismatch or justify why conclusions carry over if the query occurred post-action.
- **Insufficient connection to related tractable classes.** 
    - The paper does not discuss whether the two proposed problem classes are subclasses of already known tractable POMDPs (e.g., those with low dec related to [1,2]).
    - Tractable class 1 (factorable transition into \(d\) sub-transitions) bears strong resemblance to factored MDPs. A detailed comparison is missing: factored MDPs often achieve optimal regret with square-root dependency on the size of factored state/action spaces, but such comparison would clarify novelty and limitations.
- **Opaque algorithm and proof details.** 
    - Algorithm 3 does not explicitly detail how the \(d\) sub-transitions are estimated; the current presentation suggests they may be treated as identical, which would be invalid if sub-transitions differ per substate.
    - The proofs of Lemma 1 and Lemma 5 lack clarity regarding the expectation in Equations 40, 42, 43, making the regret bound derivation ambiguous.
    - The strategic querying mechanism—the core novelty—is not elaborated sufficiently in the main body. For instance, \(\hat{r}_h^k\) is defined only in the appendix, hindering understanding of its design rationale.
- **Lack of unified treatment of the two problem classes.** The two classes are studied in a case-by-case manner with very different algorithms and analyses. There is no investigation or discussion of whether a connection (positive or negative) between them exists, which limits a more unified understanding of POSI in POMDPs.
- **Unclear optimal policy structure for Class 1.** The guarantee of Algorithm 1 implicitly implies an optimal policy that samples a substate \(i\) from a fixed distribution and takes an action based on \(\phi_i(s_h)\). This non-trivial implication is not stated, and explaining it would better motivate the algorithm.
- **Potential artifact in upper bound of Theorem 3.** The paper highlights that the regret upper bound “decreases exponentially as \(\tilde{d}\) increases,” but this may be an artifact of the algorithm design or analysis. It is possible that the minimax optimal regret actually scales with \(|\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}|^d\) (as the lower bound does), which would not decrease with \(\tilde{d}\). Furthermore, the revealing condition \(\alpha\) appears only in the upper bound; it is known that for revealing POMDPs the optimal regret scales with \(\alpha^{-1}\), but this connection is not discussed.
- **Gaps in theorems and lower bounds.** 
    - Theorem 6 treats \(\tilde{d}=1\) and \(\tilde{d}\ge 2\) separately without unification; a general case would better illustrate the paper’s contribution.
    - Theorem 4’s lower bound does not depend on \(\tilde{d}\); a dependency on \(\tilde{d}\) would provide a more nuanced complexity characterization.
- **Missing explicit instantiation of motivating examples.** The paper would benefit from explicitly writing down the state mapping with physical quantities for the examples in Section 2, and from providing a notation table to improve readability.
- **Definitional problems.** 
    - The definition of \(\mathbb{P}_h^k\) in (3) is unclear; the counter \(\mathcal{N}^k\) depends on \(h\), and \(\mathcal{N}^k(\phi_{\hat{i}}(s), a, \phi_{\hat{i}}(s'))\) is not defined. The notation should be replaced by \(\mathbb{P}_h^k(\phi_{\hat{i}}(s') \mid \phi_{\hat{i}}(s), a)\).
    - The reward function is said to be independent of episode index \(k\), yet \(r_h^k\) is used throughout (e.g., Figure 1, Sections 2.1–2.2), creating confusion.
- **Typographical errors:** Section 2.3, line 3: \(\Phi_h\) should likely be \(\hat{\Phi}_h\).
