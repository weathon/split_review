## Merged Review

### Summary

Motion-Agent is a conversational framework for 3D human motion generation, editing, and understanding. It uses MotionLLM, a generative agent built from a pre-trained LLM (e.g., LLaMA) fine-tuned with LoRA adapters (1–3% of parameters) to bridge motion and text via motion tokenization (VQVAE). Integration with GPT-4 as a coordinator enables multi-turn conversational interactions without additional training. Evaluated on HumanML3D and KIT, MotionLLM achieves competitive performance with diffusion models and other transformer-based methods. The framework supports generation, captioning, editing, composition, and reasoning, and can generate long motion sequences through decomposition.

### Strengths

1. Novel conversational framework integrating LLMs (both fine-tuned MotionLLM and coordinator GPT-4) for human motion generation, enabling multi-turn interactions for generation, editing, reasoning, and composition. The design is considered interesting and reasonable.

2. Efficient fine-tuning approach: only 1–3% of model parameters are updated with LoRA, achieving competitive performance with minimal computational overhead relative to training from scratch.

3. Thorough experimental evaluation on HumanML3D and KIT with extensive demos and qualitative examples. The supplementary videos show good visual results and cool complex motions. Ablation studies and application demos are provided.

4. Supports multi-turn editing, reasoning, composition, and long motion sequences – valuable contributions that prior models struggle with. Table 1 clearly summarizes these advantages over several recent competitors.

5. The paper is well-written with clear explanations of the motion tokenizer/detokenizer and integration with GPT-4. The presentation (including supplementary videos) aids understanding.

### Weaknesses

1. **Efficiency claim insufficiently substantiated.** Reviewers 1 and 3 found no quantitative comparisons on computational cost, training time, or inference time for the full system versus alternatives. Reviewer 1 questioned the advantage over simply fine-tuning on a task-specific dataset. Reviewer 3 noted missing details on real-time performance, memory usage, and hardware requirements. Reviewer 2 considered the experiments thorough and did not identify this as a weakness.

2. **Quantitative evaluation gaps.**  
   - Reviewer 1 requested plots or tables showing how motion quality changes with increasing conversation turns and motion lengths to support the "infinite motion generation" claim, including analysis of coherence, drift, and memory constraints.  
   - Reviewer 3 noted lack of comprehensive quantitative comparisons on standardized benchmarks such as Human3.6M and AMASS, making it difficult to fully assess performance improvements over SOTA.  
   - Reviewer 4 observed that Tab 2 (Generation task) shows lower FID scores than SOTA and suggested constraining motion length for fairer comparison. Reviewer 4 also noted no quantitative metrics for multi-turn edited generations (Fig. 5 in-betweening) and proposed using NPSS for evaluation.  
   - Reviewer 2 found the experiments thorough and performance good; this evaluation gap was not a concern for that reviewer.

3. **Ablation studies considered limited by multiple reviewers.**  
   - Reviewer 1 requested ablation on the motion tokenizer and comparison with MoMask (which also uses RVQ-VAE).  
   - Reviewer 3 suggested ablations on different LLM sizes/types (e.g., GPT-4 vs open-source models), motion vocabulary size, quantization methods, and adapter configurations.  
   - Reviewer 5 requested comparisons of different tokenizer types and a detailed comparison between LLaMA and Gemma.  
   - Reviewer 4 noted that an ablation study is included but additional clarifications on adapter control are needed.

4. **Long motion sequence generation details are unclear.**  
   - Reviewer 2 asked for a concrete example of GPT-4’s output showing the decomposition process (like Figure 3), whether GPT-4 is used during training to decompose HumanML3D into atomic units, and how alignment between decomposed text and motion is guaranteed.  
   - Reviewer 1 wanted empirical analysis of quality degradation and coherence over extended sequences.  
   - Reviewer 4 observed that extended motions keep the original motion unchanged (concatenating m1+p1 + m2+p2) and asked for clarification whether motions are concatenated after each prompt and smoothed by the motion detokenizer.

5. **Physical plausibility issues visible in results.** Reviewer 4 noted that supplementary videos show body interpenetration, lack of floor contact, and body deformation, despite the motion tokenizer/detokenizer being trained with reconstruction losses. Reviewer 4 suggested explaining why these artifacts occur and using test-set-based multi-turn prompts with FID to provide pseudo-ground-truth metrics. Reviewer 1 and 3 also had concerns about handling out-of-domain motions and diversity/realism for complex sequences.

6. **Dependence on proprietary GPT-4.** Reviewer 3 raised concerns about reproducibility and accessibility since GPT-4 is not openly available to all researchers, and suggested exploring open-source alternatives (e.g., LLaMA, Gemma) with detailed performance comparisons. Reviewer 1 asked whether the framework could be extended to vision/audio modalities using GPT-4o.

7. **Missing related work.** Reviewer 2 noted that Bailando (CVPR 2022) and Bailando++ (TPAMI 2023) are early methods that tokenize/quantize motion for GPT-style generation and should be cited as relevant prior work.

8. **Inference pipeline and adapter usage unclear.** Reviewer 4 asked how MotionLLM.generate and MotionLLM.caption are invoked (are they called every time or only when needed? Are they separate LoRA branches as suggested by Fig. 2?). Reviewer 4 also requested explanation for how the agent chooses gender/body identity in Fig. 3 and whether all identities are kinematically compatible or could cause retargeting issues.

9. **Supplementary website issues.** Reviewer 5 reported that the provided website could not render videos, and suggested hosting on an anonymous platform (e.g., Google Pages) with more diverse prompt examples.
