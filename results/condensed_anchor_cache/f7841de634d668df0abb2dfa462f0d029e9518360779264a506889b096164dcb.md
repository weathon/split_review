## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the Identifiable Exchangeable Mechanisms (IEM) framework, a unified probabilistic graphical model that generalizes the Causal de Finetti theorem and brings together causal discovery (CD), Independent Component Analysis (ICA), and causal representation learning (CRL) under exchangeable (non-i.i.d.) data. The framework introduces “cause variability” and “mechanism variability” as relaxed identifiability conditions, allowing causal structure and representation identification under broader conditions. The authors also establish a duality in ICA techniques, linking to Time-Contrastive Learning (TCL) and other auxiliary variables.

### Strengths
- The IEM framework provides a novel theoretical contribution by unifying CD, ICA, and CRL within a single probabilistic graphical model, bridging fields that have developed independently.
- The framework generalizes the Causal de Finetti theorem, introducing cause and mechanism variability as relaxed identifiability conditions for non-i.i.d. data, which aligns with current trends in causality research toward real-world, structured data.
- The analysis connecting identifiable exchangeable mechanisms to temporal contrastive learning (TCL) is insightful, particularly the duality between cause and mechanism variability and the novel approach to identify latent noise variables by analyzing changes in the mixing mapping rather than relying solely on changes in latent distributions.
- The paper is well-structured and clearly written, with a broad Discussion and future directions section that provides valuable insights for advancing causal representation learning.
- Many insights from this work are likely to be highly significant for advancing our understanding of causal representation learning and its connections to causal discovery and ICA.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of empirical validation**: Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R4) note the absence of experimental or case-study evaluations on synthetic or real-world datasets across the three settings (CD, ICA, CRL). At least one concrete demonstration is requested to show how degrees of non-i.i.d. facilitate causal inference tasks and to bolster practical relevance.
- **Abstract assumptions and need for concrete examples**: The cause and mechanism variability assumptions remain abstract, making their practical applicability challenging to conceptualize. Several reviewers (R1, R3, R4, R6) request real-world examples (e.g., from biology or social sciences) where these assumptions hold and how the IEM framework can be applied. Specific examples/counterexamples for each subsection are also desired to clarify the framework.
- **Lack of comparative analysis**: The paper does not provide a direct comparative analysis, even theoretical, benchmarking IEM against established methods in CD, ICA, or CRL. Such a discussion would better highlight IEM’s unique contributions and clarify where it stands relative to traditional approaches (R4).
- **Loosely connected sections and insufficient inter-field discussion**: The arguments in Section 3 appear loosely connected; more detail is needed on how the framework benefits development in CD, ICA, and CRL. A deeper discussion on how advancements in one field could inform another would be beneficial (R3, R6). The shift in Section 4 from unification to OOD/interventional distributions feels abrupt.
- **Open questions and missing clarifications**:
  - Whether the framework generalizes to settings with hidden confounds between causal variables (R1).
  - If latent causal variables are identified via cause and mechanism variability, is it necessary to discuss causal structure separately? Can IEM offer new insights on the edge-change formulations in CauCA (Eq. 11) and Liu et al. (Eq. 5) for identifiability? (R5).
  - What are the real-world applications for the duality insight regarding changes in the mixing mapping rather than changes in latent distributions? (R5).
- **Minor technical issues**:
  - Notation inconsistencies: Equation (6) appears to be missing a term (e.g., $p(y^i | \psi^i)$ unclear) (R1, R5, R6).
  - Inconsistent notation for $\psi$ between Equation (3) and line 131 (R5).
  - Two mappings (from $s$ to $o$ and from $z$ to $o$) use the same function $f$; distinct notation would be clearer (R5).
  - $\hat{\theta}^{g}_{i0}$ is undefined (R5).
  - Acronym “GCL” first appears in line 78 without expansion (R5).
  - Typographical errors: “tequence’s” should be “sequence’s” (line 107) (R6); misplaced bracket in Theorem 2 (R6).
