## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper formulates supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and alignment of large language models as a constrained optimization problem. It proposes Lagrange Large Language Models (L3Ms), which use logarithmic barriers to enforce application-specific constraints without relying on heuristic-driven processes. The approach allows customization across diverse applications. Experimental results on length-constrained and helpful/harmless (HH) preferences using 7B-parameter models demonstrate the method’s ability to tailor alignments while maintaining task performance, evaluated primarily via perplexity (PPL).

### Strengths
- The formulation of SFT and alignment as a constrained optimization problem is novel and avoids traditional heuristic methods. The use of logarithmic barriers in L3Ms provides a fresh perspective that has the potential to improve LLM customization for different applications.
- The paper provides a solid theoretical foundation, including discussions on constraint types, Lagrange multipliers, the connection to KKT conditions, and the conversion of the general goal of balancing task performance and multiple requirements into a solvable objective function with logarithmic barriers.
- L3Ms demonstrate technical excellence and adaptability across applications, ensuring robust performance. The systematic approach addresses a critical need and offers practical benefits for both academic and industrial use.
- The experimental results on length-constrained and HH L3Ms show effective customization according to different constraints while maintaining performance on the original task. This demonstrates the method’s versatility and efficacy in achieving tailored alignments.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited task and constraint diversity.** Experiments mainly consider length constraints and helpful/harmless preferences. It is unclear how L3Ms would perform on more complex or demanding constraints, or on computational and reasoning tasks where task-specific metrics are needed. Reviewers request broader evaluation across more diverse tasks, including instruction-following benchmarks, to assess generalization across distributions.
- **Metrics and evaluation issues.** The primary metric is perplexity (PPL), which does not directly reflect instruction-following ability or real-world task performance. One reviewer notes that the improvement in PPL over a baseline is marginal, and another reviewer observes that in Figure 3 a baseline (MM) achieves a better performance frontier than L3M, questioning the practical significance of the PPL gains. Additionally, using reward model scores as a metric is invalid because of reward hacking (the reward model is involved in L3M training). Other metrics should be considered for evaluating alignment.
- **Baseline comparisons are incomplete.** Traditional SFT+alignment, when given the same training budget as L3M, should be included as a baseline. The paper currently does not compare L3Ms to this straightforward alternative. One reviewer also points out that the MM baseline appears to outperform L3M on the performance frontier, which deserves further discussion.
- **Experimental setups are simple and may not capture real-world performance.** The datasets used appear restricted, and the experiments are limited to models with only 7B parameters, which may restrict scalability and the ability to handle complex tasks. More comprehensive evaluations across varied data types and larger models are needed.
- **Writing issues.** Incorrect formula numbering causes confusion and disrupts the flow. Improving clarity and accuracy would enhance readability.
- **Computation overhead not discussed.** The paper does not discuss computational overhead compared to general SFT and related works (e.g., references cited in the reviews). This is a missing practical consideration.
- **Mathematical concern about the objective.** The core objective Eq.(10) is equivalent to a lower bound of a multi-objective RLHF objective with induced reward models and an SFT term (via Jensen’s inequality). This raises questions about whether the optimization truly respects the original constrained problem or merely minimizes a surrogate.
- **Hyperparameter μ.** The log barrier parameter μ is decayed during training, but the reason is not explained, and the relationship between its value and L3M performance is not explored. This is a missing ablation and analysis.

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* Reviewers 1 and 3 are more positive (score 6) and view the theoretical conversion and problem formulation as strong contributions. Reviewers 2 and 4 are less positive (score 5) and raise more concerns about experimental scope, baselines, evaluation metrics, and the mathematical derivation. One reviewer specifically notes that the MM baseline achieves a better performance frontier than L3M, while another provides a formal derivation showing the objective is a lower bound of a multi-objective RLHF objective.
