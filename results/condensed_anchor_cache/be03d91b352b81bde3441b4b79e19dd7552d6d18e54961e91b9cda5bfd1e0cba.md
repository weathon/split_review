## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ECHOPulse, the first echocardiography (ECHO) video generation model conditioned on ECG signals. It uses a three-stage pipeline: VQ-VAE-based video tokenization, ECG-video token alignment via ECG-FM and masked token prediction, and autoregressive video token generation. The method aims to accelerate generation and avoid complex conditional prompts by leveraging readily available ECG signals. Evaluations on public and private datasets are presented.

### Strengths
- **Novel and impactful idea**: Using ECG signals as a conditioning modality for ECHO video generation is under-explored and innovative (R1, R2, R4, R5). The attempt to combine video data and time-series data provides a new research direction for medical image analysis (R2).
- **Potential generalizability**: The methodology can be extended to other medical modalities like cardiac MRI, fMRI, and 3D CT generation (R3). Conditioning videos on time-series data could impact domains beyond echocardiography (R4).
- **Clear writing and presentation**: The paper is well-structured, easy to follow, with high-quality figures and tables (R1, R2). The pipeline diagram clearly covers all steps (R1). Language is proper (R3).
- **Methodological grounding**: Leveraging VQ-VAE tokenization for fast decoding addresses computational bottlenecks in generative models (R5). The approach aims to accelerate generation compared to prior methods (R1).
- **Reproducibility features**: Authors plan to release ECHO videos paired with ECG dataset (R1) and provide detailed experimental procedures and code (R2).
- **Promising application**: Synthesized video data can be used for downstream tasks like training other models (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Inadequate baseline comparisons and missing results**: Several important baselines are missing from key tables. EchoNet-Synthetic is absent from Table 3 (LVEF estimation) despite showing superior performance in reported metrics and having inference time below 3 seconds (R4, R5). Table 1 misses EchoNet-Synthetic baseline for reconstruction (R4). No generative results for EchoDiffusion or EchoNet-Synthetic on Private data (R4). The abstract claims existing methods have high computational cost and slow inference, but generation times are not compared against EchoNet-Synthetic (R5). The experimental setup for comparing with MoonShot and VideoComposer (general-purpose models) appears unfair and lacks sufficient explanation; metrics may have been taken from the HeartBeat paper without replication (R5).
- **Invalid or misleading conclusions**: Section 4.1 compares VQ-VAE metrics from ECHOPulse against VAE-only metrics from EchoNet-Synthetic (without diffusion), so the conclusion that VQ-VAEs outperform diffusion models is invalid (R5). The claim of achieving state-of-the-art results is overstated; previous works like EchoNet-Synthetic and HeartBeat show better performance (R5). Qualitative results indicate visual quality of ECHOPulse samples lags behind competitors, but this is not acknowledged (R5). The conclusion that VQ-VAEs surpass diffusion models cannot be drawn from these metrics (R5).
- **Evidence insufficient that generation follows ECG signal**: LVEF estimation in Table 3 only considers ED and ES phases (part of ECG cycle) and shows inferior R² scores compared to previous methods (R4). Figure 3 shows only one example and is hard to interpret (R4). No metrics like rFID or rLVEF are provided to quantify how much information is lost through encoding and reconstruction (R4). It is unclear how much reconstruction quality influences final generation (R4). The SSIM computation in Table 2 is problematic because SSIM requires paired input/output data, and it is not clear how ECHOPulse reconstructs a video (R5).
- **Inconsistencies and missing details in tables and methods**: Table 2 should be separated by datasets for easier comparison (R4). It is unclear how MoonShot and VideoComposer were evaluated, how text conditionings for CAMUS and EchoNet-Dynamic were generated, and why HeartBeat and EchoNet-Synthetic are singled out at the end (R5). EchoNet-Dynamic contains only A4C views, but ECHOPulse is evaluated on A2C views; models like EchoDiffusion and EchoNet-Synthetic do not report on A2C (R5). The LVEF calculation method in this paper differs substantially from EchoNet-Dynamic, making direct comparison problematic and the conclusion misleading (R5). Section 2.3 confuses Reynaud et al. (2024) and Ouyang et al. (2020) (R5). Equation 1 lacks proper citations to relevant prior work (Gu et al., Rombach et al., Esser et al.) (R5).
- **Methodological concerns and over-engineering**: The method is over-engineered with limited guardrails, and its clinical significance is questionable (R3). The GAN-based approach may suffer from mode collapse and lack of diversity; exploring diffusion models could improve output (R1). High model complexity and memory demands may limit deployment on resource-constrained devices (R3). Real-time generation is not achieved; missing computational resource analysis (FLOPs, GPU mem, FPS, latency) to assess feasibility for wearable devices (R2). It is not clear how much inference resources are consumed across pipeline stages (R2). No breakdown of computational cost by stage (R2).
- **Clinical relevance and robustness issues**: The need for a condition image is not addressed: how to obtain it in practice, how to ensure it is reasonable given variability in image quality and view angles (R2). The method relies on a single high-quality condition image without clarity on selection or handling of poor-quality inputs (R2). The paper makes no attempt to ensure fidelity and trustworthiness of generated videos (R3). No medical professionals evaluated the videos for realism (R3). There is no discussion of confounding factors that could influence generation and lead to hallucination (R3). Generating videos from ECG is an information-creating process with no constraints to prevent implausible or patient-unrelated outputs, especially without a conditioning image/video (R3). The motivation for ECG-conditioned generation over EF-conditioned generation is insufficiently explained (R4).
- **Minor issues**: Comic sans font in figures should be reconsidered (R3). Table 3 missing references (R4). Oversimplification of related work: EchoDiffusion and EchoNet-Synthetic use image and EF, not segmentation masks and clinical text as claimed (R5). The paper would benefit from a question about performance without pretraining on natural data (R4).
