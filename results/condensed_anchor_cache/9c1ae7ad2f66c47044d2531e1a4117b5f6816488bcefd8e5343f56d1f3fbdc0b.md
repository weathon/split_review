## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes FedGLCL, a federated learning (FL) framework for image classification that addresses non-IID data by leveraging a frozen, pre-trained text encoder (from CLIP/BERT) on the server. Local clients learn image encoders, and classification is performed via CLIP-style contrastive alignment of local image features with global textual class embeddings. The approach avoids separate local models and reduces variance across clients. Experiments across multiple non-IID scenarios (label and feature skew) show significant improvements over state-of-the-art FL algorithms (FedAvg, etc.), with additional scalability, efficiency, and ablation analyses. The paper also provides theoretical guarantees on generalizability.

### Strengths

- **Novel application of language-driven representation learning to FL.** Using a pre-trained text encoder (CLIP/BERT) as a fixed global anchor for contrastive learning with local image features is a creative and simple solution to the non-IID problem. The approach does not introduce extra computation or communication costs.
- **Strong and consistent empirical results.** The method outperforms several baselines across a variety of datasets (Office-Caltech-10, DomainNet, etc.) and non-IID settings. Experiments are comprehensive, comparing many baselines and including ablations (e.g., on the text embeddings, fixed vs. updated, pre-trained vs. custom).
- **Solid theoretical foundation.** The paper provides theoretical analysis explaining how the framework mitigates non-IID issues (reducing variance, overfitting), which strengthens credibility.
- **Useful ablations establish key design choices.** Appendix ablations show that (1) keeping embeddings fixed and (2) using a pre-trained language model (rather than custom embeddings) are both crucial for performance. This rules out the possibility that contrastive training alone explains the gains.
- **Well-written and clearly contextualized.** The paper is easy to follow and situates the work well within existing FL literature.

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of specification of the exact pre-trained vision-language model (VLM) used.** The paper mentions using a pretrained VLM but does not specify which one (CLIP? ALIGN? BLIP?). This hampers reproducibility and assessment of limitations (Reviewer 3). The text encoder is later identified as BERT in the summary (Reviewer 4), but the image encoder is unclear.
- **Missing comparisons and needed further evaluation:**
    - Limited comparisons with prototype-based FL methods (e.g., [1] and [2] as referenced by Reviewer 4). This would help position the contribution relative to existing approaches that also use class prototypes.
    - No evaluation on larger-scale datasets (e.g., ImageNet) to strengthen generalizability claims; current datasets (e.g., Office-Caltech-10, DomainNet) are relatively small (Reviewer 1).
    - No measurement of FedGLCL’s performance in centralized or IID-federated settings. Such experiments would clarify whether the gains come from the contrastive learning approach generally or specifically from addressing non-IID challenges in FL. Reviewer 2 also notes that related work (Fang et al., 2022) found language supervision had limited impact on CLIP robustness, so a discussion is needed.
    - No evaluation of different text encoders from various pretrained VLMs (e.g., CLIP vs. ALIGN vs. BLIP) to test sensitivity of the method to the choice of text encoder (Reviewer 3). Also, a comparison with traditional word embeddings (e.g., GloVe) is requested (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear role of semantic information from language supervision.** An ablation shows that using randomly mismatched class names yields comparable performance to correct class names. This surprising result suggests that the “broad linguistic knowledge” emphasized in the abstract may not be the key driver. The paper should expand on this finding and discuss what exactly FedGLCL gains from the language model (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited applicability beyond image classification.** The method is inherently restricted to tasks with textual class labels and requires a pre-trained model that provides good embeddings of those labels. This limits versatility compared to generic FL methods like FedAvg (Reviewer 4). The paper does not discuss adjustments for other modalities (e.g., text-only FL) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of standard errors for experimental results.** Although gaps are large, repeated runs are costly; reporting standard errors would improve reliability (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing discussion of related works using CLIP in FL.** The paper mentions works (Lu et al., 2023; Guo et al., 2023; Shi et al., 2023) but does not explain how FedGLCL differs from them or articulate the “philosophy of language-driven representation learning” (Reviewer 1). Also, no mention of initializing with pretrained image models (more practical for deployments) (Reviewer 2).
- **Potential data contamination concern.** If the datasets used in experiments overlap with the pretraining data of the VLM, the observed performance gains could partly be attributed to the pretraining stage rather than the FedGLCL framework itself (Reviewer 3). This should be addressed.
- **Clarity issues in writing.** The abstract and paper should clearly state that the federated learning setting is specifically for image-text pre-training (Reviewer 1). Additionally, the text encoder used is not consistently identified (BERT vs. unspecified VLM) (Reviewer 3).
