## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper reveals that safety alignment of open-source LLMs is only robust under default decoding configurations. By manipulating generation strategies — removing system prompts, varying decoding hyperparameters (temperature, top‑K, top‑p), and different sampling methods — the authors achieve misalignment rates from 0% to >95% across 11 models (LLaMA2, Vicuna, Falcon, MPT families) with a claimed 30× lower computational cost than GCG (Zou et al.). A new dataset (MaliciousInstruct, 100 prompts) is curated. Attack success is measured via string‑matching, a trained classifier (92% human agreement), and human evaluation. The paper also proposes a generation‑aware fine‑tuning defense to reduce misalignment. The attack does not jailbreak proprietary models such as GPT‑3.5.

### Strengths
- **Overlooked vulnerability:** The attack exposes a previously overlooked alignment failure under non‑default generation configurations, with important implications for practitioners deploying open‑source LLMs. Two reviewers (scores 8) particularly emphasized the novelty and the importance of red‑teaming all potential use cases.
- **Comprehensive experiments:** Evaluation across 11 open‑source models is more thorough than much recent jailbreaking work. High attack success rates are consistently demonstrated.
- **Computationally efficient:** The attack requires no optimization or fine‑tuning; it simply varies decoding parameters. Comparison to GCG in Table 4 shows it outperforms GCG on open‑source models under both ASR metrics.
- **Defense study:** A generation‑aware fine‑tuning approach is proposed and shown to reduce the attack success rate by a non‑negligible amount, addressing both attack and defense.
- **Evaluation improvements:** A machine‑trained classifier for harmful output detection achieves 92% human agreement (versus 86% for string‑match). A simple heuristic (checking for bullet points) reaches 93% agreement. The authors also manually inspect outputs for harmful content.
- **Well‑written:** The paper is clearly structured and copy‑edited, well above average for the venue.
- **Simplicity:** The attack is straightforward and less white‑box than optimization‑based methods (e.g., GCG), as it does not require knowledge of model parameters.

**Reviewer contrast:** Two reviewers (scores 8) were more positive about the contribution and clarity, while two reviewers (scores 6) found the attack’s applicability limited to open‑source models and raised concerns about the motivation and defense evaluation.

### Weaknesses
- **Only works on open‑source LLMs:** The attack cannot jailbreak proprietary models (GPT‑3.5, 7% ASR, unclear if maximum or average). This indicates the vulnerability may be an artifact of insufficient open‑source alignment rather than an intrinsic LLM weakness.
- **Unclear motivation for attack:** Some generation strategies (e.g., high temperature, no system prompt) degrade output quality. It is unclear why a user would deliberately switch to such configurations. This weakness overlaps with a missing user‑scenario discussion.
- **Defense method lacks clarity and evaluation:**
  - The fine‑tuning objective (Eq. 1) maximizes probabilities of both aligned and misaligned groups; one reviewer suggests a sign error (should be minus before the misaligned log‑probability rather than plus).
  - No intuition is given for why the proposed fine‑tuning works, and it is not evaluated on standard benchmarks or for impact on generation quality.
  - The defense evaluation uses a template that prepends “An aligned answer:” to responses; the paper says this is unnecessary in practice but provides no ablation.
- **Insufficient discussion of existing defenses:** No comparison to perplexity‑based filtering or other basic jailbreak defenses (e.g., Jain et al., 2023).
- **Unclear attack description and ASR computation:**
  - The paper lists 20+9+20 = 49 configurations but does not specify whether “Varied All” uses combinations (20×9×20) or single‑parameter variations. It is unclear if the reported ASR is the maximum across configurations per prompt or an average.
  - The relationship between Table 1 (per‑parameter ASR) and Table 2 (human‑evaluated harmfulness percentage) is not explained. Table 2’s numbers appear much lower than Table 1’s “Varied All” column.
  - No algorithm or pseudocode is provided; the attack description is interleaved with results.
  - Details on imposing length constraints and penalties (Section 4.3) are missing.
- **Claim of “30× lower computational cost” vs. GCG needs nuance:** GCG’s cost is constant per prompt (one optimized suffix can be reused across many prompts), while generation exploitation requires per‑prompt configuration search. If one fixed vulnerable configuration is used for all prompts, the ASR may be lower than GCG’s.
- **Human evaluation details are insufficient:**
  - Only five graduate student annotators; aggregation method and statistical significance are not reported.
  - The criteria for judging a response as “harmful” (“concrete and helpful suggestions for malicious instructions”) are vague; no edge‑case examples or inter‑annotator agreement are provided.
  - The 100 prompts from MaliciousInstruct were all used for the human study (it is a 100‑prompt dataset), but the paper says “sampled” without clarification.
- **Classifier training details are incomplete:**
  - The paper states 161k entries were collected but only 10k used for training; selection bias is not addressed.
  - Hyperparameter choices are listed but not explained (e.g., how they were chosen).
- **Missing evaluation of generation quality impact:** Changing decoding parameters likely affects fluency, coherence, and task performance. This is not measured or discussed. (Question from Reviewer 3 overlaps: “Different generation configurations may also affect the generated content’s quality. Have you evaluated that?”)
- **Dataset concerns:** The new MaliciousInstruct dataset contains 100 prompts that appear “subjectively less harmful” than AdvBench (e.g., no terrorism‑ or racism‑related violations). Example prompts are not shown in the main text.
