## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the LPath algorithm for OOD detection using VAEs. LPath first trains a VAE on in-distribution data, then extracts per-data-point sufficient statistics from the encoder and decoder (mean and variance of latent and reconstruction), and finally applies classical density estimators (e.g., COPOD, MD) on these features. A twin-VAE scheme with different latent dimensions is proposed to address the encoder-decoder trade-off. The method is motivated by a "Likelihood Path" principle and evaluated on small benchmark datasets.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a crucial problem in machine learning: reliable OOD detection, especially in safety-critical applications. The "LPath Principle" and its application to VAEs offer a promising new direction (Reviewer 1, confidence 2).
- The approach of extracting sufficient statistics per data point and doing OOD detection via a density on those features is neat and well-motivated (Reviewer 2, confidence 3; Reviewer 3, confidence 2).
- The reinterpretation of VAEs through fast/slow weights and the use of two VAEs with different latent dimensions to handle the encoder-decoder trade-off is original (Reviewer 1, confidence 2).
- The paper provides a thorough analysis of the LPath Principle from different perspectives, grounding it in established statistical principles; the combinatorial analysis is well-motivated (Reviewer 1, confidence 2).
- The proposed method achieves performance comparable to existing SOTA approaches while using smaller model sizes; the writing is clear and easy to follow (Reviewer 3, confidence 2). (Note: Reviewer 1 rated presentation 1, so there is strong disagreement on clarity – see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Performance is marginal and inconsistent:** As shown in Table 1, the performance improvement over competitors is not substantial. In several cases (e.g., vs. DDPM on certain datasets), LPath performs noticeably worse. The gains are marginal across most datasets and OOD scenarios. (All three reviewers; Reviewer 2 confidence 3, Reviewer 3 confidence 2, Reviewer 1 confidence 2.)
- **Experiments limited to small datasets:** All datasets used are small in size. It is uncertain whether the proposed concept and algorithm would be effective on more complex, high-dimensional real-life data. Benefits have not been fully demonstrated. (Reviewer 2 confidence 3, Reviewer 3 confidence 2.)
- **Conceptual flaw in the sufficient-statistic justification:** The paper argues that $T$ is a sufficient statistic for the marginal likelihood, but this is incorrect. $T$ is a sufficient statistic for the one-sample estimate of the marginal likelihood (or the ELBO), not the marginal likelihood itself. This distinction is not adequately discussed. As a consequence, Equations 23 and 24, which claim to represent the marginal likelihood, are incorrect (they hold only for the one-sample estimate). (Reviewer 1, confidence 2, soundness 3.)
- **Communication and presentation issues:**
  - The paper is convoluted and not an easy read. The "fast and slow weights" analogy seems disconnected from the actual work (which is about sufficient statistics) and adds confusion. (Reviewer 1, confidence 2.)
  - The order of presentation is unconstructive: the Likelihood Path principle is introduced in Sec. 3 but only discussed in Sec. 4. (Reviewer 1, confidence 2.)
  - The LPath principle is imprecisely phrased: it says there is "more information" without specifying relative to what, and it is repeated three times without clarification. (Reviewer 1, confidence 2.)
- **Missing experiments and ablations (from reviewer questions):**
  - Have the authors tried using larger models? Would the approach scale to hierarchical VAEs (where $T$ becomes large)? Experiences? (Reviewer 2, confidence 3.)
  - Can the method be extended to Gaussian mixture VAEs or hierarchical VAEs? Could that improve performance at a cost of model size? (Reviewer 3, confidence 2.)
  - For cases where DDPM performs much better (e.g., Table 1), is it due to model capacity? What happens if LPath's model size is enlarged and compared to DDPM? (Reviewer 3, confidence 2.)
- **Minor issues:**
  - Equation on pp. 122–123 needs revision. (Reviewer 3, confidence 2.)
  - p. 226: "While VAE optimization should already be driving Eqs. 17–19 to a small value" – why is that expected? (Reviewer 3, confidence 2.)
  - p. 893: "Theorem ??" – missing reference. (Reviewer 3, confidence 2.)
