## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper addresses causal Bayesian optimization (CBO) when the causal graph is unknown. The key insight is that only the direct causal parents of the target variable \(Y\) need to be learned, making CBO feasible in larger graphs. The authors propose a method that simultaneously learns a posterior over the parents (via Bayesian updating) and optimizes the target. For linear structural causal models (SCMs) with additive Gaussian noise, a closed-form posterior is derived; for nonlinear SCMs, a Gaussian process (GP) approximation is used. The effectiveness is demonstrated on synthetic and semi-synthetic (DREAM5) gene expression datasets.

### Strengths

- Addresses an important and challenging problem in CBO under the realistic scenario where the causal graph is unknown (R1).
- The paper is well-written, contributions and limitations are clearly stated, and the underlying assumptions are transparent (R2, R4). The transparency about limitations is especially valued in causal inference (R4).
- Derives a closed-form posterior update rule for linear additive SCMs with Gaussian noise and formalizes CBO for unknown graphs (R3).
- Proposes a GP approximation for the nonlinear case and a scalable solution for larger graphs by deriving an initial prior over plausible parents from observational data (R3).
- The method is of high impact and practical relevance; previous literature had overlooked that focusing *only* on the direct causal parents is sufficient for optimization (R4). It scales well to larger graphs (R4).
- Experiments on synthetic and DREAM5 gene expression datasets support the method’s performance (R2). Limitations are explicitly acknowledged (R4).
- The authors demonstrate a strong grasp of both causal and CBO literature, aiding exposition (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty concerns for the theoretical results (Section 4.1):** There is already existing work on Bayesian causal discovery for linear SCMs with additive Gaussian noise (cited: NeurIPS 2022, arXiv 2307.13917, NeurIPS 2021). Since identifying parents is a simpler task, the authors should clarify what makes their derivation non‑trivial compared to those approaches (R3). One reviewer (R3) found the novelty limited, while others (R2, R4) viewed the contributions as significant and high‑impact.
- **Restrictive assumptions:**  
  - Assumption 4.3 (no unobserved confounders between \(Y\) and its ancestors) is a strong condition that significantly simplifies the problem. The paper should more clearly acknowledge this limitation (R1, R3).  
  - The paper does not explain why Assumption 4.1 (additive Gaussian noise) is needed (R3).  
  - It remains unclear why the complete characterization of optimal arms from Lee & Bareinboim (2018) cannot be used to relax Assumption 4.3 (R3).  
- **Missing evaluation of feature selection errors:** The algorithm relies on an initial feature selection stage to identify plausible parents. The paper reports only false positive rates (Figure 4) but does not measure type 2 errors (false negatives when true parents are excluded). A breakdown of underperformance due to type 1 versus type 2 errors would give critical insight into the method’s limitations (R2).
- **Insufficient comparison with related literature:** The paper should review work in causal bandits that also addresses settings without a known causal graph (R1).  
- **Clarity issues:**  
  - Equation 2: why is it conditioned on \(C\) when the target is \(E[Y \mid \xi, G]\)? (R1)  
  - Notation \(X_j^c\) (complement of \(X_j\)) used in the doubly robust estimator is not introduced in the main text (R2).  
  - Section 5 references “causal expected optimization (CEO)” without defining it earlier (R2).  
  - The abstract states “focusing on the direct causal parents … is sufficient for optimization” but is ambiguous; it should explicitly say “focusing *only* on …” to distinguish from prior work that also focuses on parents (R4).  
  - Figure 3: the red line is not defined (R4).  
  - Figure 6: why are “CBO” and “random” straight lines in the right‑hand plots (trials as x‑axis)? (R4)  
- **Missing discussion of extensions:**  
  - Could the combined observational and interventional datasets be pooled during feature selection to yield better error rates? (R2)  
  - How might the approach be extended to cases where optimal interventions are *soft interventions*, or to causal graphs that violate Assumption 4.3? (R2, R3).  
- **Typos:**  
  - Line 167: \(P(Y \vert X_s = \nu, C)\) (R1).  
  - Line 215: “For the algorithm” repeated (R1, R4).  
  - Line 251: missing period (R1).  
  - Figure 4: typo “an interventions” (R4).  
  - Line 421 and line 460: typos (R4).
