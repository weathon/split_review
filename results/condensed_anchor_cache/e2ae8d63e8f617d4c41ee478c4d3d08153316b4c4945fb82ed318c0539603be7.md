## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Orthogonality-Informed Adaptive Low-Rank (OIALR) training, which uses SVD decomposition of weight matrices to only update the singular values (Σ) during most of training after an initial full-rank phase. The method aims to reduce trainable parameters while maintaining accuracy. Evaluations are performed on OneFormer (CityScapes), mini-ViT (CIFAR-10), and Autoformer (ETTm2), among others. Reviewer opinions diverge: one reviewer (score 6, confidence 1 – very low) finds the experimental methodology comprehensive and the method effective; three reviewers (scores 3, 3, 5 – confidence 4) consider the contribution marginal, the experimental validation incomplete, and key claims unsupported.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, well-organized, and easy to follow. (R2, R3, R4)
- The motivation—exploiting orthogonality in SVD bases for low-rank training—is clear and reasonable. (R3, R4)
- The idea of updating only the Σ matrix of a low-rank decomposition is interesting. (R4)
- Experimental evaluations cover diverse tasks and architectures: OneFormer on CityScapes, mini-ViT on CIFAR-10, and Autoformer on ETTm2, demonstrating versatility. (R1, R2)
- One reviewer (score 6) praises the rigorous and comprehensive experimental methodology, including use of standard tuning procedures. (R1)
- The reviewer appreciates that untuned results are reported alongside tuned ones. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal technical contribution.** Orthogonal neural networks and low-rank neural networks are widely studied; Algorithm 2 appears to be an incremental variation with no clear departure from existing methods (e.g., matrix factorization, tensor decomposition, low-rank fine-tuning via SVD). (R2) The paper does not adequately highlight unique innovation. (R2)
- **Lack of comparison with related low-rank baselines.** No empirical comparison is provided against established low-rank methods, such as low-rank matrix factorization, tensor decomposition, or low-rank approximation of a well-trained full-rank network. This makes it unconvincing to distinguish OIALR from prior work. (R2) Experiments should include baselines like low-rank approximation/fine-tuning at the last step. (R2 Q2, Q3)
- **Limited experimental validation.** Only two models were tested in the main experiments (one reviewer claims); the benchmark set is small and lacks comparisons to prior work, making it hard to verify effectiveness. (R3) Although another reviewer lists three architectures, the absence of baseline low-rank methods is a shared weakness. (R2, R3) More models and comparisons are needed. (R3)
- **Unsubstantiated claims about training time and memory reduction.** (R4)
    - Training time does not decrease significantly despite parameter reduction (e.g., from 100% to 9.97%); it is nearly identical or sometimes longer. (R1, R4)
    - Memory allocation is problematic: factorizing a matrix into three matrices increases memory cost, and this persists until the rank r is reduced to a small value. (R4)
    - RAM consumption savings are plausible but lack empirical validation (no table comparing RAM usage between OIALR and baseline). (R4)
- **Algorithm is not purely low-rank.** The method starts with full-rank training in the initial epochs, making it more akin to “full-rank to low-rank training.” The effect of removing the full-rank phase is not studied. (R3)
- **Lack of discussion on differences and advantages/disadvantages compared to prior low-rank methods.** The paper omits a rigorous analysis of computational complexity, convergence properties, and hyperparameter sensitivity. (R2) The size of the trainable Σ is the same as the original weight W = UΣV^T; it is unclear what advantage OIALR offers over standard full-rank training of W or a low-rank approximation followed by fine-tuning. (R2 Q4)
- **Missing formal definition of “stability.”** The paper argues stability of the orthogonal bases but does not provide a formal definition or explain why the proposed method achieves stability over other low-rank approaches. (R2 Q5)
- **No comparison to pruning, quantization, or sparse training methods.** The advantages over such techniques are not addressed. (R3)
- **Inconsistency in reporting tuned/untuned results.** Some experiments show both, others do not; the reason is unclear (possibly time/resource constraints). (R4)
- **Questions about Figure 1.** The stability measure initially declines and then rises; the cause of the early drop is not explained. (R4)
- **Clarification needed on tensor flattening.** The description of collapsing dimensions for tensors with more than two dimensions (e.g., a×b×c → a×(bc)) should be confirmed. (R4)
- **Minority reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (score 6) considers the experimental methodology comprehensive and the method capable of outperforming conventional setups with tuning, but this assessment has very low confidence (1). (R1) The other three reviewers find the experiments insufficient and the evidence for resource reduction weak. (R2, R3, R4)
