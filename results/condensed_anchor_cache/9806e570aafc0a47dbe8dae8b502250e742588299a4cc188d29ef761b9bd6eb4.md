## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents GraphBridge, a framework for arbitrary transfer learning across tasks and domains in Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). It augments pre-trained GNNs with prediction heads and a bridging network (a side MLP), using two tuning methods: Graph-Scaffold Side-Tune (GSST) and Graph-Merge Side-Tune (GMST). The framework is evaluated on 16 datasets across four difficulty levels (a “Task Pyramid”). Reviewers are split: two strongly positive (scores 8, confidence 3-4) praising novelty and comprehensiveness; two more critical (scores 6, confidence 4) questioning technical novelty, experimental details, and analysis depth.

### Strengths
- **Novel approach to arbitrary GNN transfer learning**: GraphBridge allows pre-trained GNNs to transfer across tasks (graph, node, etc.) and domains without modifying task configurations or graph structures; combining prediction heads and a bridging network to preserve pretrained knowledge while enabling outputs of arbitrary dimension. [R1, R3, R4]
- **Resource-efficient tuning**: Achieves high efficiency with significantly fewer parameters (5%-20% of typical models) while maintaining comparable performance. The side-tuning design (GSST, GMST) rationally mitigates negative transfer. [R1, R2, R3]
- **Extensive experimental validation**: Evaluated on 16 datasets covering multiple transfer scenarios (Graph2Graph, Node2Node, Graph2Node, graph2point-cloud), demonstrating adaptability and robustness. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- **Addresses a practical barrier**: The framework tackles the important issue of reusing GNN knowledge across heterogeneous data, which is significant for real-world applications (e.g., molecular property prediction, social networks). [R4]
- **Clear exposition and diagrams**: The paper is well-structured, with clean diagrams that aid understanding. [R3]

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty and architectural exploration**: The overall framework is a straightforward application of the pre-train/fine-tune paradigm, lacking graph-specific innovations. The side network is a simple MLP without comparison to more sophisticated architectures (e.g., attention mechanisms, co-attention networks used in multimodal models). Ablation studies on different side network designs are missing. [R2, R3; R1, R4 less critical]  
  - *Minority review (R3) suggests comparing with domain adaptation approaches to position side-tuning in broader context.*
- **Dependence on high-quality pre-trained GNNs**: Performance is intrinsically tied to the quality of the pre-trained model, yet the paper does not explore implications of varying pre-training quality or the computational cost/data requirements to obtain such models. [R1]
- **Limited/skewed baseline comparisons**: Baselines are few and not recent; the paper lacks a comprehensive comparison with state-of-the-art methods, making it difficult to assess true performance advantages. [R1]
- **Insufficient analysis of underperformance cases**: Improvements are limited on some datasets (e.g., Table 1, Easy Scenario; Table 3 Hard Task: Amazon and Flickr underperform compared to FT). The paper does not adequately explain why, e.g., the impact of label imbalance or dataset structure on performance. [R2, R4]
- **Missing analysis of computational efficiency**: The paper does not compare time and memory complexity of the side-tuning approach against full fine-tuning, either theoretically or empirically. [R2]
- **Lack of clarity in method selection and hyperparameters**:  
  - Criteria for choosing between GSST and GMST are not specified; practitioners need explicit guidance based on source/task characteristics. [R2, R3]  
  - The learnable alpha parameter (in loss combination) lacks constraints; it may allow the MLP part to dominate, effectively disabling the pretrained GNN. [R2]
- **Need for interpretability and visualization**: The paper does not explore interpretability of GraphBridge’s decision-making. Additional visualizations (e.g., confusion matrices) would help intuitive understanding of performance. [R4]
- **Adaptation to domain-specific nuances**: Some domain-specific adaptations may still be required; the paper does not thoroughly analyze how the framework handles highly heterogeneous feature spaces or graph structures, nor how much manual intervention is needed. [R1]
- **Open questions about scope**: The paper does not discuss adaptation to hypergraphs or provide proof of wide applicability of its Task Pyramid. [R1, R3]
