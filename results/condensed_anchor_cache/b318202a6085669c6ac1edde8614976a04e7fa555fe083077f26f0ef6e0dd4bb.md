## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SynQ (Synthesis-aware Fine-tuning for Zero-shot Quantization), a framework addressing three identified limitations of existing zero-shot quantization (ZSQ) methods: (1) high-frequency noise in synthetic datasets, (2) off-target pattern predictions, and (3) misguidance from erroneous hard labels. SynQ applies a low-pass filter for denoising, optimizes class activation map (CAM) alignment between full-precision and quantized models, and uses soft labels (omitting CE loss) for difficult samples. Extensive experiments on image classification tasks (ImageNet, CIFAR-10/100) with CNN and ViT architectures show improved accuracy over prior ZSQ methods, and the paper is generally well-structured.

### Strengths
- The paper clearly identifies and tackles three key limitations of ZSQ (noise, off-target patterns, hard label misguidance). The observations are interesting and the proposed solutions appear feasible.
- SynQ is the first ZSQ method, to the reviewer’s knowledge, to complete experiments on both CNN and ViT models – this breadth is appreciated.
- The use of a low-pass filter for denoising is a good point that highlights the importance of classical signal processing techniques. The visualization of the magnitude spectrum in Figure 1 is particularly engaging.
- The identification of the off-target prediction problem specific to the data-free quantization scenario is a good suggestion, and using Grad-CAM alignment as a loss function is novel.
- The paper provides detailed analyses of the three components and various experiments (e.g., ablation studies, comparisons on CNN and ViT) that enhance persuasiveness.
- The performance is validated through comparisons with multiple ZSQ baselines, and the paper is well-written and easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient prior work analysis and missing SOTA baselines:** The paper claims novelty for using soft labels over hard labels based on sample difficulty, but prior works such as Qimera (uses coefficient of superposed latent embedding as soft labels) and AIT (uses soft labels only) already address this. Moreover, current state-of-the-art results are omitted: GENIE (Jeon et al., CVPR 2023) shows better performance on CNN, and PSAQ-ViT V2 shows better results on transformer variants. These should be addressed and included as baselines.
- **Limited experimental evidence for claimed observations:** Most experiments supporting the three observations are conducted under restricted conditions. Figure 5 shows only TexQ among baseline models; the analysis for CIFAR-10/100 used in Table 1 is omitted. Figure 2 shows a heatmap for only one sample image. It is therefore difficult to confirm whether these phenomena are general across ZSQ methods or only observed in limited baselines/datasets. The authors should provide evidence across multiple baselines and datasets (e.g., Figure 5 extended to other methods, heatmaps for multiple samples).
- **Missing evaluation under low-bitwidth settings:** SynQ is evaluated on W3 and W4, but not on extremely low-bit (e.g., W2). Baselines like GENIE show W2 results; such experiments should be added. Also, performance variation with the size of the synthetic dataset is not analyzed.
- **Lack of computational cost analysis:** The paper provides no comparison of computational overhead introduced by SynQ (especially the low-pass filter and CAM alignment) relative to state-of-the-art methods. This analysis is needed.
- **Insufficient analysis of noise and low-pass filter:** The paper does not discuss how SynQ handles different types or levels of noise in synthetic datasets, nor whether performance is consistent across noise levels. No generated images (before/after low-pass filter) are shown, which would help understand the proposal. Does the low-pass filter have advantages over other ZSQ denoising approaches?
- **Unclear generality of off-target prediction and difficulty measurement:** It is unclear whether the off-target prediction problem occurs only in the data-free scenario or also with real data – does the paper examine this? For the hard sample filtering, the analysis in Figure 7 is limited: does the optimal τ value (0.5) hold across different models (ResNet, MobileNet, etc.) and datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet)? Also, the λ_CAM magnitudes in Figure 7(a) are much larger than λ_CE; is the CAM loss magnitude on average much smaller than the CE loss?
- **Limited generalization to other tasks:** The method is only evaluated on image classification; it is unclear how SynQ would perform on object detection or segmentation.
- **Minor presentation issues:** The paper repeats the limitations of prior works too much (over 3 times). Table 1 header incorrectly says “CIFAR dataset” (line 382). The experimental setting for the baseline in Table 3 (quantization algorithm) is not clearly specified; the same result is not in Table 1. In Table 5 of the appendix, experiments are based only on 3 generator-based works, but SynQ uses noise optimization; why are noise-optimization works not addressed? Those 3 works are improved by SynQ but still worse than SynQ itself – the authors should provide an opinion on this. Could SynQ be applied to other noise-optimization works?
