## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper presents RetroInText, a multimodal LLM-based framework for retrosynthetic planning that integrates molecular graph (2D), 3D conformation, and textual descriptions of reaction procedures (generated by ChatGPT). It fine-tunes MolT5 for single-step retrosynthesis and uses an attention-based fusion module to combine modalities. An A* search algorithm guided by a prediction score (synthetic complexity, reaction cost, captioning score) performs multi-step planning. On USPTO RetroBench, the method achieves up to 5% Top-1 accuracy improvement over semi-template-free and template-free baselines and is comparable to template-based methods. Two reviewers (scores 8, 8) are enthusiastic, one (score 6) sees modest gains, and one (score 3) raises fundamental concerns about motivation, incorrect claims, and insufficient evaluation.

### Strengths

- **Principled multimodal integration for retrosynthesis.** The framework jointly leverages 2D graph, 3D conformation, and textual reaction descriptions generated by ChatGPT, providing a comprehensive representation that captures both molecular structure and synthetic context. This is an original approach that goes beyond single-modality or single-step methods. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Adaptation of MolT5 for one-step retrosynthesis.** Treating retrosynthesis as a translation problem and fine-tuning a pretrained molecule language model is methodologically clever and underexplored. This suggests that molecule foundation models, rather than language-only foundation models, may be better suited for chemistry tasks. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4) *Contrast: Reviewer 3 correctly notes that MolT5 is not an LLM (line 479), but the adaptation itself is still praised by others.*
- **Novel use of LLM-generated textual descriptions for reaction context.** Generating free-text descriptions of synthetic routes via ChatGPT and incorporating them via in-context learning enriches the model with chemical reasoning beyond structure. This idea could inspire future work in LLM-assisted retrosynthesis. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Robust search algorithm with compound scoring.** The A* search uses three scores (synthetic complexity, reaction cost as negative log likelihood, and captioning score) to prioritize reactants. The prediction score effectively combines these to guide multi-step planning, bridging the gap between single-step models and real-world multi-step cost-aware synthesis. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Use of set-wise matching metric.** Instead of search success rate, the evaluation uses set-wise matching between predicted and reference starting materials in the RetroBench test set, which more accurately reflects practical multi-step retrosynthesis performance. (Reviewer 4)
- **Ablation studies clarify component contributions.** The paper thoroughly evaluates the roles of textual and structural data, though the performance gains from individual components are modest. (Reviewer 2)
- **Outperforms semi-template-free and template-free baselines, comparable to template-based methods.** This is noteworthy because the method relies on LLM generalization via in-context learning without inductive bias from templates. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses

- **Modest performance gains relative to complexity.** The Top-1 accuracy improvement is only 5% over baselines, which does not fully justify the added complexity of multimodal integration (graph, 3D, text, LLM fine-tuning, and search scoring). The 5% gain is not contextualized against typical improvements in the field. (Reviewer 2)
- **Unclear motivation for using text over graph/SMILES.** Lines 44-46 claim graph and SMILES are limited, but recent studies show graph- or SMILES-based models often outperform text-only models, including advanced LLMs. The paper does not convincingly explain how text helps resolve the alleged limitations. (Reviewer 3)
- **Lack of justification for ChatGPT-generated descriptions.** It is bold to assume ChatGPT (specifically GPT-3.5, now relatively old) can generate accurate and reliable reaction procedures. No systematic evaluation or quality control of these descriptions is provided, nor an analysis of how errors might mislead the framework. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **No exploration of alternative text generators.** The paper uses GPT-3.5 but does not compare with newer models (GPT-4o, Llama 3) or chemistry-specific LLMs. The prompt used is also not disclosed or analyzed. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Incorrect claim about MolT5.** Line 479 and Figure 1 refer to MolT5 as an LLM, but it is a text-to-text transformer for SMILES, not a general-purpose LLM. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing ablation: 1D molecular sequence.** To demonstrate the advantage of 3D spatial representation, the authors should ablate by substituting the graph encoder with a 1D molecular sequence (e.g., SMILES) encoder. (Reviewer 1)
- **Unclear justification for mutual information objective.** The motivation for maximizing mutual information between 2D graph and 3D conformation is not sufficiently presented; Table 3 does not resolve this. (Reviewer 1)
- **Attention-based fusion not compared with alternatives.** The paper does not justify why the chosen cross-attention fusion is optimal—no comparison with other fusion strategies (e.g., co-attention, simple concatenation) is provided. The computational cost and efficiency are also not discussed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing specification of search algorithm details.** The A* algorithm parameters (search iterations, candidates per step, time limits) are not given. The difference between Retro* and Retro*-0 search algorithms is not explained. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Evaluation focuses only on Top-k accuracy.** No chemical interpretability analysis, no assessment of predicted pathway quality or chemical validity, and no comparison with experimentally validated routes. This limits practical utility. (Reviewer 2)
- **No error bars or uncertainty in main results.** Performance numbers are reported without standard deviation, making comparison across models unreliable, especially given seed sensitivity of attention-based models. (Reviewer 2)
- **Incomplete explanation of prediction score training.** Equation 8c mentions only synthetic complexity score and reaction cost, but Figure 1 includes a captioning score. It is unclear how all three scores are combined in the search process. (Reviewer 4)
- **Writing clarity and figure quality issues.** Figure 1 is not sharp (should be a vector diagram). The paper would benefit from restructuring into distinct sections for the retrosynthesis model and the scoring method for search. (Reviewer 4) Citation errors exist: ChatGPT citation is incorrect (line 70), and ScScore citation is missing (line 75). (Reviewer 3)
- **Lack of case studies and systematic evaluation of ChatGPT description quality.** No examples or metrics assessing the accuracy and relevance of the generated reaction descriptions. (Reviewer 3)
- **Metric may miss useful alternative routes.** Exact match on the dataset's reference route may not capture valid alternative synthetic pathways suggested by the model. (Reviewer 3)
- **Inference time not reported.** Compared to baseline methods, how much longer does the full RetroInText framework take per molecule? (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Could the score be used as a reward for residual energy-based model reranking?** This question from Reviewer 4 suggests potential extension but also points to an unexplored direction.
