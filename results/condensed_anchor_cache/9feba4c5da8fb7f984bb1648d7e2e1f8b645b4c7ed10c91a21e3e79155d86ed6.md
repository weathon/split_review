## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a post-processing method to enforce topological constraints (non-intersection, user-defined contact ratio, minimum distance) between organs in 3D reconstructions. It fits individual DeepSDFs to nn-UNet segmentations of each organ and optimizes four loss functions based on surface-aware Monte Carlo sampling of signed distances. The method is evaluated on whole-heart (four chambers) and lumbar spine datasets, showing improved contact/interpenetration metrics vs. baseline segmentation and vanilla DeepSDF, with an ablation study confirming all loss components contribute.

### Strengths
- **Significant and under-explored problem.** Multi-organ implicit reconstruction with topological constraints is a worthwhile and relevant topic; most prior work focuses on single objects or local surface artefacts, not global organ contact priors (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Clear, professional presentation.** Figures (especially Fig. 2, 3) effectively illustrate the sampling strategy and qualitative improvements. Writing is easy to follow with no major typos (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Effective loss formulation.** The four loss functions (intersecting, contact, non-contact, data) combined with sampling produce measurable improvements over nn-UNet and vanilla DeepSDF, particularly for out-of-distribution data where prior methods fail (R1). The ablation study supports the necessity of each term (R3).
- **Medical prior conditioning.** The idea to condition contact/distance losses on user-defined values (e.g., 27% contact for LV–myocardium) is interesting and well-motivated for healthy anatomies (R1, R4).
- **Decent reproducibility.** An expert can re-implement extending the public DeepSDF code with the multi-object losses, though code is not released (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient comparison to prior art.** The paper claims the constraints have not been considered before, but several existing works already propose contact/non-interpenetration losses for multi-organ implicit modeling (Zhang et al. TOG 2022, Liu et al. AAAI 2024) and for topological medical segmentation (Gupta et al. ECCV 2022, Ganaye et al. MedIA 2019, Reddy et al. 2019). Additionally, regularized DeepSDF variants (Sander et al., Kong et al., Verhülsdonk et al.) achieve better topological preservation. No systematic comparison to these methods is provided; evaluation is only against nn-UNet and vanilla DeepSDF, which is outdated and obviously disadvantaged (R1, R3, R4).
- **Loss design lacks justification and has redundancy.** The choice of four loss functions is heuristic with no theoretical or biological motivation beyond the ablation study (R3). Moreover, the intersecting loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{intersecting}}$ is mathematically redundant with the contact loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{contact}}$ (as derived by R4: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{contact}}$ already contains $\mathcal{L}_{\text{intersecting}}$ plus $\mathcal{L}_{\text{outside}}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{single}}$ terms). The formulation strongly resembles the attraction–repulsion loss $\mathcal{L}^\mathcal{C}$ in Liu et al. 2024, with only minor differences (absolute sum vs. sum of absolutes for points inside a single object) (R4).
- **Medical grounding and clinical applicability unclear.** The paper uses a 27% contact ratio for left ventricle and myocardium but does not clarify where this value originates; the cited reference (Buckberg et al. 2018) does not seem to contain it. More importantly, enforcing healthy-population constraints biases predictions toward normal anatomy—the authors restrict to healthy subjects but do not discuss the clinical impact when pathology violates these priors, nor how a patient-specific prior could be determined. This limitation is acknowledged but not explored experimentally (R1, R4).
- **Methodology clarity issues.** The refinement of nn-UNet segmentations via the SDF latent vector is asserted (L98–99) but never explained; the exact integration is missing (R1). The point sets $\mathcal{P}_{\text{contact}}$, $\mathcal{P}_{\text{non-contact}}$, $\mathcal{P}_{\text{intersecting}}$ are pre-defined based on the overfitted DeepSDF—if the initial DeepSDF or segmentation is inaccurate, these sets will be incorrect and the optimization cannot correct the errors, potentially introducing artifacts. The method does not discuss how to handle such inaccuracies or maintain image consistency after optimization (R2). The contact-ratio and minimum-distance losses appear disconnected in formalism despite targeting the same distance range (R4).
- **Runtime concerns.** The sampling procedure uses 300k points per iteration for 10 iterations, which likely results in poorer runtime compared to regularized DeepSDF approaches that do not require such extensive sampling (R3). No runtime numbers are reported (R3).
- **Presentation of results.** In Figure 8, the distribution of topologically meaningful points near the interfaces is hard to recognize (R3). Some loss function fonts are inconsistent (R4).
- **Disagreement among reviewers.** One reviewer (R1, score 5, confidence 4) found the method impressive, especially on OOD data, and the losses sufficiently novel. The other three reviewers (R2, R3, R4, scores 3, confidence 5) considered the evaluation insufficient, the novelty overstated given prior art, and the medical grounding lacking. This positive minority view is noted but the majority critique highlights fundamental weaknesses.
