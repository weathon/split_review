## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents LocoGS, a locality-aware 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework that exploits spatial coherence of Gaussian attributes for compact scene modeling. It uses multi-resolution hash grids to encode locally-coherent attributes, combined with adaptive spherical harmonics bandwidth, pruning, dense initialization (via a Nerfacto warm-up), quantization, and entropy encoding. Experiments show significant compression (54.6×–96.6× vs. 3DGS) and rendering speed improvements (2.1×–2.4×) while maintaining or improving quality, with comparable compression to state-of-the-art methods but higher speed.

### Strengths
1. **Novel locality-aware representation** – The strategy of exploiting spatial coherence via multi-resolution hash grids is effective and novel. The paper illustrates locality with graphs and clearly distinguishes the approach from anchor-based methods (R2 notes the concept is not entirely novel but the illustration and differentiation are strong; R4 considers the approach novel).
2. **Clear writing and logical flow** – The paper is well-structured, with excellent categorization of compression methods (R1, R2, R3, R4).
3. **Comprehensive design** – Includes careful handling of initialization, pruning, and different encoding schemes per attribute. The storage size breakdown in Tab.3 gives valuable insight into compression bottlenecks (R1).
4. **Dense initialization shows improvement** – The warm-up trick improves compression performance, though R3 notes it may be unfair to baselines (see Weakness 4) (R1, R3).
5. **Significant performance gains** – Outperforms prior methods in storage, rendering speed, and quality (R2, R3).
6. **Detailed technical implementation** – Provides many useful details for applying quantization and entropy encoding, benefiting researchers in the area (R2).

### Weaknesses
1. **Extra training time** – The method requires ≈1 hour more training than baselines; dense initialization is a contributing factor. Real-applicability is limited unless decoding time is reported and optimized (R1, R3). R1 requests decoding time to assess applicability.
2. **Missing Gaussian counts for variants** – Tab.3 lacks the number of Gaussians for ablation versions (e.g., Ours-Sparse has lower FPS). Including counts along iterations would clarify the influence of initialization and whether it could help prior methods (R1).
3. **Novelty of locality is incremental** – The idea of exploiting locality in 3DGS is not new. The related-work section does not adequately address locality properties of anchor-based methods; the distinct advantages of LocoGS over such methods need sharper emphasis (R2). R4 conversely praises novelty but also notes that the local-coherence analogy to pixel values lacks rigorous mathematical justification beyond visual examples.
4. **Dense initialization raises fairness concerns** – The use of Nerfacto (instead of 3DGS) for warm-up may give an unfair advantage since all baselines start from COLMAP points. The number of Nerfacto training iterations is not specified, and the same warm-up could potentially be applied to prior methods (R3). R1 notes it “demonstrates an interesting improvement,” but does not see it as unfair.
5. **Unassessed large-scale / challenging scenes** – Performance on large datasets (e.g., Mega-NeRF, Urbanscene3D, MatrixCity, Miller‑19) is not shown. The paper lacks analysis of trade-offs in large-scale scenarios where compression is most critical, and does not discuss failure cases (e.g., metal, lighting areas) (R3, R4).
6. **Rendering slower than Scaffold-GS** – LocoGS gets lower FPS than Scaffold-GS; it is unclear whether the MLP view input is discarded when predicting attributes (beyond quantization/encoding). This missing comparison/justification weakens the claim of speed (R3).
7. **High component complexity** – Multiple components (hash grids, adaptive SH bandwidth, pruning, etc.) complicate implementation and tuning. Encoding schemes are not compared against alternatives nor justified in terms of efficiency/error rates (R4). R2 also asks for explanation of how/why three explicit attributes were selected.
8. **Missing supplementary material** – No demos are submitted; code release is not confirmed, affecting reproducibility (R3, R4).
