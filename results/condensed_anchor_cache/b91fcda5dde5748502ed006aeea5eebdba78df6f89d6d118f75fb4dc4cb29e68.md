## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces the Implicit Channel Protocol (ICP) framework for collaborative multi-agent reinforcement learning, enabling implicit communication when explicit messaging is unavailable. ICP uses a subset of "scouting actions" (actions that do not change the environment state) to encode and decode messages via a learned mapping. Two training algorithms are proposed: learning with a randomly initialized information map and with a delayed information map (first pre-trained with an explicit channel, then fixed). The framework is evaluated on Guessing Number, Revealing Goals, and the Hanabi card game, outperforming baseline methods (primarily value decomposition networks, VDN) through more efficient information transmission.

### Strengths
- **Problem importance and approach**: The challenge of constructing implicit communication channels is highly relevant to multi-agent RL, and the paper takes valuable steps to address it (Reviewers 1, 4). The hat-mapping strategy is a clever application of a classic logic problem to a broader communication setting (Reviewer 4).
- **Clarity and presentation**: The paper is well-structured, easy to follow, with clear definitions of notations and sufficient implementation details for the environments (Reviewers 1, 4). The algorithm and environments are straightforward to understand (Reviewer 4).
- **Experimental results**: ICP demonstrates effective information transmission and superior performance over baselines on the two newly designed benchmarks (Guessing Number, Revealing Goals) (Reviewers 1, 3). The method is interesting for efficient multi-agent communication by generating an information table with respect to observations and messages (Reviewer 2).
- **Reducing computational complexity**: The work contributes to reducing complexity in intention inference techniques, which is valuable for large-scale multi-agent systems (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear distinction between explicit and implicit communication**: The line between explicit and implicit communication remains blurry. In environments like Revealing Goals, agents could communicate with directional movement actions, yet ICP restricts implicit communication to scouting actions that do not change the state. The definition of scouting actions feels too narrow (Reviewer 3).
- **Heavy assumptions and limited applicability**: The framework assumes broadcasting is possible, which is not feasible in many practical scenarios (Reviewer 3). It also requires pre-identified scouting actions; the paper does not discuss how to systematically identify or design such actions in environments where they are not readily available (e.g., complex robotic manipulation) (Reviewer 1). One reviewer questioned whether the technique can be used where there is no clear scouting/regular action delineation (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**: The paper only compares against VDN, which does not perform communication. Missing comparisons with standard explicit communication architectures such as CommNet, TarMAC, SARNet, and DIAL (Reviewers 2, 4). DIAL should be added as an explicit baseline (Reviewer 4). The performance difference between ICP and the SAD baseline (green dashed line) is very close, which does not strongly support the claimed superiority (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient experimental scope**: All studied environments share a common quirk – agents cannot see their own information but can see others’ and must communicate it. The approach would be more convincing with more diverse communication challenges (Reviewer 4). The paper does not discuss scalability to larger numbers of agents (10, 20, 50) or heterogeneous agents, nor does it consider more dynamic environments (e.g., predator-prey, cooperative navigation) (Reviewer 2). Limitations regarding action space size are also not discussed (Reviewer 2).
- **Inconsistent evaluation of the two proposed techniques**: The randomly initialized map and delayed map are only compared in the Hanabi game; results for both techniques across all environments are needed (Reviewer 4). In particular, one reviewer explicitly asks: "How does the delayed map approach perform in Guessing Number and Revealing Goals?" (Reviewer 4).
- **Potential rule violation in Hanabi**: Since ICP implicitly encodes local observations into message vectors, each agent may have access to complete global state information, which could break the rules of Hanabi (where hints are only allowed to reveal color or number). Clarification is needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing explanations and minor issues**: The paper does not clarify whether the action space for agents trained with VDN is the combination of "regular actions" and "scouting actions" (Reviewer 1). The reasons for different training lengths in Figure 3b are not explained (Reviewer 1). The phrase "efficient embedding techniques" (line 268) is vague and requires clarification (Reviewer 3). There are many grammatical errors, especially with singular/plural nouns and missing definite articles (Reviewer 4). A diagram of the algorithm pipeline would improve understanding (Reviewer 1).
