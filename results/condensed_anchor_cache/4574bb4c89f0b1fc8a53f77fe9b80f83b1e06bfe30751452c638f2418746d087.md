## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers agree that UltraLightUNet is a lightweight U-shaped network for medical image segmentation with low parameter count and FLOPs, evaluated on multiple datasets. However, there is strong disagreement: two reviewers (scores 1, high confidence) argue the architecture is essentially identical to the CVPR 2024 EMCAD paper (not cited) and shows low novelty, with insufficient baselines and unclear motivation. Two other reviewers (scores 6 and 5) find the model original and well-validated, though they note limitations in theoretical justification, lack of complex/multi-class evaluation, and missing ablation studies.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and methodically explains architecture components (MKIR, MKIRA, GAG, CMFA), making it accessible.
- The reported experimental results are thorough, covering diverse datasets and settings (11 datasets, multiple tasks). The multi-kernel structures effectively capture multi-scale contexts, enhancing segmentation accuracy without adding complexity.
- The model achieves low parameter count (0.316M for 2D, 1.42M for 3D) and low FLOPs (0.314G and 7.1G), while outperforming heavier architectures (TransUNet, SwinUNETR, nn-UNet) and lightweight models (UNeXt, MedT, CMUNeXt, EGE-UNet, Rolling-UNet, UltraLight_VM_UNet). This demonstrates a practical trade-off for resource-constrained environments.
- In-depth ablation studies and parameter considerations are presented (reviewer 1). The methodology appears well-supported by rigorous experimental validation, with strong performance gains and practical implications.
- The model's lightweight design and high accuracy address a critical need for real-time and point-of-care diagnostics; open-source code is promised, which could promote further research.
- Results and methods are thoughtfully described.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and similarity to prior work**: Two reviewers (1 and 2) strongly argue that the architecture is nearly identical to the CVPR 2024 paper EMCAD (not cited) with almost no change in encoder/decoder modules. Reviewers also note that the MKDC module (depth-wise conv + pooling) is similar to prior attention mechanisms (e.g., CBAM, Scale-Aware Modulation). The overall network resembles [3][4] and uses ideas from ConvNeXt without discussing lightweight versions. In contrast, reviewer 3 considers the combination of multi-kernel convolutions and attention as a novel approach. This discrepancy highlights a major concern about originality.
- **Insufficient baselines and comparisons**: 
  - Not compared to certain SOTA networks (e.g., [2]) that perform better on datasets like ISIC (reviewer 1).
  - For Synapse, MSD Prostate, and FETA, only few baseline methods reported, and the performance for baselines is lower than in original papers (e.g., Swin UNet: 79.13 reported originally vs 77.58 here). Authors need to ensure baselines are fully optimized if they ran experiments.
  - Only seven 3D methods proposed before 2022 are compared; newer lightweight 3D segmentation networks (2023-2024) are not included, even though they may achieve better performance with lower complexity (reviewer 2).
- **Lack of training/inference time reporting**: The model uses depthwise convolutions which take longer to train than standard convolutions. Training and test time should be reported to fully demonstrate efficiency (reviewer 2).
- **Limited evaluation scope**: 
  - Datasets primarily involve simple binary segmentation of well-defined anatomical structures, not complex tasks like CT/MRI tumor/lesion segmentation with irregular shapes, varying intensities, and ambiguous boundaries (reviewer 3).
  - Multi-class segmentation is not well explored; only overall DSC reported for multi-class tasks, lacking organ-specific scores. The model's ability to differentiate multiple tissue types or disease states (e.g., tumor subregions) is not demonstrated (reviewers 2 and 3).
  - No p-values or standard deviations reported for experimental results (reviewer 2).
- **Lack of theoretical justification**: 
  - The method section lacks clear evidence or mathematical proof for why specific block designs (MKIR, MKIRA, GAG, CMFA) improve performance or reduce computational costs. Equations are described but do not provide rigorous insight (reviewers 2 and 4).
  - Insufficient motivation for the design: the review of Vision Transformers in related work is not relevant to the CNN/attention architecture used. The paper does not explain how design relates to challenges of medical image segmentation (reviewer 2).
  - The multi-scale approach lacks novel theoretical contribution; it does not clearly distinguish from existing multi-scale methods (e.g., SFCNs for breast cancer) (reviewer 4).
- **Missing ablation studies**: No ablation studies evaluating individual contributions of each module (MKIR, MKIRA, GAG, CMFA) on both segmentation performance and parameter/FLOP efficiency. This would identify the most critical component (reviewer 4; reviewer 1 mentioned ablation studies but reviewer 4 finds them missing).
- **No model interpretability**: The interpretability of attention-based modules (CMFA, MKDC) is not discussed; saliency maps or similar visualizations are absent (reviewer 2).
- **Limited overall impact**: The improvement over existing methods is small in some cases (e.g., Polyp DSC: 93.48 vs 93.29/93.18; Synapse: 78.68 vs 78.40) (reviewer 2). However, another reviewer notes the model outperforms many heavier architectures, so the small gains are with much lower complexity.
- **Missing limitations discussion**: The paper does not discuss limitations or tradeoffs of the method (reviewer 3). Questions about tradeoffs and applicability to complicated 3D tumors/lesions and multi-class segmentation remain unanswered (reviewers 3 and 4).
- **Clarifications needed**: (a) Differences between UltraLightUNet-T, S, L in terms of layers and model variants (reviewer 4). (b) Clarify how the method has only 27k parameters while EMCAD with same architecture has at least 3M (reviewer 1). (c) Lacking qualitative results for 3D segmentation on Synapse, MSD Prostate, FETA (reviewer 2).
