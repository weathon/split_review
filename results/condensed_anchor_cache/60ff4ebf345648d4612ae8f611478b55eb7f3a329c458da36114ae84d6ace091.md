## Merged Review

### Summary

Reviewers acknowledge the paper’s ambition to address causal discovery in high-dimensional spatio-temporal data using latent variable modelling and variational inference, and they note the empirical performance on synthetic and real climate data. However, there is strong criticism of the theoretical claims and experimental limitations. Most reviewers find the identifiability results weak; one reviewer (R4) argues that the proofs of Theorems 1 and 2 are fundamentally incorrect, a view not shared by all but which casts doubt on the entire theoretical contribution. Additional concerns include missing comparisons, insufficient discussion of assumptions, and lack of clarity in presentation. Ratings range from 3 to 6, with the lower scores driven by theoretical issues and the highest acknowledging the problem’s relevance.

### Strengths

- Novel framework (SPACY) for learning causal structures from spatio-temporal data, explicitly handling the high dimensionality and spatial correlations via latent factors mapped by an RBF kernel.
- Outperforms existing methods (e.g., in MCC and causal graph estimation) on synthetic data and demonstrates scalability to realistic climate systems, identifying known phenomena (NAO, AAO).
- The approach infers both lagged and instantaneous causal links.
- Problem is well-formulated and applicable to relatively high-dimensional data.
- Proof of identifiability (though contested by R4) is considered essential by some reviewers.
- Experiments are conducted on both synthetic and real-world datasets, and source code is provided for reproducibility.

### Weaknesses

- **Fundamental theoretical flaws (R4, strong)**: The proofs of Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 are incorrect. R4 states that the first step of Theorem 1’s proof wrongly equates “equal in distribution” with equality of random variables, and similarly Theorem 2 cannot deduce \(Z = M\tilde{Z}\) from distributional equality. R4 contends the current proof cannot be made correct without additional assumptions, and that linear identifiability (up to an invertible matrix) is insufficient for the intended use case because it does not yield permutation identifiability. R4 references Khemakhem et al. (2020) for correct definitions of identifiability in VAEs and Lachapelle et al. (2022) for constraints needed for permutation identifiability.
- **Weak and incomplete identifiability (R1, R2, R4)**: Identifiability is presented without a proper definition or motivation (first appears line 67). The result (Theorem 2) only gives identifiability up to an invertible linear transformation, which does not recover the causal graph or disentangle latent factors. The claim that the framework can handle “instantaneous edges and overlapping spatial factors” is not supported by identifiability. The term “structural identifiability” (line 532) is misused, as it normally refers to graph identifiability (Peters et al. 2017). R2 also notes that proving the causal graph itself is identifiable would be more useful.
- **No DAG guarantee (R2)**: The method does not ensure that the learned graph (especially instantaneous relations) is acyclic, an important constraint for causal discovery.
- **Limited experimental validation (R2, R3)**: Experiments are only on regular grids; irregular grids are not considered. The question of how/why high MCC scores are achieved despite only linear identifiability (without alignment) remains unanswered (R1).
- **Questionable modelling assumptions (R2, R3)**: A single matrix for the spatial factor may be insufficient to capture complex spatial dependencies (R3). The model assumes conditional independence given spatial factors and the causal graph – this may be violated in real data. Also, instantaneous effects may be undirected and cyclic, not causal.
- **Missing comparisons and discussion (R3)**: The paper lacks comparison with relevant prior work, e.g., Zhao et al. (2023, "Generative Causal Interpretation Model for Spatio-Temporal Representation Learning"). The novelty claim is weakened without such discussion.
- **Scalability and practical issues (R2)**: Variational inference over a complex latent space may be slow and prone to local optima. The authors should discuss scaling with latent space size and strategies for initialization/escape from local optima.
- **Presentation and notation issues (R1)**: The “preliminary” paragraph is placed in the related work section but reads like background. The problem setting uses unclear notation (e.g., \(K\)-dimensional grid but \(K\) not defined). Notation like \(F^l_d\) is less readable than \(F_{ld}\).
- **Time-varying dynamics (R2)**: The paper only considers stationary causal structures; a discussion on how to extend to time-varying scenarios is missing.
