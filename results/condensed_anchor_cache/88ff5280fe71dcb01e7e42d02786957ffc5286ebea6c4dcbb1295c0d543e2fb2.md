## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Quantile-Guided Diffusion Policy (qGDP), an offline RL method that trains a quantile network on IQL-learned Q-values to label the dataset, then conditions a diffusion policy on these quantile labels using classifier-free guidance. This enables adjusting action generation preferences (imitation vs. improvement) without retraining. Experiments on D4RL are reported. Reviewers acknowledge clear writing and informative toy experiments, but express concerns about incremental novelty over IDQL, questionable empirical benefit given small margins and extensive hyperparameter tuning, insufficient justification for value extrapolation in offline RL, and incomplete evaluation.

### Strengths
- **Clear exposition**: The manuscript is well-structured, the algorithm is described clearly, and the toy experiments (bandit, 2D) are informative and help illustrate the behavior (R1, R3, R4).
- **Addresses behavior diversity**: qGDP handles behavior generation diversity by enabling adjustable imitation vs. improvement via quantile conditioning and guidance scale, without retraining (R1, R2).
- **Competitive performance among diffusion-based methods**: qGDP achieves state-of-the-art or comparable results on D4RL relative to other diffusion-based offline RL methods (e.g., IDQL, DQL) (R2, R3; though margins are mostly small).
- **Inference efficiency**: The classifier-free guidance avoids gradient-based action selection at test time, significantly improving inference speed over DQL (which requires gradients through the Q-network) (R4).
- **Flexibility**: Allows tuning action distributions (e.g., toward higher quantiles) without retraining the diffusion model (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty / incremental over IDQL**: Most reviewers view qGDP as a straightforward generalization of IDQL, with the only addition being a quantile network that predicts multiple quantiles simultaneously to avoid retraining across different quantile values. The specific advantages over IDQL are not sufficiently differentiated; results in Table 1 are close between qGDP and IDQL (R2, R3). While some reviewers (R1, R4) consider the problem setting well-motivated and a natural extension, others find the contribution incremental (R2, R3).
- **Unclear empirical benefit / potential over-tuning**: 
  - The main evaluation (Table 1) reports results after sweeping over an extremely large number (70) of hyperparameter configurations, which is unrealistic for offline RL and likely maximizes over statistical noise (R4). When evaluated over a smaller hyperparameter space (Section 6.3), qGDP does not show clear improvement over baselines (R4).
  - Performance gains over IDQL and DQL are mostly small (R2, R3). R3 notes improvement may stem simply from searching a larger space of quantile values or a smaller grid, rather than a genuinely novel mechanism.
  - Overall summary totals in Tables 1–3 lack standard deviations; R4 suggests RLiable analysis to assess statistical significance.
- **Validity of quantile labels and value extrapolation for offline RL**: 
  - Quantile labels rely on Q-values learned by IQL, which are known to suffer from overestimation bias. Errors in Q would propagate to incorrect quantile labels, especially for higher quantiles (R2).
  - The method extrapolates high-value samples outside the training distribution based on reward function trends, but this contradicts the pessimism principle in offline RL (R3). A simple counterexample (reward first increasing then suddenly decreasing) could cause qGDP to fail (R3).
  - The quantile input *y* is constrained to [0,1] (presumably); experiments varying *y* beyond [0,1] to explore generalization are missing (R2).
- **Over-emphasis on guidance scale tuning**: The paper stresses tuning the guidance scale for diversity, but this capability is intrinsic to diffusion models themselves. The main innovation (quantile conditioning) should be analyzed separately for its effect on diversity (R2).
- **Incomplete runtime comparison**: Table 4 compares qGDP’s runtime to DQL (which is an online RL algorithm, not an offline counterpart). A fair comparison should include offline methods such as IDQL and SfBC, with details on which environment is timed (R2, R4). Explanation of why qGDP is faster than DQL is needed (R3).
- **Missing technical details and presentation issues**: 
  - The output structure of the quantile network and the choice of *N* (number of quantiles) are unclear (R3). Equation (4) contains an ambiguous expression: $V_\eta^i(s)i=1^N$ (R3).
  - Equation (11) uses a quantile loss different from Eq. (8); justification is needed (R3).
  - Figure 1 is presented without context (R4). Axis labels and text in Figures 2, 3, 4 are very small (R4).
  - Toy experiments lack a quantitative metric of success; it is unclear what the optimal or desired behavior should be. Ground truth distributions should be shown in Figure 2 (R3, R4).
  - An introductory explanation of quantile networks and their relevance is missing, hindering comprehension (R1).
- **Readability**: Section 5, while informative, is overly long and could be condensed to maintain focus (R1).
