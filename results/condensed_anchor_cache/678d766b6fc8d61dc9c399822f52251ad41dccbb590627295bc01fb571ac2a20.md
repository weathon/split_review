## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses offline RL in combinatorial action spaces (exponential growth from multiple sub-actions). The proposed Branch Value Estimation (BVE) structures the action space as a tree, evaluates only a subset of actions via beam search, and uses behavior-regularized TD learning to reduce overestimation. Experiments show BVE outperforms FAS and IQL across various action space sizes (up to 4M actions). One reviewer (score 5, confidence 5) considers the work original and significant, praising tree-based representation, beam search integration, and handling of sub-action dependencies. Two reviewers (scores 3, confidence 3) find the approach promising but raise concerns about clarity, theoretical grounding, empirical rigor, and comparisons.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles an important and underexplored problem: combinatorial action spaces in offline RL.
- The tree-structured action representation and branch value estimation are novel; the combination of beam search with RL has not been applied this way before.
- The method offers a new angle on addressing overestimation bias in offline RL.
- Empirical results demonstrate clear performance advantages over baselines (FAS, IQL), with consistent performance across environments and scalability up to 4M actions.
- Ablation studies effectively isolate the contribution of each component (though one reviewer found them ambiguous).
- The main idea is clearly explained; complex concepts are supported with effective visualizations, examples, and precise pseudocode.
- The paper follows a logical progression and transparently presents experimental setup and results.
- The computational efficiency improvements are significant; potential applications in healthcare and robotics.
- The method successfully reduces the search space while maintaining performance and handling dependencies between sub-actions (though one reviewer questioned the evidence for capturing dependencies).

### Weaknesses
**Method clarity and justification**
- The construction of the tree and Q-learning based on it is unclear. In Figure 1, are the elements in the third and fourth layers fixed or swappable? The output of \( f(s,a) \) includes \( v \) with dimensionality that varies per tree layer; is the overall output fixed or variable? (Reviewer 3)
- Equation (4) introduces a term discounting \(\|a' - \hat{a}\|\) without justification; the design rationale is missing. (Reviewer 2)
- The interaction between the depth penalty and beam search during action selection is not explained. (Reviewer 1)
- Terminal state handling in the tree structure is not specified. (Reviewer 1)
- The paper claims to eliminate the independence assumption but does not explicitly explain how BVE captures sub-action dependencies; a clearer analysis is needed. (Reviewer 3)

**Theoretical foundations**
- No theoretical analysis of convergence properties or formal bounds on beam search approximation error. (Reviewer 1)
- The conditions under which BVE is guaranteed to perform well are not characterized. (Reviewer 1)
- The relationship between beam search approximation quality and final policy optimality is not analyzed. (Reviewer 1)
- The design concept resembles binary search for the maximum value; developing convergence theory for Q-learning under this search paradigm would strengthen the paper. (Reviewer 3)

**Offline RL validity and overestimation**
- It is unclear how the tree traversal prevents overestimation in an offline setting; the behavior cloning regularization alone may contribute, but no theoretical or empirical evidence isolates the tree-based method's role. (Reviewer 2)
- The offline RL setting appears unnecessary because the method only uses behavior cloning regularization and does not further address out-of-distribution (OOD) or distribution shift issues. The method could be evaluated in an online setting first. (Reviewer 3)
- A relevant multi-agent reference (counterfactual Q-learning for OOD in combinatorial action spaces) is recommended for comparison. (Reviewer 3)

**Empirical evaluation**
- Limited comparisons: only two baselines (FAS and IQL). Recent parallel work on factorized action spaces (OpenReview forum link) offers additional baselines and environments; adaptations of BCQ, CQL, and IQL from that work should be considered. (Reviewer 1, 2)
- The ablation study in Figure 1 is ambiguous: Figure 1(b) shows a difference only when \(\alpha=0\); Figure 1(c) shows DQN performs poorly even in small action spaces, questioning its suitability as a competitor; it is unclear if DQN is used in an offline setting. (Reviewer 2)
- Empirical results in Figures 8 and 9 do not show statistically significant differences. (Reviewer 2)
- The method's robustness to different data distributions is not thoroughly investigated. (Reviewer 1)
- The impact of beam width on the performance–computation trade-off needs more systematic analysis. (Reviewer 1)
- Performance scaling with increasing numbers of sub-actions should be analyzed (e.g., learning curves of performance vs computation time or number of samples). (Reviewer 2)
- The experimental setup is artificial and lacks realistic tasks; experiments on meaningful real-world scenarios (e.g., DeepMind Control Suite with discretised actions) are recommended. (Reviewer 3)

**Scope of claims**
- The claim of "effectively capturing sub-action dependencies" needs stronger evidence from more diverse dependency types. (Reviewer 1)
- The claim of "outperforming state-of-the-art methods" is based on only two baselines. (Reviewer 1)
- The paper suggests the tree structure could be applied to other offline RL methods, but this broader applicability is not explored. (Reviewer 2)

**Missing related work**
- The paper does not cite several relevant works: Conjugate Markov Decision Processes (Thomas et al.), RL with Function-Valued Action Spaces (Pan et al.), Deep RL in Large Discrete Action Spaces (Dulac-Arnold et al.), and Conditionally optimistic exploration for cooperative deep multi-agent RL (Zhao et al.) – the last being multi-agent but with a similar tree-structured action. (Reviewer 2)

**Other inconsistencies**
- The method's potential benefit in online RL settings is not considered; given that online settings are less challenging, it is surprising the authors did not start there. (Reviewer 2)
- Hyperparameter selection for IQL and FAS is not clearly described. (Reviewer 2)
