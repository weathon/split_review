## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the Slot Mixture Module (SMM), a generalization of Slot Attention that replaces the soft k-means clustering with a learnable Gaussian Mixture Model. Slots thus represent both means and diagonal covariances, and the method also learns mixture weights for prior probabilities. Reviewers rated the paper 6, 6, 8, 5, indicating overall positive interest but with reservations. All four reviewers agree the idea is novel, intuitive, and timely, and that SMM improves performance over Slot Attention across several benchmarks. Reviewer 3 (8, confidence 5) is the most enthusiastic, calling the experiments thorough and expecting SMM to become a new standard; Reviewer 4 (5, confidence 4) is the most critical, questioning the significance of the improvements for unsupervised object segmentation and the completeness of the related work and evaluations. Other weaknesses raised include missing ablations, sensitivity to iteration count, qualitative reconstruction issues, and several presentation errors.

### Strengths
- **Novel and intuitive idea**: replacing the k-means clustering in Slot Attention with a Gaussian Mixture Model to model both means and (diagonal) covariances, yielding more expressive slot representations (R1, R2, R3, R4). The method is a simple, elegant extension that has not been explored before (R3).
- **Well-written and easy to follow**: the methodology is clearly motivated and presented (R1, R3).  
- **Timely contribution**: given the growing interest in object-centric learning and Slot Attention (R1, R3).  
- **Demonstrated improvements over Slot Attention**: across image reconstruction, set property prediction, and object discovery on standard benchmarks, particularly on more challenging settings (low resolution, harder datasets) (R1, R2, R3, R4).  
- **Thorough experiments with fair baselines**: comparisons use SA with implicit gradients (the practical state-of-the-art), and ablations against barebones k-Means and GMM show that learning variance is the key beneficial factor (R3).  
- **Qualitative editing experiments (Concept Sampling)**: show steerability of generative factors, lending further credence to the quality of the slot representations (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing ablation on the three modifications** that differentiate SMM from SA (dot product replaced by Gaussian density; updating both mean and covariance; slot representation as combination of mean and covariance). Without analyzing each change individually, their separate contributions are unclear (R2). Reviewer 3 also suggests analyzing whether SMM is more or less prone to splitting a single object across multiple slots compared to SA.
- **Limited object-discovery evaluation**: only performed on ClevrTex, which is simple, and uses the ARI metric that can easily saturate. Reviewer 4 recommends additional evaluation on more complex real-world datasets with metrics like AP (citing NeurIPS 2022). Reviewer 4 further notes that the main improvements are on supervised tasks (reconstruction, set prediction) rather than unsupervised segmentation, making the contribution less appealing for that community.  
- **Lack of discussion on when GMM’s added complexity (learnable variance) is beneficial**: Reviewer 2 asks for concrete scenarios where modeling variance is crucial and whether the evaluation datasets are sufficiently complex to justify the additional complexity. Reviewer 4 similarly questions the overall significance of the contribution.
- **Missing quantitative comparison of attention maps** between SMM and SA. Reviewer 1 suggests using a metric like IoU against ground-truth masks, and suspects that SA may produce more accurate attention maps on complex scenes because of high variance in SMM updates.
- **Sensitivity to the number of iterations**: Appendix Table 9 shows SMM is more sensitive to this hyperparameter than SA. Reviewer 2 asks whether this is due to EM convergence issues or other optimization problems, and notes it could limit applicability when computational resources are scarce.
- **Qualitative reconstruction issues**:  
  - Images in Figure 2 (96 × 96) are blurry; higher resolution (128×128 or 256×256) would allow more thorough visual comparison (R1).  
  - On ClevrTex, SMM reconstructions appear more distorted than those from SA, which is counterintuitive given the increased expressiveness. It is unclear whether this is due to limitations of the Image GPT decoder or to the slot representations themselves (R1).
- **Incomplete related-work discussion**: Reviewer 4 notes the absence of recent methods such as DINOSAUR (ICLR 2023), Odin (ECCV 2022), CutLER (CVPR 2023), Invariant Slot Attention (ICML 2023), and Spotlight Attention (arxiv 2023).
- **Technical and presentation issues**:  
  - Typo in Equation 7: the covariance matrix Σ* should be computed from the updated mean μ*, not the original μₖ (R1).  
  - Equations (1)–(2) use English words instead of mathematical symbols; vectors should be bold (R4).  
  - The function f_θ(x, μ, diag) is not clearly defined: the text gives input dimensions R^{N×D} × R^{K×D} → R^{N×K} but the actual input includes three elements (x, μ, diag); more detail is needed since this is the primary technical contribution (R4).  
  - Inconsistency between text and Algorithm 1: the text says the GRU “takes current and previous slot representations,” but the algorithm feeds only the means (R3).  
  - Figure 1 differs from Algorithm 1 in several ways: the L₂ distance for computing covariance is taken after the GRU‑mean update in the algorithm but before in the figure; the MLP and LayerNorm are absent from the figure (R3).  
  - Writing issues: the penultimate sentence of sec 2.1 paragraph 1 is hard to parse; the second paragraph of sec 2.1 needs “updating iteration as” corrected; sec 4.3’s description of the fourth channel in the spatial broadcast decoder as “for the weights of the mixture component” is confusing because slots already use mixture components π inside SMM (R3).  
- **Redundancy in claimed contributions**: Contributions (2) and (3) both simply describe experimental results and could be combined (R4).  
- **Additional unanswered questions from reviewers** (overlapping with missing analysis):  
  - Do multiple slots ever encode the same object? Does the spatial attention over CNN feature maps (like in SA) prevent this even in SMM? (R3)  
  - The argument that set prediction is a better measure of OCL competency than object discovery is not fully justified (R3).
