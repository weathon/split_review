- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 5, 3, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates categorical distributional reinforcement learning (CDRL) and proposes that its empirical success stems from a risk-sensitive entropy regularization effect. By decomposing the return distribution into an expectation component and a dispersion component (an approximation controlled by a hyperparameter \(\epsilon\)), the authors show that the distributional Bellman operator minimising KL divergence can be reframed as cross-entropy-regularized Neural FQI. This leads to a new framework (DERPI) and actor-critic algorithm (DERAC) linked to MaxEnt RL. Experiments on Atari and Mujoco demonstrate the importance of the regularization and the interplay between explicit and implicit entropy regularization.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses the important question of why distributional RL outperforms expectation-based RL (Reviewers 1, 3).
- It offers an innovative perspective by decomposing the random return representation and linking CDRL to regularized Q-iteration (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The proposed exploration framework (DERPI) is novel and sensible, leveraging the auxiliary-task quality of distributional RL for exploration (Reviewer 2). (Reviewer 4, however, questioned the added value—see Weaknesses.)
- Thorough experiments on Atari and Mujoco support the theoretical claims (Reviewers 3, 4).
- The analysis of the interplay between explicit and implicit entropy regularization is appreciated (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
**Clarity and Presentation**
- The paper is highly obscure and filled with ambiguous statements, making it extremely difficult to validate its claims (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Many mathematical objects and terms are not defined precisely (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
  - The histogram parameterization in equation (3) leaves terms \(\hat{\mu}^{s,a}\), \(p_i^\mu\), and \(\mu^{s,a}\) undefined (Reviewers 2, 3); Reviewer 1 notes the definition of \(\hat{\mu}\) in Proposition 2 is inaccurate.
  - Equation (6) and Proposition 3 are unclear and contradictory. Reviewer 1 points out that the left side claims the minimizer converges to the \(k+1\) iterate of the Q-function in FQI, while the right side claims it converges to the actual Q-value function. Reviewer 2 cannot understand how \(Z_\theta^{k+1} = \mathcal{T}Q_{\theta^*}(s,a)\) with probability 1. Reviewer 3 fails to interpret the integral on the right of (6).
  - Theorem 2 asserts convergence but does not specify the measure under which convergence occurs (Reviewer 3).
  - The “decomposition” is in fact an approximation controlled by hyperparameter \(\epsilon\), not an exact decomposition, undermining claimed insights (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 questions how \(\epsilon\) can be pre-specified given its dependence on the unknown return distribution.
- Numerous typos and inaccuracies hinder readability (Reviewer 1): p2 support defined as \(z_i\) then \(l_i\); Eq1 missing superscript \(k\); Eq2 similar; p3 “As such this … shifting problems” needs rewriting; p3 “the the”; p4 Proposition 2 \(\Delta_E^i\) confusion; p2 \(R(s,a)\) capital despite deterministic reward; p3 need notation warning that lowercase variables are given; p4 “for \(\forall\) k” should be “for all k”.
- The connection between DERPI and CDRL is not clearly established; important content from Appendix I (equation (37)) should be in the main text (Reviewer 2).
- Theoretical derivations lack intuitive explanations or proof sketches (Reviewer 3).

**Rigor and Validity of Claims**
- The paper lacks rigor in several places (Reviewers 1, 2).
  - The use of the “ideal case” argument on page 2 to support convergence of Neural FQI, reused in the proof of Proposition 3, is questionable (Reviewer 1).
  - Mismatch of assumptions in Proposition 3: the statement assumes the \(Y_i\) are representable, but the proof assumes \(E[Y_i]\) is representable (Reviewer 1).
  - The proof of Proposition 3 is sloppy: equation (29) is missing an additive \(\Delta \log \Delta\) term (minimizer unaffected), the limit-of-minimizer vs minimizer-of-limit argument is incorrect, and equation (30) is unjustified—the target should still include the \(\mu\) term (Reviewer 2).
  - Most theoretical results rely on the assumption that \(\mathcal{H}(\mu^{s,a}, q_\theta^{s,a})\) is bounded by a constant for all \(s,a\), which needs justification; the bound likely depends on support range and \(N\) and may match known results (Reviewer 3).
- Reviewer 2 doubts the novelty of Theorem 1, noting its similarity to the DKW inequality.
- Insufficient rigor in basic concepts: Q-learning is a type of TD learning, not separate; TD learning is not specific to expectation-based RL; CDRL itself is TD learning w.r.t. return distributions; and CDRL is not an actor-critic method (policy is argmax of expected atom values) (Reviewer 4).

**Significance and Novelty**
- Several reviewers question the contribution and added value. Reviewer 4 argues the “decomposition” is merely an approximation and showing CDRL with KL divergence is equivalent to cross-entropy-regularized Bellman operator rephrases a known objective without significantly new insight. Reviewer 2 notes it is already known that the categorical projection is mean-preserving, so Proposition 3 does not add new understanding. Reviewer 1 finds the paper unfit for publication due to lack of clarity and rigor. Reviewer 3 is undecided but finds theory supported experimentally.

**Risk-Sensitive Interpretation**
- There is strong disagreement with the claim that the proposed regularization is risk-sensitive (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Reviewer 2 states the algorithms are not risk-sensitive in any meaningful sense: the behavior is driven by mismatch in return estimates, not a specified risk propensity; there is no way to control or predict risk aversion. Reviewer 4 questions how the regularization connects to maximum-entropy RL (policy entropy) and views the claim that the regularization favours large dispersion as only pushing the model’s dispersion to match the target. Reviewers 1 and 2 explicitly disagree with the paper’s claim that distributional RL produces risk-sensitive policies.

**Connection to Maximum Entropy RL**
- Reviewer 2 finds the connection to MaxEnt RL interesting and novel, but Reviewer 4 is unconvinced, arguing there is no bridge between uncertainty in action selection and uncertainty in return estimates except both can be approached by reward augmentation; equation (5) regularizes cross-entropy between consecutive return distributions, not policy entropy.

**Intrinsic Uncertainty Reduction Claim**
- Reviewer 2 questions how the regularization can reduce intrinsic uncertainty when the return distribution models aleatoric uncertainty. Reviewer 4 similarly asks how a policy can reduce intrinsic uncertainty, which by definition is independent of the policy.

**Other Specific Questions (overlapping with criticisms)**
- How can the expanded distribution be ensured to retain the correct expectation? (Reviewer 4)
- How is the return distribution model ensured to remain a histogram (count-based) when minimizing KL divergence? (Reviewer 4)
- The formulation (neural FZI with KL minimization) is similar to Wu et al. (ICML 2023) on offline policy evaluation; potential connection is not discussed (Reviewer 3).
