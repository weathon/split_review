## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper empirically tests whether theoretical predictions derived from the infinite-width NTK regime hold for practical-width neural networks. It examines three areas: optimization (second-order methods like NGD), uncertainty quantification (neural bandit exploration), and continual learning. The authors find that necessary conditions (e.g., Jacobian stability) are not satisfied, and NTK-based prescriptions (e.g., the exploration parameter formula, width reducing forgetting) fail for realistic architectures.

### Strengths
- Important and timely research question: the practical validity of the NTK theory in overparametrized networks.
- The paper is very well written, easy to follow, and its intended claims are clear.
- Experiments are clearly described and convincing.
- Interesting finding in continual learning: early stopping significantly affects conclusions about width reducing forgetting (Reviewer 1).
- The neural bandit and continual learning settings provide novel insights not commonly encountered by NTK theorists (Reviewer 2). However, Reviewer 1 notes that the novelty is unclear and suggests comparing to “Empirical Limitations of the NTK for Understanding Scaling Laws in Deep Learning”.
- The paper clearly delineates the potential and limitations of its claims (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical applicability and parameterization**:
  - NTK theory requires not only infinite width but also small step size and large initialization scale (Jacot et al., “On Lazy Training”). The paper only varies width, so it may not be a fair test of the full NTK regime (Reviewer 1).
  - The paper fails to distinguish between “overparameterized theory” and “lazy training at large widths”. Alternative parameterizations (mean field / μ-param) allow feature learning at infinite width and finite-width networks can approach that limit quickly (Chizat et al., Mei & Montanari, Yang & Hu, Vyas et al.). The paper should explicitly state that it tests lazy training, not the entire overparameterized theory (Reviewer 2).

- **Logical validity of the main claim**:
  - The experiments seem to refute specific prior works (Zhang et al. 2019, Zhou et al. 2020) rather than the NTK theory itself. Failure of the Stable Jacobian conditions (7) and (8) does not imply the infinite-width limit is irrelevant; NTK theory does not claim those conditions hold. The link between the experimental failures and the broader claim about the infinite-width limit needs to be stronger (Reviewer 3). A general question is: how does the irrelevance of NTK follow from the fact that specific assumptions of a prior theory are not satisfied? (Reviewer 3).
  - Section 3.2 only shows a counterexample to a particular NTK-based exploration parameter; it does not provide insights into why width helps in certain settings or which parts of prior contradictory work (Mirzadeh et al. 2022) are incorrect (Reviewer 4).

- **Coherence and presentation of sections**:
  - The connections between Sections 3.1, 3.2, and 3.3 are disjointed. Sections 3.1 and 3.2 seem homogeneous (same assumptions), while Section 3.3 presents a different phenomenon (width helps but only with less training), causing confusion (Reviewer 4). Consider restructuring: merge Sections 3.1 and 3.2, separate Section 3.3 (Reviewer 4).
  - The title of Section 3.1 (“Training: Second-order Optimization”) is misleading because the experiments focus on Jacobian instability rather than directly testing second-order optimization failure. Second-order optimization can fail for multiple reasons (slow convergence, poor generalization); it is unspecified which the unstable Jacobian contributes to (Reviewer 4).

- **Missing experiments, clarifications, and analysis** (from reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms):
  - Section 3.1: The theoretical claim that NGD has favorable convergence over GD is not explained; which sense is it “strict”? Why test only NGD and not GD directly? (Reviewer 1)
  - Section 3.1: Why is only a subset of data used for the CNN regression experiment? (Reviewer 1)
  - Section 3.1: Would it be possible to theoretically or numerically plot λ_min and C′ in the infinite-width limit to complete the picture? (Reviewer 1)
  - For optimization: besides stability conditions, were there other signs (e.g., faster convergence) that second-order methods might work better? Or was SGD consistently better? (Reviewer 2)
  - In continual learning: what happens if tasks are made more similar? Does NTK theory then hold? (Reviewer 2)
  - The conclusions of Section 3.3 lack analysis of why width helps poorly-trained NNs but not well-trained ones, and why prior work (Mirzadeh et al. 2022) is contradicted. A deeper analysis of discrepancies is needed (Reviewers 3 and 4).
  - Which specific failure mode (slow convergence or poor generalization) does unstable Jacobian contribute to? (Reviewer 4)
