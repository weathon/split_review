## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes and analyzes a non-interactive, communication-efficient differentially private learning method called blind model averaging (BlindAvg) for convex and smooth empirical risk minimization (ERM). Each user locally trains a model (SVM or Softmax regression) and submits it for secure averaging without online synchronization. The paper provides theoretical convergence guarantees and experimental results on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and federated EMNIST. Reviews range from 1 (reject, low rigor) to 8 (strong accept, good contributions), with three scores of 3 indicating major concerns about applicability and presentation. The minority positive reviewer (score 8) finds the derivation sound and experiments reasonably extensive, while the negative reviewer (score 1) finds the paper not rigorous and hard to read.

### Strengths
- Addresses a practically and theoretically important problem: reducing communication and synchronization overhead in distributed privacy-preserving learning (Reviewers 1, 2, 5).
- Blind averaging is non-interactive (each user sends one message), minimizing communication rounds (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- Privacy guarantees (data-point and user-level DP) are formally analyzed and appear correct for SVM and Softmax regression (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- Theoretical convergence results (e.g., Theorem 5.2) and experimental support on three datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, EMNIST) are provided (Reviewers 2, 4).
- The paper separates the problem into two phases (local training and model averaging), which one reviewer (Reviewer 2) found conveniently presented and helpful for readability.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited applicability**: The method only applies to convex, smooth models (SVM, Softmax regression). No theoretical or empirical results for non-convex models (e.g., deep neural networks) are given. The reliance on strong convexity and Lipschitz smoothness severely restricts practical use (Reviewers 1, 3, 4, 5). Reviewer 2 (score 8) did not flag this as a major weakness but did not defend its broader applicability.
- **Unclear advantage over alternative DP methods**: The paper does not adequately compare output perturbation vs. DP-SGD, objective perturbation, or NoisySGD for local training. The utility-privacy trade-off relative to these methods is not analyzed (Reviewers 3, 5). Reviewer 3 asks: “If the primary contribution is reducing communication time, could the authors elaborate on the rationale for using output perturbation to privately train local models?”
- **Significant presentation and notation issues** (flagged by all reviewers except the most positive):
  - Variables introduced without clear, standalone definitions (e.g., \(d\) (input dimensionality), \(c\) (input clipping bound) in line #240; \(I\) and \(K_{\mathrm{comp}}\) in Definition 3.1 unused; “_” in line #343 ambiguous; inner product between functions line #330 not formally defined) (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
  - Phase I and Phase II are reversed in the Contributions section (line 74, 81) and in Figure 1 vs. Sections 4–6, causing confusion (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
  - Algorithm 1 does not define variable \(x\) before use; Algorithm 2 uses vague phrases (“run the client code of secure summation…”) without specifying which party sends/receives what (Reviewer 4).
  - Figure 5 has a typo (“distributed?”) and uses the same line style for global model and SVM-SGD; Figure labels are too small throughout (Reviewers 2, 4).
  - Impresice language: “spread the dataset” (line 103), “perfectly converge” (line 481), “noised” instead of “noisy” (line 146), “Gauss mechanism” instead of “Gaussian” mechanism (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Rigor gaps in theoretical results**:
  - Theorem 5.2 does not include noise from differential privacy; its proof relies on Lemma 5.1 which assumes disjoint datasets across users—an assumption not justified for worst-case DP (Reviewer 1).
  - Theorem 5.2’s claim that strong regularization makes the averaged model equal to the global model is trivial (weights pushed to zero) and not insightful (Reviewer 1).
  - The proof references “projected subgradient descent using weighted averaging” but this is not described in algorithms or body (Reviewer 1).
  - Lemma K.6 uses the undefined acronym “ADP” (Reviewer 3). Two sections in the appendix are titled “Proof of Thm 5.2”, but only one actually contains the proof (Reviewer 1).
  - Definition D.8 is poorly articulated (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing definitions and explanations**:
  - “Honest” users, the specific SGD variant used, “local DP”, and “group privacy” are not defined in the main text (Reviewers 1, 2). The distinction between local DP and privacy with a local aggregator (used in Figure 6) is unclear and confusing (Reviewer 2).
  - The connection between group-DP and local DP is not clarified, making results in Figure 6 hard to parse from the main text (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient discussion of limitations and future work**: The paper lacks a dedicated limitations section; possible improvements and extensions are not discussed (Reviewer 2).
- **Experimental presentation issues**: The four research questions are introduced without clear motivation; the connection between questions and experimental design is weak (Reviewer 2).
