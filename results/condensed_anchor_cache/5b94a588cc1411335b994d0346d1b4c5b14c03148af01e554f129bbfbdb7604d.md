## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes DexTrack, a generalizable neural tracking controller for dexterous manipulation from human references. The method integrates reinforcement learning, imitation learning, and homotopy optimization in an iterative data-flywheel pipeline to curate high-quality demonstration data and train the controller. The controller is evaluated in both simulation and real-world settings, achieving >10% improvement in success rates over leading baselines (DGrasp, PPO). Reviewers acknowledge the problem’s importance and the thoroughness of experiments. However, there are significant disagreements: Reviewer 2 (score 3) strongly criticizes presentation and evaluation methodology, while Reviewers 1, 3, and 4 (scores 8, 6, 8) are more positive but still note weaknesses in clarity, reliance on hard-to-obtain object states, incomplete analysis of homotopy generator limitations, and moderate real-world grasp robustness.

### Strengths
- Tackles an important and overlooked problem (generalizable tracking controller for dexterous manipulation), with potential impact as AR/VR data collection improves. (R1)
- Meticulous and concise description of algorithm details; framework is well-formulated. (R1, R4)
- Thorough evaluations in both simulation and real-world settings; includes baseline comparisons against DGrasp and PPO. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Provides useful insights into the importance of high-quality demonstration data and outlines a procedure to curate it from existing datasets. (R1)
- Originality in combining reinforcement learning and imitation learning with homotopy optimization as a path generator to enhance data quality and diversity. (R3)
- Solid contributions; experiments are well-presented and analyzed. (R4)
- Real-world experiments are valuable and appreciated. (R2, R3)
- *Minority positive views*: Reviewer 3 finds the paper clear, well-written, and easy to understand; Reviewer 4 states the work is well-motivated. (R3, R4) [Note: Contrast with weaknesses on presentation from R2 and R4.]

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and clarity issues**: Several reviewers found the paper hard to follow.
  - The teaser figure and method figure (Figure 2) are too dense, with unclear input/output behavior and confusing icons; the reading order is not obvious. (R2, R4)
  - Terminology (kinematic reference trajectory, expert trajectory, baseline trajectory, tracking prior, etc.) is not clearly defined at the start and is used inconsistently. (R4)
  - The pipeline overview (currently Section 3.3) should be moved to the beginning of Section 3. (R4)
  - The writing misuses or overuses terms from deep learning, control theory, and robotics (e.g., “robustness” without computational grounding; “hard-to-track trajectories” not defined). (R2)
  - The paper does not clearly state that it builds an inverse kinematics (IK) controller mapping Euclidean coordinates to joint-space position commands; this could be clarified in the abstract and introduction. (R2)
- **Reliance on object states**: The approach depends on object state information, which is difficult to obtain and limited to few datasets. The performance gain over the `(w/o data)` ablation is small, raising questions about the practical benefit of using object states. The authors should present the `(w/o data)` ablation in real-world experiments to assess crossing the sim-to-real gap. (R1)
- **Evaluation and metrics**: Success rate alone is insufficient for evaluating tracking, which is a continuous task. The evaluation does not ablatively analyze generalization beyond a single success rate criterion. The paper claims better sample efficiency but does not demonstrate it. (R2)
- **Missing baselines and ablations**: An open-loop baseline (e.g., sampling a nearest-neighbor trajectory from the dataset using a heuristic) should be compared. (R1) The reward function weighting scheme for human-robot morphology gap and retargeting artifacts should be explained. (R1) The paper does not fully explore the limitations of the homotopy generator beyond its speed (e.g., generalization across diverse objects/trajectories, specific challenging configurations, failure cases and their impact on tracking controller performance). (R3)
- **Real-world performance concerns**: The real-world robot grasp appears not consistently robust, limiting practical applicability. (R3)
- **Missing experimental details**: The source of the expert state-action trajectory is unclear (from human demonstration? How are kinematic/dynamic gaps addressed?). (R4) The definition of “better baseline trajectory” in the neighbor-selection process for homotopy optimization needs clarification. (R4) The authors are asked whether they have tested in-hand manipulation on a real robot and what failure cases occur. (R4)
- **Minor**: Typo in Section 4.1: “racking” should be “tracking”. (R4)
