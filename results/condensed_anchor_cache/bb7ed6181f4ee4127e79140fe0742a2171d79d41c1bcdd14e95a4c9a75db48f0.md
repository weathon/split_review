## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a method to fine-tune a small LLM (Llama 3.1 8B) for the industrial asset domain using synthetic data generated from ISO standards (ISO 14224:2016, ISO 17359:2018). Knowledge from tabular failure-mode tables is first converted into a Knowledge Graph (KG); then, multiple synthetic knowledge-document and question-answer generation strategies (KD_base, KD_rephrase (toddler/hard/medium/QA styles), KD_extend with entity expansion, and KD_QA) are used to create training data. A student-teacher setup with Llama-3.1-8B-instruct and Mistral 2 generates the documents, and the fine-tuned model is evaluated on the Industry4-FMSR MCQA dataset (~2,600 samples). The best setting yields about a 4% improvement over the base model, but other settings perform similarly or worse. The approach is grounded in a real-world use case and the prompts are provided. However, criticisms cluster around unclear novelty, missing intermediate validation, poor presentation, and lack of explanation for negative results. One reviewer (score 5) finds the paper well-structured and clear; the other three (scores 3,3,3) note major readability, conceptual, and methodological gaps.

### Strengths
- The paper effectively explains the need for domain-specific Knowledge Documents to fine-tune LLMs (R1).
- Real-world relevance: Uses ISO 14224:2016 and ISO 17359:2018 as data sources, closely matching how companies would apply LLMs (R2).
- Correctly notes that generating a large number of synthetic tokens does not necessarily improve the model if net information gain is low (R2).
- Observes that in a LoRA setup, model degradation sets in after Epoch 1 (R2).
- Prompts and instructions provided to the teacher/student LLMs are included (R3).
- Clear introduction and explanation of the industrial assets domain; paper is well-structured and contains detailed explanations that make it easy to follow (R3).
- Methodology is practical for industrial scenarios with small, complex relational data where KG may improve representation efficiency (R4).
- Paper is generally easy to follow, with logical flow; tables and figures illustrate findings effectively (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Poor readability/clarity (R1, R4):** English has numerous typos; intended meaning is often difficult to discern. Sections are not consistently connected; Table of Contents has problems (e.g., Section 2 missing). Some citations missing (question marks) (R4).
- **Figure 2 lacks explanation (R1):** The “Asset-Sensor-Failure Mode” and “Asset-Failure Mode” shapes are ambiguous and presented alongside the KG without clarification.
- **Entity Expansion process unclear (R1):** It is stated that “Sensor Discovery” and “Relevance Discovery” are done for KG expansion, but it is not specified whether this is entirely prompt-based, whether the entire ISO document is fed to the LLM, or why expansion is needed after the KG is already built.
- **Generated Knowledge (Fig 3) not explained (R1):** How the LLM can produce the shown output from only triple relations in the KG is unclear.
- **Missing concrete examples of each Knowledge Document type (R1):** No examples for KD_base, KD_rephrase, etc. are provided to clarify the contribution.
- **Accuracy metric undefined (R1):** In Section 5.2, it is unclear what “accuracy” refers to; no concrete details on how the QA set was created or tested.
- **Missing prompts/explanations for KD_rephrase variants (R2):** Only the QA-style prompt is given; “toddler,” “hard,” and “medium” prompts are not shown. The intention behind these four variations is not explained—whether they generate documents with varying amounts of knowledge or just stylistic variations (the latter would add no net knowledge gain).
- **KD_QA claims unsubstantiated (R2):** Lines 330-331 claim the specialized prompt generates “thought-provoking questions fostering deep analysis,” but the prompt is not presented, making the claim unverifiable.
- **KD_extend efficacy questioned (R2, R3):** Fig 8(a) shows that the model trained on KD_extend never beats KD_base, contradicting the claimed benefit (lines 456-457). The process generates many tokens but net knowledge may not increase (R2). Similar negative results are not elaborated: “having more data does not necessarily lead to better performance” (line 398) and “increasing the token count did not lead to significant improvements” (line 449) are presented without explanation (R3). This is contradictory to general results in the field.
- **Table 3: correctness of generated knowledge not checked (R2):** How would results change if LLMs were fine-tuned directly on the ISO documents instead?
- **Missing intermediate error analysis (R3):** The paper focuses on final performance but misses validation at multiple steps: KG expansion (3 subtasks), evaluation of generated document faithfulness/quality (e.g., having human experts sample-check truthfulness to KG triples).
- **Motivation for all extra steps lacking (R3):** No validation of the effect of KG expansion on the KG itself (quantity?), nor motivation for why all the different KD variations are tried.
- **Imbalanced discussion of results (R3):** Figs 7, 8, and 9 show that fine-tuning often performs indifferent or worse than baseline, but the paper only highlights the small positive gain. No explanation why one setting works and others do not; no balanced discussion of both positive and negative outcomes.
- **Novelty limited (R4):** The approach is practical but not methodologically novel. The choice of Llama 3.1-8B and Mistral Large is not well validated; the pros of using a KG vs. not using one are unclear.
- **Missing conclusion/limitations section (R4):** No discussion of what improvements the authors envision for future work.
- **Minor issues (R2):** Line 431 appears to have a typo (46.2 vs 47.4 from line 421); line 195 has “knowledge” repeated twice. It is unclear whether the datasets will be released to the community (R2). Also, how diversity in the generated tokens is ensured (not just repeated knowledge) (R2).
