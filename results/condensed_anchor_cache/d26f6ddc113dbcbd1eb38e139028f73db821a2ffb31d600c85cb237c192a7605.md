## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper investigates one-shot federated ensembles in heterogeneous cross-silo settings, proposing FENS—an aggregator based on a shallow neural network trained on a proxy dataset. Experiments on CIFAR-10, SVHN, and the FLamby healthcare benchmark show that FENS can outperform existing one-shot methods (up to 26% higher accuracy) and match iterative FL under high heterogeneity while being up to 9.1× more communication-efficient. Reviewers are split: two (scores 5) find the work valuable and well-executed despite limited novelty; two (scores 3) consider the contribution insufficient, the method not new, the experimental support incomplete, and several comparisons unfair.

### Strengths

- One-shot federated learning is an important research direction; any progress here is valuable for the FL community (R1).
- The proposed FENS method is simple, intuitive, and straightforward to implement, with low additional inference cost (R1, R3).
- Extensive ablation studies and experiments on realistic datasets (CIFAR-10, SVHN, FLamby) move progressively from toy to realistic settings (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The method significantly improves performance under high data heterogeneity (non-iid), which is the challenging setting (R2, R3).
- Experiments on data starvation and changing heterogeneity are interesting (R4).
- Ensemble methods support model heterogeneity; clients can use different model sizes (R1).
- The paper writing is fair and clear; experiments are reproducible (though code not in supplementary) (R3, R4).
- The paper highlights an open question: is iterative FL always worth it? Especially in medical settings where privacy is critical (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty and relation to prior work:** The proposed shallow neural network aggregator is a specific case of “late fusion” widely explored in multimodal ML (e.g., MLB, MCB, Tucker fusion, attention). Simply applying such an aggregator to one-shot FL is not a sufficient contribution; previous FL works have also explored similar ideas. Authors should drop the term FENS and reframe the paper as comparing late fusion schemes to iterative FL (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Unfair comparisons:** FENS uses a trainable aggregator and a proxy dataset, while it is compared to methods with no trainable aggregator or no proxy dataset (e.g., data-free FEDCVAE-ENS). The paper should compare to baselines where a linear aggregator is trained on the proxy, or the global model from iterative FL is fine-tuned on the proxy. Also, the effect of proxy dataset size should be studied (R2, R3, R4).
- **Architecture of the aggregator:** It is unclear whether the shallow network uses only logits as input. Using only logits is very limited; the network should also utilize input image embeddings or intermediate features. More sophisticated aggregator architectures (e.g., attention) should be considered (R1, R3, R4).
- **Scalability issue with MLP aggregator:** If client models have, e.g., 10K parameters and there are 10 clients, the input dimension is 100K, leading to an aggregator with at least 1B parameters even for a shallow network. This is a major practical concern (R3).
- **Inconclusive FLamby results:** FENS outperforms FL methods on Camelyon16 but not on Fed-Heart Disease or Fed-ISIC. Experiments are missing on Fed-IXI and Fed-TCGA-BRCA. Figure 4 should also include non-trained aggregation methods (R4).
- **Low performance on iid data:** The paper does not explain why FENS performs worse in the iid setting. The trainable aggregator may be hurting performance. This needs analysis, possibly with additional experiments (R3).
- **Experiment 4.2 inconclusive:** The larger improvement for FENS may be because FedAvg was already near its top performance; FENS is strictly worse than FedAvg in that experiment. This does not strengthen the method (R3).
- **Communication efficiency discussion is overdone:** Two tables (Table 1 and 2) on communication savings are wasteful because ensembles inherently require far fewer rounds. If kept, comparisons to efficient iterative FL methods (sparse/quantified) would be needed. At least move these to supplementary (R4).
- **More experiments needed for thorough comparison:** Vary heterogeneity source by introducing artificial spurious correlations; investigate scaling laws: at what client/data size does iterative FL become clearly better than ensembles? Is there a formula to predict the gap from $\alpha$ or sample size? Does that transfer to realistic settings? Consider differential privacy: for a fixed privacy budget, does ensemble perform better? (R4).
- **Model reusability argument flawed:** Nothing prevents reusing an iteratively trained model; the claim that ensembles enable easier unlearning is far-fetched (R4).
- **Specific technical questions:**
  - Why does FEDCVAE-ENS (ResNet-8) have lower accuracy than FEDCVAE-ENS (default CNN)? Is the decoder of ResNet-8 used? (R2)
  - Why set batch size to 16? (R2)
  - Is the proxy dataset (Dproxy) contained in the local datasets used for FL? Are error bars averaged across different random selections of Dproxy? (R4)
- **Presentation issues:** The paper incorrectly states Fed-Camelyon16 learns on large brain slides; it uses small ResNet features extracted from large breast slides (R4).
