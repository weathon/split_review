## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses Federated Continual Learning (FCL) in an online setting where new data arrive in sequential mini-batches that can only be processed once. The authors propose an uncertainty-aware memory management strategy that uses an estimator based on Bregman Information (BI) to compute the model’s predictive uncertainty at the sample level. The method selectively retains samples for replay, aiming to reduce catastrophic forgetting while maintaining data confidentiality. Experiments are conducted on vision (CIFAR-10/100), medical (CRC-Tissue, KC-Cell), and textual (multi-modal) datasets. Performance improvements are marginal on vision, medical, and textual tasks (Tables 1-4) but significant on the multi-modal dataset (Table 5). The empirical validation is limited to small datasets; ImageNet-1K is suggested as a minimum large-scale benchmark. Reviewers note limited technical novelty and raise questions about the methodology, comparisons, and presentation.

### Strengths
- The problem of federated continual learning in an *online* setting is meaningful and not widely talked about; it adapts to practical scenarios where data arrives in streams and can only be processed once.
- The proposed method handles class imbalance and data scarcity well, and the use of bias-variance decomposition of the cross-entropy loss for classification tasks has merits.
- Innovative use of Bregman Information for uncertainty estimation allows for selective sample retention based on epistemic uncertainty.
- The approach demonstrates significant accuracy improvements over state-of-the-art methods on the multi-modal (vision-and-textual) task (Table 5).
- Experiments are comprehensive and well-designed, covering various data types and scenarios with detailed analyses.
- The paper is generally easy to read (though some presentation issues were noted by another reviewer).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty**: The method is a straightforward application of Gruber & Buettner (2023); the uncertainty estimator and random replay sampling are not novel. Reviewer 3 specifically calls this out, while Reviewer 1 considered the approach to have merits (disagreement present).
- **Marginal empirical gains on most datasets**: Performance improvements on CRC-Tissue, KC-Cell, and generally on vision/medical/textual tasks (Tables 1-4) are marginal, often within standard deviation. In contrast, gains on multi-modal data (Table 5) are significant. Authors should explain this discrepancy, especially for datasets with more samples (CRC-Tissue, KC-Cell). Also, why is the BI forgetting score lower than FedCIL on CIFAR100 (Table 5)? Is it related to increased number of classes?
- **Scalability concerns**: The method may face scalability issues for large datasets such as ImageNet. The empirical validation is limited to small datasets (CIFAR-10/100); ImageNet-1K would be a minimum large-scale benchmark.
- **Missing comparisons**: 
  - No comparison with recent uncertainty-aware continual learning methods, e.g., [1] (cited by Reviewer 1) and Koh et al., “Online Boundary-Free Continual Learning by Scheduled Data Prior” (CLIB), ICLR 2023.
  - Limited comparisons with previous continual learning or federated learning methods beyond other uncertainty measurements.
  - No comparison with recent probabilistic methods in FCL regarding computational efficiency and predictive accuracy.
- **Unclear results in Figure 3**: On the left of Fig. 3, there is a notable performance gap between the proposed method (BI) and MFCL compared to others on only the last task. Why do BI and MFCL improve last accuracy significantly only at the last task? This needs discussion.
- **Uncertainty estimation rationale**: The claim that BI-based epistemic uncertainty is meaningful under distribution shift and identifies robust samples (L291-292) is not explicitly used in the method nor analyzed in the federated continual learning context.
- **Dataset-specific parameter**: Why does the memory size M differ across datasets? Provide justification.
- **Effect of more tasks**: It would be interesting to see the method’s performance with task numbers increased beyond 20.
- **Presentation issues** (noted by Reviewer 3): line breaks missing (L056, L271, L290), abbreviation “CF” used without definition (L072, should be defined in L048), and Figure 1 is too small with low font visibility.
