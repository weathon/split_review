## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a Neural Dynamic Kernel (NDK) to unify the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) and Neural Network Gaussian Process (NNGP) frameworks for infinite-width networks under stochastic gradient descent (Langevin dynamics). NDK is a time-dependent two-time kernel whose short-time limit recovers deterministic gradient-driven NTK behavior and whose long-time limit yields diffusive NNGP posterior equilibrium. Empirical results on synthetic and benchmark datasets illustrate two phases of learning and explore effects of depth, temperature, initialization, and regularization. The framework is also linked to representational drift in neuroscience. Reviewers agree the unification is a novel and important contribution, but diverge substantially on clarity, rigor, and novelty of certain claims.

### Strengths
- **Novel unified framework:** The NDK connects NTK and NNGP under stochastic noise, filling a gap between two previously disparate theories. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Innovative methodology:** Using Langevin dynamics, Markov proximal learning, and path integral formulations is novel and may extend to more general learning systems. (Reviewer 3)
- **Clear empirical support:** Theoretical arguments are backed by compelling experiments that visualize the two-phase dynamics and limiting behavior. (Reviewer 2) Including exploration of depth, temperature, initialization variance, equilibrium variance, and effects on generalization. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Excellent narrative in appendixes:** Main text concentrates on the main line of thought; appendices provide thorough support. (Reviewer 4; also noted as a positive by Reviewer 2 “presentation excellent”)
- **Surprising qualitative differences:** Different kernel behavior in one-layer vs. two-layer cases is a noteworthy observation. (Reviewer 4)
- **Connection to neuroscience:** The framework provides a model for representational drift (though prior work exists, e.g., [1,2]). (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Exploration of implications:** Early stopping, representation drift, and the role of regularization are discussed. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of rigorous proofs and definitions:** Fundamental results (e.g., Equation 5) are not rigorously established. Notation is inconsistent (e.g., ∇_θ f(x,t) vs. f(x,θ); K with varying numbers of arguments; derivative in \(\dot{K}(t,t',x,x')\) undefined). Variables \(\mathbf{u}(t)\) and the statistic \(S\) are not clearly defined in the main text. (Reviewer 1, echoed by Reviewer 3)
- **Equation 5 and auxiliary fields unexplained:** Main theoretical result Eq. 5+6 introduces a “field coupled to the predictor” without explanation; \(\mathbf{u}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\) are not defined until deep in the supplementary. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Missing derivation details:** Equation 16 is unclear – whether it is a standard NNGP result or a limit of Equation 13; the connection to NDK is not explained. (Reviewer 1)
- **NDK vs. NTK at t = t' not clarified:** The difference from NTK when the two times coincide is not discussed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Figure 2 missing NNGP equilibrium comparison:** The long-time limit should be plotted for direct comparison. (Reviewer 1)
- **Optimal early stopping undefined:** How the authors define the optimal early stopping point and optimum generalization error is not specified. (Reviewer 1)
- **Vague claims:** “predictor converges fast to the desired” and “provides a model of representational drift” are imprecise. (Reviewer 1)
- **Margin violation on Eq. 88** (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient literature context:** The introduction does not adequately discuss prior work on NTK-NNGP connections; the one-time extension of NTK already exists (Lemma 8.1.1 of [1]) and the distinction from the two-time NDK is not highlighted. (Reviewer 2)
- **Diffusive behavior not novel:** The diffusive phase under SGD has been reported before [2]; the paper should frame this as confirmation rather than a novel finding. (Reviewer 2; note Reviewer 2 is otherwise positive but flags this)
- **Presentation unripe / unfocused:** (Reviewer 3, score 3, “presentation 1 poor”) – The main result is introduced without necessary context; Appendix A is never mentioned; object \(m(t,t')\) is used before being defined; equations changed from a previous version without explanation (\(\tilde{u}\) vs. \(u\)). (Reviewer 3)
- **Replica method and finite-data assumption:** The replica method typically requires the data size to scale with width, but the paper claims a finite-data infinite-width regime – this contradiction is not addressed. (Reviewer 3)
- **SI eq. 27 error:** Exchange of integral and product performed one line too early. (Reviewer 3)
- **Figure 3 unclear:** Especially concerning early stopping. (Reviewer 3)
- **Root cause ambiguous:** The time dependence of NDK and diffusive behavior may stem from stochastic gradient discretization and learning rate schedules rather than from the neural network structure itself; discretization errors introduce extra diffusion, and hybrid Monte Carlo would be needed to isolate the phenomenon. (Reviewer 4)
- **Smooth gradient assumption not justified:** The paper assumes gradients become smooth near the minimum, but saddle-point manifolds or gradient capping are common; no reason is given to expect smoothness by default. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing analyses and extensions** (posed as questions that overlap with weaknesses/ablation/derivation gaps):
    - How do learning rate profiles (or a nonlinear time variable) influence the qualitative behavior of NDK? (Reviewer 4)
    - Can the framework be extended to multivariate outputs? (Reviewer 2)
    - Can it be extended to other regularizations (e.g., L1) or to tricks like dropout, batch-norm? (Reviewer 2)
    - Can bounds on the transition time from Phase 1 to Phase 2 be derived for early stopping? (Reviewer 2)
    - Is there a way to treat the large-\(N\) limit together with the large-data limit? (Reviewer 4)

*Note: Reviewers 2 and 4 rated the paper highly (8 each) and found the presentation clear/excellent, but Reviewer 3 (score 3) and Reviewer 1 (score 5) raised major concerns about rigor, clarity, and placement in the literature. The weaknesses above reflect the full range of criticisms.*
