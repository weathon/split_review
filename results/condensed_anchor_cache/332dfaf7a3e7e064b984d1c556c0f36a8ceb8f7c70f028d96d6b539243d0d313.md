## Merged Review

### Summary
ARLON is a text-to-video framework that combines autoregressive (AR) transformers with diffusion transformers (DiT) for long video generation. It uses a latent VQ-VAE nested inside a pretrained 3D VAE to bridge the two models: the AR model predicts quantized visual tokens from the VQ-VAE latent space, and the reconstructed coarse latent conditions the DiT via an adaptive norm-based injection module. To mitigate errors from AR inference, the DiT is trained with coarser visual tokens and an uncertainty sampling module. The method reduces denoising steps, generates 600-frame videos, and is evaluated on VBench and VBench-long, outperforming OpenSora-V1.2 on eight of eleven metrics with improvements in dynamic degree and aesthetic quality, while being competitive on the remaining three.

### Strengths
- The integration of autoregressive models with diffusion transformers, along with innovations (VQ-VAE token compression, adaptive semantic injection, uncertainty sampling), shows originality in addressing long video generation.
- The generated video spans 600 frames, making it relatively long.
- The motivation for using the AR model to provide semantic guidance is clear and well-motivated.
- It is a very good extension of existing architectures (e.g., OpenSora).
- The paper is well-written and clearly presented.
- Good qualitative and quantitative results overall.
- The idea of making DiT and AR models work in the same latent space is novel, with many technical improvements to bridge their gap.
- The proposed method achieves a large reduction in denoising steps while maintaining comparable generation quality.
- The two noise-resilient strategies (coarser latent tokens and uncertainty sampling) are a thoughtful addition.
- Long video results show improvement in dynamic degree, and generation using progressive text prompts yields more consistent long videos.

### Weaknesses
- In Table 2, StreamingT2V achieves a Dynamic Degree score of 85.64 compared to ARLON’s 50.42. This discrepancy needs clarification—why is the proposed method’s dynamic degree significantly lower, and how does this relate to qualitative quality? (Raised by multiple reviewers.)
- From Table 1, ARLON lags behind compared methods on several metrics (e.g., Imaging Quality, Subject Consistency) and does not reach top‑3 in many columns. Bold/underline notation for best results is missing, and the reproduction notation is absent.
- The paper lacks an ablation study on model structure and training data size.
- There is no detailed parameter comparison of ARLON versus baselines (breakdown for AR model, DiT, VAE).
- No analysis of memory footprint during training and inference, nor reporting of inference time and FLOPs.
- The main limitation (huge training computational cost) is not discussed; no information on GPU type/number or training time is provided.
- Demo videos (e.g., “A teddy bear is swimming in the ocean”) show room for improvement: lack of subject consistency and unrealistic motion, consistent with the quantitative weaknesses.
- Concern that the noise-resilient strategies (coarser latent, uncertainty sampling) may not truly simulate the errors from AR inference, potentially limiting performance.
- No discussion of failure cases or limitations in the paper.
- Figure 1 needs improvement: the roles of the two DiT models are unclear (do the frame numbers indicate a temporal coarse-to-fine interpolation?), the arrows from the AR model to different DiT models are not distinguished, and the “reference” connection between the two DiTs is too concise and unexplained.
- Figure 2 also needs improvement: modules (latent VQ-VAE + AR, outer 3D VAE, DiT) are not separated into clear zones; the latent adapter on the right is unclear in where/how it is applied; the blurry output of the outer 3D VAE is confusing (why is it blurry if it is the ground truth?).
- Several questions are left unclear: (a) In Figure 1, does the previously generated video serve as a reference for subsequent generation? Does the DiT generate videos in an autoregressive way? (b) What is the motivation for using a latent VQ-VAE nested inside a pretrained 3D Autoencoder instead of training a single-stage pixel-to-latent tokenizer? What is the additional computational cost or gain? (c) How many seconds do the longest generated videos last, and what is their performance? (d) In Tables 1 and 2, do higher scores always indicate better performance? The paper should clarify. (e) For long video generation (Section 4.2 and demos), why are comparisons only with open-source long video models and not with commercial closed-source models like in Table 1?
- Missing references: NUWA-XL, Phenaki, and GAN-based long video generation should be discussed.
- The 4×8×8 and 4×16×16 compression ratios in the ablation (Table 3?) need explanation: do they refer to the same scale training or different scales?
