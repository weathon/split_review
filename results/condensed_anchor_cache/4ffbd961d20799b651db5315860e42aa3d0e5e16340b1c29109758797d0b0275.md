## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Rare-mark-aware Next Event Prediction (RM-NEP) for marked temporal point processes, addressing the issue that rare marks are often missing in predictions due to imbalanced mark distributions. The authors propose IFNMTPP, an integral-free neural model that directly approximates the required integral over infinite time, avoiding numerical integration. The work is presented with theoretical derivations and extensive experiments on real-world and synthetic datasets. Reviewer opinions diverge sharply: two reviewers rate it highly (6 and 8) and praise its theoretical foundation, computational efficiency, and empirical results, while two reviewers are critical (3 and 5), questioning the novelty, motivation, clarity, and practical value given the modest improvement for rare marks.

### Strengths
1. **Novel problem formulation** – The RM-NEP problem provides a new perspective on next-event prediction under mark imbalance, giving rare marks equal opportunity in prediction. (Reviewer 1: interesting problem; Reviewer 4: fresh insights)
2. **Theoretical foundation** – The paper offers thorough derivations of probability distributions and the integral-free approximation, establishing a sound mathematical basis. (Reviewer 2)
3. **Computational efficiency** – IFNMTPP avoids the computational burden of traditional numerical integration (e.g., Monte Carlo) by directly approximating the improper integral via a neural network, enabling scalability to large datasets. (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 4 also notes superior efficiency)
4. **Strong empirical results** – Extensive experiments on varied datasets (real-world and synthetic) show that IFNMTPP consistently outperforms baselines, particularly in time prediction and efficiency. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
5. **Clear presentation** – The paper is well-articulated, with intuitive figures (Reviewer 1), clear problem explanation (Reviewer 3, 4), and a straightforward model design (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Unclear novelty and relation to existing work** – The distinction between RM-NEP and rare-event forecasting is not clearly explained. The paper does not discuss whether existing techniques (e.g., oversampling, undersampling of dominating marks) can be directly applied, nor does it justify why a new problem formulation is necessary. (Reviewer 1, explicitly noting lack of exploration; Reviewer 3 indirectly raises the same via the BookOrder question: mark type [1] >40% – does that meet the rare mark definition? This implies ambiguity in the problem definition.)
2. **Motivation not fully convincing** – Some reviewers question the premise: (i) rare marks being dominated is a natural consequence of imbalance; (ii) if a rare mark is important, why not model it as a single-variable process to eliminate imbalance? The paper does not adequately justify the need for a separate problem formulation. (Reviewer 1; also implied in Reviewer 3’s practical-value concern)
3. **Assumptions and limitations of the model** – RM-NEP decouples time and mark prediction; it is unclear whether this assumption holds when marks exhibit strong temporal correlations. (Reviewer 2) Additionally, the method is only evaluated on categorical marks; generalization to continuous marks is not discussed. (Reviewer 2 question)
4. **Insufficient details on integral-free approximation** – The paper does not provide a rigorous proof or intuitive explanation for why the Integral Estimator Module (IEM) achieves an integral-free solution. It is unclear how non-negative weights and monotonic-increasing activation functions lead to the desired approximation, and the adaptation of FullyNN’s IEM to marked events is presented as straightforward without detailing necessary modifications. (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 2 also notes insufficient details on the “monotonically decreasing neural network” approximation and its rationale)
5. **Clarity and completeness issues** –  
   - The paper is not self-contained: it lists many papers without summarizing or comparing how existing methods handle integration or prediction. (Reviewer 1)  
   - Some terms and notations are undefined (e.g., RMTPP, τ in the integral). (Reviewer 1)  
   - The explanation of Γ*(m,t) and its relationship to Equation (3) is unclear (specifically lines 297-299: if t=t_l then integral equals 0). (Reviewer 1)  
   - Figure 2 is unclear: symbols (v, s, f) are inconsistent with the text. (Reviewer 3)  
   - The architecture diagram (Figure 2) could better show how the integral-free formulation is implemented and how components interact. (Reviewer 4)  
   - Minor: incorrect punctuation in lines 20 and 78. (Reviewer 3)
6. **Modest mark prediction performance** – Table 3 shows only a slight improvement in macro-F1 for rare marks, and overall accuracy for both frequent and rare marks remains very low. The paper does not report per-mark precision, recall, or F1 for rare marks, which would better demonstrate the model’s ability to address the rare-mark problem. (Reviewer 3: practical value questioned; Reviewer 4: lack of sensitive metrics, suggestion to report per-mark metrics; Reviewer 4 question about how experiments empirically demonstrate effectiveness for rare marks overlaps with this weakness)
7. **Missing ablation studies** – The paper does not evaluate IFNMTPP under different imbalance ratios, making it difficult to assess robustness. (Reviewer 1; also asked as a question)
8. **Interpretability** – The neural-network-based approximation provides limited insight into why a rare mark is predicted, reducing interpretability for critical applications. (Reviewer 2 question)
9. **No baseline with undersampling** – The possibility of undersampling dominating marks as a simpler baseline is not explored or tested. (Reviewer 1)
