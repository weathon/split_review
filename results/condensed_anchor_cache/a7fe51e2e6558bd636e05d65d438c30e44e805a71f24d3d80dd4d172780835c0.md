## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a new representation, Ghost-on-the-Shell (G-SHELL), for modeling open surfaces (surfaces with boundaries). It parameterizes open surfaces by defining a manifold signed distance field (mSDF) on watertight templates, discretized on a background grid, and uses an extended marching-tetrahedra lookup table for mesh extraction. The representation is differentiable and applicable to both watertight and non-watertight meshes. Experiments show state-of-the-art performance on non-watertight mesh reconstruction and generation tasks, and competitive results for watertight meshes. Reviewers generally found the idea elegant and the results impressive, but one reviewer (score 5) expressed stronger concerns about speed and lack of complex test cases, while the other three (scores 8) were more positive overall despite noting several limitations and missing details.

### Strengths
- The studied topic (modeling thin, open surfaces) is interesting and important; it fills a gap left by most prior work focusing on watertight shapes. (R1)
- The basic idea is elegant: viewing open surfaces as sub-level sets of an mSDF on a watertight template avoids the problems of unsigned distance fields. (R2)
- The method is technically sound. (R1)
- Comparisons to prior work are compelling. (R2)
- Experimental results on open surface reconstruction and generation are impressive. (R3)
- The approach is original: treating open surfaces as floating on watertight surfaces is novel and leads to a sound mSDF-based representation with advantages over UDF-based methods. (R4)
- Methodology is well-described with clear rationale; empirical evidence supports effectiveness. (R4)
- The paper is well-written and communicates complex concepts clearly. (R4)
- G-SHELL is differentiable and efficient for both watertight and non-watertight meshes, broadening impact for computer graphics pipelines. (R4)
- Applications are notable: it enables reconstruction methods that jointly optimize topology, material, and lighting, which is crucial for scanning 3D assets from images. (R4)
- Generative modeling results look cool, though one reviewer would like to see examples beyond clothing if other datasets are available. (R2, with minor contrast)

### Weaknesses
- Processing time (3 hours) is still too long, limiting practical applicability compared to methods that achieve similar results in minutes or seconds. (R1)
- The thin shape examples in Figures 4 and 7 lack complicated geometry; the claim of arbitrary topology needs demonstration with more challenging cases (e.g., cloth with folds, wrinkles, self-contacts) to be convincing. (R1)
- Missing citations for two prior differentiable representations for open surfaces: DeepCurrents (Palmer et al., CVPR 2022) and Neural Representation of Open Surfaces (Christiansen et al., 2023). (R2)
- The mesh extraction algorithm (section 4.2) is described only briefly; the lookup table is explained only pictorially in Figure 3. Readers need more explanation of how the extended marching tetrahedra table is constructed, how transitions between surface regions are handled, and how consistent orientation at boundaries is ensured. (R2)
- The description of the generative modeling approach (paragraphs after eq. 2) is unclear: what does “unevenly weighted prediction” mean? What is the naïve diffusion loss and what is it replaced with? Why is predicting the linear interpolation coefficient instead of ν beneficial? Are there tradeoffs? If SDF normalization is an issue, would more general implicit functions be advantageous? The paper lacks a brief summary of MeshDiffusion. (R2)
- The choice of predicting the interpolation coefficient over direct SDF prediction is not adequately justified, especially concerning potential increased memory consumption. (R2)
- The “hole-opening” loss (eq. 1) seems to promote hole-closing; the role of the parameter ε and how to set it require explanation. The term “topological holes” likely refers to boundaries, not handles. (R2)
- The appendix lists many loss terms with free parameters without an ablation study; some descriptions are cryptic (e.g., how eq. 12 reduces “floaters” and “inner geometry”). Why is a second SDF regularization needed after eikonal regularization? Would eikonal regularization on ν (taking the gradient along the surface) be helpful? (R2)
- Is there an extended marching-cubes version, or only marching tetrahedra? (R2)
- It is not clear whether the modified marching tetrahedra (or cubes) with mSDF can guarantee correct topology, e.g., an isolated edge with no incident triangles. (R3)
- The experimental setting for handling specular surfaces is unclear: are lighting, geometry, and material reconstructed simultaneously, or is lighting assumed known? (R3)
- Performance on open surfaces homeomorphic to donuts or with many topology handles is not demonstrated. (R3)
- G-SHELL inherits disadvantages of implicit representations: a regular grid requires many elements for surfaces with high and unbalanced entropy; memory footprint and computational cost scale poorly, particularly for non-watertight surfaces with fine boundaries. (R4)
- Marching-cubes-like mesh extraction is computationally burdensome compared to explicit methods, becoming a bottleneck for real-time applications; mesh quality depends on grid resolution. (R4)
- G-SHELL does not model self-intersecting or non-orientable surfaces, limiting applicability to scenarios like clothing folds or organic shapes with self-intersections. (R4)
- Generative modeling experiments are only conducted on non-watertight clothing data; it is unclear how well MD with G-SHELL performs on watertight surfaces compared to MeshDiffusion. (R4)
- Comparing vertex counts to PolyGen is unfair because PolyGen generates non-uniform meshes that use vertices more efficiently. (R4)
- The paper lacks experiments on real data, especially for reconstruction; comparisons to volume-rendering baselines or NeRF+Marching Cubes would be valuable. (R4)
- It would be illustrative to visualize the watertight template being estimated jointly with the open surface in some experiments. (R4)
- Minor presentation issues (R2): typos (“generation modeling”, “validity value”, “pose-processing”); confusing terminology (calling a curve a “2D mesh” and a surface a “3D mesh”); “winding number” should be “generalized winding number” and its meaning on the surface is unclear. (R2)
