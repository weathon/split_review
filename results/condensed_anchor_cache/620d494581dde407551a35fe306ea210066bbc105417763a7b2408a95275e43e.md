## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes NEAT (NEgative-prompt-driven AlignmenT), which introduces negative prompts during training to penalize undesirable responses using a ranking loss combined with language modeling loss. Reviewers acknowledge the importance of LLM alignment but raise major concerns: limited novelty (similar ideas already appear in prior work, e.g., "Negating Negatives" [R2], "Extensive Self-Contrast" [R1], and many ranking-based methods [R4]); insufficient experiments (only one dataset HH-RLHF, metrics limited to PPL and reward score, missing experimental details); unclear motivation (existing methods already use ranking loss to avoid negative responses); heavy reliance on reward function quality; and writing quality issues. One reviewer (R2, score 1, confidence 5) is significantly more critical than the others (scores 3 each), citing strong similarity with prior work and unconvincing empirical evidence.

### Strengths
- The problem of aligning LLMs with human preferences is important (R4).
- The paper is generally easy to follow (R1) and has clear presentation with a compelling problem statement, detailed methodology, and helpful figures and pseudo-code (R3, R4). *(Note: multiple reviewers also criticize writing quality – see Weaknesses.)*
- The proposed method is straightforward to implement and initial results suggest it can help alignment (R1). *(But limited experimental scope weakens confidence.)*
- One reviewer (R3) considered the approach novel with a dual feedback mechanism; however, other reviewers strongly disagree (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and insufficient contribution**: The claimed focus on penalizing negative responses has already been explored. Reviewers note multiple prior works: "Extensive Self-Contrast" (Liu et al., 2024) [R1]; "Negating Negatives: Alignment with Human Negative Samples via Distributional Dispreference Optimization" (approximately one year ahead, highly similar idea) [R2]; and numerous preference alignment methods (DPO, KTO, ORPO, SimPO, etc.) that already use ranking-based loss to guide models away from undesirable behaviors [R4]. The "online sampling" claim is not truly online (two-stage sampling-then-training, similar to works like Self-rewarding LMs, Online AI Feedback, Self-play PO) [R1 refs]. The final loss in Eq. (8) provides extremely limited technical contribution [R4]. One reviewer (R2, score 1) is particularly critical due to strong similarity to prior work.

- **Insufficient experiments**: All experiments are conducted solely on the HH-RLHF dataset (R1, R2, R3, R4). Only two metrics are used: perplexity (PPL) and reward score (R1, R3). PPL does not directly reflect alignment quality (R3). No accuracy or other alignment-specific metrics are reported (R3). The paper does not use GPT-4 as evaluator, which is standard in the area (R4). Missing important experimental details such as learning rate, batch size, and the specific reward model used (R4). This makes the effectiveness not convincing (R2) and limits generalizability (R3).

- **Dependency on reward function quality**: The effectiveness of NEAT relies heavily on the quality of the reward function used for penalizing negative outputs; training and optimizing this reward function is challenging, which limits robustness and general applicability (R3). The paper does not specify which reward model is used (R4) or address how to ensure robustness across different scenarios (R3, question).

- **Unclear motivation**: The claim that "existing alignment methods primarily focus on positive examples while overlooking the importance of negative responses" is inaccurate – many methods already use ranking-based loss to avoid undesirable behaviors (R4). The paper also uses concepts like "example", "response", "sample", and "prompt" in a confusing and interchangeable manner, undermining professionalism (R4).

- **Writing and presentation quality**: While some reviewers found the paper easy to follow, content arrangements are inappropriate (e.g., NEAT-PP variant not well explained) (R1). There are many grammatical errors: incorrect quotation marks (Lines 73, 265); unclear phrasing (Line 162 "score the dialogue with a constant"); missing words (Line 198 "the training language model" should be "the model being trained"); missing preposition (Line 269 "leading reward hacking" should be "leading to reward hacking"); missing period (Line 269) (R4). These errors seriously affect presentation and readability (R4).

- **Questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - "What are the differences between your work and 'Negating Negatives: Alignment with Human Negative Samples via Distributional Dispreference Optimization'?" (R2)
  - "Given the challenges in training and optimizing the reward function, how do you ensure robustness across different scenarios?" (R3)
  - "Have you considered evaluating NEAT on other datasets to demonstrate generalizability and ability to outperform existing methods?" (R3)
