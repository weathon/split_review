## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper leverages partial information decomposition (PID) to analyze three sources of unfairness in federated learning (FL) — Unique, Redundant, and Masked Disparity — and relates them to global and local fairness. It introduces a convex optimization problem (AGLFOP) for characterizing accuracy–fairness trade-offs. Reviewers find the approach novel and mathematically rigorous, but raise concerns about practical utility, experimental evaluation, presentation clarity, and justification of the information-theoretic fairness measures. Reviewer 2 (score 6) is notably more critical than Reviewers 1 and 3 (score 8), especially regarding experimental weakness, lack of algorithmic solution, and practical significance. Reviewer 2 also expresses lower confidence (2) compared to the others (3).

### Strengths
- Novel investigation of the relationship between global and local fairness in FL, which according to the authors has not been studied before (R1, R3).
- Use of partial information decomposition to identify three sources of unfairness is interesting and mathematically rigorous (R1, R2, R3).
- Theorems 1, 2, and 3 are conclusive and powerful, allowing prediction of fairness based on the three sources (R1).
- The convex optimization formulation (AGLFOP) for minimizing error under mutual information constraints is a good idea (R3).
- Experiments are comprehensive in terms of datasets (synthetic, ADULT) and settings of data distributions across clients (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Justification of information-theoretic definitions:** Definitions 1 and 2 (conditional mutual information for local fairness and the PID decomposition) need more mathematical and conceptual motivation. It is unclear how the decomposition relates to existing group fairness notions (statistical parity, equal opportunity) and what advantages it offers over traditional metrics (R1, R3). Lemma 1 shows mutual information is an upper bound on statistical parity, but the looseness of this bound is not analyzed, raising doubts about mutual information being the right fairness measure (R3).
- **Computational practicality:** How Uni(), Red(), and Syn() are efficiently computed is not addressed; estimation of mutual information is likely expensive (R1). The optimization framework (Definition 5) is proposed without any solution or algorithm for solving it in practice (R2).
- **Experimental evaluation is weak:** Baselines and datasets are rare; comparison with established FL group fairness works (e.g., [1,2]) and accuracy–fairness trade-off works (e.g., [3]) is missing, limiting assessment of practical significance (R2). Visualization relies only on Pareto frontiers (Figure 3); more granular analysis (tables, additional plots) is needed (R2). Sensitivity of hyperparameters ε_g and ε_L is not explored, and methodology for setting them is lacking; no discussion of different experimental settings and datasets (R2). Experiment details (model, parameter settings) are not clearly specified (R2). Formal definitions of global and local fairness are missing (R2, minor but noted).
- **Presentation clarity:** The paper is difficult to follow. Examples in Section 3.1 should be moved to preliminaries. Lemma 1 and Lemma 2 appear in preliminaries but are results, causing confusion. Unique information is used before it is defined; redundant and synergistic information are not defined similarly. Notation is excessive. It is unclear whether theorems/lemmas follow trivially from definitions; a proof sketch would clarify (R3). The convex optimization section is too short and should be expanded (R3).
- **Lack of insightful discussion:** Experiments are comprehensive but lack discussion of interesting observations or how the mutual information perspective gives insight into local/global fairness (R3). The paper does not clearly explain whether it is merely a trivial application of PID analysis from [4] to FL (R2 question turned into weakness).
- **Missing analysis/ablation:** One reviewer questions if all theorems follow trivially from definitions (R3); another asks for more detailed trade-off results (R2) and hyperparameter selection guidelines (R2). The looseness of the bound in Lemma 1 is not characterized (R3).
