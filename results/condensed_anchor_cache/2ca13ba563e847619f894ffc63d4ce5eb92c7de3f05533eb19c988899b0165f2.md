## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper extends influence functions for data attribution in diffusion models. It uses a series of approximations—generalized Gauss-Newton (GGN) matrix, block-diagonal layers, and Kronecker-factorized (K-FAC) blocks—to make Hessian computation tractable. The framework unifies prior methods Journey-TRAK and D-TRAK and proposes K-FAC Influence. Several influence function variants are considered: IF of ELBO, IF of the loss, and IF of path probabilities. Empirical evaluation on DDPM (CIFAR-2, CIFAR-10) uses Linear Data-modelling Score (LDS) and counterfactual retraining, achieving state-of-the-art performance. The paper also provides observations on applying influence functions to diffusion models.

### Strengths
- Provides a theoretical framework that unifies influence functions, Journey‑TRAK, and D‑TRAK for diffusion models, bringing clarity to a largely empirical field.
- The framework motivates specific design choices (linearizing the network + K‑FAC‑expand) that lead to better performance (Figure 4).
- Achieves state‑of‑the‑art results on LDS and retraining‑without‑top‑influences metrics, a challenging problem.
- Systematic experimental analysis comparing multiple IF variants (ELBO, loss, path probabilities) and highlighting strengths/weaknesses of each approach, as well as limitations relative to exact retraining.
- Empirical observations (Section 4.1) help understand how to apply influence functions to diffusion models.
- The work is valuable for privacy and copyright concerns in image/video generation, and is presented clearly with both intuition and theoretical justification.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation scope.** Experiments only use DDPM on CIFAR‑2 and CIFAR‑10. It is unclear how well the method scales to larger models (e.g., LDM, LoRA‑fine‑tuned Stable Diffusion) and higher‑resolution datasets in terms of both attribution quality and compute. The empirical observations (Section 4.1) need validation on other architectures/datasets to support generalizability.
- **Scalability is not quantified.** The paper does not provide runtime, memory, or scaling analysis (e.g., time per sample on an A100, how runtime/space grows with model size). A reader cannot assess feasibility for their own large diffusion model.
- **Sensitivity to damping parameter.** K‑FAC Influence shows notable sensitivity to the damping parameter (LDS varies from ~15% to ~5% on CIFAR‑10). This range is comparable to D‑TRAK, and it is unclear how to tune the damping in practice or how the tuning procedure differs from D‑TRAK.
- **Statistical significance is unclear.** Given overlapping standard errors, it is not clear that the proposed method significantly outperforms prior approaches. Reporting statistical significance would clarify this.
- **Missing analysis of IF for marginal likelihood.** The most natural attribution target, log p(x), is discussed but not implemented. Analyzing the IF of log p(x) could help explain the counterintuitive behavior of the IF of the ELBO.
- **No evaluation with exact Hessian on small models.** An analysis on low‑dimensional models with small networks (where the Hessian can be computed exactly) would help evaluate the relative performance of the Hessian approximation techniques.
- **Concerns about Observation 3 and the ELBO proxy claim.** (a) Observation 3 might be sensitive to the amount of training data removed; with 90% removal there could be more variation in retrained models (Kadkhodaie et al. 2024, Figure 2), so the claim should be qualified. (b) One reviewer strongly disagrees with the conclusion (line 507) that the ELBO is a poor proxy of marginal probability because it is insensitive to resampling up to 50% of the dataset; they argue that diffusion models enter a generalization phase after a finite dataset size where generative probabilities remain constant even under non‑overlapping splits (Kadkhodaie/Zahra 2023). (c) The paper does not clarify whether Observation 3 holds for the simple diffusion loss in addition to the ELBO.
- **Overestimation behavior unexplained.** Observation 2 reports that F‑FAC Influence overestimates the impact of data removal, whereas Koh et al. (2019) show that influence functions underestimate impact. No explanation is offered for this discrepancy.
- **Missing technical details.** (a) K‑FAC is defined for linear and convolutional layers but not for attention layers, which are common in diffusion models. It is unclear whether prior work defines K‑FAC for attention or how it could be derived. (b) A brief summary of Grosse and Martens’ reduce vs. expand settings (e.g., in an appendix) would be helpful for readers unfamiliar with K‑FAC.
- **Limited novelty of some observations.** Observation 3 appears to replicate findings from Kadkhodaie et al. (2024, Figure 2); the paper should clarify what additional insights are provided.
