## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses privacy of client labels during API-based fine-tuning of LLMs, proposing P³EFT, a two-party split learning protocol using LoRA adapters on DeBERTa-v2-XXLarge and Flan-T5. Reviewer opinions are sharply divided: Reviewer 2 (score 5) finds the presentation clear, the methodology well-stated, and experiments convincing; Reviewer 3 (score 5) calls the topic timely but deems the idea wrong; Reviewer 4 (score 3) considers it a preliminary exploration with intriguing ideas but several flaws; Reviewer 1 (score 1) judges the description confusing and the claims overblown.

### Strengths
- The problem of privacy-preserving fine-tuning via API is important and timely (Reviewers 1,2,3,4), especially as large models become proprietary and client resources limited (Reviewer 1).
- The use of parameter-efficient fine-tuning and analysis of label leakage from gradients and activations is a reasonable starting point (Reviewer 2,4). Reviewer 2: “The authors have reviewed some recent advanced works, especially the ones related LLMs. The methodology is clearly stated, and the experimental results are basically convincing.”
- The proposed approach borrows ideas from secure multi-party aggregation, which is intriguing (Reviewer 4).
- Reviewer 2 also notes that the paper is well presented (strength), though others disagree on presentation quality.

### Weaknesses
- **Method description is confusing and incomplete.** Reviewer 1 notes that Figure 2 introduces variables never used in the text, and key procedural steps are missing: (1) how client adapters are initialized (randomly? copying server adapters?); (2) how forward features are computed (pretrained model only? adapters only? both?); (3) what the “task-specific loss function” is; (4) how gradients w.r.t. adapter parameters are obtained; (5) which adapter parameters are updated and how.
- **No clear security model.** Reviewers 3 and 4 call for an explicit security model. Reviewer 3: “Could the authors explicitly formulate the security model?” Reviewer 4 notes that even with the proposed protections, the server still possesses ∂h/∂θ, which may still leak label information; a rigorous differential privacy or information-theoretic analysis is needed.
- **Overclaims.** Reviewer 1: (a) The paper claims “privacy guarantees,” but only heuristic protections are provided; (b) The title “Privacy-Preserving LLM Fine‑Tuning Over API” is too generic – the work only considers label privacy, not full privacy; (c) The claim “lower performance overhead” is not demonstrated. Reviewer 4 adds that the claim that the server can run clustering on unaggregated activations to infer labels is insufficiently justified – specific algorithms (e.g., k‑means, spectral clustering) and their effectiveness need elaboration.
- **Insufficient novelty and overlap with prior work.**
  - Reviewer 1: The observation that PEFT leaks labels (Figure 1) is already shown in Li et al. (2022), and more generally than the binary tasks used here.
  - Reviewer 3: The formulation (Equation 2) introduces two independent servers, which contradicts the claimed “two‑party” setting (client + server becomes three parties). The approach appears to be an application of n‑out‑of‑n secret sharing; secret‑shared backpropagation was already solved in SecureML (Mohassel & Zhang, 2017) and the basic idea is similar to standard secret sharing (REF1). Reviewer 3 asks for a clear explanation of differences.
  - Reviewer 2: The technique based on zero‑order optimization and random weights for activations resembles existing work; the authors should clarify differences/novelties.
- **Missing discussion of alternative privacy methods.** Reviewer 2 notes the paper does not discuss local differential privacy on labels, noisy label learning, or source‑free domain adaptation, and should clarify why the proposed method is preferable.
- **Missing related work.** Reviewer 2 points out missing references from the privacy‑preserving motivation, e.g., “Earning Extra Performance from Restrictive Feedbacks” (2023) and “Offsite‑Tuning: Transfer Learning without Full Model” (2023).
- **Inconsistencies and presentation issues.** Reviewer 2: In the last paragraph of page 3, notation for $h$ is unclear; Figure 1 uses $h'$ while the text uses $h^*$. Reviewer 2 also asks: Are the “local layers” in Figure 1 learnable or fixed? Why is that choice rational for clients?
- **Trade‑offs not examined.** Reviewer 2 asks for an analysis of the cost/benefit trade‑off when using $n$ parallel calls to protect against an adversary knowing how $z$ is sampled.
- **Reviewer disagreement:** Reviewer 2 states the paper is well presented and methodology clear, while Reviewers 1 and 3 find the presentation poor (Reviewer 1: score 1 on presentation; Reviewer 3: score 2). This contrast is preserved.
