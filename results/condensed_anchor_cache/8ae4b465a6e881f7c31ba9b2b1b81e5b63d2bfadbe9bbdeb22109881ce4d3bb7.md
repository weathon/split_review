## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Concept Pinpoint Eraser (CPE) for erasing target concepts from text-to-image diffusion models while preserving remaining concepts. It first proves mathematically that fine-tuning only the linear cross-attention layers (query/key/value) is insufficient for both goals. To overcome this, the paper introduces non-linear Residual Attention Gates (ResAGs) added to cross-attention layers, and trains them with an erasing loss, an attention anchoring loss (using hundreds of anchor concepts), and an adversarial training procedure. Experiments across celebrities, artistic styles, and NSFW content show that CPE achieves a competitive balance, preserving non-target concepts better than prior methods while effectively erasing targets. All three reviewers acknowledge the theoretical analysis as solid and the experiments as extensive, though there are significant concerns about open-source deployability, the source of preservation gains, missing ablations, and clarity of theoretical assumptions. Reviewer 3 (score 8) is notably more positive than Reviewers 1 and 2 (both score 6).

### Strengths
- **Solid theoretical analysis**: The mathematical proof that simply updating the linear mapping matrix of cross-attention cannot simultaneously achieve good erasure and preservation is rigorous and sound (Reviewers 1,2,3; no significant flaws noted by Reviewer 2).
- **Extensive experiments across domains**: The method is evaluated on character privacy, art style, and NSFW content with multiple baselines (ESD, UCE, RECE, MACE, etc.) and shows strong preservation of remaining concepts alongside effective erasure (Reviewer 1,2,3).
- **Ablation study confirms each component**: Ablations demonstrate the necessity of the proposed components (ResAGs, attention anchoring loss, adversarial training) (Reviewers 2,3).
- **Effective preservation of non-target concepts**: CPE consistently retains remaining concepts better than prior works across multiple domains (Reviewer 3).
- **Well-organized and easy to follow**: Reviewer 3 finds the paper clearly written; Reviewer 1 notes some presentation issues (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Open-source deployability issue**: CPE uses a non-linear add-on module (ResAG) that cannot be merged into the UNet. In an open-source scenario, a user could simply delete the code for the add-on module, bypassing the security mechanism. This contrasts with methods like ESD, UCE, RECE, and MACE (which uses LoRA that can be merged) that directly modify the UNet weights (Reviewer 1).
- **Source of preservation gains unclear**: CPE uses hundreds of anchor concepts (Table D.7) to enhance retention. It is unclear whether the good preservation comes from the proposed methods (ResAGs, losses) or from these numerous anchor concepts (Reviewer 1, Question 1). A controlled experiment with a minimal set of anchor concepts is missing.
- **Missing comparison for NSFW erasure**: CPE erases four concepts (“nudity, naked, erotic, sexual”), while UCE, ESD, and RECE only erase “nudity”. Results for erasing only “nudity” with CPE are not provided (Reviewer 1, Question 2).
- **Uncontrolled use of negative prompts for celebrities**: In Appendix D.2, negative prompts are set for celebrity erasure, but it is not stated whether these negative prompts were used consistently for all baselines in the experimental comparisons (Reviewer 1, Question 3).
- **Theoretical assumption about “modes” of samples not fully justified**: For Equation 4 to hold, modes of samples must be detectable and \(\|f(E)\|_F^2\) must be “small enough” for remaining concepts. The explanation of detecting modes (classifying concepts based on embeddings) is not clear. It is unclear how modes would be defined for concepts like “Andrew Garfield”, “Bill Clinton”, and “Keanu Reeves” given that text embeddings form a distribution, not a single point. Also, the function \(f(E)\) may struggle to differentiate similar tokens across concepts (e.g., “Bill” in “Bill Clinton” vs. “Bill Murray”) (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparison of ResAG vs. alternative architectures**: The proof shows that an extra non-linear term is needed, but does not justify why ResAGs specifically are superior to other possible architectures. This comparison is missing from the discussion and experiments (Reviewer 2).
- **Robustness training appears separate from ResAG**: Adversarial training is used as a supplementary effort to enhance ResAGs. The robustness evaluation is performed directly on the model assessment, raising the question of whether the robustness achieved through direct training is weaker than that of other models (Reviewer 2).
- **Clarity and presentation issues**:
    - Some parts of the paper are not clearly presented, making it difficult to follow (Reviewer 1; referred to question section for specific comments).
    - A concise algorithm description of the training process is recommended to enhance understanding (Reviewer 2).
    - Multiple typos in the proof of Theorem 1 (Appendix B): 
        * In the theorem statement, one \(\tilde{W}_k^h\) should be \(\tilde{W}_v^h\) and one \(W_k^h\) should be \(W_v^h\).
        * Line 795, the term should be \(f_1(\tilde{W}_v^h E)f_2(\tilde{W}_k^h E)\).
        * Line 798, same typo.
        * Line 808, \(L_{W_o^i, W_v^i} M\) should be \(L_{W_o^h, W_v^h} M_1\).
        (Reviewer 3).
