## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper uses watermark distillation to train a student LM to mimic a decoding-based watermarking teacher, testing on three schemes (KGW, Unigram, Inverse Transform). It finds that watermarks can be learned with high detectability, but limitations include loss of watermark under fine-tuning on normal text and high sample complexity for low-distortion watermarks. Implications for open-model watermarking and spoofing attacks are discussed.

### Strengths
- Timely and important problem: investigating both the spoofing of watermarks and the potential for open-model watermarking.
- Introduces forgery/spoofing attacks into the LLM watermarking area.
- Extensive empirical results across three decoding-based watermarking strategies (KGW, Unigram, Inverse Transform) and various hyperparameter settings.
- Shows that distillation works for many generation parameters against all three methods when sufficient samples (640k samples of 256 tokens each, i.e., 163 million tokens) are available.
- Uses appropriate metrics (p-value) and rigorous quantitative evaluation; clear methodology and training details.
- Identifies important limitations: watermarks are lost after fine-tuning on normal text, and low-distortion watermarks require high sample complexity.
- Foundational insights into watermark learnability, with direct implications for open models and provenance risks.

### Weaknesses
- **Minority contrast:** One reviewer (score 5) was more critical than the others (score 6), questioning whether the paper is merely an experimental report without a proposed solution and whether its core assumption (replacing the decoder with a normal decoder) is practical for LLM APIs. The other three reviewers shared many of these concerns but rated overall contribution higher.
- **Limited novelty:** Knowledge distillation is a well-established technique; the paper is largely evaluative, applying existing watermarking methods with distillation. Prior works on watermark distillation (He et al. AAAI’22, Zhao et al. EMNLP’22, He et al. NeurIPS’22, Zhao et al. ICML’23) show that distilling a watermarked teacher protects against model extraction and should be acknowledged and differentiated.
- **Unclear contribution to open-model watermarking:** The learned watermark is not robust to fine-tuning, and no prior work used distillation to watermark LMs, so there is no existing security threat to mitigate. The usefulness of a watermark that lacks robustness and can be spoofed by design is questioned.
- **Missing sample-efficiency analysis:** The attack uses 163 million tokens with the same message; it is unknown whether the attack works when the provider changes keys or messages after a set number of tokens. A more insightful approach would measure the number of samples required for spoofing or derive a theoretical bound—this is absent.
- **User-specific keys not considered:** For decoding-based watermarking with different keys per user, it is unclear whether distillation or spoofing would still succeed.
- **Methodology limitations:** (a) Failure to control for model architecture (e.g., use Llama-2 as student for both logits-based and sampling-based distillation) makes it hard to compare the two techniques. (b) Only one dataset is used, limiting diversity. (c) seq-rep-3 is not evaluated on sample-based distillation.
- **Spoofing evaluation too narrow:** The attack is only tested on a simple harmful/innocuous categorization; more nuanced human evaluations across diverse topics and datasets are needed to characterize risks.
- **Other attacks on weight-based watermarking not considered:** Besides fine-tuning, attacks like pruning and further distillation should be examined.
- **Technical writing condensed:** Explanations of key concepts like distillation objectives are too terse for non-experts, impacting readability.
- **Related work insufficient:** Broader context from image watermarking and model-specific watermarks leveraging learnability of distribution differences (He, Xuanli, et al.) is missing.
- **Quantitative metrics recommended:** Only p-value is reported; AUC, FPR, etc. would give a more comprehensive detection evaluation.
- **Additional computational cost unaddressed:** The cost of the distillation approach is not evaluated or compared.
- **Distinguishability of distilled vs. original distributions:** It is unclear whether the spoofed watermark can be distinguished (e.g., it may not learn rare sequences, allowing a defender to ignore common sequences and evade detection).
- **Backdoor-based watermarking methods not covered:** The suitability of the findings for backdoor-based LLM watermarking is not discussed.
- **Paper exceeds page limit (9 pages).**
