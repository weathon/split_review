## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies Leaky ResNets, which interpolate between ResNets and fully-connected nets via an effective depth parameter \(\tilde{L}\). In the infinite depth limit, the representation evolution across layers is modeled as continuous paths (representation geodesics). The authors reformulate the \(l^2\)-regularized loss into Lagrangian and Hamiltonian forms, decomposing it into a "cost of identity" (COI) potential favoring low-dimensional representations and a kinetic energy favoring slowly changing representations. They analyze the balance between these forces and identify a bottleneck structure: for large \(\tilde{L}\), a separation of timescales emerges where representations rapidly compress to a low-dimensional space, evolve slowly, then expand to outputs. They propose an adaptive layer step-size discretization inspired by this phenomenon.

### Strengths
- The paper introduces concepts incrementally and clearly, relates quantities transparently, states limitations, and proves results rigorously.  
- The Lagrangian and Hamiltonian framework elegantly explains the bottleneck phenomenon in Leaky ResNets, offering a novel theoretical perspective and intuitive understanding via kinetic/potential energy decomposition. The "cost of identity" is clearly related to the potential energy, and its landscape is studied in detail.  
- The observation and interpretation of the trade-off between a large intermediate dimension and rapidly evolving representation through conservation of the Hamiltonian is interesting and appears fundamental, potentially leading to new algorithms or insights.  
- Numerical experiments illustrate the presented results nicely.  
- The setting considered is not far from a realistic case; the data is not treated with undue approximation and results are general.  

### Weaknesses
1. **Clarity and presentation**  
   - Theorem 4's statement is ambiguous ("for sequence" vs. "for any sequence") and the second display's domain is unclear.  
   - The derivation of the Hamiltonian decomposition (central to analysis) is omitted and would be helpful. The relationship between the action (line 161) and the two Hamiltonians (lines 335, 372) is unclear.  
   - Claims are made without clear foundation, e.g., the "middle term" on line 171 or cancellations in the Lagrangian on line 207.  
   - The paper structure could be improved: main results are scattered across sections separated by technical results rather than gathered and explained.  
   - The final three pages on the main contributions (line 342) are incomplete; it is unclear why adaptive discretization is better and in which cases it is helpful.  

2. **Scaling, assumptions, and theoretical tightness**  
   - The scaling of quantities in \(\tilde{L}\) in Theorem 4 is not transparent, making it unclear whether the bounds are tight. The choice \(\gamma = 1/\tilde{L}\) yields an O(1) term on the LHS, and it is not clear whether the bound can be improved.  
   - It is unclear how generic the assumptions made in Section 2 are for practical scenarios; simulations corroborate results but do not assess generality.  

3. **Limited empirical evaluation and practical significance**  
   - Experiments use only synthetic data with marginal impact; the practical significance and generalizability to real-world tasks are not established.  
   - It is unclear what practical problem the paper addresses; the benefits of understanding or enhancing the bottleneck structure are not well-articulated.  

4. **Connection to Leaky ReLU**  
   - The link between the in-depth analysis of the bottleneck structure and the Leaky ReLU activation function is not explicit; it is not evident how Leaky ReLU's unique characteristics contribute to or interact with the observed phenomena.  

5. **Scope limitations**  
   - The analysis focuses only on the \(l^2\)-regularization term and does not consider the regression part of the loss; it is unclear if the observations hold when the network must fit a target function.  
   - The paper appears to be a collection of observations based on intuitions around COI and kinetic energy, without clear implications for any theoretical or empirical problem. Formal results require additional assumptions and approximations that do not precisely match the intuitive arguments.  

6. **Novelty concerns**  
   - It is not clear what is novel given that Hamiltonian and Lagrangian formulations have been discussed before (though the derivation here is transparent). The "cost of identity" is a well-known concept; more contextualization of the background and new contribution is needed.  

7. **Missing derivations and unresolved reviewer questions** (overlapping with criticisms above)  
   - The derivation of the backward equation and joint dynamics at the top of page 7 is needed.  
   - It is unclear why the limiting representation dimension must be an integer.  
   - Whether the Hamiltonian can be derived directly from the Lagrangian in the standard way is not explained.  
   - Why the behaviors of the representations at the endpoints are unimportant when \(A_1\) is not fixed is not justified.  
   - The interpretation of a negative (unbounded from below) potential energy is not given.  
   - How to solve the equations of motion given boundary conditions for \(A\) at \(0\) and \(B\) at \(1\) is not addressed.  

8. **Minority contrast** – One reviewer (score 6, the highest) was more positive, finding the observations and trade-off fundamental and seeing potential for new algorithms, but still noted that the paper lacks clear implications, relies on approximations, and does not convincingly demonstrate practical usefulness. The other three reviewers (scores 5, 5, 5) were more critical on presentation, novelty, and empirical validation.
