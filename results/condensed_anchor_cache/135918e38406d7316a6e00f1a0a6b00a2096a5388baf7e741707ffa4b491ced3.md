## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PlanSearch, a method for code generation that searches over natural language plans instead of direct code solutions to increase diversity. Experiments on HumanEval+, MBPP+, and LiveCodeBench show significant gains, especially for Claude 3.5 Sonnet. Analysis links performance improvements to idea diversity. However, the method shows limited gains for o1-mini, lacks comparisons with agentic and search-based baselines, and does not include a compute-cost comparison.

### Strengths
- Figure 1 compellingly shows PlanSearch outperforms naive repeated sampling (R1).
- Broad strategy (generate plan, then solution) applicable beyond code generation (R1).
- Key insight: performance is a function of diversity of ideas; important lesson for the field that idea diversity should be taken more seriously (R1).
- Figure 30 and analysis show instruction-tuned models can sacrifice idea diversity compared to base models, a surprising and thought-provoking result (R1).
- Section 3 well-motivates search over idea sketches; Figure 3a/3b demonstrate the polarized impact of correct vs. bad plans, showing a correct sketch suffices for high accuracy even with few solution tokens (R1, R3).
- Thorough experiments across many models (Claude 3.5 Sonnet, GPT-4o, open-weight models, o1-mini) and benchmarks, with >40 figures (R1, R2).
- Analysis of diversity (Fig. 5) complements the results effectively (R2).
- Paper is well-written and clearly presented, with progressive buildup of ideas (R1, R2, R3).
- Impressive pass@200 of 77.0% on LiveCodeBench with Claude 3.5 Sonnet (R2).
- The method is well-motivated by the finding that idea generation (not execution) is the bottleneck; conditioning on a correct vs. bad plan polarizes score distribution (R2).

### Weaknesses
- The specific PlanSearch algorithm design feels somewhat arbitrary; only hyperparameter ablations (Appendix H) are provided, while alternative approaches (e.g., directly prompting with previously sampled ideas, asking the model to generate different plans) are not explored (R1). The difference between IdeaSearch and PlanSearch is simply a new API message, which seems minor (R1 question).
- Missing comparisons with important baselines: iterative/agentic methods such as ReAct, Reflexion, and AgentCoder (R2), and other search-based methods like MCTS (R3). These baselines scale to more complex tasks and would provide a more complete evaluation.
- The paper does not provide a fair compute-cost comparison: a scatter plot of pass@k vs. tokens or dollars spent is needed to show whether PlanSearch is strictly Pareto-optimal relative to standard repeated sampling and other baselines (R2).
- The opening sentence of the abstract ("scaling inference compute has not yet yielded analogous gains") is false as of November 2024, given works [1,2,3] showing inference-time compute scaling benefits (R2).
- The statement that "LLMs as chatbots [...] are oftentimes optimized to produce a single correct answer" is inaccurate; RL and DPO optimize reward/contrastive loss, not a single correct answer (R2).
- The improvement over o1-mini is marginal, suggesting the method may not be compatible with models that already use inference-time compute scaling, or that the idea diversity benefit is already captured by such models (R3).
- One reviewer (R1) was highly positive (score 10), while two reviewers (R2, R3) were more critical (score 6), specifically regarding missing baselines, compute fairness, and breadth of applicability. This contrast should be noted.
