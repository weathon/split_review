- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 5, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GIE (Guiding Invariance with Equivariance), a self-supervised method that uses rotation-equivariant CNNs (E2CNNs) to learn rotation-invariant features. An equivariance score guides an attention-weighted sum of equivariant features. Experiments on CIFAR10, STL10, ImageNet100 show improved accuracy on rotated datasets. One reviewer (rating 5) finds the approach intriguing and notes it outperforms using E2CNN with rotation augmentation alone. The other three reviewers (all rating 3) raise major concerns about novelty, positioning to prior work, experimental comparisons, and limited applicability.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written and well-structured (reviewer 1).
- The method is conceptually strong and well motivated to address a notable limitation in SSL methods (reviewer 2).
- Experiments show higher classification accuracy on rotated datasets (reviewer 2).
- The paper raises an interesting question: whether GIE is superior to simply using rotation-equivariant CNNs with rotation-based data augmentation. The demonstration that SSL+GIE+E2CNN outperforms SSL+E2CNN with rotation augmentation in rotation-invariant classification tasks suggests that SSL+GIE achieves robustness that data augmentation alone cannot (reviewer 3).
- The approach learns rotation-invariant features helpful for rotation-invariant datasets (reviewer 4); the approach is simple and easy to follow (reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear contribution over existing work:** The paper does not clearly position itself relative to prior work on learning unsupervised equivariant/invariant representations. Notably, [1] (Winter et al., 2022) proposes a very similar method for invariant and equivariant representations, is more general, and considers wider groups, but is not cited or discussed (reviewer 1). The group attention mechanism (Eqs. 1-8) was already proposed in [2] (Romero & Hoogendoorn, 2019) and [3] (Romero et al., 2020) over four years ago (reviewer 1). Many analyses and conclusions are well-known from literature: e.g., using G-CNNs yields rotation invariance (reviewer 1, citing [4] Fig. 4); Figure 5 analyses are similar to those in [1] and class-dependent statistics in [5] (reviewer 1). The method is not compared with previous related works like [1] (reviewer 1).
- **Limited technical novelty:** The work uses existing rotation-equivariant networks (E2CNN) and trains them to produce rotation-equivariant features (reviewer 4).
- **Method-specific constraints:** Requires GCNN backbones (E2CNN), limiting applicability where such backbones are impractical (reviewer 2). Not compatible with Vision Transformers (reviewer 4).
- **Experimental limitations:** Comparisons are restricted to few baselines (SimCLR, SimSiam) and not with newer SSL methods like MoCo (reviewer 4). Basic RotNet comparison on E(2)-CNN is missing (reviewer 4). Evaluation is limited to classification; applications with rotation variance (e.g., object detection, keypoint matching) would better validate the method (reviewer 2).
- **Limited usefulness on non-rotated data:** The method does not improve performance on the original (non-rotated) dataset, implying that image-level rotation is uncommon in real-world scenarios; rotation issues are more common in local features (reviewer 2; also reviewer 4). Performance is sometimes lower on non-rotated datasets (reviewer 4).
- **Parameter count vs. accuracy trade-off:** SSL+GIE+E2CNN consistently has more parameters than SSL+E2CNN due to the extra equivariance predictor. For example, SimSiam+GIE+E2CNN+EqvPred (76.54%) uses 12.83M parameters vs. SimSiam+GIE+E2CNN+group pooling (73.26%) with 11.14M parameters—a 15% parameter increase for only a 4% relative accuracy gain, which is not impressive. The paper should show that SSL+GIE+E2CNN outperforms SSL+E2CNN with fewer or equal parameters (reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient equivariant baselines:** A primary concern is that an E2CNN alone might already achieve robust rotation invariance without SSL/GIE. The paper needs to compare against E2CNN trained from scratch (not just SSL+E2CNN) and with matched parameter counts for p16- and even p32-groups, not just matched GPU memory. The p4 vs p8 experiment in the appendix uses memory matching, but parameter count matching is more important and standard in equivariant network studies (reviewer 3).
- **Missing benchmark:** Rotated MNIST, a standard benchmark in rotation-equivariant literature where E2CNN achieves >99% accuracy, is not evaluated. Its zero-background would better test rotation without cropping and facilitate comparisons (reviewer 3). The motivation (Figure 1) is debatable: rotated object recognition relies more on shape than orientation, and humans can distinguish rotated 6 and 9 (reviewer 3).
- **Statistical significance:** Results are close across methods; mean and standard deviation plots would strengthen claims (reviewer 4).
- **Presentation issues:** The paragraph on the p8 extension in Sec 4.4 does not summarize findings; it only states that the extension was done (reviewer 1). It is unclear whether the GIE components (equivariance predictor, attention mechanism) are used during testing or only the frozen backbone; Figure 1 suggests GIE is part of the pipeline but line 315-316 says a linear classifier is attached to the frozen backbone—clarification needed (reviewer 3). In Figure 4, how robustness is quantified is unclear; lower variance curves might indicate more robustness but that needs explicit discussion (reviewer 3).
- **Scope of groups:** Why restrict to discrete groups (p4, p8) instead of continuous groups, which can also be realized with regular representations (e.g., [6] Finzi et al., 2020)? (reviewer 1)
- **Disagreement among reviewers:** While three reviewers (1,2,4) rate the paper low (3), Reviewer 3 rates it higher (5) but raises critical weaknesses regarding matched parameter counts and missing baselines; Reviewer 3 says they “tend to change score based on the answer to this question” regarding parameter-matched comparisons.
