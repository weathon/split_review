## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ViMAEdit, a method combining CLIP-based target image embedding estimation, self-attention-guided iterative editing area grounding, and spatially adaptive variance-guided sampling. Reviewers generally found the paper well-written and the qualitative results compelling, with good performance on structure consistency and background preservation. However, there is a strong disagreement: one reviewer (score 1) was highly critical of the validity of core claims and the method’s practical robustness, while others (scores 5, 6, 6) were more positive but noted several missing comparisons and insufficient analysis. Common weaknesses include lack of comprehensive comparisons with state-of-the-art methods, missing ablation qualitative results, unclear embedding dimension alignment, an unfair advantage from using a separately trained IP-Adapter, and concerns about the method’s applicability to multi-object scenes, non-prominent objects, and dissimilar prompts.

### Strengths
- The method is innovative, combining three components: CLIP-based target image embedding estimation, self-attention-guided iterative editing area grounding, and spatially adaptive variance-guided sampling (R1, R2).
- The paper is clearly written and logically structured, with accessible presentation (R2, R3, R4).
- Qualitative and quantitative results demonstrate high editing quality, showing superiority in structure consistency, background preservation, and target prompt–image alignment (R1, R4). Attached code aids reproducibility (R4).
- The iterative grounding and variance-guided sampling are novel approaches that contribute to improved control and precision (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comprehensive comparisons with state-of-the-art methods**:  
  - No comparison with Guide-and-Rescale and MAG-Edit (R1). MAG-Edit has a very similar setting.  
  - No comparison with DiffEdit and FPE (R3).  
  - No comparison with InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, or Guiding Instruction-based image editing via MLLMs (R4).  
  One reviewer (score 1) noted that claims about attention-based methods (e.g., “existing methods only rely on cross-attention maps”) are inaccurate, as methods like MasaCtrl, PnP, and LEDITS++ use alternatives (auto-aggregated masks, no mask, averaged masks) (R3).  
- **Ablation study lacks qualitative results**: Visualizations are needed to confirm the role of the image embedding (R1).  
- **Unclear embedding integration**: How are \(\Delta e\) and image embeddings processed together given that text embeddings have dimension \(77 \times 768\) while image embeddings have a different structure? Clarification required (R2).  
- **Unfair advantage from IP-Adapter**: The separately trained IP-Adapter introduces additional knowledge not used by baseline methods. Separate ablation or comparison without it is needed (R2).  
- **Single-object limitation**: Results only contain a single object; unclear if self-attention grounding can locate one object among multiple (e.g., edit one dog in a multi-dog scene) (R1).  
- **Potential misidentification when target is not prominent**: The iterative grounding refines mask for the salient object, but may fail when the editing target is a minor object in a busy scene (R3).  
- **Time consumption**: No discussion of the computational cost of iterative grounding (R1).  
- **Sampling variance claim overstated**: Assertion that “sampling variance controls editing strength per pixel” risks inconsistencies without a clear mechanism to manage variance across image regions (R3).  
- **Dissimilar prompts cause incoherence**: Equation (7) estimates target image embedding from the source image; if source and target prompts have a large semantic gap, the resulting embedding may lack coherence. Method’s adaptation to such disparities is not addressed (R3).  
- **Excessive alterations**: In Figure 5, the method alters background details and adjusts object pose/view significantly, indicating lack of fine control for subtler edits (R3).  
- **User study missing**: A user study would enhance credibility (R1, R3).  
- **Complexity for practical use**: Requiring source prompt, blend word, and various conditions restricts usability for typical users (R3).  
- **Lack of justification for method choice**: No explicit discussion of why image embeddings are needed over existing methods (R3).  
- **No discussion of limitations**: The paper does not acknowledge its constraints or suggest future improvements (R3).  
- **Typo**: Line 309 “\(P_2 \times P_2\)” should be “\(P_2\)” (R2).  
- **Questions that overlap with weaknesses** (R3):  
  - How does the method handle object removal, significant pose alterations? Additional examples needed.  
  - Has ViMAEdit been evaluated against MasaCtrl, LEDITS++, PnP under varied parameter configurations?  
  - Why were DiffEdit and FPE excluded from comparisons?  
  - What are the primary constraints of the method and how might they affect applicability?
