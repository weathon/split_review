## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes AIM, a set of evaluation metrics for GNN explainability inspired by the Co-12 properties from Nauta et al. (2023). It evaluates several GNN explainers using these metrics and introduces XGKN, a new inherently interpretable model based on graph kernel networks (extending KerGNN), designed to improve explainability.

### Strengths

- The problem of evaluating and improving GNN explainability is important for XAI, especially for critical applications like drug discovery (R3, R2, R4).
- The idea of evaluating explainability from multiple perspectives (accuracy, instance-level, model-level) is sensible, and its application to GNNs is potentially interesting (R1). One reviewer notes the thorough validation across different explanation properties is a good contribution that existing work does not discuss thoroughly (R3).
- The proposed approach (AIM + XGKN) seems to strike a good balance between different metrics (R1). The minority reviewer (score 5) finds the work innovative and novel, with thorough experimental evaluation providing substantial support (R4).
- The visualization of results across different metrics (Fig. 1) is nice and useful for comparison (R5).
- Challenging common assumptions in the literature, such as the need for standardized evaluation metrics, is useful for the community (R5).
- The writing quality is generally good (R5), though other reviewers note significant presentation issues (R4, R5).

### Weaknesses

**Incomplete framing and missing prior work:**
- The paper substantially lacks references to previous works, and several central claims are contradicted by the literature (R5). Specifically:
  - Line 14-15: The claim that existing evaluation is often limited to synthetic datasets with ground truth is not supported; plenty of approaches evaluate post-hoc methods without ground truth (R5 provides refs [1,2,3]).
  - Line 15-16: The characterization of ante-hoc methods (only prototypes and kernels) is too narrow; many other ante-hoc methods exist (R5 provides refs [1,4]).
  - Line 37: XAI for GNNs is actually a very active field of research (R5).
  - Line 51: The coverage of interpretable GNNs is insufficient; attention-based methods are missing, e.g., Miao et al. (ICML 2022), Lin et al. (2020), Serra & Niepert (2022) (R1).
  - Line 60-61: The claim that few metrics are available is not supported; plenty of metrics exist for graph explanations (R5 refs [1,2,3,4]).
- The paper fails to build on prior formalization efforts such as GraphFramEx, which proposed evaluation properties for graph explainability. The differences between AIM and GraphFramEx are not elaborated (R2, R1).

**Flawed or unclear metric definitions:**
- Completeness (without): the formula is wrong – it should be I(f(G \ h(G)) != c) (R1). This metric already exists and is called fidelity- or sufficiency (R1).
- Consistency: poorly formalized; IoU(h(G), h(G)) is 1 by construction. The paper should clarify that explanations come from a distribution (R1).
- Continuity: sensible for low-level data like pixels, but not for discrete graph structures where ground truth could be a motif. Removing elements of a motif can change the label, so robust fidelity+ is more appropriate (R1).
- Contrastivity: the definition does not fit the description in Table 1 and is unclear (R1, R4). It is not clear how the formula quantifies distinguishability between explanations for different predictions (R4).
- Model-level correctness seems closer to robust fidelity+ but contradicts instance-level consistency (R1).
- M3 (Compactness): should be called non-redundancy, not compactness (R1).
- Metrics A1 and A2 (lines 149, 167) rely on ground truth explanations, limiting their applicability to real-world datasets where ground truth is unavailable (R4). The paper does not discuss how to handle such scenarios.
- The symbol "K" in the formula (line 377) is not explained (R4).
- The paper does not consider the issue of out-of-distribution data when using fidelity-based metrics, as discussed in Cooperative explanations (WSDM’23) and Towards robust fidelity (ICLR’24) (R3).
- The large number of proposed metrics (12) makes comparison between explainers difficult (R5).

**Limited novelty and significance of the proposed model (XGKN):**
- The proposed XGKN model is not very novel; it is a simple extension of existing Graph Kernel Networks (e.g., KerGNN). The new contributions on top of GKN are not significant (R3).
- Performance improvement over two other GKN approaches (including KerGNN) is not significant (R3, R2). On 6 benchmarks, KerGNN is better on 3 and XGKN is better on 3; the claim that "XGKN outperforms KerGNN delivering superior results" is not convincingly supported (R2). The authors should elaborate on why the results are superior.
- The time to extract explanations shows only small differences that likely fall into implementation details rather than fundamental improvement (R2).
- The motivation for the improvement over existing GKN approaches is not well-written (R3).

**Insufficient experimental evaluation:**
- Only two explainers (or a limited set) are evaluated, while many more (more recent) GNN explainers exist in the literature (R1).
- The comparison between XGKN and KerGNN is hard to follow because results are spread across Figures 2, 4 and Tables 3, 4 that are pages apart. These should be combined for clear comparison (R2, R4).
- Important implementation details for reproducibility are missing, e.g., how the Elbow method for obtaining explanation threshold is implemented (R5).
- The paper claims that no solution exists for extracting explanations from inherently interpretable GNNs, but post-hoc explainers can still be applied. The authors should show that their SHAP-based approach is better (R1).

**Clarity and presentation issues:**
- The introduction structure is unclear; it should clearly state the two objectives (propose metrics, introduce XGKN) and explain how XGKN addresses limitations, followed by shortcomings of current evaluation metrics (R4). The contributions section lacks clarity (R4).
- The abstract mentions "we propose a unified framework... that extends beyond synthetic datasets, ground-truth constraints, and rigid assumptions," but the proposed metrics still rely on ground truth (see A1/A2), contradicting this claim (R1, R4).
- Numerous writing and formatting errors (R4):
  - Table captions not placed above tables.
  - Line 81: "GKN" should be "XGKN".
  - Line 140: missing comma.
  - Line 141: notation should be "IoU(G1, G2)".
  - Line 142: symbol "II" introduced without definition.
  - Line 153: formula should be "II(f(G \ h(G)) ≠ c)."
  - Lines 156 and 159: "G'" used with different meanings inconsistently; also appears unexplained in line 374.
  - Lines 160 and 213: "G" font incorrect.
  - Line 238: missing "a" before "GIN".
- The proposed XGKN model is difficult to understand from the description and Figure 3; more detail and a more intuitive explanation are needed (R5).
- Line 143: "Explainers do not have to be deterministic" – PGExplainer is cited as non-deterministic, but after training it is deterministic; this needs clarification (R5).
