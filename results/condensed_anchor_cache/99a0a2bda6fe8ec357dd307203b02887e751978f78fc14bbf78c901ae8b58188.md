## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Fine-Grained Self-Alignment Optimization (FiSAO), a method that uses token-level feedback from the model's own CLIP visual encoder as a fine-grained reward to improve vision-language alignment in VLLMs without additional external data. Authors argue that coarse sentence-level rewards correlate weakly with hallucination detection and that token-level rewards are more effective. FiSAO is evaluated on multiple benchmarks and compared to several baselines. Reviewer opinions range from highly positive (score 8) to more critical (score 5), with most noting the timely idea but raising concerns about theoretical assumptions, experimental completeness, and clarity.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and timeliness**: Token-level rewards for VLMs is an emerging area, and this is one of the first works to introduce them for preference tuning. The approach avoids reliance on external datasets or modules. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5)
- **Well-defined framework**: The method has both empirical and theoretical foundations, including mathematical proofs and a clear depiction of the self-alignment pipeline. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Comprehensive experimental evaluation**: The paper tests FiSAO across many baselines and benchmarks, supporting the benefits of token-level rewards in reducing hallucinations and improving benchmark performance. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **Valuable analysis**: The paper demonstrates that sentence-level CLIP rewards show weak linear correlation with conversational metrics (BLEU, ROUGE), motivating the need for finer feedback. (Reviewers 1, 5)
- **Clear structure and presentation**: The paper is well-organized and accessible, grounding FiSAO in sound analysis. (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 4 found clarity issues, see below)

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical limitations and assumptions**:
  - The analysis assumes that CLIP provides perfect vision-language alignment, which is unrealistic; CLIP itself has alignment errors. The theorem does not specifically support the advantage of _fine-grained_ (token-level) reward over sentence-level reward. (Reviewer 4)
  - Other simplifying assumptions (linear transformations, orthogonal projection matrices, sub-Gaussian noise, Gaussian generative likelihood, infinite data) limit generality and should be stated more explicitly. (Reviewers 2, 4)
  - The MDP perspective (Section 3.3.1) seems unnecessary and not well-justified; language generation often violates the Markov property, and the section does not clearly demonstrate how this perspective highlights the benefit of fine-grained rewards. (Reviewer 4)

- **Methodology and implementation gaps**:
  - The calculation of token-level rewards is not clearly introduced (e.g., is it a token-level CLIPScore? How is it derived from visual features?). Section 3.2 and Equation (5) lack necessary logic connecting equations to practical steps. (Reviewers 4, 5)
  - The data construction for preference pairs relies on fuzzy matching of entities against ground truth, which appears manual and primitive, introducing systematic errors. More precise token selection methods should be considered. (Reviewer 5)
  - The method depends on predefined object sets (from Detic, COCO) and thresholds for distinguishing correct vs. hallucinated tokens, requiring careful tuning. (Reviewer 2)
  - Computational overhead: Token-level rewards for every generated token appear computationally intensive; no analysis of runtime or performance overhead vs. sentence-level rewards is provided. (Reviewers 2, 3)

- **Insufficient or incomplete experimental validation**:
  - **Benchmark coverage**: FiSAO does not surpass higher-performing baselines on MM-Vet; on MME^c, POPE, and CHAIR, fine-grained rewards show little to no advantage. An analysis of which benchmarks/backbones benefit most is needed. (Reviewers 1, 3)
  - **Hallucination evaluation**: Despite claims about hallucination handling, the final experiments lack dedicated hallucination benchmarks; additionally, POPE shows almost no improvement – reasons are not explained. (Reviewer 5)
  - **Baselines**: The chosen baselines are somewhat outdated; the comparison would be stronger with more recent methods. (Reviewer 5)
  - **Generalizability**: The paper mainly demonstrates improvements on LLaVA-1.5. Showing consistent gains across diverse VLLMs would better support generalizability. (Reviewer 1)
  - **Table 2 structure**: It is unclear whether FiSAO is applied on top of methods like POVID, Human-Prefer, or independently. Evaluating sentence-level vs. token-level feedback in a controlled setting (i.e., just RLHF with sentence vs. token rewards) would isolate the contribution. (Reviewer 2)
  - **Metrics**: Evaluation metrics for each column in Table 2 are not clearly stated in the main paper; definitions of baselines like POVID are missing from the main text. (Reviewer 2)
  - **CHAIR**: The paper uses CHAIR, but OpenCHAIR addresses its limitations; rationale for not using OpenCHAIR is missing. (Reviewer 2)

- **Clarity and presentation issues**:
  - Symbols such as \( S \) in Equation (7) and the normalization function \( \mathcal{N} \) are not defined when first introduced; \( S \) also overlaps with the MDP state symbol. (Reviewer 4)
  - Figure 1 shows reward distributions before the concept of "reward" is introduced. (Reviewer 4)
  - Equation (8) is overly complex and difficult to parse; notation should be clarified. (Reviewer 2)
  - The correlation analysis (Figure 2) only shows the absence of a _linear_ relationship for sentence-level rewards; token-level correlation is not presented, and BLEU/ROUGE have known limitations – using CiDEr or SBERT semantic similarity would strengthen the argument. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)

- **Scope and adaptability**:
  - The method focuses on object hallucinations; applicability to action or spatial-relationship hallucinations is not discussed. (Reviewer 1, Question 6)
  - Adaptability to multiple visual encoders (DINO-v2, SigLip) is not addressed; modifications needed for such configurations are unclear. (Reviewer 1, Question 5)
  - The size of the expanded object set \( C \) and its impact on performance is not analyzed. (Reviewer 1, Question 6)

- **Related work coverage**: Table 1 and related work omit existing token-level reward methods in NLP [1,2] and concurrent VLM works [3,4]; including them would better differentiate contributions. (Reviewer 2; noted as not a weakness in scoring but a suggestion for improvement)

- **Minority contrast**: Reviewer 2 (score 8) finds the work timely, well-structured, and comprehensively evaluated, while Reviewer 5 (score 5) considers the experiments insufficient, baselines outdated, methodology unclear, and the data construction primitive. Other reviewers (scores 6) echo mixed concerns about clarity, theoretical assumptions, and missing ablations.
