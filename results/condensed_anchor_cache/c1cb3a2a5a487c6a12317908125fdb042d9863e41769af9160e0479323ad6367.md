## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes BrainSF, a self-supervised model for forecasting whole-brain fMRI signal sequences. It uses a spatiotemporal encoding architecture to embed momentary brain signals into vector representations and predict continuous future signals. The model is trained on a large-scale fMRI dataset covering both resting-state and naturalistic stimuli conditions. It demonstrates zero-shot forecasting on unseen data and effective performance in task-based functional state decoding. The authors claim this is the first approach for whole-brain signal forecasting at scale.

### Strengths
- The spatiotemporal encoding architecture captures both spatial and temporal information. (R1)
- Extensive ablation studies validate architectural contributions and highlight the performance impact of each component. (R1)
- One reviewer found the motivation, objectives, and methodology clearly articulated and well-organized. (R2) (Another reviewer strongly disagreed on organization; see Weaknesses.)
- The research addresses an important and relevant problem: forecasting whole-brain fMRI signals. (R2)
- The pretraining approach effectively leverages a large-scale fMRI dataset including resting-state and naturalistic stimuli. (R2)
- The model trained on resting-state and naturalistic datasets intriguingly generalizes to task-phase data. (R2)
- The self-supervised approach is novel for whole-brain fMRI forecasting, with strong zero-shot performance and effective task-based functional state decoding. (R3)
- The work has potential to advance theoretical research on brain dynamics and clinical applications for diagnosing/treating brain disorders. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Organization and clarity**: The Methods section lacks clear organization and requires more detailed description of the model architecture for thorough evaluation. (R1) (Note: R2 found the organization clear; this disagreement is preserved.)
- **No uncertainty handling**: The model does not address the brain’s dynamic complexity and chaotic behavior. It lacks mechanisms for capturing uncertainty in time-series data, such as variational encoder-decoder architectures or physics-informed PDE loss terms. (R1)
- **Misleading “whole-brain” claim**: The abstract claims the model embeds whole-brain fMRI signals, but the Methods uses 1,000 parcellated ROIs (BOLD sequences X ∈ ℝ^{T×N}) rather than voxel-level 4D tensors. This misalignment and lack of rationale for using parcels over the full tensor raise doubts; prior studies also cover the whole cortex via parcels, making the “whole-brain” terminology not unique. (R1, R2)
- **No interpretability section**. (R1)
- **Metrics need contextualization** – reported R², R, MSE are not benchmarked, making it unclear how to judge their goodness. (R1)
- **Computational complexity** is significant (Section 3.3), yet the paper omits any discussion of computational time for processing large-scale fMRI datasets, raising feasibility concerns. (R1, R3)
- **Model capacity unclear**: The number of learnable parameters is not reported; the architecture appears modest for a foundation model, potentially limiting representational capacity. (R1 question)
- **Missing voxel-level details**: Voxel count per parcel is not specified. (R1 question)
- **Missing baseline comparisons**: BrainLM (Ortega Caro et al., 2023) can perform forecasting using masked prediction (sequence length 200 TR, comparable to the 30 TR horizon used here). No comparison is made to BrainLM or to advanced time-series forecasting models from repositories such as the “Time-Series Library”. Without baselines, there is no evidence that the proposed architecture is more effective or novel. (R2)
- **Model architecture appears antiquated** relative to contemporary time-series forecasting methods; without comparisons, its effectiveness is unsubstantiated. (R2)
- **fMRI auto-correlation unaddressed**: fMRI signals exhibit auto-correlation across ~±10 seconds. The forecasting horizon in Table 1 (15 TR) falls largely within this window, possibly making short-term forecasting easier. The authors should examine performance when a gap is introduced between input and forecasting window to reduce auto-correlation effects, or at least discuss this factor. (R2)
- **Limited practical utility**: Reconstructing future time points alone may not provide sufficient insight into brain dynamics. The model should be validated through additional downstream tasks such as functional connectivity analysis in resting-state fMRI or activation mapping in task-based fMRI. Zero-shot forecasting alone is insufficient validation. (R3, also implied by R2)
- **Unclear parameter choices**: The rationale for selecting 0.3 as the prediction portion (lines 214, 265) is not explained. (R2 question)
- **Unlabeled example in Figure 2**: The regions shown as examples are not specified; the figure captions and text lack clarification. (R2 question)
