## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper extends linear stability analysis to adaptive optimizers (Adam, RMSProp) by introducing a generalized coherence measure that quantifies the interaction between the adaptive preconditioner and the Hessian. It derives necessary and sufficient conditions for linear stability near stationary points and provides hyperparameter-tuning guidelines to improve generalization. Experiments on vision tasks (ResNet, ViT) validate the theory, showing correlation between test accuracy, sharpness, and maximum eigenvalue.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important problem: explaining the stability and generalization behavior of adaptive optimization methods.
- The theoretical framework is extensive, broadening conventional stability analysis to adaptive algorithms and deepening understanding of their dynamics in relation to loss surface geometry.
- The introduced generalized coherence measure is a novel way to capture preconditioner–Hessian interaction and its effect on stability and convergence patterns.
- Practical hyperparameter-tuning recommendations are provided to narrow the generalization gap between adaptive methods and SGD.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with a clear motivation and a thorough, accessible background section.
- The motivation via a preliminary experiment contrasting SGD and Adam generalization is effective.

### Weaknesses
- **Fundamental theoretical errors in the stability condition derivation.**  
  - Eq. (13) and the derived stability condition for adaptive methods are incorrect. They implicitly assume the Hessian eigenvectors align with coordinate axes (i.e., the Hessian is diagonal) or that \(P^{*-1}\) and \(H(\theta^*)\) are co-diagonalizable, which is generally false. This undermines the main theoretical result. [Reviewers 3, 4; Reviewer 2 considered the theory correct, creating strong disagreement on this point.]  
  - The linear stability condition given for SGD (Eq. 2) is also incorrect for stochastic gradient descent: it only holds for full-batch gradient descent, ignoring the effects of noise covariance and batch size. [Reviewer 4]
- **Incorrect or insufficiently rigorous proof of preconditioner convergence.**  
  The paper assumes the preconditioner converges to a constant \(P^*\) at the end of training, but this is not empirically justified. The proof in Appendix B lacks rigor—the argument that exponential decay of earlier gradients implies \(v_t\) reaches a steady state is unsubstantiated. Small changes in the preconditioner after convergence could affect the validity of the stability analysis. [Reviewers 1, 2, 4]
- **No precise definition of “linear stability.”**  
  The concept is central to the paper’s claims but is never explicitly defined, weakening the theoretical foundation. [Reviewer 4]
- **Limited novelty and incremental contribution.**  
  The work mainly confirms previously observed connections (e.g., sharp minima correlate with poor generalization) and extends existing SGD stability analysis by simply replacing the identity preconditioner with a diagonal one. The new coherence measure mostly correlates with test accuracy without offering deeper insight. [Reviewers 1, 3]
- **Missing related work and inadequate comparison with existing literature.**  
  Key works are not cited or discussed: Cohen et al. (2022) on stability of adaptive methods on quadratics, Pan et al. (2020) on why Adam leads to sharp minima, and Wu et al. (2018, 2022) on SGD stability with noise. There is no dedicated related work section. [Reviewers 2, 3, 4]
- **Insufficient experimental details and reproducibility issues.**  
  - It is unclear whether experiments (Figure 1, Tables 1, 2) were run from scratch or with multiple seeds; hyperparameters (batch size, epochs, tuning procedure) are not reported. [Reviewers 1, 4]  
  - Figure 1 shows convergence only during the initial phase, suggesting further tuning may be needed. [Reviewer 1]  
  - Sharpness computation according to Eq. (24) is not explained. [Reviewer 4]  
  - Code is not provided. [Reviewer 1]
- **Empirical validation is narrow.**  
  Experiments are limited to image classification; validation on NLP, RL, or other domains would strengthen the claims. [Reviewer 2]
- **The framework may not fully explain the generalization gap.**  
  The analysis does not account for different implicit biases and optimization trajectories of SGD versus Adam. A missing experiment: what happens if SGD is applied to an Adam-trained network near convergence? The paper also does not explain why Adam performs better on transformer-based architectures (e.g., language modeling). [Reviewer 2]
- **Presentation and writing issues.**  
  - The paper lacks a related work section. [Reviewer 3]  
  - Figure number missing in line 82 (shows “Figure ??”). [Reviewers 2, 3]  
  - \(\rho\) in line 117 is not defined. [Reviewer 3]  
  - Figure 2 shows only one minimum, which is unclear. [Reviewer 3]  
  - Minor: the paper would benefit from discussing how the framework extends to other adaptive methods beyond Adam and RMSProp. [Reviewer 2]
