## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes SE-NeRF, a teacher-student self-training framework for few-shot NeRF to mitigate overfitting from sparse viewpoints. It iteratively generates pseudo labels from a teacher NeRF (previous iteration) and supervises the student via reliability-aware distillation. Ray reliability is estimated by thresholding feature similarity between epipolar correspondences (using VGGNet). Reliable rays are distilled directly; unreliable rays are supervised using a Gaussian average over adjacent reliable rays. The procedure repeats for multiple iterations. Experiments on NeRF Synthetic, LLFF, and DTU datasets show improvements over baselines like NeRF and K-Planes.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written and well-motivated. The self-training framework is intuitive, carefully tailored to NeRF, and the two-fold supervision scheme (reliable/unreliable) is elegant and straightforward. The appendix provides thorough discussions and visualizations.
- The reliability estimation method is a novel and interesting contribution, and the experiments demonstrate that the strategy reduces artifacts.
- The ablation studies (Fig. 6, Tab. 3) effectively establish the contributions of iterative training and the distinct distillation schemes.
- Reviewer 1 finds the empirical improvements over baselines (Tab. 2) substantial. Reviewer 3, however, notes that the PSNR gain is marginal (e.g., NeRF Synthetic: 19.38 to 20.53) and that baseline performance can vary by 2–4 dB depending on view selection. This contrast in opinion should be noted.

### Weaknesses
- **Similarity to Self-NeRF:** The method closely resembles the concurrent work Self-NeRF, which also uses self-training with pseudo labels and uncertainty estimation in a few-shot setting. The authors should explicitly acknowledge whether this is independent concurrent work.  
- **Lack of details on unreliable ray distillation:**  
  * The formulation of \(\tilde{\sigma}\) and the definition of the Gaussian mask (kernel size, hyperparameters, how it is applied to ray samples) are insufficiently specified; the justification for using a Gaussian kernel over other smoothing kernels is missing.  
  * No discussion of how an unreliable ray is supervised when no reliable rays exist in its neighborhood (an empty set).  
  * Only point-wise density is distilled; color information is omitted. An ablation study on including color distillation is needed to justify this design choice.  
- **Missing camera correction:** While the method detects reliable regions, it does not correct camera pose errors – a major issue in sparse-view NeRF. The cameras remain wrong even after reliability estimation.  
- **Visual quality still unsatisfactory:** Rendered results (Fig. 1, 4, 5) show reduced artifacts but remain far from satisfactory. This raises the question of whether a self-training strategy is preferable to leveraging prior models for sparse-view NeRF.  
- **Misalignment in reliability estimation:** The method uses a 2D CNN (VGGNet) to obtain super-pixel features, so similarity is computed from pixel regions rather than per-pixel information. This seems inconsistent with NeRF’s per-pixel setting and may reduce the accuracy of ray reliability estimation.  
- **Missing ablation for unreliable ray distillation:** No clear ablation study showing the effect of removing the unreliable ray distillation component; its contribution remains unvalidated.  
- **Marginal performance improvement and baseline sensitivity:** As noted by Reviewer 3, the PSNR improvement on NeRF Synthetic is only 1.15 dB (from 19.38 to 20.53). The baseline itself is volatile, with view selection causing 2–4 dB fluctuations. A more convincing demonstration would be improving from a high baseline (e.g., 30 dB to 32 dB).  
- **Insufficient comparisons with related few-shot NeRF methods:**  
  * Missing comparisons to PixelNeRF, MSVNeRF, IBRNet (Reviewer 3) as well as SparseNeRF, SimpleNeRF, Vip-NeRF (Reviewer 4).  
  * No results on other recent representations like Instant-NGP or 3D Gaussian Splatting (Reviewer 1).  
  * The reason why SE-NeRF gains more on vanilla NeRF than on K-Planes (Tab. 2) is not explained.  
- **Discrepancy in reported baselines:** For the LLFF dataset (3-view, K-Planes baseline), Table 1 reports 14.18 dB PSNR, while Table 7 reports 15.77 dB. The authors must clarify which value is correct.  
- **Insufficient discussion of distillation method variants:** The paper mentions several ways to distill nearby reliable rays but does not discuss or compare their performance differences.  
- **Model fragility from view selection:** The strong dependence on randomly selected views is acknowledged but not discussed in enough detail. This limits the method’s reliability.  
- **Open questions that overlap with missing analysis:**  
  * How does the choice of VGGNet (vs. another architecture) affect reliability estimation?  
  * How does the method scale to larger or more complex datasets?  
  * How generalizable is the reliability estimation to diverse real-world cases with more pronounced viewpoint variety?  
  * What is the magnitude of improvement across iterations – does performance plateau after a certain number?  
- (Reviewer 1 also asks for justifications and ablations already covered above, such as the handling of empty reliable-ray neighborhoods and the omission of color distillation.)
