## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PINP, a physics-informed learning approach for fluid prediction that incorporates coupled physical quantities (velocity, pressure) into the prediction process. It uses discretized PDEs integrated into the model architecture and loss function, learning from partial observations. Experiments are conducted on synthetic 2D/3D flows and real-world precipitation nowcasting (SEVIR). Reviewer scores are 6, 6, 8, 6, with reviewer 3 being more positive (8) while others are moderately positive (6). Overall, reviewers acknowledge the combination of physics and data-driven methods and comprehensive experiments, but raise concerns about method novelty, design choices, dataset suitability, baseline implementation, and certain performance results.

### Strengths
- Incorporates equation loss (incompressible Navier-Stokes) with operator learning, integrating physics-driven and data-driven approaches by learning unobserved physical quantities. (R1, R4)
- Enhances simulation capacity of PDEs, especially for long-term prediction. (R2)
- Tested on multiple benchmarks across different scenarios, including a real-world measured dataset (SEVIR). (R2, R4)
- Ablation studies accurately explain the role of each module. (R1)
- Figures and videos vividly demonstrate the simulation process. (R1, R2)
- While the idea of PDE-based constraints is not new, the paper presents a set of methods, tricks, and ideas that make the technique work better than previous literature. (R3 – minority positive view; other reviewers questioned novelty)
- The paper is well-written and explains complex problems clearly (R3), though note R1 and R2 report grammar/expression issues, and R4 finds it overall well-written.

### Weaknesses
**Method novelty and design:**
- The method’s novelty is questionable: it appears to sandwich numerical FDM calculations between neural networks, and the motivation is unclear. (R2)
- Technical designs are underexplored or not well supported:
  - Discretized PDE loss may bring serious approximation error, as it assumes differential operators can be approximated by spatial/temporal differences, which may not hold in low-resolution data. Canonical PINNs use automatic differentiation, which is more precise. (R4)
  - Predicting pressure field seems useless: it is only used in the physical constraint loss and does not affect future prediction, adding extra load. Figure 9(a) shows removing physical constraints does not cause a serious performance decrease. The benefit likely comes from the incompressibility term, not pressure. (R4) R1 and R2 do not comment on this, but R1 notes the integration of physics includes pressure. R3 does not discuss pressure specifically.
  - The correction network design is questionable: Eq.(10) (with possible typo: comma should be "-") has inputs and outputs both expected close to ground truth, so why is it necessary? (R4)
  - The temporal loss function’s effectiveness is unclear; statistical results on how often it is non-zero are needed. (R4)
- The introduction of the method lacks an overview and is complex. (R2) The derivation of Eq.(3) (integration from t to t+1) needs more detail; meaning of Δt not explained. (R2)
- Assumptions about unobserved velocity fields (line 039) are inaccurate – velocity fields can be observed via PIV/PTV. (R2)
- Statement about initial conditions being difficult to obtain (line 102) is inappropriate; they are typically obtainable when solving PDEs. (R2)
- Table 1 categories ambiguous: if 'velocity' refers to velocity fields, it is not appropriate since FNO can also predict both velocity and pressure. (R2)
- Section 3.4 oversimplified: no justification for choosing U-Net over more advanced transformer architectures; many structural details omitted. (R2)
- Training process (Sec. 3.5) unclear: are networks trained end-to-end? How can the physical inference network learn both Pe and output flow fields simultaneously without interference? Consider stop-gradient operations like VQVAE. (R2)

**Dataset and experimental concerns:**
- Some datasets are too simple: Flow2D is laminar (low Reynolds numbers), lacking complex vortex shedding and turbulent structures, limiting assessment of handling complex fluid dynamics. (R1) Reynolds number not specified. (R1, R2)
- Real-world dataset (SEVIR) may not strictly adhere to incompressible Navier-Stokes due to phase transitions (water), questioning applicability of equation loss. (R1) MSE on SEVIR underperforms most baselines – need analysis. (R1) Compare with traditional numerical method pySTEPS (e.g., optical flow extrapolation). (R1)
- Smoke3D improvements are modest: MAE and MSE only marginal, raising questions about practical significance given added complexity. (R1)
- What is the PDE for real-world data? Is it explicitly known? How does the method handle noise in real data? (R2)
- Spatial generalization: why PINP achieves it? Need intuitive explanation. (R4)

**Baseline implementation and evaluation:**
- Baselines training details lacking; some baselines (LSM, FNO) show abnormal flickering or strange periodic shakes in supplementary, indicating potentially poor tuning. (R1, R4) NowCastNet should be eidetic but Figure 8 shows blurry predictions. (R4) Authors should recheck training or explain anomalies. (R1)
- Missing comparison with pySTEPS for SEVIR dataset. (R1)
- Efficiency: training overload due to loss Eq.(16) may cause extra computational cost compared to baselines. (R4)

**Writing and presentation:**
- Grammar and expression need improvement; some instances hinder understanding. (R1) R2 also notes lack of clarity in introductions. R3 finds the paper well-written, indicating disagreement among reviewers on writing quality.
- Velocity visualization not intuitive; suggest using pyplot.quiver to depict velocity field. (R1)
- Code and dataset links not provided; authors asked to open source. (R2)
- Title overclaimed: paper is tailored to fluid prediction, not general prediction (e.g., rigid body, magnetic fields). (R4)

**Other:**
- For Fig.9(a), specify number of experimental trials and confidence levels; two constraints appear to overlap for extended period. (R2)
- Provide detailed settings for Fluid2D: ν, dx, dt, boundary conditions. (R2)
- Experiments with more turbulent datasets recommended to enhance practical value. (R1)
- The paper does not discuss why the proposed model is capable of both nowcasting and Navier-Stokes simulation, while other neural operator-based models are not – this could be a significant advantage but is not developed. (R3)

Note: R3's minority positive view (score 8) includes praise for writing and methods, contrasting with the above concerns. Several weaknesses from R2, R4, R1 question the method's design and novelty, while R3 finds the set of techniques effective. This disagreement is preserved.
