## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a sequential graph rewiring framework (LASER) that aims to satisfy three desiderata: reduce over-squashing, respect graph locality, and preserve sparsity. It argues that spatial and spectral rewiring methods each fail on one of the last two. A specific instantiation is given, along with a theoretical analysis and experiments on LRGB and TUDatasets. Reviewers are split: some praise the clarity and novelty of the framework, while others criticize insufficient comparisons, an informal proposition, and inequitable experimental setups. Scores range from 3 to 8.

### Strengths
- **Clear taxonomy & context**: The paper provides a good summarization and taxonomy of spectral vs. spatial rewiring methods and clearly identifies the three desiderata (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Sequential rewiring idea**: The sequential, locality-aware edge addition is novel and carefully selects edges rather than adding them all at once (R1). The connection to multi-relational GNNs is also noted as novel (R1).
- **Theoretical motivation**: The anti-over-squashing and sparsity motivations are sound (R1). The theoretical analysis (e.g., Proposition 5.2) provides an explanation of why the method works (R4). However, one reviewer notes that the proposition lacks a formal statement and proof (R3, see weaknesses).
- **Strong performance relative to selected baselines**: The method outperforms SDRF and FOSR on several benchmarks (R1, R3).
- **Writing quality**: The paper is well written, clearly places itself in prior work, and is well structured (R1, R3, R4). The framework is presented clearly (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient comparison with prior work** (R1, R2, R3, R4): The experiments compare against very few methods (SDRF, FOSR). Missing comparisons include: DRew, SP-MPNN (R1); GTR (Black et al. 2023), BORF (Nguyen et al. 2023), DIGL (Gasteiger et al. 2019), Expander propagation (Deac et al. 2022), DiffWire (Arnaiz-Rodriguez et al. 2022), and transformer-based methods (Kreuzer et al., Mialon et al., Ying et al., Rampasek et al.) (R2, R3). The paper also cites effective resistance but does not compare against any method that uses it (R4).
- **Proposition 5.2 is informal and incomplete**: The statement is not formally given; the “proof” in Appendix A.2 is not meaningful without a clear theorem statement (R3). The informal version could be vacuously true (e.g., 𝒢 = ∅) (R3). This is a major flaw for reviewer 3.
- **Inequitable experimental setup** (R3): (i) FOSR uses the case L=1, while LASER uses L ∈ {2,3,4,5} with separate weight matrices per layer, making LASER networks larger. (ii) Baselines (SDRF, FOSR) are not hyperparameter-tuned; they fix the number of added edges to 40, whereas LASER tunes L and ρ. (iii) It is unclear if the reported test results come from the best validation run; Figure 3 shows test metrics for all hyperparameters, but the procedure is not explicitly stated. (iv) The paper does not state that baselines are sequentially rewired per layer with same edge counts to isolate rewiring from architecture.
- **Weak justification for locality requirement** (R1, R3, R4): The paper does not convincingly explain why adding distant edges is harmful (R1). The background for preserving sparsity is weaker than for preserving locality (R4). The claim that “local structure equals information in the graph” is questionable (R3). One reviewer notes that spectral methods (e.g., Arnaiz-Rodríguez et al.) combine long-range info with original neighborhood, challenging the claim that they are not local (R2).
- **Poor performance on graph classification** (R2): LASER does not outperform the few selected models on graph classification tasks.
- **Computational inefficiency of connectivity measure** (R1): Equation (8) requires matrix multiplication that is O(N^3), or at least O(N^2*d_max) even with sparsity.
- **Oversquashing notion not precisely distinguished** (R3): The paper conflates the information-theoretic oversquashing (Alon & Yahav) with the Jacobian-norm notion (Topping et al., Black et al., Di Giovanni et al.), which are importantly different.
- **Ablations and design choices missing** (R2, R4): (i) No study of parameter k (distance threshold) and its effect on intra-/inter-cluster connectivity (R2). (ii) No comparison with instantaneous rewiring (single snapshot, L=1) to justify the sequential process (R4). (iii) The connectivity and locality measures are computed only once on the input graph, but they may change after rewiring steps (e.g., shortest-walk distance shrinks), potentially altering the intended rewiring; no analysis of this effect (R4). (iv) No attention mechanism between snapshots, unlike prior work using multiple adjacency matrices (e.g., Abu-El-Haija et al., FSGNN) (R2).
- **Edge prediction task not suitable for rewiring** (R1): Using PCQM-Contact, an edge prediction task, does not make sense for evaluating a rewiring method.
- **Clarity degrades in Section 5** (R3): While the paper is generally clear, Section 5 (including Proposition 5.2) lacks rigor and formal statements.
