## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces MDCD-VQA, a multi-domain Chinese DocVQA dataset spanning 39 document types across 7 domains, with 5,071 images and 34,170 questions covering both extractive and abstractive question types. The authors evaluate various LLMs and MLLMs, and propose a baseline method using in-context learning with RAG. Reviewers acknowledge the dataset addresses a gap in non-English document understanding, but raise concerns about dataset novelty, baseline design, evaluation rigor, and missing experiments.

### Strengths

- Addresses a key challenge in multilingual DocVQA: the lack of a high-quality Chinese document VQA benchmark across multiple domains. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- Dataset is relatively large-scale (34k questions from 5k images across 39 document types and 7 domains), requiring substantial collection and verification effort. (Reviewer 1)
- Thorough benchmarking across multiple LLMs and MLLMs with empirical analysis, highlighting current model capabilities. (Reviewer 2)
- The dataset includes both extractive and abstractive question types, enriching evaluation. (Reviewer 3)
- The core contribution—a novel multi-domain Chinese DocVQA dataset—is a commendable addition to the field. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses

- **Limited dataset novelty**: The proposed dataset primarily derives from existing open-source benchmark datasets (Table 2), limiting its originality. (Reviewer 3, stronger criticism; contrast with other reviewers who see it as valuable)
- **Baseline method limitations**: The in-context learning baseline retrieves TopK similar samples from the training set; if the query image does not fall into MDCD-VQA categories, retrieved samples become less helpful. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing human performance**: Human performance on the benchmark should be included for better reference on difficulty and saturation. (Reviewer 1)
- **Inadequate evaluation detail and metrics**: The evaluation method lacks sufficient detail; simple post-processing (line 373) may not suffice for Chinese nuances, and metrics need careful design. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing crucial comparisons**: No evaluation of state-of-the-art commercial models with few-shot visual input (e.g., GPT-4o with 5-shot); even a subset would show benchmark difficulty. (Reviewer 2)
- **Should include specialized document-oriented MLLMs**: Comparative evaluation in Table 4 should include models like MPlugDoc and Monkey; poor Chinese performance from these would further validate dataset significance. (Reviewer 3)
- **Insufficient challenge analysis**: The best performance comes from 5-shot LLM settings (e.g., Qwen 5-shot achieves 88.68%), raising questions about whether the benchmark is sufficiently challenging and whether visual modality is needed when textual information suffices. (Reviewer 2)
- **Unclear baseline motivation**: The manuscript lacks sufficient explanation for why in-context learning is chosen as the baseline method. (Reviewer 3)
- **Question generation limitations**: It is unclear whether the automated question-generation process can produce questions requiring aggregation of information from multiple regions in an image. (Reviewer 1)
- **Equation 1 justification**: Adding image features and question features directly (Equation 1) is not in the same space; no theoretical validation is provided for why this addition represents the example. (Reviewer 1)
- **Generation configuration missing**: No elaboration on inference parameters (e.g., temperature) and their impact on performance. (Reviewer 2)
- **OCR dependency**: Using DavarOCR for content extraction; a more advanced OCR model might yield better results in light of current performance. (Reviewer 2)
- **Inadequate literature review**: The related work section is insufficient, especially regarding multimodal large models for document understanding. (Reviewer 3)
- **Reference formatting errors**: Model names incorrectly capitalized (e.g., "Qwen-vl" should be "Qwen-VL", "Layoutllm" should be "LayoutLLM"). (Reviewer 3)
- **Dataset not publicly available**: Limits reproducibility and reviewer verification. (Reviewer 2)
