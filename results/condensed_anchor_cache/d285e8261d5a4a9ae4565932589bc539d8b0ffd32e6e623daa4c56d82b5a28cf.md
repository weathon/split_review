## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Memory-Assisted Sub-Prototype Mining (MemSPM) for Universal Domain Adaptation, addressing intra-class concept shifts via sub-prototypes, a memory mechanism, adaptive thresholding, and cycle-consistent matching. Reviewer opinions are sharply divided: one reviewer (score 8) finds the contributions novel, effective, well-written, and providing strong baselines; another (score 3) argues the method does not tackle the core UniDA challenge, lacks necessary details, and has limited novelty beyond DCC; two moderate reviewers (scores 5 and 6) acknowledge the reasonable idea but raise concerns about fair comparison, clarity, and completeness.

### Strengths
- Motivating ideas are intuitive, and the technical contributions are novel and effective (R1).
- The auxiliary reconstruction task provides interpretability, which is rare in domain adaptation (R1).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1).
- Achieves large improvements over ResNet-based methods and serves as a strong baseline for future work (R1).
- Insightful visualizations that help understand the methodology (R2).
- Extensive experiments validate effectiveness across multiple settings (R2, R4).
- The sub-prototype idea is reasonable and worth trying for UniDA (R3).
- Offers a novel perspective on addressing intra-class concept shifts (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty limited by heavy reliance on DCC (R2, R3):** The core loss (cycle-consistent matching) is identical to DCC; the main deviation is weighting input embeddings via sub-prototypes. This raises concerns about incremental contribution. (R3: “only involving the idea of sub-prototype is not novel enough for acceptance”; R2: “significant portion relies on DCC … only deviation from DCC”).
- **Unfair comparison with DCC due to CLIP (R3):** MemSPM uses a CLIP-pretrained ViT while DCC uses a learnable encoder. For a fair comparison, the encoder should be held constant.
- **Does not address the core UniDA challenge (R2):** Sub-prototype mining does not inherently solve the problem of identifying common vs. private samples in the target domain; it appears more suited to general domain adaptation than UniDA specifically.
- **Missing details on sub-prototype initialization and update (R2, R4):** The paper does not clearly explain how the N memory items are produced, their relationship, or how sub-prototypes are generated and updated. Section 3.3.1 leaves significant ambiguity, hindering reproducibility.
- **Section 3.5 on cycle-consistent alignment is unclear (R2):** The concept needs more explicit reflection in training and comparison with alternative alignment strategies.
- **Sensitivity analyses insufficient (R1):**
  - *Hyperparameter K in adaptive thresholding (Eq. 5):* No ablation on K; Table 6 shows fixed threshold drops performance by ~10%, so sensitivity is critical. A baseline using higher temperature (to sharpen attention) should be explored.
  - *Hyperparameter S (number of sub-prototypes):* Fig. 3b shows performance still increasing at S=40, with 3–4% jumps between 20→30 and 30→40. Analysis should extend until saturation and include computational cost.
- **Missing ablations (R2, R1):** Table 6 only reports results without the domain alignment loss; performance without the regularized loss and reconstruction loss should also be shown. Additionally, training time and GPU memory overhead compared to DCC are not quantified.
- **Clarity and completeness (R4, R2):** The paper could benefit from clearer explanations and visual aids; it does not discuss potential drawbacks or limitations of MemSPM. Comparison with a broader set of benchmarks would strengthen the evaluation.
