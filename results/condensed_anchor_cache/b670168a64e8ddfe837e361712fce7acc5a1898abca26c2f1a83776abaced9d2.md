## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DICE, a self-alignment method that iteratively improves a DPO-trained LLM by using the model's own implicit reward model to construct on-policy preference datasets, applying a length penalty and experience replay with human data. The method achieves 27.55% length-controlled win rate against GPT-4 Turbo on AlpacaEval 2 with an 8B model. Two reviewers (scores 8, 8) find the method natural, straightforward, and promising with thorough experiments; one reviewer (score 5) acknowledges the direction but is unconvinced of its advantages over separate reward models; one reviewer (score 3) views the novelty as marginal and improvements small.

### Strengths
- The writing is clear and easy to follow (R1, R2, R3, R4). The method is natural and straightforward: using the current policy to generate on-policy samples and rewards provides easy iterative improvement without additional models or data, similar to DPO's benefit over PPO (R2).
- Experiments are thorough and promising; ablations are well-chosen (R2). Results are good on AlpacaEval 2 and Arena Hard across two model sizes (R2, R4). Table 4 shows short responses can score higher than the α=0 baseline (R4).
- The hypothesis about on-policy data helping is good; an additional hypothesis—that using implicit rewards is similar to training a reward model, so discrimination may be easier than generation—is suggested (R2).
- Length bias is clearly illustrated and mitigated by the length penalty (R4). The paper is well structured; implementation details are clear enough to reimplement (R3).
- Iterative finetuning on self-supervised data is an important direction; the use of implicit rewards for iterative finetuning is novel to the best of my knowledge (R3). R4 raised score after authors addressed concerns.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The approach itself may lack significant novelty; iterative finetuning and the implicit reward model are not new concepts (R1, R3). Length-regularized reward shaping is already a widely adopted technique (R1, R3). The length penalty appears to be the exact same approach as in Park et al. 2024 (cited but insufficiently credited); claims that it is a contribution are misleading (R4). Experience replay for mixing human data is not new (R3).
- **Missing critical baselines**: No comparison to using a separately trained reward model for iterative finetuning (e.g., He et al. 2024). Implicit rewards from DPO models rank poorly on RewardBench; prior argues they have limited generalization (Lin et al. 2024) (R3). Results should be compared against iterative DPO with a trained RM, or the implicit reward model should be evaluated on RewardBench (R3). Missing baselines: Offline AI feedback annotations instead of implicit RM (R4); Self-Rewarding DPO (Yuan et al. 2024) (R4); Iterative Reasoning Preference Optimization (Pang et al. 2024) (R4); length-regularized DPO from Park et al. (R4); reward model-based methods like PPO (R2, R1). The avg log probability reward (see SimPO) would be a good baseline given the length regularizer (R3).
- **Hyperparameter tuning issue (methodological flaw)**: The hyperparameter γ (fraction of offline data) appears to have been tuned using the final reported results, contrary to standard practice of using a validation set. Different values were used for different models (e.g., γ=0.5 for Mistral vs. different for Llama-3) without clear disclosure. This gives the method a significant advantage if the difference between γ values has a noise component. Remedy: report results for the same γ (e.g., 0.5) across all experiments. Similarly, β tuning should be clarified (was it the same across models/methods/iterations/benchmarks?). K=16 choice should be justified (R2). R2 rated the paper 8 in anticipation of correction; without adequate correction the score may be lowered.
- **Lack of theoretical grounding**: No theoretical explanation for why repeated use of the implicit reward model enhances performance; the advantage over using an additional reward model is not argued (R1). Section 4.1 is confusing and detracts from the paper; Equation (6) seems incorrect or ill-defined (“optimal response” is not well-defined; the optimal response out of all possible completions will likely be overoptimized/OOD) (R3). The notation y^* for “optimal response” is misleading; it should clarify preferred response or acknowledge multiple optima (R4). The partition function Z is foregone without discussing whether this is principled (R4).
- **Lack of improvement analysis for iterations >2**: No graphs showing performance across iterations; no root cause analysis for why improvement stops (e.g., policy becoming too deterministic) (R4). Unclear why there is no improvement past iteration 2 or 3 (R4).
- **Missing ablations/routes**: Using all K samples with a Plackett-Luce model should be explored (R4). Replaying human preference data seems similar to KL regularization to the previous policy; comparing these would be interesting (R4). What is the length bias of LLM-as-a-Judge? If large, an ablation using length penalty with this method is appropriate (R2).
- **Presentation issues**: Algorithm 1 is hard to follow; it should state that comparison dataset takes best and worst samples according to length-penalized reward; there is a typo using “pi_ref^(t-1)” instead of “pi_ref^(t)”. Abstract phrase “refinements that debias the length of the responses and enhance the quality of the preference dataset” is convoluted (R2). Line 046 typo “by itself” should be “by DPO itself” (R2). Section 4.1 as a whole is confusing (R4). Mathematical notations: why use $\hat{r}$ instead of $r^*$ (the optimal reward) (R4); equation captions for Tables 3 and 4 should clarify they are AlpacaEval 2 (R2). “subtrahend” is obscure (R2). Figure showing reward distribution does not look normally distributed (spike at 0) (R2). Tortured acronym “DICE” is already used elsewhere; consider “IFIRE” or similar (R3). Missing citation for RLHF (Christiano et al) and for Gemini Pro version used (R4). The description of “Offline DPO w/ new ref” is misleading because the reference is fixed (just the end policy of first DPO round) (R4).
