## Merged Review

### Summary
Apollo-MILP is an iterative prediction-correction framework for solving mixed-integer linear programs (MILPs). In each iteration, a bipartite GNN predicts values for unfixed variables, a trust-region search (solved by a MILP solver) produces a corrected reference solution, and the two are compared via an Uncertainty-based Error Upper Bound (UEBO) to decide which variables to fix permanently. Training uses near-optimal solutions to both full and sub-instances (with some variables fixed) as labels. Experiments on four benchmarks show superior performance over Gurobi, SCIP, and three prior ML-based methods in solution quality and gap reduction. The paper provides theoretical analysis of UEBO and ablation studies.

### Strengths
- **Uncertainty quantification is novel and interesting in this setting.** The UEBO bound is supported by theoretical justification (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Reviewer 3 calls it a good contribution; Reviewer 1 finds it new and sensible.
- **Experimental design is careful and comprehensive** (Reviewer 1): multiple baselines, four benchmarks, ablations, and hyperparameter analysis are included.
- **Effectiveness is clearly demonstrated:** Apollo-MILP achieves better objective values than both traditional solvers and prior ML methods, with over 50% gap reduction (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 3 notes well-chosen benchmarks and clear advantages.
- **The iterative predict-and-fix strategy is well-designed and the decoupling into prediction and correction is interesting** (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Discrepancy in CA benchmark difficulty:** Table 8 reports CA instances with ~1500 variables and ~2500 constraints, but Gasse et al.’s “Hard” CA instances (1500 bids) are solved to optimality by SCIP in ~136 s on average. In contrast, Table 1 shows Gurobi terminating suboptimally after 1000 s. Reviewers request clarification whether the instances are indeed the same and an explanation for the performance gap (Reviewer 1).
- **UEBO calculation is insufficiently clear**, especially the conditional distribution \(q(x \mid \hat x_i, I)\) and the second term of the bound. A small numerical example would help (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing baseline: warm-starting Gurobi with the initial GNN prediction as a MIPStart.** This is a crucial comparison to assess the benefits of the proposed framework over directly using the predictor inside a commercial solver (Reviewer 1). Similarly, parameters \(k_0, k_1, \Delta\) in Table 6 differ from the original PS paper [3]; it is recommended to use optimal parameters for baselines for a fair comparison (Reviewer 4).
- **Gurobi MIPFocus=1 setting:** The “Gurobi” row likely uses default settings; to fairly compare on primal gap, MIPFocus=1 should be used to emphasize solution finding (Reviewer 1).
- **Comparison to ND [1] and ConsPS [2] may not be reliable** without reproducing experiments from the original papers to ensure comparability (Reviewer 4).
- **Theoretical contributions are weak under strong assumptions.** Assumption 1 is considered too strong and counter-intuitive (needs empirical validation). Theorem 4 is trivial (“if trust-region problem is feasible…”) and not worth a theorem (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 sees the paper as a trivial extension of work [3] (Han et al. 2023) with no significant theoretical contribution, though other reviewers frame the uncertainty framework as novel.
- **Presentation clarity is mixed.** Reviewer 3 finds the paper well-written and clear; Reviewer 2 notes that the paper could better introduce background (e.g., entropy and KL divergence in context of MILP solution prediction) and recommends moving Algorithm 1 from Appendix B into the main text for readability. Also, notation on line 256 is confusing (Reviewer 1), and there is a missing “i” in the definition of \(q(x \mid \hat x_i, I)\) (page 6) (Reviewer 4). The WA figure is missing in Fig.3 (Reviewer 4).
- **Data collection is time-consuming** because the model requires optimal/near-optimal solutions as labels, which involves solving many instances (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited benchmarking:** Only 4 problem types are tested; although large-scale MILPs are expensive, including more (even smaller) problems would strengthen results (Reviewer 3). Missing details on continuous, integer, binary variable counts per instance (Reviewer 3).
- **Algorithmic design choices are insufficiently justified.** For example, why four iterations with 100, 100, 200, 600 seconds? What other time splits were explored? And what is the hyperparameter search space in Appendix F? (Reviewer 2). Only one variable per iteration is fixed (lowest UEBO); fixing multiple variables simultaneously could accelerate reduction and should be tested (Reviewer 3).
- **Relation to ML-based Large Neighborhood Search (LNS) is unclear.** The iterative variable-fixing strategy resembles LNS; a detailed distinction should be provided (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing ablation experiments that isolate the effect of the multi-stage correction.** Suggested: compare Apollo-MILP directly with 4 rounds of Direct Fixing and 4 rounds of PS, using identical parameters and solving times, to prove improvements come from the prediction-correction alternation, not simply from running multiple rounds (Reviewer 4).
