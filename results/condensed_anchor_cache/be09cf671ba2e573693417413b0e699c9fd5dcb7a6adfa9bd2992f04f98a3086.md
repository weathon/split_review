## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper combines Large Language Models (LLMs) with deep reinforcement learning (DQN/DDQN) for algorithmic trading. It introduces Stock-Evol-Instruct, an instruction generation algorithm that uses LLM-driven insights from financial news to fine-tune RL agents. Six LLMs (LLaMA-2, LLaMA-3, Mistral-7B, Falcon-7B, OpenELM, GPT-4o) are tested on Silver (SLV) and JPMorgan (JPM) stocks. Results show LLM-enhanced strategies often outperform traditional RL alone; Mistral-7B achieves ROIs of 53.15% (JPM) and 48.36% (SLV). Reviews range from very negative (rating 1, confidence 5) to very positive (rating 8, confidence 5), with two reviewers in the middle (ratings 6 and 3).

### Strengths
- Studies an important problem: automatic financial trading with LLM-enhanced reinforcement learning. (R1)
- Extensive experiments conducted to validate the approach. (R1)
- Presents an innovative combination of LLMs with RL algorithms, bridging the gap between LLM-focused financial tools (FinMem, FinGPT, FinRL) and DRL models that rely purely on quantitative data. (R2)
- Thorough design and evaluation with clear descriptions of methodologies, metrics (ROI, Sharpe Ratio), and prompt types (zero‑shot, instruction‑based, exemplar‑based). (R2)
- Well-structured paper with distinct sections on prompt design, trading environment, evaluation metrics, and empirical results. (R2)
- Forward‑thinking approach merging LLMs and deep reinforcement learning, leveraging LLM strength in unstructured data and DRL adaptability. (R3)
- Clear outline of contributions, especially the development of Stock‑Evol‑Instruct. (R3)
- Testing on real‑world data (Silver and JPMorgan) validates practical performance. (R3)
- Incorporates different prompt designs for LLM predictions, offering a robust testing ground for prompt optimization. (R3, R4)
- Tested a wide range of LLMs with various prompt types; writing is clear and concise in most sections. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- Literature review is not thorough; many relevant papers in LLM/AI for finance and trading are missing. Examples listed by one reviewer include FinMem, FinCon, LEVER (Yuan et al. 2023), and Yuan et al. (2021). This directly limits perceived novelty. (R1, R4 echoes missing context)
- Lack of comparisons to established baselines or hybrid approaches:
  - No comparison to a simple “buy‑and‑hold” strategy. (R4)
  - No comparison with a baseline using only LLMs for trading, making it unclear what the RL component adds. (R4)
  - Missing comparisons with FinRL, FinMem, or other multi‑modal models for trading. (R2, R1)
- Missing trading trajectory charts (e.g., equity curves), which are standard in stock trading papers. (R4)
- No clear justification for choosing JPMorgan and Silver as test stocks; for LLM‑dependent work, selection should be motivated by news coverage density. (R4)
- The relationship between the RL algorithm and the LLM is difficult to follow. The paper uses LLMs to refine rewards in addition to price‑based rewards, but this is not clearly explained in text or Figure 1. (R4)
- Inconsistencies between Sharpe Ratio (SR) and ROI performance metrics are noted but not analyzed in depth. A more detailed explanation of when and why these divergences occur is needed. (R3)
- The study relies on a relatively small set of prompts for instruction generation, which may restrict the model’s generalization across market conditions. (R3)
- Stock‑Evol‑Instruct includes in‑depth evolving steps that may introduce redundant complexity; simplifying the process or providing a clearer rationale for each step would improve replicability. (R3)
- Need a more systematic analysis of how different LLMs align with specific trading objectives beyond general NLP strengths. (R3)
- One reviewer finds the main problem is limited novelty due to missing literature review and baseline comparisons; another reviewer considers the work forward‑thinking and significant (rating 8). This disagreement reflects differing thresholds for novelty. (R1 vs R3)
- Writing can be improved overall. (R1, but R4 notes it is clear in most sections)
