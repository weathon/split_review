## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper empirically analyzes "catapults"—sharp spikes in the training loss—observed when training neural networks with SGD (and GD). The authors present evidence that these spikes occur in the subspace spanned by the top eigenvectors of the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) for both algorithms. They further propose that catapults help generalization by promoting feature learning, measured through increased alignment with the Average Gradient Outer Product (AGOP) of the true predictor. Additionally, they show that smaller SGD batch sizes produce more catapults, leading to better AGOP alignment and test performance. The work focuses on the squared-error loss and examines a variety of architectures and tasks.

### Strengths
- The paper studies an important and interesting phenomenon (spikes in training loss) that deserves attention in the community (R2, R4).
- It is well-motivated, well-written, and clearly organized (R1, R2; note: R4 disagrees on presentation, see Weaknesses).
- Provides new insights by decomposing catapults in the eigenspace of the NTK and connecting them to generalization (R1, R3, R4).
- The link between catapults and AGOP alignment is original and interesting (R4).
- Experiments are extensive and generally support the claims (R1, R3).
- Proposes simple but new postulates and verifies them across a range of tasks and architectures (R3).
- Includes some theoretical discussion (R1).

### Weaknesses
**Lack of mechanistic and theoretical explanation**
- The paper does not explain *why* catapults occur. Observations (e.g., they happen in the top NTK eigenspace, the NTK spectral norm decreases) are direct consequences, not underlying causes. A deeper analysis of the loss landscape or weight dynamics is needed, including how architectures/activations influence catapult propensity (R1).
- No rigorous theoretical proof is provided. The assumption of linearized dynamics between spikes is questionable and not justified, even in simpler settings like a 2-layer network (R2). If this assumption could be clarified, the reviewer would raise their score from 6 to 8 (R2).

**Correlation vs. causation for generalization**
- The link between catapults and improved generalization remains correlational. The paper does not establish a causal mechanism—whether manipulating catapults directly changes generalization or whether the correlation is a byproduct of other factors (R1).
- The paper shows the NTK decreases in size after a spike but does not characterize *how* it changes (e.g., whether it decreases more along its top eigenvalues) (R3).
- While AGOP alignment increases, the paper does not explain *why* spikes should increase it (R3).

**Overclaiming AGOP as the mechanism of feature learning**
- The paper presents AGOP alignment as “the mechanism through which NN learn features.” Multiple reviewers (R3, R4) consider this overclaiming given other proposed mechanisms (Information Bottleneck, NTK/kernel alignment). AGOP may not fully capture feature learning, especially for nonlinear features (R3). The definition of the “true model” \(f^*\) is also not rigorous (R4).
- Additional motivation for AGOP as a generalization measure would strengthen the paper, e.g., a low-rank matrix sensing example connecting AGOP alignment to reconstruction error (R4).

**Limited loss function scope**
- The paper uses only the squared-error loss. Cross-entropy / logistic loss is standard in realistic deep learning for classification. It is unclear whether catapults occur or whether the findings carry over to that setting, which limits impact (R4).

**Insufficient experiment details and potential confounds**
- The NTK parameterization is not clearly defined, and its necessity for the results is not justified (R4).
- Step-size selection for key experiments (e.g., Figure 3) is not specified; the description “starts small … increases” is vague. Exact numbers are not given (R4).
- For the claim that small-batch SGD improves generalization, only a single step size is used per task across batch sizes (Figures 7, 18–20). This may be unfair; Geiping et al. (2022) show the benefit can disappear with proper tuning (R4).
- Claim 2 addresses changes in the projected batch loss, but SGD experiments show the *full* loss, leaving the significance of Claim 2 unclear (R4).
- The observation that catapults occur in the top eigenspace of the NTK is not well-motivated (the top eigenvectors correspond to important examples, so the finding may be expected); the “so what?” question is unanswered, and the connection to the AGOP part is unclear (R4).

**Missing comparisons and connections**
- No results with momentum are reported (R1).
- No relation to other generalization measures (flatness via Hessian eigenvalues, trajectory tail-index) is explored (R1).
- No connection is made to saddle-to-saddle hopping dynamics; could such hoppings be equated to catapults? (R1).
- Other measures of feature learning, such as alignment between the NTK and the task, are not considered, despite the paper’s emphasis on the NTK spectrum (R3).

**Unclear or insufficiently explained statements**
- “The loss drops quickly to pre-spike levels” is vague: “pre-spike levels” and “quick” are not defined (R4).
- Notation issues: \(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{L}_5\) appears without explanation (R4); Equation (6) seems to have a dimension mismatch between \(\mathbf{f}(X_{\text{batch}})\) and the projection operator defined for the full set of \(n\) examples (R4). The choice of top eigenspace dimension \(s\) is confusing—the paper expected a larger \(s\) but found a small \(s\) sufficient, yet gives no explanation (R4).
- The related work section is a laundry list without adequate discussion of how each work relates to the present paper (R4).
- The paper claims the critical step size depends only on initialization, but does not justify why it would not vary during training (R4).
- It is unclear whether \(\eta_{\text{crit}}(X_{\text{batch}})\) needs to be recomputed for each batch, and if so, how (R4).

**Presentation quality disagreement**
- While most reviewers found the paper well-written and sound (R1, R2, R3: soundness 3, presentation 3–4), one reviewer (R4) rated it lower (soundness 2, presentation 2), citing handwavy language, insufficient experiment details, and poor structure in the experimental details section. Multiple minor issues were also raised (e.g., Latin words not italicized, “1e-2” vs “0.01”, incomplete captions, missing definitions for the NTK parameterization and “true model”). (R4)
