## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper explores scaling diffusion models (up to 2.8B parameters, dense and MoE) for mid-level visual perception tasks (depth estimation, optical flow, amodal segmentation) framed as image-to-image translation. Through extensive experiments on ImageNet pre-training and downstream fine-tuning, it examines how model size, pre-training compute, image resolution, and test-time compute (sampling steps, ensembling) affect performance, reporting power-law scaling behavior. A unified model with task-specific routing is proposed but not evaluated. Reviewers acknowledge the scope of experiments but criticize the paper’s clarity, experimental rigor, missing comparisons, and limited novelty.

### Strengths
1. Extensive experiments across multiple scales and compute regimes, demonstrating power-law scaling in pre-training loss, fine-tuning loss, and downstream metrics (e.g., delta1 accuracy) – providing a rigorous ablation of model size, pre-training compute, resolution, and test-time compute. [R1, R3]
2. Motivation is meaningful: moving beyond fine-tuning Stable Diffusion 2.1 to systematically study scaling laws for diffusion models in vision perception tasks. [R4 (minority positive)]
3. The unified framework that treats various mid-level tasks as image-to-image translation and the use of Mixture of Experts routing to handle multiple tasks in one model are noted as positive architectural contributions. [R2 (minority, score 5), but R3 counters that no unified model experiments are presented.]

### Weaknesses
1. **Writing and presentation issues**: The paper reads like a technical report, lacking scientific insight and clear connections between sections. Many details are missing (optical flow architecture, data format, supplementary material); axes labels are incorrect in Figures 3, 4, 5, 8, 9; Figure 6’s legend is wrong and its caption compares results not included in the plot. [R1, R2, R3]
2. **Insufficient baselines and comparisons for perceptual tasks**:  
   - Depth estimation lacks fair comparisons with modern methods (Marigold, GeoWizard). [R4]  
   - Optical flow uses old baselines and an artificial test set; should compare with RAFT on Sintel; performance gap is large. [R1]  
   - Amodal segmentation should be evaluated on Occluded and Separated COCO (as in pix2gestalt). [R1]  
   - Overall, the downstream experiments are not convincing due to limited datasets and outdated baselines. [R1, R2, R4]
3. **Limited novelty of the unified framework**: The idea of using diffusion for dense prediction has been explored by prior work (e.g., Lee et al. 2023), and no experiments on the claimed unified model are actually shown – only individual task models are evaluated. [R2, R3]
4. **Questionable generalization and credibility**: Pre-training only on ImageNet; limited diversity of datasets for perceptual tasks (depth, flow, segmentation) makes scaling conclusions empirical and potentially not generalizable. [R3, R4] Results largely follow the intuition that more compute improves performance, without offering deeper insights. [R3]
5. **Missing discussion of alternative fine-tuning paradigms**: Flow matching fine-tuning and deterministic fine-tuning are not discussed, yet these affect scaling behavior. [R4]
6. **Unclear evaluation metrics and analysis**:  
   - The metric “value” in depth estimation is not defined. [R2]  
   - Power-law approximation is inconsistent between depth absolute relative error and delta1 error, suggesting potential inability to generalize. [R3]  
   - The paper claims “three key findings” (line 49) but does not explicitly state them. [R3]
7. **Missing references**: Prior works such as DatasetDM (NeurIPS 2023) and Open-vocabulary Object Segmentation with Diffusion Models (ICCV 2023) are not cited. [R1]
8. **Lack of efficiency and MoE comparison**: The performance and efficiency of the Mixture-of-Experts model should be compared with direct fine-tuning from Stable Diffusion 2 and Stable Diffusion XL. [R4]
