## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents DreamFlow, a three-stage coarse-to-fine text-to-3D generation framework that uses a new optimization loss (APFO) derived by reframing score distillation as a Schrödinger Bridge problem and applying amortized sampling with a predetermined timestep schedule. The method targets high-resolution (1024×1024) outputs and claims 5× speedup over VSD (ProlificDreamer). Reviewer scores are 5, 6, 8, 6, with one reviewer (score 8) more positive about the theoretical perspective and user study, while others (scores 5, 6, 6) question technical novelty and the distinction from VSD.

### Strengths
- **Novel theoretical framing**: The perspective of treating text-to-3D as solving a Schrödinger Bridge problem for multi-view image-to-image translation is insightful and novel (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 explicitly calls the formulation novel and the amortized sampling stabilization convincing.
- **Good visual quality and faster convergence**: Visual results are comparable or better than baselines; quantitative scores are slightly better (Reviewer 1). The method achieves a 5× speedup over VSD (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4) and produces photorealistic results (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
- **Stable optimization**: The amortized sampling scheme with non-increasing timestep schedule stabilizes the loss curve (Figure 6) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- **User study**: A user preference study is conducted, which is considered more convincing than automated metrics (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 notes that visual results are comparable.
- **Paper clarity**: The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2). Experiments are sufficient (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Technical novelty overlap with existing methods**: The final ODE (Equation 12) is very similar to VSD (Equation 8), and the core optimization heavily resembles ProlificDreamer’s NeRF/mesh update procedures (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). Multiple reviewers ask for a clear, mathematical comparison between APFO’s time-derivative of noise scale and VSD’s weighting function, and explicit delineation of practical differences (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). The predetermined timestep schedule is not novel; it has been explored in prior works (DreamTime, ProlificDreamer, Magic3D) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4). The amortized sampling (inner loop per timestep) could straightforwardly be applied to VSD; no comparison is shown (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited diversity and fine-grained details**: Using a LoRA-fine-tuned score function within the SB formulation may constrain output diversity; diversity experiments are needed (Reviewer 1). Some results show weird textures (e.g., “A stack of pancakes covered in maple syrup”) and color biases (e.g., green/brown tint in cactus (Fig.4), corgi (Fig.7)) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2). The cause may be the pretrained diffusion model or the optimization process; the paper should analyze and suggest mitigations (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient evaluation**: Quantitative metrics are computed on only 20 3D assets; a larger and more diverse dataset is needed to validate improvements (Reviewer 1). The improved scores are modest. The third stage (SDXL refinement) does not consistently improve results; most generated assets show little improvement except the cottage example (Fig.13). Ablation studies comparing with and without SDXL over many prompts are missing (Reviewer 1). The ablation in the paper is limited to checking per-stage contributions, which is not novel (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear connection between motivation and method**: The paper motivates the method by addressing gradient variance from random timesteps, but it does not explain how the SB formulation or APFO specifically reduces variance (Reviewer 4). The stability visualization (gradient norm, Figure 6) is for the norm, not direction; norm variation naturally arises from random timesteps, and it is not shown why norm variance is detrimental to optimization—direction is more important (Reviewer 4). The statement “randomly drawn timestep makes optimization unstable” and leads to poor geometry is insufficiently justified (Reviewer 4). A rigorous mathematical explanation of why variance in gradient norm is problematic is requested (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing details and clarifications**: The definition of “tie” is missing from Table 1 (Reviewer 1). There are typos in Equation 13 (Φ vs φ, q vs p), and the transition from Eq.12 to Eq.13 is unclear (Reviewer 1). The CFG scale used in each stage and the GPU memory consumption of LoRA fine-tuning SDXL in stage 3 are not reported (Reviewer 2). The predetermined timestep selection (decreasing ratio over iterations) is not clearly described; it is also unclear whether the method is only applicable to a linear schedule and how it differs from DreamTime’s timestep annealing (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4). A 2D experiment demonstrating APFO’s effectiveness would strengthen the paper (Reviewer 2).
- **Request for additional analysis**: Would applying VSD with the same timestep schedule yield similar results? (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). Provide a detailed mathematical comparison of APFO and VSD, and explain where the practical gains (speed, quality) come from—preferably with isolated ablation (Reviewer 4). Broader applications of the modeling approach beyond score distillation are of interest (Reviewer 4).
