## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes 3D-Adapter, a plug-in module that infuses 3D geometry awareness into pretrained multi-view and text-to-image diffusion models. At each denoising step, intermediate multi-view features are decoded into a coherent 3D representation (Gaussian splatting or NeRF/DMTet), then re-encoded as rendered RGBD views via a fine-tuned ControlNet. The method is tested on text-to-3D, image-to-3D, text-to-texture, and text-to-avatar tasks, showing improved geometry consistency and visual quality over baselines like Instant3D and Zero123++.

### Strengths
- **Conceptually novel 3D feedback augmentation**: integrating a lightweight 3D rendering pipeline into the diffusion sampling process via ControlNet feature addition is a new mechanism that balances computational efficiency and multi-view consistency (Reviewers 2, 4, 5).
- **Clear positioning and demonstration of I/O sync limitations**: the paper explicitly analyzes why existing input/output-level synchronization degrades residual connections and texture quality, motivating intermediate-level augmentation (Reviewer 4).
- **Resource-efficient design**: the method is training-data efficient and requires only light fine-tuning (2000–4000 iterations) of the reconstruction module while keeping base models frozen (Reviewer 4).
- **Versatility across tasks and base models**: 3D-Adapter is applied to text-to-3D, image-to-3D, text-to-texture, and text-to-avatar generation using different base models (Instant3D, Zero123++, Stable Diffusion), demonstrating broad applicability (Reviewers 2, 5).
- **Extensive quantitative and qualitative evaluations**: the paper includes comprehensive metric tables (CLIP score, aesthetic score, etc.) and visual comparisons across multiple configurations (Reviewers 1, 3, 4, 5).
- **Noticeable improvements in geometry quality and coherence**: the proposed method outperforms prior two-stage methods on geometry consistency (Reviewers 1, 4); figures in challenging cases show clear improvements (Reviewer 5).
- **Effective use of depth conditioning via ControlNet**: depth maps rendered from the 3D reconstruction help enforce 3D structure during denoising (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Broad scope obscures clarity**: the paper covers too many applications (text-to-3D, image-to-3D, text-to-texture, text-to-avatar); the text-to-avatar section appears tangential to the core 3D consistency contribution. A more focused examination on a single pipeline (e.g., GRM) would be more accessible (Reviewer 1). The paper is hard to read and navigate due to scope and writing style (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Computational overhead without mitigation**: per-step VAE decoding + 3D reconstruction + ControlNet adds significant inference cost; no strategies to reduce frequency (e.g., skipping early steps) or adopt lightweight VAEs (e.g., TinyAE) are explored (Reviewer 1). Lack of detailed profiling for per-stage memory usage and inference time in text-to-3D / image-to-3D tasks, making practical applicability hard to assess (Reviewer 4). Training efficiency concerns: fine-tuning GRM while keeping the multi-view diffusion model in memory is resource-intensive; training time and memory usage not reported (Reviewer 5).
- **Understudied design choices for ControlNet**:
    - In the GRM branch, ControlNet is trained from scratch, but this is not justified via ablation (Reviewer 1).
    - Justification for using ControlNet over pure latent fusion is not well explained (Reviewer 3).
    - No analysis on why ControlNet-like feature addition (feedback augmentation) is effective, given that it does not guarantee reduction of the gap in Eq. (7) (Reviewer 4).
- **Clarity issues in exposition**:
    - Relationship between GRM and base models (Instant3D, Zero123++) is unclear: does GRM build 3D from views generated by these models, and is fine-tuning of GRM involved? (Reviewer 2)
    - Components of 3D-Adapter (VAE Decoder, ControlNet, 3D Reconstruction Model) and the “I/O sync” baselines compared in Fig. 1 are not explicitly defined (Reviewer 5).
    - Notation in tables is confusing: “3D-Adapter” is listed as if it were a standalone model, not a plugin; should be “Instant3D + 3D-Adapter” (Reviewer 4).
- **Unsupported or unclear claims about I/O sync methods**:
    - The assertion (L046-L053) that I/O sync operations (e.g., NerfDiff, DMV3D, VideoMV) disrupt residual connections and cause blurry textures is vague and not adequately supported. Residual connections within UNet should not be affected if 3D reconstruction is applied before/after denoising. Concrete evidence or examples of “blurry textures” vs. “texture seams” are missing (Reviewers 1, 3, 5).
    - The term “linearity” in the context of synchronization is not defined (Reviewer 3).
- **Marginal quality gains and suspicious visuals**:
    - In the main text-to-3D task, improvements are marginal; visuals in Fig. 1 are comparable between 3D-Adapter and two-stage pipeline (Reviewer 3).
    - Fig. 3 column 5 shows broken visuals despite high CLIP score, suggesting evaluation or implementation issues (Reviewer 3). The I/O sync baseline in Tables 1 and 5 shows a suspicious drop from A0 to A1, which is not explained (Reviewer 1). The paper should double-check these evaluations (Reviewer 3).
- **Mixed novelty assessment**: Some reviewers find the idea of 3D feedback augmentation novel (Reviewers 2,4,5), but Reviewer 3 argues that using 3D representations for synchronization has been explored before (e.g., NerfDiff), and the main contribution is a technical choice of placing the adapter in a parallel branch rather than at input/output. No decision is made here, but the contrast is noted.
- **Missing comparisons with recent methods**:
    - Image-to-3D: CRM, SV3D, InstantMesh not compared (Reviewer 5).
    - Text-to-texture: missing comparisons with DreamMat (SIGGRAPH 2024), TexPainter (SIGGRAPH 2024), Paint-it (CVPR 2024), Paint3D (CVPR 2024); missing FID/KID metrics; not compared with FlashTex (Reviewers 4, 5).
    - Text-to-3D: EpiDiff (CVPR 2024), which released code, not compared (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing related works**: EpiDiff, LRM (ICLR 2024), Free3D, SPAD, IM3D, Carve3D, Ouroboros3D, Cycle3D not cited or discussed (Reviewers 4, 5).

- **Insufficient scalability and generalization analysis**:
    - Need to discuss whether ControlNet incorporation yields similar results for other I/O sync pretrained models beyond GRM, and whether Training Phase 1 is essential for those (Reviewer 2).
    - Global semantic consistency remains challenging despite using NeRF and DMTet; raises concerns for general text-to-3D tasks outside specialized domains (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing ablations and visual evidence**:
    - No visual evaluations beyond Table 1 for augmentation guidance (Reviewer 2).
    - No results for the optimization-based 3D-Adapter without additional conditioning (Reviewer 2).
    - No dedicated pipeline figure for the process described in Section 4.2 (line 289) (Reviewer 2).
    - Rationale for specific iteration numbers (2000 for Instant3D, 4000 for Zero123++) and minimal training data not provided (Reviewer 4).
    - Reasoning/ablations for the optimization schedule (96 steps, 60% NeRF + 40% DMTet) not given (Reviewer 3).
- **Specific technical questions (overlap with criticisms)**:
    - What is the rationale for the significant metric drop in Table 1 from A0 to A1? (Reviewer 1)
    - How does output quality compare to I/O sync + tiled ControlNet, and how critical is ControlNet’s role during denoising relative to depth conditioning in IO mode? (Reviewer 1)
    - Are the two ControlNets for superresolution and depth used together? Can depth be fed to superresolution ControlNet without training? (Reviewer 4)
    - What is “texture field optimization” (line 485) – is it applied to competing methods in Table 6? (Reviewer 4)
    - How does 3D-Adapter differ fundamentally from existing I/O sync techniques (e.g., DMV3D, VideoMV) beyond the claim about residual connections? (Reviewer 5)
