## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper addresses species distribution modeling (SDM) with presence-only data from iNaturalist, using no environmental variables. It proposes a hybrid representation that combines an implicit neural network (FCNet-based) with an explicit multiresolution hashgrid (inspired by Müller et al. 2022) to better capture local spatial features. Experiments on S&T and IUCN benchmarks show the hybrid model outperforms previous implicit methods (SINR, GP, BDS) as well as purely implicit or explicit variants. The paper is generally well-written and includes extensive ablation studies. However, reviewers express concerns about the significance of the contribution given existing hybrid geospatial models, potential overfitting due to test-set-based hyperparameter selection, missing comparisons with other multi-scale implicit architectures (sphere2vec, Siren), unclear implementation details, and the decision to omit environmental variables, which limits practical impact. There is notable disagreement: one reviewer rates the paper 8 (strong positive), one rates 6 (positive), and two rate 3 (negative).

### Strengths

- **Effective hybrid approach.** The idea of combining implicit and explicit representations for SDM is promising and leads to SOTA results on standard benchmarks (S&T and IUCN), outperforming SINR, GP, BDS, and purely implicit/explicit variants (Table 1, 2). The hybrid model is computationally comparable to the previous SINR model (Reviewer 4). Experiments varying the "implicitness" parameter provide compelling evidence for the advantages of the hybrid design (Reviewer 3, 4).

- **Well-executed evaluation.** Extensive experiments investigate the impact of training data amount, number of species, and model implicitness. Error bars are provided for the S&T benchmark (Appendix), strengthening reliability (Reviewer 4). The paper demonstrates that results are not sensitive to hyperparameters (Reviewer 2).

- **Clarity and presentation.** The problem formulation, motivation for the hybrid approach, and related work are clearly described (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). Figures 1–4 help visualize the architecture (Reviewer 4).

- **Originality within SDM.** Applying a multiresolution hashgrid as an explicit component in SDM is underexplored (Reviewer 4). The paper is one of the first to combine these representations for this task.

### Weaknesses

- **Missing environmental variables.** The main results use no environmental information, but in practice such data is nearly always available and expected. Reviewer 2 argues this is a severe limitation that reduces practical impact; the current setting is more like an ablation. Both Reviewers 2 and 4 note that MaxEnt and other SDM models (e.g., [1] from arxiv 2401.02989) typically include environmental covariates and are hard to beat. The paper would be strengthened by showing results with environmental inputs.

- **Potential overfitting to the test set.** Hyperparameter (learning rate) selection appears to use test set performance, and different hyperparameters are chosen for different evaluation tasks (Tables 9–11 in Appendix). This could inflate results (Reviewer 3). The paper does not clearly describe how model selection was performed (holdout set details missing) (Reviewer 4).

- **Missing comparisons with relevant baselines.**  
  - The paper only compares with FCNet among implicit models, but newer architectures with internal multi-scale representations (e.g., sphere2vec, Siren) are designed to represent high-frequency patterns and should be included (Reviewer 2).  
  - Prior hybrid geospatial models exist: Kim et al. "Hybrid Neural Representations for Spherical Data" (ICML 2024) uses grids at multiple resolutions, and Müller et al. (2022) already describe a hybrid NeRF architecture. Rußwurm et al. (ICLR 2024) also perform species range estimation with location encodings. The claim that this is the first hybrid/explicit SDM model (L149, L239) is overstated (Reviewer 3).  
  - The paper would benefit from a comparison where environmental variables are added, as is standard practice (Reviewer 2).

- **Unclear methodology details.**  
  - The explicit representation implementation is insufficiently described: it is unclear how grids at different resolutions are "stacked," whether they share lattice points (and if so, whether feature representations are shared), and whether an offset is applied to prevent alignment artifacts (Reviewer 3).  
  - Figure 3, intended to explain the explicit method, is confusing: the multiplication of "Hashed Features" and "Resolution Layers" is ambiguous, and the color coding (cyan square, semi-transparent squares) obscures key connections (Reviewer 3).  
  - The encoding of global coordinates into the network (number of cos/sin frequency representations) is not specified (Reviewer 1).  
  - Several notations in Section 3 are not defined (Reviewer 2).

- **Hyperparameter justification and missing details.**  
  - The value of M (hash table feature size) when implicitness equals 0.5 is not given; the text only says M=8 for implicitness <0.5 and M=16 for >0.5, leaving the exact 0.5 case ambiguous (Reviewer 3).  
  - The justification for changing M with implicitness is not provided (Reviewer 3, Q2).  
  - The total number of parameters and the hash table size (L) are not reported (Reviewer 3).  
  - The paper does not discuss memory footprint relative to SINR, nor the tradeoff with a fully explicit model that could match the hybrid’s performance using a larger hidden representation (as Table 3 suggests) (Reviewer 4).

- **Missing qualitative results.** The paper does not include visualizations of predicted species ranges, which is common in this area (Cole et al., Phillips et al.). Such maps, especially showing sharp ecological boundaries (e.g., rivers), would help evaluate whether the hybrid model captures high-frequency patterns (Reviewer 3, Q3).

- **Missing error bars on several benchmarks.** Error bars are provided for S&T but not reported for IUCN and GeoFeatures results (Tables 2 and others) (Reviewer 4).

- **Potential waste of training data.** The observation cap may discard useful training examples (Reviewer 2).

- **Minor issues:**  
  - Figures are low resolution; text in figures should be larger; yellow lines on gray background have poor contrast (Reviewer 1).  
  - Table digits not aligned by significance (Tables 3, 8) (Reviewer 1).  
  - LaTeX formatting errors: `max`, `min` should be roman; brackets in Eq. 3 should be larger; `(lat, lon)` should be in roman; scientific notation for numbers <0.001; thousands separators for numbers ≥10000. Several typos: "well- suited" (L521), "to" should be "the" (L233), "Our method is the aggregate" → "Our method is to aggregate" (Reviewer 1, 4).  
  - Reference titles incorrectly capitalized ("Tensorf", "roc", "Plenoctrees") (Reviewer 1).  
  - Appendix needs text to describe tables and hyperlinks (Reviewer 1).  
  - Figures 7–10 have inconsistent y-axis scales and very small axis labels (Reviewer 3).  
  - L40: "Distribution range" should be either "distribution" or "range". L50: missing citations for iNaturalist, eBird, PlantNet. L75: presence-only SDM predates neural approaches (e.g., MaxEnt). L123: data difficulties not elaborated. L195: wrapped encoding is for boundary artifacts, not high resolution. L315: "iNaturalist" phrasing awkward. Reviewers 3 and 4 also note missing updated citation venues (Kim et al. ICML 2024, Kingma & Ba ICLR 2015, Rußwurm et al. ICLR 2024).
