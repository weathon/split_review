## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper investigates how LLMs encode truthfulness in their internal representations, focusing on exact answer tokens rather than last-token or averaged representations. Using probing classifiers across multiple LLMs (Mistral-7b, Llama3-8b), the authors find that truthfulness information is highly localized in specific tokens, that truthfulness encoding is task-specific (not universal), and that models can internally encode the correct answer while generating an incorrect one. They also develop a taxonomy of error types. The paper is well-structured and the experiments are comprehensive, providing new insights into understanding hallucinations from a model-centric perspective.

### Strengths

- **New perspective on token-level probing:** Focuses on exact answer tokens rather than last-token or averaged representations, providing a relatively new and underexplored dimension of LLM truthfulness encoding. (R1, R2)
- **Truthfulness is task-specific:** The work convincingly demonstrates that probing for truthfulness does not generalize across datasets, contradicting prior claims of universality. This introduces an important consideration for cross-task applications. (R1, R2)
- **Detailed error taxonomy:** The taxonomy of error types (e.g., "two competing answers," "consistent incorrectness") can help developers diagnose and prioritize error types during model fine-tuning. (R1)
- **Discrepancy between internal encoding and external behavior:** The finding that LLMs can internally encode the correct answer yet consistently produce incorrect outputs offers a deeper understanding of how hallucinations manifest and potential ways to mitigate them. (R3, R4)
- **Robustness across architectures:** The probing approach is tested on multiple LLM architectures (Mistral-7b, Llama3-8b), showing consistency in truthfulness encoding patterns. (R1)
- **Well-structured and clear:** The paper is clearly written and the experiments effectively demonstrate the findings. (R2, R3, R4)
- **Originality and significance:** The shift from a human-centric to a model-centric perspective on hallucinations is insightful and valuable for future research on internal factuality. (R4)

### Weaknesses

- **Generalization limited to specific tasks:** The approach may only be applicable to QA tasks; performance on open-ended questions is not shown. (R2, R3) For open-ended generation, it is unclear if the method can be applied. (R2)
- **Limited generalization across datasets/tasks:** The probing classifier shows poor cross-task generalization, with many AUC scores in the 0.5–0.6 range (only slightly better than random). Reasons for this limited generalization are not adequately explained. (R3) Also, the error taxonomy lacks quantitative analysis on consistency across varying prompts or domains; it is unclear if error types remain stable under different experimental conditions (e.g., factual vs. commonsense tasks). (R1, R2)
- **Concern about advantage of exact answer tokens over last token:** Results in Table 1 show that AUC for exact answer tokens is nearly identical to that of last generated tokens (e.g., TriviaQA). This raises the question of whether the claimed benefit of exact answer tokens is significant, especially for more generic tasks where Table 2 shows barely any improvement. (R2) Further, the performance variation across models (Mistral worse than LLaMA) is left unexplained—is it because Mistral’s answers involve fewer explanations? (R2)
- **Missing experiment to directly test localization:** The paper claims truthfulness is highly localized but does not evaluate probe performance when the model is constrained to generate short, factual answers. Such an experiment would provide more direct evidence supporting the claim. (R4)
- **Interpretability of probing classifiers is unclear:** The choice of probing layer and its impact on predictions is not explained. A case study showing how different layers and token choices affect classifier predictions would improve interpretability and practical adoption. (R1) Also, the claim that "middle to later layers typically yield the most effective probing results" (p.5, Fig. 2) seems unsupported by the figure. (R4)
- **Lack of cross-LLM generalization tests:** The paper only tests cross-dataset generalization; it does not test whether a probe trained on one LLM (e.g., Mistral-7b) works on another (e.g., Llama3-8b). This would indicate if probing captures model-agnostic truthfulness features. (R3)
- **Insufficient details on probing classifier:** The specific model used for the probing classifier and training setup (hyperparameters, optimizer, dataset split) are not adequately described. (R3, R4) For the triplet dataset construction (Section 3.1), it is unclear which model generated the responses. (R4)
- **Practical guidance missing:** There is limited advice on how these insights can be integrated into model development workflows or how to leverage the findings for general-purpose LLMs handling unpredictable tasks. (R1, R4)
- **Token-level focus may miss broader context:** The framework analyzes isolated answer tokens, potentially missing interactions across tokens in longer responses (e.g., long-form QA or summarization). Testing context windows around answer tokens or sentence-level analysis could improve accuracy. (R1)
- **Definition of hallucination is vague:** The paper does not adopt a precise definition (e.g., inconsistency between model expression and world knowledge from training). (R4)
- **Minor issues:** Error patterns were not designed to be orthogonal (p.8) and the meaning of different colored bars in Fig. 5 is not clarified. (R4) Also, the accuracy of extracting the exact answer token from Mistral-7b-Instruct is not validated (p.5). (R4)
