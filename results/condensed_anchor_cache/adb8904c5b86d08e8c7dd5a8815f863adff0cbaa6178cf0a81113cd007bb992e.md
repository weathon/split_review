## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Wyckoff Transformer, a generative model for crystals conditioned on space group symmetry. It uses Wyckoff positions as a discrete, permutation-invariant tokenization, then generates these tokens autoregressively. The final structure is completed by PyXtal or a modified DiffCSP++ for atomic coordinates and lattice parameters. The authors emphasize symmetric crystal generation, which existing diffusion models struggle with. Experiments on MP-20 show strong performance on new symmetry-focused metrics (P1, Template Novelty, Space Group, S.S.U.N.) and competitive results on standard proxy metrics (formation energy, band gap, mechanical/thermal properties). The paper also introduces four new evaluation metrics.

### Strengths

- The application of ML to materials discovery is interesting and timely, and the work tackles an important limitation of generative models for crystals (lack of symmetry). (R1, R3, R4)
- The Wyckoff representation builds in crystal symmetries naturally and provides a novel, compressed, and discrete representation that could facilitate learning of crystal symmetry with deep learning. (R1, R3, R4, R5)
- The proposed solution (two-stage process with tokenized Wyckoff positions) is simple, sound, and effective. (R3, R4)
- Good empirical results for the new symmetry metrics: template novelty, P1, and Space Group distribution. The evaluation methods and the four new metrics (P1, Template Novelty, Space Group, S.S.U.N.) provide a valuable new way of assessing generative models' ability to reproduce symmetry properties. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Material property prediction results are surprisingly good when compared against neural nets trained for energy prediction, despite using limited input (only Wyckoff tokens). (R2)
- Justification in the appendix for the selected two structure generation methods (PyXtal and DiffCSP++). (R2)
- The work is original (novel approach avoiding positional encoding) and significant for both ML and materials science. (R5 – though notes results are not yet complete)

### Weaknesses

- **Unfair comparisons and data-leak concern (R5):** Comparisons in tables may not be fair because baseline models are not conditioned on space group in the same way as the proposed model. This could expose the proposed model to data-leak, giving an unfair advantage. (R5, strong negative)
- **Limited improvement on standard de novo generation metrics (R1, R5):** S.U.N. actually decreases compared to DiffCSP, and little improvement is seen on common proxy metrics. The inductive bias from conditioning on space group limits the exploration space of materials, potentially defeating the purpose of material generation. (R1, R5)
- **Evaluation quality concerns:** De novo generation metrics were computed using an ML potential (CHGNet) instead of DFT, which may affect reliability. (R1) Also, the percentage of novel but structurally invalid generations from the proposed method is not reported. (R4)
- **Property prediction scope is limited (R2, R4):** Only two properties (formation energy and band gap) are predicted; it is unclear if the model can be extended to other properties like mechanical or electronic characteristics. (R2) The property prediction benchmark is not compelling; better benchmarks (e.g., Matbench Discovery) and more recent models (e.g., CHGNet) exist. (R1)
- **Missing experiments on generalization (R2, R4):** The model is evaluated only on the MP-20 dataset, which has a small number of Wyckoff positions; it is unclear how it would perform on crystals with many Wyckoff positions or on other datasets. The method may not generalize to more complex structures. (R2, R4) Dataset fragmentation due to conditioning on space group may also affect training. (R4)
- **Architecture and inference details are vague or missing (R4, R5):** No figure or pseudo-code of the training/generation pipeline; the central algorithm is unclear. The model architecture (how the neural network handles variable-length token sequences, whether masking is used, how the autoregressive component and property prediction head are trained) is insufficiently described. The claim of "fast autoregressive sampling" is not supported by any comparison. (R4, R5)
- **Conceptual issues with model conditioning (R5):** The claim that the model is "conditioned on previous tokens being chemical element, site symmetry etc." is inaccurate; language models condition on the joint of all previous tokens, not specifically on those fields. The number of tokens used to represent each field is not specified. (R5)
- **Invariance claim is too strong (R3):** The model is claimed to be invariant to permutations and choice of coset representative, but this is achieved only through data augmentation; a correct statement is that the model is *encouraged* to be invariant. (R3)
- **Wyckoff representation limitations (R3, R4):** Using only Wyckoff positions is not a complete representation, especially for atoms in the general position. The statement "reducing the number of parameters by an order of magnitude without information loss" is false. The performance degradation in property prediction when discarding coordinates confirms this. Also, the "enumeration" variable is based on an arbitrary convention, so for rare space groups the model may not generalize. (R3) The number of tokens and whether new tokens (not in the fixed dictionary) can be generated is not clarified. (R4) It would be better to provide absolute numbers of novel templates (instead of percentages) in Table 1a. (R4)
- **Related works discussion insufficient (R3, R4):** Several works are cited but not described, and differences from the proposed approach are not highlighted. (R3, R4)
- **Computational cost not reported (R1, R2, R4):** Training time and memory requirements are missing. The training duration (150k epochs) is much longer than existing methods (~1k epochs) and requires explanation. Inference speed/cost for WyFormatter compared to DiffCSP/FlowMM is not provided. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Missing citations and references (R1, R5):** A relevant citation (arxiv 2106.11132) should be included. Also, vague lines (42-44, 44, 74-76) lack citations, especially for factual claims (e.g., 230 distinct space groups). (R1, R5)
- **Presentation and writing issues (R2, R3, R4, R5):** Essential concepts (site symmetry, Wyckoff positions, space groups) are not appropriately introduced for readers unfamiliar with the field. The explanation of Wyckoff positions in the third paragraph of the introduction is too early and not easily understandable. Multiple typos and grammatical errors: "obey play", "tother", "lattice transition → lattice translation", "Cordiality → Cardinality", "they to be → they are", "percentage → percentage", etc. (R2, R3, R4, R5) Figure 1 is unclear (x-axis ticks, color map). (R5) A cross-reference in line 138 to section 1.2 appears incorrect. (R2)
- **Missing interpretability (R5):** The paper does not cover the interpretability of the results: why certain numbers are high or low, beyond stating observations. (R5)
- **Questions about experimental choices (R1, R3, R4):** Would it be better to fine-tune an LLM rather than train from scratch? (R1) How does the discrepancy between high Space Group value and similar S.S.U.N. value for WyForDiffCSP++ in Table 1a arise? (R2) Are there methods besides CHGNet that improve crystal structure generation? (R2) Can the model be extended to Crystal Structure Prediction (CSP)? (R4) What are the benefits of a two-stage approach over a one-shot prediction? (R4) Is CHGNet used for both relaxation and energy determination? (R4) The footnote 1 is confusing. (R3) The expression "These symmetries form energy configurations" in the abstract is unclear. (R3)
