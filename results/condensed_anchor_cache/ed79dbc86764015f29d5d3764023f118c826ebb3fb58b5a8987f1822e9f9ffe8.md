## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes I-GCG, an improved optimization-based jailbreak method building on GCG. The main modifications are: (1) diverse target templates that include harmful self-suggestion and guidance, (2) an automatic multi-coordinate updating strategy that adaptively decides how many tokens to replace per step, and (3) an easy-to-hard initialization that starts from simpler jailbreak tasks and transfers suffixes to harder ones. The method is evaluated on AdvBench and HarmBench (NeurIPS 2023 Red Teaming Track) across Vicuna-7B-1.5, Guanaco-7B, Llama2-7B-Chat, and Mistral-7B-Instruct-0.2, achieving nearly 100% ASR on AdvBench and up to ~10× speedup over vanilla GCG. Reviewers note that the work is timely and provides extensive comparisons, but disagree on its novelty and theoretical depth. Ratings are 6, 8, 6, 5; one reviewer (R2, rating 8, confidence 5) is notably more positive, while another (R4, rating 5, confidence 3) is more critical.

### Strengths

- **Relevance and timeliness:** The paper addresses an important topic in LLM safety and red-teaming (R1).
- **Extensive experimental comparison:** I-GCG is compared not only to GCG but also to several other recent baselines (Table 2) (R1, R4). Comprehensive evaluation is conducted on multiple benchmarks (AdvBench, HarmBench) and threat models (Vicuna-7B-1.5, Guanaco-7B, Llama2-7B-Chat, Mistral-7B-Instruct-0.2), providing robust empirical evidence (R2, R3).
- **High attack success rate and efficiency:** I-GCG achieves nearly 100% ASR on AdvBench across all tested LLMs and reduces required iterations and wall-clock time by up to ~10× (R2, R4).
- **Transferability:** The method demonstrates improved transferability of jailbreak prompts to different target models (R2).
- **Ablation study and reproducibility:** Ablation of the three proposed components is performed (Table 5), and the authors provide sufficient implementation details and code (R1).
- **Clear motivation with illustrative examples:** Specific examples (Figs. 1–2, highlighted sentences on p.5) clearly show the limitations of the single target template used in prior work (R4).
- **Well-defined mathematical formulations:** The multi-coordinate update and optimization objective are presented with clear equations (R2).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty and theoretical depth:** Multiple reviewers note that the improvements are incremental and that the core GCG framework is retained without deep theoretical analysis of why the modifications succeed (R4). The paper lacks a rigorous analysis of the optimization landscape, convergence properties, and trade-offs of the multi-coordinate update (R4). One reviewer (R2) is more positive about the technical contribution, but the overall consensus is that the contribution is more empirical than fundamental.
- **Scalability concerns for larger models:** The paper does not test models beyond 7B parameters. The multi-coordinate update may face increased dimensionality and memory overhead for models of 70B+ size, and it is unclear whether the convergence speed advantage would persist (R2).
- **Unaddressed defense interactions:** The work does not explore how existing defenses (e.g., adversarial training, input sanitization, output verification) might mitigate I-GCG, nor does it discuss potential counter-defense strategies (R2, R3). The inclusion of explicit harm-awareness prompts (“my output is harmful”) may narrow the attack scope to harms that LLMs have been fine-tuned to recognize – an issue that requires discussion (R1).
- **Issues with easy-to-hard initialization:**
  - The difficulty ranking of malicious questions (Fig. 5) is shown only for Llama2-7B-Chat, so the assumed difficulty ordering may not generalize to other models (R4). Moreover, the extra time cost to jailbreak an “easy” question is not justified; it remains unclear whether the time saved on the hard task actually compensates for the initial overhead (R1, R4).
  - The approach requires knowledge of which themes are easier to jailbreak, but no metric or analysis of difficulty across question categories or model families is reported (R1).
- **Paper structure and related work:** The paper is poorly structured; the related-work section cites large groups of references (up to 8) without clarifying their relevance, and misses key prior works (e.g., [1-3] for LLM-based jailbreak methods) (R1).
- **Limited generalizability of experiments:** High ASR is demonstrated on AdvBench, but evaluation on additional datasets and models (e.g., Llama2-7B non-chat, GPT-4, Claude, Llama-3.x) is lacking. The choice of ChatGPT-3.5 for ASR checks and transfer tests is noted as outdated, as this model is more vulnerable than newer ones (R1, R4).
- **Missing details and clarifications:**
  - The ASR calculation methodology (rule-based, GPT-3.5 check, manual review) is not clearly defined (R2).
  - The meaning of “average iterations” in Table 1 is ambiguous (R2).
  - The specific number of top-K token candidates used for the vanilla GCG baseline in the comparison is not stated (R2).
  - The rationale for using different target response formats in HarmBench (NeurIPS 2023) vs. AdvBench is not explained, raising comparability questions (R3).
  - The formulation of $x^S(0)$ and $x_0^S$ as constraints in Eqs. 6–7 lacks justification (R4).
- **Sensitivity to hyperparameters:** The automatic multi-coordinate update and easy-to-hard initialization may be sensitive to initial conditions and hyperparameter choices (e.g., top-K value, token combination method), but no stability or robustness analysis is provided (R3). The effect of top-K selection on suffix diversity and quality is not discussed (R3).
