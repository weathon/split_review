## Merged Review

### Summary

Most reviewers rated the paper 3 (reject), but one reviewer (R3) rated it 5, indicating a minority more positive view. The paper empirically investigates replacing the input layer of SimCLR’s standard 2‑layer MLP projection head with a pretrained autoencoder embedding, while also adjusting width and activation functions. Experiments on five small‑scale image datasets (e.g., STL‑10, CIFAR‑10) report up to 2.9 % (average 1.7 %) accuracy improvements and reduced projection dimensionality. Reviewers agree the method is simple and yields some gains, but most criticize the lack of theoretical explanation, limited experimental scope (small datasets, no comparison with other self‑supervised methods), and insufficient novelty, making the paper resemble a technical report.

### Strengths

- Method is simple, direct, and easy to implement (R2, R4).
- Could serve as a good reference for industry applications that avoid full framework fine‑tuning (R2).
- Improves projector by leveraging the autoencoder’s ability to capture high‑quality representations, with reported accuracy improvements (R1, R4).
- The hypothesis of using pretrained autoencoder embeddings in SimCLR’s projector is interesting (R3 – minority positive view).
- Provides empirical evaluation on several datasets (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of novelty and theoretical insight**: The paper is more an experimental report than a novel contribution; it lacks deeper theoretical explanation of why pretrained autoencoder embeddings improve performance (R1, R2, R3, R4). Similar ideas have already been applied in industry (R2). It is unclear why this approach should theoretically enhance SimCLR’s representation quality (R3). Further insight or theoretical analysis is needed (R1, R2). Adding t‑SNE visualizations (R1) could support the claims beyond classification accuracy.
- **Limited experimental scope**: Datasets are mostly small‑scale (STL‑10, CIFAR‑10, etc.) and may not support generalizability; differences may not hold on larger datasets (R2). The selection of datasets is not justified, potentially introducing bias (R3). No comparisons with a broad family of self‑supervised methods are provided (R4). The paper only uses SimCLR without investigating whether findings generalize to other projection heads or contrastive learning methods (R3). Additional evaluation metrics beyond classification accuracy (e.g., t‑SNE visualization) are needed (R1).
- **Missing ablations and investigations**: There are no consistent patterns for dimensions, activation function, or normalization; practitioners would need many trials per dataset (R4). The impact of the number of autoencoder layers is not explored (R4). The trade‑offs between freezing and fine‑tuning the autoencoder embeddings are not sufficiently studied; could scenarios exist where fine‑tuning is beneficial? (R2). 
- **Presentation and organization issues**: The background introduction (SimCLR, autoencoders) is too long and could be shortened or moved to an appendix (R3). The abstract and introduction contain unnecessary symbols (e.g., $g(\cdot)$, $f(\cdot)$) that should be deferred to the method section (R1). The logical narrative in the introduction needs reordering: first discuss existing projection head roles and limitations, then propose the autoencoder embedding (R1). Contributions should be concise (e.g., not list code proposal) (R1). The related‑work final paragraph should briefly discuss differences from previously mentioned studies (R1). The paper cites only 9 references, which is insufficient (R1).
- **Clarification needed**: The phrase “high‑level structure of the training data” in the discussion requires a more detailed description (R1).
