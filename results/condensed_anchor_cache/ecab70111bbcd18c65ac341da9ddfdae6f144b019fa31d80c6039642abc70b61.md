## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper identifies three core limitations of Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for fine-tuning LLMs: ineffectiveness of dropout for short training episodes, poor optimization landscape due to zero initialization of B, and problematic interactions from the scaling factor. The authors propose ALLoRA, which removes dropout and the scaling factor, and introduces an adaptive learning rate that scales per-sample per-parameter gradients inversely proportional to the parameter’s L2 norm. Experiments on various models (LLaMA variants, vision transformers) and tasks (commonsense reasoning, image classification) compare ALLoRA with LoRA and DoRA, showing improved accuracy. One reviewer (score 5) found the adaptive learning concept somewhat innovative and the experiments diverse, but the five remaining reviewers (score 3) judged the work as lacking theoretical rigor, missing comparisons with many SOTA LoRA variants, and showing only marginal performance gains.

### Strengths

*   Novel identification of three LoRA flaws: dropout instability under short fine-tuning, slow escape from zero initialization causing poor optimization landscape, and scaling factor inducing harmful layer interactions.
*   Simple, straightforward solution that removes two hyperparameters (dropout rate, scaling factor) and replaces them with an adaptive learning rate.
*   Empirical validation across diverse model types/sizes (vision and language) and tasks, including comparisons against DoRA and ablations of components.
*   The three identified flaws may be of independent interest to the community.

### Weaknesses

*   **Inadequate theoretical support for the three flaws.** The reasoning in Sections 3.1–3.3 is often inconsistent or incomplete. The connection between dropout and training steps (Section 3.1) is not properly justified (the expectation over V does not directly link to finite steps; infinite training steps do not guarantee the claimed result). The relationship between dropout and gradient spiking in Figure 1 is unclear (single mini-batch used, spiking could be due to OOD inputs; a dropout=0 comparison is needed). The claim that zero initialization causes a degenerate optimization landscape (Section 3.2) is not convincingly derived from the Hessian analysis; the landscape becoming degenerate as n decreases is not shown. The ripple-effect analysis (Section 3.3) lacks lower bounds and empirical validation; the derivation of C and the second inequality is confusing, and notation is inconsistent (W*, learning rate l used for both scalar and layer, f_o undefined).
*   **Missing comparisons with many relevant prior works.** The paper compares only with LoRA and DoRA. It omits standard benchmarks (GLUE) and recent LoRA variants: PiSSA, MiLoRA, LoRA-GA (non-zero initialization), RSLoRA (scaling factor), LoRA+, AdaLoRA, MoRA, Flexora, Hayou et al. 2024a/b. Given the pace of this direction, the novelty and contribution are not assessable without those comparisons.
*   **Marginal performance improvements.** The average improvement across experiments is ~0.3%, which may be within the standard deviation. No standard deviations are reported to assess statistical significance. Figures 3 and 4 do not clearly present absolute accuracy values. One reviewer noted that such marginal gains do not convincingly demonstrate superiority.
*   **Incomplete dropout analysis.** The paper does not compare LoRA without dropout against ALLoRA-0 (ALLoRA without dropout). It uses dropout rate 0.05 even though Table 2 shows 0 dropout is suboptimal under short fine-tuning, which is unexplained.
*   **Lack of computational overhead analysis.** The adaptive learning rate requires computing per-sample gradient norms; runtime comparison with standard LoRA is not provided.
*   **Heuristic and incremental nature.** Core changes (removing dropout, scaling factor, adding adaptive LR) are viewed as hyperparameter adjustments rather than a fundamental architectural innovation. Many issues can be addressed by simpler modifications (e.g., just removing dropout/scaling), and it is not shown why the specific adaptive LR is necessary or how it differs from standard adaptive gradient methods like AdaFactor.
*   **Arbitrary epoch assumption.** The paper assumes fine-tuning uses few epochs (2–10), but this is dataset-dependent (e.g., RTE requires 50 epochs). The claim that 10 epochs is a large number of finetuning iterations is not justified.
*   **Ablation study on mismatched settings.** Ablations are performed on smaller models (Qwen2-0.5B, Snowflake-Artic-L, OpenELM-450M) rather than on the same model+dataset configurations used in the main performance evaluations.
*   **Ripple effect argument may not hold under real LLM architectures.** Equation 3 assumes a multi-linear model; after normalizations and FFNs, the bound may not hold, weakening the argument for the scaling factor flaw.
*   **Limited clarity and documentation.** The purpose of Figure 2 is unclear (should directly compare accuracy). The justification for using inverse L2 norm in the adaptive LR (line 334) is missing. The paper does not discuss scenarios where adaptive LR might be disadvantageous, or sensitivity to the eta² hyperparameter. There are citation formatting errors.
