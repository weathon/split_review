## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Variance Reduced Meta-Continual Learning (VR-MCL), which for the first time formally bridges Meta-CL and regularization-based methods by characterizing Meta-CL as online Hessian approximation via hypergradients. To address the high variance from random memory buffer sampling, VR-MCL introduces a momentum-based variance reduction technique. Theoretical analysis shows equivalence to a penalty term in implicit Hessian estimation, and experiments on CIFAR-10/100, TinyImageNet under multiple settings demonstrate consistent improvements over SOTA methods. Note: Reviewer 3 gave a score of 1 but their review text is highly positive, praising the paper's novelty and clarity, with only the batch size assumption as a concern.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and Motivation**: The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and its motivation is clear. Table 1 and Figures 1–2 compactly summarize and visualize the iterative update process, aiding readability. (All reviewers)
- **Novelty and Originality**: First to formally connect Meta-CL with regularization-based methods via online Hessian approximation. The variance reduction technique for hypergradient estimation is a novel concept, and the unification is sound and opens a new research direction. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Theoretical Analysis**: Provides regret bounds and shows VR-MCL imposes a penalty term in the implicit Hessian estimation, ensuring cautious updates to prevent excessive changes from wrongly estimated low-curvature directions. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Comprehensive Empirical Validation**: Extensive experiments across three datasets, including ablation studies and analyses in both main paper and supplementary, validate the method’s effectiveness and the theoretical findings. (R1, R3)
- **Insightful Remarks**: The explanation (Section 4.2 after Proposition 3) that variance reduction mitigates partiality from insufficient memory data is refreshing and may motivate future work in CL and parameter-efficient finetuning. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing Comparisons**: The paper omits well-established baselines such as FTML [1] and LFW [2], which would provide a more thorough evaluation. (R1)
- **Limited Experimental Scope**: 
  - Lack of experiments on larger datasets (e.g., ImageNet-1K) or with varying numbers of tasks to test adaptability to longer sequences. (R1, R2)
  - The motivation for the imbalance CL setting is unclear; the connection between memory buffer imbalance and Hessian estimation variance is not explicitly explained, and no further analysis is provided why VR-MCL performs well in this challenging setting. (R2)
- **Notation Clarity**: After Eqn (4), the definition of \(G_{\theta_b}\) is not clear, which hinders direct comprehension of the variance estimation process and the meaning of \(\Delta_b\). (R2)
- **Strong Assumptions in Theoretical Analysis**: 
  - Proposition 2 assumes \(\theta_{(K)}\) lies in the \(\epsilon\)-neighborhood of the optimal model parameter without justification; the role of parameter \(\beta\) is mentioned but not used in the final conclusion. (R2)
  - Proposition 3 assumes the batch size for inner step adaptation is “sufficiently large” without quantification or guidelines for choosing it in practice. (R2, R3)
- **Memory Update Strategy**: The algorithm’s memory buffer update mechanism for balanced task storage is not described in sufficient detail. (R1)
- **Missing Complexity Analysis**: Quantitative comparison of time and memory complexity with baselines (especially LA-MAML) is not provided, which would help assess practical trade-offs. (R2)
