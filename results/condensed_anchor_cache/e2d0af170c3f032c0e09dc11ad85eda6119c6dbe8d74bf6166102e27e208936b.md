## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FedGT, a framework that uses group testing to identify malicious clients in cross-silo federated learning while supporting secure aggregation. Clients are partitioned into overlapping groups whose aggregated models are tested by the server; a decoding operation then pinpoints malicious clients for removal. The trade-off between privacy and security is controlled by group size. Experiments on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and ISIC2019 with 15–31 clients evaluate the method against label-flip and permutation attacks, comparing it to RFA (Pillutla et al.) and an oracle.

### Strengths
- **Interesting and novel idea**: Applying group testing to detect malicious clients in FL with secure aggregation is new and promising (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). The connection between coding theory and privacy is compelling (Reviewer 4).  
- **Solves an important problem**: The work addresses the critical challenge of Byzantine robustness under secure aggregation (Reviewers 2, 4).  
- **Well-structured and readable**: The paper presents a clear, coherent flow of ideas (Reviewer 2).  
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: The analysis covers multiple datasets, attack types, and group size settings; effectiveness is demonstrated even when applied at a single communication round (Reviewers 3, 4).  
- **Encouraging results in some settings**: The method can outperform RFA in targeted-attack, heterogeneous settings (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 2 found the method innovative and effective at mitigating poisoning while preserving privacy.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and technical contribution** [Raised by Reviewers 1, 4; Reviewer 2 and 3 found the method innovative]:
    - The core technical shift is from testing individuals to groups, but no novel testing strategy or coding scheme is proposed; the decoding directly applies the Neyman-Pearson detection from Liva et al. (2021) without adaptation to FL (Reviewer 4). The paper re-derives that work without giving proper credit and does not justify the chosen instantiation (e.g., why Neyman-Pearson is suited to the real problem) (Reviewer 1, 4).  
    - The construction of the assignment matrix A from error-correcting codes (e.g., BCH, Reed-Muller) is insufficiently explained: the connection between code distance and group-testing performance is missing, and the use of XOR-based codes for OR-based group testing is not justified (Reviewer 1).  
- **Unreasonable assumptions and hyperparameter burden** [All reviewers, especially Reviewer 4]:
    - **Assumptions**: The defender is assumed to know the number of malicious clients, the target class in targeted attacks, and δ (a threshold related to attack strength). These are unrealistic; experiments with unknown δ should be the default (Reviewers 2, 4).  
    - **Hyperparameters**: The method introduces Λ (an uninterpretable threshold), p (single probability of error in Q(t|s)), and ρ (heterogeneity dependence). These are hard to tune and results are sensitive to them (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 2 adds that the RFA baseline is not the best robust aggregator; better schemes should be compared.  
    - **Extra auxiliary dataset**: FedGT uses an additional "enhanced" test set; its composition and role need clarification, and this requirement limits applicability (Reviewer 2).  
- **Limited scalability and computational cost** [Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4]:
    - Experiments use only 15 or 31 clients; the scaling experiment (Appendix J) fails to scale to 31 for some encodings. The decoding complexity O(2^m) (m = number of groups) becomes a severe bottleneck for larger client counts (Reviewers 2, 4).  
    - Communication cost scales with the number of groups, which is substantial (Reviewer 3).  
    - The paper should provide runtime analysis (big-O) and move communication complexity discussion to the main paper (Reviewer 4).  
- **Privacy leakage concerns** [Reviewer 3, also raised by Reviewer 2]:
    - The server can compare aggregated models from overlapping groups (e.g., group A = {C1,C2,C3}, group B = {C2,C3}) to infer C1’s update. Secure aggregation does not prevent this differential inference (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 requests a more formal definition of privacy for the scheme.  
- **Experimental weaknesses** [Reviewers 2, 4]:
    - **Inferior performance in many settings**: FedGT’s top-1 accuracy is often lower than RFA and Oracle when attackers are few (e.g., Figure 3, MNIST, CIFAR-10). It only beats RFA when a large fraction of clients are malicious (Reviewers 2, 4).  
    - **Limited attacker counts**: For 31 clients, only 6 attackers are tested; this is insufficient to demonstrate robustness against collusion or larger attack fractions (Reviewer 2).  
    - **Clean accuracy degradation**: Tables 1–3 show FedGT hurts clean accuracy more than RFA and no defense, possibly due to high false-alarm (FA) rates (Reviewer 4).  
    - **Missing details**: FA and MD rates are not reported; experiments varying Λ to show FA/MD trade-off are missing (Reviewer 4). Figure 4 uses log scale that obfuscates results; a linear scale is requested.  
    - **Why CIFAR-10 attack success falls even without defense?** The authors should explain this anomaly (Reviewer 4).  
    - **Apples-to-apples comparison**: For RFA, it is unclear how many clients are securely aggregated; if RFA aggregates all clients, it operates in the “most private” setting, making FedGT’s comparisons even less favorable (Reviewer 4).  
- **Scope and presentation gaps** [Reviewers 1, 4]:
    - The paper should state explicitly in the main text that FedGT works only for data poisoning, not model poisoning (Appendix L point) (Reviewer 4).  
    - The definition of a “positive” test is missing; the notation s = d ∨ A^T needs explanation (Reviewer 4).  
    - Why does FedGT outperform the oracle in Figure 1(c)? (Reviewer 1).  
    - The choice of Neyman-Pearson detection and the simplistic Q(t|s) model should be justified: e.g., using a distribution on Hamming distance between t and s would be more realistic (Reviewer 4).  
    - If Section 5 (decoding) is kept, a justification for the forward/backward formulas should be added (Reviewer 4).
