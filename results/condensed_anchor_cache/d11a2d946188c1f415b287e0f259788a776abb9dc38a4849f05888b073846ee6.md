## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Evol-Instruct, a pipeline that uses LLMs (specifically ChatGPT) to automatically rewrite a seed set of instructions into more complex and diverse variants. These generated instructions are used to fine-tune LLaMA, resulting in WizardLM. Both automatic and human evaluations show that WizardLM outperforms baseline models Alpaca and Vicuna on several benchmarks. All four reviewers gave a score of 6, though one reviewer initially gave 5 and raised it to 6 after rebuttal.

### Strengths
- Identifies an important problem: instruction tuning data lacks diversity and difficulty. (R2)
- The proposed Evol-Instruct method is straightforward, simple, and offers a fresh perspective on generating complex instructions without heavy human involvement. (R1, R3, R4)
- The paper is overall well-written and easy to follow. (R1)
- Comprehensive evaluation with both automatic and human evaluations, increasing reliability of the claimed effectiveness. (R1, R3, R4)
- Broad applicability demonstrated: WizardLM shows strong performance across code, math, and general conversation benchmarks. (R3)
- Explicitly considers instruction complexity as a factor in improving LLM performance. (R3)
- Reported experimental results are promising and support the claim that the new dataset is better than Alpaca and Vicuna. (R1, R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Evaluation of dataset difficulty and diversity is unconvincing.** The difficulty and diversity scores are obtained from ChatGPT, the same LLM that generated the instructions, creating a circular validation. No evidence is provided that ChatGPT is a reliable evaluator for these metrics. Averaging difficulty and diversity scores further obscures their individual contributions. The WizardEval benchmark is too small and lacks sufficient detail on its construction, making its soundness as an evaluation tool questionable. (R2, R4)  
  A suggested alternative: divide the training data into subsets of increasing difficulty/diversity as judged by ChatGPT, then test if those subsets indeed lead to better performance, rather than grouping by evolution iteration (which confounds iteration count). (R2)

- **Missing comparisons against relevant baselines and datasets.** The paper does not compare against diverse instruction-tuning datasets such as Natural Instructions, SuperNatural Instructions, or the FLAN-T5 training data. Recent LLaMA-based instruction-tuned models (Tulu, Orca) are also omitted, as are other instruction data generation methods like Baize and CAMEL. The seed instructions likely differ across methods, which could account for performance differences, but no ablation study controls for this. (R1, R2, R4)

- **Limited generalizability across models.** Experiments are conducted only on LLaMA. It is unclear whether the proposed method would be effective for other architectures (e.g., T5, Falcon, Mistral) or whether using LLMs other than ChatGPT for data generation (e.g., LLaMA itself) would work. (R2, R4; also R1 suggests testing with other LLMs)

- **Technical contribution is incremental over Self-Instruct.** The main change is adding a command for the LLM to generate more complex instructions; the method’s success heavily depends on the capabilities of powerful black-box LLMs like ChatGPT. One reviewer considered the technical contribution insufficient for a top-tier venue. (R1; R4 also questions transferability when optimizing prompts for a single LLM)

- **Cost/token consumption for data collection not controlled or reported.** The comparison with Alpaca should account for the computational cost of generating the evolved instructions. (R1)

- **Potential overfitting and lack of optimality in evolution strategy.** Iteratively evolving instructions several times risks overfitting the model to specific types of instructions. The random selection between In-depth and In-breadth evolving may not always yield the optimal instruction complexity. (R3)

- **Paper organization and clarity issues.** The “3. Approach” section is poorly structured: “Prompts of In-Depth Evolving” and “Prompts of In-Breadth Evolving” are listed in parallel rather than under “Instruction Evolver”, and “Response Generation” has limited content. The name “Elimination evolving” is vague and ungrammatical. Several citations do not support the claims they accompany (e.g., regarding human annotator fatigue). (R2, R3)

- **Ensuring correctness and purpose of evolved instructions.** It is not shown how the answers to newly created prompts are verified. There is no discussion of measures to prevent bias introduced during evolution or to ensure evolved instructions do not deviate from the original goal. (R2, R3)

- **Minor issues.** The ChatGPT prompts contain grammatical problems (e.g., “a more complex version which … a bit harder to handle” should refer to the prompt, not the AI systems; “must be understood and responded by humans” is imprecise). (R2)  
  Questions about human evaluation inter-labeler agreement and whether using the full 250K instructions improves automatic scores remain unanswered. (R4)  
  The relationship between learning with Evol-Instruct and in-context learning with demonstrations, as well as scalability to larger models/datasets, is not discussed. (R3)
