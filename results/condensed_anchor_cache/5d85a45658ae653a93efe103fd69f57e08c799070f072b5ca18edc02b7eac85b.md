## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes min-p sampling, a dynamic truncation method that adjusts the sampling threshold based on model confidence by scaling with the top token probability. The method is validated on GPQA, GSM8K, and AlpacaEval Creative Writing, along with human evaluations and community adoption. Reviewers generally find the work significant and useful, though concerns are raised about limited model diversity tested, reliance on threshold tuning, missing creativity metrics, and performance at low temperatures.

### Strengths
- Novel and original twist on popular nucleus sampling (top-p); simple and appealing concept.
- Good empirical results across benchmarks (GPQA, GSM8K, AlpacaEval Creative Writing) and strong community adoption (54,000+ GitHub repositories; integrated into multiple open-source LLM implementations).
- High reproducibility: code, implementation guidelines, and repository released.
- Wide applicability: easy to integrate with existing open-source LLMs.
- Ablation shows min-p is barely impacted by output length (interesting finding from Table 6).
- Well-written and easy to follow; methodology clearly presented.
- Effective balance between coherence and diversity in text generation, especially at high temperatures.
- Includes theoretical reasoning, LLM-based logit analysis, human evaluation, and examples of distribution transformations.
- One reviewer (R3) highlights that the paper makes a compelling case for its importance despite its simplicity.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited model families tested**: Experiments are limited to Mistral models (including 123B). Results on other families (e.g., LLaMA3) are needed to demonstrate generality (R1, R2). R2 noted that showing results on both Llama and Mistral would strengthen the paper.
- **Dependence on base probability thresholds**: Table 6 shows performance varies significantly with threshold choice, meaning careful tuning is required—this limits ease of use (R1).
- **Missing creativity metrics**: The paper claims to balance creativity and coherence but only measures diversity; creativity is not directly assessed. R1 suggests adding an LLM-as-judge evaluation for creativity.
- **Missing details on human evaluation**: Inter-annotator agreement, number of participants (only “70 initial responses” mentioned), and survey template are not provided (R1).
- **Paper exceeds 10-page limit**: This could cause desk rejection; the paper could be more concise (R1, R2). R2 suggests Section 4 could be removed without loss.
- **No discussion of real-world applications**: The paper claims practical advantages but does not elaborate on specific scenarios where min-p is preferable (R1).
- **Potential difficulty for controlled generation**: R1 questions whether min-p makes lexically constrained generation harder.
- **Figure 1 caption mismatch**: (b) seems to refer to top-k and (c) to top-p, contrary to the caption (R2).
- **Performance at lower temperatures**: At temperature 0.7, min-p does not show significant advantage over top-p (Table 2). R4 asks about further decreases (e.g., 0.5, 0.3): would top-p continue to improve, and would min-p still maintain an advantage? R4 notes that all methods perform better at low temperature; min-p’s advantage is mainly at high temperatures.
- **Missing comparison against combined top-p + top-k**: R4 asks whether combining top-p and top-k could compensate for each other’s shortcomings and if min-p is more effective than such a combined method.
- **Need for evidence on benefits of higher temperature**: R3 asks whether the benefit of using higher temperature (enabled by min-p) goes beyond diversity, and requests citations or evidence for those benefits.
- **Simplicity of contribution**: While not universally seen as a weakness, R3 notes the method has no theorems and the code is less than a page; one reviewer (R4, score 6) found the contribution limited relative to other work, though others (R2, R3) valued its practical impact.
