## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a lightweight, architecture-agnostic regularizer for dynamic NeRFs. It is based on the assumption that from a fixed camera view the pixel intensity distribution remains approximately constant over short time intervals. The regularizer uses optimal transport (OT) with a sliced-Wasserstein approximation to penalize distribution mismatch between time steps, making it efficient and easy to integrate. Experiments on three dynamic scene datasets and five state-of-the-art dynamic NeRF models show consistent improvements in rendering quality.

### Strengths
- **Well-motivated hypothesis** – The assumption that pixel intensity distributions stay consistent within short time intervals is valid for typical dynamic scenes and datasets (Reviewer 1).
- **Interesting problem formulation** – First work to formulate dynamic NeRF regularization as an OT problem; the sliced-Wasserstein approximation reduces complexity from O(n³) to O(n log n) (Reviewer 1).
- **Solid experimental validation** – Evaluated on three dynamic NeRF datasets (iPhone, HyperNeRF, etc.) and across five models (e.g., T-NeRF, Nerfies, HyperNeRF, K-Planes, HexPlane), showing clear improvements (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- **Simple and architecture-agnostic** – Easy to implement (few lines of code), can be plugged into various dynamic NeRF methods (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4). Algorithm 1 is neatly presented (Reviewer 4).
- **Clear illustration** – Figure 1 effectively shows the difference between the proposed OT-based metric and simple L2 distance (Reviewer 3).
- **Technically sound** – Built on optimal transport theory; the unbiased estimator is a distinct advantage over regularizations like depth or optical flow (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Failure cases and temporal sensitivity** – The hypothesis breaks for high-speed objects or abrupt scene dynamics. The paper lacks a quantitative analysis of the temporal window within which the distribution consistency holds. This makes the method ad‑hoc for smooth motions only (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). A way to automatically determine applicability before training/evaluation is needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing comparisons with existing regularization techniques** – The paper does not directly compare against established dynamic NeRF regularizations such as random background compositing, metric depth supervision from iPhone sensors, or surface sparsity regularizers (used in Nerfies/HyperNeRF). Quantitative results (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) against these methods are absent (Reviewer 2).
- **Omitted baseline in experiments** – HexPlane (a strong baseline) is included in Table 1 but omitted from Table 2 and Figure 3. The authors must explain why it was excluded and whether this affects the comparison (Reviewer 1).
- **Inconsistency of HexPlane with regularization** – Adding the regularizer to HexPlane improves the Block scene but degrades performance on others (Paper windmill, Teddy, Wheel in PSNR). This suggests sensitivity to scene characteristics that needs investigation (Reviewer 4).
- **Efficiency claims unsubstantiated** – Training time, inference time, and memory usage are not provided. The paper claims efficiency based on complexity but lacks a computational cost breakdown (time for sliced‑Wasserstein calculation, backpropagation, etc.) (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- **Theoretical component feels disconnected** – Section 3.1 and Theorem 1 appear unnecessarily complicated and the practical relevance is unclear (Reviewer 4). Moreover, the OT components (sliced‑Wasserstein approximator) may not be novel; clarification is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited to 2D distribution, does not directly regularize 3D deformation** – The regularizer works on rendered images; improvements may come mainly from static background regions rather than dynamic content (Reviewer 3). Visual results (side‑by‑side videos) are needed to demonstrate effects on dynamic parts (Reviewer 3).
- **Diminishing returns with strong baselines** – The improvement becomes small when the baseline model already performs well (Reviewer 3).
- **Combination with other regularizations unexplored** – It is unknown whether the proposed regularizer is complementary to or interferes with existing regularizations. An ablation study combining them is necessary (Reviewer 2).
- **Baseline setting unclear** – The experimental protocol (whether all original losses are kept or only the photometric loss) is not specified for the baseline methods (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor issues** – Section 2 heading “Related works” should be “Related work”; Section 3.1 “from an images” should be “from images”; Table 5 caption exceeds column boundary (Reviewer 4).
