## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a first method to condition spatio-temporal transformer-based video diffusion models on camera poses, previously only explored for U-Net-based models. It uses a ControlNet-like block that conditions the model on Plücker coordinate embeddings, and evaluates after fine-tuning on RealEstate10K. The approach demonstrates state-of-the-art results for controllable video generation with global spatiotemporal transformers.

### Strengths
- **First exploration of camera control for spatiotemporal transformer video diffusion models** (where tokens are processed globally), as opposed to U‑Net-based models that decouple spatial and temporal generation. This shift in foundation model architecture is a critical step and provides a solid engineering reference for future work. (Reviewers 1, 4, 5)
- **Reasonable and effective design**: Plücker embeddings are used for pixel-level ray conditioning and are introduced via read cross-attention layers, matching the shape of video tokens. While this is a combination of existing techniques, it is validated as effective for transformers and provides valuable experimental insights. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Comprehensive evaluations and ablation studies**, conducted both qualitatively and quantitatively with well-defined criteria for video content quality and camera control. Baseline comparisons are fair, and specific architectural choices are shown to be necessary (e.g., Table 2). (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Excellent visual quality and motion dynamics** in generated videos, as also shown in supplemental video samples. (Reviewers 3, 5)
- **Methodology is clearly explained and easy to follow**. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4, 5)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty.** The core components (ControlNet-like module for diffusion transformers, Plücker coordinates for camera control) have been explored in prior works (CameraCtrl, Ref [1]; MotionCtrl; He et al., 2024a). The paper primarily applies existing designs to spatiotemporal transformers rather than introducing fundamentally new techniques. One reviewer notes that the contribution is more an “engineering reference” with state‑of‑the‑art results, while another reviewer (score 3) considers the technical contribution very limited. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5)
- **Unclear “projection mechanism” novelty.** The paper claims a novel projection mechanism, but it is unclear if this is simply a single linear layer, which is not a significant contribution. The extra complexity arises from the spatiotemporal nature of the transformer, but the method simply concatenates camera information along the extra dimension, a standard approach. (Reviewer 2)
- **Evaluation on a single, non‑standard transformer architecture (SnapVideo).** SnapVideo is not a typical DiT (it uses a “read” operation and attention is not on the actual tokens). Generalization to standard video DiTs is unclear. Moreover, one reviewer notes that a vanilla DiT version performs better, questioning the purpose of building on SnapVideo. (Reviewers 2, 3, 5)
- **Overfitting to training trajectories.** Quantitative comparison is given in Table 8, but no visual comparisons of generated vs. held‑out trajectories are provided. (Reviewer 2)
- **Limited trajectory diversity.** Camera trajectories come mainly from RealEstate10K and are mostly smooth, straight lines. Experiments with varying difficulty (e.g., large directional changes) are lacking. Another reviewer also questions the distribution of camera movements in terms of diversity and similarity to natural camera motion. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Insufficient baseline implementation details.** It is not clarified whether MotionCtrl and CameraCtrl baselines are implemented using a vanilla DiT for fair comparison. For CameraCtrl, it is unclear if the authors fine‑tune a camera encoder trained from AnimateDiff or implement a SnapVideo version using zero‑convolution plus a copy of SnapVideo layers, and whether all CameraCtrl variants are implemented identically. (Reviewer 2)  
  Another reviewer asks for clarification: for MotionCtrl, is one additional learned token concatenated to the video patch tokens; for CameraCtrl, does the camera encoder produce additional latent tokens? (Reviewer 5)
- **MotionCtrl baseline quality degradation.** When the base model is fine‑tuned, visual quality degrades. Would freezing the backbone yield better results? (Reviewer 5)
- **Missing trainable parameter scale** for the two baselines and the proposed method. (Reviewer 5)
- **Particle‑SfM reliability not discussed** for evaluating videos without translation. (Reviewer 2)
- **Figure 3 is hard to interpret.** It should clearly delineate video patch tokens vs. Plücker patch tokens, add variables from the formulas, and separate blocks more cleanly. (Reviewer 4)
- **Limited example variation.** Only three examples show the same scene with different camera control; most examples use input trajectories from unrelated scenes. Showing related scenes would help judge the reasonableness of the generated camera path. (Reviewer 4)
- **Notation issue** (Line 272): R₁, t₁ should be written as R′₁, t′₁ to indicate normalization. (Reviewer 5)
- **Minor:** CameraCtrl (arXiv April 2024) should not be considered a concurrent work. (Reviewer 3)
