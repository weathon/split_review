## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper identifies a base-session performance bias in few-shot class-incremental learning (FSCIL) and proposes a pipeline combining a frozen pre-trained model, a base model, and multiple classifier heads with prediction aggregation. It also introduces new evaluation metrics and two benchmark datasets (Flower102, DCC) to better measure incremental performance. Reviewers find the problem motivation and proposed metrics useful, but criticize insufficient novelty, missing analysis, unfair comparisons, and dataset suitability. All three reviewers gave a score of 5; confidence varies (3, 2, 5), with the most confident reviewer particularly concerned about novelty and experimental fairness.

### Strengths
- Reveals the fact that current FSCIL approaches fail to generalize well on novel classes (R2).
- Proposes a novel pipeline, evaluation metrics, and datasets for FSCIL (R2).
- The dominance of base class accuracy in previous metrics is indeed unfair; the proposed session-level metrics provide a more intuitive benchmark (R3).
- A baseline can be significantly improved by simply utilizing and aggregating a frozen pretrained model (R3).
- Combining various types of classifier heads is beneficial (R3).
- The method is simple and experiments are sufficient (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and motivation**: The overall method lacks novelty – using a frozen pre-trained backbone to mitigate forgetting is not new (e.g., cited [1]), and aggregating predictions from multiple classifiers is a standard ensemble operation (R3). The rationale for choosing ALICE as the baseline model over other options is not justified (R3). Missing prior work: Liu et al. "Few-Shot Class-Incremental Learning via Entropy-Regularized Data-Free Replay" (ECCV 2022) (R3). The paper lacks a clear motivation and detailed analysis of why the proposed pipeline actually reduces bias (R2). The conclusion mentions evaluating robustness to target distribution shift, but this effort is not present in the work (R2).
- **Technical inconsistencies and missing details**: In incremental sessions, it is unclear whether a test sample produces two results (from NCM classifier and Lightweight Network) and how these are reconciled (R1). In Section 3.1, N is used to represent both the number of incremental sessions and the number of classes per session, causing confusion (R2). Section 3.4 lacks details on how predictions are aggregated – if the model already obtains classification results for base and novel classes, why further aggregate them to get the final prediction? (R2). Regarding the Lightweight Network, if it overcomes catastrophic forgetting by replaying samples from previous classes, this violates the FSCIL setting that prohibits accessing data from multiple incremental sessions (R3). If a pre-trained model has already learned novel classes or similar classes, does this violate the incremental learning assumption of learning novel classes from scratch? (R2).
- **Experimental comparison concerns**: In Table 3, the reported a_0 (base session) accuracies for compared methods appear wrong – e.g., SAVC lists 81.85, FACT 75.90, ALICE 77.40 (R2). In the CUB200 benchmark, previous methods (Table 3) show significantly lower performance even on base classes than in their original papers, raising fairness concerns (R3). Comparisons may be unfair because all prior methods use backbones like ResNet-12, 18, or 20, while the proposed pipeline uses larger backbones (ResNet-50 or ViT) – the observed improvement may be due to more parameters rather than the method itself (R2,R3). An essential baseline is missing: fine-tuning the pre-trained model on base classes and using it as a new “frozen pre-trained model” would test whether the improvement comes from the pipeline or simply from better base-class representation (R3). The paper does not clarify whether comparisons are made without using a pre-trained model (R1). Concern about the accuracy of each session and session-wise comparisons (R1).
- **Proposed datasets and metrics issues**: The two new benchmark datasets (Flower102 and DCC) may not be suitable for FSCIL: they have very small base sessions (20 images/class, 1200 total for Flower102; 80 images/class, 5360 total for DCC), which challenges algorithm development as models grow larger and leads to significant evaluation variation due to limited test images (R3). There is no explanation on the choice of these two datasets or comparisons with previous benchmarks (R2). Note: “Flowers102” should be “Flowers100” because only the first 100 classes are used (R3). In Section 4.2, “IMB” is undefined – what does it mean? And why does the performance margin between base and incremental sessions measure bias? Session average is already a common metric and cannot be claimed as a contribution (R2). Whether the relevant datasets can be made public is not addressed (R1).
- **Missing experiments and ablation**: Questions about Table 8 – are experiments conducted excluding the baseline model as in Fig. 2? (R3). The paper does not directly compare results with and without a pre-trained model (R1). No ablation on the necessity of the Lightweight Network or the prediction aggregation step (implied by missing details).
