## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces FB-CPR, a regularizer for unsupervised zero-shot RL that adds a GAN-style discriminator to the forward-backward (FB) framework. The discriminator encourages the learned policy to stay close to the distribution of a state-only motion-capture dataset, conditioned on a latent skill embedding \(z\). The method is evaluated on simulated humanoid control for tasks such as motion tracking, goal reaching, and reward optimization. FB-CPR is shown to produce human-like behaviors and achieve competitive or better performance than task-specific methods and unsupervised RL baselines. All reviewers note the method is tested only on a single humanoid environment, highlighting a lack of generality.

### Strengths

- **Clear presentation and problem motivation.** The paper is well-written (R1, R2, R3) and motivates the zero-shot whole-body control setting effectively (R3, R4).  
- **Sound, clean method.** The addition of a discriminator to the FB objective is simple and reasonable (R1, R2, R3). The latent \(z\) conditioning allows steering toward useful behaviors (R3).  
- **Scales unsupervised RL to humanoids.** This is one of the few works successfully applying unsupervised RL at the complexity of humanoid control (R2).  
- **Extensive experiments and ablations.** Detailed appendix, thorough evaluation across tracking, reaching, reward optimization, and ablations (e.g., Fig. 4, Appendix) (R1, R2, R4).  
- **Challenging problem.** Addresses generalization to unseen humanoid whole-body tasks without task-specific training (R4).  
- **Competitive performance and naturalness.** Outperforms baselines and produces more human-like behaviors (implied by R1, R2, R3, R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty.** The core contribution is a discriminator-based behavioral regularizer added to FB. Data-regularization is standard in offline RL and motion priors (e.g., BC). Conditioning the discriminator on inferred \(z\) is similar to existing skill-based unsupervised RL methods (HILP (Park et al., 2024), FRE (Frans et al., 2024)) (R2). One reviewer (R3, score 8) was notably more positive, calling it a simple and effective addition, while others (R1, R2, R4, score 6) found the novelty limited.  
- **Evaluated only on humanoid environment.** The paper lacks experiments on other benchmark tasks (e.g., D4RL, manipulation, navigation), making generality unclear (R1, R2, R3, R4). Specifically, R2 and R3 ask about D4RL Kitchen, manipulation, or navigation environments.  
- **Questionable assumption about latent \(z\).** The policy dependence is reparameterized via continuous \(z\) (Eqn 4), but it is unclear whether a low-dimensional \(z\) is expressive enough to capture combinatorial policy complexity. Ablations on \(z\) dimension are needed (R1).  
- **FB objective appears marginal.** In ablation studies (Fig. 4, top right), the FB component contributes little to performance; the discriminator regularization drives most gains. Given the complexity of FB, this raises doubts about whether the FB component is worthwhile (R2).  
- **Missing comparisons to relevant methods.** No comparison to METRA (Seohong Park et al., 2024), which explicitly trains diverse behaviors (R3). No comparison to AMP (Peng et al., 2021), ASE (Peng et al., 2022), Omnigrasp (Luo et al., 2024), or MaskedMimic (Tessler et al., 2024), which are highly relevant to humanoid control with motion priors (R4). Direct comparison to FB without CPR is partly in appendix but should appear in main experiments (R3).  
- **Theoretical background not self-contained.** The FB preliminaries require many stops to re-derive intuition; more high-level explanation of the math transformations is needed (R1, R4).  
- **Data quality and robustness issues.** If the demonstration data is suboptimal or noisy, the regularization might bias the policy toward poor behaviors (R3). Performance on uncurated video footage or noisy datasets is not evaluated (R4).  
- **Real-world deployment not discussed.** No analysis of sim-to-real gap, sensor noise, or whether the method scales to real robots; the onlineness assumption (direct environment interaction) may hinder real-world use (R4).  
- **Presentation improvements needed.** Figure 2 should link \(F(\cdot,z)^\top z\) back to \(Q^\pi\) and add a post-training box. Algorithm 1 should be highlighted in the main paper (R1). TD3 may not be a strong baseline for naturalness—sequence-based methods (e.g., Diffuser) are more relevant (R1).  
- **Problem definition clarity.** The setup is not clearly explained at the start; more explicit examples or pictures would help (R4).
