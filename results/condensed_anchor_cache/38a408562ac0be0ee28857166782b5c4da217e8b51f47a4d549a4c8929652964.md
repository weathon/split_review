## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes the principle of *interaction asymmetry*: parts of the same concept have more complex interactions than parts of different concepts. It formalizes this via block diagonality conditions on higher-order derivatives of the generator mapping concepts to observed data. The authors prove that this principle enables both disentanglement and compositional generalization, unifying prior object-centric results (recovered as special cases with n=0 or 1) and extending to more flexible generators (up to n=2). For practical implementation, they propose a Transformer-based VAE with a novel regularizer on decoder cross-attention weights to minimize cross-slot interactions, combined with capacity control via the KL term. Experiments on synthetic datasets (Sprites, CLEVR6) demonstrate comparable object disentanglement to existing methods that use explicit object-centric priors.

### Strengths
- **Novel principle**: Interaction asymmetry is original and creative, formalized via block diagonality of higher-order derivatives, providing a unified theoretical framework that recovers prior object-centric results as special cases and extends to more complex interaction orders (n=2 and beyond).  
- **Solid theory**: The proofs are rigorous and sound, linking interaction asymmetry to both disentanglement and compositional generalization. Equation 4.2 nicely expresses how a function can be decomposed into disentangled and entangled portions.  
- **Practical instantiation**: The attention regularizer on Transformer decoder cross-attention is a creative, scalable implementation of the theoretical criteria; combined with VAE capacity control, it achieves strong results without explicit object-centric architectural biases.  
- **Clear exposition**: The paper is well-written and accessible, with clear motivation, intuitive explanations, and effective visual aids (figures, diagrams). Theoretical constructs are defined clearly.  
- **Empirical evidence**: Evaluated on multiple synthetic datasets (Sprites, CLEVR6) with comparisons to slot attention and spatial broadcast decoders, showing comparable disentanglement performance.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited empirical scope**: Experiments are restricted to synthetic datasets (Sprites, CLEVR6). The model’s generalizability to real-world datasets with complex object interactions, backgrounds, noise, and occlusion is untested. Broader concept types (e.g., attributes, events) are not explored. *(Reviewers 1,2,3)*  
- **Restrictive theoretical assumptions**: The results rely on several assumptions (e.g., aligned-connectedness on latent support, sufficient independence, same interaction order for all slots). Their practical implications are not fully explored, and possible relaxations are not discussed. The aligned-connectedness condition on \(Z_{supp}\) seems particularly restrictive. *(Reviewers 1,2)*  
- **Practical limitations of the regularizer**:  
  - The attention regularizer only approximately enforces minimal interactions; the gap between this approximation and the theoretical ideal is not analyzed. *(Reviewer 1)*  
  - Since ground-truth interaction order \(n\) is unknown, the regularizer weight \(\alpha\) must be tuned per dataset (e.g., Sprite likely 0th order vs. CLEVR6 higher order); the paper does not provide guidance on this tuning. *(Reviewer 3)*  
  - It is unclear whether this regularizer provides any advantage over the spatial exclusiveness inductive bias already inherent in slot attention encoders. *(Reviewer 3)*  
  - The claim of greater flexibility over slot attention methods is not fully convincing: (i) the method requires both a KL term and the regularizer term, adding hyperparameter burden; (ii) the decoder lacks self-attention, using only cross-attention to avoid slot interactions, which may limit scalability compared to slot attention architectures that can leverage off-the-shelf components. *(Reviewer 3)*  
- **Unclear definition and evaluation of compositional generalization**:  
  - The connection between the “sufficiently predictable” property (partial derivatives being fixed-degree polynomials) and the ability to generalize compositionally is not clearly established; a more rigorous argument is needed. *(Reviewer 4)*  
  - Compositional generalization itself is not empirically validated; only disentanglement is measured. *(Reviewer 3)*  
- **Relationship to prior work on disentanglement and compositional generalization**: The paper does not adequately engage with the existing debate—Montero et al. (2020,2022) claim no relationship, while Higgins et al. (2016), Esmaili et al. (2019), and Mahon (2024) claim correlations. The position of Montero et al. seems incongruent with the authors argument that both share an underlying mechanism. *(Reviewer 4)*  
- **Terminology and metric issues**:  
  - Terminology confusion: “latent” sometimes denotes dimension, sometimes vector; “block” vs. “slot” is unclear. *(Reviewer 4)*  
  - ARI metric description (lines 478–479) is not the standard clustering ARI (Vinh et al. 2009); the exact measure should be stated. *(Reviewer 4)*  
  - The proposed J-ARI metric may be misleading: the Jacobian can activate due to object reflections (e.g., metallic sphere reflecting blue cuboid triggers the cuboid’s slot), but that does not mean the slot encodes the reflecting object. A downstream task like property prediction is needed to assess whether composable abstractions are captured. *(Reviewer 3)*  
- **Ablation and hyperparameter reporting**:  
  - Ablation results (Table 1) show that \(\beta>0\) has a larger effect than \(\alpha>0\); it is unclear whether a correctly tuned \(\beta\) alone could make \(\alpha\) irrelevant. Exact values of \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are only reported as “\(>0\)” in the main paper—precise values and hyperparameter selection details should be provided. *(Reviewer 4)*  
  - Important details (e.g., number of dimensions per slot) are relegated to Appendix I and should be in the main paper. *(Reviewer 4)*  
- **Binary vs. continuous formulation**: The interaction principle is defined as binary (derivatives zero everywhere or not), but disentanglement is normally quantified as a degree [0,1]; it is unclear how to extend this framework to produce a continuous score. *(Reviewer 4)*  
- **Missing connection to Information Bottleneck**: The paper does not discuss how interaction asymmetry relates to the Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby et al. 2000, Alemi et al. 2017, Meo et al. 2024), which is relevant to disentanglement via mutual information minimization. *(Reviewer 2)*  
- **Design choice questions**: The authors use learned queries instead of randomly sampled queries as in conventional Slot Attention; this choice is not justified. *(Reviewer 3)*
