## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PDEDER (Pre-trained Dynamic EncoDER), a framework that learns a generalized dynamics encoder by pre-training on observations from multiple dynamic systems. It uses a pre-trained language model (T5) adapted through tokenization and fine-tuning to embed states into a latent space where dynamics can be more easily captured. PDEDER is pre-trained on 153 sets of observations from 24 complex systems (122 synthetic from 14 systems, 31 real-world from 10 systems) and evaluated on 18 dynamic systems for long/short-term forecasting in both in-domain and cross-domain settings. The model includes data projection, patch-based tokenization, a PLM encoder-decoder, and a Graph Neural Network (single-layer normalized Laplacian) with neural ODE integration for dynamics modeling.

### Strengths
- The proposed generalized pre-trained dynamics encoder is well-motivated and technically sound (Reviewer 1). However, some reviewers question the underlying assumptions about sharing dynamics across very different systems (Reviewer 2, see Weaknesses).
- Single model (modulo a projection layer) that aims to reconstruct and forecast dynamical systems is very hard (Reviewer 3).
- Novel approach using pre-trained models for dynamics modeling generalization (Reviewer 4).
- Comprehensive dataset collection across multiple domains: 153 observations from 24 systems with varying size (5–1024 nodes), timestamps (100–28,000 steps), dimensionality (1–10), and hyperparameters (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 4).
- Clear empirical validation through both in-domain and cross-domain experiments (Reviewer 1; Reviewer 4).
- Strong cross-domain performance after fine-tuning, even when excluding entire systems during pre-training (Reviewer 4).
- Paper is well-written overall (Reviewer 2), though some reviewers note writing issues (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2 minor comments).
- Tackles a significant challenge in modeling time series from multiple systems and emphasizes generalizability (Reviewer 2).
- Leverages recent advances in language models (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
**Assumptions and Generalizability**  
- Reviewer 2 is critically concerned about the core assumption that different systems (from different worlds, time scales, dynamical regimes) can be trained together. The paper does not quantify how much systems can differ or provide a clear list of statistical assumptions (e.g., noise type, distributional properties). For example, how does the method perform when some data is Poisson-distributed and others Gaussian? This needs mathematical discussion and explicit assumptions.  
- The trade-off between generalizability, expressivity, and interpretability is not thoroughly addressed. The paper should clarify how the model balances learning shared dynamics with preserving system-specific characteristics (Reviewer 2).  
- Reviewer 1 considers the approach well-motivated; the disagreement highlights that the authors must justify the feasibility of cross-system transfer more rigorously.

**Interpretability**  
- Interpretability is barely discussed. Using pre-trained language models and fine-tuning across observations yields a model that lacks interpretability. The SINDy analysis only uses error quantification, not interpretation of discovered components (e.g., decomposing dynamics into basic functions). Reviewer 2 expects analysis of SINDy weights for scientific insight.  
- Graph construction for real-world data is not explained. Is the graph known or computed during pre-processing? This need should be explicitly stated, as many real-world dynamical datasets lack pre-defined graphs (Reviewer 2).

**Experimental Design and Baselines**  
- All baselines (NDCN, ST-GODE, MT-GODE, TANGO) are neuralODE-based. Missing comparisons with discrete neural simulators (Reviewer 1) and with known methods that learn a generalized neural simulator trained from multiple systems (Reviewer 1).  
- Missing standard time-series forecasting models (e.g., SOTA from M-competitions like Smyl et al.) as baselines (Reviewer 3).  
- Evaluation metrics (MSE, MAE) may not capture dynamics-specific behaviors. Reviewer 3 requests mean relative absolute error and a simple baseline (e.g., last value plus numerical derivative).  
- Ablation studies do not fully isolate the contribution of each component (Reviewer 4).  
- Comparison with TANGO is limited to small-scale systems due to memory constraints; memory complexity analysis and scaling solutions are absent (Reviewer 4).  
- The selection of 24 systems lacks justification for representativeness (Reviewer 4).  
- The difference between short-term and long-term forecasting in results is not defined (Reviewer 4).  
- Experimental setting is obscure; visualizations of time-series forecasts vs. ground truth (e.g., showing forecasting horizon and different dynamical regimes) are missing, making it hard to assess validity (Reviewer 3).  
- Generalizability across novel initial conditions is not tested; the model may be tied to specific initial values used in pre-training (Reviewer 3).

**Technical Clarifications and Ablations**  
- How is the data projection module's dimension chosen? The current choice reduces to dimension 1, which may limit expressiveness for complex systems (Reviewer 4).  
- Impact of different PLM architectures (e.g., why T5 specifically?) is not explored (Reviewer 4).  
- Sensitivity to tokenization parameters (patch length 30, stride 6) is not analyzed (Reviewer 4).  
- How Eq. 3 is trained in practice (EM or global optimizer?) is unclear; an algorithm should be included (Reviewer 2).  
- Robustness to hyperparameter choices is not discussed (Reviewer 2).  
- Many results are relegated to the supplementary; the main text has unused space and could include additional results (e.g., from Appendix B) (Reviewer 2).  
- Reviewer 1 asks about the impact of changing the dynamic modeling module during fine-tuning (e.g., discrete GNNs, one-step vs. multi-step losses) and about runtime comparisons.  
- Reviewer 2 asks how many observations are needed for success and how similarity between pre-training and fine-tuning datasets affects performance.

**Connection and Novelty**  
- The connection between the pre-training objectives (reconstruction and forecasting) and learning dynamics-enriched embeddings is not formally established (Reviewer 4).  
- The core idea of using pre-trained models for time series/dynamics has been explored in recent works (Gruver et al. 2024, PromptCast, AutoTimes); the conceptual novelty is incremental (Reviewer 4).  
- Reviewer 3 questions why a language model like T5 should be used for dynamical systems and asks for intuition about which biases from language modeling transfer well to dynamics.

**Writing and Presentation**  
- Writing needs improvement: citation style (should use \citep), justification whether graph structure is fixed or evolving (Reviewer 1).  
- Minor issues: capitalization inconsistency in abstract, grammar ("more easily captured" instead of "easier captured"), related work paragraph reads like a list, missing period after ODE subtitle, capitalization errors, table formatting with mixed percentage signs (Reviewer 2).  
- Memory requirements should be discussed earlier (Reviewer 4).
