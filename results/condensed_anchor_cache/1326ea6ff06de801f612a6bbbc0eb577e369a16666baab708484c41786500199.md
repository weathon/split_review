## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies the maximum coverage problem in the turnstile streaming model, where an \(n \times d\) binary matrix \(A\) (indicating set membership) receives entry-wise updates \((i,j,\pm 1)\). The goal is to output \(k\) subsets with maximum union coverage. The authors design the first linear sketch for this problem with space \(\tilde{O}(d/\varepsilon^3)\) (abstract states \(\tilde{O}(d/\varepsilon^2)\); see Weaknesses). They then apply the sketch to targeted fingerprinting for risk measurement (finding \(k\) features that minimize users sharing identical values) and to general fingerprinting via a new sketch for the complement of \(F_p\) (\(p\ge 2\)). Empirical results show up to 210× speedup over prior work.

### Strengths
- Algorithms are interesting (R1).
- First algorithm for maximum coverage in the turnstile model (R2).
- Constructs a linear sketch that supports input updates for the maximum \(k\) coverage problem (R3).
- Experimental evaluations demonstrate a significant speedup compared to prior work (up to 210×) (R3).
- Extending known results to the turnstile model is interesting (R4).
- One reviewer (R3) found the paper overall well-written, though others disagree (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear dependency on \(k\) and space ambiguity.** The space bound is given as \(\tilde{O}(d/\varepsilon^3)\) (Theorem 1) but the abstract states \(\tilde{O}(d/\varepsilon^2)\) – this discrepancy is not addressed. The impact of \(k\) on memory is not discussed; if intermediate results for each selected subset are stored, the actual space could be much larger than claimed. This makes comparison with existing approaches difficult, especially for large \(k\) (R1, R4).
- **Poor clarity and missing details.** The writing is often confusing and lacks precision (R2, R4). Specific issues include:
  - Algorithm 1 (the sketch construction) is extremely challenging to understand: the format of the sketch is not defined (e.g., is \(H_{\le d}\) a matrix or a set of pairs?), the initialization and update process are not explained, and line 14 merely says “sketches and samplers handle updates” without specifying which sketches (R4). The description of the \(L_0\) sampler and the meaning of “if \(r\) has less than … edges among \(L_0\) samplers” (line 23) is unclear (R2).
  - The definition of a “linear sketch” is assumed; it should be stated explicitly (R4).
  - Remark 1, which discusses sampling rates and the \(l_0\)-sampler, is confusing and its connection to the algorithm is not explained (R4).
  - Lines 141–143: the estimation problem and how \(x_i\) updates are received (streaming vs. \((i,\pm1)\)) are not described, making Theorem 3’s applicability unclear (R4).
  - Line 200: do the authors mean \(L_0\) sketches, not \(L_1\)? (R2)
  - Line 209: “\(|x_i| \ge \varepsilon^2 \|x\|_p\)” – the value of \(p\) is unspecified (arbitrary or specific?) (R3).
  - Line 222 omits \(\varepsilon\) in the \(\tilde{O}\) notation, while line 224 includes it; inconsistent (R3).
  - Line 226: “\(\alpha - \varepsilon\)” appears to be a typo; should be “\(1 - 1/e - \varepsilon\)” (R3).
  - What is \(F_k\) in the abstract? (R4)
- **Linearity of components not justified.** The paper claims Algorithm 1 is a linear sketch, but it is not clear whether the \(L_0\) sampler and the \(L_1\) sketch used inside are themselves linear. The family of matrices should be defined explicitly, and the linearity of the \(L_0\) sampler must be clarified for the overall sketch to be linear (R2, R3).
- **Missing comparison with streaming submodular maximization.** A large body of work on streaming submodular maximization is relevant but not discussed (e.g., [1] NeurIPS 2020, [2] SIGMOD 2022?, [3] NeurIPS 2020) (R2).
- **Experimental results not on turnstile streams.** The empirical evaluation is not conducted on turnstile streams, so the practical performance of the algorithm under deletions is unverified (R2).
- **Specific claims need justification or correction.**
  - Claim 3.1: For \(m=1\), the construction \(A'_1\) contains approximately half the rows; this seems to require \(n/2\) memory – how does this fit in the claimed space? (R2)
  - Claim 3.2: McGregor & Vu’s proof relies on the insertion-only model; it is not obvious that it transfers to the turnstile model without additional justification (R2).
  - Line 276–277: “\(k \log d / \varepsilon^2\) is a fixed number” – how can a fixed number be OPT? This is unclear (R2).
  - Line 294: symbols \(c_1, c_2, \dots\) are used but never defined (R2).
