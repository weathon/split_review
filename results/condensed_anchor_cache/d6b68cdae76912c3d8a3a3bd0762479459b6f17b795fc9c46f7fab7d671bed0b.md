## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proves that for well-specified parametric models under covariate shift, Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) using only source data achieves minimax optimal sample complexity, under assumptions of identifiability and convex likelihood and without boundedness of the density ratio. Results are instantiated on linear regression, logistic regression, and phase retrieval. For misspecified settings, MLE can be inconsistent, and a weighted version (MWLE) is shown to be minimax optimal in certain cases. Reviewers agree the paper is technically solid and well-presented, but disagree sharply on novelty: some find it a significant step on an understudied problem, while others argue that the well-specified result is already well-known in classical statistics (Shimodaira, 2000) and the finite-sample minimax optimality may follow directly from standard parametric estimation arguments. The scope is limited to (generalized) linear models, and the assumptions are considered non-trivial.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles a well-motivated and understudied problem: sample complexity of parametric learning under covariate shift. (R2, R3)
- Provides a comprehensive set of results covering both well-specified and misspecified settings, giving a clear picture of the state of the art. (R2)
- Gives a tight answer about sample complexity in the well-specified case, identifying exactly how the mismatch between source and target impacts performance. (R2)
- Clean, well-written presentation with clear claims and good discussion of prior work; illustrated effectively with concrete models (linear, logistic, phase retrieval). (R2, R3)
- Upper and lower matching bounds are nicely presented with necessary conditions; the MWLE discussion imposes stronger conditions. (R3)
- Demonstrates minimax optimal sample complexity for covariate shift in natural models with potential real-world applications; the distinction between well-specified and misspecified cases is intriguing. (R4)
- Highlights the practical importance of using MLE under correctly specified models, potentially avoiding more advanced methods. (R1)
- The finite-sample minimax optimality of MLE under covariate shift is a main strength. (R1)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and significance are disputed.** Some reviewers (R2, R3) consider the problem understudied and the result surprising; others (R1, R4) argue that for well-specified models, it is already known from classical statistics that covariate shift is not serious (Shimodaira, 2000) and that “MLE is all we need” is a known fact. (R1, R4) R1 states the contribution lies only in finite-sample minimax optimality, which may be trivial or already covered by existing results on parametric estimation (R1). R4 argues Theorem 3.1 is essentially a direct corollary of the strong assumptions in Assumption A. (R4)
- The paper’s scope in applications is limited to (generalized) linear models, which does not convincingly demonstrate that MLE is “all you need” for a broad class. (R4)
- Technical novelty of some results is limited: the upper bound proof uses standard Taylor expansion and consistency arguments (Lemma A.2), similar to standard parametric statistics. (R2)
- The assumptions (Assumption A and C) are quite non-trivial and apply to both the model class and the source/target distributions, not just the model class as claimed. (R4)
- Lack of intuition on why simple MLE works so well in the well-specified case. (R3)
- No discussion comparing prediction vs. estimation under covariate shift; relevant nonparametric conformal prediction literature (e.g., arXiv:1904.06019, arXiv:2203.01761) is not cited. (R3)
- The finite-sample guarantees hold only above a threshold that depends on ∥I_T^{1/2} I_S^{-1} I_T^{1/2}∥; the optimal sample complexity dependence on such quantities is not analyzed. (R2)
- Would benefit from comparison to prior work, e.g., for linear regression compare with Lei et al. (R2)
- In the well-specified case where MLE is minimax optimal, it is unclear if anything is to be gained from access to target X and Y. (R3)
- For the misspecified section, it would be helpful to include lower bounds for each example. (R4)
- Paper length is excessive (50 pages); an outline of the primary proof techniques would help distinguish essential steps from routine procedures. (R4)
- Minor technical issues: in the derivation of inequality (13) on page 14, an expectation sign may be missing; notation for expectations over source vs. target should be explicit. (R2)
- The title is deemed inappropriate by R1 and R4; they suggest titles reflecting the finite-sample minimax optimality contribution rather than the “all you need” claim. (R1, R4)
- R1 questions why only parametric models are considered and whether results can be extended to nonparametric or uniform optimality. (R1)
