## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper empirically analyzes Masked Distillation (MD), a variant of Masked Image Modeling (MIM) that uses a teacher’s semantic features as supervision instead of raw pixels. It claims MD mitigates multiple forms of overfitting (attention homogenization, representation folding in high layers), introduces beneficial inductive biases from MIM, and provides a smoother loss landscape. The analysis covers attention distance, frequency spectrum, singular values, entropy, normalized mutual information, and loss landscapes. One reviewer (score 5) found the paper relatively easy to comprehend, the exploration crucial, and the experimental procedures detailed. Other reviewers (scores 1, 3, 3) raised substantial concerns about novelty, methodological rigor, and clarity of explanations.

### Strengths

*   The paper performs a diverse set of analyses for masked distillation.
*   The viewpoint is interesting.
*   Some analysis of current techniques is valuable (modality differences, gap between pre-training and downstream tasks).
*   The observation of unique features of mask distillation that could inspire modern training pipeline design.
*   Despite needing structural improvement, the paper is straightforward to comprehend.
*   The exploration of how MD shapes learned representations is crucial.
*   Detailed experimental procedures are provided, enhancing reproducibility.

### Weaknesses

*   **Novelty and overlap with prior MIM work:** The paper should compare MD with MIM (the base method it improves), but instead mostly compares with SL, SSL, and CLIP. Showing MD behaves similarly to MIM is unsurprising and contributes little. The experimental metrics (attention distance, frequency spectrum, relative singular value, normalized MI) and messages are heavily borrowed from “What Do Self-Supervised Vision Transformers Learn?” (ICLR 2023), making this largely a masked-distillation version of that paper with limited novelty. The distinction from previous regular-MIM analyses (e.g., “Revealing the Dark Secrets of Masked Image Modeling”) is unclear, further limiting impact. A minority reviewer (score 5) also notes the borrowing of analyses (attention distance, entropy) from that prior work and worries about limited impact.
*   **Missing comparisons and downstream evaluation:** No downstream performance comparison (e.g., ImageNet-1k accuracy) is provided for models with and without MD (SL, SSL, CLIP vs. SL_MD, SSL_MD, CLIP_MD). The claimed benefits of MD on attention diversity/homogeneity have no quantitative main-text results: Figure 10 (the only quantitative data) is in the appendix. The loss landscape analysis excludes SL; why is SL omitted? The connection between Hessian-based optimization stability (Fu et al. 2023, SGD) and AdamW (used for ViTs) is not justified.
*   **Incorrect or unclear metrics:** Attention diversity/homogeneity cannot be measured by entropy; appropriate distance metrics (e.g., KL divergence) should be used. Frequency spectrum analysis is questioned as meaningful for transformers vs. ConvNets. In Figure 6(a), SL_MD shows lower NMIs than SL, contradicting the paper’s explanation. In Figure 10, a smaller entropy range does not necessarily imply similar attentions – visualizing the distance between attention heads would be clearer.
*   **Missing definitions and variant ambiguity:** “Masked distillation” covers many methods (e.g., [B,C,D,E]). A clear definition is needed at the beginning. Which exact variant is used? Figure 3(c) shows multiple MD variants; can the paper’s implementation (_MD) represent all of them? A pioneer work ([F], “Exploring Target Representations for Masked Autoencoders”) should be cited and discussed. The tag set “sl, ssl, clip” in Figures 2-4 is unexplained and inconsistent with the analysis mentioning DINO, CLIP, and supervised models; some legends lack explanation.
*   **Unscientific language and lack of evidence:** Claims such as “Masked Distillation imbues the model with attributes inherent to MI” and “inductive bias of attention” are not well-defined, and the experiments do not clearly validate them. Sentences like “This phenomenon may be attributed to their extreme homogenization of background responses” are stated without backing evidence. No explanation is provided for why the observed properties help MD prevent overfitting. The “new analytical framework” is never clearly defined.
*   **Insufficient guidance from findings:** Although the paper gives new points about MD, it remains unclear how these findings can be used to design a better training pipeline.
*   **Structural issues:** The introduction excessively focuses on MIM rather than MD. Section 3 and 4 both compare properties between MD and non-MD and could be merged. A reviewer question about the need for splitting is noted.

**Note:** One minority reviewer (score 5) was more positive overall, but still identified the above weaknesses related to missing downstream comparison, unclear distinction from prior MIM work, and borrowing of analyses. Another reviewer (score 3) found the core idea interesting but the experimental details lacking.
