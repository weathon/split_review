## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SelaVPR, a hybrid global and local adaptation method to adapt the pre-trained DINOv2 foundation model to two-stage visual place recognition (VPR). Global adaptation uses parallel and serial adapters added to each ViT block; local adaptation adds up-sampling layers after the ViT backbone to produce dense local features. A mutual nearest neighbor local feature loss trains the local module, enabling efficient re-ranking without geometric verification. The method achieves state-of-the-art results on six VPR benchmarks with less training data and time, using about 3% retrieval runtime of RANSAC-based two-stage methods, and ranked 1st on the MSLS challenge leaderboard at submission.

### Strengths
- The concept is straightforward: the motivation for adapting foundation models to VPR (due to object-centric vs. landmark-focused objectives) is clearly presented. The paper is well-organized, easy to read, and presents a good overview of related work.
- **Technically novel and solid contributions**: (1) closing the gap between pre-training and VPR tasks via hybrid adaptation; (2) producing proper dense local features for VPR using DINOv2. The method is simple, not over-complicated, and achieves very fast two-stage retrieval.
- Extensive experiments on 6 VPR benchmark datasets (Pitts30k, Tokyo24/7, MSLS, etc.) yield excellent results, outperforming previous SOTA methods by a large margin. Ablation studies and visualizations confirm that the adapters focus on discriminative landmarks and eliminate dynamic interference.
- High computational efficiency: retrieval runtime on Pitts30k is <0.1s (about 3% of TransVPR). This makes contributions toward real-world large-scale VPR using pre-trained foundation models.
- The approach bridges the gap between pre-training and VPR using only a small amount of training data and time.

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and gap analysis insufficient** (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3): The paper claims pre-trained models focus on different objects than VPR, but does not provide a clear explanation or feature-space analysis of why this gap necessitates the proposed hybrid adaptation. A more detailed analysis of the differences between pre-trained and VPR feature spaces would strengthen motivation. After reading, the scientific insight is limited—“why does this method work?” is not answered. The contribution appears to be solely overcoming SOTA results without providing evidence-based understanding or interpretability.
- **No failure cases** (Reviewer 3): The paper does not show examples where the method fails, which could inform future research. It would be beneficial to see when the approach is not robust and analyze why.
- **Design choices weakly explained** (Reviewer 1): No motivation or justification for why the adapters are designed the way they are (parallel vs. serial, MLP structure). What is the hypothesis behind this design, and what experimental evidence supports it? How does this differ from existing adapter-based adaptation for foundation models?
- **Parameter count not discussed** (Reviewer 1): The proposed adapters still require fine-tuning over +50M parameters, which is much more than other methods train, on top of the +300M parameter backbone. No discussion of total capacity versus existing methods, and no experiments with adaptation of smaller pre-trained models (e.g., CNNs pre-trained on ImageNet) to see if the same approach works.
- **Data-efficiency claim not substantiated** (Reviewer 1): The authors mention data-efficiency as a key argument for using foundation models, but do not provide experimental evidence (e.g., varying training data size) to support it.
- **Missing reference** (Reviewer 1): Leyva-Vallina et al., Data-Efficient Large Scale Place Recognition With Graded Similarity Supervision, CVPR 2023.
- **Incomplete experimental coverage**:
  - *Pitts250k* not reported (Reviewer 4); it is a common VPR dataset.
  - *Re-ranking candidate count* not varied (Reviewer 4, Reviewer 3): It would be useful to show performance with different numbers of re-ranking candidates (e.g., top-100 vs. more/less) to help choose the optimal count.
  - *Transferability* (Reviewer 3): The paper finetunes on MSLS then further finetunes on Pitts30k before testing on Pitts30k/Tokyo24/7, same as R2Former. However, R2Former also reports results when trained only on MSLS to show domain transfer. Can SelaVPR still outperform R2Former on Pitts30k when trained on MSLS only?
- **Clarity could be improved** (Reviewer 2): Though overall clear, some parts need further improvement.
- **Minor review points**:
  - Why is L2 distance used for global similarity but dot product for local feature similarity? (Reviewer 2)
  - Is it feasible to re-rank top-k candidates directly using coarse ViT patch tokens, and what is the performance? (Reviewer 2)
  - Reviewer 1 is more negative overall (score 5 vs. 8,8,8), emphasizing the lack of insights, missing justification, and parameter overhead, while other reviewers found the method novel and solid. This contrast should be noted.
