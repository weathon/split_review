## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper establishes tight worst-case oracle lower bounds for minimizing functions that are both uniformly convex (degree \(q\), parameter \(\sigma\)) and have \(p^{\text{th}}\)-order Hölder continuous derivatives (degree \(\nu\), parameter \(H\)). The focus is on the asymmetric cases where \(q>p+\nu\) and \(q<p+\nu\), using up to \(p^{\text{th}}\)-order oracle access. The lower bounds are obtained by explicit construction of hard problem instances: an \(\ell_\infty\)-ball-truncated-Gaussian smoothed hard function for the first case and an adapted Nesterov-type function for the second. The bounds match known upper bounds, extending prior results for first- and second-order smoothness.

### Strengths
- The problem is important, well-motivated, and rigorously analyzed; the results are of great interest to the optimization community.
- The lower bounds are tight and match the corresponding upper bounds in these general asymmetric settings, contributing significantly to the oracle complexity literature.
- The presentation is clear, with well-explained intuition (e.g., lines 170–191) and careful situating of results relative to prior work.
- The use of \(\ell_\infty\)-ball-truncated Gaussian smoothing to achieve dimension-free smoothness is an interesting technical novelty that circumvents dimensionality dependencies that would arise with other smoothing approaches.
- The results generalize previous lower bounds for uniformly convex functions with first- and second-order smoothness, providing a unified framework.

### Weaknesses
- The case \(q = p+\nu\) is not covered; both proof techniques break down. Future work should address this symmetric case and the specific challenges involved.
- One reviewer notes that the lower bound constructions are adaptations of previously known functions (Nemirovski and Nesterov) with relatively similar analyses; the paper could benefit from a clearer summary/comparison of which parts are new versus inherited (e.g., the function in lines 218–220). The reviewer acknowledges that the key differences are explained and that publication is still justified.
- **Potential inaccuracy in claim about dimension-free smoothing (line 168):** The statement that Gaussian smoothing is dimension-free while uniform smoothing over an \(\ell_2\) ball is not appears misleading. By concentration of measure, Gaussian smoothing is nearly equivalent to uniform smoothing over an \(\ell_2\) ball of radius proportional to \(\sqrt{d}\); the comparison seems to use different approximation parameters \(\delta\). Similarly, softmax smoothing has a \(\log(d)\) factor in its approximation parameter, so a \(\delta\)-approximation may yield a smoothness constant of order \(\log(d)/\delta\) (milder but not dimension-free). The authors should clarify or revise this claim.
- **Lack of discussion about algorithm class:** The proofs are written for deterministic (or zero-respecting) algorithms. The first lower bound (Nemirovski function) should also hold for randomized algorithms via robust-zero-chain arguments (Carmon et al.), but this is not provided. The second lower bound (Nesterov function) is not a robust zero-chain and may not hold beyond deterministic/zero-respecting algorithms. The paper should explicitly state which algorithm classes the bounds hold for and discuss the implications.
- **Specific errors and typos (reviewer 4):**  
  - Line 327: In the lower bound for \(F(x_T)-F(x^*)\) the term becomes \(-4\beta\rho T\) but the preceding expression contains a factor \(p\); the omission of \(p\) is not justified.  
  - Line 783: Should be \(Z_i/\sqrt{d}\) instead of \(1/\sqrt{d}\).  
  - Lines 230, 317: Reference should be to Lemma 1, not Lemma 14.  
  - Line 934: A \(\beta^v\) appears confusingly in the first inequality.  
  - Lines 293, 296: \(x_t\) should be \(x_s\); the equality in line 296 should be \(\ge\).  
  - Line 1011: Should also be \(\ge\).  
  - Line 1019: Would benefit from a reference to Lemma 1.  
- **Informal reasoning in Section 5 (lines 402–403):** The scaling of \(\tilde{f}\) and how it directly implies that all properties translate to \(f\) is unclear and should be made more precise.
- **Missing citation:** The paper lacks a citation for at least one line of upper bounds for the case of Hölder smooth functions.
- **Notation clarification:** The symbol \(\|\cdot\|\) for derivatives (e.g., line 201) refers to the operator norm; this should be stated explicitly to avoid confusion with Frobenius norms. Additionally, Lemma 1 (i) may be missing the assumption that the function is \(L\)-Lipschitz.
