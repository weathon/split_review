## Merged Review

### Summary
One reviewer (score 6) was positive about the novelty of IBF and HTM, while the other three (scores 1, 3, 3) were highly critical, citing major weaknesses in validation, comparisons, and completeness. The paper proposes IBF for redundancy reduction and HTM for efficient long-range modeling to overcome the Lookback Window Limitation (LWL). Experiments on seven datasets claim state-of-the-art with a 1024 lookback window. However, reviewers question the definition of LWL, fairness of comparisons, choice of baselines, and overall rigor.

### Strengths
- Focus on increasing input window dimensionality, an active area in LLM research (Reviewer 1).
- Novel approach to LWL with IBF and HTM (Reviewer 2).
- Theoretical and practical innovation (Reviewer 2).
- Demonstrates the necessity of models with longer lookback windows and shows limitations of existing transformers (Reviewer 4).
- Conducted experiments using input sequences up to length 1024 (Reviewer 4).
- Combined Mamba-Transformer hybrid with the information bottleneck approach (Reviewer 4).
- Empirical validation across seven datasets, clear organization (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Incomplete definition and explanation of key concepts**: Lookback Window Limitation (LWL) is never defined or explained; cited papers do not address it (Reviewer 4). Figure 2 has no explanation in caption or text; Figure 3(a) has artifacts; axis labels missing in Figures 3 and 5; abbreviations not expanded in Figure 6 caption (e.g., "PHI" instead of "PIH"); inconsistent use of Ettm/ETTm (Reviewer 4). Minor: duplicate paragraph on page 2 about "Moreover, it does not mitigate the quadratic complexity" (Reviewer 3). Figure 6 left panel legend incorrectly states "PHI" (Reviewer 3).
- **Outdated and insufficient SOTA comparisons**: Only compared with PatchTST (2023) and S-Mamba (unpeer-reviewed 2024); FEDformer (2022) and Autoformer (2021) are too old. Missing recent methods (e.g., from ICML 2024, IJCAI 2024, arxiv 2024) – see references: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/zhang24l/zhang24l.pdf, https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/0608.pdf, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.14616, and survey https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.09898 (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). Need to include recently proposed Mamba-Transformer hybrid models in related work and compare performance in main table: e.g., SST: Multi-Scale Hybrid Mamba-Transformer Experts for Long-Short Range Time Series Forecasting, "Can Mamba Learn How to Learn?", and "A Comparative Study on In-Context Learning Tasks" (Reviewer 4). Annotate each method with its publication year (Reviewer 3).
- **Unfair window-size comparisons and experimental setup**: Table 1 only shows results for L=1024, but baselines may perform better with shorter lookback windows; provide each method's performance under its original configuration (Reviewer 2). For T=720, authors selected best results by choosing lookback windows from {24, 48, 96, 192, 336, 720, 1024}, which is unfair – the model should be superior for any input length (Reviewer 2). Recommend two tables: one with all models at L=1024, and one using each model’s optimal L (Reviewer 3). Parameter settings for baselines in Table 1 (FEDformer, Autoformer, Informer) are unclear and may replicate PatchTST results with non-matching lookback windows (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of evidence for claimed benefits**: No evidence that setting lookback to 1024 creates problems in other architectures, or that increasing to 1024 significantly improves accuracy over 512, 256, or 128 (Reviewer 1). Claim that PIH outperforms under shorter windows (336, 512) is only compared with DLinear and NLinear; Table 1 shows PatchTST appears superior (Reviewer 3). Figure 5 does not show effective filtering of redundant information or efficient use of longer inputs; more experiments on additional datasets with sufficient explanations are needed (Reviewer 4). Authors conclude "longer lookback windows are not always beneficial for all datasets," contradicting the paper's core claim – please provide a detailed explanation (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient validation of general applicability**: Claim of model-agnostic technique, but main results apply only to PatchTST. Ablation (Figure 4) applies to Transformer, Informer, Autoformer – all very weak models that any simple improvement (e.g., a residual linear layer) could boost; on ETTm1 these models are not even close to linear baselines even after the technique (Reviewer 1). Need application to recent strong transformer architectures (Reviewer 1) and to iTransformer (Reviewer 4). Provide full table comparing basemodel vs. basemodel+IBF+HTM, briefly mentioned in Section 4.2 (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing important comparisons and datasets**: No comparison against univariate baselines to justify multivariate complexity (Reviewer 1). Must demonstrate that multivariate forecasting significantly beats univariate (Reviewer 1). Since PatchTST and DLinear/NLinear assume channel independence, compare with channel-dependent models (Reviewer 3). Recent time series forecasting papers use more public datasets: add ECL, Exchange, Solar-Energy, and PEMS (Reviewer 4). No explanation of number of seeds for repeated experiments; outlier values raise suspicions that no repeats were conducted, reducing reliability (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of methodological details**: No diagram for how Transformer and Mamba are combined in HTM (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4). Disorganized notations and inappropriate figures in Methods section; include sufficient explanations, even if in appendix (Reviewer 4). Need explanation for Figure 3(c) visualization – how was it obtained? (Reviewer 2). No discussion of impact of key hyperparameters K, β, τ on model performance (Reviewer 2). Need to explain how the model extracts information more relevant to prediction for any input length (Reviewer 2).
