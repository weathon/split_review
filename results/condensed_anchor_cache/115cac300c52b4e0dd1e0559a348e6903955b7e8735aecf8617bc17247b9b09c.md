## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes Multimodal Augmented Adversarial Training (MA2T) for robust image-text retrieval (ITR), the first defense strategy that addresses multimodal adversarial perturbations and exploits one-to-many (1:N, N:1) relationships in training data to mitigate overfitting to deterministic 1:1 pairs. Experiments on Flickr30k and COCO using CLIP-ViT-B/16 show robustness improvements (1.7%–8.7%) across multiple attack types, with text augmentations and cross-modal augmentations outperforming image augmentations.

### Strengths

- The work pioneers defense strategies specifically for image-text retrieval (ITR), a recognized gap as prior adversarial defenses were focused on image classification (R1, R4). The multimodal adversarial training (perturbing both modalities) is a novel contribution to ITR robustness (R1, R2, R3).
- A comprehensive, in-depth analysis of one-to-many relationships via diverse augmentation strategies (text, image, intra-modal, cross-modal) validates their effectiveness (R1, R3, R4). The finding that cross-modal augmentations (image→text) can be more beneficial than intra-modal augmentations is supported by experiments (R3).
- Experiments are well-structured across multiple attack types (e.g., R2 notes many attack types studied) and include systematic augmentation comparisons (R2, R3). The proposed framework is relevant and adaptable to real-world scenarios involving multimodal adversarial threats (R4).
- The paper is clearly articulated and easy to read (R1), with sufficient context and explanations (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited model and architecture scope.** All experiments use only CLIP (specifically CLIP-ViT-B/16). Multiple reviewers (R2, R3, R4) strongly recommend evaluating on other vision-language models such as BLIP, ALBEF, or larger CLIP variants to assess generalizability. One reviewer (R2) notes that a small model was used; larger models may already exhibit better inherent robustness, and the improvement gains may not transfer. The absence of ablation across different foundation models makes the conclusions model-specific rather than architecture-agnostic (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Limited dataset diversity.** Evaluation is confined to Flickr30k and COCO. Reviewers (R1, R2, R4) argue these datasets may not represent real-world complexity; Flickr is considered a simple dataset (R2). Suggestions include testing on remote sensing scenes (R1) or other more challenging/domain-diverse datasets. This narrow evaluation limits the generalizability claims (R1, R2, R4).
- **Lack of theoretical justification.** The paper does not provide a theoretical explanation for why one-to-many augmentations directly improve adversarial robustness in ITR (R1, R2, R3). The choices of multimodal training setup and augmentation strategies are described as empirically driven, without a solid theoretical basis (R2). There is no formal definition of what constitutes a "good" cross-modal augmentation or how it relates to the geometry of the multimodal embedding space (R3).
- **Inconsistency and incomplete ablation.** The use of augmented pairs appears selective or applied inconsistently across attack types and scenarios, leading to ambiguity about which robustness gains are attributable solely to MA2T (R2). The ablation experiments are too simplistic; at minimum, different foundation models should have been ablated (R1). Image augmentation details are missing (types, parameters) and the potential distribution shifts induced by image augmentations are not analyzed (R3). Comparisons with other multimodal adversarial training methods (e.g., the many-to-many approach from arXiv 2024) are missing and requested (Q from R1, Q from R4).
- **Missing framework diagram.** The paper lacks a clear diagram or visual representation of the proposed MA2T framework, which limits readability and quick understanding of the architecture and information flow (R4). (Also noted as a question by R4.)
- **Minor issues.** Multiple reviewers note a typo: "conprehensive" should be "comprehensive" (line 021) and "mutlimodal" instead of "multimodal" (line 225) (R1, R3, R4). Thorough proofreading is recommended.

**Reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (R2, score 3/5) is notably more critical, questioning the fundamental link between one-to-many augmentations and robustness, the small model size, the simplicity of Flickr30k, and experimental inconsistencies. The other three reviewers (score 5/5 each) are more positive, though they also raise many of the same methodological limitations.
