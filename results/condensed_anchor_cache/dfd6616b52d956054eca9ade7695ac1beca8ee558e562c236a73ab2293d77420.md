## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes a synthetic sequence modeling task based on finite mixtures of Markov chains to study in-context learning (ICL) in Transformers. The authors show that this setting reproduces several well-known ICL phenomena (task diversity thresholds, emergence of statistical induction heads, transience of ICL) and introduces a decomposition of model behavior into four idealized algorithms along axes of unigram vs. bigram statistics and Bayesian vs. frequency-based (non-Bayesian) inference. Using phase isolation metrics and a Linear Combination of Algorithms (LCA) method, they demonstrate that trained transformers transition between these algorithmic phases depending on training steps, context length, and task diversity. They argue that ICL is best understood as a mixture of algorithms rather than a single capability. Reviewer scores range from 6 to 10: one reviewer (score 10) initially raised major framing concerns that were later resolved during discussion, while other reviewers (scores 6,6,8) appreciated the comprehensiveness but noted limited practical insights and incremental novelty.

### Strengths

1. **Well-written and comprehensive study**: The paper is clearly written, provides adequate context for non-experts, and conducts a thorough empirical investigation across multiple axes: task diversity, training steps, context length, model width, and data complexity. [Reviewers 1,2,3]

2. **Sound, novel, and unifying benchmark**: The finite Markov mixture task is a clean and flexible data generating process that replicates several previously disparate phenomena (task diversity threshold, statistical induction heads, transience) within a single setting, offering a unified framework for future ICL research. Reviewer 3 notes that Figure 3 reproduces results of two prior papers. [Reviewers 1,3,4]

3. **Novel insight: ICL as a mixture of algorithms**: The decomposition of transformer behavior into four idealized algorithms and the observation that these algorithms engage in competitive dynamics throughout training is a striking finding. The LCA analysis reveals persistent competition and explains the transient nature of ICL. Reviewer 1 highlights that the superposition in Fig. 6b shows algorithms are not forgotten but weighted. Reviewer 3 appreciates that the mixture idea can predict OOD performance. [Reviewers 1,3,4]

4. **Creative and thoughtful experimental protocols**: The tests for bigram utilization and proximity to Bayesian solutions are well-designed. The LCA technique (behavioral projection onto algorithm simplex) is an elegant and potentially widely applicable tool for behavioral analysis of models. [Reviewers 3,4]

5. **Comprehensive ablations**: The paper includes extensive ablations studying the impact of width, data complexity, and other architectural choices. [Reviewer 3]

6. **Strong contribution to understanding ICL (particularly from minority reviewer)**: Reviewer 4, despite initial severe criticisms (resolved during discussion), ultimately rated the paper 10, noting it makes a strong contribution to the science of deep learning by enriching ICL phenomenology and providing a rich framework for future research. This contrasts with other reviewers (6,6,8) who found the work valuable but cited concerns about novelty and actionability.

### Weaknesses

1. **Novelty and incremental contribution**: Several results are known from prior work. The task diversity threshold, transience, and statistical induction heads have been shown in other settings (e.g., Raventos et al., Edelman et al., Singh et al., Panwar et al., Hoogland et al.). The setting itself is similar to Edelman et al.'s infinite Markov chain mixtures. Reviewer 2 finds the overall message lacking new insights, and Reviewer 4 notes that phenomena were not previously isolated to single settings. [Reviewers 2,4] *Disagreement*: Reviewer 4 ultimately accepted the paper after revisions, but still noted the generalization is limited; Reviewer 3 found the replications valuable.

2. **Lack of actionable takeaway for real-world ICL**: The benchmark is synthetic, and it is unclear how the identified algorithms (unigram/bigram, Bayesian/frequency-based) translate to real LLMs/VLMs. Reviewer 1 and 3 would like discussion of how insights inform practical design choices (e.g., predicting OOD performance). The paper does not specify which algorithms apply to real-world ICL or how to surface them. [Reviewers 1,3]

3. **Overclaimed confidence in algorithmic phase identification**: The phase isolation methodology assumes only the four proposed algorithms; many plausible alternatives (e.g., Bayesian averaging with different prior, MLE/MAP, smoothed frequency counting) are close in function space and cannot be ruled out. The LCA method also cannot distinguish these. Reviewer 4 (W1) initially required major revisions to temper claims that the transformer "implements" specific algorithms; this was addressed during discussion. The paper should still clearly state limitations and avoid language suggesting definitive mechanism identification (e.g., "perfectly delineate", "successfully explain", "identify existence"). [Reviewer 4]

4. **LCA is not mechanistic and does not offer a fundamentally new explanation for transience**:
   - LCA is behavioral, not mechanistic; it does not reveal internal computational structure. Calling it "mechanistic" or a "causal explanation" is unwarranted.
   - The explanation that Bi-ICL gives way to Bi-Bayes due to better ID performance has already been observed in in-context linear regression (Hoogland et al., Panwar et al.) and is even less mysterious than Singh et al.'s transience because algorithms differ in ID performance.
   - The paper does not provide deeper insight into *why* the transition occurs at a specific point (e.g., during training) or at a specific rate. [Reviewer 4, W2]
   *Note*: After revision, the authors addressed these framing issues (no longer claim LCA is mechanistic or a new explanation). The original criticism remains relevant for readers to understand the debate.

5. **Unclear Bayesian vs. non-Bayesian distinction**: The dichotomy between Bayesian (leveraging mixture prior) and non-Bayesian (frequency-based) is not clearly justified. Reviewer 1 suggests ICL is always Bayesian, with the "prior" weakening as training diversity increases, which is actually supported by the results (Fig. 5.b). Reviewer 2 argues the comparison does not capture true Bayesian inference—should examine KL between model predictions and true posterior at each context length, and the analogy between "task-retrieval vs. task-learning" and Bayesian vs. non-Bayesian is not rigorous. [Reviewers 1,2]

6. **Missing control conditions and baselines for LCA analysis**:
   - No control using random/unrelated algorithms (e.g., uniform, fixed mapping) to confirm that phase transitions are not artifacts of fitting any linear combination. [Reviewer 3]
   - No baseline comparing LCA performance to fixed-weight combinations to show dynamic weighting is necessary. [Reviewer 3]
   - The residual of the LCA fit (how close the transformer lies to the simplex) is not reported, making it unclear if LCA captures meaningful behavior. [Reviewer 4, Q9]

7. **Unjustified nonuniform task prior**: The Dirichlet-sampled task prior creates skewed effective task diversity, potentially confounding experiments. This detail is only in the appendix. Uniform prior (as in Raventos et al.) would be simpler. [Reviewer 4, Q3]

8. **Convoluted phase isolation tests**: The bigram utilization test and proximity test are intricate; simpler alternatives (e.g., quantifying uniformity in rows of revealed transition matrix for bigram usage) could be considered. Missing details on test methodology (how distance to task set is defined, number of trials, how unseen chain is sampled). [Reviewer 4, Q4, Q5]

9. **Presentation issues and missing experimental details**:
   - Figure 5 legend unclear (heatmap scale), paragraph at l.341 uninformative. [Reviewer 1]
   - Parameter choices (train step 839, n=2^7) seem arbitrary; need similar figures for later steps and larger n in appendix. [Reviewer 1]
   - Confusing algorithm names: Uni-ICL/Bi-ICL connotes they are "truer" ICL than Bayesian algorithms, contradicting the paper's message. Reviewer 4 suggests "frequencies" or "induction" instead. [Reviewer 4, Q1]
   - Figure 4 caption inconsistent with main text (uses "closest task" for Bayesian algorithms, while main text uses posterior averaging). [Reviewer 4, Q2]
   - Relationship between LCA and delta metrics from Raventos et al. unaddressed. [Reviewer 4, Q8]
   - Non-ICL algorithms (unigram prior, bigram prior) not included in LCA for early training phases. [Reviewer 4, Q10]
   - Evaluation details missing: ID prior (training or uniform?), number of sampled tasks. [Reviewer 4, Q11]
   - Sequence initialization from stationary distribution only noted in code, not text. [Reviewer 4, Q12]
   - Paper structure: Section 4 (phases) might be better placed before Section 3 (phenomena). [Reviewer 4, Q13]
   - Terminology: "phases" may not match physics usage; notation overload (bold 1). [Reviewer 4, Q14]
   - Several typos (e.g., stray parenthesis, "this sorts", missing math mode, unfinished sentence, BICL). [Reviewer 4, Q15]
