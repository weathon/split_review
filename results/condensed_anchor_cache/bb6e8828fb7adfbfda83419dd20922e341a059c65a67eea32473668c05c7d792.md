## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies feature learning in deep networks, hypothesizing that model function discontinuities are attracted to label function discontinuities during training. To test this, the authors propose a novel architecture, the Deep Linearly Gated Network (DLGN), whose discontinuities are easily enumerated. Using synthetic data labeled by an Oblique Decision Tree (ODT), they provide empirical evidence that DLGN’s internal hyperplanes align with the ODT’s splits (Figures 2, 3). They also extract an interpretable decision tree from the DLGN parameters via clustering. On real-world tabular datasets, the DLGN is shown to be competitive with ReLU networks and tree-based algorithms.

### Strengths
- **Originality of DLGN architecture.** DLGN is a novel combination of deep linear networks with a gating mechanism, also interpretable as a relaxed, differentiable parametrization of a decision tree (Reviewer 1).
- **Clear, focused writing and persistence** on a challenging but tractable problem setting (Reviewer 2).
- **Mechanistic interpretability:** Unlike black-box learners, DLGN can learn non-linear features while still allowing interpretation (Reviewer 2).
- **Strong empirical support for the core hypothesis.** Figures 2 and 3 strikingly show that DLGN neuron hyperplanes align with ODT hyperplanes, and alignment improves over training (Reviewer 4). Table 1 confirms most linear thresholds match an ODT internal node (Reviewer 4).
- **Clustering-based decision tree extraction** is an interesting, empirically effective method to recover an interpretable model from the trained DLGN (Reviewer 4).
- **Competitive performance:** DLGN outperforms tree, kernel, and ReLU baselines in the ODT setting (Reviewer 2) and is competitive on real-world tabular datasets (Reviewers 1 and 2). The results on dozens of open tabular datasets are provided (Reviewer 2).
- **Framework for future research** that can inspire further theoretical investigations (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).
- **Fresh, unconventional perspective** on feature learning (Reviewer 3), offering a bold attempt that may serve as a valuable starting point (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of focus / split contributions.** The paper combines a study of feature learning via DLGN (Section 5) with a study of DLGN as an expressive, interpretable architecture (Section 6). These two contributions are somewhat orthogonal, and the paper would be stronger if it focused on one (Reviewer 1).
- **Significance questioned by Reviewer 1.**  
  - Capturing “label discontinuities” (which are essentially decision boundaries) is the very task of classification; it is not surprising that a successful model discovers them. The mystery is how over-parameterized models identify high-dimensional boundaries with limited data, which this paper does not address (Reviewer 1).  
  - The connection between DLGN and standard deep networks (ReLU) is insufficiently established. A high-level description that ReLU networks use diagonal gating matrices is not rigorous; more analysis showing that ReLU networks also align with label discontinuities under certain conditions is needed (Reviewer 1, echoed by Reviewer 3).  
  - On real UCI data, DLGN performs comparably to simple tree algorithms (e.g., random forests), not clearly better. Since random forests are also arguably interpretable, there is little evidence to prefer DLGN in practice (Reviewer 1).  
  - If interpretability is a main selling point, the paper should show interpretations of models fit to real data, not just synthetic ODT data (Reviewer 1).
- **Resemblance to ReLU networks is unconvincing (Reviewer 3).** The gating mechanism using explicit halfspaces (via sign of linear functions) seems fundamentally different from the implicit gating of ReLU activations. DLGN simplifies all gating functions to halfspaces, which may alter learning dynamics and representational power. It is unclear how this design or intuition extends to CNNs, transformers, or multi-class/regression settings. The paper currently only addresses binary classification.
- **Missing comparison with recent feature learning literature (Reviewer 3).** Works such as arXiv:2305.06986 and arXiv:2311.13774 study neural networks learning hierarchical functions of the form \(h = g \circ p\) with \(p\) quadratic. The authors should discuss whether and how the proposed discontinuity alignment mechanism can explain such hierarchical feature learning.
- **The Oblique Decision Tree (ODT) section (Section 3) lacks mathematical rigor and clear connection to the alignment mechanism (Reviewer 3).** The introduction of the ODT is too quick; a more formal treatment is needed.
- **Unused or underused concepts introduced in the paper.**  
  - The manifold \(\mathcal{M}\) (line 133) is defined but never used later (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).  
  - The local discontinuity coefficient \(\gamma(R, f)\) is defined in Section 3 but not referenced again. It would be helpful to explain results (e.g., Figure 2) in terms of this quantity (Reviewer 4).  
  - The claim that "no other hyperplane other than the internal nodes has this property" (line 192) is questionable; any hyperplane can have points of both labels on either side (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation issues (Reviewer 1).** The DLGN model diagram is missing from the main text (only formulas), making it hard to understand the architecture. Informal language (“well nigh impossible”, “handily outperform”, “succeed comfortably”, “about the same”) should be replaced with academic phrasing.
- **Missing ablations and clarifications.**  
  - Why not use a temperature parameter for the sigmoid (with possible annealing) as is common in continuous relaxation methods? (Reviewer 1)  
  - Why is DBScan used for clustering hyperplanes, and why are outliers expected to be a significant issue? (Reviewer 1)  
  - How do the authors explain that certain layers match true discontinuities more than others (Figure 2)? (Reviewer 1)  
  - In Equation (4), is there a missing transpose on \(\mathbf{u}_{i_1}^1\)? (Reviewer 2)  
  - Clarify the computational cost claim that a forward pass for Equation (1) is less than twice that of a ReLU network with \(mL\) nodes (Reviewer 2).  
  - The current experiments are limited to binary classification; it is unclear how the proposed intuition would extend to multi-class or regression tasks (Reviewer 3).
- **Reviewer disagreement on significance and readiness.** Reviewer 1 (score 5, contribution 1) is more negative, finding little significance and insufficient evidence. Reviewer 3 (score 6, contribution 3) finds it intriguing but not yet ready for publication, citing major concerns. Reviewer 2 (score 8, contribution 4) and Reviewer 4 (score 8, contribution 3) are more positive, praising the novelty and empirical results but acknowledging the specialized setting.
