## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Higher-Order Transformers (HOT), a Transformer architecture for tensor-structured data (more than two axes). It introduces a Kronecker factorized attention mechanism to reduce attention cost from quadratic in the total tensor size to quadratic in each axis’ dimension, and further leverages kernelized attention to achieve linear complexity. Experiments on multivariate time-series forecasting and 3D medical image classification show that HOT achieves competitive performance with improved computational efficiency.

### Strengths
- The paper is clear, well written, and easy to follow, providing a concise explanation of the Kronecker factorized attention mechanism.
- The research question is important: the attention operator is computationally expensive on higher‑order data, and designing specialized architectures for such data is meaningful.
- Using Kronecker decomposition to factorize attention is sound, and the theoretical proof of Kronecker decomposition is provided.
- HOT efficiently captures essential cross‑dimensional dependencies with lower complexity, overcoming limitations of prior methods that flatten the input.
- On multivariate time‑series forecasting, HOT outperforms all baselines in terms of MAE and MSE (Reviewer 1 notes it always outperforms; Reviewer 2 supports superiority in both effectiveness and efficiency).
- Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach on higher‑order data, with a clear efficiency advantage.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: Applying Kronecker factorization to attention on higher‑order data has been explored in prior work [1]; the current contribution appears marginal (Reviewers 3, 4). The paper is essentially about attention rather than the full Transformer, and the methodology is based mainly on existing Kronecker tensor decomposition [1] and kernel attention [2] (Reviewer 4). Authors must clearly distinguish their contributions from [1].
- **Evaluation validity**: The multivariate forecasting experiments reshape 1D time series into 2D tensors. Because there is no locality in the feature dimension of 1D data, this does not constitute a proper evaluation on higher‑order data; experiments on real 2D and 3D data are preferred (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 also requests experiments on typical 2D image datasets and video data to demonstrate generality.
- **Evaluation limitations – forecasting**:
  * Only MAE and MSE are reported; other metrics (e.g., MAPE) and confidence intervals for predictions are missing (Reviewer 1).
  * Improvements over baselines are minor and not statistically significant (Reviewer 1).
- **Evaluation limitations – 3D medical images**: The HOT model’s performance is less competitive (Reviewer 1).
- **Scalability and architectural flexibility**:
  * Unlike flattening‑based approaches, HOT cannot use the same architecture to process data of different orders, limiting its scalability (Reviewer 2).
  * No detailed analysis of how the method scales with more than three dimensions; the complexity analysis is only general, without a breakdown of computational bottlenecks (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing comparisons**:
  * No comparison with other tensor decomposition methods (e.g., CP decomposition, Tucker decomposition) for the attention mechanism (Reviewer 1).
  * Model parameters are not compared against baselines (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of trade‑off analysis**: The paper does not thoroughly analyze how the low‑rank approximation via Kronecker decomposition (choice of rank R, impact of kernels) affects the trade‑off between computational complexity and precision. Practical guidance for choosing these parameters is insufficient (Reviewer 4).
- **Robustness not assessed**: The paper does not include experiments to test whether the Kronecker factorized attention improves model robustness (Reviewer 1).
- **Suggested integration**: The proposed attention should be demonstrated in other architectures, such as Attention U‑Net [2], to show it is not limited to the Transformer (Reviewer 3).
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 6) is notably more positive about the model’s effectiveness and efficiency, while the other three reviewers raise significant concerns about novelty, evaluation validity, and missing analyses. This contrast should be kept.
