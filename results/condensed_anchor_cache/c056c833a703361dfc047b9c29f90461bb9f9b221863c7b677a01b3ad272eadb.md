## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes MKDT, the first effective dataset distillation method for self-supervised pre-training. It leverages knowledge distillation to reduce the high variance of SSL gradients, enabling stable trajectory matching. Synthetic datasets distilled with MKDT achieve up to 13% higher accuracy than prior work on downstream tasks with limited labeled data.

### Strengths
- First effective DD method for SSL pre-training, addressing an important underdeveloped area.
- Theoretical and empirical analysis (Theorem 4.1, Figure 1) convincingly demonstrates why naïve MTT fails due to high gradient variance, and motivates the KD-based solution. (Reviewers 1,3,4; Reviewer 4 found motivations reasonable and valid.)
- The approach is simple, clearly explained, and effectively transforms the unlabeled DD problem into a supervised KD loss. (Reviewer 4)
- Outstanding experimental performance on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny ImageNet, outperforming prior work by up to 13%. (Reviewer 2)
- Related work section is thorough and well-positions the work within the DD literature. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- Performance improvements over random subset and high-loss subset baselines are often marginal, especially on TinyImageNet (Table 3) where gains are <1%. This raises concerns about practical value given the computational cost of distillation. (Reviewers 1,4) However, Reviewer 2 highlighted strong gains on other datasets.
- Missing comparisons with KRR-ST in Tables 4,5,6,7, which is a closely related baseline, limiting assessment of MKDT’s relative advantage. (Reviewer 1)
- Theoretical analysis relies on a simplified linear model, which may not fully capture non-linear dynamics. The proof is described as trivial and the gap between theory and implementation large (Reviewer 2); Reviewer 3 found the motivation compelling. Statements such as “as confirmed theoretically above” may be overstated given the strict assumptions. (Reviewer 4)
- The role of the student model in reducing trajectory variance is insufficiently explained; it remains unclear why training a student to match teacher representations leads to lower variance. (Reviewer 1)
- Only Barlow Twins is used as the SSL objective; results may not generalize to other SSL methods (e.g., SimCLR, masked reconstruction). (Reviewers 3,4) No exploration of regularization techniques like Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) to address gradient variance was conducted. (Reviewer 3)
- Experiments are limited to small models (ConvNet, ResNet-18); scalability to larger and more modern architectures (e.g., ResNet-50+) is not demonstrated. (Reviewers 2,4) The generalization experiment uses ResNet-10/18, which are not larger. (Reviewer 2)
- Comparison to data pruning method SAS uses only 1% and 5% labeled data, while SAS is typically evaluated on larger subsets (e.g., 20%), making the comparison potentially unfair. (Reviewer 4) Why not present results for additional subset sizes? (Reviewer 4)
- No comparison to other DD paradigms (gradient matching, distribution matching) adapted to SSL; they are dismissed based on label dependency without empirical validation that trajectory matching+KD is superior. (Reviewer 3)
- Distilled images are not visualized, which would help demonstrate the method’s effectiveness. (Reviewer 1)
- Figure 1 captions lack concise takeaways explaining the significance of variance trends and justifying the KD approach. (Reviewer 3)
- Minor issues: citation format does not follow ICLR 2025 (Author, Year) requirements; typos (e.g., “synhronous” line 215, inconsistent pre-training/pretraining). (Reviewers 2,3) Line 75 inaccurately states ResNets are not ConvNets. (Reviewer 4)
