## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses communication overhead in minmax optimization by proposing Decoupled SGDA, where players perform local updates using outdated opponent strategies and periodically synchronize. Theoretical convergence and communication complexity results are given for strongly-convex-strongly-concave (SCSC) games and weakly coupled games, showing near-optimal rates. Experiments on quadratic minimax problems and toy GAN tasks support the theory.

### Strengths
- The problem of reducing communication complexity in decentralized minmax games is important and timely. (R2, R3)
- The proposed Decoupled SGDA is simple, intuitive, and practical for real-world use. (R1, R3)
- The paper provides solid theoretical convergence results in SCSC and N-player settings, demonstrating communication acceleration over standard methods. (R2, R3, R4)  Reviewer 3 specifically highlights the meaningful improvement in communication complexity.
- The introduction of "weakly coupled" games provides clear conditions under which Decoupled SGDA significantly reduces communication costs. (R1, R2)
- Experiments are compelling, corroborate the theory, and show extensions to federated learning and non-convex settings. (R3, R4) Reviewer 3 notes the experiments show interesting behavior beyond the theoretical analysis.
- The algorithm is versatile and can be adapted to other methods. (R2)
- The core research questions are well-formulated and motivated. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- The analysis is restricted to SCSC games; extension to more general convex or non-convex settings is not addressed and would strengthen the work. (R4)
- The theoretical proof techniques are considered standard and the technical contribution is weak by some reviewers. (R1: analysis seems standard; R4: weak technical contribution)  Reviewer 3 notes that a comparison of iteration complexity (not just communication complexity) with standard methods is missing.
- Notation is heavy and hard to interpret: many distinct constants (L_uv, L_vu, mu_u, mu_v) could be streamlined. (R2)  Additionally, condition numbers κ_u, κ_v are used before their definition (defined only at bottom of page 6), hindering readability. (R3)
- The conditions for communication acceleration in Table 1 are not intuitive; concrete examples (e.g., a specific class of functions or game setup) satisfying those conditions would make the results more tangible. (R2)
- The heuristic "Ghost Sequence" in Algorithm 4 (Appendix G) lacks rigorous justification; its consistent advantage over the basic Decoupled SGDA beyond empirical validation is unclear. (R4)  The specific update rule of the ghost sequence also needs clarification. (R4)
- Missing discussion: (i) behavior of Decoupled SGDA with decreasing stepsizes (R3); (ii) why only noise on self-gradients is bounded and what trade-off occurs if self-gradients are too noisy (slower convergence but unaffected communication complexity?) (R3)
- Minor issues: title should reflect "minmax optimization" rather than "games" (R1); several typos: Line 157 "do denote" → "to denote", Line 209-210 "intiualized" → "initialized" and typo in summation index, Line 390 "Decoupled GDA" should be "Decoupled SGDA", Line 402 "Decouped-SGDA" → "Decoupled SGDA", Figure 4 right figure labeled "Left" and reference to 5 may be incorrect (R3). The related work on federated learning feels misplaced given its limited use in the main paper; could be moved to appendix to focus on minmax optimization. (R3)
- The notation for the coupling function r(u,v) in line 287 is unclear; please clarify if it matches Eq. (5). (R4)
