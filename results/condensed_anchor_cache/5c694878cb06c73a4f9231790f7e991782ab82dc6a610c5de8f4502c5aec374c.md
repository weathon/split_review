## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Sequential Bayesian Meta-Continual Learning (SB-MCL), a framework that combines meta-learned neural networks with sequential Bayesian updates using exponential-family distributions. By restricting continual learning to a latent space via Bayesian updating, the approach avoids gradient computations during the inner loop. A neural network encoder maps inputs to distribution parameters for updating a latent posterior; a second network produces outputs conditioned on samples from that posterior. The encoder and model are meta-learned. The method is evaluated on multiple tasks (classification, regression, generation, image rotation prediction). Reviewers note both interesting innovations and significant limitations.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized and clearly written. Mathematical derivations are sound, and the variational lower bounds are derived carefully. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- The idea of leveraging Bayesian learning in continual learning is interesting and novel. The shift from weight-based memorization to latent-distribution memorization removes the need for gradient computation during CL. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- The use of the Fisher-Darmois-Koopman-Pitman theorem provides a theoretical grounding for why exponential-family distributions are attractive in memory-constrained continual learning. (Reviewer 2)
- The framework is tested on a wide range of tasks: classification, regression, generation, and image rotation prediction. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- The computational advantages are clear: examples can be processed in parallel, and no second-order gradients are needed. (Reviewer 2)
- Figures 1 and 2 nicely illustrate the overall framework. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Frozen meta-learned network limits plasticity.** Freezing the encoder and decoder prevents adaptation to new task distributions. The approach is analogous to using a pretrained model, which defies the core CL goal of adapting while retaining knowledge. Bayesian learning alone is insufficient for diverse tasks; evidence includes blurry VAE reconstructions. (Reviewer 1)
- **Motivation for the scenario is unclear.** It is difficult to picture a realistic deployment—especially for generative models—where training proceeds continually with only a single pass over data. (Reviewer 1)
- **Task diversity in evaluation is too narrow.** Meta-training tasks (e.g., NIST-style character recognition) are very similar to meta-test tasks and lie on low-dimensional manifolds. It is unclear whether performance gains stem from the method or from intrinsic task simplicity. More diverse, challenging tasks are needed to test expressiveness. (Reviewer 1; also echoed as a question by Reviewer 2 regarding missing scenarios)
- **The strong novelty claim is contested.** While Reviewers 1 and 2 find the combination innovative, Reviewer 3 notes that both Bayesian updating and exponential-family variational posteriors are well-established ideas, and the paper does not provide a clear theoretical justification for why *this specific combination* outperforms alternatives. The contribution is seen as incremental. (Reviewer 3, minority more negative)
- **Consequences of the exponential-family simplification are not addressed.** The paper acknowledges that exponential-family-only models are too simple, but does not analyze how the use of neural networks compensates for that restriction. The claim that the method “transforms CL into an underfitting problem” should be verified by varying network capacity and latent dimension; moreover, the “losslessness” of exponential-family sequential updates seems to contradict the idea that more parameters are needed as tasks increase. (Reviewer 2)
- **The forgetting problem is merely shifted to the meta level.** The method assumes that all meta-level non-stationarities are represented during meta-training. If the meta-test phase introduces new types of non-stationarity, the model cannot adapt without catastrophic forgetting at the meta level. (Reviewer 2)
- **Visual presentation of results is poor.** Figure 3 contains too many plots, some of which are not informative. Legends are hard to match to lines; some trend lines run outside the plotting frame. More tabular results in the main paper would be clearer. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Missing ablation and counterfactual experiments.** The method is not tested in all experimental settings (Reviewer 1 asks for diverse tasks; Reviewer 2 asks why the general model is not evaluated on every scenario). Additionally, the main results use factorized Gaussians; the effect of other exponential-family distributions on performance is not explored. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
