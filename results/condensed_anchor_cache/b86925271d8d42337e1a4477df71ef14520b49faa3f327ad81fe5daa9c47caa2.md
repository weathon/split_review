## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the twin TD-regularized actor-critic (TDR) method, which selects target Q-values based on the lower TD error (rather than directly minimizing across twin Q-networks) and penalizes the actor using those TD errors. TDR is combined with distributional learning and a modified N-step returns method called Long N-step Surrogate Stage reward (LNSS). The combined approach is evaluated on top of TD3, SAC, and D4PG in six environments from the DeepMind Control suite. The paper also provides theoretical derivations related to bias control.

### Strengths
- Addresses a core and relevant problem: balancing overestimation and underestimation in value-based deep reinforcement learning (R1, R4).
- The proposed TDR method is simple, easy to understand, and straightforward to implement (R1, R2, R4).
- The paper provides theoretical derivations and analysis (R2, R3).
- Experiments are run with a fair number of random seeds (10), and ablation studies show that each introduced component contributes positively to final performance (R4).
- Minority reviewer (R3) considers the approach a “new try” and acknowledges theoretical contributions, while other reviewers are more critical of novelty.

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient novelty and incremental contribution.** The method is a marginal combination of existing ideas: TD-regularized actor-critic from Parisi et al. (2019), distributional learning, and N-step returns, with a minor modification to the target selection rule (R1). The core TDR procedure lacks a clear theoretical justification for why choosing the target Q based on lower TD error is superior to standard minimization; it may simply trade sample-efficiency for stability through n-step returns and reduced pessimism (R4). Section 3.1 is placed under the method section but is only a literature summary, causing confusion (R1).

2. **Unreliable baseline performance.** Multiple reviewers note that the reported baselines (SAC, TD3) perform significantly worse than established results in the literature:
   - AcrobotSwingup: reported SAC ~4 at 1M steps vs. ~100 at 500k steps in the TD-MPC paper (R3).
   - Quadruped Walk: reported SAC ~196 at 1M steps vs. ~600 at 400k steps in the BAC paper (R3).
   - Reviewer 1 notes that “replicated baselines seem worse than original papers.” Reviewer 4 adds that baselines are not optimized for the DMControl suite nor for the sample‑efficiency setting (only 1M steps). Reviewer 2 questions whether hyperparameters match the original papers and whether performance would be worse than claimed. This undermines the reliability of the comparative results (R1, R2, R3, R4).

3. **Insufficient evaluation and overclaimed scope.** Experiments are limited to six easy/medium DMControl tasks, which is far too few to support the paper’s claims of “extensive experiments” and “SOTA across a wide variety of control tasks” (R3, R4). Reviewers request evaluation on the full DMControl suite (including humanoid stand/walk/run) and on MuJoCo tasks (R3, R4). The claim that D4PG is SOTA is arbitrary; other methods (BAC, RND, REDQ) achieve SOTA on various tasks (R3).

4. **Lack of direct bias reduction evidence.** The experiments only report average reward, not the actual reduction in overestimation or underestimation bias. The paper does not demonstrate that the proposed method reduces estimation error beyond improving overall performance (R2, R4). The claim that dual TD-learning (clipped double Q) “promotes underestimation early or with corrupted feedback” is unsupported (R4). Reviewer 1 questions how a sample-based TD error can serve as a sound measure for reducing estimation error and whether it leads to high variance. Reviewer 2 calls for additional experimental results showing approximate estimation error.

5. **Theoretical clarity and incorrect claims.** Section 4 is convoluted: Theorem 1 introduces “step random estimation bias ψ” but does not use ψ in the main statements, and the same quantity is re‑introduced identically in Theorem 2 (R4). The separation between assumptions and theoretical findings is unclear (R4). The claim that “the clipped double Q‑trick is designed to solve the overestimation caused by the max operator” is incorrect—the culprit is function approximation errors, not the max operator itself (R3). The statement “Our TD-regularized actor network directly penalizes the actor’s learning objective whenever there is a critic estimation error” is poorly worded; estimation errors are unavoidable in practice (R1).

6. **Ablation study issues.** The ablation includes LNSS, which is not part of the core TDR method, making it impossible to isolate the contributions of the TD‑regularized critic and actor separately (R2). The ablation should focus on these core components (R2). Subsection 3.4 only provides theoretical analysis for the advantage over prior TD‑regularized actor networks; experimental validation is missing (R2). LNSS is never explained in the main text and its description in the appendix is unnecessarily convoluted (R4).

7. **Missing comparisons to existing bias-control methods.** The paper does not compare to state‑of‑the‑art algorithms designed for over‑/underestimation, such as BAC (Ji et al., 2023), which also targets this problem and shows strong results across many benchmarks (R3). Reviewer 2 also requests comparisons to recent SOTA bias‑control methods. A direct experimental comparison (if code is available) would be needed to assess the relative effectiveness (R3).

8. **Experimental design issues.** The experiment section poses six distinct questions but provides only 1–2 pieces of supporting evidence repeatedly, forcing the reader to make indirect associations. Reviewers recommend paring down to 2–3 well‑founded questions and moving the rest to the appendix with additional experiments (R3). Table 1 and Figure 2 are redundant and should be consolidated or one moved to the appendix (R3). The paper contains a confusing statement about “the random initialization challenge caused by random seeds” that requires clarification (R2). The method uses the first Q‑network to compute the actor TD regularization term; it is unclear whether this choice negatively impacts the selection of target values (R2).

9. **Presentation and clarity.** Several typos remain (e.g., “overesdiation”, “measureed”) (R1, R4). Section 4 should be rewritten for conciseness (R4). The LNSS modification is simple but described in an unnecessarily convoluted manner in the appendix (R4).
