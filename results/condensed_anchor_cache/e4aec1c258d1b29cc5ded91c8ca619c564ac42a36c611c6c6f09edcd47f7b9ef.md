## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents Gymnasium, an open-source library providing a standard API for reinforcement learning environments, as a maintained fork of OpenAI Gym. It describes the API abstractions (Env class, FuncEnv functional API), the separation of termination and truncation in episode logic, support for various data spaces, vectorized environments (Sync/Async) for parallelization, and a suite of built-in environments from simple MDPs to MuJoCo simulations. The paper also discusses interoperability with other RL libraries. Reviewers acknowledge that Gymnasium is widely adopted and valuable to the community, but express concerns about the lack of originality, insufficient comparisons with competing libraries, minimal experimental breadth, and missing details on backward compatibility and community adoption.

### Strengths
- Gymnasium (and its predecessor Gym) is an essential and widely adopted part of RL research; many packages and works build on it. [R1, R3, R4]
- The API abstractions are simple, easy to use, and provide a consistent interface that streamlines research and enables comparison across algorithms. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
- The functional API (FuncEnv) aligns with POMDP theory and facilitates hardware acceleration (e.g., JAX). [R2, R4]
- Vectorized environments (Sync/Async) significantly improve throughput with low overhead, as shown in performance benchmarks (e.g., Cartpole, Lunar Lander). [R1, R2, R4]
- Gymnasium has an active developer community (7.1k stars, 450+ issues, 750+ PRs on GitHub). [R4]
- Introduction of explicit notions of episode termination vs truncation in the step() logic. [R4]
- More built-in environments than Gym, covering areas like offline RL and meta RL, including integration of older environments (MiniGrid, Procgen, Meta-World). [R4]
- The library supports a range of environments from basic tabular MDPs to complex MuJoCo simulations. [R2, R4]
- The paper is well-written and provides a comprehensive overview useful to newcomers. [R1, R3]

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of comparison with competing libraries**: The paper does not meaningfully compare Gymnasium to other RL libraries (e.g., Puffer, RLlib, Madrona, JAX-based environments) to help researchers choose which library to use. It should include benchmarks across the same environments for each library and explain why to use Gymnasium over alternatives. [R1, R4]
- **Limited original content**: The paper largely replicates existing documentation rather than presenting new knowledge. For the ICLR audience, who are already familiar with Gymnasium, the novel contribution is minimal. [R3] (Minority negative view: one reviewer (R3) considers the paper unsuitable for ICLR and suggests the JMLR Software Track instead.)
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation**: Benchmarks focus only on simple environments (Cartpole, Lunar Lander); more challenging, high-dimensional environments and additional results (e.g., more complicated environments, synchronous vs asynchronous ablations) are needed. [R1, R4]
- **Vectorization performance is hardware-dependent**: The choice between Sync and Async vectorization modes shows substantial variability depending on hardware, which could lead to inconsistencies. The paper does not provide tools or guidelines to help users choose the best mode for their system. [R2]
- **Missing discussion on massive parallelization and distributed training**: The paper does not address how Gymnasium handles massively parallel or distributed training environments, or integration with cloud/cluster frameworks. [R2]
- **Unclear novelty relative to prior work**: The improvements from OpenAI Gym to Gymnasium are not compared with contributions from other RL libraries (2015–present). Benefits from parallelization are attributed to external engines (JAX, Madrona) rather than the library itself. For example, Puffer has better vectorization. [R1]
- **Claim about removing barriers for safe AI is not defended** in the experiments. [R1]
- **Missing backward compatibility discussion**: Many projects still use Gym; the paper should include a section on code compatibility and how Gymnasium promotes further popularization. [R4]
- **Missing explanation for API changes**: The reimplementation of `.reset()` and deprecation of `.seed()` are not justified. [R4]
- **Incomplete related work**: Section 2.1 should include newer libraries such as d3rlpy (offline RL), TorchRL, and RLLTE; Dopamine is outdated. [R4]
- **Missing future work section**. [R4]
- **Lack of illustrative materials**: The paper would benefit from code examples showing differences from Gym, a chart illustrating the library's structure and new logic, and a table summarizing built-in environments with research targets (e.g., MiniGrid for exploration, Procgen for generalization). [R4]
- **Significant reviewer disagreement**: Reviewer confidence ranges 2–5. One reviewer (R1) rates contribution as low (2/100 under soundness) and soundness as 2/100, while another (R2,R4) rates contribution as 4. Reviewer 3 is intermediate but believes the venue is inappropriate. The lower-confidence reviewer (R3, confidence 2) also questions originality. [R1, R2, R3, R4]
