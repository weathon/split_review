## Merged Review

### Summary
This work proposes a memory-efficient method for backpropagation through linear layers using Randomized Matrix Multiplication (RMM). The method stores a random projection of the input activations onto a lower-dimensional space during the forward pass, reducing memory for the backward pass. The gradient estimator is unbiased. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of the added gradient variance and compare it with the variance from mini-batch SGD. Experiments are conducted on RoBERTa fine-tuning on GLUE tasks. The paper is well-written and easy to follow, but reviewers broadly agree on significant limitations in novelty, experimental scope, and comparisons, with Reviewer 2 being notably more positive.

### Strengths
- **Well-written and easy to follow** (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). The paper is well-organized and the literature review provides context (R2). The RMM algorithm is simple and well-motivated (R3).
- **Theoretical analysis of gradient variance** induced by the randomized matrix multiplication (R1, R2, R3). The comparison of variance with SGD is a key justification (R4).
- **Practical memory savings** are reported for fine-tuning tasks (R1). The method is shown useful on RoBERTa (R4).
- **Novel analysis** of variance in both SGD and RMM (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty and limited contribution over prior work** (R3, R4). The main idea of using random projection for activation compression is already present in Adelman et al. (2021) and EXACT (2022). The paper does not articulate clear differences. A detailed comparison with EXACT is missing, especially since the Appendix shows EXACT achieves higher accuracy on GCN2; the claimed advantage of compressing the batch dimension (instead of feature channels) is not mathematically or experimentally justified (R4). The paper brings few contributions over Adelman (R3). Reviewer 4 notes novelty concerns, while Reviewer 3 rates contribution as "poor".
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation** relative to the method’s claimed generality (R1, R3, R4). Experiments are limited to RoBERTa fine-tuning on GLUE; larger models (e.g., LLaMA-2-7B) and other architectures (convolutional networks, ViT on CIFAR-10/ImageNet) are not tested, raising questions about generalizability (R1, R4). Training is done on a pre-trained network, which masks the true impact of RMM on convergence and generalization; training from scratch is needed (R3). Section 3.4 adds nothing beyond Table 2 (R3).
- **Missing comparisons with existing memory-reduction methods** (R1, R3, R4). The paper lacks quantitative comparison to activation quantization (e.g., [2], [3]), GACT (2022), AAL (2023), DropIT (2023), and especially EXACT (2022) and GACT (2022). Without such comparisons, the practical value of RMM versus cheaper or faster alternatives cannot be assessed (R1, R4). Reviewer 4 requests at least a comparison with GACT on accuracy and memory savings.
- **Variance analysis is unclear or insufficient** (R3). Variables \(D_{SGD}\) and \(D_{RMM}\) are not clearly defined; notation in equations (10), (11) and Appendix equation (21) is inconsistent and the relationship between \(D_{SGD}\), \(D_{RMM}\), and \(D_Z\) is not rigorous. The conclusion of the variance analysis is unclear—empirical results show RMM variance is much higher than SGD, but the practical implications and trade-offs are not discussed. The theoretical upper bound is not plotted in Figure 3 (R3).
- **Computation cost overhead inadequately addressed** (R3). The comparison of computational costs \(O(BN^2)\) vs. \(O(\rho BN(B+N))\) is in the appendix and should be in the main paper. Table 3 shows the method can be slower even at \(\rho=0.1\), and the overhead of generating random matrices is not discussed (R3). Reviewer 3 questions why Gaussian RMM is chosen over computationally cheaper DCT/DFT.
- **Unsupported claim about regularization effect** (R2). The suggestion that the randomized gradient error acts as a regularizer (Section 2.3) is not modeled or justified in the paper.
- **Definitional issue with random matrix S** (R2). The paper states \(S\) requires autocorrelation \(I\), but the Gaussian matrix definition gives autocorrelation \(\frac{1}{B_{proj}}I\); these should be aligned.
- **Compression dimension choice not explored** (R1). It would be useful to know how compressing the batch dimension vs. feature channels affects the memory–performance trade-off.
- **Memory savings not fully accounted** (R1). In fully-connected layers, weights themselves have substantial memory; savings would be larger for convolutional architectures. The overhead of storing the random projection matrix and intermediate computations should be specified.
- **Missing analysis of multiple samples of \(S\)** (R2). The algorithm uses one sample of \(S\); averaging over multiple samples (as implied by Equation 4) could improve performance, but the trade-off with workload is not explored.
- **Missing analysis of network depth effect** (R2). How depth affects the variance and error from the randomized gradient is not addressed.
- **Minor errors and typos** (R1, R3). Example: In Figure 1, the RMM forward block should send \(W, b, X\) not \(W, b\) (R1). Several typos in Section 3 (R3).
