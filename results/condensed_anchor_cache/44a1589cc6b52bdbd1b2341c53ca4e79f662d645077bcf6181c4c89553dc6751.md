## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces D3IL, a simulation benchmark (MuJoCo Panda robot, five tabletop tasks) and metrics for imitation learning from human demonstrations, focusing on evaluating multi-modal behavior diversity. The metrics include behavior-level entropy (assuming uniform behavior distribution) and conditional entropy over initial states. Extensive baselines (MLPs, history-aware methods, diffusion policies, transformers) are evaluated with ablations on dataset size, history length, state vs. image inputs, and action horizon. The paper is timely and clearly written, but reviewers raise concerns about missing code/data, simplicity of environments, metric limitations, and lack of motivation for diversity.

### Strengths
- Timely work addressing the evaluation of imitation learning algorithms on diverse, multi-modal human data.
- Principled and intuitive diversity metric (behavior entropy) with clear justification and implementability.
- Comprehensive set of baseline algorithms covering recent important developments.
- Extensive ablation studies covering dataset size, visual/state-based models, history length, and action prediction horizon.
- Well-documented evaluation protocol and model selection criteria (rare in robot learning papers).
- Provides consistent insights: transformers outperform MLPs, historical inputs help transformer policies, transformers excel in low-data regimes.
- Tasks of varying difficulty; stacking-3 remains unsolved by any method, offering scope for future improvement.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing code and data**: The environment code and datasets are not provided in the supplementary materials. A benchmark paper is useless without them; code availability is essential for reproducibility and future use. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Simple environments**: All five tasks are simple tabletop block-moving tasks (MuJoCo Panda). A better benchmark would include diverse environment types (2D/3D, with different intractable elements) to increase algorithmic complexity requirements. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing runtime/control frequency evaluation**: Forward pass time or control frequency—a known disadvantage of diffusion-based models—is not evaluated. (Reviewer 1)
- **Action space ambiguity**: The action space used by the environments is not specified. (Reviewer 1) This affects reproducibility, as diffusion policies are known to behave differently under absolute vs. relative action spaces.
- **Limitation of discrete behavior enumeration**: The diversity metric requires a priori knowledge and enumeration of discrete behaviors, limiting applicability to tasks with continuous or highly variable behaviors. (Reviewer 2)
- **Unmotivated KL divergence**: The paper assumes a uniform behavior distribution in the demonstration data and uses KL divergence, which may not be appropriate for skewed datasets. A simpler behavior entropy measure would be more straightforward and avoid these issues. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of argument for the value of diversity**: The paper does not justify why multimodal policies are desirable. Deterministic policies can achieve high success rates with a single behavior, which might be preferred in many applications (e.g., factory automation). Success rate and behavior diversity need not be correlated. (Reviewer 2)
- **Conditional imitation learning not addressed**: Given that behaviors can be enumerated, one could condition the policy on a one-hot behavior identifier to achieve any behavior deterministically. The benchmark does not consider or justify why this approach is not used. (Reviewer 2)
- **Only one unsolved task**: All existing methods solve four of five tasks; only stacking-3 remains unsolved. This single variant may not provide a strong challenge for future benchmarks. Adding more tasks or mechanisms to include new tasks would strengthen the benchmark. (Reviewer 3)
- **Minority reviewer (Reviewer 2, score 6) is more critical** about the metric foundation and motivation, whereas Reviewers 1 and 3 (scores 8) find the work strong overall despite the above weaknesses.
