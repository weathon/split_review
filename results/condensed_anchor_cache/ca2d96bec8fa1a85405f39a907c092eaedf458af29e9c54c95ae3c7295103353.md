## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces GAFormer, a transformer architecture that uses a novel group embedding (GE) layer to learn spatiotemporal structure in multivariate time series. The GE layer assigns input tokens to a small number of group tokens separately for spatial (channels) and temporal dimensions. The authors claim state-of-the-art performance on several classification and regression tasks and improved interpretability. Reviewers agree the problem is important, the idea of group embeddings is novel and validated on synthetic and real data, and the experimental results are extensive. However, multiple reviewers raise concerns about missing architectural details, lack of rigorous interpretability, missing comparisons to important baselines (especially Nie et al. and other group-structure mechanisms like spot attention), and insufficient motivation or explanation of the group embedding concept. There is disagreement on interpretability: one reviewer praises the adaptive discernment and enhanced interpretability, while others find the interpretability claims subjective and unsubstantiated beyond qualitative examples.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written and motivated, with a detailed literature review and discussion of limitations. (R1, R2)
- The group embedding (GE) layer is a novel technique for learning spatial and temporal group structure in multivariate time series without relying on predefined structure or additional supervision. (R1, R4, R5)
- The proposed GAFormer achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple multivariate time series classification and regression tasks. (R1, R3, R4, R5)
- Synthetic experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the group embedding compared to baseline positional encodings. (R1, R3)
- When applied to other backbone architectures for univariate datasets, the group embedding enhances performance. (R3)
- The adaptive discernment of channel-wise and temporal groupings adds interpretability to the model's representations (claimed by R5, contested by others – see Weaknesses). (R5)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing architectural details**: The paper does not specify the number of transformer layers used (e.g., SGE and TGT modules), nor the exact specifications of the transformers. These details should be in the main paper. (R1)
- **Missing comparison to key baselines**: No comparison is made with the work of Nie et al., which is an important related transformer-based method for time series. (R1)
- **Missing comparison to other group-structure mechanisms**: The paper mentions other approaches (e.g., spot attention) but does not empirically compare the proposed GE to these alternatives. It is unclear whether GE provides unique advantages or if other techniques could achieve similar or better performance. (R3)
- **Methodology motivation and clarity**: The motivation for group embeddings is not well established. How are "groups" defined? Why is group embedding needed? The explanation of what the group embeddings stand for is vague. (R2) Additionally, the mechanism by which structure information is incorporated via group embedding is not clearly explained. (R2, question from R2)
- **Generalization ability not demonstrated**: The paper aims for generalizable representations but does not provide experiments that directly test generalization across datasets or domains. (R2)
- **Interpretability claim is subjective and not rigorously validated**: The interpretability claim (e.g., from Figure 4) is supported only by qualitative analysis, with no quantitative measure. The learned groups shown in Figure 4 are similar to groups formed by any attention mechanism; the paper does not provide additional explanations or evidence that the grouping yields meaningful interpretability beyond standard attention. (R3, R4) One reviewer explicitly disagrees with the interpretability claim, stating it "may not be right to claim interpretability based on group embedding structure." (R3)
- **Effect in low-data regime**: Since GE learns group structure in a data-dependent manner, it may not capture these properties effectively in low-data regimes compared to baseline positional encodings. (R3)
- **Computational complexity not discussed**: The method inherits quadratic complexity from the transformer, which could be prohibitive for large timesteps and multi-channel data, but this is not mentioned. (R3)
- **Order of SGE and TGE not justified**: The model applies Spatial Group Embedding (SGE) before Temporal Group Embedding (TGE). It is unclear why spatial modeling comes first and what would happen if the order were reversed. (R5)
- **Uneven effectiveness of SGE vs. TGE**: Table 4 shows that SGE is more effective than TGE, but the authors do not explain why. (R5)
- **Missing details on tokenization and hyperparameter K**: The paper does not explain how multivariate time series data is tokenized or how that differs from text tokenization. The selection of K (number of groups) is not discussed, nor is the effect of different K values. (R4)
- **Limited discussion of limitations**: The paper lacks a detailed discussion on how the group embedding technique might fail to capture complex non-linear relationships within the time series data and the impact of this on interpretability. (R4)
