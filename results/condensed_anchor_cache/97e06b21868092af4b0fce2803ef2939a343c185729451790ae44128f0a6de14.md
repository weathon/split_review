## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes MacDC for multi-target domain adaptive semantic segmentation, targeting both style and contextual gaps among multiple target domains. MacDC consists of Collaborative Domain Congregation (CDC) – image-level (style transfer) and region-level (data mixing) interpolation to create intermediate domains – and Multi-Context Masking Consistency (MCMC), a masking-based self-supervised consistency loss applied on the mixed domains. The method is evaluated on segmentation benchmarks, outperforming a few existing MTDA methods, and includes a strong baseline called NDA.

### Strengths
- Reviewer 1 (score 5) considers the claim of reducing domain-wise gaps via style and context interpolation important and reasonable, and finds the masking consistency a novel point for generalized semantic predictions across target domains.
- Reviewer 2 (score 3) acknowledges the paper is well written, contributions are clearly presented, the method outperforms existing methods in accuracy by a large margin, and the NDA baseline is beneficial for future work.
- Reviewer 3 (score 3) finds the exposition comprehensive, ablation studies thorough, experimental settings clear, and writing commendable.
- All reviewers agree the experimental evaluation is solid; Reviewer 1 notes the overall evaluation is solid though comparisons are insufficient, while Reviewers 2 and 3 praise the thorough ablation studies.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns (majority view):** Reviewers 2 and 3 (both score 3) argue the paper compiles existing techniques. CDC combines style transfer and data mixing methods well-studied in UDA (e.g., DACS [Tranheden et al. WACV 2021], DAFormer [Hoyer et al. CVPR 2022]), with no new module specific to MTDA. MCMC simply applies the MIC loss [Hoyer et al. CVPR 2023] from single-domain UDA to multiple target domains, treating all target domains (original + intermediate) as one domain, offering limited insight for multi-domain adaptation. Reviewer 1 (score 5) also notes the pipeline borrows masking consistency from MAE [2021], class mixing from Chen et al. [2022], and region-level interpolation from CowMix, FMix, CutMix, with rare new contribution, making novelty limited.
- **Insufficient baselines and comparisons:** Reviewer 1 states only four methods are compared with SOTA. Reviewer 2 suggests applying the method to other MTDA tasks (e.g., classification, detection) to broaden contribution. Reviewer 3 recommends including results from well-established UDA techniques applied to MTDA (e.g., HRDA [ECCV 2022], MIC [CVPR 2023]) for fair comparison.
- **Limited analysis of MCMC:** Reviewer 2 requests ablation studies to confirm whether performance improvement comes from better context utilization, and whether ablation on patch size and masking ratio for MCMC was performed (current settings may not be optimal for MTDA).
- **Architecture and task scope:** Reviewer 2 notes the method is only verified on ResNet101-based DeepLabV2, an old architecture. It should also be evaluated on normal-to-adverse adaptation settings (e.g., Night Driving, Dark Zurich, ACDC) to align with current domain adaptation benchmarks.
- **Potential triviality of lambda parameters:** Reviewer 1 observes all lambda parameters in Eq14 are set to 1 for any dataset, questioning whether the weighting terms are necessary.
- **Missing experiments/ablation requests (from Reviewer 2):** (1) Image-level domain interpolation uses only existing target domain styles; could DLOW [Gong et al. CVPR 2019] sample new styles between target domains and improve performance? (2) Does reported training time include the image-level style transfer process? (3) Can MacDC be applied to normal-to-adverse adaptation? 
- **Disagreement in overall assessment:** Reviewer 1 (score 5) rated the contribution positively, while Reviewers 2 and 3 (both score 3) expressed significant concerns about novelty and limited scope, resulting in lower scores. This contrast is reflected above.
