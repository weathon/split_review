## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes using Contrastive Predictive Coding (CPC), an unsupervised representation learning method, to automate feature engineering for financial time series forecasting. Embeddings are generated by maximizing similarity with subsequent time windows (positive samples) while separating from random noise (negative samples). These embeddings are fed into downstream models (e.g., linear regression, LSTM) to predict the next day’s log return on foreign exchange pairs (USDJPY, USDSGD, EURGBP). Performance is evaluated via Sharpe ratio relative to buy-and-hold. Results indicate that CPC-enhanced linear regression outperforms simple baselines.

### Strengths
- The description of the model architecture is clear (Reviewer 1).
- The paper tackles a pertinent research issue (automated feature generation for noisy financial data) and shows engagement with the subject (Reviewer 3).
- The application of CPC to the financial domain is novel and addresses the challenge of stochasticity in time series (Reviewers 2, 5). Some results are promising (Reviewer 5).
- The evaluation across multiple currency pairs (USDJPY, USDSGD, EURGBP) demonstrates an effort to test adaptability and generalizability (Reviewer 2). 
- The work attempts to use contrastive representation learning to automate feature engineering, which has potential practical implications (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
**Novelty and motivation**
- The paper offers minimal novelty: CPC was introduced in 2018 and is applied without significant modification; the only addition is custom negative sampling based on the Black-Scholes model, which does not sufficiently advance the state of the art (Reviewer 2). 
- The motivation is not convincing: the paper only states that CPC was never evaluated in finance, but does not argue why CPC is a good choice for tackling stochasticity or why it is preferable to other unsupervised representation learning methods (Reviewer 5). 
- The claim that the architecture can generate features automatically without domain expertise is not validated experimentally (Reviewer 3).

**Literature review and contextualization**
- The literature review is severely inadequate: only 11 references total, with minimal coverage of recent time series representation learning (e.g., TS2Vec, TS-TCC, TimeDRL) and forecasting (e.g., iTransformer, PatchTST, DLinear, Informer, TimesNet) (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). The authors fail to identify and bridge the gap with current research (Reviewer 3). 
- No comparison is made with state-of-the-art models or other self-supervised methods (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). The paper should include at least four SOTA models from reputable venues (ICLR, International Journal of Forecasting, etc.) (Reviewer 3).

**Experimental methodology and evaluation**
- The experimental scope is limited: only three currency pairs are tested; more assets are needed to establish statistical significance (Reviewer 1).
- Evaluation metrics are insufficient: only the Sharpe ratio is used, without point-wise measures (e.g., MSE, MAE) or distributional metrics (e.g., Wasserstein distance) (Reviewers 3, 4). Financial risk metrics such as max drawdown and Sortino ratio are missing (Reviewer 1). 
- Market friction assumptions (e.g., transaction costs, margin requirements) are not stated, and it is unclear whether transaction costs are included in the Sharpe ratio calculation (Reviewer 1). 
- The forecasting horizon is not clearly defined: is it single-day-ahead or multi-step? (Reviewer 3).
- The only experimental result table is weak (Table 1) (Reviewer 4).
- The claim that LSTMs struggle on the dataset is supported only by Figure 7 (in Appendix); no validation set for early stopping or hyperparameter optimization is used, which is fundamental for deep architectures (Reviewer 5).
- The paper finds that the lowest error rate is achieved by zero and mean models, but provides no insight—e.g., whether this suggests the forecasting methods are worse than trivial baselines or whether a mean-reverting property explains it (Reviewer 1).

**Ablation and analysis**
- No ablation study is conducted; critical components (e.g., varying contrastive architecture, different methods for positive/negative sampling, effect of CPC embeddings vs. raw features) are not investigated (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The learned embeddings are only evaluated with linear regression, limiting the impact; it is unclear why neural architectures (e.g., LSTM) cannot be used with the embeddings (Reviewer 5). 
- Figure 6 (cluster visualization) is not convincing: no clear visual separation, and no analysis of unique properties per cluster is provided (Reviewers 1, 5). 

**Data, preprocessing, and assumptions**
- It is unclear why only the closing price is used as a feature; stock prices are sensitive to external factors (news, events, economic indices), risking underfitting (Reviewer 3).
- Min-max data normalization is problematic for financial time series because extreme values can compress most of the data into a tiny range (Reviewer 4).
- The motivation for using random Gaussian noise as negative samples is not explained; the paper states negative samples should neither be too easy nor too hard, but does not justify why random noise is suitable (Reviewer 4).
- The paper appears to assume a short-only position; if so, margin requirements should be reported for practical relevance (Reviewer 1).

**Presentation and organization**
- The paper suffers from poor organization and formatting: important figures and tables are placed in the Appendix with insufficient explanation, while naive baselines and standard ML pipeline details take up ~3 pages in the main text (Reviewers 3, 5).
- The references do not adhere to ICLR format; there are missing citation details (e.g., citation 11 incomplete) and numerous typos (e.g., “normalise”→“normalize”, “maximise”→“maximize”) (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- The Related Work section is missing or insufficient; the Methodology section mixes experimental details (Reviewer 2). 
- Notation is inconsistent: e.g., x_t appears on both sides of Eq. 2, and notation differs between equations (Reviewer 5).
- The CPC-LR acronym is not defined (Reviewer 5). 
- Figure captions are too concise; several figures (e.g., Fig. 8) lack axis labels; text in Figures 3,5,7 is small (Reviewer 5). 
- The paper would benefit from more gentle introduction of domain-specific terms (e.g., allocations, buy-and-hold, return plots) (Reviewer 5).

**Reviewer disagreement note**: Two reviewers (1 and 5) gave a score of 3 and acknowledged some strengths (clear architecture, novel application, promising results), while the three other reviewers (2, 3, 4) gave a score of 1 and emphasized severe flaws in novelty, experimentation, and presentation. The majority view finds the paper well below the acceptance threshold.
