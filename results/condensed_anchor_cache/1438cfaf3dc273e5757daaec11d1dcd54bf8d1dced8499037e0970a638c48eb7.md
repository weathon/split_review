## Merged Review

### Summary
The authors propose HandsOnVLM, a VLM-based framework for reasoning about hand activities and predicting hand trajectories (curves) given videos and user language queries. They introduce two benchmarks: Vanilla Hand Prediction (VHP) and Reasoning-Based Hand Prediction (RBHP). The method encodes hand as an embedding, generates textual responses and future hand trajectories, and achieves state-of-the-art results on the proposed benchmarks. The authors promise to release the benchmarks to the community.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1, R3).
- The proposed tasks are interesting and play an important role in egocentric-related applications such as VR/AR and robotics (R1, R2).
- The idea of encoding the hand as an embedding is novel (R2).
- The method has useful applications in research and industry (R3).
- The authors promise to release two new, relevant benchmarks to the community (R3).
- The method is evaluated on multiple datasets and makes qualitatively sound predictions on unseen datasets (R3).
- The method outperforms most state-of-the-art architectures (R3).  
  (Reviewer disagreement: R1 finds it difficult to observe stable improvement from experiments, e.g., Table 1; R3 states outperformance of most SOTA.)

### Weaknesses
- **Definition of hand pose unclear.** The paper does not specify whether hand pose is based on joint positions, bounding boxes, or another representation. Qualitative results show two red and blue curves on images, which is ambiguous: do these curves represent a single point (e.g., wrist or centroid) or a more complex representation? Lack of precision hinders reproducibility and comparison (R1).
- **Insufficient dataset details.** Information about H2O and FPHA datasets is lacking, particularly regarding labels, hand pose representation, differences from Epic‑Kitchen, and annotation quality (R1).
- **Missing hand pose labels in Epic‑Kitchen.** The training set for VHP and RBHP (Appendix A.1) only uses Epic‑Kitchen, which (to the reviewer’s knowledge) does not contain hand pose labels. How were the hand pose labels obtained? (R1 question)
- **Experimental improvement unclear.** Table 1 does not show stable improvement (R1).
- **Limited baselines.** The method is compared only to naive baselines and traditional hand motion prediction methods. A missing baseline is using foundation models for video prediction and then tracking the motion as predictions (R2).
- **Ego‑motion handling.** Epic‑Kitchen contains extensive ego‑motion that makes hand motions change significantly but are largely unpredictable. How did the authors handle this? (R2 question)
- **<HAND> token decoding unclear.** How the <HAND> token is decoded to hand motions is not clearly explained (R2).
- **RBHP benchmark limited to kitchen scenes.** Numerical validation is performed only on kitchen settings (R3).
- **Missing static‑frame baseline.** Comparisons in Tabs. 1 and 2 are unfair because baselines lack the observation history (context) provided by video frames. A static‑frame version of the proposed architecture should be included to isolate the benefit of temporal modeling. The reliance on videos is a limitation; many settings do not have a history of frames (R3).  
  (Related question: did the authors test with different numbers of input frames, e.g., a single frame? How does the model handle a different number of tokens than used during training? – R3)
- **Prediction limited to curves.** The representation only predicts curves, lacking detailed hand articulation. A version involving hand poses would be useful for many settings, especially precise hand‑object interaction modeling (R3).
- **Typing errors.** “l_hand” in line 208 and “FPGA” in line 325 (R3).
