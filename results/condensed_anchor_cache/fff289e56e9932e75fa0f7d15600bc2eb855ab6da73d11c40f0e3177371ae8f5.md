## Merged Review

### Summary

Three reviewers assigned score 8, praising the paper's clear writing, sound evaluation, and the distance‑decay attention that makes infinite connectivity tractable. One reviewer (score 5) raised major concerns about novelty (similarity to PotNet), problematic derivation, unknown error bounds, and unfair comparison due to the use of stochastic weight averaging (SWA). All reviewers agreed the paper is easy to follow and the method shows strong experimental results on Materials Project and JARVIS‑DFT, but several requested additional clarifications, baselines, and datasets.

### Strengths

- Clear exposition; the paper is well‑written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 4).
- The distance‑decay attention naturally incorporates a physical inductive bias and makes the infinite attention computationally tractable (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Good experimental results with strong performance on multiple property regression tasks, outperforming GNN and transformer baselines (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Each component is validated through ablation studies, including positional encodings and Fourier‑space attention (Reviewers 2, 3).
- High efficiency: the model requires only 29.4% of the parameters of an existing transformer and shows competitive training/inference time (Reviewers 1, 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty and comparison to PotNet**: One reviewer (score 5) noted that the first attention bias (infinite interaction sum) is very similar to the idea in PotNet, which was published a year earlier. This similarity is not mentioned, and no comparison with PotNet is provided. The same reviewer also argued that the second attention bias (local radius feature summation) is not new.
- **Problematic derivation**: The derivation in the appendix (Equation S14) uses ambiguous wording (“may be”) and the coefficient in reciprocal space is not properly derived, which the one reviewer considers a major flaw (Reviewer 4).
- **Error bound unknown**: The paper repeatedly claims the method is “computationally tractable,” but the error bound of the summation \(\sum_n e^{-\|r\|^2/(2\sigma)}\) and the actual evaluation is not provided. The reader does not know the exact error between the approximation and the true infinite sum (Reviewer 4).
- **Fairness of comparison due to SWA**: The performance improvements are less significant without stochastic weight averaging (SWA, Izmailov et al. 2018). Without SWA the gains are modest, making the comparison potentially unfair if baselines did not use SWA (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited experimental scope**: The experiments only cover two datasets (Materials Project and JARVIS‑DFT) and five baselines. Additional datasets (OQMD, NOMAD) and more recent models (FAENet, Equiformer) should be considered to strengthen the claims (Reviewer 3). The reviewer also questioned why the Materials Project version used is significantly smaller than the latest release (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of discussion on symmetry**: The paper does not address how the model handles reflectional symmetry or other periodic transformations beyond translations. It is unclear whether the model is invariant or equivariant to these transformations (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient justification for \(\psi_{ij}(n)\)**: The necessity of the lattice‑vector attention bias \(\psi_{ij}(n)\) is argued only through a monatomic case (\(\mathcal{X} = \{x_1\}\)). It is not shown that the model would fail without this component in more general, multi‑element cases (Reviewer 2).
- **Clarity of figures**:
  - Figure 2 uses a matrix‑based representation that does not intuitively explain how infinite connectivity is achieved (Reviewer 2).
  - Figure 3 does not clearly show how \(X^t\) interacts with \(\mathcal{P}\) and the lattice vectors \(l_1, l_2, l_3\); the processing is hidden in formulas (Reviewer 2).
  - A concrete example with input atom types and positions would help readers see how data is processed (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing derivation step**: The rewriting of Eq. (3) as described (“With simple algebra …”) should be shown in an appendix (Reviewer 2).
- **Choice of decay function**: The paper uses only a Gaussian decay; the authors could discuss or try other decay functions and explain why (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation of results**: The results are only in tables; a graphical presentation would make them easier to interpret (Reviewer 1).
