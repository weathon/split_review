## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes WorldLM, a worldwide federated language model training system based on a “federation of federations” architecture. It uses partial model localization (attention-based aggregation for key layers, simple averaging for backbone) and cross-federation information sharing via residual layer embeddings to handle statistical heterogeneity. Evaluations on multilingual and domain-specific datasets (model sizes 75M-400M) show WorldLM outperforms standard FL, approaches local model performance, and maintains advantages under differential privacy. Reviewers agree the problem is practically important and the attention-based aggregation is an interesting direction, but disagree sharply on the strength of the technical justification, evaluation methodology, and missing comparisons. Two reviewers (scores 5) find the approach promising and robust; three reviewers (scores 3) consider the paper’s motivation, algorithm design, experimental results, and scalability arguments unconvincing.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important practical problem: training LMs across organizations with heterogeneous data and legal/privacy constraints (Reviewers 1,4,5).
- Attention-based aggregation and residual embedding mechanisms are suitable for handling data heterogeneity in language model training (Reviewer 2).
- Federations-of-federations architecture enables adaptable collaboration across jurisdictions while respecting privacy constraints (Reviewer 5).
- Extensive experiments across multiple model sizes (75M, 400M) covering perplexity, LLM benchmarks, and differential privacy settings (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- Robust performance under differential privacy, outperforming standard FL in these settings (Reviewers 4,5).
- Backbone with personalized key layers effectively captures local variations (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
1. **Unclear Motivation and Justification**  
   - The introduction covers technical and policy aspects but lacks a clear technical foundation; the transition from the challenge to the “federation of federations” solution is not fully justified (Reviewer 4).  
   - The assumption that children under the root have much more critical data heterogeneity than those under the parent is strong and unjustified; example: heterogeneity between a local orthopedics hospital and a local ophthalmology hospital may be larger than between two orthopedics hospitals in different cities (Reviewer 1).  
   - The paper’s practical application is motivated only at a high level; concrete problem scenarios are missing (Reviewer 3).  
   - The term “Worldwide” seems unnecessarily broad for a technical ML paper (Reviewer 4).  
   - The relationship to multi-task learning techniques (e.g., Cross-stitch Networks, MTAN) that also aim to update parameters from different tasks without interference is not discussed (Reviewer 4).  
   - The specific legal and privacy frameworks considered in the evaluation are not clearly stated (Reviewer 5).

2. **Unclear Algorithm Design and Presentation**  
   - Algorithm presentation needs improvement: residual layers are not explained; more information on model design is required (Reviewer 1).  
   - Figure 1 should explicitly explain the meaning of different notations (Reviewer 1).  
   - The mathematical presentation of hierarchical data distributions (Section 3.1) adds complexity without clear benefit; terms like “LDA” with values 0.0001 and 1000 are unclear (is it Latent Dirichlet Allocation?); concrete examples of how the formal setup maps to real-world scenarios are needed (Reviewer 4).  
   - Critical design choices lack justification:  
        * Why is simple averaging sufficient for the backbone while key layers require attention-based aggregation? (Reviewer 4)  
        * Why was the specific proportion of backbone vs. key layers chosen (the 30% transfer learning explanation is not convincing)? (Reviewer 4)  
        * What are the “meta-learning properties of FL” that motivate these choices? (Reviewer 4)  
        * Why is sequential training across levels (root → regional → edge) necessary rather than parallel training? (Reviewer 4)  
        * Why was this particular attention mechanism chosen, and do the results (Figure 3 spikes) suggest it does not resolve interference in weight updates? (Reviewer 4)  
   - The technique novelty is not clearly stated compared to existing PFL, client clustering, and hierarchical FL systems; a summary table or paragraph explicitly contrasting key features (e.g., attention aggregation, residual embeddings) would help (Reviewer 2).

3. **Experimental Concerns and Missing Comparisons**  
   - The “local” baseline under “Non-collaborate” performs much better than WorldLM in Tables 1 and 2; if “local” means training each client’s model exclusively on its own data, this raises fundamental questions about WorldLM’s effectiveness (Reviewer 1).  
   - The claim that 400M WorldLM can outperform Centralized training is confusing; if due to personalization, the paper should compare with more personalization-based methods (e.g., LoRA adapters per client) (Reviewer 3).  
   - The statement “For the 400M, we only had resources for comparison against centralized, as standard FL is much less computationally efficient” is unclear—simulating FL is typically cheaper than centralized training (Reviewer 3).  
   - Figure 3 shows large perplexity spikes between levels, suggesting potential instability; the implications are not discussed (Reviewer 4).  
   - Table 3 / Figure 4 suggest the method is not robust to statistical heterogeneity: restructuring the federation to combine non-heterogeneous data results in almost 2× increase in perplexity. More detailed robustness experiments (varying X% of swapping WK and PBA from 0 to 100) are needed (Reviewer 4).  
   - It is unclear if perplexity is measured on consistent validation sets across levels (Reviewer 4).  
   - The high perplexity values (in the hundreds) and large fluctuations seem too high for a modern LM; context or comparison to models of similar size is required (Reviewer 4).  
   - Ablations for robustness to DP are lacking; it is unclear which aspect (hierarchical modeling, algorithm details, attention aggregation) drives the DP robustness (Reviewer 4).  
   - MosaicML gauntlet results are presented as percentage improvements without raw scores, making it difficult to assess significance given benchmark variance (Reviewer 4).  
   - Communication costs are neither discussed nor experimentally tested; quantitative comparisons between WorldLM and other FL approaches, and how costs scale with model size and number of participants, are missing (Reviewer 2).  
   - The method shows diminished effectiveness when data within a federation lacks inherent similarity (Reviewer 5).

4. **Scalability and Practical Limitations**  
   - Scaling to billion-parameter LMs is not addressed: potential challenges in communication, memory, convergence behavior, and how the attention aggregation and residual embeddings behave at larger scales need discussion (Reviewers 2,4).  
   - The method may be resource-intensive for smaller organizations with limited computational resources (Reviewer 5).  
   - Sequential training across levels introduces practical deployment challenges that are not thoroughly discussed; stability during level transitions remains unanswered (Reviewer 4).  
   - It is unclear how WorldLM handles emerging data distributions in dynamic environments (Reviewer 5).
