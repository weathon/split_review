## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces SCAN, a dynamic bootstrapping dataset pruning method for contrastive pre-training (CLIP and MoCo). SCAN periodically prunes training samples based on loss thresholds—removing both very high-loss (ill-matched) and very low-loss (redundant) samples—and updates the pruned set via dataset mutation operations. Experiments on CC3M+CC12M+SBU+COCO for CLIP and ImageNet for MoCo show that at a 30–35% pruning rate, SCAN yields less than 1% average performance drop across downstream tasks and outperforms static coreset methods (SemDeDup, D-Pruning, Info-Batch). The method is also shown to produce coresets that generalize better than static selection.

### Strengths
- The problem of data efficiency in contrastive pre-training is important and has broad applicability.
- The proposed method is simple, straightforward, and experimentally effective.
- SCAN’s dynamic pruning approach is an advancement over traditional static pruning techniques.
- Effectiveness is validated through extensive experiments across architectures (ResNet, ViT, Swin), datasets, pruning ratios, and ablation studies (e.g., mutation epochs, pruning variants).
- The paper is well-structured and easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
1. **Data scale and generalizability concerns.** The CLIP experiments achieve only ~26% ImageNet zero-shot accuracy, far from the 70+% reported on larger datasets, making the method’s effectiveness on larger-scale data unclear. The method has multiple heuristic parameters (pruning ratio thresholds, mutation epochs) that are likely dependent on dataset, model, batch size, and loss type, raising doubts about generalizability. (Reviewers 2, 4)
2. **Missing important baselines.** The paper does not compare against standard CLIP data filtering (CLIP-score) or recent advanced filtering methods: Data Filtering Networks (NeurIPS 23 workshops), finetuned multimodal LMs (arXiv 24), and CLIPLoss/Norm-based selection (arXiv 2024). (Reviewer 4)
3. **Overclaim of novelty.** The statement “first to comprehensively study the data efficiency problem within the context of contrastive pre-training” (Line 100) is an overclaim given numerous existing data pruning/filtering works. (Reviewer 4)
4. **Performance gains over baselines are small.** In Table 2, the gap between SCAN and Info-Batch is only ~0.5%; in Table 3, SCAN and Info-Batch have similar ImageNet zero-shot performance. (Reviewer 4)
5. **Writing and clarity issues.** Experimental settings missing for some tables (e.g., Table 6: which task and model?). It is unclear how total training steps are determined across settings. The computational overhead of pruning operations (e.g., time/loss computation per step) is not quantified. Also, explain why Info-Batch collapsed on ViT-B/32 (Table 1) and Swin-Base (Table 3). (Reviewer 2)
6. **Ambiguity regarding static vs. dynamic comparison.** In Table 4, SCAN (static) combined with a model ensemble (RN50 and ViT-S/16) shows competitive results; it is unclear whether the static pruning itself or the ensemble effect drives performance. A single-model static pruning baseline is needed. Moreover, SCAN (static) produces comparable results to the dynamic method, e.g., on RN50 – does this suggest static pruning is simpler and nearly as good? Table 7 further shows ill-matched pruning (which is essentially static) matters most, consistent with static superiority. (Reviewer 2)
7. **Pruning ratio ablation limited.** Experiments only go up to 35% pruning; the relationship in Figure 3 may not be linear at higher ratios (e.g., 70–90%). The method’s performance likely relies on careful tuning of pruning ratio across datasets. (Reviewers 2, 3)
8. **Mutation epochs not sufficiently interpreted.** Ablation on mutation epochs shows that more frequent updates do not always give better results – why? Provide interpretation. (Reviewer 2)
9. **Fine-grained ill-matched vs. redundant ratio ablation needed.** Table 7 indicates ill-matched pruning is more important than redundant pruning, yet the final method uses an even 1:1 ratio based on Table 8. A finer-grained ablation across ratios (e.g., 75%/25%) is missing. (Reviewer 2)
10. **Missing baselines in Tables 7 and 8.** Results without data pruning (full dataset) should be included in Tables 7 and 8 to better contextualize performance. (Reviewer 3)
