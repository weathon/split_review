## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a self-supervised framework (NICE) for EEG-to-image decoding using contrastive learning between a frozen pretrained image encoder (CLIP/ViT/ResNet) and a trained EEG encoder (TSConv, optionally with spatial attention). Evaluated on the RSVP dataset (Gifford et al., 2022) in a 200-way zero-shot retrieval task, the method achieves state-of-the-art results (top-1 15.6%, top-5 42.8%). Extensive analyses explore temporal (100–600 ms optimal), spectral (delta, theta, beta bands informative), spatial (attention maps), and semantic (category-level similarity) aspects of the EEG signals. The approach is clearly described, and the experiments provide insights into the underlying brain processes.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, the method is straightforward to understand, and the figures are helpful.
- Originality: applying a frozen image encoder + trained brain encoder contrastive approach to EEG is novel (previously used in fMRI).
- The study goes beyond existing EEG image decoding by considering a large set of concepts (200-way zero-shot) and provides complementary analyses of temporal, spatial, spectral, and semantic patterns, supporting biological plausibility.
- Results are clearly presented, with insights into intra- vs. cross-subject testing, architecture choices, and ablation of attention modules.
- The authors are aware of prior work and provide a clear summary of related EEG-based object recognition.
- One reviewer noted clarity and good diagrams as a strength.

### Weaknesses
- **Notation and missing details**: The graph attention notation (aT meaning?) is confusing; it should be clarified whether this is a unique or established method (with references). GradCAM analysis lacks specification of which layer and gradient target. Font sizes in Figures 3b and 4 are too small (R1).
- **Missing concurrent work**: A very recent work (arxiv 2310.19812) should be briefly mentioned as concurrent (R1).
- **Unsupported secondary claims**: The claim about gamma-band decoding (high-frequency responses from temporal cortex) is not supported by evidence (chance-level gamma performance, unclear measurement). The claim that graph attention uses occipital information is contradicted by topomaps showing occipital electrodes mostly ignored; average attention weights should be analyzed instead of GradCAM (R2).
- **Scope not extended to generation**: Logical continuation of contrastive pipeline is to feed predicted latents to a generative model (Ozcelik & VanRullen 2023, Palazzo et al. 2020). The authors should discuss this as a limitation or future direction (R2).
- **Confusing analysis description**: Section A2 compares frozen pretrained vs. randomly initialized trained image encoders, but the description conflates frozen/unfrozen with pretrained/non-pretrained dimensions (R2).
- **TSConv similarity to ShallowNet**: TSConv is highly similar to ShallowNet (Schirrmeister et al., 2017); the performance difference is not explained—could be due to hyperparameters or optimization rather than architectural change. Detailed ablation of components and training parameters is needed (R3).
- **Attention modules lack quantitative spatial analysis**: The claim that self-attention and graph attention “preserve spatial characteristics” is not validated; no analysis of how they affect channel correlations or how much the residual connection influences spatial relationships (R3).
- **Graph attention novelty**: The mechanism is similar to Li et al. (2023); the authors should explain differences, whether the graph structure is prior or learned, and compare computational complexity (R3).
- **Self-supervised claim undermined by class bias**: Image embeddings from pretrained models (ResNet, CLIP, ViT) may contain class-related information, challenging the claim that learning is free from class bias. This potential leakage should be discussed (R3).
- **Typos and vague phrasing**: “We device” (p2), “has remained a challenging.” (p2), “as the widely use way” (p3), “anaylasis” (p8), “categoring” (p8). Table 1 uses s_1 but should probably be s_2. The phrase “we look for a few images that belong to the image concepts” is vague (R3).
- **Missing experimental details**: Number of trainable parameters for each EEG encoder and dimensionality of EEG features before the mapping layer are not reported, affecting interpretability of architecture comparisons (R3).
- **Figure 3 insufficiently explained**: It is unclear which dimension corresponds to EEG vs. image features; similarity between training and test concept visual features should be shown (R3).
- **Figure 2D insufficiently explained**: Time-frequency map method, depicted quantity, and range [-1,0] are not described (R3).
- **Motivation issues** (minority reviewer, score 3): 
  - Self-supervised learning is a common method in decoding tasks (e.g., EEG2speech, EEG2Image); comparing with supervised classification on traditional tasks (emotion, motor imagery) is an unreasonable motivation.
  - Many existing spatial-temporal methods (e.g., TSception, spatial-temporal graph/attention) are not mentioned, weakening the claim of novelty.
- **Insufficient experimental comparisons** (minority reviewer):
  - Only one baseline (BraVL) is included; many other EEG vision decoding works exist (e.g., Mind-Vis in CVPR). The dataset is not commonly used, so superiority is not well demonstrated.
  - No analysis of why NICE achieves better results than BraVL.
  - Encoder comparison baseline set does not include recent EEG feature extraction methods from the past five years.
- **Unaddressed problems from introduction** (minority reviewer): The four problems listed (e.g., overlooking inferior temporal cortex, focusing on 110 ms peak) are not all addressed; signals from inferior temporal cortex are not accessible via EEG, and the temporal focus is not biologically interpreted.
- **Minor reviewer disagreements**: One reviewer (score 3) finds the motivation weak and experiments insufficient, contrasting with the other three (score 8) who find the work original, well-conducted, and significant.
