## Merged Review

### Summary
The authors propose Hybrid Memory, a replay-based class incremental learning (CIL) method that combines real exemplars with synthetic data obtained via a novel data distillation variant called Continual Data Distillation (CDD). CDD generates synthetic exemplars from a sliding window of checkpoints along a single training trajectory, rather than requiring multiple trajectories. The real exemplars are then selected conditionally via a greedy algorithm to complement the synthetic set. The approach is designed to be integrated into existing replay-based CIL methods (e.g., iCaRL, BEEF, FOSTER). Experiments on CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet show improvements over replay-based baselines. Reviewers find the motivation clear and the hybrid idea interesting, but raise concerns about limited novelty, theoretical grounding, missing benchmarks, and insufficient analysis of parameters and overhead.

### Strengths
- The motivation is strong, clear, and easy to follow: addressing catastrophic forgetting in CIL by optimizing a hybrid memory of real and distilled data to overcome the diminishing effectiveness of synthetic-only replay as buffer size grows.
- The proposed Hybrid Memory can be integrated seamlessly with existing replay-based CIL methods (iCaRL, BEEF, FOSTER), making it a versatile addition.
- The paper includes theoretical discussions, ablation studies on the update strategy, investigations into the synthetic/real exemplar ratio (Fig. 4), and other relevant analyses that reinforce the validity of the work.
- Code availability is appreciated.
- **Minority positive view** (Reviewer 2, score 5): The work presents a practical solution that optimizes memory use while maintaining performance, with several supporting analyses.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty is limited.** The approach is rather straightforward: first construct synthetic samples (without considering real samples), then fix them and select real samples via greedy optimization. The sliding-window modification of data distillation is described as innovative but seems a trivial adaptation. The overall method relies heavily on existing replay-based CIL with minimal breakthrough.
- **Theoretical grounding is weak.** Assumptions 1 and 2 (underpinning Theorem 1) are not proven, and no evidence is given that they hold. Consequently, the validity of Theorem 1 is questionable; calling it a theorem may be inappropriate. There is no proof that the method generalizes beyond the specific setting—the claim that multiple training trajectories are unnecessary because replay starts from θN (rather than random initialization) is not supported; multiple trajectories are typically used to improve robustness and reduce overfitting.
- **Benchmarking is insufficient.** Only CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet are used; evaluating on more diverse datasets (e.g., Caltech256, MIT67) would enhance relevance. Importantly, recent CIL works (including FOSTER and BEEF that the authors compare to) also test on ImageNet-1k, which is missing here.
- **Numerical results for baselines are inconsistent.** The reported results for FOSTER and BEEF (including BEEF-Compress) do not match those in the original papers, where the original numbers appear better. This discrepancy requires clarification.
- **Missing standard upper and lower bounds.** The paper should report standard CIL bounds: joint training (all tasks, upper) and fine-tuning without anti-forgetting (lower). Also, standard deviations should be provided for main results (at least three runs).
- **Analysis of the synthetic-to-real ratio is incomplete.** The hybrid memory uses equal numbers of real and synthetic exemplars without justification. The ratio is a critical parameter; Fig. 4 only examines it for iCaRL on a specific setting. How does this ratio affect performance for other methods, more recent IL techniques, or datasets with larger distribution shifts? Imbalance could lead to overfitting to synthetic data. Also, it is unclear whether existing memory update techniques (e.g., Herding, or the approach in Algo. 2 of Buzzega et al.) could make the hybrid memory effective without the conditional real-data selection—this comparison/ablation is missing.
- **Missing overhead analysis.** The paper focuses on hybrid exemplars but does not analyze the computational and memory overhead of the hybrid memory structure.
- **Weak explanation for synthetic data's diminishing effectiveness.** The statement "as buffer size increases, synthetic exemplars become less effective than real exemplars, likely due to inherent important information loss in the distillation process" is not well justified. Data distillation aims to preserve important information; as synthetic buffer grows, loss should decrease. This claim may be simulation-specific and is not supported by other publications. The explanation appears inconsistent.
- **Optimization approach is not justified as most efficient.** The method uses (2)/(3) and (4) to solve (1), but it is unclear whether this is the most efficient or effective way. Greedy selection is used for (4); alternative methods are not explored.
- **Presentation issues.** The computation of AIA and LAA is not explained in the text or tables; a brief description should be included. Reviewer 2 also noted need for clarifying how base competitor results (upper part of Table 1) were obtained—whether original replay mechanisms (e.g., iCaRL with Herding) were used unchanged, and how the hybrid memory addition modifies these methods.
