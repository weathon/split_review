## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Sharpness-aware Geometric Defense (SaGD) framework for robust out-of-distribution (OOD) detection under adversarial attacks. SaGD uses Multi-Geometry Projection (MGP) and Riemannian Sharpness-aware Minimization (RSAM) together with Jitter-based perturbation to smooth the adversarial loss landscape. The authors argue that adversarial in-distribution (ID) samples should be classified as ID, not OOD. Experimental results on CIFAR-100 vs. six OOD datasets under various attacks show improvements in FPR95 and AUC. Reviewers are split: two reviewers (scores 3 and 3) raise fundamental concerns about the research setting, novelty, and practical relevance; one reviewer (score 6) finds the approach novel and effective, noting state-of-the-art results and thorough ablation experiments.

### Strengths
- Novel research direction: investigating OOD detection under adversarial attacks, a scenario with limited prior attention (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Comprehensive experimental evaluation across multiple adversarial attacks and OOD datasets, demonstrating effectiveness (All reviewers).
- SaGD achieves state-of-the-art performance on FPR95 and AUC metrics, both in clean and attacked settings (Reviewer 3).
- Introduction of Jitter-based perturbation in adversarial training to extend defense against unseen attacks (Reviewer 2).
- Employment of Multi-Geometry Projection (MGP) and Riemannian Sharpness-aware Minimization (RSAM) for OOD detection (Reviewer 2).
- Ablation experiments analyze the relationship between sharp loss landscape minimization and OOD detection performance under various adversarial conditions (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Research setting questioned:** Two reviewers (1 and 2) argue that adversarial examples should be treated as OOD, not ID, because they deviate from the natural data distribution and treating them as ID increases vulnerability to attacks. One reviewer (3) does not raise this concern and accepts the setting.
- **Limited novelty:** Two reviewers (1 and 2) find the contribution modest. Reviewer 1 notes most components in Section 3 are existing methods and the smoother regularizer is insufficient. Reviewer 2 states the method is a fusion of MMEL [1] and RSAM [4] (MGP and RSAM), with no novel insight. Reviewer 3, however, considers the sharpness-aware method novel.
- **Unclear motivations:** The rationale for using MGP, RSAM, and Jitter-based perturbation is not clearly motivated (Reviewer 2).
- **Figure 2 appears to be adapted from Figure 1 in [1]** without sufficient originality (Reviewer 2).
- **Significance of sharp loss landscape is self-evident** and already explored in existing literature [2,3]; no novel contribution regarding this concept (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of practical relevance:** Reviewer 1 questions real-world applications where OOD detection must handle adversarially attacked images. Reviewer 2 also implies the setting is not practically beneficial.
- **Missing analysis of computational complexity** of the OOD score, and how the number of ID training samples affects performance (Reviewer 3).
- **No practical procedure for choosing the threshold λ** for the OOD score, which is challenging in real applications (Reviewer 3).
- **Need for evaluation against adaptive attacks** specifically designed to exploit the OOD scoring mechanism, as per recommendations in [1] Tramer et al. (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing description:** Whether hyperspherical geometry learning is a type of adversarial defense (Reviewer 2, question).
- **Missing justification for using specific attack methods** for adversarial training; why not use other attacks? (Reviewer 2)
- **How to obtain the class prototype μ_k?** (Reviewer 2) — this detail is missing from the paper.
