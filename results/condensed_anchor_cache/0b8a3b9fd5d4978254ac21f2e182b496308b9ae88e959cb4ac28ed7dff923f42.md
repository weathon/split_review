## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes the Hyperbolic Hypergraph GNN (H2GNN), which uses a hyper-star message passing scheme to capture higher-order relations in knowledge hypergraphs while operating in fully hyperbolic space. The method is intended to avoid information loss from transforming hyperedges into binary relations or ignoring hyperedge adjacencies. Evaluations on node classification and link prediction show improvements over several baselines.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized and provides a comprehensive literature review.
- The hypergraph-specific message passing scheme (hyper-star) is original and intuitively reasonable for capturing hyperedge semantics.
- The formulation allows a flexible encoder/decoder architecture, which is highlighted in comparative evaluations.
- Extensive experiments are conducted on node classification and link prediction tasks, comparing against state-of-the-art baselines on multiple datasets.

### Weaknesses
- The paper lacks a clear, step-by-step explanation of how the model transforms a hypergraph into node embeddings (e.g., when linear transformations in hyperbolic space are used, how the diagram in Figure 3 maps to the algorithm).
- The proposed approach is not highly innovative; the core novelty lies in combining star extensions with hyperbolic transformations, and the necessity of this combination is not logically justified. Hypergraphs already have hierarchical structure, so learning directly in hyperbolic space might achieve low distortion without star extensions.
- The theoretical depth of the experimental analysis is insufficient. The paper claims “significantly outperforms” but many results are within one standard deviation of UniSAGE (e.g., node classification), and in link prediction HypE is a close contender (outperforming in some cases). The claimed accuracy range of 89.75%–74.89% for baselines in Table 2 is incorrect based on the table numbers.
- Key details are missing: hyperbolic space explanation is sparse (e.g., squared Lorentzian norm not defined); how encodings are converted to conditional probabilities (Equation 6) or confidence scores (Equation 7) is not explained; the position-specific embedding \(\mathbf h_p\) is ambiguously defined (stated as \(\in \mathbb R^d\) but subtracted from a hyperbolic vector, and its dependency on relation and position is not clear from the notation).
- The ablation study in Section 4.4 introduces terms “Hyperbolic Operation (HO)” and “Position Information (PI)” without defining them earlier, making the ablation hard to interpret.
- The paper lacks comparison against strong baselines for n-ary relational data, specifically [1] (Searching to sparsify tensor decomposition for n-ary relational data) and [2] (Role-aware modeling for n-ary relational knowledge bases).
- The poor performance of UniGNN and UniSAGE when paired with m-DistMult in Figure 4 is not explained — it is unclear whether the decoder was trained jointly or applied to frozen encoders, and why performance drops so dramatically.
- Several presentation issues reduce clarity: “Additional criteria are needed to complete this statement” about the goal of representation learning (page 2); a confusing sentence about tree embeddings (page 3); notation \(\{x_j\}_{j\in e}\) where \(j\) is not in \(e\) (pages 4–5).
- The paper does not include a visualization of star-expanded hypergraphs in hyperbolic space, which could strengthen the argument about reduced distortion.
- The experimental analysis does not thoroughly explore why H2GNN outperforms other hypergraph learning methods, beyond reporting results.
