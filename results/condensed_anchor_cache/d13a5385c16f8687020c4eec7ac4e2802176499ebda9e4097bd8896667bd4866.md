## Merged Review

### Summary

Two reviewers assigned a score of 3 and one reviewer assigned a score of 6, indicating a significant split in evaluation. The paper proposes AutoTrunc, an automatic method for selecting layer-wise truncation positions in SVD-based LLM compression. It models layer importance, applies a 0-1 Knapsack formulation, and reports perplexity improvements (24.65% on LLaMA-2-7B, 38.63% on LLaMA-2-13B at 50% compression). The positive reviewer highlights the adaptive per-layer truncation and theoretical grounding. The critical reviewers raise major concerns about limited model diversity, poor presentation clarity, overclaimed efficiency, missing comparisons (quantization, generation tasks, pruning fairness, data distribution effects), and insufficient ablation (e.g., effect of removing the lower bound, performance drop at high compression).

### Strengths

- **Good research topic**: SVD for LLM compression is timely and relevant.
- **Adaptive and importance-aware truncation selection**: AutoTrunc learns layer importance (using LambdaRank for listwise ranking) and allocates compression budgets accordingly, avoiding uniform low-rank assignment (as in SVD-LLM). This is a clear step forward.
- **Theoretical grounding**: The problem is formulated as NP-hard (0-1 Knapsack) and solved with efficient budget allocation strategies, providing a principled framework.
- **Well-written and well-organized**: The paper is easy to follow, contributions are nicely positioned relative to SOTA, and empirical quantification of the correlation between truncation position and model quality is provided.
- **Empirical quantification**: Concrete experiments on LLaMA-2-7B/13B/70B show perplexity reductions versus SVD-LLM at 50% compression (24.65% and 38.63% on 7B/13B).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited model diversity**: Experiments only cover LLaMA-2 (multi-head attention). Missing evaluation on modern architectures with Group-Query Attention (LLaMA-3, Qwen-2/2.5) and other families (Mistral, Phi-3). This limits generalizability claims and ignores potential architecture-dependent behavior.  
  *Reviewer 3 asks: Can AutoTrunc be transferred to new families or must the method be repeated from scratch? This is not addressed.*
- **Poor presentation and methodological clarity (Sections 2.2–3.2)**:
  - Why only 6×40 = 240 data points? Why measure only compression ratios 10%–50%?
  - Why collect modeling data using only uniform compression and greedy search?
  - The computed upper-bound is confusing: it depends on the manually set `Fmin` (larger `Fmin` could increase performance?) and on the learned modeling parameters (changing the linear model to a nonlinear one would change the bound). It is not clear that reaching this upper-bound is truly optimal.
  - The empirical observation and modeling are highly data-dependent. What happens if the calibration data distribution changes? No analysis is provided.
- **Overclaimed compression speed**: The paper criticizes ASVD for slow search, but AutoTrunc still requires measuring perplexity under many compression ratios (similar to ASVD) plus an additional learning-based modeling step. The claim of superior efficiency is not convincingly supported.
- **Missing critical experiments and comparisons**:
  - No comparison with quantization methods (AWQ, GGUF, GPTQ). It is impossible to assess AutoTrunc’s practical trade-offs.
  - No evaluation on generation tasks (e.g., downstream NLG benchmarks).
  - Comparison with pruning methods is unfair because compression ratios are defined differently (parameter reduction vs. actual deployed size). This needs clarification or a fairer setup.
  - No latency/memory speedup comparison with other SVD-based methods (SVD-LLM, ASVD) at multiple compression ratios. *Reviewer 1 notes that AutoTrunc achieves only ~1.1× throughput from 0% to 60% compression, while SVD-LLM reports >2× at similar levels.*
  - No ablation of the lower bound: what happens when each layer’s compression is unconstrained? *Reviewer 3 asks to show that baseline to demonstrate the efficacy of enforcing lower bounds.*
  - No analysis of performance drop at ≥50% compression (where results may degrade worse than SVD-LLM). *Reviewer 3 asks for hypotheses.*
- **Additional missing analyses**:
  - The 0-1 Knapsack capacity in practice is limited; why not use brute-force search? The NP-hard claim may be overblown.
  - Would combining SVD with quantization (e.g., W4A16) enable further compression? Which SVD approaches tolerate quantization best? Not explored.
  - The extra effort (computation/data) to derive layer-wise ranks vs. SVD-LLM is not quantified.
  - Setting an overall compression ratio as a single hyper-parameter vs. per-layer lower bounds: which is more effective? Not explored.
