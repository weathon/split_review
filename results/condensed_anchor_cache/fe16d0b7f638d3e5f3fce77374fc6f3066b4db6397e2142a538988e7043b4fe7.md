## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a neural subset selection method that models both the subset S and its superset V via permutation-invariant representations, motivated by theoretical results showing that an invariant sufficient statistic of the superset should be incorporated into the subset representation. The method outperforms baselines (e.g., DeepSet, EquiVSet) on several benchmarks, achieving higher mean Jaccard coefficients, often with large gaps. Reviewers generally find the idea simple and effective, with clear writing and adequate coverage of related work, but raise several concerns about presentation, missing details, and experimental scope.

### Strengths
- The idea of including superset information into subset selection is simple and effective, and is supported by strong empirical results (R1, R3). The proposed method achieves notably higher mean Jaccard coefficients compared to DeepSet and EquiVSet (R1).
- The paper is clearly written (R1, R2) and includes a comprehensive discussion of prior work, making it accessible to newcomers (R1, R2).
- Theoretical analysis is provided to motivate the design (R3).  
- Experiments cover multiple baselines (R2).  
- (Minority: One reviewer (R2) gave a lower score of 5, but still found the paper clear and the experiments adequate; another (R3) gave a score of 6 and found the theory a strength, though both R1 and R3 had concerns about clarity of presentation.)

### Weaknesses
- **Notation and clarity issues** (R1): Notations are overloaded (e.g., S used both as a subset and as an element in Section 3.1; relationship between x_i and S_i unclear). The meaning of Y is ambiguous—the footnote says Y_i is “probability of element i being selected,” but this conflicts with the main text where Y appears as a function value (possibly the utility function value U). These inconsistencies reduce readability.
- **Missing optimization and inference details** (R1, R3): Equation (4) presents a neural network construction, but it is unclear what objective function is used to train it and how the trained network selects a subset at test time (R3). Specifically, the connection to variational inference (Ou et al., 2022) is not explained: how does q(Y|S,V), a distribution over Y, approximate p(S|V), a distribution over subsets? How is Eq. (4) related to p_θ(S,V) or F_θ(S,V)? (R1)
- **Subset generation from superset** (R3): Equation (4) requires dividing a superset into subsets, but there are an exponential number of combinations; the paper does not describe how this is done during training or inference.
- **Lack of ablation studies** (R2): No systematic ablations (e.g., removing the superset module, varying representation dimensions) to isolate and quantify the contribution of the proposed component.
- **Limited dataset diversity** (R2, R3): Evaluations are performed only on common benchmarks; the method is not tested on a wider distribution of datasets. One reviewer (R2) asks whether similar findings hold under class imbalance, and to what degree the theoretical guarantees remain valid.
- **Baseline augmentation question** (R2): The baselines (DeepSet, EquiVSet) do not use superset information—could they be trivially improved by adding an invariant sufficient statistic of the superset? This comparison is missing.
- **Parameter count not reported** (R3): The number of learnable parameters for each baseline and the proposed method is not given, making it hard to assess whether gains come from added capacity.  
- **Reviewer confidence and disagreement**: Two reviewers (R1, R3) rated 6, while one (R2) rated 5. R2 is less confident in the contribution and asks for broader validation; R3 rates the soundness and presentation as “fair” (2), indicating more severe concerns about clarity and missing details than R2 (who rated both “good”). The minority lower score reflects a more cautionary stance on experimental scope and missing ablations.
