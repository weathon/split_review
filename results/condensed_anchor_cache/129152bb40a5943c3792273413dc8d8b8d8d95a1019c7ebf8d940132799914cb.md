## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces UDC-VIX, claimed to be the first real-world UDC video dataset, captured with a novel beam-splitter system that simultaneously records paired UDC-degraded and ground-truth videos, then aligned via DFT. The dataset is more diverse in scenarios and closer to real-world degradation than existing synthetic UDC video or still-image datasets. The paper compares UDC-VIX against a synthetic UDC video dataset using six deep-learning restoration models (e.g., UDC-UNet, etc.) and evaluates both image quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and face recognition accuracy. While the dataset is considered a useful and substantial contribution by some reviewers, several reviewers question the novelty of the data collection pipeline (beam splitter, DFT alignment, etc.) and argue the experimental validation is insufficient to fully demonstrate the dataset’s advantages. One reviewer is significantly more positive (score 8) than the others (scores 5, 6, 5), noting the dataset accuracy and comparison with synthetic data.

### Strengths
- First real-world UDC video dataset that accurately reflects actual UDC video degradations (low transmittance, blur, noise, flare) – agreed by all four reviewers.
- Novel video-capturing system using a beam splitter to simultaneously capture non-degraded (GT) and UDC-degraded videos, minimizing discrepancies between paired frames (Reviewer 1, but see Weaknesses).
- Application of DFT for frame-by-frame alignment, achieving high alignment accuracy sufficient for training deep learning models (Reviewer 3).
- Comprehensive analysis comparing UDC-VIX with existing UDC still-image datasets and two existing UDC video datasets, highlighting the limitations (unrealistic flare, white artifacts) of synthetic datasets (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- Cross-dataset validation experiments: trained and tested six models (UDC-UNet, etc.) on both UDC-VIX and a synthetic dataset; results show models trained on synthetic data do not generalize to real-world degradation (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- The dataset is more diverse in scenarios (human motions, facial recognition targets) and degradation types are closer to real-world conditions (Reviewer 2).
- Detailed description of data collection and processing pipeline; clear and concise presentation (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- Evaluation includes both standard image quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and face recognition accuracy, demonstrating practical relevance (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- Substantial amount of work with complete narrative (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty in data collection pipeline**: The beam-splitter approach and DFT alignment are existing techniques from other fields; this paper is more an engineering implementation than a novel methodology (Reviewer 1 Q1, Reviewer 3 Q1, Q5, Q6; also noted as weakness by Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient experimental validation to prove dataset advantages**:
  - No cross-testing between datasets (e.g., train on UDC-VIX, test on synthetic and vice versa) – Table 3 only shows results when trained and tested on the same dataset, making it difficult to assess the impact of different datasets on restoration models (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3 Q4).
  - Limited experiments: does not show whether models trained on UDC-VIX generalize better to other real-world UDC videos (Reviewer 3 Q4).
  - Visual comparisons use different video frames for different datasets, which is not convincing; the same frames should be used (Reviewer 3 Q3).
- **Missing clarifications and explanations**:
  - The theoretical analysis of existing dataset limitations and the strength of UDC-VIX should be in the main paper, not only in the appendix (Reviewer 1).
  - In Table 3, UDC-UNet achieves better SSIM and LPIPS on UDC-VIX while all other models show decreased performance; this anomaly needs explanation (Reviewer 1 Q2).
  - The description of the face recognition experiment (Section 5.2) is difficult to understand and needs reorganization (Reviewer 4).
  - English abbreviations in Figure 1 are not introduced (Reviewer 3).
  - The Limitations section mentions that UDC degradations vary with display pixel design; a more detailed explanation of which specific degradations are affected is required (Reviewer 2).
- **Data constraints and possible biases**:
  - Fast-moving objects are excluded from the dataset; this may disadvantage UDC-VIX compared to other datasets for motion-related scenarios (Reviewer 3 Q2).
  - The dataset is collected from a specific device; the data distribution problem (noted in Limitations) is not addressed, e.g., by also collecting from popular mobile phone brands with larger market share (Reviewer 4 Q3).
- **Concerns about practical significance and baseline comparisons**:
  - Does face recognition under UDC have real-world application significance? Does UDC currently limit face recognition performance, and what other problems exist? (Reviewer 4 Q4).
  - Are the exaggerated flares in UDC-VIX distinct from flares produced by non-UDC cameras under strong light? (Reviewer 4 Q2 – also questions whether iPhone rear camera is UDC).
- **Effect of beam splitter on GT quality**: Capturing ground-truth videos through a beam splitter halves the light, potentially degrading the GT video quality; this issue was not considered or discussed (Reviewer 3 Q1).
- **Existing models perform insufficiently on UDC-VIX**: The paper does not propose a better resolution or model to handle the real-world degradation in UDC videos, leaving the challenge open (Reviewer 1 Q3).
