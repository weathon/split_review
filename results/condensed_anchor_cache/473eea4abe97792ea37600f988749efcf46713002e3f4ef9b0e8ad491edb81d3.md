## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Gradient Normalization Scaling (GNS) and High-Frequency Adaptation (HFA) to enhance transferable adversarial attacks on Vision Transformers. GNS attenuates "mild" gradients via normalization and tanh scaling; HFA manipulates high-frequency components using DCT. Experiments on multiple ViT and CNN models show improved attack success rates.

### Strengths
- The paper is easy to follow and well motivated (Reviewer 1).
- The problem space is novel and the normalization/scaling approach is innovative (Reviewer 2).
- Achieves better attack transferability than state-of-the-art methods (Reviewer 2).
- Comprehensive numerical results across many architectures and attack methods consistently show high transferability (Reviewer 3).
- The method is simple to implement (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- The paper lacks a clear, mechanistic explanation of "mild" and "extreme" gradients. These terms are not rigorously defined, and the threshold (µ+u*σ) is not justified. It is unclear why attenuating mild gradients should improve transferability (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The normalization and scaling process is insufficiently demonstrated. Implementation details, mathematical operations, parameters, and the choice of tanh are not clearly described or justified. Several reviewers noted that normalization and scaling appear to be the same operation (Reviewer 2, echoed by Reviewer 3).
- The rationale for high-frequency adaptation is missing: it is unclear whether it improves ViT robustness or attack transferability, and why manipulating high frequencies leads to better transferability (Reviewer 2). The HFA method itself is quite unclear (Reviewer 3).
- Ablation studies are insufficient. The paper does not separately evaluate the contributions of GNS and HFA, nor does it show how they interact or which component dominates. Ablations using only GNS or only HFA are requested (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The contribution is marginal, as gradient normalization has been demonstrated in prior work [1] (Reviewer 1). All three reviewers assigned a contribution score of 2 (fair).
- Presentation is difficult to follow for a general audience (Reviewer 3). There are grammatical errors, logical breaks in the abstract, and figure annotations need more explanation (Reviewer 2). Key concepts such as "channels" in Transformers and the setup of Table 1 (which model is attacked) are unclear (Reviewer 3). The combination of GNS and HFA is not clearly explained (Reviewer 3).
