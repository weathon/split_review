## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces AlignAb, a three-stage framework for antibody sequence-structure co-design: (1) pre-training a language model on antibody sequences, (2) transferring to a diffusion model for joint optimization, and (3) alignment using AbDPO extended to Pareto optimality under multiple Rosetta energy-based objectives (repulsion and attraction). It employs iterative learning with decaying temperature scaling to generate nature-like antibodies with low repulsion and high attraction energy.

### Strengths
- **Clear motivation:** Limited 3D antibody-antigen complex data motivates multi-stage training that leverages abundant sequence data (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Well-written and detailed methodology** that is easy to follow (Reviewer 2).
- **Novel energy-based alignment using (Pareto-)DPO** to achieve Pareto optimality across multiple energy objectives, combined with iterative online exploration and decaying temperature scaling (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **State-of-the-art performance** according to Rosetta energy metrics in generating antibodies with low repulsion and high attraction energy; empirical results are considered promising (Reviewers 2, 3, 4; but see disagreement below on metric validity).
- **Disagreement noted:** Reviewers 2 and 4 found the energy-based alignment novel and effective, while Reviewers 1 and 3 raised major concerns about the validity of the energy metric and novelty. This contrast is preserved.

### Weaknesses
- **Validity of Rosetta energy as a proxy for binding affinity (high severity, high confidence for Reviewers 1 and 3):** Reviewers 1 and 3 strongly question the assumption that minimizing Rosetta energy translates to improved real binding affinity. Reviewer 3 notes the known low correlation (~0.3) for single mutations, which likely degrades further with multiple CDR mutations. Force fields bias toward hydrophobic residues, but therapeutic antibodies typically avoid hydrophobicity to prevent non-specific binding and immunogenicity. The paper does not evaluate human-likeness (e.g., TAP scores, AntiBERTy plausibility) or test alternative energy prediction methods (e.g., dyMEAN). Reviewer 1 also questions whether the computed energy is practically useful and notes a lack of discussion of Rosetta’s limitations.
- **Unfair and incomplete comparisons with baselines (high severity, multiple reviewers):** Baselines (e.g., DiffAb, MEAN) are not energy-based and are trained on raw co-crystal structures, not minimized energy states, making energy score comparisons potentially biased (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 3 recommends including models trained on minimized structures and post hoc optimization. AlignAb (based on DiffAb) performs worse than DiffAb on CDR-Ag E_att (Reviewer 4). Many recent methods are missing from comparisons: RefineGNN, ADesigner, GeoAb, HTP, AbDiffuser (Reviewer 3). AbDPO, the direct predecessor, is not included as a benchmark (Reviewers 1, 2). Reviewer 1 reports that MEA values differ significantly from those in AbDPO and questions why. Baselines do not use pre-trained language models, potentially making comparisons unfair (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing standard evaluation metrics and model architecture details (high severity, Reviewers 1, 2, 3):** The paper omits commonly used metrics: amino acid recovery (AAR), root mean square deviation (RMSD), and bond length/angle distributions (Reviewers 2, 3). Reviewer 1 notes that AAR and RMSD reflect differences to the original antibody and are not adequately explored. The model architecture is not described in detail (Reviewer 3). Without these, it is unclear whether generated structures are realistic enough to retain immune functionality (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited technical novelty (moderate severity, Reviewer 1 strongly critical; Reviewer 4 notes prior work):** Reviewer 1 argues each stage uses existing methods (ABGNN, DiffAb, AbDPO/Diffusion-DPO) with unclear novel contributions beyond combination. Reviewer 4 notes multi-stage training is not new and requests citations to prior hierarchical paradigms (NeurIPS 2023) and integration of PLMs with geometric networks (ICLR 2023, Communications Biology). Reviewers 2 and 4 considered the energy-based alignment novel; this disagreement is preserved.
- **Lack of code and reproducibility:** Code is not available (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Missing details on data processing and experimental setup:** Reviewer 1 asks whether OAS deduplication was performed and if the test set was excluded. Only 42 of 60 RAbD benchmark complexes were used (fewer than the 55 used by AbDPO); the reason is not explained. Figure 4 raises questions: why does the MEAN framework structure differ from other methods, and what is its purpose? (Reviewer 1).
- **Training efficiency not analyzed:** No quantitative analysis of computational cost per iteration, energy computation time, or number of samples needed for convergence. The claim of optimal results in “fewer than three iterations with fewer samples” is vague (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing ablation studies:** Reviewer 4 requests ablation over the three stages to quantify the benefit of each, especially given that baselines do not use pre-trained language models.
- **Limitation of only designing CDR3:** The model assumes binding consistent with the original antibody, potentially limiting exploration of diverse binding modes and risking overfitting (Reviewer 1).
- **Minor query on exploration strategy:** Reviewer 2 asks whether more advanced strategies (e.g., UCB) could replace the decaying temperature schedule.
