## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces CogVLM, a multimodal foundation model that uses trainable visual expert modules inserted into the attention and FFN layers of a frozen LLM to achieve deeper vision-language fusion than shallow alignment methods. The model is pretrained on a large-scale dataset and then instruction-tuned. CogVLM-17B achieves state-of-the-art results on 17 benchmarks across image captioning (NoCaps, Flicker30k), VQA (OKVQA, TextVQA, OCRVQA, ScienceQA), LVLM benchmarks (MM-Vet, MMBench, SEED-Bench, LLaVABench, POPE, MMMU, MathVista), and visual grounding (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg, Visual7W). The model is open-source.

### Strengths
- **Strong and broad performance.** CogVLM achieves SOTA on a wide range of vision-language benchmarks, including VQA, image captioning, visual grounding, document visual tasks, and GPT-4-based evaluations. It outperforms models of similar scale and is competitive with much larger models like PaLI-X.
- **Novel architectural module for deep fusion.** The visual expert module is a principled departure from shallow alignment (e.g., Flamingo, PaLI, CoCa) by enabling deep, trainable interactions between visual and language tokens within LLM layers, rather than only at the input. This is described as a pioneering approach to address shallow alignment in MLLMs.
- **Open-source release.** Releasing a strong open-source multimodal foundation model has significant impact on reproducibility and community progress.
- **Extensive experiments and ablation studies.** The paper provides thorough evaluation across diverse tasks and includes ablation studies that validate the design components and demonstrate effectiveness.
- **Large-scale curated dataset.** The authors constructed a large-scale dataset for pretraining and instruction tuning, which contributes to the strong performance.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and scientific insight.** The core idea of dense interaction between vision and language tokens has been heavily studied in earlier BERT-style models (e.g., BEIT-3, VLMo) that also use modality-specific experts. The adaptation to the LLM era, while effective, does not offer clear new understanding. The paper lacks actionable insights for future research—it is unclear whether further gains come from better model design, more data, or more compute. The use of in-house data further hampers reproducibility.
- **Confounding factors mask the source of improvement.** The final model includes many variables (pretraining data, instruction-tuning data, architecture, finetuning strategies). Table 6 shows only a ~4 CIDEr point difference between a 6K-step ablation and the final model trained for 120K+60K steps, raising the question of whether the long training is necessary. The contribution of the visual expert itself is not clearly isolated—the ablation studies cannot prove that deep alignment fixes the claimed issues of shallow alignment because results for a similarly-sized shallow model (with a larger visual encoder) are not reported.
- **Writing and motivation issues.** The paper makes blanket statements (e.g., that joint training weakens NLP ability) that are not compelling or conclusive. The two hypothesized drawbacks of shallow alignment—fast convergence but worse performance, and weak visual understanding/hallucination—are presented without strong evidence. The gap between BLIP-2 and PaLI-X can be explained by other factors (e.g., visual encoder size, pretraining). Moreover, the ablation studies do not directly demonstrate that these specific problems are resolved.
- **Unfair and incomplete comparisons.** Earlier methods like PaLI, CoCa, and Flamingo do not include an SFT stage, so it is unclear how CogVLM performs before that stage. The visual backbone (ViT-e, 490×490 resolution) and doubled LLM parameter count make direct comparisons difficult. Additionally, the VQA/caption, grounding, and chat models are three separate models, not a unified one (unlike GPT-4V), and there is no analysis of how a unified model would perform across all tasks.
- **Missing ablations and analyses.** 
  - Which part of the visual expert—attention FFN vs. FFN block—is more important for performance?
  - More experiments on the architecture of the visual expert module (e.g., which layers to share vs. have separate parameters) are needed.
  - The impact of VLM finetuning on the original behavior of the image encoder and LLM is not evaluated (e.g., linear probe, zero-shot visual eval, LM performance degradation).
  - The corrected LLaVA-Instruct dataset has not been released.
  - The paper does not report whether any emergent abilities were observed in the strong MLLM.
- **Reproducibility concern.** The in-house data is not available, making it difficult to reproduce the reported results and limiting the scientific value.
- **One minority reviewer (score 5) assessed the contribution as fair (2/4) and argued the paper is good engineering but lacks sufficient scientific merit for the venue, while the other three reviewers (scores 6) considered the contribution fair-good (2-3/4).** All reviewers agree on the strong performance but differ on the novelty and depth of insight.
