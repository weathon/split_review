## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper investigates whether transformers can provide theoretical guarantees for solving the entropic optimal transport (OT) problem in Wasserstein-2. The authors prove that a transformer with fixed parameters and an engineered prompt can implement gradient descent with adaptive step sizes on the dual OT formulation, yielding an approximation bound that improves with depth. The construction is independent of the number of input points, and experiments demonstrate the approach on sorting (as a one-dimensional OT case) for different input sizes. Reviewers note the paper’s novel connection between transformers and optimal transport with rigorous proofs, but also point out significant limitations regarding practical relevance, missing training analysis, limited experimental scope, and errors in presentation.

### Strengths

- The paper introduces a novel inquiry into whether transformers can provably solve optimal transport, and the construction works for an arbitrary number of points, enabling scale-independent guarantees (Reviewer 1).
- The theoretical development is rigorous, with strong mathematical formalism and clear derivation of approximation bounds that improve with depth (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). One reviewer notes that the proofs are correct to the best of their knowledge (Reviewer 4).
- The experimental validation on sorting tasks for different input sizes supports the theoretical claims (Reviewer 3; also acknowledged by Reviewer 2). This partially compensates for the lack of directly available OT experiments (Reviewer 3).
- The paper offers new insights into how transformers can function beyond sequence modeling, linking them to iterative optimization methods such as gradient descent and strengthening the iterative inference hypothesis (Reviewer 2).
- The approach provides guarantees for a broad class of discrete OT problems that arise in various applied fields (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited practical relevance of the construction.** The proof relies on a specific, artificial construction with hand-engineered prompts and parameters. Multiple reviewers question whether training standard transformers (without such construction) can achieve the same solutions in real-world scenarios (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). The analysis ignores pre-training generalization error and training error of the transformer parameters (Reviewer 4).
- **Adaptive step size deviation from standard gradient descent.** The softmax attention implementation leads to step sizes that depend on the current dual variables, which is not a direct gradient descent but a variant. This raises questions about practical convergence and whether the transformer layers truly perform gradient descent (Reviewer 4). The impact on convergence to the true OT matrix is unclear (Reviewer 4).
- **The assertion that attention patterns maintain full rank (Lines 375–384, 479–483) contradicts commonly observed low-rank and sparse attention matrices in practice** (Reviewer 1). A comment on whether low-rank/sparse transformers could also implement OT is requested (Reviewer 1).
- **The required depth is a sufficient condition; shallower transformers may also perform OT effectively**, but this possibility is not explored (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing generalization to other OT costs and real-world settings.** The paper only considers the specific squared Euclidean cost (Wasserstein-2). It does not explore other important costs such as Manhattan or Gaussian, which are relevant in logistics or finance (Reviewer 3). The analysis does not address whether transformers are capable of solving OT when trained on real-world data; a discussion comparing with in-context learning literature is needed (Reviewer 3, referring to [1],[2],[3]).
- **Limited experimental scope.** Experiments are limited to simple sorting tasks. The paper lacks head-to-head comparisons with standard OT algorithms (e.g., Sinkhorn or linear programming) on accuracy, runtime, or scalability. Demonstrations for higher-dimensional OT (e.g., d=10) are missing (Reviewer 2). Additionally, no comparison is made between the trained transformer output and the solution obtained by running gradient descent on the dual OT loss (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient investigation of prompt engineering.** The paper does not explore the sensitivity or robustness of the engineered prompt; it is unclear if minor prompt variations would significantly impact performance (Reviewer 2). The necessity of the specific prompt for theoretical guarantees versus being an artifact of the proof technique is not addressed (Reviewer 4). Could a learnable feature map achieve similar results? (Reviewer 4).
- **Equations contain errors and inconsistencies** that disrupt the logical flow and readability. Specific issues noted: line 241 (excessive vector), lines 241–242 (vector placement), line 275 (iteration index), line 286 (zero vectors/matrices), line 291 (missing λ, wrong upper left block), line 304 (wrong placement of γ). These mistakes need careful correction (Reviewer 3).
- **Notation is introduced late or missing**, making the theoretical sections hard to follow. Examples: subscript in \([Z^{(l)}_n]\) not clearly defined (line 202); matrix \(A\) not identified as attention matrix (line 299); inconsistent notation for squared norms and vectors (lines 286, 291); ambiguous column vs. row vector usage; continued use of \(2d+9\) after defining \(d' = 2d+9\) (lines 267, 275) (Reviewer 3).
- **Matrix derivations are challenging to follow** due to abrupt transitions and missing dimensions for blocks. Providing intermediate steps in an appendix and specifying dimensions upon introduction is suggested (Reviewer 3).
- **Mathematical density reduces accessibility** for a broader audience; visual aids or flowcharts would help (Reviewer 2).
- **The core idea of using transformers to unroll gradient descent is not new** and has been studied in prior work (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4). The specific application to OT, while novel, relies on the same simulation approach (Reviewer 1).
- **How this framework extends to broader optimization or machine learning problems is not discussed** (Reviewer 2).
