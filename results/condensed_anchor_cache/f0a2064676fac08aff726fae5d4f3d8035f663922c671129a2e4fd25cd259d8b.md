## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SemCLIP to address instability in vision-language models (VLMs) like CLIP when synonymous queries retrieve different images. A linguist-curated database of 114,000 similarity lists is constructed from a constrained WordNet traversal (English nouns only). A semantics-preserving textual embedding (STE) is trained via contrastive learning on these lists, and an alignment transformation maps VLM image and text embeddings into the STE space to bring synonyms closer while preserving image-text similarity. The alignment is learned from text embeddings alone, avoiding large-scale VLM retraining. Extensive experiments on 13 benchmark datasets report improved retrieval stability and quality using mAP and NDCG. The dataset and STE are released.

### Strengths
1. **Important problem**: The paper identifies and addresses the instability of VLMs when handling synonymous queries, a practical issue for vector-database-based retrieval. (R1, R4)
2. **Dataset contribution**: The linguist-curated similarity list database (114k entries, WordNet-derived) is released as an open-source resource, valuable for the community. (R1, R2, R4)
3. **Extensive empirical evaluation**: Comparisons with multiple CLIP variants across several benchmark datasets provide strong evidence for effectiveness. (R1)
4. **Meaningful evaluation metrics**: The use of retrieval metrics with images and labels (mAP, NDCG) offers a more nuanced assessment than traditional image-to-text matching. (R4)
5. **Practical framework**: SemCLIP transforms VLM embeddings to place semantically close terms and their associated images together, useful for vector-database data management without large-scale retraining. (R3)
6. **Clear presentation**: The paper is well organized with clear figures. (R2)

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited to English nouns**: The method only handles nouns, restricting applicability to verbs, adjectives, adverbs, and complex sentence structures. (R1)
2. **Potential bias in similarity list curation**: The manual curation by linguists may not capture diverse language use across domains, skewing representation for specialized/technical terms. (R1)
3. **WSD tool accuracy (≈80%)**: Errors from the word-sense disambiguation tool propagate into the embeddings, especially for polysemous words. (R1)
4. **Lack of clarity in dataset construction** (R2):
   - No illustrative figure of the construction process.
   - Unclear whether similarity is defined for nouns only or also phrases, and whether values are binary or continuous.
   - Questions about possible word-pair same meaning as single nouns but different in sentences.
   - No guarantee of diversity, reasonableness, or frequency of items in the dataset.
5. **Missing experiments on general word retrieval**: Tables 2 and 3 only report results for synonym queries; it is unclear whether learning synonymy degrades retrieval of general (non-synonym) words. (R2)
6. **Contradiction in alignment method (Eq. 4)**: The alignment transform for images relies on the nearest-text distance in the original VLM space. This assumes VLM space is reliable for image-text distance, contradicting the paper’s claim that VLM lacks sensitivity to linguistic similarity. (R3) *Minority reviewer R3 strongly emphasizes this contradiction; others did not raise it.*
7. **Limited novelty**: The core components (contrastive STE training on curated lists, nearest-neighbor-based alignment) follow existing simple algorithmic ideas without deeper theoretical insight into semantic alignment. (R3) *Minority reviewer R3; R1 and R4 rate contribution higher.*
8. **Missing standard retrieval benchmarks**: Recall scores on MS COCO and Flickr for image-to-text / text-to-image are not reported, making comparison with standard CLIP baselines incomplete. (R4)
9. **Figures and notation clarity issues** (multiple reviewers):
   - Figure 2 lacks explanation of components (A,B,C,D,J1,J2) in the caption; difficult to understand how image embeddings are pulled via alignment. (R2, R4)
   - Figure 3 missing arrows showing flow from transformation mapping to other stages. (R1)
   - Notation often confuses data samples with embedding vectors. (R4)
   - Unclear statements: e.g., lines 192-193 (“distance is a function of the word itself”), lines 290-291 (“essence of the similarity in … numerical formulation”). (R3)
   - Sections 3, 4, and 5 appear conceptually separate and need reorganization. (R3)
10. **Unaddressed questions and requested experiments**:
    - How is the semantic similarity distance `γ_j` determined? (R1)
    - Why does STE perform worse on an antonyms dataset? (R1)
    - Long-tail or rare synonyms not well represented in WordNet may affect STE. (R1)
    - The alignment transform equates image and text embeddings spatially, potentially overlooking cross-modal semantic differences. (R1)
    - Is the original VLM fine-tuned? Figure 2 suggests image embeddings are pulled during alignment, but the method description only updates the text encoder. (R2)
    - Would larger CLIP models (e.g., ViT-bigG trained on billions of samples) already handle the synonym problem? No experimental results with such stronger backbones are provided. (R4)
