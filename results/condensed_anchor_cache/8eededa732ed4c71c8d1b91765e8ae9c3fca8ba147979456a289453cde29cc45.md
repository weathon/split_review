## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper analyzes single-timescale neural actor-critic in continuous state and action spaces under the average-reward criterion, using overparametrized deep neural networks for both actor and critic. It obtains finite-time convergence to a stationary point at rate O(T^{-1/2}) + O(m^{-1/2}), where T is the number of iterations and m is the network width, via an NTK-style analysis. Reviewers acknowledge that the continuous-space, average-reward setting is important and understudied, and the paper is the first to provide such a finite-time guarantee. However, opinions diverge sharply: one reviewer (score 6) considers it a strong novel contribution, while another (score 3) finds the extension incremental and the assumptions too restrictive; two reviewers (scores 5) see value but raise significant concerns about the assumptions, lack of empirical validation, and clarity of the proof structure.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles an important and understudied problem: continuous state and action spaces for average-reward actor-critic (Reviewers 2, 4).
- It provides the first finite-time analysis of single-timescale actor-critic in this setting (Reviewer 1).
- The incorporation of Markovian sampling strengthens the result (Reviewer 3).
- The bounds are tight in terms of dependence on both the number of iterations T and the network width m (Reviewer 2).
- The analysis justifies the use of neural network function approximation through overparametrization and NTK theory (Reviewer 2).
- The paper is well-written, clearly laid out, and easy to read; assumptions are explained clearly (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The appendix is well-organized, aiding readability (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and derivation**: The proof appears largely derivative of Chen (2024) and/or Chen & Zhao [1]; the extension to continuous spaces is considered incremental by some reviewers, and the novel theoretical contributions beyond generalizing assumptions are not clearly articulated (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Practicality of Assumptions**:
    - Assumption 4.3 requires smooth value functions w.r.t. θ and uniform boundedness of optimal parameters; with normally initialized neural networks, it is unclear how this holds (Reviewer 2).
    - Assumption 4.4 posits a uniform lower bound on λ₁ across all states; for uncountably many states this is not obviously attainable (Reviewer 2).
    - Assumption 4.5: The term (π_θ – 1) may always be negative, potentially invalidating the assumption (Reviewer 1); additionally, its interpretation as an exploration condition lacks a clear or visual explanation (Reviewer 4).
    - Assumption 4.6 (geometric mixing): Typically justified in tabular settings; it is not obvious it carries over to continuous spaces with function approximation—a rigorous justification is missing (Reviewer 4).
    - Assumption 4.7, condition (c): It is unclear whether common policies (e.g., Gaussian) satisfy this condition; concrete examples beyond uniform distributions are needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Convergence to stationary point**: The guarantee is only to a stationary point, not a global optimum; some recent works provide global convergence for average-reward problems, even if in finite spaces (Reviewer 2).
- **Choice of c in Theorem 4.9**: The constant c in α = c/√T depends on complex problem parameters, making it infeasible to determine a priori; practical guidance is lacking (Reviewer 3).
- **Trade-off with m and approximation error**: As m→∞, the O(m^{-1/2}) term vanishes, but potential neural network convergence issues and the approximation error ε_app are not analyzed; the trade-off between decreasing the second term and possibly increasing ε_app is not clarified (Reviewer 3).
- **Nonlinear activation restriction**: The differentiability requirement precludes ReLU; although alternatives exist, no simulation demonstrates their practicality (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing empirical validation**: No simulations are provided to corroborate the theoretical rates, especially the dependence on m, or to illustrate the algorithm's behavior with realistic step sizes (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Clarity of proof structure**: The main text lacks a proof outline that highlights novel analytical elements; it largely points to the appendix, making it difficult to identify the new contributions (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarification needed**:
    - Equation 1: the meaning of d(a×s') is not explained (Reviewer 2).
    - Page 24: the origin of the O(1/√m) term in the expression for Z_T is not motivated (Reviewer 1).
