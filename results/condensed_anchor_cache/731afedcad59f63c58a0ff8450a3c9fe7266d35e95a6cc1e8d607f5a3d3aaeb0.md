## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the Manifold Kernel Rank Reduced Regression (MKRRR) model, combining Kernel Rank Reduced Regression (KRRR) with the Kendall shape space manifold to account for intrinsic geometric data structure. It derives a closed-form solution and provides a robustness proof. The method is applied to 3D mandibular reconstruction from Chinese skull models, with experiments comparing to PCA and PGA.

### Strengths
- The core idea of combining KRRR with a manifold framework (Kendall shape space) is convincing and interesting.
- The related works discussion in Section 2 is good.
- Derivation of a closed-form solution and a robustness proof are helpful.
- The work aims to solve a real application (3D mandibular reconstruction), which is considered novel and original by some reviewers.
- Authors demonstrate good knowledge of their domain.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty**: The closed-form solution (Equation 12) is generic and standard; the KRRR solution is already available in Mukherjee et al. (2011) with a very similar proof. The paper presents KRRR as novel despite it being well-established. The MKRRR extension is essentially replacing the Euclidean distance with a Riemannian one in a Gaussian kernel, which is a straightforward modification.
- **Robustness claim is insufficiently supported**: The claimed robustness lacks a rigorous mathematical definition and a proper theoretical proof.
- **Theoretical concern**: It is unclear whether a positive definite kernel exists using a Riemannian distance for this problem. The three kernels listed in Table 1 may not be geodesic distances or positive definite.
- **Weak experimental evaluation**: Only one dataset (Chinese skull models) is used. Comparisons are limited to PCA and PGA, which are preprocessing methods, not regression baselines—more comprehensive benchmarking is needed. The experimental figures (3, 4, 5) are noisy, barely readable, and make it hard to draw conclusions. Hyperparameters appear to be tuned on the test set, raising concerns about data leakage. The rank value seems to have no effect on performance (Figure 4). The purpose of the experiment (e.g., is it to recreate a missing mandible?) and the interpretation of accuracy from an applicative perspective (e.g., archeology) are unclear. The type of noise added in Figure 5 is not specified (Gaussian?).
- **Writing and presentation issues**: The paper is not properly written, lacking general context and motivation. Typos exist (missing space before “extend” in Section 2, yi not formatted). Several clarifications are needed: justification of the derivation from left-hand side to right-hand side in Equation 11, the meaning of “shapeRepresentation” in the algorithm, and the phrase “each point corresponds to one by one”.
- **Incomplete related work survey**: The paper omits important prior work on kernel regression with manifold learning, such as Nilsson et al. (2007).
- **Disagreement on contribution**: One reviewer rated Contribution as 3 (good), while others rated it 1 or 2, indicating mixed views on the novelty and significance of the work.
