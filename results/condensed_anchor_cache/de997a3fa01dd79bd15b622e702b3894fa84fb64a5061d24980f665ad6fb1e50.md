## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes AnyECG, a foundational model for ECG analysis that processes any real-world ECG data via a two-stage pre-training: an ECG-specific tokenizer that encodes fixed-duration fragments into discrete tokens using a Rhythm Codebook (capturing morphology, frequency, demographics), and a masked encoder pre-training to learn rhythm-event associations. Evaluations on anomaly detection, arrhythmia detection, corrupted lead generation, and ultra-long ECG analysis claim superiority over prior methods. All four reviewers assign a score of 3/5 (weak reject) with high confidence. They acknowledge the attempt to address ECG heterogeneity and the use of masked learning, but identify critical weaknesses in experimental validation, baseline comparison, dataset representativeness, and ECG domain appropriateness.

### Strengths
- The authors make a good attempt to apply masked learning (rather than contrastive learning) to ECG representation learning, which is relatively novel.
- The overall architecture figure is clear and informative.
- The tailored ECG tokenizer and Rhythm Codebook are designed to encode morphological, frequency, and demographic information, potentially improving robustness to noise and demographic shifts.
- The paper thoroughly considers real-world ECG complexities (heterogeneity, low SNR, demographic variability, rhythm-event associations) and proposes a codebook-driven standardized ECG encoding that leverages prior knowledge.
- The inclusion of morphology, frequency, and demographic decoders jointly guides learning of time and frequency domain features, with the demographic decoder being a relatively rare addition in ECG studies.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient baseline and method comparisons:**  
  - No comparison with other foundational or self-supervised learning (SSL) methods, especially contrastive learning–based approaches which are the most related.  
  - Not compared with state-of-the-art results from benchmarks (e.g., PTBXL arrhythmia detection AUC of 0.9 from CinC2021 vs. the paper’s AUC < 0.35 for similar tasks – see below).

- **Inadequate and poorly described experiments:**  
  - Ablation studies are lacking in the main text; only limited ones appear in the appendix.  
  - Downstream task definitions are unclear – the same datasets for pre-training and downstream tasks are not specified; anomaly detection task lacks definition and dataset; corrupted lead generation settings (e.g., generating precordial from limb leads) and methodology (use of morphology decoder?) are not described; “ultra-long ECG recognition” setting (length, handling of M dimensions in transformer) and meaning of “adaptation” in Table 5 are not explained.  
  - Evaluation metrics are contradictory: Section 7.2 lists PSNR, SSIM, MAE for ultra-long ECG but Table 5 shows different metrics.  
  - Anomaly detection and arrhythmia detection are not clearly differentiated (irregularities characteristic of arrhythmias).  
  - For arrhythmia detection, accuracy is typically very high; Table 3 shows low metrics (all <0.35) – need explanation of which arrhythmias and labeling strategies; PTBXL AUC achieved 0.9 in CinC2021 challenge results, so the model’s performance is questionable for real ECG tasks.  
  - The generated signals in Figure 2 lack key wave features (P, QRS, T) and deviate significantly from actual ECG – how can such signals be usable for downstream tasks?  
  - No evaluation of location-aware metrics for long-term ECG (e.g., pinpointing abnormal rhythm positions).

- **Unrepresentative and insufficient dataset coverage:**  
  - Downstream datasets are only standard 12-lead ECGs from CinC2021 (excluding Chapman, Shaoxing, Ningbo, UMich databases); no ambulatory, wearable, single-lead, treadmill, Holter, or mobile ECG data are used, undermining the claim of handling any ECG (“AnyECG”).  
  - The “Undisclosed Database” (hidden test data of CinC2021) – how was it obtained? No mention of authorization.  
  - Lack of large-scale datasets (e.g., Zenodo 4916206) or consumer device data to support a foundation model.

- **Domain knowledge and methodological issues:**  
  - The paper shows a lack of foundational ECG understanding:  
    - Padding short (10s, 1-3 lead) ECG data to 12 leads over 60 seconds inflates data volume and may lead to erroneous lead-dropout interpretations.  
    - Directly concatenating patches from different times and channels into a single sequence risks confusing temporal and channel correlations after transformer processing.  
  - Token size (1 second) is too coarse for detecting sub-second critical events (e.g., acute MI, QT prolongation).  
  - Morphology decoder: masking temporal signal may shift the fragment’s attributes to other ECG abnormalities, hindering accurate reconstruction.  
  - Time-shift sensitivity near QRS complex could cause significant loss increases – no handling described.  
  - Positional encoding for time (τ_k) and channel (σ_j) is not defined; encoding along channel dimension may not be meaningful.  
  - Vector-quantized rhythm codebook implementation and proof that a vector can replace original encoding are missing.  
  - Filter range 0.1–75 Hz is standard for diagnostic ECGs but not justified for the model.

- **Notation and presentation errors:**  
  - Inconsistent use of w and s for patch size; s is not defined upon first appearance.  
  - Figure 1 mentions “N transformer blocks” but text defines N as total patches.  
  - Figure 2: horizontal axis labeled “points” instead of time (s); vertical axis amplitude unit (mV) not confirmed.

- **Additional reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms (missing experiments, missing comparisons, ablations):**  
  - Proxy task handling when datasets lack one or more demographic attributes?  
  - Why is MAE high in corrupted lead generation? Why PSNR/SSIM chosen?  
  - Demonstration of generation results on the same GT signal.  
  - What does “Implicit rhythm-event association” mean exactly?  
  - Were training/inference times compared to baselines?  
  - Should different parts of the signal (P, QRS, T) have different weights given clinical importance?  
  - What is the basis for 0.1–75 Hz filter?  
  - Definitions: P=300 in line 343 seems to imply w=300, but earlier w is patch size.  
  - Can the model handle 1-hour+ ECGs? How is dimension M managed in transformer?  
  - How can a foundation model be substantiated without including single-lead wearable signals?

- **Disagreement among reviewers:**  
  - Two reviewers find the attempted masked learning and codebook design as strengths; one reviewer particularly critical of the model’s ECG domain knowledge, questioning its suitability as a foundation model. All agree the experimental support is insufficient.
