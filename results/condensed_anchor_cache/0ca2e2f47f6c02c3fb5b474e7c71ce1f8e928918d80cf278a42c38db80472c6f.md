## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Quantization-Aware Adaptive Coreset Selection (ACS) to improve data efficiency and robustness in QAT by selecting informative samples each epoch. Two metrics are introduced: Error Vector Score (EVS) based on first-order loss-gradient approximation and Disagreement Score (DS) measuring output distance between full-precision and quantized models. Experiments on ResNet-18, MobileNetV2, RetinaNet across CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-1K, COCO under various quantization settings compare subset ratios (10%-80%) against full-data QAT and random selection.

### Strengths
- Pioneering use of coreset selection to enhance QAT efficiency; provides a new angle for reducing QAT training cost by using fewer training data.
- Solid theoretical proof bounding the training error when selecting a core subset of samples.
- The paper is written clearly and concisely, with a detailed experimental setup that facilitates reproducibility.

### Weaknesses
- Experimental results show no clear advantage over full-data QAT: using 80% of data yields worse Top-1 accuracy and longer relative training time (e.g., Table 1/2), and even at 80% ratio full-data always achieves higher accuracy. The method fails to achieve lossless compression, and it is not shown that coreset selection can match full-data accuracy.
- The best data selection strategy varies with subset ratio (Table 4), indicating the method is not robust or universal.
- The proposed metrics (EVS and DS) have no inherent connection to QAT: gradient-norm-based loss estimation and a disagreement score that measures teacher-student output distance can both be applied to full-precision training without modification. The core idea of using gradient norm for sample importance is well-known, limiting novelty.
- Training cost comparison with prior QAT methods is missing; the paper only reports sample ratios, not real computation cost (including coreset selection, KD, SGD training) relative to other SOTA QAT approaches.
- Additional concerns raised by reviewers: (a) need for experimental results without knowledge distillation; (b) clarify the difference between EVS and Shapley values; (c) check whether the preconditions for simplification/approximation hold for ordinary QAT networks; (d) clarify how the method is specifically related to QAT given the lack of QAT-specific design.
