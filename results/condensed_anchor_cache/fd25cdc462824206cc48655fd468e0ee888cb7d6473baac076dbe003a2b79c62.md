## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper studies the expressivity of Topological Deep Learning (TDL) architectures, focusing on limitations of Higher-Order Message Passing (HOMP) for distinguishing combinatorial complexes. It identifies topological blindspots (diameter, orientability, planarity, homology) and proposes two new models: Multi-Cellular Networks (MCN) and Scalable Multi-Cellular Networks (SMCN). All three reviewers rate the paper highly (score 8) and emphasize its novelty, rigorous theoretical contributions, practical benchmarks, and empirical validation. They agree the work addresses an important gap in TDL. However, they also note significant weaknesses in presentation clarity, limited empirical scope, missing runtime scaling analysis, insufficient connection to related work (graph expressivity, cycle-invariant positional encodings), and a need for better explanation of architectural choices and empirical gaps.

### Strengths

- **Novelty and relevance**: The paper presents a fresh, essential perspective that differentiates TDL from graph deep learning and bridges TDL with computational topology. It is of significant urgency for the TDL community.
- **Strong theoretical contribution**: Provides a rigorous examination of HOMP’s expressivity limitations regarding fundamental topological and metric invariants. The analysis of CC coverings and the proof that HOMP cannot distinguish certain complexes are sound and satisfying.
- **New architectures**: MCN theoretically achieves full expressivity, while SMCN offers a computationally scalable alternative that still mitigates many HOMP limitations. The design is inspired by expressive graph architectures (IGN, subgraph GNNs).
- **Empirical validation**: The models are validated on synthetic and real-world benchmarks designed to test topological expressivity. Experiments control for parameter budgets to show effectiveness. SMCN demonstrates substantial improvements over standard HOMP and expressive GNNs.
- **Novel benchmarks**: The paper introduces synthetic benchmarks that test models on topological invariants, serving as a valuable tool for the TDL community. Three real-world graph datasets are used.
- **Acknowledgment of limitations**: The paper honestly discusses MCN’s unscalability and proposes SMCN as a remedy.

### Weaknesses

- **Poor presentation and lack of self-containment**: The architecture descriptions are opaque, especially for readers unfamiliar with TDL. Section 5 and Figure 5 do not clearly explain how multi-cellular feature spaces and equivariant linear updates are constructed in practice (Reviewer 1). Equations (1), (2), (3) lack sufficient explanation of their practical implications and relation to topological properties (Reviewer 2). Notation with multiple superscripts/subscripts is confusing (e.g., Figure 7 superscripts, group action indices) (Reviewer 2). The paper should be more self-contained: provide formulas/examples for topological invariants (diameter, orientability, planarity, homology) instead of only descriptions (Reviewer 3), and include an illustrative example for CC covering (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited empirical evaluation**: Benchmarks are limited to three-world datasets, which is insufficient to assess generalizability to more complex real-world scenarios (Reviewer 1). Missing expansions such as TUDatasets or trajectory classification tasks from Bodnar et al. (2021) (Reviewer 1, question 3). The paper lacks runtime and scalability comparisons: no wall-clock training/inference time experiment to compare SMCN’s performance against HOMP, CIN, and the backbone subgraph GNN (Reviewers 2 and 3, questions 3). There is no detailed breakdown of computational cost across pipeline stages (construction, subgraph GNN, message passing) or how runtime scales with complex size (number of cells, faces, edges, vertices, maximum dimension) (Reviewer 3). Lifting time for topological constructions is also neglected (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing connections to related work**: The paper insufficiently relates to graph neural network expressivity frameworks (e.g., Weisfeiler-Lehman test) and does not provide a smooth transition from graph GNNs to TDL (Reviewer 2). Bamberger’s (2020) result is only briefly mentioned in Section 4.1; an earlier citation in related work would help contextualize the approach (Reviewer 1, question 6). The paper does not compare with or discuss cycle-invariant positional encoding (Yan et al., 2024), where cell/cycle features are encoded via an invariant network and used as edge features in a standard GNN (Reviewer 3, question 3).
- **Lack of guidelines and ambiguous design choices**: It is unclear which multi-cellular nodes and connections to include in MCN/SMCN; practitioners need recommendations (Reviewer 1, question 2). Figure 5’s colored nodes are insufficiently explained (Reviewer 2, question 1). The group actions in Section 5 are introduced without clarifying whether they must be compatible with the underlying complex (e.g., are permutations that fix higher-dimensional simplices allowed?) (Reviewer 1, question 4).
- **Discrepancy between theoretical expressivity and empirical accuracy**: SMCN does not achieve full accuracy on cross-diameter and second Betti number tasks. The paper does not explore possible reasons (e.g., optimization, hyperparameters, subgraph GNN architecture limitations) (Reviewer 3). Further analysis, error analysis, or visualization of learned representations is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Rationale for topological invariants**: The paper does not sufficiently justify why models should differentiate topological invariants like diameter, orientability, planarity, and homology for machine learning tasks. A discussion of empirical evidence or theoretical reasoning linking these invariants to downstream performance is missing (Reviewer 3). It should also clarify why existing powerful GNN architectures do not already capture these invariants (Reviewer 3).
- **Code availability**: Code is not yet provided (Reviewer 1, question 5), which hinders reproduction and extension.
