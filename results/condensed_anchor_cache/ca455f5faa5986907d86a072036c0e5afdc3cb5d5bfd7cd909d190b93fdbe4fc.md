## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses graph-level out-of-distribution (OOD) detection, an under-explored area in GNNs. It proposes SGOOD, a framework that exploits substructure differences between ID and OOD graphs. SGOOD constructs a super graph of substructures, uses a two-level encoding pipeline on both the original graph and super graph, and applies substructure-preserving graph augmentations with contrastive learning to obtain substructure-enhanced graph representations. Experiments against several state-of-the-art methods show strong performance, often surpassing baselines by a significant margin.

### Strengths
- The approach of exploiting substructure differences for graph-level OOD detection is novel and reasonable.
- The design of substructure-enhanced representation learning and substructure-preserving augmentation is interesting.
- The paper is well-written, clearly presented, and well-organized.
- Empirical results are strong, outperforming several state-of-the-art baselines across multiple datasets.
- The paper includes competitive baselines, covering recent graph-level OOD detection algorithms and an underexplored research area.
- Addresses an important and relatively uncharted problem in trustworthy graph learning.

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and justification:** The motivation for improving OOD detection by encoding more substructure information is unclear. One reviewer (R3) strongly questions this, pointing out that in practice theoretically more powerful GNNs (NGNN, GNN-AK+) underperform 1-WL equivalent GNNs (SAG, TopK, DiffPool) on graph-level OOD detection (Table 7). This casts doubt on whether encoding more substructure is the key factor.
- **Task-agnostic substructure claim:** The claim that the extracted substructures (via modularity) are “task-agnostic” is questionable. These methods may implicitly encode biases related to graph structure that correlate with task-specific information (e.g., community structure that aligns with class labels in social networks). Relevance depends on the dataset, limiting generalizability. The paper does not analyze sensitivity to different substructure definitions.
- **Lack of distribution definition:** The paper does not clearly define “graph distribution” or explore what causes distribution differences between ID and OOD graphs. It overemphasizes substructures while neglecting the role of node attributes—two graphs with identical structure but different features can have entirely different distributions.
- **Incremental nature and unclear contribution:** The method appears incremental and redundant; it resembles a new powerful GNN rather than a specialized OOD detector. The specific contributions of SGOOD over existing powerful GNNs are not delineated. Theoretical contributions are weak.
- **Augmentation concerns:** Substructure-preserving augmentations may alter graph semantics or disrupt higher-order relationships and global graph properties that are crucial for OOD detection. Adding/removing edges while preserving substructures could introduce noise or make the model sensitive to other types of semantic shifts. Further explanation of what “more information” means and whether all substructure information should be encoded is needed.
- **Missing experiments and ablations:**
  - Performance on larger datasets and computation cost compared to other methods are not reported.
  - Sensitivity to the number of negative samples and how negative samples for contrastive loss are constructed is unclear.
  - No ablation comparing different substructure extraction methods or learning-based alternatives (e.g., hypergraph learning, graph pooling, causal learning).
- **Inappropriate or unclear baseline comparisons:**
  - GNNsafe is designed for node-level OOD detection; it is not clear how it is adapted to graph-level tasks, and the authors state it cannot be directly compared without sufficient justification.
  - OCGIN, OCGTL, and GLocalKD are primarily designed for graph anomaly detection, not graph-level OOD detection, making their inclusion as baselines questionable.
- **Presentation clarity issues:** Figure 1 does not effectively convey how SGOOD is specifically tailored for graph-level OOD detection. Table 1 (substructure statistics) lacks clarity and relies heavily on prior knowledge to interpret.
- **Minority critical perspective:** While R1 and R2 find the approach novel and the results compelling, R3 is more critical, highlighting fundamental weaknesses in motivation, theoretical grounding, and empirical evidence that challenge the core premise of the paper.
