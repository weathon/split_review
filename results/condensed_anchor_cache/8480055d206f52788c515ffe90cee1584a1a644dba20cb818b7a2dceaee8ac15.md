## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces REPresentation Alignment (REPA), a regularization that aligns noisy hidden states of diffusion transformers (DiT, SiT) with clean representations from a pretrained self-supervised visual encoder (e.g., DINOv2). Motivated by observed correlations between diffusion model features and discriminative features, and the finding that better representations correlate with better generation quality, REPA yields significant training speedups (e.g., SiT-XL matches 7M-step performance in under 400K steps → 17.5× speedup) and achieves state-of-the-art FID=1.80 on ImageNet 256×256 with classifier-free guidance. Reviewers found the idea novel, the experiments thorough, and the method simple and effective, but raised concerns about evaluation only at 256×256, generalizability to text-conditioned models, metric reliability (FID), missing ablations, encoder pretraining leakage, and lack of limitations section.

### Strengths
- **Novel and well-motivated idea**: The key finding – that self-supervised representations (DINOv2) can serve as a teacher to distill base features in a diffusion model – is novel and insightful. The paper clearly motivates REPA by first showing a semantic gap between diffusion transformer features and discriminative features (Figure 2), then showing correlation between representation quality and generation quality, leading naturally to the alignment solution. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4,5,6)
- **Thorough and extensive experiments**: Ablations cover encoder type (DINOv2, CLIP, MoCo v3, DINO, MAE), encoder scale, alignment depth (layers), alignment objectives (Cosine similarity, NT-Xent, L2, etc.), and model sizes for both DiT and SiT. All reviewers note the comprehensiveness and strong evidence across settings. (Reviewers 1,2,3,5,6)
- **Significant training speedup and final performance**: REPA dramatically accelerates training – e.g., SiT-XL/2 in 400K steps matches the performance of a model trained for 7M steps – and achieves state-of-the-art FID=1.80 on ImageNet 256×256 with CFG. The benefit is consistent across model sizes and training durations. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4,5,6)
- **Simple and easy to implement**: The solution is a straightforward additional alignment loss between diffusion features and a fixed pretrained encoder, making it a neat, efficient technique for speeding up diffusion model training. (Reviewers 1,4,5)
- **Insightful correlation analysis between generation and discriminative performance**: The paper shows a linear correlation between FID and linear probing accuracy on ImageNet, demonstrating that models trained with REPA achieve better generation quality alongside better representations. This insight is valuable and novel. (Reviewers 3,6)
- **Clean writing and presentation**: The paper is well-written with clear reasoning, easy-to-follow structure, and clear experimental tables (e.g., Table 2). (Reviewers 2,6)

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited to 256×256 resolution**: All experiments are on ImageNet 256×256. Multiple reviewers (1,4,5,6) strongly request evaluation at higher resolutions (512×512, 1024×2048). Reviewer 1 suggests using ADM’s Inception FID npz for 512×512 and DINO-FID for 256×256. Without this, the application scenario is restricted to low resolution. (Reviewers 1,4,5,6)
2. **Unclear generalization to text-conditioned models**: The paper only tests class-conditioned diffusion models (ImageNet). Reviewers (1,2,3,6) question whether REPA benefits text-to-image or text-to-video models. Reviewer 2 argues text itself already provides strong regularization, making extra representation alignment potentially unnecessary. Reviewer 3 asks whether REPA would conflict with cross-attention alignment (e.g., CLIP/T5) when using DINOv2 representations. Reviewer 1 suggests experiments with detailed textual descriptions (e.g., CUB-200). This limits the claimed generality. (Reviewers 1,2,3,6)
3. **FID reliability and metric concerns**: Reviewer 1 recommends DINO-FID because FID relies on Inception features that lose spatial/class information. Reviewer 6 notes that different metrics (FID, sFID, IS) show differing trends (e.g., in Table 2, NT-Xent outperforms in sFID but Cosine similarity in FID/IS), making it hard to assess true quality. The choice of NT-Xent is justified only with “Empirically, ...” without deeper analysis. (Reviewers 1,6)
4. **Missing ablations and analyses**:
   - **Feature visualizations**: Reviewer 1 requests visualizations of features before and after alignment to directly demonstrate the impact at the feature level.
   - **Different diffusion objectives**: Reviewer 1 asks about effects for ε‑prediction vs v‑prediction vs x₀‑prediction.
   - **CFG analysis**: Reviewer 3 wants results with classifier-free guidance included (e.g., in Figure 1) to understand benefit when strong guidance is present.
   - **Time-varying REPA**: Reviewer 3 asks whether a time‑dependent alignment weight could improve performance.
   - **Layer choice explanation**: Reviewers 3 and 6 note a discrepancy: before alignment, the minimum semantic gap is at later layers (e.g., layer 20), but after alignment, layer 8 (encoder) gives best metrics. They request explanation of why encoder layers work best despite noise in early layers, and clarification of whether Figure 3 trains one model per layer or evaluates a single model across layers.
   - **Choice of NT-Xent objective**: Reviewer 6 asks for experimental results or reasoning beyond “Empirically, ...” (line 421).
5. **Potential dataset leakage**: Reviewer 5 raises concern that pretrained vision encoders (e.g., DINOv2) are often fine‑tuned on ImageNet, and training REPA on the same dataset may leak dataset‑specific information, complicating evaluation of generation quality. (Reviewer 5)
6. **Missing dataset analysis on encoder**: Reviewer 5 asks whether REPA’s benefit holds when the encoder is pretrained on in‑the‑wild datasets (not just ImageNet). (Reviewer 5)
7. **Missing limitations/future work section**: Reviewer 3 notes the absence of such a section, which would help the community tackle remaining problems. (Reviewer 3)
8. **Minor presentation issues**:
   - Formula (8) has a parenthesis error (Reviewer 6).
   - The definition of layers in Eq. (8) is unclear; Reviewer 6 suggests using notation $h^{[n,m]}$ for patch index and layer index.
   - Reviewers 2 and 6 suggest adding relevant references: [a] *Learning Disentangled Representation by Exploiting Pretrained Generative Models* (ICLR 2022), [b] *DisDiff* (NeurIPS 2023), and [1] *Masked Diffusion Transformer* (MDT, ICCV 2023).
9. **Lack of code release**: Reviewer 1 recommends releasing training and evaluation code to support future research. (Reviewer 1)
10. **Applicability only to diffusion transformers**: Reviewer 4 asks why the method is limited to DiT/SiT and whether it can be applied to more general diffusion models (e.g., U‑Net based). (Reviewer 4)
