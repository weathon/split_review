## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper describes a Python package for off-policy evaluation (OPE) focused on importance sampling methods, with features for real-world data (uneven trajectory length), novel metrics (VWP, WeightStd), and a compute-efficient API. Reviewers acknowledge the package’s potential utility and appreciate its API design, cross-validation against existing implementations, and flowcharts. However, they raise significant concerns about paper completeness, clarity of contributions, and insufficient experimental validation. One reviewer (rating 1) considers the paper not ready for publication—lacking a clear research question and being more of a technical report. Two other reviewers (both rating 3) find the work useful but with major weaknesses in presentation, missing calibration, ambiguous novelty claims, and weak continuous-action experiments.

### Strengths
- The proposed Python package provides a useful, easy-to-use toolbox for OPE with a well-designed API that abstracts around key OPE calculations (R1, R2).
- It fills gaps compared to existing libraries (e.g., Scope-RL) by naturally handling uneven trajectory lengths (R2).
- The cross-validation benchmarks against other implementations (Scope-RL, own implementations) lend credibility to algorithmic correctness (R2).
- The paper includes a flowchart showing module connections and discusses some technique details of existing work (R1).
- Even the most critical reviewer acknowledges the paper provides an extensive explanation of the software package (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and completeness issues** (all reviewers): The paper is poorly organized and appears hastily written. Figure 2’s caption is entirely missing; Figure 1 has caption alignment problems; there are typos (e.g., “integrogate,” “effected”). Section 4 (line 380) has grammar issues, and Equation numbering is incomplete (e.g., self-normalized weight equation lacks a label and notation for ε). Table 5 has duplicated columns and lacks explanation of what “difference” means. Line 794 contains “??”. These errors undermine the paper’s professionalism (R1, R2, R3).
- **Unclear contribution and insufficient novelty** (all reviewers): The paper does not clearly state a research question or problem. The claimed contributions are ambiguous—are metrics like VWP and WeightStd novel? This is not stated in the abstract or introduction (R2). The overall contribution is small given prior art (Scope-RL, d3rlpy) (R2). The paper reads more as a technical description than a research paper (R3).
- **Missing calibration and absolute-quality validation** (R1, R2): The paper does not calibrate OPE estimates against ground truth (e.g., via MAE or calibration curves). For continuous action spaces, only ranking (Spearman correlation) is reported, not absolute accuracy; the correlations (0.3–0.5) are low, casting doubt on the claim that estimators “accurately rank” policies (R2). No justification is given for why continuous-action implementations could not be directly compared with Scope-RL (R2). Experimental results lack repeated-run statistics (mean ± std) and are insufficient to conclude package usefulness (R1, R3).
- **Methodological weaknesses in experiments**: Table 6–8 values are presented as single numbers without indication of variance or number of trials; more convincing reporting would include mean ± std (R1). The experimental validation on continuous actions is difficult to assess, and the reliance on ranking does not provide a clear picture of estimator accuracy (R2). The overall experimental results are insufficient to draw any conclusion about the package’s practical value (R3).
- **Minority disagreement**: One reviewer (rating 1) finds the paper incomplete and inconclusive, stating it is not suitable for publication, while the other two (rating 3) see potential but with major reservations. This contrast highlights the need for a major revision to meet research paper standards (R3 vs. R1/R2).
- **Unaddressed reviewer questions that overlap with weaknesses**: 
  - How are VWP, WeightStd claimed as novel contributions? (R2)
  - Why could continuous-action OPE not be directly compared with Scope-RL? (R2)
  - Are the implemented IS and DR estimators calibrated against original papers? (R1)
  - In Table 5, what does the “difference” mean? (R1)
  - Why are Spearman ranking correlations low in some instances? (R2)
  - Number of experimental runs? (R1)
