## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a self-supervised dataset distillation method built on KRR-ST, introducing three techniques: (1) low-dimensional basis parameterization (via PCA) of images and representations; (2) predefined augmentations to mitigate randomness bias in SSL; (3) approximation networks that model the shift from unaugmented to augmented views, reducing memory. The approach targets cross-architecture generalizability and shows strong performance on CIFAR, Tiny ImageNet, CUB2011, StanfordDogs, and ImageNet at 64×64, with ViT results.

### Strengths
- **Diverse datasets and architectures**: Experiments include CUB2011, StanfordDogs, and ViT performance – unusual in dataset distillation papers – and overall gains are maintained on Transformers (R1).  
- **Strategic parameterization**: Using low-dimensional bases for images and representations compresses information without sacrificing accuracy, addressing both storage and cost (R1, R2). The ablation on basis/coefficient initialization reveals sensitivity and insight (R1).  
- **Effective augmentation handling**: Predefining augmentations successfully reduces the bias introduced by random augmentations, a key challenge in SSL distillation (R1, R2, R4).  
- **Memory efficiency via approximation networks**: Learning the mapping from unaugmented to augmented views eliminates storing augmented representations – a clever solution that reduces memory usage (R1, R2).  
- **Transfer learning potential**: The method shows strong downstream task transferability, making it attractive for real-world applications with scarce labels (R2).  
- **Ablation studies and hyperparameter analysis**: Isolating the contributions of parameterization, augmentation, and approximation networks gives clear insight into each component’s impact (R1, R2).  
- **Topic value and generality**: Reducing dataset size is critical in SSL; working on self-supervised tasks (rather than only classification) supports broader feature learning for downstream applications (R3, R4).  
- **Well-written and clear**: The method is straightforward, and each component’s rationale is explained (R3).  
- **Wide-ranging experiments**: Evaluation includes multiple network architectures and varying numbers of training examples (R4).  
- **Strong baselines**: The competitive methods surveyed are comprehensive and include seminal and recent SOTA works (R1).  

One reviewer (score 8, confidence 3) was particularly positive about the transfer learning potential and ablation analyses; other reviewers (scores 5–6) were more reserved, especially regarding novelty and experimental coverage.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient scaling experiments**: Performance should be shown across multiple memory budgets (e.g., N = 10, 50, 100, 200, 500) on several datasets (CIFAR‑10, CIFAR‑100, Tiny ImageNet) to demonstrate robustness and scalability (R1).  
- **Limited resolution**: ImageNet is only evaluated at 64×64; the field now uses 128×128 or 512×512. The method’s scalability to higher resolutions, and associated computational/memory costs, are not tested (R1).  
- **Missing downstream applications**: The paper mentions continual learning and neural architecture search (line 43) but provides no experiments for such tasks, which are important for determining practical impact (R1).  
- **No efficiency metrics**: Training time per step, GPU memory usage, and a breakdown of computational cost across the five stages are not reported. Without these, it is unclear whether the performance gain justifies the compute – especially given the multi-level optimization (R1, R2). Detailed trade-off analysis between memory savings (via approximation networks) and added forward/backward passes is needed (R2).  
- **Complexity and accessibility**: The method relies on careful selection of the number of PCA components and predefined augmentations; the augmentation selection process is not clearly defined and may require significant experimentation for new datasets, making the approach hard to implement without deep expertise (R2).  
- **Limited dataset diversity in evaluation**: Experiments focus on CIFAR‑100 and similar well-structured benchmarks. The method’s performance on data with noisy labels, varying image quality, different modalities, or real-world variability (e.g., randomly taken images) is not explored (R2).  
- **Novelty concerns**: The core techniques (PCA parameterization, augmentation approximation networks) are largely adapted from prior supervised dataset distillation and existing SSL methods. The authors need to clarify what unique challenges self-supervised distillation poses and how their method specifically addresses those challenges (R3, R4).  
- **Missing comparisons with analogous SSL methods**: The paper should compare against methods that also incorporate data augmentation in SSL distillation or use similar ideas (e.g., [1][2][3] as indicated by R4; these references are not provided).  
- **Missing citations**: DataDAM (ICCV’23) and CAFE (CVPR’22) should be cited (R1).  
- **Unanswered details on approximation networks**: The number of networks, their structure, and number of layers are not given (R4).  
- **No comparison of distilled data sizes**: The paper should report the storage size of distilled datasets versus competing methods (R4).
