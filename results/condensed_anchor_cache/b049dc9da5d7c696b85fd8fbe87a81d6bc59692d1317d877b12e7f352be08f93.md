## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a hybrid RL algorithm that integrates off-policy training on offline data into an on-policy Natural Policy Gradient (NPG) framework. The method uses a hybrid loss combining TD error from offline data and estimation error from online data. Theoretically, it claims a best-of-both-worlds guarantee: when offline RL assumptions (Bellman completeness and data coverage) hold, it matches offline RL rates; when not, it retains the on-policy NPG guarantee. Experiments on continuous and image-based Comblock environments show it outperforms pure on-policy TRPO and the hybrid off-policy baseline RLPD.

### Strengths
- **Novel algorithm design** – The approach of integrating offline data into an on-policy NPG framework for hybrid RL is interesting and novel (R1, R2, R3, R4). R2 and R4, however, note limited novelty (see Weaknesses).
- **Theoretical guarantees** – Provides guarantees for two distinct cases of Bellman Completeness. Several reviewers highlight that this is the first algorithm to achieve best-of-both-worlds guarantees for hybrid RL, allowing Bellman Completeness to be relaxed (R1, R3). R2 and R4 question the claim (see Weaknesses).
- **Strong empirical results** – Outperforms TRPO and RLPD on challenging rich-observation, hard-exploration environments (continuous and image-based Comblock) (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Well-written and clear** – The paper is easy to follow, with a well-justified motivation and clear exposition of algorithm design and theoretical results (R1, R3).
- **Timely contribution** – Hybrid RL is a quickly growing area; the algorithm and analysis are a significant addition (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty (disagreement)** – R2 and R4 find the algorithm a straightforward combination of natural actor-critic and hybrid-RL, following Song et al. (2022) and previous NPG literature, and note that the technical challenge in combining them is not clarified. R1 and R3 view the approach as novel.
- **Best-of-both-worlds claim disputed** – R2 argues that the NPG coverage (all-policy) is strong, while offline RL only needs single-policy coverage; since \(C_{off,\pi^e}^2 \leq C_{npg,\pi^e}\), when offline assumptions fail the online guarantee also fails, countering the claim. R4 adds that existing offline pessimism bounds depend on single-policy concentrability, so the bound is not best in pure-offline learning. R1 and R3 accept the claim.
- **Strong assumptions** – Realizability for all \(Q^\pi\) is stronger than typical value-based methods that only require realizability of \(Q^*\) (R2). The linear critic assumption in HNPG (Algorithm 3) is mainly for theory; relaxation to neural function approximation is not discussed or tested (R1).
- **Missing experimental comparisons** – No evaluation on mainstream hybrid RL benchmarks (e.g., D4RL used in RLPD and Cal-QL) or hard-exploration Atari games (e.g., Montezuma’s Revenge used in Song et al., 2022) (R1). No ablation on how the offline dataset coverage \(\nu\) affects performance (R4).
- **Presentation issue** – In Figure 2, the curves of different methods are difficult to differentiate (R3).
- **Incomplete or unclear theoretical discussion** – The definition and implications of NPG coverage (Definition 2) and the transfer coefficient (Definition 3) are not elaborated: it is unclear how large \(C_{off}\) can be, under what conditions on \(\nu\) it is bounded (R1), and whether NPG coverage can be weakened to reflect value function class dependence (R4). Conditions for finite \(C_{npg}\) without positivity of the reset distribution are ambiguous (R1).
- **Bootstrapping versus Monte Carlo** – The paper does not discuss whether using bootstrapping (instead of Monte Carlo) for \(y\) in Eq. (1) would preserve the sub-optimality guarantee (R1).
- **Theoretical-practical disconnect** – Theorem 1 suggests the algorithm should benefit more from offline data when Bellman completeness is not satisfied, but simulations show HNPG dominates regardless; clarification is needed on how theory explains real performance (R4). Also, what sufficient condition on the policy class ensures the same sample complexity bound? (R4). When offline guarantee fails, how is the on-policy guarantee retained? (R2). More insights on superior performance in Comblock are needed (R2).
