## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MatText, a benchmarking suite for evaluating language models on materials property prediction. It provides nine text-based material representations (several novel), standardized dataset splits at multiple scales, and tools for converting crystal structures into text. Experiments with BERT (small: 4 layers, 8 attention heads) and instruction-tuned LLaMA models (LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, LLaMA-2-13b-chat-hf) on MatBench tasks (e.g., shear modulus, bulk modulus, formation energy) find that LLMs struggle to leverage geometric information and instead rely on local information. Scaling pretraining data shows limited benefits, with improvement on only 2 of 3 tasks. The benchmark is designed to encourage further development of text-based methods for materials modeling.

### Strengths
- **Important and timely area.** Leveraging LLMs for materials reasoning is exciting and potentially impactful; the work correctly identifies the key challenge of representing composition and structure.
- **Useful standardization.** Providing precomputed representations and data splits is valuable and can support follow-up research.
- **Identifies a critical limitation.** The evaluation reveals that current LLMs struggle to utilize geometric information effectively, which is essential for materials modeling.
- **Comprehensive survey and tools.** Recent methods are surveyed, and the suite includes conversion tools and standardized evaluation infrastructure.
- **Clear writing.** The paper is generally well written and easy to follow.
- **Multi-aspect experiments.** Experiments compare different text representations, data sizes, and model families (BERT and LLaMA), and include tokenization analyses (in appendix).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited engagement with prior work on encoding structured data in LMs.** The paper uses only the most naive input-output format (encoded representation → numeric value). This is not a good-faith test of LLMs’ ability to reason about materials; known methods such as tool use, code-style snippets, or natural language queries (e.g., Robocrys) are not tried. Relevant existing work (Fatemi et al. 2023, Hegselmann et al. 2023, Li et al. 2024, Yin et al. 2023) is not discussed or implemented. RASP is mentioned only in conclusions but not experimented with.
- **Thin contribution over prior work.** The data comes from existing resources (NOMAD, MatBench); the main novelty is the set of text representations evaluated on the same MatBench tasks. No representation leads to an improvement over existing graph neural network methods. The benchmark’s relationship to MatBench is not clearly delineated early.
- **Limited analysis and weak claims.** The scaling claim (that scaling pretraining alone may not suffice) is based only on increasing data size, while scaling laws require model size and training compute to be scaled in tandem. The paper does not test larger models (e.g., proprietary/closed). BERT models are very small (4-layer, 8-head). The observation that LLMs focus on local information lacks a proposed mechanism or architectural change to overcome it.
- **Tokenization issues not adequately explored.** Standard tokenizers struggle with numerical data (atomic positions, lattice parameters). The tokenization experiment (appendix) is claimed to yield no meaningful improvement, but the paper does not show examples of tokenized inputs or confirm that the method from Born & Manica 2023 was properly implemented.
- **Inherent limitations of text representations for 3D geometry.** Text may be fundamentally limited for encoding complex 3D structural and physical properties. Representations like CIF add complexity without improving performance, making the evaluation arguably unfair to LLMs.
- **Narrow range of evaluated tasks.** Only a small subset of materials modeling abilities is tested; the benchmark is not comprehensive in modeling tasks.
- **Poor exposition for non-experts.** The paper shows only numerical scores without explaining task details or results, making it hard for readers unfamiliar with the materials domain to follow.
- **Overclaimed limitations given emergent abilities.** The strong claim that LLMs fundamentally cannot capture geometric information is undercut by known emergent abilities (Wei et al., TMLR 2022) and grokking phenomena; scaling might change the outcome.
- **Missing details on methodology.** Why were instruction fine-tuned models used without instruction-style input-output pairs? What prompt formats were tried? Why were BERT models that small? Why were 2D structures excluded from MatBench? The Local-Env representation may overlook long-range interactions and periodicity; the authors do not address how to mitigate this. The limitations discussion omits the breadth of models compared (only BERT and LLaMA) and the limited scaling scope.
