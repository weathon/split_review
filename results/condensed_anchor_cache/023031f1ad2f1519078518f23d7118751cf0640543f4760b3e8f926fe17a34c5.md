## Merged Review

### Summary
SplineGS introduces non-uniform rational B-splines (NURBS) to model temporally smooth trajectories of 3D Gaussians for dynamic scene reconstruction. A set of representative NURBS trajectories is learned, and each Gaussian’s trajectory is a linear combination of these, with combination weights predicted by a multi-resolution hash table and MLP. The method achieves competitive performance on the D-NeRF and Neu3D datasets with faster training than existing methods. Reviewers agree the approach is novel and effective, but some question the degree of novelty relative to prior spline- or Fourier-based representations and the isolation of the contribution from other components (hash grid, opacity regularization). All four reviewers assigned a score of 6, but confidence varied (4–5) and contribution ratings ranged from 2 to 3, indicating disagreement on the significance of the contribution.

### Strengths
- Novel use of NURBS to represent Gaussian blob trajectories, providing temporally smooth deformations without explicit regularizers (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Competitive or state-of-the-art rendering quality on both synthetic (D-NeRF) and real-world (Neu3D) dynamic scenes, with much shorter training time (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The paper is well written and the idea is easy to understand (R3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of direct visual evidence of trajectory smoothness** – No visualizations of Gaussian trajectories (center positions and orientation changes over time) compared with other methods (e.g., 4D‑GS, D‑3DGS). Only Figure 1 provides indirect support. Video results are also missing. (R1, R3, R4)
2. **Missing deformation of scaling** – The model only deforms centers and rotations, omitting scaling. This limits expressiveness for objects that change size or shape (e.g., expansion/contraction). (R1)
3. **Novelty limited and need for comparison with similar representations** – Prior work (Spline‑NeRF uses Bézier splines; Gaussian‑Flow uses Fourier) already uses spline-like or Fourier representations for trajectories. The paper must cite these, discuss why NURBS is advantageous over Bézier splines or Fourier in the context of Gaussian splatting, and clarify that the main contribution is not just NURBS but its combination with the hash‑grid/MLP architecture. Some reviewers see the method simply as an extension of 4D‑GS with a smoothness constraint. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
4. **Evaluation limited to datasets with small camera motion** – Experiments should be expanded to include scenes with more complex camera trajectories (e.g., HyperNeRF), where baseline methods like 4D‑GS and D‑3DGS are evaluated. (R1, R2)
5. **Unclear advantage of the multi‑resolution hash table and MLP for weight prediction** – Simpler methods, such as Gaussian kernel RBF used in SC‑GS, may achieve similar or better results. The paper provides no justification for the MLP approach, nor a comparison of computational cost and performance. (R3)
6. **Training speed and FPS gains may be due to L1 opacity regularization (Eq. 15) rather than the trajectory representation** – An ablation study isolating the impact of the NURBS representation on speed and quality is missing. (R3)
7. **Inefficiency from requiring additional points for rendering** – As a trade‑off for using B‑splines as a regularizer, rendering needs \(p+M+1\) additional points per Gaussian (line 231), whereas a direct smoother regularizer would not. A comparison of rendering speed vs. quality against methods that use explicit smoothness regularization is needed. (R4)
8. **Handling of object appearance/disappearance is unclear** – The conclusion states this is a limitation, yet results on Neu3D (e.g., the flame streak scene in Fig. 6) suggest disappearing objects are handled. An analysis of how the method manages such cases (e.g., by visualizing Gaussian trajectories) is required. (R4)
9. **Missing metrics and evaluation details** – LPIPS scores should be reported. On D‑NeRF, the “Lego” scene and performance on the validation set should not be reported (as noted by D‑3DGS). For the Neu3D dataset, SSIM and training time for 4D‑GS are missing. (R3, R4)
10. **Detailed statistics per dataset are missing** – The number of Gaussians per scene, number of representative trajectories, training time, and FPS for both D‑NeRF and Neu3D datasets should be provided. (R3)
11. **Failure cases** – Provide failure examples, e.g., performance on the “vrig‑broom” scene of HyperNeRF. (R3)
12. **Uneven performance across datasets** – SplineGS shows a significant advantage over 4D‑GS on D‑NeRF but only a small advantage (0.03 dB PSNR over STG) on Neu3D. This inconsistency requires explanation. (R2, R3)
13. **Flame Salmon scene** – Results using only the first 300 frames (as done in 4D‑GS, STG, Gaussian‑Flow) should be reported. (R4)
14. **Trajectory results on Neu3D** – Provide trajectory visualizations for real‑world scenes. (R4)
