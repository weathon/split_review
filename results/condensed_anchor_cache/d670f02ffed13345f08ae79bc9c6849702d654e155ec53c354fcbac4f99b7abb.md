## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Latte, a linear-complexity attention mechanism based on a latent variable model that reparameterises softmax attention by independently applying softmax to Q and K matrices and introducing a fixed number L of latent tokens. Both bidirectional and causal variants are derived, with a causal update enabling constant-time next-token prediction. A hybrid model (Latte-Macchiato) combines local sliding-window attention with global Latte and an RG-LRU gating layer. The method is evaluated via small-scale language modeling on 8B tokens of OpenWebText, Long Range Arena (LRA), and up-training experiments extending Gemma 2.6B.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow; the derivation of bidirectional and causal forms is succinct and well-motivated.
- The approach is novel among recent linear-attention proposals; the idea of replacing softmax(QK^T) with softmax(Q)softmax(K)^T has potential.
- The method shows competitive performance on small-scale perplexity and reasonable results on LRA compared to other recent efficient attention ops.
- Connections to prior work (vanilla attention, SSMs, linear attention) are clearly drawn.
- The causal variant is important for autoregressive training and inference; experimental transparency on hyperparameters, training code, and experimental details is commendable.
- Including literal code in the appendix is good practice.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and interpretation** (R2, R4): The concept is similar to Efficient Attention (Linear Complexity) from vision, and the shift to 1D language modeling offers limited novelty. The “latent variable” interpretation is not convincingly justified: the casual graph is x → (q,k) → l, making l a learned cluster center determined by q,k. The claim that s and t are independent given l (Definition 1) is invalid because s and t iterate the same set of tokens sharing a parent (x). The overall operation is a clustering, not a probabilistic latent variable; proper causal inference is needed to support the claim. (R4 minority: stronger stance that the math is unsound; R2: the interpretation lacks clear justification.)
- **Missing comparisons** (R1, R2, R3): Important linear/efficient attention baselines are missing: HGRN, HGRN2, Lightning Attention, GLA (R2). Prior fine-tuning/adaptation works (T2R, Hedgehog, SUPRA) are not discussed (R3). For a fair evaluation, Latte should be compared with other models that include the same extra components (SWA and RG-LRU) to isolate the benefit of the core Latte mechanism (R1). The distillation experiments at 2.6B are insufficient to assess scaling; experiments at larger scales and with standard natural language evaluations are needed (R2, R3).
- **Evaluation gaps for long-context and retrieval** (R2, R3): The paper lacks long-context natural language evaluations, such as Scrolls or “Needle in a Haystack” to test retrieval capability (R2). Standard NL benchmarks (Hellaswag, ARC, MMLU) are missing, especially MMLU where linear variants have struggled (R3). The up-training experiment on Gemma 2.6B should be evaluated on these tasks to verify performance preservation at short context and improvement at long context (R3).
- **Implementation and speed comparison issues** (R1, R2, R4): It is unclear whether standard causal attention baseline uses FlashAttention (Dao et al.) or a naive implementation for runtime comparisons. If not, the speed figures are misleading (R1, R2, R4). Comparisons with state-of-the-art linear attention variants and with FlashAttention itself are missing (R2, R4). In Figure 4, the forward pass time appears almost constant; the reason should be explained (R4).
- **Dependence on SWA and LRU** (R1): The method's performance heavily relies on sliding-window attention and RG-LRU, making it unclear whether Latte has a clear advantage over simpler approaches like Luna without those components. The causal variant also requires Jax for parallel training, limiting independence from the framework.
- **Double-counting in Equation 13** (R4): The hybrid Latte-Macchiato adds sliding window attention (local) to the global Latte mechanism, double-counting the attention weights for nearby tokens. This over-emphasizes local tokens and voids the mechanism as a valid attention distribution (sum of scores > 1).
- **Sequence extrapolation** (R1, R3): The reported extrapolation ability may be largely due to sliding-window attention; it is unclear whether Latte itself extrapolates well. In Figure 6, the vanilla attention baseline should be extended using the YaRN fine-tuning–free trick for a fair comparison with the extended Latte model (R3).
- **Hyperparameters and clarity** (R4): The number of latent variables L is a hyperparameter; its scaling with sequence length is not discussed, nor is the specific value used in experiments reported. The notion of “collapsing” in Figure 7 needs explanation and citation (R4).
- **Missing ablation** (R1, R4): A direct comparison between Latte-Macchiato and a model using only sliding-window attention (window size 128) is missing (R4). Fair comparison requires replacing Latte with Luna while keeping SWA and RG-LRU identical (R1).
- **Typo** (R1): line 69: ∑^T should be ∑^t.
