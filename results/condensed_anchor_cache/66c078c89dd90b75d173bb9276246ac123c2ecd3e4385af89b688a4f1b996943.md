## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Mamba-HMIL, a hierarchical multiple instance learning method for whole slide image (WSI) classification using state space models (Mamba). It consists of three modules: hierarchical feature extraction (multi-resolution), a Mamba block with mixture of experts (MoE) for capturing patch correlations, and an adaptive selection module to focus on disease-positive regions. The method is evaluated on two subtyping datasets (TCGA-NSCLC, TCGA-RCC) and two survival prediction datasets (TCGA-BRCA, TCGA-BLCA) against many baselines (ABMIL, CLAM, DSMIL, HIPT, TransMIL, Mamba-MIL, Mamba extensions to MIL, and multimodal methods like MCAT, PORPOISE, PIBD). The paper addresses the important problem of WSI diagnosis but faces criticism regarding novelty, motivation, clarity, and experimental justification.

### Strengths
- Comprehensive experimental design with comparisons to many relevant baselines, including direct Mamba extensions (Mamba+ABMIL, Mamba+DSMIL, Mamba+CLAM-MB, Mamba-MIL) and survival prediction methods (MCAT, PIBD). (Reviewer 1)
- Consistent and strong performance: Mamba-HMIL achieves the best results across all four tasks. (Reviewer 1)
- Unimodal Mamba-HMIL outperforms several multimodal fusion baselines (Mamba+CLAM-SB, PORPOISE, MCAT) – a finding worth highlighting. (Reviewer 1)
- Good depth of ablations: components (MoE, SF/AF), different pretrained encoders (ResNet-50 vs UNI), and hierarchical feature extraction (10X, 20X, 10X+20X). (Reviewer 1)
- Addresses a practically important problem (WSI diagnosis). (Reviewer 2)
- Expands on prior MambaMIL work by incorporating multi-resolution feature aggregation, MoE, and adaptive selection. (Reviewer 4)
- Evaluated across two benchmark problems (subtyping and survival prediction) with comparisons to existing MIL models. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty**: The work largely combines existing methods. The hierarchical encoder is similar to DSMIL; the Mamba block and MoE module are identical to prior designs; the adaptive selection module is only an MLP+Sigmoid. Several studies have already applied Mamba to WSI analysis (e.g., MamMIL, Vim4Path). (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **Insufficient comparison with similar SSM methods**: The paper does not compare to Vim4Path or MamMIL. (Reviewer 3)
- **Inconsistent baseline performance**: Mamba-MIL and HIPT on NSCLC achieve AUC > 0.95 in their original papers but perform significantly lower here. This discrepancy is not explained. (Reviewer 3)
- **No clear motivation for design choices**: It is unclear why each component (Mamba, MoE, hierarchical features, adaptive selection) is beneficial or how they generalize. The paper suggests extensive hyperparameter selection without discussing why those choices are optimal. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Poor writing quality**: Numerous typos (e.g., "Propoise" for PORPOISE), missing-or-extra spacing after Mamba-HMIL, confusing question marks on lines 67 and 297, missing citations marked as "?", and an incorrect dataset name (TCGA-LUSC instead of TCGA-BLCA in line 327). (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Missing crucial implementation details**: No clear equations for SSM, MoE, or adaptive selection. Key questions unanswered: How are features from different resolutions aggregated (addition or concatenation)? How are sequences of instances generated? How is Figure 3 generated and what does GAS aggregation refer to? (Reviewer 4)
- **No evidence that MoE stabilizes SSM training**: Direct Mamba extensions already work well; no ablation with/without MoE is shown, nor any demonstration of instability. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Unclear role of Mamba vs attention**: Ablations add Mamba to existing MIL models, but there is no discussion of whether improvements are simply due to additional parameters. No comparison controlling for number of parameters. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing analysis of token interactions**: Attention weights from global attention pooling are visualized, but token-to-token interactions across Mamba layers are not shown. (Reviewer 1)
- **Fairness of a comparison**: Section 4.4 compares Mamba-HMIL to ABMIL with an ImageNet-pretrained encoder to evaluate the Mamba block; it is unclear whether this is a fair comparison. (Reviewer 3)
- **Code not available for review**, limiting empirical validation. (Reviewer 1)
- **Lack of discussion on why adding more resolutions hampers performance** and what the different sequences represent. (Reviewer 4)
