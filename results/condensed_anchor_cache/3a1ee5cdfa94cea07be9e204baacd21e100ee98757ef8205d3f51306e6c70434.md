## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a multi-condition guided framework for character image animation, targeting instability in complex backgrounds and multi-character scenarios. It introduces three additional guiders: an optical flow guider (to decouple background motion into constants and momentum), a depth order guider (to provide positional information via a skeletal dilation map and depth ordering), and a reference pose map (to better decouple character texture and pose). The network design follows prior work (ReferenceNet, denoising U-Net, temporal module). A new multi-character benchmark (~4,000 frames) is introduced. The method shows improved perceptual quality in qualitative and quantitative evaluations, particularly for two-character settings, with ablation studies verifying each component. Scores are 5, 6, 6, 6; one reviewer (Rev1) is notably more critical, calling for more experimental evidence and clarification.

### Strengths
1. **Well-motivated design for background and multi-character challenges.** The optical flow guider mitigates spurious correlations from noisy backgrounds; the depth order guider disambiguates spatial arrangement in multi-character settings; and the reference pose guider improves texture/pose decoupling (Rev2, Rev3, Rev4).
2. **Simple yet effective depth-order pipeline.** The heuristic depth-order map calculation (using dilated skeletons with depth ordering) provides spatial information without requiring segmentation masks, and its advantage over masks is explained (Rev3, Rev4).
3. **Optical flow conditioning enables training on in-the-wild data.** This allows the method to scale to noisy real-world datasets (Rev3).
4. **Impressive animation results.** Qualitative and quantitative comparisons show better quality than existing methods, especially for two-character animation and complex backgrounds (Rev1, Rev2, Rev4). The effectiveness of each component is confirmed by ablation studies (Rev2, Rev4).
5. **Novel multi-character benchmark.** The proposed benchmark fills a gap in fair evaluation of multi-character image animation (all reviewers note but not all mention; from paper, included for completeness).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited multi-character demonstration.** Most results show only one or two characters; examples with more than two characters are missing, and the claim of handling “multiple” characters is not fully supported (Rev1, Rev3). No in-depth analysis of the effect of character count or occlusion ratios (Rev3).
2. **Pose sequences for multiple characters are identical.** In two-character results, both characters follow the same pose sequence; results with different pose sequences for each character are not provided (Rev1).
3. **Head poses are static in qualitative results.** In all shown examples (Figures and supplementary videos), the head poses match the reference image while other body parts are retargeted. Without diverse head poses, assessment of facial animation quality is difficult (Rev4). This is a main concern given faces are the hardest part.
4. **Depth order ambiguity not fully resolved.** In supplementary video (00:44), the top-left character’s hand is rendered incorrectly to the back, indicating residual depth-ordering errors (Rev2).
5. **Computational cost is high.** Training requires 8 A100 GPUs (line 417-420); inference cost and feasibility on commodity GPUs (e.g., Nvidia 2060/3070) are not reported (Rev2). This is common among diffusion methods but still a limitation.
6. **Unclear source of performance gains in Table 1.** The proposed model trained on a noisy web-collected dataset outperforms AnimateAnyone trained on the clean TikTok dataset. Without ablating the dataset effect (e.g., training the proposed model on TikTok), it is impossible to know whether the improvement stems from the proposed modules or from the larger/noisier dataset (Rev3). The impact of pose diversity, data scale, and character domain diversity is not analyzed (Rev3).
7. **Skeletal dilation map restricts clothing animation.** Clothes covering a larger area than the dilation map may not be animated correctly; the kernel size’s impact on clothing quality is not investigated (Rev4).
8. **Method focuses on front-facing poses.** Performance on extreme poses (360° rotation, handstands, significant character interactions) is not evaluated (Rev2, Rev1).
9. **Network architecture description is unclear.** Figure 3 could be improved to better demonstrate the architecture, as the design closely follows prior methods (MagicAnimate) (Rev1).
10. **Missing references in Figure 2.** The specific previous methods used for the issues shown in Figure 2 should be cited (Rev1).
11. **Inference procedure for depth order map not fully specified.** How the depth order is determined during inference given only the skeleton poses of multiple characters needs clarification (Rev4). Similarly, how the reference pose guider handles a variable number of characters is unclear (Rev2).
12. **Reviewer disagreement (minority more negative).** Reviewer 1 (score 5, soundness 2) is less convinced than the others (scores 6, soundness 3), finding the depth order claim confusing and the experiments insufficient. This reviewer calls for more results with different pose sequences and more than two characters, and improved presentation of the architecture.
