## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers evaluated a paper applying topological data analysis (TDA)—specifically persistent homology and Betti curve similarity—to compare deep neural network (DNN) representations across architectures (LeNet, AlexNet, VGG-16, ResNet-18), datasets (30 disjoint 10-class subsets of ImageNet), and training epochs. The approach clusters neuron activations via k-means++ into 1000 clusters, builds weighted correlation graphs, computes persistent homology, and uses Betti curve distance as a similarity measure. Reviewer scores ranged from 1 to 6, with two reviewers (ratings 5 and 6) more positive about the novelty and potential, while the majority (five ratings 1–3) found the work insufficient for ICLR. Common critiques focused on the arbitrary use of k‑means++, lack of baselines or comparisons, limited architectural scope, absence of ablation studies, unclear practical utility, and weak empirical findings. The most positive reviewer (rating 6) praised the clear exposition and novel perspective, but still noted limitations in the dimensionality reduction step and accessibility.

### Strengths
- **Novel direction**: Applying TDA (persistent homology, Betti curves) to analyze and compare DNN functional graphs is an interesting and innovative approach that goes beyond accuracy metrics. (R1, R2, R3, R5, R7)
- **Comprehensive experimental setup**: Multiple CNN architectures (VGG-16, ResNet-18, LeNet, AlexNet) were trained on 30 disjoint subsets of ImageNet and evaluated across epochs, providing a range of measurements. (R2, R4, R5)
- **Clear theoretical exposition**: The paper provides a good background on persistent homology and Betti numbers, with clear explanations of the methodology. (R5, R6)
- **Potential for future work**: The approach offers a new perspective for model interpretability and theoretical analysis of DNNs, and may inspire further TDA applications in machine learning. (R1, R3, R5)
- **Interpretable observations**: The method produced some interpretable findings, e.g., differences when classes per training subset differ or when accuracies vary. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Approximation errors from k‑means++**: The dimensionality reduction via k‑means++ introduces approximation errors, may poorly capture high-dimensional structure, and is essentially linear partitioning. The choice of 1000 clusters is arbitrary; no ablation studies or robustness analysis are provided. The impact on persistent homology and Betti curves is not discussed. (R1, R2, R4, R5, R6)
- **No comparisons with baselines**: The paper does not compare Betti curve similarity to simpler or non‑TDA methods, e.g., statistics of activations (mean, variance, quantiles), Euclidean or earth‑mover distances between activations, or existing representation‑similarity measures (e.g., based on pairwise distance matrices). (R4, R7)  
  *Also missing comparisons to prior TDA‑based methods for NN representations such as the Geometry Score (Khrulkov et al., ICML 2018) and Representation Topology Divergence (Barannikov et al., ICML 2022).* (R6)
- **Limited architectural and task scope**: Only four older CNN architectures are tested on a 10‑class subset of ImageNet. Generalizability to transformers, RNNs, other tasks, larger‑scale datasets, or different input modalities is not demonstrated. (R1, R4, R6, R7)
- **Unclear practical utility**: The paper does not demonstrate how Betti curve similarity can be used for model selection, architecture refinement, training monitoring, or improving performance. The motivation for using TDA over other similarity measures is not established. (R1, R2, R3, R6)  
  *Specific questions: “What actionable insights can practitioners derive from the similarity scores?” (R2), “In what use cases is the method actually useful?” (R6)*
- **Lack of layer‑specific interpretability**: The global Betti curve metric does not reveal how individual layers or components contribute to observed differences, limiting its utility for detailed model analysis. (R1)  
- **Obvious / intuitive empirical findings**: The observations in Section 3 (e.g., differences between architectures, similarity at convergence) largely serve as sanity checks; no surprising or deep insights of broad interest are presented. (R7)
- **Missing related work**: The paper does not cite existing TDA‑based analyses of neural network representations, despite several relevant papers. (R6)
- **Exposition issues**:
  - **Preliminary material too long**: The introduction to TDA is overly lengthy for an empirical paper; it should be moved to an appendix. (R1, R3, R4)
  - **Figures unreadable**: Font sizes in figures (especially heatmaps) are too small, and Figure 1 is redundant. (R3, R6, R7)
  - **Broken code link**: The provided link for source code does not work, hindering reproducibility. (R1, R2)
  - **Misleading definition**: The paper states that a persistence diagram is “a visual representation of Betti numbers of the complex as a function of the filtration parameter,” which is inaccurate since different complexes can have the same Betti numbers but different persistence diagrams. (R1)
  - **Unclear experimental details**: It is not clarified whether neuron activations are pooled across all layers or per layer, nor is the total number of neurons M given for each network. (R7)
- **No ablation studies**: The effect of the number of clusters in k‑means++ on Betti curves and similarity measures is not examined; the method is only tested with k=1000. (R6)
