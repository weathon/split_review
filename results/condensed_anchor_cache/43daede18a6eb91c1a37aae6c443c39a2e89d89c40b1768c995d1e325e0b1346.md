## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes THICK, a method for learning hierarchical world models via discrete latent dynamics. It extends RSSM with a Context-specific RSSM (C-RSSM) that uses a sparsely changing discrete context variable \(\mathbf{c}_t\). The lower level updates parts of its latent state sparsely, forming invariant contexts; the higher level predicts only situations involving context changes. High-level transitions are defined by contiguous segments with the same \(\mathbf{c}_t\). THICK is combined with Dreamer and model-predictive control for MBRL/planning. Experiments on MiniHack, PinPad, and robotic manipulation show benefits on long-horizon tasks, with qualitative visualizations of context switches.

### Strengths
- The C-RSSM provides a simple yet effective way to learn sparsely changing context variables from pixels without supervision (R1, R2, R3).
- The high-level model is trained in a self-supervised manner to anticipate context changes based on the C-RSSM’s discrete dynamics (R1).
- Context switches naturally define high-level transitions, enabling variable-length temporal abstractions (R2).
- The learned hierarchies capture meaningful, interpretable abstractions related to subgoals; high-level plans can be visualized (R1).
- The hierarchical world model can be seamlessly integrated with both model-based RL (Dreamer) and model-based planning (MPC) (R1, R2, R3).
- The paper is clearly written, well-structured, and the figures (e.g., model visualizations) are self-explanatory (R2, R3).
- Experimental results support that the hierarchy improves long-horizon prediction and sample efficiency in tasks like MiniHack, PinPad, and robotic manipulation (R1, R2). R3 also notes extensive evaluation and thorough reproducibility details in the appendix.

### Weaknesses
- **Marginal performance improvement:** While R1 notes improved sample efficiency, R2 and R3 question the significance—R2 finds the improvement in PinPadFour/Five marginal, and R3 states that Figure 7 shows no significant difference between THICK+Dreamer and DreamerV2. The results on several tasks are similar to flat baselines (R2, R3).
- **Design choices insufficiently justified:** The high-level model predicts the state and action just before a context switch (\(\mathbf{z}_{\tau(t)-1}, \mathbf{a}_{\tau(t)-1}\)) rather than the context/state after the switch. The rationale for this choice is unclear (R2). Also, the need for both coarse and precise priors in Section 2.1 and the role of coarse predictions (\(\hat{\mathbf{z}}_t\), \(\hat{\mathbf{o}}_t\)) in learning \(\mathbf{c}_t\) is not explained (R2).
- **Sparsity hyperparameter tuning:** The sparsity hyperparameter for the C-RSSM needs to be tuned per environment (R1).
- **Limited flexibility of high-level model:** The high-level model operates at a fixed abstract timescale, less flexible than methods with hierarchical policies (R1). High-level plans are not actively updated during execution—replanning only occurs at context changes (R1). This also raises the question of adding mechanisms to make high-level plans more reactive to execution errors or environment changes (R1).
- **Expressiveness limitations:** The temporal abstractions may be limited compared to methods with task priors or curriculum learning (R1). Extending to multiple abstraction levels is not explored (R1).
- **Missing comparisons and baselines:** No comparison to hierarchical RL methods (h-DQN, FeUdal Networks) (R1). The paper does not evaluate on common RL benchmarks (Atari, DMC), making it unclear if the method’s limitations or task characteristics drive the results (R2). A comparison to scaling the flat world model (versus injecting hierarchy) is missing (R2). Also, the Director baseline (Gulcehre et al.) is reported with near-zero success on all tasks; no explanation is provided (R3).
- **Exploration concerns:** PinPad experiments use Plan2Explore to fill initial exploratory data; experiments starting RL from scratch would better demonstrate the method’s exploration capabilities (R2).
- **Scalability and generality:** It is unclear how well THICK scales to even longer time horizons or higher-dimensional state spaces (R1). Active exploration to shape context abstractions (instead of sparsity loss) is not investigated (R1).
