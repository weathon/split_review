## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Adam+CM, a memory-augmented variant of Adam that maintains a buffer of “critical momenta” to promote exploration toward flatter minima and address the generalization issues of Adam. The authors provide theoretical convergence analysis, toy examples, and empirical results on image classification (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet), language modeling (Penn Treebank), and online learning (TinyImageNet, 5-dataset). Reviewers found the idea novel and the presentation clear, but raised concerns about motivation, experimental rigor, baseline choices, computational cost, and whether the claimed benefits are robust in practice.

### Strengths
- The paper introduces a new concept of storing “critical momenta” in a memory buffer, overcoming limitations of previous memory-augmented optimizers. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- The method is well-motivated and presents a novel approach to optimization. (Reviewer 2)
- The paper is well-written, clear, and has a logical flow. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- Comprehensive evidence is provided, including theoretical analysis, toy examples (Goldstein-Price, Ackley loss functions), and real-world experiments. (Reviewer 2)
- Preliminary experiments on artificial loss functions show a clear advantage of CM variants in exploring and finding lower loss near the global solution. (Reviewer 3)
- The method shows potential in both supervised and online learning, with empirical improvements over vanilla Adam in combination with CG/SAM. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- The motivation for prioritizing momenta with large gradient norms is not well justified; it is unclear if and how this choice affects performance. (Reviewer 1)
- The paper associates flat minima with global minima or lower loss in toy examples, but in practice the training loss at a flat minimum is usually higher than at a sharp minimum. (Reviewer 1)
- The method is partly motivated by the performance gap between SGD and Adam, yet no comparison to SGD is provided. It would also be interesting to test whether critical momenta improve SGD. (Reviewer 1)
- Baselines are not particularly strong; notably, Adam+SAM performs almost the same as or worse than vanilla Adam on image classification tasks (e.g., EfficientNet-B0 on ImageNet), but no explanation is given. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- Results in Figure 5 (escape ratio vs. sharpness coefficient) may be sensitive to hyperparameters such as learning rate, which is not discussed. (Reviewer 1)
- The paper would be more convincing if larger-scale NLP experiments were conducted (e.g., larger language model optimization tasks). (Reviewer 2)
- On the LSTM/PTB experiment (Figure 6), both CM methods overfit after convergence, contradicting the claim that CM methods find flatter minima. (Reviewer 3)
- Reported scores on ImageNet (EfficientNet-B0) are significantly lower than established results: e.g., “papers with code” reports 76.3% top-1, but the paper reports 65.4–71.7%. (Reviewer 3)
- Experimental results are likely affected by batch size, but batch size is not included in the hyperparameter search. (Reviewer 3)
- The memory cost of the approach is not sufficiently discussed; this is critical for practical deployment in resource-constrained environments. (Reviewer 4)
- Improvements may be attributed to the additional memory and hyperparameters; the paper should discuss whether Adam+CM offers regret guarantees or optimality conditions given the added complexity. (Reviewer 4)
- The method is not compared to weight-averaging approaches such as Polyak averaging or Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA), which also improve generalization with minimal additional cost. (Reviewer 4)
- The escape ratio in Figure 5 is monotonically increasing with the sharpness coefficient; what happens if it is increased further? (Reviewer 3, question)
- In Figure 6, why does the validation perplexity of Adam+CM initially drop fast and then gradually increase, crossing over with Adam+SAM? (Reviewer 3, question)
- Why does Adam+SAM perform so poorly for EfficientNet-B0 on ImageNet in Table 2? (Reviewer 3, question)
- The correspondence between the two plots in Figure 4 would be more obvious if line colors were matched. (Reviewer 3)
- Additional sharpness measures should be considered (e.g., |λ_max/λ_5|, how quickly loss changes in a local neighborhood). The interaction between sharpness, distance traveled, and generalization is complicated and depends on hyperparameters; this should be made clear in Figures 7 and 8. (Reviewer 4)
- The buffer size C is generally set to 5; can a smaller C be used? What if CG and CM use different buffer sizes? (Reviewer 4, question)
