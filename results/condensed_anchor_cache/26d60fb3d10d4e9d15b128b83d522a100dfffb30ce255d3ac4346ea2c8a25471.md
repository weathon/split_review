## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper compares retrieval-augmentation with long-context window extension (via positional interpolation) for LLMs, using a proprietary 43B GPT and LLaMA2-70B on 7–9 long-context tasks from Scroll and LongBench (single/multi-document QA, query-based summarization, few-shot learning). It finds that a retrieval-augmented 4K-window LLM can match a fine-tuned 16K-window LLM, that retrieval improves performance regardless of context-window size, and that combining both methods gives the best results—the retrieval-augmented LLaMA2-70B-32K outperforms GPT-3.5-turbo-16k and Davinci003. The study offers practical insights for choosing between the two approaches.

### Strengths
- Clear motivation and timely comparison of retrieval augmentation vs. context window extension, an underexplored topic (reviewers 1,2,3,6).
- Comprehensive experiments with two large-scale LLMs (43B GPT, LLaMA2-70B) and three retrievers across diverse long-context datasets (Scroll, LongBench), including both model-internal training and external baselines (GPT-3.5-turbo-16k, Davinci003) (reviewers 2,4,6).
- Key findings: retrieval-augmented 4K matches fine-tuned 16K; retrieval helps irrespective of context window; the two methods are complementary (reviewers 1,5,6).
- Best model (retrieval-augmented LLaMA2-70B-32K) outperforms ChatGPT on 5 of 7 tasks and beats GPT-3.5-turbo-16k/ Davinci003 on average (reviewers 2,3).
- Well-structured, clearly written paper with thorough motivation and analysis (reviewers 2,4,6).
- Results contradict earlier negative findings (Bai et al. 2023) by using larger models, making conclusions more convincing and practically relevant (reviewers 3,5).
- Additional analyses on number of chunks, context length, and different retrievers (Table 4) provide useful insights (reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: Combining retrieval with long-context LLMs is a straightforward idea; no new technique is introduced (reviewers 3,5). Three reviewers (scores 6) highlighted this, while three (scores 8) found the contribution significant, indicating moderate disagreement.
- **Missing comparisons with other context extension methods**: Only positional interpolation is tested; other methods (ALiBi, NTK-aware scaling, native long-context models) are not explored (reviewers 4,6).
- **Insufficient analysis of retrieval settings**:
  - Ablation on the “lost-in-the-middle” hypothesis is needed: a controlled experiment varying relevant-document position would distinguish positional bias from retrieval relevance effects (reviewer 1).
  - Retrieval quality is only assessed via LLM performance; direct metrics (precision, recall, F1) on retrieved documents are missing (reviewer 1).
  - Chunking strategy (300 words) lacks justification; top-5 context lengths vary unexpectedly (not consistently ~1500 tokens), and alternative chunking (token-based, semantic) is not examined (reviewer 4).
  - Optimal number of retrieved passages is not systematically determined; performance may plateau or decline (reviewer 2).
  - Effect of retrieved-chunk order (e.g., reversing) is not studied (reviewer 3).
- **Closed retrieval setting**: The retrieval corpus consists of each task’s provided gold contexts, making the evaluation unrealistic for open-domain scenarios; helpfulness would likely drop with mismatched corpora (reviewer 3).
- **Limited model-size investigation**: Only 43B and 70B models are used; smaller models (e.g., 7B) are not tested, so the conclusion that retrieval always helps does not extend to Bai et al.’s negative results with smaller models—an apple-to-apple example comparison is absent (reviewers 5,6).
- **Inconsistent improvements on 43B model**: The 43B GPT shows less consistent gains from retrieval or longer context than the 70B, making task- and model-specific guidelines difficult (reviewer 5).
- **Narrow task coverage**: Experiments focus on QA and summarization; few-shot learning (only one task), synthetic tasks, and code completion from LongBench are omitted (reviewer 6).
- **No joint training**: Only retrieval augmentation at inference is considered; end-to-end training of retriever and LLM could yield different results (reviewer 2).
- **Missing discussion of limitations and future work**: Potential issues (e.g., exacerbating “lost-in-the-middle” by combining retrieval and long context) and avenues for better integration are not addressed (reviewer 6).
- **Longer contexts (64K, 128K) not tested**: It is unclear whether retrieval benefits plateau at larger context windows (reviewer 2).
- **Lack of empirical support for hypotheses explaining opposition to Bai et al.**: Only conjectures are offered; no evidence (e.g., comparing the same example across model sizes) is provided (reviewers 5,6).
