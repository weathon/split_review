## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces PhyGenBench (160 prompts spanning 27 physical laws across four domains) and PhyGenEval, a hierarchical evaluation framework using GPT‑4o and VLMs to assess physical commonsense in text-to-video generation. Experiments show current T2V models struggle with intuitive physics. Reviewers agree the topic is timely and important, but raise significant concerns about the benchmark’s size, the evaluation pipeline’s reliability, reliance on proprietary models, and lack of robustness.

### Strengths
- The focus on intuitive physics is novel and addresses an important gap in T2V evaluation (R1, R4, R5).
- The benchmark dataset can serve as a valuable resource for the community (R2, R3).
- Handcrafted prompts are fine-grained and cover multiple physics categories (R1).
- PhyGenEval’s three‑tiered framework (key phenomena detection, order verification, overall naturalness) is clear and comprehensively assesses physical realism (R3, R4).
- Quantitative evaluation of current models on the benchmark demonstrates the need for improvement in physical commonsense (R2, R4, R5).
- PhyGenEval’s scores align with human feedback (PCA correlation ~0.7–0.8), and one reviewer found it “promising” while another called it “closer to human value” (R2, R5).
- One reviewer (R3, score 6) rated the paper well‑written and argued the benchmark is “crucial and not well‑studied” with strong contribution (contrast: others rated contribution 2).

### Weaknesses
1. **Benchmark scale and representativeness**  
   - Only 160 prompts, compared to VideoPhy (688) or DEVIL (>800); may not capture full complexity of physics laws (R1, R5).
   - The 4‑point scale yields top model scores of only 0.5 on average, suggesting the metric is overly strict and may limit ability to distinguish between models (R5).
   - Missing discussion of related physical reasoning benchmarks (SuperCLEVR‑Physics, ContPhy, Physion) (R5).

2. **Evaluation pipeline reliability**  
   - PhyGenEval’s PCA correlation with humans is only ~0.7–0.8 – not reliable enough to replace human raters (R2).
   - Pipeline heavily relies on proprietary GPT‑4o and multiple VLMs, leading to potential hallucination, error propagation, and reproducibility issues with open‑source models (R1, R2, R4).
   - Hallucination in LLMs and VLMs is not addressed in the protocol; no error analysis for cases where PhyGenEval disagrees with human evaluation (R1, R4).
   - Success rate of GPT‑4o’s retrieval step is unclear – more details needed (R3).
   - Robustness to low‑quality videos is questionable (R4).
   - Does not demonstrate generalization of the auto‑evaluator to prompts from other benchmarks; comparisons are thus biased (R1).

3. **Dependence on specific model versions**  
   - Main results rely on an unspecified version of GPT‑4o; future comparisons would require evaluation on updated versions, hurting consistency (R4).  
   - No discussion of using different VLMs (open‑source like InternVL‑2, LLaVA‑Video, Oryx) to check scoring consistency (R3, R4).

4. **Novelty and rigor**  
   - The benchmark and evaluator lack decent novelty (R1; contrast with R4 who praised novelty). Construction heavily depends on existing generative models (R1).  
   - Human evaluation is not rigorous: appendix annotation details are confusing, instructions are not shown, and validation of annotators is missing (R4).  
   - Questions about efficiency of the auto‑evaluator (R1) and how the final score is computed (R4).  
   - Minor typos (e.g., “we pue more detailed”, mis‑referenced appendix sections) (R4).
