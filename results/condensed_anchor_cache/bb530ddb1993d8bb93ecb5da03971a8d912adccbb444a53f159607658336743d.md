## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates whether Llama-2 uses English as an intermediate language when processing non-English tasks, building on Wendler et al. (2024). The authors propose a causal intervention method—subspace rejection via orthogonal projection onto the complement of a subspace defined by unembedding vectors of semantically similar English words—and show that removing English-related information from residual stream activations impairs translation performance, while removing other languages or unrelated concepts does not. The question is important and the methodology is novel, but the experiments are limited to a single task (translation of simple terms) and a single model (Llama-2), with no replication on Gemma-2 (which fails). Presentation is poor across all reviews. There is strong disagreement: Reviewer 1 (score 5) finds the results fairly convincing despite concerns about task breadth and intervention strength; Reviewer 2 (score 1) considers the findings likely artifacts and the methodology unjustified; Reviewer 3 (score 3) finds the results convincing but narrow, with significant methodological and contextual issues.

### Strengths
- The paper aims to establish causality for the claim that LLMs use English as an intermediate language, which is important for understanding anglocentric bias. (Reviewer 1)
- A novel mechanistic interpretability scheme using subspace rejection; the methodology is clever and has potential. (Reviewer 2)
- The methodology is described in detail. (Reviewer 2)
- The experimental results in Figures 3 and 4 are convincing in showing the impact of English as a latent language and the non-impact of rejecting unrelated concepts. (Reviewer 1, 3)
- The question is interesting and important; reasonably motivated despite some clarity issues. (Reviewer 3)
- Experiments are straightforward and easy to follow. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Single task and single model**: Only one task (translation of simple terms) and only Llama-2 are studied. This limits generality for the broad claim that LLMs “think in English.” Repeating tasks from Wendler et al. (2024) would strengthen rigor; trying other tasks (e.g., cross-task interventions) would be more creative. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **No replication on Gemma-2**: The findings are not replicated on a different model, undermining the significance and suggesting the Llama-2 results may be an artefact of an unexplained phenomenon unrelated to “thinking in English.” (Reviewer 2)
- **Intervention protocol may be too strong**: Removing the English subspace may in fact be removing the component along the token’s embedding, given the high cosine similarity between intermediate representations and the unembedding layer for single-token generation tasks (Figure 5 shows high accuracy after Layer 1). This confound would naturally reduce performance and could explain the counterfactual results. Reviewer 1 suggests checking cosine similarity of intermediate representations against English and non-English token embeddings to rule out this confounding. (Reviewer 1)
- **Arbitrary subspace construction**: The subspace is defined using unembedding vectors of semantically similar words, but it is unclear how many words are chosen per example, and whether the mixed results could be explained by this choice. (Reviewer 3)
- **Erasure method may be suboptimal**: The orthogonal projection in Equation 6 is equivalent to concept erasure, but there is a broad literature on optimal linear erasure. LEACE (Belrose et al.) provides theoretical optimality guarantees and should be preferred. (Reviewer 3)
- **Poor organization and presentation**: Figure 1 is blurry; there is no related work section (even in appendix); figures are poorly organized (Figure 5 referenced before Figures 2–4 but appears two pages later); panels in Figure 5 could be reduced; citation commands are misused (\citet vs \citep); the appendix is sparse and missing references (e.g., Figures 16–24 never mentioned, it is unclear which models they correspond to; Figures 21–24 go out of margins). (Reviewer 1)
- **Insufficient motivation and background**: The introduction does not adequately argue for importance; many technical terms are introduced without explanation. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of citations**: There are 0 citations in the last 5 out of 8 pages despite substantial content; contextualization with the current state of the field is severely lacking. (Reviewer 2)
- **Poor result presentation**: Heatmaps (Figure 5) are hard to read; no baseline of no-intervention accuracy is provided (only absolute performance, making it hard to gauge effect in early layers). Plotting change in accuracy instead would be clearer. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Figure 5d** would benefit from a bicolor colorbar with 0 as the midpoint. (Reviewer 3)
- **Figures 3 and 4**: The order of graphs should match the bar order (DE, EN, ES, FR, RU, ZH). (Reviewer 2)
- **Explanations missing**: Figure 5 is never thoroughly discussed; the text in Section 3.2 does not explain how to read and interpret it. (Reviewer 2)
- **Overly detailed transformer architecture**: The subsection on Transformers includes many irrelevant details that can be moved to an appendix or omitted. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Exaggerated claims**: The introduction’s mention of theory-of-mind capabilities may be overstated; Ullman (2023) shows LLMs fail on trivial alterations. Modesty in phrasing is needed. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing important references**: Activation patching was introduced earlier (Vig et al., 2020); steering vectors appeared in Bau et al. (2019); concept erasure (LEACE) should be cited. (Reviewer 3)
- **Section 3.4 (Future Work)**: Reads more as a limitations section; language is too casual and does not adequately back up the analysis of Gemma-2. (Reviewer 2)
- **Potential for “searching for a conclusion”**: The writing conveys that proper experiments were not set up a priori, and the authors may have found results then sought alternatives to justify them, undermining the scientific method. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of numerical details**: Very few actual numerical results are provided (e.g., in table form), undermining reproducibility and detail. (Reviewer 2)
