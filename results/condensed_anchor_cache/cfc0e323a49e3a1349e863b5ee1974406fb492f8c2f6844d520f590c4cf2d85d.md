## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a two-stage method for 3D-aware image synthesis from in-the-wild, unposed images. First, an autoencoder is trained to compress an image and its estimated monocular depth into a latent code that decodes to a triplane NeRF representation. Novel views are rendered from the triplane and supervised with an adversarial loss on both RGB and depth. Second, a latent diffusion model is trained on the learned latent space to enable unconditional or conditional generation. The method avoids requiring multi-view images or canonical camera coordinates. Experiments on ImageNet and unimodal datasets show advantages over GAN-based methods such as EG3D and 3DGP.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written, well-motivated, and easy to follow. Charts and the overview figure are excellent.
- The two-stage training pipeline is interesting and each design choice is reasonable and clearly explained.
- The method requires neither posed images, multi-view data, nor explicit 3D supervision, enabling training on large in-the-wild datasets.
- Using a latent diffusion model addresses the lack of sample diversity seen in GAN-based 3D-aware methods.
- The triplane representation provides an efficient 3D-aware latent space.
- Extensive and insightful ablations support major design choices.
- Baselines are appropriate, and quantitative evaluations show clear advantages over GAN-based baselines.

### Weaknesses
- A key related work, VQ3D (ICCV 2023), is missing from discussion/comparison. Like WildFusion, VQ3D adopts a two-stage scheme (encoder → 3D latent → triplane → novel views) but uses GANs in both stages. The paper should clearly articulate the differences in latent representation, training objectives, and architecture, and provide direct performance comparisons for both the VAE and the generator to assess whether improvements come from the first or second stage.
- The method is only compared with GAN-based approaches (EG3D, 3DGP). Comparisons with recent diffusion-based 3D generation methods (e.g., GenVS, IVID, VQ3D) are needed for a complete evaluation.
- One reviewer raised a significant concern: why not use a simpler baseline – generate images with a 2D generative model, predict depth with the same monocular depth estimator, warp to novel views, and inpaint holes (as in SceneScape)? The reviewer increased their score after authors provided this comparison, but the baseline should be included and discussed.
- The visual quality of generated images is not on par with current state-of-the-art 2D generative models. The supplementary video shows many distorted novel-view-synthesis results, and the visual improvements over EG3D are not consistently clear (e.g., Fig. 9 exhibits flat geometry in the first two rows, raising concerns about 3D structure quality).
- The first-stage VAE is trained with an adversarial loss, which may bound the quality and diversity of the second-stage diffusion model. The benefit of adding the diffusion stage over simply using the GAN-based VAE as a generator is unclear beyond easier sampling.
- The reliance on a pre-trained monocular depth estimator introduces dependency on its quality and potential biases, which can propagate into the final 3D representation.
- The FID is computed with 20k samples; the standard in the field (StyleGAN, VQ3D) is 50k. Using a non-standard number limits comparability with prior and future works.
- Visual comparisons with other methods are placed in the supplementary material rather than the main paper, making it harder for readers to assess advantages. More visual evidence for mode collapse in GAN baselines (as claimed in Sec. 4.2) is needed.
- One reviewer was not fully convinced about the method’s 3D fidelity and noted that some results appear nearly flat; another noted that the paper’s contribution is fair rather than excellent (Reviewer 3 assigned contribution 2/4).
