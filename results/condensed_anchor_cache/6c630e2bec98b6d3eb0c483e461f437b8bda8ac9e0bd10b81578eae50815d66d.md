## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces SearchBench, a benchmark of 11 unique combinatorial search problems (across 5 categories) designed to test LLMs on multi-step reasoning and backtracking. Automated pipelines generate instances and evaluate feasibility, correctness, and optimality. Results show that even GPT-4 performs poorly (1.4% end-to-end text, 11.7% with code generation). The authors propose prompting LLMs to implement A* search with a Multi-Stage-Multi-Try (MSMT) method that splits implementation into two stages, each verified by unit tests, raising GPT-4’s success rate above 57%. Reviewers agree the benchmark is well-designed and the problem is relevant, but disagree on novelty, fairness of comparisons, and breadth of contribution. One reviewer (score 5) finds the benchmark and MSMT approach interesting and impactful; two reviewers (score 3) see limited novelty, over-engineering, and insufficient baselines.

### Strengths
- **Benchmark design**: SearchBench uses classic optimization problems with unique rule modifications to avoid training contamination, and provides diverse, scalable instances (Reviewer 1, 3). It evaluates feasibility, correctness, and optimality, offering a detailed assessment of LLM reasoning on combinatorial tasks (Reviewer 2).
- **Problem significance**: The work demonstrates that even advanced LLMs (e.g., GPT-4) severely struggle with multi-step reasoning and backtracking, highlighting a real gap in current models for pathfinding and puzzle-solving applications (Reviewer 2).
- **Method improvement**: The A* prompting with MSMT shows substantial improvement over direct text/code generation and other prompting methods (Reviewer 1, 2). The staged, unit-tested code generation approach is a practical way to enhance LLM performance on complex reasoning (Reviewer 2).
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: The paper evaluates multiple models (GPT-4, Llama, etc.) and prompting strategies (0-shot, CoT, A*, MSMT), providing meaningful comparisons (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of novelty in MSMT A***: The approach combines code generation with compiler/unit-test feedback, which is now a standard technique in LLM optimization. The method does not introduce a fundamentally new way to leverage LLMs (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 also notes that using LLMs to re-implement A* seems like overkill; a more lightweight approach (e.g., prompting for inputs and heuristic only, then executing an existing A* implementation) could suffice and would better isolate the LLM’s reasoning contribution.
2. **Unfair comparisons and missing baselines**: 
   - MSMT benefits from prior knowledge that the required code is for A* search. Recent LLM-based code synthesis methods (e.g., multi-step reasoning, reward modeling, planning with world models) should be evaluated under the same prior (e.g., told to generate A* code) for a fair comparison (Reviewer 2).
   - The paper does not consider recent reasoning techniques (e.g., structured process reward models, deliberate planning). It is unclear how those would perform on SearchBench (Reviewer 2).
   - Other methods (0-shot, CoT) are not given an equivalent number of trials or unit-test filtering; MSMT’s “multiple tries” and prefiltration naturally boost feasibility, making comparisons potentially unfair (Reviewer 3).
   - Figure 3 omits results for GPT-o1 with A*/MSMT A* and Code Llama with 0-shot text (Reviewer 3).
3. **Generalization and scalability concerns**:
   - The conclusions may not hold for problems that do not have a code-based solution (e.g., open-domain problems). The dataset is limited to combinatorial tasks with known algorithmic frameworks (Reviewer 2, 3).
   - The algorithm-selection issue is not addressed: MSMT assumes the solver knows to use A*; real-world problems often require identifying the correct algorithm first (Reviewer 2, 3).
   - Scalability of MSMT is unclear: the method is complex and computationally demanding; the benchmark instances are small (human-solveable scale), and state-space explosion for larger practical problems could undermine effectiveness (Reviewer 2).
4. **Potential confound in few-shot demonstrations**: Using demonstrations from other problem categories may serve as distractors rather than guides, lowering baseline performance and conflating reasoning with task recognition (Reviewer 3).
5. **Unsubstantiated claim and missing analysis**:
   - Line 268 claims that models solving SearchBench can generalize to other combinatorial problems, but no experiments support this (Reviewer 3).
   - The paper lacks detailed error pattern analysis to identify specific failures in LLM reasoning (Reviewer 2, question 4).
6. **Presentation issues**:
   - Figure 3 has font readability issues. The y-axis in Figure 5 is mislabeled as “accuracy” when it likely measures success rate or feasibility (Reviewer 3).
   - Some explanations are redundant and could be streamlined (Reviewer 3).
7. **Limited scope for broader reasoning**: SearchBench focuses narrowly on combinatorial search; it does not address how to devise LLM methods for other reasoning challenges (e.g., open-domain problems) (Reviewer 2, 3).

*Note: Reviewer 2 (score 5) finds the benchmark and method more impactful than the other two reviewers (scores 3, 3). The minority reviewer agrees with many of the critiques but sees the contributions as strong enough to merit a higher rating.*
