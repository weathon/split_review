## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents MathInstruct, an instruction-tuning dataset compiled from 13 math datasets (6 with new GPT-4-generated rationales), featuring a hybrid of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Program-of-Thought (PoT) rationales. Using MathInstruct, the authors train MAmmoTH models (7B–70B) based on Llama. The hybrid inference strategy first attempts PoT, falling back to CoT if execution fails. MAmmoTH models substantially outperform existing open-source models on nine math reasoning datasets (average gain 16%–32%), with MAmmoTH-7B reaching 33% on MATH (23% above WizardMath-7B) and MAmmoTH-34B achieving 44% on MATH, surpassing GPT-4’s CoT result.

### Strengths
- Hybrid CoT + PoT rationales and inference strategy exploit the complementary strengths of both approaches; the fallback mechanism is intuitive and effective. [R1,R2,R3]
- Comprehensive evaluation across many datasets (GSM8K, MATH, AQuA, NumGlue, Mathematics, SimulEq, MMLU-Math, etc.), model sizes, and in-domain/out-of-domain settings; Table 5 shows that dataset diversity outperforms the sum of its largest parts. [R1,R2,R3,R4]
- Expensive dataset creation effort; the dataset is a valuable resource for the community. [R1]
- Strong performance closing the gap between open and proprietary models (e.g., surpassing GPT-4 on MATH). [R1,R2,R3,R5]
- Ablation studies (centered on GSM8K) help understand the influence of subparts of the training annotations. [R4, though also criticised as insufficient – see weaknesses]
- Simple method yields effective results. [R4]
- Out-of-domain generalization is considered and shown. [R3]

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty**: The approach (using GPT-4 to generate synthetic rationales, then fine-tuning Llama) resembles prior work (e.g., Orca). Improvements may largely stem from GPT-4’s intelligence rather than a novel methodology. Some reviewers found the contribution mild. [R4,R5] *Minority view: two reviewers (scores 6) considered novelty weak; three others (scores 8) did not raise this as a major concern.*
- **Insufficient justification of dataset contributions**: 
  - Reviewer 1 reports training a 7B CodeLLaMA solely on GSM8K PoT data (from GPT-3.5-turbo) and achieving 62% accuracy, exceeding the 59.4% reported in Table 3 for CodeLLaMA trained on MathInstruct. This suggests that the marginal benefit of incorporating many datasets is unclear. The authors should compare against a CodeLLaMA baseline trained exclusively on GSM8K PoT. [R1]
  - Additional baselines: training on other individual MathInstruct datasets (e.g., MATH alone) and ablations that remove single datasets (e.g., “M+C+A+N” or “G+M+C+N”) are needed to isolate the impact of each component. [R1,R4]
  - One reviewer asks: is there a baseline that fine-tunes on the raw 13 datasets (without synthetic CoT/PoT) to assess the contribution of the hybrid rationales? [R3]
- **Lack of in-depth analysis of training data distribution**: 
  - Small datasets like TheoremQA (600 samples) may have limited impact; it is unclear whether including them harms performance on other in-domain/out-of-domain tasks. [R4]
  - The overall PoT/CoT ratio in MathInstruct is ~27% PoT (summing table sizes), but this number is not explicitly stated in the paper. [R2]
- **Hybrid prompting approach is preliminary**: 
  - Only one fallback strategy (PoT→CoT) is tested. Alternatives such as self-consistency across both modes, interleaving CoT and PoT, or letting the model decide which mode to use are not explored or discussed as unsuccessful attempts. [R2,R5]
  - One reviewer proposed interleaving CoT and PoT within a single solution as a potentially superior fusion method. [R5]
  - Reviewer 2 asks: how is the PoT trigger (“Let’s write a program to solve the problem”) used? Does the model generate PoT only when the trigger is provided, or does it mix both by default? [R2]
- **Missing details and clarification questions**:
  - GSM8K has ~7.5k training examples; how does the paper report 14k examples for GSM8K? [R1]
  - Closed model numbers (e.g., GPT-4, ChatGPT) are missing for NumGlue, Mathematics, SimulEq, and MMLU-Math; though including them incurs cost and has dubious scientific value, they would contextualize dataset difficulty. [R2]
  - Data utilization rate (filtering ratio during generation) is not reported. [R4]
  - For TheoremQA’s advanced calculations (integrals, derivatives), can the generated PoT programs be successfully executed? [R4]
  - Insufficient detail on how CoT and PoT are mixed during training: are special tokens used, or only the prompt sentence “Let’s write a program…”? [R5]
- **Generalization concern**: Supervised fine-tuning with intermediate reasoning (CoT/PoT) may still fail on harder or unseen math problems, as noted for SFT generally. [R3]
- **Lack of comprehensive error analysis**: No illustrative examples of model successes and failures are provided, limiting insight into remaining weaknesses. [R4]
