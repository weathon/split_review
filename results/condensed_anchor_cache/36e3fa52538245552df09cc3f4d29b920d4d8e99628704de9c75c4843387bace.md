## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a novel tripartite graph representation for branch-and-bound search trees and a DQN-GNN reinforcement learning model to learn node selection policies in MILP solving. It introduces three custom reward metrics and provides theoretical analysis. Reviewers are strongly divided: one reviewer (rating 6) is highly positive, praising the tripartite representation, reward design, and generalization to larger MILPs. The other three reviewers (ratings 3, 3, 3) express major concerns about weak empirical evaluation on trivial instances, questionable theoretical claims, insufficient clarity, lack of ablation, and degraded performance on larger problems. Most reviewers find the contribution limited without stronger experiments and clearer exposition.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important and underexplored problem: node selection in branch-and-bound for MILP, including the subtle issue of path switching costs.
- Introduces a novel tripartite graph representation of the search tree, with theoretical validation that it encapsulates sufficient information for node selection.
- Proposes three innovative metrics for node selection rewards and develops a DQN-GNN model that uses reinforcement learning to learn node selection policies.
- Provides theoretical results to motivate design choices.
- One reviewer (rating 6) strongly commends the model’s generalization to MILPs substantially larger than training instances, and finds the tripartite representation and reward design insightful.
- Motivation is clear, and the two technical contributions (representation + reward) stem from interesting observations.
- Experiments show promising results on small MILP instances.

### Weaknesses
- **Weak empirical evaluation:** All three negative reviewers highlight that the tested instances are trivial – the slowest baseline takes under 30 seconds, and SCIP solves them within a minute. The speedup over default SCIP is minimal (~10 seconds) and not practically meaningful. The paper lacks experiments on harder problems where solving times are on the order of hours, which is standard for this line of work. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Generalization to larger problems is inconsistent.** The trained policy degrades significantly on larger instances, undermining practical usability. One positive reviewer (rating 6) acknowledges inconsistency and asks for insights on what MILP types are suitable. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Metrics insufficient:** Only runtime and number of nodes are reported. Win/loss rates and scatter plots per instance are missing, making it impossible to assess consistency or whether average results are skewed by outliers. (Reviewer 1)
- **No ablation study:** The paper does not isolate contributions of the tripartite vs. traditional bipartite graph representation, nor the individual effects of the three reward components on final performance. (Reviewer 4)
- **Lack of comprehensive related work:** No literature review positions the paper; unique contributions over existing methods (SVM, RankNet, GNN) are unclear. Related work on ML-guided node selection for other combinatorial optimization problems is not acknowledged. (Reviewer 1)
- **Theoretical claims are questionable or insufficiently justified:**
  - Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 may not be meaningful: branch-and-bound is deterministic, so with a fixed node selection policy all information is already at the root. The proofs in the appendix appear non-rigorous. (Reviewer 3)
  - Theorem 4.2 claims node selection and variable selection are “independent”. The proof assumes a fixed tree state where variable selection decisions have already been made; varying variable selection would change the subtree, so the premise does not hold. Most MIP solving perspectives would disagree with this claim. (Reviewer 3)
  - One reviewer gave a soundness score of 1 (poor), expressing low confidence in the paper’s theoretical foundations. (Reviewer 3)
- **Tripartite graph representation is poorly explained:**
  - It is not defined how the graph is built from the underlying MIP; edges are never formally defined (especially between V and NC). Readers must infer from Figure 1. (Reviewers 1, 3)
  - The “information of a node I(N)” is never formally defined. (Reviewer 3)
  - Feature vector labels in Figure 1 (e.g., $(0,+\infty,-2)$ for a variable node) are unexplained; similarly, edge weights between V and NC (labeled with branching sign pairs) are unclear. (Reviewer 3)
  - NC vertices represent only one variable each (branching constraints), so each NC vertex connects to exactly one variable vertex, raising the question of whether the tripartite structure is necessary; it would be more useful if cuts were added. (Reviewer 1)
  - The set NC grows with every leaf node stored; potential scalability limitations are not addressed. (Reviewer 3)
- **Unclear definitions:**
  - “Historical optimal solution path” is ambiguous: is it a global optimal solution or an intermediate local optimal? (Reviewer 2)
  - “Gap reward” in Theorem 4.1 is undefined before later use. (Reviewer 1)
- **Hyperparameter n (number of nodes selected) not justified.** The rule for setting n is absent; no study of alternative values (e.g., n=30,50,100) nor whether the number of nodes is restricted for SCIP. (Reviewer 2)
- **Motivation for “From Nodes to Constraints” is unclear:** Variable selection determines the additional constraints; node selection is about exploration order. (Reviewer 3)
- **Writing flaws detract from readability:**
  - Grammatical errors and poor sentence structure appear in nearly every paragraph. (Reviewer 3)
  - Section 2 title: “Preliminary” should be “Preliminaries”.
  - Page 3: “feature vector of edge \(e_k\)” should be “feature vector of edge \(e_k\)” (typo).
  - Section 3.2: “\(V \cup C = V \cup NC = C \cup NC = \emptyset\)” should use intersections, not unions.
  - Section 4.2: “mordern” typo for “modern”.
  - Proofs lack intuitive explanation. (Reviewer 1)
