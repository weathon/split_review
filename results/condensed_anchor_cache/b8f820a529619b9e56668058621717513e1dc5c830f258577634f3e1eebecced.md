## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes BiC-Occ, a framework for vision-based 3D occupancy prediction comprising a Bi-directional View Transformer (Bi-VT) that uses tensor decomposition and truncated SVD to approximate invertible view transformation, and a Circulated Interpolation Predictor (CIP) that aligns multi-scale BEV representations via interpolation and 3D convolution. The goal is to mitigate sparsity and ambiguity in voxel labels. Experiments on Occ3D-nuScenes validation dataset show improved Scene Completion and Semantic Scene Completion performance over baselines. Some reviewers note a large margin of improvement; another finds the quantitative advantage not significant.

### Strengths
- BiC-Occ presents an innovative combination of bidirectional view transformation and circulated interpolation, which is a novel direction in 3D occupancy prediction. (R1)
- The Bi-VT module tackles sparsity by constructing reversible view transformation matrices to enhance 2D-3D self-consistency – an interesting idea. (R4)
- The sparsity and ambiguity of labels are challenging problems in occupancy, and the proposed method provides a solution with Bi-VT and CIP. (R3)
- The paper is clearly written, providing detailed explanations of each module’s function, making it easy to follow. (R1, R2)
- Experiments including ablation studies, parameter analyses, and visualizations verify the efficacy of BiC-Occ. (R1, R3, R5)
- The method demonstrates good performance on a public dataset. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns.** The Bi-VT reverse projection idea is similar to methods in FB-BEV/FB-Occ, and the work does not clearly differentiate from them. Additionally, related works such as MatrixVT, Inversematrixvt3d, and OTOcc that also use reverse view transformation for occupancy are not cited or discussed. (R2, R5)
- **Strong assumptions are not justified.** The paper assumes (A) F_img and F_BEV can be represented by a Kronecker product of two transition score matrices, and (B) invertibility via decomposed truncated SVD. It is unclear how these approximations capture complex structural features (e.g., rich details, object edges) or nonlinear 2D-3D relationships. One reviewer suspects performance gains may stem from increased model size rather than the method itself, and asks for evidence isolating the contribution. (R1)
- **CIP may increase ambiguity at edges.** Upsampling and downsampling in interpolating discrete voxel representations can cause detail loss, edge blurring, and more severe ambiguity at object boundaries, especially when mapping low to high resolution. The motivation for CIP needs clearer visualization or explanation. (R1, R3)
- **Limited experimental scope.** All experiments are conducted only on Occ3D-nuScenes. An additional dataset is needed to demonstrate generalization and avoid data bias. (R2, R3) The ablation study is simplistic – only varying hyperparameters α and β with few discrete values – and lacks unique insights into module design motivation or exploration of other critical parameters (e.g., truncation levels in tensor decomposition, interpolation scales). (R1, R2)
- **Improvement over baselines is relatively small.** From Table 1, the gain over COTR is small; additional visualizations or experiments are needed to show clear advantage over COTR and other SOTA methods. (R3)
- **Lack of targeted evaluation on sparsity and ambiguity.** The paper claims to address these issues but provides no quantitative analysis of performance in sparse regions or edge-ambiguous areas. One reviewer requests results specifically for such scenarios. (R1, R3)
- **No analysis of computational efficiency.** The paper omits runtime, model parameters/FLOPs, and inference speed. This is critical for autonomous driving applications. Reviewers note that SVD on large matrices is slow and expect efficiency comparisons with SOTAs. (R1, R2, R4, R5) A detailed trade-off analysis between accuracy gains and computational demands (especially for resource-constrained edge devices) is missing. (R4)
- **Issues with experiment reporting.** The main experiment table appears to mix results from nuScenes lidar segmentation and Occ-3D nuScenes, but the title only refers to Occ-3D. There are also typos (e.g., TPVFormer with R101-DCN); the authors should check PanoOcc for standard benchmark comparison. This carelessness makes the paper appear coarse. (R5)
