## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses distribution shifts between training and test data, specifically sub-population shifts. It argues that conventional importance weighting (the ratio \(p_{te}(x,y)/p_{tr}(x,y)\)) is suboptimal under limited training data because it neglects the increase in variance of the estimated model. The authors propose a bi-level optimization framework that simultaneously optimizes importance weights and model parameters, thereby accounting for a bias-variance trade-off. The method is validated on benchmark datasets from vision and NLP domains (e.g., Waterbirds) when applied to existing last-layer retraining techniques (Group Weighted ERM, GDRO, and others). All four reviewers find the paper well-written and organized, with clear mathematical support and intriguing reasoning that the naïve weight choice can be improved. Reviewer 4 considers it a clear acceptance; Reviewer 2 raised the score to 8 after rebuttal.

### Strengths
- The bi-level optimization of importance weights is novel and addresses the bias-variance trade-off more effectively than heuristic-based weighting, offering a practical alternative.
- The analysis using a linear regression model provides clear insights into the bias-variance tradeoff, making the proposed method more convincing (Fig. 1).
- The iterative estimation of model parameters and importance weights on train and iid-validation datasets is interesting because it reduces overfitting on the training data, distinct from conventional cross-validation and hyperparameter search.
- The experiment section is comprehensive, covering multiple datasets and several methods.
- The proposed method is useful in practice and has high potential impact because distribution shifts are ubiquitous.
- The paper is well-written, systematically defines all terms and equations with attention to detail, is mathematically well-supported, and contains relevant experiments on standard benchmarks.

### Weaknesses
- **Test-data dependence in Algorithm 1 (Reviewer 3, main concern):** Algorithm 1 (line 278 \(p_0\) and line 282 \(r\)) requires access to the test dataset for parameter initialization. This could be problematic. Group distributional robust optimization (GDRO) links to importance weighting implicitly and does not use any hints from the test dataset; therefore, the comparison between the proposed method and GDRO is potentially unfair. The reviewer asks: How important are \(p_0\) and \(r\)? Can they be set assuming a uniform test distribution \(p_{te}(g_i) = p_{te}(g_j)\)? If so, how would it affect experimental performance? *This is a severe concern for one reviewer.*
- **Computational overhead and implementation details (Reviewers 1, 4):** The computational overhead of the proposed method is not discussed. Since only the last layer is retrained, the overhead may not be large, but the paper should clarify it. Additionally, how does the additional time scale with the validation dataset fraction (\(1 - n'/n\))? What additional hyperparameter tuning is needed for optimal weights (Appendix E.4)? Would a user need to repeat this hyperparameter tuning for their own model?
- **Training/validation split trade-off (Reviewer 1):** The method requires separating the training dataset into a validation set and remaining training set. What is the trade-off? What is the best way to split? If the training dataset is very limited, will the proposed method still work?
- **Clarification of training procedure (Reviewer 2):** The overall outline of the training procedure should be clarified. Did the authors follow the last-layer retraining protocol as described in Izmailov et al. (2022)? If so, additional details about this protocol would be beneficial.
- **Access to test distribution for Group Weighted ERM (Reviewer 2):** For the Group Weighted ERM procedure, the algorithm requires the likelihood ratio between the test and training distributions. In most cases, access to the test distribution is not available. How is this ratio computed for real-world datasets such as Waterbirds?
- **Impact for larger models and datasets (Reviewers 2, 4):** Qualitative insights on how performance may vary with an increase in model scale (e.g., ViTs, SWIN) are missing. Including an example with large-scale datasets (e.g., ImageNet-1K) or a medical imaging benchmark (e.g., ISIC, MedMNIST) would enhance the impact of the proposed algorithms. The paper also identifies in the introduction that importance weighting could improve algorithmic fairness, but does not demonstrate this on a real-world fairness challenge.
- **Empirical experiment details (Reviewer 4):** The authors describe fine-tuning a network 5 times to build a sample and perform a t-test. Confirm the initialization: was the same base network used and then fine-tuned 5 times? From this set, was the last layer retrained using each algorithm’s original importance weight scheme and then compared to the paired performance with optimal weights? What was controlled in terms of random seeds? Were seeds controlling batch ordering varied between runs? Were seeds varied between the pairs of optimal vs. original weights?
- **Hyperparameter search for optimal weights (Reviewer 4):** Appendix E.4 describes some hyperparameters that had to be found for the optimal weight algorithm. Can the authors provide additional detail about how these hyperparameters are found?
- **Minor typo (Reviewer 3):** Line 112: \([L(y, f_\theta (x)]\) should be \([L(y, f_\theta (x))]\).
