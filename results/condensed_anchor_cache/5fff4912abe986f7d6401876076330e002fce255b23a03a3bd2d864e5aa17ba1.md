- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 1, 3, 6, 1

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes two strategies, New Lazy Aggregation (NLA) and Accelerated Aggregation (AA), and combines them with compression to create algorithms FedBNLACA and FedBACA for bidirectional communication-efficient federated learning. Convergence analysis is provided under non-convex settings with full/partial participation and heterogeneous data, claiming $\mathcal{O}(1/T)$ rate. Experiments claim reduced communication cost for comparable accuracy. However, reviews are sharply divided: Reviewer 3 (score 6) finds the algorithms novel, theory thorough, and experiments promising; Reviewers 1,2,4 (scores 1,3,1) find serious issues in writing, assumptions, convergence claims, missing comparisons, and reproducibility.

### Strengths
- (Reviewer 1, 3) The proposed algorithms (NLA, AA) are novel attempts to reduce communication costs. Reviewer 2 also considers the ideas “pretty novel” but notes the research gap is overstated.
- (Reviewer 1, 2, 3) Convergence analysis is provided with complete proofs, covering compression and partial participation.
- (Reviewer 1, 3) Experimental results show the proposed algorithms require less communication cost to achieve comparable test accuracy compared to existing methods.
- (Reviewer 2) The convergence rate of $\mathcal{O}(1/T)$ is not improvable in the worst-case sense for finite-sum non-convex smooth problems.
- (Reviewer 2) The work provides a good overview of some existing works and uses standard assumptions for convergence analysis (though other reviewers contest their standardness).
- (Reviewer 3) The theoretical results are thorough, covering multiple settings including compression and partial participation.
- Note: Reviewer 4 explicitly states “I couldn’t find any strengths.” The positive view is primarily from Reviewer 3 and partially from Reviewers 1 and 2.

### Weaknesses

**Writing and Readability**
- The writing lacks clear logical flow and uses inconsistent terminology (e.g., “edge client,” “device,” “local client,” “worker” used interchangeably) (Reviewer 1).
- The claim that distributed SGD is unsuitable for FL due to data heterogeneity is incorrect; the issue is expensive communication (Reviewer 1, 4). The discussion of FedAvg’s “unaffordable communication” is confusing because local updates do reduce communication (Reviewer 4).
- Unclear why downlink communication reduction is necessary, given that many prior works assume it is fast and negligible (Reviewer 1).
- The two strategies/algorithms are presented together without clear justification for why they are combined in one paper rather than separate (Reviewer 1).
- Redundant content in the “Main Contributions” section (Reviewer 1).
- Assumption 3.1 should say “a constant L” not “an L”; no explanations are given for the equations in Section 3.1 (Reviewer 1). The notation $\eta_l$ in Theorem 4.3 is unreadable (Reviewer 2).
- Pseudocode for Algorithms 1-3 is poorly typeset; should use proper LaTeX algorithm packages (Reviewer 2). Some algorithms in pseudocode do not illustrate downlink compression despite the title (Reviewer 4).
- Inappropriate personal language: “Prior to the presentation of my strategy” (line 188) is informal for ICLR and “my” is inappropriate for multiple authors; this may leak that the paper is single-author, violating conference rules (Reviewer 2, 4).
- The statement in Assumption 3.1 that smoothness implies L-gradient Lipschitz is weak; it should be proved or cited as an equivalence (Reviewer 2). Assumption 3.4 is actually a definition, not an assumption; also line 167 contains a redundant colon (Reviewer 2).
- Notations are inconsistent between Equations (1)-(2) (LAG-style subscript $m$) and the main text (superscript $i$) (Reviewer 3).
- Typos: Corollary 4.1 should refer to Algorithm 1 (not 2); Corollary 4.3 should refer to Algorithm 2 and “partial participation” (Reviewer 3).

**Assumptions and Theory**
- Assumption 3.2 (bounded gradient) is weak, not cited from the original source (S. Ghadimi & G. Lan, 2013), and does not hold for basic cases like $f(\theta)=\theta^2$ (Reviewer 2).
- Assumption 3.3 (bounded stochastic gradient variance) is impractical; it does not hold even for data-point subsampling because the contribution of individual data points is not an additive random variable with bounded variance – it depends on the iterate, curvature, etc. The ABC assumption (Arjevani et al., 2020) is cited as the most relaxed (Reviewer 2).
- Assumption 3.4 about compressors: the example of a scaled sign compressor does not satisfy it because the parameter $q$ depends on the input $\theta$; it also assumes deterministic biased compressors, which is restrictive (Reviewer 4).
- Assumption 3.1 does not account for clients having different smoothness constants $L_i$ (Reviewer 2).
- Typo in Assumption 3.2: should be $\nabla f(.)$ not $\nabla f_i(.)$ (Reviewer 2).
- The theorems claim $\mathcal{O}(1/T)$ convergence, but the term proportional to $\Omega$ in Theorems 4.2 and 4.3 does not improve with $T$, so the claimed rates in Corollaries 4.1 and 4.2 are incorrect; the algorithm only converges to a neighborhood (Reviewer 2, 4). Reviewer 2 notes that Algorithms 1 and 2 (NLAA and AA) only converge to a neighborhood, unlike SOTA EF21-BC which achieves exact convergence.
- It is unclear what $f^*$ refers to in non-convex settings; the assumption that the function is bounded below is not explicitly stated (Reviewer 2).
- No explanation is given for why larger local steps accelerate convergence under full participation but cause slower convergence under partial participation (Reviewer 1).
- The paper claims to reduce computational costs (abstract) but provides no explanation of how this is achieved (Reviewer 1).

**Missing Comparisons and Related Work**
- The claim (lines 066-069) that bidirectional compression has not been studied is an overstatement. Several works exist, including EF21-P and Friends (2022), EF21 with Bells & Whistles (NeurIPS 2021 oral), Personalized Federated Learning with Communication Compression, 3PC (Richtárik et al., ICML 2022), 2Direction (Tyurin & Richtárik, NeurIPS 2023), and others (Reviewer 2, 4).
- The work does not compare against state-of-the-art biased compressor EF21-BC (EF21 with bidirectional biased compression, arXiv:2110.03294). EF21-BC’s convergence results are strictly better: it requires only L-smoothness (weaker than Assumption 3.1) and converges exactly (no neighborhood) (Reviewer 2).
- The idea of combining FedAvg with adaptive stepsizes has been explored before (e.g., Reddi et al., 2020, “Adaptive Federated Optimization”) and it is unclear how the proposed methods improve upon those (Reviewer 4).
- The paper claims that LAG requires storing $R$ models; this is disputed: LAG only requires storing the model from the previous round and the norm square of the difference (a scalar) (Reviewer 3).

**Experiments and Reproducibility**
- All figures in the experimental section are incomplete: e.g., Figures 1,2,3,4 contain only three or six lines, whereas the number of compared algorithms is four or six respectively, suggesting missing curves or poor presentation (Reviewer 1).
- No source code is provided to verify reproducibility (Reviewer 2).
- The experimental setting is too small (Reviewer 3).
- Constants $C$ and $D$ in the experiment section are not introduced; $\epsilon$ in the theorems is not defined; $M_t$ in the examples is not explained (Reviewer 4).

**Minority Positive View**
- Reviewer 3 (score 6) finds the algorithms novel, theoretical results thorough, and experiments promising, but acknowledges that the convergence rate is not improved and the setting is small. This contrasts with the strong criticisms from Reviewers 1,2,4 regarding assumptions, convergence only to a neighborhood, missing comparisons, and poor presentation.
