## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an unsupervised learning framework for attributed graph clustering that combines modularity maximization with graph coarsening (based on Feature Graph Coarsening, FGC) and additional regularization terms (Dirichlet energy, spectral similarity, coarsening reconstruction error). The optimization problem is solved via block majorization-minimization and can be integrated into GNN architectures (Q-GCN, Q-VGAE, Q-GMM-VGAE). Experiments on benchmark datasets show improved clustering performance over several baselines.

### Strengths
- The idea of incorporating modularity into graph coarsening within an integrated framework appears novel and interesting (R2), though some reviewers note the combination is straightforward (R3, R4, R5).
- Extensive numerical experiments are performed across multiple datasets and compared with many baselines (R2, R5). The method outperforms existing methods in the experiments (R1).
- Good theoretical results are provided in Section 4, including convergence guarantees (R2, R4). The proposed algorithms are provably convergent and much faster than state-of-the-art algorithms (R4).
- The literature review is detailed and comprehensive (R3), and the paper is well-organized and easy to follow (R3).
- The method is flexible and can be combined with various representation learning backbones (GCNs, VGAEs, GMM-VGAE), which is relevant and useful (R2, R3).
- Promising clustering performances are obtained on widely used graph datasets (R3).

### Weaknesses
**Novelty and Contribution**
- The proposed clustering model (Problem 5) is a trivial combination of previous works; none of the terms are newly designed by the authors (R3). The method simply adds a modularity-based regularization term $\text{tr}(C^TBC)$ to the existing FGC objective (Kumar et al., 2023), limiting both methodological and technical novelty (R5). The paper does not explain the shortcomings of FGC and how modularity would enhance it (R4). *Contrast: R2 found the idea novel and interesting despite these criticisms.*
- The related work section is not comprehensive; it misses important references such as Kumar et al. (2023, ICML) on featured graph coarsening (R4). Modularity maximization literature is not well quoted (R2). The paper does not discuss the known limitations of modularity (resolution limit, being “harmful”) or their impact on the present work (R2).

**Presentation and Clarity**
- The paper is very hard to understand for someone with a community detection background (R1). The introduction gives a bad overview: it does not cite a good modularity reference, makes the puzzling statement that “a higher value of modularity is associated with better quality clusters” (modularity is a heuristic without strong theoretical underpinning), and claims modularity maximization “requires intensive computations” while Louvain runs in nearly linear time (R1). The “Deep Graph Clustering” paragraph is incredibly difficult to follow, using undefined abbreviations (ARGA, ARVGA, DAEGC, SDCN) (R1).
- There are many typos, language errors, and inconsistent formatting (R1, R4, R5). Examples: missing punctuation in long lists, “architecures” instead of “architectures”, inconsistent equation references (Eq. 8 vs Eqn. 8), missing table captions for Figures 2 and 4, small font size in plots (R4). The abbreviation GNN appears before its full expansion (R5). The submitted title differs from the paper title (R4).
- Ambiguous statements: “the Fiedler vector produces a graph cut minimal in the weights of the edges” is unclear; the relationship to minimum cut is not as stated (R5). The claim that non-overlapping clusters are inconsistent with real graph data is an overstatement; many benchmarks have non-overlapping classes (R5). The term “volume of inter-cluster edges” is not defined (R5). In Equation 1, $\delta(c_i,c_j)$ is not defined (R5). The meaning of “the original graph is smooth” should be clarified (R5).
- The research motivation is not sufficiently novel or clearly expressed; there is inconsistency between the abstract (mentions Dirichlet energies) and the introduction (does not mention Dirichlet energies or explain why they are used) (R4).

**Experimental Concerns**
- Experiments are carried out on only 3 small datasets in the main Table 1; additional results in Table 3 compare only two other methods (one being FGC) (R5).
- Experimental settings for baselines are not provided (R4). Hyperparameter settings for the proposed method (learning rate, training iterations, dataset splits) are missing (R4). It is unclear how the parameters $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\lambda$ are selected and how robust the results are to their choice (R5).
- The ablation studies are trivial and not informative (R3). The modularity metric comparison yields a trivial conclusion (“no free lunch theorem”) without providing insight into the interaction of loss terms or the latent space evolution (R3). The paper does not analyze *why* the proposed method achieves superior performance (R4).
- In Table 1, SCGC and MVGRL have better performance on some metrics, yet the proposed method is bolded; this may be a typo (R3). Several algorithms in Table 1 are missing references or experimental results (R4).
- The full loss of Equation (6) appears not really needed; the modularity term alone already does a good job, and other terms sometimes degrade performance (e.g., using only one regularization term) (R2). The visualization in Figure 2b uses lines connecting non-consecutive points instead of a table, which is confusing (R1). The rescaling of ARI and modularity to percentages is confusing, as ARI can be negative and modularity’s upper bound is less than 1 (R1).

**Technical and Methodological Issues**
- The optimization problem (5) optimizes both $\tilde{X}$ and $C$ and uses a penalty $\|C\tilde{X}-X\|_F^2$, yet later in Section 4.2 only $C$ is optimized. This inconsistency is not explained (R3). Why not enforce the constraint $X=C\tilde{X}$ exactly instead of adding a penalty term? (R1)
- The log-det term in the Laplacian ensures *not a single* eigenvalue is zero, contrary to the claim that it ensures a “minimal” number are zero (R1). The complexity analysis does not clarify whether it refers to a single iteration or convergence (R1).
- Questions remain unanswered: Why are the cases $\alpha+\beta$ or $\beta+\lambda$ so much worse than $\beta$ alone? (R2) If the graph structure and features are not aligned, does forcing smoothness become detrimental? (R2) What happens if clusters are affected by modularity’s resolution limit? (R2)
- The method relies on heavy modifications of FGC, but the feature graph coarsening framework does not perform well on clustering; the paper does not explain why (R4).
- NMI is biased toward fine-grained clusterings; AMI or other measures are recommended (R1).
