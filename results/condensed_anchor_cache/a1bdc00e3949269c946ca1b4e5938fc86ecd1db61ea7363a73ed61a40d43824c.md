## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MP-TPT, a test-time prompt tuning method that augments CLIP with local visual representations and class-specific text descriptions, applied over two views (global visual, local visual, language). Experiments span 15 benchmark datasets. The three reviewers disagree in overall assessment (scores: 3, 3, 5). Two reviewers (scores 3) find substantial weaknesses in novelty, clarity, and experimental evidence, while one reviewer (score 5) praises the innovation and rigorous methodology but acknowledges limited improvements.

### Strengths
- **Motivation is clear and intuitive:** Incorporating local features to capture important contextual details missed by global-only views is a well-justified idea (R1, R2, R3).
- **Innovative perspective:** The method addresses a critical limitation of prior test-time prompt tuning that relies solely on global visual features, and introduces region-based prompts for class-specific descriptions (R3 – minority positive view).
- **Extensive experimental evaluation:** The paper conducts experiments on 15 datasets, including cross-dataset and domain generalization benchmarks, and provides ablation studies that detail each component’s contribution (R1, R3). One reviewer found the ablation studies add credibility (R3).
- **Presentation clarity mixed:** While one reviewer found the paper easy to understand (R2), another found it difficult to follow due to disorganized writing and unclear figures/tables (R1).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty and missing prior work:** The core idea of using local features to enrich image representations and leveraging visual features to enhance text descriptions has already been proposed in [1] (ICCV 2023). The paper does not cite or discuss this predecessor. The only difference seems to be the test-time prompt tuning setting (R1). [1] Task-Oriented Multi-Modal Mutual Learning for Vision-Language Models, ICCV 2023.

2. **Marginal experimental improvement and lack of statistical rigor:** 
   - The best MP-TPT-L result (65.66) is only 0.2% better than the baseline DiffTPT (65.47) in cross-dataset setting, and gains are <0.5% in domain generalization (R1, R2, R3).
   - The average improvement appears driven primarily by the EuroSAT dataset (10 classes), with performance degradation on several datasets (e.g., Flowers102, Caltech101) (R1).
   - No error bars or statistical significance tests are provided, making it uncertain whether the minor gains are due to randomness (R1, R2).

3. **Incomplete comparisons and generalization gaps:**
   - Missing comparisons with PromptAlign (66.92, 63.55) and TDA (67.53, 63.89), which outperform MP-TPT on cross-dataset and domain generalization (R1).
   - The method is evaluated only on the CoOp framework; it is unclear if it generalizes to other prompt tuning methods like MaPLe or PromptSRC (R2, R3). A plug-and-play claim would require such experiments.
   - Broader comparisons with state-of-the-art methods and analysis of scenarios where MP-TPT excels or falls short are needed (R3).

4. **Clarity and presentation issues:**
   - Multiple definitions of $K$ (number of classes vs. number of selected regions) (R1).
   - Equation (7) has mismatched parentheses and unclear notation: resulting shape is $R^{WH \times d}$, but how $M$ augmented features are derived is not explained; $f^t$ is not defined (R1).
   - Equation (8) incorrectly defines a set by including "$p(y_k | \tilde{f}_i^t)$" after the colon (R1).
   - The term "local visual representation" (Line 225) is ambiguous – does it refer to CLIP patch features? (R1)
   - Section 3.2.3 is difficult to understand: the distinction between test-time tuning and test-time inference is unclear; how $f^{t*}$ and $\hat{f}^{t*}$ are generated is not explained; Figure 2c does not clearly show how Eq. 12 applies (e.g., where is $\lambda$?) (R2).
   - Table 1 does not specify how inference time is calculated (seconds?), and different datasets with varying numbers of classes should have different speeds, so the table is not self-contained (R1). MP-TPT-S shows lower inference time than TPT – the experimental settings need clarification to ensure a fair comparison (R2).
   - The interaction between local visual features and text descriptions during optimization and inference is insufficiently clarified (R3). A deeper analysis of how local augmentations influence specific categories, especially complex classes, would strengthen the paper (R3).

5. **Effectiveness of design components:**
   - The use of random masks on local features as a proxy for random cropping is questionable; one reviewer explored a similar idea in test-time prompt tuning and found it ineffective, raising doubts about MP-TPT’s design (R1).
   - It is unclear whether local patch features align with text features, and the paper does not provide visualizations or discussion to address this concern (R1, citing [1] A Closer Look at the Explainability of CLIP).

6. **Missing trade-off and efficiency analysis:**
   - The claim of 1% improvement and 4.5× speedup simultaneously is misleading because they are achieved by different variants (MP-TPT-L and MP-TPT-S) (R1).
   - The inference efficiency improvement over TPT is not justified: multi-perspective views increase computational and storage costs, yet MP-TPT-S claims lower time. A detailed analysis of computational complexity, memory usage, and the trade-off between speed, accuracy, and number of parameters is missing (R2, R3).
