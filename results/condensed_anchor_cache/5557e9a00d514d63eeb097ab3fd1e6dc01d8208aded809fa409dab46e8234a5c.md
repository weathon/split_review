## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates model collapse in self-generating feedback loops where generative models are trained on data that includes synthetic data from previous models. It contrasts three scenarios: replace (each model trained only on synthetic from previous), accumulate (all real and synthetic data), and accumulate-subsample (fixed compute budget). Experiments are conducted on multivariate Gaussian modeling, kernel density estimators, linear regression, and a 2B parameter language model (Gemma2). The authors show that model collapse is avoidable when real data is retained (accumulate), and they identify a non-trivial interaction where the benefit of synthetic data depends on the absolute quantity of real data. Theoretical proofs are provided for Gaussian settings. Reviewers appreciate the timely problem and thorough experiments, but disagree on the novelty and generalizability of the findings.

### Strengths
- Important and timely problem: the impact of AI-generated content on model training and the risk of model collapse. (All reviewers)
- Thorough experiments across multiple generative modeling settings: multivariate Gaussian, kernel density estimators, linear regression, and language models. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Theoretical insights complement empirical results, with proofs for simpler models. (R1, R4)
- Clear writing and logical structure. (R3)
- Actionable guidance on balancing real and synthetic data, including the finding of a non-trivial interaction between real and synthetic data. (R2, R4)
- The experiments are well-designed and support the claims, with clear explanations of experimental setup. (R1, R3)

### Weaknesses
Note: Reviewer 3 (score 3) was considerably more negative than other reviewers (scores 8, 6, 6) on novelty and presentation; this contrast is preserved below where relevant.

- **Limited novelty.** The work builds heavily on prior work (Gertgrasser et al., 2024; Shumailov et al.) and a large portion of the experimental section (4/9 pages) validates previous findings rather than providing distinct contributions. One reviewer (score 3) considered the contribution incremental and insufficiently justified. (R1, R3)
- **Theoretical assumptions are idealized.** The proofs assume independence and Gaussian distributions, which may not hold for real-world data with complex dependencies and non-Gaussian, multimodal distributions. This limits generalizability, especially to language and image domains. (R2, R4)
- **Limited language model experiments.** The only language model used is Gemma2 with 2B parameters, far smaller than contemporary models like Llama (7B-70B). It is unclear whether findings extend to larger models. Additionally, experiments on other generative model types (VAE, diffusion models, transformers) are missing. (R1, R2, R4; R1 also notes need for different model strengths and synthetic data qualities)
- **Accumulate-subsample experiments are simplified.** The paradigm does not address real-world constraints such as dynamic memory handling, potential bias from subsampling, or computational overhead. (R4)
- **Insufficient justification for chosen settings.** The paper does not clearly articulate why the specific settings (replace, accumulate, accumulate-subsample) are particularly important or challenging, making the experimentation seem incremental rather than groundbreaking. (R3)
- **Presentation issues.** The related work discussion is minimal and does not summarize differing methodologies and conclusions from prior work. Figures have readability issues (axis labels, font size, notation). Several references are cited as arXiv preprints despite being published. The writing contains unnecessary sensationalism (e.g., "provocative pair of claims"). Use of tweets and LinkedIn posts as references was noted as unserious. (R3)
- **Reproducibility is unclear.** No explicit reproducibility statement or code release is mentioned. (R2)
- **Missing exploration of breaking point between replace and accumulate.** The paper does not investigate whether results hold if the amount of synthetic data added per iteration varies (e.g., adding 2n instead of n). (R3)
- **Missing baseline reference.** In the blob experiment, the NLL of the true distribution is not reported, which would provide a useful reference. (R3)
- **Theorem 1 not extended to accumulate-subsample.** A reviewer questions whether the theoretical result could be extended to the accumulate-subsample setup. (R3)
- **Effect of synthetic data quality not examined.** The paper focuses on quantity but not on quality; lower-quality synthetic data might exacerbate collapse, and filtering or curating synthetic data could mitigate this. This is a missing ablation. (R1, R4)
- **Minor reviewer disagreement.** One reviewer (score 8) initially had lower confidence and after author response raised score to 8 but stated ideal score would be 7 (accept but not spotlight/oral), indicating slightly less enthusiasm compared to the other positive reviewers. (R1)
