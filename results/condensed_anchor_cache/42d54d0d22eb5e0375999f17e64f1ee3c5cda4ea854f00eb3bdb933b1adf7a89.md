## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes UnifyVocab, a method to replace an LLM's vocabulary (tokenizer) with another's by training separate GloVe embeddings for source and target tokens from the same corpus, aligning them via cosine similarity, initializing the target embedding matrix, and fine-tuning. Experiments on Pythia (base model) replacing its vocabulary with Gemma, Qwen2, LLaMa 2 & 3 tokenizers are compared against random initialization, random permutation, and FOCUS (Dobler & de Melo 2023). English evaluation covers 6 standard tasks; cross-lingual transfer covers 12 languages (+English) on 4 tasks. The method recovers on average 98% of original performance after ~10B tokens of training, improves cross-lingual transfer over FOCUS, and two-stage tuning (freezing then full fine-tuning) outperforms direct full fine-tuning. Token-level distillation with the unified vocabulary requires less data and generally outperforms sequence-level distillation.

### Strengths
- Straightforward method to replace the tokenizer/vocabulary of an LLM given sufficient data.
- Useful for multilingual/crosslingual LLM applications.
- Experiments are extensive, covering multiple model scales, tokenizer types, and tasks.
- UnifyVocab is simple and appears effective in aligning tokens between vocabularies.

### Weaknesses
- **High cost:** Requires ~10B tokens for training, which is a significant cost compared to past approaches (e.g., machine translation where separately trained embeddings adapt with <20k tokens). The method does not consistently recover the original model's performance (see Tables 4 and 5 where the original Pythia model sometimes outperforms any replacement method).
- **Unjustified embedding space assumption:** Aligning source and target GloVe embeddings via cosine similarity assumes they lie in the same vector space (because trained on the same corpus), but this is not guaranteed—even two embeddings with the same vocabulary can be quite different (as noted in prior work). The paper does not mention or test this assumption, nor does it compare an explicit alignment step (e.g., as done in WECHSEL, which the method closely resembles except that WECHSEL aligns fastText embeddings across languages while UnifyVocel extends this to same-language tokenizer replacement). The conditions under which the method succeeds or fails are not analyzed (e.g., when vocabulary overlap is as low as 6% with Gemma-Pythia, is convergence much slower? How similar is this to random initialization? Are cosine similarities considerably lower, or less one-to-one mappings chosen? What if a different similarity metric were used?).
- **Missing comparisons and citations:** The paper lacks discussion/comparison with related vocabulary adaptation methods such as OFA (NAACL2024 Findings) and ZETT (arXiv:2405.07883, which uses a hypernetwork to predict new token embeddings and could serve as a stronger baseline).
- **Sensitivity to training corpus:** The token-token alignment learned via GloVe is sensitive to the choice of corpus, reducing generality.
- **Unclear motivation for same-domain replacement:** The paper does not convincingly justify why one would replace a model's tokenizer with another model's tokenizer intended for the same domain/language; vocabulary replacement is mostly meaningful when adapting to a new domain or language, but the experiments focus on English→English transfer.
- **Specific evaluation issues:** 
  - Figure 3(b) is not meaningful because the BLEU scores are very low (~2.4), making differences between initializations negligible.
  - In Table 4, it is unclear whether "0" in the "#Tune (B)" column means no training at all after replacement; clarification is needed.
  - The legend in Figure 3 should use consistent colors and ordering.
  - The paper uses BLEU to evaluate the alignment matrix; the reviewer asks whether other semantic metrics like COMET were tried, and if not, why.
- **Minor presentation issues:** Typos in lines 174 and 177, and the original Pythia model should be bolded in Tables 4 and 5 when it outperforms all replacement methods.
