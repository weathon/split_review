## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper studies the training dynamics of differentially private (DP) fine-tuning using a Langevin diffusion approximation of DP-SGD. The authors show theoretically and empirically that DP full fine-tuning (DP-FFT) can distort pre-trained backbone features due to misalignment between the pre-trained backbone and a randomly initialized linear head. They propose a hybrid strategy, DP-LP-FFT, which first performs linear probing (DP-LP) and then full fine-tuning, and provide convergence rates for two-layer ReLU networks. The theoretical insights are supported by experiments on multiple vision datasets and architectures. Reviewers disagree on the overall contribution: one reviewer (score 8) recommends accept, while two (scores 5 and 6) are more critical of novelty, theoretical strength, and reproducibility.

### Strengths

- **Important and relevant problem:** The paper tackles the challenge of differentially private fine-tuning, which is highly relevant given the high dimensionality of modern models. (R1 S1)
- **Intriguing concept:** Splitting the privacy budget between linear probing and full fine-tuning is a potentially valuable practical approach. (R1 S2, R3 S3)
- **Interesting empirical phenomenon:** The paper identifies and investigates that DP fine-tuning distorts pre-trained features (Figure 3), and the proposed DP-LP step can theoretically reduce this distortion. (R2 S1, R2 S2)
- **Theoretical analysis:** The paper provides a rigorous theoretical analysis using Langevin diffusion as an approximation of DP-SGD to explain feature distortion, which addresses a gap in the literature. (R1 S3, R3 S1)
- **Effectiveness of the proposed method:** Experiments on multiple datasets and architectures (Tables 1 and 2) demonstrate the benefits of the hybrid DP-LP-FFT approach. (R1 S4, R2 S3, R3 S2)
- **Practical insights:** The analysis provides insights into privacy budget allocation across DP-LP and DP-FFT, which is of practical interest. (R3 S3)

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty relative to prior work:** The idea of combining full fine-tuning and linear probing is not entirely novel; similar approaches were introduced and experimentally tested by Tang et al. (2023). The paper needs a clearer discussion of how it builds on or extends this prior work, particularly regarding the specific combination under DP and whether the theoretical analysis provides new insights beyond what is already known. (R1 W1)
- **Oversimplified theoretical model:** The theoretical framework uses a 2-layer ReLU network with decoupled feature learning and classification, which is far from realistic applications. The analysis relies on a simplified Langevin diffusion rather than DP-SGD, employing a zeroth-order asymptotic expansion that effectively removes the Brownian motion (Gaussian noise) – a core component of DP training. The gap between continuous Langevin dynamics and discrete DP-SGD steps (e.g., if Δt in Theorem 3.3 is in [0,1], feature distortion may not be an issue because training starts at t=1) also raises concerns about the generality of findings. (R1 W2, R3 Weakness)
- **Restrictive and unconventional assumptions:** Assumptions 3.1 and 3.2 (and Appendix assumption E.7) are very strong, requiring specific geometric structure on data and pre-trained features that may not hold in practice. Only one previous work referenced these assumptions, limiting the applicability of the theoretical results. (R1 W3)
- **Missing experimental details:** The clipping threshold – a key parameter in DP-SGD – is not specified, making it difficult to assess reproducibility and generalizability. A sensitivity analysis of the clipping threshold is also missing. (R1 W4)
- **Insufficient empirical comparison of feature distortion:** The paper argues that DP-FFT distorts features while DP-LP avoids distortion, but it does not provide a direct empirical comparison of the learned feature representations (e.g., via t-SNE, cosine similarity). This would provide stronger evidence beyond theoretical claims. (R2 Weakness)
- **Unclear theoretical presentation and notation:** Several parts of the theoretical exposition (including the Appendix) are difficult to follow or require clarification:
  - Ambiguous notation $\sim$ in line 214.
  - The choice of zero-mean Gaussian initialization for the linear head is restrictive and not well-motivated.
  - Meaning of “optimality” of $w_j$ in line 232 is unclear.
  - Theorem 4.1: The exact statement from Ganesh et al. (2023b) is not clearly referenced; authors should point to the exact place.
  - Theorems 4.2 and 4.3 are presented without commentary; the interpretation of constants and limit behavior need explanation.
  - The claim that a greater proportion of the privacy budget should be allocated to DP-LP when the total budget is smaller (Theorem 5.1) is not clearly justified.
  - The role of parameter $\rho$ (which can make $r$ arbitrarily large) is unclear.
  - Variable $D$ on line 442 and how $t_{lp}$ can be large in practice are not explained; Corollary 5.3 derivation from the main text is unclear.
  - Steps in Theorem 3.3 proof are unclear: e.g., how formula (28) is obtained, why the probability on line 958 equals $1-2^{-h}$.
  - Equation (10) in Theorem 3.4: The range $t\in(0,\Delta t)$ may be a typo; should it be $t\in(\Delta t,\infty)$? (R1 W5, R3 Q1)
  - Typos around line 1148. (R3 Q2)
  - In line 199, why are subspaces separated by $\mathbb{I}_{x_i^\top z>0}=\mathbb{I}_{y_i=-1}$ or $\mathbb{I}_{x_i^\top z>0}=\mathbb{I}_{y_i=1}$? (R2 Question)
- **Reviewer disagreement:** While one reviewer (score 8) finds the work strong and recommends accept, the other two reviewers (scores 5 and 6) raise significant concerns about novelty, theoretical soundness, and reproducibility, indicating a need for substantial revision.
