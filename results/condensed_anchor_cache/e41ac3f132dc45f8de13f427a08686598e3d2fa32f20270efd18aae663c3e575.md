## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a hierarchical Bayesian model for few-shot meta-learning, using a Normal-Inverse-Wishart (NIW) prior to model global (shared) and local (task-specific) parameters. The conjugate NIW-Gaussian relationship enables closed-form ELBO expressions for local posteriors, avoiding costly computation graphs needed by MAML. The framework unifies several established methods (MAML, ProtoNet, Reptile) as special cases, provides PAC-Bayesian generalization bounds, and yields algorithms that are architecture-independent and integrate with neural-process-family backbones. Experiments on regression and classification benchmarks show improved accuracy and calibration.

### Strengths
- First comprehensive hierarchical Bayesian treatment of few-shot meta-learning, with full derivations, theoretical guarantees, and a range of empirical evaluations (R1, R4).
- Toy experiment (Section 4) clearly motivates the need for hierarchical modeling and shared-vs-task-specific parameters (R1, R3, R5).
- Architecture-independent: works with various neural backbones and can be combined with neural-process-based meta-learners (R1).
- Unifies MAML, ProtoNet, Reptile as special cases of the hierarchical Bayesian framework (R1, R4).
- Provides theoretical analysis using PAC-Bayesian bounds, with convergence insights (R1, R2, R3).
- Scalable training algorithm leveraging NIW conjugate properties (R2, R6).
- Empirically robust across multiple tasks (classification, regression, pose estimation) with improved accuracy and calibration (R4, R6).
- Well-written and conceptually interesting inference approach (R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and completeness claims are contested.** Reviewers 5 and 6 strongly question the novelty: similar hierarchical Bayesian models have been extensively studied in the meta-learning literature [1-10], and the claim of being “the first complete hierarchical Bayesian treatment” is not supported, especially because the paper does not compare to Bayesian neural process variants that perform full inference over all parameters (e.g., [3-6] in R5). This undercuts the central claim about uncertainty modeling. (R5, R6). *Contrast: R1 and R4 consider the framework novel and unifying.*
- **Insufficient comparison to full Bayesian meta-learning methods.** The experiments lack comparisons to other full Bayesian approaches (e.g., Bayesian neural processes). This makes it difficult to assess the true contribution and the claimed benefits of full Bayesian treatment over partial-Bayesian or deterministic methods. (R2, R5)
- **Uncertainty quantification evaluation is inadequate.** (a) Calibration is validated on only one dataset, insufficient to demonstrate robustness. (b) Classification accuracy alone does not measure uncertainty quality; missing evaluations via predictive log marginal likelihood, reliability diagrams, or tasks requiring exploration-exploitation trade-offs (e.g., Bayesian optimization, bandits). (c) Many results (Section 7) report only average values without confidence intervals, and some superior performances lack statistical significance. (R2, R3, R5)
- **Multiple approximations (ELBO, quadratic) lack empirical validation.** The reliance on these approximations (beyond downstream performance) is not evaluated, and the theoretical analysis (Section 5) itself depends on their quality. (R3)
- **Technical gaps and missing details:**  
  - Why use a distributional estimate \(q(\phi)\) if only its mode is used at meta-test time? Why not apply the same inference method as during meta-training? (R5)  
  - Is the two-stage optimization (solving for \(L_i^*\) given \(L_0\), then optimizing \(L_0\)) guaranteed to find the same solution as joint optimization? (R5)  
  - Architectural details on combining the approach with set-based backbones (e.g., CNP) are missing. (R5)  
  - Computational complexity comparison (Figure 5) is misleading: NIW-Meta with SGLD requires multiple iterations, yet the figure shows constant complexity for the common parameter. Actual training times should be compared. (R2)  
  - Choice of NIW distribution and its limitations are not discussed; could other priors be used? How does the choice affect performance relative to other few-shot frameworks? (R1, R4)  
  - The model resembles hierarchical recurrent VAEs – what are the specific advantages for few-shot learning? (R4)  
  - Theorems 5.1 and 5.2: what concrete insights do equations (14)-(15) give about hyperparameter choices (e.g., \(\Lambda\)) or the relation between posterior approximation quality and generalization error? (R2)  
  - Missing proof sketches for theorems in the main paper (only full proofs in appendix). (R1)  
  - Paper flow: the toy experiment could be better placed relative to derivations and theory. (R1)  
  - Not all results include error bounds; boldfacing protocol unclear; Table 3/4 captions do not state that classification accuracy is reported. (R5)  
- **Limited discussion of limitations and future work.** No insights into avenues for improvement or extensions. (R1)  
- **Parameter choices (e.g., number of SGLD iterations, test-time variational inference steps, number of test-time model samples) are not explained or justified.** (R3)  
- **Limited novelty:** the overall approach is similar to most Bayesian meta-learning methods that use priors to relate task parameters and variational inference for posterior inference. (R6)
