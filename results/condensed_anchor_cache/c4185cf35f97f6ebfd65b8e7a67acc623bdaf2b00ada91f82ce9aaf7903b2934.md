## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper derives closed-form Neural Tangent Kernels (GNTK) and Gaussian Processes (GNNGP) for Graph Neural Networks in the infinite-width regime, covering standard GNNs, skip-concatenate GNNs, and Graph Attention Networks (GAT). It identifies and corrects an issue in prior work (Sabanayagam et al. 2022) by checking the special case without graph structure. The methods are evaluated on six node classification/regression datasets via kernel ridge regression.

### Strengths
1. **Architecture Extensions & Closed Forms** (R1, R3): The paper extends prior infinite-width theory to skip-concatenate GNNs and GAT, providing explicit formulas for their NNGP and NTK. This is seen as potentially useful for understanding these architectures. (R3)
2. **Correction of Prior Work** (R2): The authors identify an error in previous work (Sabanayagam et al. 2022) and demonstrate its incorrectness using the special case of no graph structure. They provide and prove corrected conclusions for multiple GNN architectures.
3. **Empirical Performance** (R1): Evaluation on six datasets shows that the new kernel models (GNTK, GNNGP) perform better than previous models that did not consider infinite width.
4. **Presentation Clarity** (R3): The paper’s structure is clear and easy to follow. (R1 also rates presentation 3/4.)

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited Novelty & Prior Work Omission** (R3; R1 partially): GNTK for node-level tasks has already been studied in published papers (e.g., [1-2]), though those works did not frame it as a contribution. The recursive computation core idea is already present in [1]; the current derivation differs mainly in matrix vs. vector representation and equation reorganization. The authors do not adequately delineate their novel contributions nor discuss how the closed forms yield new theoretical insights or practical benefits. (R3) Additionally, the theory for the base GCN model was already developed (R1).  
   **Reviewer disagreement**: R2 and R1 consider the corrections and extensions a clear contribution, while R3 finds novelty limited (contribution score 2 vs. 3).
2. **Missing Comparison Baselines** (R1): The paper does not compare against kernels developed by Du et al. and Niu et al., which are natural baselines for GNTK.
3. **Lack of Analysis of Results** (R1, R3): No explanation is given for why GNTK consistently outperforms GNNs. Why do GAT\*GP and GAT\*NTK perform worse than other kernels? (R3) A deeper theoretical analysis connecting the NTK formulas to architectural efficacy is missing. (R3)
4. **Theorem 3.2 Appears Incorrect** (R3): In the expression for $\mathbf{\Theta}$, $A^\top$ should be outside the bracket, otherwise the kernel matrix is not symmetric. (This may be a typo rather than a technical flaw.)
5. **Scalability & Dataset Choice** (R3): The method is not scalable or efficient, a common limitation for NTKs. The datasets used are not large (nor even medium) as claimed in Section 4.1; large-scale experiments (e.g., datasets in [3]) are absent.
6. **Experimental Rigor** (R3): Dataset splits are not standard, standard deviations are not reported, so statistical significance of results is unknown.
7. **Notation & Formatting Issues** (R2, R3):  
   - Confusing notation: before eq. (1), $X \in \mathbb{R}^{d_0 \times n}$ and $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d_0}$ are both used; eq. (1) has a shape mismatch typo. (R2)  
   - Abuse of capital letters, unnumbered equations, line wraps inside theorem statements (3.2, 3.3, 3.5). (R3)  
   - Inconsistent precision for the Crocodile dataset; many informal expressions throughout. (R3)  
   - The OpenReview version differs from the PDF version. (R2)
