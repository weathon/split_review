## Merged Review

### Summary

Hyperion fuses multi-trial (blackbox) optimizers (e.g., TPE) and gradient‑descent one‑shot algorithms (DARTS) to jointly optimize neural network hyperparameters and architectures. It uses a smart splitter to assign parameters to the appropriate optimizer, a sub‑search‑space sampler to reduce GPU memory during one‑shot search, and orchestrates co‑optimization. Experiments on MNIST, CIFAR‑10, Fashion‑MNIST, EuroSAT, and an industrial motor dataset show improvements in custom optimization metrics and reduced GPU memory relative to non‑fused baselines.

### Strengths

- **Important problem**: Joint hyperparameter and architecture optimization is a difficult, high‑impact challenge in AutoML; treating them separately yields suboptimal solutions. The fusion of blackbox and one‑shot optimizers is a natural and interesting direction ([R1](../Users/seanschmerer/R1), [R2](../Users/seanschmerer/R2), [R4](../Users/seanschmerer/R4)).
- **Intuitive approach**: Combining multi‑trial and gradient‑descent methods is conceptually clear and motivated by their complementary strengths ([R1](../Users/seanschmerer/R1)).
- **GPU memory savings**: The sub‑space selection technique reduces the memory footprint of DARTS, a critical practical consideration. Hyperion consistently outperforms blackbox optimization and DARTS alone in terms of optimized metrics with reduced search costs ([R2](../Users/seanschmerer/R2)).
- **Ablation studies**: Experiments (Section 4.5) shed light on the design choices, e.g., the evolution of kernel size probabilities ([R3](../Users/seanschmerer/R3)).
- **Technical soundness**: Given its complexity, the system is reasonably described and technically solid ([R3](../Users/seanschmerer/R3); note reviewer 4 disagrees on clarity).
- **Well‑written structure** (majority opinion): The paper is easy to navigate ([R2](../Users/seanschmerer/R2)); one reviewer (R4) found clarity lacking.

### Weaknesses

**1. Limited empirical evaluation**

- **Trivial datasets**: Experiments use only small benchmarks (MNIST, CIFAR‑10, Fashion‑MNIST, EuroSAT). Overfitting risk is high when jointly optimizing on such data, and generalizability to large‑scale tasks (ImageNet, COCO) is unproven ([R1](../Users/seanschmerer/R1), [R2](../Users/seanschmerer/R2), [R4](../Users/seanschmerer/R4)).
- **Weak baselines**: Comparisons to DARTS and a simple blackbox (TPE) are insufficient. Methods with lower memory footprint (PC‑DARTS, GDAS, SNAS) and joint NAS/HPO algorithms (DHA [R2 ref 4], Zela et al. 2018 [R2 ref 6], JAHS, Auto‑PyTorch) are missing ([R2](../Users/seanschmerer/R2), [R3](../Users/seanschmerer/R3), [R4](../Users/seanschmerer/R4)). DARTS was chosen despite follow‑up work that already reduces its memory cost ([R2](../Users/seanschmerer/R2)–questions).
- **Obscure optimization metrics**: Custom metrics (e.g., combining accuracy and parameters) are used without motivation; this puts DARTS at an unfair disadvantage (R3). For MNIST, Fashion‑MNIST, and EuroSAT, the stated goal is accuracy but Figure 4 shows different metrics. It is impossible to compare with known state‑of‑the‑art ([R3](../Users/seanschmerer/R3), [R4](../Users/seanschmerer/R4)). The multi‑objective claim is actually a priori scalarization – true Pareto‑based optimization never happens ([R4](../Users/seanschmerer/R4)).
- **No statistical significance**: Figures 4 and 5 lack standard deviations or confidence intervals. Results on MNIST show only a 0.001 improvement, and Figure 5 implies no significance ([R3](../Users/seanschmerer/R3), [R4](../Users/seanschmerer/R4)). It is unclear whether multiple runs were performed ([R4](../Users/seanschmerer/R4) – questions).
- **Excessive search costs**: Runtime is very high (e.g., 72 GPUh on MNIST, 96h on CIFAR‑10) – trivially solvable tasks like MNIST can be solved in minutes with a simple CNN. Why was MNIST given more budget (72h) than Fashion‑MNIST, EuroSAT, Motor (20h)? No time‑wise comparison against baselines is provided ([R2](../Users/seanschmerer/R2), [R3](../Users/seanschmerer/R3), [R4](../Users/seanschmerer/R4)). The 70 GPU‑day experiment in Fig. 5 is unexplained ([R2](../Users/seanschmerer/R2)–questions).
- **Industrial dataset**: The motor dataset is not publicly available, hurting reproducibility ([R4](../Users/seanschmerer/R4)).

**2. Limited novelty and missing related work**

- **Precedence**: AutoHAS already proposed a similar fusion of multi‑trial and one‑shot optimizers ([R2](../Users/seanschmerer/R2)). Sub‑space partitioning is not new (LaMCTS, Few‑shot NAS) ([R2](../Users/seanschmerer/R2)). Joint HPO+NAS has been tackled by DHA, JAHS, Auto‑PyTorch, and Zela et al. (2018) – none are compared against ([R2](../Users/seanschmerer/R2), [R3](../Users/seanschmerer/R3), [R4](../Users/seanschmerer/R4)).
- **“Best of both worlds” claim questioned**: Hyperion retains key disadvantages of both categories (DARTS instability, many full trainings from multi‑trial), potentially becoming the “worst of both worlds” ([R4](../Users/seanschmerer/R4]).

**3. Clarity and explanation issues**

- **Algorithm description**: Section 3.4 / Algorithm 1 is overly complicated and hard to follow; a higher‑level overview is needed ([R1](../Users/seanschmerer/R1), [R2](../Users/seanschmerer/R2), [R4](../Users/seanschmerer/R4]). The full interplay of components only becomes clear on page 6 ([R4](../Users/seanschmerer/R4)).
- **Terminology confusion**: “Parameters” and “hyperparameters” are used interchangeably ([R4](../Users/seanschmerer/R4)). “FitModel()” is misleading (sounds like full training, not search) ([R4](../Users/seanschmerer/R4)–questions). The claim that Bayesian optimization learns optimized sub‑search spaces (Section 3.2) is incorrect ([R4](../Users/seanschmerer/R4)).
- **No code provided**, hampering reproducibility ([R2](../Users/seanschmerer/R2)).
- **Hyper‑hyperparameters**: The system itself has many components (splitter, sampler, TPE, DARTS) whose own hyperparameters and sensitivity are not discussed ([R3](../Users/seanschmerer/R3), [R1](../Users/seanschmerer/R1)–questions).

**4. Search space limitations and miscellaneous**

- **Tiny discrete search spaces**: E.g., learning rate only has two values (Table 1). It is unclear how Hyperion handles continuous hyperparameters (e.g., learning rate in range 1e‑3 to 1e‑5) or scales to larger spaces ([R1](../Users/seanschmerer/R1)).
- **No GPU memory benchmark on a large dataset** (e.g., ImageNet) to demonstrate the claimed savings ([R3](../Users/seanschmerer/R3)).
- **Missing details on costs**: Which part of search takes most time? Why did Fig. 5 experiments take 70 GPU days? ([R2](../Users/seanschmerer/R2)–questions).
- **Section 4.5 is not a proper ablation** – it does not test removal of any component ([R4](../Users/seanschmerer/R4)).
- **Preprocessing hyperparameters** are fixed with no justification ([R4](../Users/seanschmerer/R4)–questions).
