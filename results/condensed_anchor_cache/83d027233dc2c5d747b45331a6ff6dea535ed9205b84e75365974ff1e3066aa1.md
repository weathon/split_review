## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses continual learning for diffusion models and proposes the MAGD framework with three components: Noisy Intermediate Generative Distillation (NIGD), Class-guided Generative Distillation (CGGD), and Signal-Guided Generative Distillation (SGGD). The goal is to reduce computational cost, improve class balance, and maintain image quality during generative replay. Experiments are conducted on Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100, with reported improvements in FID and KLD over baselines.

### Strengths
- Tackles an interesting and important topic: continual learning for diffusion models (Reviewer 2).
- The proposed framework is technically reasonable with detailed explanations (Reviewer 2). SGGD is a good strategy based on SNR analysis and is considered interesting (Reviewers 1 and 3).
- Outperforms both diffusion-based and ER-based baselines in terms of FID and KLD (Reviewer 1). Training time is improved compared to prior works (Reviewer 1).
- Evaluation includes a variety of baselines and datasets, with fine-grained analysis by tasks (Reviewer 2).
- Provides sufficient methodology details and ablation studies for the proposed components (Reviewer 4). Figures illustrating the ideas are decent (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: CGGD directly incorporates classifier guidance from prior works (e.g., Diffusion Models Beat GANs on Image Synthesis, DDGR) and lacks novelty (Reviewers 1 and 3). In contrast, SGGD is viewed as more interesting, but the overall combination is not clearly distinguished from existing techniques.
- **Insufficient baselines**: Only plain ER is compared in one table, and accuracy is within one standard deviation, questioning the classification benefit given the higher training time and memory (Reviewer 1). Baselines are outdated and not comprehensive (Reviewer 4). No comparison to state-of-the-art replay methods or faster samplers like DPM-Solver / DPM-Solver++ (Reviewer 2), which could make existing methods like DDGR fast enough and reduce the claimed advantage (Reviewer 2).
- **Small datasets**: Experiments are limited to Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100, which are considered small and toy; the continual learning setup is synthetic (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). Missing experiments on larger-scale datasets like ImageNet (at least 64×64, as in DDGR) are required to assess scalability and real-world applicability (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Computational cost**: Despite aiming for rapid updates, training still takes several hours (Reviewer 1). Diffusion-based replay may be too slow and memory-heavy compared to simpler replay methods, especially if accuracy gains are marginal (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing analysis and clarity**:
  - The influence of distillation strategies vs. sampling quality is unclear. Results (Table 4) show that with 100 diffusion steps the performance gap between MAGD and DGR-distill becomes significantly smaller, suggesting sample quality dominates (Reviewer 2). The role of NIGD needs deeper analysis, especially the impact of using all intermediate noisy images given the known gap between forward and backward processes (Reviewer 3). Why the direct approach mitigates degradation, if it is equivalent for distillation, is not elaborated (Reviewer 1).
  - Missing qualitative visualizations of generated images at different stages (Reviewers 1 and 2).
  - Missing standard continual learning metrics such as forgetting rate (Reviewer 3).
  - No insight into the choice of weights for \(L_{replay}\) and \(L_{total}\) in Equations 11 and 13 (Reviewer 3).
- **Writing and presentation issues**: Notation errors (Eq. 1: sum over N instead of n; Line 232: unclear whether output is noised image or noise; Algorithm 1 line 7: typo "Generate Images") (Reviewers 1 and 3). Typos and missing spaces/punctuations at L210, L255, L303, L330; supplementary Table 3 should be centered (Reviewer 2). Overall writing is not well-organized, with jumping logic, inconsistent notations, and wrong citing formats (Reviewers 3 and 4). While Reviewer 1 found the writing and figures decent, Reviewers 3 and 4 consider it immature and in need of substantial proofreading and revision.
- **Problem motivation and scope**: The motivation is not well-justified; it is unclear whether the continual learning scenario for diffusion models is realistic or ad-hoc (Reviewer 4). The narrow scope may be less attractive to a broad audience (Reviewer 4).
