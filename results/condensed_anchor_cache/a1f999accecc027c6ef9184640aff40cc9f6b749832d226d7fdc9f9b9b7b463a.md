## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SharedLLM, a method for extending the context window of LLMs using a hierarchical dual-model architecture. A lower model compresses past context into multi-grained representations via a binary tree structure and query-aware retrieval, while an upper model acts as a decoder that integrates this compressed information. Information transfer occurs only at the lowest layers (self-injection). The approach is evaluated on language modeling up to 128K tokens and instruction-following benchmarks, showing competitive results and efficiency gains (2× over streaming, 3× over encoder-decoder architectures). The paper also includes ablation studies and efficiency measurements.

### Strengths
- Introduces an original dual-model architecture with a context tree data structure for multi-grained compression and query-aware retrieval, addressing an important research area (efficient long-context training/inference).
- Strong experimental results on several long-context benchmarks (language modeling and instruction-following tasks), with clean empirical setup and ablation studies demonstrating design effectiveness.
- High efficiency: achieves substantial speed-ups (2× over streaming, 3× over encoder-decoder) and reduced memory consumption.
- Paper is well written and organized, effectively communicating the core ideas despite the method’s complexity.
- Focuses on a practical and timely problem of extending context windows without costly continual pre-training.

### Weaknesses
- **Method complexity and optimization concerns**: The complicated design (multiple chunks, downsampling modules, sparse retrieval) makes engineering difficult and raises questions about gradient estimation and training convergence. The use of the last token embedding as a chunk representation for retrieval is particularly questionable under sparse computation.
- **Long-context performance risks**: Chunking and downsampling may lose critical information. Missing compulsory evaluations like needle-in-a-haystack (only two subtasks of InfiniteBench are used without rationale). Examples: repeating all context or multi-turn queries where dropped context cannot be recovered.
- **Missing baselines**: The experiment lacks comparisons with important KV pruning methods (StreamingLLM, H2O, SnapKV, FastGen) that are also efficient and memory-friendly. Alternative context extension methods (position interpolation, memory-augmented architectures) are not tested.
- **Presentation issues**: Figure 1 is unclear (encoder-decoder vs decoder-only, cross-attention role not well explained, “tree cross-attention” undefined, “self-injection” not shown). The concept of “information preservation” is not elaborated. Notations like compression ratio are not sufficiently clarified.
- **Incomplete experimental justification**:
  - Hyperparameters of the context tree (depth, compression ratio) are not discussed; sensitivity to tasks is unknown.
  - Handling of variable sequence length during training and inference is not addressed.
  - The role and best practice for different M values (model architecture base value) are unclear.
  - Evaluation only uses LLaMA-2; no justification for excluding newer models like LLaMA-3.
  - The method does not consistently outperform baselines on LongBench.
- **Potential validity issues in results**: Perplexity comparisons in Tables 1 and 2 may be invalid due to different experimental settings—the CEPE result is reproduced, but other results are from public checkpoints. Only two subtasks of InfiniteBench are evaluated, and the choice is not justified.
- **Missing ablation and sensitivity analyses**: Questions about retrieval policy sensitivity, compression ratio, tree depth, and injection layer settings are not addressed in the paper.
