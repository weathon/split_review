## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes SensZOQ, a framework that combines static sparse zeroth-order (ZO) optimization with quantization for on-device fine-tuning of LLMs. It identifies an extremely small (0.1%) subset of "sensitive" parameters from pre-training (by top magnitude entries of the diagonal of the empirical Fisher information matrix), transfers this mask to downstream tasks, and ZO fine-tunes only those parameters while quantizing the rest. SensZOQ enables fine-tuning Llama2-7B on <8GiB GPU memory and outperforms ICL and full-model ZO fine-tuning across several benchmarks.

### Strengths

- Identifies that only an extremely small subset (0.1%) of LLM parameters needs to be updated during ZO fine-tuning; this sparsity pattern can be derived from pre-training and transferred across downstream tasks without modification. (R1, R2, R3)
- Memory-efficient: enables fine-tuning Llama2-7B on <8GB GPU by updating only 0.1% of parameters and quantizing the rest; outperforms ICL and ZOFull-FT across tasks like SST-2, RTE. (R2, R5)
- Quantization integration further reduces memory demands without substantial performance loss. (R5, R4)
- Provides theoretical analysis: detailed convergence rate for SensZOQ with proofs (Eq.5, Eq.6); cumulative normalized sum of gradient norm squares supports the sparse sensitivity assumption; reveals that 0.1% parameters contribute about 50% of gradient norm scale. (R2, R4, R3)
- Extensive experiments across various LLMs and downstream tasks show competitive performance; solid ablation studies on weight importance metrics and detailed analysis of gradient sparsity and sensitive parameter dynamics in appendix; implementation and experiment details for reproducibility. (R1, R3, R4, R2)
- Intuitive and elegant approach, well-supported by theory. (R3, R4)

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty concerns:** The method combines existing techniques: mask construction by top Fisher diagonal entries (Sung et al., 2021), fixed sparse masks for language models (SparseMeZO, Liu et al., 2024a), and quantization (SqueezeLLM, Kim et al., 2024). The combination appears straightforward. The sensitivity mask also resembles prior work on activation outliers. (R1, R3)
- **Memory comparison may be unfair:** Mask construction requires backpropagation to obtain gradients of all parameters, costing massive memory, but this memory is not counted in the comparison. The memory cost for quantization is also not accounted. The method compares a quantized model against unquantized baselines. A clear memory breakdown (model weights, optimizer states, intermediate activations for forward and backward passes) is needed to validate the <8GB claim. (R1, R2)
- **Limited evaluation scale and task scope:** Only experiments with 7B or smaller models (max LLaMA2-7B, OPT-6.7B); results on larger models (13B, 30B) are missing. Sensitivity assessed solely on C4 dataset (natural language); generalizability to code generation, math reasoning (GSM8K), commonsense reasoning (PIQA, etc.), code generation (HumanEval), and domain-knowledge benchmarks (MMLU) is uncertain. Such harder tasks are where LLMs excel over smaller models. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Missing baselines and efficiency data:** Tables 1 and 2 lack full FO fine-tuning results (Adam, BF16) as baselines. No actual memory usage or throughput/runtime data are provided despite claims of training efficiency. Comparisons with advanced ZO methods mentioned in the introduction are missing. (R5, R2)
- **Justification against smaller models unclear:** It is unclear why one should use SensZOQ + LLMs instead of directly fine-tuning smaller decoder-encoder transformers like RoBERTa (125M/355M), which consume less memory and achieve better performance on the same benchmarks. (R4)
- **Performance explanation needed:** Why does SensZOQ outperform ZO Full FT (Table 1) and 16-bit ZO-SGD (Table 2) despite using sparsity and 4-bit quantization? (R5)
- **Transferability of sparse mask not rigorously validated:** No ablation study shows that the mask derived from C4 generalizes across different downstream tasks without re-generation. The claim that the sparsity pattern is transferable needs more evidence across diverse tasks. (R2, R4)
- **Slow convergence not empirically shown:** Missing convergence curves; only theoretical convergence rate is provided. ZO methods are known to converge slowly, but no empirical training curves are shown. (R2, R3)
- **Inference speed claims insufficiently supported:** After fine-tuning the model is still dense; the paper claims faster token generation but provides insufficient details on sparse operations to explain why. The quantization follows SqueezeLLM; a high-level discussion of why inference is faster is needed. (R1)
- **Insufficient quantization details:** The specific quantization technique is not described in enough detail for reproducibility. (R3)
- **Presentation issues:** Abbreviations like “FO” are not defined before first use. Baseline methods on the x-axis of Figure 1 are not explained in the caption or text. (R5)
- **Practical insight from theoretical analysis unclear:** The implications of the theoretical convergence rate (Section 2.3) are unclear; actionable insights are missing. (R3)
- **Assumptions about loss landscape (Assumptions 2, 3 on page 17) may not hold for LLMs:** No literature pointers are provided to justify that these assumptions always hold given the highly non-convex and complex nature of LLM loss landscapes. (R4)
- **Concerns from minority reviewer (R1, score 5):** The technical contribution is limited; the proposed workflow is straightforward and lacks novelty. Memory comparison fairness is questioned because mask construction (requiring full backward pass) is not included. (R1)
