## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MoLEx, a Mixture of Layer Experts that treats pre-trained layers as experts and uses a router to select top-k layers for activation during fine-tuning. It aims to improve parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) by enabling layer-level information exchange with minimal additional overhead. Experiments on RoBERTa and GPT-2 on GLUE and E2E show gains over LoRA baselines. Reviewers gave scores 8, 6, 5. One reviewer (8) found the idea novel, clearly motivated, and the experiments comprehensive. Two others (6,5) raised concerns about motivation clarity, efficiency claims, experimental scope, and missing ablations.

### Strengths
- **Novelty & motivation**: The idea of leveraging layers as experts in a sparse MoE for PEFT is novel and a worthwhile research direction. The paper provides theoretical proof to justify robustness in a simplified model.
- **Theoretical support**: A formal justification (Lemma and Theorem) for the method’s potential to preserve useful pre-trained information and improve fine-tuning.
- **Simplicity & compatibility**: MoLEx does not alter the model structure or add extra expert parameters; it is compatible with existing PEFT methods (e.g., LoRA) while maintaining the same number of effective parameters.
- **Empirical performance**: On GLUE (RoBERTa) and E2E (GPT-2), MoLEx achieves significant improvements over LoRA baselines with nearly equal speed, memory, and parameter cost. The experiments are well-designed and show comprehensive results across multiple tasks.
- **Computational efficiency**: The layer experts can be processed in parallel, and the router adds minimal overhead; the method scales well with model size.

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and explanation of performance gains**: The paper’s motivation and the claim that “relevant linguistic information is captured by selected experts” (Figure 1) are not fully convincing. Most NLP tasks involve multiple levels of linguistic information, making it unclear why task-specific layer selection helps. The introduction does not clearly articulate the limitations of existing sMoE methods and the specific problem MoLEx solves. More discussion or an alternative explanation for the performance gains is needed.
- **Implementation ambiguity**: The exact mixing mechanism is not clearly specified. For example, when combined with LoRA, does the forward pass compute \(x_{t+1} = x_t + \alpha u_t(x_t) + (1-\alpha) u_l(x_t)\) (where \(u_l\) is the top-1 layer output), or \(x_{t+1} = x_t + u_t(x_t) + \alpha B_t(A_t(x_t)) + (1-\alpha) B_l(A_l(x_t))\)? Training details should be clarified and a method diagram provided to complement the mathematical formulas.
- **Efficiency claims are misleading**: The statement that “these layer experts can be computed in parallel and do not increase the computational time” (lines 494-495) overlooks the increased total FLOPs per token when top-k > 1 (e.g., each input must forward through two layers for top-1). While parallelism can keep wall-clock time similar, the computational load increases, affecting fair comparison with standard PEFT. The paper should provide single-GPU inference time and training time overhead statistics, and discuss the resource cost of parallelism.
- **Misrepresentation of trainable parameters**: Tables 1 and 2 show MoLEx as having no additional parameters compared to LoRA, but the gate layer (e.g., a linear layer) has its own parameters. A clear breakdown of all trainable parameters (including the router) should be reported.
- **Hyperparameter selection**: The choice of K in top-K and the number of experts in the upcycling process is not justified. While Top-1 is common, the impact of varying K on performance should be ablated or discussed.
- **Limited model and task scope**: Experiments are confined to RoBERTa and GPT-2, which are relatively outdated. To demonstrate generalizability, experiments on more recent LLMs (e.g., Qwen-2.5, Llama-3.1) and broader benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval) are needed. Current evaluation with GLUE and E2E may be insufficient.
- **Lack of full parameter training validation**: MoLEx is only validated with LoRA. Its applicability to full parameter fine-tuning should be tested to show broader utility.
- **Token-level vs. sentence-level routing**: The appendix states MoLEx uses a sentence-level routing (all tokens in a batch go through the same experts), whereas typical sMoE operates at the token level. Exploring token-level activation would clarify the method’s adaptability.
- **Catastrophic forgetting not evaluated**: The paper claims that MoLEx “preserves useful information in the pre-trained model” (line 148), but this is only supported mathematically. An empirical experiment on catastrophic forgetting (e.g., using the TRACE benchmark) is necessary to validate the claim.
- **Presentation**: The alignment and layout of Lemma and Theorem are messy and should be adjusted for readability.
