## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes SODP, a two-stage framework for learning a versatile diffusion planner. First, a diffusion model is pre-trained offline on large-scale, sub-optimal, task-agnostic multi-task trajectories without reward labels. Second, the pre-trained model is fine-tuned for a specific downstream task using online PPO with a novel Behavior Cloning (BC) regularization to prevent performance collapse. Experiments on Meta-World (50 tasks, state-based) and Adroit (3 tasks, image-based) show that SODP outperforms BC and offline RL baselines. Reviewers generally find the idea interesting and the paper well-written, with the BC regularization and use of sub-optimal data noted as strengths. However, significant concerns are raised about evaluation fairness (baselines not given online fine-tuning), missing control experiments to isolate contributions of sub-optimal data and pre-training, missing baselines (online RL, diffusion-based RL), lack of methodological advancements for handling sub-optimal data, and insufficient justification of the gradient derivation. Reviewer 3 (score 6) is more positive, while Reviewer 5 (score 3) is most critical, particularly questioning the technical soundness of the policy gradient formulation.

### Strengths

- The paper is well-written, clearly organized, and includes illustrative figures that aid comprehension. (R1, R3)
- The framework leverages sub-optimal, task-agnostic data for pre-training, contrasting with traditional reliance on task-specific expert demonstrations or reward labels. (R2, R4)
- The fine-tuning stage introduces a novel BC regularization (BC surrogate loss) that effectively balances preserving pre-trained knowledge (preventing model drift) and exploring high-reward behaviors. This is a creative and systematic design, supported by comprehensive ablation studies comparing different regularization strategies. (R2, R3, R4, R5)
- SODP demonstrates strong empirical results, outperforming state-of-the-art BC and offline RL baselines on the Meta-World and Adroit benchmarks, with rapid convergence and robustness across diverse tasks and input modalities. (R1, R2, R5)

### Weaknesses

- **Fairness of comparisons and baseline setup:** Multiple reviewers question whether baselines were given equal access to online fine-tuning. SODP benefits from an online environment for fine-tuning (with PPO and continuous data collection), while BC and offline RL baselines are presumably trained only offline on sub-optimal data. This may give SODP an unfair advantage. The paper does not clarify whether baselines undergo any pre-training or have access to online data collection. (R1, R3, R4)
- **Missing and inconsistent baselines:** Key baselines are absent. Specifically, online RL methods (e.g., non-diffusion PPO) and diffusion-based online/offline RL methods (e.g., DDPO, DP3) should be included for a fair comparison. The baseline set is inconsistent between Meta-World (includes offline RL) and Adroit (excludes offline RL). The term "Simple DP3" in Table 2 is unexplained. (R4)
- **Missing control experiments to isolate contributions:** The paper fails to separate whether performance gains stem from sub-optimal data pre-training or from online data collection during fine-tuning. Critical control experiments are needed:
    - Fine-tuning with high-quality offline data (e.g., using only expert data in fine-tuning) vs. with online data. (R3)
    - Direct training (from scratch) with high-quality data without pre-training. (R3)
    - Direct comparison between optimal and sub-optimal pre-training data (e.g., using the first 50% of SAC replay buffer vs. the last 30% of converged SAC trajectories). (R3)
    - Varying the quality of sub-optimal data (different percentages of replay buffer, while keeping total transitions fixed). (R3, R4)
    - Experiment using only single-task sub-optimal data for pre-training to assess the necessity of multi-task data. (R4)
    - Comparison of the proposed fine-tuning method (PPO + BC) with a simpler behavior cloning (BC) only fine-tuning on collected high-reward trajectories. (R3, R5)
- **Methodological concerns:**
    - No specialized design for handling sub-optimal data in pre-training: the pre-training stage follows standard diffusion model training, identical to training with expert data. The paper does not address how the model handles noise, variance, or bias in sub-optimal transitions compared to expert data. (R3)
    - The derivation of Equations (11) and (12) is insufficiently explained. Specifically, the connection between the reverse diffusion process probability $p_{\theta}(a_t^{k-1}|a_t^k, s_t)$ and the standard policy gradient $\nabla \log \pi(a|s)$ is not clearly justified. The paper claims that applying the trust region loss to the diffusion process is equivalent to applying it to the RL MDP, but this equivalence is not established. The misapplication may overlook the challenge of improving sample efficiency in RL. (R5)
    - Action and state space limitations: SODP requires tasks to share the same action space, and it is unclear how varying state space dimensions (or different sensor modalities) are handled. No discussion of adapting input/output layers for differing action spaces. (R2)
- **Experimental details and reproducibility:**
    - Learning curves (Fig. 4) only show fine-tuning steps, omitting the substantial pre-training phase (5e5 steps), potentially understating total computational cost. (R3)
    - Lack of details on the number of fine-tuning steps, computational resources required, and associated costs. This hinders reproducibility and assessment of practical feasibility. (R2)
    - _SODP_scratch_ (model trained from scratch with online RL) fails to succeed in Fig. 7, which seems unreasonable given it has access to online interaction and rewards. Additional experiments with an online RL diffusion planner trained from scratch are needed. (R4)
    - The methodology for line 511 ("We use the same rollouts generated by the pre-trained model to approximate the target policy and initialize the replay buffer") is unclear and its validity for testing SODP_scratch is questionable. (R4)
    - Inconsistent descriptions of baseline data sources: some places (line 344, 346) suggest baselines are trained on expert data while SODP uses sub-optimal data; other places (line 376) claim all baselines are on sub-optimal data. Clarification is needed. (R4)
    - The claim in line 362 ("The baselines used in Meta-World struggled to handle this high-dimensional data structure") lacks supporting analysis or experimental evidence. (R4)
- **Problem setting and motivation:**
    - Concrete examples of how sub-optimal data would be collected in real-world settings (without rewards) are needed to validate the motivation. If rewards are available, direct offline/online RL methods could be used instead. (R4)
    - The 3D visual feature process from DP3 used in Adroit (image-based) may not be compatible with the MTDIFF baseline; it is unclear whether it was used for Diffusion Policy in Table 2 and what SODP's performance is without it. (R1)
- **Minor issues and clarification requests:**
    - Typographical error: in Line 215, $a_0^t$ should be $a^0_t$. (R2)
    - Figure 4: what does the vertical orange dashed line indicate? (R1)
    - Sensitivity of the weight coefficient $\lambda$ in Eq. 15 is not analyzed. (R4, implicit from R3's request for BC coefficient justification)
    - Clarify what "state space differs across tasks" means (dimensional differences vs. physical meaning of indices) and how that is handled. (R4)
    - Clarify line 968: does fine-tuning occur in a multi-task setting or single-task? (R4)
    - How is the target policy selected in detail? (R2)
    - After fine-tuning on a specific task, can the model be evaluated on other tasks without catastrophic forgetting? (R2)
