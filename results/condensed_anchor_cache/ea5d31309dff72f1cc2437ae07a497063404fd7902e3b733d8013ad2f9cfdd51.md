## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces ShareChatX, a large-scale synthetic spoken dialogue dataset covering emotional, audio event, and music contexts, created by prompting GPT-4o and synthesizing speech with an off-the-shelf TTS model. It also presents OmniChat, a multi-turn spoken dialogue system with a heterogeneous feature fusion module (Mix-Former) that dynamically integrates speech, emotion, and beat features. OmniChat achieves state-of-the-art performance on the DailyTalk dataset and on ShareChatX itself. The paper provides extensive analysis of synthetic data usage, including optimal real/synthetic data ratios, data scaling effects, and expert feature selection strategies. Reviewer scores vary considerably (3, 3, 6, 8), with one reviewer strongly positive and two reviewers critical of evaluation, reproducibility, and practical scope.

### Strengths
- The proposed ShareChatX dataset is the first large-scale synthetic spoken dialogue dataset covering diverse scenarios (emotion, audio events, music), which can significantly benefit related research and development of foundation models for spoken language tasks. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- OmniChat achieves SOTA performance on the real-world DailyTalk dataset and on ShareChatX, demonstrating practical impact. (R1, R2, R4) — one reviewer (R3) specifically highlights that this represents significant practical impact.
- The Mix-Former module effectively integrates multiple expert features (speech, emotion, beat) to handle diverse dialogue contexts. (R3)
- Extensive experiments and thorough analysis provide valuable insights into training strategies, including synthetic-real data ratios, data scaling, and complex scenarios. (R1, R3)
- The paper is generally well-structured and addresses a critical challenge in spoken dialogue systems (scarcity of large-scale, diverse datasets). (R2, R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Evaluation benchmark limitations:** The test sets are either similar to training (ShareChatX) or only cover chit-chat; information-seeking instructions are not evaluated. (R2) Moreover, when trained without real data, OmniChat shows only modest improvement and is worse than Qwen2-Audio on METEOR and GPT-eval. (R1)
- **Metric limitations:** Evaluation primarily uses reference-based metrics (BLEU, ROUGE-L, BERTScore, F1) that may not capture conversational quality. GPT-eval and human evaluation results are provided only for DailyTalk, not for ShareChatX. (R2, R3)
- **Lack of analysis per synthetic data type:** The paper does not isolate the individual impact of each synthetic data type (emotion, audio, music) on model performance. (R2)
- **Insufficient reproducibility details:** Model versions of audio encoders (Whisper, Emotion2Vec, Beat) are not specified. The number of trainable parameters for OmniChat and comparison models is missing. Experimental settings for baselines in Table 2 and Table 3 are not provided (e.g., whether they are trained or zero-shot). The method for manual verification (line 183) and manual evaluation (line 322) is not elaborated. Standard test splits are not clearly defined; the paper mentions random sampling (line 314). (R2, R4)
- **Synthetic data realism concerns:** The synthetic data sounds relatively plain and lacks strong emotional nuance compared to real conversations; the reliance on LLM+TTS means it is not end-to-end, leaving a gap to truly realistic spoken language tasks. (R1, R4)
- **Presentation and clarity issues:** Section 5.3 on data scale is confusing — it is unclear which “spoken dialog models” are being evaluated. (R4) Style parameters are inconsistently described (line 182: gender, pitch, speed, emotion; line 197: gender, pitch, emotion, energy). (R2)
- **Real-time latency concerns:** The model complexity may pose challenges for real-time deployment common in practical voice assistants. (R2)
- **Missing references:** Related works in audio/emotion dialogue systems (e.g., SD-Eval, EMOVA) are not cited. (R2)
- **Need for ablation on Mix-Former:** An ablation study on different components of the Mix-Former architecture is requested. (R3)
- **Specific unanswered questions:** How are music/audio overlaid onto speech (SNR or simple addition)? What is the GPT-eval prompt? Have the accuracy of style parameters (other than emotion) been evaluated? What is the inference cost of OmniChat? Are the SOTA numbers computed on the randomly sampled subset? (R1, R2, R4)
