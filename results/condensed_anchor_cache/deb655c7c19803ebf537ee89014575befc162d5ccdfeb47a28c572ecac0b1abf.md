## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces MambaTS, an improved selective state space model (SSM) for long-term time series forecasting (LTSF). It applies Mamba to multivariate forecasting by scanning variables along time in a certain order, and proposes Variable-Aware Scan along Time (VAST) to dynamically infer causal relationships among variables via random walks without return. Additional modifications include a Temporal Mamba Block (TMB) that removes causal convolution and a dropout mechanism for selective parameters. The model achieves linear complexity O(K) compared to quadratic Transformer attention. Experiments on eight public datasets claim state-of-the-art performance.

### Strengths
- The paper proposes a strategy to apply Mamba to multivariate time series forecasting and achieves empirical results comparable to state-of-the-art.
- MambaTS reduces computational complexity from quadratic O(K²) to linear O(K) by leveraging a topologically ordered linear scan, making it suitable for high-dimensional time series data.
- VAST enhances adaptability by inferring causal relationships in the absence of explicit causal graphs, using random walks to approximate dependencies and mitigate the need for exhaustive pairwise calculations.
- The introduction of VAST and causal graphs for modeling dependencies offers a unique solution to efficiently process long-term dependencies in time series data with linear complexity.
- The model is tested across various public datasets, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art models, validating its efficacy and versatility.
- MambaTS significantly reduces the computational cost traditionally associated with long-range forecasting models like Transformers by avoiding the quadratic complexity of the self-attention mechanism.
- It is interesting to see another new work on mamba for time series forecasting; some properties of mamba fit time series and it is an interesting direction to explore.
- The authors propose several designs to tailor mamba for time series application, which has its merits.

### Weaknesses
- **Proof and theoretical justification issues**:
  - The proof in Proposition 2 does not make sense; the whole concept of random walk on a causal graph with a certain cost is not well defined (R1).
  - Proposition 2 lacks formal guarantees for convergence speed; without clear bounds on the number of walks required, the approach may yield only approximate estimates, especially when practical constraints limit the number of walks. This affects consistency and reproducibility of causal estimation across varied dataset structures (R2).
  - The paper does not clearly define how the cost of a random walk without return is calculated from the training loss and distributed across transitions, or how it is used to update matrix P, or how the initial matrix P is set up. It is also unclear how to get the cost from node i to node j empirically (R5, R5 Q2).
  - The claim that random walk without return is a promising approach to estimate causal links lacks support (citations/proofs) and clear explanation of why it captures directionality of causal relationships (R5).
  - Proposition 1 assumes that a causal graph exists; what if it doesn’t? (R5 Q1).
  - The meaning of \( I \) in Equation 7 is unclear (R3).
  - In Eq (6), it is unclear how to get \( p^{(0)} \) (R5 Q4).

- **Effectiveness and comparison concerns**:
  - The method does not seem to have a large advantage over channel-independent PatchTST, which is a univariate forecasting method (R1).
  - Results in Table 2 are questionable. For example, the reviewer’s reimplementation of PatchTST (using official configurations) achieved better results than the reported MambaTS performance on ETTm2 (input length 720):
    - ETTm2_720_96.log: 0.1632, 0.2555
    - ETTm2_720_192.log: 0.2167, 0.2942
    - ETTm2_720_336.log: 0.2679, 0.3282
    - ETTm2_720_720.log: 0.3521, 0.3798
    These results suggest MambaTS may not definitively outperform all baselines, especially with no code available for direct comparison (R3).
  - The model’s performance heavily depends on the accuracy of causal graphs; robustness against poor graph structure is not extensively addressed (R4).
  - The experiments only compare MambaTS with 7 baselines; missing more recent SOTA methods such as ModernTCN, UniTST, TSLANet, Onefitsall, TimeLLM, which should be included for proper benchmarking (R4, R5).
  - Two commonly used datasets (ETTh1, ETTm1) are missing, reducing generalizability of results (R3).
  - A major difference with iTransformer and UniTST is that MambaTS models dependencies on both time and variables; a comparison with UniTST is needed (R5 Q5).

- **Efficiency and scalability issues**:
  - The theoretical complexity analysis in Table 5 lacks practical runtime comparisons. Given that MambaTS requires K-1 iterations to estimate causal relationships, its efficiency is questionable (R3).
  - VAST uses the asymmetric traveling salesman problem (ATSP) with heuristics like simulated annealing to determine optimal scanning order; this introduces inconsistency as different approximations may affect accuracy, especially with complex, dense inter-variable connections (R2).
  - Extra experiments on alternative heuristic approaches (e.g., genetic algorithms) are needed to measure how different heuristics affect scanning order and forecasting accuracy (R2).
  - The paper lacks a thorough discussion on scalability in scenarios with exceedingly large datasets or highly complex variable relationships (R4).

- **Lack of experimental details and ablations**:
  - Important implementation details are missing: patch length, the value of beta in Equation 7, whether the random walk on variables is conducted K-1 times per epoch (implying K-1 more training time cost than one epoch) (R3).
  - Section 5.2 does not clearly explain whether K-1 transitions are sufficient to estimate all variable orders, or if consistency (e.g., v1 always preceding vk) is assumed (R3).
  - Inference process for Variable-Aware Scan Along Time lacks detail in Section 5.2 (R3).
  - No ablation study compares the proposed Temporal Mamba Block (TMB) with dropout against the original Mamba block, leaving its impact unclear (R3).
  - No code is provided, limiting reproducibility (R3).

- **Clarity and presentation**:
  - The clarity of the paper needs improvement; in some parts it is hard to understand, such as the cost of a random walk without return (R5).
  - The paper does not clearly explain why a random permutation is equivalent to a random walk. Line 324 says K-1 transition tuples are derived; why not K(K-1)/2 tuples so that each (v_i, v_j) with i<j is included? (R1 Q).
  - It is not clear how many walks are required empirically and the time spent (R5 Q3).
  - Reviewer 5 notes presentation score 1; Reviewer 1 notes presentation 2; Reviewer 2 notes presentation 2; Reviewer 3 notes presentation 3; Reviewer 4 notes presentation 3. Hence clarity is a recurring concern.

- **Minority reviewer contrast**: Reviewer 4 (score 8) is significantly more positive, emphasizing the novelty of VAST, strong empirical results, and linear complexity. Reviewer 3 (score 3) is much more negative, pointing to missing experimental details, questionable SOTA claims, missing benchmarks and ablations. This split indicates strong disagreement on the paper’s rigor and validity.
