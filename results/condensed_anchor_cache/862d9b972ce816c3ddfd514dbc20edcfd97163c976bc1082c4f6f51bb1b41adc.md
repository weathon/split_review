## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Retri3D, a framework for text-based retrieval of 3D Neural Graphics Representations (3D NGRs) like NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting. It uses pretrained Vision-Language Models (e.g., CLIP) to embed text queries and rendered images, enabling efficient retrieval without training views or camera poses. Two novel modules are proposed: Neural Field Artifact Analysis (NGNA) – a multivariate Gaussian model that distinguishes clean from noisy pixels using VLM activation maps – and a Smart Camera Movement Module (SCMM) that iteratively refines camera viewpoints to minimize rendered noise. Experiments on LERF and ScanNet++ datasets show significantly improved retrieval accuracy over baselines, with orders-of-magnitude faster retrieval and lower storage, while being compatible with multiple NGRs and VLMs.

### Strengths

1. **Novel problem and framework**: Retri3D addresses the previously uncovered problem of text-based retrieval from 3D NGR stores, and is the first framework capable of efficient retrieval without requiring training views or camera poses (Reviewer 1, 4).
2. **Novel modules**: NGNA and SCMM are well-motivated and effective. NGNA uses a multivariate Gaussian to separate clean/noisy features from VLM activations, outperforming traditional NeRF uncertainty estimation. SCMM iteratively samples cleaner viewpoints, enabling high-quality embedding extraction even from noisy renders (Reviewer 1, 2, 4).
3. **Strong empirical evidence**:
   - Retrieval accuracy is significantly higher than baselines or random views on LERF and ScanNet++ (Reviewer 1, 2, 4).
   - Section 4.5 shows SMCC achieves high coverage of the scene and training portion (Reviewer 2).
   - Speed and memory benchmarks are strong: retrieval is orders of magnitude faster and storage efficient compared to baseline methods (Reviewer 1, 2, 4).
4. **Compatibility**: Compatible with various VLMs and multiple NGR representations (NeRF, 3DGS) without retraining, and is not dataset-specific (Reviewer 1, 2).
5. **Presentation**: The paper is well-written and organized; the pipeline is clear and effective, with potential for many downstream applications (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses

1. **Comparative analysis gaps**: The paper lacks comparisons with recent works like TIGER, N2F2, and ConDense. ConDense should be discussed in related work as it could apply to this retrieval task (Reviewer 1). Also, no comparison with retrieval methods that convert NGRs to simplified representations (e.g., point cloud from 3DGS) (Reviewer 3).
2. **VLM dependency**: Retrieval quality is inherently limited by the underlying VLM’s ability to handle text–visual correlations, especially for complex or subtle object relations (Reviewer 1). Only two types of queries (object labels, LLaVA-generated) are tested; queries about shapes, materials, lighting, spatial relationships, or fine-grained properties are not evaluated, leaving language understanding unexplored (Reviewer 4).
3. **Assumptions about noise are under-justified**:
   - The claim that *noise features remain consistent across different scenes and models* (line 303) is not well addressed. It is unclear whether NeRF and Splat artifacts follow the same distribution, and a mixture of Gaussians might be more appropriate (Reviewer 2). Only two NGRs (Nerfacto, Splatfacto) are evaluated; other representations may exhibit different artifact patterns, and few-shot reconstruction may amplify noisy/blurry regions, challenging the noise model’s generalizability (Reviewer 4).
   - The claim that *some content can be noise-free, but constitutes only small portions of images* (line 288) may break for clean scenes (e.g., well-trained Splats). In such cases, random views could already yield high-quality renders, potentially degrading SMCC’s advantage. Mitigation is not discussed (Reviewer 2).
4. **Effectiveness of SMCC vs. storing training views**: In Tab. 1, using 20 training views outperforms almost every SMCC design. In practice, storing a few training views alongside the NGR is feasible with tiny overhead, which diminishes the need for SMCC unless training views are unavailable (Reviewer 3). *Note: Reviewer 3 is significantly more critical on this point.*
5. **Initialization and convergence of SMCC**: The paper does not clarify how cameras are initialized in SMCC. If random, does SMCC consistently converge, or is there risk of local minima? (Reviewer 2, 3). Initializing from training views might lead to a bad choice on the test set (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 also asks about the effect of poor initial poses.
6. **Noise quantification method unclear**: The heuristic for selecting random viewpoints to supply noise/clean features for GMM training (Sec. 3.3) is counterintuitive. More detail is needed on how such random viewpoints generate valid noise features (Reviewer 3). Additionally, it would be informative to evaluate NGNA by replacing it with standard NeRF uncertainty estimation and comparing accuracy, speed, and storage (Reviewer 1).
7. **2D rendering limitations**:
   - The framework processes 2D renderings independently, failing to capture 3D-specific structures, spatial relationships, or occlusions, which limits its ability to answer queries requiring 3D understanding (Reviewer 4).
   - Uniform rendering resolution may lead to inaccuracies when querying localized or small objects (Reviewer 4).
   - SMCC’s method for ensuring comprehensive scene coverage is not detailed; important regions (e.g., corners) may be neglected in sampled viewpoints (Reviewer 4). Also, the field of view (FOV) used for rendering is not specified; wider FOV (fisheye, panorama) might improve feature capture (Reviewer 4).
8. **Scene complexity unexplored**: Retrieval accuracy in complex scenes with overlapping/occluded objects is not discussed or compared against simple scenes (Reviewer 1). It is unclear if results generalize to outdoor environments (Reviewer 4).
9. **Motivation**: Reviewer 3 requests more detailed applications to justify why the retrieval task is needed.
