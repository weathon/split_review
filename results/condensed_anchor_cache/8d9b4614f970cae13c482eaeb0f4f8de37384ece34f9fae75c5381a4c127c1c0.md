## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Adaptive Guided Erasure (AGE) for concept erasure in text-to-image diffusion models. AGE models the concept space as a graph, revealing that erasure effects are localized and asymmetric. Instead of mapping an undesirable concept to a fixed generic target, AGE dynamically selects a closely related but non‑synonymous target concept using a minimax optimization, thereby minimizing unintended side effects on unrelated concepts. The method is validated on object removal, NSFW attribute erasure, and artistic style removal, consistently outperforming fixed‑target baselines in preserving benign concepts. The authors also introduce the NetFive dataset for evaluation. All three reviewers acknowledge the importance of the problem, the breadth of experiments, and the intuitive graph‑based motivation.

### Strengths
- **Originality**: Novel graph‑based analysis of the concept space and the adaptive target selection strategy (R1, R2, R3). R1 emphasizes the creativity in departing from the fixed‑target paradigm.
- **Quality & Experimental Breadth**: Thorough empirical evaluation across multiple tasks (object removal, NSFW, artistic style) with detailed metrics and comparisons (R1, R2). The introduction of the NetFive dataset and well‑justified minimax optimization are noted (R1). R2 highlights the wide variety of experiments.
- **Clarity and Motivation**: The paper is well‑structured, clearly motivated, and the objective is easy to follow (R1). R2 notes a “clear and well motivated storyline”.
- **Significance**: The work addresses an impactful and socially relevant problem (safer generative models, content moderation, bias reduction) (R1, R2).
- **Effectiveness**: AGE minimizes unintended impacts on unrelated concepts (R3). The clever graph‑based approach yields strong empirical results (R2). Validation spans different diffusion models (R3).
- **Geometric Insights**: R1 specifically praises the discovery of locality and asymmetry in concept erasure effects, which informs the AGE design.

### Weaknesses
- **Target concept selection**: The paper lacks a detailed explanation of how optimal targets are chosen beyond “semantic relatedness”. A finer‑grained analysis of similarity degrees and the algorithms/heuristics used is missing (R1). The method’s reliance on an accurate concept graph is not discussed; inaccuracies in semantic relationships could harm erasure outcomes (R3).
- **Scalability**: The minimax optimization may be computationally demanding for large concept spaces. The paper does not analyze computational complexity, convergence time, or strategies for scaling (e.g., distributed optimization, approximation) (R1, R3). R2 also asks how the method scales with the number of concepts and how concept granularity affects performance.
- **Generalization**: The method is tested only on Stable Diffusion. Its applicability to other diffusion architectures or other generative models (GANs, VAEs) is not explored, and the challenges of adapting to different concept spaces are unaddressed (R1).
- **Evaluation metrics and human judgement**: The limitations of the concept classifiers (biases, accuracy) and the difficulty of evaluating abstract concepts (e.g., artistic styles) are not discussed (R1). R3 explicitly asks whether human evaluations were performed for artistic style.
- **Methodological novelty concern (minority view)**: While R1 (confidence 4, score 8) considers the approach original, R2 (confidence 3, score 6) notes that the techniques appear as an application of classic graph‑based ideas to a new problem; the empirical depth compensates but more methodological innovation would be welcome.
- **Performance on baselines**: R3 (confidence 2, score 5) points out that AGE does not achieve SOTA in Table 3 and requests a detailed discussion of that result. (R1 and R2 do not mention this.)
- **Presentation of results**: R2 suggests the results could better clarify why AGE so strongly outperforms other methods (e.g., Table 1). R2 also notes a minor typo at line 116.
- **Missing analyses folded as unanswered questions**:
  - How does the method ensure that erasing one concept does not inadvertently affect semantically related but benign concepts? This overlaps with the call for more detailed evaluation of side effects (R1, R3).
  - If not already explored, what are the anticipated challenges when applying AGE to other model types? (R1)
  - How should one think about the impact of concept granularity on the method? (R2)
