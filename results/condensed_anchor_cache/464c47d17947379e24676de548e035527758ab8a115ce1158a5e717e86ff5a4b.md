- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 1, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a method for fair image generation by using probabilistic circuits (PCs) to learn the latent distribution of a small fair reference dataset, then sampling fair latent codes to feed into a pre-trained generative model without retraining. The method is evaluated on CelebA with VQ-GAN and the gender attribute.

### Strengths
- The method only manipulates the latent space, thus requiring no retraining of the generative model and minimal additional computational resources. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- The approach is pluggable and does not require special network architectures, making it potentially applicable to various pre-trained models. (Reviewer 2, 3)
- The paper identifies an important negative result: fine-tuning a pre-trained model on a fair dataset still yields biased outputs, motivating alternative approaches like the proposed one. (Reviewer 2)
- The writing is generally easy to follow, and the background of probabilistic circuits is described in detail. (Reviewer 1: mostly; Reviewer 3: positively)
- The method is fairly simple conceptually, which can be an advantage. (Reviewer 1: but criticizes lack of novelty)

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and novelty of PC choice**: No clear justification for why PCs are preferred over simpler alternatives (e.g., Gaussian Mixture Models) for learning fair latent distributions. The contribution is seen as minimal—applying an existing model (PC) without substantial innovation. (Reviewer 1: severe; Reviewer 3: notes previous works already tried other models)
- **Method clarity and integration**: The methodology lacks a concise high-level summary. It is never explicitly explained how the PC is integrated with different generative models; descriptions are vague (e.g., “The training algorithm is the same as the previous case”). The formal definition of the sensitive attributes \(S\) is not clarified, and it is unclear how to ensure the PC correctly learns the attributes given they depend on sampling. (Reviewer 1, 3)
- **Insufficient experimental scope**:
    - Only one dataset (CelebA), one attribute (gender), and one model (VQ-GAN) are tested. Missing evaluations on other attributes such as race, age, hair color (e.g., BlackHair, Young, Smiling, Moustache). (Reviewer 1, 3)
    - Other generative models should be considered: GANs (StyleGAN), VAEs, flow-based models, diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion). (Reviewer 1, 3)
    - Other datasets with more varied attributes (e.g., CIFAR-10, LSUN) are needed to demonstrate broader applicability. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing comparisons and baselines**:
    - No comparison with state-of-the-art methods that also manipulate latent space for fairness, such as Liu et al. (2022) [1] (minimizing correlation distance between sensitive and non-sensitive attributes) and Teo et al. (2023) [2]. (Reviewer 2)
    - No ablation study to isolate the specific impact of the PC and sensitive attribute injection. (Reviewer 1)
- **Presentation and writing issues**:
    - Introduction is poorly organized: PC background should appear earlier (before the contributions paragraph), and claims need proper references (e.g., p.1 line 47 about practitioners’ interest in distribution fairness). (Reviewer 2)
    - Related Work section should give concrete examples of PC-based generative models. (Reviewer 2)
    - The paper does not discuss limitations or future work. (Reviewer 2)
    - Typos: Line 156-157: fair dataset is \(D_{fair}\), not \(D_{bias}\); Algorithm 2: input should be \(D_{train}\), not \(D_{val}\); citation format should use “et al. (year)” in text. (Reviewer 3)
- **Minority negative view**: One reviewer (Reviewer 1) considers the paper’s overall quality very low, with inadequate evaluation and no analysis; states the paper requires substantial revision and no author response could change their opinion (rating 1, soundness/presentation/contribution all 1). This contrasts with the other reviewers (ratings 3) who see some value but still major issues.
- **Question not addressed**: Application to fair tabular data generation was not considered. (Reviewer 2)
