## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the Brain Bandit Network (BBN), a stochastic continuous Hopfield network that controls explore-exploit decisions. The authors derive that BBN performs Bayesian posterior sampling of action values with a tunable uncertainty bias. In multi-armed bandit tasks, BBN matches human and animal choice patterns across multiple datasets, and in MDP tasks it can be embedded with RL algorithms to accelerate learning. Reviewers found the work novel, well-written, and relevant to the ICLR community, with high-quality theoretical derivations and empirical validation. All three reviewers rated the paper 8, though they raised several concerns about assumptions, applicability, and missing analyses.

### Strengths
- Novel combination of stochastic Hopfield networks with decision making / explore-exploit; extends the concept of Boltzmann machines to continuous-valued RNNs (R1, R2).
- Theoretical analysis showing BBN implements Bayesian posterior sampling with a tunable bias; analysis is new and helpful (R1, R3).
- Thorough derivations and validation through numerical experiments (R3).
- Extensive experiments in MAB tasks compared against baselines, and the remarkable match with human and mouse data across multiple datasets is novel and interesting (R1, R3).
- Connections to neuroscience are interesting; paper has high pedagogical value and is well-written (R2, R3).
- Demonstrates BBN embedded with RL algorithms in MDP tasks (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
- The bandit implementation assumes the agent has access to a memory buffer of all past experience, which may be impractical as a cognitive model (R1).
- The pseudo-code for the MDP tasks includes P_{sas'}, implying the agent has access to the true dynamics of the environment; this assumption is not justified (R1 question/criticism).
- The method requires an SDE solver, which introduces a barrier to entry; the claim that BBN can transition between attractor states multiple times within a few hundred steps needs more rigorous justification regarding numerical stability, and the sensitivity to fixed step size is not fully explored (R2).
- Tuning numerous hyperparameters requires specialized expert knowledge; the paper lacks a principled approach for determining optimal parameters in new environments (R2).
- The sensitivity analysis only explores a limited subset of the parameter space; suggested ranges are not derived from theoretical understanding of the model's dynamics (R2).
- Performance in high dimensions needs more exposition and theoretical analysis; the mechanism behind the increasing bias toward high‑uncertainty states is not sufficiently substantiated (the claim that noise anisotropy causes this requires more rigorous investigation of saddle point dynamics), and empirical evaluations are limited to a few grid‑world variants (R2).
- While the derivations are thorough, most analytic results are either existing or intuitive: the attractor structure of a two‑neuron Hopfield network with inhibitory connections is known, Hopfield networks have been shown to implement Bayesian inference (discrete case), and the dependence of the probability of staying in an attractor on the relative orientation of the Hessian and noise direction is intuitive (R3).
- The MDP part needs a fuller description (e.g., a Methods section in the Appendix) to clarify what gives rise to observed phenomena; the uncertainty Bellman equation should be mentioned (R3).
- It is not explained what makes BBN explore more than baselines; the paper does not compare exploration to a random walk policy, and does not discuss how the result would change with different state/Q‑function parameterizations (tabular vs. deep learning) (R3).
- The paper claims higher state coverage is positive, but in the unrewarded task (Figure 8 b-c) exploration seems unnecessary; the utility of exploration when rewards are present may differ (R3).
- Several concepts are undefined: MFPT (L169–174), escape efficiency (L202/208), relative/total uncertainty (L322/323); the rationale for the chosen number of T steps for BBN simulation is not explained (R2).
- Citation formatting appears incorrect (R2).
