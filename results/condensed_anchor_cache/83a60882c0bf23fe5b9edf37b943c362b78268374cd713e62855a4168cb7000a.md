## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces WaveFormer, a hierarchical transformer that uses discrete wavelet transform (DWT) to compact features and learn multi-scale token interactions for volumetric medical image segmentation. The approach is evaluated on three CT datasets (FLARE 2021, KiTS 2019, AMOS 2022) and on ImageNet-1K classification. Reviewer scores are mixed (1, 3, 3, 5), reflecting sharp disagreement. One reviewer is strongly positive (rating 5), citing an efficient and novel architecture with detailed hyperparameters; another is strongly negative (rating 1), questioning novelty, performance, and completeness. The other two (both 3) note innovation but find the paper lacks clarity, incomplete comparisons, and unsubstantiated claims.

### Strengths
- The application of discrete wavelet transform (DWT) to volumetric medical data is a compelling and innovative approach that addresses computational complexity in transformers (R1, R2).
- The methodology is novel and makes a contribution to existing methods, particularly for volumetric data (R1). Another reviewer (R4) acknowledges thoughtful description of methods and results, and detailed literature review.
- The method shows versatility for various volumetric data types (R1).
- The paper is well-structured, with clear and sound motivation from a signal processing perspective; introductory sections are clearly articulated (R1, R2).
- Evaluation includes comprehensive comparisons with state-of-the-art models (e.g., nnUNet, SwinUNETR) on biomedical data (R1).
- Experiments are performed on challenging 3D volumetric segmentation benchmarks, and detailed hyperparameters are provided for reproducibility (R3).
- Also assesses classification on ImageNet-1K; despite modest improvement, achieves comparable accuracy with reduced computational demands (R2).
- The minority reviewer (R3) finds the architecture efficient and the idea sound.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing results on KiTS 2019**: The abstract and Section 5 claim evaluation on the MICCAI 2019 KiTS dataset, but no results are provided (R1, R2). Multiple reviewers request these results.
- **Incomplete paper and missing references**: References are missing at lines 421 and 430 (R1, R2, R3, R4). The paper appears incomplete or rushed (R2).
- **Poor presentation and figure quality**: Figures and captions overlap (Figure 4; lines 403/421/430); Figure 2 (central to the method) is difficult to understand; Figure 3 is nearly a copy from SwinUNETR; text overlays Figure 4 (R1, R2, R3, R4). Table/Figure captions lack sufficient descriptions (R4).
- **Marginal improvement and lack of statistical significance**: The improvement over SwinUNETR is about +1% Dice on FLARE 2021 with similar computational complexity; statistical significance tests are absent (R2, R4). One reviewer (R4) notes WaveFormer has higher Params/FLOPs than 3D UNet, SegResNet, RAP-Net, nnU-Net, TransBTS (Table 1).
- **Limited comparison to recent models**: Only baselines pre-2022 are used. Missing comparisons with newer SOTA like MedNeXt, Auto3DSeg, UNETR++, VSmTrans, MixUNETR, 3D TransUNet, Deformable LKA (R3, R4). Many of these achieve superior performance and low computational complexity (R4). SwinUNETR and UXNet are not SOTA in 2024, so the “SOTA” claim is inappropriate (R4).
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation**: Only three CT datasets with overlapping organs (kidney, liver, pancreas, etc.) are used. No evaluation on other organ types (e.g., spine, tumors, tubular structures) or other modalities (e.g., MRI on BraTS, ACDC) to demonstrate generalizability (R4). No 5-fold cross-validation on AMOS 2022 (only 200 of 300 CT cases used) (R4).
- **Low novelty concerns**: One reviewer (R4) states that only the DWT aggregation is novel; other components (window attention, global attention) are borrowed from UNETR/SwinUNETR, and the overall architecture has been widely used. Other reviewers (R1, R2) acknowledge novelty of DWT application, so this point is contested.
- **Performance on ImageNet-1K not meaningful**: The improvement is modest and no analysis is provided to justify applicability of WaveFormer to natural images (R1). Architecture details for the 2D version are missing; it is not compared to DeiT, PVT, RegionViT, Focal, Swin on medical segmentation tasks (R4). No comparisons on semantic segmentation as a general backbone (R4).
- **Missing analysis of DWT effect**: The paper claims enlarged per-layer receptive field via DWT, but the datasets (FLARE, AMOS) require mainly local information; no explanation is given (R1). No empirical analysis of the claims in lines 205-215 about attention on DWT coefficients (R1). No visual interpretation (activation/attention maps) of how DWT captures local/global relations (R3, R4).
- **Missing details on DWT implementation**: Choice of wavelet kernel is not stated (R1, R3). Effect of multiple DWT applications and what “0” means for decomposition levels in Table 6 is unclear (R3). Fixed window size for attention determined by coarsest-level feature: how chosen? (R3).
- **Missing metrics**: No GPU memory footprint comparison; reductions in FLOPs/params are marginal (R2). Training and test time are absent (R4). Lack of p-values and standard deviations (R4).
- **Missing details on SwinUNETR reproduction**: Did the authors use ensembles, post-processing, or test-time augmentations (as in official SwinUNETR)? (R1). Which version of nnU-Net was used? (R2).
- **Wrong bold results in tables**: In Table 3, 80.9% is bolded but is not the best; 28.7M is bolded but not the best; other non-best values are bolded (R4).
- **Only qualitative comparison with UXNet** (R4).
