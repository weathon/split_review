## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes two methods to mitigate selection bias in LLMs for multiple-choice QA: Bias Node Pruning (BNP) prunes parameters in the final layer, and Auxiliary Option Injection (AOI) adds an "I don't know" option. It also introduces Choice KL Divergence (CKLD) as a new bias metric. Experiments use LLaMA3, Mistral, and Phi-3 across three datasets. Reviewers diverge strongly: three find the work moderately interesting but with significant flaws (scores 5,5,6); one finds fundamental methodological errors and incorrect claims (score 3, confidence 4).

### Strengths
- Well-written and straightforward presentation (R1, R2).
- Methods are simple and offer new perspectives: BNP is a parameter-level debiasing approach, AOI a simple input modification (R1, R4).
- Selection bias problem is well-motivated (R2).
- Experiments cover multiple model architectures and datasets, showing some performance improvements over baselines (R1, R2, R4).
- CKLD is well-motivated by addressing class imbalance in evaluation, which existing metrics may overlook (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Methodological flaw in bias source experiment (R3, strong disagreement from other reviewers not expressed):** The claim that selection bias stems from the last decoder layer is unsupported and likely trivial. 1) In decoder-only models (e.g., LLaMA3), embeddings of earlier tokens are unaffected by future tokens, so Figure 3b’s observed differences in early tokens (last 50 positions) are unexplained and likely not due to option order. 2) Different embeddings in the last layer are expected because the next token changes with option ordering; this does not demonstrate bias. The conclusion that “selection bias stems from the last decoder layer” is analogous to saying all predictions stem from that layer—no experiment is needed. (R3, confidence 4)
- **Confusion about bias metrics and definition (R3):** The paper criticizes existing metrics Rstd and RSD but appears to misunderstand selection bias. The authors’ own definition (“discrepancy between model’s response to original ordering and expected over all orderings”) does not reference the dataset’s ground truth distribution, yet CKLD penalizes deviation from that distribution, conflating bias with accuracy. Rstd and RSD are designed to be resilient to ground truth imbalance; the authors’ simulation likely contains an error (e.g., RSD appears consistent but Rstd varies incorrectly). A classifier that outputs uniformly at random (0.25 per option) would have zero bias under the authors’ definition—contrary to the paper’s claim that RSD scoring 0.25 for option A is problematic. This is actually correct for an unbiased model. (R3, confidence 4)
- **Weak justification for AOI (R4, also noted by R2):** The hypothesis that adding an “I don’t know” option reduces bias (especially when the model is incorrect) is circular. If selection bias is inherent and present regardless of correctness, introducing an IDK option does not directly address the bias mechanism; a clearer theoretical or empirical rationale is needed. R2 additionally notes it is unclear how AOI affects the distribution of choices for incorrect questions. (R4, R2)
- **Limited scope and generality of BNP (R1, R2):** BNP is limited to open-weight models using token-based evaluation (R2). Pruning parameters may cause unforeseen changes in model behavior, especially for general-purpose LLMs; stability is unclear (R1). The impact on accuracy across other tasks (beyond MCQA) is not addressed (R4). A sensitivity analysis on pruning parameters across datasets is missing (R4).
- **Lack of experimental details and statistical rigor (R2):** Section 2 is too brief. For Figure 2, the setting (zero-shot vs. in-context learning) is unspecified—this may affect selection bias behavior. Scaling trends are not discussed (smaller LMs may behave differently). Evaluation criteria for open-weight LMs (choice token probability) vs. black-box LMs (similarity-based) are not stated as consistent; if different, this could affect conclusions. For MCQ evaluation, the paper does not specify whether a fixed permutation is used or whether improvements are statistically significant across permutations. (R2)
- **Marginal improvements and instability of BNP (R1):** BNP yields only marginal performance gains and appears unstable. (R1)
- **Limited datasets and bias type (R1):** The paper uses only three datasets and addresses only one form of bias; other biases (e.g., Mikula et al. 2024) are not considered. (R1)
- **Unclear motivation for CKLD’s target distribution (R2):** Why must an LM match the dataset’s ground truth label ratio \(p_i\)? A uniform prior would seem more appropriate for an unbiased metric. (R2)
- **Ambiguous notation in Appendix A.2 (R2):** Lines 763–768 use \(z['A'] + z['_A']\) without explaining what '_A' means. (R2)
- **Missing experiments and ablation studies (R1, R2, R4):** The impact of subset size on BNP/AOI performance is not explored (R1). Larger model scales within the same family have not been tested (R2). It remains unclear why the final layer is particularly sensitive to bias—further analysis could refine BNP (R4). Alternative auxiliary options (e.g., “None of the above”) are not tested (R4).
