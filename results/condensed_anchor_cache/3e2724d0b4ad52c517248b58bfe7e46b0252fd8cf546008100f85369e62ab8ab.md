## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies catastrophic forgetting of long-tail factoids (subject-relation-object triples) in LLMs during multi-stage fine-tuning. It first demonstrates that models (LLaMa-3, Mistral-7B) lose factoid knowledge when trained on subsequent stages (especially other factoid datasets), and that standard replay techniques fail to prevent forgetting. The authors then propose REMIX (Random and Generic Data Mixing), which mixes unrelated generic data (pretraining corpora or random word sequences) into training stages to reduce interference. REMIX outperforms replay-based methods and is linked to storing factoids in earlier, more diverse layers. Experiments cover multiple datasets, mixing types, and hyperparameters. Reviewer opinions diverge: one recommends acceptance (score 8, citing clarity, relevance, thorough experiments, and originality of data mixing), while another rates low (score 3, questioning motivation, novelty, and baseline comparisons). Two others give score 5 with mixed critiques.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and organization**: The paper is very well-written, with natural section flow, clear reasoning, and well-designed tables/figures. References are recent and relevant. (Reviewer 2, supported by Reviewers 1 and 4)
- **Relevant problem**: Addresses catastrophic forgetting of long-tail facts in LLMs, an unsolved issue for updating models with new world knowledge. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **Thorough experiments**: Extensive empirical validation across multiple datasets (Key-Value Recall, PopQA, etc.), including ablation on mixing ratio, sequence length, number of stages, and different LLMs (LLaMa-3-8B, Mistral-7B). Careful checks for dataset overlap. (Reviewers 2, 4; Reviewer 3 notes rigorous coverage)
- **Original approach**: REMIX’s use of generic or random data mixing is creative and counterintuitive but mathematically justified. It outperforms replay-based methods even without access to original factoids. (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 4 calls it novel; Reviewer 3 disagrees on novelty)
- **Insightful analysis**: Layer-wise analysis (Logit Lens) reveals that REMIX shifts factoid storage to earlier layers and diversifies storage, explaining forgetting reduction. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Generalizable insight**: Limitations of replay and the proposed alternative offer value for continual learning in LLMs. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minor positive**: Problem and solution are presented clearly and are easy to follow. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear problem motivation**: The paper does not convincingly justify why memorizing factoids in LLMs is important over using traditional knowledge bases. It lacks demonstration of how factoid retention benefits downstream tasks that require LLM capabilities (e.g., complex reasoning, QA integrating multiple facts). (Reviewer 3; echoed by Reviewer 4’s question on bidirectional relational memory)
- **Lack of novelty**: REMIX is seen as a straightforward application of data mixing, without specifically addressing unique challenges of factoid memorization compared to other data mixing strategies or more sophisticated continual learning techniques. (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 1 asks for comparison with mixing Data A and Data B, i.e., a baseline of mixing the two factual datasets themselves)
- **Insufficient baselines**: Only experience replay is compared; missing comparisons with established continual learning methods such as Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC), Knowledge Distillation, parameter expansion, regularization-based, or parameter isolation techniques. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Synthetic/oversimplified datasets**: The factoid datasets (Key-Value Recall, PopQA) are synthetic atomic facts, not reflecting real-world overlapping or nested knowledge (e.g., “Shakespeare wrote Hamlet” vs. “Author of Hamlet”). Evaluation on more realistic datasets like Wikipedia or Natural Questions is needed. (Reviewer 4)
- **Lack of justification for mixing data types**: The paper uses both random word sequences and knowledge-rich text (Knowledge Pile) but does not explain why both produce similar effects. No guideline for selecting mixed data types or analyzing differential impacts. (Reviewer 4; Reviewer 3 also notes the method lacks specific adaptation to factoid problems)
- **Impact on new task performance not explored**: Does REMIX hinder the model’s ability to learn new tasks in later stages? Trade-off between memory retention and task learning is unstudied. (Reviewer 4; Reviewer 2 also questions potential degradation of other capabilities like fluency)
- **Limited multi-stage evaluation**: Experiments focus mostly on two stages; real-world models may undergo many updates. Testing on three or four stages is lacking. (Reviewer 4)
- **Resource and scalability concerns**: Mixing extra data increases computational cost, especially for large models; no discussion of resource trade-offs or scalability to domains like finance/healthcare. (Reviewer 4)
- **Minor presentation issues**: Section 4.2 uses “Figure” instead of “Table” (Reviewer 2). Section 3.2 on replay lacks detail (which LLM was used for replay experiments). (Reviewer 2)
- **Questions overlapping with criticisms**: 
  - Why is forgetting more severe when Stage 2 is another factoid dataset (Table 1), and why big drop with GSK8k? Interplay of datasets needs explanation. (Reviewer 1; also reviewer 4 asks similar)
  - For replay, what if ratio = 1.0? (Reviewer 1)
  - Combining REMIX with replay techniques: natural next step. (Reviewer 2)
  - Varying model or dataset size to evaluate link between capacity and REMIX efficacy. (Reviewer 2)
  - How is 100% accuracy on Stage 1 factoids achieved and guaranteed? (Reviewer 4)
  - Does REMIX support bidirectional relational memory (e.g., inferring inverse relationships)? (Reviewer 4)
  - Why does combining random and generic data mixing produce best results? (Reviewer 4)
  - Has REMIX been tested on generative or dialogue-based tasks? (Reviewer 4)
