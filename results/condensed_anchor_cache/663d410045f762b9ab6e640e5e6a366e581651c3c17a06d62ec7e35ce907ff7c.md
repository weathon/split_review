## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FedOA to enhance out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization in Federated Foundation Models (FedFM). It uses adapter-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning and introduces a personalized model with a feature distance-based regularization to align distributions across clients. Theoretical convergence analysis and OOD generalization guarantees are provided. Empirical evaluation is conducted on four NLP datasets using LLaMA-2.

### Strengths
- The proposed algorithm is straightforward and can be easily integrated with existing federated learning methods (Reviewer 1).
- The design of the distance-based regularization accounts for structural heterogeneity across different parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods used by local clients (Reviewer 1).
- Theoretical convergence guarantee for the proposed algorithm is provided (Reviewer 1).
- The paper is well-written with a clear structure and logical flow; the appendix provides well-organized experimental details and theorem proofs (Reviewer 2).
- The work includes rigorous theoretical convergence analysis and conducts evaluations on advanced NLP federated learning tasks using large language models (Reviewer 2).
- The ablation study is comprehensive, covering key hyper-parameters as well as detailed convergence and generalization analyses (Reviewer 2).
- The topic of federated learning with foundation models is interesting (Reviewer 3).
- The presentation, especially the figures, is good (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty.** FedOA is based on the existing federated foundation model scheme FedIT, with the primary distinction being the introduction of a distance-based regularization term for training personalized models (Reviewer 1). A similar idea of using the global prototype as regularization for local features already exists (Reviewer 3, citing [5]).
- **Theory concerns.** Theorem 1 follows straightforwardly from prior work [2]; minimizing the weighted empirical loss (objective (2) on page 3) does not ensure invariant representations, so the generalization guarantees are not assured. Since objective (2) does not satisfy Assumption 1, the conclusions in both Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 may not hold for the proposed algorithm. The authors are recommended to refer to invariant learning literature such as [3][4] (Reviewer 1).
- **Foundation model motivation not well justified.** The theoretical analysis contains no assumptions or results that distinguish FedFM from conventional FL (Reviewer 3). The wording "foundation model" is too broad: experiments only use LLaMA-2, which is insufficient even for LLM. To claim the method is for foundation models, experiments should include various models (e.g., LLM, Diffusion models, ViT, CLIP); alternatively, the authors should claim FedLLM and clearly present tailored challenges (Reviewer 3). The connection between the invariance-based FL regularization and the challenge of large parameter scales is unclear—parameter scale is addressed largely by PEFT, not by the regularization itself (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing baselines and literature.** Conventional FL baselines (which can be adapted to adapters) are not compared in experiments, although the paper claims prior methods cannot perform well in FedFM (Reviewer 3, citing [1] as a reference). The claim that existing personalization methods fall short in generalization (lines 185–187) ignores works like FedRoD [2] and FedETF [3] that can improve both simultaneously (Reviewer 3). The invariant learning motivation misses a key reference, DFL [4], which should be cited, discussed, and compared (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient evaluation scope.** Performance under partial client participation and scalability with a large number of clients are not assessed, which is important for practical FL (Reviewer 1). The evaluation datasets are limited in size and scope, covering only a few domains, which may not showcase the method’s effectiveness in diverse or large-scale FL environments (Reviewer 2). The setup of the test dataset for each client is not discussed in the experimental section (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing implementation details.** Additional details on the structure of the adopted PEFT framework should be included in the main text to aid understanding (Reviewer 1).

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (Reviewer 2, score 6) found the paper well-written with rigorous theory and comprehensive ablation, while another (Reviewer 3, score 3) strongly criticized the technical novelty and literature coverage, and a third (Reviewer 1, score 5) raised significant theoretical concerns.
