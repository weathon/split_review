## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces GIFT-Eval, a benchmark for evaluating time series foundation models. It comprises 28 datasets across seven domains and ten frequencies, totaling over 144,000 time series and 177 million data points, plus a non-leaking pretraining dataset (~230 billion data points). The paper evaluates 17 baselines (statistical, deep learning, foundation models) and provides qualitative analysis. The work addresses the need for a unified, comprehensive evaluation benchmark in time series forecasting.

### Strengths
- Covers a diverse range of datasets and sampling frequencies, facilitating comprehensive understanding of time-series models.
- Distinguishes pretraining and evaluation datasets, aiding comparison between supervised (in-domain) and zero-shot foundation models.
- Conducts substantial experiments: 28 datasets × multiple frequencies × 17 baselines; enormous computation.
- Provides a non-leaking pretraining dataset to support fairer evaluation of foundation models.
- Addresses an important and pressing gap in the community; the collection and compilation of the datasets itself is a significant result.

### Weaknesses
- **Necessity and uniqueness of datasets (R1, R3):** The datasets heavily overlap with MOIRAI. The paper argues only for quantity; it does not justify why each new dataset or sampling frequency is needed, what unique patterns they add, or whether a compact subset could reveal the same insights. Analysis of pattern coverage (e.g., trend, seasonality, entropy) versus existing benchmarks is missing. The selection appears a straightforward ensemble of available datasets (R3). The burden on resource-limited groups and reviewers is high.
- **Evaluation metrics (R1, R3, R4):** MAPE is the only point forecast metric. It is well known as biased (favoring underprediction, sensitivity near zero). Reviewers recommend including sMAPE, MASE, ND (normalized MAE), and considering geometric means for normalized metrics. Metrics like MSE/MAE should also be considered.
- **Missing baselines and related work (R1):** The paper omits relevant benchmarks (gluon-ts, pytorch-ts, ProbTS – which should be compared in Table 1) and foundation models (Timer, UniTS). Probabilistic baselines such as TimeGrad, CSDI, and GRU NVP with RevIN are also missing; these have unique advantages for distributional forecasting.
- **Hyperparameter tuning and lookback (R1):** Lookback length, a critical factor, is tuned only for MOIRAI. All other baselines (supervised and foundation models) should have their lookback length searched given the diversity of datasets and frequencies.
- **Data leakage risk and necessity of re-training foundation models (R1, R2):** TimesFM used the Electricity dataset during pretraining, yet Electricity appears in GIFT-Eval’s evaluation set. The non-leaking pretraining dataset is provided but not used for re-training any foundation model. The paper’s claim of “non-leaking” is contradictory if public checkpoints (which may have seen evaluation data) are used. All foundation models should be re-trained on the provided pretraining dataset for fair comparison.
- **Non-leaking pretraining dataset value unclear (R2, R4):** It is not stated whether the provided pretraining dataset was used to re-train all models. Its necessity for evaluating foundation models is questioned. Analysis of the pretraining data’s features (e.g., trend, seasonality) – not just test data – is missing, which would help interpret why models succeed or fail on certain patterns.
- **Covariates handling unclear (R2):** Section 3.1.1 mentions covariates, but the benchmark does not clarify which baselines can handle them or how covariates are used. Not all baselines may support covariates.
- **Division of characteristics not reasonable (R2, R4):** Grouping by multivariate vs. univariate, frequency, etc., may be misleading. For example, multivariate datasets may lack strong cross-variate correlations. The number of variates is not directly related to the six features (trend, seasonality, etc.). A more meaningful analysis would directly evaluate model performance against the six time series features for each dataset, and then slice benchmark results on those features (R3, R4).
- **Few consistent conclusions and confusing claims (R2, R4):** Several claims are unsupported: Table 6 does not show foundation models “consistently outperform[ing]” statistical and deep learning models because PatchTST also performs very well. The statement that “fine-tuning of foundation models effectively captures longer-term dependencies” (Page 8) is not justified. Moirai, designed for multivariate correlation, performs best on univariate datasets (R2). The paper also notes Moirai has good short-term prediction performance, but the original Moirai uses very long input sequences, creating a contradiction (R4).
- **Experimental settings unclear (R4):** Input/output lengths for short-, medium-, and long-term forecasting are not specified. It is unclear whether foundation models are used zero-shot or full-shot in Tables 6–10. Data splitting details are missing: “The final window of the training data serves as validation” is ambiguous; common ratios (e.g., 7:1:2) are not used.
- **Missing datasets and lack of validation against existing benchmarks (R1):** The paper does not compare results on shared datasets (ETTh, Electricity, Solar, M4) with existing studies to validate experimental protocols. Widely used datasets like Traffic, Wikipedia, Exchange are excluded.
- **Qualitative analysis is limited and non-general (R1, R2, R4):** The qualitative results only cover a few special cases; it is unclear whether the findings hold across all data. Foundation models and other models are evaluated on different data, making them incomparable. The analysis should be more in-depth, e.g., investigating why some foundation models outperform tuned classical models (due to pretraining patterns, capacity, or poor tuning) and whether observed phenomena are general.
