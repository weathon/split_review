## Merged Review

### Summary
CAT-3DGS introduces a context-adaptive triplane approach for rate-distortion-optimized 3DGS compression. It uses multi-scale triplanes oriented along principal axes of Gaussian primitives, spatial and channel-wise autoregressive models to capture inter/intra correlations, and a view frequency-aware masking mechanism to skip less impactful primitives. End-to-end training achieves state-of-the-art compression performance on real-world datasets.

### Strengths
1. **Triplane-based hyperprior with PCA guidance and multi-scale orientation:** The paper introduces a triplane hyperprior aligned with principal axes of Gaussian primitives to capture spatial correlation. Multiple reviewers praise this as an innovative and reasonable decomposition of the dense 3D hash-grid used in HAC (R2), and note its effectiveness as a hyperprior for entropy coding (R1, R3, R4).  
2. **View frequency-aware masking mechanism (VFM/FARM):** This novel pruning approach actively skips coding Gaussian primitives with little impact on rendering quality, further optimizing storage and bandwidth usage (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5). R2 specifically highlights it as a technological innovation.  
3. **Spatial autoregressive model (SARM) and channel-wise autoregressive model (CARM):** The combination of SARM for inter-correlation among Gaussian primitives (via projected 2D triplanes) and CARM for intra-correlation within each primitive effectively reduces redundancy and improves coding efficiency (R2, R3, R4). R3 notes that while autoregressive models are common in image compression, this is a unique application to 3DGS.  
4. **Exceptional rate-distortion performance:** The method achieves significant rate savings (e.g., 78× over 3DGS, 26× over Scaffold-GS) and surpasses existing 3DGS compression techniques (R5, R1, R3). R5 emphasizes the well-structured architecture with four modules addressing different redundancies.  
5. **Comprehensive ablation studies:** The paper includes ablation studies of various modules (R1, R3). Note: some reviewers find certain ablations insufficient (see Weaknesses), but the presence of extensive ablation is acknowledged as a strength.  
6. **Well-organized and clear presentation:** The paper is easy to follow and well-structured (R2).

### Weaknesses
1. **Decoding complexity and parallelization concerns:** Entropy coding is variable-length and typically decoded sequentially, yet 3DGS rendering is highly parallelized. The paper does not explain how the compression method manages parallelization without impacting rendering speed (R1). Additionally, the decoding complexity of the context models (especially CARM) may significantly increase decoding time and affect rendering speed (R1, R4). R4 further notes that the model seems complicated and coding time greatly increases due to the context model; training time is also not discussed.  
2. **Ambiguity in bit-rate measurement:** It is unclear whether real entropy coding was used during rendering or if the bit-rate was calculated based on theoretical entropy coding (R1). This affects the credibility of the reported rate-distortion results.  
3. **Insufficient ablation studies:**  
   - The ablation of CARM is only tested on one scene (bicycle), making the results not generalizable (R2).  
   - Missing ablation of PCA guidance: since several prior works already use triplanes for 3DGS/NeRF compression, an ablation isolating the PCA contribution is needed (R4).  
   - The effectiveness of view frequency-aware masking (VFM) is ambiguous: in Sec. 5.3, a 16% BD-rate reduction is reported, but it is unclear whether the "w/o VFM" configuration in Fig. 10 excludes masking altogether or only removes the weights \(p_{n,k}\) (R5). This needs clarification.  
4. **Missing comparisons with key baselines:**  
   - No direct quantitative comparison with ContextGS, an important baseline that also uses context models and has publicly available code. This comparison would clarify the improvements over prior contextual coding methods (R3, R4). R4 notes that for many benchmarks, the improvements appear somewhat marginal.  
   - The comparison with HAC is questionable because the rate parameter settings used for HAC are not the same as those used for CAT-3DGS (R2). This raises concerns about fairness of the comparison.  
5. **Incomplete exploitation of spatial redundancies:** The spatial autoregressive model (SARM) only addresses inter-redundancies within triplanes (i.e., hyperpriors), but does not exploit spatial redundancies that exist directly between anchor points (Gaussian primitives) in 3D space. This aspect is not leveraged in the current framework (R5).  
6. **Accuracy of Figure 2:** Figure 2 may not be entirely accurate, as CompGS does not use a context model (R4). This should be corrected.  
7. **Missing failure case analysis:** The paper does not identify scenarios where CAT-3DGS underperforms or which modules contribute to performance drops in such cases (R1).  
8. **Unclear motivation for channel-wise autoregressive processing:** The anchor features are latent representations without a clear mathematical correlation like the spherical coefficients in standard 3DGS. The motivation for applying channel-wise autoregressive modeling to these features is not adequately justified (R2).  
9. **Inconsistent rate parameter settings across datasets:** The authors use different rate parameters on different datasets without explanation. This inconsistency is noted by R2 and may affect the reproducibility and fairness of results.
