## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates the design space for autoregressive (AR) and masked autoregressive (MAR) image generation models. It evaluates four image tokenizers with codebook sizes from 1,024 to 262,144, proposes an improved MAR architecture (MaskGIL) with QK-Norm and Post-Norm for stable scaling up to 1.4B parameters, and introduces a combined AR/MAR inference framework. Experiments are on ImageNet 256×256 and text-to-image at arbitrary resolutions. Reviewers are split: two (scores 5,5) find the investigation valuable and well-executed; two (scores 3,3) raise concerns about limited novelty, confounding factors in the tokenizer study, and insufficient depth of analysis.

### Strengths
- Investigates a valuable problem: how to design efficient and scalable AR/MAR image generation models.
- Evaluates the impact of codebook size (1,024–262,144) on generation quality across four tokenizers in both AR and MAR settings.
- Identifies the most effective tokenizer for image generation based on the codebook study.
- Proposes QK-Norm and Post-Norm to enable stable training of a 1.4B-parameter model, facilitating scaling.
- Includes experiments at multiple scales (111M to 1.4B) on the standard ImageNet 256×256 benchmark (FID 3.71 with only 8 inference steps).
- Conducts text-to-image experiments supporting arbitrary resolution generation with a 775M model.
- Writing is clear and provides a good overview of existing methods (e.g., Figure 2).
- The unified AR/MAR inference framework is explored to combine strengths of both paradigms.

### Weaknesses
- **Overclaimed generality of codebook-size analysis.** The study compares four tokenizers that differ in architecture (e.g., LFQ, VQ) beyond codebook size; thus claims like “when the codebook size reaches a certain level, improvement in reconstructed image quality is limited” are not generally valid without an ablation that varies only codebook size within one tokenizer. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)  
  - Suggested remedy: ablate LFQ with codebook size 8,192 vs. Chameleon-VQ; rephrase claims as analysis of the four specific tokenizers.
- **Limited novelty in several components.**  
  - The codebook-size study largely replicates findings from LlamaGen (Sec. 3.1) and the conclusion that ultra-large sizes (262k) hurt performance is unsurprising. (Reviewer 3)  
  - Combining MAR (bidirectional LLaMA) with LlamaGen-VQ was already systematically explored in prior work (Li et al., MAR without VQ) which explicitly criticises the cross-entropy loss used here. This paper replaces that loss with cross-entropy without discussing or comparing. (Reviewer 3)  
  - Scaling up and CFG effects have been documented in MAR, VAR, and LlamaGen; no new insights are provided beyond adding QK-Norm and Post-Norm. (Reviewer 3)  
  - The unified AR/MAR sampling framework adds little: MAR naturally supports variable token counts per step, and a cosine schedule was already studied in prior work (MaskGIT, MAR). The paper adopts a basic approach (first 128 tokens AR, then MAR) without justifying its advantage or comparing to existing schedules. (Reviewer 3)  
- **Missing ablations and analyses.**  
  - The improvements from LLaMA over vanilla Transformer (rotary positional embedding, SwiGLU FFN vs. absolute PE, ReLU FFN) are not isolated; the key factors in the design space remain unclear. (Reviewer 1)  
  - The source of training instability in the LLaMA architecture (“slow norm growth in mid-to-late stages”) is not analyzed: Is it LLaMA-specific? Does it relate to data? Are other solutions possible? (Reviewer 2)  
  - For the unified framework: Why does running AR before MAR improve quality? Does MAR need a more accurate prior? What is the impact of cumulative AR errors? (Reviewer 2)  
  - No quantitative evaluation of FID vs. generation time (Figure 8 gives only intuition). (Reviewer 4)  
  - The claim that generating 128 tokens with AR gives an “optimal trade-off” is not supported (Figure 9 shows no clear extremum at 128). (Reviewer 4)  
  - The training procedure for MaskGIL (e.g., masking strategy) is not described. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing comparisons.**  
  - Table 3 lacks VAR, which represents a different design choice in image tokenization. (Reviewer 1)  
  - LLM architectures compared are only Llama and Transformer; stronger open-source LLMs (Qwen2, InternLM2) should be included. (Reviewer 2)  
  - Relation to MAGVIT-v2’s finding that generation quality improves monotonically up to 2^16 vocabulary: does this paper’s result contradict that? A reproduction of their plot or at least discussion is needed. (Reviewer 4)
- **Evaluation scope too narrow.**  
  - Only ImageNet is used; FID on MSCOCO 30K would strengthen the evaluation. (Reviewer 2)  
  - Tokenizer conclusions may not scale to large datasets; experiments on a larger dataset (or a representative subset) are needed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Inconsistency in tokenizer selection.** Section 4.4 proposes using Chameleon-VQ even though the earlier analysis favors LlamaGen-VQ; justification or correction is required. (Reviewer 4)
- **Redundant presentation.** Figure 4 is essentially a slice of Figure 3 (200 epoch results) and should be merged or removed. (Reviewer 4)
- **Writing issues.**  
  - “we introduce modifications like query-key normalization and postnormalization” falsely implies invention; rephrase as “we adapt”. (Reviewer 4)  
  - Line 278: “Based on the results” should specify which table/figure. (Reviewer 2)  
  - Typos: “casual transoformer” → “causal transformer”. (Reviewer 4)
- **Too much packed into one paper.** The AR/MAR combination could be a separate paper if investigated more thoroughly. (Reviewer 4)
