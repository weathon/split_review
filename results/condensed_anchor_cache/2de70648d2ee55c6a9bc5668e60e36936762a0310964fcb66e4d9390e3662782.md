## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Lotus, a diffusion-based visual foundation model for dense prediction (depth, normals). It modifies standard diffusion by: (1) predicting clean annotation (x0) instead of noise, (2) using a single-step diffusion process, and (3) adding a detail preserver that switches between prediction and image reconstruction. The model is fine-tuned from a pre-trained T2I UNet and achieves competitive zero-shot performance on several benchmarks. Reviewers generally find the motivation clear and the modifications effective, but raise concerns about overclaimed generality, limited novelty, insufficient analysis, and missing comparisons. One reviewer (score 8) is more positive overall but provides detailed technical critiques.

### Strengths
- Clear motivation and coherent explanation with carefully crafted figures that illustrate the transition from epsilon to x0 prediction, detail preserver, and single-step inference (Reviewer 2).
- Interesting analysis of predicting clean image vs. noise for conditional regression tasks; contributes to understanding diffusion mechanisms and may be relevant for other conditional uses (Reviewer 4; also Reviewer 1 calls it an interesting story).
- The three modifications (x0 prediction, single-step inference, detail preserver) are effective and achieve competitive performance across multiple benchmarks in zero-shot depth and normal estimation (Reviewers 3, 5).
- Detail preserver, though simple, is a plausible regularization to enhance detail preservation (Reviewer 4).
- Provides evidence that one-step and deterministic inference are possible from a pre-trained diffusion model, supporting the confluence of conditional generation and discriminative prediction (Reviewer 4).
- The proposed method is fast, elegant, and efficient in terms of training data (Reviewers 2, 4).
- The paper is well-written and easy to understand (Reviewer 5).
- Enhances inference speed compared to existing diffusion-based methods (from abstract, noted by multiple reviewers).

### Weaknesses
- **Overclaimed generality**: The paper claims to be a visual foundation model, but results are limited to depth and normal estimation. More tasks (segmentation, detection, optical flow, intra-dataset inference) are needed to support the claim (Reviewers 1, 5; Reviewer 2 also suggests broader evaluation beyond zero-shot).
- **Limited novelty**: x0 prediction and few-step inference have been extensively explored in prior diffusion works (DDPM, Stable Diffusion, UFOGen, Distribution Matching Distillation, etc.). These elements alone may not constitute a significant contribution (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 4 acknowledges the findings are important but urges discussion of concurrent work Garcia et al. (2024) which shows similar improvements (one-step, deterministic inference) with standard noise prediction after fixing a scheduler bug. *Note: Reviewer 4 differs from Reviewer 2 in that they still find the analysis interesting and the paper independently confirming the result, but stress the need to relate to that work.*
- **Fundamental role of diffusion and noise**: The deterministic variant (Lotus-D) works well without random noise, raising the question of whether the method is still a “diffusion” method or just a UNet fine-tuned from stable diffusion priors. The claim that performance gain comes from diffusion priors is vague and needs investigation (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 4 also notes deterministic inference is possible and should be discussed alongside Garcia et al.
- **Detail preserver clarity**: The necessity of this module is unclear given that UNet skip connections already preserve details. The image reconstruction and prediction tasks are heterogeneous and may interfere; more ablations and analysis on the reconstruction loss contribution are required (Reviewers 1, 5). Reviewer 4 calls the explanation “hand-wavy” but plausible.
- **Direct adaptation baseline inconsistency**: In Table 3, Lotus’s “direct adaptation” (Marigold-like) yields much worse performance (2-3x AbsRel) than Marigold or GeoWizard, which achieve comparable results without x0 prediction or single-step inference. Why is Lotus’s direct adaptation so poor, and would the proposed modifications have the same impact starting from a stronger baseline? (Reviewer 4) This is a key unresolved technical point.
- **Qualitative results modest**: The qualitative outputs are close to Marigold and not clearly stronger (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient analysis of x0 vs. noise prediction**: The larger variability of noise prediction could reflect true predictive uncertainty in an ill-posed task, not just algorithmic artifacts. The paper lacks a deeper, neutral discussion (Reviewer 4).
- **Single-step approach lacks theoretical grounding**: No explanation why dense prediction allows one-step inference while image generation does not. Would this generalize to segmentation/detection? (Reviewers 3, 5)
- **Missing datasets and incomplete comparisons**: DIODE omitted for depth; DIODE and OASIS omitted for normals. Some numbers in Table 2 differ from other reports; authors should verify and avoid propagating preliminary numbers (Reviewer 4).
- **Language issues**: Occasional imprecise wording (e.g., “their is a pair of autoencoders”, “the type of parameterization is a vital configuration”) (Reviewer 4).
- **Implementation questions overlapping with criticisms**:
    - If “removing noise” (line 430), what value is set in its place? Does this affect diffusion assumptions? (Reviewer 2; also Reviewer 1 asks similar)
    - Is fine-tuning the VAE/decoder necessary for dense representation? (Reviewer 1)
    - How were generative methods evaluated given randomness? (Reviewer 1)
    - Could training with variable time step t improve robustness? (Reviewer 3)
    - The task switcher uses an “either-or” selection; the loss function should more explicitly handle this trade-off (Reviewer 3).
