## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses ambiguities in the definition of semantic shift for out-of-distribution (OOD) detection by introducing precise definitions of Semantic Space and Covariate Space within the in-distribution (ID) data. It theoretically analyzes when OOD detection becomes intractable for post-hoc methods and proposes a “Tractable OOD” setting that guarantees distinguishability. The authors validate their theoretical framework through experiments on synthetic data and a subset of ImageNet-1K (ImageNet Dogs). The goal is to clarify which OOD distributions can be reliably detected by standard post-hoc methods without modifying training.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles an important and timely problem: clarifying the ambiguous definition of semantic shift in OOD detection. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- It provides clear, formal definitions of Semantic Space and Covariate Space, and introduces the concept of “Tractable OOD” to address a fundamental flaw in existing OOD detection protocols. (R1, R2, R3)
- Theoretical analysis is rigorous, with proofs that justify the proposed definitions and the conditions under which OOD detection becomes intractable. (R1, R2, R3)
- The writing is clear and well-structured. (R2)
- Empirical validation on both synthetic and high-dimensional real-world data (ImageNet-1K subset) demonstrates the practical necessity of the proposed definitions. (R1, R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Strong assumptions limit generalizability**: The theoretical framework relies on simplifying assumptions such as linear separability and Gaussian distributions for ID data. Reviewers note that these assumptions may not hold in many real-world, high-dimensional, non-linear settings and that linear separability is rarely used in OOD detection, even contradicting some known results (e.g., distributions of angles in random packing). (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Insufficient empirical evidence on real-world datasets**: All reviewers agree that validation on only one real-world dataset (ImageNet Dogs) is inadequate. The experiments should be extended to additional benchmarks (e.g., Waterbirds for spurious correlations, the full ImageNet OOD detection benchmark) to cover diverse forms of covariate shift such as domain shift, temporal shift, and feature distributional shift. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Missing evaluation metrics**: Only AUROC is reported; results on AUPR and other standard OOD detection metrics are absent. (R3)
- **Limitations on originality and novelty**:
  - The concept of tractable OOD and the distinction between semantic/covariate shifts have been discussed in prior work (e.g., NeurIPS 2022, ICML 2023), which limits the originality of the contribution. (R2)
  - One reviewer finds Corollary 1 (if OOD feature vector is similar to ID, detection is intractable) to be non-surprising and trivial, essentially formalizing an already widely-known observation. (R4)
- **The paper does not inspire new methods**: The theoretical results are derived under strict assumptions but do not lead to novel OOD detection techniques or provide clear guidance for improving existing methods in practice. (R4)
- **Practical relevance questioned**: The definitions and theorems are not clearly linked to how they could support or integrate with state-of-the-art post-hoc OOD detection methods in real-world applications. (R1)
- **Decomposition assumption is unjustified**: The paper assumes that data samples can be decomposed into separate semantic and covariate representations, but it does not provide a mechanism or guarantee that such decomposition is accurate in practice. (R3)
- **Missing code**: Code and dataset links are not provided, hampering reproducibility and community engagement. (R1)
- **Several reviewer questions overlap with the above criticisms**:
  - How can the definitions support real-world OOD detection? (R1)
  - Why do the assumptions (especially linear separability) hold, and can the authors cite other works that use them in OOD detection or representation learning? (R4)
  - What insights does the theory provide for inspiring novel OOD detection methods or future directions? (R4)

**Disagreement among reviewers**: Two reviewers (R2, R3) rated the paper relatively positively (5), acknowledging its theoretical clarity and the importance of the problem despite the limitations. The other two (R1, R4) gave lower scores (3) and were more critical, particularly about the strong assumptions, the non-surprising nature of the results, and the lack of practical impact. This contrast is preserved throughout the strengths and weaknesses.
