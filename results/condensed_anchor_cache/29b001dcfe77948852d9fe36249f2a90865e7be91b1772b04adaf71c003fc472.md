## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DAME, a post-hoc, model-agnostic, gradient-free saliency-based explanation method that uses a teacher-student distillation framework. For each input sample, a student network is trained to approximate the black-box model’s local behavior on perturbed samples, paired with a mask-generation network that produces saliency masks. Three loss functions guide training. DAME is evaluated on image (PASCAL VOC, MS COCO) and audio datasets using IoU with human annotations, human subjective ratings, and fidelity (deletion/insertion), comparing to LIME, RISE, GradCAM, Integrated Gradients, SmoothGrad, etc.

### Strengths
- Novel use of a non-linear model for local approximation (unlike LIME’s linear model) – addresses the inaptness of linear models for approximating highly non-linear black-box functions. (R1, R3)
- Works in input space, not binary mask space. (R3)
- Comprehensive evaluation: quantitative (IoU with human annotations, fidelity), human subjective ratings, and qualitative comparisons across 9 XAI methods (LIME, RISE, GradCAM, Integrated Gradients, SmoothGrad, etc.) on both image and audio tasks. (R1, R3)
- Addresses an important problem: generating saliency-map explanations with only black-box query access. (R2)
- Includes experiments on audio processing tasks (e.g., cough detection, noise detection) beyond typical computer vision benchmarks. (R2)
- Well-written and easy to follow. (R2, R3)
- Thorough appendix. (R3)
- Toy experiment establishes the shortcoming of locally linear approximations. (R3)
- The idea of using distillation for explanation is interesting. (R4)

### Weaknesses
**Evaluation concerns**
- Subjective human evaluation is not informative of actual explanation quality because we lack ground truth for a DNN’s decision reasoning. (R4)
- IoU with human annotations can wrongly penalize explanations that accurately reveal the DNN uses non-human-like features (previously shown by [1-2]). (R4)
- The fidelity measure (deletion/insertion) is not clearly described (e.g., whether pixels are removed progressively or all at once); if deletion is used, RISE has been shown consistently better than LIME in prior work [3-4], but the paper’s results do not reflect that, suggesting evaluation issues. (R4)
- Unclear that DAME offers a clear advantage over simpler baselines: RISE and SmoothGrad are often competitive or better in Tables 1 and 2, and the improvement over RISE is marginal given additional computational cost. (R2, R3)
- Audio experiments are not convincing: padding noise on two sides is an easy pattern to learn; manually annotated cough data is not released, hindering reproducibility and discussion. (R3)

**Practical issues**
- Training a new student model per input sample is a major bottleneck (computational cost). (R3)
- Likely requires careful hyperparameter tuning due to three loss functions; not plug-and-play like LIME. (R1)
- The approach is akin to a gradient-based method in the guise of gradient-free – distilling the whole black-box and then applying gradient methods may be simpler. (R3)

**Missing experiments / analyses**
- Impact of architecture design of map-generation and student networks not studied (e.g., shallow vs deep, design considerations). (R2)
- Missing baseline: distill the entire black-box into a smaller model, then apply any gradient-based method (e.g., GradCAM) to the distilled model and use those saliency maps as explanations. (R2, R3)
- Missing baseline: is the map-generation network necessary? Can the student network alone (without masking) distill the black-box from perturbations and then be used to generate saliency maps? (R2)
- Impact of number of perturbations on explanation performance not investigated. (R2)
- Impact of correlated features on explanations not studied. (R1)
- Local invariance of explanations (whether similar inputs yield similar explanations) not evaluated. (R1)
- Evaluation should be extended to benchmarks with concept annotations (e.g., CUB, AwA2, Decoy-MNIST, ISIC) to compare against gold-standard salient parts. (R2, R3)
- An instantiation of the framework with linear models (instead of non-linear) is missing to concretely demonstrate the claimed benefit over linear approximations. (R4)
- Definition of local vicinity and distance metric between a sample and its perturbations is not clearly specified; is it the same as LIME? (R1)
- The pipeline’s compatibility with alternative realistic perturbation distributions (e.g., MeLIME) is not explored. (R1)

**Other**
- Language like “mildly vs strongly non-linear” is non-standard. (R3)
- A sanity check of attribution methods on the original vs. student model (to verify they rely on similar features) is not performed. (R4)

**Note on reviewer disagreement**: While one reviewer (score 8) highlighted the importance of the problem and inclusion of audio tasks, others (scores 5-6) raised significant concerns about evaluation methodology, practical feasibility, and marginal improvements over simpler baselines.
