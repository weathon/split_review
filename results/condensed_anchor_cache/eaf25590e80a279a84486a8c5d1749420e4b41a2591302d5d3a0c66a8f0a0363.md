## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the O3 framework for continual unlearning in LLMs without requiring retained data. It uses orthogonal low-rank adapter (LoRA) modules to handle successive unlearning requests with minimal interference and an Out-of-Distribution (OOD) detector (trained with contrastive entropy loss and a glocal-aware scoring mechanism) to decide when and how much to apply unlearning. Extensive experiments across three tasks (question answering, intent classification) and seven datasets demonstrate that O3 achieves superior unlearning effectiveness and utility preservation compared to existing methods.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and motivated by real-world scenarios where unlearning requests emerge continuously and retained data is unavailable.
- It solves the critical challenge of LLM unlearning without access to retained data, a common limitation in prior work.
- The design of orthogonal LoRA enables parameter disentanglement among continual unlearning requests and shows significant improvement in evaluations.
- Design choices are justified through ablation studies.
- Comprehensive experiments across three tasks and seven datasets validate the effectiveness of O3 in continual unlearning settings.

### Weaknesses
- The evaluations focus on unlearning factual knowledge (e.g., QA datasets) rather than unsafe behaviors. Usefulness would benefit from additional evaluations on safety-oriented unlearning benchmarks such as WMDP.
- Robustness against targeted relearning attacks is not evaluated. For example, can the supposedly unlearned knowledge be solicited again after fine-tuning the model on related public datasets (as in Hu et al., 2024)?
- Scalability concern: during inference, each test instance is fed into all OOD detector backbones. As the number of unlearning requests increases, this may lead to prohibitively high computational cost.
- The experiments are limited to queries containing a single knowledge entity to unlearn. It is unclear how the framework performs when queries involve multiple knowledge entities, as the OOD detectors are trained on single unlearning requests.
- The scale of unlearning requests tested is only up to 4, which may be insufficient to fully validate the claimed capability of handling continual unlearning settings.
- A fundamental concern is that the proposed LoRA and OOD detector are external modules that block input/output rather than truly removing knowledge from the model's parameters. This raises questions about whether the model genuinely forgets or merely prevents access; the original model knowledge remains intact and could be vulnerable if the external modules are bypassed or fail. The long-term effectiveness and robustness of this approach are uncertain.
- The acceptability of retained data in fine-tuning unlearning methods is questioned: the paper's motivation against using retained data is not fully justified, as other unlearning approaches successfully employ it.
- Novelty is considered insufficient; the combination of LoRA and OOD-based input/output blocking is not a fundamentally new technique for unlearning.
- Vulnerability to attacks: if the model does not truly forget the data distribution, the method may be susceptible to targeted attacks that recover unlearned information.
