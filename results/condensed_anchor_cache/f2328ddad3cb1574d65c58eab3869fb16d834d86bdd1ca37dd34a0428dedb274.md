## Merged Review

### Summary
FDTDNet proposes a one-step lensless object segmentation network that operates directly on lensless measurements without visual reconstruction. It uses an optical-aware feature demultiplexing (OFD) mechanism to extract features by modeling a linear relationship between semantic features of measurements and visual inputs, and decouples segmentation into contour (CDM) and body (BDM) distribution maps with a contour-body interaction (CBI) module. Experiments on two datasets (DISC, DIRC) show superior performance over competing methods.

### Strengths
- **Novel OFD mechanism** models a linear equation between semantic features of lensless measurements and those of visual inputs, enabling direct feature extraction without reconstruction. Reviewer 3 highlights the originality of this modeling and the application of ML methods to lensless segmentation. Reviewer 4 (score 8) is particularly positive about this contribution.
- **Effective task decoupling** into contour (CDM) and body (BDM) subtasks with a mutual learning CBI module is well received. Reviewers 1 and 4 note this design as a strength.
- **Extensive experiments and ablations** on two datasets consistently demonstrate superior performance over state-of-the-art methods (e.g., RecSegNet). All reviewers acknowledge good empirical results; Reviewer 4 rates the paper 8/10, citing strong performance.
- **Well-motivated unified approach** compared to reconstruct-then-segment pipelines, as noted by Reviewer 1.
- **High-quality figures and tables** (except Figure 2) per Reviewer 2.

### Weaknesses
- **Privacy preservation claim is overstated and undercuts the core contribution.** Reviewers 1 and 2 argue the claim is unnecessary and potentially misleading: the OFD block may perform implicit visual reconstruction at varying scales, and the improved segmentation accuracy implies measurements carry rich scene information (e.g., gait identification from masks). Reviewer 2 notes alternative methods (single-pixel, minimalist cameras) achieving similar privacy are not discussed. Reviewer 1 suggests dropping the claim from the title.
- **Theoretical justification for Equation 1 is weak.** Reviewer 3 points out that assuming the linear relationship between original image and measurement in the image domain translates to a linear relationship in the high-dimensional, non-linear semantic feature space lacks rigorous derivation. The impact on robustness and generalizability is not discussed.
- **Dataset limitations and lack of real-world experiments.** Reviewer 2 and Reviewer 4 note the datasets (DISC, DIRC) are synthetic or captured under controlled conditions (FlatCam/PhlatCam, clear foreground-background separation). No experiments on real-world cluttered backgrounds, occlusions, or diverse illumination are presented. Reviewer 4 asks if measurements are synthesized using prior knowledge.
- **Dataset renaming and result discrepancies.** Reviewer 2 raises concerns about renaming datasets (DISC, DIRC) without justification and discrepancies in reported results for competing methods (e.g., RecSegNet) compared to original papers, undermining transparency and rigor.
- **Missing network complexity analysis.** Reviewer 4 points out the paper does not report number of parameters, FLOPs, inference time, or storage requirements.
- **Lack of comparison with reconstruction-based segmentation methods.** Reviewer 4 notes the advantages of one-step segmentation over prior two-step approaches are not experimentally demonstrated; no direct comparison is provided. Reviewer 1 also implies this would strengthen the paper.
- **Insufficient explanation and verification of hybrid loss function weight settings.** (Reviewer 4)
- **Clarity issues:** The equations for the OFD mechanism (Eqs. 3–11) are overly dense with insufficient intuitive explanation (Reviewer 2). Figure 2 lacks labeling to correspond with equations (Reviewer 2). The decoupling into CDM/BDM needs better relation to prior work like CDMNet (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited discussion of limitations and failure cases.** Failure cases are relegated to the appendix (Figure 12) rather than the main text, and potential solutions are not discussed (Reviewer 2).
- **Inconsistent transformations in OFD:** Reviewer 3 questions why A_L/A_R are transformed via downsampling and two CBRs while Y uses a PVT, without justification for the differing treatment.
- **Additional concerns from Reviewer 2:** generalization to complex datasets (small objects, cluttered backgrounds), noise handling in real-world measurements, and adaptation to edge devices given computational demands are not addressed. These overlap with missing experiments and are included here.

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* Reviewer 4 (score 8) is notably more positive about the contributions and performance, while Reviewers 1 and 2 (scores 6 and 5, respectively) raise more substantial concerns about privacy claims, theoretical foundations, dataset rigor, and clarity. Reviewer 3 (score 6) concentrates on the flawed linearity assumption. The weaknesses above reflect the majority criticisms; the minority positive view is captured in the strengths.
