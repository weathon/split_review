## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces SAD3-LM, a semi-autoregressive discrete denoising diffusion language model that interpolates between autoregressive (AR) models and discrete diffusion (like MDLM, D3PM). It divides a sequence of length L into B blocks of length L', applies a discrete diffusion process per block conditioned on previous blocks, and uses an efficient single-pass training with cached KV values. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of the NELBO tightness and gradient variance, propose data-driven noise schedules to minimize variance, and achieve state-of-the-art perplexity among diffusion language models on several benchmarks (LM1B, Lambada, AG News, Pubmed, Arxiv) while enabling generation of arbitrary-length sequences.

### Strengths
- The key contribution is demonstrating that a partly autoregressive discrete diffusion model can bridge part of the perplexity gap between diffusion-based and autoregressive language models. (R3)
- The analysis of gradient variance (Section 4) is highly appreciated and will influence future work. (R3)
- The theoretical argument showing tightness of the NELBO (Appendix C) relates the AR objective to discrete diffusion objectives in an interesting way. (R2)
- The method is incrementally novel: semi-autoregressive discrete diffusion naturally parallels block-wise parallel decoding literature, and the combination of sequential and parallel inference is a promising direction. (R2, R4)
- Good experimental section with comprehensive ablation studies, well-designed experiments, and strong results over previous discrete diffusion approaches (e.g., SSD-LM, MDLM) in terms of perplexity and number of function evaluations. (R1, R2, R4)
- The model overcomes key limitations of diffusion language models: it supports arbitrary-length generation and provides tractable likelihood estimates (unlike continuous diffusion models such as SSD-LM). (R4)
- The paper is generally well-written, easy to follow, and well-structured. (R2, R4)
- The discussion of different noise schedules and the efficient training algorithm are appreciated. (R1)

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and advantages over AR are insufficiently demonstrated**: When L'=1, SAD3-LM performs exactly the same as AR but requires significantly higher NFEs, extra tuning on noise schedules, and more complex training/sampling algorithms. The paper does not show clear advantages over AR models (e.g., speed, controllability) that would justify this added complexity. (R1, R4) A controlled text generation experiment as in SSD-LM would further showcase the benefits of integrating diffusion. (R4)
- **Missing efficiency measurements and comparisons**: No speed measurements (training, sampling, log-prob evaluation) are reported for the different block sizes. (R1) Since AR decoding already requires 1024 steps and SAD3-LM uses 10K steps (Table 7), the inference speed is not improved over AR. The authors should at least discuss how future work could target efficiency gains via diffusion distillation (e.g., consistency models). (R2, R4)
- **Pre-training with AR for 850K steps weakens the claim**: Since SAD3-LM is first pre-trained autoregressively, it may learn representations mainly from that phase. Experiments without pre-training should be performed to see how much the final model depends on it. (R2)
- **Sections 4 and 5 are poorly defined and demonstrated**: The derivation of the variance formula (Eq. 9) is unclear, and it is not clearly explained why extreme masking leads to poor variance. (R2) Moreover, when showing variance of the gradient, it is not specified whether it is the L2 norm (should be stated in the formula). (R1) The claim "Although the objectives are equivalent in expectation" (Sec 4.2) should be shown (at least in appendix). (R1)
- **Missing citations**: The authors miss relevant references on block-wise parallel decoding (Santilli et al., ACL 2023; follow-up training a consistency-like model) and on continuous autoregressive diffusion (e.g., Rasul et al., 2021). These should be discussed to clarify differences. (R2)
- **Statistical significance not specified**: While performance differences appear large, significance tests are missing. (R3)
- **Sign inconsistency in loss equations**: Eq. (3) defines a negative ELBO, but Eq. (5) writes the loss using a positive ELBO without the negative sign. The authors should correct this or use NELBO consistently. (R1, R3)
- **Algorithm 1 and 2 ambiguity**: The call x_theta(x) in Algorithm 1 is unclear—is the argument the clean sequence or the noisy sequence? Algorithm 2 seems to define x as noisy, creating confusion. (R3)
- **Lack of details for reproducibility**:  The hardware used, number of training epochs, and training time are not reported. (R1) The exact computation of PPL for SAD3-LM (Table 1, 2, 6) is unclear—how is x_{t(0)} sampled from q? What is T? Are the perplexities exact or upper bounds? (R1, R3)
- **Missing L'=1 results in Tables 2, 3, 4**: For completeness, the case L'=1 should be included. (R1)
- **Unclear definitions and notations**: (a) "computational artifacts for efficient training" (p3 l143) is undefined. (b) "The marginalization is tractable" (Sec 2.2) is ambiguous—does it refer to parallelization over blocks? The approximation should be clarified. (c) The specific Q_t used in this work is not given; how is q(x_s^b | x_t^b, x^b) computed and sampled for the ELBO? (d) The notation α' (line 212) should be defined in the main text. (e) "Masked SAD3-LMs" section title suggests a different model p, but only q changes—this should be clarified. (f) Typo: "based the" → "based on the" (p4). (R1, R3)
- **Poor AG News results**: Table 5 shows much worse perplexity on AG News than other datasets. Why? Also, AR is worse than diffusion on 3 out-of-distribution settings—do the authors think discrete diffusion generalizes better OOD? (R2)
- **Gradient variance reduction methods not explored**: Alternative methods like multiple ELBO samples or gradient clipping are not discussed until Sec 5, but should be addressed earlier. (R1)
- **Comparison to SSD-LM placed under ablations section**: It is unclear why SSD-LM is in the ablations section—is that the "naive implementation"? (R2)
