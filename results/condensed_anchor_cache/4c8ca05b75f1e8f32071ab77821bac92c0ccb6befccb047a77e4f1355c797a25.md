## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SWGA (Sliding Window Genetic Algorithm), a distributed hyperparameter optimization method for time series prediction models. It combines a genetic algorithm with a sliding window validation technique to mitigate overfitting from distribution shifts, uses Bayesian optimization (TPE) for warm‑up initialization, and supports parallel computing. The method is evaluated on several real‑life datasets using models including CatBoost, LightGBM, XGBoost, LSTM, and Transformer, with comparison only to a traditional genetic algorithm.

### Strengths
- The problem of hyperparameter optimization for time series forecasting with distribution shifts is relevant and important.
- SWGA introduces a combination of genetic algorithms with a sliding window approach tailored for time series, addressing non‑stationarity.
- The algorithm is parallelizable, which can significantly decrease computation time.
- Evaluation is conducted on a sufficient number of widely known real‑life datasets and across a variety of model types (tree‑based, LSTM, Transformer).
- The paper provides detailed computational procedures (algorithm boxes) that demonstrate impact on real‑world applications.
- The paper is clear and concise overall.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient comparison with baselines and other HPO methods:** Only a traditional genetic algorithm is used as a baseline. Missing comparisons with classic sliding window search, k‑fold validation, TPE alone, and other hyperparameter search techniques from the literature. No comparison of convergence speed is provided, despite the authors’ emphasis on computational efficiency. Scalability is claimed but no baselines are given to support it.
- **Lack of model diversity and missing statistical models:** The experiments include three tree‑based models (CatBoost, LightGBM, XGBoost), one recurrent (LSTM), and one attention‑based (Transformer). No statistical models (e.g., SARIMAX, ETS) are included, which would be natural candidates for this type of search. Search spaces for DLinear and PatchTST are given in Appendix A.1 but these models are not used in experiments.
- **Minor novelty and incremental contribution:** The method combines variations of three well‑known techniques (GA, sliding window, TPE) with minimal modifications. The warm‑up stage using TPE instead of random search is considered an obvious improvement, not a genuine contribution. The paper does not clearly explain what distinguishes SWGA from other hyperparameter optimization approaches. Two reviewers (3 and 4) regard the contribution as minor, while two others (1 and 2) see potential but raise substantial concerns.
- **Missing reproducibility and experimental details:** No code is provided; no reproducibility statement is included despite ICLR guidelines. Results are reported without confidence intervals, making it impossible to assess variance. Many implementation details are absent: the specific GA variant used for the baseline, the training procedure, and how comparability between GA and SWGA is ensured (e.g., total number of training iterations should be matched). The number of training samples is not specified: “seven historical timesteps to predict one timestep ahead” – how many segments are sampled?
- **Parameter sensitivity not explored:** SWGA’s performance may depend on window size, population size, mutation rates, and other parameters, but the paper does not investigate their impact.
- **Distribution shift claim not substantiated:** The paper claims the sliding window addresses distribution shifts, but no experiment or analysis directly tests this capability or compares with other methods that handle distribution shifts. The terminology is imprecise (e.g., “out‑of‑sample data with a matching distribution”; calling the prediction window “out‑of‑sample” may be incorrect).
- **Writing, terminology, and presentation issues:** The paper contains redundancy that could be simplified or moved to an appendix. TPE is not defined on first usage (line 68). Notation errors appear (e.g., line 141). Awkward phrasing: “But, their full” (line 107), “while domain adaptation is to” (line 161). An equation on line 140 uses two “i” indexes without numbering. Pseudocode contains unnecessary line breaks (lines 258, 263). The first two paragraphs of Section 4.1 are repetitive and the GA explanation should be in a background section. Citations should be in parentheses when not part of the main text. The 4th contribution point (scalability) is pointless without code. The 2nd contribution point (distribution shift) is not investigated in the paper.
