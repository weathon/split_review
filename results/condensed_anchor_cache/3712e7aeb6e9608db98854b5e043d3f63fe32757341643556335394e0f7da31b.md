## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GloMP-GNN for node classification on heterophilic graphs. Its core idea is to add a global virtual node to improve connectivity, then incorporate global insights via a structure-based global propagation strategy (with a Gram matrix for edge adaptation) and a feature-augmented compensatory update (multi-view combination). Experiments on eight datasets are presented.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written and easy to follow. (multiple reviewers)
- Figures (e.g., Fig. 1) provide a helpful overview of the method. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- Definitions and theorems are clearly stated. (Reviewer 4)
- The experimental section includes many baseline methods and datasets, and an ablation study shows the effectiveness of the proposed scheme. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- The idea of improving a virtual node using a Gram matrix is interesting, and the model performs well on the reported benchmarks. (Reviewer 2)
- The motivation of receiving messages from distant nodes is reasonable. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Low novelty.** The core idea of adding a global virtual node has been thoroughly studied (e.g., OGB benchmark, [1], [2], [3]), and the paper fails to differentiate its approach. The combination of existing components (global node, graph attention, embedding concatenation from H2GCN) appears incremental and not more than the sum of its parts. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Trivial or well-known theoretical claims.** Theorem 1 (product of normalized edge weights goes to zero with infinite propagation steps) is a well-known fact from PageRank/oversmoothing literature; it lacks necessary constraints (e.g., graph structure) and does not address the practical scenario of few GNN layers, so the theoretical motivation is weak. Theorem 2 is an obvious fact. Definitions of Global Intensity, Path Intensity, k-order neighbors are simple normalized counts. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)  
  *Reviewer 4 asks:* How do these definitions and theorems differ from existing work (e.g., [1]–[4] in their review)? (Q1)
- **Feature-augmented compensatory update (Eqs. 7–9) is not novel.** Eq. 8 and Eq. 9 are essentially the same as PageRank with different normalization (row vs. symmetric). The three views are an ad‑hoc combination of GAT, APPNP, and GCN without sufficient motivation or ablation to justify the specific choice. The multi-view approach may simply be an ensemble of existing methods. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)  
  *Reviewer 4 asks:* How does the combination of Eq. 7 (Gram matrix for heterophily) with Eq. 8 (GraphSAGE, homophily) and Eq. 9 (GCN, homophily) improve performance, given the apparent contradiction? (Q5)
- **Scalability and complexity concerns.** The Gram matrix computation (\( \mathbf{XX}^T \)) is quadratic in the number of nodes. Although the authors claim it is computed only once, the memory footprint is a major limitation for large graphs, contradicting the criticism of graph transformers’ quadratic complexity. The experiments are limited to small datasets (≤20k nodes) and do not demonstrate scalability. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Missing strong baselines.** Recent state-of-the-art baselines [4–7] are not included; if added, the proposed method may not be the best. (Reviewer 1)
- **Lack of theoretical analysis for why the method works.** No theoretical justification is given for the effectiveness of the approach, especially in heterophilic settings. The claim that the Gram matrix improves generalization contradicts the bias‑variance tradeoff (neighbor usage can help even under high heterophily [5]). (Reviewers 1, 4)  
  *Reviewer 4 asks:* Can the authors provide a theoretical guarantee that the Gram matrix improves generalization? (Q3)
- **Global Edge Adaptation (GEA) is essentially the same as GAT’s attention layer.** (Reviewer 4)  
  *Reviewer 4 asks:* How does GEA specifically differ from or improve upon GAT? (Q2)
- **Trainable parameter \(\beta\) in Eq. 6 may be biased.** No constraint is placed on \(\beta\), so it can saturate to 0 or 1. The authors should show how this value evolves during training. (Reviewer 4, Q4)
- **Inconsistency in multi-head attention.** The text mentions multi-head attention twice (lines 258, 283), but Eq. 3 does not include a multi-head mechanism. (Reviewer 2)
- No ablation study is provided to assess the contribution of each of the three views in the multi-view update. (Reviewer 2)
