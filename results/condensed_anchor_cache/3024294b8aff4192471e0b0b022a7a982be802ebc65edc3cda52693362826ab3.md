## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a framework to assess the logical expressivity of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) by mapping their operations to fragments of first-order logic (FO). It proposes GACNNs, a generalization of several GNN architectures using sequences of combination and aggregation functions over node tuples. The framework constructs equivalent logical formulas for arbitrary GNNs, enabling a unified analysis of expressiveness. Using this, the paper classifies multiple prominent GNN classes (MPNNs, subgraph GNNs), determines their logical expressivity, and shows consequences for homomorphism expressivity and k-WL expressivity, addressing several open problems.  

### Strengths
- Provides a new, unified theoretical framework for assessing GNN expressiveness by relating it to fragments of first-order logic. (R1, R2, R3)  
- Constructs a solid theoretical foundation through extensive mathematical proofs and derivations. (R1)  
- Classifies existing MPNNs, subgraph GNNs, and other variants within this framework, unifying different subareas. (R2, R3)  
- Offers a novel perspective for understanding GNN models by analyzing expressiveness from a logical viewpoint. (R1)  
- Affirms a conjecture from prior work. (R2)  
- Enables assessment of expressive power with respect to capturing specific graph substructures/motifs. (R2)  
- Shows how logical expressivity directly determines homomorphism expressivity and k-WL expressivity, bridging two other important analysis methods. (R3)  
- The contribution is significant and clearly positioned within the existing literature. (R3)  
- Makes good use of space, rarely making redundant statements. (R3)  

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of experimental validation**: The paper does not include experiments to verify the effectiveness, rationality, or correctness of the theoretical framework. (R1, and R1 Q5)  
- **No direct comparison with other expressiveness frameworks**: Specifically, there is no comparison with Zhang et al.’s quantitative framework (“Beyond Weisfeiler-Lehman: A Quantitative Framework for GNN Expressiveness”) nor with earlier work on logical expressivity (e.g., second-order logic approaches). The paper does not discuss relative strengths/weaknesses or what each FO operation implies about what the model learns. (R1, R1 Q1–4)  
- **Lacks practical guidance**: The framework does not give specific guidance on how to use it to develop more expressive GNN models. (R1)  
- **Presentation is unclear and imprecise in many places**:  
  - Many typos, imprecise phrasing, and inconsistent notation (e.g., “u” used ambiguously for nodes and node tuples, counting down within layers vs. counting up across layers, “generalised neighbour” undefined, “logic sets at the bottom” unclear). (R2, R3)  
  - The claim that “v” is “more general” and “obviously more powerful” at L351 is not justified. (R3)  
  - The term “quantitative” for logic sets is used without explanation of why k-WL or homomorphism tests are not similarly quantitative. (R3 Q2)  
  - Initial description of GNN outputting “true” for a node suggests restriction to binary node classification, but this is not stated explicitly. (R3 Q1)  
- **Insufficient intuition and proof sketches**: The paper lacks high-level explanations for central theorems (e.g., Theorem 3). It does not outline how the logical formula construction for GACNNs works or why it is guaranteed to exist. (R3, major)  
- **Simple example is insufficient**: The example at L187 shows only a single combination and aggregation step and does not illustrate how real GNNs are built. A more complex example (e.g., Local 2-GNN) and a full pipeline (GNN → GACNN → equivalent logic → simplification) would be beneficial. (R3, medium)  
- **Equivalent logic sets for building blocks not defined**: Theorem 3 uses equivalent logic sets for individual combination/aggregation functions, but this notion was only defined for whole GNNs. A general definition is needed. (R3, medium)  
- **Auxiliary logic set purpose unclear**: At L327, the “auxiliary logic set” is introduced without explicitly stating what it is for or how it relates to global readouts; notation should separate them. (R3, medium)  
- **Construction in Section 4.3 (L406) is clunky**: The enumeration (a–c) is just a definition that could be done inline; the presentation would benefit from being more precise and concise. (R3, medium)  
- **Missing limitations section**: The paper does not discuss limitations, e.g., applicability to graphs with node attributes, or other constraints of the framework. (R2, and R3 Q10–11)  
- **Questions left unanswered**:  
  - Why is the WL bound on SEAL weak given Proposition 9? (R3 Q10)  
  - Is the paper claiming to generalize all GNNs? (L482 seems to suggest so.) (R3 Q11)  
  - What is the reason for counting down (from K+1) within a GACNN layer while counting up across layers? (R3 Q4)  
  - How useful is the recursive construction for homomorphism expressivity? (R3 Q9)  
- **Minor self-praise**: The paper uses overly positive language like “simple” and “elegant” to describe its own results. (R2)
