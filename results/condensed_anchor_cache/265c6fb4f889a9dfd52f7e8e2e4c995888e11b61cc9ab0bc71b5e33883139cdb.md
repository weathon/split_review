## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a novel adversarial attack on sequential decision-makers (RL policies) that directly modifies observations. Its key innovation is an information-theoretic detectability constraint: the attack minimizes KL divergence between the attacked and unattacked trajectory distributions, ensuring the attack is statistically hard to detect. A dual ascent algorithm learns the attack end-to-end. Experiments across simulated environments show that the proposed attack achieves a better detectability–success trade-off than prior observation-space attacks, evading both automated anomaly detectors and human detection in a small user study.

### Strengths
* Clear positioning of the paper within the related literature (Reviewer 1).
* The information-theoretic approach to designing undetectable attacks is convincing and novel, providing an intuitive understanding of the detectability limit (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
* The attack achieves a considerably better detectability–success trade-off compared to previous attacks, as shown across multiple benchmarks (Reviewers 1, 3).
* Diverse evaluation includes several RL environments, different robustification methods, and a human study (Reviewer 1).
* The idea of undetectable attacks in the MDP setting is natural and interesting, with generally good mathematical analysis and experimental quality (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
* The presentation is dense and sometimes difficult to understand. While two reviewers found the paper clear (Reviewers 1, 3), one reviewer raised significant clarity concerns (Reviewer 2): Section 4 equations lack motivation and intuitive explanation; some mathematical details (e.g., nonparametric density estimation on line 188) appear tangential; Figure 1 is not self-contained (only fully understood after reading the rest of the paper); Figure 2 receives only high-level explanation, with unclear arrows and lines; Algorithm 1 has undefined symbols ($p(\emptyset)$, $p(o_{\text{old}}, a_{\text{old}})$, $\hat{D}_{\text{KL}}$) and no caption.
* Notation issues (Reviewer 2):
  * Line 132: should be $\text{supp } \xi(\cdot \mid s) \subseteq \mathcal{B}(s)$.
  * Line 134: definition of additive perturbations confusing – perturbations are said to be in state space $\mathcal{S}$, not in $\mathbb{R}^d$, and $\delta$ for a point mass is not introduced.
  * Line 225: “relative entropy” is actually another term for KL divergence, but the intended meaning seems to be “cross entropy”; the notation $H[X,Y]$ is standard for joint entropy, creating confusion.
* Too much notation is introduced; basic RL concepts could be moved to the appendix (Reviewer 1).
* Evaluation is limited to simple simulated environments (Reviewer 1).
* Missing runtime analysis to compare efficiency of attacks and assess scalability to more complex problems (Reviewers 1, 2).
* The paper provides little guidance on how to design better defenses against illusory attacks or how these attacks can inform more robust RL techniques (Reviewer 3).
* Unclear comparison in Figure 5: the detection rate is averaged across environments for both human and automated detection, but human detection was only tested on two environments while automated detection was tested on all four; it would be clearer to also show the automated detection rate on only the two environments used for human testing (Reviewer 2).
* Definition of “adversary score” normalization is unclear: the paper says it is normalized with respect to “the highest adversarial return in each class, as well as the victim’s expected return in the unattacked environment,” but this description is not sufficiently precise (Reviewer 3).
