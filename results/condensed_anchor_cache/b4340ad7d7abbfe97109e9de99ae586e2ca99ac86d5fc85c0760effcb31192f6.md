## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses pitfalls in privacy accounting for compositions of subsampled DP mechanisms. It shows that the worst-case dataset for a single iteration may not be worst-case after composition, and that Poisson subsampling and sampling without replacement can yield significantly different privacy guarantees (e.g., ε≈1 vs ε>10 for some DP-SGD parameters). Several reviewers found the findings practically important and timely, while others considered the contribution limited and the issues already addressed in prior work. Reviewer 3 rated the paper as an important potential reference (score 8); Reviewer 4 rated it low (score 3) and argued the pitfalls are not real problems.

### Strengths
- The findings could be highly impactful on privacy accounting practice; the paper includes a good call-to-action for practitioners and is written tactfully (R1, R2).
- The theoretical construction of the bad cases is solid (R2).
- The paper develops novel theoretical results that improve understanding of composition and subsampling; it could serve as an important reference work (R3).
- Presentation is generally clear and provides helpful intuition (R2, R3, R4).
- The topic is timely as auditing methods become more accurate (R4).
- It is useful to consider the problem because there are still no clear subsampling amplification results for the substitution relation using dominating pairs (R4).

### Weaknesses
- Proposition 11 is proved using only a figure (Figure 1), which is insufficient as a proof (R1, R4).
- Section 7’s counterexample is hard to follow and lacks formality; the paper is sparse with no appendix (R1, R4).
- The paper does not provide a viable technical solution for handling the worst-case dataset problem under replacement DP; a related reviewer question asks whether a good approximation of privacy curves exists for replacement DP (R2).
- Empirical results on more realistic datasets would strengthen the paper; a related reviewer question asks whether empirical privacy differs as much as the theoretical analysis suggests (ε=1 vs >10) (R2, R3).
- Some reviewers found the paper presents a straw-man; evidence that these are common pitfalls in practice would be more convincing (R3).
- The recommendations for practitioners should be expanded to a full section to enhance practical utility (R3).
- Definitions 3-5 would benefit from one-sentence explanations (R3).
- One reviewer (R4) argued that the contribution is very limited because both pitfalls are already addressed in prior work (Zhu et al. 2022): the first by the concept of dominating pairs, and the second by Proposition 30 which gives bounds for both Poisson and without-replacement sampling under the substitution relation. R4 also noted that the claim about Poisson vs WOR is not novel, as the differences are correctly reflected in existing results.
- The Laplace example is difficult to follow; more formal derivation is needed (R4).
- Figure 3 combines a plot and a table confusingly; splitting into separate figures would be clearer (R1).
- Presentation issues: typo in Theorem 10 ((1-γ)P + Q should be (1-γ)P + γQ); proof of Proposition 9 assumes knowledge of the dominating pair for the Gaussian mechanism without stating it formally (R1).
- The paper lacks constructive results and improvements; a related question asks what is special about hockey-stick divergence versus Rényi divergence regarding worst-case pairs, implying the same problems would apply to RDP accounting (R4).
- In response to the author rebuttal, R4 noted that while the construction of dominating pairs for subsampling without replacement under substitution exists (Zhu et al., Doroshenko et al.), the observation that there is no implementation is not a sufficient contribution for a paper.
