## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Counterfactual History Distillation (CHD) for marked temporal point processes (MTPPs), aiming to identify a minimal subset of historical events (H_d) whose removal significantly degrades prediction of subsequent events (x_o). The authors propose MTPP-CHD, which uses an encoder-decoder transformer with Gumbel-softmax to select the subset. Experiments on Retweet, StackOverflow, and Yelp datasets show that MTPP-CHD outperforms baselines in distillation quality and speed. One reviewer (R4, score 6) praised the intriguing question and thorough experimental setup, while the other three (scores 3,3,3) found the work less convincing.

### Strengths
- **Novel problem definition**: CHD is a new problem not previously investigated in the context of MTPPs (R2, R3, R4). The research perspective is novel (R4).
- **Comprehensive related work**: The paper clearly distinguishes its approach from existing counterfactual TPPs, causal inference for TPPs, and policy-based counterfactual outcomes (R3, R4).
- **Constraint refinement**: The authors identify issues with the original counterfactual formulation and add a constraint to ensure distilled events are informative (R1, R4).
- **Experimental detail**: Hardware/software specifications and hyperparameter settings are provided, ensuring reproducibility (R4). The algorithms and empirical evaluation are described (R1).
- **Speed advantage**: MTPP-CHD is much more time-efficient than baselines (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear motivation and importance**: The practical applications of CHD are not sufficiently discussed in the introduction (R2). It is unclear how the model adapts to common prediction tasks in TPPs (R4 Q6).
- **Weak baselines**: The only compared baselines are GS (greedy search) and RD (random), which are too naive (R4). The authors also compare only their method with and without refinement, omitting direct comparison with CHD-MTPP using GS (R2). Additional baselines (e.g., using recent history, RL-based methods from recommender systems) should be considered (R3 Q6, R1 Q3).
- **Effectiveness concerns**: The improvement shown in Figure 2 is minor, especially for Retweet and Yelp datasets (R2, R4). The percentages of subsequences where ppl(p(x_o|H_d)) > ppl(p(x_o|H_l)) on these datasets are small (Table 1) (R4).
- **Counterfactual and causal claims are unsupported**:  
  - The paper does not connect its approach to established counterfactual frameworks (potential outcomes / structural causal models) (R3).  
  - “Counterfactual” is used without any actual intervention; the concept does not match standard causal inference definitions (R2 Q2).  
  - The method essentially discovers Granger-causal relationships, not causal relations; Granger causality does not imply true causation (R1). The language in Section 5.2 should be revised (R1 Q4).  
- **No synthetic data validation**: All experiments are on real-world data, making it hard to verify ground truth. Synthetic data with known causal structure (e.g., using temporal logic rules) would strengthen credibility (R4 weakness 2, R3 Q5). A Markovian synthetic experiment (only last event matters) is suggested (R3 Q5).
- **Lack of ground truth and handling of chain effects**: In counterfactual evaluation it is impossible to observe both the factual and counterfactual outcomes (e.g., famous user retweeted vs not). The paper does not explain how it addresses the lack of ground truth or accounts for chain effects when removing events (R1).
- **Notation and presentation clarity**:  
  - Notation in Section 2 is confusing and unintuitive (R1). Indexing (e.g., H_t_i, H_f, H_d, H_l) is inconsistent and poorly explained (R1 Q2, R3 Q1).  
  - The model architecture in Figure 1 is not explained, particularly how the encoder-decoder “distills” history and how binary conditional distributions are used for sampling (R3 weakness 3).  
  - The overall text is hard to follow (R1).  
- **Missing ablation studies**: The authors should evaluate the impact of different MTPP models on conditional probability estimation, the effect of varying top-k selection in the history picker, and the effect of the additional constraint (R4 Q3).
- **Hyperparameter selection**: It is unclear how α and β are chosen; β must be large enough to enforce the constraint but is not justified (R3 Q3).
- **Analysis limited to one dataset**: The influence analysis (Fig. 4) is only provided for Retweet. Similar analysis for key events distilled on StackOverflow and Yelp (Table 2) is needed (R4 Q5). The finding that famous-user retweets are most influential is a known result from early 2010s (R1, citing Suh et al. 2010, Rosenman 2012); the paper should clarify how its method adds beyond that.
- **Anomaly detection possibility**: Given the small effectiveness on Retweet/Yelp, the authors should investigate whether anomalous events cause the phenomenon and whether prior anomaly screening could reduce the need for the extra constraint (R4 Q1).
- **Question about p(x_o|∅) definition**: The claim that p(x_o|∅) is an infinitesimal number (Line 175) is not justified; can the integral of the conditional intensity be guaranteed to be 1? (R2 Q1).
- **Solution quality**: Proposition 1 does not provide insight into how good the solution from Eq. (7) is (R3 Q4).
