## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces ADEPT, an offline RL algorithm that uses an uncertainty‑penalized diffusion model as a world model. The world model is iteratively aligned with the evolving policy via importance‑sampled updates, and it constrains actions by comparing outputs generated with different noises. The authors provide a theoretical upper bound on the return gap between the model and the real environment. Experiments on D4RL show strong performance, especially on suboptimal datasets, though some reviewers find the statistical significance and certain theoretical claims unconvincing.

### Strengths

1. **Novel and well‑motivated method** (R1, R2, R4). The idea of using an uncertainty‑penalized diffusion model together with importance‑sampling to address policy distribution shift in offline RL is novel.  
2. **Theoretical analysis** (R1, R3). The paper gives an upper bound on the return gap, which is regarded by some reviewers as additive and sound.  
3. **Strong empirical performance** (R1, R2, R3, R4). ADEPT outperforms many baselines on D4RL, particularly on lower‑quality datasets.  
4. **Clear writing and helpful visuals** (R2). The language is largely clear, and Figures 1 and 2 are useful. (R4 finds Figure 1 confusing; this disagreement is noted in Weaknesses.)  
5. **Informative ablation study** (R4). The ablation shows that the uncertainty penalty contributes to performance improvements.  
6. **Good related‑work coverage** (R4, with a notable exception). The paper cites the most prominent offline (model‑based) RL approaches.  
7. **Additional supporting results in the appendix** (R3).  
8. **Elegant approach to penalizing overestimation** (R4). Using the world model’s own uncertainty to constrain actions is intuitive and theoretically appealing.

### Weaknesses

1. **Lack of justification for diffusion model necessity** (R1). The method can likely be generalized to any world model with uncertainty estimation. An ablation comparing diffusion to other world model types is needed.  
2. **Overly long preliminaries (Section 3)** (R1). The section could be shortened, with full notation moved to the appendix.  
3. **Unsubstantiated claims about reward prediction** (R1). The statement that adding \(s_{t+1}\) improves reward prediction lacks an ablation study. Similarly, the uncertainty‑threshold termination should be ablated to show impact on trajectory distribution.  
4. **Poor table presentation and missing statistical rigor** (R1, R2, R3, R4).  
   - Table 1 uses maximum average bolding, which is misleading; aggregated statistics (e.g., as recommended by Agarwal et al.) would be better.  
   - Error bars / variance for *all* baseline methods are missing. Many reported results fall within each other’s confidence intervals, so claims of superiority are not supported.  
   - Only 5 seeds are used; at least 10 are recommended for statistical significance.  
   - The caption of Table 2 confuses standard deviation with standard error.  
5. **Insufficient baseline comparisons** (R3, R4).  
   - ADEPT is not directly compared to DWM (Ding et al., 2024) or PGD (Jackson et al., 2024), which are past works using diffusion world models on D4RL. The realignment mechanism that distinguishes ADEPT from those works appears to help significantly only on halfcheetah medium‑replay.  
   - There is no comparison to other methods that compensate for overestimation bias using the same base policy optimizer (e.g., adding pessimism to SAC).  
6. **Lack of additional analysis to validate the claimed mechanism** (R2, R3, R4).  
   - No analysis of out‑of‑distribution Q‑values or distribution shift error is provided.  
   - The importance‑sampled updates are not shown to help consistently across environments; the ablation shows significant gain only on halfcheetah medium‑replay (R3), and overall impact is described as minimal (R4).  
   - The effect of horizon \(H\) (set to 5) is not studied.  
7. **Mathematical rigor and clarity issues** (R2, R4).  
   - Notation is imprecise: it is unclear what \(s_t\) is sampled from in Equation 5, and the total‑variation distance in Equation 6 is ill‑defined because \(R(s_t,a_t)\) is not a distribution.  
   - Assumption 4.3 is unreasonable for stochastic environments; it may hold only for deterministic MDPs or those with Lipschitz transitions.  
   - Theorem 4.5 is not self‑contained and the proof is merely a sketch.  
   - The theoretical derivations are unintuitive, and the connection to the final reward‑gap guarantee is not clearly explained.  
   - The novelty of the theory relative to prior analyses of approximate models is not clearly delineated.  
8. **Figure 1 is confusing** (R4). The long caption and cluttered visualization make it hard to understand temporal interactions between the world model, policy, and importance sampling.  
9. **Mischaracterization of related work** (R4). The paper states that [1] “doesn’t provide explicit mechanisms to solve the distributional shift issue,” which is inaccurate – that is the core of [1]. Also missing is a reference to policy‑guided diffusion.  
10. **Unclear architecture description** (R4). The role of the MLP that predicts reward, how the diffusion model’s outputs feed into it, and the “input replacement” are not clearly explained.  
11. **Referencing issue** (R4). The appendix (line 903) lists 12 cited papers with no explanation of their relevance; they appear unrelated to the work.  
12. **Questions that imply missing analyses or clarifications** (R1, R2, R3, R4).  
    - Why is importance sampling needed when both distributions can be estimated well? (R1)  
    - How does ADEPT avoid the known failure of importance sampling when distributions are very different? (R1) Why not use MCMC? (R1)  
    - Is Assumption 4.3 reasonable? (R2)  
    - What parts of Section 4.3 are novel and what parts are taken from prior work? (R2)  
    - Why was standard deviation chosen as the dispersion measure? (R2)  
    - What parts of the theory are specific to this algorithm versus generally true for approximate models? (R2)  
    - Does the “done” flag based on uncertainty effectively truncate rollouts, and what bias does that introduce? (R4)  
    - In the proof, can the reward model really ignore \(\hat{s}_{t+1}\) as assumed? (R4)  
    - How were hyperparameters tuned – for ADEPT or taken from default values? (R4)  
13. **Minor typos and grammar issues** (R3, R4): “transits” → “transitions”, “piratically” → “practically”, missing brackets in Equations 7,8,12, etc.

**Reviewer disagreement note**: One reviewer (R2, rating 3) is significantly more critical than the others (R1:6, R3:6, R4:5). R2 questions the theory’s novelty and rigor, and argues that the experimental results do not convincingly demonstrate superiority due to missing variance and significance tests. R4 also finds the presentation and theory problematic, while R1 and R3 are more positive overall but still raise important weaknesses.
