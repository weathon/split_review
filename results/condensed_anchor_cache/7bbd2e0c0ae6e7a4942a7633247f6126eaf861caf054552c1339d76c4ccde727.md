## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses test-time adaptation (TTA) with pseudo-labels by proposing to overcome the memorization effect and confirmation bias via faster adaptation. The authors introduce a sparse cross-logit loss (Sparse-CL) to enable larger learning steps and a k-hardness negative learning loss (k-NL) to sharpen decision boundaries. Experiments on ImageNet-C and CIFAR100-C using TENT and SAR backbones show performance improvements. Reviewer scores range from 3 to 5, with two reviewers assigning 5 (appreciating the novel perspective and simplicity) and two assigning 3 (questioning theoretical justification, hyperparameter sensitivity, and experimental rigor).

### Strengths
- Provides a novel perspective on error accumulation in continuous TTA by focusing on the speed of model adaptation, a departure from prior work. (R2, R4)
- Offers insightful analysis of gradient variance for cross-entropy vs. entropy minimization, identifying it as a cause of unstable learning. (R1)
- The proposed loss functions (Sparse-CL and k-NL) are simple, effective, and can be easily integrated into existing TTA frameworks such as TENT and SAR. (R1, R2, R4)
- Achieves substantial performance gains on ImageNet-C across multiple corruption types and settings. (R3, R4)
- Discusses challenges of pseudo-label learning in TTA (memorization effect, confirmation bias) and provides a clear motivation for the method. (R1, R3)

### Weaknesses
**Theoretical justification is insufficient and contains errors.**
- The derivation from Equation 7 to 8 is flawed: the denominator in the softmax is approximated as 1 without justification. (R1, R3, R4)
- The claim that Sparse-CL enables 100×–5000× larger learning rates is not convincingly supported; comparing gradients (Eq. 11 vs Eq. 6) is unclear, and cross-entropy gradients are already bounded in [0, 2], contradicting the paper’s argument. (R4)
- No convincing explanation is given of why faster convergence alleviates the memorization effect and confirmation bias in the TTA setting (these concepts originate from centralized training). (R2, R4)
- The theoretical basis for why Sparse-CL’s gradient paradigm provides robust adaptation is lacking. (R2)
- The risk of overfitting due to fast convergence is not discussed. (R2)

**Heavy dependence on hyperparameters with insufficient ablation.**
- The method relies on hyperparameters \(s\), \(k\) (for k-NL) and balancing coefficient \(\alpha\), but their selection is only mentioned as cross-validation (\(s, k = 5\)) without ablation studies or analysis of their impact on stability and performance. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- No analysis of learning rate selection is provided, despite the paper emphasizing “larger learning steps.” (R3)
- The design of k-NL (skipping the \(s\) highest-probability classes before selecting \(k\) negative samples) is not justified; alternative sampling strategies are not explored. (R2, R3, R4)

**Experimental evaluation is limited and lacks rigor.**
- Experiments are conducted only on ImageNet-C and CIFAR100-C with TENT and SAR backbones; performance improvement on CIFAR100-C is marginal, limiting generalizability. (R4)
- Recent TTA baselines (e.g., RoTTA, SoTTA, DeYO) are missing. (R4)
- The impact of sample filtering and sharpness-aware minimization in SAR is not properly disentangled. (R4)
- No split experiments or additional analyses to validate the method’s claims (e.g., effect of faster adaptation on error accumulation). (R2)
- Figure 3 reports gradient norms using an unclear metric (differs from L1-norm mentioned in the text), and the difference between entropy minimization and the proposed method is not apparent. (R4)
- Evidence for the claim that Sparse-CL overcomes collapse cases in SAR (e.g., snow, frost) is not provided. (R3)

**Poor writing and presentation quality.**
- Unclear derivations: Equation 13 (“if \(i \neq \bar{y}_i\)”) and Equation 15 (“\(\frac{\nabla \mathcal{L}_{k-NL}}{h_i}\)”) are ambiguous. (R3)
- Redundant text at line 334 (“if \(\in \{1, 2, \cdots, k\}\)”). (R1, R3)
- Figure 2 has mismatched captions and legends; Figure 4 is poorly displayed and hard to interpret. (R4)
- Table captions are excessively long. (R3)
- Algorithm 1 is poorly presented and lacks concise explanation. (R4)
- Appendix figure numbering overlaps with the main manuscript. (R4)

**Reproducibility concerns.**
- The paper does not provide code or explicitly claim reproducibility. (R4)
