## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces UA3D, an unsupervised 3D object detection framework that estimates uncertainty at the individual bounding box coordinate level using the prediction discrepancy between a primary and an auxiliary detector. Based on the estimated uncertainty, loss weights are adaptively adjusted to down-weight noisy pseudo-box coordinates. Experiments on nuScenes and Lyft show improvements over MODEST and OYSTER (e.g., +6.9% AP_BEV, +2.5% AP_3D on nuScenes). The method is presented as simple and elegant, but several reviewers raise concerns about the baselines, scope of evaluation, and evidence for the uncertainty representation.

### Strengths

- The paper is clear, well-written, and easy to follow; the problem of inaccurate pseudo boxes is well delineated and the framework’s two phases are explained clearly.
- The authors provide an anonymous link to code and checkpoints.
- Experiments are conducted on large-scale autonomous driving datasets (nuScenes, Lyft).
- The method is simple and elegant, and it models uncertainty as a multivariate distribution (7-dimensional error vector), which is more fine-grained than previous uncertainty approaches.
- There is sufficient comparison to prior works (MODEST, OYSTER) and evidence that the estimated uncertainty correlates with pseudo-label quality.
- Visual results demonstrate improvement from uncertainty estimation.
- UA3D surpasses previous methods (MODEST, OYSTER) on the reported metrics.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited comparisons**: Only MODEST (2022) and OYSTER (2023) are used as baselines; more recent methods (e.g., CPD, CVPR’24; UNION, 2024) are omitted. Comparisons on Waymo or KITTI are missing, making it difficult to assess generalization.
- **Baseline and backbone concerns**: The dense point-wise bbox regression style is atypical for two-stage/one-stage detectors, leading to unusually low fully-supervised performance (nuScenes 22.2/18.2 AP) and raising doubts about the setup. The method is based on PointRCNN (2018) and is heavily hand-crafted with many parameters/thresholds; its transferability to other backbones (e.g., voxel-based detectors) is not shown.
- **Uncertainty representation inadequately justified**: It is questionable whether the discrepancy between two nearly identical detectors reliably represents localization uncertainty. Low-quality boxes may yield random regressions, and other factors beyond uncertainty could cause prediction differences. More qualitative and quantitative evidence is required to validate that the discrepancy is a good uncertainty measure.
- **Limited dataset usage and evaluation gaps**: Training uses only 3985 scenes and testing on 2412 from nuScenes (the full dataset is much larger). Per-class breakdown (pedestrians, vehicles, cyclists) is missing. The performance gap compared with fully-supervised (DSVT, SAFDNet) or sparsely-supervised (CoIn, MixSup) methods remains large, and the paper does not discuss how to close that gap or compare with state-of-the-art supervised methods on the full dataset.
- **Pseudo-label source not varied**: Only clustering-based pseudo-labels (from MODEST) are tested. Other potential sources (e.g., back-projecting zero-shot 2D detections from images) are not compared.
- **Lack of robustness analysis**: No experiments under different noise levels, weather conditions, or adversarial attacks are provided to assess robustness.
- **No discussion of limitations** is included.
- **Code/checkpoints were modified after the submission deadline** (Oct 15, 2024), which is a concern for reproducibility.
- **Reviewer questions overlapping with criticisms**:
    - How is the auxiliary detector initialized? (From scratch or duplicated weights?)
    - If the primary and auxiliary detectors share architecture and supervision, how can they learn distinct information? (In CPD, point density differs; here only channel dimensions differ.)
    - Would the framework improve fully-supervised detection if pseudo labels were replaced with ground truth?
- **One reviewer notes that the practical value of unsupervised 3D object detection for autonomous driving is limited**.
