## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces DASFormer, a self-supervised pretraining framework for Distributed Acoustic Sensing (DAS) data designed to perform earthquake monitoring as an anomaly detection task. The architecture combines a Swin U-Net (coarse generator) and a convolutional U-Net (fine generator) with a GAN-style training strategy and dynamic masking for self-supervision. The model is evaluated on earthquake detection and P/S seismic phase pickup, outperforming several time-series forecasting and anomaly detection baselines. The problem is of societal importance, the paper is clearly written, and the forecasting results are convincing. However, significant concerns exist regarding missing comparisons with established DAS-specific methods, insufficient ablation and sensitivity analysis, unclear generalizability, and limited methodological novelty.

### Strengths

- The research problem – self-supervised learning for distributed acoustic sensing (DAS) data – is important and not well studied; the approach addresses the lack of manual labels.
- The network architecture (Swin U-Net + convolutional U-Net) is thoughtfully designed to capture spatio-temporal correlations through a coarse-to-fine framework with noise injection and convolutional patching.
- The paper is clearly written, well-structured, and provides sufficient implementation details.
- The evaluation includes a wide range of supervised deep learning baselines and a traditional method; the proposed SSL pretraining demonstrates significant improvement across multiple metrics on both earthquake detection and P/S phase pickup.
- Forecasting results are convincing, and the method is validated on real DAS data for downstream tasks.

### Weaknesses

**Missing and unfair baseline comparisons**

1. No comparison with established DAS-specific earthquake detectors: PhaseNet-DAS (Zhu et al., 2023) and CNN-RNN (Hernández et al., 2022) are not used as baselines (Reviewers 1, 3).  
2. The baselines in Table 1 are problematic:  
   - “Aggregation-0” and “Aggregation-inf” lack clear descriptions or references (possibly LTA/STA).  
   - j-DAS, a denoising method, is used for detection comparison, which is considered unfair.  
   - The “traditional method” and “aggregation” references are unclear; best practice would be to compare directly with PhaseNet-DAS.  
3. DAS data is a special multivariate time series (ordered, equally spaced); comparing with general multivariate forecasting methods is misleading. A more relevant comparison would be with 2D image foundation models or video frameworks adapted to 1D+t (Reviewer 3).  
4. No comparison with other self-supervised learning methods such as MAE or MoCo. Because DASFormer uses more pretraining data than supervised baselines, a side-by-side SSL comparison (e.g., feature quality via linear probing) is needed to justify the proposed architecture and training scheme (Reviewer 4).  
5. The “Aggregation-0” baseline is strong (on par with or better than all deep learning models in some cases), suggesting a simpler approach might be equally effective.  
6. Literature (e.g., Makridakis et al. 2020, Nixtla) shows simple models (e.g., Random Forest) often outperform deep learning on time series; such a comparison would provide more insight.  
7. All baselines use the point-wise loss that the paper criticizes; a baseline without this weakness should be included (Reviewer 4).

**Data, preprocessing, and detection limits**

1. The data is resampled from 2000–4000 Hz down to 10 Hz without any comparative verification of the impact on downstream tasks; typical seismic sampling is 100–250 Hz (Reviewer 1).  
2. The low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) characteristic of DAS data is not addressed. The magnitude distribution and SNR of the training data are not reported, leaving unclear the model’s ability to detect earthquakes below a certain magnitude or SNR (Reviewers 1, 2).  
3. Whether the method generalizes to other DAS sensors or is site/sensor-specific is unclear; if site-specific, new self-training would be required for each deployment (Reviewer 3).

**Task clarity and evaluation gaps**

1. The paper does not clearly distinguish between P/S phase pickup and seismic detection; Table 1 mixes results from both tasks, causing confusion. The downstream phase-pickup task lacks a description of the dataset, evaluation metrics, and only mentions fine-tuning on 20 labeled data in Figure 4 (Reviewer 1).  
2. Forecasting results in the appendix are visually underwhelming, and detection performance is demonstrated only for selected examples (Figure 3 shows only DASFormer predicting effectively; verification with examples where other methods also succeed is missing) (Reviewers 1, 2).  
3. The claim that earthquakes are unpredictable is central to the method, but this limits applicability to other tasks (which may be predictable) and raises questions about performance in more predictable aftershock sequences (Reviewer 3).  
4. The anomaly score is defined between predicted and actual values in regions with data; it is unclear how the method works in a real-time setting forecasting into unobserved regions, or whether it only detects anomalies “in hindsight.” The choice of distance metric also merits discussion (Reviewer 4).

**Ablation, sensitivity, and methodological rigor**

1. No ablation experiments are provided for:  
   - The amount of training data needed for effective seismic monitoring.  
   - The individual contributions of the coarse and fine generators, or of the discriminator (GAN strategy).  
   - Why the fine generator is a convolutional U-Net and the coarse generator is a Swin-based U-Net (Reviewers 1, 3).  
2. The dynamic masking strategy is not explained, nor are its origins cited (Reviewer 3).  
3. The laborious objective function is not depicted in the architecture figure (Reviewer 4).  
4. A sensitivity analysis of hyperparameters is crucial given the public safety implications; the paper lacks any discussion of uncertainty. No quantification of false positive rate or distribution of detection performance as a function of earthquake magnitude is provided (Reviewers 2, 4).  
5. The paper is too focused on the application; there is insufficient methodological novelty. The contribution is essentially applying self-supervised learning to a new domain, with limited originality (Reviewer 2; also reflected in contribution ratings 2 in two reviews).

**Presentation and minor issues**

- The “Deep Learning on DAS Data” section in Related Work is too thin; only PhaseNet-DAS and j-DAS are covered.  
- The “Time Series Modeling” section claims existing models lack spatial information, but networks that integrate both exist and should be included.  
- Figure 1 left: axes labels for sensors and time are unclear.  
- Figure 2: what does “smm” stand for?  
- Page 2: define P and S wave abbreviations.  
- Section 3, first sentence: “as known as” should be “also known as”.
