## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers agree that Animate-X addresses the challenge of animating anthropomorphic (non-human) characters by introducing a Pose Indicator with an Implicit Pose Indicator (IPI) for motion pattern extraction and an Explicit Pose Indicator (EPI) for pose augmentation during training. A new benchmark, A2Bench, is also introduced for evaluating character animation. Overall, the motivation is clear, experimental results show state-of-the-art performance, and the benchmark is valuable. However, several concerns are raised regarding the clarity of the method, novelty relative to prior work (e.g., MimicMotion, AnimateAnyone, MagicAnimate), efficacy of the IPI module, construction details of the augmentation pool, and the benchmark’s design and diversity.

### Strengths
1. **Clear motivation and problem analysis** – The paper identifies that existing human-only animation models fail on anthropomorphic characters due to insufficient motion modeling, leading to the proposed framework.
2. **Strong visual results** – Animate-X shows notable improvements across various characters compared to existing methods; animation quality remains good even when reference characters lack limbs.
3. **Comprehensive experiments and ablation studies** – Both qualitative and quantitative evaluations are conducted on multiple datasets.
4. **Valuable benchmark (A2Bench)** – The new benchmark helps standardize evaluation for anthropomorphic character animation and is useful for the research community.
5. **Effective use of driving videos** – The method leverages driving video information to improve generalisation to in-the-wild characters.
6. **Novel design of Pose Indicators** – The implicit module integrates driving pose with reference image without a reference network, and the explicit module enhances pose robustness through augmentation.
7. **Inclusion of in-the-wild driving sequence/reference image pairs** – The evaluation benchmark contains unmatched pairs, which is more realistic for character animation.

### Weaknesses
1. **Clarity and reproducibility issues**  
   - Writing is confusing in several parts (e.g., P5 L260: what exactly is in the pose pool and how is it aligned with the reference?).  
   - The construction of the augmentation pool (pose pool and alignment pool) lacks detailed analysis, making reproduction difficult (pool sizes, pose selection criteria, translation/rescaling transformations).  
   - The anchor pose selection and the “align” process (translation and rescaling) are not clearly justified; the design seems overly complex without clear rationale (e.g., why not use random translation/rescaling?).

2. **Efficacy of the Implicit Pose Indicator (IPI)**  
   - IPI achieves only comparable performance to Animate-Anyone and UniAnimate on human videos (Tables 7, 8), suggesting no benefit for human animation. Its contribution to anthropomorphic characters is not conclusively demonstrated.  
   - Figure 7 shows that IPI has minimal impact on motion, and one example shows a missing foot when IPI is used, raising doubts about its effectiveness.  
   - In the banana animation example, motion precision is decreased, suggesting that IPI may harm motion accuracy. More experiments are needed to study this trade-off.  
   - The paper claims that IPI outperforms the reference network despite having fewer parameters, but lacks in-depth analysis of why this happens (e.g., comparing results in Table 4 and Table 1).

3. **Novelty and relation to prior work**  
   - The backbone of Animate-X closely follows prior works like AnimateAnyone and MagicAnimate, making it a straightforward extension with limited novelty.  
   - Leveraging driving videos to boost animation has been explored in prior works (e.g., MimicMotion); IPI is a similar design.  
   - Pose augmentation (EPI) has already been explored in existing methods (e.g., MimicMotion), further reducing innovation.  
   - The Explicit Pose Indicator is essentially a training-time augmentation of driving pose sequences; the paper does not clearly distinguish its advantage over test-time pose alignment, and no ablation study is provided for this comparison.

4. **Benchmark design concerns**  
   - No video samples from A2Bench are provided; only selected frames are shown. The use of T2I+I2V to generate benchmark videos is questioned: the temporal coherence of these generated videos is unclear, and the method’s ability to handle complex motion is not well demonstrated.  
   - The benchmark lacks detailed specifications: video length, frame rate, parameters of T2I and I2V models, and whether additional motion prompts were used (and if so, their diversity/complexity).  
   - Most styles in A2Bench are “3D render style”; the benchmark should include a wider variety of visual styles for comprehensive evaluation.  
   - No difficulty level split or robustness analysis for cases where the reference pose differs significantly from the candidates in the pose pool.

5. **Evaluation and comparisons**  
   - Comparisons with similar methods (e.g., MimicMotion) are missing, making the experimental results less convincing.  
   - The training dataset includes 9,000 independently collected videos; it is unclear whether baselines used the same data for training, potentially leading to unfair comparisons.  
   - The inference setting is unclear: the model’s use of input driving videos at inference is not clearly stated, and in the “Animating anthropomorphic characters” section of the supplementary material, driving videos are not shown. Including corresponding driving videos in results is recommended.

6. **Contradiction between problem analysis and proposed solution**  
   - The paper attributes failures to appearance differences between characters and humans, yet the IPI aims to learn image-level details from driving videos, which seems inconsistent with the stated viewpoint. This makes the contribution seem forced.

7. **Minority reviewer is more positive** – Two reviewers gave a high score (8) and commented positively on the method’s novelty and results, while two others (score 6) raised stronger doubts about novelty and effectiveness. The contrasting views should be noted (especially regarding IPI efficacy and benchmark quality).
