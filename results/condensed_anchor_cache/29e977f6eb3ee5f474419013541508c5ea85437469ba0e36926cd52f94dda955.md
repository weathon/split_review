## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Co-Representation Neural Hypergraph Diffusion (CoNHD) for edge-dependent node classification (ENC), where a node may have different labels across hyperedges. CoNHD models within-edge and within-node interactions as multi-input multi-output functions via hypergraph diffusion on node-edge co-representations. Reviewers acknowledge that the paper tackles an underexplored task and demonstrates strong empirical performance on ten datasets, with improvements in Micro‑F1/Macro‑F1 and training efficiency (Figure 3). However, they express major concerns about novelty, theoretical guarantees, real‑world motivation, and whether key limitations are convincingly addressed. One reviewer (score 8) is significantly more positive about the theoretical grounding and innovation, while three others (scores 5, 5, 3) find the contribution limited and the claims insufficiently supported.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an important and under‑explored problem (ENC) that has potential real‑world applications (Reviewers 2, 3).  
- CoNHD is theoretically grounded: proofs show increased expressiveness over message‑passing and ability to approximate hypergraph diffusion (Reviewer 3). One reviewer (score 8) considers the adaptive representation size (varying with node/edge degree) to be an innovative mechanism.  
- Comprehensive experiments on ten real‑world datasets, with CoNHD consistently outperforming baselines (including HGNNs and previous ENC methods) on both Micro‑F1 and Macro‑F1. Ablation studies comparing neural operators UNB and ISAB give insights into multi‑output equivariant functions (Reviewers 1, 3, 4). Training time is shown to be efficient (Figure 3) (Reviewer 3).  
- The paper is well‑organized and easy to follow (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 2 also praises the overall presentation.

**Minority/disagreement note:** Reviewer 3 is far more positive about the novelty and theoretical contributions, whereas Reviewers 1, 2, and 4 consider the novelty limited and the evidence for key advantages insufficient.  

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and contribution.** The method closely resembles EG‑GNN; the main change (multi‑input to multi‑output) is considered a trivial extension that could be simulated by concatenation (Reviewer 1). Similarities to Line Expansion and MultiSet are noted, yet performance of LEGCN and MultiSet on the same benchmarks is not reported (Reviewer 2). The multi‑input/multi‑output idea was already explored in prior message‑passing works (Reviewer 1).  
- **Lack of rigorous guarantees that the three claimed limitations are addressed.**  
  – *Non‑adaptive representation size:* The final classification weight matrix \(W\) has fixed dimensions and does not adapt to node degree (Reviewer 4).  
  – *Non‑adaptive messages:* No theoretical or empirical evidence is provided that CoNHD produces distinct features for the same node across different hyperedges (e.g., how different are \(h_{v_1,e_2}\) and \(h_{v_1,e_2}\)?) (Reviewer 4).  
  – *Insufficient direct interactions:* It is unclear what additional information gain CoNHD provides over standard node‑to‑edge and edge‑to‑node message passing, and whether this gain is purely intuitive or backed by theory (Reviewer 4).  
- **Questionable real‑world value.** The primary example (classifying author order) is already provided with the paper; it is unclear if any natural application requires ENC rather than simpler formulations (Reviewer 4). Could ENC be reduced to a star‑expansion graph edge‑prediction problem? If so, pros/cons of each modeling choice should be analyzed (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 also asks why this specific task is important beyond general node/hyperedge tasks.  
- **Efficiency concerns.** Expanding every node‑hyperedge pair leads to potential time and space complexity issues. The paper should discuss complexity with respect to number of nodes, hyperedges, and degrees (Reviewer 2).  
- **Over‑smoothing claim unsupported.** The paper mentions over‑smoothing in message‑passing frameworks but provides no experimental evidence that CoNHD is immune (Reviewer 3).  
- **Missing reference and minor error.** Reference for “problem 1” is missing. Figure 3 has a typo: “UNP‑” should be “UNB‑” (Reviewer 1).  
- **Presentation density.** Section 4 is too dense; a high‑level intuition before formal definitions would improve readability. The ADMM update part could be moved to the appendix (Reviewer 3).  
- **Open questions about generalizability and power.** Does CoNHD work effectively on other hypergraph tasks (e.g., node classification, hyperedge prediction) or only ENC? (Reviewer 4). Proposition 3 shows that CoNHD can represent message‑passing HNNs, but that does not guarantee superiority on all tasks (analogy: MLP is a universal approximator but does not outperform task‑specific networks) (Reviewer 4).
