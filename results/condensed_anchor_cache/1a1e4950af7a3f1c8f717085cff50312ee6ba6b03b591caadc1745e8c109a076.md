## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a modular reinforcement learning model inspired by hippocampal (HF) and prefrontal (PFC) interactions to show that replay can emerge naturally from reward-based learning without hardcoded replay rules. It tests on a simple grid‑world task where the reward location changes, and analyzes place‑cell activations and replay trajectories. Reviewers acknowledge the interesting premise and biological connections, but raise major concerns about clarity, limited evaluation (single environment), missing baseline comparisons, insufficient definitions of key terms, poorly explained figures, and unconvincing claims about replay generation and generalizability.

### Strengths
- The premise that replay may emerge naturally as a result of a broader reinforcement learning process is interesting (Reviewer 4).
- The hypothesis that communication between a world model (HF) and a policy (PFC) is important for generating replay is interesting (Reviewer 4).
- The model is neuroscience‑inspired and simulates replay without predefined or hardcoded replay rules; this is innovative and aligns with biological replay mechanisms (Reviewer 3).
- The authors demonstrate functional importance of replay by drawing parallels with a biological experiment (Igata et al.): replay patterns match rodent replay distribution and adaptive pathfinding, and ablation studies show that disrupting replay impairs exploration efficiency (Reviewer 3).
- The connection to animal data is interesting (Reviewer 4).
- One reviewer was more positive (score 6), finding the model design non‑trivial, the experiments well done, and the claims consistent with the experiments – though noting the limitation to a single environment (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- The paper is evaluated on essentially a single environment (grid‑world), calling into question the generality of the results for studying replay and broader RL settings (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- No baseline comparisons are provided; the experiments are extremely toy‑ish and lack comparison to any standard or alternative methods (Reviewer 2).
- Key terms such as ‘replay’, ‘place cell’, ‘replay step’, ‘phase‑lock’, and ‘one‑step information emission’ are never defined mathematically or concretely; the paper uses neuroscience jargon unfamiliar to the broader ML community (Reviewer 2). The claim that replay is ‘generated’ is never clearly defined (Reviewer 2).
- The figures are poorly explained: axes (e.g., Figure 1A/E “C2 checking time”, Figure 1D “distance”, Figure 2E axis, Figure 2A negative step count, covered y‑ticks), boundaries between stages (Figure 1C), “mask a replay step” (Figure 3B), “replace multi‑step emission with one step” (Figure 3C), “shuffle the order of information” (Figure 3D), and what the schematic replay trajectory in Figure 1F actually shows (Reviewers 2, 4).
- The metric “C2 checking times” is never explicitly introduced (Reviewers 2, 4).
- The RL task dynamics (transition, reward structure) are not precisely defined; methodological descriptions are insufficient (e.g., “pause the clock and allow the agent to explore the map randomly for 100 seconds” – unclear what inputs/outputs are) (Reviewer 2).
- In Section 3.1, it is unclear what happens to replay trajectories that do not pass through either checkpoint (Reviewer 2).
- The manifold analysis in Section 3.4 lacks clear takeaways and importance; it is unclear what conclusions should be drawn (Reviewer 2).
- The conclusion that conditions in Equation 1 are sufficient to generate replay is not well justified; alternative outcomes that would not demonstrate this are not considered (Reviewer 4).
- The necessity of replay is questionable: the model is trained such that a communication channel opens after reward, so it will naturally find a way to use that channel – disrupting it disrupts behavior, but this may be a trivial consequence rather than a deep conclusion (Reviewer 4).
- The model is structurally designed to produce place‑cell activations; when unlinked from environmental input, PFC activity yielding place‑cell activations in HF may be the only possible outcome – this makes the replay claim weak; more emphasis on actual spatial trajectories is needed (Reviewer 4).
- If weights are fixed, the role of replay is for in‑context updating, but the paper does not discuss relation to synaptic‑weight updating or standard experience replay; this conceptual link is missing (Reviewer 4).
- There is limited evidence showing how the modular framework could integrate with mainstream model‑free RL algorithms; the paper should discuss how HF and PFC could improve standard RL methods (Reviewer 3).
- The paper would benefit from a background section for readability (Reviewer 3).
- The claim that the model is “model‑based” because HF acts as a world model lacks empirical evidence that HF can accurately predict next states or rewards (Reviewer 3).
- A more detailed and convincing demonstration of replay is needed, closer to neuroscientific representations (e.g., Carr et al. 2011) and in simpler settings (linear track, W‑maze) (Reviewer 4).
- Specific questions about notation: Equations 2 and 3 use `I_replay` which asserts the conclusion; `I_rest` would be more appropriate. The meaning of `r` in `W_{h, r}` is unclear (replay or reward?) (Reviewer 4).
- In Figure 4A, what do “1st” and “2nd” refer to? (Reviewer 4)
- In testing necessity (Figure 3A): is signal replaced only during replay periods? How does disrupting HF‑to‑PFC transmission actually disrupt PFC and action selection? (Reviewer 4)
- Shuffling messages during replay has minimal effect – what is the implication, and what is an “independent package”? (Reviewer 4)
- The abstract claims “modularity” but does not clarify whether the model is a general framework that can be integrated with other blocks (Reviewer 3).
- A citation claim: Fedus et al. 2020 is said to be about hyperparameter tuning of experience replay, but the reviewer does not agree with that characterization (Reviewer 1).
- Minor: A capitalisation error in citation “(Van de Ven & Tolias, 2018)” – “Van” should be lower case (Reviewer 1).
