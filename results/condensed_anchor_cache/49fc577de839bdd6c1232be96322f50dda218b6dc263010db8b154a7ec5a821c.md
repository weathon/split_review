## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Consistent Optimal Transport (COT), which extends optimal transport to multiple probability measures with a cycle-consistency constraint that enforces that transport plans between consecutive measures compose to the identity. The authors propose Monge and Kantorovich formulations, then relax them using entropic and cycle-consistency regularization, and derive an iterative Sinkhorn-like algorithm (RCOT-Sinkhorn) for approximate solutions. COT is applied to visual multi-point matching (using cosine distances from off-the-shelf graph matching features) and to the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP-COT). The paper claims improvements on these tasks without additional feature training and includes numerical convergence analysis. Reviewers are divided: one (R1, score 10) found the paper well-written, well-motivated, and the TSP formulation very interesting with strong numeric results; two (R2, score 6; R3, score 5) found the problem novel and the approach comprehensive but raised concerns about clarity, theoretical grounding, and missing details; the fourth (R4, score 3) was highly critical, questioning motivation vs. multi-marginal OT, clarity, soundness of formulations, and the order-dependence of cycle-consistency. All reviewers found the TSP connection interesting, but with mixed opinions on its depth and justification.

### Strengths
- The problem of consistent multiway optimal transport is compelling and well-motivated by applications (e.g., point matching) (R1, R2, R3). The formulation and approach are new (R3).
- The paper is well-written and clear, with well-defined goals and detailed algorithms (R1, R2). Reviewer 1 praised the quality of writing and presentation as high.
- The connection to the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP) is a valuable and interesting addition (R1, R2, R3, R4). Reviewer 4 appreciated that the authors acknowledge limitations and do not oversell the method.
- Numerical results are strong: "often outperform, or at least stay competitive, with the state-of-the-art in all the examples" (R1). The point matching experiment in particular underscores the motivation for requiring cycle-consistency (R3).
- The paper presents a comprehensive approach including Monge and Kantorovich formulations, entropic relaxation, and optimization algorithms (R3).
- Reviewer 4 found the COT formulation intriguing and believes it may be of interest in some situations.

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and comparison with multi-marginal OT (MMOT):** The paper claims to “generalize OT to more than two marginals,” but this is precisely what MMOT does. The practical difference between MMOT and COT is not clearly explained; it remains unclear when to use which (R2, R3, R4). Reviewer 4 specifically notes the abstract says “each pair of measures” but the paper only considers adjacent measures, which is misleading. Reviewer 2 and 3 both ask for non-trivial connections beyond the trivial fact that both involve multiple measures.
- **Clarity and presentation:** While R1 praised writing quality, several reviewers found the exposition challenging. Reviewer 3 notes that transitions between sections 3.1 and 3.2 are abrupt and lack intuitive motivation, making the paper hard to read for those without prior familiarity. Reviewer 4 lists multiple clarity issues: misleading statements (e.g., abstract implying all pairwise transport), inconsistent notation (scalar product formatting, varying point count symbols), poorly formatted proofs in the appendix (equations split unnecessarily), and algorithms placed only in the appendix despite being referenced as essential in the main text. Many minor typos were noted: line 47 “there calls”, line 52 “cost of three trasnsportation”, line 69 “we contribute”, line 94 “The Monge problem is exactly not easy to calculate” should also note existence issues, line 188 Eq (7) T_k should be T_K, line 307 “RCOT” typo, line 345 “k<0” should be “k<K”, and inconsistent spelling of “travelling”/”traveling” (R2, R3). Also undefined acronyms: “MCTS” (line 69), “LAP” (line 138), “GW” (line 248), and “RCOT-PGD” (line 246, only defined in appendix) (R3). Abstract itself is hard to read (R3). Reviewer 3 also notes that the phrase “introduce the entropic regularization transforming the hard cycle-consistency” is inaccurate because the regularized version includes separate entropy and cycle-consistency terms.
- **Theoretical soundness and formulation:** Existence of a solution to COT’s Monge formulation (Eq. 6) is not addressed; a rigorous proof or discussion of conditions is missing (R3). The relaxation from Monge to Kantorovich formulation (Eq. 6 to Eq. 8) is not clearly motivated—what specific properties are relaxed and at what trade-offs? (R3). Reviewer 4 questions whether Birkhoff’s theorem applies: are optimal plans necessarily permutation matrices from the Kantorovich formulation (Eq. 8) when measures are discrete and uniform? This is critical for TSP adaptation and for the behavior of entropic regularization as ε→0. The paper lacks analytic convergence guarantees for the iterative algorithm; only numerical convergence is provided (R2, R3). Reviewer 2 asks for analytic convergence results. The definition of cycle-consistency appears order-dependent, relying only on consecutive pairs, but this dependency is not analyzed—what if the order is permuted? (R3, R4). Reviewer 4 notes the 1D example in Figure 2 likely yields automatic cycle-consistency because standard 1D OT is monotone, so the experiment barely supports the method; the authors should confirm.
- **TSP formulation details:** The mapping between COT and TSP is insufficiently explained. How exactly are nodes and edges represented? What specific cost matrices are used? More importantly, what can be said about the quality of the approximate solution compared to existing TSP solvers? (R3, R4). Reviewer 3 calls for a concrete mapping and theoretical analysis of approximation quality.
- **Cycle-consistency properties:** It is unclear whether cycle-consistency is achieved exactly or approximately, both in theory and in experiments, and at what rate. Does the iterative algorithm guarantee convergence to an exactly cycle-consistent solution? If not, what is the theoretical bound on the deviation? Empirical results on convergence rate and degree of consistency would strengthen the evaluation (R3). Reviewer 3 also notes that the regularized problem includes both entropy and consistency terms, so the term “hard cycle-consistency” is misleading.
- **Experimental evaluation and ablation:** The ablation study in Section 4.3 is unclear—"certain factors" are not specified, and the setup and conclusions need clarification. Which factors were ablated? How does performance vary with different choices? (R3). Missing computation time in Table 1 (line 471 says running time is presented, but it is not shown) (R1). Sensitivity to optimization parameters (e.g., δ) is not explored (R1). Hyperparameter ε (entropic regularization) is not discussed in the hyperparameter setting (R3 mentions δ′ but not ε). Also Figure 3 is somewhat unclear (R3).
- **Missing details and references:** The paper claims relevance to Gromov–Wasserstein (GW) computation but provides no references for algorithms approximating GW (R2). Reviewer 4 finds the GW comparison not useful as GW is fundamentally different and not introduced. The reasons for saying “matrix-vector iterative method” are unclear (R3). Also, the line “In contrast, our method employs a training-free approach that assumes consistency is satisfied on the test set, using this prior information to improve performance during inference” needs clarification (R3). Algorithms 1–5 are placed in the appendix but are referenced in the main text as essential; they should be included in the main paper if necessary or clearly marked as optional (R4).
