## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Spike-TransCNN, a hierarchical architecture that combines Spiking Transformers and Spiking CNNs for event-based object detection. The model integrates global (Transformer) and local (CNN) features with intra- and inter-stage fusion modules. It reports competitive results on Gen1 (mAP 0.336, energy 5.49 mJ) but significantly lower performance on 1Mpx (0.250) and omits comparisons with several recent SNN and hybrid models. Reviewers note that the combination of Transformer and CNN is already established in ANN, the model is effectively a hybrid ANN-SNN due to non-spiking computations, multiple module motivations are unclear, energy claims are questionable, and the writing needs improvement.

### Strengths
- First attempt to combine Spiking-Transformer and Spiking-CNN architectures for event-based object detection (R2, R1). Successfully integrates global and local information, with potential benefits for future neuromorphic designs (R4).
- Achieves competitive performance on Gen1: mAP 0.336 and energy consumption 5.49 mJ (R2).
- Hierarchical integration is well-motivated and effectively leverages the strengths of each architecture, leading to improvements in both accuracy and energy efficiency (R3). Reasonable design for Transformer and CNN branches under spiking neurons, with a feature fusion method that suits the hybrid architecture (R1).
- Introduces interesting blocks (e.g., STS, SCB) that improve performance (R4).
- Robust experimental validation including comparisons with state-of-the-art methods and energy efficiency analysis; focus on energy-efficient edge-computing applications aligns with practical needs (R3).
- High-quality visualization and graphical presentation (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The combination of Transformer and CNN is already a mature, proven paradigm in ANNs (R1). The use of LIF neurons is not an important innovation (R1). The architecture primarily transplants an established paradigm into the SNN domain (R2). The integration of global information from Spiking Transformer with local information from Spiking CNNs was first proposed in Spike-driven Transformer V2 [1], not in this work (R4). Many modules (SSA, SCB, fusion) reference previous work and do not constitute important innovations (R1, R4).
- **Missing comparisons**: No comparison with the recent SNN detector "Integer-Valued Training and Spike-Driven Inference Spiking Neural Network for High-performance and Energy-efficient Object Detection" (2024), though it is referenced in the discussion (R2). No comparison with pure SNN models like SpikeYOLO [2] or hybrid models like EAS-SNN [3] and SpikingViT [4] (R4). Also lacks comparison with hybrid SNN-ANN models to contextualize energy and performance trade-offs (R3).
- **Performance on 1Mpx dataset**: The model achieves only mAP 0.250 on the 1Mpx dataset, which is significantly inferior to existing methods (0.483). No adequate explanation or analysis for this decline is provided (R2, R4).
- **Energy consumption concerns**: The reported lower energy consumption compared to SFOD (5.49 mJ vs. 7.26 mJ) is questionable. Spike-TransCNN has double the parameters of SFOD with similar firing rates, and it extensively employs SPIKE-ELEMENT-WISE ADDITION operations that introduce non-spiking computations, undermining the claimed energy advantage (R4).
- **Unclear module motivations**: The motivation for using STS (Spiking Transformer in shallow layers) instead of SSA is not explained (R4). The motivation for SCB (CNN block) is inadequately explained; standard 3×3 and 5×5 convolutions could achieve the same objective (R4). The differences between the two types of feature fusion modules and the specific issues they address are not elaborated (R1). The design logic of the overall network leans toward module stacking without highlighting the authors' own analysis (R1).
- **Lack of theoretical analysis**: The paper overrelies on descriptive language; there is no theoretical analysis for the performance improvements of the proposed architecture (R2). The theoretical/methodological section appears to only reference previous work (R1).
- **Clarity issues**: Explanations of spike-driven token selection and intra- and inter-stage feature fusion could be clearer. Including pseudo-code or flow diagrams would help (R3). Terminology related to spiking mechanisms and attention mechanisms is inconsistent, reducing readability for non-specialists (R3).
- **Limited generalizability**: Evaluation is only on Gen1; additional event-based datasets, especially larger or more complex ones, are needed to demonstrate generalizability (R3).
- **Missing ablation on hyperparameters**: No exploration of sensitivity to number of time steps, membrane potential thresholds, etc. (R3). Additional ablation studies and in-depth analysis of the STS module are needed (R4, R3).
- **Visualization limitations**: No side-by-side comparisons with other models on challenging scenarios (occlusions, rapid movement) to highlight model strengths (R3).
- **Formatting issue**: On line 382, Firing Rate and Energy (mJ) values overlap (R4).
- **Grammatical and logical coherence**: The paper needs polishing to highlight its own contributions; the introduction focuses on SNN development rather than the problem at hand (R1). The contribution section appears scattered (R1). The introduction does not highlight innovative points (R1).
