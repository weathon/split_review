## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces ROBULT, a framework addressing missing modalities and limited labeled data in multimodal learning. It employs an information-theoretic approach with two key objectives: (1) a latent reconstruction loss to retain unique modality-specific information, and (2) a soft Positive-Unlabeled (PU) contrastive loss to utilize sparse labeled data in semi-supervised settings. ROBULT enhances performance across downstream tasks and remains robust when modalities are absent at inference. Empirical results on diverse datasets (sentiment analysis, multi-label classification) demonstrate superiority over existing methods.

### Strengths
- ROBULT successfully preserves unique modality-specific information and cross-modal synergy, supported by experimental results and ablation studies.
- The theoretical proof accompanying the method provides a solid foundation.
- Superior performance over multiple existing baselines in both semi-supervised learning and missing modality scenarios (reviewers note strong evidence from quantitative and qualitative analyses).
- Detailed experimental setup, comprehensive code and data preparation instructions enable reproduction.
- The design supports various modality types and quantities, and allows seamless integration with current deep learning frameworks, highlighting practical value.
- The research topic (multimodal learning under modality-missing and semi-supervised settings) is interesting and has potential real-world impact.
- Supplementary materials provide sufficient implementation details.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and comparison to prior work**: The idea of information decoupling (shared vs. private representations) has been explored in works such as [1] Lee & Pavlovic (2020) and [2] Daunhawer et al. (2021). The authors do not clearly articulate the difference between ROBULT and these methods. Additionally, Wu et al. [3] (ICLR 2024) also handle missing modalities and labels; a direct comparison on their task is missing. (Reviewer 3)
- **Claim of unsolved problem**: The paper states that semi-supervised learning with missing modalities remains unsolved, yet prior works such as “Missing Modality Robustness in Semi-Supervised Multi-Modal Semantic Segmentation” and “Semi-Supervised Multi-Modal Learning with Incomplete Modalities” already address this scenario. The authors should clarify the distinction. (Reviewer 4)
- **Insufficient scope of missing modality scenarios**: Experiments only consider training with all modalities present and missing modalities at test time. Other realistic settings (e.g., missing modalities in training data, varying ratios of missing modalities) are not examined. A baseline like SMIL [R1] explores severely missing scenarios that should be included. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limited semi-supervised experimental settings**: The paper tests only 5% and 50% labeled data. Standard semi-supervised practice (e.g., PseudoSeg [R2], R3) includes additional settings (10%, 20%) to demonstrate robustness. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing hyperparameter and sensitivity analysis**: 
  - The influence of hyperparameter τ (used in the contrastive loss) is not analyzed; reviewers request experimental results showing how τ affects performance. (Reviewer 2)
  - The loss weights for L_ulb, L_lb, L_rec, L_sup are not discussed—are they all set to 1? If so, why? If not, a sensitivity analysis should be provided. (Reviewer 3)
- **Lack of fusion strategy exploration**: Since ROBULT is presented as a plugin for multimodal fusion, its performance under different fusion architectures or strategies should be evaluated. (Reviewer 2)
- **Insufficient analysis of the PU contrastive loss contribution**: The paper should include experiments or discussion on how the contribution of the soft PU contrastive loss varies with the amount of labeled data (e.g., does it become more important when labeled data are scarcer?). (Reviewer 1)
- **Potential data completeness requirement**: Does training require all modalities to be present? If a sample has only one available modality, can it still be used? Excluding such data may discard valuable information and underutilize available data. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing simulation details**: The authors do not describe how missing modality scenarios are simulated in the experiments. (Reviewer 4)
- **Methodology presentation issues**: 
  - In the Methodology section (paragraph 4), the inputs to functions f^i, g^i, c are written as h^i_j, u^i_j, z^i_j, but these appear to be outputs rather than inputs. (Reviewer 4)
  - The derivation from Equation (9) to Equation (10) lacks sufficient detail; a more step-by-step explanation is needed. (Reviewer 4)
  - The rationale for using an RBF kernel to adjust the PU loss is unclear—what happens if a true positive has low proximity to the reference? This could distort the loss. (Reviewer 4)
- **Visualization missing**: The authors claim the reconstruction loss helps produce unique representations U^i, but no visualizations (e.g., distribution comparisons) are provided to demonstrate this. (Reviewer 3)
- **Writing quality**: The second paragraph of the Introduction appears incomplete and requires further development. (Reviewers 1 and 4)
- **Clarification needed on abstract claims**: The abstract states “ROBULT seamlessly integrates into deep learning architectures”—the practical integration mechanism should be explained. (Reviewer 4)
- **Interpretation of latent variables**: The meaning of the synergy latent variable S and unimodal variable Z_i is not well explained; their relationship should be clarified. (Reviewer 4)
- **Connection between missing modalities and semi-supervised settings**: The authors should discuss the relationship and potential synergies, and suggest other domains where the approach could be applied. (Reviewer 4)

**Note on reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 6, confidence 4) is substantially more positive, emphasizing ROBULT’s effective robustness, clear presentation, and practical integration value, while another reviewer (score 3, confidence 4) is more critical, calling the research incremental and the scope narrow. The weaknesses above reflect the concerns of both critical reviewers, and the more positive reviewer’s request for additional experiments (sensitivity, fusion strategies) is also included.
