## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper provides the first theoretical analysis of the expressive power of Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for fully connected neural networks and Transformer networks. It establishes sufficient rank conditions for LoRA to adapt a base model to a target model, quantifies approximation error when rank is insufficient, and provides empirical validation. The paper is considered a timely and significant contribution that offers theoretical foundations for practical LoRA usage, though reviewers raised concerns about experimental validation, metric choice, practical implications, and some unexplained empirical observations. One reviewer (R3) is notably more positive (score 8, confidence 4), while another (R2) has lower confidence in the soundness and contribution (score 6, soundness 2, contribution 2).

### Strengths
- **First known theoretical analysis of LoRA's expressive power**, addressing a gap in understanding why LoRA works (R1: novelty significant if true; R3: very timely topic, good starting point; R4: first results).
- **Theoretical results align with recent empirical advances** of LoRA on large language models (R1).
- **Comprehensive coverage** of linear models, fully connected networks (FNNs), and Transformer networks (TFNs), with both theoretical and empirical studies (R1, R4).
- **Findings offer a theoretical foundation** for applying LoRA to diverse models (Transformers, Diffusion models) and provide insights on hyperparameter selection (e.g., rank) based on model depth and width (R2).
- **Can streamline the design process** for LoRA when model architecture is specified (R2).
- **Empirical results match the rank requirements** predicted by the theoretical analysis (R4).
- R3 rates the paper as theoretically strong (soundness 4, contribution 4, presentation 4), noting it is a good starting point for understanding how/why/when LoRA works.

### Weaknesses
- **Figure 1 discrepancy:** LoRA of FNN performs on par with gradient update, whereas LoRA of TFNs significantly outperforms gradient updates. This is unexplained; a deeper analysis of architectural differences between FNNs and TFNs causing this is needed (R1).
- **Figure 3 observation:** LoRA with rank=1 matches gradient update performance. This raises the question whether gradient update learns well. The paper should explore the optimization landscape (local minima, ill-conditioning), compare learned parameters, and investigate sensitivity to initialization and learning rates (R1).
- **Experimental validation concerns (R2):** Given widespread LoRA applications (e.g., LLaMA), the authors should substantiate findings with models on different challenges and compare theoretical analysis with empirical LoRA observations. R2's soundness rating is 2 (fair), citing weak experimental validation.
- **Metric choice (R2):** Mean Squared Error (MSE) is questionable because performance scores for LLMs typically weakly correlate with perplexity or loss. A multifaceted evaluation including different metrics is needed for robust validation, especially for generative AI.
- **Lack of practical insights (R2):** The community acknowledges that very low ranks (4, 2, 1) can yield satisfactory fine-tuning. The paper does not explain how low-rank adjustments achieve effective fine-tuning; experimental outcomes lack actionable guidance for practitioners.
- **Need for clearer connection** between theoretical discoveries and previously reported empirical LoRA results to streamline hyperparameter selection (R2).
- **Lack of notation table** to help navigate the many notations in the theoretical proofs (R4).
- **Limited scope in Transformer analysis:** Focuses on self-attention layers; analysis of LoRA on other parts (e.g., feed-forward networks) is absent. Discussion of potential limitations of the current analysis is needed (R4).
- **Missing practical trade-off discussion:** The paper does not discuss trade-offs in training time, memory usage, or generalization compared to other PEFT methods or full fine-tuning, translating theoretical findings to real-world scenarios (R4).
- **Question (R4):** In Section 2, why is a L-layer linear model considered (since it is still linear)?
- **Future work suggestion (R3):** The paper could also theoretically study LoRA's effects on generalization and convergence speed, even if not required for this work.
