## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a model-free offline RL algorithm using a double-pessimism principle that conservatively estimates performance by accounting for both limited data (dataset uncertainty) and potential model mismatches (environment shifts). The method avoids transition model estimation, enhancing scalability, and provides sample complexity analysis for perturbation modeled by the ℓα-norm. Empirical results on toy environments show improvement over single-pessimism baselines. The paper claims to be the first offline model-free algorithm with a robustness guarantee.

### Strengths
- Introduces a double-pessimism principle that jointly handles model mismatch and limited dataset coverage in a model-free framework; considered a significant contribution to offline RL (Reviewers 1,2,3,5).
- Claims the first offline model-free algorithm with a robustness guarantee (Reviewer 4).
- Provides rigorous theoretical analysis: sample complexity lower bounds and convergence guarantees for both finite-horizon and infinite-horizon discounted settings (Reviewers 1,2,5). The detailed sample complexity analysis for the ℓα-norm model theoretically demonstrates efficiency (Reviewer 5).
- Model-free design avoids explicit transition model estimation, offering scalability to complex environments (Reviewers 1,2,3). The double-pessimism principle empirically outperforms single-pessimism baselines in toy environments (Reviewers 2,4). Reviewer 2 (rating 8) particularly finds the algorithm scalable, robust, and supported by extensive experiments.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited evaluation scope**: Experiments are conducted only on simple environments (toy settings). No results are reported on standard offline RL benchmarks like D4RL (Mujoco, etc.), which are widely used to test scalability and practical applicability (Reviewers 1,3,4,5). This makes it difficult to assess performance in high-dimensional continuous state/action spaces or with high-dimensional observations (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons**: No comparisons with model-based baselines (Reviewer 1) or with state-of-the-art model-free offline RL algorithms such as CQL and IQL (Reviewer 3). Without direct comparison, it is hard to gauge relative advantages (Reviewers 1,3).
- **Lack of ablation studies**: No ablation experiments analyzing the functionality or contribution of the double-pessimism principle (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 4 also notes that adding a second pessimistic term may lead to overly conservative policies when model mismatch is insignificant.
- **Uncertainty set justification and estimation**: It is unclear how to justify or estimate the uncertainty set in practice; the paper assigns hyperparameters without guidance (Reviewer 2). In real-world scenarios (e.g., robotic manipulation), perturbations may affect rewards or observations, not just transition probabilities as assumed (Reviewer 5). The ℓα-norm model is suitable for theory but its relation to real-world perturbations is unclear (Reviewers 4,5). A real-world example of model mismatch with a constructed uncertainty set would strengthen applicability (Reviewer 4).
- **Sample complexity and computational efficiency**: The method has higher sample complexity than the baseline (Shi & Chi, 2022) with respect to γ (Reviewer 4). For general perturbations, each update may require solving large optimization problems, raising efficiency concerns; details on the computational cost of κ calculation are missing (Reviewer 5). The paper does not discuss computational complexity or scalability of implementation (Reviewer 5).
- **Theoretical limitations**: The sample complexity analysis is restricted to the ℓα-norm model; broader analysis (e.g., KL divergence, Wasserstein distance) is needed (Reviewer 5). One reviewer questions whether the definition of perturbations ensures their impacts do not cascade across steps (Reviewer 5).
- **Reproducibility and presentation gaps**: Source code is not provided (Reviewer 4). Figure 2 lacks standard deviations or confidence intervals for performance curves (Reviewer 4). The paper does not discuss strategies for fine-tuning parameters per task (Reviewer 1).
