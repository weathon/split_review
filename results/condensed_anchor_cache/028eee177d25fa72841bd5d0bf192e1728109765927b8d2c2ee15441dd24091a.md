## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper rethinks knowledge distillation (KD) from a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) perspective, arguing that direct KL-divergence between teacher and student predictions can conflict with the classification objective when there is significant discrepancy between ground truth and teacher predictions. The authors treat the teacher prediction as a latent variable and reformulate the classification objective as an MoE, where each expert corresponds to a subtask conditioned on a particular teacher label, and the gating function is the teacher’s classifier applied to student features. They derive an evidence lower bound (ELBO) for this formulation and cast optimization as an Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm, providing a convergence analysis. The method, MoE-KD, is evaluated on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1K with multiple teacher–student pairs (CNN architectures), and also on transfer learning datasets. Results are reported to outperform several existing KD baselines.

### Strengths

- The highlighted issue – cases where teacher predictions and ground truth disagree – is critical, especially when the teacher is not trained at very large scale.
- The reformulation of the classification objective as an MoE with teacher predictions as latent variables is novel and intuitive.
- The derivation of the ELBO (Eq. 4) and its connection to the EM algorithm is mathematically sound and interesting.
- The method consistently outperforms previous KD methods across almost all experimented settings, including setups with large teacher–student gaps and strong training strategies.
- Experiments are conducted on widely used benchmarks (CIFAR-100, ImageNet-1K, transfer learning datasets) with multiple architecture pairs.
- The paper provides sufficient comparisons to existing methods and clearly elaborates the novelty.
- Additional analysis (linear probe setup, standard deviations in appendix) supports result quality.

### Weaknesses

- **Consistency between problem, algorithm, and approach is lacking.**  
  - How frequently do cases occur in the training data where there is a significant discrepancy between ground truth and the teacher’s prediction?  
  - From what perspective is the proposed method better than the traditional direct KL-divergence approach? Does it help learning toward ground truth more effectively when the discrepancy is large?  
  - It would be beneficial to show training results using only data samples with a large gap between ground truth and the teacher’s prediction.

- **Methodology readability is poor.**  
  - The transition from Section 4.2 to 4.3 is abrupt, and the shift from ELBO maximization to the EM algorithm lacks a clear, guiding explanation.  
  - No diagram or pseudocode is provided for the overall algorithm; it is confusing how the student model is adapted to an MoE version during training and how it makes inference without teacher predictions afterwards.

- **Ablation and component analysis is insufficient.**  
  - Table 4 evaluates removing components from the complete method but does not show the standalone effect of applying each component (e.g., using the gating function to partition the task into subtasks) on the KD baseline alone.  
  - Better to design toy experiments or visualizations (e.g., distribution of gates, sharpness) to demonstrate the method’s influence, rather than only comparing final accuracies.

- **Computation cost is not addressed.**  
  - Since the method is formulated as an EM algorithm, the computation cost seems much higher than existing methods. Comparisons of training time or FLOPs should be provided.

- **Equation (12) appears intractable.**  
  - The term \( P_{\theta}^S(Y^T=k|x_i) \) in Eq. (12) seems intractable because the teacher’s predictions cannot be derived from the student. The authors need to clarify how this is handled.

- **Experimental scope is limited.**  
  - Experiments use only CNN networks (teachers and students). How does the method perform with Vision Transformers?  
  - The paper does not discuss applicability to feature-level distillation, which is important for dense prediction tasks like object detection.  
  - The value of \( K \) (number of experts/subtasks) is not reported; how does the choice of \( K \) affect performance?  
  - Section 5.3 claims better transferability, but no explanation is given for why this occurs.

- **Related work is missing.**  
  - Xue et al. (KDExplainer, IJCAI 2021) also use an MoE model to explain knowledge distillation. The differences between that work and MoE-KD should be discussed and compared.

- **Presentation issues.**  
  - The writing should distinguish between \citep and \citet for proper citations.  
  - Clarify the relevance of the proposed method to existing knowledge distillation methods.
