## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a two-step method to improve zero-shot visual generalization in offline RL: first augment the offline dataset with transformations (rotation, color jitter, color cutout), then train a latent-space diffusion model on the augmented data to generate additional synthetic transitions. The method is tested on V-D4RL (continuous) and Offline Procgen (discrete) using DrQ and CQL. Augmentation and diffusion-based upsampling together improve generalization performance. The approach is algorithm-agnostic (only changes the dataset) and computationally efficient by operating in latent space. However, reviewers raised several concerns about the experimental design, missing ablations, and the paper’s claims regarding generalization.

### Strengths
1. **Simple, novel, and algorithm-agnostic**: The two-step combination of data augmentation and latent-space diffusion is easy to understand and does not require changes to existing offline RL algorithms; it can in principle improve any offline RL method.
2. **Performance gains**: The method significantly reduces the generalization gap (improves zero-shot performance) on both continuous (V-D4RL) and discrete (Procgen) tasks without altering the underlying RL algorithm.
3. **Latent-space efficiency**: Generating synthetic data in latent space avoids the computational costs of training and sampling diffusion models directly on high-dimensional images, making the approach scalable.
4. **Comprehensive experimental analysis**: The paper provides extensive experiments, including analysis using Jensen-Shannon divergence to quantify distribution alignment between training and test data. The method yields additional improvements when a small subset of data with distractions is included (FDD experiment).
5. **Clear presentation**: The paper is clearly written and easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited set of base algorithms**: Only DrQ and CQL are used as the offline RL components. Multiple reviewers noted that since the method only modifies the data, it should be tested on more methods (e.g., IQL, TD3+BC, EDAC) as done in related work like SynthER.
2. **Dataset size confound – need for controlled comparison**: The *Augmented* dataset has the same size as the *Baseline*, while *Augmented Upsampled* is larger. It is unclear whether performance gains come from the diffusion model’s generation or simply from using more (augmented) data. A controlled experiment matching dataset sizes is missing. One reviewer suggested: “Conducting an additional experiment where the size of the Augmented dataset is increased to match the Augmented Upsampled dataset would help isolate the potential benefits of the diffusion model's data generation.”
3. **Dependence on augmentation choices – insufficient justification and ablation**: The choice of specific augmentations (rotation, color jitter, color cutout) is not empirically justified. Reviewers noted that the discussion in Section 3.2 lacks depth: no parameters (magnitudes, probabilities, kernel sizes), no references to established online RL augmentation techniques (DrAC, SVEA, survey by Laskin et al.), and no analysis of interaction between augmentations. Why is random shift (common in image-based RL) not included? No ablation study varying the augmentation types. Performance likely depends heavily on the augments chosen.
4. **Metric for generalization (G_perf) is poorly justified**: The proposed metric \(G_{\text{perf}} = (T_{\text{test}} - B_{\text{test}})/(B_{\text{train}} - B_{\text{test}})\) is opaque. A more natural metric would be comparing \(T_{\text{train}}/B_{\text{train}}\) and \(T_{\text{test}}/T_{\text{train}}\) with \(B_{\text{test}}/B_{\text{train}}\). The current metric obscures the separation between training performance and generalization capability.
5. **Misalignment with “zero-shot generalization” claim**: The experimental results show that performance improvements come mainly from enhanced training performance, and the generalization gap between training and test environments persists. The paper does not demonstrate that the method specifically improves generalization beyond simply raising training scores. This disconnect between the main thesis and the empirical evidence requires substantial clarification.
6. **Missing ablation studies on diffusion model design**: The paper does not explore architecture choices (number of layers, latent space size, activation functions), hyperparameters (learning rate, number of diffusion steps, noise schedule), or the impact of latent space dimensionality on generation quality and downstream performance. This limits reproducibility and understanding of sensitivity.
7. **Comparison with other generative models**: The method is not compared against alternative generative models such as GANs or VAEs. Could these provide similar improvements with lower computational overhead?
8. **Computational cost concerns**: Although latent-space diffusion is efficient, training and running diffusion models can still be resource-intensive, especially when scaling to larger datasets or higher-resolution inputs. The paper does not report specific computational costs.
9. **Risk of overfitting to synthetic data**: Heavily relying on generated data may cause the model to overfit to artificial diversity, especially if the synthetic distribution diverges from real-world test distributions. The paper does not discuss mitigation strategies.
10. **Limited evaluation to controlled benchmarks**: It is unclear how the method will perform in more complex, real-world scenarios with far greater visual variation (e.g., unknown lighting, object appearances). The paper only tests on V-D4RL and Procgen.
11. **Unclear whether initial augmentation phase is necessary when using FDD**: One reviewer hypothesized that using a small amount of data from the Fixed Distraction Dataset (5% FDD) combined with diffusion upsampling (without the initial augmentation phase) could already yield good performance. This comparison is missing.
12. **Minor presentation issues**: Figures 4, 5, 6 are too large; JS divergence heatmaps (e.g., Figures 1b, 6b) with only four values are better presented as bar plots or with exact values overlaid. Typos: line 137 “invrease” should be “increase”; line 361 “tecnic” should be “technique”. 
13. **Lack of methodological detail**: The augmentation function *Augment* is applied to both state \(s\) and next state \(s'\), but it is unclear whether the same random transformation is used for both or independently sampled.
