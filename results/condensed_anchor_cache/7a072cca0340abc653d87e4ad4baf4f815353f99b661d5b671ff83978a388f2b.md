## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CityBench, an interactive simulator-based benchmark for evaluating LLMs and VLMs on urban tasks. It includes CityData (multi-source data), CitySim (simulator), and 8 tasks in perception-understanding and planning-decision-making. Experiments on 13 cities and 30 models reveal strengths in semantic understanding but weaknesses in specialized reasoning (e.g., geospatial prediction, traffic control). Reviewer opinions are polarized: scores 8, 6, 6, 5, 10. Some praise the comprehensiveness and open-source platform (R1, R2, R3, R5); others criticize shallow analysis, vague methodology, and missing comparisons (R4, R3, R2). R5 (score 10) is most positive; R4 (score 5) is most negative. All agree the topic is interesting but differ on execution and contribution depth.

### Strengths
- **Comprehensive benchmark with diverse tasks** (R1, R2, R3, R5): Includes interactive/non-interactive tasks, perception and decision-making (image geolocalization, infrastructure inference, traffic signal control, mobility prediction). Valuable to the community for evaluating LLMs in urban domain.
- **Extensive evaluations** (R1, R2, R5): 30 LLMs/VLMs tested across 13 cities. Metrics are sensible. Error analysis and geospatial bias analysis provide additional insights.
- **Open-source code and APIs** (R1, R2, R5): Publicly released code and API, substantial engineering effort with CityData and CitySim, aiding reproducibility and adoption.
- **Innovative combination of multi-modal data and interactive simulation** (R5): CitySim integrates geospatial, visual, and human activity data to simulate fine-grained urban dynamics, setting it apart from prior work.
- **Geographic diversity across 13 cities** (R2, R1, R4, R5): Cities span continents, enabling cross-city comparisons.
- **Interesting and useful categorization of tasks** (R4, R5): Perception-understanding vs. planning-decision-making.
- **Forward-looking approach** (R5): Explores potential of LLMs in real-world urban applications (planning, traffic management).

### Weaknesses
- **Vague pipeline description for data generation and quality control** (R1, R2, R3, R4):
  - Section 3.3.1: Which LLMs are used for generating instructions and filtering? A single model (e.g., GPT-4o) could bias the benchmark in favor of that model. No quantitative metrics on how much low-quality data was generated, filtered, or manually curated. (R1)
  - Criteria for “low-quality” (e.g., in GeoQA) not defined; no metrics/heuristics for filtering/rewriting. Manual checking process unclear—what constitutes “meaningful” context, especially for less familiar cities? Process for regenerating cases not specified. (R3)
  - Standards and proportions for filtering/rewriting by human annotators not provided. (R2, R3)
  - Section 3.1 CityData: Rule-based map construction tool not described (e.g., rules for lane topology recovery, relationship recognition, intersection reconstruction). Limitations not assessed. (R3)
- **Shallow analysis of model performance and underlying causes** (R4, R2, R3):
  - Good/bad performance across cities, models, and tasks is discussed only vaguely. Root causes of poor LLM performance in regression tasks (e.g., geospatial prediction) not explained. Geospatial bias is attributed only to “lesser-known” cities without evidence; socio-economic, morphological, linguistic aspects are ignored. (R4)
  - Error analysis incomplete: only LLM errors analyzed, not VLM errors; LLM error analysis only covers general issues (e.g., instruction-following, hallucinations) without task-specific insights relevant to urban tasks. (R2)
  - When LLMs are not as good as conventional task-specific methods, lessons learned are sparsely mentioned. (R4)
- **Missing comparisons and baselines** (R1, R3, R2):
  - No human baseline for interactive tasks; without it, it is difficult to gauge human-level performance. (R1)
  - No comparison or discussion with prior similar benchmark CityEval (June 2024), which also evaluates LLMs on urban spaces and overlaps in tasks. (R3)
  - Table 3 includes only language-based tasks; VLM results should also be provided for a comprehensive comparison. The distinction between testing LLMs vs. VLMs for Outdoor Navigation and Urban Exploration is unclear—both are decision-making but only one modality is tested per task without rationale. (R2)
- **Geographic diversity disagreement** (R1 vs. R2): Reviewers disagree. R2 considers 13 cities “rich geographic diversity,” but R1 notes that only 13 cities may not capture full spectrum; performance is worse on less renowned cities, suggesting the need for more fine-grained analysis. (R1, R2)
- **Data recency concerns** (R3, R4):
  - Human Activities Data from a 2016 paper (likely Foursquare data). Can 8-year-old data accurately reflect current human activity? Will old data affect findings? (R3, R4)
  - Why not use more current data for Foursquare-derived data? (R4)
- **Incomplete test results and figure issues** (R2):
  - Figure 5 left panel: lacks legend to identify cities (R2)
  - Table 2 omits LLM results due to visual input requirement; but Table 3 (language tasks) could also include VLM results. (R2)
- **Limited task diversity** (R5, R4):
  - Current tasks lack coverage of broader social, economic, environmental aspects (e.g., community planning, disaster response, housing affordability, job accessibility). (R5)
  - CityData module is merely an introduction of datasets used, not a contribution in itself. (R4)
- **Name conflict** (R3): The name “CitySim” is already in use; choosing a different name would reduce ambiguity.
- **Unclear supervision and few-shot exploration** (R1):
  - Did the authors evaluate few-shot performance? How does LLM performance scale with in-context examples?
- **Questions that overlap with missing analysis or experiments** (R1, R3, R4, R5):
  - Why is performance on New York so low despite being well-known? (R1)
  - How are satellite/street-view images sampled? What is the distribution per city? (R1)
  - On examples where the model always provides an answer (i.e., not refusals), how do different models compare? (R1)
  - Underlying factors for geospatial bias in VLMs (e.g., urban morphology, data availability)? (R5)
  - Why larger models do not always outperform smaller ones—task complexity, architecture, or data issues? (R5)
  - Strategies to mitigate formatting/logic errors in future deployments? (R5)
  - Why are the five qualities from “The Image of the City” used for GeoQA? What is the relation between those urban design qualities and the benchmarking task? (R4)
