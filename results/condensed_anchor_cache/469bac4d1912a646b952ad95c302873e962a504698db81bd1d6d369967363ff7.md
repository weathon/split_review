## Merged Review

### Summary
All four reviewers rate the paper 10/10, finding it to present a well-motivated, physically grounded method for diffusion-based illumination control. The key idea is imposing light transport consistency: an object’s blended appearance under mixed illumination equals its appearance under blended illumination. This loss enables stable, scalable training on over 10 million images from diverse sources (real light stages, rendered synthetic data, in-the-wild augmentations). The resulting model achieves state-of-the-art quality in relighting, harmonization, and normal estimation, and scales across backbones (SDXL, Flux). Reviewers note the work is exceptionally well-written and documented, with strong results and significant commercial potential. However, several request clarifications on formulation details (LDR additivity, MLP approximation), implementation choices (loss stability, normal estimation formulas), and missing comparisons/analyses (generalization, temporal consistency, comparisons to Neural Gaffer). Concerns about reproducibility due to unclear release plans and limited quantitative metrics are also raised.

### Strengths
- Well-written, well-motivated paper addressing the challenging task of illumination control; the solution works extremely well (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- The light transport consistency (IC-Light) loss is simple, physically grounded, and effectively prevents training degradation while enabling scalable training (Reviewers 1,4).
- Data preparation pipeline is solid: diverse sources (real light stages, rendered samples, in-the-wild synthetic augmentations) totaling >10M images; uses multiple intrinsic estimation methods for robustness (Reviewers 1,4).
- Training strategy scales to various backbone models (SDXL, Flux) and large-scale data, making the method immediately useful as a tool (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- Achieves state-of-the-art image quality among diffusion-based lighting control methods; results are astonishing with consistent materials (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- Supports alternative lighting control interfaces (background condition, environment maps) and side applications (image harmonization, normal estimation) (Reviewers 2,4).
- Loss design is novel: mixing strategies from augmentation/regularization newly applied to illumination editing (Reviewer 3).
- Method and results are exceptionally well-documented with practical insights (Reviewer 1).
- Significant contribution with commercial potential for photography and movie industries (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited quantitative evaluation**: lacks comparison to methods like Neural Gaffer; metrics are not diverse (perceptual, shading consistency missing); no explicit analysis of generalization to out-of-distribution data; need details on how rendering data differs from training dataset (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing discussion of intrinsic image decomposition literature**: the fundamental observation (invariant intrinsic components) is common knowledge; the paper overlooks classical methods (Retinex, optimization-based) and should list the IID methods used in the main paper, not just supplement (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear release plans**: it is not stated whether data, model weights, and training code will be released; this hinders reproducibility. Composition of dataset (diversity of foreground objects, lighting conditions, background scenes) is not detailed (Reviewer 3).
- **Figures lack condition details**: Fig. 4 and Fig. 5(c) do not state the prompt or lighting condition used, making it difficult to assess robustness (Reviewer 2).
- **Text error**: L.427 and L.431: “shadow maps” should be “shading maps” (Reviewer 2).
- **Formulation issue – LDR vs HDR additivity**: light transport additivity holds only for linear HDR images, but natural/diffusion-generated images are tone-mapped LDR; how is this misalignment handled? (Reviewer 4).
- **Material ambiguity**: single input image allows multiple material-lighting combinations; how much do prompt and seed affect material estimation and final lighting result? (Reviewer 4).
- **Implementation of lighting consistency loss**: the sum of two RGB images is approximated by an MLP in latent space – does this MLP actually enforce linearity without other regularization? The loss uses x_0 prediction, which is unstable at large t; is there special handling for this? (Reviewer 4).
- **Normal estimation formulas incorrect**: Equation 8 should use minus, not average; N_green and N_red should be defined in world space with range [-1,1] and visualized as (N+1)/2. Fig. 6 shows inconsistent coordinate systems across examples (Reviewer 4).
- **Artifacts in results**: baked-in specular highlights appear (car in Fig. 1, helmet in Fig. 4); wrong global illumination (ice-cream example in supplementary exhibits two shadows in opposite directions) (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing details on data synthesis**: more explanation and visuals for the synthesis of L1 and L2 (L.319) would be helpful; more qualitative samples from the dataset showing all methods used to generate synthetic components (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing analyses and experiments**: quantitative evaluation of normal maps against real 3D normals; temporal consistency when continuously changing the environment map during inference (Reviewer 4).
- **Questions on pipeline components**: (a) Reliability of DiffusionLight for small FOV images? (b) How is visibility/occlusion handled when estimating environment maps from in-the-wild images (e.g., concave objects)? (c) Over-exposed images produce wrong env maps even with HDR – is this handled? (d) How effective is the CLIP-based lighting filter (L.242)? Would an aesthetic score help? (e) L.246: does the “image-space” rendering pipeline consider global illumination (self-occlusion, inter-reflection)? (f) For background-conditioned inference, is the env map L set to 0 or estimated from the background? (g) In normal estimation, is the shadow map averaged over 3 channels? Why is the shadow map colored in Fig. 5(c)? Is division by two in Eq. 8 correct? Why use average of four images for ambient instead of minimum? (Reviewer 4).
- **Additional questions**: (a) Would the method perform well for general scene-level relighting (e.g., turning on/off visible light sources like in Li et al., ECCV 2022)? (b) Does the method consistently provide high-quality results, or does it require careful tuning of prompt and seed? (c) Is lighting consistency also imposed on foreground and background together? (Reviewers 2,4).
