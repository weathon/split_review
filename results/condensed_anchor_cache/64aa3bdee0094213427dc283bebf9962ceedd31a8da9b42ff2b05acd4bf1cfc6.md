## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Uncertainty-Receptive fusing (URF) for boosting ensemble predictions with uncertainty, an image acquisition model, and Gnostic Uncertainty Estimation (GUE) / test-time dropout entropy (TTD+EUA) for uncertainty quantification. Experiments are on the MURA musculoskeletal radiograph dataset for classification and segmentation. Two reviewers (scores 1 and 1) are highly critical, finding no technical novelty, severe presentation issues, missing essential sections, and strong textual similarity to prior work without adequate citation. A third reviewer (score 3) is more positive, acknowledging the conceptual foundation and the TTA+EUA results as outperforming benchmarks, but also notes clarity and structure problems. This contrast in evaluation is notable: the minority positive reviewer sees value in the approach, while the majority find the work unsuitable for ICLR.

### Strengths
- **Timeliness and importance of the topic**: Addressing reliability of segmentation systems in healthcare (R1) and uncertainty estimation in medical imaging (R2) are recognized as important and timely.
- **Comprehensive exploration of uncertainty approaches**: The paper investigates a range of solutions from uncertainty-sensitive fusion to entropy-based evaluation, with a robust conceptual foundation anchored in established prior works (R3).
- **Empirical results for TTA + EUA**: The test-time augmentation combined with entropy-based uncertainty estimation consistently outperforms several benchmarks, setting a new baseline and offering valuable insights for future research (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty and strong similarity to prior work**:
  - GUE / TTD+EUA (Monte Carlo Dropout + entropy at test time) is a widely adopted technique; the relevant sections (2.2/2.2.1/2.2.2/2.2.3) are extremely similar to Sections 3.1–3.5 of [1] without adequate reference (R1).
  - Much of Section 2.1 describing URF is very similar to Section III of [8] (no reference); it is unclear how URF differs from uncertainty-aware ensemble boosting in [8] (R1).
- **Missing or insufficiently described experimental setup**:
  - No experiments in the main text; all results are relegated to the appendix, making the main paper unreadable without it (R1, R2).
  - No clear task definition: the paper mixes classification, regression, segmentation, and calibration without explaining the actual task (R1). Segmentation is not introduced until Section 2.4; calibration is only mentioned in the appendix (R1).
  - No description of the experimental setting in the main text (R2).
- **Weak evaluation and missing baselines**:
  - URF is claimed to improve calibration, but the term “calibration” never appears in the main paper; no reasoning is given for why URF should improve calibration or why pixel-wise calibration matters for segmentation (R1).
  - No comparison against actual calibration methods (e.g., temperature scaling); the baselines in Table 1 (Pappagari, Sarawgi, Rohanian) are never introduced, and none are calibration methods (R1).
  - The regression task (evaluated via RMSE/MPIW/PICP) appears without any regression task being defined; the paper only describes a classification use case (R1).
  - Dice score improvements are not compared against state-of-the-art medical segmentation baselines like nnU-Net [9] (R1).
- **Poor presentation and structural issues**:
  - The writing is vague, superficial, and lacks coherence; e.g., the abstract mentions URF, an image acquisition model, and GUE, but the intro only discusses the importance of uncertainty without connecting to these contributions (R2).
  - Essential sections missing: no separate related work, no clear problem formulation, no experimental setup in main text (R1, R2).
  - Many inconsistencies and loose ends: Appendix A introduces Cohen’s kappa but never uses it; “Grand Challenge 4” mentioned without context; “Fluky Model” in a heading but never defined; URF_w never introduced; calibration baselines not described; GUE later called TTD+EUA; Section 2.2 seems to be about test-time augmentations but not stated; Figure 1 claimed to illustrate VF/URF/URF_w but does not; batch effects mentioned in motivation but never addressed (R1).
  - Figure 1 does not cover the described methods; the caption and figure lack information (R1, R2).
  - Sentence in Section 2.1, third paragraph is incoherent (R2).
  - The focus in title/abstract is “healthcare” but the intro narrows to musculoskeletal problems without justification (R2).
  - Many typos; overall structure does not meet high-quality writing standards; readers must constantly refer to the appendix (R3).
- **Unclear or missing methodological details**:
  - What does “modality” refer to? Most medical multi-modal work includes clinical notes, vital signs, tabular data; this paper only uses CNN-based image segmentation (R3).
  - Prior work on test-time augmentation (e.g., [10]) is not discussed, and it is unclear how the proposed TTA improves over previous approaches (R3).
  - How is entropy used for actual segmentation predictions? How to obtain a Dice score from an entropy score? (R1 question).
  - How can ECE/MCE be used as a loss function during training? (R1 question).
  - The statement “Based on the test sample results of the distinct modalities, we decide in what order to enhance these models to propagate uncertainty” – basing decisions on test samples is problematic (R1 question).
- **Too narrow for ICLR conventions**:
  - The work makes general methodological claims about enhancing classification/regression/segmentation reliability but only evaluates on one specific task (2D musculoskeletal radiograph segmentation). Diversity across datasets is required for such claims (R1).
