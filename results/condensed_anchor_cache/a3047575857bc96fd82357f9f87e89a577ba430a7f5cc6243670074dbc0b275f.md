## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Latent-EnSF, a data assimilation method that extends the Ensemble Score Filter (EnSF) by using a coupled Variational Autoencoder (VAE) to map both the full state and sparse observations into a shared latent space, where the EnSF update is applied. The method is evaluated on shallow water wave propagation and medium‑range weather forecasting. Reviewers acknowledge the importance of handling sparse observations for score‑based filters and find the empirical results impressive. However, concerns are raised about limited novelty (latent‑space DA is already established), missing comparisons with traditional DA methods (e.g., physical‑space LETKF, 4DVar), unclear training procedure of the VAE, incomplete theoretical justification for the advantage of the latent representation, and insufficient motivation for the latent‑matching loss. The review scores range from 5 to 8, with Reviewer 4 being the most positive (8) and Reviewer 1 the most critical (5); most reviewers gave a score of 6.

### Strengths
- **Addresses a critical issue**: The paper tackles the problem of sparse observations in score‑based filters (EnSF), which is a clear obstacle to realistic applications (Reviewer 4, others).  
- **Strong empirical results**: Latent-EnSF shows significant improvements in accuracy, convergence speed, and computational efficiency over standard EnSF, latent‑EnKF, and latent‑LETKF on two challenging benchmarks (shallow water, medium‑range weather forecasting) (Reviewers 3, 5).  
- **Clear motivation and structure**: The rationale for using latent space to avoid gradient vanishing is well‑explained, and the method is logically presented (Reviewers 4, 6).  
- **Thorough baseline comparison**: The experiments include comparisons with EnSF, latent‑EnKF, latent‑LETKF, and FourCastNet, providing a solid evaluation (Reviewer 5).  
- **Demonstrates feasibility**: The work shows that data assimilation in latent space can effectively overcome the vanishing‑gradient problem in EnSF (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: Performing data assimilation in a latent space is already an established approach (Peyron et al. 2021, “Latent Space Data Assimilation Using Deep Learning”; Cheng et al. 2024, “Multi‑domain Encoder‑Decoder Neural Networks for Latent Data Assimilation”). The contribution is primarily the combination of EnSF with these existing ideas (Reviewers 2, 3, 5).  
- **Missing comparisons with traditional DA methods**: No comparison is provided against standard physical‑space methods such as the Localized Ensemble Transform Kalman Filter (LETKF), EnKF, or 4DVar. Such comparisons are needed to demonstrate the practical advantage of latent‑EnSF (Reviewers 1, 4, 3).  
- **Unclear VAE training procedure**: It is not specified whether the encoders and decoder are trained at each time step or pre‑trained. If retrained every step, the claimed efficiency is questionable; the computational cost of training the neural networks is not accounted for in the timing comparisons (Reviewers 1, 6, 4).  
- **Incomplete theoretical explanation**: The argument that sparse observations cause vanishing gradients in EnSF is not sufficiently supported. Figure 8 (Appendix B) is confusing; it appears to show only the observation grid, not the gradient collapse. The empirical evidence in Section 4.1.1 is unclear (e.g., labeling of “perturbed” RMSE is constant, suggesting the system may not be chaotic) and lacks error bars, making it hard to judge statistical significance (Reviewer 3).  
- **Latent‑matching term (Equation 13) is not well‑motivated**: Why should the mean and variance of the state encoder and observation encoder be forced to match? The paper does not justify this constraint or explain how it contributes to handling sparse observations (Reviewers 5, 2).  
- **Observation noise in latent space**: How observation uncertainties are transformed from physical space to the latent space is not addressed. The observation error covariance in the latent space is assumed to be identity, but its specification is nontrivial (Reviewers 2, 4).  
- **Limitations of fixed observation sites**: The method assumes observations occur at the same spatial locations at every time step. This is not required in EnKF or EnSF; the paper does not discuss whether this restriction can be relaxed (Reviewer 3).  
- **Computational cost comparisons are potentially misleading**: LETKF can be parallelized due to local spatial correlations, but the paper compares wall‑clock times with a serial implementation. The overhead of the VAE (both training and inference) is not fully considered, and no error bars are provided for timing results (Reviewers 1, 3).  
- **Notation and presentation errors**: Equation (14) writes a “KL divergence” as a function of parameters (μ, Σ) without specifying the two distributions involved; it likely intends KL(N(μ, Σ) || N(0, I)). The reconstruction loss in Equations (11)–(13) may not be correctly marginalised over the latent variable; it is unclear whether the loss is derived from variational Bayes or is merely “VAE‑inspired” (Reviewers 1, 3, 5, 6). Small typos are present (e.g., line 222: “full‑dimensional observation in the latent space” should probably be “full space”) (Reviewer 5).  
- **Missing discussion of related work**: The paper does not discuss reduced‑order methods such as the reduced‑order autodifferentiable EnKF (Chen et al., *Inverse Problems* 2023) (Reviewer 6).  
- **Missing comparison with GLA**: The authors mention computational issues with the Guided Linear Approximation (GLA) from Cheng et al. 2023, but do not include it as a baseline, even though the latent dimensions (2r) are small enough that GLA may be efficient (Reviewer 6).  
- **Uncertainty about applicability to nonlinear observations**: The method assumes the state can be reconstructed from a single time point and that the observation operator in latent space is identity. It is unclear how the method handles nonlinear observation models, noisy observations, or irregular sampling patterns (Reviewers 1, 4, 2).  
- **Initial conditions and perturbation details are missing**: The paper does not specify the initial state distributions or how perturbations are applied to generate the perturbed trajectories (Reviewer 1).  
- **Confusing Figure 8**: The figure shows ∇ₓ P(y|x) instead of ∇ₓ log P(y|x) as claimed; also, showing the score as zero at unsampled locations is misleading—it should be shown as undefined or as a lower‑resolution grid (Reviewer 3).
