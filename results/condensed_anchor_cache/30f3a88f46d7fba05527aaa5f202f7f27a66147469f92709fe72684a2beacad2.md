## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a method to automatically optimize the weight given to different data sources during training, using a two-stage process. In Stage I, the model is trained with fixed mixture coefficients while accumulating per-source gradients. In Stage II, the validation loss on a target task is minimized by optimizing the mixture coefficients via gradient descent on a linear combination of accumulated gradients. A first-order approximation (constant G assumption) makes the gradient of the validation loss tractable. Extensions for large-scale training (checkpointing with equally spaced gradient snapshots) and multi-episode refinement are presented. Experiments are conducted on a formal language (Dyck grammar), image classification (ad hoc CIFAR10 versions), and object detection (MS-COCO), showing improved task-specific performance. The paper is generally well-written and the problem is well-motivated.

### Strengths

- The approach is novel to the knowledge of most reviewers, and the problem of optimizing dataset mixtures is well-defined and practically important.
- The method is simple, general, model- and modality-agnostic, and can be applied to any gradient-based learning pipeline.
- The paper provides a solid theoretical foundation with clear mathematical justifications for the gradient approximation, including proofs.
- Extensions for large-scale training and multi-episode optimization add practical value.
- Experimental results are presented across multiple domains (language and vision) and include ablation studies on optimizers, number of validation samples, and other factors.
- The paper is well-organized and clearly written, making complex ideas accessible.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited real-world evaluation:** The experimental evaluation relies mostly on toy or invented problems (Dyck grammar, ad hoc CIFAR10). The only real dataset is MS-COCO, but it is unclear what the distinct data sources are in that setting. A real-world NLP task (e.g., using The Pile) is expected given the paper’s motivation but is missing. (Reviewer 1, 3)
- **Lack of comparison to existing baselines:** The paper lists six previous works on data mixture optimization (including DoReMi, DOGE, clustering-based methods) but does not compare to any of them. Also, a uniform mixture baseline is not reported for the language task. Without such comparisons, it is difficult to assess the advantages of the proposed method. (Reviewer 1, 4)
- **No empirical validation of the constant G approximation:** The paper relies on an assumption that the gradient matrix **G** (per-source gradients) is approximately constant over the training interval, yet no quantitative measure is provided to verify this assumption or show how it correlates with method performance. (Reviewer 1)
- **Trivial improvements on vision tasks:** In several tables (e.g., Table 2, Table 3), the proposed method often fails to outperform the “Exact” baseline, and gains over Stage-I-only are small. (Reviewer 4)
- **Limited checkpointing analysis:** The checkpointing experiment only considers 5 and 10 checkpoints. The observation that 5 checkpoints can outperform 10 is counterintuitive and left unexplained. More checkpoints would be needed to understand the effect. (Reviewer 3)
- **No runtime or computational overhead analysis:** The paper does not quantify the additional computation and memory required by the proposed method compared to standard training or to conventional fine-tuning, despite having to accumulate per-source gradients and recompute gradients with many validation samples (e.g., 10,000). (Reviewer 3, 4)
- **Scalability not addressed:** Experiments are limited to 2 or 3 subsets. The method’s scalability to larger numbers of data sources (e.g., 10 for CIFAR10, 100 for CIFAR100) is not explored. (Reviewer 4)
- **Gradient approximation validity with non-constant learning rates:** The theoretical justification assumes a small, constant learning rate (η). No empirical evidence or further analysis is provided for adaptive or scheduled learning rates, which are common in practice. (Reviewer 5)
- **Softmax constraint not compared to unconstrained coefficients:** The paper uses softmax to normalize mixture coefficients for interpretability, but does not empirically compare performance or optimization behavior with unconstrained coefficients. (Reviewer 5)
- **Lack of theoretical analysis of distribution shift and validation set size:** The method’s success depends on the similarity between validation and test distributions and on validation set size, but the paper does not analyze the interplay between distribution shift (training→validation→test) and dataset sizes, which is critical for understanding when the approach will fail (e.g., overfitting to a small validation set). (Reviewer 2, 5)
- **Writing and notation issues:** Notation inconsistencies (e.g., **G** vs *G* in Equations 5 and 7; boldface missing in Table 1 caption). Concepts such as the implicit dependence of θ_T on α (lines 120-121) and the change of variables (line 194) lack sufficient explanation. (Reviewer 3)
- **Typos:** “paris” for “pairs” (line 307), “Qunatized” (line 402), “epsiode” (line 405), “citet” for “citep” (line 418). (Reviewer 1, 3)
- **Unclear experimental details and definitions:**
    - In the language experiment (Dyck grammar), what is the test perplexity compared to a uniform baseline? What does the “uniform” line in Figures 4/5 refer to when there are only three training datasets? (Reviewer 4)
    - In vision experiments, what does “Base” in Table 5 correspond to? (Reviewer 3)
- **Questions overlapping with criticisms:**
    - Why does 5 checkpoints outperform 10, and why does more random examples improve with 5 but degrade with 10? (Reviewer 3)
    - What is the computation and memory footprint of the method, especially when gradients are estimated from 10,000 examples? (Reviewer 4)
    - In Stage II, are gradients recomputed on θ_T or are the old (Stage I) gradients used? (Reviewer 4)
    - How does the method compare to conventional fine-tuning with the same total number of steps from Stage I and Stage II? (Reviewer 4)
    - Does the softmax constraint affect optimization performance compared to unconstrained coefficients? (Reviewer 5)
    - Are there theoretical limits under specific data distributions or tasks where gradient-based mixture optimization may underperform? (Reviewer 5)
