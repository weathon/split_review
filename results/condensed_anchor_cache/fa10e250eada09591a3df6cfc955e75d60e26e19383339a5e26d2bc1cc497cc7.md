## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes an unsupervised method called XiEff representation—a neural field-based reparameterization of the effective susceptibility tensor—for reconstructing particle shapes from Scanning Near-Field Optical Microscopy (SNOM) images. The approach integrates the XiEff representation into the Lippmann-Schwinger integral equation framework and uses an optimization strategy inspired by Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs). The method is evaluated on a synthetic dataset of near-field images, achieving high intersection-over-union scores with ground truth shapes and showing noise robustness. All reviewers agree the paper adapts existing techniques (NeRF/PINN) to a domain-specific problem. One reviewer (score 5) found the approach interesting and highlighting its novelty, but the other three (scores 3) considered the contribution poor due to missing algorithmic innovations, insufficient comparisons, and limited experimental validation. Missing key prior works on PINNs in nano-optics is a critical oversight.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written (minor formatting issues noted). (Reviewer 1)
- The method adapts NeRF and PINNs for effective shape reconstruction in nano-optics and demonstrates robustness to noise on a synthetic dataset with various particle geometries. (Reviewer 2)
- Introducing “neural fields + optics” into ICLR is an interesting endeavor. (Reviewer 3)
- Proposes a new representation (XiEff) inspired by NeRFs and PINNs. (Reviewer 4)
- Integrates XiEff into the Lippmann-Schwinger integral equation framework for near-field optics, enabling direct reconstruction of effective susceptibility from NFI data. (Reviewer 4)
- Develops an unsupervised optimization framework that improves the practicality and efficiency of near-field optical analysis. (Reviewer 4)
- *Contrast:* While Reviewer 4 views the XiEff representation and its integration as novel contributions, Reviewers 1-3 consider the work an exercise of existing methods without algorithmic innovation.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing key related work:** The paper does not discuss foundational prior works on PINNs for inverse problems in nano-optics, specifically Chen et al. (2020, *Optics Express*) “Physics-informed neural networks for inverse problems in nano-optics and metamaterials” (500 citations) and Chen & Dal Negro (2022, *APL Photonics*) “Physics-informed neural networks for imaging and parameter retrieval of photonic nanostructures from near-field data” (40 citations). This omission critically undermines the originality and positioning of the work. (Reviewers 2, 3; also implied by Reviewer 4’s request for systematic introduction)
- **Lack of quantitative comparison:** No rigorous comparison with traditional reconstruction methods (e.g., discretization, iterative methods, other diagram-based solutions) is provided. Validation relies solely on IoU, which is insufficient to demonstrate advantage over existing techniques. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Only synthetic data:** All experiments use synthetically generated NFI data; no validation on real SNOM data. The domain shift from synthetic to real experimental data is not addressed, raising practical relevance concerns. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Insufficient experiments and unrealistic complexity:** Experiments are limited to 2D, diagonal effective susceptibility cases with toy geometries. No 3D or non-diagonal cases are tested. A 3-D experimental setup with non-diagonal effective susceptibility would be interesting. (Reviewer 1; echoed by Reviewer 3 calling “2D toy results insufficient”; Reviewer 4 notes no ablation studies or parameter evaluations)
- **Missing ablated studies and parameter evaluation:** The experimental section is thin—no ablation studies, no evaluation of model parameters. (Reviewer 4)
- **Unclear definition of “interpretability”** as used in the paper. (Reviewer 4)
- **Questionable fit for ICLR:** The paper does not delve deeply into machine learning or AI concepts, and the scope may be better suited for an optics journal. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Formatting issues:** Citation format and section fragments need fixing. (Reviewer 1)
- **Minority positive contrast noted:** Despite the above weaknesses, one reviewer (score 5) considered the approach a useful contribution, citing the new XiEff representation, its physics integration, and unsupervised optimization; however, the majority found the work lacking novelty and validation.
