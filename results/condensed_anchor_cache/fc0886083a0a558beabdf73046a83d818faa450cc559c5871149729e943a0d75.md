## Merged Review

### Summary
FABFlex is a regression-based multi-task learning model for fast and accurate blind flexible molecular docking, addressing a practical scenario where binding sites are unknown and proteins exhibit flexibility. It comprises three modules (pocket prediction, ligand docking, pocket docking) with an iterative update mechanism. Reviewers agree the paper is well-written, the architecture is intuitive, and the experiments are comprehensive. All note strong performance—significantly outperforming sampling-based SOTA (e.g., DynamicBind, DiffDock, TankBind) on ligand structure predictions and achieving up to 208× speedup over DynamicBind. Positive reviewers (1, 4) highlight the practical importance and solid experimental validation; more critical reviewers (2, 3) raise concerns about novelty relative to the FABind series and overreliance on RMSD. Contrast exists between reviewers: two (scores 8) see the iterative refinement and multi-task design as strong contributions, while two (scores 6) consider the modifications incremental and the evaluation insufficient.

### Strengths
- Tackles the realistic blind flexible docking scenario (proteins flexible, binding sites unknown), which is more practical than rigid docking assumptions.
- Architecture is intuitive and decomposes the problem into three clear modules (pocket prediction, ligand docking, pocket docking) with an iterative update mechanism for continuous refinement.
- Strong empirical performance: significantly outperforms DynamicBind, DiffDock, and TankBind on ligand structure predictions (e.g., higher % RMSD < 2Å and < 5Å); also achieves 208× faster inference than DynamicBind.
- Robust generalization to unseen proteins; visualizations of the iterative mechanism effectively show ligand docking from apo to holo states.
- One of the first applications of a regression-based model to flexible blind docking; multi-task approach (predicting both ligand and pocket holo structures) is novel in this context.
- Comprehensive benchmarking, including pocket RMSD analyses beyond ligand RMSD; ablation studies and visualizations are detailed.
- Code is openly shared (though README is empty – see Weaknesses).
- At a time when diffusion models dominate, verifies the efficacy of a regression+iterative update approach for structure refinement, achieving high efficiency and strong results.

### Weaknesses
1. **Novelty and relation to FABind** (Majority, from reviewers 2, 3, 4): The work appears as a direct application of the FABind series, adding only a pocket conformation prediction module. The core architecture (FABind layer, EGNN) and training objectives are largely reused, and the iterative update mechanism is a relatively straightforward application of existing iterative refinement techniques. The paper does not provide a deep analysis of why this specific iterative strategy was chosen over alternatives. The conda environment file is named "FABind", raising anonymity concerns regarding double-blind review.

2. **Insufficient detail on FABind layer adaptation** (Reviewer 1): The paper lacks clear explanation of how the FABind layer's message-passing steps are utilized in each of the three modules. Details of node/edge feature initialization and update per module, and whether modifications were made to the original FABind layer, are missing.

3. **Reliance on RMSD metric** (Reviewer 2): The evaluation relies solely on RMSD, which does not ensure biologically or chemically plausible structures. Metrics such as semi-empirical binding affinity, protein-ligand steric clashes, and ligand strain energy should be considered (citing Posebuster, PoseCheck, PoseBench, CompassDock, PLINDER). These were not examined for the processed PDBBind’s apo and holo structures. While RMSD may increase due to holo pocket flexibility, the study emphasizes RMSD rather than more meaningful metrics.

4. **Runtime comparison concerns** (Reviewer 2): It is not specified whether protein preprocessing is included in inference runtime. Based on experience with FABind, preprocessing takes ~10–15 seconds; without it, FABind’s runtime is ~0.12 sec, and FABFlex’s stated 0.49 sec may be inaccurate. When normalized per-ligand, DiffDock (which samples ~40 conformations in ~2 sec per conformation) may be more efficient than FABFlex’s ~10–15 sec per prediction.

5. **Pocket prediction performance** (Reviewers 1, 2, 4): 
   - The model assumes a single binding pocket per protein-ligand pair, limiting applicability to systems with multiple potential binding sites (no discussion of handling multiple pockets).
   - In Table 2, models with a protein rigidity prior (e.g., FABind, FABind+) sometimes outperform FABFlex in pocket prediction metrics. The paper does not explain why—whether the flexible docking objective negatively impacts pocket prediction accuracy or causes overfitting.
   - The paper does not provide an intuitive demonstration of pocket prediction performance (e.g., visual comparison with known pockets).

6. **Missing comparisons and adaptations** (Reviewer 3): The study focuses only on blind (global) flexible docking, whereas it could be adapted to pocket-based (local) flexible docking and compared against models such as DiffDock-Pocket, ReDock, FlexPose, and DiffBindFR.

7. **Timesplit and information leakage** (Reviewer 2): It is unclear if the same timesplitting approach was used for training other DL-based methods in the benchmark, or if they were retrained for comparability. If RMSD is the metric, timesplit information leakage (as described in PLINDER) is a concern; methods for timesplitting should be clarified.

8. **Loss function and hyperparameters** (Reviewers 2, 4): The criteria for selecting the alpha values in the loss function are not clearly explained—it is not stated whether these values are learnable or predetermined. It also reads as if the pocket classification uses binary classification to identify pocket regions, which could limit atomic-level flexibility; a more adaptable approach may be beneficial.

9. **Handling of steric clashes** (Reviewers 1, 2): The paper does not address whether FABFlex’s predicted structures have atomic clashes between protein and ligand, and if so, how this is mitigated or resolved.

10. **Minor issues** (Reviewer 2): The word "subsequent" is used twice in one sentence. The code README is empty, hindering reproducibility.

**Note on reviewer disagreement**: While reviewers 1 and 4 rate the contributions highly (scores 8, confidence 4–5), reviewers 2 and 3 give lower scores (6) due to concerns about novelty, metric choice, and runtime comparability. These contrasting views are preserved in the above strengths and weaknesses.
