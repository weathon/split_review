## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an adversarial detection and purification method that uses anti-aliasing followed by diffusion-based super-resolution (ResShift) without additional training. The goal is to defend against both perturbation-based and unrestricted adversarial attacks. Experiments are conducted on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, comparing with adversarial training and purification methods. One reviewer (R3, rating 5) was notably more positive than the other three (all rating 3). The method is motivated by the observation that adversarial attacks are sensitive to small pixel changes; detection is based on output differences between the original and processed image. The paper also points out that a significant proportion of AutoAttack examples can be deactivated by rounding to integer RGB values, suggesting a flaw in robustness evaluation.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, with clear illustrations (R1, R3).
- Experiments are conducted on large-scale ImageNet (R1).
- The method does not require additional training (R3).
- It defends against both perturbation-based and unrestricted adversarial attacks (R4).
- The proposed method demonstrates higher defensive efficiency compared to previous methods (R4).
- The paper points out that a significant proportion of adversarial images produced by AutoAttack can be deactivated by transforming them to valid integer RGB values, which suggests a potential flaw in existing robustness evaluation protocols (R2).
- The paper considers unrestricted attacks, which are under-studied for adversarial purification methods (R2).
- The method is tested against several adversarial techniques and shows a clear defensive effect; the research content holds practical value and is reproducible (R3).
- One reviewer (R3, rating 5) found the contribution stronger than the others (ratings 3), though this positivity is not shared by the majority.

### Weaknesses
- The anti-aliasing+super-resolution process is conceptually similar to JPEG compression, which has been shown to be an unreliable defense [1]; the improvement (diffusion-based super-resolution) may inherit robustness overestimates from diffusion models [3,4] (R1, confidence 5).
- No adaptive attacks are evaluated, making results unreliable according to [2] (R1). Specifically, AutoAttack and BPDA are used, but (Lee & Kim, 2023) suggest these overestimate robustness of diffusion-based purification; instead, PGD+EOT with exact gradients on the full computation graph (including the proposed AA+SR) should be used. This concern may also apply to the unrestricted attacks (R2, confidence 4).
- The method is presented as detection, yet no adversarial detection methods [5,6] are compared. The evaluation metric (lines 408-413) comparing detection to purification is problematic (R1).
- The visualization of the RGB conversion result in Figure 2 appears inconsistent with the statement that rounding and clipping AutoAttack examples to integers should not produce a significant variation. The implementation of this conversion is not clearly explained (R2, question).
- The implementation of adversarial anti-aliasing (Sec. 4.2.1) is not clearly stated (R2).
- The paper lacks sufficient novelty; the methods (anti-aliasing and super-resolution) are somewhat trivial and resemble a combination of existing approaches, without novel strategies to enhance defensive efficiency (R3, R4).
- Presentation needs improvement: the logical organization between Section 4.2 ("Adversarial Example Detection") and subsections 4.2.3 ("Adversarial Detection") and 4.2.4 ("Adversarial Purification") is disorganized (R4).
- It is unclear whether the combined detection+purification yields clear improved performance over prior works when considering either detection or purification alone (R4).
- The effectiveness of the standalone purification method (without detection) is questionable; the paper does not appear to improve the purification method over previous works (R4, request for clarification).
- Insufficient analysis of differences between the proposed method and existing techniques, especially regarding performance under different attack types (R3).
- The use of generative models requires further analysis of efficiency to enhance comparisons with mainstream defense methods (R3, question).
