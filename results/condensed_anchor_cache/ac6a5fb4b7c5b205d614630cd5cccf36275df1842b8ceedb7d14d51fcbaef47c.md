## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes Compresso, a structured pruning method for LLMs. It incorporates Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) into L0 regularization during instruction tuning and introduces a collaborative pruning prompt to make the LLM an active participant in pruning. Evaluation on LLaMA-7B (pruned to 5.4B, 5B, 4.5B) across zero-shot commonsense reasoning, reading comprehension, few-shot MMLU and BBH shows Compresso outperforms the baseline LLM-Pruner. Three reviewers rate the paper 5 (fair confidence), one rates it 6 (confident), all recommend reject. The more positive reviewer highlights high performance retention and versatility; the others emphasize lack of novelty, insufficient baselines, and unresolved methodological issues.

### Strengths

- Clear writing and logical structure.
- Addresses an important problem: structured pruning of LLMs to reduce deployment cost without specialized hardware.
- Uses multiple techniques (LoRA, differentiable masks, collaborative prompt) to make training-based pruning effective and efficient.
- High retention of original LLaMA-7B performance: up to 96% on commonsense reasoning, outperforms LLaMA-7B by up to 3.91% on reading comprehension, retains up to 87% on MMLU/BBH.
- Evaluation across diverse domains (zero-shot commonsense reasoning, reading comprehension, few-shot MMLU, BBH) makes results more generalizable.
- Consistently outperforms the only included baseline (LLM-Pruner) across all settings and sparsity ratios.
- Method is efficient enough to be trained on 4 Nvidia V100 GPUs (modest hardware for 7B-scale models).
- Ablation study shows dataset choice greatly impacts pruning effectiveness, adding a valuable dimension.
- The collaborative pruning prompt is a unique, innovative approach distinguishing the work from existing pruning methods.

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty and baseline issues**:
  - Lack of novelty: many components (LoRA, differentiable masks) are taken from existing works.
  - Claim that LLM-Pruner is the only structured pruning work is questionable; missing comparisons with other structured LLM pruning works [1-7] (reviewer 1).
  - Only one baseline (LLM-Pruner) in the main experiments, making results less convincing.
  - Baseline (LLM-Pruner) uses Alpaca dataset while Compresso uses GPT4-Alpaca; authors must present baseline performance after applying GPT4-Alpaca to ensure fair comparison, given the strong effect of instruction tuning data quality.

- **Collaborative prompt concerns**:
  - The collaborative prompt is interesting but empirical support is weak and inconsistent (ablation in Table 6 is contradictory; suggest testing Compresso without prompt on both training and inference).
  - Incorporating the pruning prompt during inference substantially increases inference cost, making the comparison with traditional pruned models potentially unfair. Authors must provide overhead analysis (latency, throughput) and ideally test on other sparse patterns (unstructured, N:M) to validate general efficiency.
  - Lack of analysis or discussion of how the prompt works; prevents full understanding.

- **Missing explanations and definitions**:
  - Equation 3: what $d_h$ denotes is not explained; many undefined symbols.
  - Equation 5: what $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$ denote is not explained; no ablation study or details on how to adjust them using AdamW optimizer.
  - Hyperparameters $l=-0.1$, $r=1.1$, $\beta=2$ need references or empirical evidence to support that they are common practice.
  - Section 4 only lists numbers, lacks insights.
  - Performance improvement without post fine-tuning (Table 7) lacks explanation or discussion.
  - Claim "first to apply instruction tuning to weight pruning" lacks sufficient backing.

- **Limitations and analysis gaps**:
  - No discussion of limitations, computational cost of pruning process, or sensitivity to hyperparameters.
  - Ablation shows dataset choice greatly impacts effectiveness, implying potential catastrophic forgetting of capabilities not present in the pruning dataset. The paper does not explore this risk.
  - No detailed analysis of which parts are pruned (layer-by-layer breakdown of attention heads, FFN dimensions). This makes it difficult to understand impact on internal representation and acceleration benefits.
  - Despite being a one-shot method, how iterative application of Compresso might improve performance is not explored.

- **Minor issues**:
  - Typos: e.g., “We introduce LoRA intro pruning in a novel manner.”
  - Theoretical underpinnings of the method would greatly enhance the paper.
