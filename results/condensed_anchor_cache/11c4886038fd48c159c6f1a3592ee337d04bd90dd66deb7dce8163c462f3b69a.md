## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies Evaluation-Oriented Problem-Solving (EOP) to improve problem-solving capabilities of small LLMs (fewer than 10B parameters). It first shows these models perform poorly (often <10% accuracy) and that advanced prompting techniques from large models have limited success. The proposed EOP framework uses multiple trials (breadth-first) and a reasoning evaluator to select correct answers. Experiments on Python Puzzles, Game of 24, and Word Sorting report that EOP significantly improves small LLM performance—2% higher accuracy than GPT-4 on Python Puzzles and 27% improvement over state-of-the-art methods on Game of 24. The paper argues that with appropriate strategies, small LLMs can achieve substantial gains, challenging the notion that scaling model size is the primary improvement path. One reviewer was more positive (score 6) and praised the scientific flow and encouraging results, while the other three reviewers (scores 5) raised several critical concerns.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles an interesting and timely problem: LLMs are powerful but resource-prohibitive, so research to make them accessible via smaller models is important.
- The scientific flow is excellent—first identifying issues with experiments, then proposing the method, with well-written methodology and clearly stated results. Figures help readers understand the idea.
- The writing is clear and easy to understand.
- Instead of increasing model size, the paper designs an evaluation-based approach (EOP) for small LLMs, providing new guidance for improving LLM performance.
- The EOP framework uses multiple trials (breadth-first) instead of multiple rounds (depth-first) and incorporates a reasoning evaluator, effectively enhancing reasoning and synthesis capabilities of small models.
- Experimental results are encouraging: EOP significantly improves performance of small LLMs on Python Puzzles, Game of 24, and Word Sorting. It achieves a 2% higher accuracy on Python Puzzles compared to GPT-4 and a 27% improvement over state-of-the-art prompting methods using GPT-4 in Game of 24. The reasoning evaluator plays an important role.

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation is unclear / not well established.** The paper does not sufficiently explain why small LLMs are preferred over large LLMs, and why the challenges faced by small LLMs are critical. (R3, R4) Why not further improve large models? (R4)
- **Novelty is limited.** Evaluation-oriented approaches are increasingly studied; the first three stated contributions (understanding failure modes, emphasizing evaluators, novel prompting method) are largely consensus. (R1) The paper does not clarify the characteristics of problem-solving tasks and how EOP specifically addresses them, leaving the novelty unclear. (R1)
- **Generality is unclear.** The empirical results are mixed; in some cases the proposed methodology yields only small changes, and the paper does not consider under what circumstances EOP is likely to produce substantially better results. (R2) The problems considered (Python Puzzles, Game of 24, Word Sorting) are relatively easy; it is unclear how EOP would fare on more difficult tasks that users are likely interested in. (R2)
- **Insufficient analysis of results.** The interpretation of experimental results is too brief; a more detailed analysis is needed. (R4)
- **Missing ablation.** The ablation analysis in Section 4.3 does not show the contribution of using multiple trials. This component should be ablated to make the experiment complete. (R4)
- **Applicability depends on evaluator reliability.** The paper states that "with a reliable evaluator, small models can achieve superior accuracy" but also that "the evaluation accuracy is limited by the inherent weaknesses of small LLMs." It is unclear whether small LLMs are capable of evaluating solutions for more difficult tasks with reasonable accuracy; if not, the approach may not work. (R2)
- **Large model comparison missing.** The paper claims to propose a small-LLM-specific evaluation approach but does not conduct experiments with large models under EOP. (R1)
- **Format issue:** Line 279 has a formatting problem; appropriate adjustments are recommended. (R4)
