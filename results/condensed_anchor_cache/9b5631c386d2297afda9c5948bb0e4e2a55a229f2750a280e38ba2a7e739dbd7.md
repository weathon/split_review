## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses the suboptimal policy deviation that occurs when individual rewards are added to sparse team rewards in cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning. It introduces a multi-agent constrained policy optimization procedure (CMT) that maximizes cumulative rewards while enforcing a consistency constraint between the mixed policy (trained with individual and team rewards) and the team policy (based solely on team rewards). The constraint is formulated as a Lagrangian dual problem solved by iterative optimization of two types of policy networks. Experiments in SMAC, MPE, and GRF are presented.

### Strengths
- The consistency constraint is innovative for the targeted problem and is supported by solid theoretical guarantees (R1, R4).
- The paper is well structured with a clear, well-defined motivation (R2, R4).
- The research question – leveraging individual rewards to assist team policy learning – addresses an important aspect of MARL (R3).
- The consistency constraint is intuitively sound and well-motivated (R3).
- Extensive experiments across three benchmarks (SMAC, MPE, GRF) demonstrate the method’s effectiveness (R1, R2, R4). R2 additionally notes the experimental studies are good.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and presentation** (R1, R3): The Introduction and Related Work are overly verbose and lack clarity; including the algorithm in the main paper would improve understanding. Eq. (7) in the main text lacks a definition for \(U\) (defined only in the appendix). The phrasing at line 216–217 (“However, with the help of individual rewards, … even surpasses \(\pi_{E}^{i, *}\)”) is ambiguous – “performance” could refer to \(J_{E+i}\) or \(J_{E}\), and the latter would not justify “surpasses”. Notation \(CD_{KL}\) should be explained upon first use (or replaced by \(D_{KL}\)) (R3).
- **Gap between aim and implementation** (R2): The paper claims that modifying the reward function can alter the optimal policy, but the proposed optimization procedure makes several approximations, and it is not convincing that CMT actually fixes this drawback. There is a lack of theoretical results ensuring the optimization leads to the optimal team policy. Additionally, Assumption 1 appears trivial if \(\pi_{E+i}^{i}\) and \(\pi_{E}^{i}\) share the same policy space (R2). The description of how the two policies merge into one execution policy is unclear (R2: “During policy execution phase … generate actions … sampling trajectories …”).
- **Unclear innovation and insufficient comparison with IRAT** (R3): It is unclear whether the main contribution is the consistency constraint or another aspect. A more detailed comparison with the closely related work IRAT is needed, specifically explaining the differences between Eq.(9) in this paper and Eq.(7) in IRAT. The reasons for CMT’s superior performance over IRAT in the experiments are not discussed; an ablation study to isolate the contributions of the constraint formulation, optimization method, or other factors is missing.
- **Dependence on careful design of individual rewards** (R4): The method’s effectiveness relies on careful selection of individual rewards. The paper does not discuss requirements for individual rewards (e.g., what happens if they are inappropriate, or whether the method can still guarantee policy consistency when the scale of individual rewards is too large relative to team rewards) (R4, Questions 1–2).
- **Inconsistent baseline evaluation** (R3): In SMAC, IRAT, MAPPO, and QMIX are evaluated on 11 tasks, while LAIES and MASER are evaluated on only 5 tasks. A uniform evaluation across all baselines on the same set of tasks is needed to allow fair comparison. (Re: Question 4 from R3: when comparing with LAIES/MASER, does CMT use rule-based individual rewards from LAIES/MASER or its own constructed rewards? This should be clarified.)

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* One reviewer (R4, score 8) is notably more positive, emphasizing the theoretical rigor and extensive experiments, while the other three reviewers (R1, R2, R3, each score 5) raise serious concerns about clarity, theoretical justification, and comparison with prior work. This contrast is preserved above.
