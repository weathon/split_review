## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a targeted adversarial attack (ACE and ACE+) to protect images from unauthorized customization via diffusion model fine-tuning. It demonstrates significant performance gains over untargeted attacks, with solid experiments, a clear method, and an intuitive explanation. One reviewer (R1, rating 6) was more critical, citing missing ablations and purification tests, while three others (R2, R3, R4, rating 8 each) praised the idea, clarity, and thoroughness but raised concerns about technical novelty, missing baselines, and evaluation scope.

### Strengths
- First to propose and validate targeted attacks (over untargeted) for protection against diffusion customization; the discovery is novel and interesting.
- Method (ACE/ACE+) is simple, straightforward, easy to implement, and effective—significantly degrades customization quality.
- Clear writing, logical flow, and well-explained design choices.
- Extensive evaluation covering transferability, robustness, and insight into why targeted attacks work (consistent chaotic patterns mitigate neutralization effect).
- The proposed explanation (score-function errors, neutralization effect) is intuitive and reasonable.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical contribution**: The method relies on existing adversarial attacks (PGD) and techniques from prior works, with minimal novel adaptation (R3, R4).
- **Missing recent attack baselines**: No comparison against SDS, MetaCloak, Influence Watermarks (2024), or “Adversarial Perturbations Cannot Reliably Protect Artists from Generative AI” (R3, R4).
- **Purification experiments inadequate**: Missing purification methods specifically designed for diffusion mimicry, such as IMPRESS and GrIDPure (R1, R4). Reviewer 1 explicitly asks: “Did the authors try some specifically purification methods for the protection? e.g., the method in [1].”
- **Evaluation limited to older models**: Only tested on SD 1.4, 1.5, 2.1; no experiments on newer DiT architectures or SD3 (though SD3 lacks open-source customization pipelines – this limitation should be discussed, R3, R4).
- **Potential bias in evaluation images**: It is unclear whether the selected images were part of the original SD training sets; verification was not conducted (R3).
- **Missing ablation for ACE+**: Reviewer 1 requests a comparison between the added loss term alone in ACE+ and the proposed ACE loss, as the added term is also a targeted attack.
- **Concern about superiority claim for single-reference settings**: The hypothesis that targeted attacks avoid neutralization from multiple samples may not hold when only one reference image is used (e.g., some personalization methods). How does this explain empirical success in such settings? (R4)
- **Resemblance to prior work**: The approach is similar to Glaze (targeted style transfer) – the core technical difference needs clarification (R2).
- **Attack ratio impact**: Reviewer 2 asks what happens if only a proportion of images are attacked; this practical setting is not explored.
- **Inaccuracies in text**: In L144, the claim that “all existing protections are untargeted attacks” is false – Glaze and ASPL are targeted. In L500, the description of \(\theta'\) as the customized diffusion model appears wrong (R3, R4).
- **Algorithm details unclear**: In Algorithm 1, lines 5-7, the diffusion model parameters are optimized, but this is not explained in Eq. (4)-(5) (R4).
- **Baseline budgets not specified**: In Table 1, the perturbation budgets for baselines are not given (e.g., are they 4/255 or aligned with their own settings?) (R4).
