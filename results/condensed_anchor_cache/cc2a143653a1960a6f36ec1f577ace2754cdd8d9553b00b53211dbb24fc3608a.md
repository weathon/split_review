## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper explores whether LLMs can understand images by converting images into Scalable Vector Graphics (SVG) – an XML-based textual description. The LLM is tested on three vision tasks: visual reasoning, image classification under distribution shift, and generating/editing images via visual prompting. Results indicate that LLMs can perform decently on these tasks compared to some expert models.

### Strengths
1. **Innovative use of SVG as a bridge** – Converting images into structured XML codes is a creative approach to leverage LLMs’ text processing capabilities for visual tasks (R2, R3). The paper evaluates both discriminative and generative visual understanding tasks, drawing interesting and potentially inspiring conclusions (R3).
2. **Comprehensive evaluation** – The paper conducts both qualitative and quantitative evaluations over a variety of tasks, providing deep insights into LLMs’ image understanding abilities (R1, R2). The breadth and depth of the experimental design are commendable (R1).
3. **Robustness to distribution shifts** – A significant strength is the demonstrated robustness of the LLM+SVG approach under distributional shifts on visual data (R1, R2).
4. **Generative capabilities** – The demonstration of LLMs’ ability to generate and edit images based on chat-based feedback is particularly promising and marks a significant advancement (R2).
5. **Clarity and coverage of related works** – The paper is well-articulated and thoroughly situates its work within the existing literature (R1).
- *Note:* Two reviewers (R2, R3) found the overall contribution more positive (score 6), while one reviewer (R1) was more critical on novelty and experimental rigor (score 5).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty of components** – Individual components (SVG, LLMs, dataset choices, tasks, metrics) appear repurposed from existing literature without significant innovation or new application. A stronger contribution would require at least one novel element or a unique combination (R1, *especially noted by the more critical reviewer R1*).
2. **Insufficient ablation and comparison for performance source** – The performance gap between LLM+SVG and CNN+PNG lacks clear explanation. Differences in input representation, model architecture, and model size (e.g., GPT-4 vs. smaller CNNs) are confounding variables. An ablation study isolating the impact of SVG representation is missing. Need comparison of SVG with alternative representations using the *same underlying model* (e.g., GPT-4) (R1). Comparison of LLMs’ performance using SVGs with traditional vision-based models would provide more context (R2).
3. **Evaluation data and tasks are too simple** – The chosen datasets and tasks may inherently favor SVG due to their structured nature (e.g., simple shapes, clear geometric forms) and do not reflect complex real-world scenarios. The method may not generalize to complicated or photographic content (R1, R3). Broader tasks with varying complexity and realism are needed (R1). Also, SVG is known to be ineffective for photographic content (R3).
4. **Incomplete evaluation of multimodal LLMs** – For the comparison with multimodal LLMs, the paper only tests LLaVa and concludes about their limitations. This is not rigorous; more multimodal LLMs should be evaluated, such as BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, MiniGPT-4, mPLUG-Owl (R3).
5. **Unaddressed grounding issue** – The introduction’s question about whether LLMs can learn world models without grounding in physical interaction and visual perception remains unaddressed (R2).
6. **Missing analysis of SVG properties** – How the format, sequence, or other characteristics of SVG textual descriptions affect task performance is not explored. Also, the potential impact of minor errors in SVG representation (e.g., noise or inaccuracies) is not studied (R2, questions turned into weaknesses).
7. **Methodological limitations** – The analysis relies entirely on existing methodologies; no novel input representations, model adaptations, or new evaluation benchmarks are introduced (R1).
