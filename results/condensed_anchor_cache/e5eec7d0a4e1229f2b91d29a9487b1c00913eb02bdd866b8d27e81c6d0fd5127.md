## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper frames training data synthesis as a distribution-matching problem and proposes three improvements to Stable Diffusion: an MMD-based feature alignment loss, conditioning on visual guidance (CLIP intra-class mean features plus image captions and class names), and initializing latent codes from a VAE prior. Experiments on image classification, OOD generalization, and privacy preservation show improved performance. Reviewers find the work clear and the evaluation thorough, but express mixed opinions on novelty, significance of the MMD contribution, and the amount of gain attributable to the VAE prior versus the other components.

### Strengths
- **Clarity.** Writing, figures, and tables are exceptionally clear; the framework is sound and easy to understand.
- **Theoretical perspective.** The distribution-matching formalization (marginal data distribution and conditional label distribution) is principled and well-motivated.
- **Thorough evaluation.** Extensive experiments on diverse image classification benchmarks, including in-distribution, out-of-distribution, and privacy tasks. Comparisons to state-of-the-art methods show large absolute gains (e.g., 10%, 17%, 30% on different datasets).
- **Well-motivated innovations.** The three proposed strategies (MMD alignment, visual guidance, VAE prior) are grounded in the theoretical framework.
- **Practical relevance.** The study problem (using synthetic data to replace or augment real data, especially with privacy concerns) is interesting and useful.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns.** One reviewer notes that MMD for learning data distributions is not new, and the derived MMD loss (Eq. (7)) is identical to the simplified diffusion loss (Ho et al., 2020), thus offering nothing new for the diffusion model. The same reviewer argues that the main performance gains come from the latent prior, which is already explored in prior work (Section 3.3), making the overall contribution marginal. In contrast, another reviewer considers the three innovations well-motivated and highly effective.
- **Limited improvement over Imagen.** Comparisons with Imagen are only reported on ImageNet-1K, leaving it unclear whether the proposed method outperforms Imagen on other datasets.
- **Unclear source of prompt improvements.** The prompt includes both an image caption and intra-class visual guidance. It is not ablated whether the gains come from the caption alone rather than the visual guidance. Also, are other baselines using the same image caption?
- **Effectiveness of MMD loss.** From Table 2, the gain from adding the MMD loss compared to finetuning only is not significant, raising doubt about its practical impact.
- **Missing experimental details.** 
  - Only a single run is reported; repeats are needed to rule out confounders.
  - Several cells in Table 1 are empty (question: why?).
  - The definition of “Finetune only” and “Latent prior only” in Table 2 is not clearly stated (e.g., is “Finetune only” finetuning Stable Diffusion with the original loss? Is “Latent prior only” using the proposed prior with the pretrained model?).
  - The variable \(\epsilon_0\) in Eq. (7) is not defined.
- **Limited task diversity.** Experiments focus on standard image classification; more complex tasks (e.g., human face generation) are not considered.
- **Minor presentation issue.** Figure 3 has text overlapping with curves.
