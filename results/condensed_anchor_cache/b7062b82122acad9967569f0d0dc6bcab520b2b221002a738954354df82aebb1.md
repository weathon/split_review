## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SPIDER, an adaptive label smoothing method that correlates confidence (via a monotonically decreasing function, typically exponential decay) with the intensity of isotropic input perturbations. The goal is to improve model robustness to common corruptions and adversarial attacks. Two theoretical results are presented: one showing that SPIDER increases boundary thickness for any monotonically decreasing smoothing function, and another (for a linear classifier with softmax) linking input perturbations to flat minima. Experiments on CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet, and ImageNet compare SPIDER applied on top of no augmentation, AugMix, DeepAug, and PixMix. Reviewers find the problem important and the method reasonable, but raise concerns about insufficient baselines, missing comparisons, limited theoretical link to the design choice, and presentation issues. Scores are 6, 5, 5, 6 (confidence 3, 4, 3, 3 respectively).

### Strengths
- The paper is well written and easy to follow (R1).
- Two new techniques are proposed: a perturbation function for augmentation sampling and a label smoothing function; these are supported by findings that smoothed labels increase boundary thickness and that input perturbations relate to parameter perturbations, empirically leading to flatter minima (R1).
- The theoretical contribution is non-trivial; the theorems help understand the effects of label smoothing on boundary thickness and of input perturbations on flatness (R2). Detailed theoretical proofs are provided and validated with sufficient empirical results (R3).
- The method is concise and effective; it enhances common corruption and adversarial robustness without hurting clean accuracy (R3).
- The studied problem is important and the proposed method is reasonable; both empirical and theoretical analyses are conducted, and ablation studies assess component contributions (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing domain shift experiments**: The manuscript mentions domain shift, and §3.3.1 claims flat minima improve domain shift robustness, but no domain shift experiments are performed (R1).
- **No comparison with adversarial learning algorithms** despite claiming adversarial robustness improvements (R1).
- **Unfair baseline comparison**: Main baselines (no aug, AugMix, DeepAug, PixMix) are image-based and do not use label augmentation. Because SPIDER leverages labels, a fairer baseline would apply the **same input perturbations but with constant label smoothing** (equivalent to vanilla label smoothing). Such comparisons are missing from Tables 1–5 and Figure 2 (R1, R3, R4). Ablation Table 6 does not show universal improvements under each component (R1).
- **Incomplete presentation and missing details**: Many crucial details are absent from the main text (e.g., definition of $c$ appears only in Appendix E.1.1, architecture and optimization hyperparameters are not given). There are no pointers to the appendix, making the main text very difficult to understand (R2). A typo in Theorem 4’s subscript is noted (R2).
- **Theorems do not justify the design choice of δ and s**: The theoretical results hold for any monotonically decreasing $s$ (or even constant $s$, i.e., vanilla label smoothing) and are invariant to the exponential decay used in practice. This weakens the justification for the adaptive smoothing scheme; an extensive ablation study on $\delta$ and $s$ is needed (R2). The second theorem is orthogonal to label smoothing and relies on a linear classifier (R2).
- **Comparison with other regularization methods is lacking**: Yang et al. showed that L1/L2, large learning rate, early stopping, and cutout also increase boundary thickness. The paper does not compare SPIDER to these or to other methods that improve flatness/boundary thickness (e.g., noisy mixup, HAT). Similarly, comparisons with data augmentation methods like Mixup, CutMix, and ANT are missing (R2, R3).
- **Synergy with strong augmentations unclear**: SPIDER always requires input perturbations. It is unclear whether it can be combined with recent SOTA augmentations like RandAug, Mixup, or CutMix (R2).
- **Weak backbones and few baselines**: Experiments rely on a single backbone; “ResNet strikes back” shows that optimization alone can boost ResNet-50 by ~+4%. Generalizability across architectures is not demonstrated (R2).
- **Boundary thickness vs. robustness link questioned**: Table 6 shows that standard label smoothing (a specific case of a monotonically decreasing function) actually **worsens mCE** compared to the baseline, raising doubt about whether increased boundary thickness necessarily translates to common corruption robustness (R3).
- **Flatness improvement is modest**: SPIDER improves flatness over “No LS” but AugMix achieves flatter minima (Figure 2) (R3).
- **Title is misleading**: “Rethinking” suggests new insights into label smoothing’s mechanism, but the paper mainly adapts label smoothing for augmentation; a change is suggested (R4).
- **Missing related work on label distribution/uncertainty**: Connections to ensemble methods, knowledge distillation, and label noise in adversarial training are not discussed (R4).
