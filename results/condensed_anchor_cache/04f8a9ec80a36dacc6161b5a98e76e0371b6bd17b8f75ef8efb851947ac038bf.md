## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes V-TIFA, which uses off-the-shelf VLMs to score entire trajectories for training instruction-following agents via IQL (offline RL adapted for online use). The VLM is prompted twice: first to produce a textual summary of a timestep-annotated trajectory, then to assign a numeric reward based on that summary. Experiments on the ALFRED simulator show V-TIFA outperforms baseline reward generation methods (CLIP, R3M) that use only first/last frames.

### Strengths
- Simplicity: The idea of directly prompting VLMs to assign rewards based on entire trajectories is straightforward and sensible, leveraging VLMs' long-context capability and video/multi-image pre-training.
- Novelty of application: Using a VLM as a reward model for RL in embodied navigation/navigation tasks is a novel contribution, particularly for language-conditioned tasks in household simulation.
- Prompting strategy: The two-stage query (summary then score) and the use of timestep indices on images are interesting and effective, playing to VLM strengths.
- Comprehensive experiments in ALFRED: The paper provides thorough experiments and analysis, including comparisons of different VLMs and an ablation on comparative feedback. Figures 4 and 5 are compelling, showing precision of VLMs and performance comparisons.
- Well-written and clear concept: The paper is generally well-organized and the core idea is easy to grasp.

### Weaknesses
- **Unfair baseline comparisons**: V-TIFA processes the entire trajectory, while baselines (CLIP, R3M) only receive first and last frames. This is unfair and simplistic. Fairer baselines would use dense rewards computed over the full trajectory (e.g., average CLIP score over subtrajectories) or select the highest score. [Question: How do baselines perform when using dense rewards?]
- **Limited experimental scope**: Experiments are conducted only in ALFRED, a single environment. ALFRED is photo-synthetic, short-horizon (max 50 steps), single-room, and includes tasks with well-articulated pre/post conditions and subgoal segmentation. This does not demonstrate generality to more complex, long-horizon, multi-room, or less curated environments. Results on real robots or more complex settings (e.g., Minecraft) would be more convincing. [Reviewer 1: Lack of real-robot results; Reviewer 3: Need long-horizon tasks and multiple environments.]
- **Limited originality**: There is significant prior work using VLMs as reward functions for RL (e.g., MineDojo, Fan et al., 2022) and using VLMs for success detection. The core idea is not entirely new. The paper does not adequately compare to or differentiate from related work such as RLAIF (Lee et al., 2023, https://openreview.net/pdf?id=uydQ2W41KO) and other reward generation methods (e.g., papers listed in Reviewer 3). The abstract's motivation about "burden on environment designers" is misleading; sparse rewards are natural and realistic, and intrinsic rewards are the designer's responsibility.
- **Sparse reward inefficiency**: V-TIFA provides only a sparse reward at the end of the trajectory, which is inherently inefficient for RL due to exploration challenges. The method does not outperform the environment's own sparse reward baseline, limiting its practical value. Prior work (MineDojo) uses dense rewards from VLMs to improve sample efficiency, but V-TIFA does not attempt this. [Question: Have the authors considered using VLMs for dense rewards?]
- **Lack of discussion on VLM failure modes**: The paper does not address scenarios where off-the-shelf VLMs cannot reliably assign correct rewards (e.g., high task complexity). If VLM performance is low, additional human effort (SFT/RL finetuning on task-specific data) would be required, contradicting the claim of eliminating human effort. [Question: How can the proposed approach handle scenarios beyond current VLM capabilities?]
- **Paper clarity issues**: The discussion of RLHF is tangential and distracting, as V-TIFA's method is more analogous to robotics reward-learning papers (Cui et al., Du et al., etc.) than RLHF. The use of IQL (initially offline RL but adapted for online use) requires clarification; readers may assume offline RL usage. The ablation on comparative feedback is unpromising and feels disappointing given the RLHF focus. Visualizations of rewards/trajectories to understand VLM mistakes would be helpful but are missing. [Question: Can authors clarify language-assisted RL in line 33?]
- **Insufficient baselines and comparisons**: The evaluation only compares embedding-based methods (CLIP, R3M) and does not include comparisons with prior work that uses large-capacity models for reward generation (e.g., RLAIF, VLM-as-reward methods). The paper should define fair baselines that use similar VLM strategies.
- **Generalization concerns**: The paper does not specify whether starting positions are randomized in ALFRED. If fixed, the agent may memorize static object locations, limiting generalization. [Question: Is there a randomized starting position for each scene?]
- **Integration with training loop**: Existing works directly incorporate VLMs into the training loop for finetuning (e.g., Yang et al., Adeniji et al.). The paper's claim that "existing works do not incorporate VLMs into the training loop" (line 243) is inaccurate.
