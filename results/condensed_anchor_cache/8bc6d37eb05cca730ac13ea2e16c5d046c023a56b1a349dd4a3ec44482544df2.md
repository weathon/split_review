## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes using Dantzig-Wolfe decomposition and column generation for 3D bin packing, with a deep RL agent solving the subproblem (3D knapsack) and CPLEX solving the master problem (set partition). The authors claim improved solutions on up to 28/47 instances compared to successive RL, and improved lower bounds on 17/47 instances via replacing the RL agent with IP. Reviewers rate the paper 3 (Reject) overall, citing limited novelty, missing details, and insufficient comparisons, though acknowledging the novelty of combining RL with decomposition.

### Strengths
- The combination of deep reinforcement learning with Dantzig-Wolfe decomposition is novel. (R1)
- The authors obtained novel lower bounds for some instances of the 3D bin packing problem. (R1) Specifically, improved lower bounds compared to SOTA were found on 17/47 instances by using CPLEX to solve both master and sub-problems. (Abstract)
- Promising results are obtained on a dataset of 47 instances: the algorithm provided improved solutions on up to 28/47 instances compared to successively applying the RL agent. (Abstract, R1)
- Multidimensional bin packing has not received enough attention and capturing complex side constraints is an important practical aspect. (R3)
- The integration of RL with traditional mathematical programming methods seems feasible and yields promising results; the use of column generation improved the lower bound of the original solution, offering a tighter lower bound. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: Although the combination is novel, the novelty in the machine learning part seems limited. (R1) The methods of Dantzig-Wolfe reformulation and column generation are existing approaches; the authors did not contribute significantly by improving these methods or specifically addressing the bin packing problem. (R2) For decades, standard bin packing has been formulated using DW and column generation; the pricing problem can be solved as a simple IP or by RL (knapsack is sometimes solved as RL). Applying RL in pricing in this specific context does not yield novel algorithmic contributions. (R3)
- **Missing details in presentation**: The paper lacks important information: what are "normal patterns"?; the architecture of the deep RL agent; the training methodology of the RL agent; the sequential RL agent should be explained more explicitly. (R1) Section 2.1, the core of the authors' method, is hard to comprehend; it needs illustrations and motivation. (R2)
- **Formatting and writing issues**: a2c → A2C; Bonnet et al. (2023) → (Bonnet et al., 2023); "Zhang et al. (2021) is limited" → "The method proposed by Zhang et al. (2021) is limited"; "Fang J (2023)" → "Fang and Rao (2023)"; "Deidson Vitorio Kurpel" should be fixed; (1-5) and (6-10) should be better indented; "Jumanji bin pack" → "Jumanji binpack"; "Where v_i represents" should be part of the previous sentence; what is "liquid volume"? (R1)
- **Limited comparisons and outdated dataset**: The author's comparison with baseline methods is limited. They used a dataset introduced in 1988, which is quite outdated. They should consider recent methods like 'Attend2pack: Bin packing through deep reinforcement learning with attention' and new datasets (BED-BPP: Benchmarking dataset for robotic bin packing problems, Learning Efficient Online 3D Bin Packing on Packing Configuration Trees) to compare in terms of time cost and performance. (R2) The paper lacks a rigorous comparison to existing state-of-the-art column generation methods for 3D bin packing, necessary to demonstrate practical advantages. (R3)
- **Local optimality and guarantees**: There is an issue of local optimal solutions when employing RL to solve subproblems; it is unclear how the authors can ensure that RL will inevitably find a solution. (R2) The paper fails to address the well-known issue of lack of guarantees on solution quality when using RL in pricing, a significant limitation for practical applications where optimality is important. (R3)
- **Computational challenges and stabilization**: The paper does not adequately address the computational challenges of solving 3D bin packing with column generation. While IP solutions for the 3D pricing problem are slow, the authors do not provide a detailed analysis of computational bottlenecks or how RL specifically overcomes these beyond heuristic specificity. (R3) Column generation has known stability issues; it is unclear why stabilization techniques were not tried. (R3)
- **Missing benchmark: branch-and-price**: Branch-and-price (branch-and-bound with column generation) is a well-established and often most effective algorithm for such problems; why was this methodology not used as a benchmark? (R3)
- **RL training questions**: What architecture did you use for your RL agent? How did you train it? Which RL algorithm did you use? (R1)
- **Solver choice**: Why use CPLEX instead of Gurobi (which is now considered more efficient and both have free licenses for academia)? (R3)
- **Missing aggregated results in Table 1**: Could include aggregated results for other SOTA methods to easier understand how the proposed methodology performs. (R1)
- **Claim about existing RL solutions**: The claim that existing RL solutions have not been tested on multiple bin problems is not sufficiently justified; there are many ways to extend RL to handle multiple bins, and the paper does not demonstrate that these extensions are not viable or that the proposed approach is superior. (R3)
