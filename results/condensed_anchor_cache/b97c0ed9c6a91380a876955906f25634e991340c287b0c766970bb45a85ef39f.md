## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes using level-set parameters (weights of deep SDF networks) as a continuous data representation for 3D shape analysis, with a hypernetwork that generates pose-dependent parameters for tasks like classification, retrieval, and 6D pose estimation. Reviewers find the core idea interesting, but raise concerns about clarity, motivation, novelty, experimental fairness, and practical overhead. One reviewer (Rating 3) is substantially more critical on these points, while the other three (Ratings 5,5,5) are more positive but still identify significant weaknesses.

### Strengths
- The idea of considering neural network parameters (level-set parameters) as a data representation for 3D shape analysis is novel and interesting (R1, R3).
- The paper covers a comprehensive landscape of prior works (R2).
- Code is released, aiding reproducibility (R2).
- The hypernetwork constraints are well-designed and important; without them, correlations across shapes could disappear in high-dimensional space (R3).
- The encoder design for handling the high-dimensional parameter modality is significant (R3).
- The technique is sound, experiments demonstrate solidness, and many design/parameter details are shared (R4).
- The paper is well-written (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and Justification**: The motivation for using SDF-based level-set parameters is vague; the experimental tasks mostly belong to point-cloud processing, and the advantage of the SDF formulation is not justified (R1). The novelty relative to existing pose-dependent generative networks (for objects, human faces/bodies) is unclear; a survey of these works and a clear statement of novelty are needed (R4). Claiming a new data modality is too ambitious; broader shape analysis and more groundbreaking improvements would be expected (R3).
- **Clarity and Exposition**: Section 3.1 has poor exposition – no motivation for the hypernetwork formulation, why matrix-form entry is necessary, or how it better encodes rigid transforms (R1). Section 4.1 throws architecture details without reasoning; it is hard to see how the network leads to semantic understanding (R1). Section 3.2 and 4.1 need a major rewrite: the rationale behind the two-stage approach for parameter decomposition, the zero initialization of trainable parameters (line 236), the intuition behind Equation (6), the hard-coded concatenation of first-layer SDF weights/biases, and the selection of only layers 2 and 7 are not explained (R2, R3). The example in Line 40 is unclear (R1). The phrase “pseudo-normal distribution” in Line 50 is not defined (R1). The claim in lines 69–71 lacks support or reference (R3).
- **Experimental Fairness and Completeness**: The fairness of results in Table 4 is questionable: Algorithm 2 uniformly samples rotation space and chooses best initializations; it is unclear if baselines were given equivalent treatment (R1). In Table 3, the reported VN-DGCNN result is much lower than the original paper [Deng et al. 2021]; also, why do non-pose-equivariant methods (PointNet, DGCNN) achieve such high scores under SO(3)/SO(3) (R1)? Table 2 only includes older supervised methods (PointNet, PointNet++); more recent works should be compared (R3). Table 2 should report the number of parameters per model for fair comparison (R3). PaRI-Conv is highly competitive and even outperforms the proposed method in some categories of Tables 2 and 3 (R3). Improvements in classification and retrieval are not significant (R3). Evaluation is only on simple datasets (ModelNet40, ShapeNet); evaluation on more modern, large-scale datasets (e.g., Objaverse) is needed to assess robustness and scalability, especially given the modest gains in Tables 2 and 4 (R2).
- **Computational and Practical Overhead**: Running time is not fully reported; the 50 seconds mentioned for registration is clearly slow (R1). The method requires constructing level-set parameters for each shape before analysis, adding a preprocessing step (R3). The pipeline complicates tasks: for classification, one must first train a Hypernetwork and SDF, then design a specialized encoder for large-dimensional parameters, reducing flexibility. Adding new shapes may require retraining the Hypernetwork and SDF, which is time-consuming (R3). The statement about fast convergence during classification is misleading because dataset construction time is not counted (R3). No storage space or VRAM usage reported for the level-set parameters, despite the large dimensionality (R3, questions). Figure 3(b) shows some points from different categories mixed, suggesting potential noise in the constructed dataset that could misguide classification (R3).
- **Limitations in Generality and Scalability**: As a decoder-only network, there is no mechanism to handle novel unseen shapes, which is a major limitation for application value (R4). With more shapes, can the network truly separate pose and shape variation? Even if enforced during training, it is unclear whether this property generalizes at inference, especially at scale (R4). Changing the architecture of the SDF network may harm or improve performance, but this is unexplored (R3, question).
