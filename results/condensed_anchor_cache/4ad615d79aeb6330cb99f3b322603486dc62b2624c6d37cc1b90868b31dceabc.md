## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Conservative Density Estimation (CDE), an offline RL algorithm that builds on DICE by adding a pessimism constraint on the state-action stationary distribution, limiting it to be within a multiplicative factor of an OOD action distribution mu. The method aims to combine strengths of value-penalization (e.g., CQL) and DICE-based approaches, especially for sparse-reward and scarce-data settings. CDE is formulated as a constrained optimization problem, yields theoretical bounds on the importance ratio and performance gap, and supports policy extraction from the learned distribution. Experiments on D4RL tasks (maze2d, sparse MuJoCo, scarce-data) show that CDE outperforms several baselines. Reviewers rate the paper 8, 6, 5, 8; the majority is positive but two reviewers have notable concerns about presentation, missing benchmarks, and theoretical details.

### Strengths
- The combination of pessimistic regularization with DICE-style stationary distribution optimization is neat and well-motivated, offering a principled way to handle the concentrability assumption and OOD issues in offline RL.
- CDE determines the balance of pessimism on theoretical grounds rather than requiring a penalty-coefficient hyperparameter (unlike many pessimistic methods).
- Theoretical results provide bounds on the importance ratio and performance gap, and the mathematical formulation is sound overall.
- The paper is well-written and clearly describes the method from policy evaluation to policy extraction.
- The new procedure to convert dense-reward MuJoCo tasks into sparse-reward settings (by cutting off low-return trajectories) and the scarce-data experiments provide useful benchmarks for testing robustness.
- CDE significantly outperforms baselines on maze2d tasks and shows strong results on sparse MuJoCo and small-data regimes, supporting its effectiveness in challenging settings.
- The motivation (combining best of both worlds from value-penalization and DICE methods) is solid.

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and clarity gaps:**
  - The precise differences from DICE are not made sufficiently clear; more high-level discussion of the effect of the added OOD constraint would help (Reviewer 1).
  - The paper does not explore the impact of the policy update timing (updating only after value function convergence) – is this a key component or just a practical trick? (Reviewer 2).
  - The introduction claims “pessimism-based techniques may be prone to over-pessimism in high-dimensional state-action spaces” but does not justify the connection between curse of dimensionality and increased over-pessimism (Reviewer 3).
  - The statement that “regularization methods often struggle with tuning of the regularization coefficient” is unsupported; IQL, for example, uses the same hyperparameters across domain experiments, like CDE (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons and tasks:**
  - No comparison on D4RL Gym *-medium-replay datasets, which contain mixed-quality trajectories (Reviewer 3).
  - Important sparse-reward tasks are missing: Antmaze and Kitchen (harder than maze2d) (Reviewer 3).
  - On Adroit, the paper includes *-human datasets but not *-cloned datasets; why? (Reviewer 3).
  - Missing baselines: DecisionTransformer and Diffuser, which also claim strength in sparse reward settings (Reviewer 3).
  - The comparison between CDE and CQL is insufficient: Figure 2(d) likely shows CQL with a fixed pessimism coefficient, but a full sensitivity analysis of CQL’s penalty coefficient is needed, along with theoretical discussion of how the two forms of pessimism (in the stationary distribution vs. value function) affect learning dynamics and Bellman optimality (Reviewer 2).
- **Hyperparameter selection:**
  - While the ablation on the mixture coefficient is informative, the ablation on the max OOD IS ratio does not provide clear guidelines on how to choose it across different environments (Reviewer 2).
- **Theoretical concerns:**
  - §3.2.1 claims strong duality holds via Slater’s condition, but it is not trivial that the empirical dataset distribution d^D satisfies the Bellman flow constraint under stochastic transitions (discrete samples vs. continuous transition function) – Slater’s condition may fail (Reviewer 4).
  - The right-hand side of Eq. (7) may not exist (e.g., if \tilde{A}(s,a) is negative) (Reviewer 4).
  - The setting of d^*(s) as a uniform distribution over states in successful trajectories (only described in §B.2.2) is questionable; it is unclear this distribution will be close to \tilde{w}^*(s,a)\hat{d}^D(s,a) (Reviewer 4).
  - Some theoretical contributions (theorems) could have been moved to the appendix entirely to free space for clearer explanation of the algorithm (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing experimental details:**
  - How does performance change if the cut-off percentile (currently 75) for creating sparse MuJoCo is varied? (Reviewer 3).
  - How does CDE perform compared to baselines on dense reward settings? (Reviewer 3).
  - How are OOD actions sampled for the distribution mu? (Reviewer 4).
  - Does Slater’s condition hold for the infinite-dimensional (function) optimization problem? (Reviewer 4).
