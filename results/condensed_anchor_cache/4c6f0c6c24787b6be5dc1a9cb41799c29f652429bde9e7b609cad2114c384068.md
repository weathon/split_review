## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes SOREL, a stochastic gradient-based algorithm for spectral risk minimization. The authors reduce the problem to a min-max game via the rearrangement inequality, then apply a regularized primal-dual method with variance reduction. They claim a near-optimal convergence rate and present experiments on linear models showing competitive performance against baselines. All reviewers rate the paper 6 but raise serious concerns about correctness and clarity, with one reviewer stating the convergence analysis is incorrect and the paper is unpublishable in its current state.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an important problem (spectral risk minimization) with applications in risk sensitivity, fairness, and OOD generalization. The idea of reducing to a minimax game and applying a primal-dual algorithm (based on Lemma 1 of Blondel et al. 2020) is clever and elegant.
- It correctly points out that for “smoothed” spectral risk measures, the smoothing parameter \(\nu = O(\epsilon)\) must be included in the complexity guarantee, making “linearly convergent” methods sublinear for the original non-smooth problem. (Reviewer 3)
- The exposition is clear and well-written (Sections 1, 2, 3.1 are especially praised) – though some sections are nearly identical to prior work (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- Using the experimental benchmark of Mehta et al. (2024) allows direct head-to-head comparisons; the empirical performance is competitive with and often outperforms alternatives on ridge regression tasks with several spectral risk variants and datasets. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- The mini-batching version of SOREL is a nice practical addition. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Critical dimensional/log-unit error in theory:** Corollary 1 contains \(\log(\frac{\sqrt{n}G}{\mu^2 \sqrt{\epsilon}})\), which takes the logarithm of a quantity that is not unitless. This fails a basic sanity check: under rescaling of parameters, the iteration count would change inconsistently (e.g., \(w \mapsto w/10\) gives \(G \mapsto 10G, \mu \mapsto 100\mu\), altering the argument by a factor of 1/1000). The error originates from Lemma 3: the precision parameter \(\delta_k\) (units of loss) is incorrectly related to the strong convexity parameter \(\mu\) (units of loss/input²). Both Reviewers 1 and 3 independently identify this issue and state that the analysis is therefore incorrect. Reviewer 3 explicitly says the paper is “unpublishable in its current state”. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Unclear role of regularization vs. original spectral risk:** The paper claims (l.78) to provide convergence guarantees for the original spectral risk, but Section 3.1 immediately introduces a regularized version with a strongly convex function (Eq. 1) and an additional regularizer on \(\lambda\) (Eq. 4). This suggests the algorithm does not actually address the original unregularized spectral risk. Reviewers ask for clarification. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Missing definition and clarification of \(w^*\):** Theorem 1 uses \(w^*\) without definition. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **No discussion of how \(\mu\) interacts with desired accuracy \(\epsilon\):** It is unclear if the algorithm is robust to the choice of \(\mu\) and how to tune it in practice. The bound in Corollary 1 appears to scale as \(\epsilon^{-3/2}\) when accounting for the regularization error, which would be worse than alternatives. The authors need to clarify whether the analysis covers the unregularized objective. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of explicit time/computational complexity analysis:** Algorithm 1 has steps (lines 2, 7, 11) that require \(O(n)\) time per epoch. The authors claim these are for variance reduction but do not investigate their necessity. The overall runtime dependence on both \(n\) and \(\epsilon\) is not stated; the paper focuses on iteration complexity but neglects per-iteration cost. The \(O(n \log n)\) operation on line 5 is unclearly placed (within the inner loop? total?). Reviewers request a full complexity comparison with prior methods. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Experiments limited to linear models (ridge regression):** No nonlinear models (e.g., small neural nets) are tested, even though the theory assumes linear models (Assumption 1). Moreover, the experiments remove regularization, departing from the theoretical setting. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **No error bars or standard errors reported:** It is unclear whether results come from a single or multiple independent runs; significance of improvements cannot be assessed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Lemma 1 is stated in the main text without a proof sketch or explanation of its role.** (Reviewer 4)
- **Minor disagreement:** Most reviewers are moderately positive about the paper’s potential, but Reviewer 3 considers the theoretical error fatal and rates soundness 1, while others rate soundness 3. (All reviewers)
