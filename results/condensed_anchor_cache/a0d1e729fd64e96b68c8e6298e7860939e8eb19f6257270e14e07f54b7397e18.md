## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SynBench, a task-agnostic framework for evaluating pretrained representations using synthetic data (binary classification from class-conditional Gaussian mixtures). It measures accuracy and adversarial robustness on this proxy task and defines a SynBench-score as the ratio of area-under-curve between representation-space and input-space performance. Experiments on various vision models claim strong correlation with actual linear probing performance on downstream tasks. The paper also explores applications in robust linear probing.

### Strengths
- The idea of designing a synthetic proxy task for representation quality evaluation is interesting and offers a fresh perspective independent of real downstream datasets. (R1, R4)
- Experiments show that SynBench-Score correlates well with real-life task accuracy; the correlation is high even with limited samples. (R1, R2)
- The paper is thorough in methodology, experimental design, and evaluation, including runtime analysis, limitations, and algorithm details. (R1, R2)
- The method avoids privacy and bias issues because it uses no real data. (R3)
- The goal is ambitious; if successful, the framework would be very impactful. (R4)
- The paper addresses both robustness and transferability, which are active research areas. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Gaussian assumption on raw pixel space is unrealistic.** The paper assumes raw input data (pixel space) for each class follows a Gaussian distribution. In computer vision, this is akin to assuming a very good representation already, which undermines the purpose of representation learning. (R4)
- **Pearson correlation claim is based on extremely small sample sizes (n=5).** Table 7 shows correlations >0.9 computed from only 5 data points. Standard confidence intervals are very wide (e.g., for r=0.92, CI ≈[-0.81,1.0]), making the claim statistically unsupported. This is a potentially misleading error. (R4)
- **Hyperparameter selection is not reported.** Many hyperparameters (e.g., \(a_t\) ranging 0.7–0.9, \(\epsilon\) from 0 to 0.8, attack strength 0.2) are used without any discussion of tuning or robustness. The reader must assume they are chosen charitably to the method. (R4)
- **SimCLRv2 receives the highest SynBench-Score in Table 1, but is generally known to underperform DINO, BYOL, MAE, and supervised pretraining in typical evaluations.** This discrepancy raises doubts about whether SynBench captures practically relevant aspects of representation quality. (R4)
- **Only linear probing is considered** for evaluating pretrained models. Finetuning is a common alternative, and it is unclear how SynBench would perform in that setup. (R2)
- **Fundamental doubt about the approach:** It is questionable whether synthetic binary Gaussian mixtures, without any knowledge of downstream tasks, can predict classification performance across a broad range of real tasks. (R3) The baselines (e.g., Whitney et al. 2020) incorporate both the feature extractor and downstream data, whereas SynBench uses only the extractor and synthetic data, yet claims to outperform them. (R3)
- **Theory does not depend on properties of the pretrained feature extractor** (e.g., Lipschitz constant), which is common in similar contexts. (R3)
- **Lack of evaluation on diverse or large-scale tasks:** The paper should assess SynBench’s robustness to deviating distributions (e.g., DomainNet) and scalability to more classes (e.g., ImageNet). (R1)
- **CIFAR10 and TinyImageNet are called “real-life downstream data” (Sec. 4.3),** which many in computer vision would disagree with. (R4)
- **Questionable use of Zoran & Weiss 2012** to support that natural images can be represented by GMMs. That work focuses on small image patches, not full images. Applying a patch-based claim to entire images requires further justification. (R4)
- **Missing details:** What is the number of test samples in the synthetic dataset? (R2) In Algorithm A.6, what are \(z_1\) and \(z_2\) in Line 12? (R2)
- **Correlation analysis should be done per-task instead of only average over 27 tasks** to check if the metric performs better on OOD tasks vs. transfer learning on some datasets. (R2)
- **Individual per-task SynBench scores and their correlation with downstream performance per dataset are not shown** (only averaged in Figure 4). (R3)
- **The paper is sometimes hard to follow** and unclear whether it targets adversarial robustness, transferability, or both. (R3)
- **Conceptual concerns:** Why should it be possible to evaluate representation quality for arbitrary classification tasks without any knowledge of those tasks? Can such quality be reduced to a single scalar? (R4)
