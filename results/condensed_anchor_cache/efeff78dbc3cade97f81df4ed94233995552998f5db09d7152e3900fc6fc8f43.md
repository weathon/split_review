## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CoCoGesture, a framework for co-speech 3D upper-body gesture generation from unseen speech. It introduces GES-X, the largest co-speech 3D gesture dataset (40M meshed posture instances, 4.3K speakers, 450 hours, 15× current largest), processed via pseudolabeling (YOLOv8, SMPL-X) and a seven-step filtering procedure (including SmoothNet for jitter reduction). The method uses a two-stage pretrain-finetune paradigm: (1) unconditional pretraining of a 1B-parameter Diffusion Transformer (DiT) on GES-X, (2) finetuning with an audio ControlNet (trainable copy of the pretrained model) and a Mixture-of-Gesture-Experts (MoGE) block for adaptive fusion. The model is evaluated on BEAT2 and zero-shot on TALKSHOW. Reviewer opinions are split: one reviewer (3) finds it a solid scaling-first attempt with strong insights, while the other two (3, 3) criticize limited methodological novelty and over-smoothed, speech-uncorrelated outputs.

### Strengths
- **Dataset contribution**: GES-X, with 40M meshed posture instances across 4.3K speakers, is the largest co-speech 3D gesture dataset (15× the current largest). All reviewers agree this benefits the community. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Data processing pipeline**: The seven-step filtering procedure (including SmoothNet [Zeng et al., 2022] for jitter removal) is critical for quality and provides a reproducible methodology for scaling. (R3)
- **Model scaling insights**: The first work to systematically scale co-speech gesture generation (120M–1B parameters). Choice of Diffusion Transformer (DiT) is reasonable for large-scale training. (R3)
- **Adaptation of CV techniques**: Appropriately applies ControlNet-like architecture, zero-convolution modules, and Mixture-of-Gesture-Experts (MoGE) from text-to-image/pose-to-image to motion generation. (R3)
- **MoGE for efficiency**: Training full parameters and inferring with partial selective parameters is a clever approach for real-world large-model inference speed. (R3)
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Evaluation on BEAT2 (finetuned) and TALKSHOW (zero-shot) demonstrates SOTA results; zero-shot performance exceeds models trained on TALKSHOW. (R3)
- **Ablation studies**: Ablations are done for proposed design choices. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Methodological novelty**: Both ControlNet and MoGE are off-the-shelf components. The paper’s contribution is heavily dataset-driven; its algorithm novelty is insufficient for ICLR. (R1) (Contrast: Reviewer 3 finds the scaling framework and MoGE design innovative overall.)
- **Over-smoothed and speech-uncorrelated motion**: Generated results lack movement and show little correspondence to speech. Switching to random unmatched speech may produce no noticeable difference. (R2) Reviewer 3 does not mention this issue; instead only notes the lip-region scope.
- **Missing visual quality comparisons**: Model size comparisons are only done via metrics. Visual quality comparison of smaller models (and other baselines at similar model size) is necessary given the over-smoothed outputs. (R2)
- **Unclear audio ControlNet**: It is not explained how the audio ControlNet controls gesture generation. Unlike text-to-image ControlNet (which uses a pose or edge image as conditioning), a clear analogy for the co-speech scenario is missing. Figure 4 is too simple; a block-by-block explanation following a “U-Net for motion” architecture is needed. (R1)
- **SMPL-X bias**: The dataset uses SMPL-X for 3D mesh estimation, but SMPL-X has inherent bias from its training data. The paper omits discussing potential misalignment—this should be addressed. (R1)
- **Two-stage training rationale**: Since GES-X contains both audio and gestures, why not train end-to-end as in prior methods? ControlNet’s advantage for multitask learning is not needed here (single task: audio-to-gesture). (R2)
- **Data filtering criteria**: The criteria for filtering bad gestures in the seven-step pipeline are not specified. (R2)
- **Unfair baseline comparison**: Other baselines are trained on BEAT2, but CoCoGesture is trained on GES-X. Why not train all methods on the same dataset? (R2)
- **Lip region in visualizations**: The demo includes lip motion, but the paper focuses on body gestures. Add a note to avoid confusion about scope. (R3)
- **Presentation structure**: The current form emphasizes the dataset over algorithm novelty. The paper should highlight the CoCoGesture algorithm first, with pretraining as part of the framework, and treat the dataset as secondary contribution. (R1)
- **Missing technical details**: MoGE training and inference processes (Section 3.3) need more technical detail. (R1)
- **Additional suggestions** (from questions that are not major weaknesses but noted):
  - Releasing both raw and filtered versions of GES-X would allow exploration of tasks like training with noisy data. (R3)
  - Clarify the sequence length used during transformer training (e.g., 64 frames vs. 512 frames) and how longer motion is generated (split inference with blending?). (R3)
