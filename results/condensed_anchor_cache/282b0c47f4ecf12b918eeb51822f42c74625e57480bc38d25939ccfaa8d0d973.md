## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a parameter-efficient adaptation method for pretrained multimodal networks to handle missing modalities at test time. It introduces low-rank adaptation and modulation of intermediate features with very few additional parameters (e.g., <1%). Experiments on multimodal semantic segmentation and sentiment analysis across multiple datasets (including CMU-MOSI, CMU-MSEI) are presented. Three adaptation strategies are investigated: low-rank adaptation, scaling/shifting features (SSF), and BitFit. The method is reported to outperform existing missing-modality approaches and sometimes surpass dedicated models. One reviewer (Rating 3) found the writing good and experiments sufficient but had major concerns about clarity, math, and dataset diversity; the other three reviewers (Rating 5) found the method effective, parameter-efficient, and the paper well-written, though they noted limitations in novelty, analysis, and comparisons.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important problem: missing modalities due to sensor/hardware failure (R3, R2).
- Proposed method is simple, straightforward, and highly parameter-efficient: only 0.7% of total parameters introduced (R2, R3, R4). Performance is often comparable to dedicated models (R2, R3).
- Extensive experiments on multiple datasets (segmentation and sentiment analysis) demonstrate effectiveness over mainstream missing-modality algorithms (R2, R3).
- The paper is mostly well-written and easy to read (R1, R2, R3, R4). One reviewer noted the writing as "coherent, presenting clear contributions" (R2).
- Approach leverages parameter-efficient fine-tuning, which one reviewer found conceptually straightforward with good performance (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity of method and math**: One reviewer (R1) found the math unclear; specifically how adaptation parameters are derived, updated, initialized, and how they interact with frozen layers during training is not adequately described. Figure 1 is unclear in depicting adaptable layers' interaction with frozen layers. Another reviewer (R3) noted that no insight or analysis is provided on *why* the parameter-efficient adaptation helps with missing modality robustness—the paper relies solely on empirical results.
- **Limited dataset diversity and missing comparisons**: R1 criticized that experiments focus only on segmentation and sentiment analysis, lacking diversity in data types and tasks. R3 asked why CMU-MOSI and CMU-MSEI were chosen and noted missing comparisons with existing methods for multimodal sentiment analysis. R4 pointed out a lack of extensive comparisons with other methods (e.g., [1,2,3]) for multimodal classification, making Table 5 less persuasive. R2 also noted that no imputation baselines (mean/median, generative models) were compared beyond zero-replacement.
- **Novelty concerns**: R3 stated that applying existing techniques (low-rank adaptation, scale/shift transformations, BitFit) to adapt a pretrained model on a subset of modalities is low in novelty. No clear justification is given for choosing SSF over LoRA or BitFit to report the main results (R3).
- **Limitations of SSF and comparison gaps**: R2 observed that the SSF strategy is input-independent, which may struggle under distribution shift between training and testing; the paper provides no analysis on this. Table 9 shows a gap to BitFit, making SSF's advantages not obvious; the paper should analyze trade-offs. Table 8 shows a considerable gap to dedicated models; R2 asked if increasing adaptation parameters could close this gap, but no ablation on the number of layers or dimensionality is provided.
- **Assumption of complete training data**: R1 and R4 noted that the method excludes missing modality data from the initial training phase, providing no justification. R4 specifically stated the method assumes all modalities present during pre-training, limiting applicability to real-world scenarios where certain modalities are consistently absent during training.
- **Parameter efficiency comparison missing**: R3 questioned that there is no fair comparison of parameter efficiency between the proposed method and competing methods.
- **Reviewer disagreement on quality**: One reviewer (R1) scored soundness, presentation, and contribution as "2 fair", whereas the other reviewers mostly scored "2-3 good", indicating a split on the clarity and depth of the contribution.
