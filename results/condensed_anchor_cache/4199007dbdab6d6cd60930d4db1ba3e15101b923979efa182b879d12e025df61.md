## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a method for reconstructing the 3D dark matter distribution from weak gravitational lensing signals, using a coordinate-based neural field with positional encoding and a differentiable forward model. The approach models the continuous matter distribution and optimizes network weights via an analysis-by-synthesis physical forward model to reproduce observed shear. Results on simulated data that mimic upcoming telescope surveys show that the method outperforms traditional techniques (Wiener filter, Kaiser-Squires), especially in recovering non-Gaussian structures.

### Strengths

- **Important problem**: The paper tackles a significant challenge in cosmology – 3D dark matter reconstruction from weak lensing – which is of contemporary relevance given upcoming galaxy surveys. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Well-written and accessible**: The paper is clearly structured, and the physics background is exceptionally well-presented, making the main ideas understandable even to non-experts. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Creative combination of ML tools with physics**: The use of standard ML tools (neural fields, positional encoding) is creatively integrated with a differentiable physics-based forward model (e.g., JaxPM) and realistic simulations, providing a promising solution beyond proof-of-principle. (R1, R4)
- **Outperforms baselines**: The method demonstrates superior reconstruction compared to traditional approaches (Wiener filter, Kaiser-Squires), especially in capturing non-Gaussian features – a key advantage over methods that impose strong Gaussian priors. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Realistic simulation setup**: The experiments use realistic simulated measurements that mimic data from upcoming surveys, and the forward model uses established cosmological tools, enhancing the contribution’s credibility. (R1, R4)
- **Good technical execution**: The implicit representation approach and differentiable forward model are well-implemented. (R4)

### Weaknesses

- **Limited ML novelty**: The core ML components – neural fields with positional encoding – are standard tools; no significant new ML contributions are made, leading some reviewers to suggest the paper may be better suited for an astrophysics/cosmology journal than ICLR. (R2, R3)
- **Missing comparisons to modern methods**: The paper compares only to basic traditional baselines (Wiener filter, Kaiser-Squires). It does not compare to more recent ML-based mass mapping methods (e.g., arXiv:2201.05561, arXiv:2206.14820) or to other modern non-ML techniques such as sparsity priors (e.g., arXiv:2105.39451, arXiv:1801.08945) or wavelet-based approaches (e.g., A&A 2006/21). This omission makes it difficult to assess true performance gains. (R3, R4)
- **Unjustified hyperparameter choices**: The bandwidth of positional encodings (L=2 and 5) is chosen without justification or ablation study. Since this parameter controls the spectral bias of the neural field and capability to capture different spatial frequencies, a systematic ablation and trade-off discussion is needed to assess robustness and generalizability. (R4)
- **Poorly understood induced prior**: Traditional methods use explicit regularization; the paper claims neural fields provide a good implicit prior, but this is not validated for scientific mass mapping. The induced prior could bias reconstructions toward certain structures. The authors should analyze the power spectrum of reconstructions against expected power spectra from simulations to quantify bias. (R4)
- **Lack of training details and computational cost**: The paper does not discuss training protocols, stability, difficulty of training, nor provide information on computational resources (number of CPUs/GPUs, memory, training/inference time). This makes reproducibility unclear. (R1, R2)
- **Missing explanation of Gaussian priors in the field**: The paper does not explain why strong Gaussian priors are typically used in this application, which would help a standard ML audience understand the physics context. (R1)
- **Unclear non-Gaussian structure test**: The test using MNIST is mysterious; its relevance and interpretation for evaluating non-Gaussian structure recovery are not explained. (R3)
- **Potentially biased evaluation**: Ground truth images are blurred with a factor that maximizes cross-correlation with the reconstruction, which may bias the reader toward a more favorable impression of reconstruction quality. (R3)
- **Low cross-correlation values**: The cross-correlation between reconstruction and ground truth is small, though better than the Wiener filter. (R3)
- **Missing redshift details**: Figure 3 caption states “12 lens planes from redshift z=0 to z=1” but precise values are not given. It is also unclear why averaging over 3 lens planes is done and which redshifts those correspond to. (R3)
- **No discussion of next scientific steps**: After the 3D cosmic web estimation, which downstream science questions can be addressed? This missing context limits the paper’s impact. (R2)
- **Unclear whether ensembling yields calibrated probabilities**: The authors mention taking the median over an ensemble to get a point estimate, but it is not discussed whether this can produce a calibrated distribution of plausible mass maps (e.g., via variational or Bayesian approaches). (R4)
- **Timeliness not convincingly argued**: The paper does not demonstrate a strong advance in the field beyond a straightforward application of existing ML methods. (R3)
