## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper theoretically analyzes the convergence of FedAvg with two clipping techniques: per-sample clipping (clipping sample gradients during local iterations) and per-update clipping (clipping the entire local model update before communication). The analysis holds for arbitrary clipping thresholds and relies on standard assumptions (bounded gradient variance, bounded gradient dissimilarity, distributed smoothness), avoiding extra assumptions like uniformly bounded stochastic gradients. Key findings: per-sample clipping converges to a neighborhood whose size depends on data heterogeneity, stochastic noise, and clipping threshold, and does not achieve arbitrarily accurate solutions even with infinitesimal step sizes. Per-update clipping, by contrast, can converge to arbitrary accuracy when the step size is small enough (at the cost of more communication rounds). The paper also discusses implications for differentially private federated learning. A majority of reviewers find the theoretical contributions solid but express concerns about practical impact, experimental completeness, and connections to privacy. One reviewer (rating 8) is notably more positive, highlighting the interesting comparisons and the generality of the analysis.

### Strengths

- The analysis holds for *arbitrary* clipping threshold, while prior works assumed a large threshold or homogeneous data (Reviewer 3, 4).
- Provides the first convergence rates for both clipping methods under minimal assumptions (no uniformly bounded gradient or bounded β-moment) (Reviewer 4).
- Uncover a clear contrast: per-sample clipping is limited by data heterogeneity, whereas per-update clipping can overcome heterogeneity to reach arbitrary accuracy (Reviewer 3).
- The bounds are interpretable, directly linking convergence, data heterogeneity, and clipping threshold (Reviewer 4).
- Proofs are solid, generalize the SCAFFOLD analysis to the clipping setting, and correctly characterize the clipping bias (Reviewer 1).
- The writing is clear and insightful, and the problem of clipping in federated learning is important (Reviewer 5).
- The paper shows how the convergence results can be applied in a differentially private setting (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

1. **Unclear practical implications and comparison of methods.**  
   - It is unclear which clipping method practitioners should use and under what conditions. The comparison between Algorithm 1 (fixed learning rate) and Algorithm 2 (inner+outer learning rate) in Section 3.3 is confusing; when both inner and outer learning rates are allowed, both algorithms may achieve similar bias control, undermining the contrast drawn (Reviewer 1).  
   - The analysis does not lead to concrete hyperparameter tuning guidance (Reviewer 4).  
   - Reviewers specifically question: *“Which clipping method should we use in practice?”* (Reviewer 1, 2) and *“If no local updates are needed, why not just use private Minibatch SGD?”* (Reviewer 2).

2. **Weak connection to differential privacy (DP) and privacy-utility tradeoff.**  
   - The convergence analysis is carried out in a stochastic setting with bounded variance, which is not directly applicable to DP. DP requires a finite-sum setting where sensitivity with respect to individual data points can be bounded; the paper does not explicitly define the dataset being protected or provide a clear link between the derived bounds and DP requirements (Reviewer 2).  
   - The privacy-utility tradeoff is not studied in depth. The discussion in Appendix D.4 is brief; the paper does not plug the expression of noise variance \(v\) as a function of \((\epsilon, \delta)\) into the bound, nor compare with existing DP-SGD works (e.g., *“Differentially private empirical risk minimization revisited: Faster and more general”*, *“Unlocking High-Accuracy Differentially Private Image Classification through Scale”*). It is unclear whether the analysis brings any improvement in terms of optimal utility/privacy tradeoff or convergence time (Reviewer 1).  
   - One reviewer notes: *“Per-sample DP guarantee seems weak because it requires bounding sensitivity by the worst-case \(\|y_i\|\) over \(\tau\) local steps.”* (Reviewer 5) and asks whether there could be utility in doing *both* per-sample and per-update clipping, and how hard such an extension would be (Reviewer 5).

3. **Results do not recover the unclipped case.**  
   - When the clipping threshold \(c \to \infty\), the bounds (e.g., Theorem 1) cannot reduce to the unclipped FedAvg/LocalSGD results (e.g., Koloskova et al. 2020). This suggests a flaw in the analysis or assumptions and limits the practical applicability of the results (Reviewer 2). The paper does not explain what step in the proof causes this discrepancy (Reviewer 2).

4. **Experiments are insufficient.**  
   - Experiments on MNIST and CIFAR10 only report training loss, not test accuracy. There is no comparison under optimally tuned parameters of both methods, and no comparison with existing empirical DP works (Reviewer 1).  
   - Experiments are run for too few communication rounds (Reviewer 5).  
   - Code is not released (Reviewer 1).  
   - In Figures 1 and 2, the clipping threshold for per-sample clipping is chosen much larger than that for per-update clipping, with no explanation (Reviewer 3).  
   - The meaning of the dotted line in Figures 1/2c is not explained (Reviewer 5).

5. **Novelty relative to prior work is not sufficiently demonstrated.**  
   - The main theorems appear to be direct extensions of techniques from Koloskova et al. (2023). The paper does not clearly highlight what non-trivial additional efforts were required or where the technical novelty lies (Reviewer 3, 4). One reviewer states the contribution is “fair” because “the contribution is mainly on theoretical exploration and does not lead to practical guidance” (Reviewer 4).

6. **Counterintuitive divergence between per-sample and per-update clipping when \(\tau = 1\).**  
   - Even when the number of local steps \(\tau = 1\) (and with a local step size \(\eta_l\)), the two clipping methods yield drastically different convergence guarantees. The role of the local step size in creating this divergence is not clearly explained (Reviewer 3). Reviewers ask for clarification on how \(\eta_l\) interacts with clipping to cause such different behaviors (Reviewer 3).

7. **Convergence to high accuracy requires large clipping threshold, contradicting DP practice.**  
   - The analysis shows that to achieve an accurate solution, a large clipping threshold is needed. In practical DP learning, small thresholds (e.g., 0.1) work well. The paper does not address this discrepancy (Reviewer 3).

8. **Minor presentation issues.**  
   - Table 1: assumptions column formatting is inconsistent (e.g., sometimes “An”, sometimes “n”) (Reviewer 5). \(L\) is not defined in Table 1; \(M\) is not defined in Theorem 1; \(g_{i,t}\) in Corollary 1 cannot be found in Algorithm 1 (Reviewer 3).
