## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Zigzag Diffusion Sampling (Z-Sampling), a plug-and-play, training-free sampling method for diffusion models that leverages the guidance gap between the denoising and inversion processes to accumulate semantic information step-by-step throughout generation. By alternating denoising and inversion steps, Z-Sampling progressively injects prompt-related semantic content into the latent, improving image quality and prompt alignment, particularly for challenging prompts (style, position, counting, multiple objects). The authors provide theoretical analysis showing why this accumulation is more effective than end-to-end injection. Experiments across multiple diffusion models (including accelerated and Transformer-based ones), datasets, and metrics demonstrate consistent improvements. Z-Sampling can be combined with orthogonal methods like Diffusion-DPO and AYS-Sampling. All four reviewers rated the paper 6 and recommend acceptance.

### Strengths
- **Interesting observation and reasonable method**: The insight that the guidance gap captures semantic information, and the iterative denoising/inversion design to accumulate it, is intuitive and well-motivated (R1).
- **Strong theoretical foundation**: Rigorous analysis, including detailed proofs explaining why semantic accumulation differs between end-to-end and zigzag approaches (R1, R4). The paper accurately pinpoints reasons behind guidance failures in text-to-image diffusion (R2).
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: Extensive experiments across multiple diffusion models, datasets, and metrics, with thorough ablation studies on parameters (R1, R2, R4). Robust validation against multiple baselines and benchmarks (R2).
- **Practical applicability**: Plug-and-play, requires no model retraining and minimal code changes; can enhance existing pretrained models and combine with other optimization techniques (R1, R3, R4).
- **Clear and well-organized presentation**: Paper is well-structured and easy to follow, both theoretical insights and method details are clearly communicated (R2, R4).
- **Progressive semantic accumulation for prompt alignment**: Z-Sampling effectively aligns generated images with complex, fine-grained prompts that typically challenge standard diffusion models (R3).
- **Training-free efficiency**: As a training-free sampling method, Z-Sampling reduces computational costs while maintaining high generation quality, with performance superiority over standard sampling within the same time frame (R3). *Note: other reviewers raise computation as a weakness; this contrast is preserved.*
- **Unique contribution**: The method offers a new way to improve generation quality in diffusion models (R4). *Disagreement: R2 considers technical innovation limited (see Weaknesses).*

### Weaknesses
- **Oversaturation and unrealistic appearance**: In almost all qualitative examples, Z-Sampling produces pronounced oversaturation, with excessively vivid colors and blurred backgrounds. These results are unlikely to be perceived as high-quality by users in practical evaluations (R2).
- **Potential overlap with high-CFG sampling**: The oversaturated, unrealistic effect often occurs at very high CFG scales. Z-Sampling may essentially function as a high-CFG sampling method to enforce guidance. The paper lacks a detailed theoretical or experimental comparison between Z-Sampling and high-CFG sampling, and both approaches share similar drawbacks (R2).
- **Increased computational cost**: Z-Sampling requires additional computation due to the zigzag steps. While the paper claims better results with fewer steps, it does not fully explore trade-offs between increased computational cost and gains in image quality. A detailed analysis of computational overhead (e.g., time breakdown for denoising vs. inversion steps, scaling with resolution and number of steps) is needed (R1). *Contrast: R3 states Z-Sampling reduces computational costs within the same time frame, suggesting disagreement on this point.*
- **Limited to deterministic samplers**: The method's effectiveness is reduced with stochastic samplers due to inversion inaccuracies. DDIM inversion involves approximation errors; the paper does not explore how these errors propagate through the zigzag process and affect final image quality (R1).
- **Lacks deeper theoretical analysis on sampling path shift**: Z-Sampling alters the sampling path, creating a shift between the denoising trajectories of inference and training. Standard CFG sampling has rigorous theoretical backing; revisiting the score function to analyze alignment under Z-Sampling conditions would strengthen the foundation (R2).
- **Limited technical innovation**: The iterative denoising-and-inversion approach has been used in image editing applications and in image-to-video methods like Tune-A-Video to enhance structural coherence. The paper would benefit from further elaboration to clarify its unique contributions (R2). *Disagreement: R4 calls Z-Sampling a unique contribution.*
- **Missing comparison to other training-free enhancement techniques**: The paper mentions such methods but does not provide in-depth comparison with SAG, SEG, or CFG++. A detailed analysis of computational overhead, memory requirements, inference speed impact, and hyperparameter sensitivity compared to these methods is needed (R4).
- **Missing comparison to rectified flow priors**: The method “Text-to-Image Rectified Flow as Plug-and-Play Priors” similarly uses forward and reverse processes to enhance quality. A detailed comparison of theoretical underpinnings, mathematical formulations, assumptions, implementation ease, and generalization to different diffusion models is required (R4).
- **Insufficient analysis of CFG components**: CFG sampling incorporates both semantic information and an unconditional branch that helps with structural aspects. The paper attributes improvements solely to semantic response, missing analysis of structural and visual enhancements (R2).
- **The insight is quite evident and the method seems obvious**: The idea that latent semantic information affects generation is straightforward. Using inversion instead of random noise (as in time travel) to pull distributions back onto the intermediate data manifold seems like an obvious approach given the goal (R3).
- **Typographical errors**: In Algorithm 1, the steps for denoising and inversion are mistakenly reversed (R2). *Implicit: correction needed for reproducibility.*
- **Unanswered or insufficiently addressed questions** (from reviews, many overlap with missing experiments or criticisms):
    - How do the semantic information gain term and approximation error term interact? Equation 10 separates them, but it is unclear how they counteract or reinforce each other in practice (R1).
    - Can Z-Sampling be extended to video generation or 3D generation? (R1)
    - In Tables 1 and 2, what guidance scales and γ₁ settings were used for each model? (R2)
    - In Figure 10, how is the x-axis varied? The time taken to generate a single image should be constant (R2).
    - How might Z-Sampling be integrated into the training phase to reduce oversaturation? (R2)
    - Why is γ₂ set to 0 in most cases, meaning the reverse process disregards unconditional information entirely? A detailed theoretical explanation is needed (R2).
    - In each zigzag optimization, only one step of denoising and one step of inversion is performed. Have multiple steps been considered? (R3)
    - Figure 9: As optimization steps approach infinity, accumulated prediction errors should cause a downward trend, but the figure shows an accelerating upward trend. How is this explained? (R3)
    - Provide detailed comparisons with other training-free enhancement techniques (SAG, SEG, CFG++) (R4).
    - Provide detailed comparison with “Text-to-Image Rectified Flow as Plug-and-Play Priors” (R4).
