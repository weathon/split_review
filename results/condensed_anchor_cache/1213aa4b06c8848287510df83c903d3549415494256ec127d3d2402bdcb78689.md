## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper extends the work of Bauerle and Glauner (2021) on static spectral risk measures (SRMs) in MDPs to distributional reinforcement learning (DRL). The authors propose the QR-SRM algorithm, which optimizes a broader class of static SRMs beyond CVaR, using a two-stage optimization with an extended state space. They provide convergence guarantees and a decomposition theorem (Theorem 2) for SRMs to enhance interpretability. Experiments across four environments show the algorithm outperforms risk-neutral and risk-sensitive DRL baselines. While some reviewers praise the theoretical grounding and clear motivation, others highlight limited originality (much content adapted from Bauerle and Glauner), clarity issues in Sections 4–5, missing analyses (convergence of TD loss, approximation errors, contraction), reproducibility concerns (QR-DQN baseline scores lower than reported in literature), and lack of computational complexity analysis. Scores: 6, 6, 5, 3.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized and easy to follow, with clear motivation and problem formulation.
- Theoretical analyses are technically sound and comprehensive, providing strong support for the proposed method (convergence guarantees, regret bounds).
- The application of SRMs to DRL for more interpretable risk-sensitive policies is innovative, offering valuable insights and practical tools.
- Thorough numerical studies across four diverse environments; the proposed algorithm outperforms several baselines.
- Code is provided for reproducibility, adding practical value.
- Up to Section 4, the authors clearly outline motivations, objectives, and proof sketches, making core concepts accessible.
- The authors demonstrate a deep understanding of the limitations in risk-averse distributional RL (interpretability issues, optimization challenges) and solid mathematical foundations.
- Theorem 2 extends the temporal decomposition from coherent risk measures to general spectral risks, increasing generalizability.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited originality**: Much of the content in Sections 4 and Appendices A/B is directly adapted from Bauerle and Glauner (2021), with only minor modifications to distributional value function representation. This raises questions about novelty compared to prior work. [Reviewer 4]
- **Clarity and disconnection**: Sections 4 and 5 suffer from clarity issues. Section 5 appears disconnected from earlier sections: the dual decomposition of SRMs (Theorem 2) is unrelated to Algorithm 1 and 2. Theoretical sections, especially SRM decomposition, are dense and could benefit from more illustrative examples or simplified explanations. [Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4]
- **Missing theoretical analyses for the distributional RL extension**:
  - No convergence analysis of the Algorithm 2 TD loss update.
  - Missing analysis of approximation errors and guarantees arising from quantile discretization $\tau_i$.
  - No contraction analysis; the minimizer of the Huber loss may not be unique (see [3] in review). [Reviewer 4]
- **Reproducibility concerns**: In Table 3, QR-DQN scores on LunarLander-v2 are much lower than the ≥100 points reported in the literature after 0.1M steps. This suggests the results may not be fully reproducible, making fair comparison with baselines challenging. [Reviewer 3]
- **Experimental visualization and clarity**:
  - Vertical lines in figures are not immediately distinguishable; adjust dash spacing, line thickness, or add markers.
  - Use consistent labels for the same algorithm in legends.
  - In Table 1, when the objective is CVaR(0.1), QR-SRM with $\alpha=0.1$ does not achieve the highest value; the reasons are unclear. [Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3]
  - In Table 2, $\alpha=0.6$ appears maximized at $CVaR_{0.8}$, and $\alpha=0.4$ at $CVaR_{0.6}$ — lack of alignment with targeted risk levels raises concerns. [Reviewer 3]
  - Question: In Table 2, are cases with $\alpha=1.0$ essentially QR-DQN? [Reviewer 3]
- **Assumptions and flexibility**: The algorithm assumes fixed initial risk preferences, which may limit flexibility in dynamic environments. [Reviewer 2]
- **Extended state space justification**: The use of an extended state space to solve the inner optimization problem lacks rigorous justification. [Reviewer 2]
- **Computational complexity not analyzed**: The bilevel optimization framework and the added complexity of optimizing SRM in distributional RL may raise concerns. [Reviewer 2]
- **Missing references**: The paper omits relevant work on DRL for risk-sensitive RL, e.g., Keramati et al. (2020), Liang & Luo (2024), Chen et al. (ICML 2024). [Reviewer 2]
- **Typos**: Line 197: "Coheret" → "Coherent"; Line 230: missing $\mathbb{E}$ before $[h_l (G^{\pi})]$; Line 286: "Output" missing bold. [Reviewer 3]
- **Equation clarification**: In Line 232, should it be $h_{l+1} = \arg \max _h \mathbb{E}[h(G^{\pi^*_l})] + \int_0^1 \hat{h}(\phi(u)) du$? Is $\int_0^1 \hat{h}(\phi(u)) du=0$ inherently guaranteed? [Reviewer 3]
- **Request for practical illustration**: Could the authors use one of the four examples to illustrate how Theorem 2 (decomposition of SRMs) applies in a practical scenario? [Reviewer 1]
