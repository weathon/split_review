## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the *single-location regression* task, where only one token in a sequence determines the output and its position is a latent variable retrievable via a linear projection. A dedicated predictor resembling a simplified non-linear self-attention layer is proposed. The authors prove its asymptotic Bayes optimality, show that linear regressors fail, and analyze training dynamics via projected gradient descent (PGD), demonstrating convergence despite non-convexity. Experiments on synthetic data support the theory.

### Strengths
- **Well-written and easy to follow** – All reviewers highlight the clarity and step-by-step exposition (e.g., Section 3 connecting the construction to attention mechanisms).  
- **Mathematically rigorous** – The theoretical analysis covers both statistical (Bayes optimality) and optimization (PGD convergence) perspectives, providing a comprehensive guarantee.  
- **Novel and well-motivated task** – The single-location regression task captures token-wise sparsity and internal linear representations, and is connected to practical concerns (e.g., the [CLS] property in empirical studies). The choice of **erf** as a nonlinear weight function is reasonable for analysis.  
- **Experiments support theory** – Empirical results demonstrate convergence of the constructed model.  
- **Well-connected to practical transformer analysis** – One reviewer (R3) strongly praised the approach of solving a simpler but relevant problem completely and rigorously, noting the work’s significance given the limited theory on attention layers.  
- **Training dynamics analyzed despite non-convexity** – The paper shows that the model learns the underlying structure under PGD, a non-trivial result.

### Weaknesses
1. **Oversimplified setting** – The task assumes only one informative token (others have zero mean) and token-wise sparsity. This is far from real-world scenarios where multiple tokens contribute non-trivially. The assumption that tokens other than \(X_{J_0}\) have zero mean is a significant limitation (R1, R2).  
2. **Assumption \(p=1\) (rank-1 attention) is strong** – The paper directly assumes \(p=1\) to connect to a single self-attention layer. While low-rank properties may emerge after training, this assumption is not justified with empirical evidence; the impact of relaxing it is not discussed (R2).  
3. **Use of erf instead of softmax lacks full justification** – The paper replaces softmax with an element-wise erf activation. Although Wortsman et al. (2023) is cited as empirical support, that work includes a normalization step, making the claim “empirically not degrading performance” misleading without mentioning normalization. The different saturation/gradient properties of erf vs. softmax are not addressed; the analysis may break under softmax (R2, R3). One reviewer (R3) notes that using erf does not diminish the work, but the debate on whether softmax is necessary remains open.  
4. **Lack of non-asymptotic convergence rates** – The analysis gives asymptotic results for \(k_t, v_t\); non-asymptotic rates are needed to understand practical implications, especially with limited data or compute (R2). Reviewer R4 asks for the specific convergence rate in Theorem 5 and requests more detail in the main paper.  
5. **Initialization restricted to a specific manifold** – PGD analysis relies on initialization lying on a manifold. Generalizing to broader initializations would strengthen the results (R2, R4).  
6. **No experiments on real-world datasets** – Experiments are only on synthetic data. Real-world tasks (e.g., sentiment analysis, as in Figure 1) would help assess practical relevance; the function of the input in Figure 1(a) is unclear (R1, R2).  
7. **Motivation and technical significance questioned** – One reviewer (R4) notes that the core mechanism (identifying sparse structure then applying a linear operation) has already been explored in more realistic softmax-attention settings (e.g., Nichani et al. 2024, Jelassi et al. 2022, Wang et al. 2024), making the technical contribution of studying a simplified attention model in this specific setting unclear. The paper does not clarify the specific technical difficulties or key challenges in extending the analysis to softmax.  
8. **Weak connection to related work** – The claimed similarities to single-index models and mixtures of linear regressions (line 103) are not clearly elaborated; the differences are highlighted but the exact nature of the similarities is vague (R3).  
9. **Clarifications needed on assumptions** – The independence of tokens (line 74 vs. line 85, conditional on \(J_0\)) is ambiguous. A detailed explanation of where independence is used in the proofs would help assess relaxation possibilities (R3).  
10. **Minor presentation issues** – Figure 2 caption should explain square sizes (alignment). Line 469: missing space between “Figure 4a” and “(right)”. Line 1832 in appendix has placeholder “[XXX]” for code link (R3).
