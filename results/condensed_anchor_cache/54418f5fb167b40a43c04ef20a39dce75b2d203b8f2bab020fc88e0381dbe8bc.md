## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Deep Restoration Prior (DRP), extending plug-and-play methods to use pre-trained restoration networks (beyond denoisers) as implicit priors for solving inverse problems. Theoretical convergence guarantees are provided, and validation is performed on deblurring and super-resolution using a SwinIR-based prior. Reviewers gave scores 6, 8, 6, 5. The idea is considered interesting and theoretically sound, but multiple weaknesses were raised concerning experimental fairness, missing baselines, limited generality, insufficient statistical analysis, and lack of in-depth motivation. One reviewer (score 8, high confidence) was more positive, emphasizing the solid theory and simple extension; another (score 5) was more critical, questioning motivation and comparison fairness.

### Strengths
- **Originality & Significance:** The generalization of plug-and-play methods to non-denoiser restoration networks (e.g., super-resolution) is a simple but non-trivial extension (Reviewer 2) and provides new perspectives on implicit image priors (Reviewer 4). The work could spark further improvements (Reviewer 1).
- **Clarity & Theory:** The paper is well written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 3). The theoretical analysis and convergence proofs are clearly presented and sound (Reviewers 1–4). Authors show solid knowledge of the field and provide a comprehensive literature review (Reviewer 2).
- **Quality:** Despite limited experimental gains, the paper is a good quality piece of work (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
1. **Missing SwinIR baseline and unfair network comparison.** Reviewer 1 notes that SwinIR’s reported PSNR on Set5 is ~6 dB higher than DRP’s results; SwinIR should be included as a baseline on all datasets with an explanation. Reviewer 3 also requires a direct comparison with SwinIR. Reviewer 4 calls the comparison unfair: DRP uses SwinIR while DPIR uses DRUNet. To control for architecture, the authors should either re-implement DPIR with SwinIR or DRP with DRUNet (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
2. **Lack of multiple runs and statistical analysis.** Reviewer 1 stresses that randomness in pre-training, refinement, and Algorithm 1 is not discussed. Single runs are insufficient; multiple runs and distribution analysis are needed to assess significance.
3. **Additional evaluation metrics.** Reviewers 1 and 3 request SSIM and LPIPS alongside PSNR to avoid metric bias.
4. **Limited generality to other restoration priors.** Only a super-resolution prior (SwinIR) is tested. Reviewer 2 calls for experiments with deblurring or inpainting networks to support the claim of general applicability. Reviewer 1 asks to test multiple different implicit priors to assess sensitivity. The choice of degradation operator H (downscaling for SR) raises questions about how to select H and which operator is best (Reviewer 4).
5. **Motivation and necessity unclear.** Reviewer 4 doubts whether generalization beyond denoising is necessary given the flexibility of denoising priors. Reviewer 2 notes the lack of analysis on why non-denoising restoration networks yield better results—whether gain is due to a better prior or the network being better suited to the specific inverse problem.
6. **Additional comparative methods.** Reviewer 3 lists missing baselines: DIP-based methods (DIPFKP CVPR 2021, BSRDM CVPR 2022) and directly trained methods (BSRNet ICCV 2021, RealESRNet ICCV 2021 workshop).
7. **Assumption of MMSE estimation.** Reviewer 1 notes the method assumes the prior performs MMSE estimation; comparisons with methods trained using L1 or SSIM losses should be included to assess the impact.
8. **Complexity and algorithmic details.** Reviewer 4 highlights added complexity from the degradation operator H in Algorithm 1. Reviewer 2 asks how much accuracy is lost by solving inversions (10) and (11) with only 3 CG iterations, and whether closed-form FFT solutions are possible (since H and K are convolutions). Reviewer 3 requests a more intuitive explanation of the update step 3 in Algorithm 1.
9. **Handling real-world scenarios.** Reviewer 3 questions whether DRP can handle real-world cases beyond known degradations (non-blind setting).
10. **Similarity to IRCNN.** Reviewer 3 observes the core idea closely resembles IRCNN, with the main difference being SwinIR instead of DnCNN; the novelty should be clarified.
11. **Theoretical and presentation issues.** Reviewer 2 notes a self-contradictory sentence about necessary vs. sufficient conditions in Section 4, and that the first inequality in Theorem 2 is trivial. The definition of degenerate distribution should be any zero-measure set, not just subspaces. Style issue: references to “Kamilov et al. (2023)” and “Beck (2017)” should refer to works/papers. Typo: “Our method is as a major extension”. Reviewer 2 also suggests discussing a potential connection between Eq. (9) and analysis regularization/priors.
