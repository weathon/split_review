## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies catastrophic forgetting (CF) in LLMs during continual instruction fine-tuning from the perspective of function vectors (FVs). Key claims: (1) FV similarity between tasks correlates with forgetting. (2) CF stems primarily from biases in function activation (i.e., inability to associate input with the correct latent task vector) rather than overwriting of task-processing functions. (3) A novel training method regularizes FVs to mitigate forgetting. The approach is validated on four benchmarks using multiple Llama and Mixtral models on classification and generation tasks, with ablation studies across settings.

### Strengths
- The forgetting issue during fine-tuning of foundation models is an important problem (R1).  
- Observations and analysis through the function vector are very interesting (R1, R4).  
- The finding that CF primarily stems from biases in function activation rather than overwriting is not obvious and is well confirmed (R1, R3, R4).  
- The idea of using regularization on FVs and the FV-guided loss is novel and well motivated (R2, R4).  
- Strong ablation study across various settings demonstrates generalizability: multiple Llama checkpoints, classification and generation tasks, supervised fine-tuning and generalizability (R2).  
- Experiments clearly demonstrate ability of FVs to significantly improve performance on continual instruction fine-tuning (R2, R3).  
- Thorough study of the CF problem (R4).  
- First to introduce Function Vector as a tool in this community (R4).  
- Well contextualized in related work (R3, R4).  
- Significance: connects mechanistic interpretability to studying and mitigating forgetting (R3).  
- One reviewer found the writing easy to follow (R1) and another found it well-written and well-organized (R4).

### Weaknesses
**Clarity and reproducibility**  
- Multiple reviewers found the paper dense, terse, and hard to follow (R2: readability difficult; R3: writing terse, figures too small, hard to extract takeaways). Figures have a lot of information, are very small, and captions do not point out takeaways (R3). Some reviewers, however, found the writing easy to follow (R1) or well-written (R4).  
- Methodology lacks clarity: overall optimization objective and end-to-end training algorithm are not provided, making reproduction difficult (R2). Notations are unnecessarily complicated (R2).  
- Details of FV-guided loss (Eq. 4) are unclear: if set S consists of top-10 causal effect attention heads, does it stay the same before and after training? Is the loss recomputed at every iteration? (R2).  
- It is unclear when the KL divergence regularization is imposed (since attention heads change during training) and how model weights are updated via backprop (R2).  
- Should clarify whether regularization is imposed on just the most recently trained task or all previous tasks; loss functions (Eq. 4, 5) may need modification (R2).  
- Expand background on FV in Sec. 2.2, especially dimensions/shapes of notations (R4).  

**Evidence for FV–forgetting correlation**  
- The claim that change in FV is correlated with forgetting is not fully convincing: trends are not constant across tasks and models. In Fig. 2(a) on Object Count, 0-shot and 5-shot performance switch and do not correlate well with FV similarity; sometimes a slight change in similarity drastically changes performance, sometimes similarity decreases while performance increases (R2, R3).  
- Suggest to extend number of tasks or conduct a statistical test using correlation metrics (R2, R3). Also compare FV similarity against other predictors like L2 distance to previous model (R3).  

**Measurement issues**  
- Observation that generation tasks lead to greater forgetting cannot be measured directly using ROUGE, as it is more sensitive to longer sequences; tasks have different difficulties; should compare against an upper-bound baseline and calculate forgetting as percentage loss (R2).  

**Mechanistic interventions – alternate explanations**  
- The mechanistic interventions (Section 5) do not consider alternate explanations. One alternate hypothesis: after fine-tuning, only certain layers have changed significantly; the intervention that searches over layers may simply undo the effect of fine-tuning by returning the activations to an earlier model’s behavior (R3).  
- It is not clearly argued why FV is a measure of latent task identification (R3).  

**Limitations of the training method**  
- The method regularizes FV of particular tasks, thus prevents forgetting of those specific tasks; it does not affect forgetting as a whole. This should be clarified (R3). Also clarify whether training with FV regularization for task 1 also mitigates forgetting on an unrelated task 2 (R3).  
- Does the method affect plasticity? Can we fine-tune on a very large dataset (e.g., millions of samples) with this regularization and still maintain plasticity? When does the regularization start hurting? (R3).  
- Some experimental observations are redundant; e.g., forgetting will be model-dependent since performance on datasets/tasks is model-dependent (R2).  

**Missing comparisons**  
- Would be helpful to compare against model averaging, which is shown to be effective in mitigating forgetting in [1]; could analyze its effectiveness through FV (R1).  
- Could compare against individually fine-tuned models to see the gap between FV-guided methods and optimal performance (R2).  

**Computational overhead**  
- FV extraction requires running the model on counterfactual inputs to isolate activation patterns, then selecting top attention heads with highest causal effect. The FV-guided KL-divergence loss computes model outputs twice (with and without FV intervention). A discussion of computational time and memory overhead is needed (R2).  

**Minor issues**  
- Typos: Line 107 bracket missing, Line 970 “I” should be “We”, Line 231 “sequensce” (R2). Line 143 missing blank after “using”, Line 200 missing blank before “Unless”, references should use \citep (R4). Inconsistent punctuation after boldface phrases (R4). Equation formatting: “1e-4” should be “$1\times10^{-4}$” (R4).
