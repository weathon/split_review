- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 3, 5, 8

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a physics-based noise model and calibration procedure specifically for EMCCD cameras. The model is used to synthesize realistic noisy training data for a deep denoiser (Uformer). The authors also capture the first real-world test image dataset for EMCCD, containing macroscopic and microscopic scenes. On this testset and authentic microscopic images, the method achieves improvements over prior EMCCD and physics-based noise modeling approaches.

### Strengths
- First EMCCD denoising method that utilizes physics-based noise modeling (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- First dataset specific to EMCCD cameras (Reviewer 2).
- Clear, easy-to-follow writing and detailed explanation of the noise model, including settings and parameter estimation (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
- Experiments compare the proposed method against other state-of-the-art methods (Reviewer 2).
- Novelty: incorporates unique adjustments specific to EMCCD sensor characteristics, in particular analysis of FPN, blooming effects, and readout noise heatmaps (Reviewer 3).  
  *Note: Reviewer 1 considers the main idea—physics-based noise modeling → calibration → synthesis → denoise pipeline—a direct adaptation of ELD [1], with FPN and pre-processing from PMN [2], making the novelty limited. Reviewer 3, however, finds the EMCCD-specific adjustments sufficiently novel and commendable.*
- Experiments are excellent and likely to contribute significantly to sensitive imaging applications across scientific fields (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of clarity in key equations:**  
  – Eq. (1) and (5) do not provide dimensions of parameters (e.g., \(N_p\), \(f\), \(I\)), leaving unclear whether they are scalars or matrices. The operation ‘X’ in Eq. (1) is not defined (Reviewer 2).  
  – Eq. (5) includes \(N_r\) and \(N_q\) under the inverse blooming correction \(B^{-1}\), which is physically inconsistent: the blooming effect (charge overflow) should not affect readout noise or quantization noise. A justification or re-formulation is needed (Reviewer 3).  

- **Fig. 7: ELD baseline shows banding patterns.**  
  Why does the ELD-calibrated model produce row patterns on EMCCD sensors? ELD calibrates row noise from bias frames and should yield near-zero variance if correctly calibrated (Reviewer 1).

- **Insufficient comparison with state-of-the-art methods:**  
  – Missing comparisons with general noise modeling approaches (e.g., [3] using Poisson sampling for signal-dependent noise and GAN for signal-independent noise) that could also handle EMCCD (Reviewer 1).  
  – Stronger self-supervised denoising baselines (e.g., [4]) should be included (Reviewer 1).

- **Use of SID clean images for training is not rigorous.**  
  SID uses a Sony CMOS sensor; each sensor type has a unique RAW data recipe. Even using clean images from one CMOS sensor to train for a different CMOS sensor can degrade performance, let alone for EMCCD (Reviewer 1).

- **Section 2.3 is unnecessary** as the paper does not propose a new deep denoiser architecture (Reviewer 1).

- **Uformer denoising model not sufficiently described.**  
  – The paper should discuss specific adaptations made for EMCCD data, e.g., differences from the Uformer model in Wang et al. 2024, changes in number of layers, filter sizes, or activation functions, and justify these choices. A block-diagram or architectural overview is needed (Reviewer 2).  
  – R2 also requests visualization of noise components \(N_r\) and \(N_q\) separately, and clarification on how the proposed noise addition differs from negative binomial low-photon noise.

- **Small dataset and risk of overfitting.**  
  Only 224 image pairs are used, with 24 images for fine-tuning. No overfitting analysis (e.g., learning curves, validation set performance) is provided. The small size raises concerns about generalizability to unseen EMCCD data. Data augmentation methods could be explored (Reviewer 2).

- **Fig. 3(b): abrupt transition points and S-shaped curve fitting not justified.**  
  – At \(\log(\text{time}) = -7\) and \(-4\), the curve shows abrupt changes; the provided explanation (L252-255) is insufficient.  
  – The S-shaped fit may not capture underlying physical processes (e.g., circuit switching). A piecewise function, as used in PMN, should be considered. Lack of justification raises concerns about FPN model accuracy at these transition points (Reviewer 3).

- **Ablation study and incremental contributions are unclear.**  
  – It is difficult to directly relate components (FPNt, blooming effect, readout noise heatmap) to the ablation learning results. The preprocessing described resembles contributions from PMN. The paper should clarify which aspects are novel and demonstrate incremental improvements through ablation experiments (Reviewer 3; after rebuttal the reviewer was satisfied, but the original criticism stands).
