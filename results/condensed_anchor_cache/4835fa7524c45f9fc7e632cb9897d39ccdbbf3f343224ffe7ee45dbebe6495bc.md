## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper presents GRAIL, an end-to-end framework that uses LLMs to automate the grounding of action symbols (e.g., "turn right") from raw sensor/odometry data, generate PDDL domain and problem files, and optimize the PDDL model via pruning. The approach clusters continuous action trajectories, uses an LLM to assign natural-language labels, then prompts an LLM to produce PDDL operators. The generated domain is validated in simulation and pruned using frequency and sensitivity analysis. Evaluation is performed in a single 2D maze domain with a simulated Kinova robot executing three actions (move_forward, turn_left, turn_right). The system is compared qualitatively to an RL baseline.

### Strengths

- **Targets an important and hard problem**: Learning action and state abstractions (PDDL domains) for robot planning is a fundamental challenge, and the paper attempts to automate the traditionally manual processes of symbol generation and rule specification. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- **Creative use of LLMs as a classifier/clustering aid**: Applying LLMs to convert raw sensorimotor data into grounded action symbols is a promising and novel approach that leverages world knowledge without extensive human intervention. (Reviewers 2,4)
- **Addresses the symbol grounding problem**: The system establishes meaningful mappings between sensor data and action symbols, which is crucial for robot operation in dynamic environments. (Reviewer 1)
- **Clear presentation and diagrams**: The paper is easy to follow, with well-structured diagrams that help explain the pipeline. (Reviewers 1,3,4)

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient and narrow evaluation**:
  - Only evaluated on a single 2D navigation environment (maze) with only three discrete actions (move_forward, turn_left, turn_right). This is far too simple for the claimed generality. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
  - No quantitative results (e.g., success rate, planning time) are reported in comparison with the RL baseline or any other approach. (Reviewer 2)
  - No evaluation in more complex, dynamic, or real-world settings (e.g., moving obstacles, multi-agent, manipulation, cluttered spaces, human interaction). (Reviewer 1)
  - No robustness tests with noisy or incomplete sensor data, which are common in real-world robotics. (Reviewer 1)
  - Missing comparative analysis with existing state-of-the-art systems for symbol grounding, predicate/operator learning, and PDDL generation (e.g., Konidaris et al. 2018, Silver et al. 2021, Shah et al. 2024, Guan et al. 2023, Han et al. 2024). (Reviewers 1,2,3)

- **Method insufficient to learn grounded PDDL domains**:
  - The paper does not explain how predicates are grounded to sensor observations; the LLM generates PDDL operators directly, but the predicates are not derived from interaction experience. (Reviewers 2,3,4)
  - The operators generated by the LLM are not strictly aligned with the actual state changes caused by executing actions in the environment (i.e., not grounded in the transition dynamics). (Reviewer 2)
  - Learning a self-contained and plannable predicate set is extremely challenging; prior work requires human feedback or extensive prompt engineering, which GRAIL lacks. (Reviewer 2)
  - The paper does not report the success rate of LLM-generated PDDLs or how errors are identified and rectified during validation. (Reviewer 3)
  - How the current state is converted into an abstract state (predicate values) for planning is never specified. (Reviewer 3)
  - The method for determining which actions are applicable during planning is not described. (Reviewer 3)

- **Limited novelty and unclear contributions**:
  - The claimed automation is heavily undermined by extensive human preprocessing (nonlinear transformations, clustering, pruning) and postprocessing (hand-tuned clustering, prompt engineering). (Reviewer 4)
  - The paper states that action types like "move_forward" and "turn_right" are known a priori, making it unclear what the LLM truly learns. (Reviewer 3)
  - Many existing approaches already learn action labels and PDDL domains without requiring manual pre-specification (e.g., Konidaris et al. 2018, Silver et al. 2021, Shah et al. 2024). (Reviewer 3)
  - The task pruning technique is claimed as a contribution but is already integrated in standard planners like FF (Hoffmann & Nebel, 2001). (Reviewer 3)
  - The use of a dual-armed mobile manipulator is irrelevant to the actual PDDL task, which is effectively a continuous gridworld; a simpler robot would suffice. (Reviewer 4)
  - The "most important potential contribution" is not stated until the very end of the paper. (Reviewer 4)

- **Missing information and unclear design choices**:
  - The paper lacks clear specification of the system's inputs and assumptions (e.g., what actions are known when generating trajectories). (Reviewer 3)
  - The definition of "symbol" is ambiguous; only late in the paper does it become clear that only action labels are learned. (Reviewer 3)
  - Lines 200–209 appear arbitrary, with no intuition provided. (Reviewer 3)
  - In the action clustering step, TF-IDF and text preprocessing are used instead of another LLM call; it is unclear why the LLM was not used for the entire selection. (Reviewer 4)
  - In Figure 5d, the contents of the generated parentheses do not appear in earlier results, raising questions about how the LLM produced them. (Reviewer 4)
  - The interactive debugging scheme for validation is not clearly distinguished from prior work (e.g., Silver et al. 2024). (Reviewer 4)
  - The model optimization step is presented with considerable detail, but it is unclear if it is a necessary compensation for noisy LLM output or a general contribution, and prior work on relevance reasoning is not cited. (Reviewer 4)

- **Minor style issues**: The paper is overly wordy (Fig 1 and Section 3 largely repeat each other; part of Section 2 is repeated in the conclusion); there are sentence fragments and capitalized words in the middle of sentences; tables are captioned as figures. (Reviewer 4)
