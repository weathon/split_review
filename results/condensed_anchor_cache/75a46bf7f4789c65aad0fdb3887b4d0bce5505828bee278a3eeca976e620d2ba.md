## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes discriminative neighborhood diffusion (DND), which uses a diffusion model built from pre-trained source representations and contrastive learning to facilitate unsupervised clustering in source-free domain adaptation (SFDA). The method leverages latent k-nearest neighbors to parameterize diffusion prior densities and generates positive keys for contrastive clustering during target adaptation. Evaluations are conducted on three benchmark datasets.

### Strengths
- The idea of using diffusion models for SFDA is interesting and reasonable. Introducing diffusion models into the SFDA problem is novel and less explored (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- The proposed method is simple and effective, and the paper is overall well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- Experimental results on three standard datasets are impressive, and ablation studies are provided (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- Reviewer 2 is strongly positive, finding the solution novel and effective with no weaknesses (score 8). Reviewer 1 acknowledges the method’s simplicity and effectiveness, while Reviewer 3 notes impressive results. Reviewer 4 appreciates the investigation of the practical SFDA setting.

### Weaknesses
1. **Violation of the SFDA setting**: The diffusion model is trained using source data and requires storing source features, deviating from the standard SFDA definition where only a pre-trained source model is available and no further source data access is allowed. This makes DND more akin to traditional UDA and undermines the “source-free” claim. Multiple reviewers (1,3,4) raise this as a major concern. Reviewer 3 explicitly states it renders “free” unrealistic. Reviewer 4 notes that existing SFDA methods do not use source data to train generative models.
2. **Unfair backbone comparisons**: DND uses ResNet-101 as the encoder, while previous SFDA methods (e.g., DaC, NRC++) typically adopt ResNet-50. The performance comparisons in Tables 1, 2, and 3 are therefore not directly comparable. Reviewers 1, 3, and 4 call this a critical flaw. Reviewer 4 recommends rerunning experiments with consistent backbones. Additionally, some reported baseline numbers differ from original papers (e.g., NRC++: original 88.1, reported 87.8).
3. **Limited evaluation scope and ablation rigor**:
   - Only three small datasets are used; large-scale benchmarks like DomainNet are missing. Class-shift scenarios (e.g., partial-set DA) are not studied (Reviewer 3).
   - Ablation experiments are performed only on the simpler Office-31 dataset; results on more challenging datasets like Office-Home or VisDA-C are lacking (Reviewers 1,4).
   - Missing ablation: what if target’s kNN samples are directly used as positive keys instead of diffusion-generated ones? The influence of the number of k-nearest neighbors (k_s, k_t) is not analyzed (Reviewers 1,4).
4. **Hyperparameter sensitivity not analyzed**: The paper does not provide guidance on choosing the number of diffusion steps, the number of neighbors for source pre-training (k_s), or target adaptation (k_t). No sensitivity analysis is given (Reviewer 1).
5. **Limited technical novelty and missing comparisons**:
   - No new diffusion model is proposed; an existing model is applied without modification. The core mechanism for addressing domain shift is unclear, and the advantage over simply using source data directly is not articulated (Reviewer 4).
   - Advantages over other generative models (e.g., GANs) are not discussed. Baselines that generate samples during adaptation, such as Qiu et al. (IJCAI 2021) and Li et al. (CVPR 2020), are not compared (Reviewer 4).
6. **Writing and presentation issues**:
   - The introduction is chaotic and difficult to read. The method section spends excessive space describing the existing IADB model (Reviewer 1).
   - Typos: “To transition” should be “To transit”; “SGD optimizer” should be “SGD”; incorrect norm notation in equation (1) (Reviewer 4).
   - Figure 1 erroneously labels inverted triangles as class 0 and diamonds as class 1 (Reviewer 1).
   - Pseudocode in Algorithm 1 incorrectly uses z_{α_{T+1}} when the algorithm should end after z_{α_T} (Reviewer 1).
7. **Missing runtime analysis**: Diffusion model training is time-consuming; no runtime comparison with other SFDA methods (e.g., DaC, NRC++) is provided (Reviewer 1).
8. **Missing investigations**:
   - How would the method perform for domain generalization? (Reviewer 2)
   - Can the method work for single-epoch target adaptation, as studied by a recent diffusion-based test-time adaptation work [a]? How does DND compare to that? (Reviewer 3)
   - Does the pre-trained diffusion model bring additional gains beyond the source representation? (Reviewer 3)
   - How would different numbers of k-nearest neighbors affect performance? (Reviewers 1,4)
