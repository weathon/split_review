## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper compresses memory footprint of deep neural networks via codebook-based quantization. It addresses two limitations of prior work: (1) coarse granularity (single codebook per tensor or expensive mapping to multiple codebooks), and (2) non-proximal gradient-descent optimization of the mapping. Three improvements are proposed: neuron reordering to group similarly distributed weights (enabling per-group scaling or multiple codebooks without mapping overhead); joint learning of codebooks and mappings inspired by gradient-based post-training quantization; and a novel gradient update derived from straight-through estimation to enable proximal search. The method (JLCM) is evaluated on several models; e.g., Llama 7B is compressed to 2 Go (reported as “Go” – see weakness) and can be loaded on 5-year-old smartphones. One reviewer (R3) gave a summary that appears to describe a different method (ALAM, average quantization) but still correctly noted the reordering, joint optimization, and proximal search contributions.

### Strengths
- **Novelty of neuron reordering**: Applying a re-order method on neurons to group similar distributions and reduce memory overhead is novel. (R1, R3)
- **Joint optimization**: Jointly learning the codebook and codebook mappings is a strong contribution. (R2, R3)
- **Proximal search via modified gradient updates**: The improved gradient estimator (part 3 of the method) shows considerable impact, based on the ablation results in Table 4. (R2)
- **Evaluation demonstrates effectiveness**: The method shows advantages over baselines across multiple settings, including a detailed analysis of memory usage. (R1, R3)
- **Well-written**: The text is reasonably easy to follow and the methods appear sound. (R2) [Note: R1 and R3 rated presentation lower (fair), indicating some disagreement in clarity.]

### Weaknesses
- **Parameter selection not explained**: How the target compression ratio translates into the number of codebooks or scaling factors is unclear. The paper lacks a description of the optimization process or any heuristics used to determine these values based on weight distribution characteristics. (R1 – high confidence)
- **Clustering method unspecified**: The method to cluster neurons is not described. The claim that “two neurons far from each other in the weight matrix can be similarly distributed” lacks empirical validation (e.g., visualization or statistical analysis). (R1 – high confidence)
- **Figure 1 axes ambiguous**: The x-axis appears to have different meanings for different curves, but this is not clearly labeled or explained in the caption. (R1)
- **Missing comparison to recent LLM works**: For the LLM experiments (Table 6), the paper should compare with more recent methods such as SqueezeLLM, AWQ, etc. (R1)
- **Missing justification for dominant term**: It would be helpful to explain why the second term log(Ω(C))Ω(W) dominates in the analysis. (R3)
- **Missing evaluation beyond accuracy/compression**: Can the proposed idea bring additional benefits such as accelerating inference, training, or finetuning? Please provide evaluation results on efficiency (throughput or energy efficiency). (R3)
- **Typos and minor errors**: “the” at the beginning of sec 3.2 should be capitalized; “ram” in sec 3.1 should be “RAM”; something is missing in Equation 3. (R3)
- **Background clarity**: The challenges of the conventional approach are hard to understand; a straightforward illustration (figure or algorithm) would help. (R3)
- **Minor clarification**: Is “Go” intended or should it be “GB”? (R2)
- **Implementation details missing for offloading**: In Table 2, how is parameter offloading to disk implemented? Are transfers from disk to GPU pipelined with computation to hide latency? What batch sizes were used for each model? (R2)
- **Potential connection to prior work**: The neuron permutations resemble those used in N:M sparsification methods – this could be drawn as a related work connection. (R2 – suggestion, not a criticism)

**Reviewer disagreement**: R2 (score 8, confidence 2) found no methodological weaknesses and considered the paper well written and sound, while R1 (score 5, confidence 4) and R3 (score 6, confidence 2) raised several specific concerns about clarity, missing explanations, and missing comparisons.
