## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper applies convex duality to analyze the loss landscape of regularized two-layer ReLU networks with scalar output. It characterizes stationary points, connectivity of optimal solutions, and non-uniqueness of global optima. A key finding is the "staircase of connectivity": the topology of global optima undergoes phase transitions at critical widths \(m^*\) and \(M^*\). Results extend to vector-valued networks and parallel three-layer networks. The approach bridges mode connectivity (Garipov et al. 2018) and minimal-norm interpolation, providing a novel and theoretically deep analysis.

### Strengths
- Novel application of convex duality (Pilanci & Ergen 2020) to characterize the global optimum of regularized ReLU networks; Theorem 1 separates weight directions, enabling connectivity analysis. (Reviewers 1–4)
- Discovery of the "staircase of connectivity" relating connectivity of optimal solutions to critical widths \(m^*\) and \(M^*\), offering a precise characterization of how increasing width affects the connectedness of the optimal set and refining understanding of mode connectivity. (Reviewers 1–3)
- The framework does not require large over-parameterization, special scaling, or special data distributions, making it potentially applicable to other properties beyond connectivity. (Reviewer 4)
- Results are generalized to vector-valued outputs and parallel deep networks with skip connections, broadening the scope. (Reviewers 2–4)
- Clear writing and helpful figures (though some figures need better labeling). (Reviewer 1; partially others)
- Convex duality yields new insights into non‑uniqueness of minimal‑norm interpolators, e.g., counterexamples with one‑dimensional data. (Reviewers 1,3,5)
- Abstract concepts are clarified through toy examples and images. (Reviewer 1,3)

### Weaknesses
- The convex duality framework is restricted to ReLU networks; implications for other activation functions (e.g., sigmoid, tanh) are not discussed. (Reviewer 3)
- Theoretical results are hard to interpret in practice; connection between critical widths \(m^*\), \(M^*\) and the training process is unclear. The paper lacks empirical validation on real datasets and larger models; the staircase of connectivity is not tested on actual architectures. (Reviewer 2; also Reviewer 4 questions practical significance)
- Presentation is notation‑heavy and difficult to follow. The main text misses definitions (e.g., \(S_i\), \(u_i^*\), \(v_i^*\), \(D_i\), symbol \(P\)) and does not explain the convex reformulation in Section 2 without prior knowledge of Pilanci & Ergen (2020). The notation section should be in the main paper, not the appendix. (Reviewers 2,3,4,5)
- Section 2 does not specify dimensions of \(X\) and \(y\), derive relations among (1)–(3), or state conditions for the convex reformulation. (Reviewer 3)
- Figure 1: it is unclear whether each red point represents a unique solution or a p‑unique solution (unique up to permutation); this ambiguity affects interpretation. (Reviewer 3)
- Figure 2 (lower half) lacks axis labels, and the red/blue lines are not explained; the caption should describe axes, data points, and significance. (Reviewers 2,3)
- The significance of Section 3.3 (non‑uniqueness of minimal‑norm interpolator) is unclear: it relies on a special one‑dimensional construction, and the radius of the solution set may be small. Practical implications for function approximation are not discussed. (Reviewer 4)
- The significance of Section 4 (parallel three‑layer networks) is questionable: the analysis reduces to a two‑layer network by fixing other layers and offers no new insight beyond the standard two‑layer case. (Reviewer 4)
- Mathematical error: the triangle inequality direction is reversed (line 232). (Reviewers 4,5)
- Unclear sentences: lines 198–199, 215–216 (definition of \(S_i\)), line 223 (optimal model fit, \(u_i^*, v_i^*\), uniqueness), lines 225–226 (dependence on \(\nu^*\)), line 351 (three interpolation problems but only one discussed). (Reviewers 2,4,5)
- Proposition 1: notation for \(v_{i,1}\); construction and existence of \(s_k\) with \(\|s_k\|=1\) and \(s_n=[0,1]^\top\) require justification. (Reviewer 4)
- Equation (7): variable dimensions not specified. (Reviewer 4)
- The paper mentions three interpolation problems but only discusses the minimum‑norm interpolation problem. (Reviewer 2)
- The practical incorporation of the path of nonincreasing loss (connecting local to global minima) into training algorithms is not addressed. (Reviewer 2)
- Missing references to related work on loss landscapes with regularization and skip connections. (Reviewer 5)
- The experimental section would benefit from more detailed setup descriptions. (Reviewer 5)
- Frequent references to the appendix disrupt reading flow; relevant results should be moved to the main text. (Reviewer 5)
- A deeper discussion of the topological implications of the results would be beneficial. (Reviewer 1)
