## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FedNAMs, applying Neural Additive Models (NAMs) to federated learning for interpretability. Evaluations use three small datasets: UCI Heart Disease, OpenML Wine, and Iris. One reviewer (score 5) was more positive, noting the importance of interpretability and clear motivation. The other two reviewers (score 1) were highly critical, citing insufficient novelty (straightforward extension), lack of accuracy metrics and baseline comparisons, poor presentation, and toy datasets limiting generalizability. All reviewers agree the topic is relevant but the current work is not ready for publication.

### Strengths
- Interpretability in federated learning is an important and timely topic (R1, R2, R3).
- Motivation is clearly demonstrated (R1); the approach is straightforward and easy to follow (R1, R2).
- The problem of interpretability in FL is crucial, and NAMs had not yet been extended to this setting – this identification is a positive point (R3).
- The method offers a promising direction for model transparency across distributed datasets (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty:** The adaptation of NAMs to FL via FedAvg is straightforward; averaging K feature-specific models (where K is number of features) is a trivial extension with no new algorithmic insight (R1, R2, R3). Reviewer 3 states “I do not see why this necessitates a paper.”
- **Missing task performance results:** No accuracy metrics are reported for FedNAM; the paper claims “state-of-the-art performance” in the conclusion without any supporting numbers or comparisons (R1, R2, R3). Comparisons against standard federated DNNs are also absent (R1).
- **Insufficient experimental setup details:** Dataset statistics, client deployment specifics (split arbitrary into 3 clients), hyperparameters, and exact network structure per feature are all missing, making the work impossible to replicate (R2, R3). Two datasets are binarized without justification (R3).
- **No treatment of federated heterogeneity:** Experiments only use homogeneous data partitions; non-IID or heterogeneous conditions are not addressed, which is a core challenge in FL (R2).
- **Toy datasets:** UCI Heart Disease, Wine, and Iris are very small and are further simplified (binarization). Real-world applications often involve hundreds of features, and the paper does not demonstrate scalability (R2, R3).
- **Lack of practical relevance:** The practical benefits of FedNAM in real-world settings (e.g., medical data) are unclear; results are not interpreted with respect to client data distributions (R1).
- **Poor presentation and language:** Numerous typos and grammatical errors (e.g., lines 014, 036-037, 104, 146) and incorrect citation styles (lines 111, 114, 117) (R3). Multiple redundant paragraphs (especially in Section 2) and incongruent statements in the conclusion (R3; also R2 notes poor structure).
- **Insufficient interpretation of results:** The feature contribution analysis is not connected to client-level data distributions; missing ablation study or analysis of how client heterogeneity affects interpretability (R1, R2).
