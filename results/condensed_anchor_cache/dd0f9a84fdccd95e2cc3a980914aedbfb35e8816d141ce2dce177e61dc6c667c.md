## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes using sliced MMD with Riesz kernels (energy distance) for efficient gradient computation in MMD flows for generative modeling. It proves that the sliced Riesz kernel is equivalent (up to a dimension-dependent constant) to the original Riesz kernel, enabling gradient computation in 1D via sorting, reducing complexity from \(O(MN+N^2)\) to \(O((M+N)\log(M+N))\). Also provides bounds between MMD and Wasserstein-1 distance. The method approximates gradients with \(P\) slices, giving error \(O(\sqrt{d/P})\). Experiments on MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR10 demonstrate image generation, with best FID on MNIST and FashionMNIST but worse on CIFAR10 compared to baselines.

### Strengths
- Paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1, R3, R4).
- The proposed method is simple, computationally efficient, and clever for gradient calculation using Riesz kernels (R1, R4); it reduces complexity to nearly linear (R1, R4).
- It brings attention to Riesz kernels which may have been neglected in kernel methods (R4).
- Theorem 1 (equivalence of sliced Riesz kernel to Riesz kernel) is an interesting and significant contribution (R3). Major theoretical claims are correct and proofs convincing (R2).
- Efficient MMD computation is important for MMD flows and generative modeling (R3).
- The metric property and gradient computation are well-demonstrated (R4).
- The connection with Wasserstein distance (Theorem 2) is interesting (R4).
- Achieves best FID scores on MNIST and FashionMNIST (R1, R4).
- The approach addresses an important problem and offers an elegant solution for Riesz kernels (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Theory and contributions**:
  - Theorem 1 (sliced Riesz kernel equivalence) is considered quite simple by one reviewer (R1).
  - Theorem 2 (bounds between MMD and \(W_1\)) is not sharp and misses an important reference [1, Theorem 1] which bounds \(W_1\) by MMD with the Coulomb kernel without the power dependency of \(1/(d+1)\). Since measures are bounded, MMD with Riesz and Coulomb kernels are related by a dimension-dependent constant. The significance of these bounds for the paper is unclear; they are not used later (R3).
  - Theorem 3 (differentiability of energy distance) has a minor issue: the differentiability argument for \(V\) is not rigorous because \(|x-y|\) is not differentiable at \(x=y\). However, this is almost surely zero for densities (R3).
  - Reference to (Modeste & Dombry, 2023) about \(D_K\) as a metric on \(P_{r/2}\) appears incorrect or needs correction (R3).
  - Reference to (Numayer & Steidl, 20201, Lemma 3.3) seems wrong; Lemma 3.3 does not state that the two MMDs coincide (R3).
  - Why restrict \(r\) to \((0,2)\) and exclude \(r=2\)? Riesz kernel also defines a valid MMD for \(r=2\) (R3).
  - Can the approach be generalized to \(r\in(0,2]\)? Particularly the fast gradient computation (R3).

- **Dimension scalability**:
  - The sliced Riesz kernel differs by constant \(c_{d,r}=O(d^{r/2})\); as \(d\to\infty\) the rescaled kernel tends to zero as \(O(d^{-r/2})\), suggesting gradient estimation with finite projections becomes increasingly challenging in high dimensions. This dimension dependency is not thoroughly discussed; it would be useful to visualize relative error in gradient as a function of both number of projections and dimension (R3).
  - The bound \(O(\sqrt{d/P})\) hints at difficulty in high dimensions (R3). FID on CIFAR10 is high (47.6 vs 8.9 for NCSN and 15.0 for WGAN) and results are blurry/noisy, indicating the framework may not scale well in dimension (R4).
  - FID computed from only 1 run without standard deviation (R4).

- **Experiments and ablation**:
  - Experimental part is limited with few experiments (R1). The method works on simple datasets (MNIST, FashionMNIST) but on more complex CIFAR10 performance is poor compared to baselines (R1, R4).
  - It is unclear what component of the generative modeling algorithm (sequential training, gradient approximation, Riesz kernel, network architecture) is responsible for the good results on MNIST/FashionMNIST (R2). Without a detailed ablation study, the impact of the proposed gradient approximation method cannot be isolated (R2). Specific open questions: “Is the Riesz kernel so special that the MMD distance induced by it leads to successful generative modeling? Or is your algorithm for an accurate approximation of gradient responsible for success?” (R2).
  - The need for training a sequence of neural networks \(\Phi_1,\dots,\Phi_L\) (Section 4.2) is not well motivated. Why are these networks needed? Does the flow work without them? What are the results without these networks on CIFAR10 or MNIST? Training one network per step seems very costly and feasibility is doubted; is this standard practice? (R3).
  - Sequential training could be beneficial regardless of the specific MMD gradient approximation (R2).

- **Presentation**:
  - “Related work” paragraph in the introduction is confusing, introduces articles without clear context, and is not illuminating (R3).
  - Figures 1 and 2: the tables are not informative and hard to read (data already in plots). Suggest removal and use space for more details. Figure 1 lacks a legend for runtime (e.g., in ms?) (R3).

Note: Reviewer3 later stated after rebuttal that all concerns were addressed.
