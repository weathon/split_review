## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes AccuACL, an accumulated informativeness-based active learning method for active continual learning (ACL). It extends Fisher information-based AL to class-incremental settings, aiming to balance learning new tasks and preventing catastrophic forgetting. Three reviewers (R1, R2, R4) score 8, one (R5) scores 6, and one (R3) scores 3, reflecting a mix of positive and strong critical views. All agree the problem (ACL) is important and underexplored. The main strengths are a solid theoretical foundation (Fisher optimality, complexity analysis), promising experimental results across four CL methods on three benchmarks (accuracy +23.8%, forgetting +17.0%), and clear motivation. Weaknesses include: limited novelty (Fisher + replay are known), outdated baselines and citations, missing experiment details (annotation budget, runs, standard deviations for BAIT), low accuracy relative to recent CL SOTA, lack of comparison with few-shot CL and newer baselines, reliance on memory buffer (only works for memory-based CL), potential bias from small buffer and linear classifier recency bias, unclear description of the greedy subset selection algorithm (no convergence guarantee, missing sampling details), no empirical memory usage comparison, unconfirmed claims about AL strategies, and missing analyses (task order, budget sensitivity, problem characteristics). Reviewer R3 is notably more negative, arguing novelty is limited, accuracy is far behind SOTA, and inter-task class discrimination is a bigger challenge than forgetting. Several questions are included as criticisms.

### Strengths
- **Problem importance**: ACL (active learning in continual learning) is an underexplored and timely area, especially for online deployed models (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5).
- **Novel application**: AccuACL adapts AL to balance forgetting prevention and new task learning, extending Fisher information-based AL to a class‑incremental CL setting (R1, R2, R4, R5).
- **Theoretical foundation**: The method is grounded in two theorems (Fisher Optimality preserving), with formal analysis of time and space complexity, and an efficient approximation for accumulated informativeness scores (R1, R2, R4).
- **Clear motivation and explanation**: The paper clearly justifies the need for a new AL strategy in CL and explains the intuition behind AccuACL (R1, R4). However, R3 and R5 find some parts (Figure 1, Section 4) unclear and overly formal.
- **Extensive experiments and ablations**: Experimental evaluation covers four CL algorithms (e.g., ER, DER++) on three benchmarks (CIFAR-100, Mini-ImageNet, etc.), showing significant gains over six AL baselines; ablation studies help understand components (R1, R4, R5).
- **Reproducibility**: Code and implementation details are provided in the appendix (R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The use of the Fisher information matrix and replay mechanisms is well-known in continual learning; AccuACL largely extends existing ideas without a fundamentally new approach (R3). In contrast, R1, R2, R4 consider the combination novel enough.
- **Outdated citations and baselines**: The related work and AL baselines (e.g., BAIT, Random) are not up-to-date with recent CL advances; the paper should cite recent surveys and methods (R3, see [1],[2],[3] in that review). The current accuracy results are far below state-of-the-art CL methods, and the full dataset results are also low (R3). The paper should compare with recent few-shot continual learning approaches [4],[5],[6],[7] (R3).
- **Missing experiment details**:
  - Annotation budget and number of runs are not reported for the main experiments; standard deviation is missing for BAIT results in Figure 2 (R2).
  - No clear definition of annotation budget; no experiment showing the effect of budget limitation (R4).
- **Method limitations**:
  - AccuACL only works for memory-based CL; it cannot be applied to naive or regularization-based CL methods (R5) – the authors should acknowledge this as a limitation and suggest future work.
  - The small memory buffer may not represent the full distribution of past tasks, leading to a biased Fisher information matrix that overfits to the buffer and causes suboptimal sample selection (R4).
  - The Fisher information matrix is computed on the linear classifier, which in CL suffers from task‑recency bias (favoring new classes); this undermines the accuracy of parameter importance estimates for older tasks (R4, citing [1],[2],[3]).
  - The greedy subset selection algorithm (Algorithm 1, line 6) is unclear: no description of how subsets are sampled (size, overlap), no guarantee of convergence or optimality (R4). The incorporation of historical knowledge (Table 3) and why the memory buffer is used as initial subset are also explained inadequately (minor, R4).
- **Unverified theoretical claims**: The paper claims that AccuACL is more space‑efficient than BAIT, but provides no empirical memory usage experiment to confirm this (R5). Only time complexity is plotted (Figure 2c).
- **Unconfirmed claims about AL behavior**: The paper states that existing AL strategies focus on new features and often perform worse than Uniform selection, but the experimental results do not clearly support this: some AL baselines have better forgetting but worse accuracy. The authors should compute learning accuracy (separate accuracy on the new task) to verify (R5).
- **Missing analyses**:
  - Effect of task order on CL performance (R2).
  - Annotation budget sensitivity: Figure 2 shows accuracy and forgetting peak around AL round 10 and then decline for all methods – no explanation is provided (R2).
  - What characteristics of problems (data distribution, task similarity) make AccuACL work well or poorly? (R1)
  - No analysis of which data points are selected by different AL methods (R2).
- **Overly formal presentation**: The paper is difficult to follow; more intuitive explanations are needed (R3). More background on active learning for CL community readers would help (R5). There is a typo in the abstract (“informativeness”) (R1).
- **Future work missing**: No discussion of open problems or future directions beyond the limitations listed in the appendix (R1).
- **Unanswered questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - Why is unlabeled data not used in an unsupervised manner to enhance training? (R4)
  - In Figure 3, the choice of λ depends on unlabeled data amount – does this hold for imbalanced datasets? (R4)
  - Why is distribution score applied before magnitude score in Section 4.5? Does their order matter? (R5)
  - Could regularization-based CL methods be integrated to further improve ACL? (R5)
- **High space complexity**: The space requirements are still high for continual learning scenarios (R3).
- **Catastrophic forgetting may not be the main problem**: Inter‑task class discrimination is argued to be a greater challenge in recent CL work (R3, citing [2],[3]). The paper’s focus on forgetting may be misaligned with current trends.
