## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Zoomer, a visual prompting mechanism for multimodal large language models (MLLMs) that aims to preserve critical visual details within strict token limits. It features three components: a prompt-aware strategy to highlight relevant image regions, a spatial-preserving orchestration schema to maintain object integrity, and a budget-aware prompting method to balance global context with details. Zoomer uses GroundingDINO for region proposals and feeds cropped regions as additional visual prompts to black-box MLLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini Pro, Claude 3). Experiments on datasets including Vstar, CVBench, and RealworldQA report up to 26.9% accuracy improvement and up to 67% token reduction.

### Strengths
- **Motivation and relevance.** Multiple reviewers found the problem important. Several noted that addressing token limits and preserving fine visual details in MLLMs is a straightforward and compelling motivation. One reviewer (score 6) emphasized that fine-grained recognition is crucial and the approach makes a compelling case for using external tools to enhance comprehension.
- **Methodology.** The paper is generally well-structured and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). The pilot experiments, description of each Zoomer component, and Figure 3 illustrating the framework were appreciated.
- **Empirical scope.** Reviewers acknowledged sufficient evaluation on multiple datasets and proprietary models (GPT-4o, Claude, Gemini). One reviewer (score 3) specifically appreciated the studies on object detection backbones and multi-resolution/scale inputs. The ablation study helps understand individual component contributions.
- **Practical approach.** The method requires no architectural changes to MLLMs, making it applicable to black-box models (Reviewer 2). The potential for real-world applications in image captioning, object recognition, and QA was noted (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and relation to prior work.** Most reviewers (scores 3, 3, 3) raised serious concerns about limited novelty. Highlighting relevant subregions based on the query has been explored in V* (Guided Visual Search), DualFocus, and TextCoT. The paper does not offer new modules—Zoomer relies on GroundingDINO and NMS, feeling like an engineering extension rather than a novel method. Reviewers requested:
  - Citation and quantitative comparison with TextCoT (Reviewer 1).
  - Contextualization against V* and DualFocus (Reviewers 2, 3).
  - Clear differentiation from open-source high-resolution MLLMs such as Mini-Gemini and InternLM-XComposer2-4KHD (Reviewer 5).
- **Evaluation limitations.** Several reviewers noted that evaluations are restricted to closed-source models. No experiments are reported on open-source MLLMs like Qwen-VL or LLaVA (Reviewer 3). General MLLM benchmarks such as SEED and MMBench are omitted (Reviewer 3). More complex visual scenarios (occlusions, multiple objects with complex relationships) were not thoroughly tested (Reviewer 1). Additionally, the paper lacks analysis of computational costs and latency (Reviewer 2), and experiments with different object detection models to assess robustness are missing (Reviewer 2).
- **Error propagation from GroundingDINO.** The cascade system means errors in GroundingDINO’s object localization directly affect downstream MLLM performance (Reviewer 3). Reviewers questioned handling of implicit objects requiring reasoning (e.g., “disks on the desk” vs. total disks) and the impact of wrong object detections (Reviewer 3, Q1, Q3). The paper does not qualitatively show failure cases or discuss assumptions (Reviewer 1).
- **Technical clarity issues.**  
  - The image shrinking process (Section L323-325) is unclear: does “shrink” mean resizing, and how is quality maintained when the blank image matches original dimensions? A step-by-step description is needed (Reviewer 4).  
  - Accuracy values in the text (L144-147) do not match Table 1 (e.g., “Zommed Crop” and “Unaltered Input” reported as 0.76/0.64 in text but 0.64/0.57 in table) (Reviewer 4).  
  - The symbol T is used inconsistently: Algorithm 1 uses T as confidence threshold, Algorithm 2 as IoU threshold (Reviewer 4).  
  - The spatial-preserving orchestration schema needs more detail on how relative positions of image slices are maintained, with illustrative examples (Reviewer 2, Q1).
- **Ablation study.** Could be more comprehensive by breaking down the individual impact of prompt-aware visual emphasizing vs. budget-aware strategy across different datasets (Reviewer 2).
- **Minority positive view.** One reviewer (score 6) found the approach compelling for fine-grained recognition and validated the use of external tools, but also noted the technical clarity issues outlined above. The other four reviewers were more critical, scoring 3 or 5, and emphasized the lack of novelty and incomplete evaluations.
