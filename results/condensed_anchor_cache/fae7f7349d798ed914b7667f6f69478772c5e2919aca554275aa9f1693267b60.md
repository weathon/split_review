## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Hierarchical Multiscale Diffuser (HM-Diffuser) for long-horizon planning, combining Progressive Trajectory Extension (PTE) to stitch short trajectories into longer ones, and a hierarchical diffusion model for multiscale subgoal generation. The method is evaluated on 2D maze, Gym-MuJoCo, and FrankaKitchen tasks, showing improvements over Decision Diffuser and Hierarchical Diffuser baselines.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, well-structured, and clearly motivates the challenge of planning beyond training horizons. The method explanation is accompanied by helpful visualizations and pseudocode.
- HM-Diffuser and PTE are novel contributions. PTE’s iterative stitching of short trajectories is an innovative solution to data scarcity in extended planning. The extension of benchmarks with long-horizon versions is useful for the community.
- The approach is tested across multiple simulation domains (Maze2D, Gym-MuJoCo, FrankaKitchen), and achieves good performance on target experiments.
- The work addresses a significant limitation in diffusion-based planning—handling horizons longer than seen during training.
- The paper provides a good level of implementation detail in the main text and appendix. Related work is well-organized.

**Reviewer disagreement on overall quality:** Scores range from 3 to 6, reflecting considerable disagreement. One minority reviewer (score 3) found the claims overly ambitious, the components ad-hoc, and the evaluation insufficient, while other reviewers (scores 6) praised the novelty and clarity. Another reviewer (score 5) questioned the benchmark choices and baseline comparisons.

### Weaknesses
- **Overly ambitious claims:** The paper motivates week/month-long planning from visual observations, but this is unsupported by the experiments and detracts from credibility. Claims should be limited to demonstrated capabilities.
- **Ad-hoc components without rigorous justification:** Key design choices (linear and exponential PTE, Adaptive Plan Pondering, fixed segment sizes) appear engineered without theoretical or empirical justification. This may limit generalizability. An ablation study on jump lengths, segment sizes, and the contribution of HM-Diffuser over PTE alone is missing.
- **Unclear scoring and metrics:** The meaning of percentages in Table 2 and the numbers in Table 3 is not explained. The evaluation process for long-horizon planning in Gym-MuJoCo and FrankaKitchen needs clearer definition (what constitutes a “longer path”?). The term “score” in the tables should be defined.
- **Missing analyses and comparisons:**
  - No direct analysis of compounding errors, despite the paper claiming to address this issue.
  - No comparison to existing trajectory stitching methods (briefly mentioned without specifics, line 177).
  - No training time or resource comparisons; computational efficiency claims are unsupported.
  - No ablation study on the hierarchical multiscale diffuser vs. PTE alone; the contribution of HMD is not isolated.
  - No visualization of the subgoals generated by the multiscale planner.
  - Reproducibility would benefit from an anonymized code repository.
- **Evaluation concerns:**
  - The 2D maze task is considered trivial; results on modified versions of standard benchmarks (e.g., extended FrankaKitchen) lack suitable baselines and are not entirely convincing. The extra flexibility in trajectory length should be demonstrated on existing benchmarks with established baselines.
  - Improvements on FrankaKitchen (Table 3) are marginal.
  - The PD controller is not explained.
- **Specific technical weaknesses:**
  - Subgoal selection (segment lengths) appears arbitrary; more intuition and analysis are needed across different environments.
  - Euclidean distance for out-stretching and bridge trajectory sampling may not capture feasible paths in constrained domains (e.g., robotic manipulation). The distance threshold determination and environment-specific adjustments are unclear.
  - No feasibility checks for stitched trajectories; measures for detecting and handling infeasible trajectories are missing.
  - The hierarchical diffuser primarily uses position-based subgoals; adaptability to environments where position alone is insufficient (e.g., requiring velocity or object interactions) is not discussed.
  - Fixed segment sizes may limit generalizability, e.g., for FrankaKitchen where variable segments could be beneficial.
  - The repetitiveness of example maze trajectories (Figure 3) raises questions about diversity and its effect on performance.
  - The paper mentions adapting to visual observations in future work, but it is unclear how distance-based metrics would work in pixel-based environments.
- **Presentation issues:**
  - Section 5.3.3’s title is uninformative.
  - Figure 4’s x-axis is unusual and hard to interpret (suggestion: stack bar plot).
  - Figures 1 and 2 are insufficiently referenced in text.
  - Typo: “necessarity” → “necessity” (line 431).
