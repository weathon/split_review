## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents SPikE-SSM, a spiking state space model (SSM) that integrates spiking neural networks (SNNs) with SSMs for long-sequence learning. It introduces a parallel inference strategy via Parallel Max-Min Boundary Compression (PMBC) – similar to fixed-point iteration – to train the reset mechanism in parallel, a refractory LIF neuron model for biological interpretability and sparsity, and trainable thresholds to balance accuracy and sparsity. Evaluations are conducted on Long Range Arena (LRA) benchmarks and WikiText-103.

### Strengths
1. **Parallel inference via PMBC (fixed-point iteration) enables efficient parallel processing and training in SNNs.** Reviewer 1 considers this an innovative advancement; Reviewer 2 acknowledges it accelerates training.  
2. **The proposed LIF neuron with reset‑refractory mechanism improves biological interpretability and dynamic computation** (Reviewers 1 and 3) **and yields better accuracy and sparsity compared to other spikingSSMs** (Reviewer 2).  
3. **Paper is well‑structured and experiments cover a range of LRA benchmarks and WikiText‑103, showing some promise** (Reviewers 1 and 3).  

*Note:* Reviewer 1 rated the paper 5 (positive) and found the contributions innovative and well‑presented, while Reviewers 2 and 3 rated 3 and expressed greater reservations about novelty and rigor.

### Weaknesses
1. **Scalability not discussed.** The scalability of SPikE‑SSM to larger datasets and more complex tasks is not thoroughly addressed (Reviewer 1).  
2. **Missing comparisons with non‑SSM SOTA SNN architectures** in terms of computational efficiency and energy consumption (Reviewer 1).  
3. **Missing comparison with existing parallel‑training methods for SNNs**, specifically Fang et al. (NeurIPS 2024) and Zhang et al. (AAAI 2024); advantages of PMBC over these are unclear (Reviewer 1).  
4. **Figure 2 is unclear** – the design rationale and core concepts of PMBC are difficult to grasp (Reviewer 1).  
5. **Figure 3 caption lacks explanation** for what “x,h,x,…,x” represents, causing confusion (Reviewer 1).  
6. **No complete network architecture diagram** and no parameters/settings listed in the main text or appendix (Reviewer 1).  
7. **Logical relationship among the three identified challenges is not explained** – it is unclear whether they are progressive or parallel (Reviewer 1).  
8. **Floating‑point multiplication in the GLU (Figure 1)** – is the multiplication unavoidable, or could a more efficient alternative replace this module? (Reviewer 2).  
9. **Interpretability of reset mechanism and refractory period** – how exactly do these components enhance model performance? The nonlinear transformation introduced by reset and refractory is not clearly justified (Reviewer 2).  
10. **Core contribution is incremental** – similar ideas exist in prior work (e.g., SpikingSSM); novelty is insufficient and the proposed solutions do not clearly differentiate from existing methods (Reviewer 3).  
11. **Marginal accuracy gains** – improvements from PMBC are often less than 1% on many tasks, and benchmarks like sMNIST are relatively simple; performance on more challenging real‑world tasks is limited (Reviewer 3).  
12. **Theoretical backing insufficient** – proofs in the appendices are not rigorous and appear heuristic, weakening the PMBC contribution (Reviewer 3).  
13. **Energy‑efficiency claims are speculative** – based on theoretical operation counts rather than real neuromorphic hardware measurements; this diminishes practical relevance (Reviewer 3).  
14. **Paper is dense and hard to follow**, especially the PMBC and refractory neuron sections; methodology is difficult to replicate (Reviewer 3).
