## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces STExplainer, a global GNN explainability method that extracts global explanations via frequent rooted subtrees. It enumerates L-hop subtrees across the dataset, selects the Top-K most frequent ones belonging predominantly to a single target class, computes weighted embeddings, optimizes weights via a loss function, and merges overlapping subtrees into subgraphs. Subgraphs are clustered (k-means with a distance threshold τ), and a representative subgraph per cluster is obtained by averaging S matched subgraphs. Experiments are conducted on two synthetic datasets (BA2-Motifs, BAMultiShapes) and two real-world chemical datasets (Mutagenicity, NCI1). Results show the method provides human-readable global explanations and outperforms existing global explainers (e.g., GLGExplainer, GCNeuron) in several cases.

### Strengths

- The paper tackles the important and relatively new problem of global GNN explainability with a novel method that directly mines rooted subtrees across the dataset, avoiding reliance on instance-level explainers.
- The pipeline is intuitive and efficient (enumerating subtrees rather than all possible subgraphs), and the use of overlapping subtree merging, k-means clustering, and subgraph matching produces human-readable global explanations that are faithful to the original GNN and distinguishable across classes.
- STExplainer is empirically shown to provide better global explanations than conventional methods on two synthetic and two real-world datasets, with clear examples where other explainers (GLGExplainer, GCNeuron) fail.
- The paper is well-organized, clearly written, and provides hyperparameter settings (e.g., τ=2, λ) for reproducibility.

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty concerns (minority view)** : One reviewer finds the novelty weak, noting the method resembles a surrogation model that could be realized using traditional techniques like graphlet kernels. While other reviewers consider the approach novel, this criticism should be addressed.
- **Arbitrary design choices**:
  - The k-means clustering uses a threshold τ to ensure cluster centers are distant enough; all experiments set τ=2 with no justification or sensitivity analysis. The impact of different τ (and thus different k) on explanations is unexplored.
  - After extracting overlapping subtrees into subgraphs, the method samples S subgraphs per cluster and averages them (after subgraph matching) to obtain a representative subgraph. The rationale for averaging after the matching step is unclear—why not directly use the centroid or a single matched subgraph?
  - The subtree size L (L-hop subgraph) significantly influences results, yet the paper does not explain how L should be chosen or provide experiments showing its effect.
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation**:
  - Comparison to baselines is limited to anecdotal examples (Figure 2); no quantitative comparison (e.g., fidelity, infidelity) against other global explainers is provided in Table 1 or elsewhere.
  - Only two synthetic and two real datasets are used; more diverse datasets and additional cases where other methods fail would strengthen the case.
  - No ablation or sensitivity analysis is performed on key hyperparameters (τ, λ, number of subtrees T, M), so the stability and robustness of the method are unclear.
  - The paper does not analyze computational complexity or runtime compared to baselines, which is important given the need to repeatedly access all graph instances for subtree enumeration, merging, and subgraph matching.
- **Methodological issues**:
  - An additional MLP is used to compute subtree scores from embeddings, which itself complicates interpretability (adding a learned component).
  - The frequency-based subtree selection may not be optimal: on NCI1 (Figure 4), performance appears linked to infrequent subtrees, suggesting frequency might not be the best criterion.
  - The Top-K selection does not detect isomorphic subtrees, so isomorphic structures may receive different importance scores.
- **Presentation issues**:
  - Figure 3 is difficult to understand due to insufficient explanation, especially for the plots of original graphs.
  - Several typos: “methos” (should be “methods”), “concepts rather latent” (should be “rather than latent”), incorrect quotation marks in Appendix A5, and misspelled “Hyperparameter” in Appendix A6 title.
- **Unaddressed reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - Why not use accuracy as a metric?
  - How does the choice of L affect the method?
  - Why averaging S subgraphs instead of using the centroid or a single matched subgraph?
