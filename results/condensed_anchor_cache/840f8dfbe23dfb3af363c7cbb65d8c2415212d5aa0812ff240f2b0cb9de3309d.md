## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents COLLIE, a grammar-based framework for constructing constrained text generation tasks at different levels (word, sentence, paragraph, passage) with compositional constraints. It includes tools for automatic extraction of task instances from corpora (Wikipedia, CCNews, Project Gutenberg), resulting in the COLLIE-v1 dataset (~20,80 instances, 13 constraint structures). The authors evaluate five instruction-tuned LLMs (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, PaLM, Vicuna-7B, Alpaca-7B) and analyze their performances to reveal shortcomings. Reviewers disagree substantially: one rates the paper very low (1) while others rate 6–8 (mostly positive). Most find the paper well-written and the evaluation comprehensive, but concerns about novelty, dataset utility, and missing details are raised.

### Strengths
- Well-written and easy to follow (R1, R3, R4).
- Evaluates multiple state-of-the-art LLMs (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, PaLM, Vicuna-7B, Alpaca-7B) and shows they do not fully solve the tasks (R1, R2, R4).
- Open-sources code and dataset to promote further research (R1, R4).
- Comprehensive analysis highlighting specific shortcomings of current LLMs, e.g., in logical reasoning, counting, and semantic planning (R1, R3, R4).
- Provides a scalable method for future work to construct constrained-generation benchmarks of interest (R3).
- Combines rule-based and neural-based generation for NLP-grounded constraints (R1).
- Conceptually simple but poses non-trivial challenges that can drive LLM development (R4).
- Complementary code helps verify the approach (R4).

### Weaknesses
- The idea lacks novelty: similar constraint-based dataset construction for instruction fine-tuning has been explored before (e.g., [1]) (R2, high confidence).
- The dataset may be of limited utility because the rules are vague/arbitrary; the extracted ground truth is used only for fluency comparison, and since rules can be arbitrarily designed, the compiled dataset can be easily replicated with different engineering details (R2, confident).
- Important details for instruction rendering (e.g., how the grammar is translated into natural language) are not in the main paper; they should be moved there (R1, very confident).
- A qualitative analysis is missing for word blacklisting constraints (R1, confident).
- Insufficient detail on performance enhancement through feedback (Section 5): how feedback is used should be listed (R1 question, overlaps with criticism of missing detail).
- The limitations mention potential problems with filtering and processing functions, but these issues are not elaborated upon; more explanation of what issues exist in the extraction process is needed (R2 question, overlaps with missing clarification).
- The paper focuses on basic units (tokens, sentences, etc.) but does not discuss applicability to other constrained generation tasks such as controllable text summarization (e.g., MACSum Zhang et al., 2023); a discussion or explanation of this gap is recommended (R3, minor but specific missing discussion).
- It is unclear how the natural-language instructions derived from the same grammar differ across the three source datasets (Wikipedia, CCNews, Project Gutenberg) (R4 question, overlaps with missing explanation).
