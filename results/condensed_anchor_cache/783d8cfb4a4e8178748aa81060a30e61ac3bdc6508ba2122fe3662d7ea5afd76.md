## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ADAPT, a deep continuous prompting method that automatically determines heterogeneous context lengths by iteratively pruning prompt tokens based on a saliency criterion. Evaluated on CLIP across 11 downstream datasets, ADAPT improves average test accuracy from 79.83% to 81.70% and reduces computational overheads compared to baselines. Reviewers generally find the idea interesting and the average performance compelling, but raise concerns about few-shot robustness, missing comparisons, lack of analysis on learned masks, unclear hyperparameter choices, and presentation issues.

### Strengths
- **Novel adaptive prompt tuning**: The iterative pruning mechanism automatically determines heterogeneous context lengths per layer, outperforming fixed-length prompt tuning (R1, R4). This is a novel and interesting idea that effectively reduces parameters while maintaining or improving performance (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Broad evaluation and strong average results**: Validated on 11 diverse vision-language datasets; ADAPT achieves competitive or improved accuracy, and the best method on 16-shot tasks (R1, R2, R3). It also shows slightly fewer FLOPs than existing methods (R4).
- **Principled pruning approach**: Using a pruning-inspired saliency criterion to dynamically adjust prompt lengths is a clear improvement over manually designed homogeneous prompts (R1). Adding learnable masks to prompts of different depth is an interesting idea (R2).
- **Well-written (one reviewer)**: R2 finds the paper well-written. (However, R4 finds the presentation poor – see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Few-shot performance and data dependence**: ADAPT does not show advantages on very low-shot settings (1/2/4/8 shots). As the number of images decreases (16-shot → 1-shot), its performance becomes inferior to existing methods (e.g., MaPLe, LAMM), indicating high data dependency and lack of robustness (R2, R3). The paper should report results for 1/2/4/8-shot settings following CoOP methodology (R2, R3).
- **Missing key reference and comparisons**: The method is highly related to [1] which adds learnable masks to CLIP parameters, but this work is not discussed or compared (R2). Also missing comparison with UPT, which similarly introduces prompts in both text and image encoders (R3). Related work on dynamic neural networks is not cited (R4).
- **Insufficient analysis of learned masks and pruning behavior**: No detailed analysis of the learned binary masks is provided. For example, on EuroSAT, more context tokens are needed in the middle layer of the image encoder but in the first layer of the text encoder – this discrepancy is not explained (R3). The paper should also show that different datasets prefer different prompt token lengths to justify the adaptive approach (R4).
- **Unclear hyperparameter choices and convergence criterion**: It is unclear why training convergence is determined solely by reaching a fixed T_target, which may not be optimal across different datasets. Moreover, if the text encoder mask becomes too sparse, this training target may unduly restrict image encoder sparsity (R3). The number of tokens to prune per layer and the number of prune steps rp are not justified (R4).
- **Implementation and deployment concerns**: The highly heterogeneous prompt lengths could introduce challenges in hardware optimization, memory management, and practical deployment (R1). No explicit mechanism ensures that the text and image branches remain aligned after independent pruning, potentially causing imbalance and harming multimodal fusion (R1).
- **Performance degradation on specific classes**: On the Pets dataset, ADAPT fails to reach top-3 accuracy; this drop is not discussed or analyzed (R1).
- **Presentation and completeness issues**: The paper appears to be in a draft state (R4). There are only two paragraphs in the Introduction (R4). The appendix is missing (e.g., Figure 4 referenced in line 377 is not included in the main paper) (R4). Figure 1 does not label ADAPT as “Ours” (R4). Algorithm 1 has too many mathematical symbols, making it hard to read despite the core operation being simple (R4). R4 rates presentation as 1 (poor).
- **Stability and over-pruning risks**: No safeguards or discussion about avoiding over-pruning or ensuring stability during the iterative pruning process (R1).

*Note: Reviewers 2 and 3 (scores 5) are more critical on few-shot and missing comparisons, while Reviewers 1 and 4 (scores 6) find the method useful but note significant weaknesses in analysis and presentation.*
