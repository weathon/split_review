## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces a “guide” framework that uses a logical theorem prover (LogicGuide via Peano) to constrain LLM generation to logically sound steps, aiming to improve deductive reasoning accuracy while reducing content effects. Experiments on PrOntoQA, ProofWriter, Syllogism Validity, and ReClor show accuracy gains up to 35% and successful self‑improvement via bootstrapping. Reviewer opinions diverge: two reviewers score 5 (fair/good) and cite concerns about limited evaluation and novelty, while one reviewer scores 8 (excellent) praising the high‑level idea and strong results but also noting serious clarity gaps in the architecture.

### Strengths

- **Novel guidance framework** – The logical reasoning guide is general (most general form of deductive reasoning) and versatile across reasoning scenarios (R1).  
- **Good high‑level idea** combined with very good empirical results (R3; contrast: minority reviewer is more positive).  
- **Diverse evaluation** – Results shown for both prompting and finetuning on multiple datasets (ProofWriter, PrOntoQA, Syllogism Validity, LegalBench, ReClor) (R2).  
- **Encouraging ReClor improvements** – Finetuning on certified traces boosts performance on a challenging real‑world reasoning benchmark, even without formalization at inference time (R2).  
- **Reduction of content effects** and demonstration of self‑learning with mitigated hallucination (R1).

### Weaknesses

- **Unclear architecture / interface** – The exact input/output between the LLM and Peano is not specified; “bias the logits” is not defined; it is unclear whether Peano has built‑in axioms (e.g., deontic logic) (R3; high concern, may downgrade if not clarified).  
- **Overestimation on synthetic datasets** – PrOntoQA and ProofWriter are simple template‑based conversions (e.g., “X is Y” → (X, is, Y)), making translation trivial for modern LLMs and inflating apparent performance; results do not reflect difficulty of real‑world formalization (R2).  
- **Failure example (p. 6) “sees” vs “see”** shows brittleness in formalization, raising doubt about applicability beyond synthetic tasks (R2). Suggestion: test on a more challenging synthetic dataset like BoardgameQA (with missing knowledge) to better assess translation robustness (R2).  
- **Limited scenario scope** – All tested problems are similar in nature; in more general contexts it may be hard to identify actions/objects/relations to formalize (R1). Also, “What is an example application beyond artificial logic puzzles?” (R3).  
- **Overlap with prior work** – Using logic to enforce consistent output overlaps with Peano theorem + constrained Semantic Decoding (R1). The idea is similar to Logic‑LM (Pan et al., arxiv 2305.12295) but not discussed; novelty is weakened (R1).  
- **Unexplained ReClor results** – Finetuning on only 120 synthetic samples (modus ponens only) should cause overfitting and degrade general task ability, yet ReClor accuracy improved. The explanation lacks insight; alternative hypothesis: finetuning mainly taught answer‑format extraction, inflating zero‑shot accuracy (R2).  
- **Potential formalization failure** – No discussion of how often translation errors occur or strategies to minimize them; “is it possible that still there may be a considerably large set of answers, if so, how does your method decide on the most appropriate content?” (R1).  
- **Generalization dependence** – Bootstrapping relies on formalizable tasks; for non‑formalizable domains, no guidance is given on how many formalizable samples are needed or how to extend the guide (R1).  
- **Clarity of paper** – Presentation marked fair/good (R1: 2, R2: 3, R3: 3). The architecture description needs significant improvement (R3).
