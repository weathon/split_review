## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Bidirectional Text-Video Generative Retrieval (BGR), a framework that uses Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for text-video retrieval as a generation task. It combines two directions: video-grounded text generation (VTG, content generation) and text-grounded video feature generation (TVG, query generation). A Prior Normalization module is introduced as a plug-and-play score calibration to mitigate prior biases (especially text bias from LLMs). Experiments on benchmarks (DiDeMo, ActivityNet, MSRVTT) show improvements over existing methods. Reviewer assessments diverge: one reviewer (rating 8) considers the framework innovative and effective for bias mitigation, while others (ratings 3,3,5,5) raise significant concerns about contribution, clarity, and evaluation gaps.

### Strengths
- The proposed BGR framework with bidirectional retrieval (VTG and TVG) is straightforward, intuitive, and easy to follow (R1).
- Some empirical results are strong (R1); state-of-the-art results are demonstrated across multiple benchmarks (R2).
- The idea of using MLLMs for text-video retrieval is interesting (R4).
- The use of Prior Normalization directly addresses modality-specific biases (text bias in V2T, video bias in T2V) and improves robustness (R2).
- The paper provides a comprehensive background on video-text retrieval and detailed dataset/summary in the supplementary materials (R5).
- Prior Normalized Decoding is applied to multimodal scene understanding with a solid mathematical foundation and notable generalization improvements (R5).
- Extensive ablation studies substantiate the effectiveness of the Prior Normalization module (R5).
- Authors provide a baseline MLLM model and solutions for generative retrieval and bias reduction (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited overall contribution**: The content generation loss is implemented as contrastive losses, not fundamentally different from widely-used video-language modeling formulations. The claim of formulating retrieval as a generative task lacks unique modeling (R1). The definition of query-generation is confusing—generating a query from content does not align with the primary retrieval goal (R5). One reviewer finds the idea novel and effective, others consider the contribution limited (contrast: R2 vs. R1, R3–R5).
- **Prior Normalization lacks sufficient analysis**: It is not well explained why it is mathematically necessary (R1). The calculation of P(t) in Eq. 10 is unclear (R3). The concept is not original; it has appeared in other fields (e.g., prior normalization for Bayesian inverse problems) and needs proper citation and mathematical analysis (R5). Connection between Prior Normalization and Generative Retrieval appears weak—Prior Normalization functions more as a plug-and-play multimodal optimization tool (R5).
- **Writing and presentation issues**: Many mathematical notations introduced unclearly (e.g., v₁, v₂, t₁, t₂ in Figure 3; difference from \tilde{t}¹, \tilde{t}²) (R3). Cross-modal similarity defined as conditional probability but not explained how computed—is P_vtg(t|v) a combination of token probabilities, the last token, or cosine similarity? Same for P_tvg(v|t) (R4). Numerous similar abbreviations (vtg, tvg) make it hard to follow; a table of abbreviations is needed (R4). Figure 1 is confusing: (a) should depict representation learning matching within the database; (c) is also confusing (R5). Writing logic needs restructuring with Prior Normalization as central focus (R5). Typos exist (missing parentheses in Eq. 1) (R4). Model details are placed in the appendix but should be in the main paper (R3).
- **Computational complexity and inference speed not addressed**: Crucial to understand how the proposed decoding process affects inference speed (R1). The method requires traversing all candidate videos to compute conditional probability, and Prior Normalization processes each candidate—this is computationally intensive for MLLMs (R4). No analysis of memory and time requirements for training/inference (R2). The MLLM baseline appears to be a single-tower model with O(mn) complexity (m videos, n texts), much higher than dual-tower models (R3). Inference time on ActivityNet should be reported (R4).
- **Missing comparisons and evaluations**: The method is not specific to video; adding text-image retrieval tests (e.g., Flickr30k, COCO) would better verify effectiveness (R4). The evaluation misses relevant baselines like DiffusionRet (ICCV 2023) and GLSCL (CVPR 2023) (R2). Lack of comparison with image-text generative retrieval to show special video design (R3). Unfair comparison: the method uses significantly more parameters and pre-training data than others in Table 3; should use the same vision encoder (e.g., BLIP-2, UMT) as VideoChat2 for a fair comparison (R4).
- **Coverage and signal quality concerns**: Generated text queries (V2T) may oversimplify video content, reducing detail and diversity (R2). The T2V direction may overfit to visually striking but semantically irrelevant features (e.g., bright colors, rapid motion) (R2). The information asymmetry between videos and queries—videos have much richer content—may lead to overfitting to queries in training data; the bidirectional framework does not fully address mutual semantic consistency (R2).
- **Hyperparameter clarity**: α, a key parameter in Prior Normalization, is not clearly explained in terms of selection—fixed or dataset-dependent? No detailed hyperparameter tuning process provided (R4).
