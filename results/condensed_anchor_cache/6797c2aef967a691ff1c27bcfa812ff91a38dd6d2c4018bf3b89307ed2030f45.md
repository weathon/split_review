## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes two algorithms, MFCGD and AdaMFCGD, for distributed/federated compositional optimization under nonconvex heterogeneous data. They combine momentum-based variance reduction (STORM-style) with local-SGD and, in AdaMFCGD, a unified adaptive matrix (SUPER-ADAM framework). The algorithms are claimed to achieve sample complexity $\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-3})$ and communication complexity $\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-2})$. Experiments on robust federated learning (MNIST, CIFAR-10) and distributed meta learning are reported. Reviewers disagree strongly on novelty, technical soundness, and contribution: one reviewer (score 6) is positive and finds the algorithms novel and thorough; another (score 1) is very negative, citing serious proof flaws, unrealistic assumptions, and lack of novelty; two others (scores 3, 3) raise substantial concerns about novelty, missing comparisons, and technical weaknesses.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles a challenging compositional optimization problem in nonconvex heterogeneous settings and claims sample complexity $\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-3})$ and communication complexity $\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-2})$, comparable to the best current methods (Reviewer 1).
- Whether sample and communication complexities can be further improved for distributed compositional optimization is an open problem; adaptivity in stochastic algorithms is an interesting research area (Reviewer 2).
- The adaptive algorithms effectively reduce sample and communication complexities for the problem (Reviewer 3).
- **Positive reviewer (score 6):** The proposed algorithms are novel and well-motivated; the theoretical results are thorough, covering a general adaptive step-size framework; the experimental results are promising and achieve better performance than existing methods (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Overlap and novelty concerns:** Several reviewers note insufficient differentiation from existing work. Reviewer 1 points out that Fed-DR-SCGD (ICML 2024) achieves the same complexities and argues that a direct comparison is needed to clarify unique advantages. Reviewer 3 states the improvement is incremental: the adaptive matrix is not novel (similar to SUPER-ADAM [Huang et al. 2021b]) and, because it operates independently per client without communication, the analysis closely resembles the non-federated setting, making the contribution trivial. Reviewer 2 questions technical contributions, noting the proof appears to combine existing results [2,4,5,6] without highlighting new technical difficulties. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- **Major proof error (Lemma 6):** Reviewer 2 identifies a serious flaw: deriving (27) from (26) seems invalid because $w_t^m$ depends on $m$; the step from $\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M\langle w_t^m, \frac{1}{\eta_t\gamma}(x_t^m - x_{t+1}^m)\rangle$ to $\langle w_t^m, \frac{1}{\eta_t\gamma}(\bar{x}_t - \bar{x}_{t+1})\rangle$ is not justified. (Reviewer 2, high confidence 4)
- **Missing explicit $M$ dependence in convergence rates:** Theorems 1 and 2 do not show an explicit dependence on the number of machines $M$, which is atypical in distributed stochastic optimization (Reviewer 1).
- **Incomplete literature review:** Several recent relevant works are not cited, including Fed-DR-SCGD (ICML 2024, Reviewer 1), storm-like compositional algorithms [Yuan & Hu 2020; Liu et al. 2024], momentum-based federated compositional methods [Zhang et al. 2023], and works by Gao (ICML 2024), Wu et al. (NeurIPS 2023), and Zhang et al. (NeurIPS 2023) (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- **Missing code:** The absence of available code reduces transparency and reproducibility (Reviewer 1).
- **Sample complexity calculation question:** Reviewer 1 asks whether the total number of samples should be $2qM + TM$, which would give $\tilde{O}(M\epsilon^{-3})$ rather than $\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-3})$, and requests clarification.
- **Problematic assumptions:** Reviewer 2 notes that function $g$ in (10) is an exponential function; assuming its stochastic gradient is Lipschitz and bounded (Assumptions 2 and 3) makes no sense when the model $w$ is unconstrained.
- **Limited applicability:** Algorithm 1 (lines 18-19) requires projection onto balls with unknown problem-specific constants $C_g$ and $C_f$ (Reviewer 2). The proposed algorithms do not support client sampling (cross-device federated learning), which is highly desirable when $M$ is large (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 2 also questions how these constants were handled in the experiments.
- **Insufficient experimental baselines:** Reviewer 2 criticizes the experiments for not comparing with standard local algorithms such as FedAvg and SCAFFOLD; such comparisons are needed to justify whether the compositional problem (10) actually improves robustness over standard Federated ERM.
- **Typographical errors:** There is a typo in Eq.(4) – $a_t$ should have a square root (Reviewer 3). Also line 897 has an incomplete phrase "Assumption ,." (Reviewer 4).
- **Title confusion:** Reviewer 2 notes that "Federated Methods" in the title is misleading because federated and distributed learning are fundamentally distinct paradigms with different assumptions and requirements; using them interchangeably is confusing.

**Note on reviewer disagreement:** In contrast to the largely critical assessments, one reviewer (score 6) finds the algorithms novel, the theory thorough, and the experiments promising. The other three reviewers (scores 3, 3, 1) are considerably more negative, with Reviewer 2 (score 1, confidence 4) raising a major proof error, unrealistic assumptions, and lack of novelty.
