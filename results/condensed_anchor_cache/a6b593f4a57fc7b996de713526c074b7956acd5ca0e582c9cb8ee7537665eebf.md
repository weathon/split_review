## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes LLM4GCL, a framework integrating large language models (LLMs) into graph contrastive learning (GCL) on text-attributed graphs (TAGs). It explores two pipelines: LLM-as-GraphAugmentor (using prompts for text feature augmentation via structure-aware summary, independent reasoning, and structure-aware reasoning, plus graph structure augmentation) and LLM-as-TextEncoder (fine-tuning PLMs to encode textual attributes). Experiments on six benchmarks evaluate the augmentations under various GCL methods. Results show promise, with the strongest gains on PubMed. However, multiple reviewers find performance on other datasets (e.g., arXiv) unconvincing relative to direct LLM baselines, and the appendix (missing in the submitted manuscript) is needed to fully assess the work.

### Strengths
- **Originality and novelty**: One reviewer (Rating 5) finds the work original, presenting a novel strategy for augmenting text and graph structure in GCL. Other reviewers (Ratings 3, 3, 3) see limited novelty, especially because fine-tuning/masking strategies are common and many prior works already explore LLMs for text-attributed graphs (e.g., He et al. 2023, Chen et al. 2023, Zhao et al. 2022). The contrast is preserved: the paper’s approach is considered novel by one reviewer but lacking novelty by the majority.
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Experiments cover a wide range of GCL settings and node embedding methods across six datasets (PubMed, arXiv, Compt, Photo, Hist, and others). The study systematically explores prompt designs and LLM integration pipelines.
- **Plug-in component**: The proposed LLM-as-GraphAugmentor and LLM-as-TextEncoder can be used as plug-in components with existing GCL methods (BGRL, GRACE, etc.).
- **Addresses an important gap**: The work targets a key limitation in prior GCL – handling textual node attributes – and demonstrates potential improvements, especially on PubMed (ChatGAug significantly outperforms baselines).

### Weaknesses
- **Performance does not convincingly beat direct LLM prediction**: For arXiv, zero-shot GPT‑3.5 achieves 73.50% accuracy (He et al. 2023), matching or exceeding the best LLM4GCL results (e.g., BGRL+SAR 73.11±0.34). The independent reasoning (IDR) augmentation essentially replicates zero-shot classification, yet the proposed GCL augmentation does not surpass this simple baseline. On PubMed, reported zero-shot accuracy is 93.42%, and the GCL improvements are not shown to exceed that. A direct comparison of the LLM’s own node classification (independent/structure-aware reasoning) against the full LLM4GCL pipeline is missing. Several reviewers consider this a fundamental flaw.
- **Limited novelty and technique contribution**: The “LLM-as-TextEncoder” pipeline is viewed as essentially fine-tuning a PLM, not using an LLM directly. The fine-tuning and masking strategies are common in prior work. Existing works (e.g., Chen et al. 2023, Zhao et al. 2022) already cover similar ground. One reviewer (Rating 5) disagrees on the originality front, but the majority finds the contribution limited.
- **Weak experimental settings and missing baselines**: 
  - The few-shot evaluation (300/30/1000 splits) differs from standard GCL evaluation (e.g., 10%/10%/80% as in GCA, Zhu et al. 2021). Justification is needed.
  - Missing comparisons with leading GCL augmentation methods like GCA.
  - The GIANT baseline is not well discussed; on Hist, GIANT achieves 74.16% (second best in Table 1), yet the proposed method is only comparable and reportedly much more expensive.
  - The large-scale ogbn-product dataset is not included.
- **Lack of ablation and clarity on usage**: It is unclear whether the LLM-augmented features are used only during contrastive pretraining or also at inference time. Table 1 does not show ChatGAug results at all. Table 2 reveals that for arXiv, BGRL+SAR without LLM augmentation (73.28±0.15) slightly beats BGRL+SAR with LLM augmentation (73.11±0.34) – no analysis is provided for this negative result. Figure 4 is poorly scaled and lacks an ablation comparing ChatGPT augmentation with direct training.
- **High cost and impracticality**: LLM API costs are not reported (e.g., per dataset). Queries are expensive and rate-limited; the paper does not discuss how long experiments take or whether the cost is justified given the marginal gains over cheaper baselines like GIANT.
- **Missing appendix**: The submitted paper heavily references an appendix that was not included, preventing full evaluation of experimental setups and results. One reviewer (Rating 5) notes this must be added before a final assessment.
- **Limited scope to text-attributed graphs**: The method is inapplicable to non-text graphs (e.g., molecular graphs), reducing its generality.
- **Lack of motivation for TACL**: Why the proposed TACL method fits LLMs better than other GCL methods is not explained.
- **Questions about zero-shot nature of augmentations**: The independent reasoning (IDR) and structure-aware reasoning (SDR) prompts appear to produce zero-shot predictions using ChatGPT. This may be closer to ensembling than to graph augmentation. Showing few-shot ICL predictions and reporting the direct zero-shot performance of ChatGPT on each task is requested.
