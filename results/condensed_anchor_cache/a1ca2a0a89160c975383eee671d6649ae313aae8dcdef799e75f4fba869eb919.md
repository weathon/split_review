## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a retrieval-augmented panoptic segmentation method for open-vocabulary settings. A masked segment feature database is constructed from paired image-text data. At inference, masked segment features from the input are used to retrieve similar features and class labels from the database; these retrieval-based scores are combined with CLIP-based scores. The method is integrated with FC-CLIP and evaluated on ADE20k after training on COCO, achieving +4.5 PQ, +2.5 mAP, and +10.0 mIoU absolute improvements. Reviewers are split (scores 5,5,3,3): two find the work solid (rating 5) while two raise substantial concerns about novelty and experimental scope (rating 3).

### Strengths
- The integration of retrieval-based classification with CLIP-based scores to address domain shift between masked and natural images is a creative and promising direction. It improves recognition of unseen classes without additional training.
- Constructing a feature database from widely available paired image-text data (e.g., CC12M) is an interesting approach that avoids requiring pixel-level annotations and enables adaptability.
- The paper is well-organized, clearly written, and explains methodology comprehensively.
- Applying retrieval augmentation to vision tasks is a promising avenue, and the database construction method is notable.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty relative to prior work.** Multiple reviewers note that the central idea builds heavily on Gui et al. (knn-CLIP, 2024). The main modification is using a single CLIP backbone instead of two. Reviewers question whether this incremental change meets the contribution bar of top conferences (R3, R4). Reviewer 1 and 2 rated 5 but did not dispute this point; the contrast in ratings reflects disagreement on overall novelty.
- **Missing comparisons with state‑of‑the‑art methods.** The paper does not compare against Gui et al. (knn-CLIP), HIPIE, ODISE, OPSNet, or other CLIP‑based open‑vocabulary segmentation methods. Without these comparisons, it is difficult to assess the true novelty and performance gains (R1, R2, R3, R4). Reviewers specifically request comparisons with these methods.
- **Reliance on mask quality from Grounding DINO + SAM.** The method uses Grounding DINO and SAM to generate masks for the database and in the training‑free setup. SAM can produce suboptimal masks without human input, especially for small or occluded objects, and performance is sensitive to the quality of the initial bounding box proposals from Grounding DINO (R1, R2, R3, R4). Reviewers ask for analysis of how mask quality variations affect final segmentation (R2).
- **Reliance on database quality and coverage.** The retrieval‑based classification depends on the diversity and representativeness of the database. For objects with subtle visual differences (e.g., bird species, dog breeds) or underrepresented categories, performance may degrade significantly (R1, R2). Moreover, the method must handle classes that are neither in the database nor in the fallback dataset – reviewers request an explicit discussion of how such truly open‑vocabulary scenarios are handled (R3, R4).
- **Scalability and computational cost.** As the number of classes in the database grows, the retrieval process may face scalability issues in terms of both computational resources and accuracy (R2). No analysis of retrieval cost or scaling behavior is provided.
- **Unclear IV / OOV classification distinction.** The paper defines “IV Classification” and “OOV Classification” but does not adequately explain their difference or why a linear projection makes the former “IV”. From Figure 1, the two schemes appear nearly identical except for that projection. Reviewers ask for a clearer justification and explanation (R3, R4).
- **Open‑vocabulary evaluation setup weakens claims.** The paper uses the ADE20k training set to construct the feature database and then evaluates on the ADE20k validation set. This undermines the claim of “open vocabulary” since the same dataset is used for both database building and evaluation. Reviewers ask for clarification on how the open‑vocabulary nature is ensured (R3, R4).
- **Missing experiments on open‑vocabulary semantic segmentation.** The paper only evaluates on panoptic segmentation (ADE20k‑150). It should also report results on broader semantic segmentation benchmarks such as ADE20K‑847, ADE20K‑150, and Pascal Context‑459 (R3, R4).
- **Figure 2 contains irrelevant content.** The lower‑left corner of Figure 2 has extraneous material. Reviewers request a clean redraw (R3, R4).
- **Discussion of failure modes and generalization to unseen classes is insufficient.** The paper should include a detailed analysis of performance on classes not present in the training data, potential failure modes, and limitations when encountering novel object categories (R2).
- **Missing definition of fallback dataset.** Reviewers ask what the fallback dataset is and how it is built (R4).
