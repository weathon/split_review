## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper theoretically analyzes the impact of memory buffers on forgetting and generalization in continual learning (CL), focusing on overparameterized linear regression models. It extends error bounds from Lin et al. (ICML 2023) to two memory strategies: a reservoir sampling‑based partial buffer and a full rehearsal buffer. By deriving explicit expressions for expected forgetting and generalization errors, the authors claim that (1) larger memory requires a larger model to reduce forgetting, (2) a sufficiently large model can achieve zero forgetting, and (3) larger memory improves generalization when tasks are similar but may degrade it when they are dissimilar. Three reviewers (scores 3,3,3) find major clarity and validation issues; one reviewer (score 6) finds the analysis interesting and important but still notes significant weaknesses.

### Strengths
- **Novel theoretical contribution**: The first explicit forms of forgetting and generalization error for replay‑based continual learning in overparameterized linear models, addressing an important gap (Reviewers 1,2).
- **Practical motivation**: Understanding memory‑based CL is valuable since rehearsal is a predominant strategy in experimental works; theoretical bounds can guide future empirical findings (Reviewer 1).
- **Clear high‑level insights**: The analysis shows how memory size, model size, and task similarity interact; simple two‑task cases help readers grasp the relationships (Reviewers 2,4).
- **Well‑structured paper**: The organization is logical, and some limitations are acknowledged (Reviewers 3,4).
- **Potential for extension**: The framework could be extended to non‑linear models or deep neural networks in future work (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of experimental validation (3 of 4 reviewers)**: No empirical results—with neural networks, real datasets, or even synthetic linear settings—are provided to support the theoretical predictions. This makes it unclear whether the analysis translates to practice and challenges its usefulness (Reviewers 1,2,3). A validation analogous to Lin et al.’s DNN experiments is strongly needed (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear definitions of key concepts**:  
  - “Task” is never formally defined. It is implied that data vectors are sampled from Normal(0,1), but what changes between tasks is unspecified. This is critical for interpreting results (Reviewers 1,3).  
  - “Task similarity” is mentioned repeatedly but never mathematically defined (e.g., via a norm on ground‑truth parameters). Reviewers 1 and 4 request a formal definition.  
- **Inconsistent and confusing notation**: Examples include:  
  - In Section 3.1, \(W_T\) is the set of ground‑truth vectors, but \(y = X^T w_t\) appears to be a prediction rather than the ground truth (Reviewers 1,3).  
  - Variables \(m_{t-1}\) and \(\hat{m}_{t-1}\) for buffer sizes are inconsistently used (Reviewer 1).  
  - \(d\) denotes both input dimension and number of parameters interchangeably (Reviewer 3).  
  - The \(\text{diag}\) operator is not defined (Reviewer 3).  
  - A notation table is needed (Reviewer 4).  
- **Unclear or unrealistic assumptions**:  
  - **Assumption 1** (equal number of samples per task) is stated without justification; it is unclear if results hold for unequal task sizes (Reviewer 1).  
  - The buffer is assumed to store only one sample per old task (Section 3.2.1), which is a very strong and unexplained constraint (Reviewer 1).  
  - Features are assumed isotropic Gaussian with identical distribution across tasks (Section 3.3). This is unrealistic for CL where task distributions typically shift; the limitation is not discussed (Reviewer 3).  
  - The training loss is MSE, which is more suited for regression than classification; the choice is not justified and its limitations are not addressed (Reviewer 3).  
  - The overparameterization condition \(d > s + M_{\text{max}} + 2\) may be violated when \(M_{\text{max}}\) is large (Reviewer 3).  
- **Issues with replay‑specific modeling**:  
  - **Sample‑model correlation**: The data stored in the buffer for earlier tasks has already been used to train earlier models. When reused, this correlation creates a key challenge that is not addressed explicitly (Reviewer 2).  
  - **Reservoir sampling definition**: The paper states “the probability of any example from previous \(t-1\) tasks being in the buffer is equal,” which contradicts standard reservoir sampling (probabilities decrease over time) (Reviewer 3).  
  - It is unclear why the buffer stores no true labels; instead labels are regenerated via Eq. (1). This is especially unclear for the reservoir‑sampling case (Reviewer 4).  
- **Rigour of specific claims**:  
  - In Section 4.1, claims about the effect of memory size on forgetting are based solely on the term \(F_2^2\), ignoring that \(F_2^1\) also changes with memory size and cannot generally be assumed small (Reviewer 2).  
  - **Remark 1** states “a smaller memory buffer or larger number of parameters leads to less forgetting than without memory when tasks are highly similar,” but the reasoning is unclear (Reviewer 4).  
  - The conclusion in the abstract that “a larger memory buffer must be paired with a larger model to effectively reduce forgetting” is not clearly derived from the presented results (Reviewer 4).  
- **Missing discussion and context**:  
  - The full rehearsal setting (Section 4.2) is essentially equivalent to joint training; the relation to multi‑task learning is not discussed (Reviewer 4).  
  - The paper is not self‑contained; it heavily relies on Lin et al. (2023) without sufficient introduction or notation (Reviewer 4).  
  - Table 1 is never referenced in the text (Reviewer 3).  
- **Presentation quality**:  
  - The introduction introduces many undefined variables (e.g., \(T, s, M_{\text{max}}, d\)) and the stated challenges (l.48) are unclear (Reviewer 3).  
  - Plain English explanations of the Remarks are needed; they are currently written in math and hard to interpret (Reviewer 1).  
  - Figure 1 lacks clarity regarding its setup (e.g., whether Gaussians were sampled multiple times and results averaged) (Reviewer 1).  
  - The last sentence of Remark 6 is questionable: the figures refer to generalization error, not forgetting (Reviewer 2).  
  - Typos: l.149 missing dimension of \(x_t\); Assumption 1 should say \(t \in [1, T]\) (Reviewer 3).  
- **Limited scope of memory strategies**: Only reservoir sampling is discussed; the connection between theory and memory selection is weak, and the findings may not generalize to other selection methods (Reviewer 4).  
- **Minority positive view**: While three reviewers assign score 3 and highlight major deficiencies, one reviewer (score 6) finds the analysis clear and important but still notes several of the above weaknesses (especially missing definitions and lack of self‑containedness). This contrast underscores that the paper has some valuable ideas but is not yet rigorous or complete.
