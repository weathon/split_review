## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes 3D-CT-GPT++, a model for radiology report generation from 3D CT scans, combining an optimized 3D image encoder (CTViT-V), a LLaVA-1.5-based architecture, and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) using GPT-4 for scoring to reduce hallucinations. The model is fine-tuned on private and public datasets and evaluated with NLG metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, GREEN). One reviewer gave a score of 6 (confidence 3); three gave 5 (confidence 4). The minority higher-scoring reviewer was more positive about the novelty of DPO application and the CTViT-V encoder, while the others acknowledged the contributions but raised substantial concerns about evaluation, reproducibility, and comparisons.

### Strengths
- **Novel CTViT-V encoder**: Specifically optimized for 3D chest CT scans, integrating a slice Transformer and relative position encoding to capture spatial coherence and slice dependencies (R1, R3). One reviewer (R3) highlights this as a key improvement over 2D encoders.
- **Application of DPO with GPT-4 scoring**: Provides a scalable, cost-effective alternative to human feedback for reducing hallucinations and aligning reports with clinical needs (R1, R3). R3 notes this is the first application of DPO to radiology report generation.
- **Solid performance on NLG metrics**: Outperforms existing baselines on BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, and GREEN (R1).
- **Well-organized paper**: Clear figures, writing, and use of appendices enhance readability (R2, R4). R4 notes clarity of figures and writing about methods.
- **Original combination of techniques**: Contrastive learning for image encoder pretraining, spatial+slice transformer, LLaVA architecture, and DPO—a complete framework with sufficient workload and effective performance (R4).
- **Significance of the 3D approach**: Tackles the challenging problem of inter-slice dependencies across the Z-axis, which video-based methods fail to capture (R3). R3 also emphasizes potential to reduce radiologist workload and adaptability to other imaging modalities (MRI, X-ray).
- **Minority positive view**: One reviewer (score 6, R1) was notably more positive, stressing the novelty of the encoder and the GPT-4 preference optimization as a scalable advance. R3 was also more positive about originality and clinical potential, while R4 considered the technological progress limited.

### Weaknesses
- **Evaluation does not capture clinical relevance**: Reliance on NLG metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) is insufficient; the authors should use GREEN, RadFact, or similar metrics for diagnostic utility (R1). The model is tested on a subsection of CT-RATE without justification (R1).
- **Unfair and non-reproducible comparisons**: Table 1 compares 3D-CT-GPT++ (fine-tuned on private data) to RadFM and M3D results that are “Literature Results” without fine-tuning on the same dataset—making the comparison invalid (R1, R4). The notation “Literature Results” is insufficient (R4). The experiments are not reproducible due to reliance on multiple private datasets (R1, R4). No evaluation on the full public CT-RATE dataset (R1).
- **Missing ablations and training details**:
    - No ablation comparing CTViT-V to other 3D encoders (CTViT, CT-CLIP) in Stages 1 and 2 (R1).
    - Stage 2 pre-training is poorly described: unclear how the custom-built dataset was constructed and how the “interactive approach” was performed (R1).
    - No ablation on the scoring LLM (e.g., GPT-4 vs. alternatives) to test sensitivity (R1).
    - Missing ablation of 3D-CT-GPT++(LoRA+DPO)—especially since LoRA alone already outperforms SFT (R1).
    - No detailed hyperparameter configuration provided (R3).
- **Computational overhead claims unsupported**: The paper lists reduction of computational overhead as a major contribution but provides no quantitative evaluation (GPU memory, training time) or detailed method description (R2, R4).
- **Comparison with 2D models missing**: No baseline comparison with existing 2D models for similar tasks to demonstrate the advantages of the 3D approach (R3, R4). Specifically, what are the advantages of 2D slice encoding + Z-fusion over an entire 3D ViT as in M3D? (R4).
- **Figure quality issues**: Misalignment of lines, text overlapping borders, and inconsistent casing in figures (R4).
- **Unclear experiment setting**: The main text does not specify the training/evaluation split of datasets (R4). The subselection of CT-RATE is not described (R1).
- **Reference formatting errors**: Missing journal names in citations (e.g., Liu et al. 2023a,b) and missing reference for CT-RATE dataset (line 320) (R2).
- **Additional missing analyses**:
    - Missing strategies to mitigate potential biases in generated reports (R2).
    - How does DPO specifically reduce hallucinations compared to RLHF? Are there examples where RLHF failed and DPO succeeded? (R3)
    - Could incorporate additional clinical context or patient-specific information to further improve clinical relevance (R2).
    - Lack of detail on reconstruction techniques used to expand the dataset (R1).
    - No plan to open-source code/weights or publish Dataset-XY (R1).
