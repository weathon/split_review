## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Thetan Berserker (TB), a centroid-based clustering algorithm controlled by a single distance parameter. TB is built on a sequential processing paradigm (Thetan Sequential, TS) and aims to overcome order sensitivity while estimating the number of clusters. The method is evaluated on low-dimensional synthetic data, two benchmark datasets, and two real-world applications (superpixel extraction and 3D brain scan segmentation). Supporting algorithms (TS, TSR, TBK, TBSCAN) are introduced. Theoretical proofs are provided for properties like equidistant partitioning and convergence of cluster counts. Reviewers disagree sharply: Reviewer 2 considers the paper strong (rating 8) with consistent low runtime, reasonable mathematical framework, thorough ablation studies, and good coverage. Reviewer 1 (rating 3) finds the experimental evaluation insufficient, lacking high-dimensional tests, baselines, and robustness analysis. Reviewers 3 and 4 (both rating 5) note that while the idea is simple, the contribution lacks depth, the presentation is poor, and the experiments have significant gaps (e.g., unfair superpixel comparison, missing convergence proof).

### Strengths
- **Simplicity and single hyperparameter**: The approach is straightforward and easy to follow; it requires only one distance threshold parameter (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Time and memory efficiency**: Consistently low runtime reported across experiments on low-dimensional data (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). The sequential nature enhances scalability (Reviewer 3).
- **Theoretical depth**: The paper provides detailed proofs with simple examples, including theorems on the influence of input ordering and the functional assumptions for TB (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Ablation studies and coverage**: Thorough experimental evaluation including ablation studies that reveal key characteristics (equidistant partitioning, rapid convergence) (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Reasonable mathematical framework** supports the algorithm (Reviewer 2).
- **Interesting and important problem**; the use of a metaphor is noted (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation**:
  - Tests are performed only on low-dimensional data; high-dimensional performance is missing (Reviewer 1). Comparative runtime methods should be implemented in the same language (Python vs. C/Cython) (Reviewer 1).
  - Only one main synthetic dataset is used for cross-method comparison; properties of benchmark datasets in Table A1 are not given (Reviewer 1).
  - The baseline Thetan Sequential (TS) is not included in the experiments (Reviewer 1).
  - The brain scan experiments appear unstable and lack any baselines or comparative methods (Reviewer 1). Superpixel results are compared only to superpixel-specific methods, not to standard clustering algorithms like MeanShift that performed well on synthetic data (Reviewer 1).
  - The boundary recall accuracy for TB superpixels drops to 0.271, while the literature (NSLIC: SLIC superpixels based on nonstationarity measure) reports SLIC achieving at least 0.6 boundary recall for comparable settings (Reviewer 3). This suggests an unfair or incomplete comparison.
- **Missing noise/outlier handling**:
  - No discussion of noise or outlier sensitivity (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 asks how noise is handled in evaluations for (H)DBSCAN/TBSCAN.
  - No ablation or theoretical treatment of how the method behaves with outliers or noise points (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Parameter robustness and intuition**:
  - Setting the distance threshold is as challenging as setting the number of clusters in KMeans; a tutorial or sensitivity study is missing (Reviewer 3).
  - The method relies on a strong assumption: minimum inter-cluster distance must exceed the threshold and both must exceed the hypersphere diameters. Violations are not discussed, nor are strategies for real-world data (Reviewer 3).
  - No systematic sensitivity analysis of θ is provided (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 requests empirical evidence on sensitivity of performance to θ.
  - How hyperparameters for the proposed method and all competitors were determined across the study is not explained (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of theoretical convergence proof**: The paper does not prove why the method converges quickly when merging Berserker centroids; it only provides a case study on simulated data (Reviewer 3). A theoretical runtime proof from a stochastic perspective is also missing (Reviewer 3).
- **Poor presentation and formatting**:
  - Figure font sizes are too small to read (Reviewer 1). Figure 4 values are particularly hard to read (Reviewer 4).
  - Excessive vertical spaces (e.g., between lines 124-125 and 141-142, before Algorithm 1 and Table 1) make the paper hard to read (Reviewers 3, 4).
  - Figure placement forces back-and-forth reading; figures are placed out of context (Reviewer 2).
  - The paper is not self-contained; formal problem statement and recalls are missing (Reviewer 4).
  - Summary statistics: Adjusted Rand Score (ARS) is mentioned but never reported in the results (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited methodological originality and coverage**:
  - The contribution is not significant enough; the approach lacks originality (Reviewer 4).
  - The introduction and related work are tiny; a clear categorization of clustering algorithms (e.g., distance-based vs. others) and TB’s position is missing (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 also notes the paper is not self-contained.
  - No embedding-based clustering analyses, e.g., for text clustering (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 2 asks whether spectral clustering variants were explored.
  - The definition of the self-defined metric “Apparent Centroid distance” (AC) is unclear – it is not formally given; it appears to only consider distances smaller than 0.1, using an unexplained “magic number” (Reviewer 1).
- **Method details and combination**:
  - The method for predicting an appropriate θ (page 7, line 366) using random walks to estimate inter-cluster distances is unclear (Reviewer 3, Q1a). Reviewer 3 also asks for specification of the centroid update rule in Algorithm 1 and how TB effectively combines with KMeans/DBSCAN (Q1b).
  - How the comparative methods are chosen for individual experiments is not explained (Reviewer 1, Q4). Reviewer 3 (Q3) asks for hyperparameter optimization or at least listing settings for all compared methods.
