## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Chain of Hindsight (CoH), a technique to align language models by converting pairwise preference data into natural language sequences. CoH converts all forms of feedback into sequences of sentences (e.g., "Good: {good response}, Bad: {bad response}") and fine-tunes the model on sequences of model generations paired with feedback, using a masked SFT objective. Training is conducted on WebGPT, Anthropic-hh, and Summarization datasets. Automatic evaluation uses ROUGE on TL;DR summarization and LM-as-preference on Anthropic-hh; human evaluation covers summarization (accuracy, coherence, coverage) and helpful dialog (helpful, harmless). CoH outperforms baselines including SFT, conditional SFT, SFT with unlikelihood, and RLHF. Analyses include scaling from 0.5B to 6B and ablations on natural language templates. Two reviewers (scores 8) were more positive; two reviewers (scores 6) raised several additional concerns about training details, evaluation breadth, and inference.

### Strengths
- Novel technique (CoH) for aligning LMs with human preferences, addressing limitations of SFT and RLHF.
- Innovative in using both positive and negative feedback; can learn from any form of feedback regardless of polarity.
- Simple and scalable: maintains the same training objective as pretraining, simplifying optimization and scaling.
- Well-motivated and interesting approach; strong performance on summarization and dialogue tasks.
- Thorough experiments with multiple datasets, strong baselines, and human evaluation.
- Meaningful and impactful analyses: ablation of natural language templates (w/o lang ablation) and scaling across model sizes.
- Well-written and easy to read.

### Weaknesses
- Inconsistency in examples: Figure 1 uses 'helpful answer'/'unhelpful answer' while Section 1 uses 'Good'/'Bad' (Reviewer 1).
- Missing comparisons with important preference optimization studies: RAFT (Reward rAnked FineTuning), DPO (Direct Preference Optimization), and rejection sampling (Reviewers 1 and 3).
- Limited evaluation scope: only dialogue and summarization benchmarks tested; no standard academic benchmarks (e.g., GLUE, SuperGLUE); no tasks requiring complex reasoning or factual knowledge (Reviewer 2).
- Need more details on RLHF implementation to understand why CoH outperforms: unclear whether the trained reward model is ineffective (report RM performance in Fig. 3) or the learning algorithm is unable to leverage it; alternate baselines (rejection sampling, reinforced self-training) should be considered; correlation between RM predictions and human preferences; choice of prompts and hyperparameters for RLHF (Reviewer 3).
- Inference strategy inadequately explored: why only prompt with 'Good:' at inference time? Advantage of joint conditioning suggests exploring prompts with both 'Bad:' and 'Good:' or iterative refinement to produce better responses. Additionally, it is unclear whether the model can be controlled to generate only the good response or will generate both good and bad, potentially doubling inference latency compared to RLHF methods (Reviewers 3 and 4).
- Need clarification on the source of CoH’s advantage over conditional SFT (C-SFT): if C-SFT trains on separate "good" and "bad" examples, why does joint conditioning in CoH perform better? Is the advantage solely from joint conditioning or other factors? (Reviewer 3).
- Largest model tested is GPT-J (6B); should evaluate on larger, more recent models like LLAMA-2 (Reviewer 4).
- Training details unclear: (a) what are "past tokens" in the random masking scheme (0–5%)? How was the masking ratio determined? Was it tuned? (b) For the pretraining regularization term, how much pretraining data is used? What is the added overhead? (Reviewer 4).
- Replication without the regularization term resulted in poor generation quality (long responses, weird grammar, repetitions), suggesting the regularization is critical to maintain quality; this was not adequately discussed (Reviewer 4).
- Missing automatic evaluation with GPT-4 for dialogue tasks (Reviewer 4).
- Missing qualitative examples of model inference outputs (Reviewer 4).
- Reviewer requested ICML 2023 review feedback and revisions; not provided (Reviewer 4).
- Scaling to N-way rankings (more than two responses per prompt, e.g., WebGPT) was not discussed (Reviewer 3 question).
- Importance of loss masking during CoH training was not analyzed (Reviewer 3 question).
