## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper establishes a novel connection between ADMM and Partitioned Variational Inference (PVI) for federated learning, showing that ADMM dual variables correspond to “site” parameters in VB under isotropic Gaussian assumptions. Based on this, the authors propose FedLap (a Laplace-approximation variant of PVI) and two extensions: FedLap-Cov (learnable covariances) and FedLap-Func (functional regularisation allowing limited data sharing). Experiments on UCI, MNIST, CIFAR‑10 compare against FedAvg, FedProx, FedDyn, showing improved accuracy. The work is praised for its clarity and novel bridge between optimisation and Bayesian views, but concerns are raised about computational cost, scalability, practical applicability of FedLap‑Func, limited experimental scope, lack of theoretical justification for global recovery, and insufficient Bayesian evaluation (uncertainty quantification).

### Strengths
- The connection between ADMM and PVI is novel, elegant, and clearly demonstrated; many reviewers find the correspondence line‑by‑line convincing and a significant contribution (R1‑S2, R2‑S1, R4‑S1, R5‑S1).
- The paper is well‑written and accessible, taking the reader through prior works with readable notation and a logical progression from ADMM/PVI to FedLap and its variants (R1‑S1, R2‑S3).
- The intuitive extension to learnable covariances (FedLap‑Cov) and functional regularisation (FedLap‑Func) follows naturally from the derived connection (R2‑S2, R5‑S1).
- Empirical results are extensive, covering UCI, MNIST, CIFAR‑10 with hyperparameter tuning, and show that FedLap variants outperform both weak (FedAvg) and strong (FedDyn) baselines (R2‑S4, R4‑S2).
- The technique for ensuring positive‑definiteness of precision matrices (after Eq. 14) is noted as interesting and useful (R1‑S2).
- The paper avoids self‐promotional language, using method names throughout (R2‑minor).

### Weaknesses
- **Computational complexity not adequately analysed:**  
  Missing detailed FLOPs breakdown, communication overhead (message size, traffic), and scalability discussion. FedLap‑Cov introduces quadratic communication and storage for the upper triangle of covariance matrices, which raises concerns for many‑parameter models (e.g., ResNet‑50) and large‑scale federated settings (R1‑W1, R2‑minor, R4‑W1).
- **Practical concerns with FedLap‑Func:**  
  Sharing inputs (even a few) between clients and server alters the federated learning paradigm, raising privacy issues and requiring a trusted server – a strong assumption not always feasible. The practical benefit over FedLap‑Cov is marginal (sometimes worse), and no appropriate task is identified where this trade‑off is justified (R1‑W2, R5‑W1, R5‑Q4). The ratio of improvement to computational cost is questioned (R1‑S3).
- **Limited experimental scope:**  
  Benchmarks are restricted to UCI, MNIST, CIFAR‑10 (no higher‑dimensional or complex datasets like ImageNet). Number of clients is small (10–100), and scalability to many clients or high data heterogeneity is not tested. Statistical rigor is insufficient: the method for bolding “top two” results is unspecified; accounting for standard errors could render many differences non‑significant (e.g., FedDyn vs FedLap on CIFAR‑10 at 50 rounds) (R1‑W4, R4‑W2).
- **Lack of theoretical justification for global solution recovery:**  
  The paper does not demonstrate whether the federated local models can recover the true global solution, either theoretically or numerically (R3‑W1).
- **Connection to Bayes questioned:**  
  The Bayesian interpretation is used effectively only as a preconditioner; the method is evaluated purely from an optimisation perspective (point estimate at inference). If no uncertainty quantification or marginalisation is performed, the Bayesian framing may be unnecessary (R2‑minor, R3‑W2). The paper does not include standard Bayesian FL evaluations (e.g., uncertainty calibration) and omits relevant Bayesian FL literature such as FedPA, Langevin dynamics, and HMC methods (R3‑W3, R3‑Q).
- **Missing limitation discussion:**  
  Limitations are not clearly stated (R2‑minor, R4‑W1). For instance, the scalability of covariance matrices to high‑dimensional parameter spaces (R1‑W3, R2‑minor) and the reliance on a trusted server for FedLap‑Func are not addressed.
- **Technical clarity issues:**  
  - Equation 13: The delta approximation uses gradients evaluated at a point \(m_{\text{old}}\) rather than the current mean \(m\), which may be a necessary “hotfix” rather than a principled Taylor expansion. A typo in Eq. 13 (\(l_k(m_k)\) should be \(l_k(m)\)) adds confusion (R2‑minor, R2‑Q2).  
  - The term “Lap” in FedLap suggests a Laplace approximation, but the variational distribution remains Gaussian – clarification is needed (R5‑Q3).  
  - The dimension of the second auxiliary/dual variable (used in preconditioning) is unclear (R1‑Q3).  
  - The definition of \(\hat t_k^*\) first appears at Eq. (5) but is needed earlier at Eq. (4); the initial guess \(\hat t_k^*= q_k/p_0\) is incorrect (R5‑Q2).  
  - The number of training steps per round in Algorithm Eq. (17) is not specified (R1‑Q6).
