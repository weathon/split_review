## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MoTEF, integrating momentum tracking and error feedback for decentralized non-convex optimization with communication compression. It achieves an asymptotic convergence rate matching distributed SGD without bounded heterogeneity assumptions, and supports arbitrary contractive compressors. A variance-reduction variant MoTEF-VR is also presented. Theoretical results under the PL condition are included. Experiments compare against Choco-SGD and BEER.

### Strengths
1. **Strong theoretical advances**: MoTEF attains a convergence rate matching distributed SGD without requiring strong assumptions such as bounded gradient or global heterogeneity (Reviewers 1, 4). This resolves a long-standing theoretical obstacle.
2. **Supports arbitrary contractive compressors**: The algorithm works with variance-bounded contractive compressors without requiring unbiasedness (Reviewer 1).
3. **First linear speedup with contractive compressors**: MoTEF is the first decentralized algorithm to achieve linear speedup in the number of nodes under contractive compressors (Reviewer 2).
4. **Variance-reduction extension**: MoTEF-VR provides an improved rate in the stochastic setting (Reviewer 1).
5. **Analysis under PL condition**: Theoretical guarantees are also provided under the PL condition (Reviewer 1).
6. **Reasonable assumptions and impressive bound**: Given the algorithm’s complexity, the convergence bound is achieved under reasonable assumptions; the absence of data heterogeneity assumptions is particularly notable (Reviewer 4).
7. **Empirical insight on network topology**: Experiments show that sensitivity to the spectral gap \(\rho\) can be much lower than the theoretical bound suggests, offering valuable practical insights (Reviewer 4).
8. **Clear presentation and thorough comparison**: The paper is well-written with comprehensive comparisons to existing work; the state of the art is correctly presented (Reviewers 3, 4, 5).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty – strong similarity to centralized work**: The algorithm appears to be a direct extension of the centralized algorithm EControl (Gao et al., 2024) to the decentralized setting, without significant conceptual breakthroughs (Reviewer 2). More explanation is needed on how the three components (gradient tracking, momentum, error feedback) specifically remove data heterogeneity, and why their combination yields linear speedup (Reviewers 2, 4). The insight behind the algorithm’s development is insufficiently clarified (Reviewer 2).

2. **Output variable is not available**: Theorem 1 establishes convergence for \(x_{\text{out}} = \bar{x}_t\), the average of local variables, which is not accessible in decentralized optimization. The analysis only proves gradient norm convergence for this unattainable quantity; it should also demonstrate that the algorithm’s output (the local variables) achieves small gradient norm and consensus (\(\Omega_3\to 0\)). If \(\bar{x}_t\) is used in experiments, it would be unfair to baselines that do not have access to the global average (Reviewer 5).

3. **Incomplete analysis of total communication complexity**: The paper does not thoroughly discuss the total communication cost (e.g., total bits transmitted). Most compression methods reduce only per-iteration cost, not the number of iterations; the interplay between compression parameter, convergence rate, and total cost should be analyzed. Scaling with dimension, number of nodes, and desired accuracy is not addressed. Comparisons should include total communicated bits, not just iterations (Reviewers 1, 3).

4. **Numerical experiments are insufficient for practical claims**:
   - Only simple models (MLP) and datasets are tested; missing more realistic tasks such as transformers, ResNet on CIFAR-10, or large-scale decentralized learning (Reviewers 1, 2). CNN results are missing (Reviewer 3).
   - Baselines are outdated: only Choco-SGD and BEER (2022 or earlier), whose convergence rates are clearly worse per Table 1; CEDAS and DeepSqueeze (with closer SOTA rates) are not compared (Reviewer 3).
   - Experiments should cover a wider range of compression techniques and hyperparameters (Reviewer 1). The dataset for the MLP task is not specified (Reviewer 2).

5. **Theoretical improvement over CEDAS is marginal**: The only clear advantage over CEDAS is removing the need for an additional unbiased compressor. Whether contractive compressors are more efficient than unbiased ones in practice is not validated. A comparison of full convergence complexity (not just asymptotic rates) is needed (Reviewer 3).

6. **Suboptimal dependence on network topology**: The convergence bound has a worse dependence on the spectral gap \(\rho\) compared to decentralized algorithms without compression, which could limit applicability in sparse or poorly connected networks (Reviewer 2). The theoretical gap remains even though experiments show lower sensitivity (Reviewer 4’s observation does not negate this concern).

7. **Complex bounds with unclear trade-offs**: The bounds in (11) involve a trade-off on \(\alpha\) among three terms; a simpler unified bound would be helpful (Reviewer 4).

8. **Minor missing details and documentation**: The code URL is placed at the end of page 9 (Reviewer 4). The paper “LEAD” by Liu et al. was published at ICLR 2021, not just as an arXiv preprint; the title of “Randcom” has changed (Reviewer 5).
