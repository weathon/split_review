## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper analyzes Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for aligning LLMs with human preferences, identifying three properties it calls the 3D-properties: drastic drop in rejected response likelihood, degradation into response suppression, and dispersion effect on unseen responses. The authors provide theoretical explanations and validate these phenomena on toy models and real-world tasks (math reasoning, instruction following). They propose regularization techniques (adaptive gradient weighting via separate β⁺ and β⁻, and adding an SFT loss) and compare on-policy vs. off-policy DPO. Results are supported by experiments on MATH, Poem, and Slogan datasets.

### Strengths
- **Importance**: Studying DPO’s limitations is significant due to its widespread use; the paper addresses a crucial gap between DPO and RM-based methods.
- **Original synthesis**: It originally summarizes and theoretically analyzes several DPO degradation phenomena that were observed in previous work.
- **Mixed methodology**: Provides a balanced combination of theoretical analysis (gradient dynamics) and empirical validation (toy model + LLM experiments).
- **Well-structured**: The paper is clearly written and easy to follow; the toy example effectively supports the main claims.
- **Novel comparative analysis**: The on-policy vs. off-policy comparison on toy models is a valuable contribution.
- **Effective regularization proposals**: The adaptive gradient weighting for chosen and rejected responses is a novel and effective technique (noted by one reviewer as a strength).
- **Thorough experiments**: The experiments are well-conducted and cover both synthetic and real tasks.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The three observations have been widely studied in prior work. One of the proposed regularizations (SFT loss) is already widely used in existing preference learning approaches [1]. Another reviewer argued the regularization techniques lack substantial significance (e.g., optimal reject β is only 0.02 lower than chosen β in the Poem task; no gradient comparisons showing it addresses the large gap in Figure 2).
- **Missing comparisons**: There is no comparison with many existing DPO-improvement methods (e.g., [2], and more advanced baselines like SimPO [3]).
- **LLM scope**: Evaluation is limited to a narrow set of models (one reviewer requests Meta-Llama3; another asks for a wider range of LLMs). The Paper lacks evaluation on standard benchmarks used in preference optimization (AlpacaEval2, Arena-Hard, MT-Bench) for general instruction following; the self-built Poem and Slogan datasets are acknowledged as limited.
- **Dataset scope**: Experiments focus heavily on math; more task diversity is needed. One reviewer questions why the paper focuses primarily on math datasets.
- **Code and reproducibility**: Code is not open source, which limits reproducibility. The specific model used in the toy experiment is not named.
- **Inadequate analysis for Property 3 (Dispersion)**: The explanation lacks mathematical formulation and appears to merely restate empirical phenomena. It is unclear whether dispersion goes to unseen samples or to earlier samples from the preference dataset (since optimization is mini-batch). The toy model setup (treating each input/output as a token rather than a full prompt/response) is not a good approximation of real LLM training; improvement would require larger sample sizes and mini-batch optimization with fewer epochs.
- **On-policy vs. off-policy confounds**: The comparison relies on different data sources, introducing potential confounds. A more direct setup (e.g., historical-only vs. semi-on-policy DPO like iterative DPO) would be more robust.
- **Parameter tuning complexity**: Flex-DPO requires tuning two parameters (β⁺ and β⁻); Figure 4 provides some guidance but practical tuning guidelines are still lacking.
- **GPT-4 labeling reliability for MATH**: In Section 4.2, best and worst responses were selected by GPT-4 for MATH. Given that GPT-4’s accuracy on MATH is only slightly above 50%, this approach seems potentially unreliable; direct answer verification would be preferable.
- **Theoretical gap in Section 3.4**: The gradient analysis shifts focus from π to r, creating a disconnect with the earlier analysis on π, and the connection between RM and DPO is not fully convincing.
- **Presentation issues**: Text size in Figures 2 and 3 is small. The description of Scenarios 1–4 (key for understanding on-policy vs. off-policy) is only in the appendix; bringing it to the main text would improve clarity.
- **Figure inconsistency**: The probability distribution in the bottom-right figure does not match the leftmost figure in Figure 2 (unseen probability at 500 epochs approaches 1 in Figure 2 but is zero in Figure 1; chosen probabilities also do not align).
- **Unclear initialization assumptions**: One reviewer questions how the parameter distribution initialization assumptions apply to or relate to LLMs.
- **Unclear β effect**: The effect of different β values in real-world experiments is not discussed; detailed parameter adjustment is only provided for the toy experiment.

*Note: One reviewer (score 8) was particularly positive about the significance and thoroughness of the theoretical and empirical validation, but still raised concerns about presentation, on-policy comparison design, and GPT-4 selection reliability. Another reviewer (score 5) was less confident (confidence 1).*
