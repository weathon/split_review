## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes AdPO, an adversarial defense for large vision-language models (LVLMs) that reframes adversarial training as a preference optimization problem. The method generates image description pairs (preferred vs. non-preferred) on clean and adversarial images and applies a DPO-style loss to the vision encoder (CLIP ViT). Experiments on OpenFlamingo and LLaVA across COCO, Flickr30k, TextVQA, VQAv2 show improved robustness with limited clean accuracy drop. Reviewers are split: two (R2, R4) highlight the innovation and empirical gains, while two (R1, R3) critique limited novelty, weak evaluation scope, missed baselines, and lack of complexity analysis.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and Motivation**: Well-organized, clearly written, with a straightforward and reasonable motivation (R1, R2).
- **Novelty of Formulation**: First to treat adversarial training as a preference optimization problem; the combination of preferred (online model-generated captions) and adversarial image optimization is innovative and avoids the need for labeled data (R3, R4). (However, R1 finds the novelty weak—see Weaknesses.)
- **Effectiveness**: Non-trivial improvements over baselines (FARE, TeCoA); better clean accuracy–robustness trade-off across multiple datasets and models; significant performance gains in the reported settings (R2, R3, R4).
- **Evaluation Scope**: Well-evaluated on two LVLM architectures (OpenFlamingo, LLaVA) and four diverse tasks/datasets (COCO, Flickr30k, TextVQA, VQAv2) (R2). (R1, R3 criticize the scope as limited—see Weaknesses.)
- **Practicality**: Only modifies the image encoder (CLIP ViT); can be trained on any image dataset without human annotations (R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty Concerns**: The method is essentially a direct application of DPO to the LVLM domain; the main contribution is generating preference pairs, and all components are well-known. Contribution to the field is considered weak (R1).
- **Missing Related Work**: Many recent LVLM attack and defense papers (including survey works) are not cited, making the discussion incomplete and the comparison potentially unfair (R1).
- **Limited Model & Baseline Coverage**: Experiments use only two relatively simple LVLM models; evaluations on stronger models (e.g., GPT-4V, BLIP-2) are missing. Only two defense baselines (FARE, TeCoA) are compared; at least 4–5 baselines are needed (R1). Table 2 shows only a 2% gain over FARE² on a small sample, with no improvement at larger radii (R3).
- **Weak Attack Strength and Attack Variety**: Adversarial training and evaluation only performed at epsilon 2 and 4; robustness at larger perturbation strengths (e.g., 8) is unknown. Only PGD-like attacks are used; other strong attacks (CW, attacks with adversarial image smoothing, other untargeted methods) are absent (R2, R4).
- **Lack of In-Depth Robustness Analysis**: No analysis of how AdPO defends against existing specific LVLM attackers or why it works (R1).
- **Complexity and Efficiency Overlooked**: The computational cost, amount of adversarial data used, and overall time complexity compared to FARE and TeCoA are not reported. Since AdPO computes loss over the whole LVLM (gradients propagate through LLM) and uses online description generation, it likely has much higher cost, making the comparison unfair without standardization (R1, R2, R3). Direct adversarial training of the whole model (possible with 32 A100 GPUs) is not compared (R2).
- **Unfair Comparison Setup**: AdPO fine-tunes the whole LVLM (even if the LLM is frozen, gradients backpropagate through it), whereas FARE and TeCoA only fine-tune the vision encoder. This discrepancy in computational effort is not accounted for; stronger baselines should be allowed more epochs/data to match AdPO's capacity (R3).
- **Unclarified Phenomena**: Clean COCO performance of AdPO² exceeds that of the base CLIP model—why? (R2). The subjective example in Figure 3 (boy in field) is not clearly better than the FARE caption (R3).
- **Missing Ablations and Extensions**: No ablation isolating the contributions of the preferred vs. adversarial optimizations (R4). No comparison with other DPO variants (R2). The method is only applied to CLIP; end-to-end AdPO might yield further improvement (R2). The effect of scaling up with more high-quality DPO data is unexplored (R2).
- **Missing Downstream Evaluations**: Important LVLM capabilities such as hallucination rates and robustness to jailbreak prompts are not evaluated; degradation in these areas would reduce practical usability (R3).
- **Equation Overlap**: Equation (4) closely resembles the loss from FARE (Eq. 3 in Schlarmann et al., ICML 2024) without proper discussion, which may be misleading (R3).
- **Joint Optimization Complexity**: The joint loss adds training challenges (hyperparameter tuning, convergence); these trade-offs are not discussed (R4).
