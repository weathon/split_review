## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper reformulates the LVLM generation task as a binary classification problem to probe refusal tendencies on harmful and benign queries. It identifies two defense mechanisms: safety shift (increasing refusal probability for all queries) and harmfulness discrimination (enhancing the model’s ability to distinguish benign from harmful). Using these mechanisms, the authors propose inter-mechanism and intra-mechanism ensemble strategies. Evaluations on LLaVA-1.5 models (7B/13B) with MM-SafetyBench and MOSSBench demonstrate effectiveness in balancing safety and helpfulness.

### Strengths

- **Novel characterization of defense mechanisms**: The binary classification reformulation is a fresh, valuable angle for understanding jailbreak defenses. The two metrics (Mean Shift and Distance Change) are reasonable and help visualize differences between defense types.  
- **Clear presentation and motivation**: The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and the problem is important. The motivation for investigating jailbreak defenses is clear.  
- **Comprehensive empirical validation**: Extensive experiments across a variety of jailbreak defenses, with rigorous methodology. The analysis of inter- and intra-mechanism ensemble strategies examines the safety-helpfulness balance.  
- **Interesting insights**: Sections 4.4 and 4.5 provide valuable findings on the ensemble mechanisms.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited model diversity**: Evaluation is restricted to LLaVA-1.5 (7B and 13B). The findings may not generalize to other LVLMs (e.g., CogVLM, InternLM-XC) or to text-only LLMs. One reviewer (R1, rating 5, more critical than the other three rating 6) emphasized this limitation as a major concern, while others also noted the need for models with different architectures and a broader LLaVA series test.  
- **Insufficient assessment of response quality on benign queries**: The evaluation uses a pattern-matching judge that only checks refusal/affirmation, ignoring the actual quality or usefulness of generated responses. Several reviewers pointed out that combining multiple defenses (e.g., query refactoring like “Caption w/o image”) may harm helpfulness or introduce practical overhead. Concerns include:  
  - Refactoring defenses likely degrade response quality on benign queries.  
  - The analysis does not consider feasibility, cost-effectiveness, or real-time speed impact.  
  - The ensemble analysis lacks depth—specific suggestions for optimal combinations and justification are missing.  
- **Limited attack diversity and dataset coverage**: The datasets (MM-SafetyBench, MOSSBench) rely on direct, non-stealthy malicious queries (e.g., text hidden at the bottom of images). Experiments with stronger and more varied attack methods (e.g., sophisticated jailbreaks, multi-step attacks) are missing, raising concerns about real-world representativeness.  
- **Potential mismatch between classification analysis and generative behavior**: The mechanistic analysis is conducted on the binary classification reformulation; the insights may not fully transfer to actual generative settings. One reviewer specifically questioned the representativeness of the binary classifier (see Figure 4) and whether its power can be improved.  
- **Missing comparisons to mainstream optimization-based defenses**: The paper does not include or discuss model-level defenses such as PPO or DPO, which are standard in current safety alignment research.  
- **Presentation details**: Figure and table captions are overly simplistic (noted by R2).  
- **Unclear motivation for focusing on LVLMs**: The reformulation is straightforward and equally applicable to text-only LLMs, but the paper does not justify why LVLMs are the primary focus (noted by R3).  
- **Degrees of mechanisms**: Figure 3(b) shows that a combination of two safety-shift methods (SR + MO) increases distance from 0.5 to 0.63, suggesting defenses may exhibit varying degrees of both safety shift and harmfulness discrimination rather than being purely one category (noted by R1). This contradicts the strict dichotomy and warrants further investigation.
