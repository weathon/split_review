## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes FMP-AE, a hybrid unsupervised anomaly detection model for time series that combines a 1D-CNN-based Feature map Matrix Profile with an Autoencoder. The key novelty is a Matrix Profile loss function added to the Autoencoder’s reconstruction loss. The model is evaluated on the UCR250 benchmark dataset and shows strong precision, recall, and F1-scores. Reviewers note the paper is generally well written and the problem is important, but raise significant concerns about innovation, evaluation scope, baselines, and experimental validation.

### Strengths
- **Combining Matrix Profile with Deep Learning is a novel idea for time series anomaly detection.** The integration of Matrix Profile (global/local subsequence similarity) with an Autoencoder’s reconstruction capability is appreciated across reviewers.
- **Empirical results on UCR250 are strong.** The model shows high recall (99.60), precision (72.80), and F1 (84.12) on this benchmark, and the ablation study demonstrates the contribution of each component (Matrix Profile loss, AE). (Reviewers 1, 5)
- **The paper is well-organized and clearly written.** Figures and demonstrations help explain the method. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5)
- **The problem of unsupervised time series anomaly detection is important and challenging.** (Reviewers 3, 4, 5)

### Weaknesses
1. **Incremental methodological innovation and insufficient theoretical grounding.** The combination of Matrix Profile (MP) with an Autoencoder reconstruction loss is straightforward, mainly building on existing techniques without substantial theoretical novelty. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). There is no formal analysis of why MP loss improves sensitivity beyond reconstruction loss alone, nor how its gradients interact with reconstruction gradients during training. The mathematical properties of MP that make it suitable for local anomalies are not discussed. (Reviewer 1). The method is described as “easy” and “marginal improvement” (Reviewers 3, 4). The autoencoder structure (CNN with max-pool) is common, and the paper does not rigorously analyze the feature space learned by the combined model versus a standard AE. (Reviewer 4).

2. **Limited dataset evaluation.** All experiments are conducted only on the UCR250 dataset. This dataset consists primarily of short time series and does not represent the diversity of anomaly types encountered in practice. (Reviewer 1). Standard multivariate TSAD datasets (MSL, SMAP, SMD, PSM, SWaT, NeurIPS-TS) are missing. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4, 5). Without evaluation on longer or more complex time series, generalizability and robustness are not established. (Reviewer 1).

3. **Outdated and incomplete baseline comparisons.** The compared methods are mostly from 2021–2022 or earlier, omitting recent state-of-the-art approaches (e.g., DCdetector, ModernTCN, D3R, SimAD, NPSR, GPT4TS, SensitiveHUE, and other 2023–2024 works). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5). This prevents a fair assessment of FMP-AE’s competitiveness in the current landscape. The paper does not compare with the classic Matrix Profile algorithm using TCN/MLP feature extractors, which would help isolate the benefit of the deep learning component. (Reviewer 2).

4. **Lack of efficiency validation and complexity analysis.** The paper claims the model is efficient due to the Matrix Profile, but provides no experimental comparison of training/inference times, space complexity, or computational overhead of computing the MP versus a standard autoencoder. (Reviewers 2, 4). There is no justification for how the distance calculations of MP lead to more efficient feature extraction. (Reviewer 4). Hardware details, batch sizes, and optimization settings needed for reproducibility are missing. (Reviewer 4).

5. **Weak experimental analysis and organization.** The ablation study lacks analysis of the “1D-CNN + AE” variant, only comparing the full model to components. (Reviewer 2). Figures 4–7 do not indicate which dataset or time series is being analyzed, lack original series and anomaly regions, and lack necessary analysis or comparison with other methods. (Reviewer 2). Figure 10 is confusing with large blank areas. (Reviewer 2). The conclusions from Figures 5–7 overlap and do not provide insightful findings beyond simple performance comparisons. (Reviewer 2). The experimental organization does not effectively verify the paper’s motivation. (Reviewer 2).

6. **Misunderstanding of deep learning anomaly detection challenges.** The paper claims data imbalance harms deep learning methods, but many unsupervised methods assume fewer anomalies and benefit from this imbalance. (Reviewer 4). Additionally, the stated challenges (label scarcity, generalizability, efficiency) are not all addressed: only efficiency is claimed via MP, but label scarcity and generalizability are not solved or discussed. (Reviewer 4).

7. **Discrepancy in reported results versus ADTransformer.** The reported recall (99.60) and precision (72.80) differ from ADTransformer’s original UCR results, and the paper does not explain why. The high recall at the expense of precision raises questions about the F1 metric’s adequacy. (Reviewer 5).

8. **Introduction focus and problem motivation.** The introduction should focus specifically on time series anomaly detection (TSAD) rather than general anomaly detection, and should explain why past methods are insufficient. (Reviewer 2). The paper lacks a clear argument for why existing methods fail to address the identified challenges and how FMP-AE overcomes them. (Reviewer 3). There is no theoretical or experimental analysis showing why the method solves existing challenges. (Reviewer 2).

9. **Request for open-source code.** The code is not open-sourced, hindering reproducibility and follow-up research. (Reviewer 2).
