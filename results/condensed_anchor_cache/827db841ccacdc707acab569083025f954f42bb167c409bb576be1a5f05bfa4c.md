## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Physics Informed Neurally Constructed ODE Networks (PINECONEs), merging neural ODEs with physics-informed loss. The dynamics within a neural ODE are expanded to include a system of ODEs whose solution provides the partial derivatives governing a PDE system. The method is evaluated on 1D transport and Burgers’ equations, showing that training with first-order optimizers requires fewer iterations and achieves higher accuracy compared to standard PINNs.

### Strengths
- Original approach combining neural ODE architectures with physics-informed loss. One reviewer (R3) noted this as the first work to combine neural ODE with PINN, and another (R4, score 6) called it an “interesting framework.” Other reviewers (R1, R5) questioned novelty but acknowledged the proposal.
- The formulation extends the neural ODE system with additional differential equations, yielding efficient evaluation of derivatives w.r.t. input, which can be used for training with the PINN loss (R3).
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R2, R5). R1 also noted decent writing and organization.
- Experimental results on two simple 1D PDEs show that PINECONE reaches lower error and converges in fewer iterations than a standard PINN when both use first-order optimizers (R1, R3, R5).
- The framework provides a continuous solution (like PINN) and replaces a feedforward network with an ANODE, storing partial derivatives as system variables (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient baselines and comparisons.** The method is only compared to vanilla PINNs. No comparisons are made against other NODE-based PDE solvers (e.g., Lee & Parish, Rackauckas et al. 2021) mentioned in related work (R1, R2). Also missing comparisons with advanced PINN variants such as those with adaptive activation functions, Augmented PINNs, XPINNs, or other improved training techniques (R1, R4, R5). Without these, the true contribution and advantages over the state-of-the-art are unclear (R1, R2, R5).
- **Limited experimental evaluation.** All experiments are on 1D transport and Burgers’ equations—low-dimensional synthetic problems. The paper claims suitability for high-dimensional and real-world data, but no such tests are provided (R1, R2, R3, R5). More complex PDEs, higher dimensions, and varied boundary conditions are needed to assess practical applicability (R1, R2, R3, R5).
- **Unfair or incomplete comparison with PINNs.**  
  * Only first-order optimizers (Adam) are used, while it is known that PINNs benefit significantly from second-order optimizers like L-BFGS (R1, R2, R3, R4). The paper does not show how PINECONE compares to PINN when both use L-BFGS, nor whether PINECONE similarly benefits from second-order methods (R1).  
  * The number of iterations is not a fair metric; CPU time per iteration differs substantially due to neural ODE integration cost (R2). No analysis of total training time or memory consumption is provided (R1, R2).  
  * The vanilla PINN implementation may be suboptimal—standard PINN works well on Burgers’ without issues when properly tuned (R4). The authors do not detail activation functions, initialization, or loss formulation used for PINN (R4).
- **Methodology and clarity gaps.**  
  * The connection between the extended PINECONE system and the adjoint sensitivity method for memory-efficient derivative computation is not adequately explained. A more detailed derivation is needed (R3).  
  * One reviewer (R3) noted a discrepancy: Section 3.1 says “PINECONE reaches the minimum error of the PINN at around iteration 2,700,” but the graph shows around 1,200.  
  * The manuscript contains typos and formatting issues: missing τ in arguments of F in Eq(4) (R2); Eq(7) missing “=0” (R1); large equation in Section 2 not numbered and with a misleading equals sign (R2); typos “LBFGs”, missing parentheses in Sec 1.2, Eq(8) formatting, “2,5000” in Sec 3.2 (R3).
- **Performance limitations.** In Burgers’ equation (Fig. 2), PINECONE appears unable to learn the shock wave; the accuracy is not satisfactory even with the proposed method (R2). This raises concerns about handling solutions with sharp gradients.
- **No theoretical results.** One reviewer (R4) noted the lack of any theoretical support for the method.
- **Memory/time efficiency claims unsubstantiated.** The paper claims PINECONE is more memory- and time-efficient, but provides no empirical evidence; neural ODE solvers are known to be costly, and scaling to many ODEs may be prohibitive (R1).
- **Novelty concerns.** One reviewer (R5) stated the idea is not novel, citing existing works combining neural differential equations with physics-informed loss. Another (R1) found it unclear how PINECONE differs from methods mentioned in related work.
- **Missing dataset and problem scope.** The paper claims suitability for data-driven discovery, but does not test that capability (R3). Real-world or high-dimensional datasets are absent despite the claim (R2).
- **Minor missing details.**  
  * Loss function notation: what does s represent in the loss (bottom of page 2)? Are the arguments to ODESolve clearly defined? (R1)  
  * Supportive information for the claim “Hybrid modeling frameworks ... have yielded impressive results” is requested (R4).  
  * Network configuration details (number of layers, widths, activation) are not provided in full (R4).  
  * Reviewer 4 requested experiments using SDGD (“Tackling the Curse of Dimensionality with Physics-Informed Neural Networks”) to test scalability.  
  * Reviewer 4 asked how the method relates to other PINN variants (APINNs, XPINNs) and suggested mentioning them in related work.
- **Reviewer disagreement on overall assessment.** One reviewer (R4, score 6) found the framework interesting and rated contribution “good,” while the other four (scores 3) rated contribution “fair” or “poor” due to the above weaknesses. R4 also noted that low-dimensional problems can be solved with traditional PDE solvers, questioning the need for PINN/PINECONE in such settings. R4 further recommended comparing with practical PINN training methods that allow scaling.
