## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper provides a theoretical analysis of multi-round auto-regressive reasoning (e.g., chain-of-thought, self-refinement, debating) in LLMs, covering approximation, learnability, and generalization. It shows that Transformers with finite context windows can serve as universal approximators for Turing-computable functions via multi-round reasoning, extends PAC learning to sequence generation beyond the context window, and analyzes how generalization error propagates across rounds and how interventions constrain that error.

### Strengths
- **Important problem** – It was not previously clear whether or why multi-round reasoning works; this paper builds a theoretical foundation (Reviewer 4).
- **Wide-range theoretical analysis** – Covers approximation, learnability, and generalization properties (Reviewer 4).
- **Learnability characterization** – Shows that multi-round inference helps make learning more efficient, at least in principle (Reviewer 2).
- **Generalization analysis** – Characterizes generalization ability of such models (Reviewer 2).
- **Well-written paper** – Multiple reviewers note the paper is well written (Reviewers 1, 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Proof clarity and completeness** – Multiple reviewers find the proofs difficult to follow, with missing definitions and insufficiently explained steps.
  - Lemma 4.1 only provides a trivial sketch, not a sound mathematical proof (Reviewer 1). The proof is hard to follow: many variables are undefined (e.g., acceptance window *k*, *h_t*); it is unclear how orthogonality of the embedding is maintained after residual connections (which would destroy surjectivity of the encoding), why the Boolean-circuit characterization is necessary, or where the bound in Section A.5.2 originates (for error thresholds going to zero, the lower bound on Q goes to 1, which is trivial). Section A.5.3 lists requirements but does not provide a construction of the state-transition implementation (Reviewer 2).
  - Theorem 4.3’s proof lacks clarity: how is the output of one round encoded as a single output token, and how is the Turing machine configuration recovered from that token to proceed with the simulation? (Reviewer 2)
  - Several key terms are undefined: e.g., line 219: *Γ^* Q Γ^** is undefined; line 216: “q_0 x #” is unclear; Assumption 5.3 needs a comment on Lipschitz-continuity and boundedness of cross-entropy, akin to Assumptions 5.1 and 5.2 (Reviewer 2).
  - Line 895 has a missing citation (Reviewer 2).
  - The paper contains numerous typos and grammatical errors (e.g., line 77, 117, 120, 142, 161, 216, 797) (Reviewer 2).
  - The paper only contains theoretical proof and could benefit from figures, tables, or other methods to support understanding (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of experiments** – The practical implications are only briefly discussed; simple experiments to support the theoretical claims would strengthen the work (Reviewer 3). There are no experiments to prove the claims (Reviewer 4).
- **Prior work and significance** – The paper largely ignores prior work showing that RNNs (and by extension Transformers) are super-Turing due to continuous weight space and continuous activation functions (Siegelman, 1990s). Given that a Transformer is an extension of an RNN, it is obvious that a Transformer can simulate an arbitrary TM, making the approximation result less novel (Reviewer 1). The theoretical computational complexity of RNNs/Transformers is long known; the main difficulty is showing that available training and prompting mechanisms can yield such configurations, but Section 5 focuses on information/sample complexity rather than the training algorithm (Reviewer 1).
- **Learning and generalization connection** – The learnability analysis in Section 5 does not clearly build upon or relate to the approximation ability in Section 4; it is also unclear how the generalization analysis in Section 6 connects to both (Reviewer 2).
- **Round definition** – In Section 5.3, the definition of “round” is imprecise. If a round is a single forward pass, generating N tokens in R rounds (N/R tokens per round) is not different from N tokens in one round in terms of total forward passes. Clarity is needed on whether rounds indicate the frequency of supervising information during intermediate steps (Reviewer 2).
- **Negative effects of interventions** – The paper claims interventions (e.g., self-refinement) can mitigate error propagation, but in practice such interventions can be error-prone. How would generalization dynamics change if interventions are harmful? (Reviewer 3; this overlapping question is included as a weakness).
