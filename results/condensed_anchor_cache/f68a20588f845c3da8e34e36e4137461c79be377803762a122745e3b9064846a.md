## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes EC-Diffuser, a diffusion-based behavioral cloning method for multi-object goal-conditioned manipulation from pixel observations. It uses a pre-trained object-centric encoder (Deep Latent Particles, DLP) to decompose observations into latent vectors per object/background, then an entity-centric Transformer to handle the unordered latents while predicting actions and states, and a diffusion decoder to generate action-state trajectories conditioned on initial and goal representations. Experiments are conducted on PushCube, PushT, and FrankaKitchen. The method demonstrates improved performance and zero-shot generalization to more objects than seen during training. Code and videos are provided.

### Strengths
- **Writing and positioning.** The paper is well-written and clearly articulates the motivation for multi-object manipulation from pixels. It provides a comprehensive review of related work and clearly distinguishes its contributions (R3).
- **Novel integration.** Combining object-centric representations (DLP) with diffusion-based behavioral cloning and an entity-centric Transformer for unordered latent handling is a noteworthy contribution (R3). The idea of using diffusion to predict actions through object-level information is interesting and yields performance improvements (R2). *However, some reviewers (R1, R4) consider the novelty limited as it combines existing components without fundamentally new architectural elements; this contrast is noted.*
- **Performance.** EC-Diffuser consistently outperforms baselines, including the non-diffusion SOTA VQ-BeT and EIT-BC, especially as the number of objects increases (R2, R3, R4). Ablation studies (Table 5) show the importance of the DLP encoder and goal-conditioned diffusion (R4).
- **Thorough experiments.** The paper evaluates on three diverse simulated benchmarks and provides ablations (e.g., necessity of state generation in Section 5.4) that effectively demonstrate each component’s role (R3, R4). Generalization to different numbers of objects is shown (Section 5.2).
- **Available resources.** Code is provided and video rollouts are on the project website, enabling inspection (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty.** EC-Diffuser is a combination of existing components: object-centric encoder from DLP, transformer sequence modeling from VQ-BeT, and diffusion decoding from Diffuser (R1, R4). The core innovation lies in the specific combination rather than new architectural elements or training methodologies (R4). The paper does not sufficiently highlight unique challenges or adaptations required for the object-centric diffusion setting (R1). *Reviewer R3 found the integration novel; this disagreement is preserved.*
- **Lack of real-world evaluation.** Experiments are limited to simulated environments (PushCube, PushT, FrankaKitchen). The method’s robustness to sensor noise, model inaccuracies, and object property variations is not assessed, raising questions about practical applicability (R1, R4). It is unclear if the model can be applied to real-world datasets like nuScenes (as VQ-BeT does) or what changes are needed for real-world deployment (R4).
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons.** Only one non-diffusion baseline (VQ-BeT) is compared, and VQ-BeT performs extremely poorly on PushCube and PushT (2 of 3 test environments) (R2). Missing comparisons on environments where baselines excel, such as Multimodal Ant, BlockPush, UR3 BlockPush, or nuScenes self-driving, would better demonstrate advantages (R2). It is also unclear whether baselines could be applied sequentially for multi-object tasks (R1).
- **Heavy reliance on DLP encoder.** The method’s performance heavily depends on the DLP encoder; without it, results are comparable to VQ-BeT (R4). No experiments with alternative object-centric encoders (e.g., Slot Attention, simpler CNNs) are provided, which would test generality and robustness (R3). The paper lacks analysis of how different encoder inductive biases affect performance (R3).
- **Limited generalization experiments.** Generalization to novel object shapes, textures, or views is not tested. For example, what is the success rate when T-cubes replace normal cubes? (R1). The method also cannot capture object-centric notions in FrankaKitchen (R4). Additional studies on varying object counts, physical properties (mass, friction), and time horizons are missing (R3, R4).
- **Unclear advantages of diffusion over transformers.** The motivation for using a diffusion model over other transformer-based methods (e.g., autoregressive) is not clearly explained, nor are the unique benefits for the object-centric setting highlighted (R1, R2). An intuitive explanation of why diffusion is suitable for this task is missing (R2).
- **Missing ablations and analyses.** 
  - Is DLP pretrained separately per task in IsaacGym? Can it handle compositions where both objects are present together? (R1)
  - What is the performance when using only the frontal view? Are two views essential? (R1)
  - Why is classifier-free guidance not used, and could it improve success rates? (R1)
  - In Table 1, PushCube shows a non-monotonic success rate (1→2 objects decreases, then 2→3 increases) unlike all other methods; an explanation is needed (R3).
  - Can the model generalize to longer time horizons (e.g., 2H)? (R4)
  - Could the conditioning mechanism (AdaLN) be replaced with cross-attention or multimodal DiT token concatenation? (R4)
- **Inference runtime cost.** Diffusion iterative denoising is time-consuming, which is critical for real-time applications like autonomous driving, though acknowledged in limitations (R4).
- **Minor presentation issues.** Figures 4 and 5 overlap with table captions above them (R1, R3, R4). Tables 2 and 3 use bold text to indicate training setup; clearer column headers would improve readability (R3).
