## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CONTROL, a contrastive learning framework for open-world semi-supervised learning (OWSSL) that integrates three contrastive losses: nearest neighbor (SupNN), supervised (SupCon), and self-supervised (SelfCon). The method is applied on top of existing OWSSL pipelines (ORCA, NACH) and is evaluated on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-100, reporting accuracy improvements. All four reviewers assigned a score of 5, but with notable disagreements on soundness, presentation, and contribution (scores: 3, 3, 1, 1 for contribution; 2, 3, 1, 1 for soundness; 2, 2, 2, 1 for presentation).

### Strengths
- The problem of open-world SSL (handling unseen classes in unlabeled data) is practical and of interest for real-world deployment. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- The three proposed contrastive objectives are interesting and helpful, and the theoretical analysis motivating contrastive loss over BCE loss is presented and can facilitate future research. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- The method is straightforward to understand and its efficacy is demonstrated across multiple datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100). (Reviewers 1, 3)
- Experimental results show notable improvements over the two baselines (ORCA, NACH), and the ablation study on CIFAR-100 justifies the contribution of each introduced loss term. The analysis of improved recall of unseen-class samples provides additional motivation. (Reviewer 2)
- The proof and derivation of the objectives are comprehensive and easy to follow according to one reviewer. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear presentation and insufficient motivation.** Several reviewers noted that the paper focuses on *what* is done but not *why*, making it read like a technical report. The introduction lacks high-level motivation: e.g., why BCE-based methods are treated as a meaningful category (vs. other loss functions), the term “flexibility” in the MSE vs. BCE discussion is undefined, and the concept of “seen-unseen pairs” is introduced without explaining its relevance. (Reviewers 2, 4) Numerous grammatical errors and typos (e.g., “L” in abstract, unformatted “seen/unseen”, missing definition of “OWSSL”) hinder readability. (Reviewer 4) The narration also misses essential details: Section 3 lacks problem setting, input data format, encoder architectures, and context for how the loss functions are applied (e.g., how nearest neighbors are found, temperature parameters). (Reviewer 1) Figures 1 and 4 are unclear, overlapping, and lack proper annotations. (Reviewer 1)
- **Novelty concerns.** The losses are standard techniques borrowed from other areas; supervised contrastive loss is standard, nearest-neighbor loss resembles existing approaches (e.g., [A]), and the SimAll loss is similar to using augmented views as positives (e.g., [B]). The incremental contribution over prior work is not clearly justified. (Reviewer 3) Moreover, the benefit of contrastive learning for OWSSL has already been demonstrated in earlier works (e.g., GCD, OpenCon), making the method appear to be a straightforward combination of existing techniques. (Reviewer 4) One reviewer noted it is hard to evaluate the magnitude of the contribution because the individual losses are not novel. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing baselines and comparisons.** The paper omits comparison with highly related works: OpenCon and GCD are missing; the authors’ rationale for excluding OpenCon is unconvincing. (Reviewers 2, 4) More recent works like RoPAWS (2023) are also omitted. (Reviewer 4) The paper’s reference list (2 pages) is much shorter than that of related works (e.g., OpenCon has 6 pages), indicating a lack of thorough literature review. (Reviewer 4)
- **Insufficient justification and overclaiming.** Several claims are unsubstantiated: e.g., that prior works do not optimize features at the feature level (when many do jointly optimize features and classifiers); that OpenCon cannot achieve “continuous optimization” (concept undefined); and that BCE loss is categorically superior without clear evidence. (Reviewers 2, 4) The claim that CONTROL is a “unified” and “open-world” framework is not analyzed beyond what OpenCon already provides. (Reviewer 2)
- **Reproducibility and experimental details.** The paper lacks a reproducibility statement and does not report hyperparameter values or discuss sensitivity (e.g., the three hyperparameters for the loss terms). (Reviewer 3) The proof in Section 4.1 is considered not rigorous by one reviewer, with intermediate steps omitted. (Reviewer 3) Other reviewers also noted missing details such as the temperature parameter and the exact loss formulations. (Reviewer 1) Additionally, there is no evidence that the method improves non-OW SSL benchmarks or other OWSSL methods beyond ORCA/NACH. (Reviewer 4)
- **A potential error in formulation.** In Section 3.1, the definition of $C_{\text{seen}}$ as $C_{\text{seen}} = C_L \cap C_U$ seems incorrect; it should be $C_L$. (Reviewer 3)
- **Minor reviewer-specific questions that overlap with weaknesses.** These include: Why is BCE better than other approaches like MSE? Why is OpenCon not capable of continuous optimization? Does the method also improve non-OW SSL benchmarks and other OWSSL methods? (Reviewer 4) Why are $L_{\text{superseen}}$ and $L_{\text{SimAll}}$ bundled together in Table 3? (Reviewer 1) Can an experiment combining CONTROL with OpenCon be conducted? (Reviewer 2)
