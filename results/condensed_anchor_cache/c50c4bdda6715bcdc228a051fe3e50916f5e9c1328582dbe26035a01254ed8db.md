## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SeaDAG, a semi-autoregressive discrete denoising diffusion model for conditional generation of directed acyclic graphs (DAGs). It builds on DiGress (Vignac et al.) and borrows the multilevel diffusion schedule from AR-Diffusion (Wu et al.) to denoise layers at different speeds. A property decoder with a “condition loss” is added to enable conditional generation. Evaluation is on AIG circuit generation from truth tables and molecular generation (QM9, ZINC) using junction-tree DAGs. Reviewers are split: one (score 6) finds the paper clear and results promising; two (scores 3) consider contributions limited and comparisons unfair; one (score 5) sees value in the noise schedule but notes missing baselines.

### Strengths
- **Clear exposition and thorough supplement.** The paper is well written, with logical flow, algorithm pseudocodes, and detailed experiment settings that aid replicability (R1, R2, R3).
- **Empirical promise and compelling AIG results.** SeaDAG shows promising performance across AIG and molecule benchmarks. The utility of the non‑uniform noise schedule is convincingly demonstrated on AIG graphs (Figure 5) (R1, R3). The circuit generation examples are interesting (R2).
- **Intuitive idea of differing denoising speed per DAG layer.** This design is reasonable, improves validity, and is nicely illustrated (R2, R4).
- **Theoretical analysis.** The paper provides permutation equivariance and loss invariance guarantees (R1).
- **Diverse comparisons and metrics.** Many relevant works are compared, using thorough metrics (R1, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and relation to prior work.** The core mechanism is a direct combination of DiGress and AR‑Diffusion; the only difference is how \(p_\theta\) (Equation 11) is computed by instantiating their multilevel schedule in the Markov transition setting (R2). The paper does not adequately discuss other DAG-diffusion works (e.g., LayerDAG, Li et al. 2024a) and does not compare against them empirically despite framing SeaDAG as an improvement (R2, R4). Section 3 in particular lacks coverage of multilevel or semi‑autoregressive models (R2).
- **Unfair comparisons.**  
  - Baseline methods (PARD, DiGress, SwinGNN) are general graph generators that do not use a condition loss or DAG constraints, making the direct comparison lopsided (R1).  
  - For conditional generation, DiGress is used only with post‑hoc guidance, not with the most common conditional training approach (classifier‑free guidance, Hoogeboom et al. 2022); SeaDAG performs explicit conditional training. A fair comparison would require a conditionally trained DiGress (R3, R4).  
  - SeaDAG is given extra information at inference: the number of levels and nodes per level are sampled from the empirical distribution, whereas other methods do not receive this. This gives SeaDAG an advantage (R1, R3, R4).  
  - For molecules, SeaDAG generates junction‑tree DAGs while compared methods generate full molecular graphs. The improvement may stem from the representation rather than the model. Important baselines such as DiGress on junction trees and JT‑VAE (with and without conditioning) are missing (R4).  
- **Missing critical baselines.**  
  - Classifier‑free guidance (Ho & Salimans 2021) is the dominant conditional diffusion approach and is never compared (R3).  
  - LayerDAG (Li et al. 2024a) is not included in experiments despite being a direct DAG‑generation competitor (R4).  
  - Table 4 (conditional molecular generation on ZINC) does not report DiGress or LDM‑3DG (R3).  
  - Conditional evaluation results for PARD and SwinGNN on QM9 and ZINC are missing (R1).  
  - JT‑VAE is a natural baseline for junction‑tree generation but is absent from Tables 2 and 3 (R4).  
- **Ablation studies are insufficient.**  
  - No ablation separates the two claimed contributions (semi‑autoregressive schedule vs. condition loss) in the main text; only a unified ablation in Appendix A.3 is provided. A proper 2×2 study (neither, only semi‑autoregressive, only condition loss, both) is needed (R4).  
  - The effect of hyperparameter \(\beta\) and different noise schedules is not explored (R1, R3).  
  - The condition loss appears to hurt generative quality on average in Tables 6‑7, and its usefulness is limited to the AIG task (R3, R4). No theoretical justification is given (R4).  
  - Robustness of the chosen noise schedule (Equations 5‑6) is not tested; no reasoning is provided for why a bottom‑up schedule is used for AIGs and a top‑down schedule for molecules (R3).  
  - When removing the semi‑autoregressive component, it is unclear what noise schedule replaces it (R1).  
- **Limited evaluation scope.**  
  - Only three datasets (AIG, QM9, ZINC) are used; AIG is synthetic/simplistic, and QM9 and ZINC are both molecular and small. No evaluation on more complex DAG datasets or on unconditional tasks (which would allow comparisons with MOSES, Guacamol, etc.) is provided (R3, R4).  
  - Unconditional SeaDAG results are not shown (e.g., Table 3 has no SeaDAG row for unconditional generation) (R2, R4).  
  - Training time, memory usage, and model size are not reported (R1).  
- **Inference and sampling concerns.**  
  - The hierarchical structure (number of levels, sizes) is sampled from the training distribution and never adjusted, risking mode collapse and reduced diversity (R1).  
  - For QM9, DiGress samples node counts up to 9 from the empirical distribution, yet some molecules in Figure 10 have 12 atoms; it is unclear how extrapolation works (R2).  
- **Potential drawbacks of the molecule representation.** Converting molecules to junction‑tree DAGs may lose information and degrade performance (R1). Root‑node choice in the tree conversion may affect results; no analysis is provided (R2).  
- **Minor presentation issues.**  
  - The motivation in lines 44‑70 (“information flow”) is unclear and lacks evidence (R4).  
  - Notation problems: variable \(n\) overloaded (R4); avoid starting sentences with symbols (R4); simplify \(e_i\) vs. \(\boldsymbol{e}_i\) (R4).  
  - In Equations (3)‑(4), left multiplication should be right multiplication (R4).  
  - Define \(f_\theta\) and clarify how extensions to the graph transformer compare to DiGress (R2).  
  - “Structural validity” should be defined (R4).  
  - GraphARM is cited for guidance but not elaborated (R4).  
  - No histogram of input‑gate counts from ground truth, DiGress, and SeaDAG is given for the AIG experiment (R2).
