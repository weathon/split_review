## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a skip-\(k\)-frame block-wise main directional mean optical flow (MDMO) feature for micro-expression spotting (MES). It identifies a unique “M-pattern” in the feature curve of micro-expressions and defines rule-based spotting rules. A pixelmatch-based facial alignment algorithm with dynamic reference-frame update is used to remove global movements. Experiments on CAS(ME)², SAMM-LV, and CASME II show superior performance over state-of-the-art methods.

### Strengths

- Clear description of MES challenges (small magnitude, global movement interference) and limitations of existing fixed-reference-frame methods; the proposed method adequately addresses these limitations. [R1,R2,R5]
- The experimental setup is well-designed, including comparisons to related methods and ablation studies, leaving few gaps in justification. [R1]
- Identification of a distinctive motion pattern (M-pattern) in micro-expressions, leveraging that pattern to improve spotting. [R3,R5]
- Elimination of global movements in the early feature extraction stage via block-wise MDMO contributes to better detection. [R2,R5]
- The proposed method mostly outperforms state-of-the-art methods on three public datasets, demonstrating superior performance. [R2,R4,R5]
- Technically sound methodology and clear writing. [R2] (Note: one reviewer (R4) assessed soundness as poor, but most reviewers rated it good.)

### Weaknesses

- **Parameter selection and analysis**: Several reviewers noted that the system has many parameters (e.g., \(M_{\min}, M_{\max}\), \(k\) value) and it is unclear how they are chosen or validated (e.g., separate validation/test split). [R4,R2,R3] The method’s reliance on average ME duration for \(k = (N+1)/2\) lacks analysis of sensitivity for MEs that deviate from the mean; e.g., in Fig. 11, was spotting of some MEs easier than others? [R3] It is also unclear whether different ME types require different parameter sets. [R4] The impact on very long videos (which were omitted from experiments) is not discussed. [R3]
- **Theoretical and ablation limitations**: The paper lacks detailed theoretical analysis of why the M-pattern is effective for removing global movements, and no direct ablation experiments involving muscle intensity are provided. [R5] One reviewer questioned whether the approach is universally applicable given its rule-based nature and called for demonstration on other datasets. [R5]
- **Presentation (structure and readability)**: The paper contains many typos (specific examples listed by R1 on page 3). [R1,R3] The ordering of sections is inconsistent with the pipeline (Section 3.4 should appear before Section 3.2). [R1] Spotting rules are presented verbosely; a flowchart would improve clarity. [R1] Dataset description placed in the appendix makes the experiment section harder to follow. [R4] Some figures, particularly Figures 9 and 10, have very small font and are hard to read. [R2]
- **Missing components**: Lack of discussion on the types of micro-expressions in the datasets and how well the M-pattern fits each category. [R4] No visual example showing the elimination of global movements from feature extraction. [R2] No mention of ethical concerns implied in automating MES, which is sensitive from bias and fairness perspectives. [R1] It was unclear whether the M-curve is produced for the whole sequence or per ROI; the ordering of feature extraction and M-pattern description could be better explained. [R1]
- **Disagreement in assessment**: While most reviewers rated the paper highly (8, 8, 8), one reviewer (R4, score 5) found poor soundness (1) and presentation (1), citing unclear parameter selection and paper structure. Another reviewer (R5, score 6) raised concerns about generalizability and theoretical grounding. These contrasting views indicate that the robustness and clarity of the method are not fully convincing to all readers.
