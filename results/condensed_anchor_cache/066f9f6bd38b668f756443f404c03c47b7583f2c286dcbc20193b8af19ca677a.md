## Merged Review

### Summary
Three reviewers evaluated the paper on aligning preferences with counterfactual regret in sequential social dilemmas. Reviewers 1 and 2 gave scores of 5 and were moderately positive, praising the focus on SSDs, the causal reasoning approach, and the clarity. Reviewer 3 gave a score of 3 and was more critical, raising major concerns about the assumed full observability, insufficient baselines, and missing comparisons with stronger methods.

### Strengths
1. The paper focuses on sequential social dilemmas, a topic of interest to the community. (Rev1, Rev2, Rev3)
2. It convincingly highlights the limitations of prior methods in capturing true causal relationships between agents’ actions and outcomes, providing a clear rationale for studying reward estimation biases caused by entangled policies. (Rev1)
3. The introduction of counterfactual regret as a mechanism to align individual incentives with group objectives is an interesting contribution; it calculates the difference between maximum counterfactual rewards and actual rewards of other agents. (Rev1, Rev2)
4. A generative causal model is designed to capture the individual reward generation process in SSDs. (Rev2)
5. The paper provides some theoretical analysis of the reward generation process (e.g., Proposition 1). (Rev2)
6. The paper is well-organized, well-written, and easy to understand. (Rev1, Rev3)

### Weaknesses
1. **Theoretical support**: The proposed framework lacks comprehensive theoretical support; Proposition 1 does not fully (and understandably, given its complexity) rigorously substantiate the entire workflow. (Rev1)
2. **Causal model details missing**: The paper does not clearly present the learned causal structures or the network structure of the causal model; it is ambiguous whether the Dynamic Bayesian Network is considered part of the causal model and whether the causal model and generative model are the same. (Rev1, Rev2)
3. **Baseline selection insufficient**: 
   - The baselines are somewhat outdated. (Rev2)
   - The paper does not compare with Social Influence (also based on causal inference and cited) or Gifting, both of which achieve higher collective rewards in Harvest (over 800) than reported. Social Influence also achieves higher collective rewards in Cleanup (at least 200). (Rev3)
   - It would be beneficial to compare with the auto-aligning multi-agent incentives method (Kwon et al., 2023) mentioned in related work. (Rev2)
   - A broader range of baselines would provide a more comprehensive evaluation. (Rev1)
4. **Unexpected baseline performance**: 
   - The Selfish baseline performs surprisingly well in Coin, contrary to prior works. (Rev3)
   - SVO performs poorly despite being designed for cooperation and shows significant fluctuations in Common_Harvest_7 and Cleanup_5. (Rev2, Rev3)
5. **Soundness concerns** (raised primarily by Rev3):
   - The paper assumes a partially observable Markov game but actually grants full observability (joint observations, actions, rewards observable to all agents), which is unrealistic for many potential applications. (Rev3)
   - Observations are assumed Markovian without considering history, unlike standard POMDP/Dec-POMDP treatment. (Rev3)
   - The method requires all agents to have the same “currency” of rewards; different reward scales (e.g., one agent in Coin scaled by 10) could skew regret calculations. (Rev3)
6. **Missing experiments and measures**:
   - Regret evaluations only include alpha values >= 1 (indicating selflessness); it would be interesting to see the effect of alpha < 1 (how selfish the agents can be without compromising cooperation). (Rev3)
   - Alternative cooperation measures (e.g., matching coin rate in Coin, peace/sustainability in Harvest) are not reported in the paper or appendix; such plots would strengthen the contribution. (Rev3)
   - The paper could provide a more detailed analysis of how counterfactual regret promotes cooperation. (Rev2)
7. **Presentation issues**:
   - Some symbols appear suddenly without explanation; the sentence “To interpret the phrase: had collective actions…” shows clear signs of AI-generated text; the entire second paragraph of Section 3 is confusing. (Rev2)
   - The figures in the ablation results are difficult to read; more distinct colors are recommended. (Rev2)
8. **Typos**: 
   - “naively using individual for each agent” is missing “reward”. (Rev3)
   - “theorical” should be “theoretical”. (Rev3)
   - “rewards(For brevity” should be “rewards (For brevity”. (Rev3)
   - “Baselinses” should be “Baselines”. (Rev3)
