## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers find the decomposition of RVOS into referring, video, and segmentation factors interesting and note the effective use of off-the-shelf detectors (Grounding DINO), a tracker (OC-SORT), and a Transformer-based prompt preference learning module to select candidate tracks for prompting SAM, achieving SOTA on Ref-DAVIS17 (71.0 T&F). The paper is well-written and easy to follow. However, there is strong consensus across reviewers that the approach has limited novelty (multi-stage proposal-selection pipeline explored in ECCV2018 and CVPRW2021 champion solution), lacks evaluation on key benchmarks (MeViS, A2DS, JHDBS, ReVOS), misses comparisons with recent methods, and lacks sufficient ablation and analysis of design choices and computational cost. Two reviewers (scores 5) are more positive about the effectiveness and clarity, while three (scores 3) are more critical of the contribution depth and experiment completeness.

### Strengths
- Novel decomposition of RVOS into referring, video, and segmentation factors, enabling targeted improvements and leaving segmentation to foundation models (SAM).
- Weak annotation (boxes) achieves comparable or better performance, demonstrating potential efficiency gains.
- Prompt preference learning (Transformer-based classifier) is effective for selecting high-quality temporal prompts, leading to SOTA on Ref-DAVIS17 (71.0 T&F).
- Paper is well-written, Figure 1 clearly elucidates core concepts, and the approach is easy to follow.
- Extensive quantitative and qualitative results demonstrate effectiveness on evaluated benchmarks.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty:** The core multi-stage inference pipeline (generate candidate tracks, select best, then segment) has been explored in prior work (ECCV2018 [Video Object Segmentation with Referring Expressions]; CVPRW2021 champion solution). The proposed contribution beyond stronger foundation models is unclear. One reviewer questions what novel video-specific design exists beyond image-level detection, as the tracker does not take language input and the formulation seems applicable to Referring Image Segmentation.
- **Incomplete benchmark evaluation:** Missing experiments on large-scale, motion-expression benchmarks MeViS (multiple instances, scattered regions), as well as A2DS, JHDBS, and ReVOS. Evaluation on MeViS is especially needed to validate generalizability to multiple non-overlapping instances or semantically scattered regions.
- **Outdated and missing comparisons:** Comparisons are limited to methods from early 2023 or earlier. Recent methods (SOC, Referred by Multi-Modality, UniNEXT, DEVA, UniRef, VD-IT-2B) are not compared. No analysis is provided for why the proposed method performs worse than UniNEXT, DEVA, UniRef, VD-IT-2B on Ref-YouTube-VOS (Table? – reviewer cites missing analysis). Additionally, powerful MLLMs (e.g., GPT-4o) and Florence-2+SAM2 are not compared for candidate track identification or as alternative two-stage baselines.
- **Unclear design motivation:** The need for two proposal generation methods (frame-wise reference proposals and candidate tracks) is not justified. Table 1 shows candidate tracks outperform reference proposals; the role and reason for keeping frame-wise proposals is unclear.
- **Missing ablation and analysis:** Lack of ablation studies isolating the contribution of each component. Impact of different foundation models (e.g., alternative detectors, trackers, segmentation backbones) not explored. Computational cost (training and inference of prompt preference learning, detectors, trackers) not discussed.
- **Finetuning and diversity issues:** The finetuned Grounding DINO loses diversity and produces repetitive box proposals (L504). Details of the finetuning process (training data construction, parameters) are absent. A reviewer requests explanation of why diversity decreases.
- **Parameter count discrepancy:** Table 5 shows small parameter difference despite using only one Transformer decoder layer, while compared methods use full backbones (six encoders/decoders). Explanation is needed.
- **Minor typo:** L244 has a duplicate word (“quantitative quantitative” should be “quantitative qualitative”).
