## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes TELEPORTATION, a decentralized SGD variant that randomly activates a small subset of \(k\) nodes per iteration. Active nodes fetch parameters from previously active nodes, perform SGD, and gossip-average only among themselves. This aims to avoid the convergence rate degradation (due to spectral gap) that occurs when the total number of nodes \(n\) is large. Theoretical analysis provides convergence bounds and a method to tune \(k\). Experiments compare to Decentralized SGD across settings and graph types.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, the problem is clearly stated, and the algorithm is simple. (R1, R3, R4)
- Theoretical analysis is solid, assumptions are standard, and the proof of Theorem 1 recovers the bound of Koloskova (2020b) under a generalized activation scheme, resolving a prior disconnectivity issue. (R1, R3, R4)
- The comparison with client sampling is appreciated and helps position the work. (R1)
- The idea of sacrificing gradient updates from inactive nodes to reduce consensus error is novel. (R2)
- The hyperparameter tuning method for \(k\) is a practical contribution that may inspire future work. (R3)
- Experiments are comprehensive and persuasive, showing consistent improvement over Decentralized SGD. (R1, R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Proof flaw in Theorem 2**: A careful arithmetic breakdown of Lemma 9 (and Lemma 10) shows that the claimed linear speedup with total nodes \(n\) may not follow. The derived bound depends on one of three possible regimes, none of which yields a clean \(\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{nT})\) rate. The authors need to address whether the convergence bound truly gives linear speedup in \(n\). (R2)
- **Equivalence to DSGD on \(k\) nodes**: The algorithm can be interpreted as DSGD on \(k\) nodes where each node has access to all local objectives. Standard DSGD theory then suggests only \(\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{kT})\) convergence, not \(\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{nT})\). The paper should discuss this connection and clarify why linear speedup in \(n\) is legitimate. (R2)
- **Tightness of theoretical bounds**: The argument that activating fewer nodes improves convergence (e.g., Proposition 1) relies on an upper bound whose tightness is never discussed. Without lower bounds or empirical validation (e.g., on quadratic functions), the claimed improvement may be an artifact of loose analysis. (R3)
- **Lack of clarity on the source of performance degradation**: The paper should explicitly formulate why more nodes slow down decentralized SGD (e.g., increased variance, consensus drift, spectral gap scaling) and contrast it with fully synchronized settings where speedup is linear. A table summarizing existing results would help. (R3)
- **Practical applicability of the communication model**: The assumption that any two nodes can communicate directly is highly idealized. Realistic constraints (e.g., limited communication range, time-varying topologies) are not discussed. How the sampling and consensus weights would be implemented in a fully decentralized setting is not explained. (R1, R3)
- **Limited novelty**: The algorithm appears to be a variant of client sampling (McMahan 2017) adapted to decentralized learning; the approach may already have been discussed or implemented in practice. The claim of “completely alleviating” degradation is less interesting when a fixed \(k\)-node graph with known spectral gap is used—the spectral gap simply disappears from the bound because it is constant. (R4)
- **Missing experiments**: Only \(n \leq 100\)? Larger-scale experiments (e.g., \(n > 100\)) and plots of consensus error evolution would clarify whether the benefit stems from faster consensus convergence. (R2)
- **Sampling and weight matrix choices**: How are nodes sampled (uniformly? based on connectivity?)? How do activated nodes know the averaging weights \(\mathbf{W}\)? Would it help to vary the number of active nodes per iteration? (R1)
- **Hyperparameter tuning cost**: The method adds extra complexity; the trade-off between communication reduction and tuning overhead is not explored. (R3)
- **Data heterogeneity and privacy**: The algorithm does not account for heterogeneous data; possible modifications (e.g., optimizing \(\mathbf{W}\)) are not discussed. Additionally, revealing which nodes were active in the previous iteration could leak private information. (R1)
- **Minor issues**: The formula for \(k\) in line 268 can be simplified because \(a^{1/7} < a^{1/5}\) for \(a>1\). Figure 1 is missing black nodes. The term “Base-2 graph” is undefined. (R2, R4)

**Reviewer disagreement**: Reviewer 1 and 3 found the contribution non-trivial despite concerns, while Reviewer 2 and 4 were more skeptical, rating soundness and contribution lower. The theoretical soundness is particularly contested: Reviewer 2 identified a potential flaw in the main proof, and Reviewer 3 questioned the tightness of bounds.
