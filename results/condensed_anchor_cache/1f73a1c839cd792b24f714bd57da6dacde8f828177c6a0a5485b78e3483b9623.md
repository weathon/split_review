## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GFM (Global Flat Minima) for federated domain generalization (FedDG). It combines sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) with a global model-constrained adversarial augmentation strategy (GCA) to seek flat minima of the global model while preserving data privacy. The authors argue that existing methods either neglect data privacy or cannot extend naturally to the federated setting. Theoretical analysis shows that the GFM objective bounds the robust risk of the global model. Experiments on multiple FedDG benchmarks demonstrate GFM’s superior performance over prior FedDG and federated learning methods. Reviewers agreed on the relevance of the problem and the general soundness of the theoretical foundations but strongly disagreed on the novelty, the isolation of the core contribution from SAM/GCA, and the sufficiency of the empirical validation.

### Strengths
- The paper is well‑written and easy to follow, with a clear, linear explanation of concepts and a precise identification of the shortcomings of existing works (Reviewer 1).
- The theoretical analysis is rigorous, with detailed proofs in the appendix, and it effectively incorporates global flatness into local updates (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). This theoretical foundation is considered sound and instructive by majority reviewers.
- The exploration of the global loss landscape within FedDG is novel, and the approach of enhancing global flatness through data augmentation is an innovative and promising direction (Reviewer 4).
- A series of extensive experiments on multiple FedDG benchmarks validates the effectiveness of GFM and substantiates key claims, including Assumption 1 and the benefits of seeking flat minima (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- The paper provides sufficient literature and citations to contextualize the relevant issues (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Contribution isolation and comparison with baselines.** The ablation study (Table 2) shows that the main performance gains come from SAM and/or GCA, not from the “GFM” combination itself. Gains over FedAvg are marginal in some cases and statistical significance is not reported. FedAvg+GCA wins in 2 of 4 cases, questioning whether the adversarial augmentation is necessary or simply standard data augmentation suffices. The paper does not discuss how GFM differs from FedSAM (FedAvg+SAM) – the only novel component appears to be the augmentation network directly adopted from Suzuki (2022). Missing is an ablation study that isolates the augmentation network’s impact within GFM and a comparison of FedSAM combined with that network (Reviewers 1, 2). One reviewer (R4) also noted the absence of a comparison with Sun et al. (2023) on dynamic SAM in FL.
- **Validation of the augmented data as a global surrogate.** There is no direct empirical evidence that the augmented data distribution is closer to the true global data distribution than the local data. The claim that the augmentation helps find global flat minima remains unvalidated. A quantitative comparison of these distributions is missing (Reviewer 4). Additionally, the paper does not discuss how the method would adapt if each client possesses multiple domains instead of one (Reviewer 1).
- **Theoretical justification gaps.** Several reviewers questioned the theoretical link between seeking global flat minima and guaranteed generalization under client data heterogeneity. The statement “aggregation helps generalization” (line 191) is used without proof or citation, and no analysis is given to show that the relaxation in Assumption 1 does not affect the conclusion. One reviewer (R3) found Assumption 1 (local models may outperform the global model) counter‑intuitive and insufficient to cover all FL scenarios. Also, the definition of flatness (Equation 3) implicitly assumes θ is near a local minimum (first‑order gradient near zero), but no such assumption is stated in the paper (Reviewer 2). The novelty of Theorem 1 was also questioned – whether it is a restatement of known results rather than a new contribution (Reviewer 1).
- **Algorithmic clarity and missing details.** The min‑max optimization process for the adversarial augmentation model (ϕᵢ) and model parameters (θᵢ) is unclear. The paper lacks details on how ϕᵢ is optimized, how the iterations interact, and the convergence properties of this game (Reviewer 4). Algorithm 1 is underspecified: it does not explain how many models a client must maintain, whether the ϕᵢ are aggregated, nor the exact loop over sampling, gradient estimation, and update rules. The hyperparameter choice of γ=0.02 in SAM is given without any sensitivity analysis or guidance on selection (Reviewers 1, 4). No analysis of computational overhead (FLOPs vs. performance, number of parameters shared vs. performance) or memory cost for the augmentation model is provided (Reviewer 1). One reviewer (R3) also noted that the method may require more client‑side computation but the paper does not address how to reduce it.
- **Privacy motivation not substantiated.** The paper motivates GFM by “data privacy concerns” but does not present any specific design or analysis that enhances privacy compared to existing methods like FedSAM. The difference in privacy protection between GFM and FedSAM is not discussed (Reviewer 2).  
- **Presentation and completeness issues.** The related works and preliminaries sections are too long, while the experimental section is too short (Reviewer 2). The tables lack hyperlinks to the cited methods, making it hard to locate the original papers for each baseline (Reviewer 2). The method used to generate the loss landscape in Figure 2 is not explained (Reviewer 2). Experiments on common datasets like CIFAR‑10/100 (used by some baselines) are not reported, raising concerns about the breadth of the evaluation (Reviewer 2).
