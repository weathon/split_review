## Merged Review

### Summary
MVLight introduces a light-conditioned multi-view diffusion model that explicitly integrates HDR environment maps (split into high/low-frequency components) as lighting conditions into the text-to-3D generation process. The model extends multi-view diffusion (e.g., MVDream) and incorporates learnable albedo and normal embeddings to disambiguate lighting from intrinsic properties. It jointly generates RGB, normal maps, and albedo across views under a specified lighting environment. A two-stage light-aware Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) optimization then extracts a 3D object: first geometry and appearance (normal + albedo), then PBR materials (metallic, roughness) while keeping the same lighting environment (non-blind PBR estimation). The model is trained on a custom dataset from Objaverse with 450 HDR environment maps. Comparisons are made to DreamFusion, Fantasia3D, MVDream, and RichDreamer. Evaluation includes qualitative results, CLIP scores, and a user study with 24 participants.

### Strengths
1. **Novel integration of lighting into multi-view diffusion (Reviewers 1,2,3):** Explicitly conditioning on HDR environment maps is a simple yet effective way to generate consistent multi-view images under varying lighting. This addresses the prior lack of lighting specification in text-to-image models, enabling better decomposition of light-independent vs. lighting-dependent components.
2. **Improvements over baselines (Reviewers 1,3):** The generated meshes and albedo are clean. The light-aware SDS yields significant improvements in PBR material modeling and relighting capabilities compared to methods like RichDreamer and Fantasia3D, as supported by the user study.
3. **Reasonable architectural design (Reviewer 2):** The two-stage optimization (geometry/appearance then PBR materials) provides a structured approach. The non-blind PBR estimation (using the same lighting for diffusion outputs and material optimization) shows thoughtful consideration of lighting consistency.
4. **Sufficient experiments and ablation studies (Reviewers 1,4):** The paper includes comprehensive comparisons and ablations that validate the design choices.
5. **Well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 3,4):** The paper is clearly presented, with reasonable implementation details (GPU requirements, optimization parameters).
6. **Potential for follow-up research (Reviewer 3):** If the pretrained checkpoint is released, the model could benefit further work on relightable object generation.

**Note:** Strengths 1 and 2 are highlighted by Reviewer 3 (score 6) as particularly novel and impactful; other reviewers (1,2,4) acknowledge them but have stronger reservations about the final quality and methodological originality.

### Weaknesses
1. **Color oversaturation and unrealistic albedo (Reviewers 1,4):** In Figure 7 the generated 3D objects exhibit color oversaturation. The albedo in Figure 8 is not uniform and also oversaturated. The Metallic and Roughness properties appear incorrect.
2. **Relighting results appear completely diffuse, lacking specular/reflective qualities (Reviewers 1,2,4):** Figure 6 shows relighting results where objects look fully diffuse and unrealistic. The paper does not adequately explain why it cannot synthesize materials with high realism (e.g., high-frequency reflections).
3. **Simplified ambient lighting during optimization inadequately justified (Reviewer 2):** The method uses a simplified ambient lighting setting during the optimization stage (lines 267–269). This choice is not well motivated and likely limits the model's ability to capture complex lighting interactions and high-frequency details. Experiments with more realistic lighting models are missing. The resulting lack of specular effects is a direct consequence.
4. **Methodology viewed as roundabout, leading to information loss (Reviewers 1,4):** The two-stage approach (first train a light-conditioned diffusion, then optimize via SDS) is indirect. A more direct alternative would be to train a multi-view material generation model that directly outputs albedo, metallic, and roughness, then project onto a 3D mesh (similar to the One-2-3-45++ method) and only then use SDS for refinement—analogous to UniDream. The current SDS-based optimization is not one-to-one and causes information loss.
5. **Confusing dual role of albedo and normal embeddings (Reviewers 2,3):** The paper shows albedo and normal maps used both as conditioning embeddings and as generation targets (Figure 1). The relationship between these roles is unclear, and it is not explained how these embeddings are learned or how they influence the output. Specifically, it is unclear how to obtain albedo \(L_a\) and normal \(L_n\) during training and SDS optimization.
6. **Missing comparisons with recent lighting-controllable generation works (Reviewer 2):** The paper fails to acknowledge or compare against crucial recent methods: DreamMat (SIGGRAPH 2024), DiLightNet (SIGGRAPH 2024), and Neural Gaffer (NeurIPS 2024). This oversight undermines the claimed novelty and contribution.
7. **High computational cost without justification (Reviewers 2,3):** The method takes 2 hours on an A100 GPU per asset, yet recent methods like SF3D achieve similar or better results in seconds. The paper does not justify this overhead nor provide rough training time for the diffusion model itself. Changing lighting conditions in the light-aware SDS likely requires further optimization time.
8. **Small user study and insufficient quantitative evaluation (Reviewers 2,3):** The user study involves only 24 participants, which is too small for reliable conclusions. Quantitative evaluation relies solely on CLIP scores, which may not correlate well with lighting quality. There is no analysis of failure cases under challenging lighting or materials, and no lighting-specific metrics.
9. **Outdated design concept relative to ICLR standards (Reviewers 1,4):** Several reviewers feel the overall design concept is not sufficiently advanced and that the results do not meet the quality bar for acceptance at ICLR. (The paper scores of 3, 3, 6, 5 reflect this split; the two lower scores cite outdatedness and poor results, while the higher score (6) acknowledges novelty but notes optimization time and evaluation limitations.)
10. **Remaining open questions (all reviewers):**
    - How do the albedo and normal embeddings specifically influence the diffusion output? (Reviewer 1)
    - Clarify the batch size: is 128 per GPU or total across 8 GPUs (i.e., 8 × 128)? (Reviewer 1)
    - Why can’t the method synthesize materials with high realism (specularity)? (Reviewers 1,4)
    - Provide further justification for the simplified ambient lighting choice. (Reviewer 2)
    - How could this framework be extended to more controllable image-to-3D relightable generation? (Reviewer 3)
    - Is it possible to extract a UV map for each material to facilitate downstream editing? (Reviewer 3)
