## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an offline reinforcement learning (RL) approach for learning branching strategies in MILP solvers, introducing the Ranking-Constrained Actor-Critic (RCAC) algorithm. RCAC is designed to learn from sub-optimal or inadequate training signals, using full strong branching as the behavior policy and dual bound improvement as the reward. Experiments on synthetic and real-world problems show that RCAC outperforms previous methods (e.g., GGCN) in prediction accuracy and computational efficiency, especially with smaller datasets.

### Strengths
- **Innovative formulation:** Formulating learning-to-branch as an offline RL problem is a natural yet novel departure from traditional imitation learning methods (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Practical data collection efficiency:** The framework significantly reduces the time needed to collect training datasets compared to methods requiring high-quality demonstrations (Reviewer 1).
- **Strong empirical performance:** RCAC outperforms GGCN baselines on multiple benchmarks (Set Cover, MIS, CA, CFL, and two real-world problems), particularly on smaller datasets (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Clear presentation:** The problem formulation and algorithm are described clearly and are easy to follow (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Inspirational algorithmic component:** The idea of using the top‑k actions in \(G_\omega\) to constrain the Bellman operator is novel and could be applied to similar domains (Reviewer 5).
- **Fitting of offline RL to the setting:** Because branching trajectories are expensive to generate, offline RL is a more suitable paradigm than online RL or pure imitation learning (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient scaling and generalization evaluation:** The paper does not test how policies trained on small instances perform on larger (scaled-up) versions of the same problem families (e.g., SC, MIS, CA, CFL). Generalization ability across different instance sizes is not demonstrated (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons:**
  - No comparison against the augmented GGCN variant from “Lookback for Learning to Branch” (Gupta et al., 2022), which uses an improved loss function (Reviewer 1).
  - No comparison with standard offline RL algorithms (e.g., CQL, IQL) to isolate the benefit of the RCAC design (Reviewers 2, 4).
  - No comparison with the offline RL method for branching previously published in [1] (Reviewer 3). The claim of being the first to apply offline RL to MILP solving is disputed by one reviewer, who identified prior work.
  - No ablation with a simple imitation learning approach on the refined top‑k dataset to determine whether the benefit comes from the offline RL algorithm or from the dataset refinement itself (Reviewer 5).
- **Unclear necessity of offline RL:** Given that it is straightforward to interact with the solver online, the necessity of using offline RL (rather than online RL or imitation learning on refined data) is not clearly justified (Reviewer 5). Several reviewers also questioned why RCAC was chosen over existing offline RL methods (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Missing experimental details:**
  - The values of \(k\) used for each model and dataset are not reported (Reviewer 1).
  - The training datasets: For GGCN (H) and RCAC (H), it is unclear whether they are trained on the same dataset; the VHB dataset collection procedure needs clarification (Reviewer 1).
  - The distribution of rewards (e.g., dual bound improvement) across tree depth and how normalization is handled are not provided (Reviewer 1).
  - The specific problem family for Figure 3 is not indicated (Reviewer 1).
  - The aggregation method for Table 4 (cumulative sum over 20 instances?) and statistical significance (p‑values) are missing (Reviewer 1).
  - The test set is small (20 instances) with an unusual train/validation/test split (10k/2k/20) (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Questions about the reward function:** The choice of dual bound improvement ignores the primal bound entirely; the metric is not scale‑invariant, which could cause reward range issues across instances (Reviewer 4). One reviewer suggested comparing with primal‑dual gap improvement (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Clarity and presentation issues:**
  - Section 2.2 does not clearly distinguish between node selection and variable selection; it is ambiguous which variables (from a single node or across the tree) are considered (Reviewer 4).
  - It is unclear whether a single model is trained on all instances or separate models per dataset (Reviewer 4).
  - Section 5.2 is considered unnecessary and could be omitted (Reviewer 4).
  - Multiple typos: “hand-craft”, “pseudocsot”, “A[n] MDP”, extra brace in Equation 4, “discounted factor” (Reviewer 4).
- **Justification of FSB sub‑optimality:** The claim that FSB is sub‑optimal in some scenarios (e.g., uninformative LP relaxation, dual degeneracy) is not backed by evidence or references (Reviewer 2).
- **Effectiveness for small \(k\):** When \(k\) is small, the Q‑value distribution in the dataset becomes centered around \(-\delta\), which may harm training; the authors do not discuss how they ensure training effectiveness in this regime (Reviewer 5).
- **Connections to reward‑weighted regression:** The relationship between Equation 6 and reward‑weighted regression is unexplored (Reviewer 2).
- **Minority disagreement on novelty:** One reviewer considers the contribution incremental, simply applying offline RL to branching, and notes that the idea is not entirely novel (Reviewer 3). In contrast, other reviewers find the approach innovative and timely.
