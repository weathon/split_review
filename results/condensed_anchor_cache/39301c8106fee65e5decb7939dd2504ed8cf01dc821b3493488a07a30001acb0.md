## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces RAORL, a model-free offline RL method that uses a state-adversarial optimization framework to handle transition dynamics uncertainty without explicit environment modeling. It adapts the online RAPPO algorithm to offline settings, generating adversarial state perturbations via FGSM to build a pessimistic uncertainty set. Theoretical guarantees for the idealized version are provided. Empirical evaluations on D4RL benchmark show strong performance, especially on AntMaze, but comparisons with state-of-the-art baselines are incomplete. The writing and organization require substantial improvement.

### Strengths
- The studied problem of model-free robust offline RL is important.
- RAORL provides a lower-bound performance guarantee, distinguishing it from prior work.
- The method adapts RAPPO to offline reinforcement learning; the state-adversarial training improves (or does not hurt) performance compared to the base algorithm ReBrac, with gains most apparent on AntMaze tasks.
- Theoretical analysis includes a solid foundation and validation of the reality gap.
- Empirical results on D4RL and a robustness task show efficacy.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty and unclear distinction from prior work**: The core adversarial mechanism (FGSM) is nearly identical to RAPPO; the offline setting does not fundamentally alter its application, and no novel offline-specific challenges are articulated (Reviewer 1, 4). The method is highly similar to RORL and S4RL, but these are not compared, and the differences in adversarial training and state perturbation techniques are not clarified (Reviewer 4). The concept of a relaxed transition kernel via linear combination resembles the R-contamination uncertainty set (e.g., Rajeswaran et al., "Policy Gradient Method For Robust Reinforcement Learning") and the name "State-Adversarial" is misleading (Reviewer 3).
- **Incomplete and unpersuasive experimental evaluation**: No comparison with SOTA model-free offline algorithms such as SAC-N, EDAC, LB-SAC (Reviewer 2). RAORL’s results on Mujoco and Adroit tasks are inferior to these methods when re-implemented using the CORL benchmark (Reviewer 2). No experiments on stochastic environments, despite the paper’s motivation that model-free methods avoid model-based struggles with stochasticity (Reviewer 1). The method should be applied to other base algorithms (e.g., ATAC, IQL) to demonstrate versatility (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing comparison in robustness experiments**: The state perturbation experiments lack direct comparison with RORL; robustness claims are insufficiently supported (Reviewer 4). Figure 2 does not clarify color codes or perturbation magnitudes, and the connection between synthetic perturbations and real-world environments is tenuous (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear implementation and hyperparameters**: The method section does not specify learning objectives (loss functions) for value functions and policy, nor how adversarial states are generated and integrated (Reviewer 4). The impact of hyperparameters ε and α is unclear; a hyperparameter study is needed (Reviewer 4). Details on network architecture, optimization parameters, and hyperparameter values are insufficient for reproducibility (Reviewer 4). How to determine the attack budget ε is not explained (Reviewer 2).
- **Poor writing and organization**: The paper’s structure is not well-organized; the logical thread connecting definitions, theorems, and lemmas is missing; there is no formal problem formulation (Reviewers 2, 3). The writing contains convoluted sentences and unclear explanations (Reviewer 2, 3). Lemma 1 is confusing: formulas 1 and 2 appear identical at first glance, and the textual description does not match the mathematical expression (Reviewer 3). Self-promotional language (e.g., “A Leap Towards”, “top contender”) should be toned down (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing discussion of related work**: Model-based offline RL (e.g., MoREL, Kidambi et al. 2020) is included in related work but not connected to this paper’s theory—Lemma 1 closely resembles MoREL’s Theorem 1, but similarities/differences are not highlighted (Reviewer 1). Robust RL works on state adversaries (e.g., [3], [4]) are omitted from the related work section (Reviewer 2).
- **Theoretical presentation issues**: The definition of \(p_r\) in Lemma 3 is unclear (Reviewer 1). The definition of the uncertainty set in practice does not clearly relate to theoretical assumptions (Reviewer 1). The utility of the theory is diminished because experiments are only on deterministic tasks, contradicting the model-free advantage claim (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 notes the primary contribution (lower-bound guarantee) but finds the logical coherence weak.
