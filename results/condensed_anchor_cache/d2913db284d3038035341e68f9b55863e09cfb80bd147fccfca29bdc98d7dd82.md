## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Actor-related Tubelet (ART), an approach for detecting complex-shaped action tubes by incorporating actor-specific features (via ROI align) instead of relying solely on positional information. It introduces a metric for tube shape complexity. Experiments are conducted on MultiSports, UCF101-24, and JHMDB51-21. Reviewer opinions vary: one reviewer (R2) rates the paper 5, citing the novel use of actor features and significant improvements on complex activities, while the other three reviewers (R1, R3, R4) rate it 3 and express concerns about novelty, missing comparisons (especially frame-mAP and recent methods), limited ablation, and presentation clarity.

### Strengths
- The motivation is clear and well-illustrated (e.g., Fig. 1), highlighting the limitation of position-only methods and the value of actor-specific cues for tracking actors in complex trajectories. (R2, R4)
- The proposed metric for measuring tube shape complexity is a valuable addition and the analysis of performance breakdown based on this metric is informative. (R3, R4)
- ART achieves improved performance over prior methods on all three datasets (MultiSports, UCF101-24, JHMDB51-21). (R1, R2, R4)
- The method leverages actor features (via ROI align) to distinguish between actors, which is a departure from purely position-based approaches and yields significant improvements on complex activities, as noted by one reviewer (R2). (R2)
- Interesting experimental analysis and comparisons are provided (e.g., Fig. 3). (R4)

### Weaknesses
**Novelty and Relationship to Prior Work**
- The overall architecture is very similar to TubeR; the Tubelet Query Generator module is not clearly differentiated from TubeR’s Tubelet attention module. Complexity analysis is also unclear. (R1)
- The paper’s setup appears to provide precise actor locations from prior video analysis, which is inconsistent with previous works (TubeR, EVAD, STAR, BMViT). It is unclear whether these methods were evaluated under the same condition and how ART would work without such annotations. (R1)
- The proposed complexity metric (Eq. 2) may conflate simple actions with large motion as high complexity. (R2)
- The reliance on actor-specific features is limited to ROI align from He et al. (2017); there is little discussion or innovation beyond that. (R2)
- Previous methods also use DETR to detect actors for query generation; why they fail to capture actor-related information is not addressed. (R2)
- The paper's contribution is considered incremental rather than a paradigm shift (R3). While one reviewer (R2) sees significant improvements on complex activities, others (R1, R3, R4) find the gains moderate and the novelty limited.
- The related work section is not comprehensive; it repeats citations and does not clearly outline differences from closely related works. (R4)

**Missing Evaluations and Comparisons**
- Frame-mAP results are omitted, although the tubelet output can be converted to frame-level boxes. This omission limits comparability with standard benchmarks and existing literature. (R1, R3)
- Table 5 primarily compares with older works and omits recent influential methods such as “End-to-End Spatio-Temporal Action Localisation with Video Transformers” and “Holistic Interaction Transformer Network for Action Detection”, which may inflate the reported advantage. (R3)
- Only one ablation study is provided (temporal compensation) on one model and one dataset, showing a minor 0.5% boost. More ablations across datasets and models are needed to validate contributions. (R1)
- It is unclear whether the good performance on AVA is due to the better visual backbone used; without proper ablations, the effect of the proposed module is confounded. (R1)
- The evaluation does not clarify what video-mAP is and how it contributes to the assessment. (R4)
- The analysis of visualizations in Section 4.3 is limited to two sentences; deeper insights are needed. (R4)
- Figure 6 shows that for many actions, the actor-related method performs worse than the position-related method; the method corresponding to the blue bar is not specified. (R2)

**Methodology and Presentation Clarity**
- Notations in Section 3.1 are confusing, with tangled variable names and indexes. (R2)
- The loss function in Eq. 9 appears incorrect and should be proofread. (R2)
- The description of “easy actions” (brushing hair/teeth) as spatially stationary is misleading; such motions are subtle but still complex. (R4)
- The root causes of deformable tube shapes (e.g., camera distance, partial occlusion) are not clearly defined. (R4)
- The metric in Section 3.1 is not explained clearly; it is unclear how it differs from previous work [1] and whether it is a direct extension. (R1, R4)
- Mathematical notations are raw and inconsistent (e.g., “*” in Eq. 5 is undefined; Eq. 6 uses a weighted sum without justification). (R4)
- The reason for excluding JHMDB from Fig. 3b (single actor per video) is not clearly justified. (R4)
- The paper is unnecessarily complicated; Fig. 4 is confusing, and the method section lacks a notation table for symbols, making it hard to follow the core innovations. (R4)
- Hyperparameter selection (e.g., in Eq. 9) is not justified; the value “ps” on page 6 is not defined. (R4)
- There are readability issues such as too many underbars in Section 3.1 equations. (R3)
