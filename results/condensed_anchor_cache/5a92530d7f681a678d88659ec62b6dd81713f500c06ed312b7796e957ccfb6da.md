## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a backdoor trigger inversion (BTI) method that decouples benign features instead of directly extracting backdoor features. The method first decouples benign features, then inverts the trigger by minimizing differences in benign features between benign and poisoned samples while maximizing differences in backdoor features. It avoids scanning all classes to speculate the target label, improving efficiency. The method is adapted into backdoor-removal and pre-processing defenses. Extensive experiments on benchmark datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, GTSRB, ImageNet-100) with several backdoor attacks and baseline methods show state-of-the-art performance. The approach is novel and opposite to existing BTI methods.

### Strengths
- Novel trigger inversion method that decouples benign features rather than backdoor features, offering an insightful, intuitive, and effective approach that differs from existing works. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- Overcomes the limitation of existing BTI methods that require scanning all output classes to identify the most likely target label, thus improving efficiency. (Reviewers 1,3)
- Comprehensive evaluation across multiple datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, GTSRB, ImageNet), models (ResNet, VGG, DenseNet), backdoor attacks (BadNets, Blended, Trojan, WaNet, IAD, LIRA), and baseline methods, with state-of-the-art results. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- The method can be adapted to different defense phases (backdoor removal via fine-tuning and pre-processing trigger removal). (Reviewer 2)
- The paper discusses potential adaptive attacks (e.g., blending benign and poisoned features) and shows the method's robustness. (Reviewer 3)
- The paper is well-organized and clearly presented. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- No discussion on the limitations of the proposed approach. (Reviewer 1)
- ImageNet evaluation uses only 100 classes; scalability to the full 1000-class ImageNet and performance comparisons with other methods when increasing the number of classes are not provided. (Reviewer 1)
- Related feature-level BTI works for self-supervised learning (SSL-Cleanse 2023, Feng et al. CVPR 2023) are missing from the discussion in Section 2.2. (Reviewer 1)
- The assumption that benign features form a compact cluster may not hold; the feature space of benign samples can be large with multiple clusters (e.g., for the class 'horse'), making optimization directions potentially random. The paper does not explore different distance measurements for benign features. (Reviewer 2)
- Adaptive attacks using natural triggers (triggers that naturally appear in the training dataset) are not evaluated. (Reviewer 2)
- Performance against composite attacks that heavily mix benign and malicious features (e.g., Composite Backdoor Attack, CCS 2020) is not evaluated. (Reviewer 3)
- Missing comparison with additional baseline methods such as NONE (NeurIPS 2022). (Reviewer 3)
- The effect of trigger size is not examined; it is unclear how the method performs against backdoors with larger trigger sizes, which can be more challenging. The current evaluation focuses on relatively small triggers. (Reviewer 3)
- Computational cost is not analyzed in the main paper. While the paper claims efficiency gains by not scanning all classes, there is no concrete quantitative comparison of computational overhead against other BTI methods. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- Claimed contribution 3 (BTI-DBF) is very similar to Neural Cleanse (Wang et al., IEEE S&P 2019). The key difference appears to be using a UNet generator, but the core idea of inverting a trigger is shared. The claim of generating 'higher-quality' triggers is not rigorously justified, as the evaluation focuses on defense success rather than trigger fidelity. (Reviewer 4)
- Claimed contribution 2 (decoupling benign features from backdoor features) is similar to ABS (Liu et al., CCS 2019). The argument that ABS assumes complete separation at the neuron level is not a sufficient distinction; using a soft mask instead of a hard separation does not fundamentally alter the core concept. (Reviewer 4)
- Claimed contribution 1 ("revealing the low efficiency and low similarity nature of existing BTI methods and their intrinsic reason") is not considered a valid contribution; no adequate discussion of the "intrinsic reason" is provided, and scanning all classes is a common requirement for many backdoor detection methods. (Reviewer 4)
- The motivation is weak: if the goal is detection, no detection performance is shown; if the goal is mitigation, there is no evidence that triggers inverted by other baselines cannot mitigate the backdoor. Moreover, inaccurately estimated triggers may introduce more robustness (e.g., using varying shapes/sizes for unlearning). (Reviewer 4)
- The results in Table 1 need verification: the DSR for Unicorn is much lower than reported in the original Unicorn paper (ICLR 2023). (Reviewer 4)
- The intuition behind the method does not always hold; it fails when there is no decoupling between benign and backdoor features, e.g., when the model is compact and the trigger is globally wide (e.g., the chessboard trigger from MM-BD). (Reviewer 4)
- The decoupling method's generalization is not evaluated; if it works for the proposed formulation, it should also be applicable to other formulations like Unicorn, but this is not demonstrated. (Reviewer 4)
