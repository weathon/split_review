## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents Ferret, a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) that can understand spatial referring (points, bounding boxes, free-form shapes) and ground open-vocabulary descriptions. It introduces a hybrid region representation combining discrete coordinates and continuous features, along with a spatial-aware visual sampler to handle varying region shapes. The authors curate the GRIT dataset (1.1M samples + 95K hard negatives) and a new benchmark Ferret-Bench for grounded description and reasoning. Ferret achieves superior or on-par performance on classical referring/grounding tasks and shows reduced object hallucination. The three reviewers agree on the overall contribution but diverge on novelty and significance: Reviewer 1 (score 8) is more positive, emphasizing the support of diverse visual references and the new benchmark; Reviewers 2 and 3 (score 6) are more critical, citing limited novelty, missing ablations, and lack of open-source resources.

### Strengths
1. **Flexibility of input region types**: Ferret supports points, boxes, scribbles, polygons, and masks – one of the first MLLMs to handle such diverse visual references (Reviewer 1 S1, Reviewer 2 S3, Reviewer 3 S2).  
2. **GRIT dataset contribution**: The curated dataset (1.1M samples, 95K hard negatives) is a meaningful resource for grounding and referring instruction tuning (Reviewer 1 S2, Reviewer 2 S5, Reviewer 3 S1).  
3. **Ferret-Bench benchmark**: A new evaluation task that includes descriptions and reasoning with visual references (bounding boxes), covering capabilities not addressed by existing benchmarks (Reviewer 1 S3).  
4. **Comparisons with state-of-the-art**: The paper provides thorough evaluations against both existing and concurrent methods (e.g., Shikra) (Reviewer 1 S4, Reviewer 3 S3).  
5. **Motivation and framing**: The work is well motivated by the inherent human ability to jointly learn referring and grounding (Reviewer 2 S2).  
6. **Spatial-aware visual sampler**: The design is well-motivated and validated through experiments (Reviewer 3 S2, S3).  
7. **Effective utilization of LLMs**: The framework integrates large language models in a reasonable way (Reviewer 2 S4).  
8. **Reduction in object hallucination**: The model shows a notable alleviation of object hallucination (Reviewer 2 summary).

### Weaknesses
1. **No open-source code or dataset**: The code and dataset are not publicly released, limiting reproducibility and practical impact. Reviewer 2 would raise soundness score if they were open. (Reviewer 2 W1)  
2. **Limited scientific novelty**: The work is described as “very engineering, extensive work, but not much scientific novelty” (Reviewer 2 W3). The spatial-aware visual sampler is conceptually similar to SEEM (Zou et al., 2023) and shows only marginal improvement in ablation (Reviewer 1 W2). The idea of jointly solving referring and grounding was also explored in concurrent works (Chen et al., 2023a; Peng et al., 2023) (Reviewer 1 W3).  
3. **Missing ablation on hybrid region representation**: The paper does not ablate the hybrid region representation component, which is central to the method (Reviewer 3 W1).  
4. **No discussion of limitations or failure cases**: The paper omits any discussion of potential failure scenarios or limitations (Reviewer 1 W1).  
5. **Dataset hierarchy complexity**: The hierarchy of the GRIT dataset is complicated and may not be practical for custom use (Reviewer 2 W2).  
6. **Need for broader task validation**: The model’s performance on non-referring/grounding tasks (e.g., VQA_v2, MME, general captioning) is insufficiently validated; caption evaluation is not as strong as InstructBLIP (Reviewer 3 W2).  
7. **Performance disparity unexplained**: Ferret significantly outperforms Shikra on Ferret-Bench but not on existing datasets; no explanation is provided (Reviewer 1 Q1).  
8. **Question about grounding vs. referring mutual benefit**: Table 5 suggests that grounding helps referring more than vice versa; interpretation is missing (Reviewer 2 Q1).  
9. **Uncertainty about generated data quality**: The quality of data generated from ChatGPT and GPT-4 is not evaluated (Reviewer 2 Q2).  
10. **Potential GPT-4 bias in Ferret-Bench**: Since Ferret-Bench uses GPT-4 as a judge, and some data are also generated by GPT-4, there is a concern that Ferret’s advantage may be inflated (Reviewer 2 Q3).  
11. **Clarity on data generation process**: The paper mentions using ChatGPT first then GPT-4 to refine, but does not clarify whether ChatGPT can process images (it cannot) (Reviewer 2 Q4).  
12. **Missing evaluation detail**: Whether the Flickr30k grounded caption evaluation is fine-tuned or zero-shot is not stated (Reviewer 3 Q1).  
13. **Writing clarity**: Some phrasing is unclear, e.g., “choose MLLM as the bedrock” (Reviewer 1 Q2).
