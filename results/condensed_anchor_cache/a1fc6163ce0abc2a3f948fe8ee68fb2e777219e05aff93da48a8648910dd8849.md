## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents WASUP, an inherently interpretable neural network that combines a B‑Cos feature extractor with a Nadaraya‑Watson classification head (effectively few‑shot/prototypical learning). Support vectors per class are extracted via k‑means on training image features and then replaced with the nearest actual training image feature. Classification is based on similarity between the input’s latent vector and these support vectors. Local explanations are obtained through the B‑Cos weight‑input alignment, and global explanations through the case‑based reasoning. The method is evaluated on Stanford Dogs (fine‑grained), Pascal VOC (multi‑label), and RSNA (medical pathology), and its faithfulness is argued via axioms from Sundararajan et al. (2017). Several reviewers consider the work a straightforward engineering combination of prior ideas (B‑Cos + prototypical networks) with limited novelty, lacking critical ablations, comparisons to other interpretable models, and quantitative faithfulness evaluation. One reviewer (score 5) is more positive, highlighting the theoretical grounding and visual explanations; another (score 1) is very critical, citing poor presentation, missing comparisons, and uninterpretable similarities.

### Strengths
- Well‑written and easy to follow; Figures 1–4 are clear and support the method. Theoretical basis of B‑Cos and Nadaraya‑Watson head is explained well.
- Provides both local (pixel‑level) and global (class‑prototype) explanations.
- Solid proof that explanations satisfy faithfulness axioms (Sundararajan et al. 2017).
- Works on both CNN and ViT backbones; applied to general classification and medical domain.
- Evaluated on three diverse datasets with visual and quantitative evidence of support vectors influencing decisions, useful for debugging.
- The idea of fusing few‑shot learning with powerful B‑Cos networks is promising.
- **Minority positive (Reviewer 4, score 5)**: Good insights in proposing a network that preserves axioms; appreciates theoretical basis and visual explanations.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The method is a straightforward combination of existing ideas (B‑Cos network + Nadaraya‑Watson head, which is effectively few‑shot learning / prototypical learning). Support vectors are essentially prototypes (as in ProtoPNet). The paper masks this by renaming common parts (e.g., evidence head is a ReLU on feature map). Request to prominently clarify that the Nadaraya‑Watson head is few‑shot learning.
- **Missing comparisons**: Only compared with non‑interpretable black‑box models. No quantitative or qualitative comparison with other inherently interpretable models such as vanilla B‑Cos, PIP‑Net (Rymarczyk et al. 2022), ProtoPool, Q‑SENN (Norrenbrock et al. 2024). Missing evaluation on standard interpretability benchmarks (CUB, StanfordCars).
- **Lack of ablation study**: Cannot distinguish whether predictive performance and attribution map quality come from the B‑Cos network or the few‑shot head. Ablation needed: accuracy/mAP of pure B‑Cos backbone (without NW head) on the three datasets; impact of removing ReLU in evidence head.
- **Weak faithfulness evaluation**: Only theoretical axiomatic proof; no quantitative faithfulness measure. Axioms are not universally accepted (Integrated Gradients satisfy axioms but have low measurable faithfulness). Training with support features likely causes uninterpretable similarities (e.g., Fig 4c: plant soil similar to flower; Fig 4d: spurious keyboard correlation). A human study (e.g., Hoffman et al.) is needed.
- **Notational and presentation issues**: Errors: “||w||=1” on line 190 is incorrect; Eq. 2 for \(\hat{W}\) is recursive without initial values; term c’ in lines 163–167 undefined. Writing issues at lines 118, 190, 315, 467. Paper too long for 10 pages; results and theoretical evaluations are in appendix, making main paper not self‑contained.
- **Support vector construction**: How are initial images selected for support vectors? Random selection may cause lack of variability and bias. Experiments needed: different initial subsets, biased datasets (to expose bias), varying number of support vectors per class (k), semantic meaning of each support vector, impact of k‑means parameters. Can support vectors be cropped according to saliency map to avoid background response?
- **Global explanation limitations**: Unclear what the global explanation looks like. If an input is far from all support vectors, does that indicate the model does not know it? How to analyze global behavior and bias?
- **Positive‑only attributions**: Why use only positive‑valued vectors? Why no negative attributions? What changes if ReLU is removed? Negative attributions might indicate features not present in the class.
- **Evaluation details**: Performance changes on Pascal VOC and RSNA are subtle; need statistical significance. Pascal VOC should be evaluated with average precision, not accuracy. Since BCE loss is used, can the classifier predict multiple classes for single‑label tasks (e.g., Stanford Dogs)?
- **Computational overhead**: Original B‑Cos reported up to 60% increase in training/inference time; does WASUP encounter similar or greater overhead?
- **Other concerns**: Claim “prediction is a linear transform with temperature and bias term” is not strictly true because weights are scaled depending on test sample. Difficulty in tuning hyperparameters? Adaptability to textual networks? More thorough investigation with random seeds needed (authors already mention in appendix).
