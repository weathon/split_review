## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CocoRNA, a collective RNA design method using cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL). It decomposes the RNA secondary structure design task into sub-tasks assigned to multiple agents, trained with a centralized critic and decentralized actors. A Search-Augmented Experience Replay (SAER) mechanism is introduced to improve initial learning efficiency. The model is evaluated on the Rfam dataset and the Eterna100-v2 benchmark, outperforming existing methods in design time and success rate without requiring further training or search on new tasks.

### Strengths
- The problem of RNA secondary structure design is significant in synthetic biology and gene regulation, and the paper addresses it with an efficient, scalable method.
- The use of cooperative MARL to decompose the design problem into sub-tasks is a novel and interesting approach; the centralized training with decentralized execution provides a practical solution.
- The Search-Augmented Experience Replay (SAER) technique, inspired by Hindsight Experience Replay (HER), improves sample efficiency and computational efficiency.
- Empirical evaluations are provided on two real-world benchmark datasets (Rfam and Eterna100-v2), with robust results showing improved success rate and design time over existing methods.
- Ablation studies substantiate the contribution of individual components.
- The paper is clearly structured, with logical progression from problem statement to methodology and evaluation.
- The approach demonstrates the potential of cooperative MARL for biological sequence design.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The collective design idea has already been introduced in [1] (a combinatorial optimization approach), and the paper primarily applies established MARL methods without clear, task-specific innovations in the reward function or SAER. The contributions over [1] and over prior RL-based methods are not clearly articulated (R1-W1, R3-W2, R4-W1; reviewer 1 score 3 is most critical, reviewer 3 score 6 is more positive but still notes limited novelty).
- **No theoretical analysis of convergence**: The paper claims convergence of distributed policies to the global optimum (abstract, lines 126-129) but provides no guarantee, analysis, or even intuition to support this (R1-W2).
- **Missing analysis on sparse and delayed rewards**: The reward function (equation 12) is used, but no experiments with a sparse reward setting (e.g., R_t = 0 if H_t > 0 else C) are presented to justify robustness to sparsity (R1-W3; also R1 Q3).
- **Lack of generalization analysis across target structures**: The paper motivates CocoRNA as mitigating generalization issues of RL-based methods, but does not empirically show how it generalizes across different target structures (R1-W4; also R1 Q4).
- **Related work section needs restructuring**: The section should include RL-based methods for biological sequence design (e.g., [4,5]) as potential SOTA baselines and discuss why MARL is needed over single-agent RL (R1-W5).
- **Insufficient ablation studies**:
  - No ablation on the number of agents (grouping of positions) to show effectiveness of the decomposition; only n agents per position is tested (R1-W6).
  - No ablation on alternative decomposition schemes (e.g., position + structure assignment per agent) (R1-W7; also R2 Q4 regarding structure-based decomposition).
  - No ablation comparing separate policies vs. a shared policy across agents; it is unclear why separate policies are justified given the symmetric nature of position-based decomposition (R2-W2; also R2 Q6).
  - Training performance (e.g., learning curves) and hyperparameter sensitivity are not provided (R1-W11).
- **Reproducibility issues**: Code is not available, some implementation details are missing (e.g., how SAER goals are defined and sampled; how the SAER operation is gradually reduced), and the random dataset split may leak structure-structure similarities (R2-W3; also R1 Q5, R2 Q2, Q3, Q5, Q6).
- **Limited baselines and comparisons**: The method is compared only against RL-based baselines. Missing comparisons with search-based methods (antaRNA, MCTS-RNA) and RL+local search hybrids [3] as mentioned in the paper, as well as generative model baselines (Patil et al, 2024). These would better demonstrate CocoRNA’s relative advantage (R1-W9; also R2 Q5).
- **Model-based dependency**: The framework still relies on repeatedly applying folding algorithms for reward calculation, which may limit scalability (R2-W1).
- **Need for deeper conceptual insights**: The paper would benefit from analyzing how different decomposition approaches impact learning, task-specific challenges in RNA sequence alignment, and how partial observations might be mitigated (e.g., via GNN-based policies) (R3-W3; also R2 Q8).
- **Lack of visual aids**: No diagrams or flowcharts to illustrate the MARL framework, SAER, or the decomposition, which would aid understanding (R3-W1).
- **Insufficient explanation of agent cooperation**: Beyond the observation and reward design, more explanation is needed on how agents cooperatively solve the design task (R4-W2).
- **No discussion of limitations**: The paper does not discuss potential drawbacks, such as cases where the method may fail or trade-offs in decomposition choices (R1-W12, R4-W3; combined).
