## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces STAGE, a method for zero-shot generalization of GNNs across graphs with distinct attribute domains. STAGE constructs STAGE-edge-graphs that capture statistical dependencies between node attributes (using conditional probability matrices) instead of raw attribute values, enabling transfer to unseen domains by recognizing analogous dependencies. It provides a theoretical link between maximal invariants and statistical dependency measures. Empirical results on e-commerce and social network datasets show relative improvements of 40–103% in Hits@1 for link prediction and 10% for node classification over baselines. The paper is well written and presents extensive quantitative results, but reviewers raise several concerns about computational complexity, limited analysis of higher-order interactions, weak experimental design (lack of ablation studies, missing baselines, insufficient scalability and domain diversity), and unclear motivation linking limitations to the proposed solution. There is also an information leakage concern (earlier ICML workshop submission) and code not released.

### Strengths

- The paper is well written and easy to follow. (Reviewer 1)
- The idea of learning statistical dependencies between attributes rather than raw values to enable zero-shot generalization across distinct attribute domains is novel and domain-agnostic. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- The theoretical basis connecting maximal invariants and statistical dependency measures provides a sound foundation for the model’s generalization capabilities. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- Extensive quantitative results on multiple datasets demonstrate SOTA performance in zero-shot link prediction (40–103% Hits@1 improvement) and node classification (10% improvement) across graphs with entirely new feature spaces. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- STAGE is adaptable across domains of varied attribute types and dimensions, a crucial quality for real-world applicability. (Reviewers 3, 4)

### Weaknesses

- **Complexity and scalability concerns:** The STAGE-edge-graph is a fully connected weighted graph constructed for each edge, involving all pairwise attribute combinations. This appears computationally expensive with large attribute dimensionality. The paper lacks a detailed complexity analysis and does not demonstrate scalability on graphs larger than the E-Commerce Stores dataset (17,463 nodes—insufficient to show applicability to real-world graphs with millions of nodes and edges). (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Limited capture of higher-order interactions:** STAGE focuses on pairwise dependencies between attributes at each node pair (edge-centric), potentially missing complex multi-hop dependencies or higher-order patterns (e.g., chains of three or more nodes). The theoretical justification mentions higher-order conditional independence tests but provides no concrete examples or empirical evidence that STAGE captures such interactions in practice. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Weak experimental design and missing analyses:**
  - Ablation studies are lacking—neither the STAGE-edge-graph construction nor the choice of statistical dependency measure is ablated. (Reviewer 2)
  - The experiments in sections 4.2 (link prediction) and 4.3 (node classification) evaluate similar aspects of generalization and could benefit from a unified analysis. (Reviewer 2)
  - Comparisons with baseline methods mentioned in the introduction are missing, weakening the ability to contextualize performance gains. (Reviewer 2)
  - Sensitivity analysis across different GNN backbones is insufficient; only GCN results are shown, while STAGE’s reliance on specific architectures like NBFNet for M1/M2 raises questions about robustness across diverse message-passing mechanisms. (Reviewer 3)
  - No qualitative analysis is provided to illustrate *how* the model learns analogous dependencies (e.g., learns “income positively correlates with phone price” in training and then discovers “height positively correlates with clothing size” in a new domain) or to show which learned dependencies are actually effective on test domains. (Reviewer 4)
- **Motivation and connection of proposed method to limitations:** The introduction does not clearly analyze why the identified limitations of existing methods are addressed by STAGE; the logical leap from “failure to use node attributes” to “learning statistical dependencies” is not well explained, making it hard to understand the intrinsic research thinking. (Reviewer 2)
- **Limited domain diversity in evaluation:** Experiments focus on e-commerce and social network datasets. The method’s universality claims are weakened by the lack of evaluation on biomedical, geospatial, or other domains with diverse graph structures and attribute types (e.g., heterogeneous or mixed media data). (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing discussion on LLM variants:** The paper does not discuss how STAGE compares or could be combined with methods based on large language models for zero-shot generalization. (Reviewer 1)
- **Potential author information leakage:** This paper appears to have been previously submitted to an ICML workshop under the same name, and the current version primarily extends real-world testing datasets without addressing prior reviewer feedback. (Reviewer 2)
- **Code not released:** Although not compulsory, the lack of released code limits reproducibility. (Reviewer 2)
- **Interpretability not evaluated:** The paper does not evaluate or discuss the interpretability of the learned statistical dependencies (e.g., whether dependencies are meaningful or spurious). (Reviewer 3, question)
- **Handling of highly heterogeneous attribute domains unclear:** It is unclear how STAGE handles attributes with different data types (e.g., unstructured or mixed media) in a single domain. (Reviewer 3, question)
