## Merged Review

### Summary
LLM-Streamline compresses LLMs via two stages: contiguous layer pruning (based on cosine similarity to identify low-importance layers) and layer replacement (training a lightweight network to recover performance lost from pruning). A new stability metric is introduced to address limitations of accuracy‑based evaluation. Experiments across classification and generation benchmarks show it outperforms prior and concurrent state‑of‑the‑art pruning methods in both accuracy retention and training efficiency.

### Strengths
- Layer replacement is a novel contribution: training a lightweight network to compensate for pruned layers effectively mitigates performance loss and reduces computational requirements (Reviewers 1,2,3). Reviewer 4 finds the concept intriguing.
- The proposed stability metric addresses limitations of traditional accuracy metrics for evaluating pruned models, with thorough analysis of its advantages (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- Comprehensive experimental evaluation covering multiple benchmarks and model types, with detailed ablation studies and exploration of various lightweight network designs (Reviewers 2,3,4).
- Detailed comparison between perplexity and cosine similarity as pruning metrics effectively demonstrates the data‑sensitive drawbacks of perplexity (Reviewer 4).
- Continuous layer pruning simplifies the compressed model and is easier to accelerate on hardware compared to unstructured or other pruning methods (Reviewer 3).
- Practical implications for real‑world deployment under resource constraints (Reviewer 2).
- The paper is well‑written and easy to follow (Reviewer 4).
- Reviewer 4 additionally notes the exploration of multiple designs for lightweight networks as a strong point.

### Weaknesses
- **Note:** Reviewer 1 found no weaknesses, but other reviewers identify multiple limitations as listed below.
- **Limited sparsity range:** Sparsity levels tested do not exceed 25% (layer removal). Higher sparsity (e.g., 50% as in LLMPruner) is not evaluated, raising concerns about the robustness of contiguous pruning and layer replacement at greater removal rates (Reviewers 3,4). Reviewer 4 explicitly requests comparison at 50% layer removal against baselines.
- **Narrow architecture evaluation:** Evaluation is limited to LLaMA‑based models; generalizability to diverse architectures (e.g., MoE, GQA) and larger scales (LLaMA‑30B, LLaMA‑70B) is not demonstrated (Reviewer 3). (In contrast, Reviewer 4 considers the coverage of LLMs and benchmarks comprehensive – this disagreement is preserved.)
- **Missing comparisons with other techniques:** (a) Knowledge distillation for LLMs, e.g., Ko et al. (2024) (Reviewer 1); (b) LoRA and other fine‑tuning/compression methods, including detailed comparison of trainable parameters, training cost, and hyperparameter sensitivity (Reviewer 2); (c) Real hardware performance (inference speed, FLOPs, memory footprint) compared to other structured pruning methods (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient justification for cosine similarity:** A detailed analysis comparing cosine similarity with alternative metrics (e.g., perplexity, Euclidean distance) and explaining how it captures layer redundancy in transformers is needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Clarity of the stability metric:** The explanation lacks an intuitive definition and a concrete example of high accuracy but low stability; such clarification would improve understanding (Reviewer 2).
- **Uncertain inference speed benefits of layer replacement:** Reviewer 4 questions whether replacing layers with FFN or Transformer layers provides the full speedup expected from layer pruning, and requests a speedup comparison between layer replacement and pure layer pruning.
- **Unclear how to set the number of contiguous layers to prune (n in Eq. 3):** No elaboration on how many layers are typically removed at once (Reviewer 4).
- **One‑shot vs. iterative pruning unclear:** It is not specified whether the method is one‑shot or iterative; a comparison between the two approaches is requested (Reviewer 4).
- **Choice of MSE loss for the replacement module:** The decision to train with MSE instead of the original language model loss is not justified, and its impact is unexplored (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing computational cost details:** The training time and resources required for layer replacement training are not reported (Reviewer 3).
