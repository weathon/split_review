## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MV-CLAM, a multimodal LLM framework with a novel MQ-Former (multi-querying transformer) that aligns 1D (SMILES), 2D, and 3D molecular representations with text via a shared self-attention layer. It is evaluated on molecule-text retrieval and molecule captioning on PubChem324K, with additional demonstrations for zero-shot molecule editing and molecule-related QA.

### Strengths
- **Novel multi-view integration**: The framework incorporates 1D, 2D, and 3D molecular representations simultaneously, using MQ-Former to consolidate them into a unified token for LLM alignment. This is considered a promising approach by multiple reviewers.
- **State-of-the-art results claimed**: On the PubChem324K dataset, MV-CLAM achieves SOTA for both molecule captioning and molecule-text retrieval (reviewers 3 and 4 note improvements over baselines). However, one reviewer (score 3) disputes the captioning SOTA, referencing results in [6].
- **Clear methodology description**: The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with detailed figures (Figures 1–3) that explain the framework and training scheme. The analysis of attention maps in Appendix A.4 provides useful insights into model behavior.
- **Case studies and zero-shot examples**: The paper includes case studies and examples of zero-shot molecule editing, which help illustrate the method's potential (reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental novelty**: The approach is seen as an incremental extension of 3D-MoLM (Li et al., 2024) by simply adding a 2D branch through MAT; the MQ-Former architecture does not introduce significant structural innovations beyond the existing Q-Former. The multi-query mechanism is considered a minor variation, and the core idea of aligning multiple molecular views with text is not novel. (Reviewers 2, 4; scores 3, 3, 5, 5 – disagreement: some reviewers [scores 5] saw merit, while others [scores 3] considered innovation lacking.)
- **Limited evaluation scope**: Experiments are restricted to molecule-text retrieval and captioning on PubChem324K only. Missing essential tasks like molecule generation, property prediction, and evaluation on diverse datasets such as ChEBI-20, Mol-Instructions, and CHEBI-20 benchmark. The absence of molecule generation is a notable gap for practical applications. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Incomplete baselines and comparisons**: 
  - For molecule captioning, only a single T5 baseline is included; more recent chemical LMs (nach0, Text+ChemT5, SciFive, PRESTO, GitMol) are absent.
  - For retrieval, strong methods like RAG and ChemBERTa are not compared; other multi-modal representation learning works (e.g., [5]) are missing.
  - MolT5 is not included in Table 1, raising the question of whether scaling MolT5 could match performance.
  - No comparison of the full 1D+2D+3D model against a 1D+2D variant to justify the need for 3D input. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Insufficient ablation and hyperparameter analysis**:
  - The necessity of the two components of Molecule-text Contrasting loss is not experimentally proven; the impact of negative sample count is under-explored.
  - The effect of key hyperparameters (query token count \(K\), negative sample count in MTM loss) is not studied.
  - No ablation on the choice of SciBERT, 2D/3D encoders, or LLaMA2; it is unclear how results would change with other models. (Reviewer 3)
- **Zero-shot editing and QA underdeveloped**: 
  - The zero-shot molecule editing task is not formally defined, lacks metrics, and has no baselines or comparisons with conditional generation models.
  - The molecule-related question answering part is practically absent from the paper, despite being claimed in the abstract and results. (Reviewer 1)
- **Unclear methodology for retrieval**: The paper does not clarify whether molecule-text retrieval is performed via generative decoding (e.g., GENRE) or cross-modal embedding similarity. (Reviewer 3)
- **3D conformer generation details and necessity**: 
  - The conformer generation method (likely ETKDG + MMFF optimization) is not fully specified; the dependence of model quality on specific conformations is not assessed.
  - Since SMILES and 2D graphs can reconstruct RDKit conformations near-perfectly, the added value of explicit 3D input is questionable and unvalidated. (Reviewer 1)
- **SMILES-only modality and missing SELFIES**: The paper treats SMILES as a key modality but does not mention SELFIES, which offers robust tokenization advantages for LLMs. (Reviewer 2)
- **Implementation decisions unjustified**: Choices for SciBERT as language encoder, 2D/3D encoders, number of query tokens, and LLaMA2 (version not specified) are not motivated; the impact of replacing each component is not explored. (Reviewer 3)
- **Generalizability concerns**: The method requires 2D and 3D molecular data in addition to SMILES; it is unclear whether the model can generalize to unseen SMILES where 2D/3D representations must be generated on the fly. (Reviewer 3)
- **Presentation issues**: Some images (e.g., large image on page 18) are not vector graphics and lack titles/captions; plots have small, hard-to-read fonts. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Typos and missing citations**: Transformer misspelled (Line 102); under-specified \(A\) (Line 194); missing citation for LoRA (Line 234). (Reviewer 3)
