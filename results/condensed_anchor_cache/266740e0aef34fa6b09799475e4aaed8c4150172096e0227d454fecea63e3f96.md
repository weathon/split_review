## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper theoretically and numerically investigates the interpolation effect of score smoothing in score-based diffusion models, focusing on a simplified setting where training data lie in a one-dimensional subspace. The authors analyze a local smoothing approach on the empirical score function (ESF) and show that smoothing enables interpolation between training points and mitigates memorization. They derive mathematical properties (e.g., bounded KL divergence to a uniform distribution) and provide numerical illustrations to support their claims.

### Strengths
- The paper starts with a simple, accessible 1D two-data-point analysis to motivate insights before moving to more technical results (R1, R3).
- Well-organized with clear presentation; theoretical results on two points and one-dimensional subspace are intuitive and easy to follow (R3).
- Valuable insights: the smoothed ESF approximation yields a bounded KL divergence to a uniform distribution, unlike the direct ESF (R1).
- Novel perspective: score smoothing enables generative models to interpolate between data points and avoid full memorization, providing insight into generalization in diffusion models (R4).
- Numerical experiments illustrate the interpolation behavior, supporting the theoretical analysis (R2, R4).
- Mathematical properties of the smoothed score function are derived (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Major gap between problem and model (R2, score=5, soundness=1)**: The paper starts from memorization vs. generalization but does not convincingly link the smoothed score model to practice. Two key questions are not well-studied: (1) How do we know a neural-network learned score estimator has the specific smoothing effect studied? (2) Even if the score estimator is smoothed, how does that directly connect to overcoming memorization?
- **Limited scope and distribution-agnostic claim (R3)**: The analysis is confined to a one-dimensional subspace. It is unclear how results extend to high-dimensional practical data. The claim of being "distribution-agnostic" (line 475) is questionable given the highly simplified assumption (R3).
- **Relevance to true score (R2)**: In the one-dimensional model, the empirical score function has a closed form, but the authors study its smoothed version instead. It is not clear how properties of the smoothed score inform understanding of the true score, even in this simple case.
- **Presentation issues noted by multiple reviewers**:
  - Equation (8) should be introduced before Lemma 1, not after (R1). Lemma 1 uses the smoothed score implicitly and then redefines it.
  - Typo at line 057: $\sigma$ should be $t$ inside the square root (R1, R4).
  - In Section 3, the relationship between $\kappa$ and $t$ is confusing: Proposition 2 defines $\kappa$ by $t$, but Proposition 3 restricts $t$ to $[0,\kappa]$. It is unclear which should be specified first in practice (R4).
- **Motivation for the specific smoothing technique (R1, R4)**: The choice of local smoothing is not fully intuitive. The paper acknowledges this limitation but could discuss earlier whether, in practice, neural networks would learn anything akin to this particular smoothed score (R1). Even if the smoothed score is close to the true score in $L^2$, it remains unclear why this specific smoothing is preferable over alternatives (R4).
- **Unclear interpretation of numerical experiments (R2)**: The distribution after adding noise must be standard Gaussian stationary in all three cases, so it is unclear what new information is provided by plotting densities and histograms.
- **Unclear necessity of multiple auxiliary functions (R2)**: The paper introduces three versions of the score function ($\hat{x}_t^{(n)}$, $\bar{s}_t^{(n)}$, $\widehat{s}^{(n)}_{t, \tau}$) and a smoothed target distribution $\hat{p}_t^{(n)}$. The relation between them and the necessity of these auxiliary constructions should be clarified.
- **Missing connection to related literature (R2)**: Papers on learning Gaussian mixture models with diffusion models (e.g., arXiv:2404.18869 and arXiv:2404.18893) may be relevant. The authors do not comment on how their analysis relates to such work.
- **No direct link to memorization/hallucination (R3)**: It is not clear how the analysis connects back to the memorization/hallucination behaviors mentioned in the introduction.
