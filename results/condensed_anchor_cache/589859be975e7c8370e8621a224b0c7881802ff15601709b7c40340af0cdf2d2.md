- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 3, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes IntelLLM, a training-free KV cache compression method for LLMs that combines two eviction strategies: Center of Gravity Eviction (CGE) and Remote Gap Localization (RGL). CGE retains tokens near the “center of gravity” of attention to preserve semantic dependencies, while RGL assigns positional gaps to maintain long-range dependencies. Evaluations on LongBench with Llama-3-8B and Mistral-7B report a 50% KV cache reduction with minimal latency increase and performance sometimes exceeding the full KV cache. All five reviewers rated the paper 3 (weak reject), with ratings of 3, 3, 3, 3, 3. While they acknowledge the importance of KV cache compression, they raise substantial concerns about novelty, baseline selection, evaluation rigor, clarity, and formal support. One reviewer (R5) expressed a positive personal reaction and willingness to raise their rating with rebuttal, while others (R3, R4) rated soundness/presentation as 1.

### Strengths

- **Problem significance.** All reviewers agree that KV cache compression for long-context LLM inference is an important and timely problem (R1, R4).
- **Novel combination of strategies.** The proposed CGE and RGL are viewed as interesting techniques (R1) that provide a novel solution to memory efficiency without fine-tuning (R2).
- **Strong empirical performance on LongBench.** IntelLLM achieves 50% cache reduction while matching or exceeding the full KV cache on several LongBench datasets (R1, R2, R3). It also outperforms baselines like StreamingLLM and LM-Infinite (R5).
- **Ease of integration.** The method requires minimal code changes and no model parameter tuning, making it practical for real-world deployment (R2, R3, R5).
- **Clear analysis of attention sparsity.** Reviewer 5 specifically praised the explanation of how softmax leads to attention concentration on few tokens, calling it “insightful and well-reasoned.”
- **Compatibility across multiple models.** Results are shown for both Llama-3-8B and Mistral-7B (R2, R5).
- **Inspirational idea.** Reviewer 5 stated they “really like the idea proposed by this paper, which I personally find very inspiring” and indicated they would raise their rating with proper rebuttal and additional experiments. (This positive opinion contrasts with the overall weak reject.)

### Weaknesses

1. **Limited novelty; sparse attention already exploited.** The core observation that only a few tokens dominate attention weights has been extensively used in prior works. Reviewer 1 notes StreamingLLM and FastGen (both ICLR 2024). Reviewer 2 adds H2O, SnapKV, and PyramidKV. Reviewer 4 and 5 list further works. The paper does not adequately differentiate CGE from methods like H2O and SnapKV (R2: “GCE is pretty close to previous methods…I can only see limited novelty”).
2. **Insufficient and weak baseline comparisons.** The evaluation only includes StreamingLLM and LM-Infinite (plus a vague mention of InfiniteLLM). Reviewers call for at least: H2O, SnapKV, PyramidKV, SirLLM, IntervalLLM, FastGen, and end-to-end methods like InfLLM and MInference (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5). Reviewer 3 questions the rationale for choosing only StreamingLLM and InfiniteLLM.
3. **Missing related works.** The paper fails to cite and discuss multiple relevant KV cache compression papers from 2023–2024. Reviewers list: FastGen, SnapKV, XC-Cache, Layer-Condensed KV Cache, PyramidInfer, PyramidKV, H2O, and more (R1, R2, R4).
4. **No formal proofs for claimed theorems.** Sections 3.2 and 3.3 present two “theorems” that are actually empirical findings without any formal proof or rigorous justification (R1, R3, R4). This undermines the theoretical contribution.
5. **Evaluation is flawed and incomplete.** Specific issues:
   - Asymmetric window sizes: IntelLLM uses 4K window, baselines use 8K, making comparison unfair (R1).
   - No side-by-side efficiency comparison (R1, R5). The paper only reports 2.63% latency increase for pre-fill, but does not measure decoding speed or peak memory; Reviewer 5 notes “the speed reported…cannot state the research intention.”
   - It is unclear whether memory savings are always exactly 50% (R1); no ablation on compression rate (R3).
   - Best-performing methods are not clearly marked in the result tables (R3).
   - Evaluations differ between Mistral and Llama without explanation (R4; see missing Table 2 reference).
6. **Lack of ablation studies.** No separate evaluation of CGE and RGL (R3, R5). Hyperparameters such as \(k\) (number of top keys as “centers of gravity”), \(L_{comp}\), \(L_{near}\) are not studied (R3, R5). No comparison of RGL against alternative position assignment methods (e.g., continuous position IDs vs. no positional information for distant tokens) (R5).
7. **Evaluate on limited, easy benchmarks.** LongBench has relatively short average lengths and limited difficulty. Reviewers suggest harder/longer benchmarks: L-Eval, RULER, InfiniteBench, and retrieval tasks like Ret.PassKey (R5). Needle-in-a-Haystack test is specifically requested to validate long-range dependency claims (R2).
8. **Poor presentation and unclear writing.** Many sections are confusing:
   - The introduction and problem statement are vague (R1: “unclear what the two limitations are”; line 83: “significant memory saved?”; no speed gains reported).
   - Section 3.1 analysis lacks evidence or citations; terms like “robustness of attention distribution” undefined (R1).
   - Algorithm 1 is hard to follow; notations like \(n, m, l_{head}, l_{tail}\) and operation \([:]\) are undefined (R5).
   - Terms like “Center of Gravity” are not formally defined in the KV cache context (R5).
   - Figures 1 and 2 lack axis labels (R5).
   - Minor errors: “Llama3-7B” does not exist (should be Llama-3-8B) (R5); citation format misuse (R5).
9. **Missing implementation and reproducibility details.** No discussion of how to choose \(k\), no details on hyperparameters for baselines (R3, R5). The paper does not specify how much KV cache the baselines retain (R5).
10. **No limitations or future work section.** Reviewer 5 highlights this omission.
11. **Contrasting opinion on novelty.** While most reviewers criticize limited novelty, Reviewer 5 found the idea inspiring and would raise their rating given a strong rebuttal. This positive minority view should be noted.
