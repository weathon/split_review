## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Matcher, a training-free perception paradigm that combines off-the-shelf vision foundation models (DINOv2 as feature encoder, SAM as promptable segmenter) to perform various segmentation tasks via in-context learning. Three components are designed: Correspondence Matrix Extraction (bidirectional matching), Prompts Generation (part-level, instance-level, global-level sampling), and Controllable Masks Generation (instance-level matching with ILM postprocessing). Matcher achieves state-of-the-art one-shot results on COCO-20$^i$ (52.7% mIoU, +1.6% over specialist) and LVIS-92$^i$ (33.0% mIoU, +14.4% over generalist). One reviewer (score 8) finds the method straightforward and effective, while three others (scores 5-6) note significant engineering reliance and missing analyses.

### Strengths
- The target task (becoming a vision generalist via in-context learning) is timely and interesting; Matcher follows the direction of SegGPT in transferring VFMs to general segmentation. (R1)
- New benchmarks for one-shot/few-shot in-context segmentation are constructed and are challenging for future work. (R1)
- Performance is strong across multiple segmentation tasks (semantic, part, VOS). (R1, R2, R4)
- No training or fine-tuning is required, improving efficiency compared to specialist models. (R2, R3)
- The three designed components (bi-directional matching, diverse prompt sampling, controllable mask generation) are well motived and effective according to ablation studies. (R2, R4)
- The paper is well-written and clearly presents the framework. (R4)
- Extensive experiments are conducted, with reasonable results on many benchmarks. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- The pipeline is cumbersome and heavily engineered. Many prompt designs appear unimportant (Table 7b in appendix) and final performance significantly relies on complicated ILM postprocessing (Table 4a). (R1)
- The impressive performance likely stems from an ensemble of models (DINOv2, SAM, mask merging), which is a common source of improvement in segmentation. (R1)
- Inference time is a concern: Matcher must run both DINOv2 (large) and SAM for every test image, making it slower than SegGPT and potentially unsuitable for real-time or resource-constrained applications. (R1, R4)
- The method lacks academic insight and reads more like a technical report combining existing models and engineering tricks. (R2)
- The choice of image encoder dominates performance: MAE 18.8%, CLIP 32.2%, DINOv2 52.7% (Table 7a). This suggests encoder selection may outweigh all other design choices. (R2)
- No ablation is performed with alternative promptable segmenters (e.g., SEEM, Semantic-SAM). The claim of a general paradigm is unsubstantiated without showing the designs work on different segmenters. (R2)
- Missing analysis for cases where the test image contains no reference concept (e.g., false positives). (R2)
- No open-source code is provided; code availability would raise confidence in reproducibility. (R3)
- Comparisons to SegGPT are unfair: SegGPT uses a ViT-L backbone while Matcher uses DINOv2/CLIP pre-trained on much larger datasets. Models of comparable size should be compared. (R3)
- Implementation details are missing: (a) Why not use SAM’s own encoder as the feature extractor (given SAM’s strong training)? (b) Which layers of DINOv2 are used? (c) How is the number of clusters in the Robust Prompt Sampler chosen? (d) How does few-shot (>1) segmentation work? (e) Are failures due to localization or mask shape for few-shot cases? (R3)
- Missing ablations: (a) visualization of point prompt filtering stages (Patch-Level Matching, Robust Prompt Sampler, Instance-Level Matching); (b) random sampling the same number of positive points from SAM as Matcher generates, to estimate the upper bound of Matcher’s point selection; (c) comparison of DINOv2 correspondence with a diffusion-based approach (e.g., Stable Diffusion) on accuracy, efficiency, memory, and robustness. (R3, R4)
- Running speed comparison with related works is absent. (R4)
- It is unclear whether Matcher can segment background concepts (e.g., blue sky, pavement) like SegGPT, and performance on the more complex MOSE VOS dataset is not evaluated. (R1)
