## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper theoretically analyzes why graph neural networks (GNNs) outperform MLPs/CNNs on graph data by extending the feature learning theory of [Cao et al., 2022]. It studies two-layer GCNs trained via gradient descent under a specific generative model (SNM-SBM) that combines signal-noise node features with a stochastic block model for edges. The authors derive exact formulas for training and test accuracy, characterizing benign overfitting and showing that graph convolution enlarges the regime where signal learning dominates noise memorization by approximately factor √D^(q-2) (D = expected node degree, q>2). The theoretical results are compared to a CNN (MLP applied per node) and supported by synthetic simulations and MNIST-based experiments. While the paper is well-structured and the analysis is rigorous, reviewers had major concerns about limited novelty (closely following [1]), lack of surprising insights (feature smoothing is well-known [2]), and insufficient real-world applicability of the highly simplistic data model.

### Strengths
- **Motivation and problem formulation are important.** The paper aims to explain why GNNs work from a feature learning perspective, which could inspire better GNN designs (Reviewers 3,5).
- **The analysis is complete and rigorous for the proposed model.** The paper derives formulas for both training dynamics and generalization, treating all aspects of learning and rigorously proving the superiority of GCN over CNN under its assumptions (Reviewers 3,4). It provides detailed quantitative explanations that help build intuition (Reviewer 3).
- **The paper is clearly written and well-structured.** The problem is well formulated, proof sketches are reader-friendly, and the simulation experiments are clearly shown (Reviewers 3,4,5).
- **Several theoretical results for two-layer GCNs compared to CNNs are obtained** (Reviewer 2).
- The paper discusses both training and generalization phases (Reviewer 3). The simple simulation experiments align with theoretical predictions (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and incremental contribution.** The main theory and proof closely follow the prior feature learning theory paper [Cao et al., 2022]; the adaptation to GNNs is directly derived from that work. The authors should clearly summarize what is new compared to [1]. The core results—that GNNs benefit from feature smoothing—are well-known in the community (e.g., [Li et al., 2018]) and do not add surprising new insights or open future directions (Reviewers 4,5). The experimental observations are also already widely known (Reviewers 4,5).
- **The data model is overly simplistic and far from real-world graphs.** The two-class model uses only a template signal and its negative, with noise supported on disjoint indices (no additive noise on signal dimensions). The node feature decomposition into orthogonal signal/noise groups is artificial; there is no known method to partition real features this way. The model does not incorporate homophily—a key factor in GNN analysis—and focuses only on feature distributions while ignoring structural aspects like interclass vs. intraclass edges. The practical significance of the theoretical results is unclear; they do not translate into tangible guidelines for GCN design or training. A stronger connection to real graph structures (e.g., via adjacency or Laplacian matrices) is needed to demonstrate the impact of graph structure on generalization (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- **Lack of real-world validation.** Experiments are only synthetic or on MNIST with an artificially constructed graph; they do not show that the proposed model can approximate any real graph dataset. The MNIST experiment differs from the theory (two different templates vs. a single template with opposite signs) and the graph construction method (interclass/intraclass connectivity) is not fully explained. The paper does not demonstrate how to fit the model parameters to real data or whether the theoretical conclusions hold for real graphs (Reviewers 2,3,4). The question about whether a fully model-consistent experiment was run remains unanswered (Reviewer 4). Empirical results lack variance bars (Figures 3 and 4) to show stability (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear significance of the theoretical results.** It is not clear whether the purpose is to improve training efficiency, explain why GCNs are better, or something else. The theoretical analysis does not fully incorporate matrix A or L, so the role of graph structure is not explicitly reflected in the derived generalization bounds (Reviewers 1,2). Reviewers asked for clarification on why the model is called a CNN rather than a per-node MLP (Reviewer 4), and whether the binary classification analysis extends to multiclass settings (Reviewer 3). A more rigorous explanation of the signal-noise dimension split and its intuition is needed (Reviewer 3). Minor issue: subsection 3.4 could be part of 3.3; the use of “MLP” vs. “CNN” should be standardized (Reviewer 3); a typo “by” above Eq. (3) (Reviewer 5).
