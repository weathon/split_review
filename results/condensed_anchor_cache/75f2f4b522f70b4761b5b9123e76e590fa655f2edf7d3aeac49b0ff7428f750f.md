## Merged Review

### Summary

Three reviewers assessed this paper on improving cold-start link prediction by duplicating low-degree nodes (NodeDup, and NodeDup(L) with self-loops). The method is simple, plug-and-play, and shows significant gains on isolated, low-degree, and warm nodes without harming high-degree performance. Extensive experiments and ablations are conducted across multiple datasets and encoder/decoder combinations. Reviewers agree that the problem is meaningful and the empirical results are strong, but raise concerns about the definition of cold nodes, novelty of the augmentation, missing baselines and large-scale experiments, problematic negative-sampling setup, contradictory metrics to prior work, and insufficient mechanistic analysis of why duplication works.

### Strengths

- **Significant improvement on cold-start nodes without degrading warm/high-degree performance** – the method achieves 38.49%, 13.34%, and 6.76% improvements on isolated, low-degree, and warm nodes respectively on average across all datasets.
- **Simple, plug-and-play augmentation** – can be easily combined with arbitrary GNN encoders (GraphSAGE, GAT, JKNet) and decoders; introduces negligible computational overhead.
- **Extensive empirical evaluation** – ablation studies on duplication times, node selection criteria, and efficiency comparisons with other augmentation and cold-start methods (Cold-brew, DropoutEdge, etc.).
- **Compatibility and generality** – consistently outperforms baselines across different encoder architectures and datasets, including both transductive and inductive settings.

### Weaknesses

- **Definition of cold nodes is overly simplistic** – the threshold λ is fixed at 2 for all datasets, ignoring graph-specific properties (density, community structure). One reviewer noted that high-order connectivity (e.g., two-hop neighbors) should be considered: a node with 2 direct neighbors but 200 two-hop neighbors may be less “cold” than a node with 10 direct neighbors but no two-hop neighbors. The impact of varying λ on performance is not explored.
- **Lack of novelty in self-augmentation** – duplicating nodes and adding self-loops are well-known techniques in link prediction [references a,b,c,d cited by reviewer]. The paper does not sufficiently distinguish NodeDup from these prior works or from standard self-loop usage. One reviewer (score 3) rated contribution as poor.
- **Missing baselines** – no comparisons with classical LP heuristics (Common Neighbors, Jaccard, Preferential Attachment, Adamic-Adar, Resource Allocation, Katz, PageRank, SimRank) nor with state-of-the-art LP methods (e.g., SEAL). Baselines for degree-bias in GNNs (e.g., “On Generalized Degree Fairness in Graph Neural Networks”) are also absent.
- **Problematic negative sampling setup** – negative references are randomly sampled from all nodes, but in practice all remaining nodes should be the ranking candidates; this biases evaluation toward easy negatives. Experiments using all remaining nodes as candidates are missing; one reviewer also requested Hits@1 in addition to Hits@10 for tail nodes.
- **Inconsistencies with prior reported metrics** – e.g., Cold-brew underperforms vanilla GraphSAGE on Cora and Citeseer across all metrics (Table 1), contradicting earlier studies. The paper does not elaborate on the differences in setup/data partitions that could explain this.
- **Missing large-scale datasets** – experiments are limited to small-to-medium graphs (IGB, but not ogbn-products or ogbn-papers100M with millions of nodes). Given the claimed improvements on all node categories, verification on larger graphs is needed.
- **Insufficient analysis of why duplication helps** – one reviewer noted that NodeDup effectively only changes the sampling distribution (like upsampling), but the paper lacks a mechanistic explanation of why connecting duplicates back to the original provides a stronger learning signal beyond altered sampling. Another reviewer requested deeper analysis and case studies for low-degree nodes.
- **Unjustified claims and minor writing issues** – the statement that “GNNs struggle to produce good results on low-degree nodes” may not always hold; more justification is needed. There are spelling/grammar errors and inconsistent terminology (e.g., inductive vs. production settings without cross-citation). One reviewer noted the absence of significance tests for the reported improvements.
- **Several unaddressed questions that overlap with criticisms or missing experiments/ablations**:
    - What is the key innovation over existing node duplication techniques?
    - How does NodeDup(L) differ from standard self-loops in GNNs (and are self-loops already present in baselines)?
    - Why does NodeDup(L) consistently outperform NodeDup on warm nodes, given the same operation is applied?
    - How does NodeDup compare to self-distillation methods?
    - Why are all node categories (including warm nodes) substantially improved, not just cold nodes?
    - Can the definition of cold nodes be refined using high-order connectivity?
    - Provide significance tests for isolated/low-degree gains.
