## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes DiffMove, a conditional diffusion model for human trajectory recovery (imputing missing visited locations). The method transforms discrete location IDs into continuous embeddings, denoises these embeddings conditioned on historical and current context, then decodes to locations. Three modules are learned via multiple losses: a trajectory location encoder, a conditional embedding denoiser (with spatial and target conditional blocks), and an embedding decoder. Experiments on Geolife (GPS) and Foursquare (check-in) datasets show SOTA performance (average 11% recall improvement over best baselines). Ablation and robustness studies are included.

### Strengths

- **Novel adaptation of diffusion to trajectory recovery**: The noising/denoising operates on trajectory embeddings rather than raw locations, enabling effective discrete location recovery via continuous diffusion (R1, R2).
- **Strong modeling of spatiotemporal dependencies**: Per-user-per-day format captures periodicity; current/historical conditional denoiser learns transition, periodicity, and temporal patterns in low-sampling trajectories (R1, R2).
- **Solid experimental evaluation**: Extensive experiments on two real-world datasets (Geolife, Foursquare) with ablation studies and robustness analysis; SOTA results over strong baselines including TRILL (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5).
- **Clear presentation**: Well-written introduction and method description; easy to follow (R3). Design and implementation clearly outlined, supporting reproducibility (R5).
- **Practical relevance**: Human trajectory recovery enables downstream urban planning, transportation, and location-based applications (R3, R5).

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of novelty in design**: The mapping from discrete locations to continuous embedding for diffusion is standard (latent diffusion, discrete diffusion). The Spatial Conditional Block integrating historical/current trajectories is a straightforward combination of existing components (GCN, attention, transformer). Overall framework is similar to CSDI (R4, R5).
- **Insufficient and non-recent baselines**: Most compared methods are older; missing direct comparison with recent trajectory recovery models such as **SimiDTR**, **RNTrajRec**, and the diffusion-based **Diff-RNTraj**. Also omit relevant recovery works like **MtrajRec**, **RNTrajRec**, and Xi et al. (2019) for POI identification (R2, R3, R4, R5). The rationale for baseline selection is not explained (R3).
- **Missing efficiency analysis**: Computational overhead of DDPM plus GCN/attention is a concern; no runtime comparison with baselines provided. Adjacency matrix may increase time/memory for many location points. (R1, R4).
- **Unclear or debatable problem framing**: The 30-minute interval assumption may incorrectly mark stay points as missing (e.g., user stays at one location for hours). In check-in datasets (e.g., FourSquare) stay duration is typically unknown; marking consecutive same-location intervals makes recovery trivial. Also cannot capture movements within 30 minutes (R5).
- **Vague motivation and claims**: The stated limitation of previous methods (“complex, sparse, irregular, uncertain scenarios”) is not concretely shown to be addressed by DiffMove. The claim of “biased deterministic imputation” vs. probabilistic generation is not evaluated experimentally (R2, R3, R5). Abstract’s “11% improvement” potentially misleads; it should be reported relative to best baseline as in Table 1 (R3).
- **Insufficient dataset variety**: Only two datasets (Geolife, Foursquare); including more would test generalization (R1, R4).
- **Missing details and clarifications**:
  - How many locations are missing per trajectory? Fixed or random mask? (R3).
  - Specification of visited location embedding and mask embedding (R2, R4).
  - How GPS points are converted to discrete locations (region mapping) (R4).
  - Clarify “gather data on road network” for location representation (R1).
  - Distinction between trajectory recovery and generation; are prior works [1][2][3] predictive or generative? (R3).
- **Multiple losses**: How do different losses matter to final performance? How are loss coefficients determined? (R3).
- **Network complexity concerns**: The denoising network uses GCN, cross-attention, and transformer simultaneously; may hurt performance; GCN may not yield good results (R4).
- **Minor writing and illustration issues**:
  - Fig.1: Inconsistency in number of points (raw current 9, masked 2, current trajectory 4); components missing labels (e.g., “blue and white location icons”), legend of Fig.1 missing period in Fig.2 (R3).
  - Vague figure references: “shown in the top right part of Figure 1” (R5).
  - Wording issues: line 060 “cannot be fully exploited”? Eqn(2) hard to identify (R3).
  - Some sentences not fluent (R4).
  - Formatting: mention of “incoming/outgoing aggregated node embedding eI,s/eo,s” and cosine similarity formula representation (R2).
