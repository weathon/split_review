## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper analyzes the convergence of a second-order discretization method, SDE-DPM-2, for diffusion models. Under \(L^2\)-accurate score estimates and smoothness assumptions, it proves that SDE-DPM-2 achieves an \(O(1/\epsilon)\) sampling complexity to obtain \(O(\epsilon^2)\) KL divergence, improving over the exponential integrator (EI) which has \(O(1/\epsilon^2)\). The paper also analyzes the Runge-Kutta-2 (RK-2) method and shows it does not achieve the same improved rate, suggesting SDE-DPM-2 is more efficient. The analysis is extended to Variance Exploding (VE) SDEs, showing similar convergence.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a timely and important question in diffusion model research: which discretization schemes are most sample-efficient for inference.
- It provides theoretical convergence guarantees for a second-order SDE solver (SDE-DPM-2), improving the sample complexity from \(O(1/\epsilon^2)\) to \(O(1/\epsilon)\) for achieving \(O(\epsilon^2)\) KL divergence.
- It distinguishes between different second-order solvers, showing that not all achieve the same rate; SDE-DPM-2 outperforms RK-2 theoretically, highlighting that "not all second-order solvers are equal".
- The theory is supported by empirical experiments on Gaussian mixtures and CIFAR-10, showing modest improvement in FID with small numbers of steps and improved convergence with finer discretization.
- The analysis framework is extended to VE SDEs, demonstrating generality across forward processes (VP and VE).
- The writing is clear and provides both theoretical and empirical comparisons with the most related papers.

### Weaknesses
- **Overly strong assumptions that limit applicability:**  
  - Assumption 2 requires the time-derivative of the score function to be accurately estimated, which is stronger than the standard value accuracy assumption; it is contingent on the loss function and cannot be generally guaranteed or validated.  
  - Assumptions 3 and 4 require the score function, its gradient, and higher-order derivatives to be bounded uniformly, which is very strong near \(t=0\) (score changes singularly) and is only verified for Gaussian mixtures, limiting applicability to complex data distributions (e.g., low-dimensional manifolds).  
  - The paper does not adequately discuss these limitations or justify the assumptions for real-world data.  
- **Insufficient novelty and contribution relative to existing and concurrent work:**  
  - Concurrent work (Wu et al.) claims RK-2 is provably fast, conflicting with the paper’s claim that SDE-DPM-2 is more efficient.  
  - Li et al. (2024) already provide \(O(1/\epsilon)\) complexity under KL and better \(O(1/\sqrt{\epsilon})\) for TV, which reduces the contribution.  
  - Other convergence analyses for high-order sampling (Huang et al. on probability flow ODE, Huang et al. on reverse transition kernel) achieve better rates but are not compared.  
  - The paper lacks a comprehensive literature review, failing to cite important empirical and theoretical studies (e.g., acceleration methods: distillation, consistency, adaptive stepsizes, parallel sampling, randomized midpoint).  
  - The claim that SDE-DPM-2 is more efficient than RK-2 is overstated; Corollary 3.3 only shows that the authors’ analysis cannot provide a better guarantee for RK-2, not that RK-2 inherently performs worse, and experiments show similar performance.  
  - The extension to VE SDEs works only when overlooking the initial error (a key difficulty), which needs to be emphasized.  
- **Writing and presentation issues:**  
  - Organization is confusing: main results and experiments appear before the proof sketch and VE discussion; a more logical flow would be theorems, proof sketch, VE, then experiments.  
  - Notation is inconsistent: \(x_k\) should be \(x_{t_k}\) in Assumption 2, eqs. (11) and (13); partial derivatives w.r.t. \(x_{t_k}\) are confusing (should denote the Jacobian matrix).  
  - Many acronyms are used without expansion.  
  - Inconsistent descriptions of the difference between EI and SDE-DPM-2 (Line 201 vs. Line 401).  
  - Table 1 provides insufficient experimental detail: metrics and improvements are vaguely described, error bars are missing, and the results largely replicate earlier work (Lu et al. 2022b).  
- **Technical issues and missing analysis:**  
  - No comparison of the computational cost of RK-2 vs. DPM-SDE-2 vs. EI.  
  - The paper should more clearly delineate its contributions relative to Chen 2023, whose approach it follows closely.  
  - Assumption 4 (involving \(\nabla^3\)) is stated but never used, suggesting a mistake or unnecessary condition.  
  - Figure 1 should be redrawn in logarithmic scale to properly corroborate the claimed convergence rates; the bounds for RK-2 on Figure 1(b) are not shown.  
  - The proof could be improved to reduce dimension dependence from \(O(d^{3/2})\) to \(O(d)\) using techniques from [2] as noted by a reviewer.  
  - The proof is not self-contained: Proposition 4.2 directly references Chen et al. without justification.  
  - Assumptions 3 and 4 both bound third-order derivatives, but temporal derivatives can be expressed in terms of spatial derivatives; why treat them separately?  
  - If Assumption 2 is replaced with the standard value accuracy assumption from [1], is the result still valid? Is there a way to ensure this assumption during training?
