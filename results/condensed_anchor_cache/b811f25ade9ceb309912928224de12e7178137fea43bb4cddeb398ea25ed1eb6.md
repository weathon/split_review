## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces DockedAC, a dataset for activity cliff (AC) analysis that incorporates 3D protein–ligand complex structures. By matching ChEMBL bioactivity data with protein binding information, molecular docking (AutoDock Vina) generates a complex structure for each activity value. The dataset contains 82,836 activity data on 52 protein targets with AC annotations. The authors benchmark traditional ML and several DL methods (2D/3D) and perform ablation studies to investigate the role of 3D conformational information. Three reviewers rate 6 (weak reject/accept), one rates 5 (negative, citing insufficient improvement from 3D data). All agree the dataset is valuable but note limitations in docking quality, model selection, and analysis depth.

### Strengths
- The dataset is significantly larger than previous high-quality AC datasets, covering 82,836 activity data across 52 targets with AC annotations.
- Inclusion of 3D structural information is crucial for AC tasks because varying interaction patterns impact affinity, while prior ligand-centric methods ignore the protein environment.
- The curation process is clear and well-organized, with data and benchmarking code thoroughly documented.
- The benchmark includes a wide range of ML and DL methods (KNN, RF, SVM, ECFP, 2D graphs, IGN, SS-GNN, etc.).
- Ablation studies (Appendix B) and correlation studies between RMSE and RMSE_cliff provide informative insights into the potential of 3D GNNs for AC modeling.
- The authors generate pseudo-binding structures via docking, overcoming the 2D-only limitation of earlier AC datasets.

### Weaknesses
- **Docking quality is insufficiently validated**: Whether docking conformations (generated from a single rigid protein structure with AutoDock Vina) capture true interaction differences between AC pairs is not verified. The conformations may differ from crystal structures and may not reliably reflect actual binding modes. Using only one docking score, with no exploration of multiple scoring functions (e.g., Smina, Quick Vina, GLIDE, DiffDock, KarmaDock) or local minima, limits confidence. Using flexible docking (multiple protein rotamer states) or multiple protein structures per target would help reduce bias from crystal structure conformation.
- **No evaluation of docking data quality**: There is no quantitative analysis of whether the generated 3D conformers correctly represent the interaction differences that define activity cliffs.
- **Current 3D complex methods show limited improvement over ligand-only baselines**: In Table 2, traditional methods (KNN, RF, SVM) and ECFP-based methods often outperform 3D GNNs. Figure 4 shows performance gains from 3D information are not very significant. The reasons for this lack of improvement are not quantitatively analyzed (Reviewer 1).
- **Chosen 3D architectures are not state-of-the-art**: The benchmark does not include recent 3D GNNs or equivariant transformers (e.g., Equiformer v2). State-of-the-art 3D binding affinity prediction models (e.g., PIGNet, RTMScore, TANKBind, DSMBind, KarmaDock) trained on general datasets (e.g., PDBbind) should be evaluated on DockedAC as a test set. Including AutoDock Vina’s empirical scoring function as a baseline is also suggested.
- **Dataset splitting rationale unclear**: It is not clearly explained how the dataset is split (line 234) and whether this prevents overfitting to popular targets (e.g., GPCRs). It is unclear if 3D DL methods are trained per target or with a single model generalized to all pockets. If only one protein at a time, the approach is difficult to scale.
- **Potential bias from ECFP in data division**: Since ECFP similarity is used to define AC pairs, using ECFP features in benchmarking may create a circular argument. Results with alternative fingerprints for defining AC pairs (e.g., MACCS, Morgan) should be shown.
- **Mixing different bioactivity units**: Training on pKi/pEC50/pIC50 together (Section 4.3) may introduce issues due to systematic differences between assays. This should be addressed.
- **Lack of verification that paired ACs genuinely represent real cliff relationships**: The definition follows prior work without explicit structural analysis (e.g., Maximum Common Substructure). The distribution of shared/different atoms in AC pairs should be checked.
- **Small sample size concerns in statistical tests**: In Section 5.3, the sample size may be too small for reliable p-value calculations. Table 3 shows AC percentage at most 0.6%; the rationale for that choice is unclear.
- **Depth of analysis**: In Section 5.1, concluding that 3D information helps AC prediction from comparing IGN with 2D models is shallow. Better to compare IGN with a 2D-only version of IGN to isolate benefits.
- **Missing citations**: Sections 3.2 and 3.5 appear identical to previous work (Van Tilborg et al. 2022) and need appropriate acknowledgment.
- **Data generation details omitted**: Questions remain about how canonical SMILES are assigned, how binding sites are deemed identical (line 213), and what the manual review of docking results entails (line 227: only rejecting ligands with score ≥ 0?). A clarifying arrow in Figure 1 would help indicate lower nM is better.
- **Minority reviewer perspective**: Reviewers 2, 3, 4 assign score 6 and see value but request improvements; Reviewer 1 (score 5) is more critical, emphasizing that 3D methods fail to beat ECFP-based traditional methods and that docking quality is unvalidated.
