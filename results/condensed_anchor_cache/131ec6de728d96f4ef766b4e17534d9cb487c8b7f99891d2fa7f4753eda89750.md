## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces RVGP, a generalization of Gaussian processes for learning vector-valued signals over latent Riemannian manifolds. The underlying manifold and tangent spaces are estimated from data using a proximity graph and SVD of neighbor direction matrices. A kernel is constructed from the spectral decomposition of the discretized connection Laplacian projected onto the estimated tangent spaces. Experiments include super-resolution and inpainting of vector fields on meshes (Section 5.1) and EEG-based disease classification (Section 5.2), where RVGP is shown to improve diagnostic capabilities.

### Strengths
- Novel extension of GPs to vector-valued signals on unknown manifolds; removes the common assumption that the manifold is known, expanding applicability.
- Method is clean and simple, and demonstrably works on the presented tasks.
- Paper is well written and easy to follow overall.
- Related work is well addressed.
- Practical effectiveness is demonstrated on real EEG data, showing improved classification accuracy of disease states.

### Weaknesses
- **Data efficiency not assessed:** The super-resolution experiment (Section 5.1) uses vectors over 50% of nodes. Results with only 10–20% observed nodes are needed to evaluate the method’s data efficiency in sparse scenarios.
- **Baseline comparison too simple:** In Section 5.2, RVGP is compared only against interpolation methods and linear prediction, to which its performance is very close. This suggests the method may not be exploiting complex structure; a comparison against a kernel method or neural network would provide a more rigorous evaluation.
- **Lack of limitations discussion:** A limitations section is missing. It should cover computational cost, sensitivity to hyperparameters, and assumptions such as how the method behaves when the underlying manifold is highly nonlinear or the vector field is not smooth.
- **Unclear kernel construction (Section "Vector-field GP on arbitrary latent manifolds"):** The notation is ambiguous (e.g., what is \((U_c)_i\)?); the shape of \(O_{ij}\) appears to change from \(m \times m\) (eq. 11) to \(d \times d\) (eq. 12); the shape of \(L_c\) is defined as \(nd \times nd\) but \(\Lambda_c, U_c\) are claimed to be \(\mathbb{R}^{nm \times nm}\). Equation 15 shows \(\Phi(\Lambda_C)^{-2}\) as \(\mathbb{R}^{mn \times mn}\) multiplied with \(P_v\) of size \(\mathbb{R}^{d \times k}\), which is dimensionally inconsistent. The overall process of projecting the connection Laplacian eigenvectors onto estimated tangent spaces to build the kernel is not fully explained.
- **Sensitivity to tangent space estimation errors:** The method relies on accurate SVD-based tangent space estimation. How errors in this estimation (e.g., when the SVD does not perfectly capture true tangent directions) affect the kernel and GP performance is not analyzed.
- **Missing quantitative evaluation in Section 5.1:** The claim that RVGP “smoothly resolved the singularity by gradually reducing the vector amplitudes to zero” is not accompanied by quantitative comparison to the ground truth. The reviewer cannot judge whether the result is correct.
- **Precision issues:** “V” is defined multiply (for vectors and for graph nodes). The statement “While G approximates M it will not restrict the domain to V” is unclear. The description “sampled vectors from a uniform distribution on the sphere” is ambiguous. \(S^3\) should be \(S^2\).
- **Conceptual ambiguity about vector fields on manifolds:** Two interpretations are possible: vectors lie on the manifold, or the domain is the manifold but vectors may point off it. In Fig. 2C, vectors protrude the mesh surface. The method is expected to work for the normal vector case (2D manifold in 3D, \(m=n-1\)), but it is unclear if it applies when \(m<n-1\) because the Levi-Civita connection may be insufficient for the complementary subspace.
- **Doubts about the “unknown manifold” claim:** In Section 5.2, the connection Laplacian eigenvectors were computed from 256 mesh vertices, not from the \(n=61\) electrode locations. This seems to violate the premise of “unknown manifold.” The paper would benefit from analyzing how data coverage of the manifold affects prediction accuracy and whether the predicted vector field remains regular for points off the manifold.
- **Missing information:** It is not stated whether the dimension of the unknown manifold must be known ahead of time. A description of how the kernel construction differs from a simpler approach (using a scalar graph Laplacian kernel \(k(i,j)\) to form \(K(i,j)=k(i,j)I_{d\times d}\) and then restricting via \(\mathbb{T}_i K(i,j) \mathbb{T}_j\)) would clarify the contribution.

**Note:** One reviewer (rating 6, confidence 5) is notably more critical and confident, especially regarding precision, missing quantitative evaluation, and conceptual doubts about the vector field modeling and the “unknown manifold” claim. The other two reviewers (ratings 8) are more positive but raise concerns about data efficiency, baselines, and clarity of the kernel construction.
