## Merged Review

### Summary
Four reviewers assessed this paper on QMC-slicing for fast radial kernel summation. Two reviewers (scores 6,6) found the idea novel but incremental over Hertrich (2024), with concerns about practical scalability, experimental scope, and readability. A third reviewer (score 6) had similar reservations. The fourth reviewer (score 8) was more positive, highlighting better error rates than RFF and comprehensive theoretical analysis. All reviewers agree QMC-slicing is technically sound, but diverge on the significance of the contribution and the strength of the experiments. Reviewer 4 is the most positive, emphasizing the method’s potential.

### Strengths
- The paper introduces a novel approach to fast kernel summation via QMC-guided slicing, building on the recently introduced slicing framework (Hertrich, 2024). (R1, R2)
- A new error bound is derived that characterizes the variance of the Monte Carlo slicing estimator, confirming an O(1/√P) rate. (R1)
- The exploration of spherical quadrature rules (spherical designs) to construct slices is promising; the proposed scheme comes with a bound on the approximation error. (R1)
- The QMC-slicing method is shown to have better expected error bounds than Random Fourier Features (RFF) while maintaining similar time complexity. (R4)
- Comprehensive analysis of the slicing error is provided for the negative distance kernel, thin plate spline, Laplace kernel, and Gauss kernel; these bounds could be useful for future research. (R4)
- The methodology is well-documented, with clear explanations of error bounds and smoothness results. (R2)
- The presentation is overall clear; background on fast kernel summation, slicing, and QMC is well explained, and derivations are sufficiently detailed. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Incrementality and novelty:** The work is highly incremental over Hertrich (2024). The only new component is the QMC design for slicing; the Monte Carlo error bound revision does not sufficiently differentiate the paper. (R1)  
- **Practical feasibility of spherical designs:** The spherical design approach, studied in depth, cannot be reasonably computed for realistic d (d ≥ 3). In practice, Monte Carlo or standard Sobol sequences must be used (e.g., on MNIST), which limits the novelty. (R1)  
- **Motivation questioned:** Kernel sum computation is often not the main bottleneck in modern machine learning (distributed computing mitigates it). The paper’s emphasis on high dimension as a motivation is not fully convincing. (R1)  
- **Experimental scope:**  
  - Experiments are limited to synthetic datasets and kernel sum calculations only; application to an actual learning problem (e.g., kernel-based test, online learning) is missing. (R1, R3)  
  - MNIST is not considered high-dimensional; higher-dimensional data (e.g., d > 100) would be expected for a conference like ICLR. (R1)  
  - The method’s generalizability to real-world data with noise and variability remains unclear. (R2)  
- **Missing comparisons and analysis:**  
  - The paper lacks a detailed discussion on how the choice of QMC sequence (Sobol, Halton, distance QMC designs) affects slicing error; properties like discrepancy and uniformity are not linked to observed error rates. (R4)  
  - The observed error rates are significantly better than the theoretical bounds; the paper does not explore why (e.g., tightness of bounds, kernel-specific properties). (R4)  
  - The differentiation from existing fast summation methods (e.g., RFF) is not clearly articulated beyond error bounds. (R2)  
- **Readability and clarity:** Some parts of the theory section, especially error bounds and smoothness assumptions, are overly condensed. The paper is difficult to follow for readers not familiar with the topic. (R2, R3)  
- **Computational cost:** The optimization problem (equation (10)) required for the method is not discussed in terms of practical cost; it is unclear whether Figure 2 includes this additional expense. For large P and d, the cost may be prohibitive. (R3)  
- **Unanswered questions that overlap with weaknesses:**  
  - Scalability to very high dimensions (e.g., d=200, d=10 000) – does QMC-slicing still outperform slicing and (QMC-)RFF? (R2, R4)  
  - Generalization to other kernels (e.g., sigmoid kernel) is not addressed. (R4)  
  - Practical trade-offs in error convergence rate vs. computation time are not fully explored. (R2)  
  - No specific use case or domain is identified where QMC-slicing is expected to outperform alternatives. (R2)  
  - The point-by-point differences with Hertrich (2024) are not clearly described. (R1)  
  - Analytical complexity in time of each QMC scheme is missing. (R1)
