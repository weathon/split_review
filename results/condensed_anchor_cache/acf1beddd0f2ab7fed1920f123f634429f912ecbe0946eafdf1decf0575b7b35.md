- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 6, 3, 1, 10

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SafeCtrlBO, a safe Bayesian optimization method that uses additive squared-exponential kernels to improve performance in higher-dimensional spaces and a faster expander set computation to reduce computational effort. It is motivated by tuning multiple controllers (e.g., cascade PI controllers) simultaneously while ensuring safety. The method is evaluated on synthetic functions (up to 10 dimensions) and a hardware permanent magnet synchronous motor (PMSM) setup under field-oriented control. Reviewers agree on the clear motivation and interesting combination of ideas, but there is strong disagreement: one reviewer (score 10, confidence 2) considers it a major practical contribution, while others (scores 6, 3, 1) raise serious concerns about theoretical correctness, insufficient evaluation, safety violations, and missing implementation details. The total of 39 safety constraint violations in the hardware experiments is highlighted by multiple reviewers.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses the important problem of safe BO’s limitation to low-dimensional spaces and uses additive kernels in a safe BO setting, which appears novel. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- The proposed method leads to optimization in fewer trials, which is of great practical significance for physical systems where trials are slow and costly. (Reviewer 4)
- The paper is written clearly, provides an adequate review of related works, and explains connections to prior work well. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- The algorithm is tested on a physical control system (PMSM) that is of real practical use; tuning PI gains is common and laborious. (Reviewer 4)
- A new, faster-to-compute acquisition function is proposed. (Reviewer 4)
- Theoretical analysis of the additive kernel properties is provided, and the math appears sound to some reviewers. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- The provided implementation gives clear insights into the method and experiments. (Reviewer 3)
- The application of multiple baselines makes the results significant for the BO community. (Reviewer 3)
- The focus on the border of the safe set to reduce computational effort is a logical approach. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
**Insufficient theoretical correctness and clarity**
- Theoretical results are mostly stated in plain text instead of mathematical formulas, making them ambiguous. (Reviewer 2)
- The proofs are incomplete or potentially wrong. Specifically:
  - Lemma C.1 proof step from line 878 to 880 is incomplete; the authors need to show [K_t^{-1} k_t(x)]_i is positive, which is not done. (Reviewer 2)
  - The statement in line 894 may not hold; a counterexample is given with x_sb=1, x_oes=0, x_i=10, λ=0 where the claimed inequality fails. (Reviewer 2)
  - Theorems 4.1 and 4.2 are mostly trivial and should be placed in the appendix. (Reviewer 2)
  - Lemma 4.1 is a known result (Berlinet & Agnan-Thomas 2004, Theorem 5) and not cited. (Reviewer 3)
  - Theorem 4.2 is imprecise (cf. Fiedler 2023, Section 4). (Reviewer 3)
  - Theorem 5.1 incorrectly states “the RKHS norm of the safety functions is bounded by B”; it should be the safety functions themselves. (Reviewer 3)
  - The theoretical results clearly build on Chowdhury & Gopalan 2017 (Theorem 2) for confidence bounds without referencing that paper. (Reviewer 3)
  - More recent results on confidence bounds (Whitehouse et al. 2024) are not mentioned. (Reviewer 3)
- Theorems 5.1 and 5.2 are unclear: what is meant by “maximum allowable uncertainty for the exploration to converge to an (ϵ)-reachable safe region” and “maximum allowable uncertainty for the performance function to converge to a (ζ)-optimal function value”? (Reviewer 2)
- There is a typo in Theorem 5.1 and 5.2: “with R-sub-Gaussian” (grammar). (Reviewer 3)
- Line 333: typo “Lipschitz”. (Reviewer 3)

**Incorrect or inaccurate statements about prior work**
- The claim “Srinivas et al. (2010) demonstrated that BO methods can converge to the global optimum of unknown performance functions in fewer steps compared to genetic algorithm” is incorrect; no such comparison is in that paper. (Reviewer 2)
- The claim “SAFEOPT uses Gaussian kernels as the covariance function” is inaccurate: Berkenkamp et al. (2016) use a Matern kernel, and Sui et al. (2015) do not require a squared-exponential kernel. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- Related works Bottero et al. 2022 (safe BO without expander sets) and Khosravi et al. 2022 (safe/constrained BO for cascaded control) are missing. (Reviewer 3)
- Line 191 and 194: definitions of safe set and expander sets in Sui 2015 and Sui 2018 differ. (Reviewer 3)

**Equations and pseudocode issues**
- Equation (1) has a subscript k where t should be (the same for equation 2). (Reviewer 2)
- Pseudocode line 5: does the algorithm pick the point with maximal variance in B_n? If so, why write u_n-l_n instead of σ? (Reviewer 2)
- The presentation of outermost evaluated safe points (a_oes) is confusing: is a_oes in the dataset? Introducing D_n would help. (Reviewer 2)

**Insufficient and problematic empirical evaluation**
- The main motivation is high-dimensional performance, but all evaluations are low-dimensional with dimensions up to 10, the same as existing safe BO works. (Reviewer 1)
- Cascaded controllers are a poor example: they can be tuned efficiently sequentially, and motor controllers are not safety-critical in practice (no load attached, current limits in software). (Reviewer 1)
- Synthetic experiments only use a noise-free setting; noisy evaluations would be more realistic and strengthen the results. (Reviewer 3)
- The stage-wise approach is unclear: when to go to the next stage? This requires a heuristic that is not explained. (Reviewer 3)
- The choice of GP hyperparameters and β is not made transparent: the paper uses β=2 as a heuristic (found only in the implementation), which invalidates all proven safety guarantees. (Reviewer 3)
- Long lengthscales in the kernel lead to safety violations; shorter lengthscales are typical in safe BO and could reduce violations. (Reviewer 3)
- For hardware experiments, it is unclear what T₀ is and whether there is a switch to the exploitation stage. (Reviewer 3)

**Safety violations are not adequately addressed**
- In hardware experiments, all tested safe BO methods resulted in safety constraint violations; the proposed method had 39 violations. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- The consequences of these violations are not discussed. (Reviewer 4)
- The conclusion that the method “can be seamlessly integrated into real-world complex control applications” is too confident given 39 violations. (Reviewer 3)
- Questions remain: Is there a parameter to reduce violations? Is there a trade-off between performance and safety, and how can it be handled? (Reviewer 4)

**Specific requested experiments and clarifications (from reviewers)**
- Include an experiment with a truly high-dimensional system (e.g., distributed controllers in water/power networks) (Reviewer 1).
- Clarify when exploration/exploitation phases start and stop in hardware experiments (Reviewer 3).
- Explain how GP hyperparameters are chosen and how T₀ can be selected in practice (Reviewer 3).
- When and why do safety violations occur? (Reviewer 3)

**Contrast: a minority reviewer (score 10) is much more positive**
- Reviewer 4 calls the contribution “of major significance” and states that the combination of additive kernels and safe BO is original with significant technical insight, while other reviewers find the theoretical exposition poor, the evaluation low-dimensional, and safety violations a major flaw. (All reviewers)
