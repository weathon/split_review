## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes using LLMs as post-hoc explainers for other predictive models on simple tabular datasets. It introduces four in-context learning (ICL) prompting strategies to extract feature importance, and compares faithfulness metrics (PGI, PGU, RA, FA) against LIME and SHAP, reporting that LLMs identify the most important feature with 72.19% accuracy on average.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important problem (R1).
- The idea of using LLMs as general-purpose explainers is interesting and potentially practical; the paper takes an initial stab to demonstrate potential (R2).
- Shows that LLMs can employ existing feature attribution paradigms (e.g. perturbation-based), which could be useful if cheaper or better than existing algorithms (R2).
- Well-written and easy to understand (R3).
- The proposed method is easy to reproduce (e.g. prompting templates given) (R3).
- Provides enough experimental data to support the claims (R3).

### Weaknesses
*Note: One reviewer (score 5) was more positive overall but still raised several concerns; the other two (scores 3) were more critical, especially on soundness.*

- **Method interpretability**: The LLM-based explainer itself is uninterpretable; it is asked directly “what are the most important features?” on purely numerical datasets, oversimplifying the interpretability task and arbitrarily using logistic regression coefficients as ground truth (R1). There are dramatically cheaper ways to achieve the same goal (R1).
- **Simplicity of models and datasets**: Only simple models (logistic regression, MLP) and tabular datasets are used; no text classifiers or complex models are considered, which limits impact (R1, R2). Using text classifiers via prompting would increase practicality (R2).
- **Unsupported claims**: The suggestion that the method is better than SHAP is presumptive without deeper investigation; the method performs almost as poorly as random feature selection (R1). The claim “The second approach significantly aids the LLM in discerning the most important features” (p.6) is unjustified without a reference to a specific result (R2).
- **Missing cost-benefit analysis**: No Pareto curve or cost comparison (dollar compute and time) is provided to inform practitioners whether LLM-based explanations are preferable to LIME/SHAP (R2).
- **Insufficient experimental details**:
  1. **Metrics undefined**: Evaluation metrics (PGI, PGU, RA, FA) are only cited to other papers; explicit definitions are needed (R2).
  2. **Hyperparameters missing**: No hyperparameters or rationale for baseline explainers (LIME, SHAP) are given; performance could be due to poor hyperparameter choices (R2).
  3. **Model training details**: Details such as optimizer, learning rate, and early stopping for the logistic regression and MLP models are omitted, harming reproducibility (R2).
  4. **Parsing of LLM responses**: The process is described as “save reply to text file and use a script,” but no details on parsing strategy, robustness, or fraction of unparseable responses are provided (R2).
  5. **No baselines for most-important-feature task**: The reported 72.19% accuracy for identifying the most important feature lacks baselines – how well do LIME, SHAP, or random selection perform on that exact task? (R2).
- **Unclear strategy selection**: Three of the proposed strategies perform similarly; it is unclear which is better or when to use each; a concise discussion is needed (R2).
- **Soundness concerns**: Treating LLMs as a PCA-like model to fit data distribution and extract important features is not a guaranteed process – it is unclear how LLMs “understand” the data distribution; LLMs have known limitations in logical thinking, mathematical reasoning, instruction following, and are sensitive to input order (cites [1],[2],[3] in the paper) (R3). A better use would be explaining model outputs in natural language or using LLMs to code tools (e.g. Python) for guaranteed explanations (R3).
- **Remaining questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - “What does it mean that the logistic regression model has ‘one layer of size 16’?” – model architecture unclear (R1).
  - “Why use this approach instead of LIME, which consistently performed better?” – motivation not fully justified (R1).
  - “When did SHAP perform worst according to your metrics and what were the values?” – missing case analysis and failure-mode examples (R1).
