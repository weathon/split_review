## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes Flow Score Distillation (FSD) for diverse text-to-3D generation. It builds a connection between Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) and the DDIM (PF-ODE) generation process, arguing that consistent noise across optimization steps can improve diversity. Key proposals are (a) a world-map noise function that renders noise given camera parameters after initializing a 3D shape of noise, aiming to align noise coherently across views, and (b) a coarse-to-fine pipeline. Results show varied 3D objects from the same prompt without quality degradation. Reviewers are split: two rate 3 (weak accept/ borderline), two rate 5 (strong accept). While the problem of diversity is important and the noise‑sampling idea is interesting, reviews raise concerns about novelty (prior work already connects DDIM and SDS and uses fixed noise), insufficient analysis, unclear notation, technical soundness of the world‑map function in latent diffusion, and unfair comparisons to baselines.

### Strengths

- **Important problem**: the method targets the lack of diversity in score‑distillation‑based 3D generation.
- **Connection between PF‑ODE/SDS and DDIM**: the paper builds a theoretical link, which is valuable and could inspire further research (Reviewers 3, 4). One reviewer notes it shows equivalence between SDS gradient and a term in diffusion (R3).
- **World‑map noise function**: interesting and simple design (R1, R2), revealing relationships between initial noise map and final 3D assets (R2).
- **Consistent noise for fewer multi‑face problems**: using a consistent noise (only possible with the FSD formulation) encourages less multi‑face issues and improves consistency (R3).
- **Quality of generated 3D assets**: sharp, with fine‑grained details, consistent across examples in paper and supplement (R2). Varied 3D objects from the same prompt without performance degradation compared with SDS (R1).
- **Coarse‑to‑fine pipeline** effectively contributes to generating high‑quality 3D objects (R4).
- **Presentation**: one reviewer finds the paper well‑written and easy to understand (R4), though another reviewer strongly criticises notation clarity (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty** (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). The connection between DDIM and SDS was already established in [NewRef‑1] (DreamSampler, ECCV 2024) and [NewRef‑2] (Score Distillation via Reparametrized DDIM, NeurIPS 2024). Using a fixed noise for SDS appears in [NewRef‑2] and [NewRef‑3] (Alldieck et al., ECCV 2024) and, according to Reviewer 4, in “Diverse and Stable 2D Diffusion Guided Text to 3D Generation with Noise Recalibration” (AAAI 24) – this latter prior work is not cited, and its single‑noise training appears equivalent to the vanilla design in Sec 4.1.1. Strategies like multi‑view diffusion, scheduled noise level annealing, and SDS formulation are already extensively explored in MV‑Dream, ProlificDreamer, VSD; the innovation on noise map sampling is weak (R2). While Reviewers 3 and 4 find the connection valuable, the overall novelty is questioned by a majority.
- **Insufficient experiments and analysis** (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).  
  - The FID analysis is not convincing: only 64 3D objects (16 prompts × 4 seeds); a proper evaluation would require a user study with ~50–100 prompts and 1K–2K objects (R3).  
  - The paper lacks an in‑depth analysis of the proposed noise sampling technique (R1).  
  - Results of baseline methods (NFSD, VSD) appear much degraded compared with those in the original papers; the authors should explain why (R1, Q6).  
  - Figure 14 is insufficient to claim the limitation of using fixed noise – the authors should provide more examples with different prompts, check whether the proposed method also produces flaws, test whether using multiple fixed noises could resolve the issue instead of the proposed method, and empirically validate the claims in Sec 4.1.1 (R1, Q2).  
  - The comparison to baselines is not fair or informative because the proposed FSD uses MVDream as a stronger pre‑trained 2D diffusion model (R2).  
  - FSD still requires significant time for generation, raising concerns about practical deployment compared with text‑to‑image‑to‑3D methods that generate high‑quality 3D assets in seconds (R2).
- **Unclear/confusing notation and missing definitions** (Reviewers 1, 3).  
  - Undefined variables: `D, H, W, H_hidden, W_hidden` in Sec 4.1.2; the operation `W` (the most important part of the method) is never defined – `W_{(\cdot)}(\epsilon_p)` is described as a noise patch, not an operation (R1).  
  - `\hat{\mathbf{x_t^c}}` in Line 234 is not related to `t` (R1).  
  - In Eqn (5), the interchangeable use of `\epsilon_\phi` and the gradient of `p_t` needs clarification (R3).  
  - Eqn (11) appears to define `\hat{x}_t^c` rather than `x_t`, yet the gradient of `x_t` is used below. `\hat{x}_t^c` in Eqn (8) and Eqn (11) may have different meanings – in Eqn (8) it is an optimization parameter (image or 3D object), while in Eqn (11) if it is also an optimization parameter, one cannot take a gradient over `x_t`; only if `x_t` is the distribution of the forward diffusion process at time `t` can its gradient be estimated by `\epsilon_\phi` (R3).  
  - The paper needs a clear separation between ground truth distribution, estimated distribution, and auxiliary variables (R3).
- **Technical soundness of the world‑map noise function** (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).  
  - It does not account for depth information – noise is sampled without considering the shape of the generated object (R4).  
  - In latent diffusion models, keeping background noise static (Eq. 16) does not guarantee static gradients on rendered‑image pixels because the gradient must pass through a non‑linear, non‑local VAE encoder; this issue is also noted in Chang et al. (2024) Appendix E, raising concerns about technical soundness when applied to latent diffusion models (R4).  
  - Further clarification is needed on how the world‑map noise function consistently maintains diversity across different views without affecting 3D consistency (R2).  
  - The authors should explain why background/foreground noise are defined separately, and whether simply using the world‑map noise function for fixed noise in 3D would be insufficient (R1, Q4).  
  - The authors should provide generated images via the world‑map noise function to show that the assumption holds (i.e., 3D‑consistent changes as in Fig. 4) (R1, Q5).
- **Missing details on the FSD optimization procedure** (Reviewer 3).  
  - The detailed process of FSD in Sec 3.2 is not clear; content from Appendix H (which includes many other designs) should be moved to support Sec 3.2.  
  - The paper misses a connection explaining why using Adam to optimize Eqn 12 (a gradient w.r.t. `t` instead of `\theta`) leads to the generation goal.  
  - For the 2D example in Sec 3.2, what would be the loss function to optimize? Is it the same as the objective in Sec 4? (R3, Q).  
  - Sec 3.3 lacks a visualization to illustrate “SDS over‑smoothness and lacks diversity” instead of only text (R3, Q).
