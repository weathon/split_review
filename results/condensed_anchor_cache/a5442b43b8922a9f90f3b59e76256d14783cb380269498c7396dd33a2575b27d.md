## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies nonparametric regression with over-parameterized two-layer ReLU neural networks trained by gradient descent with early stopping in the NTK regime. It claims a sharp generalization bound of order \(\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon_n^2)\) that is distribution-free (covariates supported on the unit sphere) and recovers minimax optimal rates under eigenvalue decay rate assumptions. The reviewers’ assessments range from positive (scores 6) to critical (score 3). The distribution-free analysis is seen as a notable step, but concerns are raised about overstatement of “distribution-free” (strong assumptions on labels), marginal novelty due to known kernel results, clarity, artificial training setting (first-layer only, antisymmetric initialization), insufficient related work comparison, lack of experiments, and several unanswered technical questions.

### Strengths
- Distribution-free analysis: The generalization bound for over-parameterized two-layer NNs trained by GD with early stopping does not require restrictive distributional assumptions on the covariates (e.g., uniform distribution on the sphere) and holds for any eigenvalue decay rate of the NTK (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Weaker width requirement: The required network width depends only on \(n, d, \varepsilon_n\) and does not depend on the minimum eigenvalue of the empirical NTK matrix, which is a weaker assumption than in prior works (Hu et al. 2021, Suh et al. 2022, Li et al. 2024) (R1, R2).
- Explicit distribution-free stopping time: The paper provides an explicit stopping time \(\hat{T} \asymp \varepsilon_n^{-2}\) (R1).
- Constant learning rate: The analysis uses a constant learning rate (also achieved for SGD by Nitanda & Suzuki 2021) (R1).
- Cleaner unified analysis: The proof approach offers a more unified analysis of early stopping for the NTK that works for arbitrary distributions (R2).
- Detailed theoretical exposition: The paper gives a detailed explanation of theoretical outcomes and comparisons to existing literature (R3).
- Recover minimax rates: The result recovers minimax optimal rates obtained in prior works as special cases (e.g., \(n^{-d/(2d-2)}\) or \(n^{-d/(2d-1)}\) for uniform distribution on the sphere) (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Overstatement of “distribution-free”**: The term is misleading because strong assumptions are imposed on the label distribution: the Bayes optimal function must lie in the NTK’s RKHS with bounded norm, and the label noise is i.i.d. Gaussian. The abstract’s claim “does not require distributional assumptions on the training data” is not qualified and ignores these restrictions (R4). Furthermore, the domain is still limited to the unit sphere \(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\), which is itself a distributional assumption; the paper does not justify why a spherical domain is reasonable or how normalization may affect bounds (R3, R4).
- **Marginal novelty**: The convergence of two-layer NNs to the NTK is well-known, and early stopping guarantees for kernel methods already exist (Raskutti et al. 2014). It is unclear what new technical difficulties are overcome beyond applying existing results (R2). The authors do not clearly explain how their methods relax uniform distribution assumptions compared to prior NTK analyses (Hu et al. 2021, Suh et al. 2022), nor how their Rademacher complexity bounding technique differs (R1). It is not shown whether the kernel part of the proof could be used as a black box from Raskutti et al. (2014) (R2). Although some reviewers (R1, R3) consider the results solid, others (R2, R4) find the contribution marginal.
- **Artificial training setting**: Only the first-layer weights of a two-layer ReLU network (without biases) are trained using GD with the anti-symmetric initialization trick. Comparable papers (Suh et al. 2022, Li et al. 2024) train all layers of multi-layer MLPs; Du et al. (2019) trains both layers. The paper does not transparently discuss how this simplification eases analysis or limits practical relevance (R4). The anti-symmetric initialization has been used before (Zhang et al. 2020) and is not acknowledged (R4).
- **Insufficient related work comparison**:
  - The claim that previous studies impose strong distributional assumptions ignores Haas et al. (2024, Theorem G.5), which achieves minimax optimal rates under weaker assumptions (target function may lie outside the NTK RKHS, non-Gaussian noise) (R4).
  - The statement (lines 50-53) that NNs in the infinite-width limit are equivalent to NTKs is imprecise; Yang & Hu (2021) show that different parameterizations can lead to feature learning (R4).
  - The constant learning rate advantage is also present in Nitanda & Suzuki (2021) (for SGD) and is not compared (R3).
  - Previously shown convergence to the NTK is not mentioned (R4).
  - The antisymmetric initialization trick has been used before (Zhang et al. 2020) (R4).
- **Writing and clarity**: Despite some reviewers (R1) praising the writing, others find the paper unclear. Several terms are undefined (ReLU activation not stated, kernel \(K\) in equation (2) not identified as infinite-width NTK, “critical population rate” and “critical radius” not defined at first mention, \(f_0\) undefined, \(s\) and \(\kappa\) undefined) (R2). Theorem statements are hard to parse; the authors should specify intervals for the early stopping time explicitly (R2). Figure 1 (proof roadmap) is unhelpful because it does not state the lemmas and how they connect (R2, R4). Section 6.2 is lengthy and repetitive (R4). Notation \(f_0\) is confusing (it is a constant) (R4). Typos exist (line 264, lines 379-380) (R4).
- **Missing experiments**: No empirical validation is provided. Suggested experiments include: (i) comparing the theoretically predicted stopping time \(\hat{T}\) with the empirically optimal one; (ii) verifying the generalization error decay rate for uniform and non-uniform covariate distributions; (iii) testing the required network width scaling. Such experiments are common in related works (e.g., Du et al. 2019) (R4).
- **Technical concerns and unanswered questions**:
  - Can Theorem 6.1 be proven using existing results on convergence to the NTK? (R2)
  - Can the second part of the proof (generalization given uniform convergence to the kernel) be derived as a black box from Raskutti et al. (2014)? If not, what new technical tools are required? (R2)
  - Provide a short sketch of how uniform convergence to the NTK and the local Rademacher complexity bound are achieved, and how the analysis differs from previous work (R4).
  - The paper only gives the uniform sphere distribution as an example; what other suggestive examples follow from Theorem 5.1? (R3)
  - The paper does not show how to estimate the required network width from data (R4).
