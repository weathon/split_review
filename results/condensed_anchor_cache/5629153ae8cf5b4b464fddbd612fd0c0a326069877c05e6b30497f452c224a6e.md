## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ThermalGaussian, the first 3DGS-based approach to jointly learn and render high-quality RGB and thermal images from new views. It introduces multimodal regularization, smoothing constraints tailored to thermal characteristics, and three training strategies (MFTG, MSMG, OMMG). A new real-world dataset, RGBT-Scenes (10 scenes, indoor/outdoor, forward-facing and 360°, hand-held thermal camera), is contributed. Experiments show improved rendering quality for both modalities, a ~90% storage reduction compared to conventional 3DGS, and gains over prior NeRF-based methods.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and significance**: First work to integrate thermal and RGB modalities via 3DGS (R1, R5). The three strategies (MFTG, MSMG, OMMG) serve as a 3DGS-based counterpart to prior NeRF works [1] (R2). The weight scheduling for RGB/thermal loss in MSMG is a novel contribution (R2).
- **Effective multimodal learning**: Loss function tailored to thermal characteristics and multimodal regularization constraints allow complementary learning, preventing overfitting and reducing redundant Gaussians (R1, R3). The method improves RGB rendering quality, especially in low-light conditions (R2, R5).
- **Storage and efficiency**: Multimodal regularization reduces model storage costs by ~90% compared to conventional 3DGS, while maintaining high quality (R1, R3).
- **Dataset contribution**: RGBT-Scenes is diverse, includes both forward-facing and 360° scenes, and covers indoor/outdoor environments, providing a valuable benchmark for thermal 3D reconstruction research (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Presentation and experiments**: Paper is well-written and easy to follow. Experiments are thorough, with ablation studies that support all design decisions (R1, R2, R5). No obvious flaws in methodology (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Implementation clarity**: Insufficient detail on RGB-thermal camera calibration and alignment (R1, R3). The description of thermal Gaussian initialization and how parameters (position, covariance, opacity) relate to RGB splats is lacking, making reproduction difficult (R1). Missing specifics on thermal camera spectral sensitivity, noise characteristics, radiometric calibration, ambient temperature, and material emissivity (R3).
- **Method comparison and ambiguity**: The three strategies (MFTG, MSMG, OMMG) are presented without clear pros/cons; no model performs overwhelmingly best across all scenes, leaving users unsure which to choose (R4). The insight behind why three designs are needed is absent, and the claim that the second phase of MFTG “may not fully leverage color modality information” (L283) lacks evidence (R4). The cross-modal influence in MSMG is unclear: if Gaussians are trained separately, how do gradients from one modality affect the other? (R5). The cost functions used to train MSMG and OMMG are not clearly specified (R2).
- **Missing comparisons**: Statement of being “first to use 3DGS to model RGB and thermal images” is incorrect; [5] (Xu et al., ECCV 2024) already presented such a method (R2). No comparisons to existing NeRF-based methods for which code is available (e.g., ThermalNeRF [2], ThermoNeRF [3]) (R2, R4). The method is only evaluated on the authors’ own dataset; evaluation on at least one existing public dataset (e.g., ThermalMix [1], dataset of [2], MVTV [5]) is missing to mitigate potential dataset bias (R2, R4). Side-by-side comparisons of thermal renderings with and without the smoothness term are absent (R2).
- **Dataset limitations**: The dataset description lacks information on temperature range, occlusions, surface material variability, and lighting conditions (R1). It does not include dynamic scenes or moving objects (R1). Existing multi-view RGB+thermal datasets (ThermalMix [1], [2], MVTV [5]) are not listed in Table 1, and criteria for “multi-view consistency” and “content richness” are not provided (R2).
- **Calibration and pose strategy details**: Three strategies to obtain camera poses for thermal images are mentioned, but the one actually used in experiments is not stated; no quantitative comparison is given. The MSX strategy is patented (FLIR), limiting its practical use, while the alternatives may also rely on MSX images (R2). The use of a simple chessboard pattern printed on paper for thermal calibration is surprising and lacks physical justification; numerous works design special calibration objects for thermal cameras (R2).
- **Lack of limitations discussion**: The paper does not discuss conditions where the method may fail, such as misalignment due to parallax, calibration errors, significant temperature variations, or highly reflective surfaces (R3, R1). Real-world applicability (e.g., varying weather, industrial thermal patterns, outdoor environments) is not addressed (R3). Future work discussion is generic (super-resolution, dynamic scenes) rather than specific to thermal 3D reconstruction challenges (heat transfer modeling, anomaly detection) (R3).
- **Hyperparameter and methodology questions**: Sensitivity of \(\lambda_{smooth}\) and other hyperparameters is not ablated (R2). The meaning of “Mix50” in Figure 2 is not explained (R2). Typo in L.448: “Ours” → “ours” (R4). OMMG yields best quality but only reduces memory to 60% of 3DGS, not the claimed 90% reduction; MSMG shows worse thermal quality (drop of ~1% PSNR) despite larger storage saving (R2). Practical advantage of 3D thermal reconstruction over 2D thermal images is not justified (R4). The term “thermal equilibrium” (L314) is introduced without explanation (R4).
