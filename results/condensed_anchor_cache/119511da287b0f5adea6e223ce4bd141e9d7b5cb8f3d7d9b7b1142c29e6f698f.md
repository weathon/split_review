## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper studies SGD on loss functions with rescaling symmetry, proving that minibatch noise drives weights toward a balanced solution. It applies the theory to a diagonal linear network (arbitrary depth/width) to derive the stationary distribution of stochastic gradient flow, which exhibits phase transitions, loss of ergodicity, and fluctuation inversion—qualitative differences between deep and shallow networks. The work also contrasts SGD with (noisy) gradient descent and Langevin dynamics, showing why the Gibbs measure is inadequate for SGD. Reviewers generally appreciated the novel symmetry perspective and the analytical stationary distribution, but raised significant concerns: the model (diagonal linear network, 1D input) is highly simplified, the generality of phase transitions is unclear, precision issues exist in key statements, some findings are already known, and the practical relevance is questionable. One reviewer (R3, score 8) found the paper original and clearly written with excellent contribution; another (R4, score 3) judged the theory not entirely precise, the setup limited, and the contributions insufficiently clarified.

### Strengths

- **Novel and interpretable framework**: The law of balance equation provides a clean, interpretable lens for understanding SGD via loss-function symmetries (R1, R2, R3).
- **Analytical stationary distribution**: The paper derives the stationary distribution for a diagonal linear network as a function of learning rate, batch size, data noise, model width, and depth, which explains observed phenomena such as phase transitions, broken ergodicity, and fluctuation inversion (R1, R3, R4).
- **Insights on SGD vs. GD/Langevin**: The work clarifies why standard Langevin approximations of SGD are flawed, how minibatch noise creates a qualitative difference from gradient descent, and why the Gibbs measure is inadequate for SGD (R3, R4).
- **Depth vs. shallow contrast**: The analysis reveals properties unique to deep networks (e.g., fluctuation inversion, loss of ergodicity, inability to learn the wrong sign), providing a theoretical distinction between deep and shallow models (R1, R3).
- **Important and timely topic**: The behavior of SGD is a central question in deep learning (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Generality of phase transitions**: The manuscript does not clarify whether the phase transition diagram is a special case or general; the conditions under which the observed phase transitions occur (e.g., dependence on data distribution) are not stated, limiting assessment of broader applicability (R1).
- **Representativeness of diagonal linear network**: The analysis depends on a diagonal linear network (1D input, no nonlinear activations). Its relevance to complex nonlinear architectures commonly used in deep learning is unclear. One reviewer notes that prior works consider multivariate inputs, making this setting particularly limited (R1, R3, R4). The extension to loss functions without rescaling symmetries or to nonlinear networks is not addressed and is likely extremely difficult (R3).
- **L2 regularization not isolated**: An L2-norm regularization term is added, but its effect on the stationary distribution, phase transitions, and the interplay with learning rate and batch size is not adequately discussed. It is unclear whether the regularization is a technical convenience or fundamentally alters SGD behavior (R1).
- **Covariance matrix condition**: The boundness of the law of balance depends on the covariance matrices in Eq. (4). No guarantee on their condition (e.g., invertibility) is provided; degeneracy is not justified, and its practical relevance is ignored (R2).
- **Imprecise or misleading statements** (R4):
  - The claim that rescaling symmetry appears “in any neural network with ReLU activation” is inaccurate: for a single neuron with ReLU, the symmetry holds only for non-negative scaling factor λ.
  - In Theorem 1, C₁ and C₂ are functions of u and v, so λ₁ₘ, λ₁ₘ, λ₂ₘ, λ₂ₘ are not constants; Eqs. (5–6) do not directly imply approximate balance.
  - In the discussion after Eq. (8), the coefficient α₁v² – 2α₂v + α₃ is a function of v (hence of u and w), so the claim that the bound is “exponentially decreasing in time” is not necessarily valid.
  - There is a probable typo in the definition of C(v) (should be variance of the gradient of the loss).
  - The statement that “C can be quite independent of L” seems to contradict the intended meaning (likely “dependent”).
- **Overlap with existing work**: Some messages (e.g., SGD noise being parameter-dependent, SGD not well approximated by gradient Langevin dynamics) are already known from prior papers cited here; the paper should more carefully delineate its own novel contributions (R4).
- **Relevance to practice**: It is unclear how the theory and results (on a linear diagonal network) transfer to practical deep learning settings (R4).
