## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Domain2Vec, a method that featurizes datasets by projecting them onto a vocabulary of “meta-domains” (260 centroids from k-means clustering of 5.2TB of English, Chinese, and code data). A Qwen2-1.5B classifier predicts per-sample meta-domain probabilities; averaging yields a domain vector for each dataset. The optimal mixing ratio of data sources is then found by solving a linear system (Eq. 5) that aligns the training dataset’s domain vectors with a validation domain vector, under the Distribution Alignment Assumption (DA²). The approach is computationally efficient and scalable to new domains without retraining. However, there is strong disagreement: while most reviewers find the method effective and intuitive, one reviewer (score 3) argues that Table 3 shows no empirical gain over simple baselines and that the results should be framed as negative.

### Strengths
- The paper studies an important problem (data mixture optimization for LM pretraining) and the method is interesting, intuitive, and computationally efficient (all reviewers). It is ~3× more efficient than DoReMi (R2).
- Domain2Vec provides a training-free way to find optimal mixtures and can adapt to new datasets without recomputation (R2, R3).
- The methodology is scalable: as new domains are introduced, the same meta-domain vocabulary and classifier can be reused, avoiding the cost of re-running prior methods (R2).
- The paper is clearly written with detailed theoretical and practical explanations (R1, R3, R4; though R2 and R4 note clarity issues—see Weaknesses).
- R3 and R4: experimental results show improved downstream performance and reduced computational cost.
- **Minority negative perspective (R1, score 3)**: Table 3 shows the best Domain2Vec variant (0.483) is worse than RegMix (0.489) and worse than training only on Pile-CC (0.487). The computational savings (9.7e16 FLOPs vs. 3.5e18 FLOPs for RegMix) are negligible compared to the LM training cost (6 ND ≈ 1.2e20 FLOPs). R1 therefore finds little to no empirical gain and suggests the results should be framed as a negative result.

### Weaknesses
1. **Disputed empirical effectiveness**  
   - R1 (score 3) observes that Domain2Vec underperforms both RegMix and the “Pile-CC only” baseline on the key metric in Table 3. R2 also notes that validation loss improvements do not translate to downstream performance and that it is unclear whether improvements are statistically significant or measured on a held-out test set.  
   - Figure 5 lacks strong baselines: R2 requests comparisons to DoReMi, a simple 50‑50 mix of Pile and C4, and a deduplicated version of that mix.  
   - R1 stresses that no comparisons are made to modern pretraining datasets such as RefinedWeb, FineWeb, and DCLM.

2. **Overclaimed novelty**  
   - R3 points out that similar ideas exist in meta-learning (e.g., Cross-Table Pretraining, Dataset2Vec). The paper’s claim of being “the first” to propose Domain2Vec should be removed, and the relationship to those works should be discussed.

3. **Clarity and notation deficiencies**  
   - The Distribution Alignment Assumption (DA²) is not defined or given intuition in the abstract (R2).  
   - Notation is ambiguous: dimensionalities are unspecified, it is unclear whether $D_i^*$ in Eq. 1 is a scalar, vector, or matrix (R2). In Sec. 3.3 the loss function notation $\mathcal{L}_\theta$ is reused inconsistently, and domain vector changes from $\mathbf{v}$ to $\mathbf{p}$ (R2).  
   - Concepts such as “meta-domain”, “vocabulary”, and “semantic features” are not clearly defined (R2). R2 asks for a clearer hierarchy: meta-domain, domain, dataset.  
   - R2 and R4 question the meta-domain classifier: is it multi-class or multi-label? How are precision/recall handled for long-tail meta-domains? (R4)  
   - The paper claims prior works rely on a well-trained reference model, but Domain2Vec itself relies on pretrained models (bge-small-en-v1.5, bge-small-zt-v1.5, Qwen2-1.5B) (R2).  
   - R2 notes that Sec. 3.1’s loss (Eq. 4) appears to omit an inner argmin over $\theta$ given mixing ratio $\mathbf{r}$, implying the model parameters do not change with $\mathbf{r}$.  
   - Typos and formatting: extra comma line 66, missing period line 80 (R1, R3); redundant spaces after thousand separators (e.g., “3, 000”); “paer” for “paper” (line 220); “addictive” for “additive” (line 235); Figure 2 font too small (R2).

4. **Assumptions and limitations**  
   - DA² assumes that aligning domain vectors of training and validation sets minimizes validation loss. R3 asks: what if the training domain vector is a subset of the validation vector, or vice versa? R4 questions the use of Huber loss over distributional measures such as divergence or Wasserstein distance.  
   - The 260 meta-domains are constructed from English, Chinese, and code data only; practitioners in other languages (e.g., German) would need to create their own setup—a chicken‑and‑egg problem (R4).  
   - R4: k‑means clustering may yield suboptimal groupings; sensitivity to the number of clusters $k$ is not discussed. The paper does not explain how to choose $N$ (number of samples for a confident probability vector) or how to handle novel content not represented in the initial vocabulary.

5. **Missing ablations and baselines**  
   - R4 suggests a simple baseline: directly cluster learned feature embeddings into meta‑domains instead of training a separate classifier. Why is this not used?  
   - R2 and R4 request more baselines in Figure 5 (DoReMi, simple mixes, deduplication).  
   - The cost of constructing the meta‑domain vocabulary and training the classifier is not compared to the potential benefit, especially when the baseline “Pile-CC only” achieves competitive performance at zero extra cost (R1, R2).
