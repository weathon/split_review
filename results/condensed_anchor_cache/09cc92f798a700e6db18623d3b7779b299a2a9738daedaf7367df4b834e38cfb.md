## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a novel task of multi-grained video editing (class-level, instance-level, part-level) and proposes ST-Modulator, a zero-shot approach that modulates cross- and self-attention in a pretrained T2I diffusion model to achieve fine-grained control over video content. It addresses semantic misalignment and feature coupling by amplifying each local prompt's attention to its corresponding spatial-disentangled region in cross-attention and increasing intra-region awareness while reducing inter-region interference in self-attention. The method relies on SAM-Track for instance segmentation and operates without additional training. Experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art performance on benchmarks and real-world videos, with lower memory usage and faster processing.

### Strengths
- **Novel task and motivation:** The paper clearly defines multi-grained video editing (class-, instance-, part-level) for the first time, motivated by practical needs for flexible editing at any granularity. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Insightful empirical analysis:** Provides an analysis of why diffusion models fail at instance-level editing, identifying text-to-region misalignment and feature coupling as key obstacles. (R3, R4)
- **Effective and intuitive method:** The proposed spatial-temporal layout-guided attention mechanism modulates cross- and self-attention in a unified, training-free manner, directly targeting core issues in multi-grained editing. The approach is intuitive and easy to follow. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Strong results and efficiency:** Achieves state-of-the-art performance in qualitative experiments, surpassing previous approaches. Also demonstrates high computational efficiency with lower memory usage and faster processing times. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Resource-efficient:** Requires no parameter tuning or additional training, which is advantageous for video editing applications. (R1, R4)
- **Well-written presentation:** The paper is well-organized and complex concepts are made easy to understand; visualizations (e.g., analysis of diffusion model failures) illustrate key points effectively. (R1, R4) *(Note: reviewer 2 noted readability issues; see weaknesses.)*

### Weaknesses
- **Readability and presentation clarity:** Symbols in Figure 4 (e.g., "I", "i", "E", "p") are inadequately defined; readers must guess their meaning. Shape information (L×E) could be omitted since it is not used in Figure 2. (R2)
- **Dependence on SAM-Track for segmentation:** The method relies on SAM-Track for instance segmentation, but there is no ablation study isolating its impact. It is unclear whether performance gains stem primarily from the segmentation mask rather than the proposed attention modulation. Using Grounding-DINO + SAM-Track + prompt-to-prompt might achieve similar results, and this comparison is missing. (R2, R4)
- **Novelty vs. existing attention modulation:** The core idea of enhancing attention in relevant areas and suppressing it in irrelevant areas is already implemented in methods like prompt-to-prompt. The paper should more thoroughly discuss what is novel about the proposed approach. (R2)
- **Insufficient temporal analysis and long video handling:** The temporal aspect receives less emphasis; the method is tested only on 16–32 frames. It is unclear whether temporal consistency can be maintained over longer sequences (hundreds of frames) given the computational cost of attention mechanisms. A dedicated analysis or ablation on temporal coherence (e.g., under complex motion or occlusions) is needed. (R3, R4)
- **Need for rigorous quantitative evaluation:** The evaluation relies heavily on qualitative results, which are subjective and hard to reproduce. A benchmark and new metrics to assess edit accuracy at each granularity (class, instance, part) are needed for full validation. (R3)
- **Lack of clarity on additional control signals (ControlNet):** The method is compatible with ControlNet conditioning, but it is not specified whether shown cases require extra control inputs. No ablation study on control conditions is provided, raising fairness concerns in comparisons with methods like ControlVideo. The reported gains might be partially attributable to specific ControlNet conditions rather than the proposed method alone. (R4)
- **Part-level editing limitations:** It is unclear whether the method can support modifications beyond adding objects (e.g., changing color of clothing or animal color). (R1)
- **Missing code or algorithm details:** No code or detailed algorithm description is provided, preventing transparent validation of assumptions, control conditions, and segmentation impacts. (R4)
- **Background preservation:** Most examples in Figures 5 and 6 modify the background along with subjects. Additional examples showing selective edits to subjects only, without background changes, would demonstrate capability for such selective edits in multi-subject scenes. (R1)
- **Distinction from multi-attribute editing:** The paper does not clarify how multi-grained editing differs from multi-attribute editing. (R3)

*Note: Reviewer 3 (score 8) was notably more positive about the method's novelty and qualitative results, while Reviewer 4 (score 6) expressed stronger concerns about evaluation rigor, temporal handling, and the reliance on SAM-Track.*
