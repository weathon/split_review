- Scores: 3, 3, 5, 8

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a frequentist approach to diffusion-based inverse solvers, treating each diffusion step as maximum likelihood estimation with a noise-aware maximization (NAM) scheme and an early stopping criterion based on hypothesis testing. The method modifies Tweedie’s formula to incorporate the forward measurement model. Extensive experiments are reported on linear inverse problems across multiple datasets and noise levels. Reviewers agree the idea is novel and the experimental suite is comprehensive, but there are major disagreements: one reviewer (score 8) finds no important weaknesses, while the other three reviewers (scores 3, 3, 5) raise significant concerns about technical correctness, fairness of comparisons, and theoretical foundations.

### Strengths
- **Novel and important idea** – Addresses the noise-robustness problem in diffusion-based inverse solvers. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4; Reviewer 4 especially positive: “I really like this paper.”)
- **Extensive experimental evaluation** – Covers multiple tasks, datasets (ImageNet, FFHQ, etc.), noise levels, and includes ablation studies. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **Noise-aware scheme** – The likelihood-based stopping criterion is a principled way to handle noisy measurements. (Reviewer 3)
- **Clear presentation and argument** – “Excellent” clarity and presentation. (Reviewer 4)
- **Practical relevance** – Tests on high noise levels show potential for real-world use. (Reviewer 4)
- **Simple algorithm** – Has better performance than DPS in some settings. (Reviewer 1)
- **Discussion of code disparity** – Exposes issues with the ReSample implementation. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses
- **Experimental comparison fairness and correctness** – Some baselines (PSLD, ReSample) perform worse than DPS, contradicting literature; possible mis-implementation. (Reviewer 1) The paper uses Stable Diffusion v1.5 for baselines but a specialized ImageNet score network for the proposed method, making comparisons unfair. (Reviewer 1) Appendix C.4’s concerns about ReSample are insufficiently supported. (Reviewer 1) Additionally, comparing algorithms with different priors (pixel-space vs. latent-space) changes the problem; it is unclear which prior the author’s method uses in Tables 1, 3, and 4. (Reviewer 2)
- **Technical errors in the paper** (Reviewer 2, high confidence):
  - Equations (5) and (7) omit \(x_t\) from the drift term of the SDE.
  - Footnote (line 106) swaps score and epsilon: correct form is \(\epsilon_\theta = -\sigma_t s_\theta\).
  - Equation (9) uses the forward kernel \(p_{t|0}\) instead of the DDPM bridge kernel \(q(x_{t-1}|x_t,x_0)\).
  - Algorithm 2, line 5: gradient should be taken with respect to \(\log p_t\), not as written.
  - Line 400: should refer to the Jacobian of the score, not the gradient of the log score.
- **Problematic use of Tweedie’s formula** – Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 3.2 are confused; conditioning on \(x_0\) is irrelevant because Tweedie’s formula holds directly for Gaussian transition kernels. Equation (17) incorrectly suggests \(x_0\) can be accessed directly; only \(E(x_0|x_t)\) is obtainable. The claim in lines 303–304 about the transition kernel near \(t=0\) is incorrect (it is a Dirac only in the limit). (Reviewer 2) Even with the Gaussian assumption, reliance on Tweedie’s formula may introduce biases if the score estimate from NAM is inaccurate; paper lacks justification. (Reviewer 3)
- **Early stopping criterion issues** – Using “hypothesis testing” is misleading: only one residual sample is available per iteration, and setting \(\sigma_t\) as the critical probability \(p_{critic}\) lacks justification; this approach may not extend to Variance Exploding (VE) diffusion models where \(\sigma\) can exceed 1. (Reviewer 2)
- **Restrictive assumptions on measurement operator** – Theorem A.3 assumes \(A\) can be decomposed into a linear projection and a surjective function, which excludes many non-linear measurement models common in imaging. (Reviewer 3)
- **Computational cost and robustness concerns** – NAM adds complexity, yet there is no analysis of the number of iterations required or how it scales with problem size. (Reviewer 3) The ablation study shows sensitivity to optimizer choice, making the method less robust (Reviewer 3). Jacobian-free variants of existing methods (DPS, LGD-MC) are not compared. (Reviewer 1)
- **Lack of convergence guarantees** – No theoretical guarantees for DCS convergence or accuracy, especially with limited diffusion steps. (Reviewer 3)
- **Qualitative results not convincing** – Figures 10–19 show subtle differences between methods and minimal resemblance to ground truth; reconstructions lack fine details and contain artifacts. (Reviewer 3)
- **No uncertainty quantification** – The frequentist approach does not enable posterior sampling, a limitation for applications needing reliability assessments. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing comparison** – With Sun et al. *“Provable probabilistic imaging using score-based generative priors.”* (Reviewer 3)
- **Minor writing inaccuracies** (Reviewer 4, but considered minor):
  - Introduction claims \(A\) is generally non-invertible, yet many inverse problems (e.g., blurring, tomography) are invertible.
  - Line 50: “smoothness” appears to refer to the squared H1 seminorm, not clarified.
  - Line 68: consistency error “only exists for \(x=x_0\)” is inaccurate; the error exists everywhere but is not easily evaluated.
  - Line 214: statement about conditions (1) and (2) being sufficient is unclear on necessity.
  - Line 260: “Inverse problem solvers” is too broad; only score-based methods have that problem.

*Note: There is strong disagreement: Reviewer 4 states “no important weaknesses,” while Reviewers 1–3 list many of the above issues.*
