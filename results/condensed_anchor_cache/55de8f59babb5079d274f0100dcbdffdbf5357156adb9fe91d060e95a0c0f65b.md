## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces ACAV-1M, a large-scale audio-visual dataset curated from ACAV-100M via a pipeline that uses a multimodal large language model (VideoLLaVA) for captioning and ImageBind for filtering based on audio-caption alignment, audio-visual instance alignment, and temporal synchronization. The dataset (1M samples) is benchmarked on multiple downstream tasks (classification, source separation, retrieval, QA, etc.), and models trained on it show improved performance compared to using existing datasets.

### Strengths
- **Dataset size and motivation**: The one-million-sample scale provides a robust foundation for audio-visual representation learning, and the need for such a curated dataset with better alignment is reasonable and timely (R1, R3).
- **Alignment-aware curation pipeline**: The comprehensive process leveraging a multimodal LLM and ImageBind to ensure semantic, instance-level, and temporal consistency among audio, video, and text significantly improves dataset quality (R4, R5). The focus on temporal alignment is particularly valuable for fine-grained tasks (R4).
- **Broad downstream validation**: The paper validates the dataset across numerous well-designed tasks (classification, source separation, question-answering, retrieval, etc.), and the results are convincing and show superiority over existing datasets (R2, R4, R5). The consistent performance gains highlight the dataset’s potential to advance multimodal learning (R5).
- **Writing and clarity**: The paper is well written and the motivation is clearly presented (R3). *Note: Reviewer 5 noted writing inconsistencies (see Weaknesses).*

### Weaknesses
- **Unverified alignment quality**: 
  - Random inspection by a reviewer identified misaligned samples (e.g., “kCb7VJgc1Zc”, “_QQHFllvooU”, “JUChESSdblA”) containing voice narration without a visible talking head, raising concerns about effective audio-visual correspondence. The paper does not detail the quality control process (e.g., percentage manually checked, how post-curation misalignments are handled) (R2).
  - There is no human evaluation of the MLLM-generated captions nor an analysis of ImageBind’s cross-modal alignment accuracy on this dataset (R1). ImageBind is noted to be inferior in aligning audio and language (citing FreeBind), and its audio-language similarity is an untrained capability (R3, R2). Better models or a more in-depth discussion are needed (R3).
  - VideoLLaVA lacks an audio encoder and processes only images/video; the paper does not clarify how audio captions are generated, making it unclear whether it is appropriate for audio-visual captioning (R5).
- **Missing verification of alignment vs. dataset size effect**: It remains unclear whether performance gains stem from improved alignment or simply from dataset size/composition. No comparison is made between the filtered dataset (ACAV-1M) and the unfiltered ACAV-100M (R2, R1). A fairer comparison with AudioSet (which is larger and widely used) is needed to justify the necessity of ACAV-1M; AudioSet can serve as pre-training data for the same tasks (R3).
- **Inadequate filtering methodology analysis**:
  - The uniform threshold (50%) applied across language, instance, and temporal alignment is not justified; different types may need independent optimal thresholds (R1).
  - The amount of data filtered out is not reported, nor is the impact of data loss analyzed. Overly strict filtering may discard valuable samples and bias the dataset toward “easy to align” content, reducing diversity and utility (R1, R5). A comparison between filtered and unfiltered datasets is missing (R1).
  - Automated alignment may not be equally effective for all content types (e.g., music, ambient sounds), but no adjustment of filtering parameters by content type is considered (R5).
- **Missing dataset metadata and licensing**:
  - No metadata (video captions, timestamps, cluster categories from ACAV-100M) are provided, which would allow users to customize subsets (R2).
  - The sources of video data are undisclosed, and there is no discussion of copyright or usage rights, limiting practical applicability (R1).
- **Insufficient distribution statistics**: The paper lacks breakdowns of video types, categories, proportions, and temporal spread, making it impossible to assess dataset balance, representativeness, or potential biases (R1). Steps to detect and mitigate biases from LLMs used in captioning are not described (R5).
- **Technical omissions**:
  - Audio segments are standardized to 10 seconds, but details on how segments are cut and whether semantic integrity and alignment are preserved are not given (R5).
  - Which model size (ViT-Base or ViT-Large) is used for experiments is unstated; consistency with prior works is unclear (R2).
  - More advanced baselines, e.g., using ImageBind for audio-visual retrieval, are absent (R3).
  - Some writing inconsistencies exist: Figure 1 caption mentions “LLM (OpenAI, 2023)” while Section 3.1 uses “VideoLLaVA (Lin et al., 2023)”, and the structure of Section 3.2 could be clearer (R5).
- **Contrasting reviewer opinion**: While Reviewer 4 finds the downstream results convincing and the overall approach strong (score 6), other reviewers (scores 3,3,5,3) are notably more critical regarding alignment verification, missing comparisons, technical gaps, and dataset documentation. This disagreement underscores the need for stronger validation of the curation pipeline’s effectiveness.
