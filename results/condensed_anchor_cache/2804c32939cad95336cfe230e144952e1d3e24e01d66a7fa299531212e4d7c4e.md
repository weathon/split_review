## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FCGS, an optimization-free feed-forward model for compressing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations. It introduces a Multi-path Entropy Module (MEM) to adaptively balance size and fidelity, and inter-/intra-Gaussian context models to remove redundancies among unstructured Gaussians. FCGS achieves compression ratios over 20× while maintaining fidelity, reducing compression time from minutes to seconds. Training requires a large dataset (60 GPU days). Experiments are conducted on MipNeRF360, T&T, and DL3DV-GS, with comparisons to methods like Light Gaussians, Niedermayr et al., and ContextGS (concurrent).

### Strengths
- First generalizable optimization-free 3DGS compression pipeline, enabling compression without original training images and expanding application scope.
- Novel Multi-path Entropy Module (MEM) for adaptive control of compression size and quality.
- Inter- and intra-Gaussian context models effectively remove redundancies; hyper-prior design is well-validated.
- Quantitative evaluation is extensive: comparisons to several relevant methods on three datasets, with expressive graphs highlighting the size-quality trade-off (Figure 4); additionally compared to feed-forward reconstruction models.
- Achieves high compression rates while maintaining rendering quality, surpassing most per-scene optimization-based methods in compression speed and rate (reviewer 1). However, reviewer 2 notes it is less effective than Niedermayr et al. in PSNR/Size, and reviewer 4 notes there exist methods with better quality and higher compression rates (FCGS only compared to worse methods).

### Weaknesses
- Training data is very large (60 GPU days); performance with significantly less data is unclear, raising concerns about generalizability and practical applicability if such data is not accessible.
- The methodology section is complex and hard to follow; information is missing (e.g., how Gaussians are divided into batches in Figure 2, the criterion to split them, analysis/synthesis transform architecture, role and position of m in the pipeline not illustrated in Figure 2).
- Focuses on compression speed but does not improve over existing methods in PSNR/Size (less effective than Niedermayr et al.). The practical value of compression speed is questionable since 3DGS reconstruction takes minutes/hours and a few minutes of compression does not significantly impact overall time.
- Qualitative evaluation is insufficient: only comparisons to 3DGS in paper and supplementary; more visual comparisons to baseline methods are needed.
- Experimental presentation is disorganized: main paper uses figures without numerical tables; detailed data only in appendix, causing inconvenience.
- Running time comparison (Tables C-D) may be unfair: FCGS requires a pre-trained 3DGS, and the pre-training time is not included in the table.
- Context model design relies on heuristic priors about Gaussian relations; lacking justification or analysis of those relations (e.g., visualization of inter-/intra-Gaussian context models on real data).
- No ablation on the strategy for dividing chunks in the inter-/intra-Gaussian context model; results for several splitting designs would help.
- Arithmetic coding may incur notable computational cost for encoding/decoding; time for encoding and decoding should be reported.
- Lack of compression performance per attribute (storage size before/after for color and geometry attributes) and number of scenes needed for feasible performance.
- Limited discussion of failure cases: need examples of challenging 3DGS representations (specific geometric/textural complexities leading to artifacts or fidelity loss).
- Concurrent work ContextGS (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2024) applies a similar image-compression pipeline for 3DGS with hyper-prior and inter-Gaussian context; should be mentioned where relevant.
