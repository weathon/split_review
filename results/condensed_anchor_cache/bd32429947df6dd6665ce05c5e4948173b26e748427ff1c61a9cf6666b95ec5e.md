## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces a binary cross-entropy (BCE) / pair relevance classification loss for training text embeddings, generalizing the sigmoid loss from SigLIP. The loss replaces the standard softmax-based contrastive loss in the LLM2Vec setup (Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct) and can incorporate graded relevance labels. Experiments on MTEB, STS, and TREC-DL 2023 show moderate average improvements over the softmax baseline, with better performance on Pair Classification and Semantic Textual Similarity tasks. The method is technically sound but consists primarily of adapting an existing loss to the text embedding domain without substantial novel insight.

### Strengths
- Addresses the important challenge that instance pairs often have relationships between purely positive or negative, and the BCE loss can naturally incorporate soft/graded relevance scores.
- The paper is generally well-written and the core idea is clearly presented.
- Technically sound: reported baseline numbers match those in prior work, and extensive hyperparameter ablations are provided.
- First work to demonstrate that a pointwise sigmoid loss (SigLIP-style) outperforms the standard contrastive (softmax) loss for training text embeddings.
- The proposed loss generalizes the SigLIP loss and can utilize graded relevance labels (e.g., TREC-DL 2023), a capability not directly available in standard contrastive loss.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and contribution**: The method is a direct adaptation of the SigLIP binary classification loss to text embedding learning, strictly following the LLM2Vec setup with the loss as the only modification. The core idea is not new, and the paper does not introduce novel mechanisms or insights beyond applying an existing loss.
- **Modest performance gains**: Improvements over the standard softmax loss on MTEB, STS, and TREC-DL are minimal and not substantial enough to justify a new training paradigm, especially given the additional hyperparameter tuning overhead (e.g., logit bias β, temperature). For binary labeled data (the vast majority of text training data), the proposed loss reverts to the exact sigmoid loss of SigLIP.
- **Lack of analysis why the loss works**: The paper provides no intuition or theoretical analysis for why the pointwise BCE/sigmoid loss outperforms the softmax contrastive loss in this setting. In SigLIP, benefits were attributed to efficient large-batch processing and noise robustness; here the batch size is small (same as LLM2Vec) and noise robustness is not demonstrated. The question of why pointwise ranking (which typically lacks a global view) would be better remains unanswered.
- **Missing comparisons to existing methods**: The paper does not discuss or compare to soft-contrastive methods such as importance-weighted (IW) softmax, which also incorporate relevance scores by weighting pairs. It also fails to cite prior work on pointwise ranking with regression on absolute relevance labels (e.g., Section 2.4 of arXiv:2105.11108). The relationship to these approaches and why BCE is superior is not addressed.
- **Logit bias β is ad hoc**: The purpose of the logit bias resembles methods for addressing long-tail distributions, but no rigorous justification or connection to such methods is provided. Its interaction with the BCE loss remains unclear.
- **Evaluation limited in scope**: Experiments are only on MTEB (with reported accuracy much lower than top leaderboard entries) and on a single model (Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct). No results on other foundational LLMs (e.g., Mistral-7B, Qwen-7B) or other embedding architectures (e.g., transformer-encoder based models like e5) are given. The paper does not test whether the loss is beneficial for non-retrieval datasets (e.g., clustering, classification, STS) when included in training, or provide zero-shot evaluation on a benchmark like AIR-Bench to address possible overfitting to MTEB’s training sets.
- **Title overclaims**: The title claims a “universal” text encoder, but no deliberate methods for generalized representation learning are demonstrated; the term is not justified.
