## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses directed link prediction in Graph Representation Learning (GRL), a task traditionally focused on undirected graphs. The authors adapt successful heuristics from undirected settings, conduct extensive experiments comparing encoders, labeling tricks, structural features, decoders, and negative sampling, and propose the DirLP framework. DirLP combines a directed GNN encoder (DirGNN) with an asymmetric decoder (CMLP) and directed structural features. Experiments on multiple benchmarks (CORA, CITESEER, BLOG, etc.) show DirLP outperforms heuristic, MLP, and undirected GNN baselines.

### Strengths
- Addresses a critical gap in GRL by extending link prediction to directed settings, where real-world data often contains crucial directional information.
- Comprehensive experiments covering encoder, decoder, labeling, structural features, and negative sampling, with easy-to-follow reasoning that aids understanding.
- Robust evaluation using MRR across multiple datasets (CORA, CITESEER, BLOG, etc.).
- Modular framework design (DirGNN encoder, CMLP decoder) makes it adaptable to various graph structures.
- Clear writing and presentation that makes the paper easy to read.
- DirLP demonstrates superior performance over heuristic and standard GNN baselines (GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE) on multiple benchmarks.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty**: The key difference from SEAL is the directed GNN and directed distance, which is a minor modification. Edge-wise structural feature extraction is a single extension of the Buddy method without efficiency hashing. The finding that directed components outperform undirected ones in the directed setting is trivial/intuitive. Insights already evident in [1] (reviewer 1); principal (sic) seems trivial (reviewer 4). Minority reviewer (score 6) found the experiments comprehensive but still raised concerns about baselines and datasets.
- **Missing baselines**: No advanced directed GNNs (e.g., MagNet, DiGCN) are compared. The latest baseline is GAT (2018); no recent undirected or directed link prediction GNNs are included. No comparison with GNNs on the undirected versions of the same datasets to demonstrate the benefit of directionality (reviewer 2). Missing ablation studies quantifying each directed component’s contribution (reviewer 3, reviewer 1).
- **Dataset limitations**: All benchmarks are small; no OGB graphs (e.g., Citation2, which is naturally directed) are used. The Chameleon and Squirrel datasets contain repeated edges without filtering, potentially causing data leakage (reviewer 4). This limits generalizability and scalability claims.
- **Efficiency concerns**: The labeling trick has O(N³) time and O(N²) memory complexity, making it impractical for large-scale graphs. Extra computational cost of DirGNN vs. undirected GNNs is not discussed (reviewer 3). Lack of hyperparameter search space details for OPTUNA (reviewer 1).
- **Missing significance testing**: Performance overlap across experiments is high; no p-values or confidence intervals are provided to confirm significant differences between architectures.
- **Unclear intuition**: Figure 1 does not explain _why_ directionality improves performance (e.g., what finer-level directed distance encoding captures). The necessity of the directed setting is not clearly demonstrated without a direct comparison to undirected GNNs on undirected graphs (reviewer 2).
- **Missing hyperparameter details**: The value of α for each dataset is not shown in Table 12, and its relationship to dataset properties is not discussed (reviewer 2).
- **Spelling/grammar errors**: e.g., "principal" instead of "principle" (reviewer 1).
