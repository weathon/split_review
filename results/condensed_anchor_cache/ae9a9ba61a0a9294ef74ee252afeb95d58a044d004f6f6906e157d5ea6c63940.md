## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper tackles Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) by identifying a bias in prototype classifiers: seen categories are mistakenly classified as novel, leading to imbalanced pseudo-labeling. It proposes DebiasGCD with Dynamic Prototype Debiasing (DPD) to maintain inter-prototype margins and Local Representation Alignment (LRA) to enhance local feature consistency. Experiments across six GCD benchmarks show improvements, e.g., a 10.7% boost on `Old` classes in CUB. Code is available.

### Strengths
- The approach is well-motivated and easy to follow; it directly addresses the overlooked bias of misclassifying seen classes as novel (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5).
- Dynamic Prototype Debiasing (DPD) explicitly optimizes prototypes of known classes, relieving pseudo-label bias (R1).
- The method produces more balanced pseudo-labels and achieves significant performance gains over strong baselines like SimGCD, supported by comprehensive quantitative and qualitative visualizations (R2, R5).
- The framework (DPD+LRA) yields consistent gains on multiple benchmarks and can serve as a plug-and-play module with existing GCD methods (R3).
- Experiments across different open-world semi-supervised learning settings are convincing (R4).
- The paper is generally well-structured and clearly explains the motivations (R2, R3, R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Data errors in Table 4**: The classification ACC numbers for ImageNet-100 are inconsistent; e.g., β=0.5, All=80.4, Old=94.0, New=79.3; β=1.0, All=83.3, Old=94.5, New=77.6 (R1). Such errors raise concerns about result reliability.
- **Missing comparisons and baselines**: Table 2 omits several recent GCD methods: CMS [R1-W7/2] and SPTNet [R1-W7/3], Rastegar et al. (NeurIPS 2023) and Gu et al. (ICCV 2023) (R4). Herbarium, a commonly used fine-grained GCD dataset, is not evaluated (R3). Experiments with DINOv2, employed in recent GCD works [A, NeurIPS 2023], are absent (R2, R4).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and insufficient guidance**: The method is sensitive to β (Table 4) and other parameters; this requires extensive dataset-specific tuning with no clear optimization guidelines, limiting scalability (R2, R3). The ablation misses the choice of margin in DPD, the influence of N (number of selected prototypes), and the effect of strong data augmentation (R1, R5). Error bars from multiple runs (3–5) are not reported, despite large variation in GCD results (R2).
- **Confusing notation, unclear method details and motivation**: Several reviewers found critical parts hard to follow. In section 4.1, the notation \(p_S^{gt} = [p_i^{gt}]\) is ambiguous—whether it is the ground-truth probability or its complement—and the ranking process is unclear (R4). The objective of the ranking loss is not clearly explained (R4). When \(N>1\), the dimensions of \(p_S^{gt}(x_i)\) and \(p_S^{Top\_N}(x_i)\) differ; it is unclear how the subtraction in Eq. 4 is realized (R1). In Eq. 5 (LRA), \(z_i^{feat}\) (a vector) is placed inside the \(\exp\) operator without clarification of dimensions (R5). The reason for choosing the smallest (rather than largest or k-NN) prototype in DPD is not justified; one reviewer notes that the smallest prototype is easier to distinguish and thus less likely to cause bias (R1, R5). The choice of \(r=1\) in Eq. 4 (rather than -1) is not explained, and Line 268-269 contains a typo regarding distance comparison (R5).
- **LRA design and ablation concerns**: The patch loss (LRA) is considered peculiar because strongly augmented views may not align patch-wise; an ablation comparing it to simpler alternatives (e.g., MSE loss) is missing (R4). The effect of strong augmentation used in Fig. 2 is not ablated (R1, R2).
- **Limited novelty**: The main learning components follow SimGCD, and the ranking loss in DPD is not new; LRA lacks full explanation (R5). The paper claims bias towards misclassifying seen as novel, but one reviewer finds this counterintuitive since GCD typically biases novel classes toward old ones (as in SimGCD); more discussion is needed (R5).
- **Figure 4 discrepancy**: The title reads “Pseudo-labels ACC” but values match classification ACC from Table 2, raising doubts about Figure 1(a) as well (R1).
- **Lack of discussion on imbalanced novel class discovery**: The paper focuses on imbalanced pseudo-labels for seen classes but does not address imbalance among novel classes (R4).
- **Typos and formatting**: Multiple typos (e.g., “p_( x_i)” in Line 209, duplicate “w_i^gt,w_i^gt” in Line 269), inconsistent font sizes in tables, and incorrect indexing of Table 3 (R1, R2). Reference to AGCD (CVPR 2024) needs refinement (R5).
- **Minority positive view**: One reviewer (score 6) is notably more positive, praising consistent gains plug-and-play integration and clear structure, while others (scores 3) are more critical of novelty, clarity, and missing experiments. This contrast has been preserved.
