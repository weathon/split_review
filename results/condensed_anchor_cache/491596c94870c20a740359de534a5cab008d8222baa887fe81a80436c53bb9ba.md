## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FedDUL, a federated unlearning method for diffusion models where clients can request forgetting of concepts. The approach has two phases: Client Local Unlearning uses attention-based contrastive loss and LoRA fine-tuning (Section 3.2), and Server Model Aggregation clusters concept embeddings and performs two rounds of parameter averaging. The authors provide theoretical results claiming equivalence between federated learning and federated unlearning, and present quantitative and qualitative experiments on the Celebs and Artists datasets demonstrating that FedDUL can satisfy multiple clients’ unlearning requests while preserving generative capabilities on other concepts.

### Strengths
- The local unlearning technique (Section 3.2) – building a contrastive loss between attention maps of the target concept and the unlearning concept, with an attention loss based on the activation map – is relatively novel and well-justified (Reviewer1).
- Numerical experiments show FedDUL outperforms baselines; it can forget unlearning concepts while maintaining reasonable performance on other concepts (Reviewer1). Reviewer2 (score 5, more positive) also notes improvement over previous techniques.
- Motivation is clear and the problem is important: pre-trained diffusion models in FL may generate images revealing sensitive information, making unlearning crucial for privacy (Reviewer3).
- Experimental visualizations on the Celebs and Artists datasets illustrate the effects of unlearning (Reviewer4).

### Weaknesses
- **Writing quality**: Typos (e.g., line 053 “an novel”, line 219 “it does not related”), lack of parentheses for in-text citations, several figures not commented (Fig 2, 3), sentences difficult to parse, and symbols/equations are inaccurate (e.g., Eq (2) sums from 0 to N implying N+1 clients; Theorem 1 Eq (9) RHS depends on `i` while LHS is a loss sum over all clients; `H` in Eq (9) not defined). Citation format needs fixing throughout (Reviewer1, Reviewer4).
- **Theoretical results are weak or unsound**:
  - Eq (2) is not well-defined: what does it mean to solve an optimization “for all t∈{1,…,T}”? Does it require solving T separate problems? And the formulation maximizes divergence without any constraint to preserve generative power on non-private concepts; similar problems hold for Eq (3) (Reviewer1).
  - Theorem 1 is trivial (Reviewer1, Reviewer2, Reviewer4). Lemma 1 correctness is doubted (e.g., when p_θ_p is the normal distribution, Lemma 1 would imply both θ_g induce the same distribution), and it is a restatement of previous results without citation (Reviewer1, Reviewer4). Theorem 2 can be proven in one line from Lemma 1, offering no new insight (Reviewer2). The paper’s claimed equivalence between Eq (2) and (3) (lines 308‑310) is not proved (Reviewer1).
  - The theoretical part cannot support the claim of “leverage traditional federated learning methods to address federated unlearning issues” (Reviewer3). Reviewer3 also notes that the KL divergence is not symmetric, so Eq (17) cannot lead to Eq (13) in Theorem 1.
  - The argument that aggregated knowledge will not complement to undo unlearning is considered flawed by Reviewer3, who references evidence (Chang & Shokri, ICLR 2023) that aggregation can reinforce knowledge.
  - Lemma 1’s upper bound is trivial (Reviewer2). Overall, Theorems 1 and 2 are observations rather than theorems, and the promised “detailed proofs” are not delivered (Reviewer1, Reviewer4).
- **Loss function design and motivation unclear**:
  - The loss functions Eq (3), Eq (4), and Eq (8) are unconvincing: the KL divergence between the distributions p_θ_p(·|C_i) and p_θ_p(·|C_T) may already be large; it would make more sense to maximize [D_KL(C_i) – α D_KL(C_T)], i.e., unlearn C_i and learn C_T (Reviewer2).
  - Why minimize the Frobenius norm of the attention map for the sensitive objective to achieve unlearning? (Reviewer4)
  - The rationale for using a contrastive loss between two attention maps, rather than directly adopting existing machine unlearning methods (e.g., random labeling or assigning a safe target), is not explained. The objective appears similar to assigning another safe label to forgotten data (Reviewer3).
- **Federated aggregation scheme**: The motivation behind the clustered aggregation scheme (Section 3.3) is unclear; if all clusters have the same size, it is equivalent to normal aggregation (Reviewer1).
- **Unlearning granularity and setup not clarified**: The paper does not define whether unlearning targets client-level, sample-level, or feature-level forgetting. It is also unclear if the goal is a single global model that forgets individual clients’ data or personalized models; these are different problems requiring different techniques (Reviewer4).
- **Experiments insufficient**:
  - Only 50% of clients request unlearning; what about 10%? Would the undesired knowledge then complement each other more easily? (Reviewer3)
  - Need to consider different forgetting scenarios, e.g., class-level and sample-level forgetting (Reviewer3).
  - Provide FID score to verify that the unlearned model does not lose utility (Reviewer3).
  - The paper lacks comprehensive analysis and rigorous reasoning overall (Reviewer3).
- **Lack of novelty**: The approach is similar to existing federated unlearning literature that locally maximizes KL divergence and then averages the local models (Reviewer2).
- **Direct applicability question**: Why not directly apply existing machine unlearning or federated unlearning methods? (Reviewer3)
- **Theorem 1 interpretation**: Eq (9) is difficult to parse (dependence on i, H undefined), so it remains unclear how Theorem 1 indicates that the aggregated global model is unlikely to recover unlearnt concepts (Reviewer4).
