## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a watermarking scheme for LLMs that operates in a black-box setting (only sampling access). It uses rejection sampling to select sequences with high watermark scores based on a secret key. The authors claim distortion-free property, provide theoretical guarantees, and show experiments on MISTRAL-7B-INSTRUCT and GEMMA-7B-INSTRUCT. The scheme can be adapted for white-box settings and supports nesting/chaining multiple keys.

### Strengths
- The problem of black-box watermarking is important and having new schemes in this direction is interesting. The method is effective in a black-box setting, requiring only sampling sequences from LLMs.
- While some reviewers found the scheme novel, based on a generalization of existing ideas, with a good exploration of variants (e.g., CDF) in a principled way, others argued it is a straightforward adaptation of Aaronson’s method, using rejection sampling instead of the Gumbel-max trick, and that the setting and approach are already presented in prior work (Christ et al., COLT 2023) which studies black-box watermarking with provable security and distortion-free guarantees.
- The paper provides solid theoretical analysis, including a distortion-free proof and a lower bound on detection performance. Theorem 4.2 is carefully placed in context and analyzed for various input values. However, the distortion-free condition is complex and does not simply translate to high entropy; the scheme of Christ, Gunn, Zamir (2024) is distortion-free under no assumptions. Theorem 4.1 assumes continuous F, which is unrealistic for efficient algorithms.
- Experiments are thorough: they evaluate TPR/FPR, text quality, robustness to random token replacement and paraphrasing, and explore various scenarios and ablations. Results support theoretical claims and show competitiveness with or superiority over existing white-box schemes (KB, Kirchenbauer et al. 2023b). It is interesting that making the detector length-aware improves performance significantly.
- The framework is versatile: can be applied recursively with multiple secret keys, and adapted for white-box when probabilities are available.
- White-box results are convincing.

### Weaknesses
- Writing and presentation issues: Section 3 is dense and not reader-friendly; lacks high-level intuition, examples, and figures. Terms like g(w) are introduced but unused. The role of n-gram, l-gram, and token relations is informal. “Where we allow the left endpoint to spill over only to…” is not formally understandable. The definition of F is unclear: it is called a CDF, then described as “a single draw from a pseudorandom number generator for F seeded by integer seed s” without clarification. It is assumed without citation that F[h(K,w)] is a PRF when F is a PRG. The paper uses the 2024 style file without line numbers, making reference difficult. Overall readability is low; the scheme is barely readable for understanding details.
- Motivation and practicality are unclear. Why would end users with API-only access watermark a model they do not own, when watermarking is intended to prevent misuse? Could this allow attackers to learn the watermark? Simpler methods exist to avoid exposing logits. The black-box variant’s practicality is underdiscussed: it is hard to control token lengths and prefill first k tokens in chat APIs; the variant with k equal to text length is most practical but not evaluated. As k=50 yields at most 70 pAUC, the practical variant likely needs very high m. The method requires len/k * m queries per text, which can be prohibitively expensive. The zero-bit nature only detects watermarks, cannot infer additional information. The paper is framed around black-box but key results in Sec. 5 are white-box.
- Evaluation limitations: The authors propose AUC below fixed FPR (e.g., 1%) instead of the standard TPR @ low FPR. Is a 1% FPR practical for real-world deployment? The metric is still dominated by impractical FPRs. Short texts (~300 tokens) are not robust to paraphrasing; prior work shows robustness improves at >600 tokens; the paper should extend evaluation to longer texts. The scheme does not outperform KB (Kirchenbauer et al. 2023b) which is white-box and nearly two years old; the authors need to clarify the advantage. Provide tradeoff illustrations: distortion vs. quality using LLM-Judge, and distortion vs. robustness with examples comparing unwatermarked text, proposed watermark (optimal hyperparameters), and KB watermark (optimal hyperparameters). The distortion-free claim should be backed by qualitative examples. Missing comparisons to cited black-box baselines (PostMark and Yang et al.) and to Christ et al. (2023, COLT) which provides provably distortion-free black-box watermark with formal robustness; the paper should explain what new features it adds. Time complexity comparison with baselines is missing. Provide examples of watermarked text.
- Related Work omission: The paper suggests Aaronson and Kirchenbauer were first to embed information in LLM outputs, but “Neural Linguistic Steganography” (2019) did this earlier.
- Some reviewers found the scheme unconvincing with little novelty; others considered it novel and well-evaluated.
- Questions that overlap with criticisms:
  - Elaborate on the decision to introduce AUC until fixed FPR instead of TPR @ low FPR; is 1% FPR practical?
  - Extend paraphrasing evaluation to longer texts (>600 tokens) to match prior work settings.
  - Comment on the discrepancy between blackbox framing and whitebox results; include experiments for the practical k=L case.
  - Compare to black-box baselines (PostMark, Yang et al.) and explain why not feasible.
  - Compare to Christ et al. and explain what features are added.
  - Provide example of watermarked text.
  - Illustrate tradeoff between distortion and text quality via LLM-Judge.
