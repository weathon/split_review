## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DMQR-RAG, a Diverse Multi-Query Rewriting framework for RAG that generates multiple query rewrites via four prompting strategies (general rewrite, keyword extraction, expansion, reduction) and an adaptive selection method. Experiments use Bing + BGE-reranker, GPT-4/Llama3-8B generators, and three datasets (mostly multi-hop/complex QA). Three reviewers rate 3 (confidence 4, 2, 4); one reviewer rates 5 (confidence 4). The minority positive reviewer emphasizes effective results, extensive case studies, real-world industrial deployment, and generalizability across LLMs. The majority critique insufficient originality, lack of technical depth, missing related work, reliance on single retriever, absence of statistical significance testing, and unresolved practical costs.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important area: enhancing retrieval through query modeling in RAG.
- Assumption that different rewrites capture different query aspects is reasonable; creating diversified rewrites has not been widely explored in RAG literature.
- Comprehensive evaluation including both academic and industrial settings, extensive ablation studies on rewriting strategies and LLMs (GPT-4, Llama3-8B), and real-world deployment.
- Adaptive strategy to reduce irrelevant documents by selecting appropriate rewrites per query; shows slight outperformance over RAG-Fusion in industrial settings.
- Experiments show interesting results and ablation analyses reveal impact of different rewrite types and LLMs.
- The paper is clear and easy to follow (noted by Reviewer 1; however, other reviewers found writing unclear – see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Originality and novelty**: The core idea of combining multiple rewritten queries is already present in RAG-Fusion; all strategies (equality, expansion, reduction) use single baselines and are accomplished solely through prompt engineering – limited innovation. The paper fails to engage in meaningful discussion of prior work (e.g., query expansion, search result diversification, classical IR query modeling) and lacks a formal related work section. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
- **Insufficient technical depth**: Implementation details of rewrites are missing (e.g., how keyword extraction works, handling of proper noun noise); the adaptive selection criteria are unclear. Section 2.1 is unnecessary and lacks specifics; “transmission of information” concept is not explained. The paper mostly offers a prompt as contribution. (Reviewers 1,3,4)
- **Hypotheses not well-supported**: References (Maron & Kuhns, Baeza-Yates, Weikum & Vossen) are database-focused and do not directly support claims about natural language query diversity; known that rewriting can introduce drift. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limited retrieval setting**: Only Bing + BGE-reranker is used; claim of “without loss of generality” is unsupported. No demonstration across different retrievers (e.g., OpenAI-v3, Grit-LM, RankGPT, RankLLama) – a major weakness that prevents generalization claims. (Reviewer 3)
- **Dataset choice and scope**: Datasets are primarily multi-hop/complex QA; unclear if method works for simpler queries (e.g., Natural Questions, MSMarco, TREC-DL). Authors should test on simpler or ranking datasets and, if only for complex questions, reposition claims. (Reviewer 1)
- **Baseline fairness**: Proposed adaptive selection uses few-shot prompts while baselines use zero-shot prompts, giving an advantage. (Reviewer 2)
- **Statistical rigor**: No bootstrapped confidence intervals or p-values; selective reporting (e.g., emphasis on P@5 improvement over RAG-Fusion in Table 2 without showing non-significant differences). (Reviewer 3)
- **Cost and latency**: Generating multiple rewrites and feeding recall results into the adaptive selection prompt significantly increases tokens and inference latency; this is not discussed. (Reviewers 2,4)
- **Prompts alone do not solve mentioned problems**: Problems like noise and intent deviation are stated but not analyzed in experiments; it is simplistic to assume prompting suffices. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing comparisons**: Classical query rewriting methods, more sophisticated fusion methods from IR literature, and search result diversification approaches are not considered. (Reviewers 1,2)
- **Writing clarity**: Lacks related work section; too many acronyms; some sections are difficult to follow (Reviewer 3 – contrast with Reviewer 1's positive clarity note). (Reviewer 3)
- **Unanswered questions**: How does the method reduce noise? For what query types is each rewrite most useful? Would a small trained selector be better than LLM selection? (Reviewer 2)
