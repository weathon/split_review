## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes L4Q, which integrates Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) with LoRA to produce fully quantized LLMs while minimizing training memory. It uses a memory-optimized layer design and is evaluated on LLaMA families. The idea is timely and the paper is well written.

### Strengths
- The combination of QAT with LoRA is a timely and relevant approach for memory-efficient fine-tuning and inference (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- The method is simple, and the paper is well structured and easy to follow (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- The paper shows actual inference speedup results (Figure 4), which is rarely done in quantization literature (Reviewer 2).
- The authors provide specific implementation details and code to aid reproducibility (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited technical novelty** – The integration of LoRA inside the quantizer is straightforward and conceptually overlaps with prior work (QA‑LoRA, LR‑QAT, QLLM section 4.2). Reviewer 1 notes the precision mismatching discussion is already present in QA‑LoRA. Reviewer 2 points out high similarity to concurrent LR‑QAT [3]. Reviewer 4 states the method is essentially a special case of QLLM [1]. The contributions are considered incremental by multiple reviewers.

2. **Result discrepancies with original papers** – Reviewer 1 observes that QA‑LoRA 4‑bit LLaMA‑33B on MMLU 0‑/5‑shot reported 55.4/58.1 in the original paper, but L4Q reports only 48.9/55.0. Reviewer 3 finds a similar mismatch: for LLaMA‑1‑7B 4‑bit group‑128 MMLU 5‑shot, QA‑LoRA originally gives 38.4% (Table 6 in QA‑LoRA), but L4Q reports 35.6%, and L4Q itself achieves only 35.7% (improvement of 0.6% over fp16 baseline vs. QA‑LoRA’s 3.8%). The data in Tables 2 and 3 is substantially lower than the corresponding original papers, raising concerns about the evaluation pipeline.

3. **Missing baselines and comparisons**  
   - Traditional QAT methods (LSQ, LSQ+) and LLM‑specific QAT (LLM‑QAT [1]) are not compared, nor is more recent PTQ like SpinQuant [2] (Reviewer 2).  
   - The advanced baseline IR‑QLoRA (ICML 2024) is omitted; on LLaMA‑1‑7B 4‑bit MMLU 5‑shot it achieves 40.8% (6.2% gain) while L4Q only gets 35.7% (0.6% gain) (Reviewer 3).  
   - No comparison to QLLM [1] is provided, despite high similarity (Reviewer 4).  
   - Evaluation is limited to LLaMA 1 and 2; newer models (LLaMA 3, Phi‑3, Mistral) are not tested (Reviewers 2,3).  
   - Only LLaMA-family models are used; a non‑LLaMA architecture (e.g., OPT) should be included (Reviewer 3).  
   - The authors claim effectiveness in sub‑4‑bit quantization but do not report 2‑bit experiments, while QA‑LoRA does (Reviewer 3).  
   - The source code is not released, hampering reproducibility (Reviewer 2).

4. **Missing ablations and justifications**  
   - **LoRA initialization inside the quantizer** – It should be studied whether initialization using quantization error (as in LoftQ) improves results (Reviewer 1).  
   - **Quantization parameter initialization** – No ablation shows the impact of the proposed initialization vs. no initialization (Reviewer 3).  
   - **Group size effect** – LLaMA and OpenLLaMA use different group sizes, but no ablation examines the influence of group size on accuracy (Reviewer 3).  
   - **LoRA warm‑up** – Table 5 shows warm‑up gives at best marginal improvement (−0.3% to +0.1% on common‑sense QA), suggesting it is ineffective (Reviewer 3).  
   - **Quantization frequency** – No comparison of applying quantization every N steps vs. every step, which could affect performance (Reviewer 4).

5. **Training efficiency and cost analysis**  
   - Training time costs are not explained in detail. Because the gradient must pass through the quantizer, training costs should change relative to methods that do not insert LoRA inside the quantizer (Reviewer 1).  
   - In Table 9, L4Q is faster than QA‑LoRA for 3–13B models but 3.4 hours slower for 33B; logically, if L4Q is more efficient for smaller models, it should also be for larger ones. Moreover, since QA‑LoRA is PTQ‑based and L4Q is QAT‑based, L4Q should require longer training, but the opposite is reported (Reviewer 3).  
   - It is not disclosed whether gradient checkpointing is used; this information is needed for fair training cost comparisons (Reviewer 4).

6. **Speedup ratio inconsistency** – In Figure 4, L4Q and QA‑LoRA show identical speedup ratios under 4‑bit quantization, despite using different quantization formats (min‑max in QA‑LoRA vs. the proposed scaling in Eq. 3). This requires explanation (Reviewer 3).

7. **Incorrect table label** – Table 12 labels QA‑LoRA as “4+16 bits”; it should be “4 bits” (Reviewer 3).

8. **Presentation and reproducibility issues**  
   - The background section is overly redundant, including unnecessary derivatives for bias and scaling factors that are not used later (Reviewer 1).  
   - Notation is inconsistent (e.g., capital R for a function, capital A for a matrix) (Reviewer 1).  
   - The gradient derivation in Eqs. 9–11 is trivial and provides no insight (Reviewer 2).  
   - Related work is insufficiently discussed; a proper positioning relative to other LLM quantization and PEFT literature is missing (Reviewer 2).  
   - The claim of a novel quantization initialization is overstated; min‑max based initialization is common in the literature (Reviewer 2).

**Note:** One reviewer (Reviewer 3) is significantly more critical, highlighting major data mismatches and missing baselines that question the method’s effectiveness. The other reviewers (1,2,4) gave positive overall scores (5,5,5) but still note limited novelty and missing comparisons.
