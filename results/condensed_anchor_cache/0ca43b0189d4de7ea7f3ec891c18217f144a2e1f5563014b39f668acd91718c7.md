## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a unified optimization framework for graph coarsening that reduces large graphs while preserving desired structural properties (sparsity, scale-free, bipartite, multi-component). The framework is solved using block majorization-minimization and spectral regularization. Experiments on several benchmark datasets (up to 30k nodes) demonstrate improved node classification accuracy compared to existing coarsening methods.

### Strengths

- Graph coarsening is an important problem for scaling GNNs to large graphs. [R1, R4]
- The unified optimization framework supports multiple structural properties (sparse, scale-free, bipartite, multi-component) within a single formulation, extending prior work to a broader setting. [R1, R2]
- Empirical results show the proposed method outperforms state-of-the-art coarsening baselines (GCOND, GC-SLIM) in node classification accuracy across different GNN architectures. [R1, R2, R3]
- The motivation for structured graph coarsening is clearly explained. [R3]
- One reviewer noted the method is fast and the optimization procedures seem reasonable. [R4] (However, see weaknesses on time complexity and efficiency contradictions below.)

### Weaknesses

- **Small-scale evaluation only**: All benchmarks are small (largest ~30k nodes), which does not demonstrate scalability to large graphs where coarsening is most needed. [R1, R2, R3] Reviewers recommend testing on larger datasets such as OGB. [R2, R3]

- **Missing comparisons**: 
  - No comparison with original (full) graph performance is provided in the main node classification tables (Tables 1-3 supplementary); this is needed to quantify the accuracy-cost trade-off of coarsening. [R1, R3, R4]
  - Key baseline Kumar et al. (JMLR 2023) is mentioned in Section 2.2 but not compared experimentally. [R2, R4]
  - Only two reduction baselines (GCOND, GC-SLIM, both from 2021) are used, despite many available coarsening/sparsification/condensation methods. [R3, R4]
  - The two used baselines from 2021 are not recent enough to be called state-of-the-art. [R4]

- **Time complexity and efficiency contradictions**: 
  - Claimed time complexity is \(O(n^2 k)\), quadratic in node count, which is impractical for large graphs. [R2]
  - Experimental results (Table 7) show that coarsening+classification time is not significantly less than classification on the original graph; in the worst case (BI-GC on CORA), coarsening+classification even exceeds the original time, undermining the core motivation. [R1]
  - If the reduced graph is dense, training costs on it could be higher than on the original sparse graph. [R3]
  - Contrasting these criticisms, one reviewer found the method fast. [R4]

- **Limited novelty and insufficient distinction from prior work**: 
  - Graph reduction for GNN acceleration is not new; the coarsening objectives (log determinant, spectral regularization) are standard components; and the optimization heuristics (block majorization-minimization) are standard techniques. [R3]
  - The paper does not clearly articulate how it advances beyond prior works like Manoj Kumar (JMLR 2023) and Sandeep Kumar (JMLR 2020). [R2]
  - Section 2 lacks intuitive motivation: it does not explain why equation (1) is the right starting point, why structural properties such as multi-component and scale-free are important for coarsening, or what the mapping matrix \(C\) and its constraints mean. [R4]

- **Lack of analysis and guidance**: 
  - No analysis connects the coarsening objective values to downstream classification performance; it is unclear whether a "better" coarsening matrix (in terms of the objectives) yields better accuracy. [R3]
  - No discussion on how to choose among the four structural properties for a given scenario, nor on why imposing all constraints simultaneously is problematic, leaving trade-offs unexplored. [R4]
  - The connection between the sparsity of the \(\phi\) matrix and downstream performance is not established theoretically or empirically. [R4]

- **Presentation and reproducibility issues**: 
  - The specific GNN architecture used in Section 5 is not stated, hindering reproducibility. [R4]
  - Table 7 has confusing formatting: baselines are boldfaced instead of the proposed methods. [R4]
  - Multiple typos: "C^T" should be "C^\top" [R1]; "gdet" missing preceding text [R1]; "Wh.da." should be "Whole dataset" [R1]; first minus sign after equation (11) likely should be an equal sign [R4].
  - The literature review is too short and uninformative. [R1]
  - The last paragraph of Section 1 is vague and does not sufficiently justify why sparsity, scale-free, and bipartite structures are focused on; most scale-free graphs are also sparse, so discussing them separately needs justification. [R1]
  - Data splits used in experiments are not reported. [R3]

- **Accuracy gap**: There is a notable gap between the accuracy of the proposed coarsened methods and whole-graph training, questioning the practical usefulness of the coarsening. [R3]
