## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers agree that the idea of using an LLM-based agent (TestAgent) for adaptive human assessment is promising and comprehensive, with components for dynamic question selection, autonomous feedback, anomaly management, and diagnostic report generation. The work attempts to address limitations of traditional rigid testing methods. However, reviewers raise significant concerns about clarity, missing methodological details, lack of ablation studies, heavy reliance on synthetic data, insufficient error analysis, and questionable statistical claims. Reviewer 2 is notably more critical (score 3), while Reviewer 4 appears to be reviewing a different paper (CUFF-KT) but their comments are included as given. Reviewer 3 is generally positive (score 5) but requests more real-world validation and fairness analysis. Reviewer 1 (score 5) highlights good ideas but weak presentation.

### Strengths
- **Originality & high-level idea**: Integrating LLMs into adaptive testing is a novel and intuitive application, moving beyond rigid fixed-question formats. The paper is the first to apply LLMs in this domain.
- **Comprehensive solution**: TestAgent covers personalized question selection, cognitive diagnosis, anomaly detection (identifying guessing, ambiguous answers), autonomous feedback, and detailed report generation. The anomaly detection component is particularly compelling.
- **Error mode focus**: The identification of failure modes (hallucinations, redundant answers) is helpful (Reviewer 1).
- **Experimental range**: Extensive experiments span three domains (education/MATH, personality/MBTI, mental health) with multiple baselines (random, FSI, KLI, MAAT) and both simulated and real-world human studies (Reviewer 3).
- **Quantitative improvements**: The work claims ~20% fewer questions for accurate assessment and demonstrates performance gains (AUC, accuracy). Reviewer 4 notes an ~7% AUC improvement over baselines on multiple datasets (though this refers to CUFF-KT, not TestAgent).
- **Potential significance**: Relevance to personalized education, psychological evaluation, and ICLR community. Aligns with trends toward AI-driven human-centered assessment tools.
- **Presentation quality** (Reviewer 3): Generally well-written, logically structured, good use of figures and case studies.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and writing issues** (Reviewer 2): The paper is hard to follow, with many typos and unclear organization. Major components (Universal Data Infrastructure, cognitive diagnosis training, simulation process) are poorly explained. The concept of θ (what exactly is measured—single scalar vs. vector of sub-skills) is not concretely defined across datasets (MATH, MBTI, mental health). The origin of variables a, b, c in the cognitive diagnosis model is not given.
- **Lack of methodological detail**: 
  - Cognitive diagnosis component: type of model, training data, application method are not described concretely (Reviewer 1). 
  - Fine-tuning details (e.g., for ChatGLM2-6B) gloss over dataset composition, synthetic data generation prompts, and training procedure. 
  - Classifier (L264) lacks architecture, input/output specification, loss function (Reviewer 2). 
  - Full prompts sent to the LLM agent are not provided; prompt design is critical for reproducibility and understanding agent behavior (Reviewer 4).
- **Evaluation weaknesses**:
  - **Synthetic data reliance**: Experiments heavily use simulated students; real-world validation is limited. Generalizability to actual, less predictable responses is unclear (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 3 asks why synthetic data was the focus and whether real data could be incorporated.
  - **No ablation studies**: The contributions of individual modules (adaptive question selection, cognitive diagnosis, anomaly management, autonomous feedback) are not isolated. It is unclear if the whole system is needed or if components can be removed (Reviewers 2, 4). Reviewer 4 also asks about human-machine collaboration (e.g., human expert replacing one component).
  - **Multidimensional evaluation lacks detail**: How accuracy is determined (controls for order, bias, significance tests) is missing. The claim of statistical significance is questionable: in Table 1(a), MAAT and TestAgent+KLI have identical AUC@50 values (65.12±1.48) but only TestAgent is bolded as superior with p≤0.01. More detail on the statistical test is needed (Reviewers 1, 2).
  - **Error analysis missing**: Hallucinations and redundant errors are mentioned but not quantified or categorized by type/frequency. Impact on assessment quality is not explored. Reviewer 3 asks for a breakdown (e.g., % hallucinations vs. misinterpretations) and mitigation strategies via feedback/anomaly modules.
  - **Human study insufficiently described**: Participant instructions, TestAgent version used, whether volunteers were trained, experimental protocol are all lacking (Reviewer 2).
- **LLM reliability / trade-offs**: Using LLMs introduces hallucination risk. The paper does not discuss the trade-off between this new problem and the issues of traditional testing that motivated the work (Reviewer 1).
- **Scalability & computational cost**: Not discussed. Deploying testAgent at scale with many test-takers requires significant LLM resources; feasibility for resource-constrained institutions is unclear. Reviewer 3 suggests strategies like distillation or pruning.
- **Bias and fairness**: Not addressed. LLMs may reflect training data biases, affecting question selection or feedback across demographic groups. Reviewer 3 requests at least a high-level fairness analysis.
- **User experience comparison**: No comparative analysis of user engagement, satisfaction, or perceived accuracy against traditional methods. Reviewer 3 suggests capturing qualitative feedback on diagnostic reports.
- **Final expert analysis** (Reviewer 4): The framework still requires expert analysis at the final step, which contradicts the goal of full automation. Why not design an evaluation agent to replace/reduce expert dependence?
- **Framework diagram detail** (Reviewer 4): Could show data flow and data structures between modules (question generation, cognitive diagnosis, adaptive selection) for better clarity.
- **Appendix lacks full prompts** (Reviewer 4): For an LLM-based system, prompt design defines the task; missing constraints, instructions, few-shot examples.

### Minority / Contrasting Opinions
- **Reviewer 4** (score 6, confidence 2) is the most positive, but their comments are almost entirely about a different paper titled CUFF-KT (introducing RLPA task, avoiding fine-tuning, AUC improvement 7%). Their strengths and weaknesses do not directly correspond to TestAgent. They ask additional questions about applicability to other fields, handling large complex datasets, and avoiding overfitting without fine-tuning—none of which are addressed in TestAgent.
- **Reviewer 2** (score 3) is the most critical, citing severe clarity and reproducibility issues, and directly questions the validity of the statistical significance claims. They find the presentation insufficient (score 1).
- **Reviewer 1** (score 5, confidence 4) finds the idea good but weaknesses in explanation and evaluation prevent full endorsement.
