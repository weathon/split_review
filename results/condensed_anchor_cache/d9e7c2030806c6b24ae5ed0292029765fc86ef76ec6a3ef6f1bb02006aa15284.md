## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper unifies PCA, orthogonal NMF, and k-means under subspace clustering, proposing a closed‑form solution via SVD followed by a threshold (filter). Theorem 1 guarantees exact recovery when the data is near subspaces (small ‖Z‖). Experiments on synthetic and five real datasets show that the closed‑form solution often perfectly recovers clusters, and that applying spectral clustering on the SVD embedding (CF+SpC) extends recovery. Reviewers generally found the paper clearly written but lacking novelty and rigorous evaluation. One reviewer (R4, score 5) was more positive, noting the theoretical connection as new; the other three (R1, R2, R3, score 3) cited limited novelty, insufficient comparison with related distribution assumptions, and experimental flaws.

### Strengths
- Well‑written and easy to follow (R1, R4).
- Concise unified expression (Eq. (2)) for PCA, ONMF, and k‑means (R1, R2 acknowledges the relevance).
- Closed‑form solution for perfect recovery under well‑separated clusters (R1, R3).
- Theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) connecting ‖Z‖ and the singular‑value gap, with exact‑recovery guarantee under high SNR (R1, R4; R4 found this connection new).
- Experiments confirm the existence of a threshold below which exact recovery is achieved by the closed form (R1).
- Experiments show exact recovery remains possible via spectral clustering on the SVD embedding even when the closed‑form fails (R1).
- Algorithm is simple and easy to implement (R4).
- Subspace clustering is a relevant problem (R2).
- Deterministic error bound for data that is well‑separated (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The unified expression (Eq. (2)) is a trivial observation because it reduces to low‑rank approximation via SVD; the equivalence of PCA, ONMF, and k‑means has long been known (R1, R2). The closed‑form solution (SVD + thresholding) is standard; the refinement step is not new and was not evaluated on real data (R1). The theoretical analysis uses the standard Davis–Kahan theorem (R1). Overall, the paper lacks new ideas (R2). R4 was more positive about the theoretical connection, but still notes the method’s simplicity.
- **Strong assumption**: The “near subspace” assumption may be unrealistic; projective clustering (flats not necessarily through the origin) is more general, and a small ‖Z‖ may be impossible when \(m,n\) are large (R4). The “not too scattered” distribution assumption resembles α‑perturbation resilience used in k‑means (Cohen‑Addad et al. 2017, Angelidakis et al. 2017), but this connection is not discussed (R3).
- **Unfair experiments**: CF+SpC applies spectral clustering on the top singular vectors (which have higher SNR), while comparison methods use the original data embedding, biasing results against them (R1). R1 suggests re‑running other clustering methods on the rows of \(\hat{P}\) to test if they also achieve exact recovery.
- **Refinement step untested**: The proposed closed‑form algorithm includes a refinement step, but only CF+SpC is evaluated on real data; no empirical results are given for the vanilla closed‑form with refinement (R1). R1 asks when disjoint support of \(\hat{P}_\lambda\) can be found and whether partial recovery is possible.
- **Missing complexity analysis**: No time or memory complexity analysis; scalability to large datasets is unclear. Massively parallel k‑means methods (Cohen‑Addad et al. 2022) offer scalable guarantees (R3). SVD is time‑consuming; experiments use only small \(m,n\); no acceleration strategy (e.g., fast PCA) is discussed (R4).
- **Clarity issues**: R2 questions the uniqueness of \(U,V,Z\) and the conditions that make them unique, the definition of “smallest separation between subspace”, and whether the condition of Theorem 1 can be tested (if not, its utility is unclear). R2 finds the validation of the theorem with simulated data pointless. R3 notes that real‑world dataset sizes are not reported. R4 points out the lack of an optimisation viewpoint, i.e., the goal is \(\min\|X-UV^\top\|\) and OPT=‖Z‖; what does the closed‑form output if OPT is not small?
- **Missing comparisons and ablations**: R3 asks for a comparison of the distribution assumptions with perturbation‑resilient assumptions. R4 asks for the rate at which ‖Z‖ increases with \(n,m\) and the feasibility of the theorem’s condition for larger datasets. R4 also asks whether the reported computation time includes the search for hyperparameter \(\lambda\).
