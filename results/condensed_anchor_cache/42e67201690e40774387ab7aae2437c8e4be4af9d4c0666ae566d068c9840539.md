## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes OPRIDE, an algorithm for offline preference-based reinforcement learning that improves query efficiency via two components: a query selection strategy that maximizes informativeness using value-difference maximization, and a variance-based discount scheduling mechanism to mitigate reward model overoptimization. The paper includes a theoretical result and empirical evaluations on locomotion, manipulation, and navigation tasks, showing strong performance with very limited queries (e.g., budget 10). Reviewers highlight novelty and thorough experiments, but differ on clarity of contributions, novelty relative to prior work, and the practical relevance of the theoretical analysis.

### Strengths
- The method is novel, well-motivated, and a nice simplification compared to prior work (Reviewer 1).
- The discount adaptation schedule is a neat idea to reduce reward model overoptimization; it seems to work well in practice (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4: "simple yet effective under an extremely constrained query budget of 10").
- The empirical evaluation is thorough: multiple environments (MetaWorld, AntMaze, MuJoCo), key ablations, and comparisons to strong baselines, with overall strong results (Reviewer 1).
- The paper integrates a principled exploration mechanism that maximizes query informativeness (Reviewer 2).
- Ablation studies delineate the contributions of each component (Reviewer 2).
- The theoretical derivations appear rigorous (Reviewer 2), and the theoretical analysis provides a guarantee of efficiency under the assumption of online queries (Reviewer 3).
- Both techniques (query selection and uncertainty-based discounting) could also be useful in online RL (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is well-written and easy to read (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear key contributions**: The introduction overemphasizes the offline setting as the main novelty, but prior work commonly considers offline settings. Instead, the novel algorithm, theoretical result, and empirical performance should be highlighted more. The variance-based discount scheduling is novel but under-emphasized (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited novelty and inconsistent combination**: The two components are simple and loosely related. The connection between reward overoptimization and query efficiency is not clearly discussed—it is not straightforward why addressing overoptimization directly translates to needing fewer queries (Reviewer 2). The exploration strategy is similar to PEBBLE’s unsupervised exploration, and the discount scheduling is similar to uncertainty-based methods in traditional RL (e.g., REDQ, MOPO), without articulating specific differences or advantages. Moreover, the paper lacks strong justification for applying uncertainty to the discount factor rather than to the reward or value function directly (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient comparison to prior work**:
  - Differences from disagreement-based methods, semi-supervised offline RL, and prior theoretical work are not explained (Reviewer 1).
  - The objective in Eq. (8) is closely related to Lindner et al. (variance over value difference); more discussion is needed (Reviewer 1).
  - Related work should mention RLHF given its recent popularity (Reviewer 3).
- **Theoretical analysis issues**:
  - The theoretical variant assumes online queries are available, which is not the same as the practical algorithm (Algorithm 1). This undermines the relevance of the theory to the actual offline setting; the paper does not discuss this limitation clearly (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
  - The main paper lacks intuition or proof sketch; the bound is not compared to prior results in bandits or RL (Reviewer 1). The analysis considers a different algorithm, so it is unclear whether it justifies the proposed criterion (Reviewer 3).
  - The assumption of finite Eluder dimension is not empirically elaborated (Reviewer 2).
  - The theoretical findings about online querying being more sample-efficient are poorly linked to the algorithm pipeline (Reviewer 2).
- **Formalization and presentation errors**:
  - Non-stationary MDP definition seems unnecessary; need for both Bellman operators unclear; returns in Eq. (6) not discounted; confusion between return model vs reward model—why not use “return network” consistently (Reviewer 3).
  - Scale sensitivity of the query criterion is noted, but the variance-based criterion is also scale-sensitive (Reviewer 3).
  - Minor issues: citations placed incorrectly, missing discount factor in Eq. (2), “the optimal policy” should be “an optimal policy”, missing definitions (e.g., α′-independent), notation errors (Line 202, 265, 272, 329) (Reviewer 3).
- **Experimental gaps and limitations**:
  - Experiments only on basic RL tasks (locomotion, manipulation, navigation); no evidence for LLM/text settings where PbRL is most used (Reviewer 1).
  - Missing baseline/ablation: IDRL not run in AntMaze or Figure 2; Table 6 lacks IDRL results (Reviewer 1).
  - No ablation removing variance scheduling (and no PDS) to measure its contribution (Reviewer 1).
  - In Figure 2, does PT refer to random query? Need results of OPRIDE with random query to isolate query selection effect (Reviewer 4).
  - Query budget limited to 10 (or up to 20); for practical scenarios (e.g., LLMs), larger budgets (e.g., 200) are needed, and it is unclear if OPRIDE’s advantage deteriorates (Reviewer 4).
  - Hyper-parameter analysis on m (number of models? query selection parameter?) is missing (Reviewer 4).
  - The paper should show how Theorem 4 implies better query efficiency than random (Reviewer 4).
- **Practical downsides**: Requires training ensembles of reward, value, and Q-models, which is computationally expensive. Experiments use idealized data from perturbed expert policies; robustness to less ideal data is not studied (Reviewer 1).
- **Minority disagreement**: Reviewer 2 (score 3) is notably more negative, emphasizing limited novelty and the theory-practice gap, while Reviewer 1 and 4 (score 6) are more positive about the method’s effectiveness. This contrast is preserved in the above weaknesses.
