## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces an undetectable watermarking scheme for generative image models using pseudorandom error-correcting codes (PRC) to modify the initial latent noise of a diffusion model. The watermark is claimed to be undetectable even to adaptive adversaries, preserves image quality, and can encode long messages (512 bits robustly, up to 2500 bits without attacks). Experiments use Stable Diffusion 2.1 and compare against several baselines. One reviewer (R1, score 8) finds the work novel with sufficient theoretical and empirical support; the other three (scores 6) have significant concerns about robustness, practical acceptability of content changes, and the strength of the undetectability claim.

### Strengths
- The paper provides the first undetectable watermark for generative image models, with a formal definition and provable false positive rate (FPR) guarantees. [R1, R3, R4]
- The method is seamlessly integrated into existing diffusion model frameworks without degrading quality metrics (FID, CLIP, Inception Score). [R2, R4]
- The authors compare against several watermarking baselines (Tree-Ring, Gaussian Shading, StegaStamp, Stable Signature) and evaluate robustness under multiple perturbations and diffusion-based removal attacks. [R1, R3, R4]
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow. [R2, R4]
- R1 notes the theoretical and empirical support is sufficient. [R1]

### Weaknesses
1. **Robustness is inferior to several existing methods under heavier perturbations.**  
   - The PRC watermark has lower TPR than StegaStamp and Gaussian Shading when perturbations are stronger (e.g., TPR drops when PSNR is still >30). The evaluation metric TPR@FPR=0.01 also implies a relatively high false positive rate. [R2, R3, R4]
   - R4 suggests improving robustness by trading off quality (e.g., longer messages). [R4]
   - R2 and R4 note that PRC is less robust than StegaStamp, Gaussian Shading, and Tree-Ring overall. [R2, R4]

2. **The watermark introduces substantial content changes compared to unwatermarked images.**  
   - The method alters the signs of initial noise vectors, leading to significant differences in generated images (Figure 1, samples show completely different content). This raises concerns about practical acceptability for companies and end-users. [R2, R3]
   - This issue is especially problematic because even small noise changes can produce large visual variations. [R2]

3. **The undetectability claim is not fully convincing.**  
   - The undetectability evaluation only uses a ResNet-18 classifier; it is unknown whether other classifiers could detect the watermark. [R3]
   - Theorem 1 gives an upper bound on probability difference that is close to 1/2, which is too loose to guarantee negligible advantage. R4 asks how this bound can be improved. [R4]
   - R2 and R3 question whether the marginal improvements in Table 1 (FID, CLIP score, Inception Score) justify the claim of being the first undetectable watermark, given that prior methods also achieve competitive metrics. A significance test is needed. [R2, R3]
   - R3 notes that 500 images may be insufficient for reliable FID computation; at least 1000 are needed (citing Stable Signature). [R3]

4. **Details missing on experimental setup and algorithm description.**  
   - Figure 2 and Figure 8 do not specify how many keys were used for the other watermarking methods; R1 asks if increasing keys would increase undetectability. [R1]
   - The description of PRC.Encode_k (Algorithms 1 and 2 in the appendix) is insufficient for understanding; more explanation is needed. [R1]
   - Section 4.5 on spoofing attacks does not specify where the corresponding experimental results appear. [R1]

5. **Concerns about the implementation and results for Stable Signature.**  
   - Stable Signature should produce images highly similar to the original, but the paper shows watermarked images that are completely different. R2 and R3 question whether the Stable Signature implementation is correct. [R2, R3]

6. **Missing discussion of transfer-based or query-based attacks on robustness.**  
   - R2 asks whether such attacks were considered, especially given the lower robustness compared to baselines. [R2]

7. **The method is not entirely new; it is similar to Tree-Ring watermark (modifying initial noise).**  
   - R2 notes that modifying initial noise is not novel, and the changes are more extensive than in Tree-Ring. [R2]

**Additional reviewer questions (converted to weaknesses where they overlap with criticisms):**  
- R1: How many keys are used for other methods in Figure 2 and Figure 8? Would increasing keys increase undetectability? [R1]  
- R2: Is the implementation of Stable Signature correct? [R2]  
- R3: Are 500 images enough for FID? (Stable Signature uses 1000.) [R3]  
- R3: The undetectability result only for ResNet-18; could other classifiers detect the watermark? [R3]  
- R4: How can Theorem 1’s loose bound be improved? [R4]
