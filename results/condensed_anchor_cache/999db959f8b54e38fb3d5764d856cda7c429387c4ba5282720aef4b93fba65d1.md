## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes GI-GS, an inverse rendering framework that combines 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) with deferred shading and efficient path tracing to compute indirect lighting for scene decomposition and relighting. Reviewers find the work well-written and the problem challenging. Scores range from 6 to 8 (accept) with varying confidence. Reviewer 1 and 3 give 8, highlighting the novelty of screen-space path tracing and deferred rendering; Reviewer 2 and 4 give 6, citing insufficient evaluation, similarity to prior work, and lack of validation for key claims. There is broad agreement that the method achieves best novel view synthesis among inverse rendering baselines, but relighting performance is subpar compared to TensoIR (Tab. 1), and several technical components (indirect-lighting calculation, visibility estimation, material decomposition) are not adequately justified or ablated.

### Strengths

1. **Novel deferred shading + path tracing for indirect illumination.** The framework enables lightweight path tracing on the G-buffer to efficiently model indirect geometry-lighting interactions, which is a promising new direction (Reviewer 1, 3, 4).  
2. **State-of-the-art novel view synthesis among inverse rendering baselines.** In NVS tasks the method outperforms all compared inverse rendering methods (Reviewer 1).  
3. **Extension to scene level with meaningful results.** The method produces reasonable decomposition and relighting for full scenes, not just objects (Reviewer 1).  
4. **Decoupling of direct and indirect illumination.** This design allows good-quality relighting under varying conditions (Reviewer 4).  
5. **Intuitive and easy to follow presentation** (Reviewers 2, 4).  
6. **Screen-space visibility query via adaptive path tracing** is a novel technical element (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

1. **Relighting performance is subpar despite claims.** Table 1 shows TensoIR outperforms GI-GS on relighting metrics, and Fig. 4 shows little visible difference from GS-IR. This undermines the core claim that the indirect illumination module enables superior relighting (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Reviewer 2 asks whether the method can demonstrate superiority given that occlusion and indirect lighting are also addressed by baselines.  
2. **Normal estimation appears inadequate.** Fig. 9 (Garden, Kitchen, Counter, Bonsai) shows poor normals, which likely degrades inverse rendering quality (Reviewer 1).  
3. **Insufficient ablation of key components.** The only ablation study (Table 3) reports NVS quality changes, not relighting or albedo estimation, making it impossible to validate the indirect illumination module’s effectiveness (Reviewer 1).  
4. **No evaluation of decomposed diffuse/specular components.** The method uses IBL for direct lighting (Sec. 4.2) but never quantitatively or qualitatively evaluates the separated diffuse and specular maps, which are crucial for the lighting model (Reviewer 4).  
5. **No analysis of material decomposition (albedo, specular, roughness).** Although the pipeline outputs these maps (central to inverse rendering), only albedo is evaluated; specular and roughness are not assessed (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 asks: why no material map analysis when benchmarked methods include accessible material evaluations?  
6. **Occlusion modeling is not rigorously validated.** The paper claims occlusion is calculated via path tracing to model shadows, but no strong comparison (quantitative or visual) against methods that also provide visibility/occlusion information (Reviewer 4).  
7. **No ground truth for indirect lighting.** The modeled interreflection and bounce lighting cannot be verified; they could be residuals of reflection optimization. No comparison with physically-based rendering simulations is provided (Reviewer 4).  
8. **Technical concerns about the path tracing and visibility implementation.**  
   - The sampling strategy (spherical coordinates, line 303-305) is suboptimal; a uniform hemisphere sample (as in NeILF) would be better (Reviewer 3).  
   - The lighting model (Eq. 7) cannot model indirect specular lighting, and the approximation makes sharp shadows impossible because visibility is integrated into direct lighting (Reviewer 3).  
   - The procedure for sampling points along the ray is not clearly explained (Reviewer 3).  
   - Visibility by comparing sampled-point depth with rendered depth is incomplete: e.g., a ray pointing in the camera direction may have sampled points deeper than the rendered depth but not be occluded (Reviewer 3).  
   - Indirect light calculation (line 338-341, Eq. 13) is confusing: outgoing radiation is indexed from an RGB map, but indirect light for a surface point may not be visible to the camera (Reviewer 3).  
9. **Similarity to InvRender.** The indirect illumination calculation is similar to InvRender’s approach: using direct illumination of 3D points to compute indirect light. The paper does not clearly distinguish its method (Reviewer 2).  
10. **Inaccurate use of I_dir in Eq. 13.** I_dir is an RGB value combining diffuse and specular, not the incident illumination from a point to another. Re-rendering with the new look-at direction would be more accurate (Reviewer 2).  
11. **No real-world relighting evaluation.** Experiments use synthetic data only; no results on real-world benchmarks (e.g., Stanford-ORB) to support claims for practical effectiveness (Reviewer 2).  
12. **Minor issues:** Adaptive path tracing (projected camera only) cannot handle occluded objects (Reviewer 2). Eq. 8 notation 𝒟_{L_{n-p}} is confusing (Reviewer 2). Tab. 1 NVS column compares only relighting methods; reconstruction baselines should be included as reference (Reviewer 2).  
13. **Shadow remains in albedo for Hotdog (Fig. 4).** Reviewer 1 asks why the method still bakes shadows into albedo despite the indirect illumination module.  
14. **Direct light component not shown.** Reviewer 1 asks what the direct lighting looks like, as incorrect direct lighting reconstruction could explain poor relighting.  
15. **No ablated validation of diffuse/specular separation.** Reviewer 4 questions how the method validates decomposed diffuse and specular components.  
16. **Insufficient evidence of novelty.** Reviewer 4 challenges that both occlusion and indirect lighting are similarly calculated in baseline methods (TensoIR, Gaussian Shader, GS-IR), and the relighting evaluation does not prove better quality.
