## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a unified formalism for informed multi-label classification in neurosymbolic AI, capturing existing techniques (semantic conditioning, semantic regularization) and proposing a new method, semantic conditioning at inference (SCI), which applies constraints only during inference. Experiments on large-scale datasets show SCI captures ~75% of the performance gain of full semantic conditioning while avoiding computational costs of maintaining the full output distribution. Reviewers find the paper well-written and the formalism clean, but the overall assessment is split: one reviewer is positive (score 5, confidence 4), while the other three are more negative (scores 3, 3, 3; confidences 2, 3, 3). The minority positive reviewer highlights the practical accuracy gains and computational efficiency. The majority view is that the contribution is small and incremental, the formalism lacks novelty, and the experiments are insufficiently rigorous.

### Strengths
- The paper is well written and provides a comprehensive, clean overview of neurosymbolic approaches for informed classification (R1, R4).
- The unified formalism clearly illustrates the differences between two important previous techniques: semantic regularization and semantic conditioning (R4).
- The proposed SCI method greatly boosts accuracy on the considered tasks while avoiding the computational costs of performing MAP inference on the full distribution (R1). SCI captures ~75% of the performance gain of full semantic conditioning, which is a useful finding for practitioners (R3, R4).
- The paper employs techniques not mainstream in machine learning, bringing exposure to alternative ideas (R2).
- The background material is extensive and could serve as an expositionary piece (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and contribution**: The unified formalism is not especially novel; it is questionable as a standalone contribution because the paper could have used propositional logic and noted that other languages can be treated similarly. The current formulation makes notation heavy and explanations more opaque (R1, R3, R4). The main claimed contribution—SCI—is essentially an ablation of the full semantic conditioning method, making only a small incremental step (R3, R4). The positive reviewer (R1) also raises a major novelty concern: other works (Niepert et al., Pogancic et al., and other combinatorial-solver approaches) already compute the MPE state at inference without maintaining the full distribution, yet these are not compared. This omission puts the technical novelty into question (R1).

- **Insufficient experimental rigor**: The experiments lack ablations; e.g., what fraction of predictions violate constraints for the baseline model? (R2). There is no benchmarking against other multi-label classification approaches beyond independent prediction across labels (R2). The only baseline is an uninformed model; at minimum, a fuzzy regularization baseline should have been included (R4). Missing concrete details on the structure of constraints in each problem and how inference is handled (suggested to compress background and move such details to the main paper) (R2).

- **Missing related work and comparisons**: The paper does not discuss probabilistic graphical models (PGMs), which are based on similar formalisms. Specific multi-label classification works that did inference in undirected graphical models are omitted (Ghamrawi & McCallum; Finley & Joachims; Meshi et al.; Petterson & Caetano) (R2). The relationship to PGMs and whether the problem setups have a corresponding PGM formulation should be directly addressed (R2). Additionally, the missing comparison to combinatorial-solver approaches (Niepert, Pogancic) is a crucial omission (R1).

- **Presentation issues**: Figure 2 needs explicit explanations of acronyms (SCI, IMC, etc.) in the caption, even if they can be guessed (R1, R3, R4). The last paragraph before Section 4.1 (on fuzzy logics) and the first paragraph of Section 4.1 feel out of place; the framework's probabilistic reasoning assumption is justified too late (R3, R4). A typo in the abstract: “theoritical” should be “theoretical” (R3, R4). The paper occupies significant space with elementary background (e.g., how to use differentiable loss for a probabilistic classifier) (R2). The complexity of the prediction problems is hard to follow; background could be compressed to provide more concrete examples of data and constraints (R2, R3).

- **Unanswered questions that overlap with criticisms**: What did the new syntax specifically contribute to understanding or results? (R3). What errors does SCI make that full SC does not? Provide examples (R3). How does the method relate to probabilistic graphical models? (R2).
