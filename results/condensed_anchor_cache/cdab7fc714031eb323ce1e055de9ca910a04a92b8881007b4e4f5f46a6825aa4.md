## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes a two-stage persona-driven data augmentation method, PersonaMath, to improve mathematical reasoning in open-source LLMs. Stage 1 (Persona Diversification) uses a closed-source LLM (GPT-4o-mini) to generate CoT solutions and rewrite questions using 200k diverse personas. Stage 2 (Reflection) rewrites solutions based on incorrect answers from Stage 1. The resulting PersonaMathQA dataset (70.3K examples) is used to train PersonaMath models based on LLaMA-2-7B, LLaMA-3.1-8B, and Qwen2.5-7B. The best results are reported on the 7B model: 24.2% on MATH and 68.7% on GSM8K, outperforming some baselines while using significantly fewer training data points (17.8% of MetaMathQA). The dataset, models, and code are open-sourced.

### Strengths

- **Well-written and clear**: The paper is well-structured and the experiments are easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **High performance with less data**: The method creates a smaller but highly effective dataset, leading to strong model performance at lower computational cost—using only 17.8% of MetaMathQA’s data (Reviewers 1, 3, 5; consistent across three reviewers).
- **Strong benchmark results**: PersonaMath-7B achieves high accuracy on MATH (24.2%) and GSM8K (68.7%), surpassing several baseline methods (Reviewer 1, 3, 4).
- **Open-source release**: The dataset, models, and code are publicly available, supporting community research (Reviewers 1, 4, 5).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty**: The method combines existing approaches (persona-driven rewriting from Persona Hub [arXiv:2406.20094] and reflection learning), with minimal technical novelty. Multiple reviewers note that both directions have been explored in prior work, and this paper does not clearly differentiate its contributions (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5; strong consensus across 4 reviewers).
- **Reflection method shallow and inconsistently beneficial**:
  - The reflection stage corrected only 10% of incorrect problems. Performance on GSM8K improved by 2.5%, but performance on MATH slightly *declined*, contradicting the claim that reflection specially helps harder problems (Reviewer 1; numeric evidence cited from Figure 4).
  - The reflection approach closely resembles [Learn Beyond The Answer, EMNLP 2024], but no comparison with that work is provided (Reviewers 3, 5; direct questions on this gap).
- **Motivation for persona-driven math augmentation is weak**: Math tasks are objective and logical, whereas persona data aims to increase subjectivity and diversity. The paper provides insufficient evidence that this diversity is beneficial for mathematical reasoning, and does not explain how persona selection/design affects performance (Reviewers 4, 5; two independent but similar criticisms). One reviewer notes that the method in [Scaling Synthetic Data…] focuses on scaling, not rewriting, so the current approach may not fully leverage personas (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient baselines and related work**: The paper omits several relevant data augmentation methods and datasets, such as Orca-Math, Xwin-Math, DART-Math [2], JiuZhang3.0 [3], OpenMathInstruct-2 [4], and RefAug from [Learn Beyond The Answer] (Reviewers 1, 4, 5; multiple mentions across three reviewers). This limits the persuasiveness of the reported improvements.
- **Performance inconsistency across base models**: While the method improves LLaMA-2-7B substantially, on stronger base models (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B) it yields *negative* results on GSM8K (see Table 3) (Reviewer 5; specific evidence). Another reviewer notes that for LLaMA-3.1-8B the official zero-shot CoT score is 51.9, suggesting the fine-tuned result may represent a performance decline (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing analysis and experiments on persona methodology**:
  - How were the 200k personas selected? No explanation of the selection criteria is given (Reviewers 1, 5).
  - No ablation or analysis of how different persona types or distributions affect math reasoning (Reviewer 5).
  - The use of Word Types as a diversity metric is questioned; the reviewer suggests that diversity of reasoning processes, not just expression, would be more meaningful (Reviewer 4; direct question).
- **Connection between stages unclear**: The logical flow from persona diversification (Stage 1) to answer reflection (Stage 2) is not well motivated or explained (Reviewer 5).
- **Lower performance than prior 7B methods**: Various existing augmentation methods achieve approximately 70% accuracy on 7B models (prior to LLaMA-3), yet PersonaMath-7B achieves 68.7% on GSM8K and 24.2% on MATH. The paper does not explain why its performance is lower than those methods (Reviewer 3; specific numbers).
