## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces multiple techniques to improve consistency training (CT): removing Exponential Moving Average (EMA) from the teacher, adopting Pseudo-Huber loss, a lognormal noise schedule, a discretization curriculum that doubles steps every N iterations, dropout, and improved time embeddings. These modifications allow consistency models to achieve FID 2.51 on CIFAR-10 and 3.25 on ImageNet 64×64 in one step, improving 3.5× and 4× over prior CT, and surpassing consistency distillation. Reviewers note strong empirical results and thorough ablations, but disagree on the theoretical justification and the significance of the contributions.

### Strengths

- **Clarity & presentation.** All reviewers agree the paper is well‑written, easy to follow, and the design choices are presented with intuitive demonstrations (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- **Strong empirical results.** The final FID scores are competitive with state‑of‑the‑art diffusion models and GANs while requiring only one or two sampling steps. The model beats prior CT and distillation on CIFAR‑10 and ImageNet 64×64 (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- **Thorough ablations.** Each modification is systematically validated. The removal of EMA is theoretically motivated and experimentally supported (Reviewers 1,2,4). The whole set of improvements is extensively ablated (Reviewers 2,4).
- **Avoids pre‑trained diffusion and learned metrics.** The paper removes reliance on LPIPS, making CT more general and not limited to image synthesis (Reviewers 3,4). This is seen as a step toward broader applicability (Reviewer 4).
- **High significance.** Consistency models are a very promising class for single‑/few‑step sampling; the improvements advance them notably (Reviewer 4). The paper sets a new benchmark for CT (Reviewer 3) and establishes consistency models as competitive generative models (Reviewers 1,2).
- **Positive minority view.** The two 8‑point reviewers (3,4) are particularly enthusiastic about the technical soundness and the potential impact, calling the improvements “compelling” and “very significant”. The two 6‑point reviewers (1,2) also acknowledge the strengths but with more reservations.

### Weaknesses

- **Theoretical justification of Eq.6–7 (Sec.3.2) is incomplete.** Reviewer 1 argues that Eq.6 holds regardless of the θ/θ⁻ relation, that the gradient analysis in Eq.7 is scaled by 1/Δσ, and that the loss does not force correct learning – the model could predict a constant and still be self‑consistent (the boundary condition is what makes it work). The notion “if the consistency loss either does not exist or is unsuitable” is not well explained (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 also remarks that the paper functions as a technical exploration and lacks sufficient theoretical backing for many practices.
- **Pseudo‑Huber loss is not entirely convincing.** Reviewer 2 notes that its effectiveness is sensitive to the δ constant, it offers negligible improvement over tuning‑free LPIPS, and the concern about LPIPS bias is overstated (the feature extractors are small and overhead is minimal). Reviewer 1 asks for insight into why Pseudo‑Huber helps (possibly bounded gradients for all t).
- **Many enhancements are engineering optimizations, not foundational advances.** Reviewer 2 states that beyond removing EMA, the changes (weighted timestep selection, adaptive loss weighting, dropout, time embeddings) resemble heuristics without strong theoretical grounding. Reviewer 3 similarly notes insufficient theoretical backing. Reviewer 1 requests clarification on why insensitive time embeddings are crucial.
- **Limited evaluation scope.** Experiments are only on CIFAR‑10 and ImageNet 64×64. Generalizability to higher‑resolution ImageNet, diverse datasets (COCO, LAION), or non‑image data (audio, graphs, 3D, video) is not shown (Reviewers 2 and 4). Text‑to‑image scalability is questioned (Reviewer 2: “applicability appears confined to unguided models”). Reviewer 4 also asks whether one can train text‑to‑image consistency models.
- **Training speed is a bottleneck.** Reviewer 2 points out that CT is comparatively slow versus GANs, and convergence speed needs to be addressed for large‑scale applications.
- **Missing comparisons and ablations.**
  - Whether the proposed improvements also boost consistency distillation (i.e., does improved CT outperform improved CD?) (Reviewer 2, Q1).
  - Potential limitation of one‑/few‑step methods by network capacity; a comparison to RectFlow is suggested (Reviewer 2, Q2).
  - The exponential discretization curriculum is not included in the visualizations/ablations of Figs. 3(b) and 3(c) (Reviewer 4).
  - No explicit definitions of s₀ and s₁ in Sec.3.4 (Reviewers 1 and 4).
  - Typo: the ground truth consistency function in Sec.3.2 should use ξ, not μ (Reviewers 1 and 4).
  - Clarify whether positional embeddings directly feed σ or EDM’s ¼log(σ) (Reviewer 1, Q3).
- **Conceptual questions.**
  - Why do higher discretization steps reduce bias but increase variance? (Reviewer 3).
  - Would consistency training be effective if the generation process is a more general corruption process (not necessarily the reverse of diffusion)? (Reviewer 3).
