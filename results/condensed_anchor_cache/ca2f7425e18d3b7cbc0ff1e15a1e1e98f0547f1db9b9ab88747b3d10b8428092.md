## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a metric (TFI) to distinguish GNN-favored from GNN-disfavored features, then introduces GFS which processes these feature sets separately with GNN and MLP. All reviewers agree the paper is well-written, clearly motivated, and provides theoretical grounding. However, one reviewer (score 5) raised stronger concerns about novelty, evaluation fairness, and applicability, while the other three (score 6) were more positive. The main criticisms center on insufficient novelty of TFI, lack of controlled comparisons, incomplete theoretical analysis, and missing experiments on synthetic/heterophilic graphs.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-organized and easy to follow, with clear motivation and intuitive figures (Figures 1, 3).
- The concept of distinguishing GNN-favored and GNN-disfavored features is interesting and novel.
- Detailed theoretical proofs (e.g., Lemma 1, Theorem 1) provide a foundation for using TFI to guide feature selection.
- Extensive experiments show consistent performance improvements when GFS is applied to 8 baseline GNN architectures across 10 datasets, with 83.75% of cases showing significant boosts.

### Weaknesses
1. **Novelty of TFI**: One reviewer (score 5) strongly argues that TFI is not novel—using mutual information to measure feature-label dependence, even on neighborhood-averaged features (AX), is an incremental extension of classical feature selection (e.g., [1]). Another reviewer notes TFI appears similar to existing mutual-information-based metrics and does not clarify how it uniquely accounts for node dependencies or local neighborhood structure in graphs.
2. **Missing comparisons**: The paper lacks comparison with classic feature selection methods (e.g., [2]) and does not compare TFI against other graph-specific metrics such as label homophily, feature homophily, or mutual information. This makes it hard to assess TFI’s relative advantage.
3. **Evaluation fairness (architecture split)**: Reviewer 2 points out that Table 1 compares GFS-augmented models that simultaneously change both the feature processing and the model architecture (e.g., number of layers, hidden dimensions), so it is unclear whether the performance gains are due to feature selection or architectural changes. A controlled comparison (exact same architecture with/without GFS) is needed.
4. **Dataset split concern**: The paper uses an unusual 50/25/25 train/val/test split. Reviewer 1 questions whether GFS remains effective under standard splits (e.g., 20 labeled nodes per class), which are common for GCN, SGC, GAT, and APPNP.
5. **Inconsistency in results**: Reviewer 2 observes that Figure 2 shows minimal performance gap between GCN and MLP on low-TFI features for the Roman dataset, yet Figure 4 shows large improvements from GFS regardless of hyperparameter \( r \). This inconsistency is not explained.
6. **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Reviewer 1 notes limited robustness to the selection of \( r \). Other reviewers raise related concerns: the method’s performance on noisy graphs, extremely sparse features, and sensitivity to feature dimension is unexplored.
7. **Incomplete theoretical analysis**: The paper does not specify the conditions under which separating features is most effective, nor does it analyze how feature correlation, local neighborhood structure, or heterophily levels (e.g., alignment with high-pass filters like FAGCN/ACMGNN) affect gains. The decomposition of node features is not clearly described, and it is ambiguous whether TFI is computed once or at each layer.
8. **Lack of synthetic data experiments**: Reviewer 4 requests experiments on synthetic graphs (e.g., SBM with controlled homophily/heterophily) to isolate the method’s behavior under different connectivity and community structures. Such experiments are absent.
9. **No statistical analysis**: No confidence intervals or significance tests are provided for the selected features or for the claimed improvements.
10. **Limited exploration**: The paper only addresses node features; applicability to edge features is not investigated. The impact of initial feature dimension is also not studied.
