## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a novel multi-agent architecture for learning a discrete communication protocol without prior task knowledge. It introduces a new referential game (MRILG, based on the Lewis Game) that adds random noise to messages and allows multiple rounds of communication before final prediction. The goal is to produce robust communication protocols that generalize across noisy and noiseless conditions. Scores range from 3 to 6, with Reviewer 4 being the most positive and Reviewers 2 and 3 more critical.

### Strengths
- The paper explores a new and timely research direction: robust communication in emergent communication, with careful design of environments and training frameworks (Reviewer 4).
- A comprehensive suite of evaluations is conducted, covering various aspects of the environment and framework design, including different tasks and evaluation metrics (Reviewer 4).
- The work adeptly merges insights from human language evolution with artificial language development using deep learning, highlighting neural agents' capabilities in autonomous communication (Reviewer 2).
- The adaptation of the Lewis Game with noise and multiple rounds (MRILG) elevates realism and provides a more challenging framework, important for investigating communication under noisy conditions (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The proposed architecture is novel for learning a communication protocol without prior knowledge, with detailed analysis of the learning strategy and comprehensive modules for processing messages and images (Reviewer 1).
- The paper addresses the creation of a common language among agents for cooperation and demonstrates the ability to produce robust communication protocols that handle cases with and without noise, with implications for emergent communication in challenging environments (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of comparison to existing approaches:** The paper does not compare the proposed architecture to existing emergent communication methods, making it difficult to assess novelty and effectiveness (Reviewer 1). In particular, multi-round Lewis Game variants exist [1,2] and need to be contrasted in terms of listener architecture and game complexity; it is unclear how this work differs (Reviewer 4). The comparative approach with Ueda & Washio 2021 is superficial and lacks in-depth analysis of fundamental differences (Reviewer 2). The use of noise and its adaptation is not new; the paper should acknowledge existing works using noise (e.g., [1,2,4]) and clearly delineate novel aspects (Reviewer 3). Similarly, the noise addition approach should be compared to previous works on noisy communication channels [4,5] (Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient empirical validation and ablation:** The architecture lacks rigorous empirical validation; the evaluation is limited to the newly developed referential game without testing on a wider range of tasks (Reviewers 1, 2). Ablation studies are missing; systematic removal or modification of components would strengthen the analysis (Reviewer 1). The contribution of the multi-round setting is not convincingly demonstrated: accuracies with/without multiple rounds (Tables 3,4,7,8) do not differ much, requiring more elaboration or experiments to ablate its advantage (Reviewer 4). The results in Table 2 are not well justified—agents trained with noise performing better in noisy testing is expected; more analysis is needed to argue significance beyond the obvious (Reviewer 3).
- **Noise handling and generalization concerns:** The paper does not adequately discuss how the architecture specifically mitigates noise in the communication channel (Reviewer 1). Training and testing in the same noisy environment is insufficient to show robust communication. Other sources of noise (e.g., on sender/receiver sides) and other interference methods (e.g., random token dropping) should be considered (Reviewer 4). Cross-noise-level generalization is not evaluated (e.g., train λ=0.75 test 0.5 and vice versa), leaving uncertainty about whether the model overfits to a specific noise level (Reviewer 4). The treatment of noise via an `unk` token and the equation (1) definition raise questions: the origin of `unk` is unclear and noise is not truly applied to the message itself (Reviewer 3 question 1). Increasing λ only increases the probability of inserting noise, not necessarily the noise level (Reviewer 3 question 3). In Tables 1 and 2, NLG (with noise) shows much higher accuracy than LG(RL) – is the existence of noise beneficial for learning? (Reviewer 3 question 4)
- **Novelty concerns:** The reliance on established paradigms (Lewis Game) raises doubts about true innovation; the modifications may simply add complexity rather than bring genuine novelty in multi-agent reinforcement learning (Reviewer 2). The use of REINFORCE for LG(RL) is not a contribution as it has been used before in emergent communication [3,4] (Reviewer 3 question 5). The emphasis on noise adaptation is hardly novel and the authors fail to differentiate their approach from existing solutions (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing analysis of learned communication:** The paper does not explore or visualize the types of messages learned by the agents, which would be interesting for understanding the emergent protocol (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear definitions and notation:** The entropy term in the loss is not defined, and its motivation is insufficiently explained; a clear justification and analysis of its impact are needed (Reviewer 3). There are inconsistencies in notation: the number of rounds is defined as both N and I (e.g., section 2.2 vs page 5); the variable x is used for both the original message sent by the speaker and the prediction made by the listener; the meaning of ˆx is ambiguous (Reviewer 3 minor). Several equations are written within text making them hard to read (Reviewer 3).
- **Gradient flow and training details:** It is unclear whether gradients flow across agents (as in DIAL [5]) or are independent (Reviewer 3 question 6). The maximum number of rounds I and its variation during training are not reported; this would help validate the multi-round design (Reviewer 4 question 1).
- **Missing references:** There are references missing on page 6 (Reviewer 4 question 2).
- **Literature review limitations:** While extensive, the literature review fails to critically analyze the limitations of referenced works, leading to potential oversights in the methodology (Reviewer 2).
