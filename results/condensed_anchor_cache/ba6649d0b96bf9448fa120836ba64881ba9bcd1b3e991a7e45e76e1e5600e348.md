## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents IntRaBench, a framework for fair evaluation of interactive segmentation methods in 3D medical imaging. It compares seven SAM-based models (2D and 3D) across eight CT and MRI datasets using point and bounding-box prompts, with automatic iterative refinement. The framework propagates 2D prompts to 3D volumes to ensure realistic human effort comparisons.

### Strengths
- Highlights and addresses disparities in evaluating 2D vs. 3D open-set foundation models, particularly the lack of reproducibility details (e.g., whether a 3-point prompt was provided iteratively or all at once).
- Fair comparison of 2D and 3D iterative segmentation models is well executed via point/box propagation from 2D to 3D volume, avoiding unrealistic slice-by-slice prompting.
- The codebase is provided, extendable, and reproducible, with data preprocessing scripts and an object-oriented design that eases integration of new models and prompting strategies.
- Demonstrates the effectiveness of bounding-box refinement over point-based refinement and the necessity of iterative refinement for accurate segmentation.
- Benchmarks seven existing SAM-based methods, providing a crucial fair comparison since similar methods claim superiority but differ in training data, architectures, and prompts.

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of real-world clinical validation**: No studies involving actual clinicians to assess prompting strategies in clinical workflows or to quantify reduction in manual segmentation time. The framework simulates clinical settings but lacks real clinical analysis.
2. **Limited dataset scale and diversity**: Experiments are conducted on relatively small datasets with only CT and MRI modalities. Missing 2D medical data (X-rays, pathology images) and large-scale datasets like AbdomenAtlas (MedIA 2024) or FLARE 24 (10,000+ volumes). Additional modalities (PET, Ultrasound) should be tested.
3. **Degrees of freedom (DoF) are an unreliable measure of human effort**: DoF does not objectively reflect difficulty. Example: a 2D box has 5 DoF, a 3D box has 6 DoF, but drawing a 3D box is substantially more than 1.2× harder. The authors should clarify their perspective on this metric.
4. **Point interpolation may be unsuitable for certain shapes**: For arch-shaped structures, interpolated points can fall outside the object of interest.
5. **Missing experiments and ablations**:
   - Performance difference between center clicks (max information) vs. random or border clicks.
   - Different scribble strategies (e.g., the Scribble-prompt paper [Wong et al. 2023]) and rationale for using different strategies for false positives vs. false negatives.
   - Performance disaggregation for healthy organs vs. pathological structures (the paper notes 85% DSC for AMOS22 vs. 71% for ULS23 but does not analyze it).
   - Only SAM-based methods considered; non-SAM-based methods should be included.
   - The comparison may be unfair because the SAM-based methods were trained on different datasets and used out-of-the-box.
6. **Insufficient evaluation metrics**: Only DSC is used; add boundary-based NSD and computation efficiency.
7. **Framework details unclear**: It appears to be a re-implementation of existing SAM-based methods under the same settings, not a new out-of-the-box segmentation tool. Does it actually address the listed pitfalls or propose a new method?
8. **Presentation issues**:
   - Figures have poor resolution (especially Figure 4, which could merge point and bbox plots).
   - Missing visual examples of scribble generation for negative prompts.
   - Table 5 is in supplementary but referenced in main text; Section 2.3 could be shortened to bring key results into main text.
   - Typos: line 40 citation format, line 45 inverted comma “SAM”, line 106 “too small”, Sec 3.3 bullet point 3, Figure 3 caption “promoting” → “prompting”, line 356 “sparsitity” → “sparsity”, line 493 and line 496 missing citation for “previous literature”.
   - Title could be more specific (e.g., “3D Medical Image Interactive Segmentation Benchmark”).
   - Codebase needs more documentation and test case coverage.
9. **Limited novelty**: Several conclusions (e.g., superiority of bounding boxes over points, gap in performance for healthy vs. pathological structures) align with prior studies (e.g., segvol, SAM for Medical Image Analysis 2023/2024). The paper lacks technical novelty and interesting takeaways; suitability for ICML is doubted.
