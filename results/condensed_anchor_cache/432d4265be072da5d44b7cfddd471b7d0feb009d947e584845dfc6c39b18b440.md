## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes TANGO, a co-speech gesture video reenactment framework combining a motion graph-based retrieval with a diffusion-based interpolation model (ACInterp) and a hierarchical audio-motion joint embedding (AuMoCLIP). TANGO addresses two limitations of prior Gesture Video Reenactment (GVR): audio-motion misalignment and visual artifacts in GAN-generated transition frames. The authors introduce a graph pruning strategy, contrastive learning for cross-modal retrieval, and a homography background offset flow to handle camera motion. Evaluations on Talkshow-Oliver and a small YouTube Business dataset show TANGO outperforms existing methods. One reviewer (score 10) was highly positive, while the other three (score 8) noted some incremental contributions but acknowledged strong results and thorough experiments.

### Strengths

*   **Novel retrieval framework that resolves GVR’s core limitation:** The use of AuMoCLIP (contrastive learning) for audio-motion alignment replaces GVR’s naive audio onset feature and keyword matching, significantly improving cross-modal alignment (R1, R2, R4).
*   **Effective graph pruning strategy** for representing long gesture sequences from video (R4).
*   **AuMoCLIP provides insightful hierarchical alignment:** It merges text semantics (BERT), high-level audio (Wav2Vec 2.0 transformer), and low-level audio (Wav2Vec 2.0 CNN) features. The ablation study on low-level vs. high-level retrieval is detailed and informative, analyzing temporal (beat-level) and semantic (context-level) alignment (R4, R1).
*   **ACInterp with homography background offset flow:** The diffusion-based interpolation generates high-quality transition frames. The homography flow explicitly handles camera parameter changes, resolving background jittering and improving appearance consistency – a deeper analysis of temporal dependencies than typical ReferenceNet modules (R4, R1).
*   **Strong experimental results:** TANGO consistently outperforms prior methods (GVR, SDT) in both quantitative metrics and user studies on visual quality and audio‑gesture synchronization (R1, R2, R3). Experiments are thorough, covering multiple evaluation protocols (R2).
*   **Introduction of a new dataset** (YouTube Business) and **open-source release** of code, pretrained models, and data, facilitating future research (R1, R3).
*   **Clear motivation and relevance** of a unified co-speech gesture modeling framework (R3). Visualization results are well-executed (R3).
*   **Potential for AuMoCLIP as an automatic evaluation metric** for gesture naturalness conditioned on speech (R4, noted as a beneficial side contribution).
*   **One reviewer (score 8) considered the overall novelty moderate:** while contrastive learning for audio-motion retrieval is not entirely new, its application in gesture video reenactment is creditworthy; other additions (graph pruning, background flow) were seen as incremental (R2). In contrast, the reviewer with score 10 found the contributions substantial (R1).

### Weaknesses

*   **Missing implementation details and reproducibility:**
    *   Graph pruning: criteria for merging smaller SCCs into the largest SCC are undefined (e.g., threshold on number of disconnected nodes or SCC size) – makes the process unreproducible (R1).
    *   Training details missing: no specification of GPUs, training time, or learning rates (R4).
    *   Unclear whether AuMoCLIP and ACInterp are trained separately on each few-shot set (R2).
    *   Unclear what “leverage AnimateAnyone” means: is it used as a pretrained initialization or only the architectural concept? (R2)
    *   Gesture motion representation: the paper should clarify that 3D SMPL/SMPL‑X parameters are tracked from the first reference image and that generated 3D joints are projected to 2D using the recovered camera for pose-guided generation (R4).

*   **Claims not sufficiently justified:**
    *   The claim that BERT captures high-dimensional language semantics for co-speech gesture retrieval lacks evidence. There is no comparison showing that gestures become more language‑semantics‑aware with BERT; the improvement might come from alignment alone. An experiment using only CTC alignment information vs. BERT, along with video examples, is needed to validate the claim (R2).

*   **Missing comparisons and evaluations:**
    *   Missing comparison with recent state‑of‑the‑art *Co-Speech Gesture Video Generation via Motion-Decoupled Diffusion Model* (S2GMM) (R2).
    *   Missing discussion of related works: Qi et al. (2024) (Weakly‑Supervised Emotion Transition Learning) and Mughal et al. (2024) (ConvoFusion) (R3).
    *   Evaluations are limited to a single character (Oliver) and a small in‑the‑wild YouTube set. Generalizability to other characters is not demonstrated; if the method does not perform well on common characters used by TALKSHOW or PATS, limitations should be discussed (R2, R4). (Note: R4 acknowledges the scarcity of high‑quality data but still flags the limited scope.)
    *   Section 4.4 uses a different test set without explanation (R2).
    *   Table 3 has missing results (R2).
    *   User study: no assessment of participant engagement (e.g., response time thresholds, control questions); authors should add such discussion (R3).

*   **Technical concerns and open questions:**
    *   Generated videos occasionally exhibit stuttering, reducing fluidity – possibly due to motion graph sampling or interpolation (R1).
    *   Figure inconsistencies: Figure 3 uses blue/green without clear color correspondence for transitions; Figure 5 reuses yellow for both Wav2Vec2 transformer and input audio; Figure 5 also shows a white segment within feature representations that is unexplained; typo “merge” should be “merging” in Figure 4 (R1, R2).
    *   Writing/formatting: abbreviations GVR is used ambiguously (the general method vs. specifically Zhou et al. 2022); (Liu et al. 2022c) is called ANGLE, not ANGIE; Table 2 has methods as columns while Table 1 has them as rows; some numeric results in tables lack explanatory captions (R2).
    *   Frame interval is only tested at 4; exploring other intervals would be informative (R3, not required in rebuttal).
    *   Discrete token retrieval from the graph may reduce quality because speech is continuous; the paper should address this non‑uniform sampling issue (R4).
    *   Only one transformer layer is used for high‑level features in AuMoCLIP; adding more layers (as in Wav2Vec 2.0’s 11 layers) might improve global retrieval performance (R4).
    *   Low‑level retrieval: random search gives 25% accuracy; include F1 score and recall for a more complete picture (R4).
    *   Why is temporal contrastive learning applied to low‑level CNN features rather than to high‑level transformer features? (R4)
    *   Homography background offset flow: clarify whether it is applied only during training for stabilization or also at inference time (R4).
