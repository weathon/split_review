## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an end-to-end method for causal structure discovery and counterfactual inference from unstructured natural language using LLMs. The method extracts causal variables, builds/merges causal graphs from multiple text sources, and performs counterfactual inference with causal graph conditioning to reduce LLM biases. Experiments on synthetic Cladder data and limited real-world news articles demonstrate the framework's applicability but also reveal limitations in LLM counterfactual reasoning. Reviewers acknowledge the importance of the problem but have mixed views: one reviewer (score 6) found the approach novel and well-defined, while three reviewers (scores 3, 3, 5) raised significant concerns about novelty, insufficient quantitative validation, failure to outperform baselines, and lack of robust evaluation beyond LLM self-assessment.

### Strengths
- Explores how LLMs can support causal reasoning tasks on text data, opening new possibilities and practical challenges (R1).
- Introduces a novel end-to-end approach for causal structure discovery and counterfactual inference directly from unstructured text (R1, R4).
- Methodology is well-defined with clear steps for building and merging causal graphs (R1).
- Experiments on synthetic (Cladder) and real data demonstrate applicability (R1). Excellent motivation and problem setup with well-chosen datasets (R2). Experiments are clearly described and include ablations (R2).
- The problem is of interest to a wide audience and the idea may inspire further research (R3).
- Comprehensive end-to-end method that extracts causal graphs, performs interventions, and predicts counterfactual scenarios (R4).
- Incorporates causal graph conditioning to help reduce biases inherent in LLMs (R4).
- Paper is well-written and experiments are supportive (R4).

### Weaknesses
- Reliance on synthetic Cladder data restricts assessment of model performance in realistic contexts; only a few real-world news examples are shown, insufficient to establish applicability to unstructured text beyond controlled scenarios (R1, R2). Need evaluation on diverse domains (R1).
- Lack of quantitative performance metrics on real-world data, such as precision/recall for causal variable extraction or fidelity of counterfactual predictions (R1).
- Issues like hallucination and prompt sensitivity are not addressed, which can impact accuracy of causal inference (R1).
- The proposed method introduces many complex parts but fails to outperform the causalCoT baseline from Jin et al. (R2). Results give no new insight because all validation is done by LLMs (R2). The paper does not convincingly demonstrate that the method works in practice; stronger results on Cladder are needed (R2).
- The contribution is less significant than claimed: the methodology essentially amounts to prompting an LLM and collecting outputs, with no novel ways of applying LLMs to this problem beyond prompt design (R3). Assumptions for equation (1) are not discussed, and variables are not clearly defined (R3). No discussion on how causal variables (noun phrases) relate to the conditional probability of output tokens (R3). Unclear why LLMs are considered good estimators for the causal effect (R3).
- Results warrant more discussion, e.g., Table 1 notes "Only extracted answers are shown"—how many extractions failed? Does this introduce systematic bias? (R3).
- Existing works exist for each component step: causal discovery with LLMs (e.g., efficient causal graph discovery, RAG-based discovery) and counterfactual inference with LLMs (e.g., counterfactual examples, simulatability) (R4). The end-to-end nature may be novel but needs more discussion on related work (R4). Novelty is not clear (R4).
- Overlap with criticism/missing experiments: The decision to ignore questions where the LLM outputs an incorrectly formatted answer is unfair; results should be reported on the entire Cladder dataset (R2). Need validation beyond LLM self-validation (R2). Causal graph validation only on synthetic datasets where ground truth is known; no validation method for real-world scenarios (R4). 
- Unanswered questions that are weaknesses: Graph generation granularity (sentence vs. paragraph) and graph merging details (how variable name mismatches, edge direction mismatches are handled) are unclear (R2). Hidden variables extracted from LLM are mentioned but their use in causal analysis is not explained; do they appear in Cladder? (R2). The relation to causal representation learning (e.g., arXiv:2402.03941) is not discussed (R2). The variables being intervened in Section 3.2 and how $P(Y_0|C)$ relates to the causal question are not elaborated (R3).

Contrast: One reviewer (score 6) was more positive about the approach's novelty and clarity, while three reviewers (scores 3, 3, 5) identified serious weaknesses in novelty, evaluation rigor, and performance compared to baselines. The minority positive view is noted but does not outweigh the consensus on insufficient validation and unclear contribution.
