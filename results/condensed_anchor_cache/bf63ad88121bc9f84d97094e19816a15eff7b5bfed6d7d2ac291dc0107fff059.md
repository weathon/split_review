## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces OpenWaves, a large-scale simulated dataset of >16 million frequency-domain wave simulations based on the Helmholtz equation, using real USCT configurations. The dataset uses anatomically realistic breast phantoms from the VICTRE project (four breast types, 8000 phantoms with 256 sources each) and provides comprehensive benchmarking of neural operator baselines for both forward propagation and inverse tomography.

### Strengths
- The dataset is large-scale (16M simulations) and based on anatomically realistic breast phantoms, requiring substantial computational and storage resources; if shown to be high-fidelity, it could save researchers significant time in data preparation.
- The paper provides a thorough evaluation across multiple neural operators, including forward simulation and inverse imaging tasks, with analysis of performance, scalability, and generalization.
- The benchmarking is comprehensive, covering different organs of interest, imaging models, and a variety of baseline models.
- The presentation is clear and easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- **Originality and novelty**: The work does not introduce new methods for dataset construction. Both the phantom generation (VICTRE) and the wave simulation algorithm (Convergent Born Series, CBS) are adopted from existing resources. As a result, the dataset could be reproduced by others with sufficient computational resources, and the primary contribution reduces to saving time for terabyte-scale regeneration. *Multiple reviewers noted this concern; Reviewer 3 in particular found the contribution insufficient (score 3).*
- **Validation with real clinical data**: The dataset is purely simulated (both ground-truth phantoms and measured wave fields). No justification is provided that the phantom simulation is accurate or representative of real breast tissue distributions. Missing experiments comparing training on this dataset vs. training on real data, or evaluating on real clinical data, are needed to demonstrate the dataset's value. *Reviewers 2 and 4 emphasized that medical applications require evidence from real data; Reviewer 1 also asked for comparison to a smaller real-world dataset.*
- **Lack of quality control and realism**: The VICTRE-based phantoms inherently lack the complexities of real tissue. No external validation (clinical expert review) or quantitative metrics (e.g., comparison to statistical distributions of real breast tissue properties) are provided to assess realism. Parameter selection (breast size, nipple position, tissue density, internal compartmentalization) is not discussed, and there is no literature support for the specific breast-type distribution (1000:3000:2000:2000).
- **Insufficient discussion of differences from existing datasets**: The paper notes that other datasets use different imaging models but does not discuss differences in scale, imaging applications, computational complexity, or specific applicability compared to OpenWaves.
- **Mathematical formulation lacks rigor (Section 3.1)**: Variables are not fully defined – e.g., the symbol ∇ in Eq. 1, partial derivatives in Eq. 2, the spatial domain and function space of u(x), and the meaning of radial distance r in 2D simulations. Boundary conditions are stated but not explained in terms of numerical implementation, hindering reproducibility.
- **Scaling and efficiency analysis is unclear**: Section 4.3.2 describes variations in neural operator performance with dataset size, but the underlying rationale, convergence rates, and generalization are not analyzed. The influence of design choices (breast phantom distribution, frequency range) on the observed scaling behavior is not clarified.
- **Misleading representation of dataset size**: The paper frequently cites “16M frequency-domain wave simulations.” However, with 8000 phantoms and 256 sources each, the independent reconstruction examples are 8000; describing it as 16M may be misleading. A more accurate metric for researchers would be 8000 (or 8000×8 frequencies).
- **Missing benchmark/validation criteria**: It is unclear what evaluation criteria for a simulated dataset track exist at ICLR, leading to concern about whether the dataset meets the venue's requirements (especially given the absence of real data).
