## Merged Review

### Summary
This work proposes a nonparametric mean-covariance regression model for high-dimensional neural data (LFP and spike counts) with restricted covariates. It combines a lower-dimensional latent factor model (factor loadings and latent factors both predictor-dependent via Gaussian processes) with graph Laplacian (GL) kernels to handle covariates lying in restricted subdomains. Inference uses an MCMC algorithm with data augmentation for count data. The method is validated on simulations and applied to two real neuroscience datasets (local field potential from 13 areas, hippocampal spiking from 36 neurons). The authors claim the framework is general and can be extended beyond neuroscience.

### Strengths
- The problem of handling restricted input spaces arising from experimental conditions (e.g., non‑Euclidean, subdomain constraints) is well‑motivated and highly relevant for neuroscience applications.  
- The use of graph‑Laplacian Gaussian processes to model smoothness over such restricted covariates is a smart extension of existing work, adding a novel dimension to mean‑covariance modeling while maintaining interpretability.  
- The MCMC algorithm with data augmentation (for non‑conjugate count likelihoods) is a principled approach that handles intractable models effectively.  
- The model components and math are clearly explained individually, and the simulations, parameter selection, and results on experimental datasets are presented with a high level of detail, supporting reproducibility.  
- Experiments include two real‑world neuroscience tasks (continuous LFP and counting data), demonstrating applicability beyond synthetic settings.  
- The paper is sound overall and the method appears to work reasonably well.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty**: The core ideas are largely a direct integration of two prior works—Fox & Dunson (2015) (predictor‑dependent factor loadings via GPs) and Dunson et al. (2021) (graph‑Laplacian GP regression for restricted domains). No significant theoretical or methodological advancement beyond combining these components is demonstrated; the MCMC sampling scheme similarly relies on existing techniques. Multiple reviewers noted that the contribution is closer to an adaptation than a novel prior or method.
- **Unsubstantiated “massive neural data” claims**: The title and text emphasize “massive” data, yet no definition is given, and the datasets used (36 neurons, 200 ms bins for HC; LFPs from 13 areas without clear dimensionality) do not appear massive. Computational complexity is not analyzed (e.g., scaling with number of conditions, neurons, recording length), empirical run‑times are not reported, and sample complexity is not discussed theoretically or experimentally. The discussion on lines 467–470 itself mentions even larger future applications, underscoring the lack of present evidence.
- **Underwhelming empirical results**:  
  - In simulated data, all model fits look extremely similar; the explained‑variance differences between the proposed method and baselines (especially L‑GP) are marginal, and it is unclear whether such small improvements would be meaningful in high‑variability neuroscience contexts.  
  - The GPWP baseline (Nejatbakhsh et al., 2023) shows a dramatically different score in the Gaussian simulation, raising concerns about its implementation or experimental setup.  
  - Figures 1, 4, and 5 show little to no improvement over competitors; only Figure 6 advocates for the method. A comparison with a vanilla (non‑GL) GP approach is missing, which would help isolate the benefit of the graph‑Laplacian component.  
  - The L‑GP baseline often matches the proposed method closely, questioning the practical advantage given added model complexity.  
- **Writing and presentation clarity**: The paper is somewhat hard to follow; the scientific question and mathematical task are not clearly stated upfront. The methods section lacks a consistent motivational thread connecting components to the overall picture. Grammar issues (e.g., lines 82–84) and confusing figure references (e.g., “Figure 4” actually in the supplement) detract from readability.  
- **Assumption of independent GPs for factor dimensions**: The model assumes independent GPs for each factor dimension (for computational efficiency), which may overlook inherent dependencies between factors that could be relevant in many neuroscience applications.  
- **Code and reproducibility concerns**: The provided code is complex (many files/functions), lacks documentation, and is written in MATLAB—limiting accessibility in the ML community and likely affecting computational efficiency for MCMC. The choice of custom implementation rather than established packages is not justified.  
- **Missing analyses and ablations**:  
  - The simulation section does not systematically vary key dimensions (number of neurons, time series length, number of latent factors) to assess robustness.  
  - Hyperparameter sensitivity of the GL‑GP (ε, K, t) is mentioned but not quantitatively demonstrated (line 222).  
  - The method’s scalability to higher latent factor dimensions or to tasks with more freely‑moving behaviors is not addressed.  
  - No investigation of how well the method handles test/ new data that extend outside the range of previously observed covariates (extrapolation).  
  - The choice of a single‑trial GPWP baseline is not justified, and no comparison with other alternatives (e.g., a standard GP without graph Laplacian) is provided.  
- **Minor issues**: Typos (“massive neurons” line 71, “identifiablility” line 196, extra ‘)’ line 66); notation p and N introduced late (line 81).
