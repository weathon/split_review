## Merged Review

### Summary
Based on three reviews, the paper uses neural collapse theory to analyze differentially private (DP) fine-tuning of the last layer. It claims dimension-independent error bounds under perfect neural collapse, shows that perturbations and class imbalance reintroduce dimension dependence, and proposes normalization/PCA to mitigate. Reviewers find the topic timely and the presentation technically precise, but raise significant concerns about the practical relevance, simplicity of the setting (one-step NoisyGD, perfect neural collapse, zero initialization), disconnect from NoisySGD, lack of empirical validation on real datasets, and limited technical contribution.

### Strengths
- The phenomenon explored (empirical success of DP deep fine-tuning in high dimensions) is very interesting and timely. (R1)
- The results are presented with a high degree of technical precision and fluency. (R1)
- The result of Theorem 2 seems surprising and interesting (R1).
- The paper provides first theoretical understanding of DP fine-tuning reducing error on downstream tasks; the neural collapse setting is interesting for future research. (R2)
- The presentation is good. (R3)

### Weaknesses
1. **Difficult for readers unfamiliar with neural collapse:** The introduction and Figure 1 may be meaningless without more explanation of concepts like the K-simplex equiangular tight frame (ETF) and its relation to neural collapse. A high-level intuitive explanation with a simple example is needed. (R1)

2. **Typos and missing definitions:**
   - Bottom of page 7, \Sigma is missing a backslash. (R1, also noted as question by R1)
   - In Theorem 2, it is unclear whether the bound refers to $\gamma$ accuracy or $1-\gamma$ accuracy. (R2)
   - The paper mentions NoisySGD in the introduction but never defines it; the theoretical analysis uses one-step NoisyGD, creating a disconnect. (R3)

3. **Theoretical setting is too simple and of limited relevance:**
   - All proofs analyze one-step NoisyGD under a very strong perfect neural collapse assumption. This does not reflect the iterative nature of practical DP training (NoisySGD). (R2, R3)
   - If perfect neural collapse holds, then no further training is needed; one could set the clipping threshold G very small to get near-zero error (Theorem 2). The bound may not be useful. (R2)
   - The zero initialization ($f_0(M_k)=0$) is trivial and unrealistic. (R3)
   - The fact that $M_k$ form an ETF is well-known and not novel. (R3)
   - The practical relevance of the idealized layer-peeled model is questionable for explaining the success of full fine-tuning in De et al. (2022). (R3)
   - The analysis is limited to last-layer fine-tuning and does not address intermediate layers; it is unclear how these results shed light on full fine-tuning dynamics. (R1)

4. **Insufficient empirical validation:**
   - The proposed tricks (feature normalization, PCA dimension reduction) are not demonstrated empirically on real datasets. (R2)
   - It is unclear whether applying DP-PCA would introduce its own privacy-utility tradeoff. (R2, as question)
   - The benefit of dimension reduction (Section 4.2) is not clearly articulated: Theorem 5 requires $\beta_0$ to scale with $p$, but the non-robustness results in Sections 3.2 and 3.3 would also become dimension-independent if $\beta$ scaled similarly. It is not clear if the benefit is merely reducing perturbation magnitude or something more fundamental. (R1)

5. **Limited technical contribution:** The proofs are simple, and the technical contribution is limited. (R2, R3)

6. **Missing baseline comparison:** The model only considers $f_W(x)=Wx$; what about including a bias term, e.g., $f_W(x)=Wx+b$? (R3, as question requesting ablation/comparison)
