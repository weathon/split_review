## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes a phase-aware training schedule for flow-based generative models, introducing a time dilation mechanism to address the loss of high-level feature learning in high-dimensional settings. The authors analyze a two-layer autoencoder parameterizing a velocity field for a two-mode Gaussian mixture, derive explicit formulas for the phase transition between learning high-level (asymmetry between modes) and low-level (within-mode distribution) features, and demonstrate the approach on MNIST. Reviewers acknowledge theoretical contributions and the relevance of the problem but raise major concerns about generalizability, missing comparisons, insufficient experiments, and incomplete referencing.

### Strengths

- **In-depth mathematical background and explicit formulas**: The paper provides detailed derivation of the phase transition for the bimodal Gaussian example, including the time dilation formula (Eq. 10) that identifies the change point between phases (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Interesting and principled idea**: The concept of phase-aware training and time dilation is novel and well-motivated by the non-stationarity of the generative process (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Relevance to a timely problem**: The paper addresses an important issue in flow-based and diffusion models—critical time windows and decision points—that is connected to spontaneous symmetry breaking in statistical physics (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Potentially valuable theoretical contribution**: If better supported, the theoretical insights could guide future research on noise scheduling (Reviewer 3).
- **Minority positive view (Reviewer 4, score 5)**: One reviewer considers this *“one of the first papers to deal with this important problem”* and praises the principled approach, rigorous theoretical analysis, and promising results on MNIST. They find the paper well-written and the time dilation idea well-principled.

### Weaknesses

- **Missing important references**: The paper fails to cite and discuss prior work on spontaneous symmetry breaking in generative diffusion models, specifically Raya (2023), Ambrogioni (2023), and Li (2024), which first characterized symmetry-breaking phases and are directly translatable to the stochastic interpolant setting used here (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Lack of generalizability**: The analysis is limited to a two-mode Gaussian mixture. For data with more than two modes (e.g., three clusters with means at (−1,…,−1), (0,…,0), and (100,…,100)), it is unclear whether only two phases exist or multiple cluster splittings occur. The time dilation formula (Eq. 10) can only calibrate a single symmetry-breaking point, making it inapplicable to multiple decision points. The paper does not clarify whether the two-phase claim is general or specific to bimodal mixtures (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Weak experimental evaluation**: 
  - Only MNIST is used; more diverse datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10, CelebA, ImageNet) and other class divisions are needed (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
  - No comparison with state-of-the-art methods or other noise scheduling techniques (e.g., from the diffusion model literature) (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
  - The two provided graphs are an ablation study, not a comparison (Reviewer 1).
  - No visual comparison of MNIST samples (Reviewer 1, see Questions).
  - Lacks quantitative and qualitative comparisons overall (Reviewer 1).
- **Poor presentation and readability**: 
  - The paper is hard to follow, with grammatically incorrect sentences (e.g., the sentence beginning “Sample complexity for Gaussian Mixtures Cui et al. (2024) study…”) (Reviewer 1).
  - No clear figure showing the network architecture or the proposed method (Reviewers 1, question 4).
  - Unclear why phase transitions are not already evident from the mollification process described by the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck SDE, especially given the annealed Langevin literature (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient experimental support for claims**: The theoretical derivations are not accompanied by sufficient experiments or proofs of their practical value. The paper is almost purely theoretical without the expected level of validation for a top conference (Reviewer 1).
