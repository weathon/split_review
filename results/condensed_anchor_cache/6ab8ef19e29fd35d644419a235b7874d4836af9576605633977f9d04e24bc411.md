## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an alternative to Chain-of-Thought (CoT) for LLM planning, using Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) to generate and evaluate plans. A plan is initially sampled, then iteratively mutated to create new plans, each evaluated by an LLM for logical consistency and feasibility. UCB1 guides tree expansion. After the budget is exhausted, the highest-reward plan is used to prompt the LLM to produce a solution. Experiments use small Llama and Qwen models, with ablations on search depth, rollouts, and evaluation functions. A study uses smaller models for planning and larger models for execution. The claimed headline improvement over zero-shot CoT is 40.59%. Reviewers found the idea relatively novel but raised major concerns about method clarity, misrepresentation as MCTS, missing baselines, unsupported main result, lack of cost analysis, and insufficient reproducibility.

### Strengths
- The framework separates planning from reasoning, which could yield new insights (Reviewer 1).
- The inclusion of a study using smaller LLMs to provide plans for larger ones is welcome and addresses computational cost concerns (Reviewer 2).
- The paper is generally well organized (Reviewer 2) and easy to follow (Reviewer 3), though Reviewer 1 found Section 2.1 unclear and gave a low presentation score (1/4). The absence of critical details (noted by Reviewers 2 and 3) contrasts with the overall structure.
- Code release is a strength; the code appears clean and easy to use (Reviewer 2).
- The idea of using MCTS (or search) in the planning stage is relatively novel compared to directly applying MCTS in the reasoning stage (Reviewer 3).
- The algorithm is tested on a diverse combination of models (Llama, Qwen) and datasets (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of clarity and errors in method formulation:** Section 2.1 is very hard to follow. Notation is inconsistent: \(X\) denotes “reasoning steps” in two places (lines 140, 159) yet “problem” in another (line 151). Equation 2’s factorization only holds if \(P(X|C)=1\), which cannot be true if planning involves search over \(X\); this appears to be a typo (Reviewer 2). The distinction between planning and reasoning is not sufficiently articulated; their specific roles and interaction remain unclear (Reviewer 1).
- **Method is not MCTS:** The algorithm mutates plans and evaluates each node individually (no Monte Carlo rollouts). It outputs a single node, not a path. Therefore the proposed method is an evolutionary method, not MCTS as claimed (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient implementation details:** Initial plan sampling, mutation process, evaluator prompts, and all evaluator details are missing (Reviewer 2). Reproducibility is unclear; no prompts are provided for expansion or evaluation (Reviewer 3). Specific questions left unanswered: default parameters for Tables 1 and 3, how Q is initialized (e.g., is it 0?), and what prompt is used for expansion (Reviewer 3).
- **Main result (40.59% improvement) unsupported:** Inspection of Table 1 shows the largest relative improvement is 30.8% (17.79/57.85). The reported 40.59% cannot be found in any table (Reviewer 2). Additionally, no uncertainty measures are reported, making it impossible to assess statistical significance (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient baselines and comparisons:** Only compared against CoT and CoT Plan, not against methods cited in section 4.2 or popular reasoning approaches such as ReAct and Reflexion (Reviewer 2, 3). No comparison with prompt-engineering methods that also use MCTS or gradient descent (Reviewer 3). No normalization by inference cost (number of tokens) makes comparison of efficiency impossible (Reviewer 2). No baseline of best-of-N (BoN) sampling with a reward model, which would be a natural cheaper alternative to MCTS for increasing cost (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing cost analysis:** Despite frequent mentions of cost, no results show actual computational cost. The motivation for using a smaller LLM for planning is unsupported without cost-savings data (Reviewer 3). There is no comparison with directly using MCTS in the reasoning stage under the same cost constraint (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing failure analysis:** No discussion of failing cases. For example, on the Object Tracking dataset, the proposed method is no better than CoT across all models, yet no explanation is provided (Reviewer 3). The impact of intermediate node evaluation on MCTS performance is not discussed, leaving an incomplete understanding of the method’s effectiveness (Reviewer 1).
- **Tables hard to parse:** No bolding of the best result per benchmark, no aggregation across models, and no uncertainty. Table 2 would be better presented as plots to show trends (Reviewer 2).
- **Existing literature:** Applying MCTS (or search) to planning is already well-established; the paper would benefit from a more thorough comparison with existing methodologies to highlight its contributions (Reviewer 1).
