## Merged Review

### Summary
GeoDiffusion converts geometric conditions (bounding boxes, camera views) into text prompts for pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models (Stable Diffusion) to generate detection data. It discretizes continuous coordinates into location bin tokens inserted into the text encoder vocabulary, uses a prompt template “An image of {view} camera with {boxes}”, and applies an area re-weighting mask to balance foreground/background and small objects. The framework is claimed to be flexible, faster (4× training time), and to outperform previous layout-to-image methods in fidelity, trainability, and universality, directly benefiting object detector training.

### Strengths
- The paper is well written, easy to follow, with clear motivation (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- Experiments are extensive and cover a wide variety of tasks; ablation studies (including parameter choices) are thorough (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- The method demonstrates that layout-to-image generation can effectively improve object detector performance, especially for low-data categories; this is considered a practical and useful contribution (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Reviewer 1 (score 8) is particularly positive about this aspect and the overall results.
- Clear evaluation metrics (fidelity, trainability, universality) are used, and the method surpasses previous GAN-based approaches by a large margin (Reviewer 2).
- The approach is simple and intuitive to understand (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Missing recent diffusion-based baselines** – Comparisons in Tables 2, 3, 5, 6 omit key diffusion layout-to-image methods such as ControlNet, GLIGEN, and ReCo. The claimed improvements (e.g., +21.85 FID, +27.1 mAP) are against old GAN methods and may be misleading (Reviewers 2, 4). Reviewer 4 specifically recommends replacing Stable Diffusion with ControlNet/GLIGEN in Figure 5 for fair layout-based inpainting comparison.
2. **Unclear source of advantage over recent baselines** – It is not explained why GeoDiffusion outperforms GLIGEN (or ControlNet) in detection trainability, especially for low-data categories (Reviewers 1, 3). What is the key ingredient that makes a simple pipeline work better than more complicated designs? (Reviewer 1)
3. **Lack of evaluation on long-tail and domain shift datasets** – No experiments on long-tail detection (LVIS) or domain adaptation (GTAV→Cityscapes, Cityscapes→Foggy Cityscapes) are provided, where synthetic data would be most impactful (Reviewer 2). Whether the model can generate rare scenes (night, fog, rain) or rare classes is also not shown (Reviewer 2, Q2).
4. **Generalization and transferability concerns** – The method is sensitive to image size and aspect ratio (number of bins changes per dataset), limiting cross-dataset transfer. In contrast, Copy-and-Paste synthesis can generate objects more flexibly (Reviewer 2). Additionally, it is unclear whether location bins trained at one resolution can generate at another (e.g., 256×256 trained→512×512 grid at test time; Reviewer 2, Q4). Evaluation on additional autonomous-driving datasets (e.g., Waymo) is missing (Reviewer 2, Q1).
5. **Camera-view conditioning novelty and illustration** – The view-dependent conditioning prompt is very similar to DreamFusion’s view-dependent prompting, and no ablation is provided to demonstrate novelty (Reviewer 2). There is only one qualitative example in Figure 1 showing camera views; more illustrations are needed to clearly show the effect (Reviewer 4).
6. **Missing discussion of inconsistent performance** – In Table 2, FID is better at 512 resolution but not at 256; in Table 3, mAP for Ped and Cone is slightly lower. In Table 4, ControlNet achieves a very high AP50, far above GeoDiffusion, which is not explained (Reviewers 1, 4).
7. **Insufficient data efficiency analysis** – The authors do not ablate how much real data (and box annotations) is required to train GeoDiffusion. If the amount is much larger than what is needed to train a detector alone, the application becomes less realistic (Reviewer 2).
8. **Claimed “flexible geometric conditions” are limited** – Only location tokens and 6 view directions are supported; depth, exact 3D locations, and angles are not addressed, making the claim of flexibility questionable (Reviewer 3).
9. **Contribution clarity** – Among the two main contributions (encoding layouts into prompts and area re-weighting), it is unclear which is more important (Reviewer 2).
10. **Paper format and minor issues** – The paper exceeds the 9‑page ICLR limit, violating submission guidelines (Reviewer 3). There are minor typos: “catpion” should be “caption” in Figure 2(b) (Reviewer 1); “embarrassing simple” should be “embarrassingly simple” (Reviewer 3). Ablation tables from the supplementary (Tables 9, 10) should be moved to the main paper (Reviewer 2).
11. **4× training time measurement unclear** – How the 4× acceleration is measured is not described (Reviewer 1, Q2).
