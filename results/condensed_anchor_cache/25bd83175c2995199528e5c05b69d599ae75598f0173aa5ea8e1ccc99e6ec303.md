## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to a multi-objective setting (MODPO) without reinforcement learning. MODPO trains language models to represent collective reward models that linearly combine multiple objectives (e.g., helpfulness, harmlessness) with specific weightings, using a cross-entropy loss. The authors claim that LMs optimized with MODPO are analytically the exact solutions of the original MORLHF objective. Experiments on safety alignment and long-form QA show MODPO matches or outperforms MORLHF and other baselines while requiring 3× less computational resources.

### Strengths
- **Clear motivation and presentation**: The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and addresses the important problem of aligning LLMs with diverse human preferences without unstable RL fine-tuning (R2, R4). Clear notations and detailed implementation/ablation examples in the appendix add value for the broader community (R2).
- **Technically sound**: The paper provides a formal problem definition, clear methodology, and mathematical proofs that MODPO optimizes the desired multi-objective objective (R3, R1). Experiments across two tasks show comprehensive results and controlled KL experiments demonstrate convincing impact (R2, R4).
- **Novel adaptation of DPO**: The integration of linear scalarization into preference modeling to create a reward-free multi-objective algorithm is creative and extends the standard DPO pipeline in a principled way (R1, R3). This is among the early efforts in the area (R4).
- **Efficiency advantage**: MODPO efficiently produces a Pareto-optimal set of customized LMs, avoiding the instability and resource cost of MORLHF, thereby enhancing accessibility (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited contribution**: The method is a relatively direct extension of DPO, and the contribution is seen as incremental (R1, R4). MODPO reintroduces reward modeling, which undercuts a key advantage of DPO (R4). One reviewer was highly critical (score 1) of the overall impact (R2).
- **Unfair comparison with DPO variants**: In experiments, MODPO uses both reward labels and pairwise comparison data, while DPO variants (e.g., DPO LW) use only pairwise comparison data. This difference in data usage makes the comparison problematic and may inflate MODPO’s performance (R4). If rewards were learned from pairwise data, it is unclear whether MODPO would still outperform DPO LW (R4).
- **Limited scale and scope of experiments**: Experiments only test two objectives (safety and helpfulness), which may undersimplify real-world diversity (R1, R3). No experiments with three or more objectives are performed, nor on real-world datasets like TripAdvisor that naturally have multiple feedback aspects (R1). Testing on larger models and more tasks is needed to strengthen claims of general efficiency and customization (R3).
- **Missing ablation and analysis**: The paper does not isolate the benefits of multi-stage training (R3). The claim about “reliable customizations” is contradicted by data points in Figure 3 that lie far from the Pareto frontier, which is a shortcoming that requires explanation (R4).
- **Concerns about conditions and dataset requirements**: The method’s success may depend on specific data collection and reward quality, which are not fully explored (R2). The potential for “too safe” responses and how to steer generation away from trivial safe outputs is not discussed (R3). Scaling limitations when increasing the number of objectives beyond two are not addressed (R3). The idea of online use on unlabeled data is mentioned but not elaborated (R3).
