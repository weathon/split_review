## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FedNewton, a second-order (Newton-type) federated learning method that communicates both first-order (gradient) and second-order (Hessian) information across clients to handle heterogeneous (non-IID) data. The analysis is conducted in the Kernel Ridge Regression (KRR) setting, where the authors derive convergence and generalization bounds, achieving minimax-optimal rates and quantifying the impact of local sample size, data heterogeneity, and model heterogeneity. Experiments on synthetic and LIBSVM/MNIST datasets show faster convergence and slight accuracy improvements compared to FedAvg, FedProx, and FeDKRR. Reviewer opinions diverge: one reviewer (score 6) considered the paper very well written with strong theoretical foundations and a well-motivated problem, while the other two reviewers (scores 5 and 3) noted significant limitations in applicability, experimental breadth, and baseline comparisons. All three reviewers acknowledged the solid theoretical analysis but raised concerns about the narrow scope and missing comparisons with existing Newton-type FL methods.

### Strengths
- **Well-motivated problem:** Addresses the inefficiency of first-order FL methods (e.g., FedAvg, FedProx) under heterogeneous data by leveraging second-order information.
- **Solid theoretical analysis:** Provides rigorous convergence and generalization bounds for the KRR setting, including minimax-optimal learning rates, and analytically decomposes federated error into covariate shift and concept shift components.
- **Originality in sharing both orders:** Introduces a Newton-type approach that shares first- and second-order knowledge across heterogeneous data, which is novel for the KRR framework.
- **Clarity:** Notations, assumptions, and theoretical results are clearly defined and well stated (noted by Reviewer 2). The paper is well written and easy to follow (noted by Reviewer 3).
- **Results on homogeneous and heterogeneous distributions:** The theory quantifies how convergence rate depends on data distribution (noted by Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited applicability to simple models:** The analysis and algorithm are restricted to Kernel Ridge Regression (KRR) and do not generalize to other models or problems (e.g., deep neural networks). Multiple reviewers questioned whether the method can be applied to CNN or neural network-based models, and the paper does not address the challenges of using Hessians in deep learning (high computational cost, non-convexity).
- **Computational and practical infeasibility:** The algorithm requires computing the full local gradient and Hessian, full-batch computation on each client, and full client participation. This is computationally expensive for large-scale optimization and impractical in typical FL settings where clients are resource-constrained, partially available, or use mini-batches.
- **Insufficient baselines and missing SOTA comparisons:** The experiments compare only with FedAvg, FedProx, and FeDKRR, which are first-order or older methods. The authors do not compare with existing Newton-type FL methods such as FedNew (Elgabli et al., 2022), SHED (Dal Fabbro et al., 2024), FedNS (Li et al., 2024), FedDANE, FedNEW, or FedNL. Including such comparisons is necessary to verify novelty and superiority.
- **Marginal performance improvement and missing ablation:** The reported accuracy gains are slight (e.g., on MNIST, SOTA methods like FedDyn achieve 98% accuracy, significantly higher than FedNewton’s results). The paper does not provide ablation studies or additional analysis to show that improvements are due to the methodological contribution rather than hyperparameter tuning.
- **Limited experiments and datasets:** Experiments are restricted to synthetic data, LIBSVM, and MNIST. Other standard FL benchmarks such as LEAF (e.g., FEMNIST, Shakespeare, etc.) are not considered. There is also no empirical evidence for robustness under model heterogeneity (clients using different model architectures), despite theoretical claims about model heterogeneity.
- **Unclear formulation regarding closed-form solutions:** The paper states that Distributed KRR (DKRR) has a closed-form solution (lines 091-093), raising the question of why a Newton method is needed for a problem that can be solved directly (Reviewer 3). The authors do not clarify this apparent contradiction.
- **Lack of discussion on limitations compared to first-order methods:** The paper does not identify cases where first-order methods might outperform FedNewton or discuss the limitations of the proposed approach (e.g., when second-order information is not beneficial or when Hessian approximations degrade performance).
- **Poor organization (noted by one reviewer):** The paper should provide clearer references to the appendix and a summary of experimental results in the main contribution section.
- **Missing thorough literature review on Newton-type FL:** The paper does not sufficiently differentiate contributions from recent second-order FL methods; a thorough literature review is needed to clarify novelty.
