- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 5, 5, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary

The authors introduce Genie 2, an extension of the Genie protein diffusion model that adds motif scaffolding capabilities, including multi-motif design where relative positions of motifs are unspecified. Architectural innovations (triangular multiplicative updates) and training on AlphaFold database (AFDB) clusters are used. The model is benchmarked against several state-of-the-art methods (RFDiffusion, FrameFlow, Chroma, Proteus) on unconditional generation, single-motif scaffolding, and a small set of multi-motif problems. All four reviewers rated the paper 5 (moderate) with confidences 5, 4, 3, 3. Reviewers agree that the work is clearly presented and addresses an important problem, but raise concerns about overclaimed performance, missing baselines and ablations, limited multi-motif evaluation, incremental novelty, and insufficient analysis of key design choices.

### Strengths

1. **Unconditional generation performance on short proteins.** Genie 2 outperforms Genie and other methods when generating short proteins (Reviewer 1).
2. **Multi-motif scaffolding is a novel extension.** The paper introduces a framework for designing proteins with multiple co-occurring motifs where inter-motif positions/orientations are unspecified (Reviewer 1, 3, 4). The representation using distance matrices to specify intra-motif but not inter-motif distances is clever and flexible (Reviewer 3, 4).
3. **Clear explanation of motif conditioning.** The incorporation of motif information as node and pair embeddings is simple, SE(3)-invariant, and empirically effective (Reviewer 2, 4).
4. **Comprehensive evaluation against strong baselines.** Comparisons with RFDiffusion, FrameFlow, Chroma, and Proteus are clearly presented and fairly thorough for unconditional and single-motif generation (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
5. **Data innovation with AFDB.** Augmenting training with AlphaFold database clusters expands the structural space beyond experimentally determined structures (Reviewer 3).
6. **Well-structured manuscript.** The paper is easy to follow with sufficient background and clear presentation of methods and results (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

1. **Overstatement of performance.** The Abstract claims state-of-the-art on all key metrics, but Figure 3B shows Genie 2 performs significantly worse than Proteus on long proteins (Reviewer 1, with high confidence). This overclaim is noted by multiple reviewers.
2. **Missing baselines in benchmarking.**  
   - Unconditional generation: Recent methods MultiFlow, CarbonNovo, FoldFlow2 are not included (Reviewer 1).  
   - Single-motif scaffolding: Chroma and FoldFlow2 should have been included; Chroma is used for unconditional but omitted here—clarification needed (Reviewer 1).  
   - Motif scaffolding: The Twisted Diffusion Sampler, mentioned in related work, is not benchmarked (Reviewer 4).  
   - Multi-motif scaffolding: No baseline comparisons (e.g., RFDiffusion with random sampling of relative positions) are provided despite the small benchmark (Reviewer 3).  
   - Missing comparison to Genie 1: No direct comparison of Genie 2 to the original Genie architecture trained on the PDB, which would isolate contributions of architectural, data, and training changes (Reviewer 2).  
3. **Small and insufficient multi-motif benchmark.** Only 6 multi-motif problems are used, too few for statistical significance, and the set lacks diversity (Reviewer 3). Analysis of failure modes (e.g., single-motif design error vs. relative positioning error) and dependence on number of motifs is missing (Reviewer 1). The benchmark includes only proteins <200 residues; longer cases are unexplored (Reviewer 4).
4. **Incremental methodological contributions.** Most modifications focus on adapting input features; triangular multiplicative updates have been used in prior works (Proteus, CarbonNovo, FoldFlow2) (Reviewer 1). Training on AFDB and SE(3)-invariant node/pair encoding are established approaches (Reviewer 4). The novelty of the approach is moderate.
5. **Insufficient analysis of key design choices and ablations.**  
   - The effect of conditional training ratio on performance is not thoroughly explored (Reviewer 4).  
   - The contribution of AFDB augmentation vs. architectural changes to improved diversity is not disentangled (Reviewer 4).  
   - Low-temperature sampling is claimed to “yield better results” (line 323) without specifying which metrics (designability, diversity?) and without providing the supporting analysis (Reviewer 2, 4).  
   - Different temperatures (γ) are used for unconditional and scaffolding tasks but default values and sensitivity analysis are missing (Reviewer 4).  
6. **Low sampling efficiency.** Genie 2 requires significantly more inference steps than previous methods (Reviewer 1, high confidence).
7. **Training length limitation.** The model is trained only on sequences up to 256 residues; implications for generating longer proteins and capturing long-range dependencies are not explored (Reviewer 3).
8. **Analysis of designability on AFDB structures is unconvincing.** The switch from scRMSD to scTM is not fully justified; the full distribution of metrics should be shown. The potential bias of ProteinMPNN toward PDB could be tested with ESM-IF1 (Reviewer 2).
9. **Secondary structure distribution analysis is problematic.** Comparing to AFDB is questionable because not all baselines were trained on AFDB and AFDB may differ from real PDB distributions (Reviewer 4). The claim that Chroma, Proteus, and RFDiffusion underrepresent beta strands and loops is contradicted by the data shown (Reviewer 4, Reviewer 2’s similar concern).
10. **Missing discussion of evaluation oracle bias.** ESMFold is used as structure prediction oracle; shortcomings and potential biases should be acknowledged (Reviewer 2).
11. **Functional design claims lack validation.** The work asserts that scaffolding motifs enables functional protein design, but many functional proteins involve conformational changes or inter-domain coordination not captured by simple motif scaffolding. No experimental validation is provided (Reviewer 2).
12. **Some claims are vague or insufficiently supported.**  
    - “Better captures protein structure space” is not directly evaluated; only higher diversity is shown, but sample size (1,035 structures) is small (Reviewer 4).  
    - Equation (5) claims motifs are “enforced as a soft constraint,” but the loss does not explicitly distinguish motifs from scaffold—clarification needed (Reviewer 4).  
    - The selection of earlier checkpoints (line 806, 872) for evaluation is not explained (Reviewer 2).  
13. **Multi-chain generation with motif scaffolding is not supported.** Despite accepting chain index as input, the model cannot handle multi-chain motif design (Reviewer 4).
