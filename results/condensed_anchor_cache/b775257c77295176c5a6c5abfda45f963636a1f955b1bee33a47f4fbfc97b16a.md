## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies Single-source Domain Generalization (sDG) under large style shifts, critiquing conventional augmentation-and-align methods by analyzing the data generating process and distinguishing domain invariance from augmentation invariance. The authors propose PROF (Progressive mutual information Regularization for Online distillation of Frozen oracles) – a feature-level mutual information regularization using a pretrained oracle – and MDAR, a multi-domain alignment baseline. They aim to stabilize training and reduce mid-train OOD fluctuations.

### Strengths
- **Relevance and problem framing:** The paper tackles an essential, underexplored research problem of single-source DG for style domain shifts (R1, R4).  
- **Insightful analysis of augmentation-based methods:** The deep dive into the limitations of alignment-based sDG, especially the distinction between domain invariance and augmentation invariance, provides valuable insights (R2, R3). The discussions about augmentation quality and the effects of alignment are insightful (R2).  
- **Organization (minority view):** The paper is well-organized overall, and the related work and background sections are comprehensive and complete (R2). Reviewer 4 also notes the overall organization is relatively clear, though Section 4 is problematic (R4).  
- **Empirical improvements on benchmarks:** PROF yields performance gains on several benchmark datasets over prior augmentation-based methods (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and presentation issues:**  
  - Section 4 (method description) is not reader-friendly: loss terms are introduced as a sum before each component is explained; \( \mathcal{L}_{\text{cls}} \) is introduced in Equation (2) but clarified only later in Section 4.3. The symbol “D” represents different concepts in Sections 3 and 4 (R4).  
  - The paper is overall difficult to follow; the large number of components (ensembled generators, multiple augmentation schemes, distillation, oracle regularizer, multi-domain alignment loss) is not clearly motivated and it is unclear which parts are novel and why they work together (R1).  
  - Some presentations lack precision: the abstract claims “analyzing the data generating process” but no explicit explanation is given in the main paper (R2). Section 3 contains incomplete phrases, and a clearer explanation of why less severe mid-train fluctuation implies better domain-invariant features is needed (R2).  

- **Limited novelty / incremental contribution:**  
  - The core idea of using a pretrained oracle to minimize mutual information is not new; it is already proposed in MIRO [1] (R1, R3).  
  - The proposed network components and corresponding losses appear redundant, and the necessity of each component is not justified (R2). The technical advancement over prior work [1] is incremental (R3).  

- **Weak and mixed experimental results:**  
  - Results are mixed: in Table 2, “Ours” is not better than MetaCNN on any column, yet it is bolded incorrectly in multiple places (R1). Table 3 shows high variability and Table 4 has no baseline comparisons (R1).  
  - The paper lacks explanation of failure modes (e.g., training: Cartoon → target: Sketch: 0.70 → 3.91), and more study is needed to understand when PROF becomes unstable (R1).  
  - PROF performs well only under large domain shifts, which limits the application and contribution of the method (R2).  
  - Performance on more real-world style-shift datasets like FMoW-WILDS and Camelyon-WILDS is unknown (R4).  

- **Lack of theoretical analysis:**  
  - The paper provides only a verbal critique of sDG methods but no rigorous theoretical grounding, e.g., no generalization bounds or formal analysis to explain why PROF works (R3).  
  - There is no formal/computational definition of “mid-train fluctuations” (R2).  

- **Incomplete references to related works and baselines:**  
  - Several regularization-based DG methods are not discussed or compared: ReLIC [1], AugMix [2], CoRE [3], MatchDG [4] (R4).  

- **Missing comprehensive ablation study:**  
  - The loss function (Equation 2) contains multiple terms with sub-components, but no thorough ablation validates each component’s contribution or proves the combination is optimal. Specific missing ablations include:  
    - Performance when removing the last two terms in Equation (2) and using only generator outputs plus original examples with ERM.  
    - How the oracle model alone performs on DG and whether the improvement is due to domain leakage (the oracle was trained on a larger, potentially style-diverse dataset).  
    - The role of the domain discriminator \( D \), the projection head \( P \), and the Barlow Twins term; how simple feature/logit/probability matching would compare.  
    - Impact of using a different number of augmented views in Equation (5).  
    - Rationale for the generator design and performance with an alternative generator (R4).  

- **Lack of statistical rigor:**  
  - When measuring mid-train fluctuations, it is unclear whether results are the average of multiple runs or a single experiment, and no statistical significance is reported (R2).  

- **Uncertainty about MDAR baseline:**  
  - The benefit of the proposed MDAR over existing augment-and-align methods is not clarified (R2).
