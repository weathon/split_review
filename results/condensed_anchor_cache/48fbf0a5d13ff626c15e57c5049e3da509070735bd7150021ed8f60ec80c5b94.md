## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DP-CATE, a framework for estimating Conditional Average Treatment Effects (CATE) from observational data under differential privacy. It provides two approaches: one for answering a finite set of queries (pointwise CATE estimates) and one for releasing the complete CATE function. The finite-query method uses influence functions to bound smooth sensitivity and calibrates Gaussian noise. The functional-release method uses a calibrated Gaussian process leveraging RKHS regression. Both are designed to be doubly robust and work with any Neyman-orthogonal loss in two-stage meta-learners. Experiments on synthetic data and real medical datasets (MIMIC-III, TCGA) demonstrate the framework’s potential.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written and well-organized, making complex concepts easy to follow (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- It addresses an important and practical problem: ensuring privacy in treatment effect estimation from sensitive medical data (Reviewer 2,4).
- The DP-CATE framework is highly flexible and model-agnostic, compatible with any doubly robust meta-learner and machine learning model (Reviewer 1,3,4).
- The authors provide rigorous mathematical analysis and theoretical privacy guarantees while preserving double robustness (Reviewer 2,3,4).
- The separation between finite-query and functional-query settings allows stronger estimators in the fixed-query case (Reviewer 1).
- Experimental results on synthetic and real data validate the framework and show promise (Reviewer 1,3).
- The discussion linking doubly robust learners to smooth sensitivity (line 314) offers an interesting insight with broader implications (Reviewer 3).
- Reviewer 2 (score 8) found the problem novel and of practical interest, and noted the paper is technically sound and statistically rigorous; other reviewers (scores 6) were more cautious about novelty and some technical aspects (contrast is preserved).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing baseline comparisons**: The paper provides no comparisons to simple baselines. In 1D, one could non-parametrically estimate CATE via interval counts (COUNT(Y=1, a ≤ X ≤ b) etc.); in higher dimensions, histograms or synthetic data methods (e.g., PrivBayes) preserving marginal distributions could serve as baselines. Such comparisons would strengthen the results (Reviewer 2).
- **Unrealistic assumption for Lipschitz constant**: The functional-release algorithm assumes knowledge of the Lipschitz constant, which seems unrealistic in practice (Reviewer 1). The authors did not explain how this constant was computed for empirical results (Reviewer 1’s question).
- **Lack of consistency analysis**: The paper does not sufficiently analyze whether the proposed DP-CATE estimators remain consistent (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear identification condition**: The presentation of identification condition (3) is unclear; the assumptions (e.g., unconfoundedness) under which the optimizer of (3) represents the true CATE function should be explicitly stated (Reviewer 4).
- **Bound tightness for smooth sensitivity**: Theorem 1 builds on Avella-Medina (2021) to bound ζ-smooth sensitivity, but it is not discussed whether this bound is tight or if a more optimal bound exists (Reviewer 1,4).
- **Insufficient experimental details**: Many experimental details are sparse. For instance, how many queries were used for Dataset 1 (Figures 3,4)? What does the y-axis represent in Figures 6 and 7—CATE value or error? (Reviewer 1’s questions). The real-data experiments (Figures 6,7) show CATE appearing nearly constant along age/covariate for each treatment; a more compelling demonstration would include a dataset where CATE varies with the covariate to test whether DP-CATE captures that variation (Reviewer 1).
- **Underplayed prior work on functional DP**: The paper should more explicitly highlight prior work on functional data privacy, particularly Hall et al. (2013), to provide better context for the functional-release method (Reviewer 3).
- **Ambiguous use of “robustness”**: The term “robustness” is used but could be misleading—it is unclear whether it refers to robustness to model misspecification, outliers, or other perturbations. The authors should clarify the specific meaning of double robustness and whether that is the only type considered (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing definitions**: Certain definitions are unclear or missing. For example, the definition of Y(·) used on line 160 appears missing, causing confusion in derivations. Definitions from line 157 could be recalled for clarity (Reviewer 3).
- **Jargon may be unfamiliar**: Terms like “doubly robust” are not explained for readers unfamiliar with causal inference (Reviewer 2).
- **Inclusivity of the two methods**: The paper does not comment on the advantages or disadvantages of using the functional-release method to then answer finite queries—could the two approaches be combined? (Reviewer 4’s question).
