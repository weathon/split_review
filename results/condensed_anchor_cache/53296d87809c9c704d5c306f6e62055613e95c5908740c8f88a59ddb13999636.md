## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes SoftCLT, a soft contrastive learning strategy for time series that replaces hard positive/negative assignments with continuous weights (0 to 1) for both instance-wise and temporal contrastive losses. Instance-wise weights are defined by distance in data space (DTW-based), temporal weights by timestamp differences. SoftCLT is plug-and-play and claims state-of-the-art on classification, semi-supervised learning, transfer learning, and anomaly detection. The idea is straightforward, the paper is well-written with clear figures, and experiments are extensive covering many tasks, ablations (e.g., on temperature, distance metric, weight function), and hyperparameter analysis. Reviewers note the work is a natural extension of standard contrastive learning and benefits from a thorough empirical evaluation.

### Strengths

- **Clarity and presentation**: Well-written, easy-to-follow structure, intuitive figures (Fig.1, 2). Related work section is extensive and structured (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Novelty of soft assignments**: The idea is simple, natural, and straightforward to implement. It generalizes binary assignments and can be adapted to other contrastive learning frameworks (R1, R2, R3).
- **Extensive experiments**: Covers multiple downstream tasks (classification, anomaly detection, semi-supervised, supervised, transfer learning) and scenarios (self-supervised, semi-supervised, supervised). Comparison with soft-CL techniques from other domains is included (R1, R3, R4).
- **Ablation studies**: Ablations on hyperparameters (temperature, distance metric, weight function, trade-off λ) demonstrate the usefulness of proposed components (R1, R4).
- **Representation analysis**: Goes beyond task performance to investigate robustness to non-stationarity and semi-supervised learning (R1).
- **Complexity discussion**: Authors acknowledge additional computational cost of DTW (quadratic complexity) and suggest FastDTW as alternative (R4).
- **Amount of experiments**: Very large and diverse, well described both in paper and supplementary (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Missing comparisons and baselines**: No comparison with recent contrastive approaches such as CoST (R1), nor with earlier works that use a trade-off between instance-wise and temporal CL (Yeche et al., 2021, NCL) which SoftCLT extends from discrete to continuous neighborhoods. Also missing comparisons with Cheng et al. (2020) and Kiyasseh et al. (2021) (R4). One reviewer (R1) considers this a major concern because without detailed comparison to CoST and evaluation in the regression setting (TS2Vec code supports it) the contribution value is hard to assess; they initially do not recommend acceptance but are willing to raise score if addressed. Another reviewer (score 8, R4) still finds contribution significant despite the missing references, calling them easily addressable.
- **DTW computational bottleneck**: Using DTW for instance-wise weights is costly for long series; FastDTW increases memory and introduces approximation error, especially for highly nonlinear warping paths (R3). The scaling and saturation of sigmoid on distance can make weights non-representative (R3).
- **Assumption of temporal proximity**: Weighting neighbors heavier assumes temporal closeness implies correlation, which may not hold (e.g., distribution shifts, non-stationarity, seasonality). This is not evaluated. Work by Tonekaboni (2021) illustrates counterexamples (R3). Reviewers also question handling of non-stationarity where the next timestamp may start a different distribution, and how the method distinguishes shifts in pattern within the same time series (R2).
- **Robustness to noise, seasonality, non-stationarity**: No explicit mechanism or empirical evaluation for these real-world challenges. The paper dismisses perfect seasonality as rare, but even imperfect seasonality should be considered (R2).
- **Method clarity and terminology**: The terms “instance-wise” and “temporal” are used before being formally defined (R4). The parameter τ is called “temperature” but it acts as a lengthscale in a Laplacian-like kernel, which is misleading in the contrastive learning context (R4).
- **Choice of assignment function**: The sigmoid-based weight function is defined on R+ but tweaked to be symmetric. A Laplacian kernel (e-D/l) is more standard and avoids saturation issues (R4). Additional exploration of kernel shapes (e.g., generalized Gaussian with shape parameter β) and the trade-off α are not performed (R4). R3 notes that scaling dependence of data-space distances combined with sigmoid saturation can make weights uninformative.
- **Questions raised by reviewers** that overlap with criticisms or missing experiments:
    - How does the method handle patterns/shifts or different distributions within the same time series (R2)?
    - How is augmentation performed for instance pairs (I, i+N) in Eq. 3 (R2)?
    - How is non-stationarity managed when immediate next points start a new distribution (R2)?
    - Does the assumption of temporal proximity hold in real-world scenarios (R2, R3)?
    - Could the method be compared to NCL (Yeche et al., 2021) and evaluated in the regression setting (R1, R4)?
    - What is the impact of the shape parameter β in a generalized Gaussian kernel (R4)?

**Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 8, R4) found the contribution significant and recommended acceptance, noting that the missing prior works are easily addressable. The other three reviewers (score 6) had more reservations, with one (R1) citing insufficient comparisons as a critical weakness and another (R2) concerned about limited handling of real-world complexities.
