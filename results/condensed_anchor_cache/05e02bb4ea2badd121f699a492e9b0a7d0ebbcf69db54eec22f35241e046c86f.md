## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a framework for designing variance schedules in score-based diffusion models by treating the schedule’s inverse as a cumulative distribution function (CDF) and its derivative as a probability density function (PDF) of a “rationale” (unnormalized density). This probabilistic reinterpretation allows engineering schedules that can differ between training and sampling, and accommodates rationales without closed-form inverse CDFs via numerical approximation. Empirical evaluations on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-32 show that schedules derived from rationales such as squared L2-norm can improve FID and IS, and that separating training and sampling schedules can be beneficial. However, the novelty of many theoretical components (e.g., loss weighting equivalence, use of different schedules at train vs. sample time) is limited as they have been used previously; the central “rationale” concept is described unclearly and amounts to a straightforward reparameterization. The empirical results often lack statistical significance, and the examined schedules (VE, L2-Norm, Gaussian) include only one from prior literature, limiting the comparison. One reviewer (rating 3) is notably more critical on novelty, clarity, and significance, while the other two (both rating 5) find the work innovative and clear.

### Strengths
- Introduction of the “rationale” concept offers a new, unified way to understand and design variance schedules, allowing better control over diffusion process behavior.
- Framework leads to noticeable improvements in image generation (e.g., enhanced FID) across CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-32.
- Comprehensive empirical evaluation provides valuable insights into the impact of different variance schedules on model convergence and sample quality.
- Provides empirical evidence that separating training and sampling noise schedules can be useful and can substantively alter performance.
- Reasonably large effects on sampling performance are shown as a function of noise schedule, reconfirming its importance.
- Demonstrates that rationales without closed-form sigma(t) can still be used, albeit with additional computational expense.
- Framework offers a unified perspective that clarifies the relationship between variance schedules and probabilistic rationales, expanding the design space for diffusion models.
- Empirically validated on ImageNet-32, extending beyond CIFAR-10.

### Weaknesses
- The central concept of the “rationale” (unnormalized density) is introduced and described in an unclear fashion; the resulting computations follow straightforwardly from the 1-1 invertible relationship between sigma(t) and time.
- Many theoretical contributions have been used previously: e.g., ability to use different noise schedules at sampling and training, loss written entirely in terms of SNR sampling (or variance sampling without mean scaling), and loss weighting equivalence to sampling from a different distribution. The paper could be improved by clarifying what is truly novel.
- The proposed framework introduces complexity, requiring detailed understanding of probability distributions (CDFs, PDFs) and variance schedules, which may hinder practical adoption. Selecting or engineering appropriate rationales in real-world applications is challenging, especially without closed-form solutions.
- Reliance on numerical approximations for the inverse CDF introduces potential inaccuracies, particularly in regions where the PDF changes rapidly, possibly leading to instability or suboptimal performance.
- Training with the squared L2-norm rationale showed overfitting on smaller datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10), suggesting sensitivity to dataset size and complexity and limiting general applicability.
- Statistical significance of results is often unclear. In Table 1 and Table 2, differences between variance schedules may not be significant given the displayed error bars. For example, in Table 1, the bolded VE training + L2 sampling has overlapping ± error with other entries. In Figure 2(b), the 3rd plot shows extremely small differences among V2, squared L2 sampling, and normal sampling, and their superiority order changes with optimization step.
- Insufficient guidance on choosing the most effective rationale for specific tasks or datasets.
- A sensitivity analysis showing how different parameter values affect performance or convergence would provide actionable insights for tuning.
- The variance schedules examined (VE, L2-Norm, Gaussian) include only one from prior literature (VE), limiting the significance of the comparison. The introduced L2-Norm and Gaussian rationales receive relatively little motivation.
- Minor typos: line 52 “r/weighs/weights”; line 132 “defintion”; line 478 “1×, 2×, 2×, and 2× the base feature-size” – unclear if intended.
- Reviewer 3 is markedly less positive on novelty, clarity, and statistical rigor compared to the other two reviewers; this disagreement is noted.
