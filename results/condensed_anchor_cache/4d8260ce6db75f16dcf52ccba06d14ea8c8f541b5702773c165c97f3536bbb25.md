## Merged Review

### Summary
SocioDojo is an open-ended lifelong learning environment that emulates societal development using real-world texts and time series data (news, social media, reports, books, journals, encyclopedias, search interfaces). It includes 30K high-quality time series from finance, economics, politics, and polls. The paper defines the hyperportfolio task: agents are given initial cash and must invest in assets corresponding to time series, aiming to maximize total assets over a period. A novel Analyst-Assistant-Actuator (AAA) agent architecture is proposed, augmented with Hypothesis & Proof (H&P) prompting. In standard and tracking settings, AAA achieves 32.4% and 30.4% improvements over prior methods (Self-Ask, Plan & Solve, AutoGPT). Ablations show the importance of domain-specific analysis techniques, high-quality information sources, and the analyst component. Commission fees and a day-trading prohibition (5-day minimum hold) are incorporated. The environment sets a high bar for LLM-based agents.

### Strengths
- SocioDojo integrates a rich, continuous stream of real-world information (news, social media, reports) and a comprehensive knowledge base (books, journals, encyclopedias, search interfaces), enabling agents to grapple with real societal complexity and promote general intelligence.
- The hyperportfolio task is novel and interesting, providing a potentially automatic metric for evaluating an agent’s foresight and strategic thinking by treating time series as investable assets. It is carefully designed with commission fees and a day-trading ban that encourages long-term trend understanding.
- The proposed AAA architecture (Analyst, Assistant, Actuator) with H&P prompting outperforms strong baselines (Self-Ask, Plan & Solve) by 32.4%–30.4%. Ablation studies confirm the critical role of the analyst component and high-quality sources.
- The paper is well-written with sufficient details on data sources, work process, and ablations; easy to follow.
- The system’s complexity challenges the community to develop agents capable of sustained real-world reasoning.

### Weaknesses
- The POMDP definition in Section 3 appears disconnected; the notation is never used elsewhere in the paper.
- Evaluation relies solely on the final portfolio return as a metric, which is hard to interpret when multiple time series are fused. Missing risk-adjusted return considerations (e.g., standard deviation). The distribution of returns over time and across different periods is not shown.
- The numerical return of the hyperportfolio is not interpretable in absolute terms; it is unclear how the agent’s decisions compare to modern portfolio theory metrics (risk-adjusted return).
- For fundamental time series like GDP, existing forecasts (e.g., from reports) may be directly copied by the agent; it is unclear whether the evaluation actually tests the ability to reason from diverse information sources or simply replicates official forecasts.
- Financial time series show the highest returns despite being the noisiest and least predictable. This result requires an interpretation that is currently missing.
- The use of GPT-3.5-Turbo with non-zero temperature (0.2) introduces randomness; the paper lacks sensitivity analyses or multiple runs to assess variance and robustness of conclusions.
- The prohibition of day trading (5-day minimum hold) may be overly strict, preventing the agent from reacting to significant market changes. A comparison with less restrictive rules is needed.
- In the tracking setting, there is no direct comparison with actual market benchmarks (e.g., S&P 500) or with a passive index-following strategy; the practical value relative to standard investing remains unclear.
- The environment’s complexity may hinder adaptation by other researchers. It would benefit from introducing real-world classic scenarios or short-term benchmarks to allow phased evaluation, as well as establishing a clear lower bound (e.g., using state-of-the-art time-series forecasting methods as a baseline).
- More discussion is needed on how bias, source screening, and data diversity were handled; potential data leakage between the knowledge base and streaming messages should be addressed.
- Typos: Section/table/figure numbers are inconsistent (e.g., Equation 3.1.2, Table 3.2.1, Figure 3.2.1 point to incorrect locations; Table 1 in Knowledge Base should be Table 2).
- When multiple news articles arrive on the same day, the order of news may affect decisions; the agent’s permutation invariance is not studied.
