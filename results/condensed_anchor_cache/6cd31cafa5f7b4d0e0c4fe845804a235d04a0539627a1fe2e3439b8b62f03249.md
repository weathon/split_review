## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper frames consistency model (CM) training as value estimation in an MDP and identifies training variance and discretization error as bottlenecks. It proposes Stable Consistency Tuning (SCT) by incorporating variance reduction via STF, smooth timestep schedules (from ECT), multistep training with edge‑skipping, and guidance from a worse version of the model. On CIFAR‑10 and ImageNet‑64, SCT achieves strong FID scores (e.g., 1‑step 2.42, 2‑step 1.55 on ImageNet‑64). Reviewers note the empirical gains but consider the overall novelty limited because most techniques are adapted from prior works (SFT, ECT, MCM, PCM, “Bad Version” guidance). The MDP framework is seen as disconnected from Section 4’s contributions and unnecessary for identifying the well‑known bottlenecks. While one reviewer (score 5, Rev1) finds the methods technically correct and the experiments solid, another (score 1, Rev4) strongly criticizes the paper for unclear writing, lack of finalization, and major flaws.

### Strengths
- **Edge‑skipping inference (Sec. 4.4)** for multistep CMs, explained through L‑Lipschitz continuity, is interesting and improves sampling FID (Rev2, Rev3).
- The idea of **leveraging a lower‑variance noise estimator (STF) for consistency training** tackles a well‑identified problem, and the ablation suggests valuable results (Rev4).
- Viewing CM training as an **MDP provides an interesting perspective** (Rev3).
- The methods are **technically correct and easy to follow**; the experiments are solid and show **promising empirical improvements** on CIFAR‑10 and ImageNet‑64 (Rev1).
- The paper explores **several techniques from recent diffusion progress** and combines them effectively (Rev1).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty is limited**: most components are drawn from prior works with little or no modification: variance reduction from SFT/STF (Sec. 4.1), discretization error reduction (smooth weighting, reduced time spacing) from ECT (Sec. 4.2), guidance from “Guiding a Diffusion Model with a Bad Version of Itself” (Sec. 4.4), and multistep training from MCM/PCM (Rev1, Rev2, Rev3, Rev4).
- **MDP framework (Sec. 3) is disconnected** from the proposed methods in Sec. 4. The insights on training variance and discretization errors are already well‑known from prior work (Song et al. 2023 Theorem 2 for discretization; Song et al. 2023 Sec. 5 and Song & Dhariwal 2023 Sec. 3.4 for variance; also Dou et al., Xu et al., Pooladian et al.) and do not require the MDP view (Rev1, Rev4). The MDP development is unclear: Eq. (8) motivates a value function and reward, but the reward is never optimized; the “n‑step TD” link (line 198) is insufficiently explained; performance bounds mentioned in the introduction are never shown (Rev4).
- **Experiments focus only on consistency tuning** (initialized from a pretrained DM) and **do not demonstrate the advantage of variance reduction (STF) in consistency training** (training from scratch). Since the tuning initialization already reduces variance, such evidence is needed (Rev2, Rev3). Application of STF to conditional generation is straightforward and **not applicable to non‑categorical conditioning** (Rev3, Rev4).
- **Missing comparisons**:
  - Edge‑skipping should be compared with **aDDIM from MCM** (Rev3).
  - Multistep results should be compared with **MCM/PCM** to demonstrate advantages (Rev2).
- **Lack of clarity and missing details**:
  - **What is the relationship between the MDP and the methods in Sec. 4?** (Rev1)
  - **What is the most significant part for “stable” training?** The complexity of combined techniques makes it hard to isolate crucial components (Rev1).
  - **Additional computation cost (GFLOPs) of SCT vs. ECT** not provided (Rev2, question).
  - **Guidance application**: Figure 6 uses EMA weights at 25%/50%/75% of iterations – does the guiding technique apply **only from that pivot iteration?** (Rev2, question)
  - **Background on MCM** is missing, making the improved multistep part hard to read (Rev3).
  - **CIFAR‑10 experiments**: it is not specified whether they are conditional or unconditional (Rev4).
  - **Dataset not specified for Figure 3** (Rev4).
  - **Figures**: suspected lack of grayscale readability; Figure 4 should not connect points; Figure 5 is not referenced in the corresponding paragraph (Rev4).
- **Writing and formatting issues** hinder readability:
  - **Mathematical errors**: line 81 \(t, r\) undefined; line 114 expectation should be over \(x_0\) not \(x_0|x_t\); line 136 \(t_\lambda\) notation and \(\epsilon(x_{t_\lambda}, t)\) wrong; Eq. (7) \(\alpha_s\) should be \(\alpha_r\) (Rev4).
  - **Typos**: “weighg” (l. 149), “it is noting” (l. 154), “depent” (l. 188), “eough” (l. 196), “konwn” (l. 202), “gourd” (l. 218), “LIPIPS” (l. 413, 415), “a previous papers” (l. 523) (Rev4). Also lines 217–218: “ground” misspelled (Rev3).
  - **Duplicated references** (Song et al. 2020, 2023) (Rev4).
  - **Tables 2 and 3 captions almost overlap**; lines 523–526 font size too small (Rev4).
  - **Code not released**, hampering reproducibility (Rev4).
