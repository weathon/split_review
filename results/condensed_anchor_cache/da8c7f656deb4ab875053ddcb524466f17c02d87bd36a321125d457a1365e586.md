## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a clustering approach to improve the interpretability of Gaussian process (GP) posteriors. It assumes parameters within each cluster are identical, reducing the number of posterior parameters. The clustering is formulated as a mixed-integer quadratic programming (MIQP) problem with a weighted squared error objective based on the variational-inference-approximated posterior mean. Graph partitioning and decision tree learning are incorporated via linear inequality constraints. Experiments on small/toy datasets compare against K-means and CART. All reviewers note the idea is interesting and the MIQP formulation is novel and clever, but they raise substantial concerns about focus, interpretability justification, experimental scope, and computational feasibility. One reviewer (R2) gave a higher rating (5) and found the MIQP formulations for graph partitioning and decision tree learning particularly novel, while the other three rated the paper 3.

### Strengths
- The MIQP formulation in equations (5)-(6) is clever and offers an alternative to iterative clustering algorithms like K-means. (R1, R4)
- The MIQP formulations for graph partitioning and decision tree learning are novel. (R2)
- The idea of combining clustering with Gaussian process regression is interesting. (R3)
- The paper is well-constructed with high writing quality; the problem formulation and related work are clearly explained. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of focus and clarity.** The paper attempts to cover loosely connected topics (graph partitioning, decision trees) beyond the core GP regression theme, making it hard to follow (R1). The main goal and contribution are unclear: the abstract claims improved interpretability of GP posteriors, but the experiments evaluate only prediction accuracy and ignore interpretability (R3). The initial problem definition does not specify which aspect of GP interpretability is addressed (e.g., high-dimensional data, complex kernels, non-stationary models) (R4). Several reviewers also found sections difficult to read (R1: presentation confusing; R3: introduction reads like a stack of facts; R4: ambiguity in problem definition).
- **Insufficient and unconvincing experiments.** All experiments use only small, toy-like datasets; there is no evaluation on real-world benchmark datasets of higher dimensionality or larger sample size (R1, R2, R3). The clustering results are compared only with K-means, while many more spatial clustering methods exist (R3). The decision tree results are compared only with CART (R3). Model accuracy in Table 1 is measured using the MIQP loss function, which the proposed method minimizes by design—making the comparison potentially unfair (R2). The K-means application in Figure 5 is unclear; it appears to be simply clustering spatial locations (R1). Details for Table 1 are missing: how were the number of inducing points \(m\) chosen? How was CART trained? (R2). No computational analysis or runtime comparison is provided; the feasibility on larger datasets is not discussed (R2). The paper fails to demonstrate practical utility or significance (R2).
- **Interpretability claims not justified.** Grouping regression coefficients does not automatically improve interpretability of GP regression, where the estimated function/surface matters more than coefficients (R1). There is a risk of over-smoothing if the number of clusters is small; no simulation studies investigate this (R1). The paper does not show how the surrogate model is more interpretable than the original GP (R3, R4). Since parameter estimation happens during training and is not addressed, the approach cannot alter parameters to enhance interpretability (R4).
- **Lack of analysis of MIQP advantages over alternatives.** The paper claims MIQP overcomes local optima of iterative clustering, but provides no convergence analysis or extensive simulation studies to support this (R1). The theoretical or empirical advantage over K-means is not demonstrated (R1).
- **Conceptual and methodological concerns.** The definition of "parameters" in the GP setup is never clarified, leading to confusion (R3). The approach approximates the GP posterior mean (not the full posterior or the predictive distribution), using an already-approximated (sparse) posterior; the rationale for this choice is not explained (R2). Clustering is performed after training on test points, yet in practice new points arrive gradually—how can partitioning be done under such conditions? (R4). The combined cost of GP regression and MIQP (combinatorial and nonlinear) makes the method computationally intensive; it is unclear whether it can be practically applied even if it improves interpretability (R4).
- **Scalability and ensemble methods.** The paper does not discuss scalability of the decision tree formulation to ensemble methods like random forests or boosting (R1, implied).
- **Missing ablation and further comparisons.** No ablation studies, no comparison with alternative approximation methods (computational cost), no evaluation of over-smoothing (R1, R2, R3). Reviewer questions about convergence, over-smoothing, K-means details, Table 1 details, and scalability remain unanswered.
