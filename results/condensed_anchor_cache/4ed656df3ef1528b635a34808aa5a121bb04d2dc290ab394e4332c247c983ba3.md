## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes BiSAM, a reformulation of Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) as a bilevel optimization problem. BiSAM replaces the original min-max of cross-entropy with min-max surrogates for the 0‑1 loss: an upper bound for minimization and a lower bound for maximization. Reviewers generally acknowledge the novelty and theoretical motivation of the approach, but raise major concerns about the magnitude and statistical significance of the empirical improvements, insufficient theoretical comparison with the original SAM, and specific technical questions about the method’s behavior. One reviewer is highly positive about the soundness, presentation, and contribution, while the other three find the improvements marginal and the theoretical justification lacking.

### Strengths
- The idea of directly aiming to solve the min‑max of the 0‑1 loss and accordingly minimizing/maximizing different surrogates is novel (Reviewer 2) and insightful (Reviewer 4). The motivation of using different bounds for the min and max parts to handle the discrete 0‑1 loss leads naturally to a bilevel formulation (Reviewer 4).
- The approach is simple, scalable, and theoretically sound (Reviewer 1). It resolves the issue of optimizing the 0‑1 loss with gradients (Reviewer 3).
- The authors provide a theoretically justified lower bound for practical implementation and a clear discussion of surrogate choices (Reviewer 2).
- Numerical results show consistent improvements over SAM across many learning scenarios: standard learning, fine‑tuning, and noisy‑data learning (Reviewer 1). Experiments are conducted on multiple datasets and models (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- BiSAM can be easily embedded into SAM variants such as ASAM and ESAM (Reviewer 4).
- The presentation is clear and easy to follow (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited and statistically uncertain improvements**: The improvement of BiSAM over SAM is minor and often within the range of standard deviation, making it hard to conclude statistical significance or a genuine enhancement (Reviewer 2, 3, 4). In some comparisons, the original SAM (Foret et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2022) achieves higher accuracy than BiSAM in this paper with the same model and epochs (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 states that the performance gain is “only marginally better” and within the error range, so “it is hard to believe that it is an enhancement of SAM.”
- **Insufficient theoretical comparison with the original SAM**: The paper lacks a theoretical analysis directly comparing the generalization performance of BiSAM and the original SAM objective. The gap between minimizing the proposed objective and the SAM objective is not clarified (Reviewer 2). Specifically, there is no theoretical result comparing the gaps between \( \min_w L_D^{01}(w) \) and the solutions obtained by SAM and BiSAM, respectively (Reviewer 2, question).
- **Need for tighter-bound justification**: The benefit of approximating the 0‑1 loss via BiSAM (rather than using cross‑entropy) is unclear. The claim that the lower bound (11) provides a tighter bound than using \( L^{ce} \) in the original SAM needs justification, possibly through a synthetic example or theoretical derivation (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 also questions the statement below Equation (8) that “it would be wrong to simply replace it by cross‑entropy,” because it seems valid to upper‑bound the right‑hand side with \( L^{ce} \). Reviewers consider the theoretical contribution of the paper to be insufficiently supported (Reviewer 2, 4).
- **Inconsistent behavior across datasets**: Reviewer 3 asks why BiSAM with a tanh lower bound yields higher test accuracy on CIFAR‑10 compared to using –log as the lower bound, but the opposite occurs on CIFAR‑100. This suggests the method’s performance depends strongly on the choice of surrogate in a dataset‑specific way that is not explained. Reviewer 3 suggests combining characteristics of tanh and –log to create a new lower bound, but the paper does not address this.
- **Missing experiments and extensions**:  
  – It would be helpful to see whether BiSAM benefits other domains, e.g., NLP (Reviewer 1).  
  – For noisy‑label learning (Appendix A.3), the authors should provide results with standard noisy‑learning approaches such as mixup (Reviewer 1).  
- **Possible typographical error**: In Equation (14), the term \( -\frac{1}{\mu}\log k \) might be missing from the right‑hand side (Reviewer 4).
