## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes GTR, a feed-forward 3D mesh reconstruction model from multi-view images that improves upon the Large Reconstruction Model (LRM) with three key components: (1) architectural modifications to LRM (convolutional image encoder replacing DINO ViT, linear+pixelshuffle replacing deconvolution upsampling), (2) geometry refinement via differentiable marching cubes (DiffMC) enabling full-resolution mesh rendering and depth/normal losses, and (3) a lightweight per-instance texture refinement (4 seconds on A100) that fine-tunes the triplane and NeRF color model. The method achieves state-of-the-art 2D/3D metrics on GSO (PSNR 28.67, refined 29.79) and OmniObject3D, and supports downstream text/image-to-3D generation. While some reviewers find the results strong (Reviewers 3–5, scores 6) and the ablation studies solid, others (Reviewers 1 and 2, scores 5) express stronger concerns about novelty, missing baselines, and mesh quality artifacts.

### Strengths

1. **Rapid texture refinement**: The per-instance texture refinement is lightweight, taking only 4 seconds on an A100 GPU, and achieves faithful reconstruction of complex textures (e.g., text) (R1, R3, R5 all mention the speed and quality improvement).

2. **Architecture improvements over LRM**: Replacing the DINO ViT with a convolutional encoder and deconvolution with linear+pixelshuffle reduces artifacts and enhances high-frequency details while improving training efficiency. The design choices are validated by ablation studies (R1, R2, R5).

3. **Strong quantitative and qualitative results**: GTR achieves state-of-the-art PSNR (28.67 on GSO, refined 29.79) and outperforms baselines on both 2D (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and 3D (Chamfer Distance, volumetric IoU, mesh IoU) metrics across GSO and OmniObject3D. Qualitative improvements are especially visible for text reconstruction (R1, R3, R4, R5).

4. **Well-grounded analysis**: The paper clearly discusses limitations of the original LRM and motivates each modification (R4, R5). The ablation studies thoroughly support the authors’ claims (R5).

5. **Solid experimental setup**: Many baselines are compared (R3), and the paper is well-written and easy to follow (R3, R4, R5).

### Weaknesses

1. **Missing comparison with recent strong baselines**: The paper does not compare with Mesh-LRM, which had a published online demo before the ICLR deadline (R1). More broadly, recent works like MeshLRM, GS-LRM, and LGM have adopted similar strategies (patchify+linear encoders, separate MLPs, etc.) but are not discussed or compared (R1, R2). This omission undermines the contextualization of GTR’s improvements.

2. **Novelty is limited**: The texture refinement component has been explored in prior works (One-2-3-45++, Mesh-LRM) (R1). The architectural modifications (conv encoder vs. DINO ViT, pixelshuffle vs. deconv) are incremental and similar to proposals in MeshLRM/GS-LRM (R2). The overall pipeline closely follows InstantMesh (differentiable iso-surface extraction + depth/normal losses) (R2). Multiple reviewers note that the technical contribution is modest (R1 contribution 2, R2 contribution 1).

3. **Mesh quality is not satisfactory**: The reconstructed meshes exhibit bumpy and grid-like artifacts visible in supplementary videos. These artifacts are consistent across examples, suggesting a fundamental limitation in the geometry generation process (R1). The mesh quality detracts from the claimed “3D mesh reconstruction” goal (R1).

4. **Need for more detailed ablation and quantitative comparison of design choices**: The paper should provide per-metric numbers for each architectural variation (e.g., pixelshuffle vs. deconvolution, different encoder choices) in Table 1, and explicitly quantify the impact of each trick (R1, R2). The source of the large quantitative improvement from seemingly incremental changes is unclear without such breakdown (R3).

5. **Unfair comparison in Figure 4**: LRM takes a single front-view image, while GTR uses 4 views, making the qualitative comparison misleading (R4). The authors should match the input setting (R4).

6. **Missing metrics for generation quality**: The evaluation lacks FID or CLIP scores, which are standard for assessing generated texture/geometry quality (R5).

7. **Incomplete time/efficiency reporting**: The paper states a 4-second texture refinement but does not report the total time per shape (including geometry stage) nor a stage-wise breakdown compared to the baseline (R3, R5). The multi-stage pipeline may be slower overall (R5).

8. **Need for more discussion of failure cases and limitations**: The paper lacks an explicit discussion of common failure cases and limitations of the method (R3, R4).

9. **Clarifications needed in experimental details**:
   - Whether the DINO encoder is frozen or trainable during training, and an analysis of why ViT (with residual connections) cannot capture image details as well as a conv encoder (R2).
   - How depth and normal losses are obtained for single real-world images (R5). If from multi-view input, that should be clarified.
   - Justification for using Plücker coordinates for camera ray embedding (R4).
   - Source and quality of the internal 3D asset dataset (R4).
   - Full names and definitions for metrics: CD (Chamfer Distance) and IoU (3D mIoU?) should be stated; table captions should indicate 2D vs. 3D metric groups (R4).
   - In Table 1, some baselines achieve better CD/IoU but the authors mark their results in bold; this needs correction or explanation (R3).
   - Typographical errors: L66 "theoptimization" → "the optimization"; L195 "Lhuillier & Quan (2005).," → "Lhuillier & Quan (2005)," (R4).

10. **Open-source and real-world applicability**: Will the code/models be open-sourced? (R3). The time breakdown and failure cases are also relevant for practical use (R3, R5).
