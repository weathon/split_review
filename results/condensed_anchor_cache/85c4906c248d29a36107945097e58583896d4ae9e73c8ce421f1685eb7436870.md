## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Relaxed Rotation-Equivariance (RRE) to handle symmetry breaking in real-world scenarios, proposing R2GConv (with a learnable perturbation Δ controlled by parameter b) and building R2Net and R2Det architectures. R2Det shows strong performance on PASCAL VOC and MS COCO 2017 with fewer parameters than YOLO-based detectors, and also demonstrates effectiveness in classification and segmentation tasks. Reviewer 3 notes impressive performance-compute trade-offs and a clear illustration of the perturbation’s effect (Figure 5), but raises concerns about the theoretical framing; Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 2 acknowledge the novelty but identify missing experiments and comparisons.

### Strengths
- Novel approach: R2GConv extends existing equivariant neural networks by relaxing strict rotation equivariance via learnable perturbations, addressing a real-world limitation (symmetry breaking).
- Strong empirical results: R2Det achieves remarkable performance with significantly reduced parameter count compared to YOLO models (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). Performance-compute trade-offs are highlighted as impressive (Reviewer 3).
- Rigorous mathematical framework and theoretical underpinnings for RRE (Reviewer 1).
- The effect of the perturbation Δ is evaluated and illustrated effectively (Figure 5; Reviewer 3).
- Plug-and-play nature of ER2GConv layers facilitates integration into existing detectors (Reviewer 1).
- Enhanced R2GConv with depthwise/pointwise convolution and extensive ablation experiments confirm positive impact (Reviewer 2).
- Paper is well-written and easy to understand (Reviewer 2); but Reviewer 3 finds readability poor due to acronyms and typos – see weaknesses.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient exploration of parameter b (Δ):** Only b=0.1 gives best results; performance deteriorates for b>0.1. Experiments with intermediate values (e.g., 0.02, 0.05) in [0,0.1] are missing (Reviewer 1). While the deviation should be quantified to assess alignment with relaxed equivariance theory (Reviewer 3).
- **Theoretical framing and naming are misleading/incorrect:** “Relaxed equivariance” is mistakenly defined using ε-approximate equivariance (Definition 1), contradicting established definitions in [1]. The R2EFilter implements a learnable perturbation, not true relaxed equivariance. Figure 1a incorrectly illustrates the problem. The paper should rename methods and fix the preliminary section (Reviewer 3).
- **Discrepancy in results between Table 1 and Table 2:** On the VOC test set, the strict equivariance (b=0/SRE) AP50 and AP50:95 differ: Table 1 reports 83.8 / 64.4, while Table 2 reports 82.9 / 64.2. Authors should clarify whether SRE (Table 2) differs from b=0 (Table 1) or if the variation stems from experimental runs (Reviewer 1).
- **Dataset limitation:** PASCAL VOC and COCO do not emphasize rotation characteristics, making it hard to isolate the benefits of SRE vs. RRE. Rotation-specific detection datasets should be used to demonstrate the method’s ability to handle arbitrary orientations (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons:** Missing comparisons with established rotation detection models such as ReDet and FRED (Reviewer 2). Also lacking comparisons with more recent detectors like YOLOv11 (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of scalability analysis:** No results for larger R2Det configurations (e.g., “R2Det-L”) compared to “Large” YOLO versions (Reviewer 1).
- **Undefined abbreviations:** SO(2) (Special Orthogonal Group in 2D) and ER2GCBA (-BA undefined) not explained (Reviewer 1). Excessive use of acronyms (ENN, GConv, RRE, SRE, NRE, R2Filter, R2Lift, R2GConv, DR2GConv, PR2GConv, ER2GConv, ER2GCBA, …) and multiple typos (e.g., “Rotationa-Equivariant”, “we further exploring”, “More analysis can refer to”, “converge when 66-epoch”) harm readability; Figures 2 and 3 are overloaded (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 found the paper well-written, but Reviewer 3 disagrees.
- **Missing analysis of group order impact:** Substantial performance gap between C₄ and C₈ (Table 3) is not explained. Why would C₈ (with symmetries beyond those of axis-aligned bounding boxes) be beneficial? Also, results without equivariance are not reported (Reviewer 3).
- **Need intuitive or experimental examples** of 2D detection where breaking strict rotation equivariance is clearly beneficial (Reviewer 3).
- **Question on Figure 1 left:** The mapping from lower-left input to upper-right output should use the relaxed rotation-equivariant function (Reviewer 2).
