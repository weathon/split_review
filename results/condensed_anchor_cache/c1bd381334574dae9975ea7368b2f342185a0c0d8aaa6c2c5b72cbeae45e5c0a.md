## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes AdaQTransform, a PTQ method that learns per-channel scaling and translation on quantized output features, unifying PTQ and QAT. It claims to expand the optimization space and improve reconstruction error. Experiments cover CNNs (ImageNet), ViTs, LLMs, and image super-resolution (e.g., Set5, Set14). Scores are 6, 3, 6, 6, with clear reviewer disagreement: three reviewers rate it highly (6) while one reviewer (score 3) finds the presentation confusing and points to likely errors in reported numbers.

### Strengths
- Superior performance across diverse tasks (image classification, super-resolution, LLMs) and backbone networks (CNNs, ViTs, LLMs).
- Original unified framework that subsumes PTQ and QAT, providing a systematic perspective on existing quantization approaches.
- Comprehensive experimental validation on multiple architectures and datasets; reported results are generally convincing and extensive.
- Empirical observations in Sec. 3.2 help motivate the decoupling and AdaQTransform design; most figures and tables are self-contained.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (noted by three reviewers; one reviewer strongly disagreed on clarity).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and clarity issues (noted by one reviewer, score 3):** Figure 1 is confusing; it does not describe “case1.1.1” or the notation \(s_w'\), making the decoupled optimization space hard to grasp. Equation (2) does not define \(h(\alpha)\). The per-channel transformation itself is not clearly described or analyzed (e.g., no explicit operation of AdaRound used in the method).
- **Missing analysis of training overhead:** No comparison of the training cost (additional params \(\xi, \eta\)), number of optimization iterations, or convergence speed relative to methods like BRECQ.
- **Insufficient hardware evaluation:** The hardware experiment on TVM only reports NWQ on MobileNet-v2, which is too limited for practical deployment insight. Inference speed on hardware should be evaluated for more models and quantization methods.
- **Performance numbers in Table 6 appear incorrect (reviewer 2):** For image super-resolution, EDSR scale 4 cannot achieve 37.99 dB on Set5 in FP32, and AdaBM outperforms the reported scores with lower FAB in the original paper. The authors must verify and correct these numbers.
- **Outdated or missing benchmark comparisons:** Table 3 (object detection on MS COCO) only includes methods published before 2023; recent PTQ works should be compared (e.g., OS+, QuIP#, Quest, PTQ4DiT, SmoothQuant). Missing weight-only quantization experiments on CNNs, ViTs, LLMs, and super-resolution networks.
- **Lack of ablation and component analysis:** No ablation study isolating the effect of per-channel transformation after quantization, nor ablation of each proposed technique (e.g., per-channel scaling vs. translation, decoupled vs. coupled cases). For ViT and CNN, it is unclear whether simply optimizing BN or LN scale factors during PTQ yields similar gains.
- **Vague performance claim:** The statement “improves current best PTQ by 5.7% on W2A2-MobileNet-v2” does not specify which metric (accuracy? PSNR?). The corresponding discussion is not found in Sec. 4.
- **Missing 2-bit ViT results:** For ViT classification, only higher-bit results are shown; 2-bit PTQ performance on ViTs should be provided to fully evaluate low-bit effectiveness.
- **Hardware-friendliness concern:** Unlike RepQ-ViT which reparameterizes scales for hardware-friendly layer-wise/log2 quantization, AdaQTransform introduces per-channel factors that may not be hardware-friendly; no discussion or mitigation is given.
- **Missing limitations and failure cases:** The paper does not discuss any failure cases or limitations of AdaQTransform.
- **Missing citation:** A recent QAT work (Wang et al., “Learnable Lookup Table for Neural Network Quantization,” CVPR 2022) should be cited in the related literature.
- **Unanswered technical questions (from reviewer 2):** (a) Why is the weight quantization step \(s_w\) fixed while other parameters are learned? (b) Is the bias parameter \(b\) not updated during PTQ? (c) Is the scale in Equation (13) learnable? These should be clarified or justified.
- **Unaddressed relation to existing work:** The relationship between AdaQTransform and Outlier Suppression+ (OS+, EMNLP 2023) is unclear; both handle scaling and translation, but no comparison of their strategies for determining scaling/translation parameters is provided.
