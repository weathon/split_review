## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes integrating a sinusoidal function into low-rank decomposition (LoRA) to increase effective rank without adding parameters. It provides theoretical justification and demonstrates improved performance over vanilla LoRA across ViT, LLMs (RoBERTa, LLaMA 3-8B), NeRF, and 3D shape modeling. Reviewers acknowledge the simple, elegant idea and rigorous derivations, but point out missing comparisons with advanced LoRA variants (DoRA, LoLDU, MELoRA), insufficient rationale for choosing the sine function, unclear hyperparameter tuning guidelines, and lack of experiments on standard datasets and CNN architectures. Reviewer scores ranged from 6 to 8, with substantial agreement on the need for additional validation and analysis.

### Strengths
- **Theoretical justification**: The paper provides mathematical proof that sine-activated low-rank matrices increase rank without extra parameters. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Simple and effective idea**: The approach is straightforward yet demonstrates strong empirical gains across diverse tasks. (R2, R3, R4)
- **Broad task coverage**: Evaluated on ViT classification, LLM fine-tuning (RoBERTa, LLaMA 3-8B), NeRF (vanilla NeRF on LLFF), and 3D shape modeling (Thai Statue), including extreme low-rank settings (e.g., rank k=1 in NeRF). (R1, R2, R3)
- **Illustrative figures**: Figures 2 and 3 help understand the effect of the frequency parameter and the method’s representational power. (R2, R3)
- **Motivation**: Addresses a highly relevant problem—parameter-efficient learning for practical deployment. (R3)
- **Potential impact**: If proofs hold, the method could significantly influence the domain of low-rank adaptations. (R2)
- **Competitive performance when replacing full-rank weights**: The method shows promising results for direct model compression. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of rationale for sine function selection**: The paper does not explain why the sine function is chosen over other nonlinearities, how its periodic nature interacts with low-rank structure, or why it improves performance without extra parameters. (R1, R3, R4) Reviewer 4 asks: since other nonlinear functions are almost useless, why not directly give the sine function in Equation 3?
- **Missing comparisons with state-of-the-art LoRA variants**: The method is compared only to vanilla LoRA (self-implemented) and underperforms relative to methods like DoRA on commonsense reasoning. Missing comparisons with LoLDU, MELoRA, and other recent improvements. (R1, R2)
- **Hyperparameter \(\omega\) tuning unclear**: No guidelines for selecting \(\omega\); whether it needs per-task tuning, how to choose optimal values, and whether grid search was used. Figure 10 suggests an optimum, but intuition about its value is lacking. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
  - Reviewer 4 adds: Does rank always remain lower than the original regardless of \(\omega\)? For high \(\omega\) (200 vs 400) rank appears almost equal (Figure 3 right).
- **Why are w and g non-learnable?** Reviewer 4 asks: could they be learnable? Would that cause gradient problems? Should different layers have different w/g? What are recommended values?
- **Missing space/time complexity analysis**: No analysis of training computation overhead or inference slowdown, especially in resource-limited settings. (R1)
- **Lack of quantitative results and tasks**: For 3D shape modeling, no quantitative evaluation on a standard dataset; also missing evaluation on text-to-image generation. (R1)
- **Insufficient implementation details**: For each application, specifics of how the sine-activated low-rank matrix is integrated are not provided. (R1)
- **Applicability to CNNs not discussed**: No experiments or discussion on integrating the method into convolutional layers. (R3)
- **Why does increased rank improve performance?** Intuition missing – adding noise can also increase rank without improving performance; the paper should clarify the link between rank increase and performance gain. (R2)
- **Not applied to all linear modules of LLM**: Reviewer 4 asks why low-rank adaptations are not implemented on all linear layers in Section 4.1.
- **Minor presentation errors** (R2):
  - Figure 4 referenced as Table 4.
  - Equation notation issues (line 182/765: \(A_{ij} \neq 0\) vs \(A_{ij \neq 0}\)).
  - Operator norm in equation 5 has an extra “|”.
  - Observation in line 786 claimed important for equation 11 but seems trivially true.
  - Line 799 references “Equation equation 13” (likely cref issue).
  - Sentence starting in line 855 is garbled.
- **Overfitting terminology**: Using “overfitting” for NeRFs (line 480) may be confusing since overfitting is often desired there. (R2)
- **Figure 1**: Using compression rate instead of parameter counts makes comparison with other SOTA methods harder. (R2)
