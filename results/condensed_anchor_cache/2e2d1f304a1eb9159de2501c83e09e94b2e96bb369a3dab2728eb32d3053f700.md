## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a Transformer-Modulated Diffusion Model (TMDM) for probabilistic multivariate time series forecasting. It unites a conditional diffusion generative process with a transformer by using the transformer’s output as the condition in both the forward and reverse diffusion steps. The manuscript is well‑presented and easy to read. Experiments on six datasets evaluate the method using CRPS, QICE, MSE, and MAE, and ablation studies show the impact of different components.

### Strengths
- The paper is well‑organized and clearly written.  
- The proposed approach is generic: any point forecast model can be wrapped to produce probabilistic forecasts.  
- TMDM achieves consistently good results against several strong baselines across multiple datasets.  
- Ablation studies demonstrate the contribution of individual components and the ability to wrap different point‑forecast models (e.g., Transformer, RNN).  
- The method is inspired by conditional diffusion processes (Han et al., 2022) and adapts them to the time‑series forecasting setting.  
- Extensive experiments are conducted, and the method has potential to benefit the probabilistic forecasting community.  
- One reviewer noted that the ablation studies and consistent improvements are convincing; another considered the paper’s contribution to have potential despite relying on known building blocks.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**: More state‑of‑the‑art methods that also model uncertainty should be compared, e.g., cST‑ML (which uses a VAE to capture traffic dynamics). The authors should provide explanations or additional experiments.  
- **Granularity**: It is unclear whether the model works for time‑series data with different granularities.  
- **CRPS sum discrepancies**: The CRPS_sum results reported for baselines in the appendix (Table 6) differ substantially from published values. For example, the paper reports 3.92 for TimeGrad on Exchange, while the TimeGrad paper reports 0.006; CSDI on Electricity is reported as 4.54 vs. 0.017 in the CSDI paper. These differences need a clear explanation, and reproducing baseline results on their own published settings would strengthen the evaluation.  
- **Novelty not sufficiently highlighted**: Both the transformer and the conditional diffusion model are drawn from existing literature. The paper must justify why their integration is non‑trivial and describe the specific technical challenges overcome. The third claimed contribution (two new metrics) is considered weak because both metrics already exist.  
- **Related work discussion**: The paper should discuss and contrast other works that combine transformers with probabilistic models for time series, such as Transformer + Probabilistic Circuit (e.g., for forecasting and uncertainty estimation) and Predictive Whittle networks.  
- **Clarity of conditional representation**: The generation of \(\hat{y}_{0:M}\) is unclear. The transition from eq(9) to eq(10) is omitted; the roles of \(\tilde{\mu}_z\), \(\tilde{\sigma}_z\), and \(\mu_z\) are not explained, and the dimensionality of \(z\) (scalar or vector) is not stated.  
- **References not up‑to‑date**: Many arXiv versions have since been published in conferences or journals.  
- **Additional specific questions** (should be addressed as missing details or experiments):  
  * How does \(\mu_z(z)\) differ from \(\mathcal{T}(x_{0:N})\)? What happens if the NNs are omitted and \(\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{T}(x_{0:N}), \mathbf{I})\) is used instead of \(\mathcal{N}(\mu_z(z), \mathbf{I})\)?  
  * Does the output dimension \(C\) (introduced in the introduction) equal the dimension \(d\) of \(x_t\) and \(y_t\)?
