## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces a framework for realistic UAV vision-language navigation (VLN) spanning a simulation platform (OpenUAV), a dataset (~12k trajectories), a new benchmark (UAV-Need-Help) with three levels of assistant guidance, and a baseline method (UAV Navigation LLM) that uses hierarchical trajectory generation. The platform models 6-DOF continuous flight dynamics using AirSim and Unreal Engine 4. The proposed method significantly outperforms baselines but still falls far short of human performance. Reviewers agree the work is comprehensive and addresses a gap in realistic UAV VLN, though they raise concerns about novelty relative to existing platforms, missing ablations, evaluation details, and practical deployment.

### Strengths

- **Realistic flight dynamics**: The platform supports continuous 6-DOF trajectories and realistic flight physics, moving beyond the oversimplified discrete actions used in prior UAV VLN work. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Comprehensive open-source simulation**: OpenUAV integrates AirSim and UE4, providing diverse environments, realistic flight dynamics, and algorithmic support (e.g., efficient parallel data collection). (R1, R2)
- **First realistic UAV VLN dataset**: The 12k-trajectory dataset captures true 6-DOF flight dynamics with language instructions, filling a gap for realistic UAV navigation research. (R1, R2)
- **Novel assistant-guided benchmark**: The UAV-Need-Help benchmark introduces three levels of guidance (low, medium, high), making the task more adaptable and practical for complex aerial environments. (R1, R2)
- **Hierarchical LLM-based navigation model**: The proposed architecture first predicts a target pose then a fine-grained trajectory, leveraging multimodal LLMs (Llama-vid, Vicuna-7B, EVA-CLIP) and a trajectory decoder. (R1, R2, R3; R4 notes strong baseline performance)
- **Multiple baselines and human baseline**: The paper includes several baselines and a human operator baseline, enabling clear performance comparisons. (R1, R3)
- **Thorough experimental validation**: Evaluations cover different difficulty levels and scenarios, with generalization to unseen objects (though drops on unseen environments). (R1)
- **Parallelization and scalability**: The platform supports efficient data collection and evaluation in parallel environments. (R2)
- **Complete package for community research**: The paper provides a platform, dataset, benchmark, and baseline model together, opening possibilities for future UAV VLN research. (R3)

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of novelty comparison with existing platforms**: The paper insufficiently differentiates from AerialVLN (which also claims support for continuous actions) and other UE+AirSim combinations (e.g., CityNav). A detailed comparison of flight dynamics, trajectory control, rendering fidelity, sensor accuracy, and VLN support is missing. (R3) One reviewer (R3) specifically found the contributions modest, giving a lower contribution score (2 vs. 3 from others).
- **Unclear delineation from AirSim**: OpenUAV heavily relies on AirSim; the paper does not clearly specify which components are directly adopted, which are modified, and which are entirely novel. (R3)
- **Naming conflict**: The name “OpenUAV” already exists for another platform (cited). The paper should acknowledge this and distinguish contributions. (R3)
- **Missing real-world validation**: No experiments with physical UAVs are provided to validate how well the simulation matches real flight dynamics and whether models trained on OpenUAV transfer to the real world. (R1, R2)
- **Small dataset size**: 12k trajectories is relatively small compared to ground-based VLN datasets, which may limit model learning. (R1)
- **Low success rates even with assistance**: SR remains around 16–17% even with the highest assistance level (L3). The L1 oracle-path guidance yields only a minor increase. (R1, R4)
- **Missing evaluation without assistant**: No baseline is reported without any guidance, making it impossible to judge the true difficulty of the task or the autonomy of the system. Temporal input and memory could potentially reduce the need for assistants. (R3)
- **No discrete action baseline**: The claim that continuous actions are necessary for realism is not validated; a discrete-action baseline (e.g., discrete Move Forward/Turn/etc.) and qualitative comparisons of trajectory smoothness are needed. (R3)
- **Ablation studies lacking**: Missing ablations on key components: hierarchical vs. direct trajectory prediction, individual assistance levels, number of sensors (e.g., fewer cameras), removal of the trajectory decoder, and impact of environmental conditions. (R2, R3, R1)
- **Visual encoding neglects history**: The method uses only the latest frame (16+1 visual tokens) without any history, raising concerns about how the model aligns navigation history with instructions. (R4, R3)
- **No demonstration of DAgger effectiveness**: The paper mentions using DAgger for closed-loop simulation but provides no experiments to show its effect. (R4)
- **OSR vs. SR discrepancy**: OSR is nearly double SR (e.g., ~32% vs. ~16%), suggesting methods may achieve high OSR by extensive exploration; this needs discussion or additional experiments (e.g., path efficiency metrics). (R4)
- **Missing comparison with ground-based VLN methods**: No direct comparison with ground methods like Seq2Seq, DUET, NavGPT2, or with other LLM-based VLN approaches (Navid, NavGPT) that use similar components (Llama-vid, Vicuna-7B, EVA-CLIP) or waypoint-based trajectory planning. (R2, R4)
- **No analysis of real-time/edge deployment**: Computational requirements, latency, and feasibility of running the LLM-based model on resource-constrained UAV hardware are not discussed. (R1, R2)
- **Lack of safety mechanisms**: No discussion of fallback strategies or failure recovery when navigation fails (e.g., collision, getting stuck). (R1) Also, it is unclear whether the UAV can autonomously recover from navigation failures without external assistance. (R2)
- **Unclear scenario definitions**: The 22 “scenarios” are not defined; it is unclear whether they are different environments (forest vs. city) or specific events. A list/table with environment types, object densities, and challenges is needed. (R3)
- **Incomplete dataset split details**: A detailed breakdown of which objects and scenes are used for training, validation, and testing (e.g., number of instances per object) should be provided in the supplementary. (R3)
- **Figure 3 inconsistency**: The figure shows the front-view image as input to the trajectory completion module, but the text states it takes the front-view visual token *T_img* (processed by ViT). (R3)
- **Lack of video results**: No video demonstrations of ground-truth trajectories or method performance on the platform are provided, which would help intuitive understanding. (R4)
- **Missing DAgger teacher model details**: The teacher model used in DAgger is not described (architecture, how it generates expert trajectories). (R3)
- **Human baseline details**: It is unclear whether the human operator was trained or performed zero-shot. (R3)
- **Extensibility not discussed**: Whether the platform and dataset can be extended to other UAV tasks (mapping, exploration, surveillance) and what modifications would be needed is not addressed. (R2)
