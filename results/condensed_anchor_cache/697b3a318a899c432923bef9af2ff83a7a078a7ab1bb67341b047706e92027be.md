## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces MILLET, a framework that frames Time Series Classification (TSC) as a Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) problem, making deep learning backbones (FCN, ResNet, InceptionTime) inherently interpretable without compromising (and sometimes improving) predictive performance. MILLET replaces global average pooling with one of four pooling methods (attention, instance, additive, conjunctive) and adds positional encoding. Evaluations on 85 UCR datasets and a new synthetic dataset (WebTraffic, designed for interpretability evaluation) show that MILLET produces sparse, high-quality explanations quickly, with conjunctive pooling achieving the best balanced accuracy and interpretability.

### Strengths
- **Novel application of MIL to TSC**: The framework is a novel and flexible approach for making deep TSC models intrinsically interpretable (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Well written and carefully justified**: All design choices are clearly motivated, and the paper is well structured (R1, R2, R3).
- **Thorough experimentation**: Evaluation covers 85 UCR datasets plus a newly proposed synthetic dataset, providing robust evidence (R1, R2, R3).
- **Low added complexity**: MILLET adds minimal overhead to existing backbones while preserving or improving performance (R1, R4).
- **Synthetic dataset contribution**: The WebTraffic dataset is a helpful benchmark for interpretability, scalable and grounded in real-world patterns (R1, R4).
- **Conjunctive pooling yields improvement**: The proposed conjunctive pooling consistently boosts balanced accuracy across backbones, including outperforming state-of-the-art methods HC2 and Hydra-MR (R1, R4).
- **Sparse and fast explanations**: MILLET produces high-quality explanations with fewer forward passes than SHAP, CAM, etc. (R4).
- **Positional encoding enforces temporal ordering**: Important for modeling time series constraints (R4).
- **Up-to-date references and literature coverage** (R3).

### Weaknesses
*Note: One reviewer (R2) identified no weaknesses; the following points were raised by the other three reviewers.*

- **Missing comparison with prior interpretability methods for TSC**: The paper does not quantitatively compare MILLET against established feature-attribution methods such as TSR (Foumani et al.), DynaMask, WinIT, TimeX, LIME, or LIMESegments. These works also provide synthetic datasets and metrics; omitting them limits the evidence for MILLET’s interpretability advantage (R1, R3, R4).
- **Interpretability metrics are not validated for time series**: AOPCR and NDCG@n are ported from computer vision without proper adaptation. For example, NDCG@n may penalize methods that highlight the start or end of a missing-data region instead of its center, leading to misleading scores. The temporal dependencies in time series are not addressed, and no justification is given for the metrics’ suitability (R1).
- **Limited baseline comparison for TSC performance**: The paper should compare with a wider set of state-of-the-art TSC models beyond HC2 and Hydra-MR, including non-deep methods like ROCKET/MiniROCKET and deep models with different architectures (e.g., Transformers). This would better demonstrate MILLET’s generalizability and competitive standing (R3, R4).
- **Missing algorithm complexity analysis**: A detailed complexity breakdown for each pooling method (attention, instance, additive, conjunctive) with respect to time series length and number of instances, as well as the impact of positional encoding, is not provided. Such analysis is necessary to assess scalability (R4).
- **Synthetic dataset experiments are limited**: The WebTraffic experiments should vary the number of records, timestamps, and classes to probe robustness and generalizability (R3).
- **Heavy reliance on supplementary material**: Essential methodological details (e.g., training settings, hyperparameters) are pushed to the supplement, forcing the reader to navigate away from the main paper (R3).
- **Missing analysis of hyperparameter sensitivity and class imbalance**: The paper does not investigate how hyperparameter choices (e.g., dropout rate, positional encoding dimensions) or class imbalance affect MILLET’s performance (R4).
- **Figure 6 is unclear**: It is not specified whether the plotted data points correspond to different pooling methods or multiple runs of the same model, reducing interpretability (R1).
- **Intuition behind conjunctive pooling’s success**: The authors do not provide a clear explanation for why conjunctive pooling yields better performance than other pooling variants (R1).
