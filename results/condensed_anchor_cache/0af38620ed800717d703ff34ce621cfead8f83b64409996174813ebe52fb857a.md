## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Graphex Mean Field Games (GXMFGs), which extend Graphon MFGs to handle sparse graphs (including power-law networks) by using graphexes as limiting objects. A learning algorithm based on online mirror descent, exploiting a core-periphery structure, is proposed. Theoretical convergence guarantees and empirical validation on synthetic and real-world networks are provided.

### Strengths

- Clear, compelling motivation: extending Graphon MFGs to sparse graphs is natural and addresses a real-world gap where dense graphons are insufficient (R1, R2, R3).
- The hybrid graphex approach (core vs. periphery) is intuitive and the algorithmic adaptation is well-motivated (R1, R3).
- Paper is well-written, organized, and provides a thorough discussion of prior related work (R1, R2, R3).
- Theoretical analysis is principled and nontrivial, complemented by supporting empirical studies that strengthen the arguments (R2, R3).
- Introduces a genuinely new extension of MFGs to sparse graphs (R1, R3).

### Weaknesses

- The model is abstract at points; theoretical results may be less relevant for this conference – a deeper focus on the learning algorithm would have been beneficial (R1).
- Several technical gaps and typos hinder correctness checking (R1):
  - Lemma 1: missing measurability (or stronger) condition on \(f\) with respect to \(\alpha\) for the integral to be well-defined.
  - Assumption 2 (used in Lemma 1) is unclear: it references an equilibrium policy \(\boldsymbol{\pi}\) that is not known a priori, and the set of admissible deviations for the Nash equilibrium is not defined.
  - Algorithm 1, line 14: \(Q^{k,\mu^{\tau_{\max}}}\) is not explained or recalled.
  - Notation inconsistencies: state space is both \(\mathcal{X}\) and \(X\) (Section 3); \(\mathbb{G}^{\infty}_{\alpha,t}\) may or may not depend on \(\boldsymbol{\mu}\) (bottom of page 4).
- No baseline competitors (e.g., LPGMFG, GMFG) are compared against; while the unique advantage is handling sparse graphs, empirical comparison (or a discussion of why it is unsuitable) would strengthen the evaluation (R3).
- Figure 3a appears to show curves diverging rather than converging as \(k\) increases – it is unclear whether the curves are coloured correctly (R3).
- Reviewer 2 (score 8, low confidence) found no significant weaknesses but raised the following minor points:
  - An intuitive explanation for Assumptions 1(b) and 1(c) would improve readability.
  - Discussion of extending the approach to infinite action spaces would be beneficial.
  - Including simulation code would enhance reproducibility.
- Reviewer 2’s positive assessment contrasts with the more critical views of Reviewers 1 and 3 (both score 6). Reviewer 2 acknowledges limited familiarity with the central parts and therefore lower confidence in the assessment.
