## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes conceptual counterfactual explanations using semantic graphs (scene graphs). For a given input, the counterfactual is the scene graph from a different class with the minimal Graph Edit Distance (GED). To circumvent the NP-hard exact GED computation, the authors train a GNN to embed graphs so that distances in the embedding space approximate GED. The method is evaluated on image (CUB, Visual Genome) and audio (COVID-19) datasets, and compared with prior semantic-based methods (CVE, SC). Quantitative and human subject studies are presented.

### Strengths
- The idea of applying GNNs to approximate GED over scene graphs for counterfactual explanations is novel and interesting (Reviewers 1,2,3,4). Reviewer 1 describes it as a simple, elegant solution similar to multi-dimensional scaling.
- The method empirically outperforms prior works (CVE and SC) across multiple benchmarks, achieving smaller edit distances (Reviewer 1, 3). Human evaluators consistently prefer the proposed counterfactuals and find them significantly easier for distinguishing classes (Reviewer 1, 3, 4).
- The approach is model-agnostic and can be applied to any input representable as a graph, demonstrated on both image and audio modalities (Reviewer 2, 3, 4). The edits are inherently interpretable and actionable due to grounding in semantic concepts (Reviewer 3, 4).
- The method is computationally more efficient than computing exact GED for all pairs (Reviewer 1). Code is provided for reproducibility (Reviewer 2). The paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Fidelity to the target model (Major, Reviewers 2 and 3):** A core concern is that the semantics used are derived from human annotations (scene graphs) rather than from the internal representations of the classifier being explained. This may lead to counterfactuals that are semantically plausible to humans but do not accurately reflect the model's decision boundaries (Reviewer 2). For example, a model may rely on subtle texture or color variations not captured by semantic graphs, and imperceptible adversarial perturbations could change predictions without affecting the semantic graph (Reviewer 2). Further, GED may not correlate with distances in the classifier's feature space; the paper does not provide quantitative analysis of this correlation (Reviewer 3). The method's use as an explanation for model predictions, given its model-agnostic nature, was questioned by Reviewer 1 ("how will these counterfactual explanations be used? What insights can they provide?").

2. **Evaluation metrics and comparisons (Major, Reviewers 1, 3, 4):** The primary quantitative metrics (e.g., average GED, ranking correlation) are based on graph edit distance, which is the very quantity the model is trained to approximate. This creates a bias in favor of the proposed method, as competitors are not trained on GED (Reviewer 3). More method-agnostic metrics (e.g., classifier confidence change, semantic plausibility independent of GED) would strengthen claims (Reviewer 3). Additionally, the claimed superiority over the competitor SC is not convincingly supported by the numbers: results in Table 5 are quite close, and both models perform similarly, with SC sometimes retrieving samples with comparable low edits (Reviewer 3, 4). The qualitative examples also show similar outputs (Reviewer 4). Inconsistent baseline sets across tables (CVE missing in Table 3, SC missing in Table 4) hinder direct comparison in user studies (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 also noted uncertainty about the significance of the work and the utility of minimal GED as the selection criterion.

3. **Dependence on scene graph construction and annotation (Major, Reviewers 1, 2, 3):** The method requires scene graphs for each data point, which are often not available. For datasets without ground-truth graphs (e.g., CUB), graphs must be constructed, and the resulting counterfactuals will vary depending on the construction method (Reviewer 1). The performance of the approach heavily depends on the quality of the scene graph extractor, which is not analyzed (Reviewer 3). The reliance on massive annotation limits generalization: the fixed set of semantic concepts derived from training annotations cannot capture novel or out-of-distribution concepts (Reviewer 2). The authors should consider large vision models for more flexible semantic representations (Reviewer 2). The reviewer also questioned how to construct scene graphs from semantic attributes when not available (Reviewer 1) and whether editing different attributes (e.g., stripe pattern vs. color) should be weighted equally (Reviewer 1).

4. **Assumption of dense retrieval database (Reviewer 4):** The method assumes the retrieval database of scene graphs is "dense enough" to cover the counterfactual space. As graph dimensions increase, the combinatorial explosion of possible edits makes coverage difficult; the paper does not discuss failure modes in realistic sparse scenarios.

5. **Method novelty and relation to prior work (Reviewers 3, 4):** The contribution is largely empirical; none of the individual techniques is claimed to be novel, and their application is interesting but not groundbreaking (Reviewer 4). The distinction from the prior work SC (Dervakos et al., 2023), which also uses GED but over knowledge graphs, is unclear (Reviewer 3). The paper is not the first to employ graphs for visual counterfactuals; the novelty lies primarily in the use of GNNs for fast GED approximation (Reviewer 3). This should be more clearly articulated.

6. **Technical evaluation gaps (Reviewers 1, 3, 4):**
   - The error between the approximate GED from the GNN and the true GED is not reported (Reviewer 1).
   - The choice of cosine distance in Equation 4 vs. Euclidean distance (which aligns with the training objective) is not justified (Reviewer 1).
   - The claim that computing GED for only N/2 pairs is adequate is only empirically validated; a theoretical justification is missing (Reviewer 4).
   - The choice of Places365 for the classifier on Visual Genome (vs. ImageNet) is not motivated (Reviewer 3).
   - The explanation for why a neural network is needed instead of computing exact GED for all pairs with more compute was questioned (Reviewer 1).

7. **Presentation and minor issues:**
   - Font sizes in figures are too small; vector graphics should be used (Reviewer 2).
   - Editorial: define acronyms before use (e.g., in abstract) (Reviewer 4).
   - The concept of "actionability" mentioned in the introduction needs definition (Reviewer 4).

Note: One reviewer (Reviewer 4) gave a higher score (6 vs. 5), finding the idea interesting and valuing the empirical evaluation, but still raised concerns about empirical novelty and database assumptions. Other reviewers (1,2,3) were more critical, especially regarding fidelity to model and evaluation bias. These different viewpoints are reflected in the strengths and weaknesses above.
