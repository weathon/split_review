## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the unsupervised evaluation of unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) models. It proposes a metric called the Transfer Score, which combines a spatial uniformity measure from classifier weights with transferability and discriminability measures (clustering tendency via Hopkins statistic and mutual information) computed on target features. The metric is designed to select the best UDA method, tune hyperparameters, and choose checkpoints without target-domain labels. Experiments are conducted on Office-31, Office-Home, and DomainNet across several UDA methods.

### Strengths
- The problem is important and currently under-studied; an unsupervised evaluation metric for UDA is valuable.
- The proposed Transfer Score is novel, intuitive, and its calculation process is clearly explained.
- The paper is well-organized, easy to follow, and the code is provided as supplementary material.
- Experiments demonstrate effectiveness in three evaluation tasks: method selection, hyperparameter tuning, and checkpoint selection.
- The metric is shown to correlate with target-domain accuracy and is useful in practical settings.

### Weaknesses
- **Assumptions and limitations:** The metric relies on clustering tendency and class balance (via Hopkins statistic and weight uniformity). In real-world tasks (e.g., cross-domain semantic segmentation), class imbalance is common, and the Hopkins statistic does not account for class-specific density variations; the uniformity measure may not reflect minority-class performance. This raises concerns about reliability under severe imbalance.
- **Redundancy with existing UDA objectives:** Several UDA methods explicitly optimize mutual information (Eq. 4) or clustering criteria (Eq. 1) during training (e.g., [2,3, SHOT]). For such models, the Transfer Score may become a redundant measure of how well the model achieves its own training objective rather than an independent assessment of transfer performance.
- **Missing comparisons with existing unsupervised validation methods:** The three tasks are unsupervised model evaluation problems, yet the paper does not compare against existing baselines such as SND, DEV, or other “transfer score” metrics from [a] that are directly applicable. The correlation analysis in Figure 1 is limited to one UDA task (Office31 A→W) and one model (DANN); more comprehensive analysis with multiple metrics is needed.
- **Limited experimental scope:** Only six UDA methods are evaluated, and hyperparameter tuning (Task 2) uses only five candidate sets. The selection of tasks from datasets (e.g., Office-Home: four tasks; DomainNet and Office: similarly sparse) is not justified. Many state-of-the-art UDA approaches (especially those using adversarial training or self-training, e.g., [b,c,3,4]) are absent, making it unclear whether the metric generalizes to sophisticated adaptation techniques.
- **Overclaiming novelty:** The paper claims “TS is the first metric to perform simultaneous model comparison and selection without target-domain labels,” but unsupervised validation and model selection are existing research fields (e.g., out-of-distribution error prediction methods [K-Means Clustering Based Feature Consistency Alignment; Confidence Optimal Transport; Leveraging Unlabeled Data to Predict Out-of-Distribution Performance]). The claim should be moderated.
- **Handcrafted formulation without justification:** The Score in Definition 2 appears handcrafted; no analysis is provided to show why this specific combination is optimal or how the design was reasoned.
- **Ablation study weakness:** Table 3’s ablation is insufficient; it should follow the format of Fig. 2 to clearly evaluate the contribution of each term.
- **Equation error:** The representation of Equation 1 is incorrect (in particular, the term i*(i-1) and the use of i).
- **Sparse result reporting:** The paper does not provide complete results for all tasks across all datasets × all UDA methods × all baselines (like in Fig. 3). The experimental results appear sparse.
- **Missing evaluation on source-free UDA:** Since the metric does not use source data, its effectiveness on source-free domain adaptation methods is not examined.
- **Unsubstantiated claims about degeneration:** The statement that adversarial/self-training can lead to model degeneration and negative transfer is not proven, and it is unclear whether the proposed metric can be applied to improve performance in those settings.
- **Minor unexplained observation:** In Table 1, the improvements of the last four methods on DomainNet (p→c) are very small, yet no explanation is given.

**Disagreement note:** One reviewer rated soundness as 2/4 and contribution as 2/4, citing insufficient validation and missing comparisons, while other reviewers rated both as 3/4. The overall rating consensus is 6/10 across all reviewers.
