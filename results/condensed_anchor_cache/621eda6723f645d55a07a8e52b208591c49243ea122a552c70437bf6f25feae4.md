## Merged Review

### Summary
STBench evaluates LLM spatio-temporal analysis via 15 tasks and ~70,000 QA pairs across four dimensions: knowledge comprehension, spatio-temporal reasoning, accurate computation, and downstream applications. 13–14 LLMs (GPT-4o, Gemma, Mistral, etc.) are tested under ICL, CoT, and fine-tuning. LLMs excel at knowledge comprehension but struggle on other dimensions; CoT/ICL/fine-tuning improve accuracy. Reviewers agree the benchmark is useful and the paper well-written. One reviewer (score 8) finds the benchmark methodically designed and extensive. Three reviewers (score 5) raise major concerns about task design validity, classification logic, experimental rigor, and insufficient motivation relative to existing benchmarks.

### Strengths
- Well-written, clear structure, easy to follow.
- Benchmark covers multiple aspects of spatio-temporal analysis (knowledge comprehension, reasoning, computation, downstream applications).
- Extensive experiments: 13–14 LLMs evaluated across three settings (ICL, CoT, fine-tuning), with results showing model size matters but not for all tasks, and most models struggle with multi-step reasoning.
- Demonstrates that CoT, ICL, and fine-tuning improve model accuracy.
- Emphasizes the importance of spatio-temporal reasoning, which is often ignored in LLM studies.
- Creation of a spatial-temporal dataset (15 tasks, ~70,000 QA pairs) is useful for future research.

### Weaknesses
- Related work section needs better organization and clarification. Limitations of existing benchmarks are not discussed in detail; what tasks are unique to LLMs?
- Motivation for a new benchmark is not fully convincing.
- Claim that Roberts et al.’s multimodal benchmark is not applicable to single-modal LLMs contradicts the later evaluation of multimodal models. Multimodal benchmarks better represent real-life scenarios.
- Task classification (knowledge comprehension, reasoning, computation, downstream) is vague and logically unclear. Example: why is trajectory prediction not in knowledge comprehension?
- Some tasks appear meaningless or poorly motivated:
  - Urban Region Function Recognition: using a region’s boundary line and two randomly sampled POIs is uninformative; the boundary line is irrelevant and two random POIs can mislead.
  - POI identification: disturbance is added to POIs, but the effect of disturbance magnitude on performance is not analyzed.
- Many tasks (Point-Trajectory Relationship Detection, Point-Region Relationship Detection, Trajectory-Region Relationship Detection, Direction Determination, Navigation, Trajectory-Trajectory Relationship Analysis) can be solved with deterministic algorithms. Why use LLMs instead of an LLM Agent calling functions?
- Data format (Sec. 4.1) simplifies tasks to Yes/No or multiple choice (only two prediction tasks are free-form). This is a strong, unrealistic assumption. Impact of different formulations on accuracy should be tested.
- The benchmark is constructed from a few pre-existing datasets; tasks are independent with no dependencies, which would better assess LLM capability.
- Experimental evaluation is not solid:
  - ICL, CoT, and fine-tuning experiments do not include all models. The chosen models differ greatly in capability, leading to expected results (e.g., always use the latest LLM).
  - Justification for selecting specific models for ICL (Gemma-2B, Llama-2-7B) and CoT (only Gemma-2B) is missing.
  - Prompt templates appear generic; it is unclear if they are fair to all LLMs.
  - Interpretation of results is shallow: more detail is needed on why LLMs struggle (lack of spatio-temporal training examples, known weaknesses in numerical operations).
- Missing details in appendices and setup descriptions.
  - How many data points are sampled from Yelp for POI category recognition?
  - What is “shorter trajectory” length in point-trajectory relationship detection?
  - Urban region function recognition description is confusing; an example is needed.
- Difference between ChatGPT model version and GPT-4o is not clarified.
- A sentence in Section 5.2 is erroneous: “ChatGPT and GPT-4o outperform poorer than most open source models on…” needs fixing.
- Claim that “ChatGPT refuses to answer the questions of prediction” requires clarification (models rarely say “I do not know”).
- Did fine-tuning increase performance on all tasks? This would indicate feasibility of a generalized spatio-temporal LLM expert.
- Citation errors in Section 2 (format: e.g., “(Ji & Gao, 2023) evaluated…” should be “Ji & Gao (2023) evaluated…”).
- One reviewer suggests the work is more suitable for the NeurIPS data & benchmark track than the ICLR research track.
