## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes SDA-Net, a sub-domain adaptation framework for fine-grained segmentation that addresses intra-domain (sub-domain) shifts within a single large domain—termed the “soft domain gap.” The method divides a domain into multiple sub-domains using feature-similarity clustering, trains a sub-domain classifier (SDC) to recognize the sub-domain of an input at test time, and fine-tunes a baseline network (BN) for each sub-domain via a self-supervised procedure. Two new loss functions are introduced: a “sieve loss” and a “fine-tuning loss.” Experiments on WHU, BDD100K, and ADE20K are reported. The work is well-motivated by preliminary experiments showing the existence of soft domain gaps, and the paper is generally clearly written. However, all reviewers except one (R3, score 6) rate the paper low (score 3), citing significant weaknesses in experimental design, comparisons, novelty, and missing analyses. R3 finds the theoretical analysis thorough and the experiments reliable, but still notes several limitations.

### Strengths

- **Novel problem framing**: The paper identifies and addresses intra-domain sub-domain shifts (soft domain gaps), which are distinct from traditional cross-domain shifts. R3 finds this insight valuable, and R1 notes it offers a new perspective on domain adaptation.
- **Original proposal**: SDA-Net innovatively combines sub-domain recognition with fine-tuning of a baseline network (R1, R4). The simple yet effective idea of adapting to sub-domains at test time is highlighted.
- **Loss functions**: The sieve loss and fine-tuning loss are creative solutions for density-based sub-domain optimization (R1, R4). R2 notes that adding extra supervising signals is a common but effective practice.
- **Broad applicability**: The framework is tested on multiple domains (scene parsing, autonomous driving, aerial imagery) using WHU, BDD100K, and ADE20K benchmarks, demonstrating generalization (R1, R4).
- **Clarity and motivation**: The manuscript is well-organized, with detailed illustrations and equations (R2). Preliminary experiments clearly demonstrate the soft-domain-gap problem (R2, R3).
- **R3’s positive view** (minority): R3 (score 6, confidence 4) considers the work effective, praising the thorough theoretical analysis and reliable experiments (though other reviewers disagree).

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient and outdated comparisons** (R2 – high confidence 5, R1, R4): 
  - The baseline networks (UNet, CCNet) are outdated. The method should be evaluated on modern architectures like Swin Transformers to demonstrate generalizability (R2).
  - Comparisons are only against traditional domain adaptation methods, not against state-of-the-art fine-grained segmentation approaches that use attention or multi-scale fusion (R1). 
  - The claim that previous domain adaptation algorithms fail on intra-domain shifts is not empirically supported—no experiments comparing with SOTA domain adaptation methods (e.g., Pipa, Transferring to Real-World Layouts) are provided (R2, R3). 
  - The evaluation is unfair because SDA-Net uses extra unsupervised signals; a fairer comparison would be to incorporate the proposed losses into existing SOTA segmentation models and measure gains (R2). 
  - R4 notes references are not up-to-date.

- **Limited novelty of technical components** (R1, R2, R3, R4):
  - Sub-domain division relies on simple feature-similarity clustering, which may not capture complex underlying data structure and could lead to suboptimal groupings. An adaptive strategy is suggested (R1).
  - The sieve loss bears resemblance to density-based losses (e.g., Focal Loss); the fine-tuning loss has similarities to gradient-based optimization techniques. The paper does not provide a rigorous mathematical justification for the sieve loss’s effectiveness (R1, R4).
  - The concept of “soft domain gap” lacks a clear mathematical definition distinguishing it from conventional domain shift (R3). R3 questions how this gap differs from notions exploited by other recent DA methods.

- **Missing analyses and ablations** (multiple reviewers):
  - No sensitivity analysis on the number of sub-domains or the division strategy. The impact of clustering errors on performance is unexplored—if sub-domain division is inaccurate, could the method degrade significantly? (R1, R3, R4). R3 specifically asks about the effect of inaccurate density-based clustering.
  - No ablation comparing training overhead, computational complexity, or inference time relative to baselines (R1). How does the method scale with dataset size? (R3).
  - Lack of visualization results; only quantitative tables are provided, making it hard to assess qualitative segmentation quality (R3).
  - No discussion of limitations, such as scalability constraints or computational resource requirements (R3). 
  - R1 points out a puzzling result in Table 1: the fully trained model on all sub-domains outperforms any single sub-domain’s training/test split, which seems to contradict the underlying motivation. R1 asks for an explanation.
  - The code link is broken (R4).

- **Theoretical and methodological gaps** (R3, R4):
  - The sub-domain classification strategy (density probability vector) is not explained in detail; how is the SDC trained? (R3).
  - The paper does not discuss how to determine the optimal number of sub-domains across datasets (R3, R4 related).
  - R3 questions whether using ResNet-18 as the base architecture is too weak to capture complex details.
  - R4 suggests the framework could be extended to multimodal data (visual + LiDAR), but this is a missing exploration rather than a current weakness.

**Reviewer disagreement**: R3 (score 6, confidence 4) is the only positive reviewer, finding the work effective and well-analyzed. In contrast, R2 (score 3, confidence 5) and R4 (score 3, confidence 5) are strongly critical of the experimental rigor and comparisons. R1 (score 3, confidence 3) shares many of these concerns. The overall assessment across reviewers is that the paper’s novelty is limited and the evaluation is insufficient to support its claims.
