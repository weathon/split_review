## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes PoincareNorm, a normalization method to mitigate over-smoothing in GNNs by extending the Dirichlet energy to higher-order discrete derivatives of node features. The method generalizes PairNorm using Poincaré inequality–based bounds. Theoretical analysis shows equivalences among proposed measures and existing ones. Experiments on three datasets (Cora, CiteSeer, PubMed) for semi-supervised node classification compare PoincareNorm against baselines including PairNorm, GraphNorm, NodeNorm. Reviewer scores vary: one reviewer (score 6) found strong theory and clear presentation; three reviewers (score 3) raised concerns about motivation, distinction from prior work, and empirical significance.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important topic (over-smoothing in GNNs) with a clever idea reusing existing tools (PairNorm, Dirichlet energy).
- Provides a thorough literature review on over-smoothing characterization, emergence, and mitigation methods, establishing a sound set of baselines.
- Includes theoretical foundation (Poincaré inequality, bounds on Dirichlet energy, decay rate analysis) and informative discussion of existing GNN normalization methods.
- Conducts thorough analysis motivated by prior decay-rate evaluations; noticeable improvements of PoincareNorm over existing methods in Table 1.
- Presentation is clear and easy to follow according to one reviewer; other reviewers found clarity lacking (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation for higher-order derivatives is insufficiently articulated.** It is not clearly explained why first-order Dirichlet energy is inadequate for characterizing over-smoothing or how higher-order derivatives connect to the phenomenon. This disconnect weakens the theoretical framing. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **Distinction from prior work is unclear.** Section 3.1 lifts definitions from Rusch et al. (2023a) without explicit citation in definitions/theorems, blurring the line between novel contributions and existing results. The paper spends most of its space showing equivalence among metrics (e.g., Theorem 4.2: “to control ε_D(X) and Laplacian energy … we need only to control TPD_p(Δ_μX)”) but never explains the *advantages* of the proposed measures over previous ones (e.g., PairNorm). (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Connection between Poincaré norm and higher-order derivatives is not justified.** The paper motivates higher-order derivatives but then introduces PoincareNorm, whose relation to non-Euclidean geometry is unclear. A reviewer asks: “Is p-PoincareNorm_0 (page 3) a norm? Can you demonstrate this?” (Reviewers 3, 2)
- **Experimental gains are marginal or suboptimal in Tables 2 and 3.** While Table 1 shows clear improvements, PoincareNorm’s performance against GraphNorm and NodeNorm is often comparable or worse in deeper-layer settings. This undercuts claims of addressing over-smoothing across all depths. (Reviewer 2)
- **Figure 1 interpretation is ambiguous.** The left plot’s y-axis is not labelled (presumably accuracy?). Best accuracies occur with few layers; PoincareNorm only outperforms others in a region where all methods perform worse than shallow models. This raises doubt about whether the method truly combats over-smoothing. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing details on experimental protocol.** It is unclear how numbers in Tables 1–3 were computed given varying layer counts per model (GCN, GAT, SGC). Per-layer breakdowns would demonstrate stability. (Reviewer 1)
- **Lack of evaluation on large-scale graphs.** Scalability and applicability to real-world, complex graphs (e.g., OGB) are not assessed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Over-smoothing mitigation context is incomplete.** The paper would benefit from a broader overview of over-smoothing techniques to better situate PoincareNorm’s unique contributions and limitations relative to other approaches. (Reviewer 1)
- **Technical errors:** “Ponicare” (page 7) should be “Poincare”. (Reviewer 3)
- **Problem scope is defined too late (Section 3).** Until then, the paper’s focus is ambiguous. (Reviewer 3)
- **One reviewer (score 6) found the paper theoretically sound and clearly presented**, but this view is contradicted by other reviewers who found the presentation unclear and motivation lacking.
