## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes recurrent alternatives to the transformer self-attention mechanism, building on the Linear Transformer (Katharopoulos et al., 2020) and subsequent works (Peng et al., 2021). The authors introduce ReLiT and its low-rank approximation AReLiT, which incorporate a learned gating mechanism and a learnable feature map (based on outer products) to address issues of unbounded positive value accumulation and fixed kernel functions in prior linear transformers. The methods are evaluated on partially-observable reinforcement learning tasks (T-Maze, Noisy CartPole, MysteryPath, Memory Maze) and compared to the GTrXL baseline. The authors report 40–50% reduction in inference cost and >50% memory reduction while achieving similar or better performance (up to 37% improvement on harder tasks). The paper is generally well-written and the low-rank approximation is theoretically motivated.

### Strengths

1. **Well-motivated and clearly presented** The paper explains in detail where inference costs arise in vanilla and linear transformers, and the problem of unbounded positive value accumulation in recurrent state updates is clearly identified. The writing is easy to follow.

2. **Strong empirical efficiency** On RL environments, ReLiT and AReLiT achieve 40–50% cheaper inference and >50% memory reduction compared to GTrXL while maintaining or improving average reward. This demonstrates clear practical benefits under memory/compute constraints.

3. **Creative theoretical component** The low-rank approximation of the Kronecker delta function by cosine functions in AReLiT is well theoretically motivated and inspiring. The approximation reduces space complexity from O(d²) to O(dk) (k ≪ d), making the approach more practically viable.

4. **Good choice of evaluation environments** The selected RL tasks (especially T-Maze and Noisy CartPole) are well-suited for testing long-range dependency modeling and contextual memory, as they require remembering past observations/actions over long horizons.

5. **Incremental but original contribution** While building on prior linear transformer work (Katharopoulos et al., 2020; Peng et al., 2021), the introduction of an index-wise gating mechanism and learnable feature map represents a meaningful extension that the community will find significant.

### Weaknesses

1. **Missing direct comparisons with key baselines** The experiments do not include comparisons with the original Linear Transformer (Katharopoulos et al., 2020) or the gated variant from Peng et al. (2021), from which the work directly builds. The paper only compares against GTrXL, leaving unclear whether improvements stem from the proposed components or simply from using a recurrent linear architecture. Similarly, no comparison is provided with other recent recurrent transformers such as RWKV (Peng et al., 2023) or Recurrent Memory Transformers.

2. **Inadequate ablation and isolation of contributions** The paper lacks a thorough ablation study that disentangles the impact of the gating mechanism, the learnable feature map, the outer-product representation, and the low-rank approximation. Only a single ablation appears in the supplementary (Section K). Without isolating each component, it is impossible to know which part drives performance gains. For example: what happens if the low-rank approximation is used with a fixed kernel function, or if the gating is removed but the learnable kernel retained?

3. **Unclear motivation and necessity of the learnable feature map** The claim that the kernel function is “learnable” is misleading because the kernel class is still fixed to a specific functional form (outer-product based). It is not justified why this particular parameterization is superior to simply using a small neural network for the feature map φ, nor is there a clear explanation of why the outer product structure is needed. The paper does not demonstrate that the learnable kernel outperforms fixed ones in practice.

4. **Inconsistent and potentially incorrect reporting of complexity** 
   - The time complexity of standard self-attention is given as O(N d²), whereas it should be O(N² d + N d²) (reviewer 2). 
   - The claim that Linear Transformer inference cost per token is independent of context length N is presented without clarifying that the cost for an entire sequence still scales linearly with N. This comparison is not made consistent: standard transformer costs are given for a token matrix, but linear transformer costs are given for a single token.
   - A step-by-step derivation of time and space complexities for all variants (standard, linear, ReLiT, AReLiT) is needed to substantiate the efficiency claims.

5. **Lack of evaluation on standard transformer benchmarks** The experiments are exclusively on RL tasks. Given that the paper is motivated by general-purpose sequence modeling, evaluation on standard tasks such as language modeling (e.g., WikiText-103, PG-19) or long-range dependency benchmarks (Long Range Arena – LRA, ListOps) is necessary to assess general applicability. The choice of RL only is not sufficiently justified, especially when prior linear transformers (Katharopoulos et al., 2020; Peng et al., 2021) were evaluated on language and image tasks.

6. **Insufficient empirical investigation of the “positive addition” issue** The paper cites unbounded positive value accumulation in the linear transformer recurrence as a key motivation, but provides no empirical evidence that this actually causes failure in practice, nor does it demonstrate how the gating mechanism alleviates it.

7. **Missing wall-clock time and memory comparison** The paper reports frames per second and memory savings, but lacks a direct comparison of wall-clock time for training and inference across methods (including the original linear transformer). Such results would more convincingly demonstrate the practical advantage.

8. **Related works omissions** The discussion of efforts to extend transformer context length is incomplete. Important directions such as LongFormer, Recurrent Memory Transformer (RMT), and RWKV are not mentioned, and the paper would benefit from a qualitative comparison with these approaches.

9. **Title and contribution framing** The title “Recurrent Linear Transformers” is overly general; the main contributions are a gating mechanism and a learnable feature map, while the recurrent view itself predates the paper. The relation to Peng et al. (2021), which already introduced scalar gating, should be much more prominently highlighted.

10. **Minor issues** 
    - There are a few typos (e.g., “In the RL” → “In RL”, “addresses” → “address” in Section 3).
    - The paper claims that a positive feature map is necessary for kernel functions, but technically a kernel can be defined without requiring positive feature maps; this point requires clarification.

*Note: Reviewer 4 was notably more critical (score 3) regarding the unclear motivation and missing baselines, while Reviewer 1 was more positive (score 6) about the theoretical contribution and RL results; these contrasts are preserved in the weaknesses above.*
