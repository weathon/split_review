## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes watermarking techniques for diffusion models (DMs) to address legal challenges in copyright protection and generated-content monitoring. It details two pipelines: one for conditional/unconditional DMs (embedding a fixed watermark string during training) and one for Stable Diffusion via DreamBooth (using an uncommon trigger prompt “[V]”). The authors provide empirical ablation studies and practical guidelines. All three reviewers acknowledge the relevance of the topic, the clarity of the writing, and the thoroughness of the experiments, but they raise significant concerns about novelty, watermark quality, insufficient robustness evaluations, and ambiguity about the intended copyright/detection scenarios.

### Strengths
- Addresses a highly relevant and timely issue (copyright protection and content monitoring for generative models).
- The manuscript is well-written and easy to follow, with clear organization and supportive visualizations.
- Experiments are thoroughly and rigorously executed, covering multiple settings (unconditional, class-conditional, text-to-image).
- The proposed watermarking pipelines are efficient and show robustness to some common distortions (masking, noising, brightening), which could have practical implications.
- The work attempts to introduce watermarking into the diffusion model domain, representing a step toward model-level protection (originality).

### Weaknesses
1. **Novelty and technical contribution are limited.**
   - The first pipeline (embedding a fixed watermark string during unconditional/conditional training) has been extensively studied in prior work (e.g., Yu et al., 2022) and is a direct adaptation of existing techniques without significant modifications for diffusion models.
   - Watermarking Stable Diffusion via DreamBooth appears to have limited novelty because DreamBooth is inherently designed for personalizing concepts; the paper simply replaces the concept with a watermark image without providing a new algorithmic insight.
   - The core idea of associating a bit string with training samples to embed a watermark is not novel, and the paper does not sufficiently explore the unique challenges posed by diffusion models compared to other generative models.
   - One reviewer notes that the approach may be incremental, and the paper should provide a more detailed justification for the novelty.

2. **Copyright and detection scenarios are unclear.**
   - The paper does not explicitly state whether the watermarking is intended to protect the model owner or the end-user who downloaded the model. This distinction is crucial for understanding practical implications.
   - If the method is also meant for detecting generated content, the experimental section lacks a comprehensive evaluation (metrics, datasets) of the ability to distinguish real from generated images. The purpose and corresponding evaluation remain unclear.

3. **Watermarked image quality is unsatisfactory.**
   - The average PSNRs reported (e.g., in Table 1) fall below 30 dB. In contrast, most watermarking schemes achieve satisfactory visual quality when PSNR is above 40 dB. This indicates a significant impact of the watermark on visual fidelity.
   - The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the types of distortions introduced or their perceptual impact, nor does it discuss the trade-off between robustness and image quality.

4. **Robustness evaluation is incomplete.**
   - Only three distortions are considered: masking, noising, and brightening. Missing are common manipulations crucial for real-world use, such as JPEG compression (with varying quality factors), rotation, scaling, cropping, shearing, deformation, blurring, and contrast adjustments.
   - The paper does not discuss the method’s resilience to deliberate adversarial attacks aimed at removing or modifying the watermark (e.g., finetuning the latent diffusion model with the trigger prompt “[V]” again to erase the watermark).

5. **Practical limitations of the proposed pipelines.**
   - For unconditional or class-conditional generation, the watermark string is fixed during training. Injecting a new watermark string requires training a new model from scratch, which is time-consuming.
   - One reviewer raises questions about whether it is feasible to watermark only a portion of the training dataset (e.g., 30% or 50%) and how this affects the watermarking objective. This is an important ablation that is missing.
   - For the trigger prompt approach, the paper only considers the uncommon identifier “[V]”; it is unclear whether other rare identifiers (e.g., ‘!M~’) would work, and whether conclusions remain unaffected.

6. **Missing quantitative guarantees and comparisons.**
   - The lower bound of bit-wise accuracy for the watermark is not specified, which is an important metric for assessing robustness.
   - One reviewer references the related work “The Stable Signature” (Fernandez et al., ICCV2023) that should be discussed or compared, as it tackles similar watermarking in latent diffusion models.
