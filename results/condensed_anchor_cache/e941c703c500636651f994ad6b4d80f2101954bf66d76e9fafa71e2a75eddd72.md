## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies convergence guarantees for score-based discrete diffusion models under the CTMC framework on general state space \([S]^d\). It proposes a discrete-time sampling algorithm using score estimators at fixed time points and derives KL divergence and TV distance bounds that are nearly linear in dimension \(d\), matching state-of-the-art continuous models. The analysis uses a Girsanov‑based method and introduces properties of the discrete score function. Reviewers agree the paper addresses an important theoretical problem and provides rigorous bounds, but raise concerns about clarity of notation, strength of assumptions (particularly Assumption 2 requiring constant \(L\) independent of \(d\)), incremental nature compared to prior work (Chen & Ying), justification for early stopping in the discrete setting, lack of practical experiments, and insufficient comparison with existing methods.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles a timely and important problem: theoretical analysis of discrete diffusion models with finite state spaces and discrete time, with relevance to LLMs (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- It provides rigorous convergence bounds for KL divergence (Theorem 1 with early stopping, Theorem 2 without) and TV distance that scale nearly linearly in dimension \(d\), aligning with state‑of‑the‑art continuous models (Reviewer 2, 4).
- The discrete‑time sampling algorithm (Algorithm 1) enables efficient sampling without simulating the continuous CTMC path, and score clipping is introduced to handle extreme estimates (Reviewer 2).
- The Girsanov‑based convergence analysis is a creative adaptation of continuous‑domain techniques to the discrete setting (Reviewer 2).
- Assumptions are weaker than those of existing algorithms (Reviewer 4), and the theoretical framework may facilitate further algorithmic improvements (Reviewer 4).
- Proofs have been checked and are scientifically correct (Reviewer 3).
- The problem is interesting and the authors have pushed further existing methods (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
**Clarity and Notation**
- The paper is often unclear: notation is confusing, important details are omitted, and mathematical clarity is lacking (Reviewer 1, Presentation score 2).  
  - Proposition 1: Convergence of \(q_t\) is not defined (what “approaching” means); the limit is a fixed matrix times \(p_{data}\), not necessarily uniform.  
  - Line 280: The statement “With rate \(Q^{\leftarrow}_t\)…” is not explained or referenced.  
  - Equation (4): No reference or proof is given.  
  - The comparison of proof techniques with SDE‑based diffusion models is insufficiently detailed; specific challenges in adapting to CTMCs should be highlighted (Reviewer 3).

**Assumptions**
- Assumption 2 (full support, uniform bounds, ratio constant \(L\) independent of \(d\)) is considered very strong and not easy to verify (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).  
  - No general class of distributions is known to satisfy it (Reviewer 1).  
  - The assumption that \(L\) is independent of \(d\) is doubtful, especially for LLM applications where \(d\) is large; its validity needs rigorous justification and a numerical analysis of \(L\) as \(d\) grows (Reviewer 3).  
  - It may restrict the model’s applicability (Reviewer 2).  
- Theorem 2: The quantity \(\kappa_i\) can be undefined when some probabilities are zero in a discrete distribution (Reviewer 1).  
- Uniform bound on scores and estimators (line 361): The bound \(C_1\) must depend on \(\delta\); this dependence should be quantified (Reviewer 1).

**Comparison with Prior Work**
- The main proof ideas are very similar to Chen & Ying (2022); the extension from \(\{0,1\}^d\) to \([S]^d\) is considered incremental by one reviewer (Reviewer 3). Even so, the required justification for mixing time bounds in the general state space is lacking (Reviewer 3).  
- The comparison with Chen & Ying in the paper’s table is unclear; the differences in assumptions should be explicitly stated (Reviewer 4).  
- No runtime or convergence speed comparison is provided with other discrete sampling methods (e.g., uniformization), which would substantiate claims of improved efficiency (Reviewer 2).  
- There is limited discussion on when to use the discrete CTMC approach versus discretized continuous diffusion models for discrete data (Reviewer 2).

**Early Stopping**
- The necessity of early stopping in the discrete‑time scheme is not justified. Score matching errors are large near \(t=0\) in continuous dynamics, but the discrete scheme may behave differently; a detailed explanation of why early stopping is needed and how it relates to potential score function divergence at \(t=0\) is missing (Reviewer 3).

**Generalizability and Practical Impact**
- No experimental tests are performed; the practical impact of the theoretical contribution remains unknown (Reviewer 4).  
- Generalization to more complex discrete spaces (e.g., graph‑based or hierarchical state spaces) is not discussed (Reviewer 2).  
- The algorithm requires maximizing the estimated score function (Algorithm 1, line 3); it is unclear how easy this is in practice and whether concavity assumptions are needed (Reviewer 1).

**Tightness of Bounds**
- The tightness of the derived bounds is unclear; a comparison with bounds from continuous diffusion models would help identify fundamental differences (Reviewer 4).  
- Adaptive step sizes could reduce discretization error; the effect on the convergence bound is not explored (Reviewer 2).

**Minor Issues**
- Typos: line 187 “scala”, line 206 “Nota”, line 323 “We” capitalized mid‑sentence (Reviewer 1).  
- Some statements (e.g., line 296: reverse process time‑inhomogeneous, line 723: Kronecker sum) lack clear justification or references (Reviewer 1).
