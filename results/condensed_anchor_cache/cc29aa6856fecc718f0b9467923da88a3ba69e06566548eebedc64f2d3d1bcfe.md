## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the value discrepancy problem in latent Bayesian optimization (LBO) by using a normalizing flow (NF) as the generative model instead of a VAE, ensuring a one-to-one invertible mapping between latent and input spaces. The authors propose SeqFlow, an autoregressive sequence-specific normalizing flow, and a token-level adaptive candidate sampling (TACS) strategy. They demonstrate strong performance on molecule generation benchmarks, significantly outperforming existing LBO and other optimization methods.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles an important and previously recognized problem in latent BayesOpt—the value discrepancy (or misalignment) between latent and decoded objective values, which has been observed since Stanton et al. (2022) [11] and independently in recent work (not yet public).
- The work is the first to introduce normalizing flows as an encoder-decoder model in latent space Bayesian optimization, representing a novel departure from prior VAE-based approaches.
- The TACS procedure for selecting token positions to vary is sensible and closely resembles similar techniques used in MaskGIT (Chang et al., 2022) and LaMBO-2 (Gruver et al., 2024), though with differences in normalization.
- The methodology addresses an important pathology in LBO by enforcing invertibility, and the paper is well-written and well-motivated.
- Evaluation is extensive on a widely-used benchmark (PMO), where NF-BO with TACS greatly outperforms a suite of competing methods including RL, genetic algorithms, and other BO methods, often achieving much better solutions in a fraction of the oracle calls. This suggests broad interest to the optimization community.
- The method components (autoregressive flow, TACS sampler) appear straightforward to implement and use for further applications.

### Weaknesses
- **Value discrepancy verification missing**: The core claim that improved performance stems from eliminating value discrepancy is not properly tested. The current comparisons to baselines are confounded by many differing implementation choices. An ablation that intentionally reintroduces value discrepancy (e.g., making the smallest possible change to the code) is needed to causally attribute the gains.
- **Missing simple baselines**: The paper does not compare to the simplest prior solutions to value discrepancy: frequently decoding and checking the actual objective value [1], and reinitializing latent variables from improved solutions [3].
- **Value gap not systematically measured**: The value discrepancy (actual vs. latent objective) is only quantified in an illustrative figure in the introduction; no systematic measurement or reporting is provided.
- **Incomplete literature view**: The paper has an incomplete view of previous literature on this topic (e.g., missing citations for originating BO papers, missing discussion of related works like [4–8] that consider diversity or structure latent spaces).
- **Reproducibility concerns**: Code is not provided. Crucial details are missing: the GP surrogate model type (vanilla/sparse), the acquisition function (EI/PI/Thompson sampling), kernel choice, and GP hyperparameter optimization procedure. Without these, the empirical results are not reproducible from the manuscript alone.
- **Confusing notation in Section 4.2**: The description of SeqFlow is abstract. Unclear what L, F represent; Equation 8’s argmax variable is ambiguous; the embedding dictionary e is never fully specified (e.g., how are embeddings computed?).
- **Missing specification of acquisition function**: It is never explicitly stated in the text or appendix which acquisition function is used.
- **TACS weaknesses**: TACS will tend not to choose positions that have not been varied in the past, making it heavily reliant on a complete characterization of the search space in the initial data (cold-start problem). The temperature hyperparameter is presumably tuned by looking at performance curves, which may overestimate real-world performance; no adaptive heuristic is proposed.
- **Expressivity tradeoff**: Normalizing flows impose a restrictive parameterization that often reduces expressivity; the authors do not discuss this tradeoff or its potential impact on optimization.
- **Deterministic decoder implications**: The problem formulation for LBO with normalizing flows assumes a deterministic decoder, differing from the usual probabilistic decoder formulation. The implications for the optimization process are not discussed.
- **Sensitivity to λ in similarity loss (Eq. 14)**: Not analyzed.
- **Table 1 score definition**: It not specified directly in the main paper what the reported score represents (the sum of scores of individual PMO tasks); only found later in the appendix.
- **Errorbar reporting**: In Figures 5 and 7, the method for computing error bars (standard error vs. standard deviation) and the number of trials are not reported in captions.
- **Minor writing issues**: Typos (line 50 “makes the value discrepancy problem more and makes”, line 484 missing table number, line 485 “metrics”, line 893 “number of query points”), missing capitalization in references, missing full stop in Equation 4, unclear notation changes (v in Eq. 3, y as scalar, L in Eq. 8).
- **Missing details**: Relation to Ho et al. (2019) unclear (line 255); embedding e not fully specified; contrastive loss (Eq. 13) not discussed in relation to deep metric learning or the possibility of incorporating label information to improve GP fit.
- **Ablation question**: Would TACS combined with a VAE model (instead of a flow) improve as much as with the proposed approach?
- **Hyperparameter choices**: How was the λ parameter in the similarity loss chosen? Are results sensitive to it? Was coordinate ascent considered instead of scalarization?
- **Unclear remark on GP posterior**: Line 331 states “sampling the full posterior function from the GP posterior distribution is infeasible”—it is unclear why this is relevant or if Thompson Sampling is assumed.
- **Algorithm 1**: TopK on line 2 is not defined.
- **Diversity and temperature**: For the rano task, why does lower temperature reduce diversity (Appendix C)?
- **Embedding dictionary specification**: In Appendix F, how is the embedding dictionary specified?
- **Suggested benchmarks**: The authors could consider additional test functions and baselines, e.g., Ehrlich test functions from the poli project (Stanton et al., 2024) for rapid ablation.
