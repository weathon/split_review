## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LoR-VP, which introduces low-rank matrix multiplication into visual prompting to share information across image patches. Reviewers are divided: one is highly positive (score 8, confidence 4), one moderately positive (score 6, confidence 3), and one more critical (score 5, confidence 4). All agree the paper is well-written and the method is simple and novel. However, concerns are raised about missing comparisons (e.g., VPT), limited evaluation scope (only classification, only ImageNet variants for OOD), insufficient justification for linear probing, lack of theoretical guarantees, and weaknesses in ablation studies and preliminary analysis control.

### Strengths
- Identifies limitations of existing pad-based visual prompting, which restrict prompt-image interaction to a small set of patches (R1, R2).
- Novel visual prompt design using low-rank matrix multiplication that enables shared and patch-specific information across rows and columns (R1, R3).
- Organic integration of LoRA and Visual Prompting – combining two established concepts in a seamless way to enhance both parameter efficiency and performance (R3).
- Method is simple yet effective, with clear preliminary analysis motivating the design (R2, R3).
- Well-written, easy to follow, with clear background and reasoning (R1, R2, R3).
- Extensive experiments across seven network architectures and multiple datasets show convincing improvements: up to 6× faster training, 18× fewer prompt parameters, and 3.1% average performance gain over SOTA (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing exploration of alternative sharing strategies:** Only low-rank multiplication is tested; other ways of sharing information across patches are not considered (R1).
- **Superficial conclusions:** The paper does not offer deeper insight into experimental results or the strengths/weaknesses of the proposed approach (R1).
- **Linear probing justification insufficient:** The use of linear probing for label transformation is simple; it is unclear why it is more appropriate than other transform models (R1).
- **Questionable claim about pad-based interaction for ViT:** The assertion that VP parameters are restricted to interacting with a limited set of patches is questionable for ViT backbones, where padded tokens interact with all tokens via self-attention. The degree of this interaction and its effect on spatial locality preservation or dilution needs further investigation (R2).
- **Missing comparison with VPT:** Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) [1], a more prevalent method, is not compared or discussed, making it hard to assess relative performance (R2).
- **Insufficient OOD evaluation:** Out-of-distribution experiments use only ImageNet variants with minimal domain gaps, all on general object recognition. A benchmark like AutoVP [2] with fine-grained and domain-specific tasks is recommended for more comprehensive generalization analysis (R2).
- **AutoVP vs. LP baseline issue:** LP often outperforms AutoVP, contradicting the AutoVP paper. The performance gap between LoR-VP and LP is minimal, raising questions about whether the gains come from low-rank adaptation or from using LP. Replacing AutoVP’s output transformation with LP would clarify (R2).
- **Evaluation limited to image classification:** As a general PEFT method, the approach is only tested on classification; no experiments on segmentation, detection, or captioning (R2, also R1 question).
- **Preliminary study lacks variable control:** The impact of image scaling is not isolated from patch-specific information. Design 4 (Patch-Same) may succeed due to not scaling the image rather than shared prompting, leaving ambiguity about the true cause of improvement (R3).
- **No formal mathematical proof for row/column information link:** The methodology lacks a formal proof of how information across rows and columns is linked; the inductive bias assumptions remain vague. The low-rank approach does not explicitly guarantee preservation of the natural relationship between neighboring pixels (R3).
- **Lack of theoretical guarantees:** The method relies on empirical results without providing guarantees on convergence, optimality, or robustness, which may limit adoption in critical applications (R3).
- **Ablation studies unclear on component contributions:** Comparisons across output transformations (ILM, FLM, LP) do not separate the contribution of the output transformation from the LoR-VP component. Additionally, ViT models show a noticeable performance drop with ILM and FLM while Swin models do not; this discrepancy is not investigated or explained (R3).
- **No analysis of failure cases or edge scenarios:** Robustness evaluation (Section 5.3) focuses on OOD generalization but does not test against adversarial attacks or high noise, which are critical for real-world deployment. Without such analysis, the method’s reliability in challenging environments is unclear (R3).
