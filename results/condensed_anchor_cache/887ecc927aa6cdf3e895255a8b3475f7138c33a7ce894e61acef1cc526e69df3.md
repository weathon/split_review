## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Distilled Linear Multistep Solver (DLMS) to learn a faster diffusion sampler with fewer function evaluations (NFE). A lightweight designer network dynamically predicts the coefficients, time step schedules, and scaling factors of a linear multistep solver, trained by minimizing the Euclidean distance between its trajectory and a teacher solver’s trajectory. The method achieves notable FID scores (e.g., 3.23 on CIFAR10, 7.16 on ImageNet-64, 5.44 on LSUN-Bedroom, 12.52 on MS-COCO with 5 NFE) and claims over 10× faster search efficiency than RL-based frameworks, training for Stable-Diffusion in under 12 total GPU hours. Two reviewers (score 8) found the idea novel and experiments convincing; two reviewers (score 6) raised concerns about resource costs, missing comparisons, limited analysis, and adaptability.

### Strengths
- Well-motivated method that can be initialized using existing solvers and is simpler than reinforcement learning approaches.
- Unlike model distillation, solver distillation (DLMS) can be used for downstream tasks like image restoration.
- Flexible framework unifying existing linear multistep solvers and enabling dynamic solver strategies for different ODE trajectories.
- Experiments cover multiple contexts (unconditional, conditional, latent space, pixel space) and multiple datasets (CIFAR10, ImageNet-64, LSUN-Bedroom, MS-COCO) with convincing FID improvements, especially at low NFE.
- Compared to a number of diffusion-specific solvers.
- Designer network training is more efficient than search-based solvers.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons and literature gaps:**  
  - Method is not compared to other solver distillation methods such as DPM-solver-v3 (Zheng et al., 2023) or Bespoke Non-Stationary Solvers (Shaul et al., 2024).  
  - A closely related paper (R4’s [1]) that computes optimal coefficients per timestep via a closed-form quadratic solution is not mentioned; its negligible computational cost should be contrasted.
- **Limited evaluation metrics:**  
  Only FID scores are reported; adding metrics like IS or CMMD would increase confidence (R1).
- **Designer network details and resource concerns:**  
  - “Bottleneck feature” used as input is not explained—only a reference given (R2).  
  - Size of the designer network is not provided (R2, R3).  
  - Training requires a separate lightweight network per pre-trained model, which may be resource-intensive (R4 notes not every institute has 8 A100/H100 GPUs).  
  - Algorithm 1 implies each NFE configuration may need an independent designer network; computational cost of training/storing multiple networks is not addressed (R2, R3, R4).  
  - How the designer network ensures \(t_N=0\) (or becomes reasonably small) for different NFE choices is unclear (R2, R3).
- **Time scaling factor and stability:**  
  Motivation and explanation of the time scaling factor’s interaction with the noise schedule are lacking; analysis of its impact on convergence and overall stability is missing (R3).
- **Adaptability to transformer-based diffusion models:**  
  The designer network relies on U-Net intermediate features; discussion on potential challenges and modifications for transformer architectures is absent (R3).
- **Figures and tables not self-contained:**  
  E.g., Figure 4 dashed lines significance is not described (R1).
- **Training time discrepancy:**  
  Abstract claims “under 10 total GPU hours” for Stable-Diffusion, but line 398 reports ~12 hours (1.5 × 8 = 12). Clarify if this is rounding or different claims (R1).
- **Selection of interpolation steps M:**  
  Results for LSUN-Bedroom (Latent-Diffusion) and MS-COCO (Stable-Diffusion) use M=1; teacher with double steps should theoretically outperform student—why not? And why M values differ across setups? Would higher M ever hurt performance? (R4).
- **Need for trajectory-specific illustration:**  
  Provide concrete examples of how the designer network adapts solver parameters (coefficients, time steps) across different ODE trajectories to support the claim that a unified choice is suboptimal (R3).
- **Minor writing issues:**  
  Typo “can be carry out” (line 129); unnecessary word “precious” (line 189); reformatting needed for “PLMS (iPNDM)” and “AMED-Plugin (Zhou et al., 2024)” and “MS-COCO (2014)” and “Handcrafted (best)” (R1).
