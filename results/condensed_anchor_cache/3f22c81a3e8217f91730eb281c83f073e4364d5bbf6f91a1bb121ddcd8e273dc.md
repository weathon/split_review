## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proves the fast equilibrium conjecture for SGD on normalized networks in a more general setting than prior works (Wang & Wang 2022; Li et al. 2022c), removing the assumptions that minima are isolated, form a unique basin, and that the minima set is analytic. The main technical contribution is to show that with probability close to 1, trajectories do not escape the initial basin in exponential time, using Arnold-Kliemann's condition and large deviation principles. Experiments on MNIST support the theory.

### Strengths
- Solid mathematical contribution that extends the fast equilibrium conjecture to a more general setting by removing non-generic assumptions (unique basin, analyticity). (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- Clear and well-structured presentation with pleasant notation; easy to grasp main contributions. (Reviewers 2, 3; Reviewer 4 notes mostly clear with minor errors)
- Remarks 1.4 and 1.5 provide well-supported arguments that Assumptions 1.2 are natural and Assumptions 1.3 are non-generic, effectively motivating their removal. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- Demonstrates mastery of the subject with useful citations and discussion of existing results; narrative and discussion leave little doubt about correctness. (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 2 followed proof at high level)
- Originality in using Arnold-Kliemann's condition (to remove analyticity) and the large deviation principle of Dembo-Zeitouni (to remove unique basin assumption). (Reviewer 4)
- One reviewer (Reviewer 3) is particularly enthusiastic: finds the paper very well-written, the Brownian motion projection discussion interesting, and expects to return to it for details.

### Weaknesses
1. **Inconsistent notation for mixing times**: There is confusion between the abstract ($e^{O(\eta^{-1})}$) and introduction ($e^{O((\eta\lambda)^{-1})}$ and $e^{O((\eta\lambda)^{-1/2}}$). This should be clarified and unified. (Reviewers 3, 4)
2. **Experiments limited to MNIST**: Only MNIST is used, which is nearly linearly separable and lacks high dimensionality/complex feature interactions. The fast equilibrium conjecture may hold due to MNIST's simplicity, but validity on more complex datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10, ImageNet) remains unclear. (Reviewer 4; Reviewer 3 also suggests illustrating rates with evidence from datasets/networks)
3. **Missing related work discussion**: The paper does not cite or discuss works on linear mode connectivity and the lottery ticket hypothesis (Frankle et al. 2020; Gupta et al. 2019), which argue that iterates stay in the same basin when starting from the same initialization. Also missing are analyses of SGD/Local SGD dynamics near the manifold of minimizers (Damian et al. 2021; Gu et al. 2022). (Reviewer 1)
4. **Mislabeling of second experiment as "stochastic weight averaging" (SWA)**: SWA averages parameters at different iterations along one trajectory, whereas the paper averages parameters at the same iterations from different trajectories. (Reviewer 1)
5. **Paper is too abstract; needs concrete examples**: The authors should provide specific examples where the removed assumptions (unique basin, analyticity) are too restrictive, to illustrate why the generalization is needed. (Reviewer 1; also related to Reviewer 2's question about whether remaining assumptions are essential and could be weakened)
6. **Need theoretical reasoning for why Assumption 1.2 might be essential** to prove the fast equilibrium conjecture. (Reviewer 2)
7. **Result does not explain fast convergence of normalized networks compared to non-normalized networks** (the motivation in the abstract). One reviewer (Reviewer 3) rates contribution as fair for this reason; another (Reviewer 4) notes significance is limited because it only relaxes assumptions for an existing conjecture rather than discovering new phenomena.
8. **Several minor errors and typos**:
   - First line of Section 4: "Assumption 1.3. (ii)" should be "Assumption 1.3. (i)". (Reviewer 2)
   - "We now stop assuming Assumption 1.3.(ii)" should be (i) (about unique basin removal). (Reviewer 4)
   - "Recall that (Li et al., 2022c) also assumes Assumption 1.3.(i)" should be (ii) (about analyticity removal). (Reviewer 4)
   - "Figure 5 shows" should be "Figure 1". (Reviewer 4)
   - Uncommon citation format used in some places. (Reviewer 4)
   - Abstract uses $\tilde{O}(1/\eta\lambda)$ which should be $\tilde{O}(\frac{1}{\eta\lambda})$. (Reviewer 4)
9. **Open technical questions that represent gaps or missing analysis** (should be addressed or discussed):
   - Does the noise structure affect the convergence rate? (Reviewer 2)
   - Would it be possible to achieve a similar result if $L$ had a homogeneity degree $>0$? (Reviewer 2)
   - Are the three remaining assumptions (Assumption 1.2) all essential? Can they be weakened? (Reviewer 2)
   - What is the convergence rate to a local minimum if the noise term is dropped? Provide analysis in a simplified setting. (Reviewer 3)
   - Is there a guarantee from previous literature that constant gradient noise in the non-normalized setting translates to the noise projection structure in the normalized case (formulas 4-5 vs discrete update)? (Reviewer 3)
10. **Lack of illustrative figure**: Consider adding a headline figure showing the speed of convergence to the stationary distribution and the proven rate, to attract broader interest. (Reviewer 3)
