## Merged Review

### Summary
The four reviewers find that this paper proposes RealSurf, a surface representation framework for LiDAR point cloud segmentation that extends the synthetic-domain RepSurf method to real autonomous driving scans. The framework includes a Point Sliding Module and LiDAR-based surfel reconstruction with FPS downsampling. It is evaluated on nuScenes, SemanticKITTI, and Waymo, reporting SOTA results. Three reviewers rated it 5 (fair), one rated it 6 (positive). The minority reviewer (R4) is more positive about contribution and soundness (Contribution 3, Soundness 3) compared to the others (Contribution 2, Soundness 2–3). All agree the paper is well-written and the experiments are extensive, but raise concerns about technical novelty, fair comparisons, and missing analyses.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1, R4).
- The motivation is practical and reasonable for processing LiDAR point clouds (R1).
- Extensive evaluation on three popular outdoor benchmarks (nuScenes, SemanticKITTI, Waymo) with state-of-the-art results (R1, R2, R3).
- Thorough ablation studies demonstrate the effectiveness of each proposed module (R2).
- The framework is novel for LiDAR point clouds and can be integrated into any point-based network (R3).
- The idea of extending RepSurf to real LiDAR data is interesting (R4).
- Using a basic PointNet++ backbone, the method achieves top-tier performance on multiple demanding datasets (R3).
- Experimentation supports the capability of the proposal for segmentation (R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited technical contribution** – The core ideas (Point Sliding Module, FPS downsampling) are common techniques. Point Sliding is a data augmentation method that jitters points, which is routinely used to handle noisy real scans (e.g., MinkowskiEngine dataloader) (R2). The contributions relative to RepSurf are incremental; the main effect of the added strategies seems to be making real data uniform and resilient to noise (R1, R3).
2. **Potentially unfair comparisons** – The entire framework is essentially PointNet++ with tangent-plane information added; the large improvements over PointNet++ (e.g., ~50 points in Table 2) suggest that some state-of-the-art tricks or unfair settings may be involved. Comparisons should unify settings and show results on top-performing methods, given that RealSurf is framework-agnostic (R3). No comparison with naive baselines such as Occupancy Network + Marching Cubes (R2).
3. **Missing comparisons to recent methods** – No comparison with recent point cloud works (R1) or transformer-based extractors (Point Transformer, Voxel Transformer) (R4). A hybrid point-voxel approach (Point-Voxel CNN) is not considered (R2 question).
4. **Writing and presentation issues** – Some sections are complex and could be clarified; there are many lines with only one word, which is unprofessional and should be avoided (R3).
5. **Motivation not sufficiently emphasized** – The core motivation (whether it is primarily the Point Sliding Module) is subtle; the contribution appears incremental and could be better highlighted (R3).
6. **Unanswered questions / missing experiments** that overlap with criticisms:
   - Would the same strategies (downsampling, densification) benefit synthetic data? (R1)
   - After the densification process (Fig. 2), why does the number of points not increase? (R1)
   - How many points are left after different amounts of downsampling? (R2)
   - How does the method perform on indoor dense point clouds with more occlusions? (R2)
   - Is the Point Sliding hyperparameter (0.5 for Gaussian on SemanticKITTI, Fig. 7) consistent across benchmarks? (R2)
   - Is the method limited to PointNet++? (R2)
   - The Point Sliding Module introduces randomness (coefficients from uniform or Gaussian); there is no analysis of variance in results across multiple runs or stability of the method (R4).
   - Clarify how the FPS downsampling in RealSurf differs from that in PointNet++; is it used in the same way with modifications? (R4)
   - Can the method generalize to low-level point cloud tasks (denoising, upsampling) given its reliance on surface normals and triangle meshes? (R4)
   - Can it be applied back to dense or synthetic point clouds, and would the computational overhead justify it when simpler methods already perform well? (R4)
   - Provide a detailed analysis of computational complexity (time and memory) compared to PointNet++ and other competing methods (R4).
