## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents GETMusic, a framework for multi-track symbolic music generation. It introduces GETScore, a 2D token grid (tracks × timesteps) where each cell contains a pitch token and a duration token; polyphony is encoded by flattening note combinations into single tokens (vocabulary up to 1555 for piano, larger for drums). GETDiff is a discrete diffusion model that corrupts target tracks by masking tokens and learns to predict them conditioned on source tracks, supporting arbitrary source-target combinations. The model is evaluated against several baselines on objective metrics and a listening test.

### Strengths
- First application of discrete diffusion to symbolic music generation (Reviewer 1).
- GETScore is well motivated, clearly formulated, and novel in the context of prior work. It is compact compared to piano-roll representations and could be useful for other generative and MIR tasks (Reviewer 3). The representation addresses several important conditional generation scenarios (Reviewer 2).
- Generated samples sound good (Reviewer 1); the demo page is impressive (Reviewer 3).
- Evaluation is thorough with a significant number of experiments; the proposed approach performs well on both objective metrics and subjective listening (Reviewer 3). The paper is well-structured and clearly presented (Reviewer 2).
- **Minority positive view (Reviewer 3):** Judges GETScore as a serious contribution and the framework as an advancement within non-autoregressive symbolic music generation.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and missing references:** The overall contribution is limited because diffusion models for symbolic music and conditional track generation have already been explored (e.g., AccomMontage, SongDriver – Reviewer 2; CoCoNet – Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). The training objective (randomly masking tokens and predicting them) and inference procedure (blocked Gibbs sampling) are highly similar to CoCoNet (Huang et al., 2019), which should be referenced and the differences explicitly discussed (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). Other relevant works such as the Anticipatory Music Transformer are also omitted (Reviewer 3). The related work survey is incomplete regarding recent conditional generation studies (Reviewer 2).
- **Unsatisfactory handling of polyphony:** The approach of flattening pitch combinations into single tokens leads to a vocabulary of 1555 tokens for piano, which is insufficient to represent all pairs of piano keys. The drum vocabulary is nearly three times larger than the piano vocabulary, raising questions about consistency (Reviewer 1). A better alternative would be to sum/average token embeddings of active pitches on the input side and sample binary presence/absence of each pitch independently on the output side (Reviewer 1).
- **Ablation and experiment design flaws:** The ablation comparing GETDiff with an autoregressive version (14 separate prediction heads) is not well designed: predicting note length cannot directly condition on the predicted pitch, introducing mathematical issues. A more faithful comparison would be against a transformer-decoder trained on a flattened GETScore (context length 7168) (Reviewer 3). The experiments also lack validation of the representation method against alternative representations and the diffusion model against baseline diffusion models (Reviewer 2). Baseline comparisons should include more recent methods than the 4‑year‑old PopMAG (Reviewer 2).
- **Taxonomy and expressivity limitations:** The paper’s division of representations into “image-based” vs. “sequence-based” is confused; each axis (pitch, time) should be considered separately. The dense time axis cannot easily handle expressive timing or triplets without exploding the dimension (Reviewer 1). GETScore cannot represent concurrent notes within a single track that have different offsets (Reviewer 3). Dynamic factors like velocity and tempo variation are not modeled, making it unclear how GETScore compares to sequential representations like REMI (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of clarity on diffusion method novelty:** It is unclear how GETDiff goes beyond standard discrete diffusion. The use of condition flags is new, but the paper does not discuss why previous discrete diffusion methods did not need them (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing comparison:** A direct comparison between GETScore + CoCoNet’s training procedure (same representation, different model) would help isolate the contribution of the diffusion model (Reviewer 3).
- **Clarification needed:** GETScore is symbolic-based; why is it measured in hours? (Reviewer 2).
