## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper theoretically analyzes data augmentation in long-tailed learning, identifying that traditional augmentation methods reintroduce imbalance—creating an intertwined problem of inherent data-wise imbalance and extrinsic augmentation-wise imbalance (“two birds”). To address this, the authors propose Dynamic Optional Data Augmentation (DODA), which adaptively selects augmentations per class by maintaining a class-specific augmentation probability distribution during training. The method is tested on CIFAR-100-LT, ImageNet-LT, and iNaturalist 2018. Reviewers agree on the clear motivation and extensive experiments, but differ on methodological rigor and missing baselines.

### Strengths
- The motivation of choosing suitable data augmentation per class is practical and innovates traditional long-tailed learning. (R1, R3)
- Theoretical analysis explains potential risks of data augmentation in long-tailed settings, including that it may bring hypocritical performance improvements. (R1)
- Extensive comparative experiments on mainstream benchmarks (CIFAR-100-LT, ImageNet-LT, iNaturalist 2018) with analyses at different levels; code is submitted. (R1, R3, R4)
- The method can be orthogonally used with other long-tailed learning algorithms (e.g., LDAM, RIDE). (R4)
- The paper is well written, easy to read; visualization of the training process provides intuitive understanding. (R1, R2, R4)
- The approach shows potential for gains by simply using augmentations efficiently without much computational overhead. (R2)
- Many related studies are reviewed in the appendix. (R4)

### Weaknesses
(Reviewer 4 stated no weaknesses, while Reviewers 1, 2, and 3 raised several concerns.)

**Missing baselines and ablations**
- A critical missing baseline is keeping the per-class weight distribution in DODA fixed as uniform during training. This would isolate the effect of the adaptive adjustment from simply adding extra augmentation. Currently, performance gains could be attributed solely to increased augmentation variety. (R3, confidence 3)
- The method is compared with state-of-the-art long-tailed methods (e.g., LDAM, RIDE, BCL) but not with PaCo (Cui et al., 2021) or NCL (Li et al., 2022). Results on these methods are needed to assess DODA’s true potential. (R2, confidence 5)
- The algorithm optimizes over 10 predefined augmentation functions with fixed strengths. The choice of these 10 augmentations appears arbitrary; no justification or ablation study on the number, type, or strength of augmentations is provided. The fixed strengths may not be optimal across classes or datasets. (R2, confidence 5)
- UniformAugment (LingChen et al., 2020) is a relevant baseline for random augmentation application; it is missing from comparisons. (R3, confidence 3)
- Standard deviations are missing from the main tables; they should be provided in the appendix. (R3, confidence 3)
- A decline in performance is observed in the RIDE column of Table 1 (R3 question, confidence 3). The paper does not explain this drop.

**Scope and applicability**
- The method is limited to intra-class augmentations (applied per single class) and does not incorporate inter-class augmentations like Mixup or Remix (Chou et al., 2020), which have proven effective in imbalance settings. How DODA would combine with Remix for complementary benefits is unexplored, limiting its scope. (R2, confidence 5)
- The paper lacks discussion on scalability to other domains (e.g., graph classification) where long-tailed distributions also occur. (R1, confidence 5)
- The paper does not verify whether the method would be effective on balanced datasets, though the authors suggest it might be. (R3, confidence 3)

**Clarity and explanation**
- In Section 4.3, the claim that “DODA can avoid more classes being sacrificed” is not intuitively supported by Figure 4. A more detailed explanation is needed. (R1, confidence 5)
- A reviewer asks: for contrastive methods like BCL, how is DODA used to generate multiple views of the same image for different classes? The paper does not clarify this. (R2, confidence 5)
- Minor typographic issues: “its” in the caption of Figure 6 and quotation mark problems in LaTeX. (R3, confidence 3)
