## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SLIM, a low-switching primal-dual algorithm for tabular episodic CMDPs with a known strictly safe base policy and known deterministic rewards/costs. It claims improved regret bounds and constant constraint violations (both Õ((SAH^5K)^{1/2}) and Õ(1) violation). The method uses a Bernstein-based exploration bonus, low-switching model updates, and an empirical CMDP with a pessimistically adjusted threshold. The main claimed advance is removing dependency on S and full H from the regret, but this is achieved only for the setting with a known safe policy.

### Strengths
- Achieves a regret bound that reduces dependency on state-action space (S) and planning horizon (H) compared to prior work, which is potentially helpful in large-scale environments.
- Solid theoretical contribution: Reviewer 3 checked Theorem 4.1 and confirmed correctness; proof sketch is clear.
- Detailed comparison table (Table 1) helps situate the result relative to existing algorithms.
- The paper is well-written, with a clear motivation and proof outline.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns** (Reviewer 2, 4): The algorithmic ideas heavily rely on Zhang et al. (2024), with low-switching and analysis borrowed from that reference. The primal-dual framework, optimistic reward/cost bonuses (Efroni et al. 2020), and Bernstein-style bonuses (Jin et al. 2020) are all well-studied. Reviewer 4 is particularly critical: “the contribution is too poor to reach the acceptance threshold for a top conference such as ICLR.”
- **Unclear benefit of low-switching** (Reviewer 1): No formal guarantees or quantitative measure of switching frequency for SLIM; the claimed improvement in regret from reduced switching is not explained step-by-step. How does low switching directly lead to the improved dependency on S and H?
- **Technical clarity needed on empirical CMDP** (Reviewer 1, 3): The motivation and technical advantage of the pessimistic gap Δ_k (subtracted from τ) is unclear. Why this specific gap, and how does it lead to constant constraint violation? Is the improvement driven solely by this construction?
- **Comparison fairness** (Reviewer 1, 4): The assumptions (known safe policy π^0 and its cost c^0) differ from many existing works, making direct comparison difficult. Reviewer 4 notes that papers like Liu et al. (2021) and Stradi et al. (2024) analyze the more general case without known safe policy, and that here the knowledge of a strictly feasible solution should guarantee safety at each episode (not just cumulative).
- **Constraint violation definition** (Reviewer 4): The violation metric allows cancellation across episodes, which is weak. With a known safe policy, one might expect per-episode safety. The paper’s constant violation bound is achieved at the cost of allowing negative violations.
- **Lack of experiments** (Reviewers 2, 3): The paper is theoretical but would benefit from empirical validation—even simple simulations comparing with prior CMDP algorithms—to demonstrate the advantage in practice. Reviewer 3 would increase score if empirical results were provided.
- **Limited scope** (Reviewer 2, 3, 4):
  * The setting is tabular MDP; claims about “large-scale complex environments” are unsubstantiated given the tabular assumption.
  * No discussion of extension to continuous state/action spaces (e.g., linear MDP). What are the primary challenges?
- **Open questions** (Reviewer 2):
  * Are the Lagrangian multipliers zero when constraint violation is allowed? How to compare algorithms in terms of the Lagrangian function?
  * What are the KKT conditions in this setting? Can the constraint violation be directly compared to algorithms that bound the Lagrangian function?
