## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper revisits ℓ0‑constrained optimization via Hard‑Thresholding (HT) gradient descent, reinterpreting HT algorithms as biased gradient methods. The authors categorize biases into memory‑biased and recursively‑biased, study their interaction in both First‑Order (FO) and Zeroth‑Order (ZO) settings, and propose two new algorithms: SARAHT (FO) and BVR‑SZHT (ZO). Experiments include ridge regression (simulated, dimensionality 5) and black‑box adversarial attacks (relegated to appendix). The paper receives mixed reviews: one reviewer (score 6) praised the theoretical framework and clean analysis, while three reviewers (score 3) found novelty limited, presentation very poor, and experimental validation insufficient.

### Strengths
- The theoretical framework is novel, offering a clean reinterpretation of HT algorithms as biased gradient methods and thorough analysis of bias interactions under bounded MSE conditions.
- Rigorous convergence analysis is provided for both FO and ZO settings, including clear characterization of bias cancellation effects under the HT operator.
- Multiple evaluation metrics are used (IFO, IZO, NHT).
- The topic (study of biases in HT for ℓ0 regularization) is underexplored and could potentially yield new insights; studying ℓ0 regularization directly may bring fresh ideas.
- Theoretical analysis is solid overall.

### Weaknesses
- **Experiments are weak and insufficient:**
  - The main paper contains only ridge regression on simulated data (dimensionality 5), contradicting the claimed “large‑scale machine learning” scope.
  - Black‑box adversarial attack results are only in the appendix; they should be moved to the main paper to demonstrate practical benefits, especially for ZO optimization.
  - No evaluation on real‑world large‑scale datasets (suggested: MNIST, CIFAR‑10 for sparse feature selection; Colon Cancer / Leukemia gene expression; Reuters or 20 Newsgroups for text classification).
  - No comparison with LASSO (e.g., SAGA on Lasso) to benchmark sparsity‑promoting methods on both convergence and runtime.
  - Comparisons with more recently proposed first‑order and zeroth‑order stochastic hard‑thresholding algorithms are missing, so the advantage in convergence rates and complexities is unclear.
  - Experimental setup is poorly described (no references, dataset details absent), hindering reproducibility.

- **Novelty is limited:**
  - Many first‑order and zeroth‑order stochastic hard‑thresholding algorithms already exist; the proposed SARAHT, BSVRG‑HT, BSAGA‑HT, and BVR‑SZHT add little new beyond combining existing components.

- **Presentation, writing, and clarity are very poor:**
  - The paper is not self‑contained: symbols such as φ, ν, ℕ₀ are introduced without definition or reference.
  - Notation is inconsistent: The smoothness constant is denoted Lₛ in Assumption 3 while the function is called ρₛ⁺‑strongly smooth; in Section 4, fᵢ is assumed νₛ‑RSS and Lₛ‑RSC, swapping the constants; γ is used both with and without subscript.
  - Major logical inconsistencies: Remark 1 states “the MSE of g(x) does not completely determine the convergence” but then suggests “using the MSE of ∇_HT as a substitute” without justification. Remark 4 claims “Hard‑Threshold can counteract some bias, thus accelerating convergence,” which directly contradicts the earlier statement in Remark 1.
  - Several lemmas and theorems (e.g., Theorem 3) are presented with little or no explanation, leaving their implications unclear.
  - Results are presented without discussion; theorems 1, 2, 3 lack interpretation.
  - Many unnecessary capitalizations (e.g., Hard‑Threshold, Zeroth‑Order, First‑Order).
  - Incorrect citation style (e.g., line 060: “William (de Vazelhes et al , 2022)”; line 063: “Yuan (Yuan et al, 2024)” should use citet or proper formatting).
  - Notation ∇_t^{BSAGA} is confusing and non‑standard.
  - Figures have hard‑to‑read titles, captions, and numbering.
  - Excessive use of abbreviations makes the text hard to read.
  - Numerous grammatical errors, typos (e.g., line 225: “algorirthm” instead of “algorithm”; repeated misuse of “recursively” instead of “recursive”).
  - A complete rewrite is recommended.

- **Theoretical assumptions are strong and their limitations undiscussed:**
  - Restricted Strong Convexity (RSC) and Restricted Strong Smoothness (RSS) may fail in deep neural network optimization (highly non‑convex), problems with heavy‑tailed noise, or high‑dimensional settings where restricted eigenvalue conditions break down.
  - No analysis of algorithm behavior when these assumptions are violated, nor modifications or relaxations proposed.
  - The paper does not analyze performance on non‑smooth optimization problems, or the computational overhead of tracking multiple biases.
  - No study on how bias interactions extend to other sparsification operators (e.g., soft thresholding) or to constrained optimization problems.
  - Asymptotic bounds on the per‑iteration computational complexity of the proposed algorithms are not provided.

- **Confusion about the ridge‑regression + ℓ₀ setup:**
  - Using ℓ₀ together with ridge regression re‑introduces hyperparameters, undermining one argument (that ℓ₀ avoids hyperparameters). How does the algorithm perform under the ℓ₀ constraint?
