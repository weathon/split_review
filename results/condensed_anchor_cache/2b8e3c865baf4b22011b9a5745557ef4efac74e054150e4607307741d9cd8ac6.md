## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes in-parameter knowledge injection: fine-tuning LoRA on synthetic QA pairs extracted from external knowledge, then merging those adapters into the model. It compares with in-context knowledge injection across fact extraction, comparative reasoning, and multi-step reasoning tasks on 1B, 3B, and 8B LLMs. The main finding is that in-parameter injection benefits complex reasoning tasks while in-context remains better for simpler fact extraction.

### Strengths
- **Novel idea**: Embedding external knowledge directly into model parameters rather than input context is a conceptually interesting direction for overcoming context length limits and enabling deeper integration (R1, R4).
- **Systematic comparison**: The paper provides a clear, structured experimental design with progressive task complexity that effectively reveals trade-offs between the two paradigms (R2).
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Evaluations are run on 1B, 3B, and 8B models with multiple settings; decisions and prompts are detailed in the appendix; the test suite is relevant and diverse for the scope (R1, R3, R4).
- **Well-written**: The paper is clear, figures are informative, and the exposition is easy to follow (R4).
- **Important topic**: Addressing the limitations of fixed-context knowledge injection is practically relevant (R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty and insufficient differentiation from LoRA and prior work**: The in-parameter method is essentially LoRA adaptation on synthetically generated QA pairs. The paper does not adequately acknowledge this overlap or explain how it differs from existing PEFT methods (e.g., Adapter) or knowledge editing (Fast Model Editing at Scale, Mitchell et al., 2022) (R2, R4). It lacks an ablation study to isolate the impact of synthetic augmentation from the core LoRA adaptation (R2). A baseline where a separate LLM encodes the knowledge (without LoRA) is also missing, making it unclear whether gains come from the encoding process or simply additional trained parameters (R1).
2. **No clear delineation of knowledge types that benefit each method**: The paper uses task complexity as the sole criterion, but a more granular analysis based on knowledge structure (e.g., factual vs. procedural, relational vs. attribute-based) is needed. Which kind of knowledge should be injected via parameters and which via context remains an open question (R1).
3. **Quality of QA extraction is a bottleneck**: The method relies on generating QA pairs from the knowledge base. These pairs may not cover all details in the context, leading to information loss. There is no detailed analysis of extraction quality, especially when using a weaker model for generation. Moreover, LoRA tuning does not guarantee that all knowledge from the QA pairs is learned (R3).
4. **Potential harm to general model performance**: Since LoRA changes parameters, the method may degrade the model’s performance on other tasks. The paper does not evaluate this side effect (R3).
5. **Limited task and model scope**: Experiments are restricted to fact-based reasoning tasks (extraction, comparison, multi-step reasoning) and relatively small models (1B, 3B, 8B). It is unclear whether the method generalizes to open-ended generation, creative writing, dialogue, or larger models (e.g., Llama 3.1 70B, 3.2 11B) (R1, R2, R4). Notably, in-context learning performs surprisingly poorly on comparative tasks; a reference with larger models would help interpret this (R4).
6. **Lack of theoretical analysis**: The paper does not explore the underlying mechanisms of how in-parameter injection affects internal representations, limiting generalizability beyond the specific experimental setup (R2).
7. **Missing efficiency comparison**: There is no comparison of computational cost (FLOPs, latency) between in-context and in-parameter injection (R4).
8. **Reviewer disagreement on overall assessment**: Two reviewers (R1, R3) rated the paper 6, citing novelty and comprehensive experiments, while two (R2, R4) rated it 5, pointing to limited novelty and scope. All reviewers, however, agree on the major weaknesses listed above.
