## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers largely agree the paper is clearly written and presents a simple, parameter-efficient fine-tuning method (zero-initialized attention) for LLaMA that extends to multi-modal tasks. However, they diverge on the technical novelty and the empirical support: one reviewer (score 8) finds the method effective and efficient, while another (score 5) considers the innovation limited (echoing ControlNet) and the performance gain over LoRA incremental. A third reviewer (score 6) also raises concerns about limited comparisons and scalability. All reviewers note the need for stronger baselines and more rigorous evaluation.

### Strengths
- The method is simple to implement and yields good results. The zero-initialized attention mechanism could be helpful for future prompt-tuning research.
- The writing is clear, easy to follow, and the figures (especially Figures 1, 2, 3) are of high visual quality and effectively showcase the proposed method.
- The paper demonstrates efficiency in terms of learnable parameters (1.2M), training data (52K demonstrations), and training time (<1 hour on LLaMA 7B). Compared to Alpaca, the proposed method is more effective yet efficient on the same LLM and data.
- The proposed method has potential in various domains: it extends naturally to vision-language (with an image encoder) and can be incorporated with transformer-based vision models (ViT, CLIP) instead of full fine-tuning.
- The paper provides extensive qualitative examples and ample technical details to facilitate replication.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited comparisons and baselines**: 
  - The multi-modal evaluation is weak. On MME, LLaMA-Adapter scores 973, while BLIP2 (an earlier baseline) achieves 1293 and LLaVA-1.5 (fully fine-tuned Vicuna 7B) reaches 1510. The paper should include such empirical backups to show effectiveness in the multi-modal domain.
  - For instruction-following, only qualitative comparisons to Alpaca and Alpaca-LoRA are given. Quantitative comparisons on the same task are missing.
  - Newer base LLMs (MPT, Falcon, LLaMA-v2) are available; adding comparisons or applying the proposed method to them would strengthen the case.
- **Technical innovation concerns** (minority reviewer more negative): 
  - The zero-initialized attention closely mirrors the zero-initialized convolution in ControlNet (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023). It appears more like an engineering design than a groundbreaking technical contribution.
  - Performance improvement over LoRA is marginal: training time reduces from 1.5h to 1h while accuracy is comparable. An ablation reducing LoRA’s rank could further reduce its parameter count and speed, which is not explored.
  - The paper claims enabling fine-tuning on mobile devices, but this lacks empirical support (memory reduction does not guarantee computation reduction).
- **Narrow evaluation scope**: 
  - Evaluations should include counterfactual reasoning and object hallucination benchmarks (e.g., [R1,R2,R3]).
  - Scalability to larger base models (13B/33B) is not tested, nor is scalability to larger instruction datasets.
  - The second example in Figure 4 has an error: the output program produces repetitive numbers.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and choices**: 
  - The number of insertion layers (30 out of 32) is a sensitive hyperparameter. Section 3.2 warns against early insertion, while Section 4.3 shows increasing layers improves performance. More explanation is needed for this trade-off (e.g., what would happen if all 32 layers were used?).
  - Random initialization of adaptation prompts vs. zero-initialization: would random init allow full insertion layers?
- **Equation 7 and attention formulation**: 
  - When the gating factor g grows larger than 1, the rescaled attention scores no longer sum to 1; is an additional normalization step needed? Equivalently, would a different rescaling [softmax(a)·g; softmax(b)·(1-g)] be more appropriate?
  - The possibility of learning large amounts of data/tasks is questioned: few learnable parameters may limit capacity for many tasks.
