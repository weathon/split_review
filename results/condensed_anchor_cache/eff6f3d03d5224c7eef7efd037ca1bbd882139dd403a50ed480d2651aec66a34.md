## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces the Intra-fused Gromov-Wasserstein (IFGW) distance, a metric that combines Wasserstein and Gromov-Wasserstein distances to capture both feature and structural information of graphs within a single optimal transport framework. The proposed method uses distance matrices of features rather than the features themselves, aiming to address limitations of Fused Gromov-Wasserstein (FGW) in cross-domain settings. The authors discuss entropic regularization for computation, define IFGW barycenters, and present experiments on graph clustering, point cloud classification, and graph similarity.

### Strengths

1. The problem of comparing structured data (graphs, point clouds) with both feature and structural information is important and difficult (Reviewers 4, 5).
2. The IFGW formulation is clearly described and motivated; the use of a convex combination of metrics to enable cross-domain comparison within the GW framework is sensible (Reviewers 4, 5).
3. The exposition is helped by helpful pedagogical elements: Table 1 summarizing prior metrics, color-coded equations (Equation 8 vs. 9) to highlight changes, and itemized comparisons between GW, FGW, and IFGW in the "From Distance to Discrepancy" section (Reviewer 2).
4. The paper provides clear explanations of prior work (GW, FGW) and the differences between existing and proposed methods (Reviewer 3).
5. Quantitative clustering experiments are conducted (Reviewer 3).
6. The framework is well-positioned to extend prior GW results (e.g., barycenters) straightforwardly (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

1. **Novelty concerns (major, multiple reviewers, severe).**  
   - The core idea—using GW distance with a compound metric on cross-domain data—is not new. The compound metric approach appears in earlier graph kernel work (e.g., Mahé et al. 2004) (Reviewer 4).  
   - None of the propositions or theorems are new; they are directly taken from Peyré et al. 2016 (Reviewer 4).  
   - It is unclear how IFGW differs from a vanilla GW problem with a dedicated cost matrix (Reviewer 5).  
   - The claim that FGW cannot handle cross-domain data is questionable; FGW already allows cross-modal data on both nodes and edges "it is just a matter of metrics" (Reviewer 4).

2. **Formulation clarity and correctness (major, severe).**  
   - The transition from Equation (10) to Equation (11) appears to be incorrect (Reviewer 5).  
   - The paper states that intra-domain distance matrices \(C\) and \(D\) can include values like \(d(x_i, x_j)\) and \(d(x'_k, x'_l)\); but in FGW, a shortest path (or any metric) could already be used—the novelty of this replacement is unclear (Reviewer 5).  
   - IFGW essentially reduces to a GW problem with cost matrices involving two (weighted) cost functions (Equation 12), raising questions about whether it is more than a trivial reformulation (Reviewer 5).  
   - The claim that the GW distance is not convex, yet the problem in (3) is convex, suggests confusion between the set of permutation matrices (non-convex) and the Birkhoff polytope (convex) (Reviewer 4).  
   - There is a confusion between graph isomorphism and graph matching; GW solves graph matching (NP-Complete), which should be discussed (Reviewer 4).  
   - The notion of "graph order" (page 4) is unclear—does it mean the feature dimension is the same? (Reviewer 4)

3. **Missing theoretical analysis (moderate, multiple reviewers).**  
   - The paper does not prove or disprove whether IFGW satisfies the four metric axioms: non-negativity, identity of indiscernibles, symmetry, and the triangle inequality (Reviewers 1, 2).  
   - The paper should clarify the novelty of generalizing GW to pseudo-metrics/semi-metrics given that the GW problem is already well-known in general gauge measure spaces (Reviewers 1, 2). Provide specific examples of how this generalization differs from prior work (Reviewers 1, 2).

4. **Insufficient experiments and comparisons (major, severe).**  
   - Experiments lack comparisons to state-of-the-art methods for graphs, such as FGW (Vayer et al. 2020), KerGM (Zhang et al. 2019), and GWL (Xu et al. 2019) (Reviewer 4; also Reviewer 3's question 5).  
   - Only clustering is evaluated in Section 3.1; why not classification, which is standard for graph distances? (Reviewer 5)  
   - \(\alpha = 0.5\) is fixed rather than cross-validated (Reviewer 5).  
   - In Table 2, IFGW’s improvement over FGW is minor, weakening the claim that "IFGW outperforms FGW in clustering tasks across all evaluated datasets in general" (Reviewer 1).  
   - Section 3.3 (graph similarity on molecules) uses only two molecules; it is impossible to draw conclusions from visual inspection alone (Reviewers 3, 5).  
   - The paper claims advantage in cross-domain settings, but very few cross-domain experiments are conducted (Reviewer 3).  
   - Experimental setup details are missing: for KMeans clustering (Table 2), what features of graphs were used? (Reviewer 3, question 4)  
   - Figure 2 does not clearly support the claim that "FGW tends to be sensitive to the feature distances between graphs" (Reviewer 3, question 3).  
   - Figure 3 does not clarify what \(\nu\) represents or what conclusions should be drawn (Reviewer 5).  
   - Section 3.2 (point cloud classification) needs more details on the experimental setup and conclusions (Reviewer 5).

5. **Incomplete notation and writing (moderate, multiple reviewers).**  
   - Symbols are insufficiently defined: e.g., \(\Sigma_m\) in Definition 1, \(X\) in Definition 2, \(\mathcal{T}\) in Equation (16), \(D\) below Equation (19) (Reviewers 3, 4).  
   - The paper has many typos and incomplete sentences; e.g., between the first and second paragraphs of Section 1.1, the text jumps from GNNs to OT without transition (Reviewer 4). The line after Equation (7) and its simplification needs more details (Reviewer 5).  
   - In the paragraph after Equation (2), the matrix \(T\) is described as "doubly stochastic"—does this restrict to uniform masses? (Reviewer 5)  
   - The \(\otimes\) symbol in Equation (4) is ambiguous (tensor product or Kronecker product?) (Reviewer 1, question 4).  
   - The QAP (Koopmans-Beckmann) appears on page 3 without prior introduction; it should be included in related work (Reviewer 4, minor 2).  
   - Define "smooth" and "cross-domain" as used in Table 1; GW is reported as not applicable in cross-domain context, which is confusing (Reviewer 5, also Reviewers 1, 2). Why is UOT included in Table 1? (Reviewer 5)  
   - Clarify the distinction between uppercase and lowercase subscripts in KL projections (Equations 14, 15, 17) (Reviewer 1, question 7).  
   - Add a comma at the end of Equation (7) (Reviewer 1, question 5).  
   - Provide a citation for "APSP" in Section 2 (Reviewer 1, question 2).  
   - Support the introduction's claim of "strong performance in graph classification and clustering" with citations (Reviewer 1, question 1).  
   - Clarify what "which can be simplified as..." right after Definition 2 means (Reviewer 3, question 2).
