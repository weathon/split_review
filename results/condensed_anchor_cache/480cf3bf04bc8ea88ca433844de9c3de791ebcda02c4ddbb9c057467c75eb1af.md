## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a framework for Transformer circuit analysis using Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) and Transcoders, combined with a hierarchical tracing method and an automated GPT-4o workflow. The approach extracts fine-grained features from GPT-2-small and constructs interpretable subgraphs. Experiments include subject-verb agreement, in-bracket features, and indirect object identification. Reviewers agree the methodology is clearly presented and the idea of using Transcoders for graph-based explanation is promising. However, they raise major concerns about insufficient evaluation, lack of baseline comparisons, missing ablation studies, limited model scope, and weak evidence for faithfulness or generalizability. Ratings range from 1 to 5, with minority reviewers more positive (5) but still highlighting critical gaps, and others (1, 3) pointing to insufficient justification and missing materials.

### Strengths
- The combination of SAEs and Transcoders to address non-linear MLP outputs for circuit analysis is a novel and promising approach.
- The hierarchical tracing method (gradient-based pruning in an approximated graph) is interesting and potentially efficient and scalable.
- The use of both a threshold-based and an LLM-based (GPT-4o) approach for identifying important features is a valuable contribution.
- The paper is well-written and clearly presented; figures and visualizations are clean and helpful.
- The explanation of the indirect object identification circuit is noted as possibly the most complex behavioral explanation in a language model to date.
- The process is applied to multiple non-toy behaviors in GPT-2-small, and some faithfulness evaluation is provided (ablations for subject-verb agreement).
- The framework offers potential for deeper insights into model internals; interpretability of LLMs is a timely problem.

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of Baseline Comparisons**: No comparisons with existing methods (e.g., linear probes, standard SAE circuits, indirect effect-based methods, model editing approaches [1,2]). This is essential to assess the effectiveness and advantages of the proposed approach. (R1, R2, R3)
2. **Insufficient Quantitative Evaluation**: Evaluation focuses only on necessity (ablation) but not sufficiency; metrics like faithfulness and completeness (e.g., causal scrubbing) are not applied, even though prior work shows plausibility can be misleading. (R2, R5) The authors should provide a reason if sufficiency is not considered. (R2)
3. **No Comparison Between Threshold and LLM-Based Methods**: The paper does not compare the two feature selection approaches (threshold vs. GPT-4o) on metrics like sufficiency and necessity. (R2)
4. **Limited Model Scope**: The method is only demonstrated on GPT-2-small; it is unclear if it transfers to other architectures (e.g., LLaMA, Mixture of Experts). (R1, R3)
5. **Missing Materials for Reproducibility**: No code, hyperparameters for hierarchical tracing, or description of manual tracing process are provided. Equations 1 and 2 have an undefined variable (y). (R5)
6. **Lack of Ablation Studies**: No ablation studies on key hyperparameters like λ in Equations 1 and 2. (R3)
7. **Small-Scale Experiments**: The experimental section is limited to a few examples rather than extensive benchmarks across diverse datasets. (R3) Only for subject-verb agreement is any faithfulness measurement given; for in-bracket activation, only percentage of feature activation is reported; graphs are only provided for some tasks. (R5)
8. **Unjustified Claims of Explanation Quality**: The paper implies high reliability (e.g., “transparent view of model’s decision-making process”) despite weak faithfulness evidence. Prior work shows interpretability illusions are common when relying on plausibility. The in-bracket SAE feature is unlikely to be fully explained by “is this token in brackets,” as activations vary. (R5)
9. **Manual Tracing Limits Scalability**: Manual tracing remains necessary for in-depth analysis (e.g., IOI circuit), which limits scalability for large models or complex tasks. (R4, R5) The faithfulness of the IOI graph is not evaluated. (R5)
10. **Lack of Justification for Direct Effects**: The paper focuses on direct effects (lines 215–217) without justifying why indirect effects are not considered or comparing with indirect-effect methods. (R2)
11. **Limited Validation of Automated Workflow**: The effectiveness of GPT-4o for automated circuit analysis is not substantiated with benchmarks or comparison metrics. (R1) Examples of graphs produced by GPT-4o would better illustrate its quality; human ratings alone are insufficient. (R5)
12. **Potential Lack of Novelty**: The use of SAEs for feature extraction is well-explored; the paper does not clarify what novel insights the approach brings beyond existing SAE-based frameworks or traditional linear probes. (R1)
13. **Lack of Theoretical Analysis**: There is insufficient theoretical insight into why the proposed methods work. (R4)
14. **Missing Task Descriptions**: The subject-verb agreement tasks are not described, and the distribution for the in-bracket task is not specified. (R5)
15. **Questions on Transferability and Scalability**: Can the framework transfer to other Transformer architectures? (R1) How does the need for manual tracing change with task complexity? (R4) What is the exact process for generating the IOI graph? (R5)
