## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes SUMMER, a multimodal emotion recognition in conversations (MERC) framework that uses Sparse Dynamic Mixture of Experts (SDMoE) and Hierarchical Cross-Modal Fusion (HCMF) to fuse text, audio, and visual modalities. A retrograde distillation strategy is introduced where a unimodal teacher guides the multimodal student model. Experiments on IEMOCAP and MELD datasets report state-of-the-art performance, especially on minority and semantically similar emotions. Reviewers’ scores are split: two reviewers (scores 5) find the results promising and the method well-designed; three reviewers (scores 3) indicate major concerns with clarity, motivation, reproducibility, and missing baselines.

### Strengths

- The retrograde distillation technique (unimodal teacher guiding multimodal student) is novel in MERC, and ablation experiments demonstrate its effectiveness (R1, R4).
- The method achieves SOTA performance on IEMOCAP (6-ways) (R4, R2, R3). 
- The sparse dynamic MoE and hierarchical cross-modal fusion modules are well-designed (R4). The overall combination of strategies enhances performance (R5).
- The paper provides a detailed explanation of model components in Chapter 3, with clear visual aids (Figures 2 and 3) (R3).
- An error analysis is included, highlighting improvements on minority and similar emotions (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Inconsistent/confusing presentation across text and figures:**  
  - Fig. 3(b) shows HCMF with four inputs, but Fig. 2 shows HCMF_text with one input and HCMF_*** with three (R1).  
  - Section 3.4 describes SDME, Section 3.5 HCMF, but Global MoE (part of HCMF) is detailed in Section 3.4 (R1).  
  - In Fig. 2, “discourse-speaker embedding” is placed in the SDMoE module, but the text assigns it to the “unimodal reconstruction” module (R2).  
  - Fig. 3(b) attributes Global MoE to HCMF, but the text places it in SDMoE (R2).  
  - Section 3.5 mentions a single-text modality teacher, but its connection is not shown in Fig. 2 (R2).  
  - A “Residual” part in HCMF (Fig. 2) and the classifier are not described in the paper (R2).  
  - The label “excited” (line 73) mismatches Figure 1’s “excitement”; the description of “dynamic changes in facial expressions and vocal tone might mislead the model to classify it as anger” lacks sufficient explanation (R3).  
  - The text describes four modules, but Fig. 2 only shows three (R4).  
  - Notations (e.g., \(W_g\)) are not clear, equations (4–6) contain errors, and there are typos in Figures 2, 3(a), and 6 (R5).  

- **Unclear motivation for key design choices:**  
  - The introduction fails to justify why Mixture of Experts (MoE) is necessary for MERC; citations on MoE (line 76) are limited (R3).  
  - The need for Knowledge Distillation (KD) is not well motivated; references for KD stop at 2021 (R3).  
  - The third stated contribution largely overlaps with the first two (R3).  
  - The claim (line 53) that existing methods “may overemphasize earlier positive expressions” is only stated with “if” and does not explain *why* or *how* (R5).  
  - The motivation for SDMoE and HCMF specifically is unclear (R4).  

- **Poor reproducibility and missing experimental details:**  
  - Hyperparameters (loss weights, distillation temperature, soft-label parameter) are listed but no specific values or ablation studies are provided (R1, R4).  
  - The choice of \(2\sigma\) in Equation (3) is not explained (R5).  
  - Code is not available (R4).  
  - Parametric experiments are missing (R2).  
  - Model complexity (e.g., runtime, parameters) is not evaluated (R2).  

- **Insufficient comparisons and missing baselines:**  
  - No comparison with other distillation-based MER methods: Multi-Task Momentum Distillation (TAFFC2023), Decoupled Multimodal Distilling (CVPR2023), Hierarchical Knowledge Distillation (TMM2024) (R1).  
  - Missing recent LLM-based baselines like InstructERC (R4).  
  - Some methods appear to outperform SUMMER on MELD, but direct comparisons with more recent literature are needed (R2, R5).  
  - In the results (line 411), “excitement” class improvements are claimed over CHFusion, but Table 1 does not show class-specific results for CHFusion (R3).  

- **Other issues:**  
  - Some comparison method results are reported as zero, which is unusual (R1).  
  - LFNet (line 196) is incorrectly claimed as a proposed method; it is a common sampling strategy in action/dynamic facial expression recognition (R1).  
  - The whole framework depends on unimodal encoders, yet their individual evaluation is absent (R4).  
  - Minor inconsistencies: “Unimodal Reconstruction” in text differs from Figure 2; loss weights in Eq. (15) are unclear (R4).  
  - Testing on more datasets is suggested (R3).
