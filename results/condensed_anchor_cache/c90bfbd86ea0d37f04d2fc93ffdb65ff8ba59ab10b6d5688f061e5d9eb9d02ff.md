## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces DIVA, a post-training framework that fine-tunes CLIP’s visual encoder using a frozen text-to-image diffusion model. CLIP’s outputs (global token + randomly sampled local tokens) condition the diffusion model, and the reconstruction loss backpropagates to update the CLIP encoder. Experiments on MMVP-VLM, LLaVA-based multimodal understanding, segmentation, and 29 zero-shot classification/retrieval benchmarks show improvements but also raise concerns about consistency, cost, and alignment.

### Strengths
- **Novel idea**: First work to leverage generative feedback from a diffusion model to improve CLIP’s fine-grained visual perception (R2, R4, R5).
- **Clear writing**: The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R1, R4, R5).
- **Significant improvement on MMVP-VLM**: 3–7% gain on this challenging benchmark (R1).
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Evaluation spans MMVP-VLM, LLaVA backbone, segmentation, 29 classification/retrieval benchmarks, and includes ablation studies for key designs (R1, R4, R5).
- **Intuitive and compatible**: Method is straightforward and can be integrated with various vision-language models (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of clear motivation and analysis**: Why CLIP fails on fine-grained cases (e.g., quantity, color, structure) is not analyzed—it could be due to architecture, training strategy, or data bias. The proposed diffusion-based solution appears ad‑hoc without a principled justification for why it is superior to alternatives (R1, R2).
- **Heuristic design choices**:  
  - *Visual Recap Density*: The fraction of local tokens used differs across CLIP variants with no principled selection method; the paper does not explain why different models require different sampling strategies (R1, R3, R4, R5).  
  - *Number of condition repeats (N=2)*: No ablation or justification for why N=2 is chosen; effects of N=10 or 100 are not explored (R1).  
  - *Part token selection*: Only random sampling is considered; whether a better selection strategy exists is not examined, and the proportion varies between training and inference, raising generalizability concerns (R5).  
  - *Empty text condition*: In Table 6, only empty text is used; it would be informative to see if adding image‑associated text descriptions further improves results (R5).
- **Inconsistent and marginal improvements**: Across Tables 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, and 8, not all metrics show improvement, and many gains are small (e.g., 0.4 absolute) with no reported variance or statistical significance tests (R2, R3). Table 3 bolds the proposed method even when it scores the same as the baseline, which is misleading (R3).
- **Potential visual‑language misalignment**: Because only the visual encoder is updated and the text encoder is unchanged, cross‑modal alignment may degrade. The paper evaluates only image classification and unimodal retrieval (text‑to‑text retrieval is redundant since the text encoder is frozen); missing are cross‑modal retrieval tasks that directly measure alignment (R2, R4, R5). No special loss (e.g., on the [CLS] token) is applied to preserve the original alignment (R5).
- **Training cost**: Sampling multiple random noise states per image is computationally expensive; the paper does not discuss whether the gains justify this cost or compare with cheaper alternatives (R2).
- **Ambiguity in method description**:  
  - Section 3.3 does not specify how visual features are combined with empty‑text embeddings when fed into the diffusion model (simple concatenation? how are they treated as text embeddings?) (R3).  
  - Justification for treating visual patch embeddings as text embeddings is missing; implications for the diffusion model’s behavior are not discussed (R3).  
  - It is unclear whether positional embeddings are used for patches when they replace text embeddings (R3).  
  - Rows 3–4 of Table 6 are confusing: if textual condition is used, how can the vision encoder be trained? (R1).
- **Missing evaluation details**: The exact procedure for evaluating on MMVP (how image‑text matching is performed, how accuracy is calculated) is not described, making results hard to interpret and reproduce (R3).
- **Minor typo**: Line 350 has an extra “for” (R5).
