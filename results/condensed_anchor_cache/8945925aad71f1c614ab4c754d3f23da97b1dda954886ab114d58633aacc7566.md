## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces GRED, a graph learning architecture that aggregates nodes at different distances and uses a parallelizable linear recurrent network (specifically an LRU) over the chain of distances to encode neighborhood structure, aiming to achieve long-range modeling with lower complexity than graph transformers and without positional encodings. Reviewers generally found the paper well-written and the experiments thorough, but raised significant concerns about novelty, limited comparison to existing k-hop methods, missing ablations (especially on the hyperparameter K), computational cost analysis versus MPNNs, and questionable suitability of the RNN direction assumption. One reviewer was substantially more negative (rating 3 vs. 6), citing limited novelty and lack of source code.

### Strengths
- The proposed architecture provides a simple and intuitive alternative to graph transformers, avoiding ad-hoc positional encodings while enabling long-range interactions.
- The evaluation is thorough, covering multiple benchmarks and comparing against a wide range of GNN/transformer baselines, with results supporting the effectiveness of GRED.
- The theoretical analysis demonstrates expressiveness superior to one-hop MPNNs and provides insight into the RNN filtering of k-hop neighbor features.
- The computational efficiency advantage over equivalent transformer models (e.g., Table 4) is well demonstrated.
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow, with comprehensive explanations of the architecture.

### Weaknesses
1. **Novelty and connection to existing k-hop methods** (minority reviewer, rating 3): The approach is very similar to prior work that uses update rules of the form \(h_u^{(t+1)} = COM(h_u^t, AGG_{u,1}, ..., AGG_{u,k})\); the main difference is using an RNN for the COM function, which one reviewer felt is not a significant departure. The paper lacks a proper discussion and detailed comparison to methods like [1] and [2] that also operate on k-hop neighborhoods. The specific choice of RNN over other aggregation techniques needs stronger justification.

2. **Missing ablation on hyperparameter K** (Reviewers 1 and 2): There is no sensitivity analysis showing how performance varies with different K values, nor guidelines for selecting K across datasets. It is unclear whether the optimal K is consistent across graphs with different structures (e.g., large diameters).

3. **Incomplete computational efficiency analysis**:
   - Only compared against transformers, not against MPNNs. For larger K and larger graphs, the k-hop neighbor computation may introduce significant overhead that is not shared across nodes (unlike iterative 1-hop message passing). A breakdown of preprocessing, aggregation, and recurrent step costs is needed.
   - Memory consumption and FLOPs are not reported. The speedup over GRIT may be misleading because the performance gap between GRIT and GRED is still significant (Reviewer 2). A fairer comparison would involve an architecture with similar task performance.
   - One reviewer (Reviewer 4) noted that all typical GNN drawbacks (e.g., oversmoothing) likely also apply to GRED, as it can be seen as another form of message passing with different weights.

4. **Limitations of the receptive field** (Reviewer 2): The distance processed is still limited by the choice of K and number of layers, which may be problematic for graphs with very large diameters. The paper should provide insights on how super long-distance information affects performance and discuss this limitation explicitly.

5. **Directionality assumption of RNN** (Reviewer 4): The RNR assumes information flows from K-hop to (K-1)-hop, but information can also go “outward” (e.g., from k-2 to k-1). The suitability of this unidirectional assumption for graph data is questionable. One reviewer asked how performance changes if the LRU is replaced with a vanilla RNN.

6. **Limited experimental scope** (Reviewer 3): Experiments focus on long-range benchmarks; no results on standard datasets like TUDatasets. This raises concerns about generalizability to graphs with varying sizes, densities, and structural properties.

7. **Missing source code** (Reviewer 3): Code is not provided, hindering reproducibility and transparency.

8. **Missing non long-range benchmark evaluation** (Reviewer 1 question): It is unclear whether GRED underperforms MPNNs on tasks that need only local information—this potential overkill should be evaluated.

9. **Unclear figure element** (Reviewer 2): Figure 1(a) is ambiguous about what “skip” represents.
