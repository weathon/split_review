## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces MDBench, a synthetic multi-document reasoning benchmark (1k instances, 300 human-verified) generated by starting from structured tables (TabFact), augmenting via GPT-4o to add cross-row dependencies and reasoning challenges, then converting rows into documents. Experiments evaluate several LLM families (GPT, Claude, Llama, etc.) with zero-shot, one-shot, and CoT prompting. Even frontier models achieve exact match <70%. Reviewers are split: Reviewers 1 and 4 (score 5) praise the idea, difficulty, and controlled generation; Reviewers 2 and 3 (score 3) raise major concerns about dataset quality, real-world alignment, missing baseline comparisons, and insufficient validation details.

### Strengths

- The idea of using structured data (single tables) to automatically generate a multi-document QA dataset is interesting and novel. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- The benchmark demonstrates that frontier models struggle on multi-document QA, potentially motivating further work. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- The paper explicitly states desired benchmark goals (Section 3.1), and the authors are on board with those goals. (Reviewer 3)
- The generation pipeline is reasonable and useful for controlled, challenging example creation while mitigating data contamination. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- Experiments cover diverse reasoning settings (Multi-hop, Numeric reasoning) and compare zero-shot, one-shot, CoT prompting across multiple LLMs. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- Interesting findings comparing multi-document vs. table-based reasoning performance. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses

1. **Dataset quality and real-world representativeness (major concern, especially for Reviewers 2 and 3):**
   - No evidence that MDBench captures real-world multi-document challenges: long context, many documents, contradictions, redundancy, complementarity, lack of order. One stated goal is "Grounded in Real-World Scenarios," but this is unsubstantiated. (Reviewer 3)
   - No statistical details or demonstrations of actual documents; they may be short (single sentences/paragraphs) rather than document-length. (Reviewer 3)
   - Dataset is too small for statistical significance: only 300 human-verified out of 1k. (Reviewer 4)
   - Quality control process not clarified in main text (2 pages left). (Reviewer 4)

2. **Missing validation details and human evaluation statistics:**
   - Human evaluation for 300 instances lacks: number of annotators per instance, inter-annotator agreement, guidelines, how many instances rejected and why. (Reviewers 1, 3)
   - Only "hand-verify" mentioned, no further details. (Reviewer 3)
   - No statistics correlating human judgments with automatic validation; quality guarantees unconvincing. (Reviewer 1)

3. **Insufficient comparisons with related work and baselines:**
   - Does not properly relate to prior multi-document synthetic generation (Schnitzler et al., 2024 – MoreHopQA; Sprague et al., 2023 – MuSR); differentiating aspects unclear. (Reviewer 1)
   - No comparisons with other synthetic data generation methods from Section 2. (Reviewer 2)
   - Fails to evaluate more related inference baselines for multi-document settings (e.g., methods in Section 2). (Reviewer 2)

4. **Lack of analysis on why LLMs fail and how to improve:**
   - No convincing analysis of failure modes or suggestions for improvement. (Reviewer 2)

5. **Missing experiments:**
   - Did the authors try training a stronger model on the dataset? (Reviewer 2)
   - No comparison of LLMs with different input context window sizes; longer documents might show larger benefit of compressed tabular representation. (Reviewer 1)
   - Why one-shot instead of few-shot? If due to length constraints, long-context methods could be considered. (Reviewer 2)

6. **Data generation and instance selection questions:**
   - How were tables from TabFact selected? (Reviewer 1)
   - How is correctness of synthetic data ensured? (Reviewer 2)
   - Were any generated instances filtered out (human or automatic)? What fraction? (Reviewer 1)
   - "Counterfactual or frictional twists" are mentioned (Line 148-150) but not defined; such twists could reduce trustworthiness; how are they validated? (Reviewer 4)

7. **Presentation and readability issues:**
   - Examples and demonstration process are difficult to read; paper unnecessarily hard to follow. (Reviewer 4)
   - Tables and figures need improvement. (Reviewer 2)
   - Typo in Table 4 (extra "z" at bottom of last column). (Reviewer 1)
   - No complete data instance example in manuscript; including one in Appendix would clarify. (Reviewer 1)

8. **Groundedness and answerability concerns:**
   - Unclear how many questions are fully grounded in real-world concepts; some may be answerable without input tables/documents, reducing validity. (Reviewer 1)

9. **Alternative prompting methods not explored:**
   - Could Tree-of-Thoughts (Yao et al., 2024) or other XoT methods be applied like CoT? (Reviewer 2)
