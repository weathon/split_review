## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Positive Mining Graph Contrastive Learning (PMGCL), which uses a Beta Mixture Model (BMM) to estimate the probability that a node is a true positive relative to an anchor node, thereby improving positive sample selection in Graph Contrastive Learning (GCL). The method introduces a contrastive loss that supports multiple positive pairs per anchor. Experiments on citation and co-authorship networks claim state-of-the-art performance on node classification and clustering tasks. Reviewers’ scores range from 3 to 6, with concerns about novelty, outdated baselines, missing theoretical analysis, presentation quality, and insufficient experimental validation.

### Strengths
1. **Relevance of the problem**: Better distinguishing positive from negative examples in GCL is an important research direction (R1, R4).
2. **Novel approach to positive mining**: The idea of using a mixture model (BMM) to identify true positives is considered a new contribution by some reviewers (R2, R5). R2 states it “provides a new idea for graph contrastive learning”; R5 highlights “a new approach called PMGCL.”
3. **Clear explanation and reproducibility**: R2 notes the paper “clearly explains the principle and implementation steps,” making it easier to understand and reproduce.
4. **Computational complexity analysis**: R2 appreciates the detailed analysis showing that PMGCL “does not bring excessive computational burden.”
5. **Multi‑positive contrastive loss**: The design of a loss function that accommodates multiple positive pairs per anchor is praised as an improvement over traditional GCL losses (R3).
6. **Strong empirical results on diverse datasets**: PMGCL is evaluated on citation and co-authorship networks, showing superior performance on node classification and clustering (R1, R3, R5). R5 notes “SOTA performance compared with baselines.”

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty and prior use of mixture models**: The application of BMM (or GMM/DMM) for positive mining is seen as non‑novel. R1 states these are “well‑established models” and the approach lacks theoretical contribution. R4 explicitly notes that “this method has already been used in Graph Contrastive Learning in ProGCL.” In contrast, R2 and R5 consider the method novel.
2. **Outdated baselines and missing recent comparisons**: Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R3, R4) agree that the experimental section compares only with older methods, with PiGCL [1] being the only baseline from 2024. Missing SOTA methods include:
   - ProGCL (ICML 2022), AFGRL (AAAI 2022), iGCL (TNNLS 2024) – (R2)
   - ImGCL (AAAI 2023), MA‑GCL (AAAI 2023), GraphACL (NeurIPS 2024), ASP (AAAI 2023) – (R1)
   - AutoGCL, GA2C – (R3)
   - Also, R4 suspects reported baseline results are underreported, using public splits rather than the standard 10% training split, weakening the claim of SOTA.
3. **Poor presentation and writing quality**: Several reviewers raise concerns:
   - Spelling/grammar errors: e.g., Figure 4 “Pos‑probability with anthor” should be “anchor” (R1); lines 290 and 304 contain awkward phrasing (R3).
   - Unclear explanations: The method introduction is not clear (R4); notation issues (e.g., ‘s’ in eq. (1) needs specific expression, matrix vs. real‑number notation) (R4); formalization not rigorous (e.g., “k nodes” italics, “Eq” abbreviation) (R3).
   - Figure quality: Figures 2 and 3 need improvement (R4); Figure 4 is poor (R3); incorrect figure reference in line 227 (R1); inconsistent formatting in Algorithm 1 (R1).
4. **Lack of theoretical analysis and justification**: The paper lacks robust theoretical support. R1 calls the approach “heuristic”; R2 notes insufficient analysis of advantages over expanding negative samples and no theoretical justification for the multi‑positive loss function.
5. **Missing experiments and analysis**:
   - No comparison of training/inference times before and after applying PMGCL (R1, R4); actual running time on large graphs is missing (R4).
   - The proposed loss is not tested with other GCL frameworks (e.g., DGI loss, Barlow Twins, graph group discrimination) (R5); similarly, Table 4 does not include losses like graph Barlow Twins or BGRL point‑to‑point losses (R3).
   - Adaptability to other losses and scalability on large graphs (e.g., ogbn‑arxiv) is not evaluated (R5).
   - Meaning of “node number” in Figure 3 and other places unclear (R4); performance on public splits for citation graphs not reported (R4).
   - No ablation on the ratio of positive/negative samples and its impact (R2).
6. **Reuse of a previously published mixture‑model trick**: As noted by R4, the core idea of using BMM for hard positive mining already exists in ProGCL, which further reduces the novelty.

*Note on reviewer disagreement*: Reviewers R1 and R4 are most critical (scores 3), especially regarding novelty, presentation, and missing analyses. R2 (score 5) and R5 (score 6) are more positive, highlighting the new idea and strong empirical results. This contrast is preserved above, e.g., “considered a new contribution by some reviewers” vs. “limited novelty.”
