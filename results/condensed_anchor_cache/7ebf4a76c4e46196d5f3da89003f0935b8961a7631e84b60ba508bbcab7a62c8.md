## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper analyzes post-training via iterative self-improvement loops for LLMs (supervised fine-tuning and preference optimization), comparing SFT, DPO, and alternating SFT-DPO. The authors show that while benchmark pass@1 accuracy improves, other metrics such as output diversity and OOD generalization can regress—a phenomenon called *self-improvement reversal*. Experimental results indicate plateaus or declines after ~3–4 iterations, and the paper proposes an evaluative framework beyond pass@1. One reviewer (score 8) found the experiments comprehensive and insightful; three others (score 6 each) acknowledged timeliness but raised concerns about presentation and conceptual clarity.

### Strengths
- Timely and well-motivated topic; thorough review of related work.
- Experiments are well-designed and provide a comprehensive understanding of internal mechanisms (Reviewer 2, 3, 4).
- Reveals that pass@1 accuracy can lead to mistaken judgments about model progress; proposes new metrics (Reviewer 1, 2).
- Figure 2 is particularly insightful, showing correct answer coverage relationships not previously discussed (Reviewer 2).
- The iterative self-improvement algorithm and most analyses are general and applicable to arbitrary domains with minimal changes (Reviewer 3).
- Interesting reversal results for diversity (variety of metrics) and OOD generalization (Reviewer 3).
- Interesting findings: most methods decline after ~4 iterations; lesser powerful models gain more but powerful models achieve better max performance; M_1 performance affects subsequent iterations (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- Writing could be improved, though core ideas are clear (Reviewer 1).
- Figure presentation issues:
    - Figure 1: Hard to compare SFT vs. DPO vs. SFT-DPO across rows because different rows have different y-axis scales (Reviewer 3).
    - Figure 2: Left figure is difficult to understand; providing example questions/answers would help (Reviewer 3).
    - Figure 3: x-axis unlabeled, caption too short, and main takeaway unclear (Reviewer 3). One reviewer found it unsurprising that pass@N increases with N (Reviewer 3).
    - Figure 5: bottom y-axis range [90,95] is too narrow; scale is unclear; the blue dotted line is not explained (Reviewer 3).
- Section 5.1 (Improvement Sets): It seems trivial that accuracy@N increases with larger N (Reviewer 3).
- Section 5.3 (Group Disparity): Unclear why not simply compare level 1 vs. level 5 accuracy directly (Reviewer 3).
- Correct answer coverage appears potentially at odds with solution diversity; the paper does not resolve how to reconcile these metrics (Reviewer 3).
- Question raised: Is solution diversity inherently desirable in domains like mathematical reasoning? It is possible that higher-performing models always have lower diversity (Reviewer 3).
- Some conclusions are expected, e.g., results from Table 3 are related to a prior work [1] (Reviewer 2).
- In the introduction, it is not apparent why the new metrics (e.g., diversity) are important for solving these reasoning tasks (Reviewer 4).
- Missing experiment: whether results hold with RL-based post-training (Reviewer 1 question).
- Table 3 lacks clear labeling: the x-axis should be labeled “sampling N” and the model used should be specified (Reviewer 4 question).
