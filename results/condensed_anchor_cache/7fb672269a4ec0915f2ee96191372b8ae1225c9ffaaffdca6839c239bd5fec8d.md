## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes NegLabel, a post hoc zero-shot out-of-distribution (OOD) detection method for vision-language models (VLMs) that leverages a large number of negative labels mined from extensive text corpora. It introduces a novel OOD score that combines affinities with both ID labels and negative labels, along with a grouping strategy. Theoretical analysis using probabilistic modeling (Bernoulli, binomial, Poisson binomial) explains the mechanism. Extensive experiments on multiple OOD detection benchmarks and VLM architectures (e.g., CLIP-B/16) demonstrate state-of-the-art performance and robustness to domain shifts. Reviewers acknowledge the novelty, solid experiments, clear presentation, and theoretical insights, but raise concerns about assumptions, missing comparisons, presentation details, and theoretical justification.

### Strengths
- **Novel and important research direction**: The paper pioneers zero-shot OOD detection with VLMs, addressing an important gap. It provides a clear motivation and identifies key issues in existing methods (e.g., negative label selection). (R2, R3, R4)
- **Extensive and solid experiments**: Experiments cover OOD detection, hard OOD, robustness to domain shifts, and multiple VLM architectures. Results show large and consistent gains over several SOTA baselines (e.g., MCM, CLIPN, ZOC). The experimental section provides interesting insights and strongly supports claims. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Clear presentation and flow**: The paper is well-written, well-structured, easy to follow, with quality illustrations (Figure 1). Intuitive motivation is supported by evidence rather than mere wording. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Theoretical analysis**: The paper includes theoretical analysis starting from data modelling, using probability tools to justify the score function. This analysis is new, illuminating, and easy to follow. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Extensive ablations**: The paper provides thorough ablation studies. (R1)
- **Potential to motivate future work**: The clear demonstration of issues and solutions can inspire more research in this important area. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Assumption about CLIP's latent space (R1, confidence 5)**: The paper assumes ID images and ID label embeddings are close in CLIP space, but this is false—textual and visual embeddings lie on different manifolds (Weixin Liang et al., 2022). Using ID labels as proxies may yield unexpected results. Authors should be cautious and discuss this limitation. (R1)
- **Missing comparisons and baselines (R1)**:
    - Comparison with CLIPN (Wang et al., 2023) and ZOC (Esmaeilpour et al., 2022) is missing in the main paper; ZOC comparison appears in supplementary but should be in main text with a consistent setup (ImageNet-1k, not ImageNet-200). (R1)
    - Simple and strong baselines Mahalanobis (Lee et al., 2018) and SSD (Sehwag et al., 2022) are not included. (R1)
    - The OOD scoring formula appears identical to a baseline in Fort et al. (2021, last section); the novelty relies on the mining algorithm and grouping strategy, not the score itself. A comparison with this baseline is needed. (R1)
- **Misleading baseline presentation (R1)**: Classifying MSP, ODIN, Energy as purely zero-shot is misleading—MCM is zero-shot MSP on CLIP's output probabilities. The setting should be clarified. (R1)
- **Lack of ablation on grouping parameter n_g (R1)**: No ablations are provided on $n_g$ (number of groups). How is it set and what is its impact? (R1)
- **Hard OOD detection terminology (R1)**: Is this task better qualified as Open-Set Recognition? (R1)
- **Figure 1 statistical interpretation (R2, minority reviewer score 6)**: The right subfigure raises a question: what distribution should the similarity scores for negative labels follow given the selection process? Should they be uniform or specific? This needs more elaboration. (R2)
- **Algorithm 1 presentation (R2)**: Comments in Algorithm 1 are too long; a high-level description of the purpose would improve readability. (R2)
- **Grouping strategy placement (R2)**: The grouping strategy appears in Section 3.2 instead of 3.1 (before the score function). It seems like a pre-processing step and should be logically placed earlier. (R2)
- **Generality of Appendix B.3 (R2, R3, R4)**: The claim that the Poisson binomial case in B.3 is more general than the binomial case in the main text lacks sufficient intuition or explanation. Why is it more general and crucial for real-world OOD data? (R2, R3, R4)
- **Performance variation across datasets (R3)**: Some OOD datasets (e.g., in Table 2 based on CLIP-B/16) yield extremely high AUROC (>90%) and low FPR95 (<10%) across methods. Why is OOD detection easily addressed on some datasets? More explanation is needed. (R3)
- **Overly strong results on CLIP-B/16 (R3)**: For results based on CLIP-B/16 with various ID datasets, performance is too high. Explanation needed. (R3)
- **Cosine similarity choice in Eq. (5) (R4)**: Only cosine similarity is used; other similarity choices should be explored or justified further. (R4)
- **Table 2 placement (R4)**: Many results in Table 2 are already excellent (AUROC>90%, FPR95<10% in most cases), making the table less necessary in the main content. Consider moving to appendix to free space for more interesting results from the supplementary. (R4)
- **Selecting all distinct words from ID classes (R4)**: What happens if we select all different words from the ID classes? An ablation or discussion is missing. (R4)
- **Bernoulli vs. binomial relationship (R3)**: The paper clarifies that binomial distribution is a sum of Bernoulli variables, but this is a well-known result—clarify if any deeper insight is intended. (R3)
- **Naming consistency in algorithms (R3)**: Keep naming consistent in Algorithm 1 and other algorithms. (R3)
- **Short related work section (R1)**: The related work is relatively short, especially for a prolific field. A discussion on the sense of OOD detection for foundation models trained on vast data would be welcome. (R1)
