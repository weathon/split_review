## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses detecting adversarial suffix jailbreaks on LLMs by using perplexity (PPL) computed via GPT-2. Adversarial suffixes are gibberish, leading to high PPL. A straightforward PPL threshold suffers false positives, so the authors train a Light-GBM classifier on PPL and token length to improve detection. The work highlights the difficulty of achieving low false positives and notes that human-crafted jailbreaks are not detectable by PPL alone.

### Strengths
- Focuses on a pressing and timely safety issue: jailbreak attacks on LLMs (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Core idea is clear and easy to understand; the empirical results provide a useful reference for future work (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Regular prompts are collected from varied datasets (human-crafted and machine-generated), better reflecting real-world scenarios (Reviewer 1).
- The paper explicitly points out that perplexity filtering cannot detect human-crafted jailbreaks, providing insight into the nuances of different attack types (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Provides a pioneer investigation with comprehensive experiments showing that adversarial suffixes are identifiable via perplexity, and offers useful analytical insights for future defense research (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 rated the contribution higher (3/3) than the other reviewers (2/3), indicating a difference in perceived significance.

### Weaknesses
- The overall idea is fairly straightforward given the obvious gibberish nature of adversarial suffixes; the paper lacks deeper technical contributions such as:
  - Evaluating the robustness of the perplexity filter (and the token-length feature) against adaptive attacks where an attacker optimizes to evade detection (Reviewers 1, 2, 3—implied via requests for evading-attack evaluation and discussion).
  - Evaluating different base models for computing perplexity (only GPT-2 is used) (Reviewers 1, 3).
  - Investigating susceptibility of the token-length feature to evasion attacks that lengthen suffixes with filler text (Reviewer 1).
- Writing and presentation quality is poor: the current state hinders comprehension, lacks clear structure, and does not adequately highlight technical contributions (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Specific issues include superfluous spaces (Reviewer 1) and overall poor readability (Reviewer 2).
- No evaluation is conducted under an adaptive attack setting where the adversary knows the defense and can optimize the attack accordingly; this vulnerability is critical because GPT-2’s perplexity could be exploited (Reviewer 2).
- The realistic scenario where benign prompt tokens are long and the adversarial suffix is short is not addressed—overall PPL may remain low, making detection challenging (Reviewer 2).
- The use of perplexity is presented heuristically with limited intuitive motivation; the underlying mechanism by which adversarial suffixes exhibit high perplexity is under-explained (Reviewer 3).
- The paper acknowledges but does not address how to detect human-crafted jailbreaks using perplexity; this limitation remains unresolved (Reviewers 1, 3).
