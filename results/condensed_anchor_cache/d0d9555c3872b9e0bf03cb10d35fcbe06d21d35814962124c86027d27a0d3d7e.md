## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes using knowledge distillation (KD) to mitigate model collapse in models trained on synthetic data, evaluating on image generation (VAE, DDPM) and text summarization (T5). Reviewers are divided: two (scores 3) find the idea intuitive and note the multi-task experiments, while two (scores 1) criticize the work as lacking novelty, having poor presentation and insufficient experiments. All reviewers highlight serious issues with formatting, missing baselines, weak definitions, and incomplete analysis.

### Strengths
- The idea of using knowledge distillation to mitigate model collapse is intuitive and a plausible direction (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The paper conducts experiments on multiple tasks: VAE and DDPM for image generation, and T5 for text summarization, demonstrating cross-domain applicability (R2, R4).
- The structure of the paper is clear and the method is easy to follow (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty**: The method simply applies conventional KD without comparing alternative KD approaches; the contribution is considered “too obvious” (R1).
- **Missing references**: The paper lacks proper citations for related work (R1).
- **Insufficient experimental design**: Experiments are limited to hyperparameter tuning; there is no assessment of model collapse severity over iterative cycles of data generation and retraining (R1).
- **No formal definition of model collapse**: The paper does not define the phenomenon for text or image models, and metrics like test set loss and ROUGE are questionable indicators of collapse (R4).
- **Simple datasets and limited model scale**: Datasets for image generation are too simple (R4); the paper should use more complex datasets, more advanced image models, and higher-resolution images to prove generalization (R2, R4).
- **No comparison with existing methods**: There is no baseline comparison with prior approaches for mitigating model collapse (R2, R4).
- **Lack of theoretical/empirical analysis**: Results and findings are presented without deeper analysis or explanation (R4).
- **Proposed method underperforms after longer training**: For language models, the method remains worse than the model \(M_0\) after longer steps; the authors do not analyze this degradation (R4).
- **Formatting and presentation issues**:
  - The template does not follow ICLR 2025 requirements; the style file has been modified (R1, R2, R3, R4).
  - Line styles are unorganized (R1).
  - Main text contains many unnecessary figures that belong in the appendix, making the paper resemble an experimental report; it fails to reach ten pages (R3).
  - Motivation and methodology description are unclear, and the submission appears unprofessional (R3).
