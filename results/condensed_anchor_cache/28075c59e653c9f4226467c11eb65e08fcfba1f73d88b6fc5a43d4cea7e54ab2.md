## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Self-RAG, a framework that trains a single LM to adaptively retrieve passages on demand, generate responses, and self-reflect using special reflection tokens (retrieval, relevance, support, usefulness). Training data for these tokens is generated by a critic model distilled from GPT-4. The generator is trained on augmented instruction-tuning data to output both target responses and reflection tokens. At inference, the reflection tokens enable controllable decoding. Experiments on fact verification, open-domain QA (short-form), and long-form generation show that Self-RAG (7B and 13B) outperforms non-proprietary baselines and often matches or exceeds ChatGPT and other large models.

### Strengths
- **Novel integration of self-reflection and RAG**: Combines retrieval and self-reflection in a single LM, addressing the key limitation of vanilla RAG (indiscriminate retrieval) by enabling dynamic, on-demand retrieval and self-evaluation. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Controllability and interpretability**: Reflection tokens allow users to tailor generation to specific requirements (e.g., factuality, citation accuracy) and provide interpretable signals about retrieval and grounding decisions, offering an alternative to RLHF with better controllability and interpretability. (R2, R3, R4)
- **Reduced reliance on human annotation**: Training data for reflection tokens is generated using GPT-4 through prompting, minimizing human labeling. (R4)
- **Strong empirical performance**: Self-RAG significantly outperforms non-proprietary LLMs and retrieval-augmented baselines on most benchmarks, including fact verification, open-domain QA (especially PopQA), and long-form generation factuality/citation metrics. (R2, R3) *Note: Reviewer 1 disputes some comparisons (see Weaknesses).*
- **Addresses relevance and grounding**: The special tokens explicitly model passage relevance and whether the generation is grounded/helpful, tackling core RAG problems. (R4)
- **Easy adaptation**: The framework can be adapted to generate responses with desired properties via token-level control. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons and ablations**:
    - No ablation separating the self-reflection contribution from RAG; only `IsSup` token is ablated, while other critique tokens (e.g., `IsUse`, `IsRel`) are not. (R1, R2)
    - No study of the effect of varying retrieval threshold; threshold is predetermined. (R2)
    - Ablation only tests 'Retrieve top1', but Self-RAG uses top5 or top10. (R2)
    - No comparison to prompting-based dynamic retrieval alternatives (e.g., Active RAG by Jiang et al.) to assess benefits of the offline training approach. (R3)
    - No attempt to combine self-reflection with RAG without pre-training (i.e., via prompting or in-context learning). (R1)
    - No analysis of how variations in simple RAG details (prompt, top-k, order of retrieval) affect results. (R1)
- **Evaluation concerns and potential data leakage**:
    - In Table 2, Ret-ChatGPT (ChatGPT with retrieval) performs worse than ChatGPT on most short-form tasks; Reviewer 1 suspects data leakage or improper RAG implementation. (R1)
    - Alpaca 13B with retrieval shows reduced accuracy on PubMed (in addition to reduced MAUVE), suggesting retrieval may hurt. (R1)
    - Self-RAG without retrieval already outperforms many baselines (Figure 3.a); Reviewer 1 questions whether this indicates data leakage that would invalidate results. (R1) *[Minority view: other reviewers found ablations sound and convincing (R3), so this is a strong disagreement.]*
    - Claim that Self-RAG outperforms ChatGPT on open-domain QA is disputed: Table 2 shows ChatGPT and Ret-ChatGPT dominate except on PopQA. (R1)
- **Critic model and reflection token limitations**:
    - The paper does not evaluate the LM's accuracy in predicting reflection tokens (only critic model accuracy is reported). (R2)
    - The reflection tokens provide post-hoc selection among candidates, but do not guide generation from the start; if no candidate is good, they may not help. (R4)
    - The critic model is distilled from GPT-4; GPT-4 may make different retrieval decisions than small LMs due to its larger parametric knowledge, making annotations potentially unsuitable for smaller models. (R4) *(Related: Reviewer 3 asks about triggering rates of retrieval tokens across tasks.)*
    - Possible bias in the critic model; no analysis of reflection token distributions or correlation with retrieval threshold. (R2)
- **Impact on other capabilities and training data**:
    - It is unclear if training for factuality and self-reflection hurts other aspects such as instruction following, creativity, or reasoning. (R3)
    - The generator sees each passage independently and cannot synthesize multiple passages. (R3)
    - Questions about required training data quantity and whether careful sampling (e.g., focusing on fact-seeking slices) is necessary. (R3)
- **Computation and latency**:
    - The repetitive self-reflection process likely increases computational cost and latency; no numbers or comparisons are provided. (R1)
    - *(R4 asks whether reflection tokens affect generation beyond post-hoc feedback, which overlaps with the above point about guiding generation.)*
- **GPT-4 evaluation agreement**:
    - Reviewer 1 questions the claim of high agreement between GPT-4 and human evaluations for reflection tokens, citing PandaLM's reported 66% F1 accuracy; authors are asked to provide supporting data or clarify the discrepancy. (R1)
