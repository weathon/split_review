## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Qinco2, a deep-learning-based vector quantizer that improves upon QINCo by extending residual quantization (RQ) with a neural network that adapts each codebook based on the current reconstruction. QINCo2 adds three main improvements: (i) faster encoding via codeword pre-selection (using a smaller approximate network to generate top candidates, then re-ranking with the full network) combined with beam search to maintain quality; (ii) a fast approximate decoder based on pairwise additive codewords to create accurate short-lists for search; (iii) optimized training procedures and architecture tweaks. Experiments on four datasets show substantial gains – e.g., 44% better reconstruction MSE for 16-byte compression on BigANN and 24% better search accuracy for 8-byte encodings on Deep1M – with extensive ablation studies demonstrating the effect of each component.

### Strengths
- The proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance on several benchmarks, with significant improvements in quantization error and retrieval accuracy (all reviewers).
- Extensive empirical evaluations across multiple datasets, including detailed breakdowns of each adjustment’s effect on quality and encode/decode time relative to QINCo (R1, R2, R3, R5).
- The beam search and codeword pre-selection are a clear advancement for encoding efficiency without sacrificing accuracy (R2).
- The pairwise additive decoder is an effective tool for faster approximate decoding that captures codeword dependencies (R2, R5).
- The method is faster for retrieval tasks, which is important for industry-scale applications (R4, R5).
- Figures are well-crafted and make the paper easy to understand (R3). One reviewer (rating 8) additionally praised the paper as concise, well-written, and easy to read, and found the improvements very significant (R5). Another reviewer gave a lower rating (6) but still acknowledged that the method is “concise and effective” and that experiments are extensive (R5). Note: one reviewer (R4) found the paper hard to follow (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Latency and practical applicability**: The encoding/decoding speed of QINCo2 is much slower than PQ and RQ (nearly an order of magnitude lower QPS in low-recall regions, Figure 6). The paper does not compare latency of the full retrieval pipeline with other methods. Reviewers question why improving accuracy at the cost of QPS is necessary and whether the method is practical in real-time scenarios. Several requests: add a latency comparison of the full pipeline (R1), provide a more comprehensive comparison of encoding/decoding speed against QINCo (as in [1] Table 3) (R1), and analyze computational bottlenecks and potential optimizations (e.g., hardware acceleration) (R2). One reviewer doubts that PQ+RQ with a cheap storage medium could beat QINCo2 in both storage and decoding cost and asks for an explicit use case quantification (R3). Additionally, comparisons with conventional RQ/PQ are only at R@1; at relaxed settings (R@5, R@10, or with re-rank) the recall gap may narrow while QINCo2 remains much slower – this should be evaluated (R1, R4).
- **Limited novelty and theoretical depth**: The work is primarily an engineering refinement of the original QINCo, combining existing ideas (codeword pre-selection, beam search, pairwise additive decoding) without groundbreaking novelty (R3, R4). The iterative training and multiple heuristics make the approach seem like a refinement rather than a fundamental advance; reliance on hyperparameter tuning may limit robustness (R2). Both this paper and the original QINCo leave the optimization process unclear: the loss function is non-differentiable but SGD is used, with only vague details about how the optimization is actually carried out (R4). More mathematical analysis or theoretical proofs (e.g., convergence of beam search, optimality of codeword pre-selection) would strengthen the paper (R2).
- **Architecture and training details missing or insufficient**:
  - In Table 3, “Improved Architecture” slightly improves search accuracy on lower-dimensional datasets (BigANN, Deep). It is unclear whether this improvement stems from increasing network size or from other architectural changes. The paper should provide a detailed parameter comparison (number of layers, layer sizes, activation functions) rather than just a parameter count (R5).
  - The “Improved Training” incorporates larger training sets but yields only limited gains. With a fixed 70 epochs and sequential acquisition of 10M splits, it is unclear whether the model achieves optimal convergence; a convergence analysis (training loss over time) for both QINCo and QINCo2 is needed to justify the training procedure and the choice of 70 epochs. Also, it is unclear if the learning rate was adjusted with increased dataset size (R5).
  - In line 205, the paper states that network \(g\) uses the same architecture as \(f\). Did the authors experiment with alternative architectures for \(g\) (e.g., a simpler linear projection or shallow NN)? A discussion of design choices and trade-offs in computational cost vs. accuracy is needed (R2).
  - In the beam search description (Figure 2, “Keep \(A\) candidates for each beam”), did the authors consider keeping a single candidate set for multiple beams? The effects on search accuracy and computational efficiency should be discussed (R2).
- **Comparisons and missing results**:
  - The paper does not compare with non-uniform quantization methods that use adaptive codebook selection or dynamic bit allocation (R2).
  - The paper claims in Section 4.1 that recall percentages at ranks 1, 10, and 100 are considered, but R@10 and R@100 results are not included in the paper (R5). These are also needed to assess the recall-efficiency trade-off at more realistic operating points (R1, R4).
  - Dataset names in Table 3 should be consistent with those used in Section 4.2 (i.e., BigANN1M, Deep1M, Contriever1M, FB-ssnpp1M) (R5).
  - A statement in Section 4.3 about “2M successive least-squares problems” in RQ-based codebook approximation is confusing because there are only \(M\) steps in RQ (R5).
- **Presentation and clarity**: While most reviewers found the paper well-written and figures helpful (R3, R5), one reviewer found the paper very hard to follow. Specifically, it is not completely clear why introducing a second neural network for pre-selection speeds up encoding (and increases training time) (R4). The same reviewer also noted that the paper lacks a clear explanation of how the non-differentiable objective is optimized with SGD (R4).
- **Reproducibility**: Source code is not released; given that the models are small and trained on open datasets, releasing code would aid reproducibility (R3).
- **Applicability to other domains**: The paper does not explore extending QINCo/QINCo2 to large language model embeddings (e.g., LLaMA family) by adapting to different embedding spaces and dimensionalities (R2).
