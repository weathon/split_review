- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a theoretical framework for multi-agent generative flow networks (MA-GFlowNets) with four algorithms (Centralized Flow Network, Independent Flow Network, Joint Flow Network, Conditioned Joint Flow Network), a local-global principle, and experiments on a toy hyper-grid environment and the StarCraft 3m map. Reviewers generally agree it is one of the first works to extend GFlowNets to multi-agent settings, but raise major concerns about clarity, novelty, experimental evaluation, and missing theoretical details. One reviewer is notably more positive (rating 5), emphasizing originality and strong hyper-grid results; the other two (ratings 3) find the text very hard to follow and the StarCraft results unconvincing.

### Strengths
- Originality and significance: One of the first works to study the extension of GFlowNets to multi-agent cooperative tasks (Reviewers 1, 2). Provides an extensive theoretical framework and results (Reviewer 2).
- Proposes four distinct algorithms (CFN, IFN, JFN, CJFN) and discusses their training complexity and performance differences (Reviewer 1).
- The direction of applying GFlowNets to RL tasks is noted as novel (Reviewer 2).
- On the toy hyper-grid environment, the proposed methods outperform MAPPO and MCMC baselines in terms of modes found and L1 error (Reviewer 1). However, Reviewer 2 and 3 find the StarCraft results weak (see Weaknesses).
- Reviewer 3 finds the paper easy to understand (though missing details), whereas Reviewer 2 finds the text very unclear (see Weaknesses — this contrast is noted).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and presentation** (all reviewers): Reviewer 2 reports heavy and inconsistent notation, missing definitions (e.g., state map \( S \) never used, transition kernel depends only on action, equation \(\prod_i p^{(i)} \circ S \circ \pi = \mathrm{Id}\) unexplained, task not formally defined, local rewards \( R^{(i)} \) defined only later, their expectation is intractable, and GFlowNet theory does not support stochastic rewards). Objects used in theorems appear undefined before use (line 241). Reviewer 1 finds the flow-matching formulations different from the original GFlowNet and hard to follow, and suggests using the original formulations. Reviewer 3 says the paper is easy to understand but lacks important details.

- **Novelty concerns** (Reviewers 2, 3): Reviewer 3 states the claim “GFlowNets cannot support multi-agent systems” ignores prior work (Li et al., 2023) that already explored multi-agent GFlowNets; the paper fails to clearly articulate fundamental differences, making the contribution seem incremental. Reviewer 2 asks how the framework differs from Luo et al. (2024) (Multi-Agent Continuous Control with Generative Flow Networks). Reviewer 1 notes the main challenge of extending GFlowNets to multi-agent settings is unclear, as there appears to be no technical difficulty.

- **Experimental evaluation limitations** (all reviewers):
  - Only the simplest StarCraft II map (3m) is evaluated (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). The proposed JFN falls behind 3 out of 4 baselines (including Independent Q-Learning) on win rate, contradicting the abstract’s claim of superiority (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 asks why only JFN results are presented for StarCraft.
  - Very high L1 errors in Figure 3 suggest poor learning and that algorithms cannot sample the reward distribution in practice (Reviewers 1, 3). Insufficient analysis of why these errors are so high and how they affect practical utility (Reviewer 3). No comparison to single-agent GFlowNets (Reviewer 3).
  - Marginal performance improvements over baselines do not provide compelling evidence of effectiveness (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 suggests correcting the claims and presenting diversity metrics (e.g., measure of sample diversity) instead of (or in addition to) win rate.

- **Missing theoretical and methodological details**:
  - Only the flow-matching objective is studied; applicability to other GFlowNet learning objectives (detailed balance, trajectory balance) is not discussed (Reviewer 1).
  - The paper does not address how the proposed method handles cyclic state transitions in StarCraft II, while GFlowNets traditionally assume acyclic state spaces (Reviewer 3).
  - The motivation for using multi-agent GFlowNets in the chosen tasks is not well justified; many problems could be solved with single-agent GFlowNets, but no rationale for the multi-agent approach is provided (Reviewer 3).
  - Local rewards are defined via an intractable expectation; using stochastic rewards is mentioned but is not supported by the GFlowNet theory the paper builds on (Reviewer 2).
