## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Hi-Patch, a hierarchical patch graph network for Irregular Multivariate Time Series (IMTS). It uses intra-patch and inter-patch graph layers to capture local and global temporal and inter-variable dependencies at multiple scales. The intra-patch layer models fine-grained dependencies within each patch via fully connected graphs, then aggregates to patch-level nodes. Multiple inter-patch layers are stacked to extract progressively coarser features. The method is evaluated on 8 datasets for forecasting and classification, outperforming various state-of-the-art models. The writing is clear and well-structured. However, several weaknesses regarding technical novelty, presentation, experimental design, and missing analyses are raised across reviewers.

### Strengths
- Hi-Patch offers a unique hierarchical architecture that effectively handles irregularity and multi-scale nature of IMTS, extracting features from fine-grained to coarse-grained (Reviewers 1, 3).
- It addresses an important challenge in time series analysis: handling irregular, asynchronous observations across multivariate data with distinct sampling rates (Reviewers 1, 4).
- The approach is an application-oriented extension of multi-scale methods, modeling three dependency dimensions: same variable across different times, different variables at same time, and different variables across different times (Reviewer 2).
- Patch aggregation is elegant and simple; a unified Multi-Head Attention (MHA) mechanism is effectively used across both intra-patch and inter-patch layers (Reviewer 2).
- The methodology is well-structured and systematically evaluated on 8 datasets, with ablation studies providing insight into component contributions (Reviewers 1, 4). Performance is superior to a range of state-of-the-art models (Reviewers 1, 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty.** The multi-scale approach is well-established in other domains; the intra-patch/inter-patch distinction is largely a reconfiguration of existing graph network concepts (Reviewer 4). One reviewer (score 5) specifically noted that innovative aspects distinguishing Hi-Patch from existing patching or GNN methods are not well articulated (Reviewer 1).
- **Presentation and figure clarity.** The abstract and introduction lack clear articulation of the connection between multi-scale and multivariate modeling; the explanation of intra-patch and inter-patch layers is confusing. Figures 1 and 2 are ineffective: Figure 1 lacks readability, and Figure 2 fails to clearly illustrate the intra-patch and inter-patch processes (e.g., the right side of Figure 2 is unclear on intra-patch aggregation). Moreover, the framework in Figure 2 is not easily understood (Reviewers 3, 4). References should follow a uniform style (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing precise mathematical description.** The paper presents only intermediate transformation steps rather than a full, step-by-step description of how inputs are transformed into outputs across the entire pipeline (Reviewer 3).
- **Concerns about experimental comparisons and parameter tuning.** There is potential imbalance in parameter selection across compared methods, which could skew the comparative analysis (Reviewer 1). 
- **Limited datasets and generality.** IMTS datasets are scarce, leading to the inclusion of classification tasks. The model should be tested on traditional fixed-frequency datasets (e.g., ECL, ETT, PEMS) in an appendix to demonstrate generality. Given Hi-Patch’s structure, it should be able to handle fixed-sampling-rate data directly (Reviewer 2).
- **Invalid experiment in Appendix F.1.** The experiment on varying input length does not yield substantial conclusions and fails to reflect the model’s ability to learn longer-term temporal dependencies. It should be replaced with a line chart, fixing either input or prediction length and varying the other (e.g., on PhysioNet, use input lengths {12, 24, 48, 96, 192} hours to predict 24 hours ahead) (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing handling of empty patches.** When given a fixed patch length (e.g., 10 seconds) and no observations occur within a patch (e.g., V1 and V2 missing data between 10–20 time steps in Figure 2), it is unclear how the intra-patch graph is generated. The model overlooks missing values, which may cause significant information loss (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Complexity and efficiency.** The hierarchical architecture may increase training/inference time, which is a drawback for real-time applications. A complexity analysis (with comparisons to other baselines) is missing (Reviewers 1, 3). Additionally, entanglement of spatial and temporal correlation modeling may further increase training complexity (Reviewer 3).
- **Interpretability analysis missing.** Interpretability is important in fields like healthcare and climate science but is not addressed (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing explanation of practical query design.** For real-world IMTS prediction, input should include a query; how the query should be designed in practical applications is not explained (Reviewer 2).
- **Patch size selection.** In Figure 5(a) and (b), patch size 3 might be a better choice; the effect of patch size deserves deeper analysis (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear meaning of d in Equation 4.** The symbol d is not defined (Reviewer 2).
