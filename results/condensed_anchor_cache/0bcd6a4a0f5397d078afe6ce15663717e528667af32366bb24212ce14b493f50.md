- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 6, 3, 8

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies the generalization ability of temporal graph learning (TGL) algorithms (GNN-, RNN-, and memory-based) under a finite-wide over-parameterized regime. It derives an upper bound on the generalization error that depends on the number of layers/steps and a feature-label alignment (FLA) score. Based on this, it proposes SToNe, a simplified model with few layers and small FLA. Reviewers agree the topic is relevant and the goal is well-motivated, but disagree on the strength of the theory, the novelty of SToNe, and the empirical performance compared to baselines. One reviewer (score 8) finds the theory clear and SToNe’s empirical results strong; another (score 3) finds the theory’s advantage unclear, the method not novel, and the empirical gains unconvincing. Two other reviewers (scores 5 and 6) have concerns about the framing of the theoretical contributions and gaps in the proofs.

### Strengths
- The considered problem of temporal graph learning is relevant and fitting for ICLR; the goal of explaining design choices for TGL is well-motivated (Reviewer 1).
- The connection between FLA and model performance in TGL is interesting, and introducing FLA to TGL is original; the main body is well organized (Reviewer 2).
- The theoretical analysis and proofs are very detailed, with ample material in the appendix; the discovered relation between FLA and generalization error is intriguing (Reviewer 3).
- The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) is clear, depends on reasonable assumptions, and covers many TGL models; the subsequent analysis of architectures, hyperparameters, and FLA is compelling (Reviewer 4).
- SToNe shows strong empirical performance on link prediction in transductive and inductive settings, with considerably fewer parameters and better running times; the supplementary material contains helpful ablation and sensitivity analyses (Reviewer 4).
- Empirical results suggest that constraining the number of layers and FLA indeed helps in designing TGL algorithms (Reviewer 1).
- The idea is original and the quality of the idea is great (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical framing**: The result is only an upper bound, not a lower bound, so it can merely *indicate* desirable aspects of methods, not explain performance differences (Reviewer 1). The advantage of FLA over existing generalization bounds (e.g., Rademacher complexity, PAC-Bayesian) is not articulated; it is unclear if FLA provides additional insights or is just another measure correlated with generalization error (Reviewer 3). FLA is defined via gradients, so it can be used for all gradient-based algorithms and is not specific to TGL; the analysis seems equally applicable to static graphs, weakening the claim of relevance to TGL specifically (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 asks for a comparison with existing generalization bounds and discussion of key differences in assumptions and implications.
- **Proof gaps and ambiguity** (Reviewer 2, confidence 3): (1) Definition of FLA depends on the gradient of \(f\) w.r.t. \(\theta\); ReLU networks are non-smooth (gradient undefined at 0), and it is unclear how this is handled theoretically and practically. (2) In Theorem 1, when \(\theta_0\) is randomly initialized, \(R\) is a random variable; comparing two random variables in the result is vague, and the interpretation of the comparison across different initializations is unclear. (3) Lemma 5 relies on Lemma 8 from Zhu et al. (2022), which assumed \(W\) is a Gaussian random matrix, but in this paper \(W\) comes from SGD; that lemma may not be applicable, leading to flaws in bounding \(\|\partial \text{loss}_i(\theta)/\partial W^{(l)}\|\). (4) The construction in the proof of Theorem 2 is confusing (e.g., why \(\langle \nabla_\theta f_i(\theta_0), \theta \rangle = y_i(B+B')\)) and it is not clear how \(R\) makes a difference in the result.
- **FLA-related issues** (multiple reviewers): The authors claim that memory blocks increase FLA (page 7, second bullet), but no formal argument is provided; is this always true? (Reviewer 1). Rankings in Figure 3 do not seem consistent across methods and need further explanation (Reviewer 4). The invertibility of \(JJ^\top\) is not clearly discussed; the referenced result (Nguyen, 2021) depends on the assumption that at least one layer has more parameters than samples, and it is not clear that the used architectures satisfy this (Reviewer 4). The current experiments only show the effect of FLA on SToNe itself; empirical FLA comparisons across different methods on real datasets are missing (Reviewers 2, 3). In experiments, the authors still use the generalization gap (difference between train/val AP) rather than directly using FLA to compare algorithms (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited novelty of proposed method SToNe**: SToNe is essentially a GNN method augmented with input data selection; it is similar to existing methods except for the feature engineering component, and the input selection does not represent a significant algorithmic innovation (Reviewer 3). However, Reviewer 2 describes the design as novel and based on theoretical findings, so there is disagreement on this point.
- **Empirical performance concerns** (Reviewer 3, confidence 4): Some existing methods (e.g., TGN, GraphMixer) achieve similar average precision on the first five datasets; the only clear improvement is on the UCI dataset, but it is unclear whether UCI has a special distribution or setting. Most methods perform well on all datasets except UCI; why? (Reviewer 3). In contrast, Reviewer 4 (score 8) states SToNe shows clearly strong empirical performances, including in the supplementary material. The empirical comparison is not universally convincing to all reviewers.
- **Presentation and clarity issues** (Reviewers 1, 4): The presentations of methods in Section 3 are unclear without checking the literature – no context for Algorithm 1 in the optimization literature, the time-encoding vector is never clearly defined, and the concept of temporal events for RNN is not clear (Reviewer 4). A clear illustration of SToNe would be useful (Reviewer 4). Theorem 1 does not discuss the dynamics w.r.t. \(m^*\), so it is unclear whether the chosen \(m \ge m^*\) in the experiments is relevant (Reviewer 4).
- **Typos and minor errors**: Several listed on page 1 (last line: "has demonstrated"), page 4 (notation for \(\text{loss}^{0-1}_i\)), Algorithm 1 (conditioned misspelled), Assumption 1 ("have \(\ell_2\)-norm"), Theorem 1 ("R is fixed" appears twice), and others (Reviewer 1). Multiple typographical errors in the supplementary material as well (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing ablation justification**: The need for LayerNormalization in SToNe (e.g., vs. no normalization or BatchNormalization) is not discussed (Reviewer 4, question Q1). The list of additional experiments in the supplementary mentioned in the “More experimental results” paragraph should be completed (Reviewer 4 remark).
