## Merged Review

### Summary
UniCBE is a uniformity-driven comparing-based evaluation (CBE) framework that jointly optimizes accuracy, convergence, and scalability via three decoupled sampling probability matrices (\(P^{acc-l}\), \(P^{con-l}\), \(P^{sca-l}\)). Experiments on AlpacaEval (and some on MT-Bench) show a Pearson correlation over 0.995 with ground truth, saving ≥17% of evaluation budget, and over 50% when new models are introduced. Reviewers generally acknowledge the importance of efficient LLM evaluation and the value of addressing multiple objectives, but raise concerns about motivation clarity, reliance on GPT-4 as the sole automatic judge, complexity of the framework, limited runtime analysis, insufficient comparison with prior work, and a potential contradiction between uniform sampling and improved sample efficiency.

### Strengths
- Efficient evaluation methods are essential for increasingly large-scale LLMs; the paper addresses relevant limitations of conventional CBE. (R1)
- The integration of accuracy, convergence, and scalability in a uniformity-driven approach is a valuable contribution, setting UniCBE apart from single-objective methods. (R1)
- The problem is important and non-trivial; the proposed method provides a solid theoretical foundation, and experimental results demonstrate the efficiency of the method. (R2)
- The experiments cover three dimensions well, and ablation studies on each variant show most variants play their roles well. (R2)
- The study of using preference signals with lower evaluation budgets is an important research direction with huge potential. (R3)
- UniCBE introduces a straightforward yet impactful approach to balancing three critical objectives; the quality is supported by extensive empirical analysis across diverse models, benchmarks, and settings, including testing assumptions in Section 3 and performance validation across multiple configurations in Section 5.4. (R4)
- The paper is clearly presented with structured key concepts and experimental results. (R4)
- The experiments are extensive and convincing with strong results showing the advantage of the proposed method. (R5)

### Weaknesses
- **Automated judge assumption.** The framework assumes preference signals from automated judges (especially GPT-4) are consistent with human judgment, a potentially risky simplification given known limitations of automated preference evaluations. The reliance on GPT-4 and GPT-3.5-turbo as evaluators could benefit from validation against human judgments or additional LLMs (e.g., Claude) to establish greater reliability and generalizability across evaluator types. (R1, R4)
- **Complexity and interpretability.** The formulation of multi-dimensional sampling matrices and their interaction may be overly complex for practical implementation and difficult to interpret for further tuning or adjustment. The weighting scheme introduces abstraction that could obscure the impact of individual components on overall performance, making it unclear how to diagnose issues or adjust parameters when the system behaves unexpectedly. (R1)
- **Unclear motivation.** Why previous CBE methods cannot perform well in all three aspects (accuracy, convergence, scalability) is not sufficiently clarified. (R2)
- **Missing runtime analysis.** The paper mentions the \(O(NM^2)\) runtime of previous CBE methods as a motivation but lacks runtime analysis for UniCBE, providing only approximate budget savings rather than a formal complexity comparison. (R2)
- **Limited handling of dynamic settings.** While UniCBE shows promise for periodically introduced new models, it may be less efficient in highly dynamic, real-time evaluation settings where models or samples are constantly introduced at high frequencies. The paper could enhance the scalability analysis by incorporating real-world scenarios where models enter and exit dynamically. (R2, R4)
- **Need for more performance comparisons.** The paper could be improved with more performance comparison against existing work and state-of-the-art methods. More statistical and experimental conclusions for the scenario of large-scale preference learning are expected. (R3)
- **Novelty concerns.** Balancing accuracy, convergence, and scalability may need further justification, as similar uniform sampling strategies to address sampling biases have been discussed in prior works (e.g., Vabalas et al., 2019), which could diminish its uniqueness. (R4)
- **Figure readability.** The readability of all figures could be enhanced by widening the lines in each plot. (R4)
- **Steerability and hyperparameter control.** Since UniCBE is based on three matrices (\(P^{acc-l}, P^{con-l}, P^{sca-l}\)), can users steer between these objectives by adding hyperparameters for each matrix? Could this be quantified through experiments? (R4)
- **Sensitivity to sampling and aggregation choices.** The choice of greedy sampling over probabilistic sampling and Bradley-Terry over Elo appears significant to success. Can a small experiment demonstrate that UniCBE maintains effectiveness across different sampling and aggregation settings? (R4)
- **Contradiction between uniformity and sample efficiency.** The authors argue that to avoid bias, budget should be allocated uniformly. If so, how can this method be more sample-efficient than random sampling? One possible reason is that if model A is much better than B and B much better than C, then comparing A and C often is unnecessary. But if that is the reason, it would seem to contradict the uniform assumption. More insight is needed. (R5)
