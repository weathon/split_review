## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the first natural language-based speech evaluation corpus, generated from authentic human ratings, which includes MOS scores and detailed multi-dimensional text descriptions. It proposes an alignment approach with LLM distillation (ALLD) to guide an audio LLM in extracting quality-relevant information and generating meaningful responses. Experiments show ALLD achieves MSE 0.17 on MOS prediction, A/B test accuracy 98.6%, and BLEU scores of 25.8 and 30.2 on two tasks, outperforming prior regression models and task-specific models. The work aims to enhance speech quality awareness in audio LLMs for real-world agents.

### Strengths
- Proposes MOS prediction within a popular audio LLM scheme; method clearly presented and effectively detailed (Reviewer 1).
- ALLD achieves best performance across all systems according to evaluation metrics; BLEU score demonstrates the efficacy of the distillation strategy (Reviewer 2).
- Introduces a novel dataset pairing MOS ratings with natural language-based assessments across multiple quality dimensions (noisiness, coloration, discontinuity, loudness), enabling descriptive evaluations (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- ALLD framework is interesting, leveraging token-level distillation based on implicit reward modeling from DPO (Reviewer 4).
- Shows speech quality evaluation can be integrated into multitask learning without degrading other tasks (Reviewer 4).
- Addresses a critical need for reliable automated metrics and reduces high costs of subjective evaluation; significance for real-world auditory agents (Reviewer 3).
- Comparisons between traditional regression methods and descriptive audio LLM-based approaches offer valuable insights into fine-tuning and demonstrate effectiveness of natural language guidance (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **MOS standard conflict:** Example in Section 1 gives MOS=2.4 as a decimal, but ITU-T defines MOS as an integer between 1 and 5. This discrepancy suggests a fundamental misalignment with the official standard, impacting validity (Reviewer 1).
- **Incremental contribution:** Extending audio LLMs to MOS prediction appears a relatively incremental extension of prior works like SALMONN, which already covers a wide range of audio understanding tasks. Methodological novelty may be limited for ICLR (Reviewer 1 – minority; score 5). Note: other reviewers (scores 8,8,6) considered the work original and significant.
- **Incomplete training pipeline description:** SFT is not mentioned until Section 4.3, leaving readers with the impression RL alone trains the model. Details of the SFT stage (dataset, loss function, training procedure) are missing. The term “full-ft” appears later but it is unclear if it is synonymous with SFT; inconsistent terminology harms reproducibility and interpretability (Reviewer 1).
- **LCC and SRCC not improved on certain datasets:** For LIVE and P501 datasets, improvements in LCC and SRCC are not observed. A clear explanation is needed (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4 – question incorporated as criticism).
- **Descriptive language subjectivity:** Descriptive evaluations are inherently subjective to users. Unlike numeric scores (e.g., BLEU), it is unclear how to maintain uniformity in descriptive style across generations (Reviewer 2).
- **Ambiguity in synthesizing sub-dimension preferences:** When System 1 outperforms System 2 in one sub-dimension but falls behind in another, the framework for generating an overall preference judgment (A/B) is unclear. The dataset relies on LLM responses to make arbitrary decisions using sub-dimensional scores as reasoning, raising concerns about consistency and interpretability (Reviewer 3).
- **Potential bias from same Teacher model:** The Teacher model used during distillation is also the description generator for the training dataset. This could bias BLEU scores, which are computed on synthetic descriptions from the same Teacher. Generalizability of ALLD on independently generated descriptions (e.g., a smaller set of human descriptions) is not explored (Reviewer 3).
- **Unsourced claim about VoiceCraft edits:** The paper claims human listeners cannot distinguish between original and VoiceCraft-edited speech but provides no direct perceptual test or citation to support this. This undermines dataset reliability (Reviewer 3).
- **Dataset generation details lacking:** The data generation process is not clearly described; it appears the corpus is from previous work with LLM-generated descriptions, but prompts, the LLM used, and potential biases are unspecified (Reviewer 4).
- **Improvement over Full-ft not significant:** While ALLD outperforms Full-ft, the improvement appears marginal. The paper lacks statistical significance analysis and ablation studies (e.g., performance without token-level distillation or RL fine-tuning) to attribute gains to specific components (Reviewer 4).
