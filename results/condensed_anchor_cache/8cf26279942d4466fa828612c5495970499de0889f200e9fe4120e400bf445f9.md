## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper addresses Noisy Partial Label Learning (NPLL), where the true label may not be in the candidate label set (CLS). The authors derive a generalization error bound depending on noise rate and average CLS length. Based on this, they propose a method that progressively separates noisy from normal samples and reconstructs shorter CLSs for both. Experiments on multiple benchmarks (e.g., CIFAR-100 with severe noise, +11.57% over SOTA) show strong performance. Reviewers generally find the work novel and well-executed, but raise concerns about theoretical precision, certain method details, hyperparameter sensitivity, and missing comparisons.

### Strengths

1.  **Theoretical foundation**: The generalization error bound that identifies noise rate and CLS length as key factors is insightful and guides the method design. (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5)
2.  **Method grounded in theory**: The proposed progressive sample separation and CLS reconstruction naturally reduce noise rate and shrink CLS size, aligning with the theoretical findings. (R1, R2, R3)
3.  **Strong experimental results**: Extensive experiments on multiple benchmark datasets (e.g., CIFAR-100 with severe noise) show significant improvement over state-of-the-art methods (e.g., +11.57%). (R1, R2, R3, R5)
4.  **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-organized and easy to follow. (R2, R4, R5)
5.  **Plug-in capability**: The method can enhance existing PLL methods on NPLL datasets. (R3)
6.  **Novel problem formulation**: The work is considered a groundbreaking contribution to the NPLL problem with significant theoretical advancement. (R4)

### Weaknesses

1.  **Theoretical imprecision**: The variable \(f_y(x)\) is undefined. The convergence statement (line 138) is imprecise: even as \(n \to \infty, \epsilon \to 0, \alpha \to 1\), the Rademacher complexity may not converge to zero, so \(R(\hat{f})\) may not converge to \(R(f^*)\), nor does \(\hat{f}\) necessarily converge to \(f^*\). (R2)
2.  **ECK metric and discrepancy rationale insufficiently justified**: The rationale for why a discrepancy between CLS-based and KNN-based pseudo-labels indicates noisy samples is not fully explained. Reviewers question how the model’s training state influences similarity, why KNN-based pseudo-labels are more reliable when the ground truth is outside the CLS, and what the theoretical basis for ECK is. Experimental support for the claimed discrepancy is requested. (R1, R2)
3.  **KNN reliability concerns**: When the non-CLS is large, the probability of noisy samples with high KNN-based pseudo-labels in the non-CLS increases, potentially misleading noise detection. When many samples are noisy, neighboring samples may also be noisy, making the KNN-based pseudo-label unreliable. (R1, R4)
4.  **No comparison with recent work (Lv et al., 2024)**. (R3)
5.  **Lack of hyperparameter sensitivity analysis**: The method introduces several hyperparameters (warm-up epoch, percentile change rate, trade-off parameter for CLS reconstruction, KNN parameter \(K\)) but no systematic analysis of their impact or robustness across datasets/noise conditions is provided. (R1)
6.  **Computational efficiency concerns**: Training the classifier on the entire dataset every epoch introduces high time complexity, limiting practical applicability for large datasets. (R4)
7.  **Ablation study shows \(v_2\) ineffective on CIFAR-10**: The \(v_2\) component appears not to help in separating normal and noisy samples on this dataset. (R5)
8.  **Datasets are few and small**: More evaluation on larger datasets is needed to validate effectiveness. (R5)
9.  **Warm-up stage under high noise**: The impact of the warm-up stage (which uses noisy labels) is not analyzed; if warm-up produces poor features, biases may propagate. (R2)
10. **Missing ablations/requests**:
    *   Whether Eq.(2) requires prior knowledge of the CLS of normal samples. (R1)
    *   How to decide the parameter \(K\) in KNN. (R1)
    *   Whether the operation of adding/removing labels (Eq.(10)) destroys CLS diversity, and why it is beneficial. (R1)
    *   Derivation of the second inequality in Eq.(18) is needed. (R1)
    *   Duplicate references (lines 648-654). (R1)
    *   Could network prediction \(p_j\) be used instead of KNN-based \(q_j\) in Eq.(6)? Empirical comparison requested. (R3)
    *   In Eq.(3), why use CE with \(\tilde{q}\) as target instead of using the KNN-based pseudo-label directly as target? (R5)
    *   In the sentence below Eq.(10), \(c'\) should be \(\arg\min\) instead of \(\arg\max\). (R5)
    *   Theoretical justification for the assumption that reliable samples become more reliable and unreliable samples become more unreliable as training progresses. (R4)

*Note: Reviewer 4 (score 5) was more critical, particularly on computational efficiency and KNN reliability, while other reviewers (scores 6) were generally more positive.*
