## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Chain of Manipulations (CoM), a mechanism that enables VLMs to solve visual problems step-by-step using intrinsic manipulations (e.g., grounding, zoom in) and producing intermediate results (e.g., boxes, images). The authors propose: (1) a flexible design of manipulations, (2) an automated data generation pipeline (70K samples from GPT-4, GroundingDINO, PaddleOCR), (3) a compatible VLM architecture for multi-turn multi-image reasoning, and (4) a training procedure. They also manually annotate 7K high-quality samples for mathematical problems. The resulting model, CogCoM (17B parameters), achieves SOTA on 9 benchmarks across 4 categories. Code, model, and data will be publicly released.

### Strengths
- The proposed method of defining image manipulation operations to solve queries requiring detailed visual inspection is intuitive and clear.
- Extensive experiments demonstrate strong empirical performance, achieving SOTA on multiple benchmarks across various tasks.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with many qualitative examples.
- The automatically collected CoM dataset (70K) and human-annotated mathematical reasoning data (7K) have potential utility for the broader community.
- The contributions are sound: the automated data generation pipeline includes a pseudo-verification step for correctness, and human annotation adds high-quality data.
- Code, model, and data are publicly released.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and related work:** The core idea of training VLMs to generate programs using visual expert modules is not new. Multiple reviewers note strong similarity to prior visual programming methods such as VisProg, ViperGPT, GPT4Tools, LLaVA-Plus, Visual Sketchpad, V*, Visual Program Distillation (CVPR 2024), and Ferret-v2. The paper lacks quantitative and qualitative comparisons to these methods and does not adequately discuss the relationship—especially whether the defined manipulations (ZoomIn/Crop) are a specific case of operations in those works. This omission weakens the claimed novelty and contribution.
- **Unfair evaluation:** Comparisons in many benchmarks are against single-turn VLMs (e.g., CogVLM). Since CogCoM uses multi-turn reasoning with CoM, this makes the comparisons unfair. A proper evaluation would require multi-turn baselines or an ablation isolating the CoM component.
- **Limited grounding improvements:** While VQA benchmarks show large gains, improvements on grounding benchmarks (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg) are not significant. No analysis is provided to explain whether grounding improvements are limited to intermediate steps in complex reasoning chains or also benefit direct grounding.
- **Data contribution unclear:** The training data consists of 70K automatically generated samples and 7K human-annotated samples. The paper does not ablate the contribution of each dataset separately, nor does it justify why both are needed or whether one alone suffices. Additionally, the automatic collection filters only positive paths (35% success rate); no analysis of the distribution of these paths is provided—they may be biased toward certain question categories, limiting diversity.
- **Insufficient ablations:** The second-stage training uses multiple datasets (MultiInstruct, LLaVAR, ShareGPT4V) alongside the proposed CoM data. No ablation isolates the effect of each dataset, making it difficult to attribute improvements specifically to CoM. A reviewer notes that a single ablation (removing multi-turn data) shows minimal impact for two out of three tasks, raising questions about the actual contribution of CoM.
- **Inference ambiguity:** A reviewer questions whether CoM is used only during training data generation or also during inference. The inference-time overhead and number of VLM calls (single vs. multiple) are not clearly specified, creating ambiguity about practical deployment.
- **Presentation issues:** The related work is relegated to the appendix, making it harder to understand the paper’s contributions in context. Several grammar and orthography mistakes are present. Figure 2 may have a labeling error (“Large Language Model” should be “Vision-Language Model”), and the title uses ‘visual’ instead of ‘vision’.
- **Limited dataset size:** The human-annotated CoM dataset contains only 6K images; a reviewer notes this may lack visual diversity.
- **Missing experiments on different base VLMs:** CoM is only applied to CogVLM; it is unclear whether weaker VLMs can benefit or if strong base VLMs are a prerequisite.
- **Alternative to module-based approach:** The method uses GPT-4 to generate pseudo-annotations by calling external tools (GroundingDINO, PaddleOCR). If this module-based approach works well for data collection, the paper does not compare against using such a pipeline directly at inference (e.g., ViperGPT-style inference), leaving the advantage of training CoM unclear.
