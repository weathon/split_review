## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates strategies for fitting learning curves from performance samples on small training volumes to predict model performance on the full dataset, with the goal of reducing training time for model and hyperparameter selection. The work introduces a framework and notation for sampling strategies, proposes several strategies, and evaluates them on simulated learning curves and real image classification datasets (ImageNet, LSUN, plus a plant dataset that is mentioned but not evaluated). Key findings include that sampling two volumes with a wide spread provides a good trade-off between accuracy and cost, and that increasing the number of sampled volumes or sampling more frequently yields limited additional benefit.

### Strengths
- **Practical, cost-effective approach**: Addresses the important problem of reducing training time for model selection and hyperparameter optimization by predicting learning curves from small subsets. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Empirical results on real datasets**: Demonstrates that near-optimal model performance can be achieved with roughly half the training budget on LSUN and ImageNet. (Reviewer 2)
- **Comprehensive evaluation framework**: Includes both simulated learning curves (modeling small-data, power-law, and irreducible-error regions) and experiments with popular CNN architectures on standard datasets. (Reviewer 1)
- **Provides sampling-strategy insights**: Shows that a wide spread of volumes is beneficial, and that sampling more volumes does not significantly improve prediction compared to selecting more models. (Reviewer 1)
- **Well-structured presentation**: The paper is clearly written and contextualized within prior work on learning curves and neural scaling laws. (Reviewer 1)
- **Timely and interesting topic**: Predicting learning curves has the potential to save substantial computational costs, and a systematic exploration of strategies is valuable. (Reviewer 4)

Note: Reviewer 1 rates the paper highly (score 6, confidence 3) and sees clear novelty, while Reviewers 2, 3, and 4 each give score 3 (confidence 2) and question the novelty and clarity.

### Weaknesses
- **Power-law assumption not justified or tested**: The paper assumes a power-law relationship for learning curves without discussing its limitations, when it may not hold for many models or tasks. Alternative forms are not explored. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Novelty concerns and overlap with prior work**: The data-collection strategy appears fundamentally similar to the “training in rounds” approach of Mahmood et al. (2022b). The paper does not clearly differentiate itself. (Reviewers 2, 3)  
  - Reviewer 2: “I do not see much fundamental difference between the data collection strategy in Mahmood et al. (2022b) and subset sampling in the paper.”
- **Single subset often suffices, undermining the need for scaling-law estimation**: On ImageNet, most model-selection performance is already achieved using a single subset (i_1); this result is based purely on validation performance, not on fitting a learning curve, weakening the motivation for the proposed strategy. (Reviewer 2)
- **Evaluation limited in scope**: Only CNN-based image classification on LSUN and ImageNet is considered (the Plant dataset is mentioned in Section 5 but no results are shown). The generalizability to NLP, speech, other domains, or different model architectures (transformers, etc.) is unaddressed. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Lack of statistical significance testing**: Results report means and standard deviations over 30 runs but no statistical tests (e.g., confidence intervals, hypothesis tests) to quantify differences between strategies. Conclusions are drawn from interpreting graphs. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Clarity and presentation issues**:
  - The notation (C_s, C_t, C_N, etc.) is hard to follow. (Reviewer 4)
  - It is confusing to call the test-set loss “out-of-distribution” (OOD) when the test set comes from the same distribution as the training data. (Reviewers 2, 4)
  - Figures 2 and 3 are difficult to interpret: the x-axis, the meaning of C_s, and what individual sampling points correspond to are not clearly explained. (Reviewer 4)
  - Missing experimental details: actual performance achieved on each dataset, the range of hyperparameters explored, and a clear description of the synthetic dataset (if used). (Reviewer 2)
- **Need for more advanced sampling strategies**: Only simple static sampling strategies are evaluated; non-uniform sampling frequencies or active/adaptive approaches could yield better results. (Reviewer 3)
- **Abstract cost definition**: Costs are defined only in terms of training volume; incorporating real runtimes or FLOPs would give more practical insights. (Reviewer 3)
- **Limited gains temper impact**: The improvements over simpler baselines are modest, reducing the apparent impact of the proposed framework. (Reviewer 3)
- **Sensitivity analysis missing**: The simulated learning curve model depends on several parameters; their influence on the conclusions is not investigated. (Reviewer 3)
