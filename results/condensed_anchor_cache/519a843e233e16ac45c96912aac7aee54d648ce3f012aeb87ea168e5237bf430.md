## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes D5RL, a new offline RL benchmark with robotic manipulation and locomotion domains, including state-based and image-based tasks, and supports offline RL and online fine-tuning evaluation. Experiments show that existing offline RL algorithms (including CQL, TD3+BC, IQL) perform poorly on most tasks.

### Strengths
- The introduction of new domains and datasets offers the community a broader range of choices, potentially supporting easier development of offline algorithms (R1).
- The benchmark is well structured, motivating the need for a new benchmark, presenting criteria, and describing tasks and datasets clearly (R2). Many tasks capture real-world challenges such as extrapolating policies, environment changes, and different viewpoints (R3).
- Both image-based and proprioceptive state modalities are covered across settings and tasks (R3). The datasets are gathered from platforms embodied in the real world, which may facilitate sim-to-real transfer (R3).
- The benchmark provides evaluation results for online fine-tuning, which is meaningful because most offline RL works neglect the benefit of small portions of online data to address distribution shift (R4).
- Reviewer 3 is more positive overall, emphasizing the benchmark's real-world relevance; Reviewers 1,2,4 express significant reservations.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited diversity and incremental nature relative to existing benchmarks.** Several reviewers find that the benchmark does not significantly extend D4RL or other existing benchmarks. Reviewer 1 states that challenges like temporal compositionality are already observable in previous benchmarks (D4RL Maze2D, AntMaze, FrankaKitchen-Mixed, Calvin) and that D5RL has less diversity than D4RL. The paper does not explicitly justify why this benchmark is better for each challenge or when to choose it over prior ones. Reviewer 4 argues that the core manipulation tasks (pick-and-place) are similar, lacking the diversity of benchmarks like RoboMimic or RL-ViGen that include tool use or complex assembly; different robot arms are a minor variation. Reviewer 4 also calls the title "D5RL" misleading as it imitates D4RL and may push the community toward limited improvements on a similar benchmark.
- **Missing analyses and ablations.** Reviewer 2 notes that the near-zero returns for most methods (except BC and IQL) raise concerns: it is unclear whether the difficulty stems from visual complexity, long horizons, sub-optimal data, or a combination. No variance is reported for results. Reviewer 2 requests a random policy baseline to ensure results are not due to chance. Reviewer 1 asks for more information on the image-observation Franka Kitchen: is failure due to incomplete offline RL algorithms or lack of image representation learning? Can the task be completed solely with the provided offline data, and if not, is there an alternative data source for the community? Reviewer 2 also asks if there is only one dataset for legged locomotion and whether multiple qualities (like D4RL) can be provided; how accuracy of tracking target speed (0.75 m/s) is measured; and whether hiking is a sparse-reward task.
- **Benchmark may be too difficult or unfeasible.** Reviewer 2 finds that most methods fail to achieve meaningful performance, making it unclear if progress can be measured. The lack of variance also compromises robustness. Reviewer 4 raises a stronger concern: the poor performance of SOTA algorithms may indicate that the settings are unfeasible due to problem formulation or framework of offline RL, not just task difficulty. Reviewer 4 suggests that the benchmark may introduce artificial difficulties that hinder algorithms. Reviewers 2 and 4 both note that the absence of clear direction for improvement limits the benchmark's utility.
- **Incomplete and imbalanced algorithm selection.** Reviewer 2 points out that the benchmark includes too many value-based methods (CQL, TD3+BC, IQL) but misses return-conditioned or generative methods such as Decision Transformer (DT) and Diffusion-QL (DD), which have been shown to excel on sparse-reward and long-horizon tasks. Online DT is also a relevant baseline for the offline-to-online setting. This omission weakens the conclusions about algorithm difficulty. Additionally, Reviewer 2 questions why IQL shows a large gap relative to similar methods (CQL, TD3+BC), which often perform similarly on D4RL locomotion; this discrepancy suggests hidden factors favoring IQL or suboptimal implementations for this benchmark.
- **Online fine-tuning evaluation lacks details.** Reviewer 2 observes that Section 5.2 mentions studying offline RL and online fine-tuning for Franka Kitchen but does not explain how the task facilitates online finetuning. Reviewer 1 adds that the offline-to-online adaptation contribution appears incremental, as any simulation environment with a rule-based reward can be used for online fine-tuning.
- **Limited legged locomotion tasks and no real-world validation.** Reviewer 3 notes that only a few tasks exist for A1 legged locomotion; additional tasks like jumping and obstacle avoidance would be valuable. Reviewer 3 also stresses that no real-world evaluation is performed, leaving the extent of sim-to-real transfer unclear. Reviewer 1 similarly questions whether the benchmark domains are significantly more realistic than existing tasks, and whether the image observation tasks challenge representation learning separately.
- **Low technical contribution and limited insights.** Reviewer 4 states that the paper introduces no novel algorithms or analysis techniques, and the evaluation primarily shows failures without providing a clear direction for improvement, reducing its impact for a top-tier venue (ICLR). Other reviewers (1,2) also note the lack of ablation and missing comparisons to prior benchmarks, which limits the technical depth.
