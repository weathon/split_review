## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a benchmark to evaluate foundation models for remote sensing, focusing on robustness to changes in resolution and spectral bands. Tasks include scene classification (RESISC45, BigEarthNet) and change detection (LEVIR-CD, OSCD). The authors evaluate several existing foundation models and also pretrain variations of a self-distillation-based model (iBOT) on MillionAID with different augmentations and a pretrained mask decoder. They report that most models struggle to generalize across scales and bands, and that frozen backbones preserve generalization better than fine-tuned ones.

### Strengths
- The topic is highly valuable: benchmarking remote sensing foundation models is crucial for advancing the field, and addressing unique requirements like resilience to scale and spectral variations fills an essential gap (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The experiments on spectral bands and the pretrained mask decoder are interesting (Reviewer 1). Design choices are reasonable and significant benchmarks are run with multiple trials generating error bars (Reviewer 3).
- The paper makes an effort to connect benchmark results with real-world implications, enhancing relevance (Reviewer 2). It reveals limitations of current models and covers two practical challenges (resolution and spectral band variation) in a comprehensive way (Reviewer 4).
- The text is clear and well written (Reviewers 3, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited task scope**: Only scene classification and change detection are explored. Missing critical tasks like semantic segmentation, object detection, regression, and novel tasks in ecology/climate/agriculture that desperately need foundation models due to label scarcity. The paper does not justify why these tasks were excluded and how their inclusion would provide a more comprehensive evaluation (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Missing important foundation models**: The evaluation excludes many well-known remote-sensing-specific models such as Scale-MAE (designed for resolution variations), SatMAE (temporal/locational encoding), RemoteCLIP, Skyscript, DOFA, SSL4EO-S12, MMEarth, msGFM, and SatMAE++. Including these would benchmark architectures purpose-built to handle the complexities of remote sensing (Reviewers 1, 2). Also missing comparison with smaller models like ResNet50 to illustrate trade-offs (Reviewer 1).
- **Unfair comparisons and lack of clear generalizability definition**: Different foundation models are trained on different datasets and use different augmentations (e.g., DINOv2 uses random cropping/scaling, while others may not). This makes direct comparisons potentially unfair. The paper does not provide a clear definition of generalizability, nor does it discuss how differences in training data and augmentations impact conclusions (Reviewer 1).
- **Hyperparameter concerns**: The paper uses the same learning rate across all models and does not optimize learning rates separately for backbones versus task-specific heads (classification, segmentation, change detection). This makes performance comparisons unconvincing (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient coverage of variations and factors beyond resolution/spectral bands**: The study omits important variables such as geographical location, times of day, seasonal variations, and environmental conditions (weather, time of year). It also does not include ablation studies on model/dataset size or compare different pretraining datasets (e.g., FAIR1M, MillionAID). This leaves significant gaps in understanding broader adaptability (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 4 additionally notes that the claim that generalization to other geographical locations is hard to study is underwhelming, given that datasets like EuroSAT/LoveDA cover various landscapes and Sentinel-2 provides year-round data.
- **Lack of novelty and no proposed solutions**: The paper only tests and applies existing architectures/metrics; it offers no new algorithm, architecture, methodology, or proposed solution (e.g., new loss mechanism, architecture redesign) to address the generalization problems it identifies. The conclusion that frozen backbones preserve generalization is intuitive and widely accepted, making the repeated training seem redundant (Reviewer 4).
- **Disagreement with the authors' resolution assumption**: Reviewer 3 strongly disagrees that inference-time images are more likely lower-resolution. In many real-world scenarios, high-resolution data (private satellites, planes, drones) is used for specific tasks while leveraging decades of low-resolution data, and satellite imagery is improving over time, so adapting low to high resolution is paramount. The paper does not address this direction.
- **Inadequate interpretation of contradictory results**: The paper shows contradictory results across different tasks/datasets but provides insufficient analysis and discussion (Reviewer 3). For example, the hypothesis that "self-supervised models require less data" is stated without explanation (Reviewer 3). Additionally, the paper provides no useful information on *why* foundation models generalize badly; simply reiterating that large models suffer from overfitting does not clarify the issue (Reviewer 4).
- **Data and dataset selection concerns**: The paper does not justify the choice of datasets (RESISC45, BigEarthNet, LEVIR-CD, OSCD) relative to alternatives, nor does it discuss potential label quality issues (e.g., BigEarthNet V2 is a significant improvement over BigEarthNet). The authors should assess data quality and potential impact on results (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 notes that the study could have varied the type of pretraining datasets to provide more meaningful insights.
- **Lack of evaluation of different pretraining methods**: The paper discusses self-distillation-based models but does not compare alternative pretraining methods like DINOv2 or EVA, which are known to affect robustness and generalization differently in remote sensing contexts (Reviewer 2).
