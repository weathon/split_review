## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper extends Gaussian Process Latent Variable Models (GP-LVM) by replacing the Gaussian process with a Q-exponential process (QEP), resulting in QEP-LVM, which subsumes GP-LVM when q=2. The authors derive the ELBO for Bayesian QEP-LVM, establish connections to probabilistic PCA, and introduce a mechanism to optimize q. Experiments on Swiss roll, oil flow, and MNIST demonstrate performance improvements over GP-LVM.

### Strengths
- **Motivation and intuition**: The motivation for generalizing GP-LVM is intuitive and reasonable (R1).
- **Theoretical derivation**: The research is solid: the relationship between QEP-LVM, GP-LVM, and nonlinear PCA is shown, the tractable ELBO is derived, and a Bayesian approach to obtain the optimal q is developed (R1, R2). The mathematical details appear well executed, though not exhaustively verified (R2, R3).
- **Flexibility and generalization**: The generalization of GP-LVM offers more flexibility with a tunable complexity parameter q>0, leading to improved performance on small-scale toy problems (R3).
- **Clarity (majority view)**: The paper is well-organized, clearly written, and provides a thorough review of related work (R1, R2, R4). One reviewer explicitly finds it “easy to read” (R4).
- **Experimental demonstration**: Experiments on Swiss roll, oil flow, and MNIST show that QEP-LVM outperforms GP-LVM in terms of visualization and quantitative metrics (R1, R3, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of quantitative metrics**: The evaluation relies heavily on visual inspection. For example, Figure 5 does not show significant differences between QEP-LVM and GP-LVM; clustering metrics like Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) or Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) are needed to objectively compare the latent representations (R1). Quantitative comparisons with other dimensionality reduction methods (e.g., Isomap for Swiss roll, neural networks for MNIST/oil flow) are missing (R2). Table 2 shows that AUC results for the Gaussian case are within standard errors, which should be highlighted and discussed (R3).
- **Computational overhead and time complexity**: The paper does not report training/testing time, especially for the additional ELBO computation during inference (R1, R4). A complexity analysis and running time comparison between GP-LVM and QEP-LVM on a dataset as large as MNIST is needed (R4). The computational complexity and scaling behavior (e.g., how training scales with sample size, effect of inducing points M) are not discussed (R3).
- **Limited experimental scope**: The experiments use small-scale, relatively artificial datasets (Swiss roll, oil flow, MNIST) and do not convincingly demonstrate substantial improvement over GP-LVM (R2). Testing on larger datasets or more practical tasks (e.g., robotics) would strengthen the impact (R2). The parameter space of q is not adequately explored; the paper mainly focuses on 0<q<2 without clear justification for this range or a sensitivity analysis of the model to q (R2, R3, R4). The choice of kernel and its impact on performance is not examined (R4).
- **Claims not fully supported**: Several claims lack explicit derivation or experimental support: (a) “More regularization” from replacing GP with Q-EP – the form and connection to standard regularization (e.g., Lasso) are not clarified (R3). (b) The connection to probabilistic PCA is claimed but not explicitly worked out (R3). (c) “Automatically determine the dimensionality of the nonlinear latent space” is asserted but not demonstrated (R3). (d) Claims that a representation is “superior” or “best” are not defined in terms of specific criteria (R3).
- **Presentation and clarity (minority view)**: One reviewer finds the presentation hard to follow and unclear, in contrast to the positive clarity comments from other reviewers (R3). Specific issues include: (i) The latent variable definitions in Section 2.2 are confusing – f is used for the latent function in eq. (1) while X is called the latent variable later (R3). (ii) Remark 2 mentions regularization of singular values by q>0 without defining c(q) or detailing which singular values (R3). (iii) Remark 4 shows an extra term when q=2, raising a discrepancy with the claim that the Gaussian case is recovered; this needs resolution (R3). (iv) The paper lacks an algorithmic overview of training and inference (R3). (v) Many notations and symbols are insufficiently introduced (e.g., ⊗, ∧ in lines 179/196, q-ED notation could be ambiguous) (R2, R3).
- **Missing ablation and hyperparameter discussion**: The effect and choice of hyperparameters, especially β, are not discussed or experimentally shown (R3). The robustness of training to these hyperparameters is unknown (R3). The model is trained “in two stages” but the stages are not clarified (R3).
- **Minor presentation issues**: Spelling errors (e.g., “Schölkopf”), incorrect citation formatting (e.g., Kleppe & Skaug should be lowercase, citations with brackets inside text), and inconsistent font sizes in Table captions (R3). These should be corrected.

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* While three reviewers rate the paper’s clarity and organization positively (R1, R2, R4), one reviewer (R3) rates Presentation as 1 (poor) and finds the paper hard to follow, which led to an initially lower score (later raised after rebuttal).
