## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces sparse feature circuits—causally implicated subnetworks of human-interpretable features (from sparse autoencoders, SAEs) that explain LM behaviors, in contrast to prior circuits built on polysemantic neurons or attention heads. Three contributions: (1) a circuit discovery method using SAEs with linear approximations of indirect effects, validated on models like Pythia-128M and Gemma-2-2B; (2) SHIFT, which ablates task-irrelevant features (e.g., gender) to improve classifier generalization without disambiguating labels; (3) an unsupervised, scalable pipeline that automatically discovers thousands of circuits for model behaviors, with an interactive website.

### Strengths
- **Addresses core interpretability challenge:** Uses SAEs to decompose model behaviors into monosemantic, interpretable features, solving the polysemanticity problem of prior neuron/attention-head circuits. (Reviewers 1–4)
- **Empirical results are strong and well-validated:**
  - Subject-verb agreement circuits reveal clear compositional structure (distinct pathways for relative clauses vs. prepositional phrases). (Reviewer 3)
  - Human evaluation: BiB circuit features score 81.5 interpretability vs. 36.0 for neurons. (Reviewer 3)
  - SHIFT achieves 93.1% profession accuracy while reducing gender bias to near chance (52.0%). (Reviewer 3)
  - Thorough ablation studies on SAE width, layer selection, manipulation coefficients; consistent advantages over neuron-based approaches. (Reviewer 3)
- **Scalable and novel:** The method can be scaled to thousands of automatically discovered behaviors in billion-parameter models, enabling unsupervised interpretability. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **Practical application (SHIFT):** Removes spurious features without disambiguating labels, offering a real-world tool for debiasing. (Reviewer 2)
- **Quantitative evaluation:** Faithfulness and completeness metrics for both feature and neuron circuits provide objective comparison. (Reviewer 2)
- **Well-written and timely:** Provides clear background on SAEs; method components are presented clearly; interactive website improves accessibility. (Reviewers 1, 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Reliance on human judgment for SHIFT:**
  - No inter-annotator agreement or consistency analysis for selecting spurious features; scalability limited as manual inspection becomes harder for larger models. (Reviewers 1, 2)
  - No clear protocol for how number of inspected features scales with model size/task complexity, nor whether inspection can be parallelized or automated. (Reviewer 2)
- **Dependency on SAE quality:**
  - SAEs require significant compute (2B tokens) and can suffer from instability (dead features); limited to decoder-only models (Pythia-70M, Gemma-2-2B). (Reviewer 3)
  - No analysis of sensitivity to different SAE training parameters or architectures; poor SAEs could harm interpretability and circuit discovery. (Reviewer 2)
  - SAE errors are not fully interpretable yet—need discussion on how to perform surgical edits despite these errors. (Reviewer 1)
- **Evaluation scope is limited:**
  - Primarily tested on simple grammatical tasks (e.g., subject-verb agreement); not evaluated on semantically complex tasks (multi-turn dialogue, reasoning, question answering, summarization). (Reviewers 2, 3)
  - Most unsupervised pipeline results are qualitative; lacking a quantitative benchmark with metrics like sparsity, causal influence, or robustness to adversarial perturbations. (Reviewer 4)
- **Theoretical gaps:**
  - No formal characterization of when SAE features capture meaningful concepts vs. memorized patterns. (Reviewer 3)
  - No analysis of when the linear approximation of indirect effects breaks down (especially relevant for early layers where integrated gradients improve accuracy). (Reviewer 3)
- **Dataset limitations:**
  - Method requires contrastive sample pairs, which are hard to define for long-form generation, algorithmic reasoning, or tasks like “generating creative metaphors.” (Reviewer 4)
- **Correlation vs. causation concern:** Ablating gender features and observing performance drop is not universally convincing as causal intervention; additional feature ablation studies needed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Code and data not released.** (Reviewer 3)
- **Minor issues:** typos (line 283, 201, 492), citation formatting errors (line 95–96), unclear passage (line 454–457). (Reviewer 1)
