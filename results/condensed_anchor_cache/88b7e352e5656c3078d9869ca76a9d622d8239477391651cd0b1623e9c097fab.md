## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers generally find the paper a valuable contribution to understanding feature learning, introducing the interaction tensor and a toy combinatorial model that can theoretically explain phenomena like Generalization Disagreement Equality (GDE). The paper is well-written, provides empirical validation, and offers a novel perspective. However, there is disagreement about the model’s level of abstraction: three reviewers consider it a useful, analyzable framework (scores 8,8,8), while one reviewer (score 6) views it as overly simplistic, insufficiently motivated, and lacking connections to real training dynamics. All reviewers note issues with hyperparameter sensitivity (thresholds), and several point to missing analyses (multi-class, distribution shift, binary simplification, post-training focus). Concrete concerns about specific figures, notations, and minor errors are also raised.

### Strengths
- The problem of feature learning is important, and the paper offers a new, creative perspective via the interaction tensor and combinatorial analysis.
- The framework is practical and supported by solid theoretical analysis; it can be fully analyzed and is able to reproduce GDE without explicitly assuming calibration.
- Empirical results on multiple datasets are provided, with clear experimental details.
- The paper constructs a natural dataset where deep ensembles are not well-calibrated in-distribution, breaking GDE – this is valuable.
- The simple toy model, while abstract, allows closed-form derivations of accuracy and agreement, and the findings add nuance to the understanding of GDE.
- The paper is very well-written and clearly presented (most reviewers rate presentation 3–4).

### Weaknesses
- **Model oversimplification and limited realism** (noted particularly by one reviewer, score 6, while others find it a useful abstraction):  
  The toy model reduces features and data points to binary (dominant/rare) categories and assumes fixed numbers of features per type. This may not capture nuanced interactions in real-world datasets. The model is also highly abstract, and it is unclear what simplifications from real deep learning lead to it; the connection to training dynamics (e.g., gradient descent biases) is missing. The assumptions may be as strong as or stronger than the calibration assumption that GDE typically requires.
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and lack of guidance**:  
  The construction of the interaction tensor depends on thresholds (γ_corr, γ_data), and the model also uses thresholds for dominant/rare classification. All reviewers raise concerns about how these are set and how sensitive results are to their choice. For example, in Figure 2b, the line separating rare from dominant features is ambiguous, and the choice may affect the observations in Figures 3a and 3c.
- **Questions about empirical significance** (noted by the minority reviewer):  
  In Figures 3a and 3c, the reported differences appear small, and it is not clear that the observations are statistically significant given threshold choices.
- **Limited scope of analysis**:  
  The theoretical framework currently only covers binary classification; multi-class extension is not discussed. The framework does not consider distribution shifts.  
  The analysis focuses on the post-training state, ignoring the dynamic evolution of features during training; thus it cannot explain phenomena like mode collapse or feature hierarchy emergence.
- **Lack of modeling continuum**:  
  The binary distinction between dominant and rare features may be too coarse. One reviewer suggests modeling the “rare-dominant” variation as a spectrum rather than a forced split.
- **Notation and presentation issues**:  
  Notation uses generic letters (i, j, k, etc.) that are confusing; a figure summarizing all notation (p_d, p_r, etc.) would be helpful.  
  Figure 1 is vague and should be replaced with a clearer version.
- **Minor errors**:  
  In Section 5, data generating process paragraph, second-to-last sentence: samples \(n_r\) rare (not dominant) features.  
  Appendix C, equation line 34: the two \(\neq \emptyset\) might instead be \(= \emptyset\).
- **Claim about rudimentary understanding of feature learning** (minority reviewer):  
  The paper states that theoretical understanding of feature learning is rudimentary, but recent works (e.g., Damian et al. 2022, Wang et al. 2022, Nichani et al. 2023, Dandi et al. 2023, Moniri et al. 2023) already provide more detailed analyses; the claim may be overstated.
