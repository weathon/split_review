## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper extends Whittle index to a multi‑agent RL setting with multiple discrete actions and a global state constraint (total inventory), proposing WIMS and a deep MARL algorithm WIMSN. The method is evaluated on real inventory problems with up to 2307 SKUs, outperforming OR‑based and MARL baselines. Reviewers diverged strongly: one was very positive (score 8, confidence 3), another very negative (score 3, confidence 4), and one moderate (score 5, confidence 3). This merged review preserves that contrast.

### Strengths
- **Novel adaptation of Whittle index to a MARL setting with multiple actions and a state constraint.** The paper bridges two gaps: constraint on state (not actions) and multiple actions per agent (reviewer 1). Indexability is defined, and a sufficient condition is provided (reviewer 1 notes the condition that the optimal policy should not reduce replenishment quantity when inventory cost decreases; reviewer 3 confirms indexability is defined and sufficient conditions are given). Reviewer 2 acknowledges the broad applicability across fields including inventory management.
- **Efficient implementation and scalability.** The Whittle‑index‑based policy can be computed efficiently (reviewer 3), and the algorithm scales to thousands of SKUs (reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 notes that the joint inventory constraint can be satisfied computationally.
- **Comprehensive empirical evaluation.** The algorithm is compared with both OR methods (e.g., static policies from 2017/2018) and MARL baselines (e.g., IPPO) (reviewer 1). The adaptive nature of WIMSN – no retraining required when constraints change or SKUs are recombined – is emphasized (reviewer 1).
- **Well‑structured and clear presentation.** Technical concepts (e.g., WIMS) are explained with simple examples (reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 finds the framework novel.
- **Originality of the vector Whittle index.** The paper measures the cost of unit budget consumption and generates the Whittle index as a vector; the sufficient condition for indexability is reasonable (reviewer 1). The contributions are noted to be applicable to other problems with global state constraints (reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical guarantees.** The proposed WIMS is largely heuristic (reviewer 2). No proof of asymptotic optimality is given, which is a key property of Whittle index policies in the restless bandits literature (reviewers 2 and 3). The solution does not guarantee satisfaction of the state constraint, creating a gap between the problem formulation and the algorithm (reviewers 2 and 3). The tuning of the dual variable λ<sub>g</sub> is not theoretically justified (reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 also notes that assumptions and inherent challenges of the Whittle index in a MARL context are not explored, which could lead to practical problems. Reviewer 3 specifically asks whether asymptotic optimality can be proven for the unconstrained version, and whether any theoretical results from restless bandits or MARL extend to this constrained setting.
- **Satisfaction of the joint inventory constraint is unclear.** The WIMS policy controls each SKU separately; only the dual variable couples them. Reviewers 2 and 3 question how the constraint is actually enforced and why the Whittle index structure is crucial rather than a simpler inventory policy with dual variable updates. Reviewer 3 asks for a comparison with existing single‑agent inventory control methods combined with the same dual penalization.
- **Problematic independence assumption.** Reviewers 2 argues that SKUs are not independent in large‑scale systems (e.g., substitution effects) and that the paper lacks justification for this assumption. Reviewer 1 hints at a related concern: interplay of local constraints (e.g., unique storage per SKU) with the global constraint is not discussed.
- **Missing details on dataset and evaluation metrics.** The paper does not specify how the data is partitioned into training, validation, and test subsets – essential for replicability, especially with time‑series data (reviewer 1). The dataset source and its representativeness are unclear (reviewer 1). Reviewer 1 also notes that other inventory metrics (e.g., fill rate, stockout rate) are not reported. Reviewer 1 questions whether Figure 2’s comparison is fair because IPPO was not retrained when the capacity changed (while WIMSN adapts without retraining).
- **Computational complexity and training cost.** Reviewer 2 points out that Table 4 shows WIMS takes significant computational costs (in minutes), raising concerns about adaptability to dynamic settings. Reviewer 1 asks about training time comparisons and the computational importance of the replay buffer; also asks for clarification of “batch” in the training updates.
- **Literature review is not up‑to‑date.** Reviewer 1 observes that the main comparisons are with works from 2017/2018, and more recent works on the same topic are not considered.
- **Limited motivation for dynamic constraints.** Reviewer 1 finds the assumption that the total inventory capacity changes across seasons not very reasonable: if the constraint changes only infrequently (e.g., once a season), retraining existing algorithms may not be burdensome.
- **Minor concerns.** A typo in Proposition 3.3: the third case should read Q(s,1)=Q(s,0) (reviewer 1). The abbreviation “OR” is introduced without explanation (reviewer 1). No discussion of possible improvements or future work (reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 asks about connections to fully competitive/cooperative MARL settings.
