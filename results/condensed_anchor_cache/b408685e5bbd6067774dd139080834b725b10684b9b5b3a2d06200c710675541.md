## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a diffusion-based method to generate counterfactual explanations (CEs) for 3D point cloud classifiers, using an encoder-decoder pipeline with novel loss functions (counterfactual, proximity, distribution, diversity) and an SDS loss. The authors also propose evaluation metrics and a benchmark for 3D CEs. The work is motivated as the first CE method tailored to 3D point clouds, aiming to bridge a gap in explainable AI for 3D data.

### Strengths
- The topic is meaningful and has broad potential beyond explainable AI, e.g., studying the domain gap between synthetic and real data in the diffusion era. (R3)
- The paper is well-written, self-contained, and easy to follow, making it accessible even to readers not familiar with counterfactual explanations. (R1, R3, R4)
- The method of learning a residual in an encoder-decoder pipeline to generate counterfactual samples is straightforward yet powerful. (R1)
- The implementation is described in great detail, making the work likely reproducible. (R1)
- The used evaluation metrics are suitable and well motivated. (R1)
- A lot of qualitative examples are provided, and the visualizations are aesthetically pleasing, which is important for an explainability method. (R1, R4)
- By utilizing a diffusion model for CE generation, the method combines novel loss functions and constraints that enhance data fidelity and semantic consistency in 3D counterfactual examples. (R2)
- The paper introduces a unique application of counterfactual explanations within 3D point cloud classification, offering a new approach to explainable AI in this area. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited to one classifier and dataset:** Experimental validation is restricted to a single classifier (DGCNN) and a single dataset (ShapeNet) with a binary classification task. This narrow focus limits generalizability and does not demonstrate robustness across diverse architectures (e.g., PCT, PointNet, Recon) or more complex real-world data (e.g., autonomous driving scenes). (R2, R3, R4)
- **Insufficient ablation study:** The final loss is a complex combination of four losses (L_cf, L_prox, L_div, L_st) plus SDS loss. The ablation study does not clearly show the individual contribution of each loss component, missing combinations (e.g., L_cf + L_prox + L_div without L_st) and leaving uncertainty about whether all losses are needed and well balanced. Reviewer 3 specifically questions the need for SDS loss, noting it improves generation quality but its direct contribution to counterfactual sample generation is unclear and requires justification and ablation. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Lack of comparison to other XAI methods:** No comparison is made with existing explainable AI methods for 3D data, even those adapted from 2D. A common strategy like the perturbation test (used in 3D contexts, e.g., arXiv:2403.07706) could provide relative performance insights. (R3, R4)
- **Limited technique innovation:** The method largely reuses diffusion-based CE techniques from the 2D field without specific design tailored to 3D point clouds. The difference between 2D image and 3D point cloud CE generation is not clarified. (R3)
- **Lack of quantification of semantic relevance:** The paper claims human-interpretable counterfactuals but does not thoroughly quantify the semantic relevance of the generated explanations, limiting the interpretability and effectiveness of the approach. (R2, R4)
- **Qualitative examples are debatable:** In the airplane category, concrete semantic changes could also stem from noise, and changes in chairs seem minor. For Figure 3b, the semantic change (bent body) is not clearly indicated; it would be helpful to have the idea in the caption rather than only in Section 4.2.2. (R1, R4)
- **Missing details on diffusion model and training setup:** Specific architecture, data size, optimizer settings, and other implementation details are lacking, which would improve reproducibility. (R3)
- **Computational cost not discussed:** Diffusion models are computationally expensive, especially for high-dimensional 3D point clouds. The paper does not address strategies for managing computational load, which limits practical applicability. (R3)
- **Not applicable to autonomous driving:** For point cloud tasks in autonomous driving (safety-aware), the technique does not seem usable, yet this is a key domain for explanation. (R3)
- **No discussion of limitations and societal impact:** The paper omits a discussion of practical constraints, broader implications, and ethical challenges, which is important for XAI development. (R3)
- **Inconsistent reference formatting and typos:** E.g., L572/574 missing “NeurIPS” and “ICML” in conference names; L265 missing closing parenthesis *f(x) ≠ f(˜x)*; L523 "has been evaluated on a for a limited amount" needs correction; subsection header 3.1 should be "ENCODER-DECODER" not "ENCODE-DECODER". (R3, R4)
- **Missing relevant literature:** Some prior 3D CE works (e.g., Rasal et al. 2022, Maser et al. 2023, Peng et al. 2024, Ohara et al. 2024 in the medical domain) and Interpretable3D: An Ad-Hoc Interpretable Classifier for 3D Point Clouds (AAAI) are not cited. (R1, R3)
- **Limited analysis of counterfactual insights:** The analysis focuses on AE vs. diffusion comparison rather than what can be learned from the CEs (e.g., which features are crucial for detecting a chair). The binary division (chair vs. not chair) is vague; providing the misclassified class would improve understanding. The reviewer asks: for a new unseen airplane, with a limited budget of points to remove/add, where should resources be focused according to the method? (R4)
- **Reviewer 2 is notably more negative:** While other reviewers give positive ratings (6,5,6), Reviewer 2 (score 3, confidence 2) finds experimental results unconvincing due to limited and narrowly focused evaluations, and questions whether the method's claims are validated. (R2)
