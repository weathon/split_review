## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CMO, a data-driven circuit symbolic learning framework for discovering lightweight, interpretable, and generalizable scoring functions for node selection in circuit synthesis (specifically logic optimization). CMO’s main technical contribution is a Graph Enhanced Symbolic Discovery (GESD) framework that distills knowledge from a GNN via MCTS-based symbolic regression to produce symbolic functions. It is claimed as the first graph-enhanced approach for this task. Experiments on EPFL, IWLS, and an industrial Huawei HiSilicon dataset show that CMO outperforms prior GPU-based and human-designed heuristics in inference efficiency and generalization, and integration with the Mfs2 heuristic achieves up to 2.5× speedup on CPU while maintaining optimization quality. Reviewers generally agree the work is novel and well-evaluated, but raise concerns about theoretical justification, experimental clarity, dataset descriptions, and definition of scope. Reviewer 3 (score 8) is more positive about the paper’s clarity and experimental coverage, while Reviewers 1 and 2 (scores 6) note missing analysis and presentation issues.

### Strengths
1. **Novel framework**: CMO is the first graph-enhanced approach for learning lightweight, interpretable symbolic scoring functions that generalize to unseen circuits in circuit synthesis / logic optimization (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
2. **Well-written for an ML audience**: The paper provides a good introduction to the domain, making it accessible to readers less familiar with hardware design (Reviewer 2).
3. **Solid benchmarks and comprehensive evaluation**: The choice of benchmarks (EPFL, IWLS, industrial Huawei dataset) and state-of-the-art heuristics (e.g., Mfs2) is appropriate, with extensive experiments demonstrating effectiveness (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).
4. **Significant speedup with maintained optimization**: CMO achieves up to 2.5× faster runtime on CPU while keeping comparable optimization performance (Reviewer 2).
5. **Method clearly introduced**: The technical description of CMO, including the GNN-guided MCTS symbolic regression, is clearly presented (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of theoretical justification**: The paper does not sufficiently explain why the combination of GNN, MCTS, and symbolic learning is effective. Specifically, how does the inductive bias of the GNN interact with the MCTS search and the generalization of symbolic learning? A deeper analysis or theoretical framework is needed (Reviewer 2).
2. **Unclear link between methods in Sections 4.1 and 4.2**: The relationship between the two components is not visualized or elaborated. For example, the role of \( s_i \) in Section 4.2 and the computational flow involving \( s_i \) are unclear (Reviewer 1).
3. **Terminology mismatch**: The paper uses "circuit synthesis" broadly, but the actual focus is logic optimization (node-level transformations on AIGs). This may mislead readers about the scope; the paper should explicitly clarify that it addresses logic optimization, not full synthesis (Reviewer 3).
4. **Ambiguity in dataset labels**: It is unclear whether node-level transformation labels indicate immediate local improvement (e.g., node reduction in a subgraph) or correlate with final optimized circuit quality. The method used to determine effectiveness (heuristic vs. oracle) also needs explicit definition (Reviewer 3).
5. **Insufficient dataset description for ML audience**: Basic statistics (graph sizes, number of nodes/edges, depth) and visualizations of circuit graphs are missing. Such details would help the ML community understand the data complexity (Reviewer 2).
6. **Technical inconsistency in loss function**: The text states that mean absolute error and focal loss are used, but Equation (4) is an MSE loss. The paper must correct this and justify the chosen loss (Reviewer 2).
7. **Confusing experiment presentation**: The distinction between offline and online phases is unclear; the offline phase reads like an ablation study. The main experiments (e.g., generalization) should be highlighted, and it should be clarified which experiments are central versus ablation (Reviewer 3).
8. **Unclear baseline choice in Experiment 4**: The paper compares CMO with other symbolic regression methods, but does not justify why these are relevant. If this is an ablation of the search method, it should be labeled as such (Reviewer 3).
9. **Ambiguous training setup**: It is unclear whether the GNN is trained on mixed datasets from EPFL, IWLS, and Huawei, or whether separate GNNs are trained per dataset. The rationale for either approach affects generalization claims (Reviewer 3).
10. **Unaddressed generalization questions**: Can CMO generalize to other logic optimization methods (e.g., Rewrite) beyond Mfs2? Can a GNN trained on one dataset generalize to another? These open questions are not explored (Reviewer 3).
11. **Vague phrasing on limitations**: Statements like "cannot capture effective information from specific circuit distribution" are vague and should be replaced with explicit limitations of human-designed heuristics and how machine learning overcomes them (Reviewer 2).
