## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces FoMo-0D, a zero-shot foundation model for unsupervised outlier detection (OD) on tabular data. It leverages Prior-data Fitted Networks (PFNs) – a Transformer trained on synthetically generated datasets drawn from a novel data prior (Gaussian Mixture Models for inliers, with methods from ADBench for outlier synthesis). At inference time, FoMo-0D directly predicts outlier/inlier labels via a single forward pass given a training set and a test point, bypassing model selection (algorithm choice and hyperparameter tuning) entirely. Extensive experiments on 57 benchmark datasets against 26 baseline methods are conducted.

### Strengths
- The paper tackles an important and challenging problem: unsupervised model selection for outlier detection.
- The proposed method is technically sound and well-motivated.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.
- Ample experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of FoMo-0D, with results outperforming the majority of baselines.
- The approach represents a paradigm shift for OD, training a single foundation model for many downstream tasks without selection or tuning.

### Weaknesses
- **Scalability and inference efficiency:** FoMo-0D shows inferior inference time compared to several baselines (Table 3) because it must forward-pass the entire training set during inference. This raises concerns about latency and memory overhead for large training sets (millions of samples) and for time-critical real-world applications. (Reviewers 1, 4; also echoed in questions about managing large numbers of samples.)
- **Limited prior complexity:** The use of GMM for synthetic data generation may not capture the complex, non-Gaussian, or highly multimodal distributions common in real-world tabular data. The paper provides no in-depth insight into why this simple prior works well, nor an analysis of its limitations and failure cases. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Baseline comparisons are not competitive:** The evaluation lacks comparison with recent advanced unsupervised/semi-supervised OD methods, particularly contrastive learning approaches (e.g., based on SimCLR) that do not require retraining and run efficiently. The reported baseline set may not represent the current state-of-the-art. (Reviewer 3 – more negative on this point)
- **Novelty concerns:** The combination of inlier prior and outlier synthesis has been explored in prior work [2]. The use of GMM (from ADBench) for inlier generation and existing outlier synthesis methods appears incremental. The paper does not clearly differentiate its technical contributions from that prior work. (Reviewer 3 – more negative on this point)
- **Missing actual detection metrics:** The paper reports only rank-based metrics rather than actual detection metrics (e.g., AUC). Showing absolute performance values would make it easier to assess the magnitude of improvements and compare directly with future work. (Reviewer 3)
- **Limited evaluation on high-dimensional data:** The OOD detection experiments cover CIFAR-10, Fashion-MNIST, MNIST-C, MVTec-AD, and SVHN, but do not include more challenging, high-dimensional benchmarks such as ImageNet-level datasets from OpenOOD v1.5. It is unclear how FoMo-0D scales to higher-dimensional data and more intricate outlier patterns. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of adaptability:** The paper does not explore whether performance could be improved by post-training or fine-tuning on real data, a common practice in NLP and CV. It also remains unclear whether the model can be adapted when labeled data are available. (Questions from Reviewers 1, 4)
