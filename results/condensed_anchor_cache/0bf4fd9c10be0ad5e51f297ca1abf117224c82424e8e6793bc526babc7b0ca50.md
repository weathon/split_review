## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Personalized Large Vision-Language Models (PLVM), which enable interactive dialogues using referential concepts (e.g., “Mike and Susan”) instead of generic descriptions. PLVM uses an “Aligner,” a pre-trained visual encoder that extracts features from reference images during inference, allowing new concepts to be added without test-time fine-tuning. A synthetic dataset of paired images is generated using IP-Adapter to train the model. The authors claim PLVM outperforms existing methods (e.g., YoLLaVA, LLaVA) in recognition and QA tasks. The reviewers note disagreement: two gave score 3 (major weaknesses in evaluation and novelty), two gave score 5 (valuable contribution despite limitations).

### Strengths

*   **Aligner enables real-time personalization without test-time fine-tuning.** This is consistently highlighted as a practical advantage over models like YoLLaVA (R1, R2, R3).
*   **Synthetic dataset creation.** A comprehensive dataset is constructed to train the model on diverse personalized concepts (R1).
*   **Superior performance in recognition and question-answering.** PLVM shows higher mean accuracy compared to prior models (R2, R4).
*   **Ablation studies in Tables 2 and 3** demonstrate robustness across varying parameters (e.g., context embedding tokens, weights for Yes/No answers) (R2).
*   **Practical seamless integration of new concepts.** No additional cost or retraining is required during inference (R2, R4).
*   **Original combination of existing ideas** (visual encoding and personalization) applied to dialogue systems (R3).

### Weaknesses

*   **Figure 2 needs to be redrawn** – it contains little content but occupies large space; overall motivation and figures are disorganized (R1).
*   **Figure 4 shows little visible difference** between methods; the intended message is unclear (R1).
*   **No comparison with RAG methods.** Given that training-free RAG is a popular baseline, the paper must explain why a trained model is needed and provide a direct comparison (R1; echoed in R3’s demand for fair comparisons).
*   **Lack of detailed analysis on visual encoders (CLIP, ViT).** The impact of different encoders on accuracy, computational cost, and adaptability is not explored (R2).
*   **Experiments limited to simple visual/recognition tasks.** Complex object relationships, actions, or multi-step reasoning are not evaluated (R2, R4).
*   **Evaluation on only 246 images with 34 identities** raises scalability concerns; performance on larger, more diverse datasets is needed (R2).
*   **Training on a tailored dataset gives an unfair advantage.** Competitors (e.g., LLaVA) should be fine-tuned on similar data or their expected performance under the same conditions should be reported (R2, R3).
*   **Generalization to real-world, unstructured data is unvalidated.** Additional tests under different lighting, occlusions, and resolutions are needed (R2, R3).
*   **Potential bias from the synthetic dataset** is not addressed; the dataset’s diversity and representativeness should be explained (R2).
*   **Topic is not novel – just constructing another dataset.** The paper should stress new insights and challenges for this task (R3).
*   **Clarification needed on <sks> token** – why is it chosen to denote the personalized concept? (R3).
*   **Unclear difference between “LLaVA” and “LLaVA (with prompt)”** – how each works must be stated (R3).
*   **Comparisons in Fig. 4 and Table 1 are unfair.** PLVM receives a reference image, while LLaVA and GPT-4V only receive textual prompts – this makes input information unequal and the comparison meaningless (R4). A proper baseline using multi-image LVLMs (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2-VL) that can process reference images directly should be included to test whether the built-in encoders suffice (R4).
*   **Impact of MLPs and cross-attention modules is not validated.** Ablations removing these components are required to show their necessity (R3).
*   **Scope limited to humans.** The method seems trained for facial recognition only; it cannot handle other objects (e.g., cats, toys, houses) unlike Yo’LLaVA, restricting generalization (R4).
*   **Questions about handling multiple concepts in one prompt** (e.g., “Is A on the left or right of B?”) remain unanswered (R4).
