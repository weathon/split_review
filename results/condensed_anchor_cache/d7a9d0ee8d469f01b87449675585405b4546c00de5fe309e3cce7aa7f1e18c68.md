## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviewers agree the paper addresses an important and under-explored problem: structure-preserving text-based editing for few-step diffusion models. The proposed feature injection method (SPEdit) is simple and efficient, and generally outperforms the older baselines P2P and PnP on two benchmark datasets. The paper is clearly written and provides useful analysis of the differences between few-step and multi-step diffusion models. However, reviewers raise significant concerns about the limited comparison with recent methods, insufficient evaluation metrics (lack of user studies), questionable generalizability to non-rigid edits and other model architectures, the reliability of the SVD-based decomposition, visual inconsistencies, and the overall technical novelty.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written, well-organized, and provides necessary analysis to support its claims (R2, R3, R4).
- The problem is highly relevant and under-explored: structure-preserving editing for few-step diffusion models (R4).
- The proposed feature injection method is simple, efficient, and often effective in preserving spatial layout; it outperforms the compared baselines (P2P, PnP) on two benchmark datasets (R1, R2, R3).
- Injecting non-residual features from the source to the target generation process is novel and shows effectiveness, according to R2. R4 acknowledges that the use of SVD for structure-appearance decomposition is a contribution, though other parts draw from prior works.
- The analysis of why attention maps are less effective in few-step DMs is valuable (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient comparisons with recent methods**: Only compared with PnP (CVPR 2023) and P2P (ICLR 2023), both nearly two years old. Missing comparisons with recent methods such as FreePromptEditing (CVPR 2024), InfEdit (CVPR 2024), MasaCtrl, FPE, and others that also explore fast/few-step models (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Evaluation metrics are insufficient**: Only automatic metrics (CLIP score, structure distance) are used; user studies are recommended by both R1 and R3 to assess subjective image quality and fidelity.
- **Limited applicability for non-rigid editing**: The method is best suited for rigid edits (e.g., style changes), but its effectiveness for non-rigid edits (e.g., making a dog “jumping”, raising a person’s arm, object removal, pose alteration) is unclear. R1, R3, and R4 note that many alternative methods exist for structure-preserved translation, and the method may struggle with more extensive transformations. Performance on PIE-Bench for broader tasks is not reported (R3).
- **Generalization to other models**: Only tested on SDXL-based few-step models (SDXL-Turbo, LCM). Generalization to other few-step models like Diffusion Transformers (e.g., Flux, SD3) is not shown (R1, R4).
- **Instability due to layer selection**: The method requires finding an appropriate “sweetspot” layer for different models, which is a practical limitation (R1). It is unclear whether it adapts to other generative architectures.
- **Reliability of SVD-based decomposition**: Using top‑k significant singular vectors (Sec. 3.2) to extract structure-related features is questionable. For images where the foreground occupies nearly the entire area, the significant singular vectors may capture appearance rather than structure (R2). There are also no quantitative experiments assessing the effectiveness of filtering out appearance factors (R2).
- **Visual inconsistencies**: In Figure 1, the edited result introduces a lower right leg that is not present in the source image, while PnP does not have this issue (R3). Noticeable differences in details (e.g., duck’s tail in Fig. 7) may be due to the inversion process (R4).
- **Limited technical novelty** (reviewer disagreement): R2 considers injecting non-residual features novel and effective. However, R4 argues the primary contribution is the SVD decomposition, while other components (e.g., spatial matching with correspondence maps) closely resemble prior works like DreamMatcher and Consistory. The analysis of why few-step models differ is presented but lacks a detailed mechanistic explanation (e.g., why adding LCM-LoRA to SDXL makes attention layers less influential) (R4, R2 question 1). R3 notes that the discussion of limitations is absent.
- **Missing ablations and clarifications**:
  - No comparison with varying attention injection timesteps in PnP/P2P to adjust structure preservation (R1).
  - No experiment injecting appearance features from target into source (as in P2P) to justify the chosen injection direction (R2).
  - Not explored whether using multiple blocks for feature injection improves results (R3).
  - The caption for Figure 3 is ambiguous; it should clarify whether structure distances are shown for attention‑ or feature‑injected images and specify the parameters used for attention injection (R3).
  - Line 55 states that unspecified attributes should be preserved, but the method does not clarify how users explicitly select which attributes to preserve or edit (R3).
- **Additional missing experiments**:
  - Performance on low‑level editing tasks (lighting, haze removal, text erasing) is not explored (R4).
  - Need more results on editing only part of an image while leaving other regions unchanged (R2).
  - The dataset used for the experiments in Fig. 4(a) is not specified (R2).
