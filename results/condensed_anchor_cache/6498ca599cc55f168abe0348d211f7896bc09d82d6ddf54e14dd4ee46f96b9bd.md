## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper provides an alternative derivation of the implicit regularization result from Li et al. (2022) using multiscale methods for elliptic PDEs (Pavliotis and Stuart, 2008). The authors analyze the Fokker–Planck equation associated with the SDE approximation of SGD in the small-learning-rate limit, obtaining the same two-phase dynamics: a first deterministic phase that drives parameters to the manifold of minimizers, and a second phase where noise induces a drift along the manifold, acting as a regularizer. The derivation follows the intuition of Blanc et al. (2020) but is more general in handling arbitrary noise covariance, and it studies the density evolution rather than the stochastic process directly.

### Strengths
- The implicit regularization of optimization algorithms is an important and highly relevant topic.
- The paper introduces a new technical tool (multiscale analysis of elliptic PDEs) to a machine learning audience, offering a different perspective from the original proof in Li et al. (2022) (which analyzed the distribution of the SGD trajectory).
- The heuristics behind the argument are clear, and the derivation makes sense to readers with some background in multiscale methods.
- The method is more general than Blanc et al. (2020) because it can handle general noise covariance.
- The paper is crisp and gets to the point quickly; the main part is just four pages.
- The idea of analyzing the density and associated PDEs via perturbation theory (rather than working directly with the SDE) is a potentially valuable simplification.
- One reviewer (score 5) was significantly more positive, emphasizing the novelty of the technical tool and the clarity of the heuristics.
- The approach could be applicable to other multiscale problems in deep learning (e.g., grokking, Power et al., 2022).

### Weaknesses
- **No new results**: The paper derives a result already established by Li et al. (2022); the authors themselves state no novel findings. Because the contribution is primarily expositional, it must meet a higher standard of clarity, which it does not.
- **Not rigorous**: The derivation contains a sequence of unjustified approximations. Assumptions on the loss function and gradient noise covariance are never stated, and the singular perturbation expansion is not well-defined.
- **Local analysis only**: The argument in equations (5)–(10) appears to be only a local analysis; it is not clear how it yields global conclusions.
- **Unclear advantage over prior work**: The motivation for introducing a new technique in light of Li et al. (2022) is not explained. What does the multiscale approach add? The authors do not comment on any new implications or broader insights.
- **Poor mathematical exposition**:  
  - Non‑standard notation is used without definition (e.g., $P\cdot \nabla P$ in Eq. (13); the trace inner product; isomorphism symbols; $\perp$ and $\parallel$).  
  - The derivation lacks logical flow—claims are stated without justification, and the appendix presents various facts without making clear which are essential or how they connect to the main text.  
  - The reference to Pavliotis and Stuart (2008) points to the entire 350‑page book rather than a specific theorem or statement.  
  - In Appendix E, a “comment” in Appendix B is invoked to justify a local orthogonality relation, without a precise equation number.  
  - Assumptions (e.g., non‑degeneracy of $H$ on $\Gamma$) are stated only at the very end of the derivation, hindering logical flow.  
  - The auxiliary variables introduced (paragraph after Eq. (4)) are mentioned but their dynamics are not shown; it is not demonstrated how they relate to the original process.
- **Limited applicability to discrete SGD**: The argument applies only to the SDE approximation (Eq. (2)), not to the original discrete SGD steps. It is unclear whether the same homogenization technique can be applied directly to the discrete updates.
- **Missing end‑to‑end implications**: No concrete conclusions are drawn about SGD behavior from the derived average generator. The paper would benefit from elaborating on the broad implications and potential applications of the technique.
- **Technical questions that overlap with criticism**:  
  - Is $\Phi(\theta_0)$ the same map induced by gradient flow as in Li et al. (2022)? (R1)  
  - How is Eq. (16) recovered from Eqs. (14a,14b)? (R2)  
  - What is the justification for passing from a possibly highly dependent object to orthogonal Gaussians in the approximation after Eq. (A8)? (R4)  
  - What does the notation in Eqs. (E14‑E15) mean? Is $\rightarrow$ implication? (R4)  
  - Is it required that the manifold $\Gamma$ have constant rank? Are there interesting cases where it does not? (R4)  
  - Can this technique yield any new implication beyond Li et al. (2022)? (R3)
- **Typos**:  
  - Eq. (14b): $\overline f^i$ should be $\overline f^k$, and $\partial \theta^*$ should be $\partial \hat \theta$.  
  - Eq. (20), second row: unmatched subscripts/superscripts $l,k$.
