## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes a Multi-Armed Router (MAR) that applies multi-armed bandit theory for per-query LLM selection in an online manner, balancing exploration and exploitation. It also introduces WildArena, a dataset of 4,029 real-world user queries with responses from seven leading LLMs scored via LLM-as-a-Judge. Most reviewers (1–3) rate the paper as 5, praising the novel formulation and dataset; one reviewer (Reviewer 4) rates it 3, expressing stronger concerns about motivation, experimental soundness, and dataset novelty.

### Strengths

- Formulates LLM routing as an **online multi-armed bandit problem**, a novel perspective that dynamically balances exploration and exploitation, overcoming limitations of static regression-based methods.
- Addresses an **important practical challenge**: selecting the most cost-effective LLM per query without needing access to model internals, reducing costs while maintaining response quality.
- **WildArena dataset** (4,029 real-world queries, 7 LLM responses, evaluation scores) fills a gap in real-world open-ended query data and is a valuable community contribution.
- The problem can be viewed through the lens of **recommendation**, opening further research opportunities (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

**Motivation and Problem Framing**  
- The downstream use-case is not clearly stated: is the router designed for daily user queries, instruction-tuning data collection, preference data collection, or something else? (Reviewer 4, W1)  
- The claim that no suitable dataset exists is questionable; Ultrafeedback provides a similar setup with multiple models and aspect-based ratings (Reviewer 4, W1.1).  
- The paper does not compare against existing LLM recommendation approaches such as ZOOTER and RouterBench; such comparisons would clarify where MAR stands (Reviewer 1, Q3).  

**Experimental Evaluation and Soundness**  
- The evaluation is **not comprehensive**: there are no ablation studies, no detailed comparisons with state-of-the-art methods, and no significance measures on cumulative regret plots (Reviewers 1, 4).  
- **Cost-efficiency claims are not convincingly supported**: running 7B/32B models incurs GPU costs, and API-based batch inference (e.g., GPT-4) has significantly reduced costs; actual cost estimates (beyond cumulative regret) are needed (Reviewer 4, W2.1).  
- **Figure 4 shows GPT-3.5 nearly matching MAR** (performance difference ~1%); in practice, using a single model is simpler. The paper should explain when MAR is worth the overhead and when GPT-3.5 fails (Reviewer 4, W2.2).  
- The **validity of LLM-as-a-Judge scores** is not established: how do these correspond to human evaluation? What does it mean to perform well on WildArena? (Reviewer 4, W2.3)  
- The claim that MAR reaches the ideal scenario after 200 steps is not analyzed for sensitivity across different dataset types; the suboptimal regime in the first 200 steps may be problematic (Reviewer 4, W2.4, Q3).  
- The paper does not account for **model version updates** (e.g., “gpt4-turbo-latest” → “gpt4-turbo”) which can shift response behavior and make routing recommendations outdated (Reviewer 3, Weakness 1).  
- **Within-model response variability** due to top-p sampling causes large reward fluctuations, raising concerns about routing reliability based solely on the query (Reviewer 3, Weakness 2).  
- **Figure 3 lacks axis labels** (Reviewer 2).  

**Algorithm and Implementation Details**  
- Insufficient detail on the specific contextual bandit algorithm, network architecture, and hyperparameters, hindering reproducibility (Reviewer 1).  
- The algorithm presentation is poor: in Algorithm 1, Line 10 defines reward r = g(s,c) where s is the embedding of the query, not the query itself – why is the embedding used, and how? (Reviewer 2)  
- The **update rule for Δ** and its relation to exploration versus exploitation is unclear, especially since Algorithm 1 evaluates all K models per query (Reviewer 2).  
- No justification for **post-processing**: GPT-4 scores were manually adjusted by –0.06. What is the rationale? Is this based on a target score distribution? (Reviewers 2, 3)  

**Dataset Construction and Biases**  
- The manual GPT-4 score adjustment (–0.06) is arbitrary and introduces bias, potentially skewing comparisons (Reviewers 2, 3).  
- LLM-as-a-Judge may suffer from **inconsistency, positional bias, and lack of normalization**; the paper does not analyze rating statistics or address these issues (Reviewers 1, 2).  
- Measures to ensure **diversity and representativeness** of WildArena queries are not described; potential dataset biases are not discussed (Reviewer 1).  

**Minor Writing Issues**  
- Several grammatical errors (e.g., missing article in title), improper use of quotation marks, and `\citep` vs `\citet` / `\citealt` breaking reading flow (Reviewer 4, W3).  

**Reviewer Disagreement**  
- One reviewer (Reviewer 4, score 3) is notably more critical: they question the novelty of the dataset, find the experiments unsound (soundness rating 1), and are not convinced of the practical benefits over simpler baselines. The other three reviewers (scores 5) share many of the same weaknesses but are less severe in their assessment.
