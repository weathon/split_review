## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses model extraction attacks (MEAs) on large language models (LLMs) by proposing Locality Reinforced Distillation (LoRD), a method that incorporates reinforcement learning (RL) to mimic the alignment process (e.g., RLHF) of victim models. The authors claim LoRD reduces query complexity and provides watermark resistance. Three reviewers (scores 5, 6, 5) find the problem meaningful, the method novel, and the experiments extensive, with well-written presentation and interesting analysis (e.g., Figure 5). However, the same three reviewers also note limited improvement over the MLE baseline, lack of explanation for design choices, missing ablation studies, and questionable rigor in theoretical analysis. One reviewer (score 3) is strongly critical, arguing that the loss derivations are unprincipled, the theoretical analysis lacks mathematical rigor and relevance, the experiments do not evaluate alignment/safety, and the empirical gains are negligible.

### Strengths
- The paper is the first work to consider the alignment procedure (RLHF) of LLMs in model stealing, addressing a practical and meaningful gap (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- LoRD operates in a fully black-box threat model, making it highly practical for stealing commercial LLMs (Reviewer 3).
- The method does not simply imitate victim generation (MLE), so it can avoid replicating some watermarks in the victim model (Reviewers 1, 3).
- LoRD reduces the cost of queries compared to baseline MLE (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Extensive and comprehensive experiments across multiple domains and commercial LLMs (Reviewer 1).
- The paper includes theoretical analysis on convergence and watermark resistance (Reviewer 1).
- The authors responsibly discuss ethical concerns and provide potential defense strategies (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is well-written with clear structure, well-displayed tables and figures; the analysis about Figure 5 offers novel and interesting insights (Reviewers 3, 4).
- For each task where LoRD underperforms, the authors explain possible reasons (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Loss function derivation lacks rigor and justification** (Reviewer 2, supported by Reviewer 3 and 4):
  - The derivation from Eq. (6) to $R_{\theta_\phi}$ (L228) is unclear; the connection between the reward model in RLHF and the proposed log-ratio is not explicitly stated (Reviewer 2).
  - The scaling term in PPO is dropped (L239) without ablation study or theoretical justification (Reviewer 2).
  - The claim that an extra exponential noticeably increases runtime (L248) is weak; computational cost is negligible compared to model passes (Reviewer 2).
  - Using only the logarithmic part of the KL divergence (L250) is unprincipled; effects and implications are unexamined – no ablation study (Reviewer 2).
  - "Selected tokens" (L251) are not clearly defined; how sampling affects the loss needs clarification (Reviewer 2).
  - The KL divergence term in Eq. (9) is applied to logits, not distributions, making it mathematically incorrect to call it KLD (Reviewer 2).
  - Wrapping the loss with a sigmoid (L264) is unexplained; its effect on loss landscape and training dynamics is unclear (Reviewer 2).
  - The removal of a term in Eq. (8) is not explained or ablated (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Theoretical analysis lacks rigor and relevance** (Reviewer 2):
  - Proposition 1 is not a well-defined mathematical statement; proofs in appendix do not directly support it (Reviewer 2).
  - Proposition 2 is too general and does not provide specific insights about LoRD; it merely states MLE can match victim performance (Reviewer 2).
  - Section 4.2 on query efficiency is handwavy and lacks mathematical derivation or source; Reviewers 2 and 4 find the analysis confusing, e.g., the claim that ideal query times reduce to $O(V^{NQ} \times C)$ is unclear (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Experiments show limited improvement over MLE baseline** (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). Reviewer 2 notes almost no improvement; Reviewer 4 says limited improvement. One reviewer (score 3) is more positive but still acknowledges the method is not highly effective on every task.
- **No evaluation of alignment/safety** (Reviewer 2): If the attack aims to mimic RLHF, safety is a primary concern; the current experiments only test downstream tasks, for which MLE already suffices, undermining the need for an alignment-aware attack. This criticism is not rebutted by other reviewers.
- **Watermark resistance evaluation** (Reviewer 4): Choosing the vocabulary-splitting watermarking method is inappropriate because MLE already shows high p-values; the authors should test backdoor-based watermarking methods for more convincing results.
- **Method design issues**:
  - The victim model’s response is not used in deciding chosen/rejected responses or in the objective (except cold start); it is unclear how the victim guides preference alignment (Reviewer 4).
  - The objective function (Eq. 8) closely resembles SimPO; a simpler, more direct approach (sample two responses, use victim to label chosen/rejected) is not tried (Reviewer 4).
  - The regularization term is essentially direct distillation of the victim, so calling it “regularization” is questionable (Reviewer 4).
  - Parameters (thresholds) are provided but their impact and sensitivity across different local/victim models are not analyzed (Reviewer 3).
  - The method is domain-specific, which should be clarified in the introduction (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation issues** (Reviewers 2, 4):
  - Figure 3 adds no significant information beyond text and Figure 2 (Reviewer 2).
  - Section 3.1 dives into technical details without providing intuition first (Reviewer 2).
  - Table 1 is cluttered with too many metrics (e.g., precision, recall, F1); consider reporting only F1 (Reviewer 2).
  - Ethical statement placement should follow Author Guide (after main text, before references) (Reviewer 4).
  - Typos/presentation errors: Eq. (4) should bold $y_{i,<j}$; Eq. (6) inconsistent use of $\hat{y}$ vs $y$; Figure 2 Step 4 should use “or” instead of “and”; Appendix misuses `\citet` (should be `\citep`) (Reviewer 4).
- **Specific questions that highlight missing analyses**:
  - Figure 6 shows inconsistency in watermark resistance across $\lambda$ values despite Eq. 11 suggesting consistency; more analysis needed (Reviewer 1).
  - How does choice of local model (only Llama3-8B tested) impact results? (Reviewer 1) Also, effectiveness on commercial models that do not provide generation probabilities (Reviewer 3).
  - In Table 1, LoRD outperforms the victim for Data-to-Text (E2E NLG with 64 queries); could this be overfitting? (Reviewer 1)
  - In Eq. (9), what differences/impacts would using $y^+$ instead of $y^-$ cause? (Reviewer 3) Similarly, does removing $P_{y_{vic}}$ in Eq. (8) have negative effects? (Reviewer 3)
