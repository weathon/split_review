## Merged Review

### Summary
Five reviewers assessed the paper, which proposes Margin-Based Intra-Class Contrastive Distillation (MICCD), a method that trains a teacher with an intra-class contrastive loss plus margin loss to enrich soft labels for knowledge distillation. Scores range from 3 to 5 (ratings: 5, 3, 5, 3, 3). Two reviewers (R1, R3) gave higher scores (5) and highlighted the interesting observation and theoretical contributions, while three (R2, R4, R5) gave lower scores (3) and pointed out limited novelty, insufficient significant improvements, and missing experiments/theoretical gaps. All agree the paper is well-written and structured, but there is strong disagreement on soundness and contribution. R4 rated soundness and presentation as 1, while others gave 2–3.

### Strengths
- **Theoretical foundation**: The paper provides formal proofs linking intra-class and inter-class distances, and shows that λ trades off between them. R1 and R5 consider this a strong contribution; R4 finds it inspiring for the ICLR community. (R1, R4, R5)
- **Novel perspective on enriching soft labels**: The observation that teachers trained only with ground truth may lack intra-class diversity is interesting and meaningful. (R1, R3)
- **Margin loss for stability**: The addition of margin loss to counteract training instability is a practical contribution. (R5, R1, R3)  
  *However, R3 notes this claimed advantage is not experimentally demonstrated, and R1 questions its robustness on small/imbalanced data.*
- **Writing and clarity**: The paper is well-written, clearly organized, and easy to follow. (R2, R3, R5)
- **Empirical evaluation breadth**: Experiments span multiple architectures (ResNet, WRN, VGG) and datasets (CIFAR-100, Tiny ImageNet, CINIC-10). (R1)
- **Practical caching mechanism**: Pipeline-based caching is provided to reduce overhead. (R1)

### Weaknesses
1. **Novelty and positioning**:  
   - The core idea combines existing concepts (contrastive learning + margin loss) and is not considered innovative. (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5)  
   - Missing comparisons to closely related works: CKD (Contrastive Knowledge Distillation from a Sample-Wise Perspective), CRD (Contrastive Representation Distillation), PromptKD, and dynamic temperature methods (e.g., Li et al. AAAI 2023) and logit standardization (Sun et al. CVPR 2024). (R3, R5)  
   - The paper does not differentiate its method from CKD or other intra-class contrastive approaches. (R5)

2. **Empirical results – significance and generalizability**:  
   - Improvements on CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet are modest/insignificant. (R1, R2)  
   - Without RKD, the proposed method underperforms existing methods. (R3)  
   - Experiments are limited to image classification; missing validation on NLP, time series, or other modalities. (R1)  
   - No experiments on large-scale datasets like ImageNet-1K or MS COCO (for object detection), which are standard in KD benchmarks. (R2, R3, R5)  
   - Only a few network architectures compared; more should be used. (R5)  
   - Hyperameter sensitivity analysis is lacking for λ, margin threshold δ, batch size, and threshold θ. (R1, R3, R5)  
   - No ablation studies isolating contributions of intra-class contrastive loss and margin loss. (R1, R3, R5)  
   - No analysis of training stability or convergence speed (e.g., test accuracy/loss curves) despite claims of improvement. (R3, R4)  
   - No report of teacher model accuracy trained with the proposed loss; not shown whether the teacher itself improves. (R4)  
   - No direct comparison of training the student directly with the combined losses (CE + margin + KD) instead of only via the teacher’s soft labels. (R4)

3. **Theoretical gaps and assumptions**:  
   - The assumption that cross-entropy loss is equivalent to inter-class contrastive loss is not theoretically or experimentally justified. (R4)  
   - The two defined distance metrics (inter-class and intra-class distance) are not shown to satisfy the four axioms of a metric space. (R4)  
   - The theoretical analysis only addresses the trade-off between intra/inter-class distances but lacks deeper explanation of how intra-class diversity actually benefits student learning. (R1, R5)

4. **Computational and training overhead**:  
   - Retraining the teacher is required; no comparison of training time vs. standard KD or other methods. (R3, R2)  
   - Increased model complexity may hinder deployment; quantitative analysis of FLOPs/memory is missing. (R1)  
   - No discussion of the computational cost of the additional loss terms. (R1)  
   - Performance of student when combined with other KD methods (beyond RKD) is not reported, nor is a comparison of additional parameters vs. gains. (R4)

5. **Robustness and tuning difficulty**:  
   - On small or imbalanced datasets, intra-class contrastive loss may still cause training instability; experiments on such settings are absent. (R1)  
   - Tuning α and λ adds complexity with no sensitivity guidelines. (R1)  
   - Common hyperparameter settings for KD (e.g., 240 epochs on CIFAR-100, batch size 64) are not used; impact unclear. (R3)  
   - The framework uses augmented samples as positives in the intra-class loss, but the specific augmentation techniques are not stated. (R4)  
   - The threshold δ for margin loss is not reported. (R4)

6. **Missing experiments and comparisons requested**:  
   - Compare against teachers trained with inter-class contrastive loss. (R3)  
   - Provide ablation for threshold θ and batch size. (R3)  
   - Report test accuracy and loss curves for at least one teacher model to demonstrate instability and the effect of margin loss. (R4)  
   - List the teacher models used for baseline methods. (R3)  
   - Evaluate on CIFAR-100 with standard 240-epoch schedule. (R3)

7. **Presentation and formatting issues** (R4, R5):  
   - \citep/\citet misuse; scalar/vector notation not differentiated; equation referencing inconsistent; possible typo “Constrastive earning” (should be “Contrastive learning”). (R4)  
   - Notation: \(f\) in Eqs. (4) and (7) could be teacher or student, unclear. (R4)  
   - Clarify that KD loss is KL-divergence, not CE, in Eqs. (2) and (3). (R4)  
   - Some references are incorrectly formatted. (R4, R5)  
   - Missing key references: PromptKD, CKD. (R5)
