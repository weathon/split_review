## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper extends DreamerV3 with an augmented temporal contrast (ATC) loss as an auxiliary objective to encourage the world model to focus on task-relevant endogenous features while ignoring exogenous distractions. The method is evaluated on Atari 100k and Distracting Control Suite. Reviewers praise the clarity of the motivation but note that the work is a straightforward combination of existing methods (ATC and DreamerV3), lacks novelty, and presents limited and unconvincing experimental evidence. Multiple critical implementation and writing issues are identified, and key components of the method (especially how ATC filters out irrelevant information) remain poorly explained.

### Strengths
- The limitation of reconstruction-based MBRL (overemphasis on exogenous features) is clearly outlined with an intuitive example, and the motivation for learning robust representations is well communicated.
- The proposed addition of ATC loss to DreamerV3 is sensible and the combination is novel (though one reviewer rated contribution 3, while another rated it 1, indicating disagreement on novelty vs. straightforwardness).
- Subsection 5.3 and 5.4 show promising results, and subsection 5.5 identifies an interesting limitation of ATC that may inspire future work.
- The paper is relatively easy to follow and the experimental setup (Atari 100k, Distracting Control Suite) is a reasonable starting point.

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of novelty and insufficient differentiation from prior work.** The method is a direct application of ATC (Stooke et al., 2021) to DreamerV3 without algorithmic or theoretical contributions. The paper does not clearly delineate which components (e.g., InfoNCE loss, target encoder with EMA, data augmentation) are inherited from ATC and which are new. No comparisons are made against prior robust-representation methods such as DreamerPro, Iso-Dream, DBC, or Denoised-MDP (R1-W3). Broader exploration (e.g., other MBRL frameworks like TDMPC) is missing (R3-Q2).

2. **Unclear how the ATC loss learns endogenous features.** The paper fails to justify why temporal contrastive loss filters out irrelevant information, since it only encourages similar representations for temporally close states regardless of task relevance. A theoretical justification or empirical analysis (e.g., measuring endogenous vs. exogenous feature correlations) is needed (R1-W1, R4-W2). The forward-predictive scheme does not explicitly incorporate actions in the K-step prediction, and it is unclear how the model handles stochasticity and multiple possible trajectories, especially with discrete actions (R1-W2). The additional recurrent predictor is not justified; could the RSSM not serve this purpose? (R2-Q1).

3. **Serious exposition and presentation issues.**
   - Section 4 is confusing: variable \(x_t\) is introduced without explanation of what produces it; later a decoder producing \(x_t\) is added, forcing re-reading; the transformation matrix \(W\) is undefined; the residual notation \(p_t = x_t + h_\phi(x_t)\) is not explained; the relationship between online encoder, target encoder, and EMA update is unclear (R2-W2, R3-W2).
   - Mathematical formulations are sloppy: no equation numbers; variables used liberally (e.g., \(T\) in the main loss equation likely incorrectly refers to episode length and the expectation does not account for the replay buffer) (R3-W3).
   - Typos and errors: "scricter" → "stricter" (line 125); incorrect symbols \(\hat{r}_t\) and \(\hat{o}_t\) (line 186); incorrect arrow between \(h_t\) and \(z_{t+1}\) in Fig. 1 (R1-W6, R2-W5).
   - Figure 2 is unclear: it is not specified whether reconstructions come from a real trajectory or a model rollout; no quantitative metrics; inconsistent starting states; ball is too small; no legend inside the figure (R2-W1, R4-W4). Questions remain about reconstruction quality at 100k steps (R4-Q1). Figure 3 lacks a description of evaluation metrics (R3-Q1). Table 1 is hard to read; consider replacing with a summary figure (R3-W4).
   - Other presentational suggestions: reorder Section 5.4 to the beginning of Section 5; merge Sections 6 and 7; label rows and add whitespace in Figure 2; number equations (R2-W5). Variables like \(g_\phi\) appear without prior introduction (R2-W5). Clarify frame skip: if skip=4, then K=4 covers 16 frames (R2-Q2).

4. **Experiments are limited and unconvincing.**
   - Only three random seeds are used; ablation studies are limited to three games, reducing generalizability (R2-W4).
   - Subsection 5.1 uses a single subjective demonstration on one task with one seed to claim improved representation – insufficient (R3-W4).
   - Subsection 5.2 shows only three environments (R3-W4). Sample efficiency is not shown as return vs. samples in Figure 3 (R3-W4).
   - No ablation separates the contribution of image augmentation (random shift) from the ATC loss – necessary to isolate each component’s impact (R1-W5, R3-W5, R4-Q2).
   - The method performs worse on Distracting Control Suite; no intuition is provided (R3-Q3). The experimental environments (Atari and DM Control Suite) are considered simplistic; more realistic environments like CARLA are suggested (R1-W4). A comparison against simple data augmentation for robustness is missing (R4-Q2).
