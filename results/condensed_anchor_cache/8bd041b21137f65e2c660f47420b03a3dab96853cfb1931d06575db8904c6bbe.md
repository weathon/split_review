## Merged Review

### Summary
Two reviewers (R1, R3) rate the paper very low (1/5), citing insufficient novelty and weak experiments; two (R2, R4) rate it moderate (3/5), appreciating the simplicity and empirical gains but noting significant missing analyses and limited evaluation. The paper proposes SQT (Standard Q-target), a conservative actor-critic that subtracts the standard deviation of an ensemble of Q-networks from the Q-target to reduce overestimation bias. It is applied to TD3, SAC, and TD7 and tested on a few MuJoCo tasks. The core idea is described late (Section 8), and the main contribution is the additive uncertainty penalty.

### Strengths
- **Simple modification** that is easy to understand and implement (all reviewers).
- **Empirical improvement**: Table 2 shows that adding SQT consistently improves performance over the three baseline algorithms on the four evaluated tasks (R2, R3, R4; however R1 and R3 consider the results extremely weak and the evaluation very limited).
- **Some theoretical analysis** is provided (R4, though other reviewers found it lacking or unclear).
- **Clarity**: Overall paper is clear and easy to follow (R1, R2; R3 and R4 note presentation issues).

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of novelty and prior work**:
   - The exact idea (subtracting ensemble std from Q-target) was previously published in Ghasemipour et al., NeurIPS 2022, as *Shared Targets (Method 2)*. This prior work is not cited (R1).
   - The paper does not adequately discuss related ensemble-based methods (e.g., REDQ, TQC) and does not cite relevant ensemble learning papers in the Background (R2, R3).
   - The contribution is considered insufficient: simply adding std to the Q-target without ablation on the penalty term (e.g., why std vs. variance) or explanation of its advantage (R3).

2. **Insufficient analysis of bias reduction**:
   - The paper only shows final performance, not how the bias changes. Missing experiments like estimating true Q-values via Monte Carlo returns (as in REDQ) or following the methodology of Double Q-learning (R2, R4).
   - No comparison to claims in arXiv:2110.01548 that min over critics can be equivalent to subtracting std; the current work appears to contradict that without discussion (R4).

3. **Limited and questionable evaluation**:
   - Only four MuJoCo tasks are tested; Hopper is missing (R4). More environments (other MuJoCo, DM Control, Atari) are needed (R2, R3, R4).
   - No statistical significance: only mean scores are reported; no learning curves or standard deviations are provided (R4).
   - The claim that SQT (global std) outperforms SQT_tuple (per-tuple std) is based on only one out of four tasks – this does not support the claimed advantage (R4).
   - Offline RL results (e.g., D4RL tasks used in TD7) are absent, even though the approach is naturally relevant to conservatism (R4).
   - Missing comparisons to more recent algorithms (DreamerV2, REM) (R3).

4. **Missing technical details**:
   - No adequate discussion of hyperparameters: the trade-off coefficient \(\alpha\), batch size, ensemble size – and their sensitivity is not studied (R2, R3, R4).
   - No comparison of computational cost vs. baselines (R2).
   - Abbreviation UTD is introduced in Line 309 without definition (R2).
   - The main method is described in Section 8 (of the paper), while most results are shown earlier; the approach should be introduced in Section 4 (R4).

5. **Presentation and formatting**:
   - Equation (10) appears in pseudocode but is never explained in the main text (R3).
   - Citation format is inconsistent; abbreviations like RL and MDP are not defined upon first use (R3).
   - The motivation behind the formula in Equation 20 is unclear: what are \(s'\) and \(a'\) under the mean part? Are they random samples from the replay buffer? If so, why is a random set useful for learning a particular \((s,a)\) pair? (R4)

6. **Reviewer disagreement on overall assessment**: R2 and R4 find the strong empirical results promising despite weaknesses, while R1 and R3 consider the experiments too weak to support any contribution. R1 is the most critical (rating 1, confidence 4), R2 the most positive (rating 3, confidence 4).
