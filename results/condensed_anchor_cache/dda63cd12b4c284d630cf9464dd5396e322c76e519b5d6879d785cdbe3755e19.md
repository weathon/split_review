## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses the task of instantaneous trajectory prediction (using only two observed locations) for autonomous driving. It proposes ITPNet, a plug-and-play module that first reconstructs unobserved historical latent features via a backward forecasting loss, then applies a Noise Redundancy Reduction Former (NRRFormer) to filter noise and redundancy from these reconstructed features before feeding them into a downstream predictor (HiVT or LaneGCN). Experiments on Argoverse and nuScenes show improved ADE/FDE over baselines when only two prior observations are available. The method also outperforms prior works on limited-observation prediction (MOE, Distill).

### Strengths
- **Practical and overlooked problem**: Tackles the important real-world scenario where an agent appears with very few prior observations (e.g., emerging from behind an obstruction). Multiple reviewers commended the motivation and novelty.
- **Novel idea and sound design**: Using backward forecasting to recover unobserved historical representations and then filtering via NRRFormer to mitigate noise/redundancy is a creative and justified approach (supported by the observed performance drop with large N).
- **Plug-and-play compatibility**: ITPNet can be attached to any trajectory prediction backbone. The paper demonstrates integration with two popular open-source models (HiVT, LaneGCN), facilitating adoption.
- **Strong empirical gains**: Significant improvements in ADE/FDE over HiVT and LaneGCN baselines when using two observations. Outperforms MOE and Distill on the same task.
- **Ablation study**: Table 2 systematically evaluates the contribution of each component (backward forecasting, continuous loss, NRRFormer), and Table 3 studies the effect of the number N of predicted historical steps.
- **Clear and well-organized writing**: The paper is easy to follow, with a logical structure that supports the claims (one reviewer rated presentation 4/4).
- **Two datasets, two backbones**: Effectiveness is validated on both Argoverse and nuScenes with two different backbone architectures, increasing generalizability.

### Weaknesses
- **Baselines are outdated**: One reviewer (score 5, high confidence) notes that LaneGCN and HiVT are far from current state-of-the-art. The paper would benefit from comparison with more modern open-source models such as QCNet or MTR. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limited real-world deployment analysis**: The method adds considerable parameters and computation. When deployed, the system would need to run both the original full-history predictor and ITPNet for newly appearing agents, potentially doubling inference cost. Moreover, ITPNet+HiVT with two observations still underperforms HiVT with the standard 2-second observation input, raising doubts about its practical benefit when some agents have longer histories. (Reviewer 1; contrasted by other reviewers who see the value for instantaneous cases)
- **NRRFormer and continuous loss provide only marginal gains**: Multiple reviewers note that in the ablation study (Table 2) the improvements from the continuous loss (cts loss) and NRRFormer are very small or questionable. Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 4 state “NRRFormer barely provides any performance boost.” Reviewer 2 requests confidence intervals or multiple seeds to confirm consistency. The small gains raise concerns about the necessity of these components.
- **Backward forecasting N=10 hurts the best K=1 mode**: Reviewer 1 observes that with N=10 and NRRFormer, performance on the best (K=1) prediction mode is actually worse than without the module.
- **Lack of evaluation with varying observation length (T)**: The main experiments only use T=2 prior observations. It is unclear whether ITPNet improves performance for larger T (e.g., T=10) or becomes detrimental. Both Reviewer 2 and Reviewer 4 request a curve showing performance as T varies, to match real-world systems that use all available history.
- **Missing clarification of baseline training**: Reviewer 3 questions how the baselines were trained – whether with only two observations, all observations, or a filtered subset. Since the datasets include agents with varying history lengths, the training procedure must be specified to ensure a fair comparison. (Reviewer 3; also Reviewer 2 asks for clarification)
- **Contrastive (cts) loss explanation and effect**: Reviewer 3 and Reviewer 2 note that the paper lacks a clear explanation of why the cts loss improves reconstruction and prevents trivial solutions. The impact of the cts loss appears weak (Table 2), and the margin parameter δ in Eq. (7) is not specified as learned or fixed. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3)
- **Method uses extra labeled data**: The backward forecasting loss requires ground-truth positions of unobserved past states, which may not be available in all datasets (e.g., when tracking begins). The baselines are trained only on two observations, so the proposed method effectively uses more supervision. (Reviewer 2)
- **Assumes clean initial 2D poses**: The paper truncates fully observed trajectories to simulate the instantaneous scenario. In real-world cases of a vehicle suddenly emerging, the 2D observations may be noisy or inaccurate due to limited tracking, potentially reducing the reported gains. (Reviewer 2)
- **Unclear detail about mean initialization**: Reviewer 3 points out that the paper states \(\hat{v}^{unobs}_1\) becomes the mean of \(V^{obs}\) on page 5, but does not explain how the mean is computed (per-agent, global, or per timestep). This ambiguity affects reproducibility.
- **Missing comparison fairness with MOE/Distill**: Reviewer 3 notes that while MOE and Distill also tackle limited-observation prediction, the paper does not apply them on the same backbones (HiVT, LaneGCN) as the proposed method, potentially giving an unfair advantage. (Reviewer 3)
- **Only single-mode backward forecasting**: Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 3 ask why the backward forecasting predicts only a single past trajectory rather than multiple modes (like future prediction). Reviewer 3 also suggests experimenting with multiple past predictions.
- **Contrastive loss and NRRFormer effect for smaller N**: Reviewer 2 notes that Table 3 does not show how NRRFormer affects smaller N values (e.g., N=3). The analysis on N is described as “rushed” and without careful tuning. Reviewer 4 similarly asks if N=3 with NRRFormer yields better performance.
- **Minor writing issue**: “straightly” should be replaced with “straight” (Reviewer 2).
