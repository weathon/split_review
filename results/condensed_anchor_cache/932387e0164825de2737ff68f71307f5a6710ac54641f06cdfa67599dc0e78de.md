## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces EAST, a method that fine-tunes an LLM (Llama-3-8B-Instruct) for simultaneous machine translation (SiMT) by constructing interleaved source-target sequences with special read/write tokens. Training data is generated by segmenting and rephrasing parallel sentences using GPT-4. The approach aims to enable adaptive read/write decisions and efficient KV cache reuse. While some reviewers find the work interesting and well-written (R3), the majority raise substantial concerns about limited novelty, unfair baselines, insufficient evaluation, and missing analyses. Ratings range from 3 to 6, with most at 3.

### Strengths
1. **Novel use of interleaved sequences**: Explicit read/write tokens in the training format enable efficient KV cache reuse during inference and avoid traditional wait-\(k\) policies (R2, R3).
2. **Valuable dataset and SFT method**: The SiMT dataset tailored for LLMs (SiMT-De-En-660K, SiMT-Multi-90K) supports fine-tuning for simultaneous translation and may be useful for future work (R1, R4).
3. **Multilingual and document-level evaluation**: The evaluation covers eight language pairs and document-level SiMT, which is often ignored in prior work (R5).
4. **Demonstrated SiMT ability**: Experimental results (e.g., Figure 3, Tables 1–2) show that the fine-tuned LLM acquires simultaneous translation capability with competitive scores (R1, R3, R4).
5. **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty**: The idea of using interleaved source-target pairs for SiMT via multi-turn dialogues was already explored in Conversational SimulMT [1]. The main contribution—using GPT-4 to generate and rephrase the interleaved data—is more an engineering effort than a methodological innovation (R1, R4, R5).
2. **Unfair and unconvincing baselines**:
   - Comparisons (Figure 7, Table 2) pit EAST (Llama-3-8B) against methods using smaller Transformer models or Llama-2. The performance gain may stem from the stronger foundation model, not the proposed policy (R1, R4, R5).
   - No comparison with other recent LLM-based SiMT systems. The single exception (Agent-SiMT) uses a different foundation model, making the advantage ambiguous. Moreover, when latency is very large (near offline), the performance gap widens, suggesting the improvement comes from GPT-4-distilled target text rephrasing rather than the streaming strategy (R3, R5).
   - To isolate the contribution of the SiMT dataset, a direct comparison with offline translation (fine-tuned on the same LLM) using a wait-\(k\) strategy is needed (R4).
3. **Insufficient evaluation rigor**:
   - **AL-CA metric misused**: This speech-to-text latency metric is reported in Table 2 and Section 4.4, but the paper does not specify which speech dataset was used or how the metric was computed (R1, R4).
   - Missing latency-aware metrics (e.g., different latency-quality tradeoff plots) and human evaluation for SiMT quality (R2).
   - The claimed “interpolation effect” for latency lacks sufficient empirical support (R2).
4. **Unexpected experimental result**: EAST-Only-Stage-II (fine-tuned on multilingual SiMT-Multi-90K) outperforms EAST-Stage-I (fine-tuned on larger De-En SiMT-660K) on De-En. This contradicts expectations and requires explanation (R4).
5. **Data filtering and efficiency concerns**:
   - Only 660K sentence pairs are used from WMT15 De-En (which contains 4.5M pairs). The filtering criteria and their potential bias are not explained, raising questions about representativeness (R3).
   - No experiment analyzing performance as a function of training data size, leaving data efficiency unclear (R3).
6. **Lack of policy analysis**: The paper does not analyze the quality of the adaptive read/write policy learned by the LLM (e.g., how it decides when to read vs. write) (R4).
7. **Robustness ignored**: No evaluation on noisy or non-fluent input, which is common in real streaming scenarios (R2).
8. **Alternative training paradigm not considered**: The question of whether an RL approach could improve the learned read/write policy remains open (R2, as a suggestion for missing ablation).
