## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes EvoSeed, an evolutionary strategy-based framework that uses CMA-ES to optimize the initial seed vector of a conditional diffusion model, generating natural adversarial examples that misclassify classifiers across object classification, safety content detection, and commercial API tasks. One reviewer (score 5) rated the paper higher than the other two (score 3). All agree the paper is well-written and validates effectiveness across tasks, but identify significant weaknesses in novelty, baseline comparisons, experimental rigor, and justification of design choices.

### Strengths
- Writing is clear and well-organized, easy to follow.
- The approach of modifying the seed rather than the generative model is intriguing and novel.
- Validates attack effectiveness across multiple classification task scenarios, demonstrating versatility.
- Provides qualitative analysis of generated adversarial examples from multiple perspectives.
- EvoSeed can serve as a diagnostic tool to probe classifier weaknesses and understand misclassifications.
- Metrics such as Attack Success Rate (ASR) and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) provide strong evidence for efficacy.
- For an HSB (score 5) reviewer, the paper offers value as a red-teaming tool for understanding classification system limitations.

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient novelty and missing baselines**: Prior works (AdvDiff, Xu et al. 2024, AdvDiffuser) also use diffusion models to generate natural adversarial examples, yet the paper does not articulate how EvoSeed differs from them nor compare results to these methods. Additionally, no comparison is made to a simple two-step baseline: generate an image using a standard diffusion model then attack it with a traditional adversarial method (same pixel-space threat model). This baseline is needed to establish whether EvoSeed offers meaningful improvements over a more efficient approach. The paper also places excessive focus on different application areas without explaining technical differences among tasks, and compares against only a single existing method.

2. **Unclear rationale for model choices**: The paper does not explain why specific diffusion models and victim models are chosen for each experiment (Sections 4.1, 4.3, 5.1 use different models). The criteria for selecting victim models across tasks are unclear. Furthermore, the rationale for modifying the seed rather than the generative model is largely absent; the authors should clarify the primary benefits of this design decision.

3. **Quantitative evaluation limitations**: Quantitative results are reported only on CIFAR-10 and MNIST for object classification, limiting generalizability. No quantitative results are provided for other tasks (e.g., safety detection). The perturbation norm used (ε=0.3) is ten times larger than the standard 8/255≈0.03 commonly used for CIFAR-10, raising fairness concerns even in the latent space. A distance metric comparing the adversarially generated image to the original generated image is missing, which would clarify the magnitude of changes. These points question whether EvoSeed actually generates imperceptible or photo-realistic adversarial examples.

4. **Unsubstantiated claims and incomplete validation**:
   - The claim (line 87) that generated samples come from the image distribution is not substantiated; some images appear unnatural (e.g., shovel/panda in Fig. 8). EvoSeed does not constrain changes in pixel space, unlike methods that impose L_infty or L_2 norms.
   - The claim in Section 4.1 that EvoSeed "outperforms adversarial image generation using Text-to-Image Diffusion Models like Liu et al. (2024b) and Poyuan et al. (2023)" lacks quantitative validation.
   - The claim in Section 4.2 that "Schramowski et al. (2023) provides prompts to bypass these classifiers; however, we use simple prompts that effectively generate inappropriate images" is not experimentally supported.
   - The abstract claim about understanding safety mechanisms needs comparison with existing works on text-to-image safety (Unsafe Diffusion, SneakyPrompt, SurrogatePrompt).
   - The explanation for the volcano image (Fig. 7) – that confidence drops due to reduced smoke/fire areas – is not clearly evidenced; confidence could drop due to invisible texture changes, a common factor in adversarial attacks.

5. **Computational concerns**: EvoSeed relies on iterative optimization, making it significantly slower than other adversarial attacks. The adversarially generated images differ substantially from the original generated images, and in some cases appear unnatural.

6. **Similarity to prior work**: The approach shares strong similarities with Blau et al. (2022), which uses diffusion models for adversarial defense by optimizing latent variables with perturbation constraints. The paper does not compare to this work or explain how EvoSeed offers new insights.

7. **Minor issues**: The claim (line 355) that standard adversarial attacks do not provide explainability is contradicted by prior research (Etmann et al., 2019) showing adversarially trained classifiers have perceptually aligned gradients.
