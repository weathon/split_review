## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes an inexact stochastic proximal point algorithm (isPPA) for stochastic composite optimization. Under mild assumptions that avoid smoothness and strong convexity (local Lipschitz continuity, quadratic growth), it establishes stability, almost sure convergence, and last-iterate convergence rates: O(k^{-β}) for expected squared distance to the solution set and O(k^{-β/2}) for the KKT residual. Lower bounds derived via the regularized Fréchet mean problem show these rates are tight up to constant factors. Numerical experiments on synthetic data confirm the theoretical rates for different β. Reviewers expressed a range of opinions; one found the contribution very nice and the analysis sound, while others raised concerns about practical applicability, comparisons, and clarity.

### Strengths

- Proposes isPPA under weaker assumptions (local Lipschitz continuity, quadratic growth) than prior work, broadening applicability to nonsmooth settings.
- Provides tight last-iterate convergence rates (Theorems 2.4, 2.7) for distance to the optimal solution set and KKT residual.
- Establishes lower bounds via the regularized Fréchet mean problem, showing rates are tight up to constant factors.
- Proves stability of isPPA under mild conditions, extending stability results for exact sPPA.
- Non-asymptotic last-iterate rates in expected squared distance to the optimal set are provided.
- The problem setting, assumptions, and theoretical results are clearly stated, with necessary comparisons to existing literature.
- Numerical experiments align with theoretical convergence rates for different β.
- One reviewer (rating 8) considered the contribution very nice and the analysis sound.

### Weaknesses

- **Double-loop structure and inner-loop complexity**: The algorithm is double-loop and relies on solving subproblems inexactly. The paper does not provide a general method for solving these subproblems to satisfy SCA for arbitrary problems (only Appendix F.1 for a specific problem). The computational complexity of the inner loop is not analyzed, making it difficult to assess overall cost. This nested structure is rarely used in practice; single-loop algorithms are more common. The accuracy parameters (e.g., ε_k = γ α_k^2) are conservative, and practical tuning of γ is not discussed.
- **Missing iteration complexity for subproblems**: For general nonsmooth convex settings, no iteration complexity is given for solving the subproblems, and no comparison of computational cost between sPPA and isPPA is provided.
- **Insufficient demonstration of practical advantage**: The claim that the method requires weaker conditions than all existing methods is not supported by a concrete machine learning example where other methods fail. The experiments use synthetic datasets and somewhat outdated problems (Proximal SVM, regularized Fréchet mean); they do not test more general (e.g., nonconvex) machine learning problems or compare against state-of-the-art algorithms.
- **Need clearer explanation of “mild” assumptions**: The paper should better explain why the assumptions are less restrictive compared with previous work and how the results differ from prior theoretical analyses. The claim that global Lipschitz continuity is required despite bounded iterates needs clarification, and the argument that the elastic net regularizer violates Lipschitz continuity (Lines 68–69) appears inconsistent with the boundedness assumption.
- **Stability analysis novelty**: Deriving the stability result from existing exact PPA is not particularly challenging; the technical difficulties are not clearly stated. Given the boundedness of iterates, local Lipschitz continuity is natural, but why global Lipschitz is needed remains unclear.
- **Unsubstantiated extension to nonconvex**: The claim that the methodology readily extends to nonconvex settings (Lines 84–85) is not substantiated. A comparison with the proximal point method for weakly convex functions (Davis & Drusvyatskiy, 2019) is missing.
- **Theoretical details needing clarification**:
    - (i) The conditions in Remark 1.1 are insufficient to ensure Assumption 2 (e.g., indicator function of x ≥ 0 has unbounded subgradient).
    - (ii) Whether a high-probability bound on the distance to the optimal set is achievable (Theorem 2.3).
    - (iii) Can the assumption sup‖x_k‖ < ∞ be removed for a cleaner result?
    - (iv) The impact of minibatch size m is not commented on.
    - (v) Why Criterion SCB implies SCA (Line 219, eq. (2.2)) is not explained.
    - (vi) The term “KKT residual” for ‖x - prox_φ(x)‖ needs justification.
    - (vii) Theorem 2.4 uses ε_k = γ α_k^{3/2}, but experiments use ε_k = γ α_k^2; the effect of this exponent difference is not discussed.
- **Typo**: Line 440: “iteration(k)” should be “iteration k”.
- **Experiment design**: The experiments resemble an ablation study without comparisons to other state-of-the-art algorithms, limiting the demonstration of practical applicability.
