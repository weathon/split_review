## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents SHypX, a model-agnostic post-hoc explainer for hypergraph neural networks (HyperGNNs) for node classification. It provides both local (instance-level) explanations via Gumbel-Softmax sampling of salient subhypergraphs balancing faithfulness and concision, and global (model-level) explanations via unsupervised concept extraction from embedding clusters. The authors introduce four synthetic hypergraph datasets and a generalized fidelity metric. Experiments on synthetic and four real-world datasets show improvement over baselines by up to 25 percentage points in fidelity on average. Two reviewers found the work novel and well-written (ratings 5, 5), while two raised significant concerns about motivation, evaluation, and generality (ratings 3, 3).

### Strengths
- First work to provide both local and global explanations for HyperGNNs (the global aspect has not been addressed previously).  
- Introduces a new sampling-based local explanation method that avoids attention mechanisms, adapted to hypergraph structures.  
- The four synthetic hypergraph datasets are an original contribution for structured evaluation of hypergraph explainability.  
- Paper is well-written, clear, and the experimental design is solid with good results on synthetic data.  
- Addresses an important gap in HyperGNN interpretability.

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation for specialized hypergraph explainer is insufficiently justified.** Several reviewers question why GNN explainers (e.g., GNNExplainer) cannot be straightforwardly applied after converting hypergraphs to bipartite graphs. The paper lacks concrete failure cases when using existing GNN explainers on such a construction.  
- **Real-world evaluation is limited.** Datasets are small; even simple MLPs achieve competitive performance, questioning whether the hypergraph structure is adequately tested. Performance gains on real-world data are minimal and differences between baselines are small, making it hard to confirm SHypX’s advantage.  
- **Synthetic datasets not provided** (no open-source release), reducing reproducibility and utility. Their specific usefulness for evaluating explainability is also not clearly argued.  
- **The local and global explainers are completely decoupled** – the global method is essentially transposed from prior GNN work (GCExplainer), and the paper does not explore global explanations derived from local ones (e.g., using the local explainer in a global aggregation). The title implies a deeper relationship.  
- **Global explanation evaluation is weak.** Only one metric is used; Figure 4 is confusing; no visualizations of explanatory subhypergraphs (even on synthetic data) are provided. The global explainer is also criticized as another black‑box, contradicting the claim that SHypX “doesn’t rely on additional black-box networks.”  
- **Baselines are insufficient and comparisons incomplete.** Only a few baselines are used; in particular, GNNExplainer should be tested on a bipartite graph representation (where hyperedges become nodes) rather than the lossy graph conversion currently used. The current GNNExplainer results show very low fidelity (Fid-acc 0.00, Fid-KL ~0.00), which is hard to interpret.  
- **Fid+ is not evaluated for SHypX or for GNNExplainer**; only Fid- is reported, creating an incomplete picture. The paper acknowledges Fid+ is “not appropriate” but should still be shown for completeness.  
- **Method limitations and design choices lack justification.**  
  - The mean-field approximation (line 212) is used without literature support specific to hypergraphs; no alternative approximations are explored.  
  - The local explainer produces only a single explanation subhypergraph per node; why not multiple or a set is not addressed.  
  - For node classification, a trivial method returning only immediate neighbors might achieve high fidelity – this would make the explainer’s value questionable.  
- **Related work is not thoroughly reviewed.** More recent GNN explainability methods and their applicability to hypergraphs should be discussed. The limitations of existing model-agnostic approaches for hyperGNNs are not clearly established.  
- **Presentation issues:**  
  - Equation (3) is unclear; several typos (e.g., “explainiability” line 75, “Pr(v ∈ e_sub = 0)” line 206).  
  - “Coherent explanations” (line 80) not defined.  
  - InfoNCE (line 106) lacks context (noise-contrastive estimation).  
  - GCExplainer is referenced in the method (line 259) but not introduced earlier; reference should be added near line 244.  
  - Figure 1’s “concept-to-class decision tree” icon needs explanation.  
  - Figure 2 caption is confusing.  
  - Results for local and global explanations should be separated clearly.  

**Specific reviewer questions (overlapping with above weaknesses):**  
- Please define “concept” (line 251): is it a cluster in the GNN embedding space with representative equal to the node closest to the geometric center?  
- Line 249: is the “latent embedding space” the final layer output before softmax?  
- Have you tried other global explanation methods like GLGExplainer?  
- Have you tried other local explanation techniques from the GNN literature?  
- Has similar Gumbel‑Softmax sampling been used in GNN explanation?  
- For the local explainer, why only a single subhypergraph?  
- Why only evaluate Fid– and not Fid+?  
- Could you present hypergraph as bipartite graph and apply GNNExplainer directly?  
- For node classification, what happens if a method returns only first‑hop neighbors as explanation – would fidelity metrics still be meaningful?
