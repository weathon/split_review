## Merged Review

### Summary

Reviewers are split. Two (R4, R5) rate 8 and find the method effective, with significant improvements in CRPS-sum and on high missing ratios, and well-designed architectural components. Three reviewers (R1, R2, R3) rate 3–5 and consider the novelty limited (diffusion + Mamba is an incremental combination; bidirectional/channel modeling is not new), the evaluation insufficient (missing standard benchmarks, inconsistent missing rates, no empirical efficiency runs, only synthetic missing data, no parameter sensitivity), and the motivation/presentation unclear. All agree that time series imputation is an important problem, and the complexity analysis is provided, but the experimental validation and theoretical grounding are contested.

### Strengths

-   **Novelty and focus:** The idea of using Mamba as the backbone of a diffusion model for time series imputation, specifically focusing on channel dependencies and backbone selection, is considered novel and interesting by some reviewers (R2). The bidirectional and channel modeling blocks (temporal attention, channel modeling) are judged well-designed for capturing temporal and channel dependencies (R4, R5).
-   **Effectiveness:** The method shows significant improvements in deterministic results on high missing ratios and in the CRPS-sum metric compared to baselines (R4, R5). R4 and R5 note the CRPS-sum improvement is “very significant”.
-   **Efficiency:** The integration of Mamba provides linear complexity for sequence data. R4 states “the running time of the proposed method is significantly lower than the other baselines”. R3 acknowledges that a complexity analysis is provided (though the same reviewer notes empirical time cost is missing).
-   **Experimental evaluations and comparisons:** Some reviewers (R2) find the experimental evaluations and comparisons good. R4 and R5 praise the detailed code for reproducible experiments.
-   **Reproducibility:** R4 comments that the “detailed code for reproducible experiments” is provided. R5 agrees the code is available. (Note: R3 opposes on reproducibility, see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses

-   **Limited novelty and contribution:** Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R3) state that the paper is a straightforward combination of diffusion models and Mamba, without significant methodological novelty. The bidirectional modeling technique is not new, with R1 citing similarity to bi-LSTM. The paper is seen as an exploratory study of model combinations without a clearly defined technical, theoretical, or practical research question (R2). R3 adds there is no theoretical result to support the proposed techniques.
-   **Evaluation breadth and standard benchmarks:**
    -   Missing standard time series imputation datasets: R1 and R4 point out PhysioNet Challenge 2012 (healthcare) and air quality (AQI) datasets are commonly used but not included. R2 notes that the ECG experiment from the SSSD paper (a key baseline) is omitted.
    -   Only synthetic missing values are used (R3); real-world incomplete datasets are not tested.
    -   Inconsistent missing rates across datasets: R3 asks why MuJoCo uses 70%,80%,90% missing while Electricity uses 10%,30%,50%, and why not consistent across all.
    -   Baselines not compared on all datasets: R3 suggests comparisons should be done on all datasets, not a subset.
    -   R1 criticizes the inclusion of forecasting as an afterthought, diluting the imputation focus.
-   **Motivation and justification:**
    -   The motivation for using Mamba as a backbone is not well-stated; it is unclear what limitation of CNN, Transformer, or SSM backbones is being addressed (R2). The claim of “channel-dependent” low complexity is considered a different use of SSM, not a critique of existing methods.
    -   The necessity of capturing inter-channel relationships for imputation is not explained (R2). R5 questions whether the model can handle distance-based dependencies in inter-channel modeling.
    -   The paper does not clearly explain why Mamba is suitable for inter-channel modeling given that SSMs assume temporal order (R2). R4 asks why unidirectional channel modeling is used in the CMB block (Fig 3c).
-   **Empirical efficiency and scalability:**
    -   Despite claiming linear complexity, the experiments do not include running time / time cost comparisons (R3). R4 notes efficiency is only compared in terms of time complexity, not actual runtime memory or parameters (R4 also requests training memory and model parameters details). R3 asks for scalability experiments on additional large real-world datasets.
-   **Experimental rigor and missing analyses:**
    -   No parameter sensitivity analysis (R3).
    -   No ablation study on channel modeling (R2).
    -   R3 questions the reproducibility: there is no guideline for how to use the code or reproduce each table/figure.
-   **Implementation and clarity issues:**
    -   The implementation details of probabilistic time series imputation are unclear (R5). R5 also asks for more details on why the model is probabilistic and how the mask is constructed.
    -   The conditional diffusion model setup is not clearly described: conditions and how they are used are not explained (R5). R4 asks for clarification on mask strategies for datasets with and without original missing values.
    -   R4 questions the claim in Table 1 that SSM models have “partial global dependency”.
-   **Reviewer disagreement on efficiency:** R3 states that time cost is “totally ignored” in experiments, while R4 claims running time is significantly lower. This contrast is present.

*Note: Strengths from R4 and R5 (high ratings) emphasize effectiveness and well-designed blocks, while weaknesses from R1,R2,R3 (lower ratings) focus on limited novelty, insufficient evaluation, and missing analyses. The above list preserves all distinct points from all reviewers.*
