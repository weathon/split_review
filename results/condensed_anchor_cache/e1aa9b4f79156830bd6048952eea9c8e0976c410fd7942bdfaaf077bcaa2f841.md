## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a synthetic video instruction-following dataset, LLaVA-Video-178K, containing 178,510 videos with detailed captions, open-ended QA, and multiple-choice QA. Videos are selected for dynamic scenes from 10 sources, captioned via a recurrent three-level pipeline, and QA pairs (16 question types) are generated using GPT-4o. The model LLaVA-Video is fine-tuned from LLaVA-OneVision on this dataset combined with four public datasets, using a slow-fast video representation. Experiments show performance gains across multiple video benchmarks.

### Strengths

- **Recurrent multi-level captioning pipeline** that generates and refines captions at three temporal levels. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Dynamic video selection** by detecting scene count, filtering out static videos that could be summarized by a single frame. (Reviewer 1)
- **Comprehensive question types**: 16 different types defined for QA generation, more than previous benchmarks. (Reviewer 1)
- **Extensive experiments** across multiple video benchmarks, with thorough ablation studies and use of diverse datasets, demonstrating robustness. (Reviewer 2)
- **Potential benefit to the video understanding community**; the dataset complements existing datasets and improves performance when added to training mixtures. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Well-structured paper** with clear explanation of problem, methodology, and experimental design; high-quality figures illustrating dataset attributes. (Reviewers 2, 3)

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty relative to prior work**: The hierarchical/recursive captioning strategy has been explored (e.g., Video ReCap, CVPR 2024) and is not cited/differentiated. The use of GPT-4o for generating captions and QA pairs essentially distills its video capabilities, similar to ShareGPT4Video (NeurIPS 2024); this work is an incremental scaling from ~40K to ~1.3M QA pairs. (Reviewer 2)
- **Heavy reliance on GPT-4o** without exploration of non-GPT-based annotations or their effect on model performance/diversity. (Reviewer 2)
- **Dataset pipeline unclear** both from diagrams and verbal explanation; not sufficiently differentiated from prior automatic dataset construction methods. (Reviewer 4)
- **Level-1 and Level-2 descriptions generated on fixed time intervals (10s and 30s)**, not event- or scene-specific; the hierarchical approach relies heavily on previous time steps without capturing longer-term temporal information, and the aggregated information may be less meaningful. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Videos mostly ≤180 seconds** with singular scenes and little dynamic transition – may hinder long video understanding and raises questions about the significance of hierarchy and its impact on data quality. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Model architecture has limited generalizability and robustness**; the slow-fast video representation with arbitrary parameter s cannot adaptively handle diverse video lengths and scenarios. (Reviewer 4) – Other reviewers (3) note the representation is more an engineering trick than a conceptual advancement.
- **Multi-choice QA pairs appear tailored to fit existing evaluation benchmarks** rather than reflecting real-world video understanding scenarios, raising concerns about broader utility. (Reviewer 2)
- **Claim that dynamic scenes improve performance lacks direct experimental evidence** – no statistical comparison of dynamic scenes in new vs. existing datasets and no ablation study on this claim. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing ablation study details**: Table 3 does not present individual results of LLaVA-Hound combined with the other three datasets alone, nor results of LLaVA-OV alone, nor results with LLaVA-Video-178K added last. This makes it difficult to verify that the improvement is not due to disadvantages of LLaVA-Hound and that the new dataset adds knowledge beyond existing datasets. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- **Out-of-domain performance loss on EgoSchema** (Table 4) not explained; EgoSchema contains Ego4D videos that may have been observed in the training mixture. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **The 16 forced QA categories**: unclear if they were derived from manual review of how many videos, or automatically summarized from existing datasets; also unclear how unsuitable or low-quality QAs are filtered out. (Reviewer 4)
- **The model’s strong performance is largely due to fine-tuning from a powerful base model** (LLaVA-OneVision), overshadowing the core contributions. (Reviewer 2)
- **Scientific insights limited** – the dataset provides practical value but lacks deep motivation and analytical support for its design choices. (Reviewer 3)
- **Minority reviewer (Rating 3) is more negative** than the other three (all Ratings 5), particularly regarding clarity of the pipeline, generalizability of the architecture, and contribution. Points above attributed to Reviewer 4 are part of this minority opinion.
- **Typos and errors**: line 184/186: “condtion” → “condition”; line 400: apparent formula error (inconsistent with line 871); line 415: “consider green” → “considered as”. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **Questions about humor/fact understanding** – e.g., Figure 4 examples only cover factual questions, not deeper understanding like humor. (Reviewer 1)
