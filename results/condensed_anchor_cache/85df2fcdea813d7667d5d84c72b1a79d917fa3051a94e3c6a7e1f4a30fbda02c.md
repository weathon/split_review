## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Generalized Heavy-Ball Momentum (GHBM) for federated optimization, with variants LocalGHBM and FedHBM. GHBM uses momentum computed over multiple past rounds to mitigate partial participation bias without bounded heterogeneity. Theory shows convergence to a first-order stationary point for non-convex functions. Experiments on vision and language tasks claim improvements over several baselines, especially in large-scale heterogeneous scenarios.

### Strengths
- Tackles an important problem: federated optimization under heterogeneity and partial participation.
- Novel formulation of momentum (GHBM) that extends classical heavy-ball; variants are robust and communication-efficient by design.
- Theoretical convergence rate extends Cheng et al. (2024) to partial participation without bounded heterogeneity, thereby advancing prior theoretical results.
- Diverse experimental evaluation across multiple real-world computer vision and language tasks shows consistent gains over compared methods.
- The approach is straightforward to implement, making it promising for practical FL systems.

### Weaknesses
- **Assumption 4.3 (Cyclic Participation) is a major concern.** Multiple reviewers (R1, R3, R4) note it is too strong. R4 states that periodic participation is disconnected from the experiments and that the difference between cyclic and random selection is analogous to SGD vs. incremental GD; the proof does not demonstrate that convergence properties hold under random selection. R1 asks for an intuitive explanation and how critical this assumption is for the theory. R3 requests further discussion. The claimed theoretical improvement (achieving full-participation rate under partial participation) strictly relies on this assumption, and R4 argues that under the same assumption, FedCM's theory could reach identical conclusions—so the advancement may not come from algorithmic novelty.
- **Insufficient comparison with key baselines.** R1 notes missing discussion of ScaffNew, MIME, and especially CE-LSGD (Patel et al.), which provides lower bounds that this work appears to circumvent without explanation. R2 highlights the absence of SCAFFOLD-M (from Cheng 2024), which has convergence guarantees under unbounded heterogeneity and partial participation *without* cyclic participation. R2 calls this a major weakness, as Cheng 2024 showed SCAFFOLD-M outperforms Fed-Avg-M under severe heterogeneity. R4 notes that Table 3 omits FedCM and FedADC results that appear in earlier tables.
- **Limited theoretical contribution.** R2 (score 3) states the analysis is based on Cheng 2024 and the only novelty is the generalized momentum. R4 calls the approach incremental, an extension of FedCM and FedADC rather than a new concept. R1 points out that the literature overview omits recent works that achieve accelerated communication complexity via control variates (e.g., ScaffNew, CE-LSGD).
- **Presentation and clarity issues.** R1 reports broken equation links (e.g., Eq. (122) in the main text), undefined symbol \(G\) in Lemma 4.4, and difficulty referencing appendix notations (Eq. (11),(12)) for Lemma 4.6. R4 notes a typo in Line 142 (\(\theta^{t,J_i}\) should be \(\theta_i^{t,J_i}\)).
- **Counterintuitive results in Figure 1.** R1 and R4 both question why the variance of gaps is larger in the i.i.d. case than in the non-i.i.d. case, contrary to expectations. R4 asks for the meaning of \(\alpha\) in that figure.
- **Incomplete justification for key design choices.** R4 questions the form of Equation (3) and calls the explanation overly simplistic. R1 asks whether the proposed method targets cross-device or cross-silo settings; the local version may not be suitable for cross-device due to memory overhead similar to SCAFFOLD (a limitation noted by Reddi et al. 2021). R1, R2, and R4 all ask for clarification on how GHBM interacts with server momentum and how the results would change with different \(\tau\) (R4 asks for general result of Theorem 4.7 under any \(\tau\) and how to recover \(\tau=1\)).
- **Unexplained experimental details.** R4 notes that 10,000 training rounds is much larger than typical (baselines often use ≤1,000 rounds) and asks for the number of local steps \(J\). R4 also asks about the selection of \(\beta\): it appears to depend on \(T\) and \(J\), meaning it is not a constant hyperparameter.
- **Disconnect between theory and experiments.** R2 points out that although the paper repeatedly states that existing methods fail in large-scale settings, this claim does not follow from the theoretical convergence results. There is no explanation whether the improvements come from faster convergence or from bias toward better-quality solutions. R1 questions whether the convergence rate benefits from local steps (since from Eq. (122) the local step size must decrease with \(J\)).
- **Heterogeneity assumption appears inconsistently in proofs.** R4 notes that Lemma 4.4 and Corollary 4.5 adopt a bounded heterogeneity assumption (with \(G\) terms), but the influence of \(G\) is not carried into later conclusions, and it is unclear whether Equations (8)-(9) remain valid for later results.
- **Lack of precision in claims.** R4 points to Line 381, which states that increasing \(\tau\) does not increase the overall error; R4 cannot find proof that the term \(\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{k=t-\tau+1}^t U_k\) is constant order. R2 notes the introductory claim that “existing momentum-based FL methods still theoretically rely on bounded heterogeneity in partial participation” is misleading given SCAFFOLD-M’s guarantees.
