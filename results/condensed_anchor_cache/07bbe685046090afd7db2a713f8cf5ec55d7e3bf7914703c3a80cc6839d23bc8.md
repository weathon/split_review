## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper theoretically analyzes the geometry of self-attention networks, focusing on the “lightning” variant (no normalization). Using algebraic geometry, it studies identifiability (fibers) and dimensions of the neuromanifold. For single‑layer, it characterizes generic fibers (mostly 1‑dimensional, rank‑1 cases higher), computes the dimension, and shows the neuromanifold is closed with boundaries. For deep networks, a virtual weights parametrization is introduced and the dimension is computed. The analysis is extended to normalized self‑attention (softmax) for a single layer (proved) and deep networks (conjectured with experimental support). All reviewers judge the mathematical contributions as novel and rigorous.

### Strengths
- Clear exposition that balances algebraic‑geometry language with the ICLR audience’s context.
- Empirical popularity of attention mechanisms and relative lack of theoretical understanding justify the exploration.
- Lightning self‑attention is identified as a fruitful compromise between tractability and realism; the hierarchy of modules (single‑layer, deep, normalized) is useful for future analysis.
- The virtual weights parametrization enables an elegant analysis of deep lightning self‑attention.
- The conjecture on fibers for deep normalized self‑attention (proved for one layer, tested experimentally) is communicated in a way that could advance the topic.
- Results are described well and are relatively straightforward to follow; the mathematics appear correct.
- The analysis is novel and rigorous; understanding the dimension and topology of the neuromanifold is consequential for learning dynamics and overparameterized regimes.
- The study of linear self‑attention with algebraic‑geometry tools is a credible contribution.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of practical implications:** Despite computing that the dimension of the neuromanifold for a contemporary LLM is ~8.2 B versus ~8.6 B parameters, no attempt is made to prescribe how to exploit this gap (e.g., for efficient parameterization or pruning). No experiment (even a toy one) connects the neuromanifold dimension to sample complexity or generalization. The paper falls short of answering “so what?” (R1, R3, R4).  *One reviewer (R4) explicitly states that the results do not offer a significantly better characterization than counting parameters and questions whether the small difference has any practical relevance for expressiveness or trainability.*  R3 notes that the implications of Theorem 3.13 are not discussed, and Corollary 3.5 is too vague to provide practical insights.
- **Gap between studied model and practical transformers:** The model omits (1) the softmax normalization (critical for proper attention behavior – the analysis may not transfer), (2) residual connections (essential for training deep networks), and (3) element‑wise MLPs. While the simplification is acknowledged, it is unclear what can be inferred about realistic models. Furthermore, the softmax extension suggests no parameter redundancy, further limiting any immediate practical guidance. (R2, echoed by R1’s “reasonably realistic” caveat)
- **Presentation and accessibility for the ML community:** The paper is overly mathematicized; too many technical proofs and proof sketches remain in the main text (e.g., lines 165‑173, 227‑240, summary of the proof of Theorem 3.13), which could be moved to the appendix. The purpose of Figures 2 and 3 is unclear. There is almost no review of related literature. The abstract does not reflect the motivation given in the introduction. (R3, R4)
- **Missing experimental description:** The code uses multiple trials (5–8 iterations) to extract the maximum rank Jacobian, but this procedure is not described in the experimental section (L472‑484). Why are these trials needed? (R1)
- **Non‑smoothness and optimization:** The implications of the non‑smoothness of the manifold \(\mathcal{M}_{d,d',a}\) from an optimization standpoint are not discussed. (R1)
- **Unanswered extension questions:** The paper does not address how the analysis could be modified to include residual connections, MLPs, or to study approximate fibers (parameters mapping to *approximately* the same output) instead of exact fibers. (R2, R3)
- **Critical minority opinion:** One reviewer (R4) finds that the results may be appreciated by the algebraic geometry community but are unlikely to be well appreciated by the ML community in their current form, because the geometric properties are not connected to concrete training aspects (convergence rates, generalization, robustness). The other reviewers, while more positive about the theoretical value, share the concern that practical consequences are insufficiently drawn.
