## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces ZeroBubble, a pipeline parallelism scheduling strategy that splits the backward pass into activation gradient (B) and weight gradient (W) computations. This increases scheduling flexibility, enabling schedules (e.g., 1F1B1W) that reduce pipeline bubbles nearly to zero under synchronous training. The authors also develop an automatic scheduling algorithm for unbalanced F/B/W runtimes and a technique to bypass optimizer-step synchronization with a rollback mechanism. Experimental evaluations on Megatron GPT models show up to 15-30% throughput improvement over 1F1B baselines under similar or relaxed memory limits.

### Strengths
1. **Key observation and creativity**: Splitting backward into B and W to reschedule pipeline-independent work (weight gradients) is a creative and insightful approach (R1, R2, R3, R4). This general principle (rescheduling producer/consumer-independent workloads to balance bubbles) should hold for any pipeline parallelism workload (R4).
2. **Theoretical and analytical depth**: The paper provides detailed analysis of memory and TFLOPs trade-offs, fostering intuition about schedule behavior (R1, R2, R3).
3. **Practical automatic scheduling**: Two optimization implementations (HEURISTIC and ILP) handle real-world unbalanced F/B/T runtimes, not just the equal-time assumption (R1, R2, R4). The algorithms outperform standard 1F1B and interleaved 1F1B (R1, R2, R4).
4. **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-organized, with illustrative figures that are easy to follow (R1, R4). (Minority view: R3 suggests writing improvements, noted in Weaknesses.)
5. **Experimental results**: Experiments show up to 20-30% improvement over 1F1B and close to the zero-bubble upper bound (R1, R4). R2 and R3 acknowledge theoretical and experimental support but note limitations.

### Weaknesses
1. **Overselling “zero bubble” and related claim**:
   - Achieving amortized zero bubble requires a large enough number of microbatches (e.g., m >= 2p). Performance under m < p is rarely discussed, and experiments or roofline analysis in that regime are missing (R4).
   - Other methods (e.g., PipeDream, PipeMare) achieve zero bubble (flushless, though with altered backprop or more memory). The claim of being “first to successfully achieve zero pipeline bubbles” is overstating; “near zero bubble” is more accurate (R4).
   - The title “Zero Bubble (Almost) Pipeline Parallelism” is too ambitious; the method is not truly zero bubble (R3). *Disagreement:* R1 and R4 still consider the contribution significant but agree the claim should be tempered.

2. **Memory concerns and impact on throughput**:
   - The method nearly doubles memory usage compared to 1F1B, which limits applicability for larger LLMs (R2, R3). R3 argues that increased memory forces smaller micro-batch sizes, reducing efficiency; the paper’s focus on bubble rate rather than throughput and memory is misguided.
   - It is unclear whether peak memory refers to per-worker or entire pipeline (R4). Clarifying this is needed for fair comparison.
   - R4 suggests that recomputing activation gradients (not activations) during W could save peak memory in H2/2P cases; this is not explored.
   - R1 asks to evaluate memory-efficient schemes (1F1B*, ZB-1p) with larger micro-batches than ZB-2p to confirm the evaluation is not biased towards ZB-2p.

3. **Missing experiments and evaluations**:
   - **Synchronous optimizer step**: The paper should evaluate the performance of a synchronous optimizer step (without the rollback mechanism) to understand the trade-off between simplicity and throughput (R1).
   - **Alternative scheduling**: The paper could explore scheduling W before B, which is less memory-efficient but allows staggering the optimizer step while preserving synchronous semantics (R1).
   - **Unbalanced runtimes**: Experiments only use sentence size 1024, where forward, backward, and weight gradient times are almost equal (Table 1). Experiments or roofline analysis with significantly different F/B/W runtimes (e.g., very short sequences) would better validate the proposed algorithms (R4).
   - **HEURISTIC vs ILP**: The runtime of ILP and an ablation with HEURISTIC only are not reported; their performance difference is unclear (R4).
   - **Figure 5 variability**: For ZB-1p versus 1F1B-I, sometimes ZB-1p is better and sometimes not. An explanation is needed (R2).
   - **Bubble rate focus**: The paper emphasizes bubble rate, but practitioners care more about throughput and memory. The experiments show limited improvement when memory constraints are tight (R3).

4. **Writing and presentation issues** (minority view from R3):
   - Use different colors for B (in Figure 3) and Backward (in Figure 2) to improve clarity.
   - The title is too large given the method is not truly zero bubble (overlaps with point 1).

5. **Bypassing optimizer synchronization details**:
   - The rollback mechanism in Section 4 only works for INF/NAN cases; for grad-norm, each stage needs the global reduced norm. If the optimizer depends on partially reduced state, how does the method produce the same numerical result as 1F1B? (R4)
   - The requirement of a rollback mechanism complicates adoption, especially with model checkpointing (R1).

6. **Other questions requiring clarification**:
   - What is “local search” in Section 3.1? Definition needed (R4).
