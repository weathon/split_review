## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes **Prompt Diffusion**, which uses a diffusion model to refine prompts from a generic state to a sample-specific one, enhancing generalization across distribution shifts. The method is plug-and-play and modality-agnostic, applicable to textual, visual, and multi-modal prompt learning. Experiments across 15 datasets show robust improvements in base-to-new, cross-dataset, and domain generalization. Reviewers note strong empirical results and a clear conceptual motivation but raise concerns about computational cost, reliance on the quality of initial prompts (making it a two-stage process), sensitivity to noise, limited analysis for non-CoCoOp backbones (e.g., VPT, MaPLe), and insufficient justification for choosing diffusion over simpler statistical alternatives (e.g., ProDA, Bayesian Prompt Learning). R3 is notably more critical, especially regarding the practical resource demands and motivation clarity.

### Strengths

- Generates customized prompts per test sample via diffusion, enabling finer-grained generalization and avoiding mixing domain details with class labels (R1).
- Consistently outperforms baseline prompt learning methods across multiple tasks (R2, R3).
- Plug-and-play and modality-agnostic, seamlessly embedding into existing textual, visual, or multimodal prompt learning frameworks (R1).
- Efficient ODE-based sampling reduces inference to 5 steps, offering a good trade-off between improvement and computational cost (R3).
- Intuitive idea: addressing fixed-prompt limitations by overfitting prompts per sample and then learning a generative transition from random to overfitted prompts (R2).

### Weaknesses

- **Computational overhead and practicality:** The method requires per-sample iterative optimization during training and introduces a diffusion model, which may significantly increase training time and GPU demands compared to conventional prompt learning. R3 argues that this undermines the parameter-efficiency of prompt learning and makes the approach impractical for resource-constrained settings. A comparative analysis of training cost is requested (R1, R2, R3).
- **Two-stage dependence and noise sensitivity:** The process first learns base prompts, then applies diffusion; final performance depends on the quality of the initial prompts. The diffusion stage relies on random noise vectors to generate prompts, which may cause uncontrollable fluctuations and instability in predictions. The authors should address how to cope with this randomness (R1, R2).
- **Limited backbone generality:** The pipeline is primarily demonstrated with CoCoOp (including its meta-net π). It is unclear how the method applies to other prompt learning methods that do not involve π, such as VPT (visual prompt tuning) and MaPLe (multi-modal prompt learning). The paper also appears to focus on VPT-shallow; applicability to VPT-deep is not discussed (R1, R2).
- **Missing ablation on prompt length:** The length of prompts in prompt learning methods can affect performance; the paper does not ablate how Prompt Diffusion interacts with prompt length. Ablation studies are requested (R2).
- **Insufficient justification for diffusion over simpler alternatives:** The paper does not clearly articulate why a complex diffusion model is necessary rather than simpler statistical methods like ProDA or Bayesian Prompt Learning. The specific advantages of the diffusion approach over these alternatives are not quantified (R3).
- **Missing related work on instance-level prompts:** There are existing works that generate instance-level prompts (e.g., [1]); the paper should discuss and compare with them to position the contribution (R2).
- **Limited empirical exploration beyond image-text:** The experiments are confined to image-text classification tasks; discussion of potential challenges and adaptations needed for other modalities (e.g., audio, video) is lacking (R3).
- **Loss weight β unclear:** The setting of the two loss weights β in Equation (8) lacks experimental analysis; further ablation is requested (R2).
- **Quantification of the problem:** The paper does not fully articulate or quantify the performance degradation of existing SOTA prompt methods under distribution shifts, creating ambiguity about the critical nature of the addressed issue (R3). Specific examples are insufficient.
