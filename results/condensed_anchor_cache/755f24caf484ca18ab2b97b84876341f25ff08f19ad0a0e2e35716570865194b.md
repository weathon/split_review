## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes to improve neural network decision boundaries by combining a sample’s own prediction with a weighted average of the predictions of its k-nearest neighbors in the latent space. The method is evaluated on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 using ResNet-10 and VGG13, reporting small gains in accuracy, robustness to label noise and adversarial attacks, and limited interpretability.

### Strengths
- **Simple and general method**: can be incorporated into any layered neural network for classification without modifying architecture, training procedure, or dataset (R2; R1: “makes sense”, R3: “relatively inexpensive algorithm with clear goals”).
- **Nice idea**: stabilizing predictions via k-nearest neighbors in a learned feature space where samples of one class are already close (R2).
- **Broad evaluation**: assessed on four criteria (accuracy, label noise, adversarial robustness, interpretability) (R2; R3: “broad and practical view”).
- **Potential usefulness**: interesting for the community (R2; R3: “approach interesting … potential to become a useful contribution”).
- **Minor performance gains noticeable**: small but observable improvements over baselines (R1).

*Disagreement*: R2 judged the paper well-written and well-structured; R1 and R3 found the writing poor, figures hard to comprehend, and the organization weak.

### Weaknesses
- **Poor writing and presentation** (R1): Figs. 2, 3, 5 difficult to understand; Algorithm 1 messy; (R3) Section 2 sentence ambiguous (“sample to predict itself”), typos and grammatical errors throughout.
- **Weak/outdated baselines**: models used (ResNet-10, VGG13) on CIFAR-10/100 produce low accuracies (CIFAR-10: ~85%, CIFAR-100: ~58%) compared to standard pretrained models (ResNet-18: ~95.5%, Wide ResNet: ~81.1%) (R1, R3). Gains are thin (<1% on CIFAR-10, 1–2% on CIFAR-100, Table 6) and may be easier to obtain from poor baselines (R1). Authors do not explain why they used self-trained weak models instead of freely available high-accuracy pretrained ones (R3).
- **Insufficient or missing experiments**:
  - No comparison with state-of-the-art adversarial robustness methods (e.g., adversarial training) (R1). For adversarial detection, missing comparison with Moayeri & Feizi 2021 (self-supervised embedding space) (R3).
  - No comparison with misclassification detection literature (e.g., Zhu et al. 2023, Jaeger et al. 2022) (R3).
  - No comparison with other kNN-based methods (e.g., Papernot & McDaniel “Deep k-Nearest Neighbors”) or randomized smoothing (Cohen et al.) (R2).
  - Table 1 references outdated (mostly 2019–2020); missing references for adversarial robustness and accuracy (R3: possible typo).
  - Majority of experiments on CIFAR datasets; no ImageNet experiments (R3).
  - No significance tests to assess whether minor improvements are statistically meaningful (e.g., Brunner-Munzel test) (R2).
- **Breadth of claims not well-supported** (R1):
  - Interpretability claim weak: identifying nearest neighbors is local and does not reveal how the decision is made (R1). Comparing test to training samples is common; need clear examples of usefulness (R3).
  - Robustness claim unsupported by comparisons (R1).
- **Key motivation insufficiently justified**: The paper claims decision boundaries are suboptimal and lie in low-density regions, but provides no empirical evidence that boundaries suffer from overfitting due to few training samples (R2, R3). The discussion around Figure 4 seems hypothetical, not based on an actual model (R3). “Optimal decision boundary” is not clearly defined (R2).
- **Lack of analysis and motivation for design choices**:
  - Distance metric in latent space: no discussion of how choice (Euclidean vs. cosine) affects performance (R2).
  - Choice of latent layer arbitrary; not specified how layers were selected (e.g., based on test set performance?) nor how results vary across layers (R3).
  - Adversarial attack target “ground truth class + 1” is non-standard; common approach uses closest misclassification – justification needed (R2).
  - Table 5 hyperparameter tuning steps appear counterintuitive (R2).
  - Table 6 best results with k=1, undermining the averaging idea; claim of “sparse space” not convincingly supported (R2).
  - Claim about “nature of the latent space” unsupported; t-SNE/UMAP plots could provide evidence (R2).
  - Figure 2 measure may be incorrect if one class encapsulates another (R2).
  - Table 2 caption unclear which column corresponds to adversarial results (R2).
  - Computational cost not discussed (R2).
- **Missing related work**: No mention of Mixup (Zhang et al. 2018) which directly governs decision boundaries and influences robustness (R3). No connection to misclassification detection literature (R3). Idea of instance-based classification in feature space is not new (R1 cites Wang & Sabuncu 2022 as a more convincing related work).
- **Visualization and figure issues**:
  - Figures 6 and 7 lack axis labels, appear to go over page limit (R3).
  - Figure 4 discussed before Figure 2, placed two pages after its text citation (R3).
- **Minor issues** (R2): typographical errors (missing blanks, “samplesGoodfellow”, “nearest neighbors(NNs)”); Equation 2 missing punctuation; Section 3.3 “Figure 4” should be Table 4; abbreviation NN confusing (neural network vs. nearest neighbor); learning rate 0.11 seems high; calling 50k samples “small” is questionable. English proofreading needed (R2, R3).
