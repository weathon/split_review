## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces EGWB, a graph partitioning method based on a Gromov-Wasserstein (GW) barycenter problem over a single input graph. It relaxes the target structure and distribution constraints in Gromov-Wasserstein Learning (GWL) by jointly learning a diagonal target structure matrix and the cluster masses. The authors propose a block-coordinate descent algorithm with entropic regularization, prove convergence, and establish theoretical connections to Min-Cut, NMF, and Max-Dicut. Empirical results on synthetic and real data show improvements over existing GW-based methods. Reviewers are split: two (R1, R3) find the framework novel, theoretically well-founded, and empirically strong; two (R2, R4) identify significant omissions, incomplete analysis, and presentation issues.

### Strengths

- **Unifying framework**: Generalizes existing GW learning methods by introducing a GW barycenter problem; adding constraints recovers prior methods. Connections to Min-Cut, Non-negative Matrix Factorization, and Max-Dicut are explicitly derived (R1, R3, R4).
- **Algorithm with convergence guarantees**: Alternating minimization employs entropic regularization and successive upper-bound minimization. Theoretical convergence proofs are provided (R1, R3, R4).
- **Theoretical contributions**: First analysis of concavity for this learning problem; equivalence between Monge and Kantorovich formulations under appropriate conditions (R1, R4).
- **Empirical performance**: Outperforms state-of-the-art graph partitioning methods (GWL, SpecGWL, etc.) on synthetic and real-world directed and undirected graphs. Demonstrates robustness against edge noise (R1, R3).
- **Informative synthetic experiments**: The synthetic data example clearly illustrates the advantage of the barycentric formulation (R1).

### Weaknesses

- **Readability and presentation issues**: Notation confusion (MGW used both as mapping and objective; $\pi$ and $\Gamma$ interchanged; Fig.3 axes unlabeled). Typos (e.g., page 1: $G_1(D_1,P_1)$ error; page 2: grammatical mistake in limitations; page 6: EGWB abbreviation not defined). Terminology between “graph partitioning” and “graph clustering” is used inconsistently; the experiments appear to be partitioning rather than clustering (R2, R3).
- **Omission of closely related work**: The complete semi-relaxed GW (srGW) barycenter problem [Vincent-Cuaz et al., 2022] is not compared. That work studies spectrum-preservation for graph coarsening and provides theoretical properties relevant for partitioning. The proposed diagonal barycenter should be rigorously contrasted theoretically and empirically. The srGW solver used is essentially the mirror-descent algorithm from that same paper with added concave regularization—this relation should be clearly stated (R4).
- **Initialization dependency**: The method relies on initial transport plans from GWL and SpecGWL. It is unclear whether a less informed start (e.g., uniform distribution or k-means based initialization) yields comparable results. No systematic comparison of initialization strategies is provided (R3, R4).
- **Missing ablation and sensitivity studies**: The strongly concave regularization and continuation scheme introduce several hyperparameters. An ablation study over these parameters is not conducted. Figure 11 shows sensitivity to these hyperparameters, but the dataset used is not specified. Benchmarking of running times is absent (R4).
- **Incomplete concavity analysis**: Figure 1 experiments lack details (e.g., what $D’$ and initial transport plan were used, what solver was employed; whether findings are specific to heat kernels or extend to other PSD matrices like Laplacians). The theoretical justification for extremal points being solutions is incomplete: it only shows that for optimal target masses $\nu^*$, the GW problem is concave, but does not prove that extremities of the semi-relaxed coupling set (hard-clustering matrices) are solutions. The applicability of the second condition (Equation 19) is not discussed (R4).
- **Convergence proof concerns**: Theorem 3 does not clearly relate to the algorithm implemented in practice with entropic regularization; it is unclear when convergence applies. Lemma 2 appears incomplete (differentiability at borders, continuity arguments without topology), though later acknowledged as correct with a need for clearer proof overview. More generic convergence proofs for two-block BCD already exist [D] and could be cited (R1, R4).
- **Unclear choices and robustness**: How to set $K$ (number of clusters) is not addressed. Robustness when $K$ exceeds the true number of clusters is not tested; theoretically, zero masses could allow detection, but this is not discussed or empirically verified. No zero-mass analysis or comparison with the flexibility of [Vincent-Cuaz et al., 2022] is provided (R2, R3, R4).
- **Section 3.3 claims not justified**: The statement that enforcing a srGW target to identity “results in each cluster containing an equal number of data points” is not clearly supported. The claimed connection to NMF may actually correspond to the complete barycenter rather than the diagonal one (R4).
- **Dataset details missing**: It is not reported whether datasets in Section 4.2 are symmetric or asymmetric, nor whether directed graphs are symmetrized. Only AMI is reported in the main text; the other four metrics are only in the appendix (R2).
- **Synthetic data inconsistency**: In Figure 4, GWL and SpecGWL perform significantly better than in Figure 2; in Figure 4, GWL outperforms SpecGWL given the true cluster size distribution. The reasons for these differences and for why EGWB succeeds where others fail are not explained (R3).
- **Computational cost not evaluated**: The average number of iterations for convergence and total runtime (including initialization from GWL/SpecGWL) are not reported (R3).
