## Merged Review

### Summary
The reviews are mixed (scores 5, 6, 6, 8). The paper proposes a two-stage optimization-based camera-only adversarial attack on camera-LiDAR fusion models for 3D object detection. Reviewers acknowledge the importance of the problem and the large performance drop achieved. However, significant concerns are raised about the threat model, limited applicability to decision-level fusion, insufficient comparison with existing attacks and single-stage methods, feasibility of large adversarial patches, weak real-world validation, writing clarity, and lack of motivation for image-specific challenges.

### Strengths
- The paper studies an important and interesting concern: vulnerability of multi-sensor fusion to adversarial attacks in autonomous driving (R1, R4).
- The proposed method can dramatically deteriorate system performance by only attacking a single modality (R4): mAP drop from 0.824 to 0.353, detection score from 0.728 to 0.156 (Table/Figure evidence).
- The approach is evaluated on multiple feature-level fusion models as well as a camera-only model, and includes both simulated and real-world experiments (R1, R3). A demo code is provided for clarity (R3).
- Reviewer 2 emphasized the sophisticated synthesis of camera-LiDAR fusion models and the rigorous experimental validation in diverse environments (though this is contrasted by other reviewers who found the real-world validation weak).
- Reviewer 2 also praised the PointAug method for generating realistic-looking adversarial examples (note: this method does not appear in the paper; it is a potential misunderstanding by the reviewer).
- Reviewer 3 found the overall method makes sense and the motivation (attacking from camera input as more practical) reasonable.
- Reviewer 4 found the paper well-written and the topic interesting.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited applicability to decision-level fusion and some fusion paradigms**: The proposed attack is shown to be ineffective on decision-level fusion models (e.g., Baidu Apollo), which are widely used and tend to rely more on LiDAR. Even for data-level fusion, failure in the image modality may not completely compromise the system if LiDAR data remains unaffected (R1, R2, R3). This raises concerns about universality.
- **Lack of threat model and attacker capability analysis**: The paper does not specify the threat model, what information is available to the attacker, how feasible it is to access that information in a real-world setting, or the attacker's capabilities (R1, R2). This omission undermines the security analysis (R2).
- **Insufficient comparison with existing attacks and single-stage methods**: The paper does not quantitatively compare its two-stage approach against other single-modal attacks on fusion models (e.g., [1,2] that attack all three fusion types) or against a single-stage optimization. Without such comparisons, it is hard to justify the effectiveness or advantage of the technical contributions (R1, R4). Reviewer 4 specifically noted that only showing performance drop is not enough; a benchmark against SOTA single-stage attacks is needed.
- **Feasibility and practicality of the adversarial patch**: The patch size required is large (minimum 1m x 1m, Table 7), making it impractical to place on a pedestrian or vehicle (may hide license plate, illegal). The paper does not discuss how to deploy such a patch in the real world (R1, R4). Furthermore, the assumption that the patch does not influence LiDAR data (intensity, extra points) is not justified. The projection of the patch onto the 3D scene to ensure physical plausibility and avoid unrealistic artifacts is not addressed (R4). The patch may also be placed in undeployable areas (e.g., sky) if not constrained properly (R4).
- **Weak real-world evaluation and ignored environmental factors**: Real-world experiments are limited. The paper does not evaluate the impact of varying light conditions, distance between camera and patch, view angle, or weather. The patch uses a special color pattern that may be affected by these factors (R1). This contrasts with Reviewer 2’s assessment of “rigorous” validation; the majority found it insufficient.
- **Impact on vehicle motion/control unclear**: It is not shown whether the perception system is consistently deceived or if the vehicle’s trajectory is affected by the attack (R1).
- **Data dependency and generalizability**: Experiments are based on a limited dataset (likely one driving dataset). Concerns about biases introduced and lack of evaluation on different sensor configurations, environmental conditions, or object types (R2). No ablation or sensitivity analysis for single-modal vs. multi-modal methods was provided (R4, question).
- **Writing clarity and method motivation**: The method description on pages 4-5 is unclear, with long paragraphs and insufficiently explained notations (e.g., proj_x in Eq. 6, mask sharing, patch sharing). The paper does not elaborate on the specific challenges of image-only attacks in autonomous driving scenes (e.g., lighting, weather, dynamic objects) (R3). Reviewer 3 also asked: Is the noise patch shared by the whole dataset (seen changing in object-level but not scene-level)? Is the mask in Sensitivity Distribution Recognition shared by the whole dataset? What is the form of proj_x in Eq. 6 and why is it necessary?
- **Computational overhead and resource constraints**: The paper does not discuss the computational cost or hardware requirements for generating adversarial patches, especially for real-time deployment (R2).
