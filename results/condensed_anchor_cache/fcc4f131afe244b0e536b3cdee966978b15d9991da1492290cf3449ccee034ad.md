## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes iGraphMix, a novel Input Mixup method for node classification that addresses irregularity and alignment issues by generating virtual nodes and edges through interpolation of features, labels, and sampled neighboring nodes. The authors provide theoretical analysis showing improved generalization and experimental validation on several benchmark datasets.

### Strengths
- **Novel approach to graph augmentation**: iGraphMix is a simple yet effective method specifically designed for node classification, building on Input Mixup while handling graph-specific challenges (irregularity and alignment). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5)
- **Theoretical analysis**: The paper provides mathematical proof that training GNNs with iGraphMix reduces the generalization gap, offering insights into the regularization effect. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5)
- **Extensive experimental evaluation**: The method is evaluated on multiple real-world datasets (e.g., Citeseer) and outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of micro-F1 score. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5; but Reviewer 4 notes marginal improvement – see Weaknesses)
- **Good organization and clarity**: The paper is well-structured, with clear explanations of the method, theoretical results, and experimental setup. (Reviewers 1, 2, 5; Reviewer 3 notes presentation could be improved)
- **Versatility and usability**: iGraphMix can be easily applied to various GNN architectures and combined with other augmentation methods. (Reviewers 1, 2, 5; but Reviewer 4 questions the versatility claim)
- **Comprehensive comparisons**: The method is compared against multiple baselines including M-mixup and other graph augmentation methods. (Reviewers 2, 3; but Reviewers 3 and 4 note baseline limitations – see Weaknesses)
- **Strong positive assessment from one reviewer (score 8)**: One reviewer is absolutely certain and finds the method well-motivated, thoroughly evaluated, and with significant contributions in originality, quality, clarity, and significance. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited baseline comparisons and missing robustness evaluation**: The current baselines are quite simple and limited; more advanced graph augmentation methods (e.g., GraphMix [Verma et al., AAAI 2021], G-mixup [Han et al., ICML 2022], Graph Mixup with Soft Alignments [Ling et al., 2023]) are not included. (Reviewers 3, 4) Furthermore, mixup methods are typically evaluated on robustness to label/feature/structure noise, but such experiments are missing. (Reviewer 3)
- **Marginal and inconsistent improvement**: The performance gain of iGraphMix over the second-best method is always less than 1%. The improvement is not consistent across all datasets, raising questions about practical significance given the added complexity. Experiments on more challenging datasets are suggested. (Reviewer 4; also implied by Reviewer 3's call for stronger baselines)
- **Lack of ablation study**: The paper does not ablate the three components (input features, edge structure/adjacency, labels) to understand their individual contributions. A systematic ablation is needed. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Missing hyperparameter and sensitivity analysis**: The impact of key hyperparameters (e.g., number of virtual nodes, mixing coefficient λ) is not analyzed. It is unclear how performance changes when λ approaches 0 or 1, or how λ interacts with the number of virtual nodes. (Reviewer 2)
- **Unclear theoretical steps and missing intuitive explanation**: (a) Eq.(4): It is unclear how adjacency matrices A and A' (with their permuted counterparts) can be directly added, and whether the masking matrix M is symmetric. (Reviewer 3) (b) In Appendix B, the equality \(AX = AX' = A\tilde{X}\) is not well justified; a rough proof idea before details would help. (Reviewer 4) (c) The calculation of the generalization gap in Sec. 6.2 is unclear; the test loss of iGraphMix is reported much higher than "no augmentation", which contradicts the claimed smoothing effect and needs justification. (Reviewer 4)
- **Computational complexity not analyzed**: No theoretical or empirical analysis of time and space complexity compared to other augmentation methods, nor how the method scales with graph size. (Reviewers 1, 2, 5) A diagram showing time cost comparison would be helpful. (Reviewer 1)
- **Limited discussion of limitations and failure cases**: The paper does not discuss when iGraphMix might be ineffective, e.g., on graphs with certain characteristics (high heterophily, different sparsity or degree distributions) or when over-smoothing occurs. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing details on experimental setup**: More specifics on datasets, GNN models, and performance metrics are needed. (Reviewer 5) Additionally, why only Micro-F1 score is reported (and not Accuracy) is questionable. (Reviewer 4)
- **Concerns about training procedure**: Does iGraphMix train GNNs exclusively on virtual nodes (no original nodes), similar to standard Mixup? This is not clearly explained. (Reviewer 4)
- **Versatility claim not substantiated**: The claim that iGraphMix is versatile and can be combined with other augmentation methods requires further explanation and examples. (Reviewers 4, 5)
- **Missing reference**: The statement "Citeseer dataset contains only 1.71% connected edges of labeled nodes out of all edges" lacks a reference. (Reviewer 1)
- **Minor presentation issues**: (a) Eq.(6) notation \(\tilde{Z}_{v,v'}, \tilde{Y}_{v,v'}\) is misleading as subscripts are used for both rows and columns. (b) "matrix" should be "matrices" in line below Eq.(1). (c) More generally, the presentation could be further improved for clarity. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing discussion of related graph mixup for graph classification**: The paper would benefit from a more thorough discussion of existing graph mixup methods for graph classification to better place iGraphMix in the literature. (Reviewer 4)
- **Potential extension beyond node classification**: No discussion of applicability to other tasks like link prediction or graph classification. (Reviewer 2)
