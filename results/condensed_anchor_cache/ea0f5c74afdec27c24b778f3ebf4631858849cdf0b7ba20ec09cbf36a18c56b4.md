## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents FACTBENCH, a dynamic benchmark of 985 hallucination-inducing prompts across 150–213 topics for evaluating LM factuality in real-world user interactions. The authors also introduce VERIFY, a pipeline that classifies LM responses as supported, unsupported, or undecidable based on retrieved web evidence and a verdicting LM. VERIFY is shown to achieve higher correlation with human judgments than existing methods. The authors release human-annotated factuality data on 5,519 content units. Using VERIFY, they evaluate several GPT, Gemini, and Llama3.1 models and find that proprietary models perform better, Llama3.1-405B underperforms 70B due to higher undecidable rates, and Gemini1.5-Pro has a high refusal rate (25% over-refusal).

### Strengths
- FACTBENCH is a novel dynamic benchmark that addresses limitations of static benchmarks by capturing evolving factuality challenges in real-world LM interactions.
- VERIFY's three-way categorization (supported, unsupported, undecidable) provides a more nuanced and rigorous factuality assessment than previous work, with experimental results showing the highest alignment with human judgments.
- The authors release human-annotated factuality data on 5,519 content units, a valuable resource for future research.
- The benchmark covers multiple topics with three difficulty tiers (Easy, Medium, Hard), enabling finer-grained evaluation.
- The categorization into context-independent vs. context-dependent statements facilitates more detailed hallucination detection analysis.
- A weighting factor in the hallucination score accounts for both unsupported and undecidable units, adding robustness.
- The authors carefully selected datasets and established comprehensive baselines for fair and robust comparisons across methods.

### Weaknesses
- The usefulness evaluation criteria and scoring process for prompt selection are not well-justified; more details are needed on how criteria were determined and how scores from two LMs were combined.
- VERIFY relies on a single LM (Llama3-70B-Instruct) for unit extraction, labeling, and decontextualization; the potential bias from using one model is not adequately addressed.
- FACTBENCH evaluation is limited to a small set of LMs; a more comprehensive evaluation across a wider range of models would strengthen claims.
- The weighting factor α in the Hallucination Score is set to 0.5 without sensitivity analysis or ablation study to justify this choice or assess its impact on correlation with human preferences.
- Only three annotators were hired, with relatively low inter-annotator agreement (Cohen’s Kappa 0.52, 0.55), raising concerns about annotation reliability and robustness. The difference in factuality labeling between VERIFY and Factcheck-GPT is minor (≤0.01 for Factual labels in Table 2), and VERIFY shows noticeably lower performance in Tables 1 and 3, further suggesting reliability issues.
- While FACTBENCH is described as dynamic, the paper lacks specifics on the update process: conditions for adding or removing prompts, update frequency, and criteria for integrating new prompts. The choice of BERTopic over general clustering methods and how its parameters were tuned (which could affect benchmark content) is not explained.
- The experimental design risks circularity: VERIFY is used both to classify data and then to evaluate factuality.
- VERIFY’s methodology lacks innovation in recall-oriented hallucination detection, which is critical in high-stakes domains (e.g., medical, encyclopedic) where minimizing hallucinations is essential.
- Difficulty ratings for prompts are based solely on scores from multiple large models; this classification method is unconvincing and needs a broader, more comprehensive approach.
- Building FACTBENCH involves multiple steps, each relying on relatively simple, heuristic approaches, which may limit the benchmark’s novelty and robustness in capturing nuanced factuality challenges.
- There is a lack of qualitative analysis for ambiguous samples or Tier 1 (Hard) prompts, leaving gaps in interpreting how VERIFY handles especially difficult cases.
- VERIFY shows limited interpretability for complex or context-dependent responses; the paper does not discuss how its outputs could provide actionable feedback for model improvement or how it handles interdependent factual claims.
- The high refusal rate in Gemini1.5-Pro, especially in the Hard tier, is mentioned briefly; more details are needed on refusal categories, their distribution across prompts/tiers, and whether misclassified or unjustified refusals occurred. It is also unclear whether the Hallucination Score as defined (Eq. 1) is meaningful—this should be verified.
- A typo appears in the text: line 305 references Table 2 when it should be Figure 2. (Presentation issue.)
