## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces an updated recommendation guide for multivariate time series generation (MTSG), addressing gaps in prior guidance (lack of diffusion-based methods, limited exploration of channel-independent frameworks). It proposes an enhanced central discriminator (CCD) that uses frequency-domain transformations, TimesNet, and attention mechanisms to improve GAN-based models, especially on small-sample datasets. Multiple reviewers raise concerns about the clarity of the paper’s core contribution, the scope of experiments, missing baselines, and presentation quality, while also noting that CCD achieves gains on small data and that the recommendations are practically useful.

### Strengths
- **Comprehensive coverage and practical recommendations**: The paper provides a wide-ranging empirical analysis and recommends MTSG method selection based on dataset size and computational resources. It prioritizes both channel-independent and diffusion-based frameworks, giving users a more complete guide for choosing generation methods. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- **CCD innovation and performance gains**: The enhanced central discriminator (CCD) introduces frequency-domain transformations and a central discriminator to capture inter-channel dependencies. It demonstrates clear performance gains on small-sample scenarios, making GAN-based models comparable to VAE-based models (which dominate that setting) while offering better efficiency than diffusion models. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Methodological clarity and organization**: The paper is well-organized, with each module clearly explained and the overall methodology easy to understand. The language is generally clear and fluent. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Detailed experimental reporting (excluding hyperparameters)**: Experiments are comprehensive in detail and the results are thoroughly presented. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Unclear main contribution and weak motivation**: The paper’s central contribution is unclear—it appears to simultaneously propose a new method (CCD) and a recommendation guide, but the CCD is a marginally novel improvement on GANs (reshaping time series to 2D and applying 2D convolutions, a common technique). The analysis shows CCD still defeated by VAE on small-scale data and by diffusion when resources are sufficient. The motivation for addressing limitations with diffusion on small-sample datasets is theoretically weak because GANs typically outperform diffusion in that setting, yet CCDGAN does not significantly outperform Diffusion-TS, undermining the claimed rationale. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3) *Reviewer 3 explicitly questions whether the paper’s aim is to sell a new method or a recommendation.*
- **Insufficient dataset diversity and questionable generalizability**: Experiments are limited to stock, energy, and medical/EEG datasets—only two of the ten datasets used in the main benchmark TSGBench. This constrained set does not demonstrate the method’s claimed flexibility across diverse domains (e.g., air quality, OD flow, traffic forecasting, or other spatial-temporal forecasting scenarios). The credibility of TSGGuide’s generalizability suffers as a result. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- **Missing up-to-date baselines and comparisons**: The compared baselines are not current (latest from 2023); newer methods like TimeGAE and DLT are omitted. Moreover, despite the emphasis on a channel-independent framework, there are no comparisons with channel-mixed framework methods. This omission is especially problematic for financial datasets where inter-channel correlations are strong and channel-independent approaches may overlook inherent dependencies. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Lack of hyperparameter details and reproducibility**: Key hyperparameters for the proposed method and compared methods are not provided (e.g., the weighting factor gamma in Equation 6). The paper also does not specify how datasets are split into training, validation, and test sets. This makes reproducibility difficult and prevents assessment of the method’s robustness. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Presentation issues**: Some equations are not clearly presented (e.g., the notation in Eq. 4 is non-standard). The writing is confusing in places, making the contribution hard to discern—one reviewer spent several days trying to understand the core contribution. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **Well-known findings**: Some of the recommendations, such as the finding that diffusion models have large training and inference loads, are already well-known, reducing the novelty of the guidance. (Reviewer 3)
- **Insufficient exploration of method strengths/weaknesses across scenarios**: Section 5 explains CCD’s advantages on small-sample data mainly from a time series classification perspective but lacks discussion of other possible reasons. It also does not sufficiently explore the strengths and weaknesses of diffusion models versus CCD in different scenarios. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing detailed training time analysis**: The paper should provide a more granular analysis of training time—e.g., whether longer training for one method is due to more iterations or higher per-sample computational load. (Reviewer 3)
- **Unclear motivation for synthetic datasets**: In Section 5, three synthetic datasets are used, but the motivation for choosing those datasets is insufficiently justified. (Reviewer 4)

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* Reviewer 3 is more positive (score 6), highlighting the usefulness of the recommendations and the improvement in GAN performance, while Reviewer 2 is more negative (score 3), questioning technical novelty, baseline recency, and dataset diversity. The majority of reviewers (1, 2, 4) express reservations about the paper’s novelty and scope.
