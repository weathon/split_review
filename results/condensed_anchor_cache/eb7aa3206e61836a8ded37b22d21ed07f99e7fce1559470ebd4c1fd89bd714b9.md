## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Deep Temporal-Aligned Gradient Enhancement (DeepTAGE), a method to improve SNN optimization via two components: TAGE dynamically adjusts surrogate gradients based on membrane potential distributions at each time step, and STDS applies deep supervision across both network stages (spatial) and time steps (temporal). The method is validated on CIFAR10, CIFAR100, ImageNet-1k, and DVS-CIFAR10, achieving state-of-the-art results. The paper is well-written and organized.

### Strengths
- The approach is novel and well-motivated, addressing the observed phenomenon that membrane potential distributions shift away from the firing threshold as time progresses. TAGE adapts surrogate gradients to this distribution, and STDS further alleviates gradient vanishing.
- The writing is clear, making the paper easy to follow.
- Extensive empirical results on static (CIFAR10, CIFAR100, ImageNet-1k) and neuromorphic (DVS-CIFAR10) datasets demonstrate strong performance, with particularly notable gains on large-scale benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet ResNet-18 accuracy ~1% below ANN).
- One reviewer (score 8) specifically praised the comprehensive analysis and that the method achieves substantial accuracy improvements without increasing inference computational costs.
- Previous SNN surrogate gradient work focuses on layer-wise differences, while this paper addresses temporal adaptability across time steps.

### Weaknesses
- **TAGE analysis & effective gradients**: Multiple reviewers noted a lack of theoretical analysis of how TAGE influences SNN performance and firing rate (Reviewer 3), and how it ensures the provision of effective gradients (Reviewer 3). Another reviewer requested analysis of how STDS affects gradient flow, specifically whether gradients backpropagated through shallow layers increase significantly (Reviewer 4).  
- **Energy and firing rate concerns**: Several reviewers raised energy implications. One noted that in DeepTAGE the firing rate appears higher than in traditional methods, potentially increasing energy consumption, and asked how to balance energy and performance (Reviewers 2, 3). One reviewer (score 8) stated the method adds no inference computational cost, creating a contrast that needs clarification.  
- **STDS details and potential conflicts**:  
  – Reviewers requested details of the auxiliary classifier networks: number of layers, impact on overall performance, and discussion of extra training overhead (Reviewers 3, 4).  
  – One reviewer (score 5) questioned whether applying CE loss at each stage might limit shallow layers from learning elementary features and cause gradient conflicts across layers; the authors should provide additional insights (Reviewer 1).  
- **Generalization to other surrogate gradients**: Several reviewers asked whether TAGE can be applied to surrogate gradient functions other than the arctangent function (Reviewers 3, 4).  
- **Motivation and distribution visualization**:  
  – The motivation would be strengthened by showing membrane potential distributions over longer time steps (beyond 4) to emphasize the problem (Reviewer 1).  
  – Reviewers requested evidence that the distribution shift is mitigated after training with TAGE, i.e., show membrane potential distribution after applying TAGE (Reviewers 1, 2).  
- **Reproducibility and details**:  
  – For the ImageNet results (Table 2, ResNet-18), reviewer requested the relevant model parameters (Reviewer 3).  
  – The spiking ResNet-19 in Table 1 and Sew ResNet in Table 5 both report 81.39% accuracy; reviewer asked if this is a coincidence and what base model Sew ResNet uses (Reviewer 3).  
- **Clarification requested**: One reviewer asked to interpret the sentence on line 168: "Our approach is anticipated to not only stabilize the learning process but also to enhance the representational capacity of SNNs by improving their spike generation capability" (Reviewer 3).
