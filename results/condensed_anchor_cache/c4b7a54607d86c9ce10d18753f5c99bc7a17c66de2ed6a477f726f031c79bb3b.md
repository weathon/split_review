## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes FedPFT, a personalized federated learning (PFL) method that addresses feature-classifier mismatch by using client-specific prompts fed into a shared self-attention module. The module transforms local features to align with the global classifier. Experiments on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny ImageNet show gains over several PFL baselines. All reviewers acknowledge the importance of the feature mismatch problem and that the method yields positive empirical results. However, the majority raise significant concerns about inaccurate framing of PFL, insufficient novelty given prior work, missing essential baselines, weak connection between motivation and method, and presentation issues. One reviewer (score 8) found the observation and prompt module promising but still cited critical missing experiments.

### Strengths

- The problem of feature-classifier mismatch in FL is an important and intuitive challenge.
- The proposed prompt-based method is simple yet effective, evidenced by consistent accuracy gains across experiments.
- Code is provided, aiding reproducibility.
- The ablation study supports the claim that the prompt module helps compute better separable features.
- The illustrative figures (Fig 1, Fig 2, Fig 6 in appendix) make the paper fairly easy to follow.
- To the reviewers’ knowledge, no prior FL work has used prompts in this manner – the approach is novel in its methodological perspective within FL.

### Weaknesses

- **Inaccurate framing of PFL and GFL.** The abstract/intro incorrectly suggest that PFL emerged simply because GFL performs poorly under data heterogeneity. PFL and GFL are distinct fields with parallel objectives (personalization vs. generalization). Refer to FedRoD for a proper understanding.
- **Novelty of the key observation is not new.** The finding that FedAvg’s feature extractor outperforms many PFL methods after linear probing was already established in FedBABU (ICLR 2022). The paper overclaims novelty. It should add a FedBABU row in Table 1 and temper its claims.
- **Missing baseline comparisons.** Several essential baselines are absent:
  - FedBABU, FedAvg-FT, FedBABU-FT (fine-tuned versions).
  - Methods that address feature-classifier mismatch directly: CCVR, FedETF, FedFA (FedFA uses feature anchors; FedETF uses a projection layer similar to FTM). None are compared.
  - Recent PFL works such as GPFL, FedALA.
  - Parameter-efficient finetuning categories: prompt finetuning (SGPT, FedOTP, FedAPT, pFedPT), adaptor-based (FedAvg+Linear in Appendix F), and finetuning partial layers (FedPer, FedBN). The paper should either compare to these or justify why not.
  - A train-then-personalize baseline: finetune the classification layer after FL training ends (not just at each round).
  - A personalized classifier regularized via a proximal term (e.g., Ditto).
  - A local adaptor with the same number of additional parameters as the prompt (to isolate parameter vs. design advantage).
- **Insufficient related work.** The related work section lacks:
  - A GFL subsection covering feature enhancement and classifier freezing (FedBABU, FedDecorr, FedFN, SphereFed, FedETF, FedDr+).
  - A PFL subsection covering parameter decoupling and the two-step freeze-then-finetune approach.
  - Correct citation format: most citations should use `\citep` instead of `\citet` per ICLR style.
- **Weak connection between motivation and method.** The paper does not convincingly explain why the proposed prompt-based module is *the* solution to the mismatch problem, nor why all its components are necessary. A synthetic dataset that reproduces the mismatch and shows FedPFT alleviating it (compared to other baselines) would strengthen the argument.
- **Presentation issues.**
  - The term “prompt” is not clearly defined in this FL context; background is needed for self-completeness.
  - Claims like “enhances the synergy between the feature extractor and classifier” are used repeatedly without precise definition.
  - Figure 1: both (a) and (b) show 100% accuracy, so it does not convey accuracy advantage; it should highlight inter-class separability instead. The figure also underemphasizes the prompt novelty.
  - Table 1: the difference between probe and match accuracy is minimal, diminishing the purpose of the matching approach. A fine-tuning row (FedBABU-style) should be added.
  - The methodology section is verbose/redundant: the algorithm is written twice (before Sec 3.2 and in Algorithm 1).
  - The contrastive learning part (Collaborative Contrastive Learning) is not explained in the main text; it is only briefly covered in the appendix, where the explanation remains unclear. It also distracts from the main message. If kept, a detailed description and citation of MOON (Model-Contrastive FL) are needed.
  - t-SNE visualizations occupy significant space but are not conclusive; they should be moved to appendix and compared with competitive baselines (FedRoD, pFedSD).
  - Appendix P (convergence analysis) is generic; no FedPFT-specific parameter appears, so it does not connect to the motivating problem. The remark incorrectly treats R (from Peter-Paul inequality) as problem-specific.
- **Experimental limitations.**
  - Only 40 clients are used; scalability to 500 or 1000 clients is not tested.
  - Participation rate is not considered: if a client does not participate every round, its prompt becomes stale, potentially affecting feature alignment training.
  - No train/test accuracy convergence curves across FL rounds are provided; overfitting is not demonstrated explicitly.
  - The code repository lacks implementation/scripts for the other personalized baselines compared in the paper.
- **Questions that overlap with criticisms or missing experiments (to be addressed):**
  1. **Dimension of h_k (L230):** It appears to be (1 + n_k)×m×C, making it much larger than the original classifier dimension (m×C). Is this correct?
  2. **Number of prompts n_k:** Is this a hyperparameter per client? How should it be chosen?
  3. **Two-step local update (L230):** Using separate local epochs R_f and R_a for feature transformation and classifier adaptation reduces efficiency compared to a single R. Provide guidelines on allocating R_f and R_a given a total R.
  4. **Dot-regression vs. prompts:** The goal appears to be increasing inter-class diversity. Dot-regression (FedDr+, based on Neural Collapse) also aims to do this. Does the prompt-based approach offer a clear advantage?
  5. **Alternative prompt usage:** Have the authors tried conditioning all layers or only batch norm layers with prompts? Reporting negative results would benefit the community.
  6. **Ditto-style baseline:** A personalized classifier regularized toward FedAvg’s classifier is a natural baseline; why is it considered unnecessary?
  7. **Training behavior:** How does the prompt-based module affect gradient communication and convergence compared to simpler head-finetuning? No analysis of communication cost or convergence speed is provided.
