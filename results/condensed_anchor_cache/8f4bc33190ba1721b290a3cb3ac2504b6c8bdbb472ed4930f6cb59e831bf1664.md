## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Score Forgetting Distillation (SFD), a data-free method for machine unlearning in diffusion models. SFD aligns the conditional scores of “unsafe” classes/concepts (e.g., object classes, celebrity identities, nudity) with those of “safe” ones, using a score-based MU loss built into the score distillation objective, enabling one-step generation without real data. Experiments on class-conditional (CIFAR-10, STL-10) and text-to-image (Stable Diffusion) models are presented.

### Strengths
- **Novelty**: SFD is the first work to approach class and concept unlearning in diffusion models from the score distillation perspective (Reviewer 1). The combination of score distillation and machine unlearning, applied to both class-conditional and text-to-image models, is creative (Reviewer 3). Using distilled DMs for MU is a novel direction (Reviewer 4).
- **Clarity of presentation**: The paper effectively explains complex concepts and is well-organized, with informative figures, tables, and algorithm pseudo-code (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Thorough empirical evaluation**: Comprehensive experiments covering multiple unlearning tasks (object classes, celebrity identities, nudity) and datasets (CIFAR-10, STL-10, Stable Diffusion) with quantitative metrics (Unlearning Accuracy, FID, IS, Precision, Recall) and qualitative results (Reviewers 1, 3). Ablation studies are included (Reviewer 3).
- **Strong theoretical foundation** (Reviewer 2).
- **Application significance**: Machine unlearning in diffusion models is crucial for safety and trustworthiness of generative AI; a data-free method that accelerates both unlearning and sampling contributes to practical security (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing related work section**: The paper lacks a dedicated related work section to highlight differences among existing methods, making it harder to identify the paper’s unique contributions (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Lack of robustness evaluation against adversarial prompts**: No evaluation against adversarial text prompts (e.g., UnlearnDiffAtk) that can circumvent unlearning, a common tool for evaluating unlearned T2I models (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Missing runtime/efficiency analysis**:
    - Overall running time of the unlearning procedure compared to other methods is not provided (Reviewers 1, 3). Without this, it is unclear if the method is practical for large-scale or hot-update scenarios (Reviewer 3).
    - Generation time comparison is missing, even though the one-step generator is a claimed advantage (Reviewer 2).
    - Computational cost of pretraining a score function is not addressed (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 3 questions: if training time is excessive, why not filter harmful data and retrain from scratch?
- **Weak or missing baselines and comparisons**:
    - Stronger state-of-the-art unlearning methods such as AdvUnlearn are not considered (Reviewer 2).
    - No comparison with unlearning applied to other distilled/one-step models (e.g., SiD/DMD distilled models, consistency models) or with reduced-step samplers (e.g., 50-step DDIM, 10-step DPM-Solver++); such ablations would clarify the specific advantage of integrating unlearning into score distillation (Reviewer 3).
    - Reviewer 4 asks for justification of choosing Score Identity Distillation (SiD) over Diff-Instruct or DMD; could these be integrated into SFD? The source of baseline results (e.g., from original papers) is unclear, and comparability of Table 1 settings with prior work (e.g., SalUn) is not explained.
- **Experimental inconsistencies**: Table 1 compares with ESD, SA, SalUn, but these baselines are missing from Tables 2 and 3 without explanation (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing evaluation on standard MU scenarios**: Style forgetting and random data forgetting are not considered (Reviewer 4).
- **Sequential unlearning not evaluated**: The method has only been applied to models that have not undergone prior distillation; it is unclear if SFD works effectively for sequential forgetting of multiple classes (e.g., airplane, bird, car, cat, deer from STL-10) and whether it outperforms other unlearning methods in such scenarios (Reviewer 3).
- **Perceived incremental contribution**: The method builds on recent score distillation work (SiD) and machine unlearning; the primary novelty (integration of MU into distillation) could be viewed as incremental (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 4 similarly notes that the contribution is not clearly separated from prior work.
- **Missing alternative comparison**: Forgetting first, then distilling, is an alternative approach to achieve similar efficiency; the authors do not discuss or compare this (Reviewer 4).
- **Minority reviewer contrast**: Two reviewers (Rating 8) emphasize novelty and thoroughness; two reviewers (Rating 5) find the contributions incremental and the empirical study less comprehensive, with specific weaknesses as listed above.
