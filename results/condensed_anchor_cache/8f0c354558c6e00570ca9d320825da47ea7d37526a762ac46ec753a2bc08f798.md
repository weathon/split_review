## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a multi-aspect knowledge distillation method that uses Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to improve image classification and object detection. The approach involves: (1) querying an LLM to generate multi-aspect questions about each class, (2) extracting logits from an MLLM for those questions, and (3) expanding the student model’s output dimensions to distill those logits via binary cross-entropy loss alongside the standard cross-entropy loss on class logits. Experiments are conducted on multiple fine-grained and coarse-grained classification datasets and extended to object detection. The method consistently improves baseline performance.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, with a clear and logical flow that makes the method easy to understand.
- The core idea is simple but appears effective, and the authors claimed novelty in distilling multi-aspect knowledge about abstract and complex concepts.
- Extensive experiments are conducted: six fine-grained datasets, two coarse-grained datasets, and an object detection task (Table 7). Figures and tables are well-organized.
- Ablation studies and visualizations (e.g., t-SNE embeddings, multi-aspect response visualization) are comprehensive and insightful (Reviewer 3 noted this as a strength; Reviewer 1 also acknowledged the effort in knowledge transfer design).
- The method is combined with traditional network models like ResNet in a straightforward manner.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental scope and outdated baselines:** Experiments use only small-scale datasets (e.g., CUB-200, Stanford Dogs, etc.) and ResNet18/34 as the primary student model. No experiments on ViT-based models or larger datasets such as full ImageNet are provided. More complex models like ResNet101 should also be tested to assess scalability. (Raised by Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Missing comparisons to other knowledge distillation methods:** The only KD baseline compared is the basic KD (Hinton et al.). Many improved KD methods (e.g., [1,2] in reviewer comments) are omitted, both for classification and for object detection (e.g., SOTA detection KD methods). (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Insufficient novelty and principle explanation:** The method directly adopts the MLLM output logit for distillation without fully justifying why the MLLM improves performance. The teacher (MLLM) has many more parameters than the teacher in traditional KD; it is unclear whether gains come from the large parameter count or the inherent properties of the MLLM. No comparison is made to using only the large vision encoder of the MLLM. (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **MLLM hallucination and correctness concerns:** No validation or filtering step is applied to the multi-aspect questions generated by GPT-4o or to the responses from InternVL. Table 1 shows that MLLMs perform poorly in zero-shot classification on fine-grained datasets, raising doubts about whether the distilled multi-aspect logits provide correct, non-hallucinated information. The impact of hallucination on student performance is not analyzed. (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Unclear training/inference integration:** It is not clearly explained when the multi-aspect logits are extracted from the MLLM and how they are incorporated into the training or inference objective. (Reviewer 4). Additionally, missing training curve of the MaKD loss over iterations makes it hard to assess convergence. (Reviewer 1).
- **Object detection extension is weak:** Improvement in detection (Table 7) is very limited. No comparisons to strong detection baselines are provided. The method’s design for detection is unclear (e.g., whether input to MLLM is the whole image or a cropped object box). No experiments on larger detection datasets like LVIS. (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Random logits also improve performance (Table 3(b)):** Reviewer 3 notes that even with random logits, performance increases, which undermines the claimed mechanism and warrants explanation.
- **Incomplete computational cost analysis:** Section 5.5 discusses training time but does not account for the time required by the MLLM to annotate the training dataset (i.e., generate logits for all images). (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of overall evaluation of multi-aspect responses on test dataset:** While Fig. 4 and 5 show per-image responses, there is no dataset-level quantitative evaluation of the model’s multi-aspect response accuracy. (Reviewer 1).
- **Minority reviewer contrast:** Reviewer 1 and 2 are more critical of the technical novelty and incremental contribution (soundness=2, contribution=2), while Reviewer 3 is slightly more positive about the idea’s effectiveness and the comprehensive ablation studies (soundness=3, contribution=3). Reviewer 4 indicated they might reconsider their score based on responses to the hallucination and question-generation issues.
