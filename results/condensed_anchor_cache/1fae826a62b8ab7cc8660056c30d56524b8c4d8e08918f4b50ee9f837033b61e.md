## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a topology-aware loss function for segmentation of curvilinear structures (e.g., neuronal arbors, retinal vessels). The method extends the concept of simple points from digital topology to supervoxels (connected voxel sets). For false positive and false negative masks, it classifies connected components as “critical” if removing (for false positives) or keeping (for false negatives) them changes the number of connected components compared to ground truth. This identifies split and merge errors. A linear-time (O(n)) algorithm using BFS on graph structures is developed and theoretically proven, assuming the underlying graph is a tree. The loss function adds extra weight to such critical components. Experiments are performed on the EXASPIM2 (mouse brain, 3D) and DRIVE (retinal fundus, 2D) datasets, comparing against several topology-aware baselines. The reviewer community expresses mixed views: one reviewer rates the paper higher (score 6), citing good notation, clarity, and contributions; two reviewers rate it lower (scores 5), raising concerns about novelty, evaluation thoroughness, and clarity of algorithm details.

### Strengths
- Mathematical notation to communicate the methodology is clear and helpful (R1).
- Didactic images facilitate understanding (R1).
- Training details, including the continuation scheme for the loss weight, are explicitly mentioned (R1).
- The approach extends the topological concept of simple points to supervoxels and correctly identifies critical connected components that correspond to split/merge errors (R1, R3).
- The proposed algorithm achieves O(n) complexity using BFS and standard graph algorithms, which is much cheaper than existing topology-aware methods (typically O(n log n) or O(n²)) (R3).
- Adequate proofs of the runtime are provided in the supplementary (R3).
- The method is simple, effective after optimization, and can be easily integrated into existing segmentation frameworks (R1, R2).
- Verification on both 3D (EXASPIM2) and 2D (DRIVE) datasets demonstrates cross-dimensional applicability (R2).
- Visualizations show improved segmentation quality compared to baseline (R2).
- The algorithm’s design makes practical use tractable (R1, R3).

### Weaknesses
- Algorithm 2, a critical component, is very vague; the explanation under Corollary 3 does little to clarify its logic. The reliance on multiple nested conditional statements without a high-level explanation makes it difficult to grasp the core logic and practical implementation (R1).
- The O(n) runtime guarantee is explicitly stated only under the assumption that the graph is a tree. This restrictive assumption is not adequately highlighted in the theorem statement itself, and its implications for real-world data (which often deviates from tree structures) are not discussed (R1).
- The method is only sensitive to changes in the number of connected components; it does not address other topological errors such as changes in genus (e.g., creation/destruction of loops or handles). This limitation is not acknowledged (R1).
- The approach introduces hyperparameters α and β in the loss function, but no systematic guidance or ablation study is provided to show their impact on performance. The lack of analysis on hyperparameter sensitivity limits practical applicability (R1, R2, R3). (Reviewers ask: what are the values of α and β in the experiments?)
- Visual comparison with the baseline is missing in Figure 5, making it hard to qualitatively assess improvements (R2).
- Performance metrics are not described using formulas (R2).
- Missing comparison with clDice, a state-of-the-art topology-preserving loss function for tubular structures. Such a comparison is necessary to assess relative effectiveness (R2, R3).
- No evaluation is performed using topological metrics such as clDice, Betti Matching, or Betti Number, even though the paper claims to produce a topology-aware network (R3).
- No statistical significance test (e.g., t-test) is performed to determine whether improvements (numbers in bold) are statistically significant (R3).
- Limited novelty: extending simple points to superpixels/supervoxels is a straightforward conceptual step that does not introduce a fundamentally new theoretical framework or address an unconsidered challenge (R3).
- Several text clarity issues: definitions (e.g., missing subscript explanations for S, unclear why m and l differ), potential typos (Section 3.1.2 line 2: “false negative mask” should be “false positive mask”; “lemma” appears extra), and confusing interplay between Definition 1 and Section 3.3 (R1).
- Some reviewer questions overlap with criticisms: Why are positively critical components computed using S(ŷ ⊖ ŷ⁺) while Algorithm 2 only requires S(y ⊖ y⁻)? Conditions 1 and 2 are never formally defined (R1). Why does the segmentation become more slender compared to baseline in Figure 4, and can the loss function optimize edge sharpness? (R2).
