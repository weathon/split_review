## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Optimal Brain Apoptosis (OBA), a pruning method that computes the full Hessian-vector product for each parameter, building on Optimal Brain Damage (OBD). By decomposing the Hessian across layers and identifying non-zero inter-layer submatrices, OBA enables efficient second-order Taylor expansion for importance estimation. It is evaluated on VGG19, ResNet32, ResNet50, and ViT-B/16 on CIFAR10, CIFAR100, and ImageNet. Reviewers note that the Hessian computation is tractable and empirical results show promising pruning performance, but raise concerns about baseline comparisons, limited related work context, and the handling of the first-order term.

### Strengths

- OBA calculates the full Hessian-vector product rather than approximations, providing more accurate parameter importance estimation (Reviewer 1).
- Supports both structured pruning (neurons, channels, layers) and unstructured pruning (individual weights) (Reviewer 1).
- The method reduces computational complexity of Hessian-vector products; Reviewer 1 notes optimized calculation, and Reviewer 3 was surprised that pruning cost was not as high as expected given Hessian involvement.
- Solid theoretical foundation: authors derive Hessian structures for series and parallel connectivity with clear proofs (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- Empirical results are promising: consistent improvements in accuracy, parameter reduction, FLOPs reduction, and throughput across multiple backbones and datasets (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 notes “significant reductions in FLOPS for virtually unchanged performance.”
- Comprehensive evaluation includes pruning performance (accuracy, parameter reduction, FLOPs, throughput) and pruning cost (training and pruning time) (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Computational expense**: Full Hessian-vector product, even with optimizations, may still be expensive for larger and more complex networks (Reviewer 1). However, Reviewer 3 found pruning cost lower than expected, indicating a disagreement on the severity of this issue.
- **Practical impact**: Improvements in accuracy and speed are modest and not consistently significant across all experiments; gains are not transformative (Reviewer 2). This contrasts with the more positive assessments from other reviewers.
- **Outdated baselines**: The method is compared only to seven methods from 2016, 2017, and 2019. No comparisons to more recent pruning works (e.g., arxiv 2203.04248, 2208.11580, 2210.04092, 2112.00029) (Reviewer 3). This raises questions about fair evaluation.
- **Insufficient related work context**: The derived Hessian structures overlap with prior work (Wille 1997, Buntine & Weigend 1994, Wu et al. 2020, Singh et al. 2021) (Reviewer 4). The reduced-complexity Hessian-vector product is well-known (Pearlmutter 1994) (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 suggests moving related work into the main paper rather than an appendix.
- **First-order term omission**: The Taylor expansion used for pruning omits the first-order term, but networks are rarely trained to a perfect minimum. The justification for this omission is weak, introducing potential uncontrolled error (Reviewer 4, who also asks in L087 whether the first-order term is tracked). Reviewer 4 also notes that OBS (mentioned at L058-9) does not discard second-order information but approximates it with the Fisher information matrix, making the description inaccurate. The claim at L095 that the Gauss-Newton approximation is insufficient lacks supporting evidence (Reviewer 4).
- **Generalizability concerns**: The method may require additional work to extend to architectures like RNNs or State Space Models (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2 asks about extending to RNNs with backpropagation through time). Experiments are limited to a specific set of architectures (CNNs and ViT), leaving generalizability across tasks/domains unestablished (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 1 also notes that experiments feel outdated (datasets reminiscent of 7–8 years ago).
- **Minor LaTeX issue**: Subscripts (e.g., l_out) should be formatted as text (l_out) for readability (Reviewer 3). Variables a,b,c,d,e in Equation 3 are not defined (Reviewer 3).
