## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Mowst, a mixture-of-experts method for node classification that decouples node self-features (handled by a light-weight MLP "weak expert") from neighborhood structure (handled by an off-the-shelf GNN "strong expert"). A confidence mechanism based on the dispersion of the MLP's prediction logits determines when to activate the GNN (low confidence). A training loss encourages expert specialization and creates a soft splitting of the graph. The method is evaluated on 6 standard benchmarks (homophilous and heterophilous) with 4 backbone GNN architectures, achieving significant accuracy improvements at a computation cost comparable to a single GNN. A variant for directed graphs is also introduced. Theoretical analysis (propositions, theorems) is provided. The GitHub repository is at https://github.com/facebookresearch/mowst-gnn but is private and not accessible.

### Strengths
- The idea of mixing a weak expert (MLP) and a strong expert (GNN) with a confidence mechanism is novel and addresses a significant problem in GNNs: balancing self-features and neighborhood structures (R1, R2, R4, R5, R6).
- The proposed solution is intuitive and relatively simple (R4, R5). The technical details of the algorithm are well-explained (R5). The mathematical aspects are clear (R5).
- The experimental setup is comprehensive: benchmarks include both homophilous and heterophilous graphs, ablation studies, visualization of learned embeddings and training dynamics, and tests with 4 backbone GNN architectures (R1, R2, R4).
- The method is effective — it achieves strong accuracy improvements over existing methods (R3, R4). The proposed method consistently outperforms baselines (R1, R3).
- The paper provides theoretical support through propositions and theorems (R4, R6). Authors add theoretical analysis to show superiority of the proposed loss (R1).
- The approach handles model capacity and the ensemble/MoE strategy on graphs is an interesting contribution (R5).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and clarity are a major concern.** Multiple reviewers note the paper is not well-written (R1, R5, R6), while one reviewer finds it clear (R2). Specific issues:
  - The introduction lacks a clear statement of the limitations of existing GNNs that Mowst addresses; motivation for using MoE rather than simple ensemble is not sufficiently justified (R6).
  - Many claims are unclear or insufficiently supported: (a) “GNNs are both expensive to compute and hard to optimize” — yet many efficient GNNs exist; Mowst still uses a GNN (R1). (b) “similar neighbors” is ambiguous; the paper’s argument that aggregating similar neighbors may not be advantageous is not well-justified (R1). (c) The claim that the MLP helps “clean up” the dataset for the GNN is not explained — how does a weak classifier provide high-quality data? (R1). (d) The statement that the system is “biased and inherently favors the GNN’s prediction” is hard to follow (R1). (e) The difference between model-level and layer-level mixture, and why the former is easier to optimize and more explainable, is not clarified (R1). (f) The “soft splitting of the graph” is ambiguous — is it nodes or edges? (R1). (g) The reason why the weak expert is placed at lower order (MLP first) and strong expert at higher order is not justified; alternatives (e.g., compute confidence from GNN, learnable gating) are not compared (R2). (h) The claim that the MLP denoises for the GNN during training needs more explanation (R6). (i) The term “indistinguishable self-features” (Section 2.3) is not clearly defined (R6).
  - Notations are difficult to digest, inconsistent, and sometimes undefined (e.g., the symbol ∘ in Proposition 2.2 is not defined; confidence function definition is missing) (R1, R4, R6). "GCN" on page 6 is used without introduction (R6).
  - Propositions and theorems are presented informally (R6). The notion of ‘expressiveness’ in Propositions 2.5/2.6/2.7 is not mathematically defined; conditions and implications are unclear (R6). Several proofs are not referenced in the main text (R6).
  - The introduction section heavily relies on citations — about 40% of the introduction is references; similarly on page 6 — making it difficult to read (R5).
  - Minor grammatical/spelling errors (R4, R6): e.g., “the number graph convolution” (Section 1).
- **Algorithms and loss function require clarification.**
  - Algorithm 1 (inference) and Algorithm 2 (training) are ambiguous: there is no initialization step for expert parameters/hyperparameters; how Algorithm 1 can produce predictions when experts have not been trained is unclear (R5).
  - The loss functions (Eq.1 and Eq.3) appear problematic: all elements (confidence, predictions, true labels) are known in advance, so the loss function has a fixed value; it is unclear with respect to which parameters it is minimized (R5). The authors should clarify the optimization objective (R5).
  - The confidence mechanism uses a random value q (uniform), compared to the confidence score to decide expert selection. This resembles acceptance-rejection sampling; but the confidence is tied to predictions while q is independent — this may not be a precise selection method (R5).
  - In Algorithm 2, what methods are used to learn MLP weights and GNN weights? Convergence rates are not discussed (R6).
  - Why does the confidence function need to be quasiconvex? What happens if it is not? (R6)
- **Theoretical analysis has weaknesses.**
  - The theorems have weak connection to the learning objective; it is hard to follow conclusions; the analysis would benefit from clearer statements about convergence, stability of confidence, or guidelines for choosing the confidence function (R1, R6).
  - The variance and negative entropy functions (below Proposition 2.2) are not formally defined nor referenced (R6).
  - The claim in Proposition 2.5 (that L_Mowst upper-bounds L*_Mowst) is not conditioned on the choice of C (R6).
- **Missing comparisons and ablations limit empirical evaluation.**
  - Missing baselines for handling heterophily, such as H2GCN (R1).
  - Mowst*-GIN results are missing from Table 1; these would help assess whether Mowst enhances performance on top of GIN (the top model on Penn94) (R2).
  - The experimental comparison may be unfair: Mowst should be compared against a GNN with an MLP skip connection (both have similar expressiveness and number of parameters) to isolate the benefits of the proposed approach from simply adding parameters or skip connections (R2). A GNN+MLP sc baseline is missing (R2).
  - The paper does not study the effect of more than two experts (e.g., multiple weak or multiple strong experts), limiting the demonstration of scalability. Only the two-expert setup is tested (R4, R6). The supplement briefly mentions this but no results (R4).
  - To substantiate the claim that the success comes from mixing weak and strong experts (rather than just combining MLP and GNN), additional experiments comparing combinations of shallow/deep MLPs and shallow/deep GNNs should be performed (R3).
  - The introduction to each dataset (construction, node features) is insufficient (R3).
  - The correlation between edge density and the MLP’s contribution is not explored (R4).
  - A sensitivity analysis of computational complexity is missing; the cost of the confidence mechanism (even with a simple MLP) should be clearly explained (R5).
  - The GitHub code is private and not accessible (R4).
- **Failure cases and limitations are insufficiently discussed.**
  - The case where the MLP is both wrong and high-confidence (common with imbalanced classes or outliers) is not addressed. If both experts are biased in opposite directions, the confidence mechanism may incorrectly favor the GNN’s wrong prediction (R2). The paper does not discuss how Mowst handles or fails in these scenarios (R2).
  - The limitations of using MLP as an expert are not explored: if Algorithm 1 often generates small q, the MLP is selected in most cases, but what guarantees prediction quality? What are the advantages of MLP over other candidates? (R5)
  - The limitations of an off-the-shelf GNN as the strong expert are not discussed (R5).
  - The ability to scale the model capacity beyond two experts is limited; the paper does not address this (R6).
  - Main challenges of using Mowst are not identified (R6).
- **Missing details and minor issues.**
  - The paper mentions “node prediction” — does the task refer to node classification? (R4)
  - The confidence score solely depends on MLP’s output, creating a bias toward the GNN; the paper claims this bias is desirable but does not fully justify why (R1).
  - In Section 3 on symmetric gating functions, more relevant works should be cited (R6 gives references).
  - The sparse gating function is said to make optimization harder due to discontinuity, but some sparse gating functions (e.g., temperature softmax) are continuous (R6).
