## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LEAF (Learning to Extrapolate and Adjust for Forecasting), a two-stage meta-learning framework for online time series forecasting under concept drift. Drift is classified into macro-drift (stable, long-term) handled by an LSTM-based extrapolation module that tracks prediction model dynamics in latent space, and micro-drift (sudden, short-term) handled by an adjustment module using a meta-learnable surrogate loss. A warm-up offline phase provides initial parameters for online learning. Experiments on multiple datasets (including three newly released electric load datasets) show performance gains over baselines, and ablation studies are provided.

### Strengths
- The paper focuses on an important and practical problem: online time series forecasting under concept drift.  
- LEAF is a technically sound framework that combines several ideas (latent-space extrapolation, per-sample adjustment via surrogate loss, parameter generation from embeddings) in a unified manner.  
- The paper is well-written, well-organized, and clearly explains the identified drift types and how each module addresses them.  
- Extensive experiments are conducted, including comparisons with multiple baselines, ablation studies, and a case study, demonstrating empirical effectiveness.  
- The authors open-source three electric load benchmark datasets that exhibit diverse concept drifts, a valuable contribution for further research.  
- The framework is model-agnostic, intended to work with any deep prediction model (though the claim is questioned—see Weaknesses).  
- One reviewer (score 8, high confidence) found the approach particularly significant and praised the ablation and visualization results; three other reviewers rated 5–6 and one rated 3, indicating overall moderate but not uniform enthusiasm.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and related work**:  
  – The LSTM-based extrapolation for macro-drift is close to prior work (e.g., Deep State Space Models for Time Series Forecasting); the solution may be a strong baseline rather than a novel contribution. Some reviewers suggest more aggressive extrapolation approaches.  
  – The paper ignores related literature on surrogate gradient and on metric learning for data stream classification (e.g., Fischer et al. IJCNN 2016; Wang et al. IJCNN 2019) that address similar drift problems.  
  – Missing comparisons with important recent baselines: TimesNet (ICLR 2023) and Non-stationary Transformer (NeurIPS 2022). A simple retraining baseline (e.g., retrain model when 90% of new data arrives) is also omitted.

- **Experimental setup and fairness**:  
  – The naïve setting appears unfair: baselines are trained only on warm-up data, while LEAF uses both warm-up and meta-train data. A fairer comparison would train baselines on the combined warm-up + meta-train data.  
  – The key to beating baselines is the micro-drift adjustment. Without it (Table 1 vs. Table 2), LEAF does not outperform simpler methods, raising questions about the necessity of the complex extrapolation module.  
  – Missing evaluation on financial/futures datasets (e.g., NASDAQ 100) that exhibit more abrupt phenomena than the power datasets used (which have cyclical patterns).  
  – The MAPE metric is missing from the evaluation.  
  – Variance (e.g., standard deviation across five trials) is not reported, making it unclear if results are statistically significant.  
  – The effect of the forecast horizon is not analyzed; all experiments use horizon 24 without justification.

- **Efficiency and complexity**:  
  – No comparison of GPU memory, running time (training and inference), or space/time complexity. A breakdown of computational cost per component (queue management for macro-drift, surrogate loss calculation, parameter generation) is needed.  
  – Adjusting embeddings per sample may require loading model checkpoints per sample, which could be very inefficient.  
  – The paper combines many ideas; some reviewers suggest simplification rather than complication, and the cost–benefit of each component is unclear.

- **Clarity and missing details**:  
  – The computation of the surrogate loss \( L_{\text{surrogate}} \) is not clearly explained. The role of the loss network \( s \) and how the loss is computed require more detail.  
  – The architecture of the relation network \( R \) and embedding function \( g \) is underspecified (number of layers, activation functions, embedding dimensionality). Overfitting risk with an MLP-based relation network is not discussed.  
  – The model-agnostic claim is not substantiated: it is unclear which parameters are generated (e.g., only the final linear layer or all parameters) and how large the generated parameter size is for different models (especially large Transformers). Integration into diverse architectures is not demonstrated.  
  – Several notations and figures are confusing: what is STOPGRAD in Eq. (7); does \( H_p \) in Eq. (7) refer to the adjusted or base embedding; in Figure 3(b), why is an adjustment block placed before \( L_{\text{surrogate}} \)?  
  – The visual image resolution is too low, hindering readability.

- **Theoretical and ablation gaps**:  
  – No theoretical analysis of convergence, generalization bounds, or interactions between extrapolation and adjustment modules under different drift scenarios.  
  – Ablation studies need to isolate the individual contributions of the adjustment module and the extrapolation module more clearly (e.g., sensitivity to hyperparameters of the meta-learning process).  
  – A sanity check baseline—using the model trained in the previous period without any adaptation—is missing.  
  – It is not explored how or whether mechanisms from rehearsal-based methods (ER, DER++) could be incorporated into LEAF.

- **Additional reviewer questions** (overlap with above criticisms):  
  – What dataset is shown in Figure 1a?  
  – How do external features or time-related features affect the meta-learning process?  
  – For the reproducible reproducibility, detailed MLP architecture (layers, activation, dimensions) is needed.  
  – Is it possible to incorporate ER/DER++ methods into LEAF as future work?
