## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a novel motion-language representation paradigm that uses lexicalization to create a shared vocabulary space for both motion and text, enhancing interpretability. A multi-phase pre-training strategy (LexMLM, CMMM, LexMMM, LexCMLP) is introduced to align sparse lexical representations across modalities. Comprehensive experiments on HumanML3D and KIT-ML show state-of-the-art performance on retrieval and captioning tasks.

### Strengths
1. The idea of introducing a lexical representation paradigm to the motion‑language domain is novel and non‑trivial (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). It addresses the limitation of dense alignment in CLIP‑like frameworks by providing an interpretable mapping where each feature element carries specific semantic meaning (Reviewers 1, 4).
2. The multi‑phase training strategy effectively optimizes the motion‑language space, and the model achieves remarkable improvements over baselines across multiple benchmarks (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
3. The paper is well‑written, well‑organized, and easy to follow, making the methodology accessible (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
4. Qualitative results (e.g., Figure 5) show good visualizations that validate interpretability (Reviewers 3, 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of ablation on the need for lexical representation (Reviewer 1, confidence 4).** The authors claim existing text encoders fail to align semantic keywords, but do not verify whether a stronger text encoder (e.g., T5-XXL) would make lexical representation unnecessary. Controlled experiments showing that even a powerful encoder struggles to align with motion are needed to justify the core motivation.
2. **Insufficient implementation details (Reviewers 1, 3).** The Lexical Disentanglement Head that transforms dense embeddings into sparse lexical representations is only cited, not described; the paper should provide explicit implementation steps for reproducibility. Also, the method for visualising the lexical representation in Figure 5 is not specified.
3. **Missing experiments on text‑to‑motion generation (Reviewers 1, 4).** Since text‑to‑motion is a primary application of motion‑language pretraining, the model should be tested on this task, especially with flexible/creative language (e.g., “wave the hands and jump in an S‑shaped path”). This would provide stronger evidence for the effectiveness of the lexical vocabulary space.
4. **Concerns about dataset size and generalization (Reviewers 2, 3 confidence 3–4).** Pre‑training is performed only on HumanML3D and KIT‑ML; it is unclear whether these datasets are sufficient to learn robust lexical representations. Generalization experiments on unseen datasets (e.g., Motion‑X) are desired to demonstrate robustness and whether the learned representation overfits.
5. **Interpretability and quality of lexical representation (Reviewer 2, confidence 3).** The word cloud in Figure 5 contains seemingly meaningless words, suggesting the representation may be noisy or not compact. Further analysis of word–synonym overlap, semantic density in the joint space, and uneven distribution is needed (also raised by Reviewer 3).
6. **Presentation and formatting issues (Reviewer 3, confidence 4).**  
   - Figure 2: caption order is wrong; harder to follow without stronger correspondence in the main text.  
   - Figure 1: missing variable labels in the alignment part.  
   - Figures 3, 7: fonts are too small.  
   - Figure 6: sequential motions overlap; use more frames or a larger interval.  
   - Figure 8: missing caption to clarify comparison (e.g., what red fonts indicate).  
   - Typos: L148 and L192 have spacing errors.
7. **Computational complexity (Reviewer 4, confidence 4).** The multi‑phase training may be overly complex. The paper lacks a breakdown of computational resources per phase (GPU memory, training time per epoch, number of parameters per sub‑network), which is critical for reproducibility.
8. **Potential overfitting in captioning (Reviewer 1, question).** Captioning results in Appendix A.0.4 show generated captions nearly identical to ground truth, raising overfitting concerns. Zero‑shot evaluation on a different dataset (e.g., Motion‑X) is recommended to validate generalisation.
9. **Inconsistency in acronym definition (Reviewer 1).** In line 81, the connotation of “LexMLM” is incorrectly stated and does not match the abstract.
