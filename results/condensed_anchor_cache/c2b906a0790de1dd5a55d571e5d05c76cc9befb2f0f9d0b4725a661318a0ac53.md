## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Disentangled Heterogeneous Collaborative Filtering (DHCF), which combines intent disentanglement and multi-behavior modeling via a parameterized heterogeneous hypergraph and behavior-wise contrastive learning. DHCF aims to capture relational dependencies across different user behaviors (e.g., page views, favorites, purchases) and latent intent factors. Experiments on three public datasets report improvements in HR and NDCG for top-10 recommendation over strong baselines.

### Strengths
- The task of recommendation with heterogeneous interactions is interesting and valuable.
- The paper is well written and easy to understand (Reviewer 1; however, Reviewer 2 finds the methodology lacking clarity – see Weaknesses).
- Extensive experiments, including an ablation analysis, are conducted to validate the model.
- The hypergraph structure allows modeling of multiple interaction types and is parameterized to learn hyperedge weights.
- Contrastive learning is used to improve robustness against data sparsity.
- The proposed method outperforms baseline methods on HR and NDCG for top-10 recommendation, and ablation is included.

### Weaknesses
- The methods (Sections 3.1–3.5) follow very common design paradigms and bear strong similarity to HCCF and ICL; the hypergraph construction and message-passing appear as straightforward adaptations, and the disentanglement module lacks a clear explanation of how it differs fundamentally from existing disentanglement methods in collaborative filtering. A rigorous comparison detailing architectural differences and their impact is needed. (Reviewer 1)
- The two loss functions in Section 3.6 are fundamentally contrastive losses operating at different granularities (node and graph) but are presented as separate paradigms; the paper lacks theoretical justification demonstrating that they are not redundant and that they contribute to learning distinct aspects. (Reviewer 1)
- Figure 1 has too small font size. (Reviewer 1)
- The distinctiveness and advancements over existing literature are not sufficiently highlighted; a more comprehensive comparison pinpointing exact areas of improvement and innovation is needed. (Reviewer 2)
- The methodology lacks clarity and detail; algorithms need explicit elucidation, and the paper would benefit from visual aids or concrete examples to visualize the hypergraph structure and behavior-wise contrastive learning. (Reviewer 2)
- Missing examination of scalability to larger datasets and sensitivity to hyperparameter choices. (Reviewer 2)
- The model consists of multiple components, making it difficult to optimize and converge, and thus hard to apply in real-world cases. (Reviewer 3)
- The robustness analysis is not convincing: in Figure 3, baseline methods achieve similar performance on different user groups, whereas a proper robustness test should show stable performance when baselines degrade; testing on tail items would be more appropriate than user groups. (Reviewer 3)
- Unanswered questions that overlap with criticisms:
  - Why were experiments conducted on different user groups rather than tail items for robustness? (Reviewer 3)
  - Is the meta‑learning process considered when calculating complexity in Section 3.7? (Reviewer 3)
  - What evidence supports the convergence of the learnable hypergraphs? (Reviewer 3)
  - How does DHCF specifically address the failure to exploit relational dependencies across behaviors? How does the parameterized heterogeneous hypergraph capture complex interactions? How does it disentangle latent intent factors? How does behavior‑wise contrastive learning facilitate adaptive data augmentation at node and graph levels? (Reviewer 2, overlapping with lack of clarity and missing explanation)
