## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes 3DS (Decomposed Difficulty Data Selection), a two-stage model-centric approach for selecting supervised fine-tuning data for LLM domain adaptation, tested on medical domain adaptation with a newly curated dataset. Stage 1 uses prompt-driven filtering with the target model to rate data quality across five dimensions. Stage 2 selects data based on three difficulty metrics: Instruction Understanding (perplexity), Response Confidence (conditional perplexity), and Response Correctness (perplexity against reference), plus an attention-based importance weighting for token-level calibration. Data are selected within a pre-specified moderate difficulty range. Reviewers agree that the method empirically outperforms baselines (including fine-tuning on all data) and that the dataset contribution is significant. However, reviewers raise concerns about missing limitations discussion, dataset release/ethics, missing ablations, imprecise terminology, lack of active learning references, and missing comparisons with medical-domain LLMs.

### Strengths
- **Effectiveness**: The approach clearly and consistently outperforms baselines, including fine-tuning on the full dataset. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Innovation**: Using the target model (instead of a proprietary LLM) for data selection and the attention-based importance weighting to refine perplexity-based uncertainty estimates is novel and supported by evidence. (R1, R3)
- **Dataset contribution**: The curated medical dataset (size reported as 1.9M by R1, 19M by R2; unclear) is a significant contribution with potential for broad reuse. (R1, R4)
- **Clarity and structure**: The paper is generally well-written and easy to follow, with a clear motivation and structured presentation. (R1, R2, R3; R4 dissents on precision – see Weaknesses)
- **Ablation studies**: The experiments showing the effect of different difficulty levels (D1–D3) support the hypothesis that moderately difficult data is most learnable. (R2)

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient discussion of limitations and societal impact**: The paper does not engage with potential bias amplification arising from using the target model for selection (self-reinforcing loop), nor with fairness/ethical concerns in the medical domain. R1 explicitly notes this lack, citing literature (Gebru et al.; Hong et al. on CLIP). R2 also requests a fairness discussion. R3 states limitations are acknowledged, but the depth is inadequate. (R1, R2)
2. **Dataset curation and release**: The dataset is not publicly released; no Datasheet for Datasets is provided; no ethics review is mentioned; the crawling of doctor-patient Q&A raises terms-of-service and sensitivity concerns. (R1, R3)
3. **Missing comparisons and baselines**:
   - No comparison with GPT-4 based annotation or manual data selection; a cost-benefit analysis versus GPT-4 is absent. (R3)
   - No comparison with medical-domain fine-tuned LLMs (e.g., MedPALM, MediTron). Without this, it is unclear whether gains stem from the selection strategy or the base LLM capability. (R3)
4. **Technical imprecision and lack of theoretical grounding**:
   - Core concepts are not clearly defined: “model’s inherent knowledge distribution”, “data difficulty”, “preference gaps”, “learning requirements”. No justification is given for why a middle range of difficulty metrics is optimal. (R4; also echoed in R3’s questions)
   - The term “domain adaptation” is misused (standard meaning involves no labeled target domain data; see Ben-David 2010). (R4)
   - The paper lacks references to active learning literature, despite strong parallels (e.g., uncertainty sampling). See Mindermann et al., Karamcheti et al. (R2)
   - Standard metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) are used without citations. (R2)
5. **Missing ablations**: No ablation removes Stage 1 or Stage 2 entirely to determine their individual contributions. It is unclear whether Stage 2’s perplexity computations could be replaced by additional prompts in Stage 1. (R2) This relates to a question about collapsing Stage 2 into Stage 1; the reviewer would increase the score to 8 if this ablation is added.
6. **Threshold and sensitivity analysis**:
   - It is not stated whether difficulty thresholds (τ_low, τ_high) are chosen on a held-out validation set or on test data; selection on test data would hurt generalizability. (R4)
   - No experiment shows sensitivity to the training data budget (fixed at 5K). (R3)
   - How a practitioner chooses the appropriate difficulty range for a given model size is not addressed. (R2)
7. **Missing implementation and reproducibility details**:
   - Algorithm 1 does not discuss the case when S_mid is empty. (R3)
   - The aggregate function in Equation 5 is undefined. (R3)
   - k-center sampling for diversity is mentioned but not explained. (R3, R4)
   - Hyperparameter selection procedures are not reported. (R3)
8. **Questionable metrics**: Using perplexity to measure “Instruction Understanding Difficulty” is not clearly justified; a high perplexity may not correspond to comprehension difficulty. (R4)
9. **Minority reviewer disagreement on clarity**: While most reviewers found the paper clear, R4 rated presentation 2/4, describing the motivation as “hand-wavy” and technically imprecise. This uneven clarity is a weakness. (R4)
