## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) framework for controlled LLM generation under syntactic and semantic constraints, building on the probabilistic programming language of Lew et al. (2023). Constraints are encoded as an unnormalized product \(p_{LM}(x)\phi(x)\) with an energy function \(\phi\); SMC provides approximate unbiased sampling via token-level masking, weight correction, and adaptive resampling. The method is evaluated on four tasks: Python code generation (DS-1000), text-to-SQL (Spider), goal inference, and molecule synthesis, using prompted Llama 3 (base or instruct, varied by task). Ablations confirm that weight correction and resampling improve downstream accuracy, and KL divergence analysis shows that each component brings the sampling distribution closer to the target product-of-experts distribution. The final SMC method outperforms all ablated variants.

### Strengths
- Solves a key problem in constrained generation frameworks such as Guidance, SGLang, and Outlines, which achieve control via greedy token masking and thus produce biased samples. SMC provides a relatively practical and scalable way to approximate unbiased sampling from the conditional distribution.
- The specific SMC recipe—token-masking, weight correction, and adaptive resampling—is novel for the tested tasks and is placed into a coherent probabilistic framework. Reviewers note that weight correction and resampling are new algorithmic components.
- Controlled ablation study demonstrates that each component (token‑level potential, weight correction, semantic potential, resampling) improves downstream performance and reduces KL divergence from the target distribution. The quality of approximation is shown to correlate with accuracy.
- Evaluations span four challenging, diverse real‑world domains; the benchmarks are well chosen and provide strong empirical evidence for the method’s effectiveness.
- Derivations and the divergence analysis are of high quality and provide insight into the behavior of the method.
- The probabilistic framing is a valuable conceptual contribution that furthers the perspective of treating sequence generation as conditional sampling.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing baselines and comparisons.** The paper does not compare against other SMC-based methods that are directly relevant, such as the SMC approach in arXiv:2306.03081 (with/without‑replacement resampling), nor against the methods of Lew et al. (2023) or Zhao et al. (other non‑SMC baselines are also absent). A natural baseline would be a regular SMC variant that applies grammar constraints, weight correction, and resampling without the semantic potential, to further isolate the contribution of each component.
- **Limited model and generality.** Only a single model (Llama 3.1 8B) is evaluated; it is unclear whether the benefits persist across other similarly‑sized LLMs or scale to larger models. The absolute numbers on DS-1000 (28.5%) are far below reported SOTA (e.g., 39.2% with Codex-002), and on Spider prior work reports 67.0–81.9%. Although model choices differ, these gaps raise concerns about the method’s practical impact on stronger models.
- **Particle analysis and sample complexity.** The paper uses 10 particles without justifying this choice. There is no analysis of how performance or approximation quality varies with the number of particles, nor a comparison to a sample‑and‑rerank approach that draws many independent samples. The sample complexity of SMC relative to naive importance sampling is not studied.
- **Computation cost.** No evaluation of the efficiency (runtime, token overhead) of the proposed SMC algorithm is provided.
- **Lack of theoretical grounding.** No theorem rigorously proves the reduction in KL divergence that is shown empirically in Figure 2. A formal justification would strengthen the manuscript.
- **Proposal distribution exploration.** The paper does not investigate alternative, potentially better proposal distributions (e.g., an LLM fine‑tuned or prompted with task‑specific supervision).
- **Writing and presentation issues.** Some phrasing is vague: “some” in lines 41–44 and the applications referenced in lines 157–158 are not clearly specified. Notation is difficult to follow in places and Section 2’s exposition of importance sampling’s computational burden is drawn out. The right part of Figure 1 and Table 2 appear to convey the same information; reviewers suggest keeping only one to avoid redundancy.
- **Instruct version choice.** The instruct version of Llama 3.1 is used only for the SQL task; the reason for this discrepancy is not explained.
