## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DiNO-Diffusion, a self-supervised method for training latent diffusion models conditioned on DiNO image embeddings, applied to chest X-ray generation using 868k unlabeled images from MIMIC-CXR. It reports FID scores as low as 4.7, up to 20% AUC improvement in classification data augmentation, and 84.4% Dice in zero-shot lung lobe segmentation. Reviewer opinions are split: one reviewer (rating 8) finds the work original, well-executed, and significant, while the other three (ratings 5,3,3) raise concerns about novelty, missing baselines, limited domain value, and insufficient comparisons to related methods. The reviews agree that the paper is clearly presented, but disagreement exists on the strength of the contributions and experimental validation.

### Strengths
- **Thorough ablation and analysis** (R1): Table 1 examines multiple configurations (DiNOv1/v2, reconstruction vs. interpolation, different rs ratios), providing valuable insights. Qualitative visualizations and failure case analysis (Figure 5) clarify limitations and directions for improvement.
- **Clear motivation and good idea** (R2, R4): Applying self-supervised learning to condition diffusion models for sparsely-annotated medical imaging is well-motivated and creatively integrates pretrained DiNO features.
- **Well-presented and easy-to-understand** (R2, R3, R4): The structure is clear, visuals effectively show qualitative comparisons and segmentation outcomes, and key components (DiNO embeddings, conditioning mechanism) are explained.
- **Strong quantitative results** (R4): Significant improvements in data augmentation (up to 20% AUC increase) and impressive zero-shot segmentation (84.4% Dice) demonstrate effectiveness. Scalability is shown by training on over 868k images.
- **Originality and significance** (R4): Self-supervised training removes the need for large annotated datasets, broadening diffusion model applicability in medical imaging. The method is adaptable to other modalities and state-of-the-art diffusion models.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty** (R1, R3): There are no substantial modifications to the Stable Diffusion backbone; the integration of DiNO is not shown in Figure 1 and is only briefly described in the methods section. Similar works (Self-Guided Diffusion [R3 ref 1], Guided diffusion from self-supervised features [R3 ref 2], Learned representation-guided diffusion [R3 ref 3]) already use self-supervised image embeddings to guide diffusion, but the paper does not reference or compare to them. The related work section is insufficient.
- **Missing baseline comparisons** (R2, R3): The paper does not compare against latent diffusion models (LDM) [R2 ref 1], ControlNet [R2 ref 2], or SegGuidedDiff [R2 ref 3]. It lacks ablation showing whether DiNO embeddings are more informative than the LDM’s own encoder embeddings for downstream tasks (e.g., segmentation). The paper does not discuss why SSL-guided generation might outperform end-to-end finetuning approaches.
- **Insufficient medical domain validation** (R3): The three evaluated tasks (reconstruction, interpolation, zero-shot lung lobe segmentation) do not demonstrate value for clinically relevant challenges such as lesion detection, especially for pathologies that current X-ray models miss. Critical evaluations with lesion-focused downstream tasks are missing.
- **Semantic diversity and controllability are unclear** (R1, R1 Q6): Generated images rely on features computed from existing training images or interpolated features, offering no guarantee of out-of-distribution samples or control via interpretable conditions (as text conditioning would allow). It is also unclear how image embeddings from unseen or out-of-distribution images can be obtained for controlled generation.
- **Data augmentation and fully synthetic training settings are fundamentally similar** (R1, R1 Q5): Both require real data to train the diffusion model, so results are expectedly similar, as reported. The difference is only in whether real images are used during downstream training, not in independence from real data.
- **Annotation for downstream tasks is not addressed** (R1, R1 Q1): The paper does not explain how synthetic images can be paired with semantic labels for supervised downstream training, since the diffusion model is conditioned on an unannotated image descriptor.
- **Visual artifacts not explained** (R1 Q3, R1 Q4): Reconstruction sometimes produces horizontally flipped images (Figure 1b(i)), and generated images appear darker than real images (Figure 2). It is not discussed whether these differences affect clinical meaningfulness.
- **Only one dataset tested** (R2): All experiments use MIMIC-CXR; no validation on other chest X-ray datasets (e.g., CheXpert) or other modalities is provided to demonstrate generalization.
- **Limited segmentation comparison** (R4): No comparison to state-of-the-art medical segmentation models such as MedSAM or MedSAM2 is provided for the zero-shot segmentation task.
- **Minor presentation issues** (R1 Q2, R4): Figure 1 does not reference DiNO despite the title, and the caption for Figure 1 should define DiNOv1-Diffusion vs. DiNOv2-Diffusion. Availability of the dataset to the community is not clarified.

*Note:* One reviewer (rating 8) was significantly more positive and considered these weaknesses minor, while the other three reviewers (ratings 5,3,3) found them more critical, especially regarding novelty, baselines, and domain relevance.
