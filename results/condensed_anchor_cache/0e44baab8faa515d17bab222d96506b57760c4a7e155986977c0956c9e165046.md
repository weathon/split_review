## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a plug-and-play frequency prompt mechanism for image restoration, consisting of a prompt generation module and a prompt integration module that encode different frequency information into feature spectra via dual-dimensional attention. Experiments on general and all-in-one tasks across 15 benchmark datasets demonstrate effectiveness for both CNN-based and Transformer-based backbones. The three reviewers scored the paper 5, 3, and 3. One reviewer (score 5) praises the extensive experiments and plug-and-play nature; the other two (score 3) see limited novelty, unfair training setups, and insufficient frequency-domain analysis.

### Strengths
1. Extensive experiments verifying effectiveness: the method is validated on a wide range of image restoration tasks (15 benchmark datasets for five representative tasks) under both specific and all-in-one settings. [R1, R2, R3]
2. The proposed mechanism is plug-and-play and applicable to both CNN-based and Transformer-based architectures, enhancing existing models. [R1, R3]
3. Most prompt-based methods operate in the spatial domain; this work formulates prompts from the perspective of frequency, presenting an interesting methodology. [R3]
4. The paper is well organized and easy to understand; qualitative and quantitative experiments are adequate. [R2]

### Weaknesses
1. **Novelty concerns**: Both frequency learning ([1] Selective Frequency Network, [2] Focal Network, [3] IRNeXt, [4] Hybrid Frequency Modulation Network) and prompt learning ([5] PromptIR, [6] Prompt-In-Prompt, [7] Sequential and Prompt Learning, [8] Multimodal Prompt Perceiver) or their combinations ([9] AdaIR, [10] Seeing the Unseen) have been extensively explored in image restoration. The proposed addition of a subtraction branch for high-frequency prompts is incremental. [R2, R3]
2. **Unfair training setup**: 
   - The vast majority of compared algorithms are trained using only L1 loss; this method likely uses additional frequency losses, making gains not attributable solely to the proposed prompt mechanism. [R2]
   - The method uses a much larger patch size (256×256) than typical baselines, which significantly weakens the persuasiveness of main results. [R2]
3. **Missing efficiency analysis** despite title emphasizing "efficient": No comprehensive comparison of parameters, FLOPs, and on-device inference latency (beyond Part C results in Appendix). [R2]
4. **Insufficient frequency-domain analysis**: 
   - The method only uses global average pooling (GAP) as a low-pass filter and fast Fourier transform (FFT) in the Prompt Integration Module, without deeper understanding of why the frequency-domain mechanism is effective or how it differs from spatial-domain prompts. [R3]
   - In Figure 4, two images produced with only low-frequency and high-frequency prompts show no apparent frequency-domain distinction; both seem concentrated in the low-frequency region, making it difficult to discern roles of each prompt. [R3]
5. **High similarity to PromptIR [1]** (NeurIPS 2023): The Prompt Generation Module shares the same name and similar design (differing only by a subtraction branch for high-frequency). The Prompt Integration Module has a similar name, and it is questionable whether modulating frequency-domain features with channel and spatial attention is appropriate, or whether direct integration without FFT might be more effective. [R3]
6. **Contradiction in design rationale**: 
   - The abstract states "these algorithms mostly operate in the spatial domain," but the proposed method also operates in the spatial domain (Figure 1) even though it influences spectral changes. [R1]
   - The all-in-one task seems to use a general prompt, not a task-specific prompt, differing from the authors' expectations of prompt learning. [R1]
7. **Design inconsistency and missing clarification**:
   - In Equation 3 of Section 3.1, the calculation of the high-frequency component weight w omits GAP, while Figure 1 applies GAP after removing the low-frequency component from input X to obtain the high-frequency component. If implemented as in Figure 1, the high-frequency branch undergoes a low-pass filter, so features no longer correspond to true high-frequency components, which is unreasonable. [R3]
   - Feature dimensions within the Prompt Generation Module and Prompt Integration Module require further clarification to convey specific operations more clearly. [R3]
8. **Missing comparisons**: The authors did not compare the proposed method against other all-in-one restoration models on specific tasks, despite emphasizing effectiveness in specific tasks. [R1]
