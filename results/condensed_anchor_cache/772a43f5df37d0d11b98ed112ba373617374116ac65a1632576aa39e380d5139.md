## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a weakly-supervised audio separation framework that uses the pretrained CLAP model to enforce semantic consistency between separated audio and single-source text prompts, without requiring single-source audio during training. It integrates three training paradigms: unsupervised (MixIT), weakly-supervised (bi-modal contrastive loss plus reconstruction loss), and semi-supervised. Experiments show a 71% SDR boost over the unsupervised baseline (reaching 97.5% of supervised performance) and a further 17% improvement when augmenting supervised learning. The approach is evaluated against baselines like LASS-Net and includes ablations.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and provides sufficient detail for reproducibility.
- The technical novelty is commendable: using CLAP to validate separation output consistency and integrating loss objectives for both intermediate semantic consistency and separation content accuracy (e.g., CLIPLoss). The extension to semi-supervised learning broadens potential data usage.
- The proposed method yields a significant performance improvement over the unsupervised MixIT baseline (71% SDR boost).
- The novel extension of MixIT with conditioning is appreciated.
- Comprehensive experimental evaluation with self-implemented ablations and extensive analysis of existing work strengthens the contribution. Ablation tests underscore the superior performance compared to existing techniques.
- The framework uniquely integrates unsupervised, weakly supervised, and semi-supervised training for language-conditional source separation; experimental results validate efficacy.

### Weaknesses
- **Supervision source ambiguity**: The distinction between “weakly-supervised” and “semi-supervised” training is unclear. In Table 1, the “Proposed Framework” row likely refers to the semi-supervised version; authors should rename “Supervised” column to “Fully supervised” or specify mixture vs. single-source training data per model (Reviewer 1).
- **CLAP as implicit supervision**: Since CLAP is pretrained on large-scale (potentially noisy, multi-source) data, the method effectively becomes supervised with unpaired data, even when no single-source audio is used. This difference should be explicitly clarified throughout the manuscript (Reviewer 1). Furthermore, CLAP is trained on the same weakly-labeled data that the separation model uses (e.g., AudioSet), so the constraint may not be as clean as intended—further experiments are needed to verify that CLAP is enforcing semantic alignment rather than memorizing correlations (Reviewer 2, confidence 5).
- **CLAP vs. timbre classifier**: A critical weakness (Reviewer 2) is that the “semantic alignment” from CLAP primarily restricts timbre, not content, given the simple prompt templates (e.g., “[this is the sound of [class]]”). The authors should provide an ablation study comparing CLAP’s semantic loss to a simpler timbre classifier (trainable from the same source-type labels) to demonstrate that CLAP is truly necessary. This reviewer assigned a weak reject due to this concern and the performance comparison with LASSNet.
- **Performance relative to stronger baselines**: On the MUSIC dataset, the semi-supervised SDR (7.9 and 9.9) does not significantly outperform LASSNet (9.75 and 10.45 dB), suggesting that a simpler text or audio query model without explicit semantic similarity might suffice (Reviewer 2). Additionally, Reviewer 1 recommends including stronger text-based baselines like the heterogeneously conditioned model from [A], which already outperforms LASS-Net.
- **Missing human evaluation and demos**: Reviewer 1 suggests adding human evaluations for best baseline vs. proposed method. Reviewer 2 requests a listenable demo page to assess separation quality and understand how CLAP enhances the result.
- **Generalization to large datasets and background noise**: Reviewer 3 questions how performance scales with dataset size. Also, in weakly-supervised settings where background noises not mentioned in the prompt exist (e.g., city motor sounds when prompt only says “people talking and birds chirping”), it is unclear how the model handles such discrepancies.
