## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a federated learning anomaly detection approach that detects attacks, removes malicious models without harming benign ones, and uses zero-knowledge proofs to ensure honest execution. Reviewer assessments diverge significantly: Reviewer 1 (score 6, confidence 2) was positive, Reviewer 2 (score 5, confidence 3) was moderately positive but raised substantive concerns, and Reviewer 3 (score 3, confidence 4) was highly critical.

### Strengths
- The paper is well written, well structured, and easy to follow. (Reviewer 1)
- The proposed approach detects attacks and performs defense only when attacks occur, and further identifies and removes malicious client models without harming benign ones. (Reviewer 2)
- The method has been compared to a significant amount of related research. (Reviewer 1)
- The authors proposed a new method for FL anomaly detection. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
1. **Threat model description is insufficient.** (Reviewer 1) Lacks details on adversary capabilities (adaptive attacks, knowledge of global model architecture, level of control over local training). Should be described based on adversary goals, knowledge, and capabilities.

2. **Experimental design not fully justified.** (Reviewer 1) The selection of the ‘second-to-the-last layer’ in Experiment 1 is not sufficiently explained. The significance of the “VERIFIABLE ANOMALY DETECTION USING ZKP” section is unclear; the research objectives there need more emphasis.

3. **Assumption that malicious clients < 50% is not robust.** (Reviewer 2) The paper does not discuss limitations or strategies if this threshold is exceeded. No analysis of defense behavior when the majority of clients are adversarial; skewed distributions could cause malicious updates to be incorrectly deemed benign.

4. **Use of the Three Sigma Rule assumes a Gaussian distribution without justification.** (Reviewer 2) No empirical or theoretical support that client behavior follows a normal distribution. Impact of non‑Gaussian distributions on false positives/negatives is not addressed.

5. **Limited novelty.** (Reviewer 2) The core algorithms (Krum, Three Sigma Rule, zero‑knowledge proofs) are well‑established. The integration lacks sufficient insight into how the combination offers meaningful advantage; the paper does not articulate the specific FL challenges it addresses or how it overcomes limitations of prior work.

6. **Evaluation on small‑scale datasets only.** (Reviewer 2) Using FEMNIST and CIFAR is insufficient for large‑scale FL. Testing on industry‑grade datasets and analysis of the computational and communication overhead of zero‑knowledge proofs in large‑scale settings are needed.

7. **Missing comparison with FL anomaly detection methods.** (Reviewer 3) The paper does not compare with FLDetector or similar approaches.

8. **Missing strong attacks in evaluation.** (Reviewer 3) The paper does not test against Baruch et al. 2019 (NeurIPS “A Little Is Enough”), Bagdasaryan et al. 2020 (AISTATS backdoor), or Xie et al. 2019 (ICLR DBA).

9. **Missing analysis of the number of malicious clients.** (Reviewer 3) The influence of varying the number of malicious clients on detection performance is not explored.

10. **Grammar issues.** (Reviewer 1) Occasional typos, e.g., “In this ppaer” on Page 5.

**Reviewer disagreement:** Reviewer 1 (score 6, confidence 2) praised clarity and structure, while Reviewer 3 (score 3, confidence 4) rated presentation and soundness as fair (2) and was most critical about missing comparisons, attacks, and parameter analysis. Reviewer 2 (score 5, confidence 3) identified fundamental assumptions (Gaussian distribution, malicious‑client threshold) that were not justified and questioned the novelty of the integration.
