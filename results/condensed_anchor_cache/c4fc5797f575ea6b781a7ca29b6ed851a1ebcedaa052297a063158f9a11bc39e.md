## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Compositional VLM (CoVLM), which introduces special communication tokens to enable iterative, bidirectional communication between a large language model (LLM) and a visual detection network. These tokens allow the LLM to request relevant image regions during text generation, explicitly composing visual entities and relationships. The model is pre-trained on a large set of grounded image-text pairs (COCO, CC3M, CC12M, Visual Genome, SBU, LAION400M) and evaluated on compositional reasoning benchmarks (HICO-DET, Cola, ARO) as well as referring expression comprehension and VQA, outperforming prior VLMs by large margins.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important and timely problem: imbuing VLMs with compositional visual understanding.
- The object-centric approach and multi-step iterative communication between vision and language are novel and interesting, going beyond simple global feature alignment.
- Strong quantitative performance with significant improvements over existing baselines across multiple compositional reasoning tasks (e.g., HICO-DET, Cola, ARO) and competitive results on traditional tasks (referring expression comprehension, VQA).
- The paper introduces a new dataset of object-level bounding box annotations paired with image-text pairs, aiding model training.
- The model design is systematic, with a clear token framework for communication between the detector and LLM.
- The idea is simple and presented intuitively, making it easy to follow (though one reviewer found the presentation brief and hard to understand; see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation clarity**: One reviewer found the method description brief and hard to understand, especially the generation of communication tokens, and recommended a pseudo-algorithm and more readable input/output steps. Another reviewer praised the presentation as excellent, creating a disagreement on clarity.
- **Pre-training data overlap and generalization concerns**: Multiple reviewers noted that the pre-training datasets (COCO, Visual Genome, etc.) overlap substantially with evaluation benchmarks (Cola uses GQA/Visual Genome, ARO uses Visual Genome, HICO-DET uses COCO objects, RefCOCO/VQA v2 share COCO and Visual Genome). This raises questions about how well the model generalizes beyond training distributions. One reviewer specifically asked for evaluation on a less overlapping dataset like PACO.
- **Lack of ablation studies**: No ablations are provided to isolate the contributions of different components (choice of encoder, pre-training data, communication tokens). The paper also lacks analysis of how the communication tokens affect performance; for example, the large margins over KOSMOS-2 in Table 1 are unexplained.
- **Oversimplification of compositionality**: One reviewer argued that the framework treats compositionality as a simple concatenation of object-centric features, neglecting nuanced interactions and contextual dependencies. This may limit true compositional understanding, e.g., when relationships are not explicitly stated in training data or objects appear in different spatial arrangements.
- **Rigid token implementation**: The manual and fixed token positioning (e.g., `<obj>` around the object, `<box>` after it) reduces flexibility. The model may struggle with complex sentences such as "a man between two dogs" where multiple instances require distinct bounding boxes, or with nested relationships and non-canonical orderings.
- **Operational inefficiency**: The need for pre-parsing sentences and manually inserting tokens even at test time introduces preprocessing bottlenecks, potentially impeding real-time applications and adding error sources (e.g., ambiguous parsing of "a black dog" as either "a <obj>black dog</obj>" or "a black <obj>dog</obj>").
- **Scalability and annotation dependency**: Heavy reliance on precise bounding box annotations for every object in large, diverse datasets limits scalability. The model's ability to generalize to unseen object combinations or relationships remains unclear.
- **Missing qualitative analysis**: Very few qualitative examples are provided (only 4 in supplementary); there is no discussion of failure modes or why the model performs better on "rare" classes in HICO-DET.
- **Sensitivity and missing experiments**: The model's sensitivity to prompting templates is not explored. Also, the large performance gap between CoVLM and KOSMOS-2 on compositional tasks, but a small gap on referring expression comprehension, is not explained.
- **Lack of attribution and justification**: One reviewer noted that the pre-training procedure incorporates engineering tricks from KOSMOS-2 without adequate attribution, and that justifications for sub-component choices are missing.
- **Time complexity**: The iterative communication between the detector and LLM raises concerns about inference speed; a reviewer asked for time complexity analysis per inference.
