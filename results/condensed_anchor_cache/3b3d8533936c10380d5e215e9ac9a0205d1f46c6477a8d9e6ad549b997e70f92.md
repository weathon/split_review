## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers agree that DreamLLM presents a unified framework for joint multimodal comprehension and generation, with strong experimental results. One reviewer is notably more positive (score 8) and emphasizes the synergy and comprehensive experiments, while two reviewers (scores 6) highlight concerns about data contamination, missing model details, and alternative baselines. All reviewers note the promising approach but identify specific weaknesses that require clarification or additional analysis.

### Strengths
- The framework enables synergistic multimodal comprehension and creation by late-fusing a text-LLM, Stable Diffusion (SD), and a CLIP model. Through the use of dream queries and score distillation, it avoids the information loss inherent to intermediate image representations (e.g., CLIP features) and markedly improves image generation ability.
- The model is trained on a carefully curated combination of datasets, including text-image pairs, interleaved multimodal corpora from the internet, and instruction datasets. Joint training of these data yields a synergy between text and image understanding as well as generation.
- The three-stage training pipeline (alignment pretraining, interleaved generative pretraining, supervised finetuning) successfully enables free-form interleaved generation, a capability not achieved by prior MLLMs.
- Comprehensive experiments are conducted across multiple benchmarks (MS-COCO, MMBench, MM-Vet, etc.) in zero-shot understanding, image generation, and interleaved generation. The reported results are strong and convincingly demonstrate the model’s versatility.
- The approach of integrating diffusion models with LLMs through a differentiable dream token is reasonable and is likely to inspire follow‑up work in the area.

### Weaknesses
- **Data contamination / leakage concerns.** The training data includes LAION‑COCO (BLIP captions on LAION images) and LLaVAInstruct (derived from COCO), which could inflate performance on COCO‑related benchmarks (Tables 1 and 2). It is unclear whether the model evaluated in Table 1 is after instruction tuning. Without fine‑tuning SDv2.1 on the same collected dataset and reporting FID on MS‑COCO and LN‑COCO, it is impossible to attribute improvements to the architecture versus simply fine‑tuning a strong diffusion model on a large corpus.
- **Missing details about model implementation.** Key aspects are not clearly described:
  * How is the multi‑token dream query implemented? For decoder‑only training, every token needs a loss – what is the loss of each dream query token during training? Are they generated sequentially or all together at inference? Exactly how are gradients backpropagated through the dream query to update both the diffusion model and the language model?
  * In interleaved joint training, for the second/third image generation, does the model condition on the dream query of the first image, only the visual encoder outputs, or both?
  * For stages 1–3, what is the final loss? Are both L_DM (Eq. 5) and L_MLLM (Eq. 6) used, and if so, are they weighted?
  * During I‑GPT training, which components are updated – the visual projector, condition projector, and dream embedding, or only the MLLM transformer?
  * In Section 5.1, the CLIP loss L_CLIP is not explained: which two embeddings are used, and how is this loss applied during training?
- **In‑context learning and generalization are not evaluated.** The paper does not demonstrate in‑context comprehension ability (e.g., k‑shot learning for image understanding), which is critical for assessing adaptability. One reviewer explicitly asks whether training samples used as in‑context examples could improve evaluation‑time performance.
- **Lack of ablation studies on training data.** No ablation is provided on the choice, combination, or filtering of the training datasets. It is unclear how different data sources or filtering strategies contribute to the overall performance, making it hard to determine the optimal setup or identify bottlenecks.
- **Free‑form interleaved generation suffers from consistency issues.** As visible in Figure 3, the fine‑grained details of generated objects (e.g., phones) vary significantly across images that should depict the same specific object (different models, styles). This indicates that replacing the CLIP text encoder with a strong LLM does not provide sufficient control over object attributes.
- **Necessity of the <dream> token is not justified.** An alternative baseline would be to let the LLM first output a text description and then feed that description directly into the diffusion model using its original text encoder. Table 2 shows that specialist models still outperform DreamLLM, so the naive alternative might perform as well or better while preserving the LLM’s original language capabilities.
- **Hypothesis missing for text‑task improvement.** Table 4 reports that DreamLLM outperforms the pretrained text‑only LLM on text tasks, but no hypothesis is offered to explain this improvement.
