## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an automated pipeline using knowledge graphs (KGs), SPARQL, and LLMs to programmatically generate knowledge-intensive QA benchmarks at scale, avoiding manual annotation. Subgraphs are sampled, entities masked, SPARQL queries created, and LLMs translate them into natural language questions with ground truth from the KG. A benchmark of 1.32M questions is generated – an order of magnitude larger than existing benchmarks. SOTA LLMs are evaluated in zero-shot, RAG, and CoT settings; they struggle to recall and leverage world knowledge, and their confidence in reasoning outcomes poorly reflects confidence in the underlying knowledge.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important problem: robust evaluation of LLM reasoning capabilities with scalable, automatically generated benchmarks.
- The idea of leveraging KGs and SPARQL as an intermediate representation for generating questions is neat, intuitive, and novel; the pipeline is an elegant approach for automated curation.
- The pipeline is completely automated, scalable, and can be transferred to other domains or scenarios (one reviewer, score 6, emphasized this transferability and reasonableness).
- Benchmark size is very large (~1.32M), justifying the scalability motivation and enabling unprecedented knowledge coverage.
- Uses open-source KGs (T-REx and Wikidata) to ensure factual knowledge is included in LLMs’ pre-training corpora, making experimental results on reasoning valuable.
- Experimentation is well-done: tests three major LLM settings (zero-shot, RAG, CoT) and accounts for question complexity (number of unknowns).
- The authors clearly explain the limitations of their work and focus area.
- Many basic methods for enhancing LLM reasoning are included in experiments, which is sufficient (acknowledged by the more positive reviewer with score 6).

### Weaknesses
- Insufficient detail on how the benchmark addresses knowledge coverage (e.g., topic coverage, how KGs are created, how well they cover underlying knowledge). No evaluation or ablation of individual pipeline steps or design choices (e.g., why SPARQL is better as IR, with relevant citations).
- Missing citations for several claims: (a) “We find state-of-the-art LLMs, such as GPT-4, demonstrates strong capabilities of translating SPARQL queries into natural language questions” (Section 2.2); (b) “Less labor intensive approaches often rely on customized domain-specific language as IR … may not generalize well to novel knowledge” (motivation section).
- Data quality of generated natural language questions is not rigorously verified. Only 1200 questions were manually reviewed, and selection was based solely on number of unknowns, not on question type or SPARQL pattern. It is possible that LLMs are predominantly poor at converting certain kinds of SPARQL queries, so the Language Check Benchmark (LCB) needs further evidence. Moreover, the quality of the entire pipeline is inherently limited by the LLM used for SPARQL-to-NL conversion.
- The naturalness of generated questions appears to decrease as the number of unknowns increases (e.g., the example in Figure 3 is awkward). No concrete examples are provided for queries with 1, 2, and 3 unknowns; criteria for judging naturalness are missing.
- Results lack in-depth analysis and interpretation. The four key findings are presented without sufficient quantitative or analytical support; some are not particularly novel (resemble well-known findings). Gaps between LLM performance and expected knowledge reasoning could be due to information recall, reasoning errors, or hallucination, but only anecdotal examples are given, not quantitative breakdowns.
- Missing baseline comparisons: Tables 8–11 lack results for GPT-4o, with no explanation why. More generally, there is insufficient comparison between the automatically generated benchmark and existing template-based/manually curated benchmarks regarding question quality, coverage, and utility. It is unclear whether the higher volume of questions meaningfully improves benchmark evaluation.
- The logical progression and structure of the paper feel unclear. Experiments with RAG and CoT appear more like testing SOTA LLMs than demonstrating the quality of the proposed benchmark pipeline, which is the stated focus.
- Claim that “ground truth incompleteness is not a significant issue” because LLM performance is similar across T-REx and Wikidata (despite different triple counts) is unconvincing – the difference in triple counts may not be directly related to ground truth incompleteness.
- The knowledge transparency analysis (Section 3) is confusing and lacks clarity on several points:
  - In “Limitation and alternative,” the conclusion that `log Pr[a|q,M] = 1 > K_M(q,a)` is mathematically incorrect because Pr[a|q,M] ≤ 1, so log Pr[a|q,M] ≤ 0.
  - The motivation for proposing K_sum(q,a) and K_min(q,a) and how they measure reasoning quality is not explained.
  - The rationale for replacing log Pr[a|q,M] with log Pr[Y|q_a,M] is not provided.
- The English in the paper could be refined; there are several minor and major typos. Figures and tables need better arrangement to improve readability.
- One reviewer (score 5) noted that the claims about knowledge transparency and the four findings would benefit from more detailed experimental analysis and clearer connection to the pipeline’s quality.

*Note: One reviewer assigned a score of 6 (with confidence 3) and found the benchmark creation reasonable, transferable, and the experiments sufficient; the other three reviewers assigned scores of 5 (with confidences 3, 3, and 5). The weaknesses above reflect majority concerns, but the more positive reviewer’s perspective is preserved in Strengths.*
