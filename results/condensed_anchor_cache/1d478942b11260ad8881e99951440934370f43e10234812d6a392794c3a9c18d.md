## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes RoRA-VLM, a retrieval-augmented framework for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) addressing two challenges: (1) retrieval inaccuracies due to modality discrepancies between visual and textual query components, and (2) handling irrelevant/noisy information in retrieved multimodal knowledge. It introduces a two-stage retrieval process (Image-Anchored Entity Retrieval + Query-Expanded Text Retrieval) and a robust augmentation method combining adversarial noise injection during training with a query-oriented visual token refinement strategy. Experiments on OVEN, InfoSeek, and Enc-VQA show improvements over baselines like LLaVA-v1.5. All three reviewers note the clear motivation and structure; novelty and experimental fairness are more contested.

### Strengths

- Clear motivation and well-structured presentation (R1, R2, R3). The paper is easy to follow and thoroughly explains the problem and approach (R1, R3).
- The two-stage retrieval (image-anchored entity retrieval followed by query-expanded text retrieval) is a novel and effective way to bridge visual and textual information, improving retrieval accuracy for multimodal queries (R1, R3).
- The paper directly tackles the practical issue of noise in retrieved multimodal knowledge with a dedicated noise-resilience strategy, relevant for real-world applications (R1, R3).
- Experimental results demonstrate effectiveness on OVEN, InfoSeek, and Enc-VQA benchmarks (R2, R3). Note: R2 considers this a strength, while R1 and R3 raise fairness concerns below.
- Reviewer 3 emphasizes strong novelty: addressing both retrieval inaccuracies from modality discrepancies and noise in retrieved content with an innovative anti-noise approach. Reviewer 2 is less convinced, noting that incorporating multimodal knowledge and adversarial training are not new in vision-and-language tasks. *Minority contrast*: R3 rates novelty high; R2 rates it lower.

### Weaknesses

**Method clarity and detail**
- Section 3.2’s first-stage retrieval (K-NN in CLIP space for WIT images) is a well-known concept and could be moved to the appendix; the main paper should only briefly describe it (R1).
- The description of image encoding into a sequence of visual embeddings (Line 270) is unclear: How are image patches encoded to the same dimension d? Does the method use internal CLIP patch features before the final projection? The authors need to elaborate (R1).
- Figure 2 lacks sufficient detail of the methodology and should be refined (R2).
- A figure illustrating the two-stage retrieval process would benefit the Introduction (R2).

**Inconsistency in design motivations**
- The two-stage retrieval is image-entity driven; would a composite image+query retrieval better handle modality differences? (R3).
- The motivations behind Query-oriented Visual Token Refinement (denoising) and Adversarial Noise Injection (adding noise for robustness) appear conflicting. If both are used during training, the refinement might remove the noise the model is supposed to learn from. A more principled approach would apply token refinement only at inference and adversarial noise only during training (R3). R1 also notes that the provided example (Figure 4) does not convincingly show robustness to noise – attention focuses on question-correlated words like “height”/“width” rather than demonstrating noise rejection (R1).

**Experimental fairness and comparability**
- In Table 1, only RoRA-VLM and Wiki-LLaVA* use retrieval augmentation; other baselines appear to be zero-shot. RoRA-VLM is fine-tuned on knowledge-intensive datasets while many baselines may not have been trained on such data. This raises fairness concerns – the reported gains might partly stem from different training regimes rather than the method itself (R1, R3). The authors should clarify which models were fine-tuned on which datasets and compare against other RAG methods under equivalent conditions (R1).
- Evaluation protocols are insufficiently described: What training set was used for each baseline in Table 1? Were they fine-tuned on the same data? (R1). The paper should separate Results, Ablation Study, and Discussion into distinct sections for clarity, and key experimental details are missing (R1).
- Reviewer 2 also notes the correspondence between ablation variants in Table 2 and the proposed modules is unclear; an ablation of the two-stage retrieval itself is missing (R2).

**Lack of ablation on key parameters**
- No ablation studies on the number of retrieved knowledge snippets (m), the number of top-k images in the first stage (k), or the number of visual tokens used in refinement (l). Their impact on performance is unknown (R3).
- The description of “average pooling (kernel size 2, stride 2)” applied to N CLIP vectors is unclear – how does this relate to image patches? The authors should elaborate on the experiment (R1).

**Evidence for noise robustness**
- The experiment in Lines 405–419 does not convincingly show the model ignores noise. A stronger test would be to provide one relevant sample with two randomly sampled irrelevant ones and show the answer does not change, or to compare against providing only the single relevant sample (R1).
- Figure 4 appears to show attention to words correlated with the question (e.g., “how wide” attends “height” and “width”) rather than ignoring noise generally (R1).

**Minor clarity issues**
- Line 257: Does “top-(k-1)” actually mean top-2? (R1 question, overlaps with missing clarity).
