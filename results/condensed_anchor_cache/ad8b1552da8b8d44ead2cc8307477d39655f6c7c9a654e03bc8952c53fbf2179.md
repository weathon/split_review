## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DVF, a method for value function estimation in continuous control using conditional diffusion models. DVF learns a generative model of the discounted state occupancy measure from state sequences (without reward or action labels) and uses it together with a learned reward model to score actions. The value function is then estimated by sampling states from the occupancy model, weighting by reward, and the policy is optimized by backpropagating through these components. The authors evaluate DVF on offline RL tasks (D4RL, PyBullet) and Maze2D exploration, outperforming BC and CQL baselines. Reviewers agree the idea is novel and interesting, but raise serious concerns about presentation clarity, missing comparisons to diffusion-based offline RL methods, insufficient baselines, technical errors, ambiguity in policy conditioning for offline settings, and weak framing relative to existing diffusion-based RL works.

### Strengths
- Presents a novel paradigm for RL without temporal difference learning or explicit dynamics modeling, by diffusing the occupancy measure (R1, R2, R3, R4). R2 specifically highlights not requiring reward or action labels.
- Good theoretical connections to existing RL works (R1). R3 notes the policy embedding conditioning enables generalization to unseen policies.
- The occupancy model enables a new form of exploration beyond single-step predictions (R1, R4).
- Shows promising qualitative and quantitative results, outperforming BC and CQL on several D4RL and PyBullet benchmarks (R2, R3, R4). R4 emphasizes beating well-known algorithms.
- Demonstrates how DVF can leverage offline data for tabula rasa exploration, enhancing learning efficiency (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and clarity are major issues across all sections.** Key definitions are missing or confusing: in Section 2, Δt appears without definition (R1); the phrasing “explicit conditioning” is unclear (R1); Equation 8 omits the dependence of the diffusion loss on θ (R1); the Q‑function notation Q(s,a,φ(π)) is introduced without definition (R1). The methods section is extremely difficult to follow, with many typos, notation mistakes, and an incorrect math equation: Eq. 12 is wrong because it omits a dynamics term involving the action (R3). Figure 1 incorrectly uses the same s_{t+Δ} subscript for different Δ values (R3). The description of the averaging step needed to obtain a Δt‑independent occupancy estimate is skipped (R3). Details about which networks are trained, their objectives, inputs, and outputs are not clearly stated, requiring “a lot of effort to parse” (R3). R4 finds the two policy‑conditioning schemes (scalar enumeration vs. sequential rollout embedding) ambiguous and insufficiently explained, especially their applicability in purely offline RL.

- **Baselines are weak and lack comparison to state‑of‑the‑art offline RL methods, particularly those based on diffusion models.** All reviewers note that only BC and CQL are used as baselines. R1 requests including Implicit Q‑learning (IQL), Trajectory Transformer (TT), Diffuser, and Score‑Guided Planning (SGP). R2 specifically asks for diffusion‑based offline RL methods: Diffusion‑QL, Decision Diffuser, and IDQL. R3 states that DVF (non‑pooled) actually performs *worse* than Diffusion‑QL on every task in a standard D4RL table, and that even with curiosity rewards the Maze2D results are worse than those in the Diffuser paper (Table 1). R4 recommends model‑based (e.g., [1],[2]), model‑free (e.g., [3],[4]), and reward‑conditioned (e.g., RvS [5]) methods. R4 also notes that CQL often achieves negative returns and beating BC/data on random/mixed datasets is not impressive.

- **Missing full evaluation and ablation experiments.** R3 requests evaluation on the full suite of D4RL tasks as done in Diffusion‑QL Table 1. R1 points out that computational cost is not discussed (training and inference expense compared to baselines). R3 asks for a concrete discussion of differences and trade‑offs relative to other diffusion‑based offline RL methods.

- **Policy conditioning for offline RL is insufficiently justified and potentially flawed.** R2 notes that the assumption that the behavior policy μ is known is unusual in offline RL and asks whether it can be removed; the sensitivity of results to approximations of μ is not addressed. R4 elaborates: for offline RL, obtaining rollouts from the current policy (as in the sequential embedding scheme) would not be truly offline; the scalar scheme along the gradient path may fail because state occupancy can change drastically between updates. R4 requests more insights into why these conditioning strategies work.

- **Minor technical issues and missing clarifications.** R3: Reference to “Figure 5 – Returns are normalized by average per‑task data performance” is unclear. R3: Why was Perceiver I/O chosen over a standard U‑Net architecture? R3: The abstract’s claim that RL performance scales with parameters and demonstrations is arguably more appropriate for supervised learning, and the term “demonstrations” appears prematurely.

- **Framing and related‑work omissions.** R3 is most critical: the paper is written as if little existing work applies diffusion models to offline RL, failing to cite Diffusion‑QL, AdaptDiffuser, Diffusion Policy, and many others. R4 similarly notes that offline RL works such as model‑based/model‑free methods are missing from related work.
