## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a plug-and-play post-training pruning solution for large language models (LLMs) with two components: Relative Importance and Activations (RIA), a metric that jointly considers weights and activations, and Channel Permutation, which rearranges channels to preserve important weights under N:M sparsity. Experiments on LLaMA, LLaMA-2, and OPT (7B–70B) evaluate perplexity on Wikitext-2 and zero-shot accuracy on 5 commonsense datasets. RIA alone outperforms magnitude pruning, SparseGPT, and Wanda; together with Channel Permutation, N:M pruned models match or exceed the original LLaMA2-70B on zero-shot tasks. The appendix includes combination with SparseGPT reconstruction and practical speedup results (Table 5). Reviewers agree the methods are simple, effective, and well-evaluated, but raise concerns about novelty, baseline comparisons, limited calibration data, and the practical impact of small perplexity gains.

### Strengths
- Both relative importance of weights and activation-aware pruning for unstructured LLM pruning are novel; the consideration of relative weight importance is a novel approach. (R1)
- Channel Permutation reformulated as a linear sum assignment problem and solved with the Hungarian algorithm is a novel and effective technique for N:M sparsity. (R4; R1 calls it “simple yet effective”; R3 notes the heuristic is simpler and more scalable than Pool & Yu 2021)
- The proposed pipeline (RIA + Channel Permutation) is simple to implement and provides state-of-the-art post-training pruning performance with negligible performance loss. (R1, R2, R3)
- Comprehensive experiments on strong LLaMA models (7B–65B and LLaMA2-70B), not just older OPT, across multiple model sizes (7B–70B) and diverse tasks (language modeling and zero-shot classification). (R2, R3)
- The paper includes theoretical analysis, empirical results, clear visualizations of key algorithms, and practical hardware speedup measurements (Table 5). (R2, R3)
- Appendix studies combining RIA with SparseGPT reconstruction and further analysis. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: RIA is seen as an incremental extension of Wanda’s uniform sparsity idea (extending across columns). The permutation reordering benefit for N:M sparsity was previously found by Pool & Yu (2021); this paper’s main contribution is a simpler, more scalable heuristic based on average activations. (R3) Reviewer 4 also finds RIA incremental but considers the Hungarian-algorithm-based permutation genuinely novel. (R4)
- **Small improvements for unstructured sparsity**: RIA’s perplexity gains over prior work are only 0.1–0.2 points on Wikitext-2; the linear sum assignment permutation has minor impact. Perplexity increases from the dense baseline are still large, especially for 2:4 sparsity, raising doubts about practical usefulness. (R3)
- **Limited calibration data**: Using only 128 samples from C4 may not sufficiently capture the full activation distribution of large language models, potentially leading to suboptimal pruning decisions. (R2) The reviewer questions whether a larger calibration set might improve robustness. (R2)
- **Insufficient experimental support for motivation**: The paper claims “channel corruption” when removing entire channels, but lacks preliminary experiments to empirically validate this claim. (R1)
- **Missing baseline comparisons**:
    - Comparison with activation-only unstructured pruning (e.g., inspired by AWQ’s activation-aware quantization) is missing in Table 2. (R1)
    - No comparison with other structured pruning methods (e.g., Dejavu) on both performance and inference speed. (R1)
    - For N:M sparsity, experiments on smaller models (e.g., Llama2-7b) and higher sparsity ratios than 50% (e.g., 4:8) are absent; such results could better highlight advantages over SparseGPT and Wanda. (R4; R3 also asks how 4:8 performs with channel permutations)
- **End-to-end latency vs. layer-wise speedup**: Table 5 provides layer-wise speedup; the reviewers suggest reporting end-to-end latency to evaluate whether N:M sparsity is truly effective compared to structured pruning methods like LLM-Pruner. (R4)
- **Limited ablation**: Only Table 2 explores the hyperparameter \(a\) (set to 0.5). The reviewer asks if \(a=0.5\) is generally the sweet spot and if additional ablations were performed. (R3)
- **Clarity issues**:
    - The connection between Figure 2 (activation correlation across datasets) and the claim about activation outliers being independent of dataset and model part is unclear; even with high correlation, outliers could be eliminated. (R1)
    - The reviewer questions why “channel” is used in Transformer models, which lack traditional channels or columns. (R2)
- **Minor issues**: Title “Plug-and-Play” is not explained; the hyperlink in the 6-page appendix appears incorrect. (R1)
- **Reproducibility**: No code is provided in the supplementary; the authors are expected to open-source their work. (R3)
- **Synergy with quantization**: It would be valuable to test whether RIA/Channel Permutation can be combined with post-training quantization methods like SmoothQuant or AWQ for further gains. (R1)
