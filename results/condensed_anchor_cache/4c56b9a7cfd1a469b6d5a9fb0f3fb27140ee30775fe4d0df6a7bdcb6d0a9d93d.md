## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Residual Bit Vectors (ResBit), a hierarchical bit representation for categorical data, to address the linear dimensionality growth of one-hot encoding and the “out-of-index” problem in simple bit representations. ResBit is used to build a tabular diffusion model (TRBD) and is also applied for conditioning GANs and in classification tasks. Experiments on several tabular datasets, CIFAR-10, and a small image dataset report mixed results: on tabular data the method is sometimes on par with simple log₂ encoding, on GAN conditioning it shows reduced visual diversity, and on classification it offers no clear advantage.

### Strengths
- Interesting extension of Analog Bits (Reviewer 1, confidence 2).  
- Good theoretical fundaments (Reviewer 1).  
- Very simple to understand and implement (Reviewer 3, confidence 3).  

**Contrast:** Reviewer 2 (confidence 4) found no strengths in the paper, citing poor performance compared to baselines and unsubstantiated claims. This disagreement highlights that the claimed strengths are not universally supported by evidence.

### Weaknesses
**Performance and empirical validation**  
- On 5 tabular datasets ResBit is clearly better on only 2 (CC, AR), on par on BD and AD, and the IS dataset (1338 instances) is too small to be convincing (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 reports that ResBit sometimes performs much worse than baselines. The GAN conditioning results visually appear less diverse and classification accuracy shows no strong edge over one-hot encoding (Reviewers 2, 3).  
- The three components (ResBit, TRBD, conditioning GAN) are not well evaluated; in particular ResBit is not directly compared with Analog Bits (Reviewer 1).  
- Runtime comparison between TabDDPM and TRBD is unfair because they use different network architectures (different number of layers); identical architectures should be used to isolate the effect of ResBit (Reviewer 2).  
- The claim of reduced complexity (Section 4.4) is not meaningful when performance is not competitive with existing methods (Reviewer 2).  
- Experimental setting should follow TabDDPM’s 15 datasets and many baselines, not just a limited set (Reviewer 1).  
- Evaluation on image datasets is weak: CIFAR-10 has only 10 classes so the benefit of ResBit is not shown; experiments on ImageNet (high cardinality) are needed (Reviewer 2). Running on any image dataset in general is suggested (Reviewer 1).  

**Out-of-index and motivation issues**  
- The claim that ResBit fully solves the out-of-index problem is unproven. The example N=50 works, but proof for any natural number is missing; a counterexample (e.g., N=51) may exist (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 believes a well-trained model would learn to avoid sampling non-existing categories, and no evidence is given that the out-of-index issue is the main cause of performance problems.  
- The explanation of why increasing dimensionality causes model learning to fail is unclear (overparameterization can help). No references or specific failure modes (non-convergence vs. overfitting) are provided (Reviewer 2).  
- The loss explosion/disappearance in Section 4.1.4 is attributed to large dimensions, but dimensions are not particularly large; this could be fixed by normalization or smaller learning rates. Evidence such as a comparison of training dynamics is missing (Reviewer 2).  

**Presentation and clarity**  
- The paper is poorly written and hard to follow (Reviewers 2, 3). Section 2 reads like a catalog of ML methods rather than a coherent presentation (Reviewer 3). Stylistic issues include “that this”, “very widely used”, “limit the increase in dimensionality to a logarithmic increase” (Reviewer 3). Abstract and introduction present methods in inconsistent order (Reviewer 1).  
- Figure 1 refers to a reference paper instead of illustrating the proposed method; more illustrations are needed overall (Reviewer 1).  
- Descriptions of key concepts (e.g., “model learning to fail”) are vague and lack references (Reviewer 2).  

**Missing comparisons and experiments**  
- No direct comparison with Analog Bits, the direct predecessor (Reviewers 1, 2).  
- Formal proof that ResBit avoids the out-of-index problem uniformly is missing (Reviewer 2).  
- Practical task examples showing ResBit outperforms Analog Bits are requested (Reviewer 1).  
- Experiment on ImageNet for image classification is suggested to test scalability and maintain performance (Reviewer 2).  
- The US states example should be connected to a real practical application (Reviewer 1).
