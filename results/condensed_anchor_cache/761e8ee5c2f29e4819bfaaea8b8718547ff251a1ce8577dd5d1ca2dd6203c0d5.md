## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper adopts a data-centric perspective on link prediction, identifying three critical factors: local structural proximity (LSP), global structural proximity (GSP), and feature proximity (FP). It introduces a latent space model to analyze relationships among these factors and empirically reveals an incompatibility between feature proximity and local structural proximity—a finding that explains why GNNs for Link Prediction (GNN4LP) consistently underperform on edges where the FP factor dominates. The study offers practical guidelines for model design and benchmark dataset selection.

### Strengths
- **Unique data-centric approach**: Shifts focus from model design to understanding underlying data factors; provides valuable insights for the link prediction task (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 also notes the interesting empirical finding of tension between FP and LSP.
- **Systematic identification and empirical validation** of three critical factors (LSP, GSP, FP) and their interrelations (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). Reviewer 4 calls the result that these factors should be considered differently a “significant contribution.”
- **Revelation of incompatibility between feature proximity and local structural proximity**: Highlights a vulnerability in existing GNN4LP models; suggests that links driven mainly by feature similarity may be hard to predict (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 describes this as an “interesting empirical finding” but notes it may be known.
- **Comprehensive experiments and analysis**: The work is thorough, with clear motivation and well-grounded experiments (Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).
- **Clear writing and presentation** (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty questioned and core finding known** (Reviewer 1: contribution poor; Reviewer 4: proposition interesting but not so novel). Reviewer 1 argues that the central insight (“features should be considered”) is already known to the community, and the latent space model resembles standard node embeddings. In contrast, Reviewer 2 finds the revelation of incompatibility significant (contribution excellent). Missing comparison with state-of-the-art embedding works that examine similar issues (Reviewer 1).
- **Latent space model motivation and explanation are insufficient** (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4). The model’s parameters **r** and **β** are not defined or motivated in the main paper (Reviewer 4). The theoretical model is not used in the experiments (Reviewer 4). The paper does not clarify how the latent space model differs from standard node embeddings (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 asks for clearer motivation of why such a model is used.
- **Readability and self-containedness**: The main paper relies heavily on the appendix for important content (e.g., limitations, broader impact, future work; details of parameters **r** and **β**). Reviewer 4 states that the paper cannot be understood without reading the appendix and suggests submitting to a journal if 9 pages are insufficient. Other reviewers found the presentation clear (Reviewer 3), so there is disagreement on readability.
- **Missing analysis on MRR** – only Hit metric is used; MRR is also important for link prediction (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of discussion on dynamic network evolution**: The work shows correlation with network evolution, but this aspect is not elaborated, especially for dynamic cases (Reviewer 2).
- **Role of heterophily in Feature proximity not addressed** (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 asks for an example in a homophilic graph where FP and LSP (or FP and GSP) conflict, and suggests the conflict may actually stem from inappropriate FP under different link‐formation mechanisms.
- **Missing comparison with state‑of‑the‑art embedding works** that examine the same issues (Reviewer 1, overlapping with latent space model weakness).
