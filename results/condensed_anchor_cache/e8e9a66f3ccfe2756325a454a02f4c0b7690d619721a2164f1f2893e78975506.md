## Merged Review

### Summary
Fair4Free proposes a three-stage generative method for fair synthetic data using knowledge distillation: train a VAE teacher model with a fairness regularizer on the biased dataset, distill its fair representation into a smaller student model using only noise (data-free distillation), then combine the student encoder with the teacher decoder to generate synthetic samples. Experiments are performed on tabular and image datasets (CelebA, Colored-MNIST) and claim 5% improvement in fairness, 8% in utility, and 12% in synthetic quality over state-of-the-art models.

### Strengths
- Addresses the important problem of generating fair synthetic data when the original training data is private or restricted (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The data-free distillation stage enhances privacy protection and allows synthetic sample generation without accessing real data (Reviewers 2, 3). The use of a smaller student model reduces computational demands and enables deployment on edge devices (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Most reviewers find that the method outperforms baselines on fairness, utility, and synthetic quality metrics, with rigorous experimental validation (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). However, one reviewer notes that the performance gains over compared methods in downstream tasks (Tables 2 and 3) are minimal (Reviewer 1).
- The experiments include a wide range of evaluation metrics to thoroughly evaluate the method (Reviewer 1). The evaluation covers both visual and tabular data (Reviewer 4). The method is presented in a well-organized and easy-to-understand manner (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Data-free claim is misleading.** The teacher model requires training on the biased dataset using a specific fairness-aware loss (regularized distance correlation minimization). Therefore the method is not truly data-free; it cannot be applied when the original training data is completely inaccessible (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4). One reviewer questions why data-free distillation is needed at all if the teacher still needs data – a single model could be trained from scratch to generate fair data (Reviewer 4). The reliance on a specially trained teacher with an explicit fairness constraint limits applicability and prevents leveraging pre-trained large generative models (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing ablation studies and unclear design choices.** The contributions are unclear: why combine the student encoder with the teacher decoder rather than using the teacher directly? An ablation study is needed to justify design choices and to understand what the distillation step adds (Reviewer 1, implied by Reviewer 4).
- **Insufficient quantitative evaluation on image data.** Visual experiments (CelebA, Colored-MNIST) lack quantitative metrics such as Inception Score or Fréchet Inception Distance (FID). Comparisons with established generative models (e.g., EDM) are absent, making it difficult to assess generation quality and the fairness-quality trade-off (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of implementation details and clarity.** Hyperparameters for the proposed and compared methods, details of synthetic data generation during evaluation, training of the random forest downstream model, and explanations of evaluation metrics (DPR, EOR) are missing (Reviewer 1). Overall writing quality needs substantial improvement (Reviewer 1).
- **Need for deeper downstream-task analysis.** Performance on synthetic data should be compared to non-synthetic (original) data, with a breakdown across subgroups and edge cases to assess real-world utility (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3). Aggregate metrics alone are insufficient (Reviewer 3).
- **Limited to single sensitive attributes.** The current model addresses bias only for one sensitive attribute, ignoring intersectional biases involving multiple attributes (Reviewer 2, also implied by Reviewer 3). Scalability to larger or more complex datasets is not demonstrated (Reviewer 2). The multi-stage distillation pipeline introduces complexity and overhead (hyperparameter tuning, computational resources) that is not thoroughly evaluated against the benefits (Reviewer 3).
- **Robustness to data distribution shifts is unexplored.** The paper should investigate performance under covariate shift and concept drift (Reviewers 2, 3). No measures are proposed for adapting to dynamic data distributions (Reviewer 2).
