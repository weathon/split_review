## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a method for directly minimizing the second-order objective (trace of the Hessian) in flow matching for maximum likelihood estimation, unlike prior work that minimized only upper bounds. The approach uses the gradient of the conditional vector field and Hutchinson’s trace estimation to compute the second-order objective without simulations. Experiments on 2D synthetic and high-dimensional image datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, ImageNet) show improved negative log-likelihood and 2-Wasserstein distance compared to some baselines, but not on all datasets. One reviewer (score 6) is more positive, while two reviewers (score 3) find the contributions limited in novelty and experimental validation.

### Strengths
- Directly minimizes the high-order (second-order) objective instead of its upper bound, offering a more reliable optimization of likelihood (all reviewers).  
- Use of the gradient of the conditional vector field and Hutchinson’s trace estimation reduces the computational cost of calculating high-order objectives (Reviewer 2).  
- Demonstrates better negative log-likelihood and 2-Wasserstein distance on 2D synthetic and high-dimensional image datasets (Reviewers 2 and 3).  
- Robustness across diverse dataset types, including both 2D synthetic and high-dimensional images (Reviewer 3).  
- Extends the scope of previous work on high-order objectives in flow matching (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- Theoretical results are not novel, and the improvement is marginal (Reviewer 1). One reviewer (score 3) explicitly states this, while another (score 6) finds the direct minimization contribution strong.  
- Lacks rigorous formal guarantees and convergence proofs that direct minimization always outperforms minimizing upper bounds; no clear demonstration of avoiding local minima or saddle points (Reviewer 2).  
- Theoretical assumptions (e.g., bounding Fisher divergence by high-order objectives) may not hold in practice; the paper does not justify the conditions under which they are valid or the impact of their violation (Reviewer 2).  
- Experiments are incomplete: the method did not achieve state-of-the-art on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, and on MNIST it was only compared with one method (Reviewer 2).  
- Computationally intensive for very high-dimensional datasets: requires explicit computation of the Hessian matrix, which scales quadratically with the number of model parameters, limiting applicability to large-scale cases (Reviewer 3).  
- Lack of detailed experimental settings: learning rate, number of training epochs, optimization algorithm (e.g., Adam/SGD), batch size, and learning rate scheduling are not clearly stated, hindering reproducibility (Reviewer 3).  
- Minor formatting issue: tables have excessive empty space below them, affecting readability (Reviewer 3).
