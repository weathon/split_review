## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates when and why MLPs achieve competitive performance in node classification, using an information-theoretic perspective. The authors propose a tractable metric to quantify mutual information between node features and graph structure, showing that when structural information is already encoded in features, MLPs perform well. Based on this, they introduce InfoMLP, which maximizes mutual information between node embeddings and graph structure via a two-step preprocessing pipeline: (1) generating a graph-augmented feature matrix via non-parametric fusion of node features and powers of the transition matrix, and (2) maximizing mutual information between embeddings from raw features and those from augmented features. The method is designed to be as efficient as a vanilla MLP during training and testing. Experiments on several small benchmark datasets show strong improvements over baselines on both homophilous and heterophilic graphs. One reviewer (score 8, high confidence) was very positive about the contributions; two reviewers (score 3, high confidence) were critical about theoretical foundations, empirical results on standard/large datasets, and clarity of the presentation.

### Strengths
- Provides an intuitive explanation for when MLPs succeed in node classification based on mutual information between features and structure; this perspective is novel and serves as a strong argument (Reviewer 1, high confidence).
- The proposed InfoMLP method is novel and interesting, motivated by the observation that MLPs work well when structural information is partially contained in features; it exhibits strong improvements over baselines on both homophilous and heterophilic graphs (Reviewer 1).
- Nice methodology for comparing/integrating MLPs and GNNs, with a good analysis of mutual information (Reviewer 2).
- Efficiency is a major advantage: InfoMLP has the same complexity as a standard MLP in training and testing, making it suitable for large-scale industrial graphs where conventional GNNs are expensive (Reviewer 3).
- Empirical results on the selected small benchmark datasets are promising (Reviewer 3). The additional results in the appendix, however, were noted to be pessimistic (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of rigorous theoretical framework**: The mutual information justification remains largely empirical. A formal analysis (e.g., bounds on generalization error or a detailed study of the relationship between mutual information and downstream task performance) would significantly strengthen the core argument (Reviewer 1, high confidence; Reviewer 3 also notes unsupported claims in Sec 3.1).
- **Unclear definition of Z_aug vs Z_mlp and “ideal” representation**: Both are outputs of the same MLP encoder, but the mechanism by which Z_aug incorporates graph structure while Z_mlp does not is not explicitly defined. The term “ideal” node representation is not well-defined; properties such as discriminability, smoothness, or alignment with graph structure should be specified (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear connection between feature fusion and H(A|X) maximization**: The paper states the objective is to maximize H(A|X), but the implementation uses a non-parametric mapping that fuses X and A. There is no guarantee that the proposed X_aug actually maximizes conditional entropy. An intuitive explanation or an ablation study (e.g., visualizing learned representations or quantifying adjacency reconstruction from X_aug) is needed (Reviewer 1).
- **Poor results on standard datasets**: InfoMLP fails on Cora and is outperformed on large datasets by simple GNN architectures like GCN and SGC (Reviewers 2 and 3). Since efficiency is a key advantage, performance on larger datasets is particularly important; the main text should include those results (Reviewer 3).
- **Augmented features via multi-hop diffusion are insufficient**: The simple linear combination of powers of the transition matrix may not capture complex structural relationships, especially in graphs with intricate connectivity patterns. The method lacks an adaptive weighting mechanism (e.g., attention or learnable weights per hop) (Reviewer 2).
- **Simplified MI loss**: The mutual information objective is implemented as a Euclidean loss for efficiency. Reviewer 2 questions whether this simplified loss is critical and suggests exploring Rényi estimators instead.
- **Entropy estimation is terse**: The estimation of H(A|X) relies on positive vs. negative distributions. Reviewer 2 requests measuring the overlap between these distributions using, e.g., the Chernoff bound.
- **Claims in Section 3.1 are not well justified**: (a) The hypothesis that MLP success is due to overlap between X and A is not generally correct; a counterexample with a balanced two-community stochastic block model (q=0, p=1) and one-hot node IDs yields I(X;A)=0 yet strong Laplacian regularization would let an MLP perform nearly as well as GCN (Reviewer 3). (b) The term “capacity” is vague—it is unclear whether it refers to representational power or generalization capability (Reviewer 3). (c) The claim that a well-trained MLP might implicitly derive an estimated graph structure is neither theoretically justified nor empirically verified (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 recommends either deleting Section 3.1 or rewriting it with clear assumptions and precise language.
- **Gap between H(A|X) and H(A|hat A)**: Equation 1 proposes H(A|X) = inf_f H(A|hat A) with hat A = f(X). The paper does not bound |H(A|X) - H(A|hat A)|, and the choice of l2 distance for f is not justified (Reviewer 3).
- **Theorem 1 equality**: Reviewer 3 questions why equality holds in H(A|X) = inf_f H(A|hat A); based on the proof it should be ≤. The authors need to clarify.
- **SGC training/testing time discrepancy**: In Table 5, SGC is reported to have slower training and testing time than InfoMLP, but once preprocessing is done SGC is essentially linear logistic regression. Why is this the case? (Reviewer 3)
- **Which embeddings are used for prediction?**: The paper states Z_aug are used for label prediction, but the method description suggests Z_mlp should be used. The authors should report performance for both Z_aug and Z_mlp for comparison (Reviewer 3).
