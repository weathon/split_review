## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CAReNet, a convolutional+attention+residual architecture for low-data image classification, trained from scratch. It uses a CAReNet block (bottleneck + parallel grid/window attention) in the final convolutional block. Experiments on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, STL-10, and Tiny-ImageNet compare top-1 accuracy against ResNet, VGG, MaxViT, and CoAtNet. The authors claim CAReNet matches ResNet50 with fewer parameters and outperforms it by 2.61% on Tiny-ImageNet and 1.9% on STL-10.

### Strengths
- The problem of designing models that work well in low-data regimes is well-framed and motivated in the Introduction (Reviewer 2).
- The authors carefully review and reassemble existing components (convolution, attention, residual connections) into a compact network (Reviewer 3). Experiments on STL-10, MNIST, and Tiny-ImageNet demonstrate some parameter efficiency (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and incremental contribution.** The architecture uses standard methods and has been proposed many times before (e.g., shifted window attention popularized by Liu et al. [3], similar blocks in d’Ascoli et al. [4], and earlier works referenced in Dosovitskiy et al. 2021). Reviewer 4 (score 1, confidence 5) is particularly critical, finding the work of minor significance with very poor novelty and clarity. The motivation for combining convolution and attention is not clearly articulated (Reviewer 1). The claimed benefits (“enhanced feature extraction”, “bolster information flow”) are not specific to this design (Reviewer 2).

- **Incomplete and misleading literature review.** Missing key works such as CvT (Reviewer 1). Incorrectly cites Khan et al. (2022) as introducing ViTs or their fusion with CNNs; the correct attribution is Dosovitskiy et al. (2021) (Reviewer 4). Self-supervised methods like SimCLR are mentioned but not differentiated in problem formulation (Reviewer 2).

- **Insufficient evaluation and poor empirical comparisons.**
  - Only four small datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, STL-10, Tiny-ImageNet); no large-scale dataset (e.g., ImageNet-1K) to assess scalability or generalizability (Reviewers 1,2,3,4). CIFAR-100 is omitted (Reviewer 2).
  - Missing comparisons with modern architectures: Swin-T, ResNeXt, ConvNext (Reviewer 3). On Tiny-ImageNet more than ten published models achieve higher accuracy than CAReNet but are not cited or compared (Reviewer 2).
  - No error bars reported, even for small-scale experiments (Reviewer 4).
  - Table 3 shows only marginal improvement over ResNet18 on MNIST despite higher parameter count, and Table 1 bolds all CAReNet results even when it performs worse, which is misleading (Reviewers 1,4). Only top-1 accuracy is reported; top-5, precision, recall, F1 are missing (Reviewer 1).
  - The VGG16 result of 10% accuracy on STL-10 is anomalous and likely erroneous—the authors should verify preprocessing, implementation, and training procedure (Reviewer 1). The use of STL-10 (labeled subset only?) is unclear (Reviewer 2).
  - Table 1’s “Model size (MB)” metric is not clearly tied to the problem definition (Reviewer 2). It is unstated whether any model uses extra data (Reviewer 2).

- **Missing ablation studies.** No ablation to isolate the contribution of the CAReNet block or other design choices (Reviewers 1,3,4). Removing the CAReNet block is a natural first step (Reviewer 1). The architectural decisions (e.g., extra convolution at end of bottleneck block, downsampling in all residual blocks) are not explained or justified (Reviewer 4).

- **Poor clarity and presentation.**
  - Figure and table captions lack essential details (Reviewer 1). Figure 1 does not show intermediate resolutions or explain why all residual blocks downsample (Reviewer 4).
  - The main contribution—the CAReNet attention—is described only as “an attention mechanism operating in parallel across both grid and window patterns” with no further explanation (Reviewer 4).
  - The methods section dwells too long on common components (Reviewer 2).
  - Title contains an erroneous “CONFERENCE SUBMISSIONS” suffix (Reviewer 2). In-text citations for well-known methods (VGG, ResNet, SE, Transformers) should be parenthetical (Reviewer 2). Parameter counts should be in millions (Reviewer 2). “Tiny-Imagenet (ours)” is a typo (Reviewer 2).

- **No computational complexity analysis.** FLOPs or theoretical complexity compared to baselines are not provided (Reviewer 1).

- **Overstated claims.** Calling a 2.61% improvement over ResNet50 a “groundbreaking stride” is inappropriate (Reviewer 2). The claim of “robustness” is not supported by the narrow experimental scope (Reviewer 2). The paper states it focuses on “architecture-specific training strategies” but no such strategies are presented or tested (Reviewer 2).

- **Unclear or missing analysis of successes.** The paper does not investigate why CAReNet performs relatively well on STL-10 (Reviewer 4) or what applications might benefit (Reviewer 4).

- **No comparison to similar prior architectures.** Variations combining convolution and attention have been proposed before (e.g., references in the ViT paper) but are not compared (Reviewer 4).

- **Problem formulation mismatch.** Transformer-based models are criticized for scaling issues, yet recent works show Transformers scale more efficiently (Reviewer 2, citing [2] Figure 12). This mismatch is not addressed in experiments (Reviewer 2).
