## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces LLAMBO, a modular approach that integrates Large Language Models (LLMs; specifically GPT-3.5) into Bayesian Optimization (BO) for hyperparameter tuning. LLAMBO uses natural language prompts to leverage LLM capabilities for zero-shot warmstarting, surrogate modeling (generative and discriminative variants), candidate point sampling, and an end-to-end BO procedure. The approach is evaluated on several benchmark datasets (including Bayesmark), private tasks, and synthetic tasks. Empirical results show strong performance, especially in early search stages with sparse observations. The paper is generally well-written, provides extensive analyses, and is considered novel and timely by all four reviewers. However, several concerns are raised regarding experimental methodology, reproducibility, conceptual justification, and missing ablations.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and Significance:** To the best of the reviewers’ knowledge, this is the first work to integrate LLMs into all stages of BO/HPO in an encompassing manner (warmstarting, surrogate modeling, candidate sampling, end-to-end). The topic is of high interest to the BO community.
- **Comprehensive Experimental Evaluation:** The paper demonstrates LLAMBO’s effectiveness across several public (Bayesmark) and private datasets, with separate evaluations for each component and an end-to-end comparison. Extensive analyses are provided to understand how and why performance improves.
- **Modular Design and Strong Results:** LLAMBO is modular, allowing individual components (warmstarting, surrogate, sampler) to be used within existing frameworks. The overall system shows promising empirical performance, especially in the early search regime.
- **Clarity and Presentation:** The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and illustrated with helpful figures and plots. The wealth of ideas and corresponding experiments are impressive (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Conceptual Issues with LLMs for Non-Sequential Data (Reviewer 4):** The HPO data passed to the LLM has no natural sequential structure. The authors permute the order of past observations to derive uncertainty, but this “hack” lacks statistical motivation (unlike bootstrapping in random forests). The mechanism by which an LLM can parse and compare numeric values to understand the loss domain is not explained (Reviewer 1).
- **Domain Knowledge and Generalization (Reviewer 1):** It is unclear why an LLM should have any domain knowledge about hyperparameter tuning from its training data. When provided with past observations in the prompt, generalization is assumed (#), but the underlying reasoning is not justified.
- **Prompt Design and Ablation (Reviewers 3, 4):** No ablation studies or analysis of prompt templates are provided. The authors likely did not arrive at the final prompts in their first trial. The effect of omitting specific instructions (e.g., “Do not recommend values at the minimum or maximum of allowable range, do not recommend rounded values”) is not explored. The design process of prompts is missing.
- **Reproducibility and Model Choice (Reviewer 4):** The use of a closed-source GPT-3.5 model (exact version not specified) undermines reproducibility. The model may be updated over time, and training data details are unknown, making claims about meta-data leakage impossible. Strongly recommend using publicly available LLMs (e.g., Llama 2).
- **Benchmark Choice and Simplicity (Reviewers 1, 4):** The evaluation is limited to relatively simple benchmarks (Bayesmark: low-dimensional, continuous spaces, traditional ML models). More challenging benchmarks (e.g., HPOBench) should be used. The approach may fail on high-dimensional problems due to limited token length (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing Baselines and Comparisons (Reviewers 2, 4):** 
  - No comparisons with neural network-based BO algorithms (e.g., [R1]) that also improve surrogate modeling.
  - No fully random baselines provided for warmstarting and end-to-end evaluations.
- **Experimental Design Flaws (Reviewers 3, 4):**
  - No uncertainty estimates (standard deviations or confidence intervals) on any plots. Results may be statistically meaningless.
  - No wall-time plots; overhead from LLM queries is not shown, making it impossible to compare practical efficiency.
  - End-to-end evaluation uses very few iterations; curves should be extended to see long-term behavior.
  - Some results appear contradictory to previously observed behaviors (e.g., SMAC’s random forest beating GP on low-dimensional spaces; SMAC doing nothing in early iterations; HEBO performing surprisingly poorly compared to SMAC). These require explanation.
  - In Figure 7, the random baseline appears to do nothing for three of four plots, which seems questionable.
  - All curves start at the same point in Figure 8 (iteration 0) without clear explanation.
- **Presentation and Accessibility (Reviewers 3, 4):**
  - Related work is moved to the appendix; the main paper should be self-contained. (Reviewer 4 suggests moving the weaker generative surrogate into the appendix instead.)
  - In plots, only colors distinguish curves, and red/green are mixed, harming accessibility.
  - Several minor errors: missing bracket in Appendix (p.15), Figure 16 description mentions two graphs but only one shown, step functions not used for regret over iterations.
- **Analysis of Failure Cases and Negative Alpha (Reviewers 2, 3):** The paper does not discuss failure cases or when LLAMBO performs poorly. The role of negative alpha in candidate sampling (page 8) requires clarification (why does negative alpha improve average regret?).
- **Venue Fit Concern (Reviewer 3):** The reviewer notes the paper may be more suitable for a natural language processing conference (ACL, EMNLP) than ICLR, as it does not involve explicit representation learning.
