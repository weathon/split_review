## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LagrangeEmbedding, an untrainable encoder that uses piecewise linear Lagrange basis functions to generate feature vectors. The method is evaluated on data fitting, MNIST classification/super-resolution, and AG News classification, with theoretical error bounds and a claim that only a linear layer needs training. Reviewers find the idea novel and appreciate the theoretical grounding, but raise major concerns about overclaims, lack of baselines, limited scalability to high-dimensional/complex datasets, missing related work, and insufficient methodological details.

### Strengths
- Novel idea: provides a new perspective for unsupervised representation learning without training (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 3 specifically notes it is a “novel and non-standard direction.”
- Theoretical justifications: universal approximation property and an error-bound formula; code is provided and results appear reproducible (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 2 also acknowledges theoretical bounds on approximation error.
- Evaluated across multiple tasks (data fitting, computer vision, NLP) (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 notes “some detailed examples and analyses are provided.”
- No training/backpropagation required for generating embeddings; reduces gradient computation (implied by the paper, noted by Reviewer 2).
- Limitations are discussed (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 appreciates the clear writing and good visual illustrations.

### Weaknesses
- **Over-promises and under-delivers**: The title and broad claims (e.g., “unparalleled level of explainability”) imply comparability to neural networks, but few quantitative comparisons are provided (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 1 also notes the absence of SOTA comparisons and requests analysis of the gap even if accuracy is lower. Reviewer 3 states that the main goal of learning transferable representations from big, complex, high-dimensional datasets is not achieved and that (at least currently) the paper is unlikely to make the impact it implies.
- **Weak baselines and missing comparisons**: For MNIST, a basic 2-layer CNN achieves 98% accuracy in the first epoch, outperforming the proposed method; no such direct baseline is given (Reviewer 2). If test accuracy is 97.25%, this is far below LeNet’s ~99.3%, and the claim of equivalence to a 6-layer CNN is unsupported (Reviewer 3). In data fitting (Table 1), Random Forest already achieves near-perfect R² scores, saturating performance and questioning significance (Reviewer 2). The comparison with neural networks in Sec 3.1.2 lacks details on network architecture and training parameters (Reviewer 2). No comparisons to kernel methods, infinite-width neural networks [1,2], or simplex-based kernel approximations [3] are provided, even though these are highly relevant (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 also asks whether the proposed method is totally original and requests a detailed related-works section.
- **Scalability and domain-shift concerns**: The method appears to work only on low-dimensional or simple datasets (toy tasks, MNIST) and cannot handle big, complex, high-dimensional data; Reviewer 3 is skeptical it will ever scale because FEM is not new. Reviewer 1 questions whether the method can extract non-low-level features. Because there are no learnable parameters, domain shift between training and test sets is problematic: the mesh is computed from the training set, and unlike neural networks, fine-tuning is impossible (Reviewer 3).
- **Unjustified and ad-hoc components**: The projection layer in Sec 3.2.1 is introduced without explanation and appears hand-crafted with “no trainable model parameters” but requiring careful design (Reviewer 2). The dimensionality reduction technique in Sec 3.2.1 lacks intuition (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing timing and efficiency evidence**: Speed is touted as an advantage, but no wall-clock timing comparisons are provided for computing the embedding (Reviewer 2). The method runs in non-parallel manner, as acknowledged in the future directions (Reviewer 1).
- **Incomplete theoretical and mathematical details**: Missing proofs or references for the two properties of the Lagrange basis function in Section 2 (Reviewer 3). The derivation $(n_t/d!)^{1/d} = O(h^{-1})$ is not explained (Reviewer 3). Definition of $m$ in $x^{(m)}$ (Section 2.1) is not given (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 3 questions whether the Lagrange function $\mathcal{L}_i$ is actually an indicator function rather than a linear one.
- **Typographical and presentation issues**: In Eqn(2), $y^{(j)}$ should be $y^{(i)}$ (Reviewers 1, 2). “given function F (x) to be fitted” has formatting errors (Reviewer 1). SVR is not defined (Reviewer 1). In Sec 3.3 and Appendix D.2, “the neural network” appears to be a typo (Reviewer 2) — it is not clear if these refer to the proposed method or a baseline, and no quantitative results are given for the proposed method in Sec 3.3.
- **Missing details on implementation**: Reviewer 1 asks how the method can be extended to complex real-world tasks. Reviewer 2 asks how the pre-processing layer in Sec 3.3 is implemented. The paper does not address how test-set performance is measured on MNIST if the embedding depends on a mesh computed from the training set (implied by Reviewer 3’s domain-shift question).

**Note on reviewer disagreement**: Reviewers 1 and 3 (scores 6) are more positive about the novelty and potential, while Reviewer 2 (score 5) is more critical, particularly about overclaims, missing baselines, and lack of timing. All three, however, agree that the method is limited to simple tasks and lacks necessary comparisons and depth.
