## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses open-world long-tailed semi-supervised learning (OLSSL) by introducing a more realistic setting (ROLSSL) that removes the assumption of identical distributions between known and novel categories and further assumes labeled samples are much fewer than unlabeled. The authors propose a dual-stage post-hoc logit adjustment (DPLA) method that first adjusts logits based on sample frequency and class count, then dynamically readjusts using estimated unlabeled data distributions to mitigate category bias. Experiments on CIFAR100 and ImageNet100 report up to 50.1% improvement over the OpenLDN baseline.

### Strengths
1. **Problem originality.** The combination of long-tailed, semi-supervised, and open-world learning is interesting and practical. Most prior work focuses on only one or two aspects; the ROLSSL setting realistically reflects real-world scenarios where distribution relationships between known and novel categories are unknown and labeled data is scarce. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5)
2. **Motivation and clarity (positive minority).** The paper is well-motivated and easy to follow according to Reviewers 2 and 3. The problem setting is well-described.
3. **Simplicity and ease of implementation.** The proposed DPLA approach is simple, does not involve complex strategies or additional learnable parameters, and is easy to implement. (Reviewer 5)
4. **Experimental performance.** DPLA consistently outperforms the OpenLDN baseline on all tested datasets (CIFAR100, ImageNet100) with up to 50.1% improvement. Experiments on six datasets (including variations of imbalance ratio and novel class ratio) demonstrate robustness and adaptability across different data imbalance conditions. (Reviewers 2, 3, 5)
5. **Underexplored problem.** The task of handling both long-tailed label distribution and novel classes in unlabeled data is underexplored; this paper provides a strong baseline. (Reviewers 1, 5)

### Weaknesses
1. **Writing clarity.** Several reviewers found the paper difficult to follow:
   - ‘S/N Consistency’ in Table 1 is never explained. (Reviewer 1)
   - Definitions of \(\mathcal{L}_{pair}\) and \(\mathcal{L}_{reg}\) are missing; the paper should be self-contained. (Reviewer 5)
   - Equation (2): why is argmax used instead of a distribution adjustment? (Reviewer 4)
   - Equation (3): definition of \(F_{y_i^l}\) is unclear; with limited labeled samples, will this sample frequency be biased? How are \(C_{base}\) and \(S_{base}\) defined? (Reviewer 4)
   - Relationship between the equation in line 244 and Eq. (2) is not clarified. (Reviewer 4)
   - For unlabeled samples: how is the sample ratio \(\pi_c^r\) in Eq. (4) obtained when class labels are unknown? How are \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) defined? What is the meaning of \((\alpha - \beta)\)? (Reviewer 4)
   - Does \(\tau\) in Eq. (7) denote the same \(\tau\) as in Eq. (2)? If so, why does the operation become plus instead of minus? (Reviewer 4)
   - The rationale for having different logit adjustment strategies for labeled and unlabeled data is not well explained. (Reviewer 5)
   - The method is called two-stage, but Eq. (8) optimizes labeled and unlabeled samples together. (Reviewer 4)
   - Figure 1: right side shows a question mark; does it represent unlabeled samples? Why does the labeled branch also include unlabeled samples? (Reviewer 4)
2. **Novelty concerns.** 
   - The proposed setting is very similar to SimPro (ICML’24), which is not discussed in related work. (Reviewer 1)
   - The basic loss functions \(\mathcal{L}_{pair}\) and \(\mathcal{L}_{reg}\) are well-known from novel class discovery and GCD. Logit adjustments are prevalent in long-tailed classification (e.g., DebiasPL, CVPR’22). Simply applying model predictions in logit adjustments is naïve and provides limited insight. (Reviewers 2, 5)
   - The method primarily addresses the long-tailed problem; little innovation is offered for the open-world problem itself. (Reviewer 4)
   - A logit adjustment strategy for open-world SSL has been considered in L2S (IJCAI’24) – not discussed. (Reviewer 5)
3. **Outdated and missing baselines.** 
   - Most recent comparative method is ECCV 2022; recent GCD methods (GCD, CVPR’22; SimGCD, ICCV’23) and long-tailed GCD methods (Novel Class Discovery for Long-tailed Recognition, TMLR’23; Generalized Categories Discovery for Long-tailed Recognition, ICCV’23 workshop) should be included in Tables 2 and 3. (Reviewer 2)
4. **Unrealistic assumption.** The method requires the ground truth number of novel classes (line 200), which is unrealistic in practice. No discussion is provided on handling scenarios where the number of new classes is unknown. (Reviewers 2, 4)
5. **Complexity and hyper-parameter sensitivity.** 
   - The pipeline involves multiple stages and numerous hyper-parameters (e.g., \(\alpha\), \(\beta\), \(\tau_1\), \(\tau_2\)), making generalization to new settings difficult. Tables 3 and 4 show that non-optimal scaling factors significantly impact performance, even when the number of classes is the same (CIFAR-100 vs. ImageNet-100). No automatic tuning or sensitivity analysis is provided. (Reviewers 3, 5)
   - The impact of hyper-parameters \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) in Eq. 4 is not studied. (Reviewer 5)
   - Analysis of \(\tau_1\) and \(\tau_2\) is missing. (Reviewer 5)
6. **Insufficient benchmark scale.**
   - Experiments are limited to small/medium-scale datasets (CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100). Larger benchmarks such as ImageNet-LT, iNaturalist, ImageNet-1k, or ImageNet-22k should be included to assess generalizability. (Reviewers 1, 3)
   - Real-world semi-supervised datasets with long-tailed distributions and novel classes are needed. (Reviewer 5)
7. **Missing performance breakdown and trade-off analysis.** 
   - The paper reports only overall accuracy on known and novel subsets. It does not break down performance for many-shot, medium-shot, and few-shot classes. Logit adjustment methods often improve few-shot performance at the expense of many-shot classes; this trade-off is not discussed. (Reviewer 3)
8. **Backbone limitation.** Experiments use only ResNet; results with ViT backbone should be included. (Reviewer 2)
9. **Notation.** The notations should be more concise and clear. (Reviewer 2)
10. **Technical distinction from DebiasPL.** DebiasPL (CVPR’22) also applies logit adjustment to mitigate class imbalance from pseudo-labels. A discussion on technical distinctions is needed. (Reviewer 3)
