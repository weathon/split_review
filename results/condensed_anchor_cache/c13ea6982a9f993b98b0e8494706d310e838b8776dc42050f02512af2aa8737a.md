## Merged Review

### Summary
Most reviewers find the paper addresses an important topic but has limitations in novelty, strength of results, empirical evaluation, and clarity of definitions. Reviewer 1 (score 5) is more critical, arguing that the identifiability results follow directly from prior work, that the paper mischaracterizes existing work (e.g., Hälvä et al.), and that the obtained identifiability is weaker than claimed. Other reviewers (scores 6) are more positive but still note strong assumptions, lack of practical demonstration of identifiability, and issues with terminology and presentation.

### Strengths
- Good presentation and readability (Reviewer 1).
- Important topic: extending identifiability to sequential latent variable models; results cover a general class of MSMs and SDSs without assuming independence of latent components (unlike nonlinear ICA) (Reviewer 1).
- Provides identifiability conditions for first-order MSMs with non-linear Gaussian transitions up to permutations, and for non-parametric first-order MSMs (Reviewer 3).
- Provides conditions for SDSs identifiability up to affine transformations of the latent variables and non-linear emission (Reviewer 3).
- Discovery of time-dependent causal structures in time-series (Reviewer 3).
- Identifiability results of switching dynamical systems are proposed (Reviewer 2).
- Experiments on real data despite the theoretical focus (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
1. **Novelty and reliance on prior work** (Reviewer 1, score 5, more critical): The identifiability approach follows quite directly from previous works. The MSM proof uses mixture model presentation known from [1]; the SDS result is a direct application of Kivva et al. (2022). Identifiability of SDSs with nonlinear emission has already been established in Hälvä et al. (2021) (contrary to the paper's claim that they assume linear SDSs). For autoregressive transitions, the IIA-HMM model [2] is not discussed. The estimation algorithm does not mention seminal work [3] and does not explain why it does not apply or why the current approach is superior.

2. **Mischaracterization of prior work** (Reviewer 1): The paper incorrectly claims that Hälvä et al. (2021) assumes linear SDSs; their identifiability results (Theorems 1 and 2) do not make such an assumption. This mistake appears in two places in the paper.

3. **Weakness of identifiability results** (Reviewer 1, supported by Reviewer 3): By relying on Kivva et al., the results inherit its limitations: the need to know the family of the noise distribution, and the reliance on piecewise linear emission functions, which is less general than works allowing almost any injective \(f\). The identifiability is up to affine transformations (\(f(\mathbf{z}) = A f'(\mathbf{z'}) + b\)), which is weaker than nonlinear ICA where individual coordinates are identified (\(z_i = h(z_i')\)). Reviewer 3 notes that conditions such as invertibility are strong and limit practicality.

4. **Lack of practical demonstration of identifiability** (Reviewer 3, echoed by Reviewer 1): The empirical studies, particularly the salsa data experiment, are very qualitative and would benefit from quantitative evaluation metrics (Reviewer 1). There is no clear demonstration that the learned representations actually achieve the claimed identifiability or that identifiability yields practical benefits (Reviewer 3).

5. **Clarity of terminology and definitions** (Reviewer 4): The paper misdefines “causal identifiability” in the introduction and related work. The correct meaning in causal inference is whether an intervention can be computed from observational distribution and causal diagram, not whether the causal structure can be pinpointed (that is causal discovery). This confusion persists: Section 6.2 correctly uses “causal discovery” but elsewhere uses “identifiability.”

6. **Ambiguities and missing details in presentation** (Reviewers 2 and 4):
   - It is unclear whether the number of states \(K\) needs to be specified as prior information and whether it is identifiable or how to select it practically (Reviewer 2).
   - The definition of identifiability in this work should be stated more clearly (Reviewer 2).
   - In the background, is it important that \(K < +\infty\)? (Reviewer 4)
   - Use different symbols for densities rather than \(\theta\) for everything (Reviewer 4).
   - The heading “Theoretical considerations” is ambiguous; better called “Methods” (Reviewer 4).
   - The notation \(\{1,\ldots, K^T\} \rightarrow \{1,\ldots,K\}^T\) is unclear (Reviewer 4).
   - “Mild measure-theoretic considerations” is vague; what does “mild” mean? (Reviewer 4)
   - Inconsistent use of \(|\) and \(\mid\) in densities (Reviewer 4).
   - Typo: “indentifiability” in Definition 3.2 (Reviewer 4).
   - Figure 2 is poor quality (Reviewer 1).
   - Imprecise language: “\(p(z)\) is identifiable up to affine transformations” — clarify whether parameter identification or identification of \(z\) (Reviewer 1).
   - Define \(B\) in Definition 2.1 (Reviewer 1).
   - Grammar: “The generative model consider in this work” (Reviewer 1).
   - Equation (4) uses \(p_a\) but earlier \(p_\theta\); explain what subscript \(a\) means (Reviewer 1).

7. **Lack of simple illustrative example** (Reviewer 2): The paper would benefit from a simple example to illustrate the theoretical identifiability result.
