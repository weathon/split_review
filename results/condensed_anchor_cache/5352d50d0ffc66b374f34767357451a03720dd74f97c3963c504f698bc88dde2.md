## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses zero-shot visual attribute recognition by proposing generative prompting, which reformulates attribute recognition as measuring the probability of generating a prompt that expresses the attribute relation. It is order-sensitive and designed for object-attribute decomposition. Experiments on VAW and a newly introduced VGAR benchmark show that generative prompting outperforms contrastive prompting and achieves strong performance against SOTA despite being trained without annotated data. One reviewer (R2, score 8) found the approach strong with no major weaknesses, while three reviewers (R1, R3, R4, all score 5) raised significant concerns about novelty, technical depth, and experimental completeness.

### Strengths
- The use of existing language models for attribute recognition is interesting (R1).
- Experimental comparisons are important (R1).
- The method shows strong performance against SOTA despite being trained without annotated data (R2).
- Clear ablations demonstrate the value of the proposed method (R2).
- The key idea of using generative prompting/modeling to solve complex localized reasoning tasks is an interesting direction (R2).
- The VGAR benchmark, which unifies object and attribute recognitions, is broadly useful for the community (R2).
- The proposed method is easy to understand (R3).
- The generative prompting approach seems new for attribute recognition research (R3).
- Given the development in LLMs, using a captioning solution for visual understanding is non-trivial (R4).
- The discussion about the limitations of CLIP-based works makes sense (R4).

### Weaknesses
- The main contribution is prompt engineering, not a new method advancing the state-of-the-art from an algorithm or theory viewpoint. Prompt engineering is not novel even from an application perspective, as companies already work on it (R1, R3, R4: multiple reviewers share this concern).
- The technical significance and novelty are weak; the paper looks like a simple prompt engineering paper. More varied prompts should be tested to support the claim (R3).
- A theoretical explanation of why generative prompting is better than contrastive prompting is missing. Without this, the technical contribution is weak as a machine learning paper (R3).
- In Table 3, the performance of the proposed method is not noticeably better than the baseline model TAP (R3).
- The experiments are incomplete:
  - Results with other LLM models beyond CoCa are needed (R3).
  - In Tables 4 and 5, the proposed model is not compared against state-of-the-art methods (R3).
  - Evaluation on additional attribute recognition datasets (e.g., UTZappos, MIT) or HOI detection datasets (e.g., HICO, V-COCO) is missing (R3, R4: both request more datasets).
  - Previous non-LLM works should still be used as baselines (R4). 
  - Fairness considerations when using large datasets like LAION should be discussed (R4).
  - Detailed analysis of long-tailed distribution and results is missing (R4).
- The method section is too brief (R4).
- No new insights are provided; many other works use captioning via LLM for action/object recognition, visual relationship understanding, VQA, etc. A broader discussion covering more domains is needed (R4).
- More discussion or insights about new weapons (e.g., large datasets beyond images and text) and compositional zero-shot learning (CZSL) would improve the contribution (R4).
- Presentation issues: many wrong quotation marks (“xx”) and weird green boxes appear (R4).
