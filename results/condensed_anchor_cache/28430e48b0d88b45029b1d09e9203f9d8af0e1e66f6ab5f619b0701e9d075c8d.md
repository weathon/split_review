## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses the generation of diverse outputs for ill-posed image restoration, arguing that posterior sampling often yields near-identical results due to the heavy-tailed posterior. The authors explore three post-processing strategies—Uniformization, K-Means Centers, and Farthest Point Strategy—to extract semantically diverse subsets, and propose a simple diffusion guidance mechanism that encourages dissimilarity among concurrently generated images during the reverse process. The method is evaluated on super‑resolution (4×/16×, with and without noise) and inpainting, and validated through user studies. Two reviewers give a rating of 8, one gives a 6 (the latter finds the technical contribution limited). The paper is generally well‑written, the problem is interesting, and the practical solution shows measurable diversity improvements.

### Strengths
- Well‑motivated, well‑written, with a clear narrative that identifies an inherent conceptual limitation of posterior sampling (heavy‑tail causing lack of semantic diversity) and explains the root cause.
- Offers a thoughtful analysis of what constitutes “meaningful diversity” and explores three principled post‑processing strategies (Uniformization, K‑Means Centers, Farthest Point Strategy).
- Proposes a practical, straightforward diffusion‑based guidance method that is simple to implement, adds negligible computational overhead, improves diversity, and is reproducible (code provided).
- Conducts extensive user studies to demonstrate that the proposed approach is significantly preferred over standard posterior sampling.
- Considered by two reviewers to have good/novel contribution; one reviewer (R2) disagrees on the level of novelty (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental scope** (noted by R1): The evaluation is restricted to super‑resolution and inpainting; a highly relevant task like image colorization (e.g., face colorization from grayscale) is omitted, which would better demonstrate meaningful diversity.
- **Missing analyses and ablations** (R1 questions):
    * Alternative designs not examined: e.g., maximizing sample variance or average pairwise distance (LPIPS) instead of the proposed nearest‑neighbor repulsion; effect of diversified guidance on the sample mean compared to the posterior mean / distortion‑minimizing estimator.
    * It is unclear whether diversity guidance could be incorporated during training (e.g., in conditional diffusion models like SR3, Palette, InDI, I2SB).
    * Connections to related GAN work on mode‑seeking and minority coverage (Mao et al. 2019, Yu et al. 2020) are not discussed.
- **Missing prior citations** (R2): The observation that random sampling yields limited diversity for long‑tailed distributions was previously noted by [a] (image generation) and [b] (diverse solutions with GANs); these should be cited.
- **Contribution level disagreement** (R2): One reviewer finds the technical contribution rather limited, while the other two rate it as good/novel.
- **Presentation and clarity issues** (R2): The type of down‑sampling used for super‑resolution is not specified. Additionally, Fig. 14’s degraded input appears odd and DPS results look like down‑sampled images; captions in Figs. 28–29 are confusing (inpainting vs. super‑resolution).
- **Insufficient algorithmic discussion** (R1): The experimental section (Sec. 6) could be expanded to show more results and to better explain the design choices made in Sec. 5.
