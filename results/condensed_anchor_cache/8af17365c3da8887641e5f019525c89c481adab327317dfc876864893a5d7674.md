- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 3, 6, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes using fixed attention patterns based solely on positional information (positional Transformers) to improve out-of-distribution (OOD) value generalization on algorithmic tasks like sorting, cumulative sum, and min/max. The authors provide a theoretical link to the Parallel Computation with Oracle Communication (PCOC) model to argue expressivity, and present experiments comparing positional Transformers to standard Transformers. Reviews were mixed: some praise the clear exposition and theoretical grounding, while others question the novelty, experimental design, and practical relevance.

### Strengths
- **Clear exposition and thorough theoretical explanation.** Multiple reviewers (1, 3, 4) note the paper is well-written, with diagrams, equations, and detailed background material that makes the architecture and theoretical claims easy to follow. The theoretical analysis linking positional Transformers to PCOC is highlighted as a strong point (Reviewer 1: "demonstrates both experimentally and theoretically that attention mechanisms utilizing only positional information possess significant expressive power"; Reviewer 3: "establishes the expressive power of positional Transformers"; Reviewer 4: "thorough theoretical explanation of how the proposed positional transformers can simulate any algorithm falling under the Parallel Computation with Oracle Communication (PCOC) model").
- **Potential of decoupling structure and computation.** Reviewer 2 acknowledges that decoupling query/key from value and making attention fixed across layers may have positive effects on generalization, and suggests promise for graph-structured tasks like molecule-related problems.
- **Experimental demonstration of OOD improvement.** Several reviewers note that the paper presents experiments showing OOD value generalization gains. However, strength is tempered in weaknesses.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited theoretical novelty.** Reviewer 1 argues the PCOC simulation is not surprising: since MPC can simulate PCOC and PCOC is a special case, and because "one-hot positional information can easily implement any fixed attention pattern," the theoretical contribution is limited, especially as prior work [1] achieved similar results without positional information. Reviewer 2 similarly states "the perspective of Transformers as a graph convolution network with a learned attention mask is not new" and that PCOC "is strongly reminiscent of message-passing in graphical models." Reviewer 4 (W1) questions how the theory explains *better* OOD performance, noting that dynamic attention (Sanford et al.) should also approximate the same algorithm class.
- **No hypothesis for why a less expressive architecture outperforms standard Transformers.** Reviewer 4 (W2) finds the main claim "surprising and difficult to believe without further details." The classic Transformer is a universal approximator that should be able to learn these tasks; if positional embeddings are concatenated, one can construct a classic Transformer functionally equivalent to any positional Transformer. Scaling inputs by λ scales the pre-softmax attention matrix by λ² (adjusting softmax temperature), making the sensitivity to input scale predictable and expected. Reviewer 4 therefore suspects the reported results may stem from implementation details rather than a fundamental advantage.
- **Experimental design may bias against standard Transformers.** Reviewer 1 (weakness 2) observes that for vanilla Transformers, adding absolute positional information to scalar inputs "significantly impacts the original data" and forces the model to process both value and position via summation, naturally disadvantaging it. They suggest additional experiments using nonparallel vectors to denote each integer and predicting target labels one by one. Reviewer 2 also finds MSE uninformative and asks for accuracy plots. Reviewer 4 (W2) echoes concerns about experimental setup.
- **Limited applicability and practical value.** Reviewer 4 (W3) argues that improving algorithmic reasoning in isolation is "unnecessary" as the tasks can be solved exactly with code; value would only come if used as a subroutine in more complex tasks, which the paper does not study. Reviewer 4 also warns that the rigidity of fixed attention would hurt performance on NLP or mixed tasks that require flexibility. Reviewer 3 (weakness 1) notes that positional attention cannot model relative relationships between different inputs X, and is suitable only for numeric tasks that do not require one-hot encoding, "constraining its utility in a broader range of applications."
- **Missing comparisons and ablations.** Multiple reviewers request additional evaluations:
  - Reviewer 2: "Can you plot accuracy on the tasks you’ve benchmarked on? MSE doesn’t really seem informative here."
  - Reviewer 3: The architecture resembles a learnable adjacency matrix GNN; "could the authors compare the results with those achieved by a GNN under the same sentence length?"
  - Reviewer 1: Suggests using nonparallel vectors and sequential prediction to test superiority more convincingly.
  - Reviewer 2 and Reviewer 4 (Q1) ask for more details on parametrization of P: why use W_q and W_k rather than direct parametrization of A (Eq. 2)? Reviewer 2 also asks which tasks work with just an embedding table vs. requiring prior processing from another Transformer model.
- **Minor issues.** Reviewer 3 notes typos (missing periods on lines 156, 356, 364). Reviewer 2 finds the write-up on PCOC "strongly reminiscent of message-passing in graphical models" and is "not sure what this perspective contributes."
- **Disagreement among reviewers.** While one reviewer (Reviewer 3, score 6) is more positive and emphasizes the value of the theoretical analysis, the others (scores 5,3,5) are more critical, questioning novelty, experimental fairness, and broader relevance.
