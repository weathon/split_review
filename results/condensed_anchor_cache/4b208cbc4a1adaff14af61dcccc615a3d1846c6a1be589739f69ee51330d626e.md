## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a temporal microwell cell dataset of cancer and healthy cells imaged over a 5-day period under various experimental conditions. The authors propose a pipeline: feature extraction via a U-Net encoder plus manual features (cell density, proliferation rate), multi-head attention to aggregate across time points, affinity propagation clustering of image sequences, visual explanation via Grad-CAM using cluster labels, and text explanation based on auxiliary predictions of manual features. A comparison of encoder architectures and an ablation study are provided on the new dataset.

### Strengths
- The dataset is new, publicly available (though availability is not explicitly stated), with unique acquisition protocols relevant to cancer/immunotherapy research.
- The paper addresses an important and well-motivated problem: explainable clustering to gain insight in the absence of ground-truth labels, with potential benefit to the ML-for-healthcare community.
- The pipeline, while largely composed of existing components (U-Net, multi-head attention, Grad-CAM, affinity propagation), demonstrates a feasible engineering approach for processing temporal microwell data.
- The use of clustering to overcome missing ground truth, combined with visual (Grad-CAM) and textual explanation modules, is a sensible design choice.
- Ablation and evaluation studies are presented (though reviewers found them insufficient or unclear).

### Weaknesses
- **Methodological novelty is very limited.** The framework is a direct assembly of widely used existing ideas (U-Net encoder, multi-head attention, manual features, affinity propagation, Grad-CAM, auxiliary classifiers) with basic adaptation to the specific image data. No new algorithmic contributions are offered. One reviewer (rating 1) judged the novelty, clarity, and validation as substantially deficient, more severely than the other three (rating 3).
- **Critical details are missing or unclear:**
  - How pseudo-labels for live/dead cells are derived and used; the loss function for training the feature extractor; the specific architecture and dimensionality of the encoder output.
  - How hand-designed features (density, rate of change) are computed and why neural networks cannot capture them; how these features are integrated with U-Net features.
  - What “preprocess” in Fig. 2 entails.
  - Whether annotations are dense/complete and how they are obtained; Fig. 3 shows overlapping cancer and healthy cell annotations (e.g., the same cell marked as both in Day 0).
  - Whether each individual image or each temporal sequence is clustered (the text is inconsistent).
- **Experimental validation is weak:**
  - No quantitative evaluation of the clustering itself (e.g., against any ground-truth grouping, silhouette scores, or correlation with treatment outcomes). The paper does not show that clusters correspond to meaningful biological or treatment conditions.
  - Comparisons in Table 1 are not adequately described: abbreviations (SE, SP, PC, JS, DC) are unexplained; the task is ambiguous; a U-Net encoder without skip connections is essentially a large CNN, making the ResNet comparison trivial.
  - No qualitative examples or case studies of interesting findings derived from the clusters.
- **Text explanations do not actually explain the clustering.** They are based on manual features predicted by an auxiliary model, not on the features used for clustering, so they cannot justify why images are grouped together.
- **Ablation studies** are not informative or well-described; they lack detail and are written unclearly. More detail should be relegated to supplementary material.
- **Writing and presentation are problematic:** The paper is hard to follow; biological concepts are assumed unclear for an ICLR audience; ML methods are described vaguely; the writing is cluttered with excessive adjectives (e.g., “comprehensive” used 11 times); there are typos (e.g., “IMPLEMENT DETAILS” instead of “IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS”; stray text fragments like “ground truth-remove” and “a text explanation module-remove sentence” in page 2).
- **Dataset justification is missing:** No comparison with or discussion of drawbacks of previous cell datasets; why a new dataset is necessary is not explained.
- **Explanations are not validated by human experts** (e.g., for Grad-CAM or text outputs).
- For the above reasons, reviewers rated soundness 1–2, presentation 1–2, and contribution 1–2.
