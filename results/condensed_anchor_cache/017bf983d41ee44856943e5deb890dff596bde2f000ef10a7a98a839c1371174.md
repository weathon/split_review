## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents AniHead, a text-to-3D head avatar pipeline that uses the FLAME parametric model for shape and a fine-tuned latent diffusion model (with a mean texture token) for texture. Training data is generated via SDS optimization (DreamFusion-style), then used to train feed-forward shape and texture predictors. The method claims fast inference (~1 minute, vs DreamFace’s ~5 minutes) and requires no manually annotated 3D data. Qualitative and quantitative comparisons against DreamFace and other text-to-3D methods are provided.

### Strengths
- **Efficiency**: The method offers substantially faster inference (~1 minute) compared to existing text-to-3D face methods (e.g., DreamFace ~5 minutes). (Reviewer 2)
- **No dependence on annotated datasets**: The pipeline does not require large-scale annotated 3D head data for training – training data is generated via SDS. (Reviewer 1)
- **Use of FLAME**: The FLAME representation enables faster inference and animation. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2)
- **Qualitative quality**: Decent facial quality is achieved; celebrity avatars bear resemblance to real people. (Reviewer 2)
- **Support for diverse prompts**: The method handles specific prompts for special characters (animation-style heads) and supports shape/style editing. (Reviewer 2)
- **Clarity and soundness**: The paper is well-written and easy to follow; most design choices are well-motivated. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The approach heavily borrows from DreamFusion (SDS for training data), DreamFace (CLIP + LDM architecture), and prior identity-token concepts (DreamBooth/DreamFace). The main novel element – the mean texture token – is similar to domain-specific prompt tuning used in DreamFace. The advantage of FLAME over ICT-FaceKit (used by DreamFace) for animation is not clearly demonstrated. (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2)
- **Quality constrained by FLAME representation**: FLAME’s limited expressiveness loses fine geometric details (ears, nose, eyes, subtle variations). The method cannot synthesize 3D hair components, reducing realism. Overall realism is lower than DMTet/NeRF-based approaches. (Reviewer 1)
- **Lower resolution and poor skin color diversity**: The generated texture maps are only 256×256, while DreamFace outputs 4K textures. All generated avatars exhibit nearly identical skin color (e.g., Mark Zuckerberg and Morgan Freeman in Figure 8 have the same skin tone). This indicates severe lack of diversity in age, race, and skin color. (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3)
- **Qualitative results less impressive than DreamFace**: Reviewer 3 found results not as visually compelling as the DreamFace baseline. (Reviewer 3)
- **Training data selection is problematic**: From 600 SDS-optimized samples, only 50 were selected (manually balancing gender, ethnicity, age). 50 samples is extremely small for a generalized generator, and it is unclear why the same balance could not be achieved by simply designing the input text prompts more carefully. (Reviewer 3; also echoed by Reviewer 2’s concern about generalization)
- **Missing evaluations**: No 3D views from non-frontal angles (e.g., side, back) are shown, no user study for perceptual quality, no ablation study quantifying the contribution of the mean texture token, and no failure cases are presented. (Reviewer 2)
- **Unclear writing and missing details**: Several statements are ambiguous (e.g., “cumbersome two-stage generation process”, “specific designs” unlisted, “meticulously crafted to encapsulate essential texture information” not explained). Typos exist (e.g., “our propose”, Equation 2 ϕ should be ε_ϕ). Missing citations for several related works. (Reviewer 2)
- **Technical questions remain unanswered**: 
  - Is the method fundamentally limited to 256×256 textures, or can it scale to 4K? (Reviewer 3)
  - Why is there no variation in skin color despite diverse text prompts? (Reviewer 3)
  - The “data-free” claim is ambiguous because SDS is used to generate training data. How is the accuracy of that training data ensured for generalization? (Reviewer 2)
  - Which specific pretrained LDM is fine-tuned? (Reviewer 2)
  - How is the mean texture token obtained at test time? (Reviewer 2)
  - How is texture consistency with geometry ensured without ground truth UV textures for fine-tuning? (Reviewer 2)
  - Is there any ablation on the guidance scale parameter used in SDS to justify the claim “low value gives more realistic outcomes”? (Reviewer 2)
