## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a multivariate time series imputation method using a deep reservoir computing architecture (DL-DRCN) composed of cascaded echo state network (ESN) layers with decreasing reservoir sizes. A layer-by-layer training scheme is introduced, and theoretical results on echo state property (ESP) and convergence are provided. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets (Rossler, etc.) show imputation performance against baselines including GRU-D. The method interprets missing data imputation as recovering the trajectory of an underlying dynamical system.

### Strengths
- **Originality / Contribution**: The introduced RCN architecture (cascade of ESN layers with readouts between layers) is considered original by most reviewers. The method is well-rooted in reservoir computing literature and straightforward to implement.
- **Theoretical analysis**: Authors provide rigorous analysis of ESP (Proposition 3.1), convergence rates, and complexity. The theoretical grounding in dynamical systems theory is appreciated.
- **Experimental rigor**: Large selection of baselines, hyperparameter studies, and results on both synthetic and real data. Positive imputation performance is demonstrated.
- **Clarity of exposition** (partially): For some reviewers the paper is well-written and logically structured. The motivation and method appear elegant and well-motivated.
- **Relevance**: The topic of missing data imputation in multivariate time series, especially block missingness, is important and timely. The work can have significant impact for spatio-temporal data analytics.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns** (minority reviewer R2 more negative): The approach is seen as essentially a standard deepRC/deepESN architecture with readouts per layer (Gallicchio et al., 2017, 2018). The claim that the method is “fundamentally different” from deep ESN is not substantiated; the structural difference (readout between layers) is not explained in terms of performance or efficiency gains. Reviewer 5 adds that the idea of using RC for imputation is not novel and missing references [2,3] on RC imputation.
- **Weak vanilla baseline comparison**: Missing comparison against a single-layer ESN with basic imputation (e.g., repeatedly applying Eq. 3). Qualitative results for a 1-layer version (similar to Fig. 2) are not shown. A single-layer RC might already perform well on tasks like reconstructing the Rossler system (R4), and the advantage of multiple layers is not clear without ablation. Reviewer 5 notes theoretical reliance on the reservoir containing the ground truth $\bar{Y}$; questioned whether this holds for arbitrary inputs (e.g., constant $U$, non-stationary processes).
- **Theoretical generality flawed** (minority reviewer R2): The error bounds (Theorems/Corollaries on page 6) are proven for $Y_{\omega:T}$ (the portion without missing values), but it is unclear how they generalize to the actual missing portions of the time series. The claim that the algorithm “converges to the ground truth time series $Y_{1:T}$” (puzzling, should be $Y_{\omega:T}$ as clarified by R3). Reviewer 5 adds that the theoretical results assume the reservoir is large enough to contain the complete ground truth, but with the ESP property the reservoir cannot represent arbitrary (e.g., non-stationary, chaotic) sequences; no assumptions about the $U$–$Y$ relationship are made.
- **Empirical analysis limited in scope and depth**:  
  - Only 10% missing values are reported for the real dataset Table 3, while the original baseline paper includes 50% and 90% missingness.  
  - Confidence intervals in Table 2 (top) overlap with GRU-D; statistical significance is not discussed.  
  - Comparisons with more recent spatio-temporal imputation methods (e.g., IJCAI 2024) are missing.  
  - No analysis of how the deep structure affects imputation (e.g., multiple time-scale filtering insights from Gallicchio et al., 2017).
- **Missing ablation experiments**: No experiment isolating the effect of multiple layers vs. a single-layer RC. The benefit of multi-layer reservoir computing over a simple repeated application of Eq. 3 is not demonstrated.
- **Presentation/clarity issues** (multiple reviewers):  
  - Numerous typos and errors: line 134 superscript errors, line 165 “$\boldsymbol{Ar}+\boldsymbol{Bu}\in\mathbb{R}^N$”, line 158 contraction on wrong component, line 163 “activity function”, line 215 “wigth matrix”, line 229/231 “w” instead of $\omega$, line 307 $\mathcal{K}$ vs $k$, line 373 $N_x$ vs $N$, line 394 missing words.  
  - Notation confusion: “reservoir space” defined as row space (p. 5, line 269) vs. usual state space; inconsistent use of $\bar{Y}$ for both line 216 and ground truth; $Y_{1:T}$ vs $Y_{\omega:T}$ in Fig. 1 caption; $u_t$ dimension (p.2: $u_t\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times p}$ vs. $p$-dimensional).  
  - Unclear separation of novel contributions from known literature (e.g., contractivity, dynamical systems interpretation not cited; mixing reservoir computing and ESN as synonyms is incorrect).  
  - Parameters $\omega$ (relaxation time) and $d$ (reservoir node degree) are not defined when first used.
- **Strong assumptions without discussion**: Not all values can be missing from every covariate at once; missing values not allowed at the beginning of the time series (due to transient/ESP). Reviewer 5 questions handling of univariate case (no $U$) and simultaneous missing across all covariates.
- **Lack of hyperparameter discussion**: Hyperparameters (leaky, spectral radius, sparsity) are fixed across datasets except reservoir size, yet the leaky parameter typically depends on signal speed; justification is missing.
- **Efficiency claim unsupported**: The claim of greater efficiency is not quantified experimentally.
- **Incomplete referencing**: The introduction lacks recent RC references (e.g., [1],[2] for latest RC methods). Literature on ESP in deep RC (e.g., [4]) should be cited. The proof of Proposition 3.1 may not be new and should connect to existing criteria (e.g., Yildiz et al., “diagonal Schur stable”).
- **Complexity statement error**: The paper states $O(KN^2T)$ for matrix inversion, but inversion is $O(N^3)$ (correctly noted in Appendix Table 4).
