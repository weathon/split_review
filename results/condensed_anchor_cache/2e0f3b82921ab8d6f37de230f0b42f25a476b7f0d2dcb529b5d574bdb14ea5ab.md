## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies uniform expressivity of GNNs for GC2 logic queries. It proves that uniform expressivity is not possible for GNNs with a wide class of Pfaffian activation functions (including sigmoid, tanh), answering a question by Grohe. However, it shows that such GNNs can still express GC2 queries with number of parameters logarithmic in the maximal degree (almost-uniform expressivity), and even log-log dependency for certain activations. Experiments validate theoretical estimates.

### Strengths
- Well-motivated: rigorously understanding expressive power of GNNs with size/depth quantification provides a novel perspective distinct from graph isomorphism or standard function approximation, and is relevant to real architectures.
- Considers a rich family of activation functions (arctan, tanh, sigmoid) via the general definition of Pfaffian functions, allowing future generalization to other activations.
- Provides both negative and positive results: uniform expressivity limitation and almost-uniform expressivity with logarithmic parameter complexity, bridging the gap between too-strict uniform expressivity and unconstrained general expressivity.
- Fine-grained separation between constant-size and near-constant-size architectures is theoretically interesting and practically relevant since most graphs are small enough for the log-log ∆ scaling.
- Mathematical results appear correct; proof ideas (distinguishing similar tree graphs and constructing separations with step-wise activations) are intuitive.
- Definitions are well-defined and accessible; results are nontrivial and impactful for understanding GNN expressivity.

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and notation errors** (multiple reviewers): Many incomplete sentences, unclear phrasing, and inconsistent notation hinder comprehension. Examples: Theorem 3.1 last sentence does not parse; line 197 “two blue neighbors” should be “red”; Figure 2 lacks $l_i$ labels (presumably leaf nodes); notation $\nu_{t,m}(k)$ vs. $\nu_t(k,m)$ inconsistent; $\sigma^N$ in Definition 5.1 not explained (exponentiation or composition?); Definition 2.4 introduces Pfaffian chain but not Pfaffian functions; Example 2.1 has ambiguous color and undefined $E(x,y)$; Corollary 5.5 says tanh instead of arctan; Figure 3 uses only colors, no linestyles; line 291 limit over vector $y$ unclear; line 250 grammar (“superpolynomial if it non constant”); Definition 2.4 “U” should be “$U$”; page 7 line 350 redundant “the”; difference between Corollaries 5.6 and 5.7 appears to be a typo.
- **Lack of clarity on Pfaffian and step-like activations** (R2, R3): More context is needed: explicit parameters linking tanh/sigmoid to a Pfaffian chain, more examples of step-like activations, and intuitive discussion of how their parameters affect GNN behavior.
- **Step-like activation definition concerns** (R3): (a) ReLU is excluded; is the set of step-like activations large enough to cover most practical cases? Could the maximal set be characterized? (b) The paper does not explain the meaning of $\sigma^N$ in Definition 5.1 (c). (c) Corollary 3.5 relies on sigmoid/tanh being step-like (with $\eta=0$), but Proposition 5.3/5.4 show this only holds when these activations are implemented by multi-layer NNs (3-layer for tanh, 5-layer for sigmoid). This restriction on GNN structure should be mentioned when GNNs are defined, and its practical implications discussed. (d) Using multi-layer NNs as pointwise activation functions raises parameter-growth concerns: when input dimension changes, the number of parameters may grow linearly with input dimension, leading to $O(d^2)$ total parameters, contradicting the claimed logarithmic dependency. The paper does not address this.
- **Unclear experimental connections and missing justification** (R1, R2): (i) How the number of layers in the experiment (line 495) connects to the theoretical results is not explained. (ii) The conjecture about SGD difficulty with step-like activation functions (line 500) is stated without evidence. (iii) Questions about learnability: since (approximate) linear threshold activations are not practical due to lack of bounded derivatives, are the authors concerned about learnability of the constructions in Section 5? (iv) The significance of parameters $H$ and $\epsilon$ in Theorem 5.1 is not explained; can the network weights in that construction be bounded?
- **Potential limited practical impact** (R4): Almost-uniform expressivity may already be sufficient for real graphs, so the limitations of sigmoid/tanh networks may not be severe, and the paper may have limited impact on guiding practical GNN design.
- **Tree definition ambiguity** (R1): Around line 335, the definitions of $T[0,k,m]$ and $T[1,k,m]$ seem to assume a rooted tree from $s$ with no backtracking and that $s$ is not a leaf; this needs clarification.
- **Missing comparison** (R4): Is any unbounded non-linear activation (e.g., ReLU) sufficient for uniform expressivity? The paper does not address this.
