## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CoIC, consisting of a Context-based Instance-specific Modulation (CoI-M) mechanism and a rain-/detail-aware contrastive learning strategy, to train CNN- and Transformer-based models on mixed datasets for single image deraining. The method extracts joint rain-/detail-aware representations capturing both rain and background components, using these to modulate models via CoI-M. Experiments show consistent improvements over baselines (BRN, RCDNet, DGUNet, IDT, DRSformer) and generalization to real scenes (RealInt). Reviewer scores range from 5 to 8; one reviewer (rating 8) found the approach interesting but noted several limitations, while another (rating 5) raised concerns about fairness, generalization, and necessity.

### Strengths
- Well-written, clear, easy to follow (R1, R3, R5).
- Meaningful contribution to the deraining community (R1).
- Consistently improves previous baselines (R1, R3, R5).
- Instance-specific method may generalize to other low-level vision tasks (e.g., dehazing, desnowing, super-resolution) (R1).
- CoI-M mechanism modulates CNN/Transformer using GAP and a feature extractor to capture spatial and channel information (R2).
- Contrastive learning strategy extracts joint rain-/detail-aware representations, using a rain layer bank D_R and a contrastive loss (R2).
- Statistical analysis in Figure 1 (image dominated by rain as density increases) is suitable (R3).
- Experiments are sufficient; results better than others; visualization on RealInt demonstrates generalization to real scenes (R4).
- Works well on RealInt in terms of NIQE (R5).
- Idea of generating negative examples for both rain and background and modulating image-specific information into networks is interesting (R5).

### Weaknesses
- Missing analysis of computational overhead: parameters, FLOPs, and inference time increase (R1, R4).
- Training fairness not confirmed: whether training iterations are identical for all compared methods (BRN, RCDNet, DGUNet, IDT, DRSformer) (R1).
- Request for training curve comparisons for each baseline vs. its CoIC variant (R1).
- The title “Instance-specific” may be misleading because training is on Rain13K as a whole dataset; adopting a continual learning setup would better justify the term (R1).
- Deraining methods with high PSNR/SSIM tend to overfit synthetic data; unclear if CoIC alleviates this for real-world generalization (R1).
- Should consider using real rain images as negative exemplars to improve real-world generalization (R1).
- Overfitting to synthetic data remains a concern despite claims of generalization (R1).
- Inadequate comparisons with existing semi-supervised/transfer/contrastive deraining methods: Memory Oriented Transfer Learning (CVPR 2021), Syn2Real Transfer Learning using Gaussian Processes (CVPR 2020), Unpaired Deep Image Deraining Using Dual Contrastive Learning (CVPR 2022). These are similar to CoIC and should be compared to demonstrate benefits (R2).
- Need to distinguish from contrastive learning in blind super-resolution (R4) and from other contrastive deraining methods (Chen et al., Ye et al., CVPR 2022) (R3). Specifically, differences in loss function, positive/negative pair construction, and handling of coupled rain and background details (R4).
- Unclear how positive and negative exemplars are selected; justification needed and discussion of differences with existing contrastive deraining methods (R3, R5).
- Use of Gaussian blur to construct negative background exemplars needs justification because it may not model occlusions in heavy rain (R5).
- Related work section is insufficient: missing heavy rain restoration methods and literature on modulation techniques (R5).
- Performance gain on strong models like DRSformer and DGUNet is limited (Table 1); benefits may not be consistent across architectures, questioning necessity (R4, R5).
- Visual results not impressive: remaining rain streaks visible in Figure 4 and 12; in Figure 12 (g) comparable to (h), indicating limited improvement. Combined with Table 1, necessity further questioned (R5).
- Observation in Figure 1(b) (image dominated by rain) is superficial; why use contrastive learning when rain density seems the most important factor? The paper claims distinctness from instance-discriminative contrastive learning (He et al., 2020) without explanatory details (R5).
- Introduction’s description of embedding space (not instance-level discriminative, background not discriminative) contradicts the goal of learning instance-specific features and mismatches the method (constructs negatives from rain types and Gaussian blurred background) (R5).
- Claim about long-tail distribution across mixed datasets is not supported; would it be easier to synthesize more rain images? Also unclear whether training on mixed dataset improves individual dataset performance (R5).
- Figure resolution and font sizes are low; should be improved for readability (R3).
