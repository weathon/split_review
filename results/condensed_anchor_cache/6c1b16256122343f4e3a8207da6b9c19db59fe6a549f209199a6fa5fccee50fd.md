## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes GatedMTL, a multi-task learning framework that uses a learnable gating mechanism at each layer for each task to select and mix features from a shared memory bank and task-specific branches. A sparsity regularizer encourages a target amount of sharing to control computational budget. Experiments on CelebA (3 tasks), NYUD-v2, and PASCAL-Context with convolutional and transformer backbones are presented. One reviewer (rating 6) found the method refreshing and well-supported; the other three (rating 5) raised concerns about novelty, insufficient baseline comparisons, presentation clarity, and practical limitations.

### Strengths
- Well-written, intuitive, and easy to understand (R1, R2, R3).
- The focus on the performance vs. compute (FLOPs/size) trade-off is refreshing (R1, R2).
- Extensive empirical evaluations: multiple challenges (CelebA, NYUD-v2, PASCAL-Context), multiple backbones (convolutional and transformer-based) (R1, R2, R4).
- Includes discussion and evaluation of negative transfer and backbone size (R1).
- Achieves competitive performance across different computational budgets (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing and outdated baselines.** The paper omits comparisons with MoE-based MTL methods (MMoE, PLE, DSelect-k, Deep Safe MTL, MSSM) (R3, R4) and recent MTO approaches like PCGrad, CAGrad, NashMTL, and Random Weighting (R1, R4). The included baselines (DWA, Uncertainty) are outdated (R1). The proposed method is very similar to MoE-based architectures but lacks discussion and comparison (R4). Auto-λ results are missing from Tables 3 and 4 (R3).
- **Scaling and training cost.** Training involves T individual models, which scales poorly with the number of tasks in time and memory (R1). The paper reduces CelebA to only 3 tasks (vs. typical 40) without discussing scalability (R1). The claim of comparable training time lacks empirical support (R1). Furthermore, the initialization of task-specific branches with single-task weights (Sec. 4.1) requires training T single-task models first, imposing a large computational overhead and making comparisons unfair to baselines that do not use this initialization (R4). Selecting the hyperparameter τ_t also requires training T+1 STL models and analyzing gating distributions, which is not practical for practitioners (R1).
- **Unclear novelty and justification.** The core idea of gating to balance shared and task-specific features is not new in MTL (R3). The proposed gating mechanism is mathematically similar to a simplified variant of DSelect-k (Eqs. 2, 6) and to existing MoEs, without clear differentiation (R3, R4). The paper lacks a clear explanation of why a gating mechanism is superior to simple parameter sharing or adaptive weighting (R3). Placing task-specific gating in the encoder (rather than decoder) is not justified; the encoder’s role is extracting shared representations, and the gating may disrupt this (R3, R4).
- **Ambiguity in method description and presentation.** Section 3 and Figure 1 are confusing: it is unclear whether the encoder is shared, what Ψ and Φ_t represent, and how shared/task-specific features are obtained per layer (R4). The phrase “for a given computational budget” is never concretely defined; τ_t is not parameter size or FLOPs, and the sparsity regularizer λ_s cannot guarantee a specific target budget (R4). The distinction between the five different GatedMTL variants in Table 1 (and others) is unclear, making results hard to interpret (R3). The metric Δ_MTL is brittle (sensitive to low-magnitude metrics and incomparable task metrics); a rank-based metric would be more robust (R1). No standard deviations across multiple random runs are reported (R2). Parameter sizes should be reported but are missing (R4). The notation is inconsistent: Δ_MTL and Δ are used interchangeably (R3); the symbol ⊙ is not defined (R4); β^l does not appear in the overall objective (Eq. 5) (R4); the choice of ω_t in Eq. (1) is not explained (R4). The bibliography has duplicate references (Kendall, Maninis, Auto-λ, etc.) and incorrect citation formatting (R1, R4). No source code is provided (R2, R4).
- **Additional weaknesses.** Task interference may still occur because the shared feature branch acts as a memory bank from which all task-specific gates can learn (R2). The paper provides no qualitative feature visualization (e.g., feature maps or embeddings) to demonstrate how gating affects representations or separates task-specific and shared features (R3). The paper does not discuss any limitations, which can mislead readers (R1).
- **Reviewer disagreement.** One reviewer (rating 6, confidence 4) was more positive, highlighting the refreshing performance-vs-FLOPs emphasis, extensive validation, and good presentation. The other three reviewers (rating 5) found the novelty marginal, baseline comparisons insufficient, the method hard to distinguish from MoE, and presentation clarity lacking.
