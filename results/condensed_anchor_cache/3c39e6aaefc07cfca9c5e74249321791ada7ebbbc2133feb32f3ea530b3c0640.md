## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes H-PAD, a reconstruction-based hybrid prototypes learning model for multivariate time series anomaly detection (MTSAD). It learns normal prototypes from patches of different sizes to capture short-term (point) anomalies and period prototypes to capture periodical anomalies, aiming to address over-generalization. Evaluation on five benchmark datasets shows state-of-the-art performance against several baselines, with ablation studies confirming the contribution of each component.

### Strengths
- The authors are familiar with current SOTA baselines and evaluate against them on five datasets (Reviewer 1).
- Clear motivation: existing models can only detect point anomalies; H-PAD addresses this by combining local (patch) and global (period) prototypes (Reviewer 2, 3).
- The architecture design using query-based reconstruction in both time and frequency domains is motivated and explained with technical details (Reviewer 1).
- The reconstruction approach using both time and frequency features, together with sparsity and periodicity constraints, helps avoid over-generalization (Reviewer 3).
- Comprehensive experiments including ablation studies validate the importance of patch and period prototypes (Reviewer 1, 4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Writing quality and clarity**: Several non-standard terms need definition, e.g., “different local sizes”, “local” (spatial within series or general?), and “patch” (fixed-length segments? overlapping handling?). The term ‘local’ is vague (Reviewer 1). The definition of how local vs. periodic information is extracted should be elaborated (Reviewer 1, 3). There are typos such as “reference phase” for “inference phase” (p.1), “learn and memory prototypes” for “learn memory prototypes” (p.2) (Reviewer 1). Also line 65: “MSTAD” should be “MTSAD” (Reviewer 3). The text in Figures 1, 3, and 4 is too small (Reviewer 4, 5). Figure 1 should be optimized to better describe the motivation (Reviewer 2). The principle of mapping to a higher dimension (D > C) is not clear; if C is already large, will the method still be effective? (Reviewer 5).

2. **Missing implementation details and reproducibility**:
   - How are patch sizes {z1, …, zm} and k (top-k amplitudes of FFT) selected? Are they hyperparameters or data-derived? (Reviewer 1, 4). The paper should clarify that z1=1 corresponds to the original series X (Reviewer 3).
   - The mechanism of memory networks/prototypes (memory implementation, initialization, update during training) lacks detailed explanation (Reviewer 1).
   - The update gate and linear transformation matrices U_z, W_p (line 201) are introduced abruptly; their function and relation to context are unclear (Reviewer 3).
   - The simple weighted average fusion of local and periodic reconstruction (direct averaging of temporal and frequency domain reconstructions) may cause information loss; the rationale for this approach is not justified, especially since the two domains have different characteristics (Reviewer 3, 1).
   - Optimization algorithm, learning rate schedule, early stopping criteria are not specified (Reviewer 1).
   - Code is not provided, raising reproducibility concerns (Reviewer 5).
   - The setting of all hyperparameters (e.g., sparsity constraint, alpha_1/2/3 in loss function) for both H-PAD and baselines is not detailed; alpha parameters appear manually adjusted but no approximate ranges or adaptive method is described (Reviewer 3, 5).

3. **Lack of rigorous evaluation and missing experiments**:
   - Affiliation precision/recall and RAP/RAR are used but not mathematically defined; justification for using these over standard precision/recall/AUC is missing (Reviewer 1). Given class imbalance, AUC should be added to Table 1 (Reviewer 2).
   - The performance gain in Table 1 is marginal; testing on 3 more datasets is recommended (Reviewer 5).
   - No parameter sensitivity experiments (e.g., effect of patch sizes, periodic lengths, sparsity constraint values) are conducted (Reviewer 3, 1).
   - The datasets may not contain significant non-point anomalies; visualization in Figure 5 appears to show only point anomalies (period anomaly in Figure 5(c) seems to have same period but anomalous amplitude, so why is it classified as period anomaly?) (Reviewer 1, 2). The paper should add more types of anomalies in visualization analysis to demonstrate H-PAD’s benefits (Reviewer 2). Evaluation on simulated time series with controlled anomaly types could further verify that patch and period prototypes capture multi-scale and multi-period information (Reviewer 1).
   - Raw precision, recall, and AUC metrics are missing (Reviewer 1). How does model performance change with dimensionality? (Reviewer 1).
   - The cases in Figure 5 are overly simple and do not reflect the advantage of the proposed method (Reviewer 5).

4. **Missing connection between claimed overfitting problem and method design**: The paper claims existing algorithms overfit to anomalies, but the method design (multiple patches, periodic prototypes) does not explain how it mitigates this issue. Why does using multiple patches prevent overfitting when current algorithms also leverage contextual information? The rationale and thought process behind design choices (e.g., patch sizes, prototype update, sparsity constraint) are not justified (Reviewer 4, 1). The sparsity constraint’s implementation and role are not explained clearly (Reviewer 3). As a result, the mathematical formulations are not clearly connected to the intuitive idea of learning different patches and periodic prototypes (Reviewer 4).

5. **Lack of related work**: The paper misses relevant works such as “Joint Selective State Space Model and Detrending for Robust Time Series Anomaly Detection” (Reviewer 5). Also, the advantages of the proposed FFT-based decomposition over common frequency-domain methods are not discussed (Reviewer 4, question 3).

6. **Limitations discussion missing**: The paper does not discuss computational complexity, sensitivity to hyperparameters, performance on different types of time series data, or extension to streaming/online anomaly detection (Reviewer 1). The weight parameters in the loss function lack explanation of their basis, making generalization to new datasets difficult (Reviewer 3). Statement in contribution 2 “reconstruct abnormal series to be normal ones” is inaccurate and needs clarification (Reviewer 3, 4).
