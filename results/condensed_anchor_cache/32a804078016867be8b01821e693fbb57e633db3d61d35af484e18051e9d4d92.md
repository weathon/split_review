## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces TEST, a TS-for-LLM method that transforms time series (TS) data into LLM-understandable embeddings without fine-tuning the LLM. TEST tokenizes TS data, trains an encoder via three contrastive objectives (instance-wise, feature-wise, text-prototype-aligned) to align the TS embedding space with the LLM’s embedding layer, and uses learnable soft prompts to help the frozen LLM process TS. Experiments on classification, forecasting, and representation tasks with eight frozen LLMs (including LLaMa-13B, GPT2, BERT) show that TEST achieves comparable or better performance than SOTA TS models, particularly in few-shot and generalization settings. The paper also provides a theoretical justification for the TS-for-LLM paradigm.

### Strengths
- The exploration of using LLMs for TS without extensive pre-training or fine-tuning is an intriguing and practically accessible research direction. (R1, R4)
- The non-fine-tuning approach achieves performance comparable to supervised baselines, demonstrating effectiveness in few-shot settings. (R1)
- The paper clearly identifies the core difficulty: creating embeddings that the LLM can understand. (R3)
- The TEST method proposes a novel, similarity-based, instance-wise, feature-wise, and text-prototype-aligned embedding, effectively enabling LLMs to handle TS without compromising language ability. (R2, R3)
- The writing and explanation of the method (including Algorithm 1) are straightforward and clear. (R2, R3)
- The theoretical analysis about casting TS data into patterns and then translating them into text is an interesting hypothesis. (R3)
- The experiment design is comprehensive, covering seven dimensions and five baseline models; the improvements of TEST are clearly visualized (shaded pink area in top-left Figure 3) and are quite significant. (R3)
- Introducing TS embeddings to LLMs (rather than searching for prompts) is a strong point; freezing the LLM yields competitive performance, especially with larger models (e.g., LLaMa-13B outperforms others). (R4)
- The paper shows potential for few-shot learning and generalization, and the embeddings learned (composed mainly of adjectives/sentiments) are interesting to analyze. (R2, R4)
- The paper is well-structured and easy to read. (R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Prototype selection unclear:**
    - The paper lacks clarity on how the representative prototypes (value, shape, frequency) are chosen. Are they derived from training data or fixed a priori? If derived, what algorithm/method is used? Are they selected randomly or via a principled approach? (R1)
    - The statement “we choose P representative text embedding tp as pivots/prototypes” does not specify what type of text P represents. (R2)
- **Missing ablation studies:**
    - No ablation study examines the necessity of the decoder component. Experiments without the decoder should be run to demonstrate its role. (R1)
    - Ablation studies on the proposed components and objectives are missing: performance without soft-prompt, without feature-wise objective, and isolated contributions of instance-wise, feature-wise, and text-prototype objectives individually and in combination. (R1)
- **Incomplete explanation of contrastive learning details:**
    - The paper assumes non-trivial familiarity with contrastive learning. The “jitter-and-scale” augmentation strategy is not explained: what are the mathematical operations, parameters, and ranges used for these transformations on TS data? (R4)
- **No code provided:** Reproducibility is hindered; at least pseudocode for key algorithms (contrastive loss, embedding process) should be provided. (R4)
- **No zero-shot evaluations:** Despite the motivation, the model is not evaluated on unseen datasets after training on one dataset, limiting assessment of generalization. (R4)
- **No time execution analysis:** Training time for the embedding model and inference time are not reported, broken down by component or LLM size (e.g., comparison between LLaMa-13B and BERT-110M). (R4)
- **Results only aggregated across datasets:** Performance per dataset (especially for forecasting) is not shown, making it difficult to assess method strengths/weaknesses on individual tasks. (R4)
- **Forecasting gap not analyzed:** There is a significant gap between TS4LLM, LLM4TS, and traditional methods in forecasting. The paper does not analyze the reasons for this discrepancy. (R2)
- **Scalability and generalizability concerns:**
    - The results seem “too good to be true.” Testing one of the used prompts on an open-source model would improve reception and show scalability to other frozen LLMs. (R3)
    - No concrete example of how TS data is transformed into the format consumable by the LLM; a picture illustrating the connection between TS data and text data to the LLM would help readers understand input/output. (R3)
    - It is not clear whether the method would scale to other frozen LLMs beyond those tested. (R3)
- **Categorization issue:** Is it appropriate to isolate LLM+TS into two distinct categories (LLM4TS and TS4LLM) when they can be fused (e.g., using adapters after obtaining good TS embeddings, then fine-tuning with LLM4TS)? (R2)
- **Additional missing baselines and analyses:**
    - One-fits-all is not used in all forecasting experiments; why? (R4)
    - Other baselines like DeepAR, MQCNN, Wavenet, NHiTS, NBeats, ARIMA, ETS, seasonal naive are not considered. (R4)
    - For prompt length, smaller models (GPT-117M) perform better, contradicting the observation that larger models generally perform better; how does this fit? (R4)
    - LLM-QA shows no variance; why is it depicted that way? (R4)
    - The analysis of embeddings per model (e.g., BERT-335M, GPT2-774M) is missing. (R4)
    - It is unclear if the forecaster is point-wise or probabilistic. (R4)
    - Performance when using a random (first checkpoint) LLM is not reported; what is the gap compared to pretrained weights? (R4)
- **Typos:** Figure 3.g: “spesific” → “specific”; Figure 2: “Fine-turn” → “Fine-tune”. (R4)
- **Questions raised by reviewers that overlap with missing experiments/ablation:**
    - Would parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA outperform soft prompts? (R1)
    - What is the limiting performance in terms of model size? (R4)
    - Are there concrete examples of TS transformation and LLM output? Could they be added to the appendix? (R3)
    - Will the encoder and soft-prompt be released? (R3)
