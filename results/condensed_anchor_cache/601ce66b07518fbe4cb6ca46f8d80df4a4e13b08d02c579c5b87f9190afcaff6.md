## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Self-Alignment Optimization (SAO), a framework that aligns language models without external human or AI-labeled feedback. SAO uses persona-based role-play (seeded from Persona-Hub) to generate diverse prompts, produces two responses per prompt, self-ranks them via the model as judge, and applies preference optimization (SimPO) on this self-generated dataset. Experiments on Gemma-2-9B-it and Llama-3-Instruct-8B show improvements on AlpacaEval 2.0 (e.g., Gemma reaches 69.2% LC, +18.1%, and 66.0% WR), MT-Bench, and Arena-Hard, while maintaining performance on downstream NLP tasks (e.g., GSM8K, MMLU). Ablations examine dataset size, diversity, and optimization methods.

### Strengths

- **Resource efficiency**: The framework removes the need for expensive human annotations or external AI-labeled preference data, making alignment more accessible and cost-effective, especially for smaller organizations. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5)
- **Substantial alignment improvements**: Models fine-tuned with SAO achieve large gains on alignment-specific benchmarks: Gemma-2-9B-it-SAO reaches 69.2% LC and 66.0% WR on AlpacaEval 2.0, and its Arena-Hard WR jumps from 52.6% to 70.1%. Llama-3-8B-Instruct-SAO shows a 16.4% WR increase on AlpacaEval 2.0 and a 16.1% WR increase on Arena-Hard. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4, 5)
- **Comprehensive evaluation and ablations**: The paper systematically evaluates on multiple alignment benchmarks (AlpacaEval 2.0, MT-Bench, Arena-Hard) and objective tasks (Open LLM Leaderboard). Ablations in Figure 3 explore dataset size, optimization algorithms, persona role-play influence, and self-judgment capabilities. (Reviewers 1, 2, 5)
- **Persona-based prompt generation**: Using a persona library to seed diverse prompts is a novel contribution that enriches input diversity and shows effective results. (Reviewers 3, 5; note: one reviewer [score 6] highlighted this as a strength, while others considered the overall novelty limited)
- **No regression on downstream tasks**: SAO-tuned models maintain or slightly improve performance on objective NLP benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K, MMLU), indicating alignment gains do not come at the cost of general capabilities. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4 note minimal improvement)

### Weaknesses

- **Limited technical novelty**: Most components (persona-based prompt generation, self-generation/self-judgment, SimPO) are adapted from prior work. The main difference is using a persona library to seed prompt generation. The paper lacks a detailed analysis of how combining these known components yields emergent or synergistic effects beyond their individual contributions. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5; one reviewer [6] considered the persona-based generation novel)
- **Unsubstantiated "dataset-free" claim**: The framework is not fully data-free; it uses a persona library (Persona-Hub) as a seed. The claim should be “free of external annotators and prompt spaces.” (Reviewers 4, 5)
- **Small improvements over direct counterparts**: SAO’s gains compared to using SimPO with external datasets (e.g., Gemma-2-9B-it-SAO vs. Gemma-2-9B-it-SimPO) are minor. This suggests self-generated preference data may currently be less effective than external data. (Reviewer 1)
- **Lack of comparison with existing prompt spaces and reward models**: If persona-based generation is a key novelty, experiments should compare against using existing open-source prompt spaces (e.g., UltraFeedback, HelpSteer). If self-judge is a key contribution, comparisons to external trained reward models or other LLM-as-a-judge settings are needed; reward accuracy experiments would help clarify. (Reviewer 5)
- **Insufficient comparison with prior self-alignment work**: The paper does not compare or discuss relevant methods like Self-Rewarding LMs, SALMON, OAIF, or SAMI, which also explore self-improvement. (Reviewer 2)
- **Evaluation consistency issues**: The paper places heavy emphasis on AlpacaEval 2.0, risking overfitting to one benchmark. The main results for baseline + SAO should be combined across all three alignment benchmarks in one table (like SimPO’s presentation). (Reviewers 1, 5)
- **Methodological concerns about evaluation**:
  - Using GPT-4o-mini as a judge for Arena-Hard and MT-Bench is questionable given its relative weakness. (Reviewer 2)
  - Table 2 uses few-shot settings for instruct models; zero-shot would be more appropriate. (Reviewer 4)
  - Improvements on Open LLM Leaderboard are minimal to non-existent. (Reviewers 2, 4)
- **Surprising performance gap requires further analysis**: The improvements on AlpacaEval 2.0 are very large (+18.1% LC for Gemma, +10.4% for Llama) but no corresponding improvement on downstream NLP tasks. This discrepancy needs deeper investigation, including examining the data distributions and potential biases in self-generated data. (Reviewers 1, 4, 5)
- **Dependence on initial model capability**: SAO likely requires a well-performing base model for effective self-alignment. The paper only tests on 8B/9B models; it is unclear if smaller models (1B, 3B) would be able to generate coherent responses and judge reliably. (Reviewer 3)
- **Output format consistency risk**: Models may fail to produce outputs in the desired format during self-generation or self-judgment, leading to training instability. The paper does not describe corrective measures, filtering, or constraints. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing experiments and questions left unanswered**:
  - How does SAO compare when using existing prompts (e.g., from a public dataset) vs. persona-based prompts, keeping all else fixed? (Reviewer 3)
  - Why are different prompts used for MT-Bench and AlpacaEval? (Reviewer 3)
  - Why is there such a large difference between SAO’s effectiveness on Llama-3 and Gemma-2? Possibly because Gemma-2 is a better judge when prompted; an experiment comparing LLM-as-a-judge accuracy between the two models would help. (Reviewer 5)
  - Experiments with larger models (≥70B) are needed to support the claims. (Reviewer 4)
- **Practical relevance not fully substantiated**: The paper does not clarify scenarios where being fully dataset-free provides measurable benefits over, e.g., using a single improved RLHF technique like SimPO. Alignment research often values large performance gains over data cost alone. The efficiency trade-offs vs. cheaper alignment techniques (prompting, representation editing, test-time decoding) are not discussed. (Reviewer 1)
- **Section 3 is overly long and contains common knowledge**; the paper could be shortened. (Reviewer 1)
