## Merged Review

### Summary
HawkesVAE combines Neural Hawkes Process (NHP) with VAE to generate synthetic clinical event sequences (event type + time-gap). It is evaluated on seven oncology trial datasets against LSTM VAE, PARSynthesizer, and DDPM using downstream death classification (ROC-AUC), ML inference score (real-vs-synthetic discriminator), and Distance to Closest Record (DCR). The method generally outperforms baselines on average, but confidence intervals often overlap. Reviewers are split (scores 5,5,3,3): two find the problem important and the combination interesting, while the other two raise significant concerns about novelty, missing baselines, clarity, and unconvincing empirical strength.

### Strengths
- Addresses the challenging and important problem of generating synthetic medical time-series / sequential competing-risk events, especially from limited data (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- The proposed combination of Hawkes Process and VAE is interesting and sensible (Reviewers 1,2).
- Experiments are appropriately chosen, thorough, and use multiple real-world datasets (Reviewer 4).
- The approach appears sound (Reviewer 4).
- Uses three complementary metrics (downstream task performance, ML inference score, DCR) to quantify synthetic data quality (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns**: The paper is not the first to combine VAEs with Hawkes processes. Prior work includes Sun et al. (2021), Lin et al. (2021), and Pan et al. (2020). Reviewer 3 considers it a straightforward application of NHP and VAE; Reviewer 4 asks what the primary novelty is beyond the logit-based sampling scheme.
- **Missing baselines**: Several relevant methods are not compared: Transformer Hawkes Process (Zuo et al., 2020), Cox-Hawkes (Miscouridou et al., 2022), and the above VAE+Hawkes works. CTGAN is cited but not included as a baseline (Reviewers 1,4).
- **Empirical performance not compelling**: While HawkesVAE often shows the best average numbers, confidence intervals frequently overlap with DDPM on both ROC-AUC and ML inference score, and many results are not statistically significant. Reviewers 3 and 4 note that bolding HawkesVAE in Tables 2,3,5 is misleading when baselines perform similarly or better (e.g., overlapping CIs not bolded). Reviewer 1 questions why training on synthetic HawkesVAE data occasionally beats training on original data.
- **Presentation and readability**: Reviewers 1 and 3 find the presentation poor: multiple typos (“mulidimensional”, missing punctuation), missing parentheses and citations, unconventional notation (capital Δ instead of ∇), missing φ in ELBO KL-divergence, incomplete sentences (contribution 3, p.3 “h(t)s”). Reviewer 4, in contrast, finds the paper generally well written.
- **Lack of clarity on model specifics**:
    - Parametrization of \(p_\theta(S_z|z)\) and \(q_\phi(z|S_z)\) and how \(z\) is used in the log-likelihood are not described (Reviewer 3).
    - The decoder sampling scheme (greedy vs. logit-based) is not provided formally or mathematically (Reviewer 4).
    - Encoder and decoder functions are unspecified (Reviewer 3).
    - Figure 1 is unclear: how event indicator [0,1,1,0,0] pairs with two event time vectors [1,3,5,6] and [1,2,3] (total seven time points) and with event lengths [4,3]; whether the model outputs event types or requires them at inference (Reviewers 2,3).
- **Downstream task definition unclear**: Is the classification task binary prediction of death? Are death events removed from the end of training trajectories? What happens if a trajectory has no events? (Reviewer 1). How is the external death label incorporated—predicting time of death or binary occurrence only? (Reviewer 2).
- **Handling of event types and severity**: It is not specified what event types are available (e.g., general vs. serious adverse events), how severity is encoded, or whether generating a serious event changes subsequent event probabilities differently than a minor event (Reviewer 2).
- **Context for constraining event types**: Reviewer 1 questions in what application one would know or constrain event types within a sequence at inference, and why giving more information (“Events Known”) leads to less accurate order.
- **Motivation for synthetic data**: The benefits of using synthetic data over real data (e.g., privacy) are not explicitly motivated, especially since the generative model requires real data for training (Reviewer 3).
- **Sequence length assumption**: Does the model require the same sequence length for all patients? (Reviewer 4).
- **Error reporting not specified**: It is unclear whether reported errors are confidence intervals or standard errors (Reviewer 1).
