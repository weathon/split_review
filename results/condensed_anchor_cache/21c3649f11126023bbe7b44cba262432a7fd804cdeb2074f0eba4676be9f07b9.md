## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ProteinAdapter, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method that integrates frozen sequence and structure embeddings from pre-trained large protein models (ESM-1b and ESM-IF1) using a Mamba-based fusion module, followed by a multi-scale predictor for downstream tasks. The method is tested on over 20 tasks (PEER, ATOM3D, etc.) and claims state-of-the-art performance in single-task and multi-task settings. One reviewer rated the paper a 5, praising its clarity and extensive experiments. The other four reviewers rated it a 3, acknowledging the approach’s computational efficiency and competitive results but raising major concerns about novelty, baseline transparency, limited improvement over ESM-1b alone, and insufficient rationale for the Mamba architecture.

### Strengths
- Well-written paper with a clear narrative that effectively explains motivation and method details (Reviewer 1).
- Extensive experiments across over 20 tasks, demonstrating competitiveness against state-of-the-art methods (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5).
- Innovative use of a Mamba-based adapter for merging 1D sequence and 3D structure embeddings, which is computationally efficient (Reviewers 2, 4).
- The method efficiently leverages frozen pre-trained large protein models with few additional trainable parameters, offering a compute-efficient alternative (Reviewers 2, 4, 5).
- Table 6 shows better performance with fewer trainable parameters and higher training efficiency (Reviewer 4).
- The multi-scale predictor is well motivated by biological properties of proteins (Reviewer 4).
- The topic of studying multi-scale complementarity of sequences and structures is well highlighted (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited improvement over ESM-1b**: In Table 1 and Table 4, ProteinAdapter performs similarly to ESM-1b, making it unclear whether the additional complexity is justified (Reviewers 1, 5).
- **Insufficient baseline comparison detail**: It is not specified how baseline methods were implemented (e.g., fine-tuning vs. linear probing, full model vs. partial fine-tuning) or whether numbers were cited or reproduced, undermining fairness assessment (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Mamba rationale missing**: No clear justification is provided for choosing Mamba over alternatives (e.g., LSTM, Transformer, cross-attention). The claimed advantage of Mamba (fast long-sequence processing) is not relevant for typical protein tasks (Reviewers 1, 3, 4, 5). Ablation substituting Mamba with cross-attention is missing (Reviewer 2).
- **Novelty concerns**: The idea of combining sequence and structure embeddings has been explored in prior works (e.g., ESM-GearNet, SaProt, PromptProtein). The contributions (multi-level complementarity and multi-scale modeling) are not substantially novel, and the method feels like “gluing together pLMs and inverse folding models like Lego blocks” (Reviewers 3, 4, 5).
- **Ablation study incomplete**: Only GO and EC tasks are ablated; it is unclear whether these are sequence-only or sequence-structure tasks. Missing comparisons: fine-tuning last layers of ESM-IF1 for 3D/(3+1)D tasks, substituting Mamba with cross-attention, comparing multi-scale predictor to a Transformer or MLP within the same framework, and testing other aggregation methods (e.g., pooling) (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Data leakage concern**: In multi-task learning and the Fold Classification task (Table 2), auxiliary tasks involve structure-related information, and the “without structure” ablation replaced structural input with a sequence embedding layer rather than using real or pseudo structures from PDB, rendering the control invalid (Reviewer 4).
- **Misleading “multi-task” terminology**: The paper trains individual tasks with an auxiliary secondary structure loss but evaluates only the main task, which is not true multi-task learning. A true multi-task evaluation would test all tasks together (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Missing comparisons**: (3+1)D experiments (Table 5) omit comparison with SaProt (from Table 2) without explanation (Reviewer 1). A comparison with ESM3 is missing (Reviewer 5). Only ESM-1b and ESM-IF1 are used; generality to other LPMs (e.g., ESM-2 variants) is not explored (Reviewer 5).
- **No discussion of limitations or insights**: The paper presents empirical improvements but does not analyze failure cases, biases of pre-trained models, or scientific insights gained beyond benchmark numbers (Reviewers 3, 5).
- **Writing and presentation issues**: Missing citations for Mamba and for background claims in the introduction; citations for sequence models are outdated (3-4 years old); SaProt is not described in the main text; experimental settings are referenced to another paper rather than fully stated in supplementary materials; typo “blod” for “bold” in figure captions, missing arrows in Figure 3; vague dataset descriptions without statistical details and full sources (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **No efficiency data**: Training time and inference time are not reported, only parameter count (Reviewer 4).
