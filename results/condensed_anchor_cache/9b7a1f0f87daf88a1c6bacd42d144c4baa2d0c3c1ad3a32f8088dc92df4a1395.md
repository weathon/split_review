## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Constrained Diffusion Implicit Models (CDIM) for solving noisy linear inverse problems using pretrained diffusion models. It modifies the DDIM sampling process to enforce a constraint on the final output: exact recovery for noiseless observations, and a constraint on the residual distribution for noisy observations (via KL divergence). Experiments on FFHQ and ImageNet 1k demonstrate faster inference (e.g., ~2 seconds vs. ~70 seconds for DPS) and improved FID scores. The method also includes early stopping based on residual variance and an adaptive learning rate.

### Strengths
- **Efficiency**: CDIM achieves 10–50× wall‑clock speedup over previous conditional diffusion methods (e.g., DPS). Specific numbers: ~2 s vs. ~70 s for DPS (Reviewer 4). It is faster than both DDPM‑based (DPS) and DDIM‑based (DDRM) baselines (Reviewer 1).
- **Exact recovery for noiseless problems**: By incorporating the forward linear operator as a hard constraint, CDIM exactly satisfies the constraint when observations are noise‑free (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).
- **General noise model**: The method handles general noise models, not only Gaussian, via a KL‑divergence constraint on the residual distribution (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). A new choice of η ensures convergence for KL optimization and stable L² optimization (Reviewer 3).
- **Core idea**: The combination of the DDIM sampling process with an optimization perspective to maintain alignment between the posterior mean and the observation is a clear contribution (Reviewer 2).
- **Well‑written and comprehensive**: The paper is easy to follow and conducts thorough experiments on efficiency and accuracy of different diffusion‑based posterior sampling algorithms (Reviewer 2). The modification of DDIM to optimize Tweedie estimates of \(\hat{x}_0\) is clearly presented (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited contribution**: Multiple reviewers note that the core acceleration stems from switching from DDPM to DDIM, which is already known (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4). The performance improvement over DPS is not clearly attributed to the proposed constraint versus the mere use of DDIM (Reviewer 4). The contribution is considered incremental; one reviewer gave soundness 2 (others 2,2,3) (Reviewer 1,2,3,4).
- **Early stopping criterion unclear**: The rationale for using the variance of residuals as an early‑stopping proxy is not explained. The connection between minimizing KL divergence (noiseless case) and minimizing squared error via early stopping is not established. The paper lacks discussion on how the variance of residuals relates to the actual noise level and how this varies with different noise distributions, especially for general (non‑Gaussian) noise (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3). The necessity of early stopping is not clarified, and its effectiveness in noise‑agnostic tasks is doubted (Reviewer 3).
- **KL optimization underperforms L² optimization**: In Tables 1 and 2, L² optimization achieves better performance than KL optimization on most tasks, making the KL‑based approach appear less meaningful (Reviewer 3). The method is also less effective than DSG (not compared) on FFHQ and ImageNet (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing baselines and comparisons**: The paper does not compare with recent methods that also improve efficiency or handle inverse problems with diffusion models, such as DSG (Reviewer 3), DMPlug (Reviewer 2), latentDPS, blindDPS, fastEM, and other arXiv works (Reviewer 4). Including these would better position the contribution.
- **Unclear source of performance gain**: It is not shown how much improvement the optimization part provides over naive DDIM (without constraint) (Reviewer 2). The best result with increased optimization steps is not reported (Reviewer 2). A complexity analysis breaking down computational cost per step is missing (Reviewer 1).
- **Equation clarity and typos**: Several equations lack clarity. Equations (6) and (7) would benefit from explicit inclusion of \(x_{t-1}\) (e.g., \(x_{t-1} = f_\theta(x_t)=...\)) (Reviewer 4). The explanation around lines 195–202 is confusing regarding the use of Tweedie’s formula to estimate \(\hat{x}_0\) vs. \(x_0\) (Reviewer 4). There is a deductive error in Eq. (4): should be \((1-\alpha_t)\nabla_{x_t}\log q(x_t)\) not \(\sqrt{1-\alpha_t}\) (Reviewer 3). Additional typographical errors: page 9 line 482 missing closing parenthesis; page 13 line 672 “CALCULATIONG”; line 696 “Gaussian Kernel of size ‘61x61’ ~” (Reviewer 1).
- **Misleading “3D” claim**: The abstract claims “3D point cloud reconstruction,” but the experiment is only 2D image completion based on projected points, which is far from true 3D reconstruction (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing PSNR in Table 1**: PSNR measurements are not reported, while FID is given. This limits comprehensive performance assessment (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).
- **Missing quantitative results for additional applications**: Time‑Travel Rephotography and Sparse Point Cloud Reconstruction are shown only qualitatively. Quantitative metrics would strengthen the evaluation (Reviewer 1).
- **Naming inconsistency**: The paper uses “conditional diffusion implicit models (CDIM)” in the text but “Constrained Diffusion Implicit Models” in the title, which may cause confusion with existing conditional diffusion models (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of mathematical details**: More derivations for equations such as Eq. 13 and Eq. 14 would be helpful (Reviewer 2). The calculation of Var(r) is not clearly shown (Reviewer 3).
