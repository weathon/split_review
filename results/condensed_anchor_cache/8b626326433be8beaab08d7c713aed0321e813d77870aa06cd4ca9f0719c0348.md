## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes Self-Contemplation (SEC) prompting, a variant of in-context learning (ICL) that removes the need for human-crafted demonstrations. SEC operates in two steps: first the LLM generates demonstrations conditioned on the query sample, then those demonstrations together with the query are used for the final prediction. Experiments on arithmetic reasoning, commonsense reasoning, multi-task language understanding, and code generation benchmarks show that SEC generally outperforms zero-shot learning and achieves results comparable to ICL with hand-crafted demonstrations, especially when using GPT-3.5-turbo. However, SEC underperforms on other GPT models, and concerns about generality, bias propagation, and analysis depth are raised.

### Strengths

- The paper is well written, clear, and easy to follow (R1, R2).
- The proposed method is simple and straightforward to understand (R1, R3).
- The experimental evaluation covers a wide range of standard tasks (arithmetic reasoning, commonsense reasoning, multi-task language understanding, code generation) and is well-designed (R1, R4).
- The idea of relying solely on the target LLM to generate demonstrations is interesting, as it may produce demonstrations better suited to the query sample (R2).
- SEC eliminates the time-consuming process of curating hand-crafted demonstrations, offering a more scalable approach to ICL (R2, R3, R4).
- The finding that a model can generate its own examples and match human-crafted demonstrations is insightful and suggests a meaningful, scalable alternative to traditional ICL (R4).
- While three reviewers rated the paper 6, one reviewer (score 8) was significantly more positive, emphasizing the thoroughness of experiments and the importance of moving beyond hand-crafted demonstrations.

### Weaknesses

- **Significance / novelty is limited**: The method can be viewed as a specific two-step chain-of-thought (CoT) usage (first generate demonstrations, then use them), making the contribution appear incremental (R1, confidence 3).
- **Missing comparisons with automated demonstration selection**: The paper only compares to hand-crafted ICL. Several automated demonstration selection and curation methods exist (e.g., ACL 2023 findings, arXiv:2211.04486, arXiv:2310.10707, arXiv:2302.05698, arXiv:2104.08786) and SEC likely underperforms against these more sophisticated baselines. These should be included (R2, confidence 5).
- **Bias propagation**: Relying on the LLM to generate demonstrations can amplify existing model biases; SEC does not address this issue (R2).
- **Limited model scope**: Only closed-source GPT models from OpenAI (primarily GPT-3.5-turbo) were tested. It remains unclear whether SEC generalizes to other LLMs, especially open-source models. An analysis across multiple language models is needed for scientifically universal conclusions (R2, R3, R4; R3 confidence 4).
- **Contradiction in demonstration quality claim**: The authors claim self-generated demonstrations are better than hand-crafted ones, yet performance drops as the number of demonstrations increases. A qualitative analysis verifying the quality claim is missing (R2).
- **Insufficient analysis of demonstration correctness**: The analysis in Section 4 and Appendix B examines only 20 correct and 20 incorrect predictions—far too few for statistical significance. A deeper investigation is needed into why incorrect few-shot demonstrations can lead to correct final predictions (and vice versa), including error type classification and how the model interprets flawed examples (R3, confidence 4).
- **Performance degradation from wrong self-generated demonstrations**: Once the LLM generates incorrect demonstrations, overall performance may drop significantly. The authors should report whether any domains experienced such declines (R1, question overlaps with criticism).
- **Missing direct baseline**: A direct comparison with Auto-CoT (Zhang et al., 2022) is necessary to contextualize SEC’s performance (R4).
- **Lack of combined approach**: It would be illuminating to test whether combining human-crafted demonstrations with SEC-generated ones improves coverage of correctly answered questions (e.g., on GSM8K) (R4, question as missing experiment).
- **Applicability limited to large models**: The method likely works only with sufficiently capable LLMs that make few reasoning errors; the paper would benefit from a systematic evaluation of when SEC is usable (R4, confidence 4).
- **Presentation issues**: Figure 1 contains typos (“follwing”, “Demonetration”). Table 2 is difficult to read due to cramped spacing (R4).
