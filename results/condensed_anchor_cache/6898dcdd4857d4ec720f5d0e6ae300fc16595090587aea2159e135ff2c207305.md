## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proves that contrastive learning with the standard InfoNCE loss (SimCLR) is equivalent to spectral clustering on the similarity graph defined by data augmentations, and extends this equivalence to the multi-modal CLIP model. Based on maximum entropy principles, the authors argue that exponential kernels are natural for capturing local similarity structure and propose a Kernel‑InfoNCE loss that replaces the Gaussian kernel with mixtures of exponential kernels. Preliminary experiments on CIFAR datasets show modest improvements in classification accuracy. All reviewers agree on the core theoretical claim, but there is considerable disagreement: one reviewer (score 1) found the presentation unclear and notation problematic, while the other three (scores 5–6) were positive overall but raised significant technical, motivational, and experimental concerns.

### Strengths

- Important theoretical result establishing equivalence between InfoNCE loss and spectral clustering, thereby bridging theory and practice for a widely‑used self‑supervised method.
- Novel analysis that interprets both SimCLR and CLIP through a probabilistic graph coupling perspective and connects them to (generalized) spectral clustering.
- Proposal of Kernel‑InfoNCE with a mixture of exponential kernels, which achieves improved classification accuracy on several benchmark vision datasets (CIFAR‑10, CIFAR‑100) compared to the standard Gaussian kernel.
- Clear illustration of the analysis (Figure 1) and well‑stated motivations and contributions in the introduction.

### Weaknesses

- **Fundamental motivation questioned** – One reviewer considers the equivalence “easy to obtain” because both InfoNCE and spectral clustering operate on pairwise relationships, and the paper’s claim of surprising insight is not sufficiently justified. The motivation for the kernel‑InfoNCE variant is also seen as weak and heuristic rather than driven by a clear practical need.
- **Presentation and notation issues** – The paper lacks guideposts to explain the purpose of each section and how theoretical derivations connect to practical implications. The definition of spectral clustering itself is omitted, assuming background that many readers may not have. Equation (1) uses ambiguous notation: it is not explicitly a sum over multiple query samples. The term “flip” for data augmentation and the probability 1/9 are undefined and appear arbitrary.
- **Technical errors in the entropy‑based derivation (Sections 5.1–5.2)** – The problem (P1) uses entropy regularization to promote sparsity in α, but the maximum‑entropy solution for a discrete random variable is uniform (α_i = 1/n), contradicting the sparsity goal. The formulation (P2) is neither the Lagrangian nor the standard Lagrangian dual; it is misleading to claim that minimizing (P2) gives an upper bound of (P1) without a rigorous derivation. The role of the dual variable τ inside the objective is not justified. The claim that Theorem 5.1 “naturally” leads to a loss for neighborhood similarity is not convincingly established; the derived form appears to be an arbitrary choice that happens to be exponential.
- **Kernel‑InfoNCE lacks rigorous justification** – The exponential form in Theorem 5.1 does not directly yield an RBF kernel (which requires a squared Euclidean distance in the exponent), so calling the proposed loss “kernel‑InfoNCE” is misleading. The connection between the theoretical derivation and the chosen kernel (e.g., Laplacian, Simple Sum) is not established; the method remains heuristic.
- **Experimental evaluation is limited and unconvincing** – Improvements over SimCLR are not obvious; at 200 epochs and 400 epochs the gains are small and no statistical significance is reported. Tables 1 and 4 lack mean and standard deviation over multiple runs, so the robustness of the results cannot be assessed. The choice of kernels in the MoCo experiment (Laplacian and Simple Sum) and the parameter choices in the synthetic experiment (p=1,q=0; p=0.75,q=0.2) are not justified.
- **Missing explanations and rigor in the main derivation** – The rationality of treating K_Z and π as Markov Random Fields and comparing induced distributions on subgraphs is not adequately linked to the data‑augmentation process. The term “unitary out‑degree filter” in the definition of W is non‑standard and left unexplained. After Lemma 2.4, the conversion of cross‑entropy loss into attraction and repulsion terms is asserted without a rigorous derivation; the connection to Lemma 2.5 is not made explicit enough.
- **Related work is poorly handled** – Remarks on prior work are sometimes improper or misleading, and the positioning relative to HaoChen et al. (2021) and Van Assel et al. (2022) could be clearer.
