## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents SpikeZIP, a framework for converting ANNs to SNNs via an ANN→QANN→SNN two-step conversion. It includes SNN-friendly morphing (including residual connection re-routing, RCR), paths-ensemble training (PET), operator fusion, and neuron replacing. A theoretical proof of equivalence between QANN and the converted SNN is provided. Experiments on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet with VGG-16, ResNet-34, and YOLOv3 show improved accuracy-latency Pareto-front performance. Reviewer scores are 6, 3, 3, 3, 3, indicating one positive and four negative evaluations. The positive reviewer found the paper rigorous and effective; the negative reviewers raised major concerns about novelty, clarity, and experimental analysis.

### Strengths
- (R1, R3, R4, R5) The paper delivers a rigorous theoretical proof of model-level equivalence between Quantized ANNs (QANNs) and their converted SNNs, addressing a gap in existing literature and providing a solid foundation for the conversion process.
- (R1, R3, R5) The proposed paths-ensemble training (PET) algorithm considers SNN temporal information when fine-tuning QANNs, showing a degree of originality and effectively improving accuracy-latency trade-off.
- (R1, R5) The reconsideration/adaptation of SEW-ResNet (residual connection re-routing) for ANN-to-SNN conversion is noted as an innovative component.
- (R1, R2, R4) Impressive Pareto-front results on ImageNet (e.g., 73.92% with VGG-16 at 9 timesteps, 74.21% with ResNet-34 at 11 timesteps), outperforming some existing methods in accuracy and energy efficiency.
- (R3) SpikeZIP focuses on reducing computational cost and energy consumption, with comparative analysis including power consumption metrics, essential for neuromorphic hardware.
- (R3) Demonstrated adaptability to various architectures (ResNet, VGG, YOLOv3) enhances utility for real-world edge computing.
- (R4) Abstract and introduction are logically clear and well-structured.
- (R5) The paper is exceptionally well-written with clear organization; Introduction and Related Works provide an understandable overview.
- (R5) Scientific significance: reinforces standards in ANN-SNN conversion (equivalence) and provides a novel solution for low-latency, high-accuracy SNNs.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty and prior art (majority reviewers disagree with R1):**
    - (R2, R4, R5) The method of first training QANN and then converting it to SNN has already been proposed in SpikeZIP-TF [1]; this paper does not adequately differentiate its approach.
    - (R1, R2, R4) Several components are not novel: residual connection re-routing (RCR) is the same as SEW-ResNet (ADD, AND, IAND) and does not constitute innovation beyond a different purpose.
    - (R1, R4) Operator fusion, neuron replacing, input and bias encoding have been widely applied; specific differences and advantages over existing techniques are not distinctly justified.
    - (R1) The theoretical proof in Section 3.5 appears similar to existing work [2]; mathematical differences are not clearly delineated.
- **PET lacks clear motivation and analysis:**
    - (R1, R2, R4, R5) The motivation for using three parallel paths (quantization levels {2,4,8}) is insufficient and seems arbitrary. No ablation experiment comparing PET to a single-path QANN (standard quantization-aware training) is provided; only Figure 6 is shown without analysis. The mechanism by which PET improves SNN accuracy is not explained.
    - (R2) The relationship between quantization levels in PET and resulting SNN performance is not analyzed; the choice of paths appears heuristic.
    - (R3, R4) Detailed pseudocode or flowchart for PET is missing; a direct comparison between PET and conventional quantization-aware training is needed.
- **Weak logical connection between theoretical proof and method:**
    - (R1) The theoretical equivalence proof does not appear to inform the design or implementation of SpikeZIP; the method could work without it. The impact of T_eq on experimental results is not discussed.
- **Experimental and reporting issues:**
    - (R2, R5) In Table 4, the reported timestep for MS-ResNet34 is 6; this contradicts the originally reported value in that work (likely error in data collection).
    - (R2, R5) Lines 405-407 contain a descriptive error: SNN inference timesteps cannot be less than ANN with T=1.
    - (R2) In Table 3, symbols (triangle and -) mentioned in caption are not visible in the table.
    - (R2, R5) The experimental results are not satisfactory: performance in Table 3 is inferior to some recent works (e.g., [2] from ICASSP 2024: "Optimal ANN-SNN Conversion with Group Neurons").
    - (R2) In Table 4, SpikeZIP-PR comparison is confusing (missing SpikeZIP-P, ResNet, ImageNet). Fig. 5 uses SpikeZIP-P while Table 4 uses SpikeZIP-PR without explanation.
    - (R2) When T=16 in Table 3(b), SpikeZIP-PR has lower accuracy than offset; no explanation.
    - (R2, R5) Energy calculation in Figure 7 right half: the SEW residual structure is not spike-driven (floating-point multiplications exist after residual connection), so the calculation may be inaccurate if those layers were treated as spike operations.
- **Unclear/confusing descriptions:**
    - (R1, R2, R5) Lines 17-20: claim about “theoretic equivalence” is imprecise; need context on how this work differs from prior attempts.
    - (R1, R2, R5) Last row of Table 1: description of quantization levels is vague; symbols (n, n_mp, n_sp, i) should be defined separately.
    - (R2, R5) Lines 119-121: punctuation errors and unclear abbreviations (“ana.”, “mem calib.”, “eq.”).
    - (R2, R5) Lines 250-252: description of weight sharing between major and sub-paths is confusing; should be stated earlier.
    - (R4) “Analog input encoding” is actually direct coding; should use the standard term.
    - (R4) The term “Pareto frontier” is not explained.
- **Missing experiments, comparisons, and analyses:**
    - (R2, R3) Missing ablation studies evaluating each component of SpikeZIP separately; most analyses are parameter sweeps.
    - (R2) PET should be compared to Stochastic Precision Ensemble (AAAI-21) for completeness.
    - (R3) More extensive comparison with learning-based SNN training methods on accuracy and energy efficiency is needed.
    - (R3) Testing on architectures beyond VGG, ResNet, YOLOv3 (e.g., recurrent or transformer-based SNNs) would demonstrate generalizability.
    - (R3) Testing on actual neuromorphic hardware (e.g., Intel Loihi) would validate energy and latency improvements.
    - (R4) A comparison of the theoretical equivalence proof with existing proofs in EMRS and Radix (Table 2) is needed.
    - (R2) The choice of ST-BIF neuron (instead of IF/LIF) is not justified; compatibility with neuromorphic hardware and overhead should be discussed. The role of spike tracer (St) needs more explanation.
    - (R4) The encoding method in Section 3.2 (δv) is unclear; a small figure illustrating differences from direct encoding and its advantages would help.
- **Writing and formatting issues:**
    - (R1) Abstract: last part has issues.
    - (R1) Missing definitions: QANN, SOTA, LSQ-based (line 183).
    - (R1) Typos: line 39 “))” → ‘)’, line 87.
    - (R1) Duplicate notation: V^{in} in Eq.1 and Eq.7 meaning different things.
    - (R1) Line 327: triangle symbol not in table; line 347: no appendix provided.
    - (R1) Table 3 font too small; notation concentrated in Table 1 inconvenient; order of Figures 2 and 3 should be swapped.
    - (R1, R4) ST-BIF neuron should be referenced and explained at first appearance (line 85 or in contributions).
    - (R4) Keywords included in abstract; inconsistent capitalization in Table 1; “ReLU” vs “RELU”; Fig.3 caption uses past tense; “SZIP” in Fig.3 not defined.
    - (R2, R5) The SEW residual connection is not spike-driven, contradicting claims of SNN-friendliness; the paper should address this non-spike-driven issue.
- **Reviewer questions overlapping with weaknesses (explicitly kept):**
    - (R1, R4) Why does PET increase accuracy? Specific mechanism needed.
    - (R1) How does T_eq affect experimental results?
    - (R2) Why use ST-BIF instead of IF/LIF? What happens with IF/LIF?
    - (R2) What is the basis for dividing into three paths? Benefits of weight sharing between paths?
    - (R2) Why does L1-norm comparison use SpikeZIP-PR in Table 4 but SpikeZIP-P in Fig.5?
    - (R3) What assumptions are required for QANN-SNN equivalence? Provide explicit constraints.
    - (R4) Does SNN-friendly morphing solve the non-spike-driven architecture problem?
    - (R5) Why not use the term “direct coding” instead of “analog input encoding”?
