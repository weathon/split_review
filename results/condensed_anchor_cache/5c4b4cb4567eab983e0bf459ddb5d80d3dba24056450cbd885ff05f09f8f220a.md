- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 5, 5, 8, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper theoretically studies in-context learning (ICL) expressivity of a single-layer, single-head, attention-only transformer with fixed weights, which can be “programmed” by the prompt (context). It asks whether for any continuous function on a compact domain there exists a context (sequence of token pairs from a finite vocabulary) that makes the transformer approximate that function (universal approximation property, UAP). Main results: (1) With unrestricted real-valued token embeddings, the transformer achieves UAP (via an explicit construction linking it to a single-hidden-layer MLP, Lemmas 2 and 3). (2) With a finite vocabulary and no positional encoding, UAP is not possible (Theorem 6). (3) When positional encoding (PE) is added, UAP is restored under sufficient conditions that make the set of PE-augmented tokens dense in the input space (Theorems 8 and 9; Corollary 10 for ReLU attention only). The proofs leverage the Kronecker Approximation Theorem. The paper distinguishes between softmax and ReLU attention mechanisms.

### Strengths

- The paper introduces a novel theoretical framework for analyzing the expressivity of a fixed-weight transformer through changes to the prompt alone, distinguishing it from standard network expressivity studies. One reviewer (R1) found this framework ambitious and well-done.
- It establishes an elegant connection between single-hidden-layer MLPs and the attention mechanism (Lemma 2 for ReLU, Lemma 3 for softmax via exponential activation), leading to clean UAP proofs for the unrestricted embedding case. R1 found this connection interesting and thought-provoking.
- The negative result (Theorem 6) that finite-vocabulary prompts without positional encoding cannot achieve UAP provides a theoretically grounded limitation of ICL. R4 praised this proof.
- The use of the Kronecker Approximation Theorem to derive conditions for UAP with positional encoding is mathematically elegant and yields explicit sufficient criteria. R3 particularly commended this approach.
- The paper clarifies the theoretical benefit of positional encoding from an approximation theory perspective, a novel contribution compared to prior ICL+UAP work (e.g., Yun et al., Luo et al., Petrov et al., 2024b). R4 noted it is the first work attempting to bridge positional encoding and ICL through UAP.
- The discussion of ReLU attention is appreciated and allows a weaker condition for UAP (density in the unit hypercube only, Corollary 10), though it does not apply to softmax. R4 considered this a plus.
- R3 found the theoretical advances significant and the proof based on Kronecker’s Theorem effective; the paper provides clear mathematical conditions for UAP.

### Weaknesses

- **Gap in the proof of Theorem 6 (finite vocabulary without PE):** The initial proof only provided a one-directional connection from MLPs to transformers (Lemma 2), but proving non-UAP requires ruling out the possibility that other contexts (not corresponding to the constructed MLP family) could achieve UAP. R1 identified this as a major gap and noted the reverse direction requires further justification. Later revisions attempted a fix, but a new gap concerning softmax attention was found. The authors proposed a fix that R1 accepted, but the final proof’s correctness for both softmax and ReLU was not fully verified by all reviewers. R1 initially lowered their score to 3 (reject) over this gap and later raised to 5 (borderline reject) after the fix, but still had concerns.
- **Section 4 results do not apply to softmax attention:** The positive results for UAP with positional encoding (Corollary 10) rely on ReLU activation; the analogous conditions for softmax are not established. R1 noted this as a significant limitation.
- **Imprecise and opaque presentation:** R2 found many statements imprecise and opaque, particularly the proof sketch of Theorem 8 and the connection between the density of the set \(S = \{x_i + P_x^{(j)}\}\) and the density of \(X^{\top} B^{\top} C\). The reasoning linking the finite vocabulary to limitations on \(UY\) and \(X^{\top} B^{\top} C\) is unclear. R1 also noted presentation issues remained after revision.
- **Strong and implausible assumptions:**
  - The use of ReLU for attention scoring (instead of the standard softmax) is non-standard in practical transformers. R1 noted they had never seen ReLU used in place of softmax for query–key affiliation scores.
  - The positional encoding is assumed to be dense in \(\mathbb{R}^{d_x}\) (or in the unit hypercube for ReLU), which is an extremely strong condition. After training, the positional encoding is fixed, so one cannot freely choose PEs for each prompt. R2 questioned whether a single fixed PE can satisfy the density requirement, and R1 acknowledged this assumption is a tall order.
  - The assumption that network weights \(B, C, F\) are non-singular and full-rank is strong.
- **Inadequate discussion of closely related work:** The paper cites Petrov et al. (2024b) but does not clearly differentiate its contributions from that work, which also proves UAP for prompted transformers. R1 requested clarification on the relationship. Other relevant works (RoPE, RPE, Yun et al., Luo et al.) are only briefly mentioned, and R4 requested a discussion on how results might extend to relative (RPE) and rotary (RoPE) position encodings.
- **Practical relevance and lack of experimental validation:** The framework fixes the transformer weights and optimizes over the context to fit an arbitrary function. R2 questioned whether this setting commonly arises in practice, noting that in typical ICL, the model must adapt on the fly to arbitrary contexts without modifying the context itself. No experiments or simulations are provided to illustrate the theoretical claims or their practical implications. R3 also noted the lack of experimental validation and limited practical insights for practitioners. R2 added that this reduces the significance of the work.
- **Assumptions not clearly enumerated:** Important assumptions (e.g., the density property on lines 378-379) are easy to miss. R4 suggested the paper would benefit from a clear “Assumptions” section or bullet list.
- **Minor technical issues and questions:**
  - R1 noted that in the definition of attention, \(Q\) and \(K\) have undefined shapes; \(V\) is initially described as \(d_y \times d_y\) but later must be \((d_x+d_y) \times (d_x+d_y)\).
  - Equation (10) uses an undefined symbol \(h\) (should be the activation function \(\sigma\)) and uses \(x; Z_{:,1:n}\) inconsistently with later usage of \(x; X, Y\).
  - The definition of feed-forward networks (FNNs) does not allow softmax activation as written (due to normalization), yet the paper discusses softmax FNNs. R1 asked for clarification.
  - The family of transformers in equation (11) fixes context length \(n\), but Theorems allow unbounded \(n\). R1 noted the definition should permit any positive integer \(n\).
  - Norms used in approximation statements are not specified (e.g., Lemma 1, Theorem 6 uses \(C(K)\) norm but not defined). R1 raised this as a question.
  - The claim on line 309 that “finite-dimensional spaces are compact” is false without additional boundedness assumptions. R1 questioned this and noted the proof of Lemma 5 uses \(\epsilon_0 = 0.1\) but the statement requires it for any \(\epsilon_0 > 0\); the constructed function should depend on \(\epsilon_0\).
  - In Theorem 8, the function \(f\) is not introduced. R1 pointed out typographical errors (line 383, 431: \(i\) instead of \(j\); lines 58, 111, 283, 383 have typos). R2 also noted a missing hypothesis in line 383.
  - R4 (minor) noted that Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) should be mentioned in Section 1.2.
- **Only absolute positional encoding and single-layer transformers are considered:** R4 requested discussion on how results might generalize to relative positional encodings (RPE) or rotary (RoPE), and to multi-layer transformers. R2 questioned whether the density property can hold for a fixed learned PE.
- **Reviewer disagreement:** Reviewer 3 (score 8, confidence 2) considered the theoretical contribution strong and well-proven, praising the use of Kronecker’s Theorem and the clarity of conditions. In contrast, Reviewers 1 (score 5, confidence 4) and 2 (score 5, confidence 2) raised serious concerns about proof gaps, presentation clarity, and practical relevance, with Reviewer 1 identifying multiple issues that required extensive revision. Reviewer 4 (score 6, confidence 3) found the work intriguing but pointed out missing discussion of assumptions and other PE types. The positive UAP claims are thus viewed with caution by a majority of reviewers, and the paper’s overall soundness remains disputed.
