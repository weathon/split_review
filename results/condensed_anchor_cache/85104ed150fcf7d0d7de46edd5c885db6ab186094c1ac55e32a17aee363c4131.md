## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes RealEra, a framework for semantic-level concept erasure in text-to-image diffusion models. It introduces neighbor-concept mining to address the “concept residue” issue—where erased concepts can still be generated via semantically related prompts—by adding random perturbations to the erasure concept embedding and erasing the expanded neighboring set. Beyond-concept regularization aims to preserve specificity for unrelated concepts. Optimization uses a closed-form solution followed by LoRA fine-tuning. Experiments show improved erasing efficacy and generality, but reviewers raise substantial concerns about experimental validity, baseline coverage, theoretical justification, and trade-off handling.

### Strengths
- Tackles a significant and timely AI safety problem: preventing generation of unsafe or protected content even when prompts use semantically related wording (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Paper is well-structured, clear, and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 2, 5).
- Neighbor-concept mining demonstrates a thoughtful approach to exploring concept space relationships (Reviewer 1).
- Using Euclidean distance and cosine similarity to constrain the neighboring search space effectively reduces the search space (Reviewer 4).
- The straightforward method shows some effectiveness in improving the generality of concept erasure (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient comparison to related methods.** Missing baseline comparisons to recently proposed approaches including Reliable and Efficient Concept Erasure (Gong et al., [A]), Receler (Huang et al.), Geom-Erasing (Liu et al.), and Ring-A-Bell (Reviewers 1, 4, 5). In particular, [A] also uses adversarial training for neighboring concepts, making direct comparison essential (Reviewer 4). The beyond-concept regularization is similar to SPM’s preservation loss for random embeddings with low similarity; the paper does not clarify key distinctions or provide comparative experiments (Reviewer 5).
- **Abnormal or inconsistent experimental results.** In the nudity erasure experiment (Table 2), AC performs *worse* than the original SD v1.4, contradicting the results reported in [A] where AC significantly outperforms SD v1.4—reviewers request hyperparameters and explanation for this discrepancy (Reviewers 1, 4). RealEra itself generates 93 nude images versus 66 in [A] (Reviewer 4). Object erasure with SPM yields notably lower results than expected (Reviewer 1). In Appendix Tables 3 and 4, MACE clearly outperforms RealEra in terms of the Hₒ score (Reviewer 5). The qualitative results for erasing Jennifer Lawrence (Figure 4) do not strongly suggest that Katniss Everdeen is removed as a test of generality (Reviewer 5).
- **Specificity is sacrificed for generality, with limited quality metrics.** The CLIP score on MS-COCO 30K is relatively low, indicating insufficient preservation of unrelated concepts (Reviewer 1). FID and CLIP scores for generation quality are missing (Reviewer 2). Overall, the method appears to trade specificity for generality without clear overall performance improvement (Reviewer 5). Concerns remain that specificity would degrade further under massive multi-concept erasure like MACE (100 concepts) given the observed drop with a single concept (Reviewer 5).
- **Lack of robustness and generalization tests.** Prior work uses adversarial perturbations on input prompts (e.g., Prompting4Debugging, Ring-A-Bell) to reveal whether erased concepts can still be regenerated; such tests are essential to confirm that concept residue is truly addressed (Reviewers 2, 5). The paper only tests single-concept erasure; performance under massive multi-concept erasure is untested (Reviewer 5). Experiment details on how synonym classes are found are missing (Reviewer 2).
- **Theoretical and methodological gaps.** There is no theoretical guarantee that related but non-target concepts remain unaffected; only limited empirical support is provided (Reviewer 3). The paper does not justify why Euclidean-distance/cosine-similarity–close embedding vectors are also semantically close—this claim needs both empirical and theoretical backing (Reviewer 3). The neighbor radius is set via manually selected hyperparameters without principled criteria; this may not capture all related concepts and risks inadvertently affecting unrelated ones (Reviewer 2, 5). The approach as a whole is fairly simplistic (Reviewer 2).
- **Parameter selection and trade-off not justified.** The D and S values in Figure 5, which control the generality-specificity trade-off, lack explanation of selection criteria or comparison to parameter choices in other baselines (Reviewer 5).
- **Missing experiments.** Artistic styles erasure is not tested, which would broaden the assessment (Reviewer 1). Code release status is not confirmed (Reviewer 3).
- **Minor issues.** Quotation mark errors (lines 088, 209) and inconsistent terminology (“concept residue” vs “concept residual” in limitations) (Reviewer 3).
- **Unanswered critical questions.** Why use a closed-form solution before LoRA fine-tuning rather than applying LoRA from the start? (Reviewer 2). How precisely can the method unlearn a concept without harming related concepts (e.g., unlearning Tobey Maguire may affect Spider-Man or other actors)? (Reviewer 3). If NSFW safety checkers can be bypassed by modifying them, is the motivation of using erasure for undesired generation prevention still convincing? (Reviewer 3). The distinction between beyond-concept regularization and SPM’s preservation loss is not clarified (Reviewer 5).

**Reviewer disagreement:** Reviewer 4 is notably more critical (rating 1), strongly questioning the experimental validity, the discrepancy with [A], and the omission of adversarial training–based related work. Reviewers 2 and 3 (rating 5) acknowledge the problem’s significance but raise substantial concerns about theoretical grounding, experimental completeness, and robustness testing. Reviewers 1 and 5 (rating 3) find the work moderate, citing insufficient baselines and unconvincing performance trade-offs.
