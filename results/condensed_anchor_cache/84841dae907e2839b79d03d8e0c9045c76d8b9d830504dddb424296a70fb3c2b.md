## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Multi-Scale Window-based Transformer for image inpainting, using multi-scale windows to capture contextual information, a modified polarized self-attention (MW-PSA) to integrate multi-window features, and a Selective Mask Update (SMU) method to update mask tokens. Experiments at 256×256 resolution show quantitative and qualitative improvements over some baselines. Reviewers generally found the method easy to follow and effective, but most (scores 3) considered novelty limited and experiments incomplete. One reviewer (score 5) was more positive, praising the design rationale, the token-update strategy, and the professional presentation; this reviewer saw the method as the first successful implementation of such technologies in inpainting. All reviewers expressed high confidence (5) in their assessments.

### Strengths
- The multi-scale window parallel self-attention (parallel MCA across windows) incorporates diverse contextual information and achieves superior performance compared to existing models (Reviewer 1). The MW-PSA mechanism robustly explicates its design rationale and is effectively validated in large mask inpainting tasks; it is noted as the first successful implementation of such technologies in inpainting (Reviewer 2).
- MW-PSA efficiently fuses features obtained through MCA in both channel and spatial dimensions (Reviewer 1).
- The Selective Mask Update (SMU) strategy for updating valid tokens within masks enhances task efficiency and the quality of generated results (Reviewer 2). The method is easy to follow (Reviewer 3).
- The paper outperforms the compared baselines in FID and qualitatively (Reviewer 4). Quantitative and qualitative evaluations show superiority over several existing models (Reviewer 1).
- (Reviewer 2, minority positive) The presentation is professional, fluent, and has almost no expression errors; it clearly elucidates the authors’ contributions.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The combination of multi-scale training and window-based attention is widely used in computer vision, and the combination is considered naive and intuitive, with insufficient in-depth discussion (Reviewer 3). Similar multi-scale window transformer designs have been proposed in prior work but are not cited (e.g., [1] Ren et al. 2022, [2] Cheng et al. 2023) (Reviewer 2). The MW-PSA appears to be a simple modification of existing PSA under parallel multi-window inputs, and MCA is directly borrowed without substantial innovation (Reviewer 2). The paper does not discuss key differences from multi-scale dilated convolution (e.g., AOT-GAN [Zeng et al. 2022]) (Reviewer 3). Other techniques (MW-PSA, MCA) are from previous works; SMU lacks comparisons to adaptive mask adjusting methods (Partial Conv [Liu et al. 2018], Gated Convolution [Yu et al. 2019]) (Reviewer 3). Some existing works share similar insights (Reviewer 4).
- **Incomplete experiments**: All experiments are only on 256×256, yet the paper claims “high-quality” image inpainting; higher-resolution experiments (e.g., 512×512) are needed. Comparing methods like CoModGAN (trained on 512×512) at 256×256 is unfair (Reviewer 1). No comparison with diffusion models (e.g., stable diffusion inpainting) that show superiority on large missing areas (Reviewers 1, 4). The experiments are not fully convincing (Reviewer 1). The improvement over MAT (Table 1) is marginal (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing metrics**: PSNR-oriented metrics are completely omitted. Transformer-based methods often excel on fidelity, and for downstream tasks, completion results should demonstrate fidelity. Including such metrics is imperative (Reviewer 2).
- **Inadequate ablation studies**: Only qualitative discussions are provided. Ablation experiments on U-Net network settings, module-level details, and hyperparameters (e.g., the k value in the Top-k selection of SMU) are needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Concerning design motivation**: The claim that simply concatenating a U-Net network strengthens local textures and enhances fine details is both perplexing and unconvincing; regression-based CNN methods of this nature are often considered to cause over-smoothing and detail loss (Reviewer 2).
- **Serious writing/citation issues**: (Reviewer 3) The writing is non-standard. Citation problems include: incomplete references (missing publisher/journal names), incorrect publication years (e.g., “High-fidelity pluralistic image completion with transformers” was published in CVPR 2021 but marked as 2018), and wrong citations (Yan et al. 2018 and Zhang et al. 2018a are irrelevant to attention or transformer). (Reviewer 1) “Places368-standard” appears in Table 1; mask color differs between row 1 and row 2 in Figure 1. The paper’s conclusion incorrectly changes to video inpainting, suggesting a resubmission issue that should be corrected (Reviewer 1).
