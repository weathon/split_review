## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper benchmarks seven local zeroth-order optimizers (all SPSA-like) on a suite of noiseless variational quantum learning problems with randomized circuits, Hamiltonians, and parameter initializations. The authors find that simple heuristics such as vanilla SPSA often outperform more elaborate optimizers, and that hyperparameter tuning is critical. The work is viewed by most reviewers as a useful empirical investigation, but one reviewer (score 5) is more positive, while the other three (scores 3) raise major concerns about scope, novelty, and reproducibility. All reviewers note that the insights are interesting but the paper does not provide theoretical explanations or extend to noisy, larger-scale settings.

### Strengths

- Systematic evaluation of seven local zeroth-order optimizers across partially randomized tasks, providing practical guidance (e.g., SPSA often works best).
- Valuable insights that go beyond specific quantum learning tasks; addresses the problem of “blind” optimizer selection in QML.
- Experiments are illustrated clearly with convergence curves and box plots; manuscript is generally well written and understandable.
- The experimental setup is described at a level of detail that appears reasonable to some reviewers.
- The paper tackles an important topic—performance of classical optimizers for variational quantum systems.

### Weaknesses

- **Lacks key benchmark infrastructure:** No released source code, no dedicated website, no public leaderboard. Strongly hinders reproducibility and comparison. [R1, R4]
- **Insufficient problem characterization:** Tasks are only vaguely described. Specific quantum circuits (beyond “RandomLayers”), number of qubits per experiment, parameter ranges, and simulation method (e.g., statevector vs. density matrix) are not clearly stated. Makes it hard to replicate or assess generalizability. [R1, R3]
- **Limited scale and scope:** Experiments use very few qubits (maximum not explicitly given but clearly small). The chosen tasks do not convincingly represent real-world QML problems; variety is limited. Scalability of optimizers to larger systems is not demonstrated. [R3, R4]
- **Purely empirical, lacks theoretical insight:** Several interesting findings lack explanation. For example, why does adamSPSA converge much slower than SPSA in 1D Ising and 2D Heisenberg tasks? Is this due to zeroth‑order gradient noise or a unique quantum Hamiltonian phenomenon? Similarly, the 95% confidence intervals in Figures 1 & 3 are surprisingly thin given non‑convex landscapes—possibly because initial guesses are confined to a small region or hyperparameter tuning used only 3 random keys; does this reflect general quantum learning landscapes? [R2, R3]
- **Noise is not considered:** Only the noiseless setting is studied. On real quantum hardware, expected values can only be estimated with finite samples, making noisy function evaluations the norm. SPSA is known to be sensitive to such fluctuations, so a benchmark with an explicit noise model (or even approximate loss evaluation) is needed to be practically relevant. [R2]
- **Novelty and prior work:** The core results (SPSA works well, hyperparameter tuning is important) are already known from earlier benchmarks (e.g., [2] mentioned). The paper does not adequately build on or distinguish itself from the growing lineage of QML optimization benchmarks. Missing citations that would help place the work (e.g., randomised Hamiltonian benchmarks). [R4]
- **Presentation and audience:** Minor readability issues (e.g., two consecutive sentences starting with “beyond”); Figure 1 would benefit from showing exact solutions as horizontal lines. The paper assumes decent QML knowledge and is not tailored to the primarily classical ML audience of ICLR. [R4]
- **Questions overlapping with weaknesses:**
  - Do the main results hold under barren plateaus? [R3]
  - What theory guarantees that the randomized circuit is truly random? [R3]
  - What are the exact parameterized circuits used? [R3]
  - Is there a scalable analysis? [R3]
  - Would the code be made available upon acceptance? [R4]
- **Overall, the paper was seen as incremental**: one reviewer (score 5) appreciated the rigorous empirical approach but also noted limited novelty; the other three (score 3) found the contributions too modest, the experiments insufficiently characterized, and the insights not significantly advancing the field. [All]
