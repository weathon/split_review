## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper tackles audio-visual segmentation (AVS) by proposing AVSAC, which uses a Bidirectional Audio-Visual Decoder (BAVD) and an Audio Feature Reconstruction (AFR) loss to strengthen audio cues and mitigate visual dominance. Experiments on the AVS benchmark show improvements of 1–4% in F-score and 2–5% in mIoU over prior methods. Three reviewers gave a score of 6, while one reviewer (score 3) raised stronger concerns about novelty and missing comparisons.

### Strengths
- **Direction and motivation**: The focus on improving audio cues to counter visual dominance in AVS is well motivated (R1, R3, R4). Most AV work overweights the visual modality, so the proposed emphasis on audio is a sensible direction.
- **Clarity and readability**: The paper is well written and easy to follow (R2, R3, R4). The connection between the method and its performance is clearly presented.
- **Simple yet effective design**: The approach (bidirectional cross-attention + AFR) is a straightforward extension that yields non-trivial gains (R1, R2).
- **Ablation coverage**: Ablations cover multiple aspects of the method thoroughly (R1, R4). In particular, Table 5 shows that increasing decoder layers leads to a significant performance improvement (R2).
- **Experimental depth**: Both quantitative and qualitative results are provided (R4). The backbone reaches state-of-the-art level, although some baselines are a bit old (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns** (R1, R2, R4; R4 is most critical): The bidirectional cross-attention mechanism is not entirely novel—similar designs (audio→video and video→audio attention) have been used in prior multimodal works [1, 2] (R1), and the overall bidirectional framework resembles concepts from [a, b, c] (R4). Additionally, the Audio Feature Reconstruction (AFR) module is very similar to the bidirectional generation module in AVSBG [1] (R2) and can be considered a preliminary version of [d] (R4).
- **Missing comparisons** (R4): The paper does not include the SOTA method AQFormer [f] in Table 1. For example, AQFormer (ResNet50) achieves 55.7 mIoU on the MS3 split, while the proposed method achieves only 51.13. Similarly, AVSegformer’s performance should have been listed in Table 6 for the AVSS setting. A discussion of these discrepancies is needed.
- **Uncited related work** (R4): The introduction’s discussion of dataset bias in AVS aligns with findings in [e], which should be properly cited.
- **Unclear justification for claims** (R1, R3): 
  - Removing self-attention improves performance, but it is unclear how this supports the claim that audio features are “rich” (R1).
  - The paper initially frames modality imbalance as a key problem, but the method only indirectly addresses it by strengthening audio cues. It is not shown that a more balanced flow between video and audio directly leads to better performance, and the deep motivation for why visual dominance is harmful remains underdeveloped (R3).
- **Missing definitions** (R1): The losses \(L_{dice}, L_{mask}, L_{afr}\) should be clearly defined in the main paper.
- **AFR loss behavior** (R1): The AFR loss forces feature similarity between \(F_{AGV}\) and \(F_{audio}\). It is not explained how this reduces the bias described in Section 3.3 (e.g., single-source vs. multi-source scenarios).
- **Out-of-view moving objects** (R1): How does the method behave when the sounding object is outside the field of view or moves in and out of the frame?
- **Attention visualization** (R2): Figure 5 does not clearly show which regions the attention mechanism is focusing on.
- **Language quality** (R3): The English has some issues and should be thoroughly proofread (e.g., using a language tool).
- **Efficiency and redundancy** (R3): Could the architecture be optimized for efficiency? Some cross-modal connections might be redundant.
- **Equation 1 clarification** (R3): In Section 3.2, what does the dot product in MLP(·) refer to in Equation 1?
