## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a continual test-time adaptation (CTA) method that uses pre-computed source prototypes and exponential moving average (EMA) target prototypes to align features. The approach is simple and easy to implement, showing modest gains when combined with existing CTA methods on CIFAR-100-C and ImageNet-C. Reviewers raise major concerns about novelty (prototypical alignment and EMA are well-known), reliance on source data (unfair comparison to methods like CoTTA), limited evaluation (missing CIFAR-10-C, natural shifts, NOTE baseline), marginal performance (<+0.5% accuracy, inconsistent), and writing/presentation clarity.

### Strengths
- The problem of continual test-time adaptation is important and practical (Reviewers 1, 4, 5).
- The proposed approach is simple and easy to implement (Reviewers 1, 4, 5).
- The paper provides comprehensive comparisons with closely related works and extensive ablation studies on EMA weight and loss components (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The online estimation of target prototypes and the MSE loss make the method efficient in terms of adaptation time overhead (Reviewer 4).
- One reviewer (Reviewer 3) highlighted the originality of combining prototypes from both source and target domains, and another (Reviewer 5) praised the paper as well-written and meticulously executed – though these views are in the minority regarding novelty and presentation (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty / Incremental contribution**: The prototypical alignment and EMA updates are well-established in domain adaptation (DA) and test-time adaptation (TTA); the paper lacks clear innovation beyond combining known techniques (“A+B+C”). (R1, R3, R4, R5)  
  - R1: “prototypical alignment has been extensively explored … difference marginal.”  
  - R3: “various loss components … previously discussed … method might just be a combination.”  
  - R5: “lack of technical innovation … no new theoretical insights.”
- **Unfair comparison due to source prototype dependency**: The method requires pre-computed source prototypes from source data, whereas prior CTA methods like CoTTA operate without such dependencies. This makes direct comparisons potentially misleading and limits applicability to off-the-shelf pre-trained models without source data. (R2, R3)
- **Limited benchmark evaluation**: Missing CIFAR-10-C experiments (R2), and experiments are restricted to artificial corruption shifts (CIFAR-100-C, ImageNet-C) without natural shifts or temporal correlations (R5). Missing comparison with the NOTE [1] baseline (R5). Missing experiments with architectures without batch normalization (e.g., ViT) (R4).
- **Marginal and inconsistent performance gains**: Reported improvements are often less than +0.5% accuracy (Tables 1-2), not consistently observed across datasets or base methods (R4, R5). The method degrades EATA under small batch sizes without explanation (R4). One reviewer (R5) found the empirical evidence unpersuasive.
- **Writing and presentation clarity**:  
  - Figure 1 is overly intricate and disorganized (R3).  
  - Problem definition unclear – symbols \(k\) and \(t\) both used for time (R4).  
  - The unsupervised loss \(L_{unsup}\) is inadequately defined; no explicit mathematical formulation (R3).  
  - Algorithm 1 is unclear: line 5 computes \(z\) but the algorithm returns \(\hat{y}\); not clear if predictions are produced batch-by-batch online (R4).  
  - Captions for Tables 3 and 4 need improvement (R4).  
  - Related work section is missing from the main paper (R4).  
  - Introduction does not clearly state contributions (R4).  
  - Overall writing quality is average with imprecise word usage; reviewer recommends proofreading by a native English speaker (R1).  
  *(Note: R5 found the paper well-written, but this is a minority view.)*
- **Hyperparameter tuning ambiguity**: The grid-search details (search space, criteria) for the EMA weight and source alignment loss weight are not clearly reported. It is unclear whether source prototypes \(P^s\) are initialized using training or validation data (R4). The paper also does not specify whether a similar hyperparameter search effort was applied to baseline methods, raising fairness concerns (R4).
- **Methodological limitations**:  
  - Restricted to classification tasks – class prototypes are not generalizable to segmentation or other tasks (R2).  
  - The algorithm returns predictions after processing all data rather than in an online manner (R4).  
  - Unclear how the approach differs from TTAC in computational complexity and assumptions (R4).
- **Missing comparison with stronger baselines**: The analysis should compare against EATA+TTAC (a combined clustering-based method) rather than EATA alone in Figures 4 and 5 (R4). The paper also does not clearly justify why the proposed components are necessary given that EATA without them already discriminates clusters well (R4).
