## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Q-Adapt, a two-stage progressive instruction tuning framework to adapt Large Multi-modal Models (LMMs) for Explainable Image Quality Assessment (EIQA). It aims to resolve the conflict between two EIQA tasks – overall quality explanation and attribute-wise perception answering – by first fine-tuning the LMM with LoRA for universal perception knowledge, then introducing instruction-adaptive visual prompts via V-T Generator and T-V Prompter for bi-directional multimodal interaction. The method achieves performance on par with 8B models using a 3B model and shows good results on several benchmarks. Reviewer scores were 5, 5, 6, 5, with one reviewer (R3) being more positive.

### Strengths
1. **Rising research direction.** The use of MLLM for visual quality assessment is a timely and worth-investigating topic (R2).
2. **Comprehensive experiments and results.** The paper provides extensive experiments, with the model achieving performance comparable to 8B models while using only a 3B backbone (R3, R4). Experimental results look good on several datasets (R2).
3. **Simplicity and insight.** The approach is simple, straightforward, and easy to implement (R3). It offers valuable insight into task conflicts within EIQA (R3). (Note: this positive assessment comes primarily from the minority reviewer R3, who gave a score of 6, while other reviewers rated 5 and noted several limitations.)
4. **Clear related work and implementation description.** The related work section, especially the categorization of MLLM-based visual quality assessment, is well written (R2). The implementation details of the proposed method are described in detail (R1). The paper is generally well written (R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Unclear motivation and insufficient explanation of task conflict.** The paper claims the two EIQA tasks are conflicting but does not adequately explain why this conflict arises or provide concrete examples. The motivation should be clarified early in the introduction rather than deferred to Line 220 (R1, R3). One reviewer (R2) noted the tasks could complement each other, which would undermine the claimed motivation.
2. **Unclear benefit of including the perception answering task in the second stage.** Given the paper highlights the negative impact of the perception answering task during joint training, it is unclear why it is included at all. The paper should provide a detailed analysis of how the two tasks benefit from and conflict with each other, and how the proposed method mitigates these issues (R1, R3). A missing ablation study that fine-tunes with only the quality explanation task in the second stage is needed to evaluate the contribution of the perception answering task (R1 [8], R3 [8], R4 [2]).
3. **Limited novelty of V-T Generator and T-V Prompter modules.** These central modules appear very similar to the Q-Former and VTC module from [1] (Instruction Tuning-free Visual Token Complement). While application to IQA may be new, the contribution to the broader CV and MLLM communities is limited. The paper must clearly articulate the novel aspects beyond domain application (R1, R3).
4. **Confusion in Figure 3 and experimental details.** The caption of Figure 3 refers to the quality explanation task, but Line 216 and the y-axis suggest results for the attribute-wise perception answering task. This discrepancy creates ambiguity (R1, R3). It is unclear whether data volume was kept consistent across the two tuning settings, and why the model tuned specifically for the perception task performs worst (R1, R3). More detailed experimental setups, including exact dataset sizes and implementation details for competing methods, are needed (R2).
5. **Potential data volume and model size limitations.** In Table 1, Q-Adapt-3B is worse than Co-Instruct-8B when trained on the Co-Instruct dataset (which is larger than Q-Instruct). This raises the question of whether the method would fail when data is large enough, or if the difference is due to model parameters. Experiments using the same LLM as Co-Instruct-8B are suggested (R4). The method is also worse than vanilla LMM in several experiments in Tables 1 and 3 – reasons should be explained (R1).
6. **Missing comparisons and ablation studies.** Comparisons with other recent MLLMs such as LLaVA-OneVision, LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave-7B, and Qwen2-VL should be included (R1, R3). An ablation study directly comparing MLP vs. Q-Former as the connector is needed (R1, R3). The sum score for Qwen-VL-Max in Table 2 should be 5.18, not the current value; all numerical data should be carefully verified (R1, R3).
7. **Need for evaluation of generated textual descriptions.** The quality-relevant textual outputs should be evaluated for both correctness and diversity, as template-like descriptions are unlikely to be perceived as explainable (R2). The visualizations in Figure 4 need clearer explanation on how they were generated and how to interpret/compare results (R2). One reviewer (R3) also requested examples of failed feature visualizations.
8. **Redundancy and clarity of the architecture description.** The information flow in stage two (Figure 2) appears redundant – both raw visual features and processed features are sent to the T-V prompter and then combined – and requires further justification (R2). The paragraph starting at Line 244 in Section 3.3 should be merged with Section 3.4 to avoid misleading readers about the connector’s role (R1, R3). The term “progressive” is misleading as it describes only a two-stage training method (R2).
9. **Missing details on evaluation metrics and analysis.** The meaning of scores 0, 1, 2 in Table 2 and how the GPT score is calculated should be clarified (R1). The paper analyzes only Q-bench-A1 results in Table 1 but not Q-bench2-A1; why? (R1). The computational costs of the V-T generator and T-V prompter modules should be reported (R3).
10. **Minor weaknesses.** The repeated use of “(i)” and “(ii)” in the second paragraph of the Introduction disrupts readability; vary symbols when listing multiple points (R1, R3). There is a typo in Table 6: “$Q$-$Former^{Co}$” should be “$Q$-$Former^Q$” (R1, R3).
