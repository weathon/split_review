## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the Gaussian Process Hyperbolic Latent Variable Model (GPHLVM) to embed human motion taxonomies – hierarchical classifications of grasps, manipulation skills, and whole-body support poses – into a hyperbolic latent space. It extends standard GPLVM to hyperbolic geometry by using graph-based priors and distance-preserving back constraints to preserve the taxonomy’s hierarchical structure. The model is evaluated on three distinct taxonomies: contact-aware whole-body pose sequences, hand grasps, and bimanual manipulation motions. It is shown to outperform Euclidean GPLVM and VAE-based counterparts in preserving graph distances, to encode unseen taxonomy nodes with lower stress, and to generate plausible trajectories via geodesic interpolation.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and achieves high presentation quality despite page constraints, making its heavy theoretical content accessible (R1).
- The theoretical contribution is strong: adapting GPLVM to hyperbolic latent space and combining it with graph-based priors and back constraints is principled and novel (R1, R3).
- Compared to deep neural network approaches, the GPLVM framework offers higher data efficiency and inherent uncertainty quantification (R1).
- The model is evaluated on three different human motion taxonomies, demonstrating its ability to embed high-dimensional pose data (e.g., full-body, hand, bimanual) into a compact 2D hyperbolic space that preserves hierarchical structure (R1, R3, R4).
- The proposed GPHLVM achieves lower stress than Euclidean GPLVM and VAE baselines, and can properly encode unseen taxonomy instances and entire new nodes (R1, R3, R4).
- Trajectory generation via geodesic interpolation in the learned latent space yields plausible motions (R4).
- The paper provides extensive technical details in the main text and appendix, including background on GPLVMs, Riemannian geometry, hyperbolic manifold, and variational inference (R4).
- The method appears sound and the formulation is well-suited for the taxonomy learning task (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
- The motivation for robotic applications is not clearly demonstrated. No concrete downstream task (e.g., in a simulator or on a robot) is shown, leaving it unclear how the learned latent space would benefit real-world problems (R3, R4).
- The paper makes an overclaim: “this paper is the first to leverage the hyperbolic manifold for robotic applications” – but the proposed solution does not check collisions during interpolation and has high training/decoding times (Appendix reports >100 seconds in 3D space) (R4). One reviewer (R1) considered the contribution important; another (R4) found the impact unclear and the claims overblown.
- Important comparisons are missing: (i) popular human pose latent spaces like VPoser or adversarial poser could help show the importance of the geometric priors (R3); (ii) hyperbolic graph convolutional networks (e.g., HGCN) and neural geodesic interpolation (e.g., Geodesic GNN) are not discussed or compared (R4); (iii) other human motion generation works (e.g., TEACH, Human Motion Diffusion as Generative Prior) are not situated (R4).
- The significance of the improvement of GPHLVM over the regularized Euclidean GPLVM is unclear. All models improve stress with regularization, so the importance of hyperbolic geometry alone for downstream tasks is questionable (R3). Moreover, no trajectory generation from GPLVM is provided for comparison (R1). The improvement in stress should be related to differences in the observation space, either quantitatively or qualitatively (R1).
- The paper would benefit from additional page space; key material (e.g., VAE comparison, computational complexity) is relegated to the appendix. A journal version might be more appropriate (R1).
- Hyperparameter choices (e.g., kernel type, length scales, variance) introduce many tuning parameters and the interaction between the base model and back-constraint kernels is not explored (R1). This may limit accessibility for practitioners.
- It is unclear how the hyperbolic latent variables are obtained – more detail on the construction of the embeddings is needed (R2).
- The uncertainty provided by the GP in the latent space (via the hyperbolic kernel) is not actually used in the model; its role should be elaborated (R2).
- Figures 2 and 4 are hard to interpret; the match between taxonomy graph structure and latent space is not obvious. The authors should select specific examples (e.g., K2H2 and FH) for clarity and place them next to the ground-truth graph (R3).
- The computational cost of optimizing GPHLVM (including sampling) is not reported; scalability to larger datasets is a concern, as a 2D latent space may not capture the full range of human motion (R3).
- The model’s applicability to other hierarchical/graph-based problems (e.g., simple toy examples) is not demonstrated, which could help clarify its strengths and limitations (R1).
- It is not shown whether the learned taxonomies can be applied in a simulator with collision-aware interpolation (R4).
