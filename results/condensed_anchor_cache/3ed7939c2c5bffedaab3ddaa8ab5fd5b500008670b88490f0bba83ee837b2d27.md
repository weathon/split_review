## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper derives a data-dependent generalization bound for linear State Space Models (SSMs) under a Gaussian process input assumption. Based on the bound, the authors propose an initialization scaling rule (using \(1/\sqrt{\tau(\theta)}\)) and a new regularization term to improve robustness and generalization. The methods are evaluated on a synthetic example and the Long-Range Arena (LRA) benchmark, and compared against the S4 baseline.

### Strengths
- The generalization bound in Theorem 1 explicitly incorporates temporal dependencies of the input, and the “Comparison” section effectively explains why this is important and missing from prior work.
- The scaling and regularization strategies are novel to the SSM literature and the paper demonstrates their potential through experiments.
- The paper is generally well-written; each section is clear and motivates the subsequent parts.
- Studying scaling properties of initialization and regularizers is an important research direction; the paper connects to the question of whether HiPPO is a special case in a broader family.
- Deriving a regularizer directly from a generalization bound is an intuitively appealing approach.
- The derivations are presented in impressive detail, and the intuitive explanations are sufficient for grasping core concepts.
- The experimental results show promise and support the theoretical claims.

### Weaknesses
1. **Gaussian process (GP) assumption is unrealistic for common sequence data** (Reviewer 3, also echoed by Reviewer 1).  
   - The assumption may be reasonable in non-temporal settings (e.g., linear regression), but most time-series inputs (e.g., flattened MNIST images) cannot be modeled by a single GP because distinct classes have unique features that are not captured by randomness in the GP. Random samples from a fixed GP would rarely produce recognizable digits.  
   - The paper does not validate how well the GP approximation holds in practice, leaving a gap between theory and methodology.  
   - The bound’s mean and variance parameters depend on the GP and are independent of data, which seems inconsistent with the claim of a “data-dependent” bound (Reviewer 2).

2. **Experimental evaluation is incomplete and does not fully convince** (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).  
   - The main improvements are shown only on a near-pathological synthetic example; gains on LRA are marginal.  
   - Path-X, arguably the only really challenging LRA benchmark, is missing; the authors are encouraged to complete this benchmark.  
   - No ablation study of the regularization hyperparameter \(\lambda\) is provided; it is unclear how \(\lambda\) affects training and generalization across different tasks.  
   - For the new initialisation, Table 1 shows that adding scaling improves training accuracy but *worsens* generalization accuracy, directly contradicting the claim that scaling makes the model more robust in terms of generalization (Reviewer 3).  
   - The paper would benefit from evaluation across multiple model sizes, sensitivity to hyperparameters, and experimental repeats to ensure reliability.  
   - A concrete runtime comparison is needed to confirm the claim that the method’s time complexity equals that of S4.  
   - Additional experiments in the supplement (Figures 3 and 4) are not discussed in the main text; they should be better explained, or moved to the main paper if important.

3. **Lack of comparisons and generality** (Reviewer 1).  
   - No comparison to the initialization or metrics proposed by Orvieto et al. (2023).  
   - It is not evaluated whether the proposed initialization/regularization can be applied to other SSM variants (e.g., S4D, S5, Liquid-S4, MEGA).

4. **Theoretical gaps and missing analysis** (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3).  
   - The bound in Theorem 1 should explicitly show how temporal dependency affects the generalization error; it is also unclear how the sub-exponential property and Borell-TIS inequality (both temporally independent tools) yield a temporal-dependency bound.  
   - The paper does not provide theoretical guarantees (e.g., variance analysis) for the scaling rule \(1/\sqrt{\tau(\theta)}\) compared to HiPPO.  
   - The dependence of \(C_T\) on \(T\) in Theorem 1 is not given; this is important because the discretization size \(\Delta\) is trainable, changing the continuous-time horizon.  
   - The analysis is limited to one-layer linear SSMs, but experiments use multi-layer networks with nonlinearities (e.g., GLU). How the theory translates to multiple layers and how nonlinearities interact with the SSM parameters are not discussed (Reviewer 1, Q2 and Q7).  
   - The regularizer (9) targets intermediate LTI-system outputs, but in a multi-layer SSM only the final output has a known target; it is unclear how the regularizer is applied per layer (Reviewer 3).  
   - A generalization analysis of the regularized model (9) is missing, which could strengthen the theoretical contribution (Reviewer 2).  
   - The connection between Proposition 1 (stability) and the robustness discussion is not clearly explained, and the type of stability (numerical, asymptotic) is ambiguous (Reviewer 3).

5. **Missing details and clarifications** (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).  
   - The rescaling step in Algorithm 1 (line 7) is not fully described: how does it behave across epochs? Is \(\tilde{C}\) reinitialized every epoch? How does rescaling at each layer affect other layers in a multi-layer setup? (Reviewer 1, Q3).  
   - The paper does not consider the feedthrough matrix \(\mathbf{D}\); it is unclear how to incorporate it or whether it also needs scaling (Reviewer 3).  
   - The notation \(\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\cdot)\) in Theorem 1 is not specified (e.g., which variables are suppressed; it clearly does not hide \(\log(1/\delta)\)) (Reviewer 3).  
   - Table 2: clarification needed whether “w/o (8,9)” corresponds exactly to the original S4 model, as the reported numbers are slightly lower than in the original paper (Reviewer 1, Q1).  
   - In Figure 2, (a) what kind of noise is used—Gaussian white noise or Brownian motion? (b) The training loss shown should be labeled “Training set MSE”, since it is not clear if it includes the regularization term (Reviewer 1, Q4/Q5).  
   - Zero-shot transfer to different sampling frequencies is an appealing S4 property, but changing the frequency may alter the scale of temporal dependencies; the paper should discuss any drawbacks of the proposed method for zero-shot transfer (Reviewer 1, Q6).  
   - The regularizer (9) improves training accuracy, which is counter-intuitive; a justification for this behavior is needed (Reviewer 3).  
   - Final formatting note: matrices and vectors should be boldface to comply with ICLR guidelines (Reviewer 3).
