## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates how prompt variations affect latent representations (specifically the EOS token hidden state) in LLMs for zero-shot binary classification. Using IsoScore to analyze isotropy and clustering to group prompts, the authors explore whether prompts alter intrinsic dimensionality and whether prompts can be grouped by their impact on performance and representations. Results show prompt formulation influences latent representations but is model-family dependent, and clustering does not group by prompt similarity.

### Strengths
- The idea of studying the effects of prompts through the geometry of latent representations is interesting (R1).
- The paper addresses the intriguing question of how prompt representations relate to model performance, with a specific focus on prompt formulation’s impact on robustness (R2).
- The general question of how LLM internals (hidden states) evolve/change when parts of the input (the prompt) change is interesting and could be useful for downstream applications such as detecting adversarial prompt injection attacks (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited scope of study.** Only the EOS token representation is analyzed, which is an oversimplification—it may not attend to meaningful parts of the context and thus fail to capture interesting patterns (R1). This raises the question of why isotropy of the EOS token is specifically relevant (R3).
- **Insufficient task diversity.** Only binary sentiment classification datasets are used. Hypotheses should be verified across a broader range of tasks, including open-ended generation (R1, R2 Q4).
- **Absence of clear or practical insights.** The paper lacks practical takeaways beyond observing that the EOS token representation depends on the prompt (R1). The results sound negative—"we cannot link the IsoScore to the performance of the model and prompt"—and this is not deeply explored; it remains unclear how this finding relates to prior work showing isotropy is a good property for embedding models (R3).
- **Missing definitions and insufficient technical detail.** IsoScore is not defined; only a high-level explanation is provided (R1, R2 Q2, R3). The mathematical formulation of IsoScore and the precise computation are missing to the point of hindering reproducibility (R3). Isotropy is never formally defined, and the paper does not explain why isotropy is a desirable property for decoder-only models (beyond references to embedding models) (R3). The metric “RIS” is not carefully defined (e.g., “value of k is equal to itself” is unclear) (R1).
- **Disorganized presentation and pervasive writing errors.** Numerous typos, missing spaces, misplaced periods, inconsistent citation usage (e.g., \citet vs. \citep), unexpected line breaks, and misplaced punctuation throughout the text (R1, R2, R3). Specific examples: L16 “heuristics rules”; L24 “their impact”; L101 “section 4” not capitalized; L174 missing period; L178 “Dimensionnality”; L184 “PCA get some limitations”; L214-215 k not in LaTeX; L284 “We describe, as follow,”; L290, L306 missing colons; L303 “we constraint”; L317 “First analyse results of isoscore”; L322 “Since IsoScore is a recent algorithms”; L323 “accross”; L483 “This allows us give answers” (R3). Figure 3 lacks illustrations, making it hard to understand (R2).
- **Inadequate related work and lack of comparison to existing methods.** The related work section on LLMs is not closely tied to the paper’s focus. Missing citations to LogitLens and its derivatives (e.g., arxiv.org/pdf/2303.08112) which already study how changes in the prompt change model internals (e.g., detecting prompt injection via features from model internals, which relates to Hypothesis HP2) (R3). No comparison to existing prompt robustness evaluation methods such as PromptRobust (Zhu et al., 2023) to demonstrate advantages (R2 Q3).
- **Unclear experimental choices and motivations.** The motivation for grouping prompts and how the groups correlate to the model’s generation focus is unclear. Why cluster based on the last layer rather than the embedding layer? (R2 Q1). Can the IsoScore be used as a selection criterion for picking a good prompt? The introduction suggests establishing a “direct correlation between prompts, latent representations, and model performance” but this is not deeply explored (R3).
