## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ROUSER, a robust action-value representation learning method under the information bottleneck (IB) framework. It learns representations that maximize mutual information with action values for long-term information while minimizing mutual information with state-action pairs to discard irrelevant features. Since true action values are unknown, ROUSER decomposes the representation into one-step rewards and representations of subsequent pairs. The method is designed to be applicable to various visual RL algorithms (e.g., DrQ, SRM, QRDQN). Experiments are conducted on DeepMind Control, MuJoCo, and ProcGen, covering both continuous and discrete control domains, with unseen background and color distractions.

### Strengths
- **Theoretical foundation**: The method is well-motivated mathematically with detailed derivations of the IB objective (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **General applicability**: ROUSER can be integrated with any visual RL algorithm that learns a critic; it is applied to DrQv2, SRM, and QRDQN and shows consistent improvement (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Experimental breadth**: Evaluation covers both continuous and discrete control tasks, including Domains such as DeepMind Control, MuJoCo, and ProcGen (Reviewers 1, 4). ROUSER outperforms several state-of-the-art methods in eleven out of twelve tasks across unseen background and color distractions.
- **Motivation clarity**: The motivation for learning robust action-value representations via reward representations is clearly presented (Reviewer 4).
- **Clarity of writing** (mixed opinion): One reviewer found the paper clearly written and easy to follow (Reviewer 4). Another reviewer, however, found the paper not clearly written and in need of improved flow, structure, and wording (Reviewer 3). This contrast is preserved.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing generalization benchmarks and baselines**:
  - Experiments on the standard DMC-GB benchmark (with `color_hard` and `video_hard` settings) are missing. The six DMC tasks used (e.g., `reacher_easy`) are often used for sample efficiency, while DMC-GB includes tasks like `walker_stand` designed for generalization evaluation (Reviewers 1, 2).
  - Comparisons with generalization-focused methods are absent: SECANT [Fan et al., 2021] and PIE-G [Yuan et al., 2022] (Reviewer 1); TPC, TIA [5], PIEG [6], and MIIR [Wang et al.] (Reviewers 2, 4). Reviewer 2 notes that incorporating Q-values to mitigate visual distractions is a common design choice (citing [1,4]), and ROUSER should be evaluated against those prior works.
  - Reviewer 4 requests a discussion of differences between ROUSER and MIIR (which also uses an IB framework on Distracting DMControl).
- **Missing training curves**: Reviewer 4 notes that training curves on the Distracting DMControl benchmark for Figures 1 and 2 are absent, making it difficult to assess sample efficiency, convergence rate, and stability. Curves would enable comparison of learning dynamics with baselines.
- **Baseline grouping and figure presentation**: In Figures 1 and 2, Reviewer 4 recommends reorganizing results to clearly separate standard image-based RL methods (e.g., DrQv2, CURL) from robustness-focused methods (e.g., SRM, CRESP, RAP), and including axis labels directly on plots (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Writing and clarity issues** (Reviewer 3):
  - Paper flow and structure need improvement. Suggestion: provide a big-picture overview at the beginning of Section 4, and list objectives or add pseudocode from the appendix at the end.
  - Specific unclear or awkward wording: line 227 ("one of action-value representations"), line 270 ("we support that a compressed reward representation..."), line 299 ("model a reward model" → reword), line 364 ("supports" → "allows"), line 466 (rephrase to "motivated by the result in theorem 4.4, we extend ROUSER to..."), line 502 ("all methods' prediction" unclear), line 504 ("use a 2 layer mlp with a learning rate of 1e4" – awkward), line 515 ("by searchings alpha" ungrammatical), line 539 ("sheds light" → prefer "we believe this method is general and applicable to other problems, such as self-driving").
  - Section titles 5.2 and 5.3 are uninformative ("Applicability and effectiveness"); suggest concrete titles like "Extending ROUSER to tasks with discrete actions" and "Analyzing learned representations".
  - Figure 4 should include axis labels within the plot itself, not only in the caption.
  - Typo in line 430: "Nore" should be "Note" (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing ablation / clarification questions** (overlap with criticism):
  - Section 5.3: the analysis of long-term information uses a fixed length of 300; Reviewer 4 asks how effectiveness changes with varying lengths (e.g., 50, 100, 500) – indicative of an ablation that is missing.
  - Line 506: Reviewer 3 asks what exactly is being predicted in the frozen evaluation (e.g., average of 300 future rewards?) and what the MSE is computed between – this points to a missing experimental detail or clarification.
