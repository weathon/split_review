## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses exposure bias in accelerated diffusion sampling by leveraging a manifold hypothesis. The authors propose a training-free manifold constraint that calibrates intermediate sampling variables using pre-computed statistics (mean, variance) of denoising observations, aiming to reduce error propagation. The method requires minimal hyperparameter tuning and is evaluated on high-resolution image and video datasets, reporting a FID of 15.60 with 10-step SDXL on MS-COCO (2.57 FID reduction over baseline). Experiments show improvements over DDIM baselines across multiple models and datasets, and the method can be combined with prior work (DDIM-MCDO^\dagger). The paper includes theoretical analysis and geometric justification, though several reviewers raise concerns about derivation rigor.

### Strengths
- Simple, training-free method with marginal computational overhead; no retraining or intensive hyperparameter tuning required.
- Consistent FID improvements across multiple high-resolution datasets and diffusion models (e.g., Table 3: MCDO with 4 steps outperforms DDIM with 5 steps).
- Well-written and clearly motivated, with good structure and effective visualizations (reviewers 2, 4, 6).
- Extends prior manifold constraint theory with deeper analysis of pixel variance and exposure bias (reviewer 6).
- Method can be combined with existing techniques (e.g., DDIM-MCDO^\dagger) (reviewer 5).
- Evaluated on multiple tasks (image and video generation), with convincing quantitative results (reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5, 6).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical derivation flaws in Section 4.2.** Reviewers 2 and 6 identify multiple issues: Eq. (15) is missing an expectation operator and misuses orthogonality; Eq. (12) approximation $\frac{1}{n}\sum \hat{x}_i \approx 0$ lacks justification; assumption $E[\epsilon_\theta^t]=0$ is not always valid; the connection between Eq. (18)–(19) and pixel variance $Var(\cdot)$ is unclear; Assumption 3 (distribution of $\hat{x}_0$ equals $x_0$) is too strong (reviewer 3). Reviewer 3 finds the proof well-supported given assumptions, but the majority consider the derivation weak and lacking rigorous justification.
- **Missing comparisons to other accelerated samplers.** The paper only compares to DDIM. Multiple reviewers request evaluation on DPMSolver, PNDM (reviewers 1, 2, 5), DPM-Solver++ (reviewer 5), Heun (reviewer 6), rectified flow, consistency models, SD3, FLUX (reviewer 1). Results for NFE around 50 are also needed (reviewer 5). There is no discussion of applicability to high-order samplers (reviewer 6).
- **Pre-computation cost not addressed.** Computing reference statistics requires a full 1000-step denoising sequence, incurring substantial cost when applying the method to new datasets/domains (reviewer 4). The paper does not discuss strategies to reduce this cost or analyze trade-offs with fewer pre-computation steps.
- **Sample selection bias.** Only 20 samples used for estimating statistics; diversity (prompts, etc.) not discussed (reviewer 4). Using samples from the same model may introduce bias (reviewer 4). Figures 4 and 6 rely on only 64 samples, raising concerns about statistical significance (reviewer 6).
- **Visualization quality issues.** Qualitative examples show over-saturation and obvious color shift artifacts (reviewers 3, 6; Figs. 7, 12). Reviewer 3 suggests showing oracle 1000-step results for comparison.
- **Assumption that manifold can be understood solely by variance is too strong.** Reviewer 5 recommends toning down this claim to an approximation sufficient for performance.
- **Potential FID “hack” concern.** Reviewer 5 asks whether adjusting mean/variance of generated data without MCDO to match $q_0$ would achieve similar gains, questioning whether the method is truly addressing exposure bias.
- **Missing details and unclear explanations.** “Denoising observations” needs precise definition (reviewer 4). Need further elaboration on derivation of Eq. (11) and (16) (reviewer 3). Figure 3 x-axis unclear (reviewer 2). Explanation for $\epsilon_t = \sqrt{n}$ when $n$ large (L261) missing (reviewer 3). Generalization of Fig. 3 observation from one sample not justified (reviewer 3).
- **Effect of CFG scale not explored.** Larger CFG scales may worsen exposure bias, but experiments do not vary this (reviewer 6).
