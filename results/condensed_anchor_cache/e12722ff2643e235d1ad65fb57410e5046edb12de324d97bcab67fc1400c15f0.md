## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper identifies a “Feature Overlapping” phenomenon in SNNs, proposes Temporal Differential Decoupling (TDD) to separate static and dynamic features, and introduces a Gradient Sensitivity Criterion (GSC) and Differential Domain Low-Sparsity Approximation (DDLA) algorithm to reduce spike counts. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 show up to 80.9% spike reduction per timestep and 57.8% total spike reduction with minimal accuracy loss. One reviewer (score 6) was more positive, highlighting the novel perspective and potential energy benefits; the other three reviewers (each score 3) raised significant concerns about scope, clarity, comparisons, and methodology.

### Strengths
- Novel discovery of the Feature Overlapping phenomenon in SNNs (reviewers 1,4).
- Introduction of TDD to separate static and dynamic features and reduce redundant computations (reviewers 1,4).
- GSC and DDLA algorithms that further reduce spikes (reviewers 1,4).
- Demonstrated strong spike reduction on CIFAR-10/100: up to 80.9% reduction per timestep (reviewers 2,3,4).
- Paper is generally well‑written with color‑coded equations (reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited comparison and baselines:** The paper lacks comparisons with other methods for reducing SNN power consumption (reviewer 1). The baseline without DDLA is not presented, so the actual spike reduction and accuracy loss attributable to DDLA are unclear (reviewer 2). No comparison to simple baselines such as SNNs trained with spike‑count regularization (reviewer 2), SNNs with at‑most‑one‑spike (e.g., Hwang & Kung 2024), or surrogate‑gradient training with activity penalties (reviewer 3).
- **Unclear advantage over existing low‑timestep methods:** For CIFAR-10/100, established SNNs achieve strong results at very low time steps (e.g., T=1 or 2). In Table 2, TAB Jiang et al. achieve 94.52% accuracy with 4.44 spikes at T=2, while the proposed method uses 7.01 spikes at T=6 to reach 94.38% (reviewers 3,4). It is not clear that the method is beneficial for these datasets. Larger or more challenging datasets (e.g., ImageNet) are needed to assess scalability and significance (reviewers 2,3,4).
- **Missing analysis of computational cost reduction:** It is unclear whether the claimed reduction in computational resources applies to training, inference, or both, and on which metrics (memory, speed, energy). Since training is typically sequential on GPU, it is not obvious that reducing spikes reduces training cost per timestep (reviewer 3). Further, simulating for T=6 timesteps is not considered long, raising questions about the practical benefit (reviewer 3).
- **Questionable existence on temporal/neuromorphic data:** All experiments use static image datasets (CIFAR-10/100). It is unknown whether the feature overlapping phenomenon exists on neuromorphic or time‑series data, where temporal patterns are more pronounced and redundancy may be lower. The paper claims to evaluate on event‑based datasets (line 91) but no such experiments are presented (reviewers 2,3,4).
- **Presentation and explanation issues:** The DDLA algorithm (Algorithm 1) is not clearly explained; notations are not adequately defined, and the application of GSC to reduce spikes is insufficiently detailed (reviewer 2). The handling of static features after TDD separation (e.g., whether they are set to zero) is not explicitly described (reviewer 4). Line 716 states \(F^{t}_{l-1} = X(t)\) when \(t==1\); this should only hold for the input layer (reviewer 4). The core algorithm is placed in the appendix without thorough explanation (reviewer 4). Figure font sizes are too small (reviewers 1,3), and Figure 1 lacks color bars (reviewer 3).
- **Feature overlapping as inherent property:** The feature overlapping observed may be an inherent characteristic of the LIF model, since the membrane potential \(u[t]\) is derived exclusively from \(u[t-1]\). The claim that “temporal feature components are often repeatedly calculated across multiple time steps” (line 264) is misleading because computing \(u[t]\) does not require recomputing earlier \(\{u[t']\}\) (reviewer 2).
- **Unsupported biological relevance:** The paper claims biological plausibility but only tests integrate‑and‑fire (IF) neurons, not leaky integrate‑and‑fire (LIF) neurons, and trains for only 6 timesteps – very short for biological modeling (reviewer 3). This divergence should be acknowledged in the discussion (reviewer 3).
- **Missing ablations and controls:** No analysis of whether the GSC can effectively distinguish dynamic from static features (reviewer 2). No comparison to SNN methods that use at‑most‑one‑spike (reviewer 3). No quantification of temporal feature correlation (reviewer 3). No error bars on Figure 5 (reviewer 3).
- **Implementation details and minor issues:** Implementation details (batch size, learning rate, epochs, how images are fed as input to the spiking network) are missing (reviewer 3). Code is not provided (reviewer 3). There are typos (e.g., line 375 rendering `f_(` instead of `f(`, line 450 missing whitespace) and citation formatting errors (reviewer 3). The Heaviside step function (Eq. 2) should be denoted \(H\) for consistency (reviewer 3). The term “third‑generation neural network” (line 39) is unclear (reviewer 3). Li et al. is cited twice on line 46 (reviewer 3).
