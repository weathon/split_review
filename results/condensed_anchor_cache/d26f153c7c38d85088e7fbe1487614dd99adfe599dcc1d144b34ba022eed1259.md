## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a counterfactual-based evaluation framework for activation steering in LLMs—a technique that modifies hidden representations during forward propagation to guide outputs in a desired direction. The framework compares base and steered generations and introduces metrics such as Format-Preserving Rate (FPR), Steering Success Rate (SSR), and Side-Effect Rate (SER). Experiments on Llama3‑8B, Llama2‑7B, and Exaone‑8B across diverse datasets examine steering success, format preservation, side effects on irrelevant tasks, and the effect of steering position within the sequence.

### Strengths
- The problem is relevant, particularly for interpretability and faithfulness research, as linking prompting to model behavior is useful for growing models.
- The paper presents a novel evaluation framework for representation steering that is studied independently from task‑specific performance—an approach the reviewers note has not been done before (R3). One reviewer found the framework robust for investigating activation steering across multiple dimensions (R1).
- The introduced metrics (FPR, SSR, SER) help formalize desired properties of steerability and are meaningful for understanding this property (R2).
- Empirical findings are well‑presented and highlight key observations: format preservation improves when steering is applied to higher layers, whereas steering below the 0.4 percentile leads to significant format disruption. The steering outcome can succeed or fail regardless of whether the format is preserved (R2). The work also explores the impact of steering position within a sequence, providing valuable insights (R1).
- The framework captures critical aspects such as format preservation and effects on unrelated tasks (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and contribution:** Several reviewers found the contribution limited. Some considered the primary difference from prior work to be simply using additional parameters to adjust intermediate hidden activations, and the work does not feel complete (R1: “contribution slim”; R4: “minimal contribution”, “not complete”). In contrast, R3 considered the framework novel because it is independent of task performance.
- **Limited experimental scope:** The experiments evaluate only one specific steering type on a few tasks and models (R2, R3). No models above 8B parameters are included, raising questions about generalization across model sizes (R2). Even within the tested models (Llama3‑8B, Llama2‑7B, Exaone‑8B), trends appear to differ across datasets; it is unclear whether key findings generalize (R2). Adding more tasks and steering methods would strengthen the paper (R3).
- **Metric sensitivity and generalizability:** The proposed metrics remain sensitive to model, dataset, and the normalization criteria used. The statement that “the normalization term can be chosen for the condition‑specific measures” suggests the need for hyperparameter tuning, which limits the utility of the measures as generalizable tools (R2). The analysis of steering success can vary depending on the normalization criteria (R2). R4 also notes that evaluation is done solely through self‑defined metrics, which may bias results.
- **Clarity and presentation:** The paper contains vague expressions and underspecified experimental choices (R1). For example, “increase the concept of output” (line 073) is confusing (R1). The purpose of “in‑context knowledge” and “parametric knowledge” in Figure 2 is unclear, and why activations on the same layer as the steered representations are not affected is not explained (R1). The reason for using [S] and [A] tokens (line 131) is not given (R1). The order in the bottom panel of Figure 1 (“steered output” before “additional prompt”) is confusing (R1). Examples or deeper discussion of what constitutes “side effects” would improve interpretability (R3). L268–269: citations for Llama3‑8B‑inst and Exaone‑8B appear broken (R3).
- **Motivation and method questions:** The motivation for studying steering in an in‑context‑learning setting is unclear, as steering is commonly applied outside few‑shot settings (R3). Is steering at a single token a standard approach? (R3)  One reviewer suggests testing on open‑ended settings (not just binary pairs) to show robustness (R4). Another reviewer is skeptical that the counterfactual framework truly measures steering effects due to a lack of specific controls, raising a potential correlation/causation issue (R4).
- **Related work overlap:** Sections 6.3 and 6.4 cover topics already explored in prior work (needle‑in‑the‑haystack tasks, long‑context reasoning); the specific link to steered representations is not presented convincingly and the observations are similar to those in prior long‑context work (R4).
- **Technical error:** In line 207, the definition of $\mathrm{rel.fail}$ contains a typo: the second term $S_{y\rightarrow y}$ should be $S_{y\rightarrow *}$ (R1).
