## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DeYO, a test-time adaptation method that uses a new confidence metric PLPD to overcome entropy’s unreliability under biased scenarios. Reviewers (scores: 8, 6, 6, 8) recognize the novelty of PLPD, the empirical strength across multiple architectures and datasets, and the theoretical analysis of entropy’s shortcomings. However, several weaknesses are raised: unclear explanation of TRAP/CRP and PLPD motivation; missing baselines (MEMO, SENTRY, average entropy over augmentations); limited theoretical scope (spurious correlations only); insufficient ablation and parameter sensitivity analyses across settings; and unclear justification for performance under label shift / batch size 1. Reviewer 4 is notably more critical, questioning conceptual clarity, related‑work placement, and the need for the approach given existing theoretical results.

### Strengths
- The proposed DeYO method is simple yet effective for improving the stability and performance of entropy‑based TTA (R1).
- PLPD is novel and interesting, providing new insights for the community (R1).
- Strong empirical evidence: extensive experiments with various architectures, datasets (ImageNet‑C, ImageNet‑R), and mild/wild test scenarios (R1).
- Empirical and theoretical analysis of the entropy metric for TTA (R2).
- PLPD requires negligible computational cost compared to EATA or SAR (R2).
- The observation and theoretical demonstration that “entropy is not enough” on spurious correlation shifts is motivating and interesting (R3).
- Good results on several datasets with different distribution shifts (R3).
- The problem is well motivated: spurious features can cause low entropy (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity of TRAP/CPR and PLPD motivation**: The terms “TRAP” and “CRP” are hard to understand; authors should refine the names and give higher‑level explanations in the Introduction (R1, R2). The motivation for choosing patch‑shuffled input as \(x’\) also needs clearer explanation (R2).
- **Missing theoretical justification beyond spurious correlations**: The motivating observations and theoretical support are conducted only on spurious correlation shifts (semantic level). Can this also be found/proven for other shifts like covariate shifts? (R3)
- **Missing important baselines**:
  - Table 2 and Table 3 lack baselines: MEMO (entropy average over augmentations) and SENTRY (sample filtering via consistency over augmentations). SENTRY operates in UDA, but consistency over augmentations is standard (R4).
  - No baseline that selects samples based on average entropy over multiple augmentations – a trivial extension of prior work (R4).
  - MEMO is missing despite likely being effective for entropy averaging; average entropy over augmentations may already be low for spurious‑correlation‑dominated predictions (R4).
- **Role of entropy in DeYO**: PLPD is combined with entropy in experiments; what role does entropy play in sample selection, and how does it help PLPD incorporate CPR factors? (R3)
- **Insufficient justification for label shift and batch size 1**: Why should the approach improve TTA under label shift or with batch size 1 (Table 5)? Augmentation‑based sample selection seems tailored for spurious correlations, not label shift. Were baselines properly hyperparameter‑tuned for those settings? (R4)
- **Insufficient ablation and parameter analysis**:
  - Parameter sensitivity (Figure 7) should report results under more architectures, datasets, and test scenarios to demonstrate generality (R1).
  - Ablation studies (Table 6) should be conducted on different models, datasets, and test scenarios (R1, R3).
  - Did the authors try other transformations (e.g., data augmentation) instead of patch shuffling? Would they also work to incorporate CPR factors? (R3)
  - How are thresholds in Eq. (9) defined? (R3)
  - What are the numbers and sizes of patches for patch shuffling? Do they influence adaptation? (R3)
- **Unclear concepts and notation**:
  - The term “disentangled latent vector” is not properly referenced or explained; Eq. (6) lacks explicit justification for its form (R4).
  - Notation \(x^{T>>C}\) is not described and may not be necessary (R4).
- **Related work placement and scope**: Placing all related works in the appendix is not good practice; the TTA related‑works section in the appendix is extremely sparse (a single paragraph) and should be much broader (R4).
- **Relevance of prior theoretical work**: Past work (NeurIPS 2020) shows self‑training avoids spurious features – how does that relate to the paper’s claims? (R4)
- **Potential mitigation via pretraining**: The effect of spurious correlations might be removed by incorporating transforms (cutmix, random cropping, patch shuffling) during pretraining itself. Would the proposed approach still help in such cases? (R4)
- **Behavior of PLPD vs entropy across distribution shifts**: Why do PLPD and entropy sample selections behave differently for different distribution shifts in Table 6? How should one select methods for different shifts? Is there theoretical support? (R3)
