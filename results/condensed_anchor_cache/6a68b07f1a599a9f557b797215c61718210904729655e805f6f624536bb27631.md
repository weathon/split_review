## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates how large language models (LLMs) integrate internal parametric knowledge (PK) with external contextual knowledge (CK), categorizing their relationships into Supportive, Complementary, Conflicting, and Irrelevant. It introduces EchoQA, a benchmark synthesizing evidence from four existing QA datasets (ALCUNA, ConflictQ, MuSiQue, OpenBookQA), and evaluates several LLMs (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B) under three hand-crafted prompts. The main finding is that models tend to suppress PK when CK is available, even when CK is complementary or irrelevant, and that tailored instructions only partially mitigate this. Reviewers acknowledge the interesting direction and comprehensive experiments but raise serious concerns about the problem definition, contribution novelty, experimental rigor, and practical relevance.

### Strengths
- **Important phenomenon identified:** The paper sheds light on the observation that contextual information can prevent LLMs from recalling parametric knowledge (Reviewer 1). The direction of exploring PK-CK interactions is interesting (Reviewer 2).
- **Comprehensive evaluation:** Experiments involve several major LLMs (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B, ChatGPT, Gemini) across diverse reasoning tasks and multiple instruction variants (Reviewer 1). The analysis includes four QA datasets from different domains and six LLMs, demonstrating considerable effort (Reviewer 3). The experiments are well-structured and findings insightful (Reviewer 4).
- **New dataset (EchoQA):** The benchmark, constructed by synthesizing evidence types, could be valuable for future research on PK-CK interplay (Reviewer 3). The taxonomy (Supportive, Complementary, Conflicting, Irrelevant) offers a useful framework (Reviewer 5).
- **Interesting fine-grained findings:** Notable observations include significant variance in results when different prompts are used (Reviewer 3). The paper reveals high-level insights such as LLMs disregarding internal knowledge in the presence of contextual knowledge (Reviewer 4).
- **Clear presentation:** The paper is well-structured and clearly written (Reviewers 4, 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Reproducibility failure:** One reviewer could not reproduce the core results on three LLMs (Llama3, ChatGPT, Gemini) using examples from the paper (Reviewer 1, confidence 4).
- **Problem definition validity:** Several reviewers question the premise. It is unclear whether LLMs should be expected to recall PK when the prompt provides distracting or conflicting information—especially in conflicting cases, relying on context appears natural and even desirable (Reviewers 1, 2). The paper does not convincingly justify why suppressing PK is a flaw (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited novelty and prior work overlap:** The contributions are modest, building directly on prior work [1] and [2] with a similar setup released over a year ago (Reviewer 3). The focus on relationships beyond conflict (supportive, irrelevant) adds little surprise or additional insight; supportive evidence was excluded from main results for being uninteresting, yet it remains in the paper (Reviewer 3). More relevant prior work on PK–CK interactions and reasoning QA should have been discussed (Reviewers 4, 5).
- **EchoQA dataset concerns:** The dataset is a reformatted/processed version of four previously released datasets; the effort and novelty in compiling it are unclear (Reviewer 1). The definition of PK is problematic: the types of knowledge considered (scientific, factual, commonsense) may not be comprehensive or could be biased; PK is model-specific, so the benchmark may not generalize across models (Reviewer 2). It is also unclear which model(s) were used to establish PK—is it model-specific or a single surrogate? (Reviewer 4).
- **Ad hoc and insufficiently motivated prompt design:** The three hand-crafted prompts appear arbitrary, and their influence is large but not deeply analyzed. Results may not translate to real-world scenarios (Reviewer 3). Further discussion of prompt template effects (e.g., Table 5) should be in the main body (Reviewer 4).
- **Lacks actionable solutions or deep insights:** The paper only highlights the problem without proposing any solution or mitigation (Reviewer 1). The findings are a series of observations without a cohesive conclusion or actionable guidance for future RAG system design (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing ablations and model-type analysis:** Experiments only use base models (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B) without considering instruction-tuned variants (DPO, PPO, SFT), which might better balance PK and CK (Reviewer 5). Deeper analysis of how different models perform across settings is needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Specific gaps and errors:**
  - Table 2 appears incomplete for ConflictQ/MuSiQue/OpenBookQA (Reviewer 3).
  - Figure 2 does not include the “speak out loud” instruction variant (Reviewer 3).
  - The term “popularity” of training corpus knowledge is undefined (Reviewer 5).
  - Typos noted: L72 (“LLMs leverage” should be “LLMs’ leverage”), L812 (“reals” should be “reveals”), L267 (“ck” formatting error) (Reviewers 3, 4).
  - Question: Why include the supportive type if it is excluded from main results? (Reviewer 3).
