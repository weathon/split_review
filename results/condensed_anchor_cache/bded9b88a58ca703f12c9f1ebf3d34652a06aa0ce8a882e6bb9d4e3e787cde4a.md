## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes using LLMs (LLaMa3, Qwen2) to automatically generate concept banks for concept bottleneck models and introduces a CLIP-based multimodal evaluation metric to assess concept quality. Three research questions are investigated: (1) whether LLMs can generate concept banks comparable to ConceptNet, (2) whether unimodal text-based semantic similarity suffices for evaluating concept–class associations, and (3) whether multimodal information improves evaluation. Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and CUB-200 show that LLM-generated concepts outperform ConceptNet on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, but not on the more complex CUB-200; multimodal models outperform unimodal approaches for capturing concept–class similarity.

### Strengths
- The problem of automating high-quality concept generation for interpretable concept bottleneck models is important and practically relevant (R1, R2, R3).
- The approach reduces reliance on manual annotation (R5).
- Introducing an unsupervised, training-free evaluation metric based on multimodal similarity (CLIP) enhances scalability (R1, R5).
- Experiments cover three datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, CUB-200) and compare unimodal and multimodal evaluation methods (R1).
- The three research questions are well-defined and address the LLM’s standalone generation capacity, unimodal evaluation efficacy, and the role of multimodal evaluation (R1, R2, R4).
- One reviewer (score 5) found the methodology innovative and comprehensive; the majority (scores 3) were less positive about novelty and experimental scope.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The method is very similar to prior work that uses LLMs for concept generation and multimodal embeddings (e.g., CLIP) to select relevant concepts [1, 2] (R2, R3, R4). Simply prompting LLMs to generate concepts does not constitute a significant advance over iterative generation approaches [2] (R4). The claimed distinctions (e.g., using LLaMa3/Qwen2 vs. paid GPT-3 API) are incremental (R2).
- **Insufficient experiments relative to claims**: Only one text embedding (BERTScore) and one multimodal model (CLIP) are used, yet the paper draws broad conclusions about “unimodal” vs. “multimodal” similarity (R2). Experiments lack ablation studies to validate component choices (R3, R5). There is no statistical verification (e.g., t-tests) for performance differences between LLM- and ConceptNet-generated concepts (R1, R5).
- **Performance limitations on complex datasets**: On CUB-200, LLM-generated concepts do not outperform ConceptNet, suggesting dataset-specific constraints and the need for fine-tuning or additional steps (R1, R3, R5).
- **Methodological details missing or unjustified**: The prefix tuning process may introduce human bias; automation for different tasks is not addressed (R1). The filtering approach (e.g., character limits, top-k selection in Algorithm 1 & 2, class-matching process) lacks rigorous justification (R3, R5). The choice of BERTScore as the evaluation metric is not explained, and alternatives are not discussed (R3). How text and image alignment/consistency is assessed remains unclear (R3).
- **Scalability and computational cost not discussed**: Extensive LLM prompting and multimodal filtering may be costly; the paper does not address efficiency or resource constraints (R3).
- **Poor writing and formatting**: The related work section is overly long (3 of 9 pages) (R2, R4). There are grammatical errors, unclear sentences (e.g., “as generate” line 210), formatting errors in the abstract (“gener- ating”, “seman- tic”) (R2, R4), and a missing reference (line 123) (R2). Figure 1 (system architecture) lacks sufficient explanation (R1).
- **Dataset representativeness**: The chosen datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, CUB-200) are relatively small and outdated, limiting generalizability (R1). The study does not incorporate more diverse or domain-specific datasets.
- **CLIP biases and alternatives**: CLIP may not capture domain-specific subtleties and has robustness issues [3]; using only CLIP may limit reliability (R1, R3). Additional multimodal models should be considered (R1, R3).
- **No ablation studies**: Missing experiments that validate the impact of different components (e.g., prefix tuning, filtering parameters) (R3, R4, R5).
