## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers acknowledge the paper’s theoretical contributions to understanding representational limitations in value decomposition and the novelty of the MUD framework, but raise critical concerns about unclear exposition, insufficient differentiation from prior work (particularly QPLEX and Wang et al. 2020), and incomplete experimental evaluation. Reviewer 1 (score 6, confidence 5) is most positive, praising the fundamental problem and theoretical proofs. Reviewers 2 (score 5, confidence 3) and 3 (score 5, confidence 4) are more critical, especially regarding novelty, clarity, and experimental depth.

### Strengths
- The paper studies a fundamental problem in value‑based multi‑agent reinforcement learning and provides theoretical proofs of representational limitations for Linear Mixing Functions (LMF) and Strictly Monotonic Mixing Functions (SMMF).  
- The proposed Mixing for Unbounded Difference (MUD) framework is a novel approach to achieving complete representational capacity under the IGM constraint, supported by mathematical reasoning.  
- The theoretical derivations are substantial, meticulous, and well‑structured.

### Weaknesses
- **Writing clarity and exposition**: The paper is difficult to follow (R1) and suffers from unclear expressions and inconsistencies with prior research (R3). Specific confusions include:  
  - The definition of “complete representational capacity” in Eq 6 is not well justified (R3).  
  - The distinction between “monotonic mixing” and “strictly monotonic mixing” is confusing and appears redundant; all IGM‑compatible methods are strictly monotonic (R3).  
  - The meaning of the optimal representation ratio (Eq 11) and how low w* indicates optimal representation interference is unclear (R3).  
  - The final algorithm is unclear: how Eq 12 and Eq 13 are applied in Eq 10 is not explained (R3).  
- **Disconnected contributions**: The analysis of LMF and the subsequent SMMF extension seem separate and not well integrated (R2).  
- **Insufficient differentiation from prior work**:  
  - MUD shares significant similarities with QPLEX; the paper lacks a detailed discussion of the differences, and the reported QPLEX performance on SMAC differs substantially from the original paper, raising questions about the implementation (R2).  
  - The relationship with Wang et al. (2020) [1] is not clearly elucidated; their results (LMF in indecomposable MMDP has unbiased TD target in Sarsa, and on‑policy LMF with fitted‑Q‑iteration has a fixed‑point Q‑value that derives the optimal policy) may be stronger than the claims made here (R3).  
  - The representational interference problem has been previously discussed (Wang et al. 2020), which reduces the novelty of the gradient‑shaping solution (R2).  
- **Incomplete experimental evaluation**:  
  - The proposed method shows a clear advantage on SMAC but performs similarly to baselines on the easier Predator‑and‑prey task, without explanation (R2).  
  - Ablation studies are missing, e.g., on the number of mixers in SMAC, which would clarify whether multiple mixers are theoretically necessary or merely a performance trick (R3).  
  - The single‑step matrix game is trained on‑policy; it is unclear whether off‑policy training would also converge to the optimum, and whether QTRAN can converge in this setting (R3).  
- **Theoretical gaps and missing discussions**:  
  - The paper does not discuss other combination types that are stronger than IGM but not perfect, as requested (R1).  
  - It is not clarified whether the final algorithm possesses complete expressiveness or is necessary and sufficient for IGM (R3).  
  - The finding that LMF has representational limitations is not surprising given prior work; a more interesting direction would be to explain why LMF works well empirically in many tasks (R2).
