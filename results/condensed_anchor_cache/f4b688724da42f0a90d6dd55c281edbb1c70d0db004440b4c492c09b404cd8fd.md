## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes Multi-round Conversational Jailbreaking (MRCJ), a black-box jailbreak attack that incrementally escalates harmfulness over multiple conversation turns, exploiting the LLM’s in-context learning to override safety alignment. It introduces the Multilingual Unsafe Conversations Dataset (MUCD) – 12,000 questions across 6 harm categories, 4 malice levels, and 10 languages. MRCJ achieves >90% jailbreak success rate (JSR) with <5 queries on weak models, but all five reviewers rate the paper 3 (weak reject), citing insufficient novelty, missing baselines, weak tested models, and a lenient jailbreak threshold. While the dataset is considered useful and the writing clear, multiple reviewers note the method lacks distinction from many existing multi-turn jailbreak works (e.g., Crescendo, Chain of Attack, Many-shot jailbreaking) and the comparisons are outdated (only 2023 baselines). There is consensus that the paper does not demonstrate strong technical novelty for a top ML conference.

### Strengths

- **Clarity and presentation**: The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and figures/tables are clearly organized (all reviewers).
- **Dataset contribution**: MUCD (12,000 prompts, 6 harm categories, 4 malice levels, 10 languages) is valuable for LLM security researchers studying multi-turn conversational attacks (R2, R3, R4, R5).
- **Comprehensive experiments**: The evaluation covers multiple LLMs, harm categories, and ablation studies (e.g., category and malice ordering), clearly illustrating the attack’s effectiveness (R3, R4, R5).
- **Query efficiency**: MRCJ uses far fewer queries than prior black-box attacks like PAIR (R2).
- **Original framing**: While some reviewers found the method obvious, R2 notes that automating human-inspired incremental jailbreaking via an offline dataset is uniquely framed.
- **Security significance**: The topic is important for ensuring LLM robustness and exposes real vulnerabilities in extended conversations (R2, R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of novelty and insufficient distinction from prior multi-turn jailbreak works**:
  - The core idea is very similar to many-shot jailbreaking, Crescendo, Chain of Attack, Speak Out of Turn, and others, but the paper does not discuss or compare against them (R1, R3, R4, R5). Specifically, R5 lists 9+ related works before September 2024 that are ignored.
  - The incremental addition of malicious content over turns is not a fresh concept; the intuition that in-context learning can override safety is straightforward (R3). R3 and R5 state that the paper does not clearly differentiate MRCJ from fabricated Q&A prefilling (R3) or from the Crescendo attack which also exploits “incremental exploits” (R3).
  - The greedy procedure lacks inherent connections between consecutive questions, so it does not genuinely model a strategic multi-turn adversary (R4).
  - R1 also notes the method seems unnecessary: one could simply concatenate many-shot prompts into a single dialogue.

- **Outdated and missing baselines**:
  - Only 2023 baselines are compared (GCG, PAIR, AutoDAN, etc.), even though 2024 multi-turn attacks exist and are more relevant (R3, R4, R5).
  - Many-shot jailbreaking, the most direct related method, is not included as a baseline (R1).
  - Several 2024 multi-turn attacks (e.g., Crescendo, Chain of Attack, Speak Out of Turn) are absent, making the comparison unfair (R4, R5).

- **Weak tested models and lack of generalization**:
  - Tested models (Vicuna-7B/13B, Llama-3 chat) are weak and not expected to be robust; attack saturates quickly. The paper would be far stronger with results on frontier models (GPT-4, Claude, Gemini, Llama-405B) or strongly defended small models like CYGNET (R2, R4).
  - Vicuna is known not to be safely aligned; it is unclear whether the Llama-3 model used is the base or chat version (R1). R2 notes no results on models with extensive refusal training where other attacks approach 0% JSR.

- **Lenient jailbreak success threshold**:
  - Using harmfulness score ≥3 (1–5 scale, where 3 = “somewhat harmful”) as success inflates JSR. All reviewers criticize this; R2 and R1 ask for thresholds at 4 and 5, and note that no human annotation study validates the GPT-4o judge (R1, R2). R1 also questions why the paper does not report average harmfulness scores.
  - The reliance on zero-shot GPT-4o scoring without correlation to known classifiers (e.g., HarmBench) or human review is a significant soundness issue (R2).

- **Dataset creation and evaluation issues**:
  - The MUCD dataset’s sourcing and categorization process is unclear; if questions come from prior work, insufficient credit is given (R4). Because the dataset is static, providers can easily fine-tune to block it, reducing long-term usefulness (R4).
  - Only 50 prompts from AdvBench are used for evaluation, which is not robust (R1).
  - Multilingual construction and the definition of high/medium/low resource languages are not explained (R5).
  - Prompts across categories and malice levels are disconnected, so they do not form a true multi-turn conversational dataset (R2).

- **Methodological and reporting flaws**:
  - The number of queries reported in Table 1 (≤10) seems inconsistent with the method’s need to iterate through 6×4=24 seed attempts; Table 5 shows much larger numbers. Clarification is needed (R2).
  - The definition of “increment” is only prompt engineering (Minor/Moderate/Significant/Extreme Malice Potential) with no quantitative measure (R5).
  - No case studies or examples of actual jailbroken outputs are provided (R1, also implied by R2).
  - Typos and grammatical errors: incorrect citation command (\citet vs \citep), “safety security”, “a LLMs'” to “a LLM's”, etc. (R3, R5).
  - Writing issues: Line 224 claim that prompt construction is opaque for black-box LLMs is misleading (R3).

- **Reviewer disagreement**: R2 found the method uniquely framed; R1, R3, R4, and R5 considered the novelty insufficient or the work a minor variation on existing ideas. R3 states the paper would have been more appropriate for ICLR 2024 but not now given the explosion of similar works.
