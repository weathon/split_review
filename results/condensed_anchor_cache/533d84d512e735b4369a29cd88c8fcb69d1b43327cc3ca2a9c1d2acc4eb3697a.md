## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes representing inequality constraints as Signed Distance Functions (SDFs) to solve inverse design problems, enabling constraint composition via boolean set operations. Two algorithms for computing SDFs are introduced: one for Shepard Interpolation Neural Networks (SINNs) and one for piecewise linear (ReLU) networks. Experiments on MNIST, CelebA, and ZINC-250K compare against a gradient-guided design (GDD) baseline. Reviewer opinions are sharply split: two reviewers (scores 5, 5) find the idea interesting and clearly presented; one reviewer (score 3) considers the empirical evaluation insufficient; one reviewer (score 1) rejects the paper due to unclear motivation, outdated generative models, extremely limited baselines, computational impracticality, and poor experimental results.

### Strengths
- SDFs provide a reasonable way to represent inequality constraints, and composability across constraints via max/min operations (or soft logsumexp) is well-motivated (Reviewer 1, 3).
- The algorithms for computing SDFs for SINNs and piecewise linear neural networks are novel and come with solid mathematical proofs (Reviewer 4; also noted by Reviewer 1, 3).
- The paper is well-written, well-organized, and easy to follow (Reviewer 2, 3).
- The method is validated on image (MNIST, CelebA) and molecule (ZINC-250K) datasets (Reviewer 1, 3).
- Searching for a feasible point via a single adjusted gradient step (when the SDF is differentiable) is conceptually appealing (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
* **Insufficient empirical comparison to state-of-the-art methods.** Only a single baseline (gradient-guided design, GDD) is compared across all tasks. Missing comparisons include: constrained optimization solvers (e.g., linear programming for piecewise linear networks), other inverse design approaches (e.g., classifier‑free / classifier‑guidance in diffusion models [Reviewer 3], RLHF for non‑differentiable objectives [Reviewer 4]), off‑line black‑box optimization techniques, and target generation methods. This makes it impossible to assess the practical advantage of the proposed approach (Reviewer 2, 3, 4; Reviewer 1 also notes no reference to SOTA performance on each task).

* **Experiments are small‑scale and use outdated generative models.** VAE‑based models are used instead of modern diffusion or flow‑based models. Standard inverse problems (e.g., image inpainting, super‑resolution, deblurring) are not addressed. The image tasks (MNIST, low‑resolution CelebA) are too simple; higher‑resolution (e.g., 1024×1024) and more complex tasks (e.g., medical imaging, equivariant molecular design) are needed to demonstrate scalability and practical utility (Reviewer 2, 3, 4; Reviewer 1 also asks for scaling to higher‑dimensional latent spaces).

* **Unclear advantage over simpler gradient‑based alternatives.** The motivation for using SDFs instead of directly optimizing the constraint objective \(M(x)\) (e.g., via gradient ascent or \(-\mathrm{sign}(M(x)-k)\|M(x)-k\|_2^2\)) is not justified. For inequality constraints with a fixed threshold, a high metric always indicates better performance, making the thresholding operation seemingly unnecessary. Direct gradient summation also provides composability (Reviewer 4; Reviewer 2 asks: “What is the advantage over other constrained optimization solvers?”).

* **Computational and scalability concerns are not addressed.** Computing the SDF via the proposed iterative algorithm requires backpropagation through the whole iterative procedure, which is expected to be extremely computation‑ and memory‑intensive. No sampling time or memory analysis is provided. The method is limited to classifiers that are piecewise linear nets or SINNs, making it impractical for large pre‑trained evaluators like CLIP. Scalability to larger datasets, bigger models, and higher‑dimensional latent spaces is unclear (Reviewer 4; Reviewer 2 notes exponential growth of boundary points for ReLU nets; Reviewer 3 asks about time/memory complexity).

* **Missing technical details in the main text.** Key definitions, algorithms, and theoretical statements are relegated to the appendix. The main text leaves many questions unanswered; the appendix is clearer (Reviewer 1). Specific unclear points include: precise definition of “search algorithm” in Theorem 1, algorithm for one‑dimensional \(x\) only (Algorithm 1), how the gradient of the SDF is computed from the algorithm, and how closeness to the starting point is controlled or assessed (Reviewer 1).

* **Potential errors in formulation and baselines.** Equation (2) is introduced but never used; only the feasibility problem (1) is addressed. The gradient guidance baseline (GDD) appears to use an objective designed for equality constraints (line 119‑120), not inequality constraints, which may be erroneous (Reviewer 1, 4). A more direct SDF calculation \(-\mathrm{sign}(M(x)-k)\|M(x)-k\|_2\) does not require any iterative algorithm, contradicting the paper’s claim of intractability (Reviewer 4).

* **Poor experimental results.** Figure 3 shows generated images that do not resemble the desired digit classes (e.g., in (a)–(d) the target digit is not generated at all, and (g) is hardly distinguishable). The captions are confusing; it is unclear which image is the starting point and which is the baseline output. The results look more like an adversarial attack than successful inverse design (Reviewer 4).

* **Lack of composability demonstration with many constraints.** The paper claims composability as a key contribution, but experiments use only 2–3 constraints. More constraints (both aligned and competing) should be tested to validate the approach (Reviewer 1).
