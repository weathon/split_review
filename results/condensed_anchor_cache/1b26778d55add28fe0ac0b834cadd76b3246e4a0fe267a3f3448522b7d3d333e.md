## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper demonstrates that random initialization in long-sequence benchmarks (e.g., Long Range Arena) severely underestimates Transformer performance. Self-pretraining (SPT) – denoising objectives on only the downstream task data – dramatically improves accuracy for Transformers (8–15% gains across tasks) and also benefits state space models (SSMs), closing the performance gap between vanilla Transformers and S4. The paper further shows that manually designed biases (e.g., HiPPO kernels) become largely redundant when SPT is used, and that simplified SSMs can compete with complex counterparts after SPT. The findings suggest that data-driven priors via pretraining are essential for reliable evaluation of architectures on supervised tasks.

### Strengths
- The paper provides a thorough evaluation of SPT and its impact on long-sequence benchmarks, clearly quantifying the gap in current evaluation practices.
- The experimental results are strong and convincing: SPT is efficient (uses only downstream data) and extremely effective, making previously “unable” Transformers competitive on LRA.
- The analysis of convolution kernels learned via SPT compared to HiPPO kernels is novel and interesting.
- The paper demonstrates that data-driven initialization benefits both Transformers and SSMs, and that SPT provides especially good initializations for SSMs at smaller data scales.
- The investigation of explicit priors (structured parameterizations) and effects across data scales adds depth to the study.
- The paper questions the common LRA evaluation procedure; this is a fresh and important direction for the field.

### Weaknesses
- **Cost/compute analysis missing.** The paper does not report computing requirements for SPT (e.g., time spent pretraining vs. fine-tuning). It is unclear how SPT total training compares to standard training from scratch. This is a significant omission: if one method requires pretraining while another does not, cost comparisons are needed to determine practical superiority. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- **PathX-256 results not fully explained.** SPT fails to close the transformer/SSM gap on PathX-256 (even improving SSMs by 20 points). The authors do not investigate why SPT is less effective here; this warrants further analysis. (Reviewer 2)
- **LRA is small/synthetic.** Most experiments are on LRA (relatively small or synthetic tasks). Adding larger or less synthetic tasks would strengthen the paper and broaden its audience. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Not the latest models.** The main SPT results (Tables 1, 2) use older Transformer and SSM variants. No experiments with efficient attention methods (e.g., from Table 1) trained with SPT are provided, even though the paper argues SPT would improve them. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Structured biases still help (minority view).** Even with SPT, S4 with structured biases outperforms less structured methods on almost every task (exceptions: Text, Retrieval in Table 2). The paper claims these biases become redundant, but the data suggest they still provide consistent benefits. The reviewer believes the traditional procedure is somewhat predictive of final ordering and would welcome a discussion. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing ablations and clarifications.**  
  - *Data scale vs. SPT effectiveness:* In Figure 3, SPT does not provide monotonically increasing gains as data size decreases; no guidance on when SPT becomes harmful (Reviewer 1).  
  - *Model size variation:* No experiments with different model sizes to test if the phenomenon generalizes (Reviewer 3).  
  - *Hybrid models:* Hybrid architectures (SPADE, MEGA) outperform both Transformer+SPT and S4+SPT on several LRA tasks; would SPADE+SPT or MEGA+SPT further improve? Not addressed (Reviewer 3).  
  - *Training epochs parity:* Are S4/Transformers trained with the same total number of epochs as S4+SPT/Transformer+SPT (including pretraining+finetuning)? Without parity, baseline models might be undertrained (Reviewer 3).  
  - *Pretraining on text corpora:* The Pythia experiment (Table 2) is meaningless because the downstream tasks are not in the language domain; separating this experiment or using a language downstream task would be informative (Reviewer 3).  
  - *Table 2 ambiguities:* “Transformer + Rotary embeddings” is likely a typo (was it trained without SPT?). The “X” symbol conflates “computationally infeasible” and “unreported”; these should be separate symbols (Reviewer 4).  
  - *Complex valued parameterizations:* Methods in Figure 2 still use complex-valued parameters even when randomly initialized. Is this still necessary with SPT? Since complex values can be problematic for scaling, it would be interesting if SPT removed the need (Reviewer 4).
- The paper focuses on benchmark accuracy but does not discuss inference efficiency (e.g., efficiency is often a key motivation for new methods, not just LRA performance). (Reviewer 4)
