## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies provable learning of symmetric single-index models using symmetric neural networks (a DeepSets variant) under the squared Vandermonde density over complex vectors. The teacher model is symmetric: its parameters are coefficients of power-sum polynomials, with an analytic link function of bounded degree. Gradient flow on a modified correlation loss (with regularization) recovers the hidden direction up to an \(s\)-th root of unity, where \(s\) is a notion of information exponent adapted to the symmetric setting; convergence time scales exponentially in \(s^2\) and logarithmically in the approximation error. A provable initialization scheme and an explicit activation satisfying the required regularity are provided. The analysis exploits orthogonal properties of the Vandermonde distribution that are analogous to Hermite polynomials for Gaussian inputs.

### Strengths
- Important problem: provable convergence guarantees for neural networks trained with gradient-based methods; provides a new theoretical framework for symmetric networks.
- First positive result for learning symmetric single-index models with gradient flow. One reviewer considered this a significant contribution and the techniques of independent interest for Vandermonde marginals; another found the contribution moderate.
- Clear presentation that explains the rationale behind assumptions and the choice of loss functions.
- Generalizes the notion of information exponent to the symmetric setting, showing it controls learning complexity.
- Goes beyond the standard two-layer fully connected network and Gaussian input assumptions of prior single-index analyses.
- Related literature is covered well.
- Explicit student model proposed with precise convergence bounds; experiments on synthetic data are provided.
- Proof techniques (complex calculus, identity of Vandermonde distributions) may be useful for future work on learning under such marginals.

### Weaknesses
- Assumptions are restrictive:  
  * Teacher symmetric structure (each parameter influences each variable identically) limits applicability.  
  * Link function must be analytic and supported on monomials of degree at most \(\sqrt{d}\).  
  * The activation is only verified for a specific choice not commonly used in practice.  
  * The student link function \(g\) requires full knowledge of the teacher link function \(f\).  
  Though acknowledged as a first attempt, practical relevance is questionable.
- The squared Vandermonde density is mathematically convenient but not standard; it is never explicitly defined in the manuscript.
- No finite-sample analysis; results hold for population gradient flow with exact gradients, not SGD. Recent works on single-index models focus on sample complexity; this work does not address sample complexity or PAC learning guarantees. One reviewer noted that gradient flow time can be rescaled, so it does not directly give a well‑defined complexity measure upon discretization. Another reviewer asked whether the results can be translated to standard PAC guarantees and what the main obstacles would be.
- Conceptual novelty questioned by one reviewer: the analysis follows a now‑standardized template (inner product similar to Gaussian case, correlation loss, information exponent). It is unclear what is conceptually new beyond adapting to symmetric polynomials and Vandermonde distribution. Another reviewer considered the framework new and the techniques of independent interest (explicit contrast).
- Missing a converse lower bound on convergence time (e.g., a counterpart to Theorem 4.2) to strengthen the characterization.
- The modification of the correlation loss (Eq. 14) deviates from the standard single-index learning setup; it is unclear whether the setting still qualifies as single-index learning.
- Several writing issues and typos:  
  * “corresponds”, “any other works that demonstrates”, “dynamcis”.  
  * Assumption 4.4(iv) should be \(\Omega(\sqrt{N})\) instead of \(O(\sqrt{N})\); Lemmas 4.5, 4.6 should be \(\exp(-\Omega(N))\) instead of \(\exp(-O(N))\) (and similar issues elsewhere in the appendix).  
  * Notation clash: the summary statistic \(m\) in Theorem 4.1 conflicts with its use as an index.  
  * The \(\inf\) in the definition of information exponent appears unnecessary.  
  * Theorem 4.2(iii) condition \(v_0 = 1 - r_0^2\) could be more intuitively stated as \(\|Aw_0\| = 1\).  
  * Lemma 4.5 should specify the randomness is over \((a_m)\).  
  * The experiments (Figure 1) are unclear: how does the initial alignment correspond to the gradient flow time?
- One reviewer suggested discussing connections to recent work on anisotropic Gaussian data, where structure in the covariance can reduce or remove dependency on the information exponent; similar observations might be possible here.
- Minor suggestions: after Eq. (6) the phrase “In other words” is confusing; Corollary 4.7 should delete “And”; Figure 1 y‑axes could be shifted to hide unused range.
