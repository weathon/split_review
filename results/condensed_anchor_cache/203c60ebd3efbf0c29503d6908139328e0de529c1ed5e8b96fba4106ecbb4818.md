## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a point cloud-based instance completion model that operates within scenes. It introduces a seed generator that predicts object centers and offsets, and integrates sparse scene constraints via cross-attention. The method is evaluated on a newly constructed synthetic dataset, ScanWCF, which provides watertight and collision-free ground truth. Experiments show that the approach outperforms existing methods RfD-Net and DIMR on the instance scene completion task.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with clear illustrations.
- The method includes several well-motivated design improvements: VI-PointConv in the partial encoder, object center and offset prediction for aligned seed patches, integration of scene constraints into the seed generator through cross-attention, use of global attention (rather than local) in the coarse-to-fine decoder, and a surface normal prediction module enabling mesh reconstruction via NKSR. All components are verified in ablation studies.
- The method addresses a fundamental and important problem: point cloud completion in real scenes by leveraging both object-centric information and scene visibility/occupancy.
- The new dataset ScanWCF improves upon existing benchmarks (e.g., ScanARCW) by providing watertight and collision-free ground truth, which is a useful contribution for future research.
- Experiments demonstrate significant improvements over the state-of-the-art instance scene completion methods RfD-Net and DIMR (Tables 1 and 2). Reviewers 1 and 4 found the results promising and the technical novelty sufficient for a conference publication.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty of the overall architecture:** Reviewer 2 noted that the method follows the same "top-down" architecture as RfD-Net, and the performance boost primarily comes from better-quality individual modules rather than a fundamentally new approach. The main contribution is the object completion module that uses scene context.
- **Justification for global attention over local attention is weak:** Reviewer 1 points out that the claim that local attention struggles with non-canonical shapes is insufficiently justified. The paper should investigate why global attention is inherently better for non-canonical objects and whether the improvement stems from the encoder (VI-PointConv) rather than the attention type.
- **Missing details on implementation:** Reviewer 1 raised several missing specifics: how the global shape descriptor is generated (MLP layers, activation, pooling), why the “patch seeds” are named as such and how they relate to patches, and the exact parameters (kernel size, stride, padding, initialization) of the transposed convolution used for upsampling.
- **Limited ablation gains on scene constraints and object completion task:** Reviewer 3 observed that the improvement from scene constraints (Table 4) is small on metrics like UHD and %COL. On the object completion task (Table 3), each proposed module yields only a modest improvement individually, and the paper does not report performance with all modules combined. This weakens the evidence for the necessity of each component.
- **Generalization to unseen objects and noise conditions is not discussed:** Reviewers 1, 3, and 4 all noted that the paper does not evaluate how the method performs on object categories not seen during training, or under varying levels of noise/incompletion. The paper should discuss these limitations and the conditions under which the method might fail.
- **Comparison of instance segmentation is missing:** Reviewer 3 pointed out that the proposed method uses a separate segmentation method (Mask3D) while existing methods (RfD-Net, DIMR) train segmentation and completion jointly. Without a fair comparison (e.g., using the same segmentation backbone), it is unclear whether the performance gain comes from the segmentation improvement rather than the completion module alone. A related question: how would the completion module perform in the absence of any scene constraints (Reviewer 2)?
- **Paper organization is confusing:** Reviewer 1 noted that the scene-aware object completion (Sec 3.3) is described separately from the seed generator (Sec 3.2) even though it is part of the generator. The current exposition makes it harder to follow the overall flow.
- **Framing of “robust to scale and pose” is misleading:** Reviewer 3 argued that in instance scene completion, scale and pose are already handled by the scene context, so this claimed contribution is unnecessary and not a true innovation.
