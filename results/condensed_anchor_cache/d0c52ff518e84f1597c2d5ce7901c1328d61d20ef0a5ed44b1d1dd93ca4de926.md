## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces three large-scale time series forecasting datasets from the CloudOps domain, the largest with billions of observations. It benchmarks models trained from scratch, fine-tuned, and zero-shot, evaluates various transformer architectures, and investigates scaling laws for model and data size. A proposed pre-trained baseline achieves a 27% error reduction on the largest dataset.

### Strengths
1. The paper provides three new large CloudOps datasets (largest with billions of observations) with detailed processing steps, addressing the lack of large-scale time series datasets. This is considered a relevant contribution by all reviewers.
2. The paper is well-written, clearly explains pretraining/fine-tuning setups and a taxonomy for time series data, and uses well-structured terminology.
3. The study evaluates multiple model architectures (transformer variants) and provides comprehensive benchmarks against classical and deep learning methods (e.g., N-BEATS, Autoformer, FEDformer, DeepAR), serving as a useful reference.
4. It investigates the impact of scaling model parameters and training data size, showing that the proposed architecture benefits from scaling.
5. One reviewer highlighted that this is the first pre-trained time-series model specifically designed for the cloud operations domain (R2); another reviewer considered the lack of architectural novelty a weakness, reflecting disagreement on contribution.

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of novelty / minimal architectural adaptation**: The approach is primarily training a large transformer on a large dataset without significant architectural or methodological innovations. The paper mainly benchmarks existing architectures without novel adaptations tailored to the cloud operations domain (R1, R2).
2. **Limited analysis of transfer learning and distribution shift**: Pre-training and zero-shot testing appear to occur within the same dataset, raising concerns about the generality of zero-shot performance to diverse or new datasets/domains. The paper lacks rigorous analysis of distribution shift between pre-training and fine-tuning datasets. The diversity of time series within each CloudOps dataset is unclear, which could lead to overfitting, especially for large models (R1, R2).
3. **Inadequate baseline model details**: Essential information about baseline models (e.g., model size, training duration) is missing. For instance, DeepAR is second best in Table 5, but it is uncertain if its parameter count is comparable to the proposed transformers. The paper should clarify whether baselines are trained from scratch or pre-trained (R1, R2). Additionally, it is unclear whether deep learning benchmarks (N-BEATS, Autoformer, FEDformer) are only trained on designated training sets; training them on the pre-training set with the same loss function would help attribute gains to architecture vs. dataset size (R2).
4. **Limited improvements from larger models**: Gains from the "Large" and "xLarge" models are marginal compared to the base model. The xLarge model is over eight times larger but shows only minor improvement, suggesting potential overfitting or limited dataset complexity. The paper does not sufficiently explore reasons for this saturation (R1).
5. **Unclear initialization**: It is unclear whether the model is randomly initialized. Exploring pre-trained language models as initialization might be beneficial (R2).
6. **Lack of dataset challenge analysis**: The paper does not analyze fundamental challenges of the new datasets, such as missing values, strength of seasonality, trends, or distribution shifts (e.g., black-friday events, COVID lockdowns). Diversity between the three datasets is not characterized. Qualitative examples of test windows and forecasts per model (e.g., why naive performs well on ali2018 and proposed baselines excel on azure2017) are missing (R3).
