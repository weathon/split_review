## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes SPEED, which combines selective prediction with early exit DNNs by placing Deferral Classifiers (DCs) at each layer to assess sample hardness before early exit. The method is evaluated on text and image classification tasks, reporting 50% risk reduction and 2.05× speedup. Reviews are mixed: two reviewers (scores 5) find the idea novel and the experiments comprehensive; three reviewers (scores 3) raise concerns about writing clarity, baseline comparisons, computational overhead, and code reproducibility.

### Strengths

- The combination of selective prediction and early exit to address overconfidence is novel and addresses a critical problem in DNN deployment (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- Using true class confidence (rather than max confidence) for training deferral classifiers is reasonable and helps mitigate overconfidence (Reviewer 1).
- Extensive evaluation across multiple datasets and domains (NLP and vision) with both in-domain and cross-domain settings (Reviewers 2, 3, 5).
- Reported improvements: 50% risk reduction and 2.05× speedup compared to full inference (Reviewers 3, 4).
- Good generalization capability across domains without retraining (Reviewers 3, 5).
- Writing generally clear for some reviewers (Reviewers 1, 2), with well-structured methodology and related work (Reviewer 3).
- Idea of referring hard samples to an expert is appreciated (Reviewer 5).

### Weaknesses

- **Baselines and comparisons**: Baselines only consider final-layer output; early-exit methods (only PABEE-SR included) are not adequately compared, making efficiency claims less supported (Reviewer 1, also echoed by Reviewer 5). Multiple reviewer questions ask for comparisons to methods with early exit options.
- **Missing ablation studies**: No ablation showing performance when using only Exit Classifier (EC) without Deferral Classifier (DC), or vice versa (Reviewer 1). Also missing: impact of removing DCs, sensitivity to DC architecture (Reviewers 1, 2), and threshold sensitivity (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Threshold justification**: The 33% threshold for labeling hard samples (Section 3.4) lacks theoretical justification; no exploration of how different thresholds affect performance (Reviewers 3, 4). Reviewer 3 notes the code uses mean confidence and variance thresholds (alpha, beta) that differ from the paper description.
- **Computational overhead**: Training and maintaining DCs at every layer incurs substantial overhead in training time, inference latency, and memory, especially for deep models (Reviewer 2). The paper does not quantify this overhead (Reviewer 3). Suggestions: place DCs at a subset of layers or use shared classifiers (Reviewer 2).
- **Expert deferral details**: The method defers hard samples to an expert but does not address practical aspects such as expert availability, cost, or disagreement among experts (Reviewers 3, 5). Reviewers ask what the expert looks like.
- **Questionable claim in motivation**: The claim that high “fake confidence” at shallow layers implies poor predictions at deeper layers (line 80) is debatable; early layers may give overconfident wrong predictions that could be corrected later (Reviewer 1).
- **Reproducibility concerns** (Reviewer 3, most critical): The provided code does not run, missing data files (`data/SST2/SST2.csv`), and the code does not clearly match the described procedure (e.g., lack of implementation for grid search, multiple runs, coverage calculation). Several points of confusion: how were multiple runs managed? How does speedup calculation in code (`12*len(data_loader.dataset)/sum(exit_lis)`) match the paper's method? The number of exit points (num_exits=12) not stated in paper. Loss function for DC training is BCE in code but not specified in paper.
- **Related work similarity**: The approach of training a classifier based on true class confidence is similar to Corbière et al. [1]; differences should be highlighted (Reviewer 1).
- **Writing and presentation issues**: Several reviewers find the paper unclear (Reviewers 4, 5). Examples: Introduction jumps to solutions without defining the two issues (Reviewer 4); motivation section starts abruptly with Figure 2a without explaining importance (Reviewers 4, 5); technical terms not defined (fn, dn in Figure 1; Early Exit BERT not explained on first use; SST-2 dataset not described) (Reviewers 4, 5). Typos: “Defferal Classifiers” line 152, missing periods, misspelled method names in Table 3, “atlayer i” line 220, “Similar to 3,” line 250, “DCs using the the” line 253, inconsistent “back-bone”/“backbone” (Reviewers 1, 5). Figure 2 has distorted aspect ratio and compressed fonts (Reviewer 4). No reference to Appendix A.2, Figure 3 missing caption (Reviewer 5).
- **Domain mismatch**: Paper repeatedly mentions autonomous driving as application but experiments focus on language and image classification, which is confusing (Reviewer 5).

Note: While two reviewers (scoring 5) found the writing clear, others (scoring 3) found it unclear and poorly organized. The reproducibility issue is raised strongly by Reviewer 3 and not contradicted.
