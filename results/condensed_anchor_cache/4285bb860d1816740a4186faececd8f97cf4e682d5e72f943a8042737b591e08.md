## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Diversified Multiplet Upcycling (DMU), a method to construct a Mixture of Experts (MoE) architecture for CLIP by fine-tuning a dense pre-trained checkpoint via Multistage Contrastive Learning (MCL). The resulting CLIP-MoE is evaluated on zero-shot retrieval, zero-shot image classification, and as a vision encoder in LLaVA-1.5. All reviewers found the approach promising and efficient, with improvements over baselines. However, scores were generally moderate (5, 6, 5, 5; one reviewer was more positive at 6 but also raised significant concerns). Weaknesses centered on insufficient motivation and analysis, limited comparisons, unclear ablations, and missing experiments on fine-grained tasks, expert count scaling, and MLLM stability.

### Strengths
- First attempt to apply MoE to a CLIP-style model and early incorporation into contrastive learning (Reviewer 2).
- Achieved MoE for CLIP and proposed the Diversified Multiplet Upcycling method (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Novel method for initializing multiple experts via Multistage Contrastive Learning (Reviewer 2).
- Efficient: does not require retraining from scratch (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Covers a wide range of tasks (zero-shot retrieval, classification, MLLM understanding) and shows improvements over OpenAI CLIP large, LongCLIP, and LLaVA-1.5 (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- DMU is more efficient than simply upcycling the FFN to a MoE (Reviewer 1).
- Well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 2, 3).
- Provides a new perspective on fine-tuning CLIP, validated with proper experiments (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Motivation and analysis**: The claim that CLIP encodes coarse-grained information is not novel, and the paper lacks in-depth analysis of why this issue arises and how the proposed method addresses it (Reviewer 2).
2. **MCL rationale unsubstantiated**: The claim that clustering in CLIP’s feature space simulates grouping by attributes (e.g., color, shape) is not convincing. No t-SNE visualization, routing analysis, case study, or independent downstream performance per expert is provided to demonstrate that experts focus on different details. Concrete evidence (e.g., showing distinct capabilities on datasets with those features) is needed (Reviewers 2, 3).
3. **Insufficient zero-shot classification benchmarks**: Only ImageNet, ImageNet-O, ImageNet-V2, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 are included. The standard CLIP benchmark requires ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R, ImageNet-Sketch, and ObjectNet (Reviewer 1).
4. **Limited zero-shot comparisons**: Mainly compared with Long-CLIP, which is designed for sequence extension rather than improving zero-shot performance. More state-of-the-art methods should be included (Reviewer 2).
5. **Missing fine-grained classification**: Performance on fine-grained tasks (e.g., Stanford Cars, FGVC-Aircraft, Flowers102) is not reported, though the paper claims richer information encoding (Reviewer 4).
6. **Missing MM-Vet benchmark**: This benchmark is of interest for evaluating multimodal understanding (Reviewer 1).
7. **LLaVA-1.5 performance not consistently improved**: Replacing CLIP with CLIP-MoE in LLaVA-1.5 does not significantly improve over five benchmarks; some cases show decline, and results on MME and POPE fluctuate considerably. Additional benchmarks or reasoning cases are needed to demonstrate richer information encoding in MLLMs (Reviewer 4).
8. **Ablation design unclear**: The comparison “w/o S1, S2” in Table 4 introduces too many variables (number of experts, whether experts were fine-tuned, dataset used). It is unclear if this refers to a 2-expert model. Since the full CLIP-MoE uses 4 experts (and 4 MCL stages), the rationale for comparing against a model missing the first two stages is not explained. More distinct ablation studies isolating each component are needed (Reviewers 2, 3).
9. **No analysis of expert count**: The choice of 4 experts is not justified. Experiments varying the number of experts (e.g., 2, 8, 16) and measuring both performance and computational cost are missing (Reviewers 3, 4).
10. **Mislabeling in Table 1**: For text-to-image retrieval on COCO Recap-DC, CLIP-MoE’s R@10=79.4 is lower than Upcycling’s 79.9 but erroneously bolded, which could mislead readers (Reviewer 3).
11. **Typo**: Line 488 states “Table 2” but should refer to “Figure 2” (Reviewer 2).
12. **Unanswered methodological questions**: (a) In MCL, is each FFN initialized from the previous stage’s FFN or from the pre-trained CLIP FFN? (Reviewer 2). (b) Why does upcycling appear less effective than direct fine-tuning in Table 1, yet “w/o S1, S2” in Table 4 (which also lacks MCL stages) outperforms both methods significantly (e.g., on Flickr I2T)? (Reviewer 2).
