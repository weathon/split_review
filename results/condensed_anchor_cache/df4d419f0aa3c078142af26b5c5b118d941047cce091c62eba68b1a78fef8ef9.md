## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates threshold selection strategies for neural network monitoring (e.g., OOD detectors). It compares four strategies for constructing threshold optimization datasets (ID-only, ID+Target, ID+Target+Other, ID+Other) on various image classification benchmarks. The main findings are that knowledge of runtime threats greatly helps threshold optimization, and that without that knowledge, relying solely on in-distribution data is superior to adding generic threat data.

### Strengths
- The paper explores an important but understudied component of the OOD detection/monitoring pipeline: selecting a threshold (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Two useful empirical findings are identified (Reviewer 1).
- The experiments have wide coverage, spanning multiple architectures, image datasets, and different OOD detection methods (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The work addresses a crucial topic for deploying neural networks in critical applications, linking several fields (OOD, uncertainty quantification, reject-based classification) (Reviewer 3; note: this reviewer gave a rating of 1 but still acknowledged the topic's importance).

### Weaknesses
- **Premise and novelty**: The basic premise is questioned – threshold-independent metrics (AUROC, AUPR) exist because different applications have different error tolerances, so using thresholds as the unit of evaluation is ambiguous (Reviewer 2). Threshold-based rejection has been studied for decades (Chow 1970) and many prior techniques are not considered; the suitability strongly depends on domain-specific costs, making general rules hard (Reviewer 3).
- **Contribution unclear**: One reviewer found the contribution unclear (Reviewer 3). Another noted the main contribution is empirical findings, so the experimental design should be more thoroughly motivated and limitations phrased explicitly (Reviewer 1).
- **Unsurprising results**: The results are not surprising: fitting the rejection mechanism to reject data from a certain distribution improves performance, and adding unrelated data makes rejection harder (Reviewer 3). Prior work typically tunes thresholds on a validation set from the same distribution as training (possibly with synthetic data); the assumption of knowing the threat is unrealistic (Reviewer 3).
- **Experimental design issues**:
  - The study is not repeated over multiple seeds; variance of results is unclear (Reviewer 1).
  - Aggregation over many experimental settings may obscure findings; non-aggregated results should be presented in an appendix, and plots with different markers could be used (Reviewer 1).
  - The choice of OOD detection methods and threats is not motivated in sufficient detail (Reviewer 1). The first four pages could better guide the focus on threshold search (Reviewer 1).
  - Additional details needed for reproducibility: parameters of adversarial attacks, etc. (Reviewer 3).
  - Experiments only cover the image domain; extension to other classification tasks is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation and clarity**: The presentation obscures simple concepts (e.g., relation between \(f\) and \(m_f\), what "threat data" can entail). Table 1 is hard to understand (Reviewer 1). The abstract and introduction are too general; results should be stated more concretely (Reviewer 1). Weaknesses of the analyzed approaches are not discussed (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing discussion**: The substantial literature on cost-sensitive learning, which formalizes requirements, is not discussed (Reviewer 2).
- **Reviewer disagreement**: There is significant disagreement among reviewers: Reviewer 1 rated the paper 5 (contribution fair), Reviewer 3 rated it 1 (contribution poor). Reviewer 3 found the results unsurprising and the contribution unclear, while Reviewer 1 considered the findings useful.
