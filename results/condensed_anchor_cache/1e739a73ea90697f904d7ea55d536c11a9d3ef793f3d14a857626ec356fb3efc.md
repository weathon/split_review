## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the Hyperbolic Vision Transformer (HVT), a fully hyperbolic extension of the Vision Transformer that operates in the Poincaré ball. It replaces Euclidean components (positional embeddings, self-attention, feed-forward layers, normalization, optimization) with hyperbolic counterparts using Möbius transformations and hyperbolic distance. Experiments on ImageNet show accuracy improvements over a Euclidean ViT baseline. Most reviewers (scores 3,3,3,3) identify significant issues with novelty, missing citations, and insufficient experimentation, while one reviewer (R3, score 5) finds the work well-executed with strong experimental validation.

### Strengths
- Proposes a fully hyperbolic ViT architecture, including hyperbolic positional embeddings, attention mechanism, feed-forward network, and optimization, with detailed mathematical formulations (R1, R3).
- Clear breakdown of operations and components aids reproducibility (R2, R3).
- Shows promising results on ImageNet, outperforming a Euclidean ViT baseline (R1, R4).
- Scales fully hyperbolic learning to ImageNet-level, a positive sign for the field (R5).
- One reviewer (R3) notes the work is well-executed with strong experimental validation and clear presentation.

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of novelty and poor citation of prior work**: Most components are not new and rely entirely on established hyperbolic operations without adequate citation. Missing references include: Hyperbolic Neural Networks++ [a] (self-attention in Poincaré ball, linear layers), PoincaréResNet [b] (fully hyperbolic batch normalization, residual connections, linear layers), Bdeir & Landwehr 2024 [c] (curvature learning), and surveys [d,e] (R2, R5). The method section (except the last sentence) contains no citations, making it impossible to distinguish novel contributions from re-implementations (R5). Hyperbolic linear layers, residual connections, gradient clipping (Dhingra et al. 2018 [f]), and Riemannian Adam (Becigneul & Ganea 2019) are all previously known but not always cited (R1, R5). The paper claims to be better than previous hyperbolic models (e.g., Hyperformer) but never compares against them (R2). Fully hyperbolic vision models already exist in both Poincaré and Lorentz models [b, c] (R2).

2. **Insufficient experiments**: Only one image classification task on ImageNet (Table 4) and one ablation (Table 5) are reported. Additional evaluations are needed, including other datasets (e.g., CIFAR-100), fine-tuning from the ImageNet pretrained model, runtime and memory footprint comparisons (expected to be much higher for HVT, but important for scalability claims), and ablations of geodesic regularization, layer scaling, and hyperbolic positional encoding (R1, R2, R5). The paper does not explore other vision tasks beyond classification, despite claiming domain focus as the main difference from prior work (R4).

3. **Questionable claims and missing analysis**:
   - The claim of scalability (lines 362, 468) based on parameter count ignores that hyperbolic models are computationally inefficient; runtime and VRAM requirements should be reported (R2).
   - The statement “Poincaré ball model for its computational suitability in vision tasks” (line 94) is not justified or referenced (R2).
   - The curvature in positional embeddings (Eq. 13) appears to be a learnable parameter unrelated to the curvature of the Poincaré ball (R5).
   - ReLU and LayerNorm (Eqs. 10-11) are applied in tangent space, not hyperbolic; this is not clarified (R5).
   - The paper claims theoretical analysis, but none is provided; a suggested analysis is to show the Euclidean transformer is recovered in the limit \(c \to 0\) (R1).

4. **Insufficient explanation and motivation**: The reasons for design choices (e.g., why hyperbolic positional embeddings use Möbius scalar multiplication followed by Möbius addition) are not explained (R1). The paper focuses on engineering improvements rather than providing deep insight into why hyperbolic adaptations lead to better performance (R3). The novelty is primarily integration of hyperbolic geometry without clear unique innovations (R4).

5. **Missing comparisons and clarifications**: The paper should compare against a Euclidean ViT with learnable scaling parameters \(\beta\) in residual connections and head-specific scaling factors \(\alpha_h\) (R1). The impact of hyperbolic positional encoding in Table 5 is unclear – it is either missing or not updated (R2). The batch size configuration for training on 8 A100s is not specified (R2).
