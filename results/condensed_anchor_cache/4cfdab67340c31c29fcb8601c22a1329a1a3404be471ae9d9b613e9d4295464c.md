## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CCDM (Channel-aware Contrastive Conditional Diffusion Model) for multivariate probabilistic time series forecasting. It introduces two main innovations: (1) a channel-aware conditional denoising network that combines channel-independent dense encoders (CiDM) for intra-variate dynamics with channel-mixing diffusion transformers (DiT) for cross-variate correlations; (2) a denoising-based temporal contrastive refinement loss that maximizes mutual information between past observations and future forecasts to complement standard denoising diffusion training. The authors provide theoretical insights linking the contrastive objective to forecasting error bounds. Empirical results on multiple datasets claim superiority over existing diffusion-based forecasters in MSE and CRPS.

### Strengths
- The paper proposes CCDM with two key architectural innovations: (a) a channel-aware conditional denoising network using channel-independent dense encoders and channel-mixing diffusion transformers to capture both intra- and inter-variate temporal correlations; (b) a denoising-based temporal contrastive refinement loss, inspired by InfoNCE (Oord et al.), to explicitly maximize mutual information between past observations and future forecasts, complementing standard denoising diffusion training (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- Reviewer 2, the minority positive reviewer (score 8), finds the idea of extending the diffusion loss with a contrastive loss **new and interesting**, and considers the architecture a **novel combination of known building blocks**. Other reviewers question novelty (see Weaknesses).
- The paper provides a thorough proof and theoretical derivation of an upper bound on the denoising diffusion-induced forecasting error (Reviewer 2; other reviewers note limitations—see Weaknesses).
- Empirically, the method demonstrates favorable performance on several datasets, especially in CRPS (Reviewers 2,3; improvements are dataset-dependent and challenged by Reviewer 4—see Weaknesses).
- The method is efficient, end-to-end trainable, and seamlessly integrates with original diffusion optimization (Reviewer 1).
- The paper includes a comprehensive bibliography (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and theoretical contribution**: Reviewers 1, 3, and 4 question novelty. The diffusion loss and mutual information concepts have been previously explored (Reviewer 1); the use of contrastive learning within diffusion is not intuitively motivated and appears arbitrary (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 3 notes the contribution is **extremely simple**—essentially applying contrastive learning to diffusion models—with insufficient discussion of challenges. The theoretical insights in Section 4.3 largely repeat existing work (Tsai et al., 2020, regarding mutual information bounds) (Reviewer 1); Proposition 1's upper bound cannot be computed in practice, requiring a KL approximation—this is standard and does not effectively link the two losses (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 3 finds the theoretical insights **not cohesive**. Reviewer 4 notes the architecture is **incremental**: CiDM is from TiDE, and the Channel-wise Diffusion Transformer combines the DSW structure from Crossformer with diffusion, making CCDM an "incremental summary" rather than innovative.
- **Presentation and figure clarity**: Figure 1's bidirectional arrows create ambiguity about when each loss (Equations 5 and 6) is applied; Figure 2 does not clearly explain the implementation and timing of negative time series augmentation (Reviewer 1). It is **unclear why the channel-independent module (CiDM) is necessary** given the Diffusion Transformer can naturally handle multivariate input; **no ablation study justifies CiDM's presence** (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 notes minor wording/notation issues (e.g., y_0 and y_K introduced one section too late; p. 6 line 275 "both a positive sample"). Both Reviewer 2 and Reviewer 3 find **Figure 5 hard to interpret**: it gives the impression that contrastive learning has **almost no positive effect**; Reviewer 3 requests error bars to support the claim that contrastive learning is helpful. Reviewer 4 asks for a clear strategy for choosing the density ratio function and constructing negative/positive instances to prevent model collapse.
- **Experimental evaluation and baselines**: Several reviewers note missing comparisons. Reviewer 1 points out the **lack of short-term prediction results using CSDI's benchmark datasets**. Reviewer 3 notes the paper does not compare with recent diffusion-based forecasters (TimeDiT, Latent Diffusion Transformer) from its own related work section. Reviewer 4 extensively criticizes the baseline selection: only four diffusion baselines are shown (SSSD, CSDI, from 2021); many recent models are missing, including LLM-aligned models (OneFitsAll, TimeLLM, LLM4TS), pre-trained TS base models (Timer, Moriai, Moment, UniTime), specific-dataset models (PatchTST, iTransformer, ModernTCN), recent diffusion models (Diffusion-TS, mr-Diff, MG-TSD), and self-supervised models (SimMTM). **Even against the four baselines shown, CCDM shows almost no improvement**: on ETTh, MSE is only **0.0017 lower** than TimeDiff; on Exchange, Appliance, and Weather, CCDM is **worse** than TimeDiff and CSDI; on Electricity and Traffic, average MSE improvement is **less than 10%** (Reviewer 4). Reviewer 1 notes the **absence of mean and variance statistics**, which is critical for diffusion models' inherent uncertainty, and the ablation results in Table 8 appear inconsistent.
- **Methodological concerns**: Reviewer 4 argues that contrastive learning is **discriminative** and may not fully train a generative model; the training task (maximizing mutual information) differs from the downstream generative prediction task, and **no fine-tuning strategy** is discussed. The **computational overhead** of generating N negative samples per iteration per training step is significant and not analyzed (Reviewer 4). The optimal number of negative samples N is **intractable** and must be found by hyperparameter search (Reviewer 2; also noted in p. 20 section A10 and Figure 6).
- **Ablation contradictions**: In the ablation study (Table 8/Table 2), removing contrastive refinement only improves MSE by **1.2% on ECL and 1.08% on Traffic**, suggesting negligible benefit. Reviewer 4 points out a contradictory pattern: on datasets with small channel numbers (ETTh: 7, Weather: 21), removing the channel-wise design drastically increases MSE by **23.34% and 40.28%** respectively, but on the large-channel dataset ECL (321 channels), performance **barely changes** (5.71% MSE increase). This suggests the **channel-wise design fails to capture cross-channel representations for high-channel datasets**, undermining the paper's claimed contribution of establishing cross-channel connections in diffusion models.
- **Missing OOD evidence**: Despite claiming improved out-of-distribution (OOD) performance (line 289), **no empirical evidence is provided** (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 1 also notes that the **two-stage training process is not clearly explained**.
