## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Unbounded Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (UKANs), which remove the need for bounded grids in KANs by using a coefficient generator (CG) model that dynamically produces B‑spline coefficients on an infinite symmetric grid. The authors also present wrapKAN, a GPU‑accelerated library that reduces B‑spline evaluation complexity by a factor of O(grid size) compared to prior implementations. Experiments on regression, classification, and generative tasks compare UKAN against KAN and MLP baselines. One reviewer (score 6, confidence 3) was more positive, highlighting reduced computation time and better or comparable accuracy; the other two reviewers (scores 5, confidence 2 and 4) found the contributions significant but noted limited performance gains over KAN, concerns about the advantage of unbounded grids, and presentation issues.

### Strengths
- **wrapKAN reduces computation time** compared to the original torchKAN (Reviewer 1).
- **UKAN offers better or comparable performance** in regression, classification, and generative tasks (Reviewer 1).
- **The integration of a coefficient generator (CG) model** that dynamically produces B‑spline coefficients enables UKANs to handle unbounded domains, a major advancement over existing KAN architectures (Reviewer 2).
- **GPU acceleration reduces computational and memory costs**, making UKANs practical for large‑scale applications previously out of reach for KANs (Reviewer 2).
- **Significant speed‑up for inference using KANs**; because KAN nodes are more expressive than MLP nodes, this has potential for wide applicability (Reviewer 3).
- **Potential for wide applicability** due to the expressiveness of KAN nodes being combined with faster computation (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Advantage of the unbounded grid is not convincingly demonstrated.** Why are grid updating or data normalization in KAN not preferable? If the grids are unbounded, does an infinite number of coefficients become necessary, and how does this differ from grid updating? (Reviewer 1)
- **Performance improvements over KAN are limited** (Reviewer 1, 2). In Tables 3 and 4, the reported gains are not substantial and could simply be due to increased parameters or randomness (Reviewer 2). The paper should evaluate UKAN on a broader range of datasets to strengthen claims (Reviewer 2).
- **Interpretability trade‑off not addressed.** KAN is known to be more interpretable, especially for symbolic formulas (as cited, arXiv:2407.16674). Improving downstream task performance may not be the most important direction, and the potential interpretability trade‑offs between KAN and UKAN are not discussed (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing comparisons on popular LLM‑style tasks.** Including experiments that clearly show speed vs. accuracy on language prediction tasks, including standard MLP‑based LLMs, would strengthen the paper. How does the method compare to standard MLP architectures when total compute is kept the same? (Reviewer 3)
- **Insufficient latency/speed comparison for Figure 4.** For the setup in Figure 4 (UKAN vs. KAN vs. MLP), the paper should provide a direct comparison of latency and speed of each technique (Reviewer 3).
- **Presentation issues:**
  - Notions such as grid size and other KAN‑specific parameters are not clearly explained for readers unfamiliar with KANs (Reviewer 3).
  - The speed‑up and accuracy tradeoffs should be highlighted early in the paper (Reviewer 3).
  - In Equation (2), the variables δ and g are not defined (Reviewer 3).
  - Figure 3 should be smaller and Figure 4 larger for readability (Reviewer 2).
  - Typo in Figure 3 caption: the KAN paper is cited twice, and the use of ‘[32, 32]’ appears unnecessarily (Reviewer 2). The manuscript also lacks line numbers (Reviewer 2).
- **Method for speeding up is somewhat straightforward** (Reviewer 3).
