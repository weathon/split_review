## Merged Review

### Summary
TEAL (Training-Free Activation Sparsity in LLMs) is a training-free method that induces activation sparsity in LLMs with modern activation functions (SwiGLU, GeGLU, GeLU). It zeros out the p-th layer-wise percentile of hidden-state activations (closest to zero) across all linear projection layers in Transformer blocks. Thresholds are selected via a greedy search minimizing L2 error on a calibration set. Evaluated on Llama-2, Llama-3, and Mistral families (7B–70B), TEAL achieves 40–50% model-wide sparsity with minimal perplexity and reasoning degradation. Custom Triton kernels yield wall-clock speedups up to 1.53× at 40% sparsity and 1.8× at 50% sparsity. The method is also compatible with weight quantization.

### Strengths
- **Relevance, simplicity, and clarity**: TEAL addresses an important applied problem—accelerating inference for widely used LLMs with non-ReLU activations. It is conceptually simple and ships with custom Triton kernels that facilitate adoption. The paper is clear, well-written, and easy to follow (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Strong empirical results**: The method achieves substantial sparsity (40–50%) without retraining, leading to real speedups (up to 1.8×) with minimal degradation in perplexity and downstream accuracy. Experiments span multiple model families and sizes, consistently showing that TEAL outperforms competing methods (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4). End-to-end throughput experiments demonstrate practical value (Reviewer 3).
- **Applicability to modern architectures**: TEAL works with GeLU, SwiGLU, and GeGLU, making it suitable for the latest LLM families. It applies sparsity to all weight matrices in a Transformer block—unlike CATS—achieving greater overall sparsity with less performance loss. It also supports weight quantization for additional efficiency gains (Reviewer 2).
- **Insightful analysis**: Activation distributions for Llama-3-8B are visualized and analyzed, providing intuitive justification for the approach (Reviewer 2). The greedy search method is straightforward yet effective (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The core idea of magnitude-based pruning is similar to prior work, which may limit the perceived innovation (Reviewer 1, score 6, lower confidence 2; other reviewers rated 8 but did not flag this as a weakness). 
- **Missing comparisons and unfair baselines**: 
  * No comparison with structured/semi-structured pruning methods (e.g., Mask-LLM, 2:4 sparsity) that offer a balance between performance and hardware efficiency (Reviewer 2). 
  * The comparison with ReLUfication without fine-tuning is unfair; authors should either discard it or replace it with the fine-tuned version (Reviewer 2).
- **Generalization and scope concerns**: 
  * The calibration set (C4) and evaluation set (WikiText) may not be distributionally similar; the paper should address whether pruning decisions generalize to other datasets (Reviewer 1). 
  * Newer models may use capacity more fully, potentially making activation sparsity less effective; TEAL’s advantage for such highly optimized models needs justification (Reviewer 1). 
  * Applicability to other architectures (Mixture-of-Experts, Mamba) is not discussed—specifically, how magnitude-based pruning interacts with conditional computation in MoE layers or selective-state-space models in Mamba (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing analysis and statistics**: 
  * No standard deviation of sparsity per sample is reported; although sparsity varies across inputs, no statistical information is provided (Reviewer 4). 
  * No detailed memory-footprint analysis, especially for long contexts: memory for histograms, thresholds, temporary buffers, and intermediate results. Scaling behavior with model size and sequence length is not addressed, nor whether memory overhead becomes a bottleneck (Reviewer 3).
- **Fine-tuning potential**: Although TEAL is training-free, a discussion or experiments on simple fine-tuning (e.g., LoRA with <100K tokens) could reveal whether further improvement is possible. If post-sparsification fine-tuning is prohibited, that should be stated. Additionally, any limitations or difficulties when training or fine-tuning a sparsified model are not addressed (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing details and clarifications** (several reviewer questions overlap with missing information or potential issues):
  * Code availability is not mentioned (Reviewer 1). 
  * Formal definitions of matrices (e.g., \(W_{up}, W_{down}\)) and layers (MLP, SwiGLU) are missing (Reviewer 2). 
  * The following points are insufficiently explained: why activation sparsity error increases with batch size (Figure 8) (Reviewer 2); why theoretical latency in Figure 3 is higher than TEAL at small sparsity (Reviewer 3); whether the Triton kernel needs to be rewritten for different GPU architectures (Reviewer 4); how inference speed was tested in Table 3 (autoregressive text generation? input and output lengths?) (Reviewer 4); whether sparsity level changes based on text domain (code, novel, scientific paper, etc.) (Reviewer 4); whether the Deja vu kernel can be applied directly to SwiGLU/GeGLU transformers (Reviewer 2); whether the result of \(\text{SiLU}(xW_{\text{gate}}) \odot xW_{\text{up}}\) is also sparsified and if there are other places where activation sparsity is applied (Reviewer 2); which recent work is referenced at line 107 (Reviewer 2).
