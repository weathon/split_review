## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes PG-Diff, a diffusion model for reconstructing high-fidelity fluid flow fields from solver-generated low-fidelity inputs (different grid resolutions), rather than from artificially downsampled high-fidelity data. The method introduces an *Importance Weight* strategy during training (based on wavelet transform to emphasize high-frequency components) and a training-free *Residual Correction* method during inference (gradient descent on PDE residuals) to address the distribution shift between low- and high-fidelity simulations. Experiments on four 2D turbulent flow datasets are presented. Reviewers agree the problem setting is important, but raise significant concerns about novelty, experimental validation, and technical justification.

### Strengths
- The paper correctly identifies a practically important issue: most prior super-resolution works assume low-fidelity data is downsampled from high-fidelity, whereas real solver-generated low-fidelity data at coarser grids exhibits different dynamics and larger deviations. This problem setting is worth studying. (Reviewer 1, 4, 5, 6)
- The proposed wavelet-based importance weighting is technically well-motivated for emphasizing high-frequency structures and consistently improves performance over baselines in ablation studies. (Reviewer 1, 5)
- The residual correction step during inference is an interesting development that enforces physical consistency with governing PDEs. (Reviewer 6)
- The paper includes four challenging 2D turbulent flow benchmarks with varying characteristics, and the experiments (including ablations and hyperparameter analysis) are rich and clearly presented. (Reviewer 1, 2, 4)
- The writing and figures are generally clear and easy to follow. (Reviewer 5, 6)
- The method outperforms several baseline models on the reported metrics. (Reviewer 1)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and prior work**: The problem of upsampling solver-generated low-fidelity data has been posed and addressed before (e.g., Sarkar et al., 2023; Ogoke et al., 2024). The core idea of using a diffusion model for super-resolution is already established, and the contributions (importance weighting and residual correction) are seen as incremental improvements. (Reviewer 1) Several related works are not cited, including: Fu et al. (2024, Learning on Graphs), Esmaeilzadeh et al. (2020, SC20), Ren et al. (2023, JCP), Gao et al. (2021, Physics of Fluids). (Reviewer 3) The diffusion model guidance technique is similar to prior work on conditional diffusion for inverse problems (DPS, Chung et al. 2022; Mardani et al. 2023, residual corrective diffusion) that is not discussed. (Reviewer 4, 5, 6)
- **Distribution shift claim unsupported**: The paper claims that other models fail due to large distribution shifts, but provides no analysis or experiments to support this. Since diffusion models noise data to near-identical distributions, similar performance should be expected regardless of low-fidelity source. (Reviewer 1)
- **Problem setting and motivation unclear**: The introduction does not clearly state the challenges and limitations of the problem setup; overall presentation is messy in places. (Reviewer 2)
- **Data resolution concerns**: High-fidelity data is simulated on a 256×256 grid for Re=1000+. Prior work (Shu et al., 2023; Kochkov et al., 2021) uses much finer grids (e.g., 2048×2048) for similar regimes, raising questions about whether the high-fidelity data is truly well-resolved. (Reviewer 4, 5)
- **Wavelet importance weighting lacks justification**: The linear mapping from wavelet coefficients to weights (Eq. 6) appears arbitrary and hacky; no intuitive explanation or theoretical motivation is provided. The same effect could be achieved with a simple Laplace operator. Quantitative results (Table 1) show minimal improvement from this component. The connection to reweighting strategies from Simple Diffusion (Chen, 2023) is not explored. (Reviewer 4, 6)
- **Residual correction instability and hyperparameter sensitivity**: Performance degrades when using more correction steps, indicating the method asymptotically worsens with more refinement. This raises concerns about robustness and practical applicability. (Reviewer 1) The choice of optimizer (Adam) in Algorithm 1 is not justified; it is unclear if Adam is re-initialized each step. (Reviewer 5) The residual \( R \) is not properly defined in the main paper, and the calculation is not detailed enough to assess physical relevance. (Reviewer 6)
- **Runtime comparison unfair**: The comparison against a numerical solver only includes the full high-fidelity simulation runtime, not a coarsened solver that achieves similar L2 error to the proposed method. This misrepresents computational benefits. (Reviewer 1)
- **Generalization claims not validated**: Tests on smaller domains (subset of training domain) and smaller dt do not convincingly demonstrate out-of-distribution generalization. The experiments do not show truly unseen distributions. (Reviewer 1)
- **Evaluation of physics coherence is vague**: Reported residuals are large with no reference scale (e.g., residual of reference DNS). No spectrum analysis or mesh convergence study is provided. The paper shows that low- and high-fidelity simulations have similar vortex structures (Figures 4, 8), which weakens the supposed challenge. (Reviewer 5) Reviewers recommend adding standard image SR metrics (PSNR, SSIM) and using clearer benchmarks (e.g., Kochkov et al. 2021 Fig. 2) where low- and high-fidelity diverge significantly. (Reviewer 3)
- **Visual results not superior**: The proposed method does not show clear visual superiority over other diffusion models. (Reviewer 2)
- **LPIPS metric justification weak**: The argument that LPIPS (trained on ImageNet) generalizes to CFD applications is hand-wavy and unsupported. LPIPS is not standard for physics simulations. (Reviewer 1)
- **Multiscale evaluation overclaimed**: The paper claims best or near-best results on all subdomains, but Appendix Figures 11–13 show cases where the method is not the best. (Reviewer 1)
- **Missing real-world robustness**: Experiments are conducted only on clean simulated data; the effect of noise (Gaussian, shot noise) in low-fidelity inputs is not tested. (Reviewer 2)
- **Long-term correction not addressed**: The method only reconstructs a single high-fidelity frame, but the key challenge of multi-scale simulations is that solutions diverge over time. The paper provides no mechanism for continuous correction of the low-fidelity simulation trajectory. (Reviewer 6)
- **Model trained on Re=2000 performs worse on Re=2000 test case (Table 3):** No explanation is given for this counterintuitive result. (Reviewer 1)
- **Bicubic upsampling is surprisingly slow (Table 8):** This suggests an error or unusual implementation. (Reviewer 1)
- **Choice of \( T_{guide} \) not defined:** In Algorithm 1, the reverse process starts from \( x_{T_{guide}} \); how this timestep is chosen and why it does not wash out necessary information is not explained. (Reviewer 3, 6)
- **Diffusion baseline setup unclear:** It is surprising that PG-Diff outperforms conditional diffusion models in Table 1; reviewers question how the diffusion baseline is configured. (Reviewer 6)
