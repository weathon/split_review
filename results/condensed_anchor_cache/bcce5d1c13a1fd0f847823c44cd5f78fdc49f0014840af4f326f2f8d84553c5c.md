## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper applies the maximal update parameterization (muP) framework to second-order optimization, deriving scaling laws for learning rates, initialization, and damping terms specifically for K-FAC and Shampoo. The scalings are designed to keep feature learning stable as network width grows and to enable hyperparameter transfer across widths. The approach is tested on MLPs, CNNs, and ResNets. A side contribution shows that a particular setting of K-FAC produces an implicit bias toward NNGP. After the rebuttal, the authors improved mathematical formality and extended the derivation to layer-wise Gauss-Newton and cross-entropy losses.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and originality**: Generalizing the muP heuristic to second-order optimization (K-FAC and Shampoo) is new (Reviewers 1, 2).  
- **Practical utility**: The derived concrete scaling laws are useful; they yield new insights into the damping parameter for K-FAC and support the use of zero initial weights for the last layer (Reviewer 1).  
- **Technical correctness**: The work is technically sound; the underlying heuristic (Δh ~ O(1) as width → ∞) is validated experimentally (Fig. 1) (Reviewer 2, very confident).  
- **Careful experimental design**: A sanity check (Fig. 5) confirms results are stable across a wide range of learning rates (Reviewer 2).  
- **Empirical validation**: Experiments show muP outperforms standard parameterization (SP), especially as width grows large, consistent with the infinite-width limit (Reviewer 2).  
- **First analysis for second-order HPs**: This is the first work to analyze optimal hyperparameters for second-order methods in this setting, and the experiments confirm the theoretical predictions (Reviewer 3).  
- **Side contribution**: Demonstrating that a specific K-FAC parameter setting has an implicit bias toward NNGP (Reviewer 3).  
- **Improvements after rebuttal**: The revised version improves mathematical formality (Appendix A) and extends the derivation to layer-wise Gauss-Newton and cross-entropy losses, showing the approach is systematic rather than ad hoc (Reviewer 1).  
- **Clarity and thoroughness**: The paper is clear for readers already familiar with muP (Reviewer 2), and the experiments are thorough (Reviewer 1).  
- **Reviewer confidence**: Reviewer 2 is absolutely certain, having carefully checked the math and related work.

### Weaknesses
- **Mathematical presentation**: Initially informal (e.g., Proposition 4.1 phrasing “the optimization becomes valid for…”, Appendix A). This was improved after the rebuttal, but the original presentation was a weakness (Reviewer 1).  
- **Theory scope**: The original theory covered only least-squares losses, though numerics suggested broader applicability. After the rebuttal, the authors added derivations for cross-entropy and Gauss-Newton, but this remains a limitation (Reviewer 1).  
- **Scale of experiments**: The real-world benefit would be clearer if the methods were demonstrated on larger-scale networks than those tested (Reviewer 1).  
- **Theoretical depth questioned**: The extent of the theoretical contribution is not large (largely combining existing methods); the main value is the derived scaling laws rather than new mathematical techniques. Does this work introduce results of wider interest beyond K-FAC and Shampoo? (Reviewer 1)  
- **Narrow method focus**: The work is limited to K-FAC and Shampoo. It does not discuss how muP could apply to Newton, Gauss-Newton, or generalized Gauss-Newton (Reviewer 2, question that doubles as a criticism of scope).  
- **Inconsistent claims about learning rate transfer (Section 5.2, Figure 6)**: Two different interpretations of “transfer” are used: (i) optimal learning rate is stable with width, and (ii) with a fixed learning rate, performance improves with width. In the MLP case, K-FAC with SP shows better transfer of the optimal learning rate than with muP—the muP optimal learning rate shifts toward smaller values as width increases, contradicting claim (i). The second claim (fixed lr improves with width) is more convincing but differs from the first. The intended meaning and claims need clarification and reconciliation (Reviewer 2).  
- **Table 1 limitation**: The muP scalings coincide with NTK scaling for all hidden weights across SGD, Shampoo, and K-FAC; only input and output weights differ (Reviewer 2).  
- **Missing context**: The paper assumes familiarity with muP; readers not already aware of that work need more background (Reviewer 3).  
- **Missing standard deviations**: Experimental results in Table 3 do not report standard deviations, making it hard to assess variability (Reviewer 3).  
- **Variable-layer-width analysis missing**: The analysis assumes uniform width across layers; it is unclear how changing widths individually would affect the conclusions (Reviewer 3, question implying a missing ablation).  
- **Clarity of “transfer”**: The claims in Section 5.2 and the legend of Figure 6 should be made consistent and clearly defined (Reviewer 2, also covered above).
