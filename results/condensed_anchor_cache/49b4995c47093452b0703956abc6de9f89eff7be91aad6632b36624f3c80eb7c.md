## Merged Review

### Summary
Point2SSM is an unsupervised method for building correspondence-based statistical shape models (SSMs) directly from raw point clouds. It uses a DGCNN encoder with a self-feature augment (SFA) attention module to map input points to correspondence points (CPs), trained with Chamfer loss and a pairwise mapping error. Evaluated on spleen, pancreas, and left atrium datasets, Point2SSM outperforms existing methods in surface sampling and correspondence and demonstrates robustness to noise, sparsity, and missing data. The paper is well-motivated and addresses limitations of traditional SSM methods that require clean meshes or templates.

### Strengths
- Well-written and motivated, with clear differentiation from prior work and a thorough literature review.
- Novel unsupervised framework that directly constructs SSMs from point clouds, bypassing the need for noise-free meshes, binary volumes, or predefined templates.
- Uses a DGCNN encoder to incorporate local neighborhood information and an attention module for self-supervised correspondence mapping.
- Results show clear improvements over several recent methods in surface sampling accuracy and correspondence consistency across three anatomical structures (spleen, pancreas, left atrium).
- Comprehensive experiments demonstrate robustness to noisy, partial, and sparse input, which is critical for clinical application.
- Visualizations and numerical evaluation are convincing and well done.
- The attention module provides clear benefits – a nice finding.
- Discusses different mapping algorithms in detail and experimentally validates global correspondence finding.

### Weaknesses
- One reviewer was notably less positive, emphasizing that the technical novelty is limited – the method is built on existing architectures (DGCNN, attention) and losses without new insights or analysis; this is considered a major weakness that is hard to address.
- The method has significant overlap with ISR (Chen et al., 2020); the inspiration/similarities should be acknowledged and cited in Section 3.1, and the differences clarified.
- The claim that enforcing CPs as convex combinations of input points “increases surface sampling accuracy” is not fully justified; an empirical statement or ablation is needed.
- The benefit of adding new samples to an existing model is not explained (e.g., continuing training or a different mechanism).
- The statement in Section 2.3 that previous methods “are prone to overfitting … and are not robust to noise, missingness, and sparsity” is strong and should be softened or further justified.
- Parameter count: Point2SSM has many more parameters than other models (App. E, Table 6); upscaling the others might narrow the gap – clarification needed.
- The effect of minibatch size on the pairwise correspondence loss is not studied.
- Only three anatomical structures are studied; more are needed to demonstrate generalizability.
- The objective describes reconstructing a sparse set of points but in practice uses N=M=1024, raising a concern about the initial objective.
- Pre-alignment of input point clouds is required; automated alignment or robustness to orientation variations would increase applicability.
- Lack of uncertainty quantification in outputs is a concern for clinical decision-making; strategies for integration should be considered.
- Performance with sparse data is not fully explored; optimization for better capture of anatomical variability with limited data could be investigated.
- The pairwise loss is computationally expensive, yet no comparison to groupwise point registration is provided to contextualize the trade-off.
- DPC may not be optimal as a pairwise/groupwise correspondence model; it neither considers instance optimization (shown to improve point registration) nor creates an unbiased mean (simply selects one reference cloud).
- Evaluation could benefit from additional point-distance metrics capturing uniformity (e.g., standard deviation of local point-to-point distances) and rasterization-based metrics for overlap in high-curvature areas.
- Compared models differ in many aspects (e.g., encoders), making it hard to isolate the impact of the correspondence mechanism; a direct comparison using the same DGCNN encoder for both correspondence and autoencoder baselines would clarify.
- Minor presentation issues: specify “Euclidean neighbors” as k-NN; improve figure readability (small text, blue on blue in Fig. 5); include perturbation examples in supplementary; clarify how sparse points are sampled (random vs. farthest point sampling); fix redundant use of “unordered” in Section 3.1.
