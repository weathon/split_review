## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper analyzes latent space dynamics of LLMs to understand jailbreak success. The authors extract a “jailbreak vector” from the activations of one jailbreak class and show it can mitigate jailbreak effectiveness from semantically-dissimilar classes, suggesting a shared internal mechanism. They also find that effective jailbreaks reduce the model’s perception of prompt harmfulness (PCA and cosine similarity analyses). Experiments cover multiple jailbreak tactics across 7B–14B models (Vicuna 7B/13B, Qwen 14B, and one other). They acknowledge exceptions where low harmfulness does not yield low ASR (Section 5.4).

### Strengths

- Provides a new perspective by analyzing latent space dynamics for jailbreak understanding. (R1)
- Demonstrates that different jailbreak types may share a common internal mechanism, and that a jailbreak vector from one class can be used to improve or reduce safety via activation steering. (R1, R3)
- Covers many different jailbreak methods, increasing the evidence for a common mechanism. (R1)
- Offers possible mitigation solutions: the shared jailbreak vector can be used for defense without additional fine-tuning. (R2, R4)
- The paper is well-written with a clear claim and presents clear empirical observations across multiple LMs and multiple jailbreak tactics. (R4)
- The discussion of models’ perception of harmfulness is insightful; the paper does not over-claim and presents findings objectively, including exceptional cases (Section 5.4) and noting that low harmfulness does not always lead to low ASR. (R3, R4)
- (Minority positive: R4 finds no significant weaknesses, while other reviewers raise substantial concerns. This contrast is preserved.)

### Weaknesses

- **Limited model range and lack of diversity:** Only 4 models are evaluated, and critical choices are poorly justified. Vicuna (trained only on ShareGPT without RLHF/DPO) is not representative of current SOTA 7B/13B models. Models behave inconsistently: Vicuna 7B shows better cluster patterns than Qwen 14B Chat, and Vicuna 13B activations are more scattered than Vicuna 7B. It is unclear whether patterns hold for larger, better-aligned models (e.g., Llama 2/3) or models with different alignment techniques. (R1, R2, R3 Q1, R4 Q2)
- **Missing evaluations for practical use:** Subtracting jailbreak vectors reduces ASR but may degrade model helpfulness, correctness, or truthfulness. One experiment (line 371) even induced content repetition. The paper should evaluate on general benchmarks (e.g., MMLU, AlpacaEval) to assess utility. (R1, R3)
- **Related work is insufficiently connected:** The related work section omits recent studies that directly explore jailbreak mechanisms (e.g., adversarial perturbations in the embedding space, the role of specific layers in processing harmful requests). This limits the paper’s context within LLM security research. (R2)
- **Presentation of methodology is unclear:** A workflow figure showing how jailbreak vectors are calculated and used for mitigation would greatly improve understandability. (R3)
- **Concerns about the cosine similarity finding (Section 5.2):** The positive cosine similarity between jailbreak vectors could be an artifact of representation degeneration (all representations clustered in a cone), not evidence of a shared mechanism. This needs careful re-evaluation. (R3)
- **Layer selection is not validated:** The paper selects a single middle layer (layer 16 for 7B; layers 20 for 13B/14B) without ablating over different layers (e.g., lower, upper, or multiple layers). The choice could influence results; an ablation is needed. (R2 Q, R3 Q3)
- **No automatic jailbreak attacks tested:** The study only uses pre-defined tactics; it should also examine automatic attacks like PAIR or TAP, which discover tactics dynamically via LLMs. Their latent space dynamics would further test generalizability. (R4)
- **Harmless dataset quality is unclear:** The harmless dataset was generated by instructing ChatGPT; it is not stated whether human annotators checked the samples for quality. (R3 Q4)
- **Inconsistent behavior across models is unexplained:** The paper notes that LLMs do not behave the same (line 256) but does not investigate whether this is due to data or architecture. (R3 Q5)
- **Sampling vs. greedy decoding:** The paper uses no sampling for decoding, but real-world chatbots often use sampling with temperature. A study of temperature selection would verify the robustness of the conclusions. (R3 Q2)
- **Safety training status of models is ambiguous:** It is unclear whether all evaluated models underwent RLHF for safety; this could affect the validity of the analysis. (R3 Q1)
