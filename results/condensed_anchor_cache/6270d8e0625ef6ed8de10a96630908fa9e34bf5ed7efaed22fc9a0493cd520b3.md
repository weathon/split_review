## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces HyPoGen, a hypernetwork that generates policies for unseen tasks without any demonstrations by simulating gradient-based optimization. It biases the hypernetwork towards optimization through a chain-rule-structured iterative update in a latent space, decoding back to policy parameters. Experiments on locomotion and manipulation benchmarks show HyPoGen outperforms state-of-the-art baselines. Two reviewers (ratings 8) found the architecture novel, the writing clear, and the experiments extensive, especially the analysis showing that the iterative process reduces loss. Two reviewers (ratings 6) raised concerns about high variance in the results, missing cost/ablations, and limited task diversity; they still acknowledged the method is well-motivated and the improvements are interesting.

### Strengths
- Well-written and clearly organized, with thorough discussion of related work and motivation for the architecture design.
- Significant and consistent performance improvements over state-of-the-art methods on locomotion and manipulation benchmarks, using the same policy network and specification encoder.
- Novel architecture: HyPoGen introduces inductive biases by simulating multi-step gradient descent, generating pseudo-gradients conditioned on task specifications and enforcing chain-rule structure across policy network layers.
- Addresses a realistic and difficult problem: generalizing policy generation to unseen tasks without any demonstration.
- Extensive experimental validation including case studies (Table 4) showing that the iterative optimization process effectively reduces loss, supporting the claim that the hypernetwork emulates optimization.
- Insightful analysis section ("Does HyPoGen actually perform optimization?") that demonstrates the connection to iterative optimization.

### Weaknesses
- High variances reported for Table 1/2 (see Table 13/14) are on the order of 100–200, making it harder to interpret the average gaps (often tens of units). Exact confidence intervals should be reported in the main text. (Reviewer 4, rating 6)
- Training process in Section 4.2 is described briefly; pseudocode in the appendix would clarify the end-to-end training of all parameters (including θ₀) using BC loss (Eq. 1). (Reviewer 2, Q1)
- Analysis of generated policy statistics with different θ₀ (Table 3) is ambiguous: it is unclear whether other hypernetwork parameters are retrained for each θ₀ value. If not, the statistics may not reflect adaptation; if retrained, the hypernetwork could still be remembering a fixed set of parameters for each specification given θ₀. (Reviewer 2, Q2)
- Computational costs of HyPoGen not addressed; comparison with baselines (e.g. HyperZero) needed. An ablation is missing: does the majority of benefit come from the delta formulation (Eq. 5) alone, without the full chain rule (Eq. 6/7)? (Reviewer 4)
- Generalization shown qualitatively in Fig. 4–9 is not clear except for Fig. 4. More precise metrics are needed, e.g., reporting performance within vs. outside one standard deviation of the training task distribution. (Reviewer 4)
- Requested additional analysis: quantify similarity between predicted pseudo-gradients and true gradients from standard gradient descent to strengthen the claim that the hypernetwork emulates optimization, rather than memorizing an update path. (Reviewer 3)
- Missing experiments: applicability to discrete action spaces; potential difficulties or benefits. (Reviewer 1)
- Lack of experiments on more distinct tasks (e.g., Meta-World [1]) instead of only speed/size modifications. (Reviewer 1)
- Variance of success rate not shown; only average success rate is provided. (Reviewer 1)
- Task specification sampling: is it uniform? Is the source-test partition uniform? For Cheetah (Table 6), with ~40 specifications and 40 samples, nearly every specification could appear in either source or test. (Reviewer 2, Q3)
- Dimension of the latent compressed space is not reported. (Reviewer 2, Q4)
- What happens if HyPoGen is run for many more steps (Fig. D)? Does loss continue decreasing or overfit? (Reviewer 4)
- Strategy for choosing the number of hyper-optimization steps; do harder (e.g., more out-of-distribution) tasks require more steps? (Reviewer 4)
- Does HyPoGen scale to larger policy networks or harder environments (currently only 2-layer MLPs)? (Reviewer 4)
- Reference to learned optimizers [arXiv:2209.11208] could be relevant; also, would directly training the hypernetwork on true gradients (exactly emulating backpropagation) yield benefits? (Reviewer 3, questions that overlap with the gradient alignment weakness)
