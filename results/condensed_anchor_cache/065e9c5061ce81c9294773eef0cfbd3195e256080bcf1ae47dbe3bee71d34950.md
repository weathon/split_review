## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the optimization and generalization of two-layer transformers (softmax attention with trainable query-key parameterization plus a linear layer) optimized with Sign Gradient Descent (SignGD) on a linearly separable noisy binary classification dataset. The authors identify four stages in the training dynamics, prove fast convergence but poor generalization, and show that Adam exhibits similar behavior under the same conditions. They argue that both SignGD and Adam require high-quality data. Experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets support the theoretical results.

### Strengths
- **Novel four-stage analysis of training dynamics:** The paper provides a comprehensive theoretical framework that decomposes the complex transformer optimization into interpretable stages (R1, R3). This deepens theoretical understanding of how SignGD optimizes softmax attention with trainable query-key parameterization, and to our knowledge is the first such analysis (R3).
- **Important practical insight:** The demonstration of fast convergence but poor generalization on noisy data is a valuable finding that can guide optimizer selection and dataset preparation in practice (R1). It also offers a new perspective on why Adam may struggle with generalization in noisy settings (R2).
- **Strong theory-experiment combination:** The theoretical proofs are validated by experiments, which strengthens the overall message (R2). The analogy to Adam makes the results practically relevant (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Unrealistic assumptions limit applicability:** Several assumptions are overly strong and deviate from real-world conditions.  
  - \( d = \Omega(\text{poly}(n)) \) and \( \sigma_p = \Omega(d^{1/4} n^3) \) (noise variance scaling polynomially with sample size) are not typical; the large noise variance may overshadow the signal (R3). These need justification or sensitivity analysis.  
  - The two-layer transformer omits multi-head attention, layer normalization, residual connections, and depth—modern architectures are much deeper (R2, R3). The defined value vector \( v = \bar{w}_{v,1} - \bar{w}_{v,-1} \) (difference of averaged parameters) is artificial and lacks clear connection to how value parameters are used in practice (R3).  
  - The i.i.d. Gaussian noise assumption may not capture structured noise in real-world transformers, which often exhibit robustness to noise—a discrepancy the paper does not adequately address (R1).  
- **Contradictory empirical observation challenges core claim:** In Figure 2(d), test loss for less noisy data (SNR=1.0) is higher than for noisier data (SNR=0.5) when using SignGD, contradicting the expectation that higher-quality data improves generalization. This undermines the theoretical claim that poor generalization is due to noise. An explanation or additional analysis (e.g., overfitting on cleaner data) is required (R1).  
- **Clarity gaps in training dynamics exposition:** The description of the four stages (Section 3.1) lacks explicit mapping of training steps to stages and does not illustrate transitions (e.g., pre- and post-transition values of key noise, query noise). Providing data plots before and after critical transition points (e.g., Stage I→II in Figure 1(a)) would greatly improve clarity. The current focus is on within-stage behavior only (R1).  
- **Limited validation on deeper/realistic architectures:** The analysis is restricted to a two-layer transformer. It is unclear whether the identified dynamics (four stages, fast convergence, poor generalization) persist in multi-layer, multi-head transformers used in practice (R2, R3). Testing on deeper architectures or real transformer models would be valuable (R1, R2, R3).  
- **Insufficient explanation of SignGD-Adam differences:** Figure 2 shows notable differences between signGD and Adam (e.g., the sign of \(\langle w_{Q,s}, y_i \epsilon_i \rangle\), linear vs. sublinear convergence) but the paper provides only minimal explanation for these differences and their implications for understanding Adam’s practical behavior (R3).  
- **Minor presentation issues:** Variable \( i \) in the equation at Line 168 is not explicitly defined (though inferable) (R3).  
- **Missing suggestions for improving generalization:** The paper shows that both SignGD and Adam generalize poorly on noisy data but does not discuss potential strategies to mitigate this (e.g., regularization, data augmentation) (R2).
