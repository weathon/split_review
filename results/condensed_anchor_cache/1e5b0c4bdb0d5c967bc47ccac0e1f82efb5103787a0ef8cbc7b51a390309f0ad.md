## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a non-uniform patch sampling strategy to emphasize cerebellum structures in brain MRI super-resolution. The motivation is correctly defined, but the methodological contribution is trivial and the experimental validation is incomplete. Reviewers agree the work does not meet ICLR standards.

### Strengths

- Motivation: the focus on preserving fine-grained cerebellum structures in super-resolution is well-defined and clinically relevant. (R2, R3, R4)
- Overall analysis and questions raised are accurate and important factors for the community to consider. (R3)
- Qualitative results in Fig 6 show improved structure in the cerebellum. (R1)
- The paper is easy to follow. (R4)
- The state-of-the-art review is written correctly. (R2)

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient technical contribution for ICLR.** The method simply augments the dataset with more cerebellum patches, a well-known class-balancing trick in machine learning. The authors use out-of-the-box architectures and only experiment with a sampling strategy. R1 and R4 call it trivial and not significant; R1 states the shortcoming cannot be fixed within the review period. **Contrast:** R3 rated contribution as 3 (good), but still gave an overall rejection rating (1), indicating the idea has merit but the execution lacks depth.
- **Validation methodology is insufficient.** The authors assume standard Cartesian sampling of k-space and neglect the phase of the signal when transforming data to k-space. They do not discuss how accelerated acquisitions (e.g., SENSE, GRAPPA) affect the low-resolution simulation, despite the HCP data being acquired with parallel imaging. (R2)
- **Analysis of low-resolution input is incomplete.** The claim that using 6.25% of k-space is closer to real-life low resolution is not supported by evidence. The effect of different k-space fractions (scales) and the impact on the model’s ability to synthesize missing information should be investigated and discussed. (R3)
- **Critical issue with patch-balancing method.** If patches are created after extracting the cerebellum via segmentation, patches that overlap between cerebrum and cerebellum are lost. No explanation or discussion of this is provided. (R3)
- **Missing experimental investigations.** The balancing scheme is only tested with a 50-50 proportion; other balances, patch sizes, and the effect of input resolution on the balancing experiment should be studied. (R3)
- **Inconsistency with related work.** The related work mentions transformer-based models, but the analysis includes only CNN and GAN-based models without any explanation for their exclusion. (R3)
- **Table 1 is unclear.** Row one lists "HR & LR" while other rows list "HR & SR". It is also unclear what the "Baseline" model is (e.g., zero filling in k-space?). (R1)
- **Figure 4 appears to have a bug.** The HR and SR image columns look identical. (R1)
- **Code not provided.** The paper mentions a GitHub repository, but reviewers did not receive any code. (R3)
- **Typo:** "KernalGAN" should be "KernelGAN". (R2)
- **Venue mismatch.** Several reviewers suggest the work might be more suitable for a medical image analysis venue (e.g., Medical Image Analysis, MELBA, ISMRM) rather than ICLR. (R1, R2, R4)
