## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces DNABERT-S, a genome model fine-tuned from DNABERT-2 to produce species-aware DNA embeddings. Two key innovations are proposed: (1) Manifold Instance Mixup (MI-Mix), which mixes hidden representations at randomly selected layers and trains the model to predict the mix proportions, and (2) Curriculum Contrastive Learning (C²LR), a two-phase scheme: Phase 1 uses weighted SimCLR to pull same-species embeddings together; Phase 2 applies MI-Mix as a harder contrastive task. Evaluations across 23 datasets demonstrate gains in species clustering (e.g., doubling Adjusted Rand Index), metagenomics binning, and few-shot classification (e.g., matching 10-shot baseline performance with 2 shots). The authors also release a benchmark dataset.

### Strengths
- **Novel application of i-Mix to DNA hidden states**: MI-Mix adapts the i-Mix idea to DNA sequences by mixing hidden representations at a random layer, solving the lack of a natural input mixing strategy for sequence data (R2, R3).
- **Comprehensive evaluations**: Extensive testing on 23 datasets covering unsupervised clustering, metagenomics binning, and few-shot classification, plus thorough ablations (R1, R2, R3).
- **Strong empirical gains**: DNABERT-S significantly outperforms VAE and transformer baselines—e.g., doubles ARI in clustering, identifies twice more species from unlabeled sequences, and exceeds top baselines in 10-shot classification with only 2 shots (R1, R2, R3).
- **Clear presentation and well-motivated design**: The paper is well written, with a clear goal, solid methodological choices, and a shared benchmark dataset that is useful to the community (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty of core techniques**: MI-Mix and C²LR are adaptations of known methods (i-Mix, Mixup, SimCLR, curriculum learning). The paper does not sufficiently justify why these strategies are particularly suited for metagenomics, nor does it explain why mixing hidden states is superior to mixing input sequences for DNA (R1). One reviewer is notably more negative on novelty (score 5) while others (6, 6) acknowledge the new application is still a contribution (R1 vs. R2, R3).
- **Missing critical baselines and benchmarks**:
  - No comparison to standard bioinformatics tools BLAST or MMseqs2 (R1).
  - No comparison to state-of-the-art metagenomic binning methods COMEBin (Wang et al. 2024) and CLMB (Zhang et al. 2022) (R3).
  - The established CAMI II challenge benchmark for metagenomic binning is not used, despite being referenced (R3).
  - Table 1 lacks a baseline that also uses species labels for training; all compared models are either untrained or trained on language-modeling objectives only (R2).
  - A full per-dataset performance table for the classification tasks (showing results for each baseline on each dataset) is missing (R3).
- **Insufficient analysis of C²LR’s contribution**: Removing C²LR causes only a –1.13 to –1.17 decrease in performance, questioning its utility given the added computational cost. The paper could be simpler by focusing solely on MI-Mix (R2, R3).
- **Ablation study lacks depth**: The synergy between weighted SimCLR and MI-Mix is not explored mechanistically—e.g., no analysis of the embedding space showing how the combined approach improves cluster separation (R1).
- **Potential data leakage**: GenBank is a pre-training source; the paper does not verify whether training and test datasets overlap, nor does it quantify any such overlap (R1). Additionally, fair comparisons based on sequence identity are not performed (R1).
- **Lack of meta-level details and computational cost analysis**:
  - No table comparing number of parameters, embedding dimensions, inference time, or memory usage across models (R3).
  - Time and memory consumption of different embedding methods during training and inference are not reported (R1 question).
  - Hyperparameter selection and tuning process are not described (R1 question).
- **Unclear parameter justification and preprocessing details**:
  - The threshold of 100 sequences per species for classification is not justified (R1).
  - Redundancy reduction steps are not thoroughly explained (R1).
- **Figure issues**:
  - Figure 1: marker size obscures clustering details (R1).
  - Figure 4 layout overlaps with text (mentioned at line 435 by R1, R2).
- **Missing analysis of impact of dataset complexity**: Performance of metagenomic binning under varying numbers of genomes and highly imbalanced relative abundances is not explored (R3 question).
- **Unclear relationship to prior work**: The difference between Manifold Instance Mixup and the original Manifold Mixup (Verma et al. 2019) is not clarified (R3 question); more discussion is needed (R2).
- **No discussion of extension to other biological sequences**: The paper does not address whether the method can be applied to RNA or proteins and what adjustments would be required (R1 question).
