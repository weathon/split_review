## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates resolution preferences across vision-language tasks, proposes an empirical formula to determine the optimal resolution for a given task, and introduces a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method to adapt LLaVA to that resolution. Three reviewers scored 5 and one scored 6. The positive reviewer praised the investigation and extensive experiments, while the others raised critical concerns about limited baselines, lack of comparison with dynamic-resolution models, insufficient task selection, superficial analysis, and presentation issues.

### Strengths
- Interesting and well-motivated exploration of resolution preferences across different vision-language tasks, showing that resolution influences performance (R1, R2).
- The empirical formula for determining optimal resolution (based on image complexity and uncertainty variance) is a novel contribution (R2, R3).
- Parameter-efficient fine-tuning technique (e.g., adding LoRA and training only key parameters) effectively extends pretrained VLLMs to the selected resolution – method lifts performance of LLaVA (R1, R2, R4).
- Comprehensive experiments on optimal resolution calculation and validation, with solid logic and well-organized presentation (R1, R3). One reviewer (R1, score 6, confidence 4) specifically found the experiments extensive and well-organized.
- Task-driven dynamic resolution is a meaningful direction (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited baseline / poor generalization**: The investigation uses only LLaVA. This single-model architecture raises concerns about broader applicability; findings may be specific to LLaVA’s design (R1, R4). Two reviewers noted the baseline is too limited (R4 confidence 5; R1 confidence 4).
- **Ignored existing dynamic-resolution methods**: The paper assumes all VLLMs have fixed resolution, which is false. Many models (e.g., MiniCPM-V2, Qwen2VL) already use dynamic resolution; the paper does not discuss or compare with them (R2 confidence 5).
- **Missing comparison with other dynamic-resolution techniques**: No comparison with tiling-based methods like InternVL’s dynamic number of tiles, which would help assess novelty and effectiveness (R3 confidence 4).
- **Task selection misses resolution-critical datasets**: The chosen tasks lack highly resolution-dependent benchmarks such as DocVQA, where resolution matters most (R2 confidence 5).
- **Task-wise resolution selection is superficial**: The relationship found between task and optimal resolution may be overfitting to specific benchmarks rather than capturing sample-wise or abstract task categories. A single resolution for a whole image ignores regions with varying complexity; sample-wise adaptation would be more efficient and justified (R3 confidence 4).
- **Pipeline is not end-to-end**: The two-stage process (determining resolution then fine-tuning) looks unsightly, like cascading two VLMs, and requires heavy image preprocessing. An end-to-end design would be preferred (R4 confidence 5).
- **Insufficient analysis of QA dependence**: Optimal resolution should also depend on the specific question (e.g., “panda presence” vs. “bamboo count”) – not only image complexity and model uncertainty. The paper lacks analysis on this (R3 question).
- **Statistical distribution of key variables not examined**: The optimal resolution is determined from means of image complexity C(T) and uncertainty variance V(T) across all samples in a task. Their variance within tasks is not reported; high variance would undermine the significance of the mean (R3 question).
- **Missing experimental details and presentation issues**: Training data specifics are not provided; the experimental section lacks detail while the case study is lengthy (R3). Figure and table captions are too sparse, requiring the reader to search the main text for interpretation (R3 confidence 4).
- **Discrete image representation not addressed**: The paper does not consider whether discrete visual representations in VLLMs suffer from similar resolution problems (R1 question).
