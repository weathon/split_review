## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a training-free method for multi-character consistency in visual storytelling. It identifies self-attention leakage across frames as a key problem. The approach uses LLM-based chain-of-thought reasoning to localize subjects, then generates images with a bounded cross-attention layer to enforce layout, a bounded cross-frame self-attention layer to reduce inter-character attention leakage, and a cross-frame token-merging layer (plus negative token unmerging) for fine-grained consistency. The method is 30× faster than training-based methods (e.g., textual inversion, DreamBooth-LoRA) and outperforms prior methods quantitatively, qualitatively, and in user studies.

### Strengths

- Addresses an important and under-investigated problem: multi-character consistency in visual storytelling (R1, R3, R4).
- Well-written paper with a comprehensive related work review and a good analysis of the self-attention leakage issue (R1, R3, R4).
- The bounded cross-frame self-attention layer is effective in reducing inter-character attention leakage, as shown in qualitative results (R1).
- The negative token unmerging is a nice minor contribution that further improves consistency (R3).
- Method is training-free and optimization-free, making it runtime-efficient (zero training cost, fast inference) (R1, R3, R4).
- Integration of LLM for layout planning is well-motivated and reduces manual effort (R4).
- Strong empirical results demonstrate superiority over baselines both quantitatively and in a user study (R3).
- Adaptable across domains without further training (R2).

### Weaknesses

- **Missing quantitative ablation:** No quantitative ablation of the individual contributions (bounded cross-attention, bounded cross-frame self-attention, token merging, and unmerging). Qualitative ablation in Fig. 9 suggests within-frame self-attention bounding is most impactful, but this is not backed by numbers. (R1, R3)
- **Unclear explanation of bounded cross-attention layer (Sec. 4.2):** The description is hard to follow. Notation in Eq. (4) is problematic (intra-image but uses N×N instead of K×K); the idea behind adding log of M is not explained. Multiple reviewers requested a clearer intuitive explanation and better justification. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Similarity to prior work and lack of innovation (minority view):** One reviewer (R2, score 3) found the approach lacks innovation, as it resembles cross-attention masking from (Tewel et al., 2024) and combines RPG and SDSA. The claim that using cross-attention masking is infeasible (L160–161) is vague and unsupported by images/data. Other reviewers (R1, R3, R4) found the method reasonable and well-motivated.
- **Missing exploration for more than two characters:** Paper claims multi-character consistency but only shows results with two characters. Results with n>2 would strengthen the claim. (R1, R3)
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity:** No analysis of how sensitive results are to hyperparameters (α, β_d, timestep for token merging) or how to tune them. (R4)
- **Relative scale inconsistencies:** Qualitative examples show scale inconsistencies (e.g., a duck appears much larger in the second image). Unclear whether this stems from storyboard layout planning or the removed cross-attention. (R3)
- **Notation issues:** In line 205–206, "number of desired storyboard frames N" should be "B" (R4). Term "negative token unmerging" seems to be either "negative token merging" or "token unmerging" – terminology is inconsistent. (R3)
- **Missing baselines:** SeedStory is used in separate comparisons (Fig. 7 & 8) but is missing from main quantitative tables (Table 1 & 2). Clarify why. (R1)
- **Edge cases unclear:** No discussion of when or why the method might underperform, especially with more objects or complex scenes. (R2)
- **Claims in abstract:** Abstract claims "30× faster than training-based methods", but other optimization-free methods also achieve similar speedups; this claim should be contextualized. (R3)
- **Fig. 3 needs clearer elaboration:** Depiction of self-attention leakage between sub-figures (a), (b), (c) is not well linked; more detailed examples or visualizations would help. (R1, R2)
- **Dataset statistics missing:** Main paper does not mention statistics of generated multi-subject prompts (relegated to supplementary L798). Include in main paper. (R1)
- **Minor typos:** L200 "fine-grain", L419 "cross-frame". (R1)
