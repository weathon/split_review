- Scores: 3, 6, 5, 3, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes the Non-Backtracking Graph Neural Network (NBA-GNN), which updates messages without incorporating the message from the previously visited node, thereby reducing redundancy caused by backtracking walks in standard message-passing GNNs. The authors provide theoretical analysis linking non-backtracking updates to alleviated over-squashing (via sensitivity bounds) and to expressiveness for stochastic block model recovery. Empirical evaluations on long-range graph benchmarks (LRGB) and transductive node classification tasks show competitive performance. Reviewers are divided: some find the idea interesting and experiments convincing; others consider the novelty limited, the theoretical contribution weak, and the computational cost insufficiently reported.

### Strengths

- The proposed NBA-GNN addresses an important issue in GNNs—redundancy of message flows and its impact on downstream tasks—and using non-backtracking updates is a well-motivated approach (R2, R3). The concept is drawn from established work on random walks (R5).
- The paper is generally clear, well-written, and illustrated with helpful figures (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5).
- The empirical evaluation demonstrates clear improvements over standard GNNs, particularly on long-range tasks (e.g., Tables 1-2) and heterophilic node classification (R2, R3, R5). Results on LRGB are convincing (R1, R5), and the method achieves state-of-the-art on several datasets (R3, R5).
- The theoretical connection to stochastic block model recovery (Section 4.2) provides relevant support for expressiveness (R2, R4). The analysis of over-squashing via sensitivity is a reasonable attempt (R3).
- One reviewer (R2) found the approach novel and the analysis thorough, rating 6.

### Weaknesses

1. **Insufficient acknowledgment and discussion of closely related prior work.** Several reviewers note that the idea of using non-backtracking operators in GNNs was previously proposed in Chen et al. (ICLR 2019) “Supervised Community Detection with Line Graph Neural Networks.” That work introduced Line Graph Neural Networks (LGNN) which perform message-passing on a directed line graph implicitly enforcing non-backtracking. The paper under review does not sufficiently differentiate NBA-GNN from LGNN, nor does it include LGNN in experimental baselines (R1, R3, R4, R5). Additionally, earlier work on backtrackless walks in graph kernels (Mahé et al. ICML 2004; Aziz et al. IEEE TNNLS 2013) uses a highly similar transformation of undirected edges into directed nodes to prevent backtracking but is not discussed (R5). Redundancy-free message passing using simple paths (Chen et al. NeurIPS 2022) is only cited in passing; a detailed comparison on redundancy removal and over-squashing is missing (R5).

2. **Theoretical results are not convincing or have limited novelty.**
   - Lemma 1 and Theorem 1 provide only upper bounds on sensitivity. Since the bound for NBA-GNN is larger than that for standard GNNs, the conclusion that NBA-GNN alleviates over-squashing is not supported—a larger upper bound could simply indicate a looser bound, and the actual derivatives could be equal or even smaller for standard GNNs. The result is at best a weak indication (R1, R3). For moderate-to-large node degree \(d\), the decay rates of the two bounds become very close, yet Table 1 shows large performance gains on dense graphs, casting doubt on the bound’s tightness (R1).
   - The oversquashing analysis relies on a proposition that holds only for trees, far from the setup of NBA-GNNs on general graphs (R1).
   - The second theoretical analysis (Section 4.2) is essentially a restatement of known results from the spectral clustering community (e.g., Bordenave 2015, Stephan & Massoulié 2022) and adds limited novelty (R1, R4).
   - Theorems 2 and 3 are unclear: the learning task is not precisely defined, it is not explained how learnable parameters affect the result, and it is not shown whether standard (WL-class) GNNs cannot solve the same tasks (R5). Expressivity compared to GIN is not empirically evaluated (R5, Q1).

3. **Missing implementation details and computational cost analysis.**
   - The construction of the non-backtracking operator and the precise message update rule incorporating the non-backtracking constraint are not clearly described. It is unclear whether the non-backtracking adjacency matrix is precomputed or updated on-the-fly, making reproducibility difficult (R2). This also makes it hard to assess the claimed connection to LGNN (R4).
   - The time and memory complexity of NBA-GNN is high (two embeddings per edge), but only briefly mentioned in Limitations. Preprocessing time is not reported, nor are runtime/memory comparisons with baseline GNNs (including those designed for long-range dependencies). A detailed breakdown of computational cost (training and inference) is needed (R2, R3, R5). One reviewer (R3) notes that the additional expense is insufficiently explored.

4. **Novelty is limited.** Several reviewers (R1, R4, R5) consider the contribution incremental given the prior existence of LGNN and other non-backtracking graph methods. The theoretical analysis is light and the architecture is very similar to existing work (R4). Reviewer 4 gave contribution score “poor” (1).

5. **Weaknesses in experimental evaluation.**
   - Results are reported as the maximum of two variants (NBA-GCN and NBA-GIN), which can inflate performance; separate results should be reported (R5).
   - Only two out of five LRGB datasets are tested; performance on the other three (e.g., Peptides-func, Peptides-struct, and possibly others listed in Table 2 caption) is omitted (R2, Q). The paper states “three datasets” in Figure 2 but it is unclear which ones.
   - No experiments on the contextual stochastic block model (CSBM) with node features are provided (R4, Q).
   - The role of LapPE (Laplacian positional encoding) is not ablated: results for standard GIN/GCN with LapPE on LRGB are missing, making it hard to attribute gains to non-backtracking rather than to LapPE (R5, Q5).

6. **The begrudgingly backtracking variant is not well justified.** The intuition for why allowing limited backtracking (begrudgingly) performs better is unclear, and whether redundancy is inherently problematic is not addressed (R5, Q4). A natural generalization to \(k\)-hop non-backtracking is not discussed (R5, Q3).

7. **Minor issues.**
   - Missing references on spectral expressivity of GNNs (Kanatsoulis et al., Ribeiro, A.) (R1).
   - The paper can be wordy and repeats observations (R1).
   - The limitations paragraph should be expanded given the high computational overhead (R1).
   - Figure 3 caption contains repetitions (R5).
   - Reference to arxiv:1306.5550 (first use of non-backtracking matrix for node classification) is missing (R4).
