## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Consistent Proxy Tuning (CPT), an extension of Proxy-tuning that adds a consistency term to the proxy model’s training objective by including the frozen black-box model’s logits. This aligns the training objective with the inference ensemble. The method is evaluated on both LLMs (Llama family) and CLIP-like VLMs. Reviewers are split: two find the method clear, well-motivated, and effective (scores 5); two find the contribution minor, the accuracy gains marginal, and the experimental setup or comparisons incomplete (scores 3). All agree the paper is well-written and easy to follow.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and simplicity**: The paper is well-written, with clear logic that is easy to follow. The proposed method is straightforward, elegant, and easy to implement, making it beneficial for practitioners. (Reviewer 1, 2, 3)
- **Effective motivation and implicit regularization**: By incorporating the large model’s logits into the loss function of the small model, the training objective becomes closer to the ensemble effect used during inference. This acts as an implicit regularization, ensuring the small model’s output aligns with the actual inference combination form. (Reviewer 2)
- **Comprehensive experiments**: Extensive experiments are performed with both LLMs and VLMs, and actual training time is reported. The extension to vision-language models further demonstrates the method’s applicability. (Reviewer 1, 2, 3)
- **Importance of the problem setting**: The considered setting of black-box tuning is important and of broad interest given that the most capable models are closed-weight. (Reviewer 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and minor contribution**: The method is a very minor addition to the original Proxy-tuning [Liu et al. 2024]—directly optimizing the small proxy model during training is a trivial remedy. It does not fundamentally change the approach. (Reviewer 3, 4)  This contrasts with the more positive view that the addition is a natural and effective improvement (Reviewer 1, 2).
- **Marginal accuracy gains**: In most cases, the accuracy improvement over vanilla Proxy-tuning is too little relative to zero-shot and white-box tuned results. While consistent tuning yields slightly better numbers, the advantage may not outweigh the disadvantages. (Reviewer 3, 4)
- **Significant computational cost**: CPT requires computing logits from the black-box model for every training sample, introducing additional memory/computational overhead during training. For truly black-box models like GPT or Gemini, this incurs potentially large API bills. This limits practicality for larger-scale models without high-resource infrastructure. (Reviewer 1, 2, 3) The method also requires the black-box model to be accessible during optimization, which contradicts the strict black-box setting. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing comparisons to prior work**: The paper ignores a substantial body of previous black-box optimization methods, particularly **Ormazabal et al. (CombLM, 2023)** which proposed a very similar approach (tuning a small proxy model and combining with the black-box output). Other relevant works [Diao et al. 2022, Cheng et al. 2023, Chen et al. 2023, Guo et al. 2023, Oh et al. 2023, Liu et al. 2023] are also not discussed or compared. The authors should compare not only on accuracy but also on the number of API calls. (Reviewer 3)
- **Incomplete baselines for NLP tasks**: Prompting techniques such as chain-of-thought (CoT) can be applied in the black-box setting and should be included as baselines, at least for NLP tasks. (Reviewer 4)
- **Unjustified vision experiments**: The experiments on vision tasks use CLIP-like models, which are not closed-source. The black-box fine-tuning setting for such models is not well-justified. The authors should evaluate on autoregressive VLMs like OpenFlamingo or Llama 3.2 Vision, which have closed-source counterparts (e.g., GPT-4o). (Reviewer 4)
- **Lack of analysis on why proxy tuning works and the consistency benefit**: The paper does not provide intuition or deeper analysis for why proxy-tuning is effective, or how the inconsistency is defined when both small and base models access logits over the full vocabulary (and similarly for the CLIP case). The claim that “inconsistency may lead to sub-optimal solutions” lacks support from references or theoretical reasoning. (Reviewer 1, 2) Furthermore, it is not always beneficial to maintain consistency between training and inference (e.g., Direct Preference Optimization does not enforce it); the paper should discuss scenarios where consistency is advantageous and where it is not. (Reviewer 2)
- **Sensitivity to hyperparameters**: The impact of parameters α_train and α_test (shown in Figure 2) indicates that careful tuning is necessary, which may complicate implementation. (Reviewer 2)
- **Experimental design positioning**: The paper positions CPT as a type of parameter-efficient fine-tuning, but it is more appropriately viewed as task-oriented efficient fine-tuning (similar to the original Proxy-tuning work). More interesting use cases (domain adaptation, imbalanced learning) would strengthen the paper. (Reviewer 1)
