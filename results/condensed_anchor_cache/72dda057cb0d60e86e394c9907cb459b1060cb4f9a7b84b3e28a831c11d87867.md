## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents a large-scale empirical analysis of offline model-based reinforcement learning using DreamerV3 across 31 environments (DMC, Metaworld, MinAtar). The main finding is that encountering Out-of-Distribution (OOD) states at test time is a primary cause of performance degradation for offline agents. The authors propose mitigation strategies: incorporating exploration data and adding limited on-policy samples based on the world model loss ratio. While the paper is thorough and well-structured, several reviewers (scores 3,3,5,6) find that many conclusions replicate well-known issues from offline RL and that the solutions are not sufficiently principled or generalizable.

### Strengths
1. **Thorough experimentation:** Extensive experiments across various environments (31 tasks using DreamerV3) provide strong support for the claims. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)
2. **Clear presentation:** The paper is well-organized, with clear definitions, consistent notation/plotting, and a logical trajectory from analysis to mitigation strategies. (Reviewers 2,3,4)
3. **Good motivation and structure:** The analytical approach to understanding deficiencies in model-based RL is appreciated, and the paper is well-referenced with good coverage of related literature. (Reviewer 4)
4. **Specific finding clearly communicated:** The role of OOD states as a key challenge is clearly presented, and experimental evidence (e.g., world model loss comparisons) effectively illustrates the issues. (Reviewers 1,3)
5. **Reproducibility information provided:** Necessary details for reproducibility and compute requirements are included. (Reviewer 4)
6. **Minority more positive view (Reviewer 4):** Reviewer 4 highlights that the paper is well written and formatted, covers a broad range of settings, and notes that the motivation (asking "what is the best data collection strategy for MBRL?") is good despite reservations about significance.

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of novelty and trivial findings:** Many insights are already established in prior work. The issue of "lack of self-correction causing OOD errors" is well-known in both offline RL and offline MBRL (e.g., CQL). Using on-policy samples or exploration data for broader coverage has been studied in offline model-free settings. The mixed reward function and OOD detection using ensemble+supervised learning are standard practices, not innovative. (Reviewers 1,2,3,4)  
   - Specific evidence: Reviewer 1 notes that the root cause (OOD states) is the same as in offline model-free RL; Reviewer 2 cites references [1][2][3][4]; Reviewer 3 notes the exploration-exploitation trade-off is well-established; Reviewer 4 states the findings are “quite trivial” (e.g., training on expert trajectories leads to OOD overestimation, and adding online data improves performance is not surprising).
2. **Insufficiently effective and principled solutions:** The proposed mitigation strategies (exploration data and on-policy samples based on world model loss ratio) lack a principled approach for when/how to incorporate data. The method relies heavily on task-specific hyperparameters (e.g., exploration weight, OOD ratio threshold), limiting generalizability across environments and datasets. No systematic parameter selection process is provided; the paper mentions upper bounds derived from Active agent performance, implying the Active agent must be trained first. (Reviewers 2,3)  
   - Specific evidence: Reviewer 2 notes “no principled approach for deciding when and how to incorporate these data”; Reviewer 3 notes “task-specific parameter adjustments” and “no systematic way to determine these parameters, relying instead on an unspecified process.”
3. **Unrealistic benchmarks and limited generalizability:** The benchmarks (DMC, Metaworld, MinAtar) are not very realistic. Conclusions may not hold for more powerful simulators where collecting exploratory data is harder (e.g., early termination). Missing experiments on more realistic domains (Maniskill, LeggedGym, HumanoidBench). (Reviewer 1)  
   - The paper also assumes the ability to curate offline datasets (e.g., adding exploration data), which conflicts with the offline RL assumption that data is pre-collected without access to the online environment. (Reviewer 4)  
   - Claim that current efforts focus on expert data (line 29) is misinformed; D4RL includes random, medium, mixed policy datasets. (Reviewer 4)
4. **Missing comparisons and ablations:**
   - No decoupling of world model and policy network: e.g., use the world model of the Active agent but train policy from scratch (Passive variant) to analyze coupling. (Reviewer 1)
   - No horizontal comparisons across different MBRL algorithms; only DreamerV3 is used. (Reviewer 2)
   - No experiments on environments slightly different from training environments to test if Active agent superiority disappears when world model cannot accurately predict states. (Reviewer 1)
   - Unclear intuition behind the Tandem agent: an offline policy that mirrors the Active agent’s action order is unusual and unlikely to be employed offline. (Reviewer 3)
   - Reviewer 4 questions how the Tandem agent (same seed, same data) can perform differently; only possible explanation is hardware indeterminism? This lack of analysis undermines claims.
5. **Ambiguities and confusions in the paper:**
   - Abstract lines 19-23 are confusing: suggesting “problem with offline learning is that it collects data online” is contradictory. (Reviewer 4)
   - Figure 1 shows only an agent, no demonstration of the world model. (Reviewer 4)
   - Explanation of the Active agent is unclear. (Reviewer 4)
   - Equation 5 introduces a policy input reconstruction loss not used elsewhere. (Reviewer 4)
   - Line 217: DreamerV3 already has online data collection, so the argument is unclear. (Reviewer 4)
   - Lines 234-237: unclear statement about Passive agent’s catastrophic mistakes. (Reviewer 4)
   - Line 300: claim that imagined trajectories have higher world model loss than replay buffer trajectories is trivial (equivalent to test vs. training loss). (Reviewer 4)
   - Section 4.1: Assuming we can roll out a policy for collecting offline data is misguided; if such interaction is possible, we could just do online RL. (Reviewer 4)
   - Section 4.2: Claim that offline data is “often cheaply available” conflicts with earlier statement that “collecting expert trajectories is expensive.” (Reviewer 4)
   - World model loss as a pessimistic indicator of performance degradation: Figure S2 shows instability and scale variation across environments; interpretation is unclear when loss is unstable or small. (Reviewer 2)
   - Missing pseudocode for the proposed approach limits clarity and reproducibility. (Reviewer 3)
   - Minor issues: bibliography formatting errors, bulk references without explanation, equation 1 using π on both sides, undefined critic loss term Rt, whitespace on pages 8-9, missing space after period on line 514. (Reviewer 4)
6. **Lack of deeper analysis:** Most analysis reproduces previous findings (compounding error, distribution shift) without additional insight specific to the MBRL setting. (Reviewer 2)  
   - Specific request: provide deeper analysis beyond standard narratives, e.g., causes and impacts of OOD errors uniquely in MBRL. (Reviewer 2)
