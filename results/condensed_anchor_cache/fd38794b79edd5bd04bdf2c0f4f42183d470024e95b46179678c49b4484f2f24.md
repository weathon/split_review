## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces SinMDM, a diffusion model that learns from a single motion sequence. It uses a shallow UNet with QnA (local) attention to restrict the temporal receptive field, preventing overfitting and promoting diversity. SinMDM is applied to spatial and temporal in-betweening, motion expansion, style transfer (harmonization), and crowd animation. It is compared against Ganimator, GenMM, and MDM baselines using coverage, diversity, SiFID, and user studies, showing state-of-the-art performance and efficiency.

### Strengths
- First work applying diffusion models to single-motion learning.
- Paper is well-written, clear, with high-quality supplementary video.
- Key idea: reducing the receptive field (via shallow network and local QnA attention) to avoid overfitting and encourage diversity is simple, intuitive, and effective.
- Architecture is lightweight and efficient; the use of relative temporal positional embeddings is noted.
- Results are visually impressive and backed by thorough numerical evaluations (coverage, global/local diversity, inter/intra-diversity differences, SiFID, computational efficiency) and user studies.
- Demonstrates several interesting applications (spatial/temporal composition, harmonization, style transfer, crowd animation) that leverage the diffusion framework at inference time without retraining.
- Core takeaway (Reviewer 4): reducing temporal receptive field successfully enables training generative motion models with very small data.
- Reproducible with provided code, data, and pretrained models.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: Most techniques are adapted from existing works (diffusion for motion [MDM], single-instance generation [SinFusion], guidance injection [ILVR], QnA attention). Reviewers 1 and 2 rated contribution as 2 (fair), while Reviewers 3 and 4 gave 3 (good). The minority reviewer (score 6, confidence 3) found the overall contribution less significant, while the other three reviewers (score 8) considered the approach effective but still incremental.
- **Questionable quality of editing applications** (minority view): Spatial composition appears like simple cut-and-paste of upper/lower body; style transfer is seen as temporally synchronizing step pace rather than true style transfer. Other reviewers considered these results impressive, indicating disagreement.
- **Trade-off between narrow receptive field and temporal smoothness**: Narrow field leads to abrupt transitions (e.g., unnaturally fast turns) and, as shown in Table 3, a wider receptive field gives better FID but reduces diversity. Reviewer 2 questioned how smoothness is guaranteed given the narrow field.
- **Lack of justification for UNet over sequence-specific architectures**: Reviewers asked why UNet (designed for spatial data) is preferred over 1D convnets, WaveNet, LSTMs, or TCNNs, especially since motion data is sequential and the artificial 2D reshaping may not be optimal (Reviewer 3). The term “single input UNet” also requires clarification (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons**: Reviewer 2 requested a comparison with Ganimator using the HumanML3D representation (the paper uses HumanML3D features). 
- **Unaddressed failure cases**: Reviewer 1 asked under what circumstances the method fails.
- **Limited exploration of motion representation**: Only the HumanML3D format is tested; no experiments with other motion representations (Reviewer 1). The paper should discuss how representation choice affects performance.
- **Minor**: The phrase “arbitrary topology” in the abstract may imply cross-skeleton generalization, which is not intended (Reviewer 4).
