## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Genie, a three-stage method (content preparation, generation, filtering) that uses LLMs with in-context examples to automatically generate high-quality content-grounded data for tasks like long-form question answering and summarization. The generated data is filtered using reward model scores and format checks. Models trained on this synthetic data (e.g., for LFQA on ELI5/ASQA, summarization on CNN/DailyMail) match or outperform models trained on human-written data, with superior faithfulness. The method is also applied to the medical domain. Human evaluation confirms the synthetic data's naturalness and quality.

### Strengths
- The paper is well written and presents a clear, systematic three-stage methodology (content preparation, generation, filtering) that ensures quality and faithfulness (R1, R3).
- Using LLMs to generate high-quality datasets is logical and intuitive (R2).
- Experiments demonstrate effectiveness for both QA and summarization tasks, with models trained on synthetic data achieving parity with or outperforming human-data-trained models (R1, R2, R3).
- Multiple forms of evaluations are conducted, including human evaluation and comparisons across multiple datasets (ELI5, ASQA, CNN/DailyMail) (R1).
- The paper provides insightful empirical findings comparing synthetic vs. human data, particularly regarding faithfulness (R3).

### Weaknesses
- Novelty and scope are limited: the two-stage generation+filtering approach is obvious and has been tried in prior works; applying it to content-grounded tasks without tackling fundamental task-specific challenges may not meet the bar for ICLR (R1). The paper lacks critical contrast to prior works; R1 is open to increasing score if such context is provided.
- The paper claims high noise in existing datasets (e.g., news domains) without empirical validation (R2). Additionally, the test set originates from the same noisy source; a Multi-Dimensional Quality assessment with inter-annotator agreements on both original and synthetic datasets is needed to demonstrate genuine quality improvement (R2). The paper lacks specifics on annotation details (R2).
- The reason why synthetic data improves performance on noisy test sets is not explained (R2 question, overlaps with missing analysis).
- The cost-efficiency argument is questionable: using synthetic data from Llama 70B to train a Llama 70B model may not be economical if the LLM itself works well. Directly sourcing text from the web can be free, and existing filtering methods can ensure quality (R2).
- The paper exaggerates contributions: content-grounding generation is a broad paradigm (Instruction+Input→Output), but only two tasks (long-form QA and summarization) are validated, and data construction relies on the format and source (Wiki) of previous datasets, raising doubts about generality and practicality in an open-world context (R3).
- The paper reads more like an empirical exploration with limited inspiration for future work (R2).

Note: Reviewer 1 rated the paper 6 (positive), while Reviewers 2 and 3 rated 5 (more critical). The above weaknesses reflect a consensus across reviewers, with R1 also acknowledging the novelty concern.
