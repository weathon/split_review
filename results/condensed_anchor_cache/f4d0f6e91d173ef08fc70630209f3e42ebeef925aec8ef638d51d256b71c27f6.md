## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies distributed logistic regression with heterogeneous, separable data and proposes two Local GD variants (Two-Stage Local GD and an unimplementable Local Gradient Flow) that provably converge at rate \(O(1/(KR))\) when using large step sizes \(\eta \gg 1/K\) after a warmup period. This improves over prior worst-case \(O(1/R)\) bounds for Local GD. Reviewers generally found the work novel and well-written, but a minority flagged a potential proof issue in the main theorem, and others questioned the practical significance, motivation for the two-stage estimator, experimental breadth, and baseline comparisons.

### Strengths
1. **Novel insight into local steps for distributed logistic regression**  
   (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3) The paper identifies a setting where local updates provably accelerate convergence by exploiting the logistic loss landscape rather than reducing client dissimilarity. This provides a stepping stone for future work on understanding when local SGD can be beneficial in practice.

2. **Clear theoretical contributions**  
   (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4) Convergence rates for Two-Stage Local GD and Local GF are rigorously derived and improve upon existing worst-case bounds for Local GD. The proof sketch effectively conveys the key idea of using large step sizes after a warmup period.

3. **Well-written and well-organized**  
   (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3) The paper is clearly structured and the exposition is easy to follow.

4. **Experimental validation**  
   (Reviewer 1) Experiments on synthetic and MNIST datasets support the theoretical claims, though the experimental scope is limited (see weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited generality and scope**  
   (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4) The analysis relies heavily on properties of the logistic loss and linear separability. No discussion is provided on extending the results to other loss functions (e.g., self-concordant functions as in Bach 2014, or any loss whose derivative is bounded by the loss) or to settings where separability is replaced by an interpolation condition. The paper does not explore modifications needed for losses commonly used in neural networks or generalized linear models.

2. **Potential proof issue affecting the main theorem**  
   (Reviewer 2 – minority high-scoring reviewer; score 8 but could not recommend acceptance due to this issue) When going from eq. (296) to (297) and (352) to (353), the bound \(-(2\eta-4\eta^2 H)(F(\bar{w}_t)-F(u)) \le -\eta(F(\bar{w}_t)-F(u))\) is used under \(\eta \le 1/(4H)\), but this holds only when \(F(\bar{w}_t)-F(u) \ge 0\). Lemma 17 is claimed for any \(u\), yet \(u\) is not necessarily the minimizer. This affects Corollaries 1 and 2 and therefore Theorem 1. The reviewer suggests checking whether the results can be recovered with small regularization.

3. **Weak motivation and clarity of the two-stage estimator**  
   (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4) The paper does not convincingly justify why a two-stage approach with two different step sizes is necessary or natural. One reviewer asks whether multiple stages with adaptive step sizes could further improve communication complexity. Another reviewer finds the choice arbitrary and notes that practical guidance (how to choose step sizes, how to compute the lower bound on \(\gamma\) required for the first stage) is missing. The computational overhead of the two-stage procedure versus a single-stage approach is not discussed.

4. **Insufficient experimental validation and baseline comparisons**  
   (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4) Experiments are limited to small synthetic datasets and MNIST, which do not capture realistic scale, heterogeneity, or high dimensionality. The paper lacks direct comparisons to standard algorithms such as Minibatch SGD or full-batch GD (including using warmup) to demonstrate practical advantage. The left column of Figure 1 shows only one curve with no explanation. The claimed rate improvement over \(O(1/R)\) is not shown to be practically significant relative to simpler methods.

5. **Toy setting for Local Gradient Flow**  
   (Reviewer 2) The Local GF guarantee is for \(M=2\) workers, one sample per worker, and uses an unimplementable gradient flow subroutine. This makes it a questionable model for realistic distributed optimization.

6. **Dependence on unknown parameter \(\gamma\)**  
   (Reviewer 3) The duration of the first stage grows as \(\gamma\) decreases, requiring a lower bound on \(\gamma\) to be known and shared across agents. This limits practical applicability.

7. **Presentation issues and missing details**  
   (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4) The paper inconsistently switches between “Local GD” and “Local SGD” without clarifying the distinction. The meaning of \(r_0\) in Theorem 1 is not clearly interpreted. The justification for the bound in Equation (13) is insufficiently explained (Lemma 3 application). The subfigures in Figure 1 are missing explanation for the left column. The question of whether the \(O(1/(KR))\) rate is optimal in any sense is left unaddressed.
