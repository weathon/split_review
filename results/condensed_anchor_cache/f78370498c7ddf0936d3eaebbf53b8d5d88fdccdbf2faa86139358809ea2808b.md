## Merged Review

### Summary
A transformer-based Diffusion Planner is proposed for closed-loop autonomous driving planning. It uses a diffusion model to jointly predict motion of all agents and generate a trajectory for the ego vehicle, modelling multi-modal driving behavior without rule-based refinement. Classifier guidance, based on safety and comfort costs, is applied during the diffusion denoising steps to steer the ego trajectory. The method is evaluated on the nuPlan benchmark (Test14 random benchmark) and a newly collected 200-hour delivery-vehicle dataset. The authors claim state-of-the-art closed-loop performance and robust transferability.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and motivation**: The use of diffusion models for the planning task is considered novel by most reviewers (Reviewers 2, 3); it addresses limitations of imitation learning such as multi-modal behavior and out-of-distribution scenarios. Reviewer 4, however, notes the combination of existing techniques and questions the technical novelty (see Weaknesses).  
- **Architecture and integration**: Jointly modeling prediction and planning as a future trajectory generation task reduces complexity (Reviewer 1). The transformer-based DiT architecture and the classifier guidance mechanism for adaptable planning are clearly articulated (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).  
- **Strong empirical performance**: The method surpasses both learning-based and rule-based baselines on the nuPlan leaderboard and shows impressive closed-loop performance and transferability on the delivery-vehicle dataset (Reviewers 2, 3, 4). Performance is supported by multiple baselines and detailed ablation studies (Reviewer 4).  
- **Practical design**: The model is carefully tailored for practical application, with comprehensive descriptions of implementation, data augmentation, normalization, and experiment design (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparison with PLUTO on delivery-vehicle dataset**: PLUTO (a hybrid method) outperforms Diffusion Planner on NuPlan, but the paper does not report PLUTO’s performance (with or without rule-based refinement) on the delivery-vehicle dataset. Reviewer 1 explicitly asks why this comparison is absent.  
- **Insufficient benchmarking**:  
  - Only the Test14 random benchmark (~280 closed-loop scenarios is used). Results on the Val14 benchmark and the Test14-Hard benchmark (more diverse/challenging scenarios) are missing (Reviewers 2, 3).  
  - Closed-loop reactive simulation and open-loop results are not provided (Reviewer 3).  
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons with other diffusion-based planners**:  
  - The paper should include comparisons with other diffusion-based planning strategies (e.g., [1], [2]) to demonstrate relative advantages (Reviewer 3).  
  - Several diffusion-based motion planners for nuPlan (e.g., [4], [5], [6]) have been published; the authors cite [4][5] but do not include them as baselines (Reviewer 4).  
- **Unclear technical novelty and contribution statement**:  
  - Reviewers 1 and 4 request an explicit, dedicated paragraph at the end of the introduction that clearly outlines which limitations the Diffusion Planner addresses and what specific novel aspects are introduced.  
  - Reviewer 4 states that the paper’s claim of being “the first to fully harness the potential of diffusion models” is vague; the model essentially combines existing techniques (transformer architecture, classifier guidance with manually designed cost functions, data augmentation). This reviewer sees the work as having high practical value but limited novel technical contribution. *Contrast:* Reviewers 2 and 3 consider the framework novel and well-motivated.  
- **Incomplete related work review**:  
  - The discussion in Sec. 3.2 should be moved to a related work section and extended. It misses an important line of research on diffusion models for closed-loop simulation (e.g., [1], [2], [3]) (Reviewer 4).  
- **Guidance evaluation and sensitivity**:  
  - Table 1 only reports results with collision guidance. Questions remain about how the model performs with other types of guidance (especially when multiple guidance costs are used simultaneously) (Reviewer 4).  
  - When multiple guidance terms are combined as a weighted sum, the paper does not discuss whether the scales of different cost terms and their gradients are balanced, nor the sensitivity of the guidance performance to energy function design and cost weights (Reviewer 4).  
- **Clarity issues**:  
  - Line 195: Condition C should be mathematically defined (Reviewer 1).  
  - Lines 466–477: The phrase “Due to space limitations” is unclear; what space limitations? (Reviewer 1).  
  - Appendix A, Figure 8: A legend is needed (Reviewer 1).
