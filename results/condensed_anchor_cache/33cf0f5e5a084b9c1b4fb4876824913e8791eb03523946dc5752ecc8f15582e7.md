## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper theoretically and experimentally analyzes gradient masking in diffusion-based adversarial purification, proposes randomness replay to improve robustness estimation, and introduces a reverse-only diffusion model (RevPure). The reviewers find the core idea—dissecting the roles of randomness and learned priors—interesting and the theoretical illustrations clear. However, they raise concerns about limited experimental scope (datasets, architectures, generation quality), insufficient theoretical depth (overly specific examples, unclear notation), missing metrics to quantify gradient masking, and limited novelty relative to prior work.

### Strengths
- Solid theoretical analysis of the key mechanisms linking randomness in diffusion models to gradient masking; illustrations (e.g., Figure 1) and theorems are clear and easy to follow (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The simple example in Section 4.2 provides good intuition for how randomness causes gradient masking and why EOT fails (R1).
- Interesting observation that the sequential denoising process transforms adversarial examples in a direction orthogonal to the original adversarial perturbation; this insight is novel and valuable for future work (R1, R2).
- Finding that the forward process of diffusion models is unnecessary for adversarial purification, consistent with prior observations in DensePure (R2).
- Writing is well-motivated, with clear introduction, related work, and methodology (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited datasets**: Experiments are only on CIFAR-10; results on larger-scale datasets (e.g., ImageNet) and simpler ones (e.g., MNIST) are needed to demonstrate generalizability of findings (R2, R4).
- **Limited architectures**: Only WideResNet-28 is tested; experiments on diverse architectures such as ViTs and ResNets are required to assess robustness across model families (R2).
- **Generation quality vs. robustness**: The study of FID impact uses only two models (EMA DDPM and Non-EMA DDPM); a more comprehensive set (e.g., checkpoints from different training epochs or models with varying FID) is needed to draw conclusions (R2).
- **Theoretical analysis lacks depth**: The example in Section 4.2 is overly specific; a more general probabilistic theory connecting randomness to gradient masking and explaining how randomness replay improves estimation is missing. The analysis does not clearly link gradient masking to specific diffusion properties (noise schedule, learned score function). Theoretical validation of the diffusion model’s ability to remove adversarial projections (e.g., via spectral analysis of the diffusion operator) is also lacking (R1).
- **Theorem presentation unclear**: Notation in Theorem 1 (e.g., meaning of chi-squared distribution, relationship to adversarial space) and underlying assumptions (nature of the adversarial space) are not sufficiently explained. The chi-squared distribution should be properly introduced (R1, minor R2).
- **Missing metric for gradient masking**: The paper does not provide a direct measure of gradient masking (e.g., cosine similarity between the true gradient and the gradient used in attacks). Observing a drop in attack effectiveness alone is insufficient to conclude gradient masking (R4).
- **Novelty concerns**: The work is built on the DiffPure framework and presents one limitation. Prior work (Carlini et al. 2019) studied attacks on systems with randomness and proposed verifying attacks if randomness is fixed. The difference between randomness replay and that prior method needs explicit discussion (R3).
- **Structure confusing**: Diffusion models and gradient masking are introduced early, but the theoretical analysis is general and applicable to any preprocessing-based defense, making the connection to diffusion models feel loose (R3).
- **Experimental results weak**: Tables show limited results; more comprehensive experiments should be provided (R3).
- **Clarity issues**: Several points need elaboration: (a) why a purification system with randomness causes “a model that does not have useful gradients” (R3); (b) the example about accuracy and perfect attack approximation (“accuracy” meaning and why empirical robustness is a good approximation) (R2); (c) why the forward process is unnecessary given the observation that denoising removes adversarial projections (R3) – illustrating the forward process in Figure 4a would help; (d) how the proposed method affects inference time (R2); (e) writing issue: “Since the successful rate of applying the attack is the cdf of χ2(n), and n is the dimensionality of the data…” is grammatically awkward (R3).
