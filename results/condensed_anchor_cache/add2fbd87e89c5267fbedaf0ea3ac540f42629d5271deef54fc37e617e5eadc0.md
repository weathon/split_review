## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes **Causal Diagnosticity**, a framework to evaluate faithfulness metrics for natural language explanations (NLEs) from LLMs. Using model editing, it generates faithful/unfaithful explanation pairs across three tasks (fact-checking, analogy, object counting) and tests seven metrics (post-hoc and CoT-based) on four LLMs. The main finding is that CC-SHAP outperforms other metrics, but all show substantial room for improvement. Reviewer opinions range from positive (R1, rating 6) to negative (R3 and R5, rating 3), with moderate views (R2, rating 5; R4, rating 5). R1 highlights originality, rigor, and significance; R3 and R5 raise fundamental concerns about synthetic explanations, simplicity of tasks, and whether the ground truth for faithfulness is valid. All reviewers recognize the topic's importance and the extensive evaluation, but most criticize the core assumptions about explanation generation, generalizability, and the necessity of model editing.

### Strengths

- **Novelty and relevance**: The idea of extending diagnosticity to natural language explanations via causal model editing is novel and timely (R1, R3). It addresses a critical need for benchmarking faithfulness in LLM explanations, which is important given widespread LLM use (R1, R2, R4).
- **Rigorous and comprehensive experiments**: Extensive evaluations across three tasks (fact-checking, analogy, object counting) and multiple LLMs, with ablation studies and analysis of alternative editing methods (R1, R2, R3, R5). The inclusion of both post-hoc and CoT-based metrics is thorough (R1, R2). Results are clearly presented in tables and figures (R1).
- **Clear writing and motivation**: The paper is wellorganized; the problem is clearly motivated, related work is discussed, and the approach and results are well described (R1, R2, R5). The main idea of using two models for causal diagnosticity is consistently emphasized (R5).
- **Evidence that existing metrics are weak**: The framework demonstrates that many current faithfulness metrics perform poorly, providing useful corroboration of known flaws (R5). CC-SHAP is consistently shown to best discriminate faithful vs. unfaithful explanations (R1, R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Synthetic explanation assumptions not validated**: The framework assumes that the synthetically generated faithful explanation (\(\overline\epsilon\)) is indeed faithful to the original model (\(\overline\theta\)) and that the unfaithful explanation (\(\widetilde\epsilon\)) is not. This assumption is unsubstantiated and may not hold in practice (R1, R2, R3, R4). Reviewers note: the editing may not cause the intended reasoning path—e.g., for the Rihanna example, the model might simply remove the entity from a set, not follow the synthetic explanation's causal chain (R3). It is unclear why perplexity (used in Section 5.2 to verify faithfulness) indicates faithfulness, and perplexity itself is not evaluated as a metric (R4). If low perplexity correlates with faithfulness, this could be a confound (R4). The authors should provide an analysis of how well synthetic explanations align with actual model-generated explanations (R1, R3) and whether such explanations would even be generated by the LLM (low probability of decoding) (R3).
- **Limiting to open-source models and simple tasks**: Model editing requires access to model weights, restricting experiments to open-source LLMs (e.g., LLaMA, Falcon) and excluding closed-source models like GPT-4 (R4). The three tasks are basic (short CoT responses to simple questions), raising concerns about generalizability to complex, real-world scenarios (e.g., medical QA, multi-step reasoning) (R1, R5). The datasets are overly simplistic and the metrics evaluated are considered premature by some (R5 referencing Lanham et al. 2023, Bentham et al. 2024, Parcalabescu and Frank 2023). A broader range of tasks and longer explanations is needed (R1, R5).
- **Unclear necessity of the two-model setup**: Reviewer confusion about why two models (\(\overline\theta\) and \(\widetilde\theta\)) are needed. Since \(\widetilde\theta\) is used only to generate the unfaithful explanation, one could instead directly modify the faithful explanation \(\overline\epsilon\) to create an unfaithful version, making the edited model redundant (R2, R4, R5). The justification for "causal" in the framework is not fully explained—a simpler edit-compare setup may suffice (R2, R5).
- **Limited novelty and theoretical grounding**: The extension of diagnosticity from feature attribution to NLEs is straightforward; the paper appears more applied than novel (R2). Existing work (Chan et al. 2022b) used random text as unfaithful explanations, which is argued against but without sufficient backing (R2). The framework overlooks other aspects of reasoning faithfulness (e.g., consistency, coherence) and could benefit from complementary metrics or a discussion of limitations (R1).
- **Missing related work and alternative approaches**: The paper does not discuss alternative ways to generate and evaluate LLM-generated explanations, such as [2310.11207] and [2024.findings-acl.19], nor work that questions whether LLMs can explain via NLEs at all ([2405.04382]) (R3).
- **Presentation issues**: Figure placement is disorganized and hurts readability (R4). There is a suspected bug in the legend of Fig. 6 (R2).
- **Open scientific questions** (integrated from reviewer questions):
    - How are the synthetic explanations generated? Are they produced by the LLMs themselves? (R2, R4)
    - Is changing a single fact (e.g., "Paris is not the capital of France") sufficient to override all pretrained knowledge and force a specific reasoning path? (R5)
    - How can the framework be adapted to more complex/realistic scenarios? (R5)
