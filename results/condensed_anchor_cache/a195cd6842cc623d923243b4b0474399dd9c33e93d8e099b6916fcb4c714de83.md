## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Multi-Student Diffusion Distillation (MSD), a framework that distills a pretrained teacher diffusion model into multiple single-step student generators, each specializing in a subset of conditioning data. The method uses existing distillation techniques (Distribution Matching Distillation DMD and Adversarial Distribution Matching ADM) and introduces a teacher-score-matching (TSM) stage to initialize smaller student architectures. MSD aims to improve the quality–speed trade-off by allowing student size reduction while maintaining or exceeding teacher-level FID through ensemble-like capacity increase.

### Strengths
- Paper is well-written, easy to follow, and clearly presents the multi-student distillation setting (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The idea of using mixture-of-experts for distillation is intuitive; assigning each student to a subset of conditions effectively increases model capacity without additional inference latency (R2).
- TSM initialization solves the cold-start problem when student architectures are different from the teacher (R2).
- Different distillation objectives and better initialization are empirically effective; finetuning with adversarial training further improves quality (R1).
- Empirical results are strong: achieves SOTA FID on ImageNet-64 × 64 (1.20) for one-step generation and competitive FID on zero-shot COCO2014 (R2; also reported in Table 1).
- Encouraging results on text-to-image (StableDiffusion) compared to several one-step methods (R2).
- Discussion on data partitioning is valuable, and the review of related work is thorough (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty**: The approach simply extends distillation to multiple students; partitioning the input space and training separate models is a known strategy, and the adaptation to diffusion model distillation lacks substantial technical depth (R4). One reviewer (R2, rating 8) considered the idea novel and commended the implementation, but other reviewers (R1, R3, R4, all rating 3) found the contribution incremental.
- **Marginal performance improvement**: For text-to-image with SD 1.5, the FID improvement is only 0.15 despite using 4 sets of parameters, increasing memory and orchestration complexity (R1). In Tables 1 and 2, gains over DMD2 are small and require more training resources; smaller student models show a significant performance drop (R4, Table 1). The trade-off between model size and performance is not adequately addressed (R4).
- **Lacks strong motivation**: No comparison with efficient architectures, pruning, or caching strategies that also achieve smaller models/lower latency (R1). The paper does not provide novel insights that would justify broad adoption (R1).
- **Missing diversity and mode‑collapse evaluation**: Unclear how well MSD recovers the marginal data distribution. Metrics such as LPIPS_Diversity and Precision‑Recall should be reported to rule out feature or mode collapse (R1).
- **Missing comparisons**: No CLIP score reported on COCO-2014/2017 (could also use HPSv2) (R1). No comparison with standard (non‑adversarial) distillation variants to validate the advantage of adversarial distillation used in MSD (R3). The central claim that students outperform the teacher in Table 1 is not validated for text-to-image SD models (R2).
- **Generality concern**: Method is demonstrated only on DMD and ADM. Preliminary results with other distillation approaches (e.g., Consistency Distillation) on a simple dataset like Mixture‑of‑Gaussian would show generality (R2).
- **Insufficient analysis of the partitioning function**: The paper claims (Line 257) that conditions within a partition should be semantically similar, but no experiments support this; the implementation uses consecutive classes only. A random partitioning baseline is missing (R2, R3). For text-to-image, the inference‑time selection of the appropriate student is unclear because text conditions are not naturally disjoint (R2).
- **Training and deployment costs**: Total training compute is not reported, and the method requires more model storage for deployment; no solution is offered (R1, R4). The added complexity of orchestrating multiple students is not justified by the small FID gains (R1, R4).
- **Minor issues**:  
  – Line 15’s claim that student inference speed is limited by teacher architecture is misleading – student only depends on teacher during training (R3).  
  – Partition function notation \(F(\cdot)=(\cdot,\cdot|\cdot)\) needs proper definition (R2).  
  – MSD results in Table 1 appear to use equal‑sized students; results for smaller‑sized students (as in Fig. 5c) should be included or omitted with explanation (R2).  
  – In Table 1, confirm that NFE=1 because only the student responsible for the given condition is used (R2).  
  – Ablation studies are only quantitative; qualitative analysis (sample inspection) is needed (R3).  
  – Code is not provided, preventing verification of implementation details (R3).
