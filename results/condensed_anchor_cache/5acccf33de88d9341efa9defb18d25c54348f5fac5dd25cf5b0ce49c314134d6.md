## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes EVA, a data-driven initialization for LoRA using SVD on activation vectors and rank redistribution based on explained variance. The method is evaluated on language generation, understanding, image classification, and reinforcement learning. Reviewers generally find the idea straightforward and the experiments comprehensive, but raise significant concerns about theoretical justification, novelty, insufficient empirical gains, and implementation details. Reviewer 1 is most critical of the minimal improvement and practical significance; Reviewer 2 is more favorable but emphasizes missing comparisons and unusual hyperparameter choices; Reviewer 3 notes unconvincing faster convergence and unclear figures; Reviewer 4 questions novelty and the motivation for the initialization. All reviewers request stronger theoretical grounding.

### Strengths

- The proposed idea is straightforward and easy to understand/reproduce (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- The paper is well-written (Reviewer 2).
- Experiments are comprehensive, spanning language generation/understanding, image classification, and reinforcement learning, validating generalizability across multiple domains (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- The data-driven initialization manner is promising (Reviewer 3).
- The additional computational burden is clearly stated (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of theoretical justification.** Reviewers across the board question why performing SVD on activation vectors and initializing LoRA matrices with right-singular vectors is beneficial. The connection between maximizing explained variance in the activation space and optimal fine‑tuning is not established; no theoretical reasoning or intuition is provided for faster convergence or better performance (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Limited novelty.** SVD‑based initialization (PiSSA) and rank redistribution (AdaLoRA) have been explored previously. Applying SVD to activations is the only new element, but this technique already appears in the pruning and quantization literature. The core idea of using activation patterns for parameter initialization has also been studied (Reviewer 4).
- **Minimal and possibly insignificant improvements.** In most tables the gains over baselines are marginal; it is unclear whether they are statistically significant or practically meaningful, especially given the small number of LoRA parameters. Reviewer 1 specifically doubts whether the initialization can have a substantial impact on final performance (Reviewer 1). Other reviewers share this concern indirectly.
- **“Faster convergence” not convincingly demonstrated.** The claim is only supported on Llama‑3.1‑8B/MetaMathQA. Loss curves show fluctuations of similar magnitude to the claimed speedup; the gradient norm plot shows PiSSA with a larger norm but no faster convergence than Random, contradicting the claim. Additional validation on other tasks is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons with relevant works.** The paper does not compare with rsLoRA, LoRA+, and LoRA‑GA (a data‑driven initialization). This makes it hard to assess practical advantages and novelty (Reviewer 2).
- **Rank redistribution analysis insufficient.** Adjusting the rank of small matrices (e.g., 16→17 in (16,512)) vs. large matrices (16→17 in (16,4096)) adds very different parameter counts, making comparisons within the same budget unfair. Hyperparameter search for ρ at {1,2} shows that uniform rank (ρ=1) sometimes yields better results, suggesting rank redistribution is not consistently beneficial; the paper provides no analysis of when or why it helps (Reviewer 2).
- **Unusual experimental setting.** The scaling factor α is set to 1, whereas α=32 is common for rank 16. A comparison with standard settings is needed to show robustness (Reviewer 2).
- **Figure and formula clarity.** Figure 1 has text that is too large, while Figure 2’s left part uses text that is too small. Figure 1 fails to clearly illustrate how matrix A is constructed from right‑singular vectors, the incremental update process, and the adaptive rank allocation. A mathematical formulation for “incrementally update” in Section 3.2 is also necessary (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Missing analysis of data‑type differences.** Table 7 shows performance variation across natural, specialized, and structured data, but no in‑depth explanation is provided for these differences (Reviewer 3).
- **Computational overhead not fully justified.** SVD introduces additional cost that may erode vanilla LoRA’s efficiency advantage; the trade‑off between this overhead and the reported gains is not adequately discussed (Reviewer 1). (Reviewer 4 notes the burden is stated, but the practical impact is not assessed.)
- **Naming ambiguity.** The relationship between “Data‑driven Initialization” (Section 3.2) and “Explained Variance” in the method name is unclear; “Explained Variance” appears to cover only part of the proposed scheme (Reviewer 3).
