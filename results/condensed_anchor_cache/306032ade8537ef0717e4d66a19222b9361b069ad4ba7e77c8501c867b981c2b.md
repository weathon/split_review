## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper presents AAEDY, a semi-supervised framework for anomaly detection in dynamic graphs that trains only on normal data. It combines an adversarial autoencoder with three edge encoding methods (spatial, temporal, contextual) and graph diffusion to capture multi-level information, detecting anomalies by comparing original and reconstructed edges in low-dimensional space. Experiments on six real-world datasets with injected anomalies show AUROC improvements over state-of-the-art baselines. Reviewers agree the problem is interesting and practical, especially the normal-only training approach, but raise significant concerns about novelty, experimental methodology, clarity, and writing quality.

### Strengths

- **Relevant and practical problem:** Anomaly detection in dynamic graphs is important, and the focus on semi-supervised/normal-only training addresses the real-world difficulty of obtaining labeled anomaly data. (R1, R2, R5)
- **Performance improvements:** AAEDY demonstrates notable AUROC gains over existing baselines on datasets with injected anomalies, suggesting potential effectiveness. (R1, R2, R5)
- **Edge encoding design:** The framework merges spatial and temporal information via three encoding methods, and graph diffusion acquires connectivity top-k nodes of the target edge in a time-sliding window, combining edge encoding with neighbor information. (R4)
- **Loss function design:** Adversarial loss, contextual loss, and encoder loss are combined into a single total loss function to ensure reconstruction while avoiding fooling the discriminator. (R4)
- **Paper structure:** The paper is well-structured and easy to follow. (R3, partially R1 – but R1 also notes clarity issues for ICLR standard)

### Weaknesses

**Technical novelty and justification (majority concern – R1, R2, R3, R4, R5)**
- The paper lacks novelty: it primarily combines existing techniques (autoencoder, adversarial idea, TADDY) without clear insights or justifications. (R1, R2, R5)
- The intuition behind the three encoding methods and the importance of Eq. (3) for anomaly detection is not articulated; superiority over existing encoding methods is not justified. (R2)
- Section 3.2 on anomaly detection presents ambiguous contributions; the added embedding layer does not represent significant novelty. (R2)
- The use of autoencoder and adversarial ideas for anomaly detection is well known; the approach is similar to VAE-based anomaly detection (e.g., “Variational Autoencoder based Anomaly Detection using Reconstruction Probability”). The authors should explicitly discuss how AAEDY improves upon that method. (R4, also R2)
- Graph anomaly detection based on diffusion feels outdated; the paper does not meet the originality standard of ICLR (R3 suggests a lower-tier venue).
- The Introduction does not clearly state the motivation or distinguish AAEDY from existing semi-/unsupervised dynamic graph anomaly detection methods (beyond the lack of labeled data), nor discusses the challenges in designing the model. (R3, R5) The connection between abnormal edges and safety in financial transaction systems needs clearer explanation. (R5)

**Experiment methodology and evaluation**
- **Anomaly injection method:** The methodology for generating abnormal data is inadequately explained (no clear description in main paper or appendix). The authors should use multiple types of injection methods and discuss their effects. (R2) The evaluation currently uses anomalies injected into datasets without real anomalies, limiting realism. (R1)
- **Evaluation metrics:** Given the scarcity of anomalies, reporting only AUROC is insufficient; AUPR should be included to better reflect anomaly class performance. (R1)
- **Need comparison to supervised methods:** The paper should include a comparison to supervised methods and provide empirical or theoretical justification for why the semi-supervised approach is advanced without negative samples. (R4)
- **Hyperparameter settings:** Hyperparameters vary significantly across datasets; the authors should clarify how these settings are determined and discuss their sensitivity. (R1)
- **Dataset limitations:** Datasets are small in node count; evaluation on large-scale graphs would be insightful. (R1) Additionally, the best results are obtained with subgraphs of size 2–3 and contextual nodes around 7; the authors should discuss whether other previous time steps and further neighborhood nodes are redundant. (R4)
- **Threshold setting:** It is unclear how the anomaly score threshold is set (by experiment or artificially) and how AUC changes with different thresholds. (R4)
- **Time complexity:** The time complexity of the Data Preprocessing step is not reported. (R4)

**Scalability and practicality**
- The framework includes multiple modules (graph-based reconstruction, subgraph sampling) that are prone to limited scalability and long runtime; these concerns should be discussed. (R1)
- The practical feasibility of obtaining noise-free normal examples is questionable; the default training set is 80% normal data (annotated as entirely noise-free), which is challenging in practice. (R1)

**Writing, clarity, and presentation (multiple reviewers)**
- The paper contains numerous grammar errors, missing spaces, and typos (e.g., line 055, 532, 250, 275, 55-56). (R1, R2, R3, R5) An inconsistency appears in Section 4.5: “AEEDY” used instead of “AAEDY”. (R3)
- Symbols are not explained clearly and timely (e.g., C_{v1} in Eq. (2)). (R5)
- A problem formulation section is missing. (R5)
- Related works and baselines are not sufficiently up-to-date: most cited works are before 2021; static GAD works after 2021 are missing, and unsupervised contrastive learning-based graph anomaly detection methods are omitted. (R1, R3, R5)
- It is unclear how baseline methods were adapted to the normal-only anomaly detection setting. (R1)
- There is a repetitive sentence on line 281 (“resulting in a dissimilarity between z and z” … “causing dissimilarity between z and zˆ”). (R4)
- Variables like top-k should be italicized, and each formula should be followed by appropriate punctuation. (R3)
- Overall clarity needs improvement for ICLR standard; the paper is not easy to follow. (R1)
