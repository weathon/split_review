## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a lightweight ranking adapter for CLIP to handle text-driven image ordering tasks (e.g., facial age estimation, object count sorting, image quality assessment). The adapter uses learnable text prompts and a ranking-aware cross-attention module that computes text-conditioned visual differences between image pairs. The approach reframes CLIP into a learning-to-rank (LTR) task with a single "sort by X" instruction, bypassing the need for multiple task-specific prompts. It outperforms fine-tuned CLIP on several benchmarks and achieves competitive results against specialized models. Results are reported on a single 3090Ti GPU, with no fine-tuning of the CLIP encoders. The paper includes qualitative examples and ablation studies on design choices.

**Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (rating 3) found the paper unclear and questioned the core mechanism, while three other reviewers (ratings 8, 6, 8) found the writing clear and the method well-motivated. The same low-confidence reviewer raised major technical concerns; others were generally positive about the elegance and results.

### Strengths
- **Clear motivation and writing** (Reviewers 2, 4): The goal of using a single ranking prompt instead of multiple captions is well explained. The method diagram and logical flow make the approach easy to follow. (Note: Reviewer 1 disagreed on clarity; see Weaknesses.)
- **Generalizable across tasks** (Reviewers 1, 3, 4): The adapter handles diverse ranking tasks (age estimation, image dating, object counting, quality assessment) without heavy task-specific tuning, offering a versatile solution.
- **Strong experimental performance** (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4): Consistently beats fine-tuned CLIP on multiple benchmarks and rivals specialized models. Ablations validate design choices (e.g., ranking-aware attention, learnable prompts).
- **Lightweight and efficient** (Reviewer 4): Requires only training of the ranking modules while CLIP encoders remain frozen; runs on a single 3090Ti GPU.
- **Abundant qualitative examples and analyses** (Reviewer 4): Appendix covers future directions like multi-attribute ranking and cross-domain transferability.
- **Simple and elegant solution** (Reviewers 3, 4): Replacing multiple rank-specific prompts with a single instruction simplifies the architecture.

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and reproducibility** (Reviewer 1): The paper is challenging to follow. Symbols in Eq. (5) and ΔO are introduced without explanation. The role of the two heads (regression vs. ranking) and which head produces the final output per task is unclear. How MAE is computed for age estimation/dating (given the model performs ranking, not regression) is not explained. What exactly is being ranked in each experiment is insufficiently described. Lack of provided code hinders reproducibility.
- **Questionable core mechanism** (Reviewer 1): The attention in Eq. (1) normalizes across tokens from both images, but the resulting submatrices \(A_i\) and \(A_j\) are not individually normalized, raising doubt about whether \(O_i\) and \(O_j\) are valid attention-weighted combinations. If \(A_j\) is near zero, \(O_{i,j}\) mostly reflects \(O_i\), failing to capture relative differences. The authors report that normalizing \(A_i\) and \(A_j\) makes no significant difference (Table 6), and replacing subtraction with concatenation has only marginal impact, undermining the claimed importance of the differential mechanism. It is also unclear if using \(O_i + O_j\) would change results.
- **Method description changed** (Reviewer 1): The updated Eq. (3) averages the difference of \(O_i\) and \(O_j\) over \(M\) tokens and passes it through an FFN, deviating from the original description where \(O_{i,j} = O_i - O_j\). Whether reported results correspond to the original or updated formulation is unclear.
- **Technical contribution questioned** (Reviewer 3): The technical contribution may not be strong enough given the simplicity of the adapter.
- **Insufficient comparison with related methods** (Reviewer 2): The paper should clearly delineate architectural and functional differences from OrdinalCLIP, L2RCLIP, and NumCLIP, especially regarding how the ranking-aware attention learns visual differences vs. direct alignment with numerical values.
- **Missing comparisons to multimodal LLMs** (Reviewer 2): No comparison with models like InstructBLIP or similar architectures, limiting benchmarking against current leading approaches.
- **No evaluation on complex counting benchmarks** (Reviewer 2): The performance on TallyQA and CLEVR is not shown, leaving questions about generalization to more intricate visual reasoning.
- **Single backbone and image size** (Reviewer 4): All experiments use CLIP with ConvNeXt-L and resize images to 320×320, while prior works (LR-CLIP, OrdinalCLIP, NumCLIP) use ViT-B/16 at 224×224. This raises fairness concerns. Results with the same backbone are needed to confirm generalizability.
- **Only pairwise ranking loss used** (Reviewer 4): It would be informative to explore other losses (triplet, ranked list loss from Wang et al. 2021), which might eliminate the auxiliary ranking module.
- **Missing symbols and formatting** (Reviewer 3): In Eq. (2), symbols \(V_i\) and \(V_j\) are not explained. Dimensions of certain symbols are missing. In Figure 3, \(A_i\) is a matrix but \(P\) represents dimension; meaning of \(A_i(1-P)\) is unclear. Citation formatting (lines 160, 161, 309, 417) is inconsistent with line 424. Column margins in Tables 1 and 2 are too wide (right edge extends beyond page). Consider adding count numbers to results in Figures 5 and 6 as done in Figure 7(a).
- **Handling of multiple images** (Reviewer 3): It is unclear whether the relational ranking-aware attention weights are frozen or trainable, and how the module handles more than two images during training/inference.
- **Separate adapter per task** (Reviewer 2): The paper does not clarify whether separate adapter networks are required for different tasks, which would impact generalization to unseen scenarios.
- **Missing experiment: general counting** (Reviewer 4): Have you tried ranking images by the number of objects irrespective of category (e.g., sorting by "count" of whatever object is present)?
