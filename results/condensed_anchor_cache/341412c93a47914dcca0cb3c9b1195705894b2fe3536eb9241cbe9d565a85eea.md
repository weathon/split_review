## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper applies concepts from stochastic thermodynamics and optimal transport (Benamou-Brenier formula, Wasserstein-2 distance) to derive a lower bound on training time (the "speed limit") for neural network parameter distributions evolving under gradient flow or Langevin dynamics. Analytical expressions are given for linear regression and neural networks in the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) regime. Under power-law assumptions on the NTK spectrum, the authors claim the training dynamics achieve near-optimal efficiency in a scaling sense. Small-scale experiments on CIFAR-10 with CNNs and FCNs show an initial inefficient phase followed by a longer optimal regime, though the quantitative match to theory is mixed.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and perspective**: Reviewers agree this is an original application of speed-limit ideas from stochastic thermodynamics to deep learning training dynamics, which have not been seen before.
- **Mathematical rigor** (disagreement: Reviewer 3 finds the analysis technically sound and mathematically rigorous, Reviewer 1 rates soundness only 2 fair, questioning derivation steps; see Weaknesses).
- **Clarity** (disagreement: Reviewer 2 and Reviewer 3 find the paper easy to read and relatively clear; Reviewer 1 rates presentation 1 poor, citing missing formal theorems and unclear notation for Z₀, Zₜ in Eq. 4; Reviewer 4 also requests formal theorems and clearer notation).
- **Interpretations**: The derived speed-limit formulas for linear regression and NTK learning provide interesting interpretations (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Experimental section**: Despite being narrow (only CIFAR-10, small networks, full-batch, low learning rate), the experiments give enough results to evaluate the theory's significance and limitations (Reviewer 2). The experiments show an initial inefficient phase followed by an efficient phase, qualitatively consistent with the theoretical picture.
- **Literature review**: Reviewer 3 notes that the paper cites relevant prior work and provides a good review of entropy production bounds; however, multiple reviewers note the absence of a dedicated related works section.

### Weaknesses
1. **Narrow theoretical scope (all reviewers)**  
   Only linear regression and the NTK (infinite-width linearized) regime are analyzed. These are linear models and do not capture finite-width neural networks, non-linearities, feature learning, or non-convexity. Continuous‑time SGD is a strong simplification; practical discrete updates may behave differently.  
   *Reviewer 1 (confidence 5) stresses this as a severe limitation, noting it is unclear if bounds derived in the NTK regime would be larger or smaller for finite networks and how scaling changes.*  
   *Reviewer 3 asks for experiments varying the feature-learning parameter (e.g., α in Chizat & Bach 2018).*

2. **Unclear practical significance and takeaways**  
   The bound only tracks the time to go from initial to final parameter distribution, not the time to reach a near-stable point or near-zero potential, or to achieve low test error. It is not shown how the bound could be used to compare optimizers, design better training strategies, or relate to compute-optimal scaling.  
   *Reviewer 2 (confidence 2): “It is rather unclear whether there are any takeaways for practitioners.”*  
   *Reviewer 4 (confidence 2) echoes the same concern, and asks whether the claimed near-optimal rate contradicts faster convergence of heavy-ball dynamics.*

3. **Interpretability and computability of the Wasserstein‑2 distance**  
   The speed limit is phrased in terms of W₂, which is hard to compute or interpret in general. The paper only obtains interpretable results in limiting special cases (no noise, infinite parameters, delta‑function marginals).  
   *Reviewer 3 (confidence 4) asks whether the optimal transport formalism adds beyond when marginals are delta functions (i.e., the 2‑norm of weights).*  
   *Reviewer 2 notes that the data distribution is assumed Gaussian to derive Eq. 10; CIFAR‑10 images are not Gaussian, so the mismatch in Fig. 1.d is unsurprising.*

4. **Empirical validation vs. theoretical claims**  
   Several quantitative discrepancies are reported:  
   - Fig. 1.b shows actual training time is 10–30 × larger than the bound Tₛₗ, while the text claims an O(1) factor. (Reviewer 2, confidence 2)  
   - The trajectory‑length ratio (Fig. 1.e) is O(1), so the claim of “near optimal” is based on that metric rather than time. (Reviewer 2)  
   - Experiments use very small networks (Myrtle‑5 on CIFAR‑10) and a learning rate of 10⁻⁵; it is not justified that this rate is small enough or the network wide enough to be in the NTK regime. (Reviewer 2)  
   *Reviewer 3 notes the initial transient period involves kernel eigenstructure realignment (citing Atanasov et al. 2021), which is not captured.*

5. **Possible errors in equations (Reviewer 1, confidence 5)**  
   - Eqs. (7), (27), (28) should have squared Wasserstein distance, not the distance.  
   - Derivation of Eq. (6) from Eq. (5) may not be straightforward because ∇_θ ln p in the integrand could depend on β.  
   - Derivation of Eq. (41) in Appendix A.6 appears to lack normalization factors (log Z₊/Z₋) from forward/backward distributions; it is not clear these cancel.  
   *Other reviewers did not comment on these technicalities; this criticism comes from the most confident reviewer.*

6. **Edge cases and unexplored scenarios**  
   - The role of nonzero initial weights: how much entropy production is due to θ₀ ≠ 0? The case of a perceptron or NTK starting at θ₀=0 is not covered (Reviewer 2).  
   - High‑noise regime: the formalism suggests the perceptron learns in “zero time” when it actually cannot learn at all (Reviewer 3).  
   - Power‑law scaling in the residues leads to an inefficiency factor that grows with dataset size; it is unclear if this matches real datasets (Reviewer 3).  
   - The bound only considers a fixed target parameter state, not the dynamics of convergence to a stationary point or near‑zero loss (Reviewers 3, 4).

7. **Missing formal theorems and related‑works section**  
   Multiple reviewers (1, 3, 4) note the absence of clearly stated theorems (e.g., “Theorem 1”) and the lack of a dedicated related‑works section. Notation (Z₀, Zₜ in Eq. 4) is not defined.

8. **Reviewer disagreement summary**  
   - Overall scores: 3 (R1), 6 (R2), 8 (R3), 6 (R4). R3 is substantially more positive, rating contribution 3 good and finding the paper technically sound; R1 is most negative, rating soundness 2, presentation 1, contribution 2.  
   - R1’s criticisms about derivation errors and narrowness are made with high confidence (5); R2 and R4 have lower confidence (2).  
   - R3 disagrees with R1 on technical soundness (R3: rigorous; R1: only fair) and on clarity (R3: clear; R1: poor).  
   - All agree that practical significance is unclear and models are too simple, though R3 still finds the results “intriguing.”
