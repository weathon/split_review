## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CARSO, a defense that blends adversarial training (AT) and adversarial purification. It uses an adversarially-trained classifier and learns to map its internal representation of a potentially perturbed input onto a distribution of clean reconstructions via a conditional VAE. Multiple samples are classified and aggregated. Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and TinyImageNet-200 under AutoAttack show improved robust accuracy over baseline AT.

### Strengths
- The idea of combining adversarial training and purification via generative modeling of internal representations from an adversarially-trained classifier is interesting (R1, R4).
- The paper is easy to follow and the studied problem is relevant (R2).
- The proposed method C+AT/rand-AA improves robust accuracy over the baseline AT/AA (R3) and achieves competitive robust accuracy against AutoAttack on CIFAR-10/100 compared to prior methods (R2, Table 2).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited experimental evaluation and missing baselines**:
   - Evaluation is restricted to CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and TinyImageNet-200; larger-scale datasets like ImageNet are not tested (R1, R3, R4). One reviewer notes that adversarially-trained ImageNet models are readily available (R1).
   - Missing comparisons with state-of-the-art defenses such as AWP [1], SCORE [2], ADML [3] (R4). Another reviewer notes that Table 2 only vaguely refers to "sota in RobustBench" and the comparison may be unfair due to potentially different settings; the exact method and reference should be explicitly given (R2).
   - Clean accuracy is only compared to standard adversarial training (2018), not to more recent SOTA methods (R2). Adversarially pre-trained classifiers are used, but methods following the same setting are not compared for fairness (R3). One reviewer strongly asserts that current SOTA ADML increases clean accuracy significantly while CARSO decreases it, undermining the claimed contribution (R4, confidence 5).
   - Only CNN architectures are used; experiments with Transformer structures are needed for completeness (R4, citing ADML).
   - No comparison of training or inference time with other defense methods (R3).

2. **Insufficient ablation and unclear contribution of components**:
   - It is unclear whether robustness gains come from the combination of AT and purification or from the many “tricks” used (R2).
   - The smoothness penalty added to the reconstruction loss is not well explained: its mechanism, effect on reconstruction quality and adversarial robustness, and specific form are not provided (R1). The paper lacks analysis comparing this penalty to other regularization techniques (R1).
   - The ablation study (Table 2) only varies AT versus CARSO; there is no comparison of the purification component alone against CARSO+purification (R4). One reviewer questions why the motivation for separating scenarios is based on clean accuracy when the same drop occurs across scenarios; the logic seems inappropriate (R4).
   - The major contribution relative to ADML is not understood: clean accuracy decreases significantly while ADML increases it, yet ADML is not compared directly (R4).

3. **Clarity issues and missing descriptions**:
   - The framework description needs improvement (R1). Specific unclear aspects: the role of the auxiliary encoder $\mathcal{D}$ in sampling – it appears unnecessary, but the sampling process seems to rely on its output (R1).
   - Section 4.5 describes a DGDN-based decoder, but Figure 1 depicts the decoder conditioned on the output of auxiliary encoder $\mathcal{C}$, creating a discrepancy (R1).
   - The analogy to cognitive science concepts (cued recall and recognition) is not clearly explained and does not aid understanding (R4, also noted by R3's question).
   - It is not explained how learning the purifier from the feature space of an adversarially-trained classifier differs from learning from a normally-trained classifier (R1).

4. **Missing related work and motivation**:
   - The paper does not clearly identify prior works that also combine adversarial training and purification in a unified framework (R3). The neurocognitive inspiration is not elaborated sufficiently (R3).

*Note on reviewer disagreement:* While three reviewers rated the paper 5 and found the method interesting, one reviewer (R4) with high confidence (5) strongly criticizes the contribution, calling it insufficient compared to ADML, the limited experiments, and the unclear advantage. R4's specific points about ADML and clean accuracy degradation are not addressed by other reviewers and indicate a fundamental disagreement about the significance of the results.
