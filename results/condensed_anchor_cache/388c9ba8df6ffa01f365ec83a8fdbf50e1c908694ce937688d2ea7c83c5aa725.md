## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes ModernTCN, a pure convolution architecture for time series analysis. ModernTCN is inspired by modern convolution blocks from computer vision (e.g., ConvNeXt) and uses depthwise convolution (DWConv) with large kernels and grouped pointwise convolutions (ConvFFN) to capture cross-time, cross-variable, and cross-feature dependencies in a decoupled manner. The model is evaluated on five mainstream time series tasks: long-term and short-term forecasting, imputation, classification, and anomaly detection. It achieves state-of-the-art performance while maintaining the efficiency advantage of convolution-based models, outperforming Transformer and MLP baselines.

### Strengths

- **Clear and well-structured writing** that is easy to follow (Reviewer 1).
- **State-of-the-art performance on five diverse time series tasks** (long-term and short-term forecasting, imputation, classification, anomaly detection) with consistent gains over Transformer-based, MLP-based, and task-specific baselines (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Efficiency advantage**: the convolution-based architecture provides strong performance while being computationally efficient, offering a better balance of performance and efficiency than Transformer/MLP models (Reviewer 1).
- **Extensive and well-designed ablation studies** that systematically dissect the contributions of individual components (e.g., depthwise convolution, grouped pointwise convolutions, variable mixing embeddings) and demonstrate the importance of large effective receptive fields (ERFs) (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Valuable contribution to pure-convolution approaches** for time series analysis, an area seldom explored given the recent dominance of Transformers and MLPs; the paper modernizes TCNs by adapting ideas from computer vision while addressing time-series-specific challenges (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Comprehensive experimental setup** covering multiple tasks and sufficient comparisons with existing work (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses

- **Unclear connection to variable-independent models**: The paper claims that cross-variable information is critical, but does not test whether the proposed ConvFFN cross-variable mechanism can be applied as an orthogonal improvement to variable-independent methods like PatchTST or DLinear. This missing analysis limits understanding of generalizability (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient explanation of variable-mixing embedding**: The paper states that discarding the variable dimension (variable-mixing embedding) hurts performance, but does not provide a clear architectural-level explanation of how information flow differs when the variable dimension is maintained vs. discarded, nor why univariate forecasting (Appendix E) remains competitive while multivariate degrades (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of theoretical analysis on effective receptive fields**: The paper attributes performance gains to larger ERFs but acknowledges the lack of theoretical justification; a deeper mathematical or empirical analysis of ERF size and its relationship to kernel size and depth would strengthen the claim (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing ablations on key components**:
  - The exact architecture of the “undecoupled ConvFFN” in Table 4 (layers, kernel sizes, activations) is not specified, making it hard to interpret the ablation (Reviewer 3).
  - The impact of RevIN normalization (used in regression tasks, Appendix B.2) is not isolated; an ablation study is needed to determine how much performance comes from RevIN alone vs. the proposed architecture (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons with recent state-of-the-art baselines**:
  - Short-term forecasting: Wang et al., "Make Transformer Great Again for Time Series Forecasting: Channel Aligned Robust Dual Transformer" (2023) is omitted (Reviewer 2).
  - Anomaly detection: Yang et al., "DCdetector: Dual Attention Contrastive Representation Learning for Time Series Anomaly Detection" (KDD 2023) is omitted (Reviewer 2).
  - Long-term forecasting: Li et al., "Revisiting Long-term Time Series Forecasting: An Investigation on Linear Mapping" (arXiv 2023) shows an MLP-based model beating PatchTST in some cases and should be compared (Reviewer 2).
- **Selection of results unclear**: The paper does not specify whether the best results from different input lengths are selected based on validation loss or directly from test set performance (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing multivariate short-term forecasting results**: Short-term forecasting experiments are reported only for univariate datasets; results on multivariate datasets (e.g., those used for long-term forecasting) are not provided (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient clarity on block details**: The design of the DWConv and ConvFFN blocks should be more clearly described for readers without a modern CNN background (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing ERF visualization for TimesNet**: Figure 1 shows ERFs of various models, but does not include TimesNet (another convolution-based model using shorter kernels and more layers). Adding TimesNet would help substantiate the claim that larger ERFs drive performance (Reviewer 3).
- **Code not available**: The code repository was not accessible at the time of review, hindering reproducibility (Reviewer 2). (Note: paper states code will be released.)
- **Minor errata**: “Enlaring” should be “Enlarging” in Section 5.2 (Reviewer 3).
- **Hyperparameter search space not documented**: The paper uses very different hyperparameters across tasks (e.g., channel number, kernel sizes, patching size, strides, number of blocks) but does not specify the search space or tuning procedure (Reviewer 1).
- **Difference between projection layer and convolution stem**: Reviewer 1 requests clarification on how using a convolution stem differs from using a linear projection layer as in PatchTST (Reviewer 1).
