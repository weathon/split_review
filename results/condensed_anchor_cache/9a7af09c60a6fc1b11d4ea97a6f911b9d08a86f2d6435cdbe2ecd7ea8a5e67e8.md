## Merged Review

### Summary
This work proposes Improved Probabilistic Cross-Modal Embeddings (PCME++) to address two shortcomings of PCME: expensive Monte Carlo approximation and loss saturation due to false negatives. It introduces a closed-form probabilistic distance (CSD), pseudo-positives to mitigate false negatives, and mixed sample data augmentation for probabilistic matching. Experiments on MS-COCO Caption, CxC, and ECCV Caption demonstrate effectiveness against state-of-the-art ITM methods. Additional evaluations on noisy correspondence and application to prompt-filtering for zero-shot classification are provided.

### Strengths
- Well-organized and easy to follow presentation (R1, R2, R3); Figures (especially Fig1 and Fig5 per R1, Fig2 per R2) are informative.
- Analyzes the many-to-many nature and inherent issues in Image-Text Matching, building on previous uncertainty-based methods like PCME (R2).
- Solid experimental results supporting effectiveness on MS-COCO, CxC, ECCV Caption with SOTA comparisons (R2).
- Extensions to noisy image-text correspondences (R1, R2) and uncertainty-based prompt-tuning (R2) further demonstrate advantages.
- Focuses on two important issues: many-to-many matching and sparse annotations (R3).

### Weaknesses
- The work is deeply coupled with PCME, potentially limiting inspiration and extensibility for other work (R1). [Note: R2 and R3 do not raise this issue.]
- The closed-form sampled distance (CSD) measures uncertainty but not the area of overlap between modalities; more analysis is needed (R1).
- Several techniques (Mixup, CutMix) are proposed in prior work, and loss functions are shared with PCME; the contribution and improvements need clarification (R1).
- Figure 2 is difficult to understand; interpretations and formulations of comparison losses should be provided (R1). [Note: R2 found Fig2 informative, indicating reviewer disagreement on clarity.]
- The motivation cites expensive computations due to Monte Carlo approximation, but no comparison or analysis of computation costs is presented in experiments (R1).
- When using ViT-B/32 backbone (Table 1, 2), PCME++ is inferior to VSE_infinite. Additionally, P2RM and DAA originally outperformed PCME with a large margin, but their reported performances here are lower than PCME. Explanations are needed (R3).
- It should be investigated whether the proposed pseudo-positives (PP) and mixed sample data augmentation (MSDA) can be applied to P2RM and DAA; if not, explanation required; if yes, comparisons should be made (R3).
- The datasets CxC and ECCV Caption both originate from MS-COCO; further verification on larger datasets like CC3M is needed (R3).
- Recommended comparisons against recent works on uncertainty modeling (MAP, CVPR 2023) and noisy correspondence (Deep Evidential Learning, ACM MM 2022) are missing (R3).
- The advantage of PCME++ when scaling up backbones (Table 1) lacks further discussion; does consistent improvement of PCME and PCME++ relate to probabilistic modeling? (R2, question).
