## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a camera-controlled image-to-video diffusion model (CamI2V) built on DynamiCrafter. It conditions on camera poses via Plücker embeddings, introduces epipolar attention for cross-frame geometry, and adds register tokens to handle occlusions or missing epipolar correspondences. It also improves evaluation by replacing COLMAP with GLOMAP for pose estimation. Reviewers generally find the method technically sound and showing improved controllability, but raise significant concerns about novelty relative to concurrent works (CamCo, CVD, CameraCtrl) and insufficient experimental validation of key components, especially the register tokens.

### Strengths
- The paper is well written with clear figures (Fig. 1, Fig. 2). The exposition of clean vs. noisy conditions and the register token idea is neat.
- Epipolar attention is a straightforward, well-motivated extension to temporal attention that enables explicit multi‑view 3D reasoning. It is plug‑and‑play, requiring no retraining of other modules.
- The register token for degenerate cases (occlusions, rapid motion, non‑overlapping frames) is technically sound and addresses a limitation of prior epipolar‑based methods like CamCo.
- The method is evaluated against reproduced baselines (MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl) under a common training framework, ensuring fair comparison.
- Quantitative results on RealEstate10K show state‑of‑the‑art camera controllability (e.g., 25.64% improvement per abstract) with only minor loss of dynamics/generation quality.
- The use of GLOMAP (a dense SfM pipeline) for evaluation provides more robust camera pose consistency metrics.
- Qualitative results demonstrate generalization to out‑of‑domain images, and the model requires only 24 GB/12 GB memory for training/inference on 16‑frame 256×256 sequences.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns are a major theme across reviewers.** Epipolar attention and Plücker embeddings have been used in 3D generation (Tseng et al., 2023) and video generation (CamCo, CameraCtrl, CVD). The paper’s primary claimed contribution—register tokens for occlusions and non‑overlapping frames—is seen as a minor improvement and is not validated by any ablation. Two reviewers (R1, R2) gave a rating of 6 and indicated they would raise it if the ablation were provided, while others (R3, R4) gave 5 and found the novelty insufficient overall.
- **Missing ablation for register tokens.** No experiment isolates the effect of the register token. The paper claims it handles rapid camera motion, occlusions, and dynamic objects, but provides no evidence. This is a key missing experiment (the main difference from CamCo). One reviewer suggests comparing to a trivial alternative like averaging all K/V features.
- **Incomplete method description.** The dual role of Plücker embeddings—as a global positional encoding (similar to CameraCtrl) and as input to epipolar attention—is not clearly stated in Section 3; readers must infer it from Section 5.3. This obscures the architecture.
- **Concept of clean/noised conditions is under‑developed.** Figure 1 distinguishes clean vs. noisy conditions, but this classification does not lead to any new architectural design or theoretical analysis (e.g., how clean conditions reduce uncertainty more effectively). The choice of epipolar attention is motivated by multi‑view geometry, not the clean/noisy dichotomy. Some reviewers consider this framing redundant.
- **Quantitative gains are modest.** In Table 1, the advantage over CameraCtrl is minor; in Table 2, epipolar attention alone yields limited improvement. The claim of “significantly” improving controllability (Line 534–535) is overstated. Cross‑view consistency is highlighted qualitatively but not included as a metric in the tables; reviewers suggest it should be added.
- **Evaluation pipeline improvement is unconvincing.** Replacing COLMAP with GLOMAP and fixing GT camera parameters to registration is a trivial change; no comparison demonstrates its effectiveness for low‑resolution or dynamic scenes.
- **Qualitative results are limited and sometimes flawed.** All examples show only zooming‑out camera motion (the easiest case); no panning, tilting, or more complex trajectories are provided. One example (“Ours_I2V_on_unseen_image_3.gif”) shows strong inconsistency across frames (the 5th frame from the left). These limitations undermine claims about handling large, non‑overlapping movements.
- **Epipolar attention assumes static scenes.** The hard mask is derived from static geometry; its effect on dynamic objects is not discussed, raising concerns about degradation.
- **Multi‑resolution epipolar mask not explained.** Figure 5 shows a multi‑resolution mask, but the text does not discuss its motivation, how different resolutions affect attention, or the trade‑off compared to a single‑resolution mask.
- **Hard mask vs. soft mask not compared.** Prior work (Tseng et al., 2023) uses a soft epipolar weight matrix; a comparison of effectiveness and computational cost (e.g., running time) is missing.
- **Multiple guidance scale lacks novelty.** This technique is adopted from prior work and lacks explanation or experimental support; reviewers question listing it as a contribution (Line 133–134).
- **Minor presentation issues:** Citation in Line 208–209 should be checked; the “Project” module in Figure 4 and its learnable weights are unclear; notation (e.g., latent code should be bold: \(\mathbf{z}_t\)) needs consistency.
