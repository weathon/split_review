## Merged Review

### Summary
SHARP shares parameters across adjacent layers in pretrained LLMs and introduces low-rank recovery parameters (LoRA) to maintain performance. It employs a two-stage process: Single Layer Warmup (SLW), which uses L2 loss to align the shared layer’s output with the original, and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to recover full model performance. Experiments on Llama2-7B show that SHARP reduces stored MLP parameters by 38% to 65%, reduces model storage by 42.8%, and cuts total inference time by 42.2% on mobile devices (iPhone) while recovering perplexity on in-distribution tasks using ≤50k fine-tuning data. Ablations cover the ratio/location of layer merging and recovery parameterizations. Comparisons are made only with Direct Sharing baselines.

### Strengths
- **Novelty & direction:** Follows a relatively new methodology for efficient inference – adjacent layer sharing in a post-training way, rather than training from scratch (R1). Layer sharing is presented as a new compression approach for pretrained LLMs, and the runtime speedup on real devices demonstrates a promising research direction (R3). The combination of parameter sharing with LoRA fine-tuning is an innovative compression avenue (R4).
- **Motivation & observations:** The work is motivated by the robustness of LLMs when replacing adjacent MLP layers and makes new observations supporting the layer-sharing strategy (R1). The paper provides a solid study on ways to regain performance after layer merging, with experiments on both the ratio/location of merging and the finetuning approach (R3).
- **Method heuristics:** The two-stage process (SLW + SFT) provides good heuristics for layer sharing; low-rank weights are introduced to predict subsequent layers (R1). The initial warm-up using L2 norm of the original layer’s output can be done in parallel, which helps improve performance (R2).
- **Efficiency gains:** The method reduces model parameters by >50% while maintaining similar performance on some tasks (R2). The speedup is notable for mobile/edge computing scenarios (R4). The approach is orthogonal to quantization, so further compression may be possible (R2).
- **Presentation:** The paper is overall well-written and easy to follow (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Significant performance loss on downstream tasks:** Despite recovering perplexity on pretraining data (Tables 2 and 3), there is a significant accuracy degradation on most downstream tasks after SHARP. For example, Table 6 shows that while SHARP slightly exceeds the original on CommonsenseQA, it suffers substantial drops on other tasks (R2, R3, R4). This raises doubts about overall utility (R2), potential overfitting to the fine-tuning data, and a lack of analysis on generalization to unseen data or task-specific susceptibility (R3, R4). No cross-validation or analysis of catastrophic forgetting is provided (R4).
- **Lack of comprehensive comparisons:** Experiments are not compared with state-of-the-art efficient inference methods such as LLM pruning (which also drops parameters), MobileLLM, or other compression categories (R1, R4). Without such comparisons it is unclear whether SHARP offers a genuine advantage (R4). The paper only compares against Direct Sharing baselines (R1).
- **Concerns about model size trade-off:** It is unclear whether a larger model (e.g., LLaMA2-13B) compressed by SHARP to the size of a smaller model (e.g., LLaMA2-7B) would actually outperform the originally smaller model. If not, the compression complexity may not be justified (R2).
- **Ambiguity in “communication” definition:** The paper does not clearly define what “communication” means in the context of parameter sharing (internal, flow between layers, or external). This makes it difficult to assess the true benefits; speedup might be limited by memory bandwidth rather than parameter sharing (R4).
- **Validity of latency analysis on mobile:** The latency analysis uses 4-bit quantization to fit the model on an iPhone, which degrades model performance and is not a valid implementation of the proposed method in isolation. This suggests weight sharing alone is insufficient for edge deployment (R1).
- **Generalizability and complexity of the fine-tuning scheme:** The two-stage finetuning is a general compression technique, not specific to layer sharing; the paper lacks justification for why it is particularly well-suited for this scenario (R3). The SLW stage using L2 norm introduces training complexity and risk of failure for models more complex than Llama-2-7B (e.g., if SLW fails, training may become unstable) (R2).
- **Missing ablations and further experiments:** No results are shown for applying only Stage 2 (SFT) in Table 2 (R2). It is unknown whether attaching LoRA adapters to the entire model (not just shared layers) would improve performance (R2). Results on larger models (e.g., Llama-3.2) and after quantization are missing (R2). The effect of mismatched fine-tuning data (e.g., GPT4-Alpaca for Arxiv-math) is not explored (R3).
- **Minority reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (R1, score 6) is more positive, seeing SHARP as a promising and well-motivated approach, while another (R2, score 3) is more negative, doubting its overall utility due to task-specific failures and training complexity. The other reviewers (R3, R4, both score 5) note the innovative aspects but agree on significant performance loss and lack of comparison.
