## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a multimodal retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) question-answering system that combines ColPali (as retriever) and GPT-4o (as multimodal LLM) on a custom dataset of images, text, and QA pairs. While reviewers acknowledge the intuitive direction and practical relevance of document QA, they unanimously find the work lacking novelty—treating it as a straightforward combination of existing models—and criticize poor writing, insufficient dataset description, limited experiments, and missing methodological details. One reviewer (R3, score 1) is particularly negative, finding no strengths and recommending rejection.

### Strengths
- Integrating multimodal retrieval into vision-language models is an intuitive direction (R1).
- Document question-answering is a critical task; using large models aligns with current trends (R2).
- Introducing multiple modalities to improve answer accuracy in document QA is a sensible approach (R2).
- The paper contributes a dataset containing images, text, and question-answer pairs (R2).
- The proposed paradigm is presented clearly and easy to follow (R4).
- The system achieves fast response time while maintaining quality (R4).
- Evaluation metrics are described clearly (R4).
- *Note: R3 (score 1) stated no strengths.*

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty**: The framework is a common retrieval pipeline; the paper merely combines ColPali and GPT-4o without significant component optimization, pipeline innovation, or data optimization (R2, R3, R4). Claimed novelty is overstated (R3). This is not the first multimodal RAG system (R4 cites [a,b,c]). R2 and R4 question the main contribution.
- **Inconsistent claim regarding ColPali**: The paper claims the retrieval system is based on ColPali (which processes documents as image patches using large VLMs), but the proposed framework is completely different from ColPali (R3).
- **Insufficient dataset description**: The so-called “high-quality” dataset is described only roughly (Section 4.1.1). Missing: data sources, proportions of data types (e.g., professional papers, industry reports, white papers), construction process, basic statistics (document/QA pair counts), and discussion of necessity vs. existing datasets (R2, R3, R4). R2 asks for a comparison with existing datasets (e.g., arXivQA, TAT-DQA, InfoVQA). R3 and R4 also note these gaps.
- **Limited experiments**: Only one experiment on the proposed dataset, which is not open source and vaguely described (R2, R3, R4). No evaluation on public benchmarks (R2, R4 list arXivQA, TAT-DQA, InfoVQA). R1 notes only baseline model numbers are provided, making it hard to assess improvements over prior methods. R4 points out that Section 4.3.3 reports response time only for the proposed system, not a comparative table including baselines. No training parameters are provided (R2).
- **Poor writing and presentation**: Figures are low-resolution, captions lack detail (R1). Basic concepts (e.g., F1, precision/recall) are over-explained, wasting space (R1). Figure 1 is blurry (R4). The paper’s flow is not fluent; many details are missed (R3). R3 finds the paper poorly written and difficult to read, recommending rejection.
- **Limited literature review**: Prior work in multimodal RAG (e.g., [1,2,3] cited by R1) and Self-RAG (R2) is not sufficiently acknowledged or compared. Differences from these works are unclear (R1, R2).
- **Missing method details**: Only a simple overview (Section 3.1) and a blurry figure (Figure 1) are provided. No discussion of the integration process between ColPali and GPT-4o or how multimodal information is processed and combined (R4). Details of the retrieval component and QA model are missing (R3).
- **Experimental ambiguity**: It is unclear where the ranking documents come from (R3).
- **Reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms**:  
  - What are the main differences between this work and prior ones (e.g., [1,2,3])? (R1)  
  - Can the authors provide more detailed information about the proposed dataset (data source, ratio, necessity)? (R2, R4)  
  - What is the main contribution and improvement over simply combining ColPali and GPT-4o? (R2, R4)  
  - Where were the documents collected and where are the ranking documents from? (R3)
