## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents FlexAL, a framework for active learning of PDE surrogate models that acquires only a subset of time steps from numerical solver trajectories instead of full trajectories. The missing steps are approximated by the surrogate itself, reducing acquisition cost per trajectory and allowing broader trajectory selection. An acquisition function based on variance reduction (via an ensemble of surrogates) selects which time steps to query. Experiments on Heat, Korteweg–de Vries, Kuramoto–Sivashinsky, and incompressible Navier-Stokes equations show RMSE improvements over baselines (e.g., SBAL, QbC, random sampling). Reviewers are split: some find the idea natural and the paper clear (R1, R3, R4), while others see limited novelty and only marginal gains over simpler approaches (R2, R5).

### Strengths
- The motivation (reducing cost of generating full trajectories) is well presented, and related work is reviewed thoroughly (R1).
- The proposed algorithm is a clear generalization of existing PDE active learning methods – instead of acquiring full trajectories or initial conditions, any combination of time points can be queried (R3).
- Comprehensive experiments on several benchmark PDEs (Heat, KdV, KS, Navier-Stokes) with comparisons to multiple baselines (R1, R3).
- The idea is natural and “nearly simple”, which is a strength (R4).
- The method tackles an important practical problem in surrogate modeling for PDEs (R5).

### Weaknesses
1. **Lack of novelty and PDE-specificity**  
   - The method closely resembles prior active learning works; the approach (ensemble disagreement, acquisition function) is similar to existing schemes (R2, R5).  
   - Little that is dedicated to PDEs is exploited – analogous approaches exist for game engines, video simulation, 3D scenes, and those baselines are not compared (R5).  
   - The method is ad-hoc and heuristic: there is no guarantee that the ensemble captures true uncertainty or avoids collapse if all models wrongly agree (R5).

2. **Limited improvement and unexplained performance gap**  
   - In Fig 3, RMSE of FlexAL is only ~10% better than SBAL, random, or QbC for KdV, KS, and Navier-Stokes. The much larger improvement on the heat equation requires investigation (R2).  
   - The increased runtime of FlexAL (Table 4) is a weakness. The additional optimization cost may be significant compared to full-trajectory methods, and the paper does not show results under equal wall-clock time (R3).  
   - The paper does not quantify cost reduction in terms of number of solver calls, total data acquisition time, or similar metrics – only final model accuracy is reported (R1).

3. **Insufficient clarity of methodology**  
   - How sparse sampling works for the numerical solver is unclear. If a pattern masks intermediate steps, does one still need to simulate them to obtain later queried steps? The explanation of how the surrogate is used to generate missing states and how this reduces solver calls is missing (R1, R4).  
   - The handling of causal dependencies in rollouts is poorly explained. If the surrogate is used for masked steps, the training data changes over epochs (as the surrogate evolves), creating an asymmetry between early and late time points – this needs clarification (R4).  
   - Algorithm 1 requires line-by-line description; current description is too high-level for reproducibility (R1).  
   - Notations are undefined: the space 𝕏 (p2, line 95) (R2).  
   - The uniqueness of the evolution operator G_{t0} is doubtful – given u(t) and u(t+Δt) there may be infinitely many PDEs satisfying both (R2).  
   - Equation 2: wouldn't a PINN loss help if the PDE is known? (R2).  
   - The acquisition function is based on variance reduction, but the rationale for this choice over other candidates is not discussed (R3).  
   - Figure 1 does not effectively illustrate the cost reduction; showing cost increase side-by-side with baseline would be better (R1).

4. **Questions that reveal missing experiments or ablations**  
   - Why does FlexAL perform so much better on the heat equation? A controlled ablation or analysis is needed (R2).  
   - Can the training epochs be reduced for all baselines, not just FlexAL? What effect would that have on results? (R3).  
   - Table 1 reports log-RMSE around 0.1, which corresponds to RMSE ~1.0 – is the surrogate learning anything reasonable? Consider reporting relative RMSE (R3).  
   - Table 4 would benefit from context of how much runtime a numerical solver needs for Navier-Stokes, to judge efficiency (R3).  
   - What is the impact of the hyperparameters T and ε (line 262)? (R3).  
   - The method is tested only on relatively simple PDE datasets; applying to more challenging tasks would address concerns about novelty and heuristic nature (R5).

5. **Minor issues**  
   - The abbreviation “QbC” is introduced late (line 298) despite earlier use (R3).  
   - The name “FlexAL” is not particularly flexible compared to other active learning/design-of-experiments methods (R4).  
   - Eq. 3 uses empirical loss inside expectation, which is nontypical (R5).  
   - Algorithm 1 line 7 notation needs clarification (R2).
