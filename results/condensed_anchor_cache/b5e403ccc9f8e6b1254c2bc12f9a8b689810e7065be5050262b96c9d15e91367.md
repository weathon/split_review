## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Artificial Kuramoto Oscillatory Neurons (AKOrN), a neuron model whose activity lives on the unit sphere and is updated via Kuramoto-like dynamics, enabling synchronization/binding among neurons. The model can be plugged into fully-connected, convolutional, and self-attention layers. It is evaluated on unsupervised object discovery (synthetic and natural images), adversarial robustness on CIFAR-10, and Sudoku reasoning, showing competitive performance against slot-based models, Transformers, and other baselines while roughly matching parameter/FLOP counts.

### Strengths

- The neuron architecture is novel, well-defined, and biologically inspired, with a concise formula that can be combined with mainstream architectures (CNN, Transformer).
- The model achieves impressive performance across a diverse set of tasks: unsupervised object discovery (scales to natural images without pretrained DINO), adversarial robustness (built-in property of the dynamics), and reasoning (Sudoku).
- Soft grouping/clustering via synchronization is an important mechanism often missing in conventional ANNs; the paper addresses this gap.
- The physical origin of the model yields a principled energy function that is informative about task performance (e.g., Sudoku correctness, classification accuracy).
- The work extends the binding-by-synchrony idea beyond object discovery to reasoning tasks and adversarial robustness.
- A novel conceptual argument is provided: synchronization enables competitive learning, which compresses representations and facilitates abstraction.
- Thorough experiments with meaningful comparisons to relevant baselines (slot-based, Transformers), controlling for model size and FLOPs.

### Weaknesses

**Missing evaluations and comparisons**
- The paper lacks a direct comparison with standard convolutional networks on classical image classification benchmarks (CIFAR, ImageNet), making the general applicability of AKOrN unclear.
- Fairness of the object-discovery comparison is not fully clarified: AKOrN is trained from scratch, while some baselines (e.g., Lowe et al. 2024) use pretrained parameters.
- Comparisons to related synchrony-based models are mostly absent: Lowe’s Rotating Features, spike-based oscillation models, and other Kuramoto-in-ANN works are not included as baselines in Figs. 3–5 and Tables 1–3.
- The model is not compared with Ising/Hopfield or modern Hopfield networks on the Sudoku task to isolate the contribution of Kuramoto dynamics from general attractor dynamics.
- The paper does not include segmentation results on binary datasets (e.g., Zheng2024) where color cues are absent, which would test symmetry breaking more cleanly.

**Insufficient analysis and explanation of mechanism**
- The internal dynamics of the oscillators are not analyzed: it is unclear how synchronization patterns relate to learned features and ultimately drive performance.
- The rationale for adversarial robustness is not explained; it may be a general property of dynamical models rather than specific to Kuramoto. A comparison with non-synchrony dynamical ANN models is suggested.
- The claim that energy indicates task performance (Sudoku correctness, classification accuracy) lacks a solid mechanistic explanation.
- The relationship to prior work (Lowe’s Rotating Features, Chandra, Lipton) is insufficiently clarified. Equation (6) is very similar to the binding mechanism in Lowe’s work; the novelty of asymmetric connections vs. symmetry-breaking terms is not adequately distinguished.
- Generalizing the original Kuramoto model to high dimensions: the Proj term in Eq. (2) is not an equivalent generalization of \(\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i)\)—it acts after the summation rather than pairwise, making it uncertain how much it contributes to synchronization. An ablation of Proj is missing.
- Representing neuron activity on the sphere loses the ability to encode feature presence (amplitude), which is a significant conceptual deviation from standard binding-by-synchrony and could limit inference in uncertain or weakly related tasks.

**Missing ablations and experiments**
- Computational cost is not reported: no training/inference time comparisons, and baselines may benefit from GPU optimizations that the AKOrN implementation does not enjoy.
- No toy or synthetic task is provided to illustrate how the dynamics lead to observed advantages.
- The dependence on dimensionality \(N\) (typically 2 or 4) is acknowledged but not systematically investigated.
- No error bars are reported for most results; only Table 3 shows error bars over 5 seeds.
- The code is not provided, hindering reproducibility.

**Clarity and presentation issues**
- Several figure/table references are malformed (e.g., “Fig1” without period; missing spaces).
- Notation can be confusing: \(x_{chw}\) should be \(x_{c,h,w}\); the variable \(C\) (neural population size) is not defined early; line 201 has an inconsistency with initial state definition.
- The abstract’s first two sentences and the introduction’s first paragraph are confusing and uninformative.
- Language is occasionally overly strong (“far better,” “vastly better”).
- Metrics in tables are not always clearly defined (e.g., Table 1: % objects correctly identified?).
- The implementation of the Sudoku task is unclear: is the full 9×9 grid fed at once or one digit at a time? How does \(x_i\) taking value \(c_i\) when given affect Eq. (2)?
- The sentence at line 425 (“the energy is a good indication of the solution’s correctness”) needs elaboration.
- “Common corruptions” (line 464) should be briefly described.
- The observation that confidence and accuracy are linearly correlated (line 506) is not surprising and its significance is unclear.
- The logic at line 527 is not explained.
- The notion that small \(N\) results in strong regularization is introduced abruptly in the discussion without prior mention.
- Typo: line 150 “set C a silhouette” should be “set C as a silhouette.”
- The neuroscience link is tenuous: modeling neurons as high-dimensional points on the sphere is not standard in computational neuroscience; the claim that the paper follows a “more modern dynamical view of neurons as oscillatory units” (line 36) is overstated given the long history of oscillations.

**Questions overlapping with criticisms (relisted as weaknesses)**
- Why is the symmetry-breaking field \(C\) needed beyond simply receiving input?
- The claim that “binding between neurons leads to competitive learning and compression for abstraction” needs either a reference or experimental support to avoid overclaiming.
- Comparison with non-synchrony dynamical ANN models is needed to disentangle whether robustness stems from Kuramoto dynamics or from dynamics in general.
