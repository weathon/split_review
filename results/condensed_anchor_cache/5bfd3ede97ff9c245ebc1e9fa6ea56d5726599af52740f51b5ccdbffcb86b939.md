## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a backdoor attack called "Certified Copy" that trains a backdoored model using a novel cost function to ensure neuron activations on clean and poisoned inputs are similar, only diverging from clean model behavior when triggered. The method involves two stages: training with poisoned samples as an extra class, then removing the extra class and fine-tuning using augmented datasets. The attack is evaluated against seven state-of-the-art defense mechanisms (Neural Cleanse, TAO, ABS, TABOR, NNoculation, IBAU, STRIP) on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-50 and BadNet baseline, showing most defenses fail to detect it.

### Strengths
- The idea of training a backdoored model to have the same hidden representations as a clean model while retaining malicious behavior is interesting and inspiring (R1, R2, R6). Reviewer 2 is particularly positive, rating this 5.
- The attack manipulates backdoor behavior in the latent representation space, making detection more challenging (R2).
- The presentation is motivating and easy to follow (R2, R4, R5), and the paper is well-structured (R4). (Reviewers 3 and 7 strongly disagree on presentation quality.)
- Experiments are sound and include extensive evaluation against many defenses (R2, R5) and ablation studies on activations and detection results (R6).
- The motivation (ensuring similarity of neuron activations for clean and backdoored samples) is rational (R6).
- The evaluation shows the attack's evasiveness with respect to a number of defenses (R4).
- The experiments at least cover ResNet-50 on CIFAR-10 (R7, noted as a redeeming feature despite overall negative rating of 1).

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty and insufficient comparison with existing attacks** (R1, R3, R4, R5, R6, R7). Leveraging representation similarity loss to evade detection has been proposed before (R3). The attack is similar to "Latent Backdoor Attacks on Deep Neural Networks" (Yao et al., CCS '19) – a detailed comparison is needed (R4). The method only improves the outdated BadNets attack (R1). Comparisons needed against more advanced attacks: Blended (Chen et al. 2017), WaNet (Nguyen & Tran 2020), label-consistency (Turner et al. 2019) (R6). The paper does not clearly articulate new insights over existing attacks that also aim to evade defenses (e.g., "Revisiting the Assumption of Latent Separability for Backdoor Defenses", Qi et al., ICLR '23) (R4). Specific comparisons requested with Hong et al. 2022 (Handcrafted Backdoors) and Jia et al. 2021 (Entangled Watermarks) loss used to bring poison and clean activations closer (R5). The claim that "most attacks do not follow assumptions" is not a good reason to skip comparisons (R7).

2. **Weak motivation and questionable practical relevance** (R1, R5, R7). The threat of downloading a model from a model zoo and deploying in security-critical domain may not be realistic; examples lacking (R5). The claim that fine-tuning is not practical is disputed – fine-tuning is common in practice (R1). The paper does not consider scenarios where victims adapt the pretrained model to downstream datasets (R4). Reviewer 7 (rating 1) finds the motivation unclear and cannot identify the strength over existing attacks.

3. **Missing robustness evaluation** (R1, R3, R5). There is likely a robustness-detectability trade-off which should be analyzed (R5). The attack should be tested against backdoor removal defense methods (R3). The attack may be ineffectual in physical world due to trigger sensitivity to appearance/location from data augmentation; transformation-based defenses (Li et al. 2021) should be evaluated (R1).

4. **Insufficient experimental scope** (R2, R3, R6). Only one backdoor attack (BadNet) is used; results for Blended, WaNet, label-consistency are needed (R6). Backdoor detection and attack methods tested are too old (2018-2019) (R3). Efficiency on large datasets (e.g., ImageNet) is not addressed (R6). Evaluation against feature-level backdoor detection (SSL-Cleanse, Zheng et al. 2023; Feng et al. CVPR 2023) is missing (R2).

5. **Lack of clarity in design rationale** (R2, R3, R5, R6). Why does the MAE term control trigger location? (R2). How to set hyperparameters α₁, α₂, α₃, α₄ in the cost function? Are they data-dependent? (R6). The name "Certified Copy" is confusing – "certified" typically implies formal guarantee (none exists) (R5). Figure 4 is vague (R3, R7). The method appears limited to all-to-one attack (only one extra class in first phase) (R2). Will the augmented dataset be biased because true pattern + true location is less frequent, potentially affecting attack performance? (R2).

6. **Poor presentation and writing** (R3, R7). Figures are not well-prepared; typos and grammatical errors (R7). Example: bold question mark in what should be a white square icon (R1, R5). Reviewer 3 rated presentation 1 ("poor") and Reviewer 7 rated it 2 ("fair"). However, other reviewers found presentation good (R2, R4, R5).
