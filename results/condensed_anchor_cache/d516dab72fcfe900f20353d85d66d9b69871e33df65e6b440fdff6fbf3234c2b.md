## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper identifies a Jensen gap in group max-min fairness (MMF) constrained optimization for recommendation under mini-batch sampling. It reformulates MMF as a group-weighted objective and proposes FairDual, a dual optimization method to bridge the gap. Theoretical analysis provides a sub-linear convergence rate and bounded Jensen gap. Experiments are conducted on multiple datasets (reviewers note two datasets) with various backbone models (e.g., three skeleton models). The paper is generally well-written with clear motivation, but reviewers raise concerns about experimental scope, baselines, theoretical assumptions, and clarity.

### Strengths
- Strong motivation supported by both theoretical and empirical analysis of the Jensen gap in Section 4 (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5).
- Novel perspective: reformulating MMF as a group-weighted optimization and using dual optimization to separate the fairness constraint from the predicted score, resulting in a simple group-weighted BCE loss (Reviewers 1, 2).
- The problem of group MMF is significant for fairness in recommendation (Reviewer 2).
- Clear algorithm description and detailed experimental analysis, including evaluations of effectiveness, Jensen gap analysis, case study, training efficiency, and parameter analysis (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Experiments conducted on large-scale backbone models align with the motivation (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The paper is generally well-written with a clear structure (Reviewers 3, 5).
- Theoretical results appear sound, with a guaranteed bound for the Jensen gap and empirical verification showing lower gap compared to baselines (Reviewers 4, 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited datasets and baselines:** The experiments use only two datasets (e.g., as noted by multiple reviewers), which is insufficient to demonstrate generalization. More widely-used datasets from prior fairness recommendation work should be included (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Moreover, the baselines are not sufficiently representative: the paper mainly compares re-weighting methods but omits optimization-based methods mentioned in the introduction (e.g., Abernethy et al. 2022, Cousins 2022, Demidovich et al. 2023, Agarwal et al. 2018). Other fair recommendation methods from related work, especially those designing group weighting, should also be included (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Clarity and presentation issues:** Several parts of the paper are unclear or require revision. For example: the meaning of symbol \(n_i\) in Formula (1) is missing; the description of user interactions and mini-batch sampling in Section 4 is confusing (e.g., why emphasize users with more than \(K\) interactions? why keep most recent \(K\)?); the purpose of emphasizing random batch sampling is unclear; the convergence examination under different batch sizes in Figure 1 is unclear; the meaning of \(P\) is missing; the handling of the first batch in Algorithm 1 (computing \(g\)) is not explained; sampling \(Q\) items may introduce noise and requires more discussion; figure and table placement disrupts reading; and the abrupt association with large language models after the method section is not motivated (Reviewer 1).
- **Theoretical concerns:** The convexity assumption is too strong and impractical for large-scale recommendation models, which are typically non-convex (Reviewer 3). Additionally, the theoretical proofs contain parts that need clarification: the equivalence in Theorem 1 (Problem (15) vs. (2)) needs explicit solution for \(t\) and \(\boldsymbol{b}\); the organization of Lemma 2, Lemma 3, and Theorem 3 is disorganized (Lemma 3 should precede Lemma 2); the weight \(\boldsymbol{s}_g\) in the group-weighted form (4) is not intuitively explained (e.g., when it is larger or smaller) (Reviewer 2). The connection between the dual formulation and the original max-min fairness problem, and how complementary slackness is bounded to yield the convergence rate, needs further clarification; a proof sketch would be beneficial (Reviewer 5).
- **Sampling-based ranking bias:** In Algorithm 1, the method samples \(Q\) items to compute ranking scores (\(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{w}}_b\)), which may introduce bias. A theoretical analysis of this bias or an alternative random sampling approach is needed; the impact on the convergence rate of the Jensen gap should be tested (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Experimental observation questions:** MRR is not statistically significant for top-5 results with RecFormer and BigRec, despite significant improvements in NDCG and MMF; insights are requested (Reviewer 4). NDCG values reported exceed 1, which is theoretically impossible; also, the number of items in the truncated list \(K\) is not specified (Reviewer 3). The visualization in Figure 3(c) lacks obvious differences; quantitative measures (e.g., divergence of distributions) are needed (Reviewer 4). The Jensen gap shows non-monotonic variation with batch size, which is not explained; a more rigorous analysis of this behavior and its consistency with theory is needed (Reviewer 5).
- **Insufficient analysis of the proposed method:** The analysis of the group-weighted BCE loss is insufficient; a deeper understanding of the weight behavior would help explain experimental phenomena (Reviewer 2). The method’s applicability to other loss functions and other fairness constraints (e.g., user group fairness, or both user and item groups) is not explored (Reviewers 1, 5). Computational complexity, storage, and parameter size comparisons with lightweight methods (e.g., static/dynamic group weighting) are missing; it is unclear if the additional overhead is worthwhile (Reviewers 1, 5). Could popularity debiasing (e.g., IPS-based) be combined with FairDual? (Reviewer 2)
- **Implementation details and parameter settings:** The implementation description is oversimplified, hindering reproducibility (Reviewer 1). The parameter \(\lambda\) is stated to range from 0 to 5, but Figure 3 shows values up to 10 (Reviewer 1). Minor typos: Line 324 should be \(\hat{c}_{u,i}=d(e_u,e_i)\); Line 325 should assume normalized embeddings; Line 357 should use \(Q\) instead of \(L\); Line 979 the minus sign placement is wrong (Reviewer 2).
