## Merged Review

### Summary
The three reviewers give scores 6, 3, 5 (confidence 4, 1, 3). All agree the goal of self-supervised universal segmentation is important and the idea of online pseudo-mask generation is reasonable. However, they raise major concerns about presentation clarity, insufficient experimental depth, and lack of comparisons. Reviewer 2 (score 3, low confidence) is substantially more negative, finding the paper very difficult to follow and the experiments limited. Reviewer 1 (score 6) is positive about the technical contributions. Reviewer 3 (score 5) is moderately positive but notes presentation shortcomings and missing ablations.

### Strengths
- The method integrates pseudo-mask generation directly into the self-training pipeline, streamlining unsupervised segmentation training and avoiding offline multi-stage alternation. (R1, R3)
- Online Agglomerative Pooling (OAP) is efficient, generating universal pseudo-masks for a single image within tens of milliseconds. (R1, R3)
- OAP addresses step-by-step limitations of prior models: the Identify step is enhanced by incorporating spatial similarity to maintain node feature consistency across layers; the Merge step uses normalization to avoid merging semantically different but spatially close nodes and suppresses boundary interference. (R1)
- Semantic pooling constructs a fully connected graph from instance pooling output, enabling multiple disjoint regions of the same semantic class to be merged – a key challenge for semantic segmentation. (R1)
- A decreasing threshold strategy is used: earlier layers handle high-level background mergers, later layers consolidate complex foreground semantics, producing hierarchical and detailed segmentations efficiently. (R1)
- The framework integrates components from Mask2Former’s mask decoder and DINO’s local-to-global matching for universal segmentation. (R1)
- The proposed method achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on COCO, PASCAL VOC, Cityscapes, and UVO for instance, semantic, and panoptic segmentation. (R1, R3)
- The goal of self-supervised learning for segmentation is important given annotation cost. (R2, implicit R3)  
  *Note: Reviewer 2 finds the idea only “ok” and notes teacher-student frameworks are old in classification/detection.*

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and clarity**: The paper is very difficult to follow. Key concepts are not clearly defined (e.g., “graph-partitioning”, “level-specific”, “online pretraining”, “local view” vs “global view”, the roles of instance and semantic pooling, the meaning of T, edge formation adjacency). The details of OAP mechanics (graph initialization: how V^0, E^0 are initialized, why M^0 has different size from M_i) are not thoroughly explained. (R2, R3)  
- **OAP mathematical description**: Equation (3) is hard to understand. In Eqn (4), why is w_s multiplied by S^s_ij as a weight rather than linearly combining w_f S^f + w_s S^s? How to ensure S^f and S^s are at the same scale? Why S_ij in [-1,1] and how to find the threshold? (R2)
- **Why averaging features of tokens inside a mask can cause bad merging**: Not explained. (R2)
- **Method details unclear**:
  - Why zero-initializing adapter parameters makes OAP generate meaningful masks during early training. (R2)
  - Whether the mask decoder outputs both instance-level and semantic-level masks. (R2)
  - How outputs from the student head are matched to the teacher head. (R2)
  - Why OAP’s output can be used to train the student network (whether the teacher or OAP keeps high-quality masks). (R2)
  - The statement “edges are formed solely between nodes that are directly adjacent horizontally and vertically” – if all tokens are adjacent, every token could be a subtree for a certain token. (R2)
- **Lack of qualitative and visual results**: No qualitative examples illustrating the specific problems addressed and the improvements. No visual comparison of generated pseudo masks with other methods. (R1, R2, R3)
- **Missing ablation experiments**: The query-wise self-distillation component lacks experimental validation – a dedicated ablation is needed. More generally, a detailed ablation study is beneficial (though Figure 4 is good). (R1, R3)
- **Incomplete experimental evaluation**:
  - Comparison to UnSAM is missing. (R2)
  - An apple-to-apple comparison on the wide benchmarks used in CutLER (Table 2 of CutLER) is needed. (R2)
  - Table 2 uses AP^{box} for instance segmentation evaluation; mask AP should be used instead. (R2)
  - The evaluation is limited; more comprehensive experiments are needed to trust effectiveness. (R2)
- **Efficiency not quantitatively demonstrated**: The claimed time-saving of single-stage vs multi-stage is not supported by concrete quantitative time consumption differences. (R1)
- **Resource analysis of SCC algorithm missing**: No detailed analysis of CPU usage, time consumption, drawbacks, or aspects needing improvement. (R1)
- **Comparison with semi-supervised/weakly supervised methods and generative models**: No discussion of how the self-supervised approach compares in performance to semi-supervised or weakly supervised methods. No cost comparison with text-to-image generation approaches (e.g., stable diffusion) for annotation cost reduction. (R1)
- **Domain generality not verified**: Experiments use general-object datasets; applicability to other domains is not discussed. (R1)
- **Threshold selection is too heuristic**. (R2)
- **Typo**: Line 434 “textttval” should be “\texttt{val}”. (R1, R2)
- **Tables and figures**: Recommended to place at the top of each page. (R2)
