## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a reinforcement learning with CLIP feedback (RLCF) framework for test-time adaptation (TTA) of vision-language models (VLMs) to improve zero-shot generalization. Given a test sample, the VLM is optimized to maximize the CLIP reward provided by a frozen CLIP model. The method is applied to three tasks: image classification, text-to-image/image-to-text retrieval, and image captioning. Experiments on ImageNet demonstrate effectiveness over baseline methods such as TPT, KD, and Pseudo-label.

### Strengths
1. **Novel use of RL with CLIP reward for TTA**: The paper introduces a reinforcement learning-based reward function using CLIP as feedback during test time. One reviewer (R3) considers this novel and interesting, while another (R2) notes similarities to [1] but concedes the framework’s universality across tasks.
2. **Universal across tasks**: RLCF is demonstrated on image classification, text-image retrieval, and image captioning within the zero-shot framework, showing broad applicability.
3. **Strong empirical results**: Extensive experiments on ImageNet for all three tasks show superior performance compared to recent state-of-the-art methods (e.g., TPT, KD, Pseudo-label). Improvements are significant and consistent.
4. **Clear presentation**: The paper is well-organized, easy to follow, with clear motivation, method descriptions, and nice visualizations (e.g., Fig.1, Fig.6 showing progression from worst to best).
5. **Addresses an important problem**: Improving zero-shot generalization of VLMs under distribution shift is a worthwhile research problem (highlighted by R3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Incremental nature and missing comparison with [1]**: The proposed RLCF bears strong resemblance to the method in [1]; the modifications (sampling strategies, reward baseline) appear incremental. A direct comparison between RLCF and the method in [1] is absent, making it difficult to ascertain the precise improvements (R2).
2. **Limited KD baseline evaluation**: The evaluation of KD is limited to its original 2015 formulation. Recent advancements in KD and an ensemble model of student+teacher should be compared to properly assess the claim that feedback combines strengths (R2).
3. **Missing baselines in image captioning**: Results from CLIPCap and CapDec using the CLIP-ViT-L/14 architecture (the teacher model) are not included, which would provide a more comprehensive benchmark (R2).
4. **Insufficient implementation details for TPT+KD**: The implementation of TPT+KD lacks sufficient detail regarding hyperparameters and training procedures, hampering reproducibility (R2). Typo: “kD” should be “KD” on page 8.
5. **Only zero-shot, not few-shot**: The method is only tested in zero-shot setting; applicability to few-shot learning is not explored (R1).
6. **Unclear data augmentation for text in classification**: Figure 2 shows a data augmentation strategy for image classification; it is unclear whether the same augmentation is applied to textual data (R1).
7. **Visual progression incomplete**: Figure 6 shows only step 1 (worst) and step 4 (best) of the generation process; showing intermediate steps (step 2, step 3) would better illustrate the transformation (R1).
8. **Reliance on high-quality CLIP feedback**: The method depends on good CLIP reward quality. CLIP performs less satisfactorily on fine-grained datasets (e.g., FGVC Aircraft, EuroSAT, CUB) and in specialized domains where its pre-trained representations may not capture nuances. This could lead to suboptimal adaptation under significant distribution shifts (R3).
9. **Use of large CLIP model for reward**: The reward model uses CLIP-ViT-L/14. Discussion of robustness with smaller, less reliable CLIP models (e.g., CLIP-ViT-B, RN50) is lacking. This raises practical applicability concerns for resource-constrained settings where smaller CLIP models would be more feasible (R3).
10. **Unclear design choices in reward formulation**: In Eq.(4), why clip CLIP score to non-negative? The statement “it encourages all model behaviors” needs clarification. In Eq.(5), what is the effect of not subtracting the expectation term? More explanation on the weighted reward sum for RLCF-S and the momentum buffer for RLCF-S-M is needed (R3).
