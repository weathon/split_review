## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Block Reflector Orthogonal (BRO) layers for constructing Lipschitz neural networks with certified robustness. BRO uses low-rank parameterization to avoid iterative approximations, improving memory and time efficiency. The authors also introduce a Logit Annealing (LA) loss to improve certified accuracy. Extensive experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet show state-of-the-art certified robustness with fewer parameters. Reviewers note strong theoretical foundations, significant efficiency gains, and thorough ablation studies. However, serious concerns about the orthogonality proof for convolutions, limited scalability to larger datasets, marginal/inconsistent performance gains, incomplete comparisons, and loss function motivation are raised. Overall scores range from 3 to 6; the two low-scoring reviewers (R1, R4) are much more critical of theoretical validity and experimental rigor.

### Strengths
- The paper is well written with clear motivation and the BRO contribution is well stated (R1, R5).
- The BRO layer provides significant computational and memory efficiency gains over existing orthogonal layers (SOC, LOT) by avoiding iterative approximation algorithms (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5).
- Strong theoretical analysis supporting the proposed methods, including mathematical proofs for orthogonal parameterization and loss function motivation (R2, R3).
- The Logit Annealing (LA) loss is a novel, theoretically-motivated loss function that improves certified accuracy by learning appropriate margins for most data points (R2, R3).
- BRONet achieves state-of-the-art certified robustness on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet with fewer parameters compared to baselines like LiResNet (R1, R3, R4, R5).
- Comprehensive ablation studies and empirical analysis validate each component (BRO layer, LA loss) (R1, R3).
- The BRO method leverages FFT for convolutions in a similar fashion to the Cayley approach (R1).
- The LA loss may serve as a scalable method to enhance training for Lipschitz neural networks (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical soundness of BRO convolutions**: One reviewer (R4, score 3, confidence 4) raises serious concerns about the orthogonality claim for the convolutional case, stating that without an explicit proof that Algorithm 1 yields orthogonal convolutions the certified robustness results are meaningless. Specific issues: unclear notation in Algorithm 1 (confusion between c_in, c_in’, c_out; line 4 missing pad in FFT: should be \(\tilde{V}:= \text{FFT}(V^{\text{pad}})\)); Proposition 2 dimension mismatch (if \(J \in \mathbb{C}^{m \times m}\) then \(J\tilde{V}J^{*}\) requires \(m=n\)); equation 14 should be \((\tilde{V}^* \tilde{V})^{-1} = J(J\tilde{V}^*\tilde{V}J^*)^{-1}J^*\); unsubstantiated assumptions like \(\text{FFT}(I)=I\) and \(\text{FFT}((V^{\top} \circledast V)^{-1}) = \text{FFT}((V^{\top} \circledast V))^{-1}\). R4 also states that the convolution orthogonality derivation is unclear and no proof is provided for lines 205-214. Other reviewers (R2, R3) note that BRO is not a universal orthogonal parameterization and lacks quantitative analysis of approximation error. R2 specifically requests a measure of the Frobenius norm between the learned BRO matrix and the ideal orthogonal matrix, and an exploration of how the rank parameter affects approximation quality and overall performance. R2 and R3 ask about the choice of rank and its sensitivity.
- **Limited scalability and lack of large-scale experiments**: Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R3, R5) criticize the absence of experiments on ImageNet or larger datasets. R1 notes that LiResNet (Hu et al. 2024) has shown scaling to ImageNet and asks for certified results on ImageNet. R2 questions whether BRO’s low-rank parameterization can capture complex dependencies in foundation models, potentially imposing a bottleneck. R5 states that experiments on CIFAR and Tiny-ImageNet may not fully demonstrate scalability and robustness on larger or real-world benchmarks. R3 also notes limited experiments beyond Tiny-ImageNet. The two minority reviewers (R1, R4) emphasize this gap more strongly.
- **Inconsistent or marginal performance gains**: R5 observes that certified accuracy in Table 1 is non-monotonic with respect to perturbation budget (e.g., lower at 72/255 than 36/255), which is counterintuitive and suggests a potential flaw in method or implementation. R1 also notes that BRO does not perform well at large radius, possibly due to loss parameterization. R5 further points out that improvements in Table 3 are marginal (e.g., 0.2-0.4%) and lack standard deviations or confidence intervals, making it unclear if gains are beyond random variance. R1 finds the many moving parts in experiments confusing and suggests focusing on comparison with the state-of-the-art (LiResNet).
- **Incomplete comparisons**: R1 notes that BRO is not compared to the Cayley approach in Figure 2 (memory/runtime) and asks whether Cayley orthogonal layers could appear there. R1 also points out that the results of the latest version of Hu et al. 2024 (June 2024) are not used, leading to a large gap; similarly, SLL results in Table 1 are from an earlier version with an erratum (see arxiv Table 7). R3 requests comparison with other certified defense methods beyond Lipschitz approaches and with empirical defenses. R4 asks whether authors tried LiResNet + BRO only (without LA loss) for Table 2 to isolate the backbone contribution.
- **Loss function concerns**: R4 finds the motivation for the LA loss convoluted and not clearly connected to the CR loss analysis; the theory in Theorem 1 does not directly inform LA. R4 suggests that the CR loss should be fully stated and compared with LA, and notes that the results in Figure 8 and Section C.2 are more interesting than the main paper’s write-up. R1 suggests that poor performance at large radius may relate to the loss parameterization. R3 notes that LA loss hyper-parameters require manual tuning.
- **Lack of ablation on activation function**: R2 asks whether the choice of activation function affects the Lipschitz constant, which is not addressed.
- **Implementation and presentation issues**: R4 identifies many typos and unclear notation throughout the paper, particularly in Algorithm 1 and propositions, and rates presentation as 1 (poor). While other reviewers found the paper well-organized, the minority reviewer’s concerns about clarity in the theoretical sections are serious. R4 also suggests implementing the convolution without FFT as a debugging check, and experimenting with fully connected classifiers on CIFAR-10/MNIST to verify orthogonality where it is clear.
- **Minority contrast**: Two low-scoring reviewers (R1: 3, confidence 4; R4: 3, confidence 4) are much more critical, with R4 questioning the core theoretical validity. The three higher-scoring reviewers (R2: 6, confidence 2; R3: 6, confidence 3; R5: 6, confidence 3) are positive but still note limitations. R2 had low confidence (2). The methodological concerns from R4 substantially weaken the credibility of the results for the minority.
