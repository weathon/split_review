## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FishLeg Surgeon (FLS), a new second-order pruning method for unstructured pruning (one-shot and gradual) that uses the FishLeg optimizer to estimate the inverse Fisher information matrix. FLS introduces a flexible tensor factorization parameterization and a meta-optimization procedure to compute weight importance for the Optimal Brain Surgeon framework. Evaluations are performed on a small autoencoder (MNIST) and ResNet-50 (ImageNet). All reviewers note the novelty of the approach but raise significant concerns about the limited scope of experiments, marginal empirical gains, missing comparisons with state-of-the-art methods, and insufficient analysis of computational costs and sensitivity to hyperparameters. Reviewer 1 is the most positive (score 6), while Reviewer 3 and 4 are negative (score 3) and find the contribution insufficient for ICLR.

### Strengths
* Novel application of the FishLeg optimizer to network pruning, extending it to model the full inverse Fisher information matrix with preconditioning and an efficient, flexible parameterization (Reviewer 1).
* The hypothesis that an approximated inverse Fisher via FishLeg meta-optimization can provide a practically tractable importance measure for OBS is a novel idea (Reviewer 2).
* FLS shows improvements over oBERT and M-FAC on the autoencoder (Figures 2,3) and at sparsity levels 70%, 80%, 90% on ResNet-50 (Reviewer 1, 3). The paper is easy to follow (Reviewer 3).
* Neural network compression is an important area; the proposed method brings marginal improvements over previous second-order methods (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
* **Insufficient empirical validation and narrow scope.** The evaluation is limited to a small autoencoder (MNIST) and ResNet-50 (ImageNet). No experiments on transformer architectures (e.g., BERT, ViT, DeiT), LLMs, or tasks such as object detection/NLP are provided (Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 4). For ICLR, pruning research should at least include transformer benchmarks (Reviewer 4). The paper claims to address accessibility of large models but only tests on ResNet-50, which anyone can run; no demonstration on larger models or scalability analysis is given (Reviewer 1, 2).
* **Marginal gains on ImageNet and missing comparisons with stronger baselines.** The results on ImageNet (Table 1) are marginal (e.g., 62% accuracy at 75% sparsity for ResNet-50) (Reviewer 1, 4). Competing approaches like CAP achieve ~75% accuracy at the same sparsity without using second-order importance (Reviewer 3). The paper does not compare against CAP, SparseGPT (for one-shot pruning on large models), or other recent pruning methods (Reviewer 3, 4). The baselines M-FAC and oBERT are not state-of-the-art for this benchmark (Reviewer 3). It is unclear whether M-FAC and oBERT were run from scratch (Reviewer 3). Some reviewers question whether the method’s advantages translate to more than marginal improvements (Reviewer 4).
* **No computational cost or memory analysis.** Efficiency is claimed but no timing breakdown is given. The time for computing the inverse Fisher and the pruning steps should be compared to baselines (Reviewer 1). Memory consumption must be quantified: storing the FIM approximation may require ~17GB for ResNet-50 while the model itself uses <3GB (Reviewer 1 question). The computational cost of the meta-optimization process (for λ) should be explicitly quantified and compared to other methods (Reviewer 2).
* **Parameterization and hyperparameter sensitivity.** The inverse Fisher parameterization (Eq. 9) with low-rank plus diagonal structure may limit expressiveness and lead to suboptimal pruning decisions. The authors should explore sensitivity to the rank, different diagonal forms, and provide a thorough justification (Reviewer 2). The meta-optimization of λ introduces additional hyperparameters (learning rate, number of iterations) that are tricky to tune; a sensitivity analysis and practical guidelines are missing (Reviewer 2).
* **Lack of conclusive demonstration of superiority.** The proposed importance metric is not convincingly shown to be superior. A concrete example (e.g., a specific layer in ResNet-50) should be provided to demonstrate that the importance scores disagree with competing methods (e.g., OBC) and that the proposed method leads to lower loss change. It should be shown that weights pruned by FLS are indeed less important than those pruned by other methods (Reviewer 2).
* **Contribution considered insufficient for ICLR** by some reviewers (Reviewer 3, 4) due to the above weaknesses, especially the limited evaluation, marginal gains, and lack of comparison with newer methods.
