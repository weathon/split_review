## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents HyperDT, a novel extension of CART decision trees to hyperbolic space. It replaces Euclidean axis-aligned hyperplanes with geodesic submanifolds and uses inner products to adapt the splitting rule, eliminating the need for Riemannian optimization, numerically unstable exponential/log maps, and pairwise comparisons. The method achieves constant-time decision complexity and is extended to HyperRF, a random forest. Empirical evaluations on synthetic (mixture of Gaussians) and real-world datasets (biological sequences, graph embeddings) show that HyperDT/HyperRF outperform prior hyperbolic decision tree methods (HoroRF) and Euclidean counterparts.

### Strengths
- **Novelty**: The paper introduces a novel and innovative extension of decision tree algorithms to hyperbolic space, a direction underexplored in the community (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Efficiency**: HyperDT eliminates computationally intensive Riemannian optimization, exponential/log maps, and pairwise comparisons, achieving constant-time decision complexity and asymptotically faster training than HoroRF (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 5).
- **Simplicity and clarity of derivation**: The approach leverages inner products to transform Euclidean axis-aligned splits into hyperbolic geodesic boundaries, with closed-form equations for decision boundaries and a clear explanation of the candidate hyperplane selection process (Reviewers 3, 5).
- **Visual presentation**: Figures 1 and 2 provide strong intuition for how axis-aligned splits generalize to hyperbolic geometry (Reviewer 3).
- **Empirical performance**: Competitive or superior accuracy compared to HoroRF and Euclidean baselines on multiple datasets (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Practicality**: The Python implementation adheres to standard scikit-learn API conventions, bridging theory and application (Reviewers 4, 5).
- **Valuable theoretical insight**: The finding that inner products can be used to adapt decision tree splits to hyperbolic space is a theoretically insightful contribution (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical justification and analysis**:
  - There is no theoretical analysis explaining why geodesic boundaries outperform horosphere-based splits (HoroRF) on the synthetic Gaussian mixture data or other datasets (Reviewer 1). The practical advantages of geodesics over horospheres are not rigorously justified; trade-offs between boundary types (e.g., convexity, decision region complexity) are not discussed (Reviewer 4).
  - The CART-like greedy, top-down construction is likely to yield suboptimal solutions, but this limitation is not acknowledged (Reviewer 5, score 5, confidence 4—notably more critical on this point).
  - The restriction to axis-aligned geodesics (using dimensions 0 and d only) is motivated, but it is unclear how the construction generalizes to arbitrary hyperplanes intersecting ℍ^D. The assumption that all dimensions except 0 and d are zero appears limiting (Reviewer 3; also Reviewer 5, question 1). Alternative inductive biases (e.g., spherical coordinate splits) are not explored or compared (Reviewer 1).

- **Clarity of derivation and parameterization**:
  - Sections 3.2 and Appendix A.2 contain unclear steps: Eq. (11) suggests a single intersection point rather than a geodesic; the role of vector **u** in the derivation of Eq. (24) and the transition from Eq. (24) to Eqs. (25-27) are not explained; the connection between dimension d' in Eq. (12) and the normal vector characterization (Eq. 8/9) is missing (Reviewer 3). Eq. (10) is missing “\cos” (minor) (Reviewer 3).
  - The explanation of why axis-aligned hyperplanes lack clarity in the hyperboloid model is insufficient (Reviewer 5, question 1).
  - It should be explicitly clarified that the data input for HyperDT are hyperbolic representations (e.g., embeddings); a separate embedding step is needed for Euclidean data (Reviewer 3).

- **Experimental evaluation breadth**:
  - Datasets are limited: all tested have ≤1000 samples and ≤16 dimensions; the paper does not evaluate on larger or different types of data, and all datasets appear particularly well-suited to the method (Reviewer 2).
  - Only shallow trees (depth=3) are used; performance with deeper trees (e.g., T>3 or 7) is not shown (Reviewer 2).
  - Missing comparisons with other advanced decision tree algorithms (e.g., global optimization methods such as [1] from Reviewer 5; additional methods referenced by Reviewer 2 [1], [2]).
  - Missing comparisons with other hyperbolic classifiers: hyperbolic logistic regression, hyperbolic SVM (Reviewer 4). The only hyperbolic baseline is HoroRF, which is insufficient to demonstrate advantages over alternative hyperbolic classifiers.
  - Ablation studies are lacking: no comparison of equidistance mid-angles vs. naive mid-angles in terms of accuracy and execution time; no justification for the chosen candidate hyperplane selection (Reviewer 5). Additional metrics such as average number of nodes per tree are not reported, which would help support claims of fewer nodes needed with geodesic boundaries (Reviewer 5).
  - A suggested baseline (suggested by Reviewer 1) is not included: transform Poincaré ball data into spherical coordinates and run a standard decision tree to compare against methods that lie between hyperbolic and Euclidean trees.

- **Scalability**: Only tested on small datasets (samples <1000, dims ≤16); scalability to larger datasets is unclear (Reviewer 2).

- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 5, confidence 4) is notably more critical, emphasizing the suboptimality of greedy CART-style construction and the lack of ablation/comparisons, while other reviewers (scores 6–8) agree on contributions but also note similar weaknesses in evaluation depth and theoretical analysis.
