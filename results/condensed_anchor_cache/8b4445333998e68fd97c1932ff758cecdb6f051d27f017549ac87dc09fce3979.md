## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper addresses atomic steric clashes in geometric diffusion models for 3D structure-based drug design by introducing a constrained sampling process that incorporates a non-convex distance constraint into Langevin dynamics using proximal regularization. The method is applied at inference time, requiring no training modifications. It is evaluated on the CrossDock2020 dataset using TargetDiff, showing reduced clash ratios. Reviewer 2 is substantially more positive (score 6), finding the approach novel and effective; the other three reviewers (scores 3, 3, 3) are more critical of novelty, evaluation, and writing.

### Strengths

- The problem of atomic clashes in generated molecules is clearly identified as a major shortcoming of geometric diffusion models, with strong evidence that it violates physical principles such as Van der Waals force and hydrogen bonding (R2, R1, R4).
- The proposed method only modifies the sampling (inference) process, making it applicable to different pre-trained score‑based models without retraining (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- The use of proximal regularization to handle non‑convex distance constraints is novel in the context of structure‑based diffusion models for drug design (R2).
- The empirical clash ratio is effectively reduced (R1, R2, R4). The method also improves connectivity, mean CR, and stability of generated molecules (R3).
- The problem formulation and presentation are clear, and theoretical analysis is provided (R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited novelty**: The idea of reducing clashes is not new; the method is similar to the validity guidance in DecompDiff (R3, R4). The contribution is considered modest (R4).
- **Threshold choice lacks biological justification**: The 2 Å cut‑off for clash/non‑clash appears ad‑hoc. PoseCheck uses 0.5 Å (R1). The choice is not explained in terms of protein/ligand properties or whether it generalizes across datasets (R4). No sensitivity analysis on the threshold is provided (R1).
- **Evaluation scope is narrow**: Only CrossDock2020 and TargetDiff are used; generalization to other datasets (e.g., PDBBind) and diffusion models (e.g., DiffSBDD, SBDDDiff) is unverified (R1, R2). The method should be tested on other diffusion‑based models to confirm agnosticism (R1).
- **Metrics and benchmarks are incomplete**: Table 1 uses metrics from Hoogeboom et al. (unconditional generation), which are not appropriate for pocket‑conditional generation. Standard metrics from MiDi, TargetDiff, and PoseCheck (docking score, strain energies, protein–ligand interactions) should be used (R1). Vina score improvement is marginal despite being a key metric for spatial interaction (R3, R4). Performance gains in validity, novelty, uniqueness, diversity, SA, QED, Lipinski, and bond distributions are marginal (R4).
- **Computational cost not discussed**: Proximal constrained sampling adds overhead; there is no analysis of runtime or optimizations to scale to larger datasets (R2). A case study comparing generated molecules with experimental crystal structures is missing (R2).
- **Writing and clarity issues**: The paper is poorly written and hard to follow, with undefined terms, notational errors, and typos (e.g., “tTargetDiff”, “2Abetween”) (R1, R2). Details of how clash is computed are insufficient, making reproduction difficult (R1). The abstract mentions Van der Waals and hydrogen bonding, but it is unclear whether the model better captures these interactions specifically (R4).
- **Missing comparisons**: The method should be explicitly compared with the validity guidance in DecompDiff (R3). Applicability to other diffusion‑based docking algorithms like DiffDock is not explored (R2).
