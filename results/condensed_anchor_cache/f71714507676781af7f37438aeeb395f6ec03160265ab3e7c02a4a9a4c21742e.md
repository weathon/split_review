## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes TIS-DPO (Token-level Importance Sampling for Direct Preference Optimization), which addresses the limitation of standard DPO that treats all tokens equally. It argues that optimal DPO data has equal expected rewards per token; since that ideal dataset is unavailable, it uses importance sampling on the actual dataset with token-level importance weights estimated from contrastive LLMs (three methods: contrastive prompts, separate SFT on winning/losing, forward/reverse DPO). Experiments on harmlessness, helpfulness, and summarization tasks show improvements over baselines like DPO, PPO, IPO, TDPO, KTO.

### Strengths
- Novel idea of using contrastive LLM likelihood as token-level weights for importance sampling.
- Important area of improving offline preference alignment with DPO.
- Great insight about the limitation of sequence-level DPO, introducing the need for nuanced token-level optimization.
- Surprisingly good improvements against DPO baseline using token-level importance sampling.
- The method addresses a limitation of DPO (equal token treatment) and enhances optimization efficiency.
- Evaluated across multiple datasets (Anthropic-HH, PKUSafe, Summarization), showing robustness and versatility.
- Practical use of actual dataset with importance sampling instead of assuming ideal dataset.
- Thorough proof and derivation process for the TIS-DPO objective.
- Clear and well-structured method.
- Comprehensive experimental evaluation: multiple metrics (MT-bench, win rates), multiple baselines (DPO, PPO, IPO, TDPO, KTO).
- Nice appendix.

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical validity concern:** The proof applies Hoeffding inequality which requires i.i.d. variables (token rewards), but tokens in a sentence are not i.i.d. along with their observed rewards, casting doubt on the theoretical analysis.
- **Questionable motivation:** Token-level DPO seems misaligned with how preference data is collected (holistic judgments by annotators, not token-by-token evaluations).
- **Accuracy and reliability of token importance estimation not thoroughly validated.**
- **Assumes tokens can be evaluated independently, ignoring context dependencies in language.**
- **Contradiction with DPO's core advantage:** Introducing reward models for token importance estimation contradicts DPO's advantage of avoiding reward modeling; suggests comparing against PPO where rewards are estimated.
- **Lack of in-depth analysis on why three contrastive methods measure token importance:** No clear justification for why prediction probability differences correlate with token importance; no exploration of potential biases from each method; no theoretical explanation for observed performance differences among the three methods.
- **Fixed token importance weights:** Weights are generated before training and remain fixed; as the model evolves, token importance may change, potentially leading to suboptimal performance.
- **Scalability and generalizability unclear:** Unclear whether every model requires its own weights or if weights transfer across models, architectures, sizes, or datasets; no experiments validating transferability.
- **Limited experimental scope:** Experiments do not cover code and mathematics domains where token-level importance might be even more crucial.
- **Additional computational cost:** Constructing contrastive LLMs and estimating token-wise weights increases computational costs and training time compared to simpler baselines; no discussion of computational trade-offs or scalability implications for larger models; unclear how many LLMs are needed (possibly 3?).
- **Sensitivity to data annotation noise:** TIS-DPO(S/D) may be more sensitive to noise than DPO; suggested experiment adding 20% label noise (swapping chosen/reject) and comparing performance degradation.
- **Overfitting risk:** TIS-DPO(D) in Table 1 shows a performance improvement far exceeding expectations, suggesting a probable risk of overfitting; suggest cross-validation to test.
- **Hyperparameter complexity:** New hyperparameters (k, μ, L, U in Eq. 19) introduced; limited sensitivity analysis; no clear guidance for selection in different scenarios; also conflict in usage of μ in Theorem 2, eq. 14, and eq. 19.
- **Missing analysis of sequence length and token position effects:** No investigation of how sequence length affects performance or of the relationship between token position and estimated importance; method may be sensitive to sequence length where longer sequences may have higher rewards.
- **Table 2 presentation should be corrected.**
- **Missing DPO result in Table 1.**
- **Need exact reference for DPO token-level interpretability claim:** "Although Rafailov et al. (2024a) demonstrate that DPO possesses a certain degree of token-level interpretability" – need to point to exact section or figure.
- **Weaker datasets used:** HH and Summarization are relatively poor quality compared to newer alignment datasets like Ultrafeedback and HelpSteer2; could also align stronger models like Gemma2-9B or Llama-3-8B.
- **Better for safety than helpfulness:** Need explanation or intuition for why improvements are much better for safety alignment than helpfulness.
- **Convergence experiments:** Need empirical experiments to see if TIS-DPO leads to faster convergence or better convergence, and how to design such experiments.
- **Win rate vs MT-bench discrepancy:** In Table 1, win rate increases significantly while MT-bench score is not improved comparably; need explanation.
- **Which model is being used as the competitor in win rate evaluation?**
- **Reproducibility of baselines:** Not confirmed whether all comparative experiments (e.g., TDPO on Anthropic-HH) were reproduced; need clarification.
- **Note:** Zeng2024a and Zeng2024b are different versions of the same paper.

**Reviewer disagreement:** Two reviewers (scores 8, 10) are very positive, praising the novelty, insights, and strong experimental results. Two reviewers (scores 5, 5) are less convinced, raising foundational concerns about motivation, theoretical validity, reliability, scope, and computational cost.
