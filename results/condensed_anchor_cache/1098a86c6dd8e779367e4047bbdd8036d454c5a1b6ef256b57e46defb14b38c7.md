## Merged Review

### Summary
MindFormer introduces a transformer-based architecture for multi-subject fMRI-to-image and fMRI-to-text decoding. The model uses a learnable subject token and subject-specific linear projectors to align fMRI signals, and leverages IP-Adapter embeddings (reduced to 16×768 dimensions) to condition a Stable Diffusion model for image reconstruction or an LLM for caption generation. Evaluations are performed on the NSD dataset across low-level (PixCorr, SSIM, AlexNet) and high-level (Inception, CLIP, EffNet-B, SwAV) metrics, plus text metrics. The method achieves strong performance, especially with limited training data. Reviewers are split: Reviewer 2 (score 6) finds the framework sound and convincing with high performance, while Reviewer 3 (score 1) and Reviewer 1 (score 3) raise major concerns about novelty and missing comparisons; Reviewer 4 (score 5) is moderately positive but identifies methodological overlap with prior work.

### Strengths
- **Methodology clarity and reproducibility.** The paper is clearly written with detailed implementation steps (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Strong empirical results.** The method outperforms baselines on most visual and textual similarity metrics, particularly in the low-data regime (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).  
  *Reviewer 2 specifically highlights higher performance on both image and text recall metrics.*
- **Convincing ablation of the learnable subject token.** The token improves performance when multiple subjects are used together, demonstrating the benefit of multi-subject training (Reviewer 2).
- **Sound framework for both image and text recall.** The architecture, including the IP-Adapter conditioning and use of Stable Diffusion / LLM, is well motivated and implemented (Reviewer 2).
- **Reduced embedding dimensionality.** The IP-Adapter outputs 16×768 embeddings, which helps reduce model size – a potential advantage for scaling to more subjects (Reviewers 2, 3).  
  *Reviewer 3 notes this as a strength but also calls for actual size comparisons.*
- **Strong fMRI-to-text performance with few fine-tuning epochs.** The method shows efficient transfer to text generation (Reviewer 3).
- **Extensive experiments and ablation study.** Multiple experiments analyze the effectiveness of the proposed components (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty; key designs already in prior work.** The subject token was introduced in CLIP‑MUSED (Zhou et al., ICLR 2024) and the subject‑specific linear projection was used in MindEye2 (Scotti et al., ICML 2024). The contributions are incremental and more applicable to computer vision than brain decoding (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).  
  *Reviewer 2 also notes a minor weakness in component novelty.*
- **Missing comparison to MindEye2 (the main competitor).** No quantitative comparison is provided, especially in the limited‑data setting where MindEye2 showed state‑of‑the‑art results. The linear projection is not novel given MindEye2’s subject‑specific ridge regression layer (Reviewers 1, 3).  
  *Reviewer 3 is particularly critical: “comparison with MindEye2 is essential to validate effectiveness.”*
- **Efficiency claims not validated.** The paper claims a smaller, more efficient model, but no actual numbers are given for model parameters, memory usage, or inference time. Embedding dimensions “257×768 and 77×768” are confusing when first introduced (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Lack of interpretability analysis.** No fMRI‑space saliency maps are provided to understand how the model uses brain regions (e.g., visual cortex areas) or to identify common neural patterns. Visualizing the latent space of fMRI‑image pairs (common vs. subject‑specific clusters per semantic category) would strengthen the claim of semantic alignment (Reviewer 2).
- **Subjective claims without evidence.** The statement (lines 175‑178) that Versatile Diffusion’s variability introduces “challenges in brain decoding” and “reduces fidelity” is unsupported by literature or experiments (Reviewer 1).
- **Writing and presentation issues.** Equation 3 is missing a negative sign; Figure 3 has unclear connecting lines (Reviewer 4). The paper does not explain how Stable Diffusion is modified to accept a 16×768 image embedding as a condition (Reviewer 4). Whether the **nsdgeneral** ROI (primarily visual cortex) is appropriate for fMRI‑to‑text reconstruction is also questionable (Reviewer 4).
- **Questions about IP‑Adapter vs. ViT.** No ablation compares IP‑Adapter to a standard ViT projection; the advantage is not demonstrated (Reviewer 1).  
- **Unexplained results in Table 3.** Setup **(a)** shows better low‑level performance than setup **(b)** when the dataset is larger – no explanation is provided (Reviewer 1).  
- **Limited insight from low‑level reconstruction.** The method appears to focus only on high‑level features; it is unclear whether low‑level reconstruction (as in MindEye series) is unnecessary or omitted (Reviewer 4).
