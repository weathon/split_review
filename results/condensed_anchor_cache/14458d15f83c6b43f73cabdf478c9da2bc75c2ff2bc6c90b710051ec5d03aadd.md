## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces MultiUI, a 7.3M-sample dataset from 1M websites for text-rich visual understanding, leveraging accessibility trees and LLMs (Llama 3 70B, GPT-4o-mini) to generate nine task types (captioning, QA, OCR, grounding, action prediction). Models trained on MultiUI (e.g., LLaVA-based) show significant gains on GUI benchmarks (48% on VisualWebBench, 19.1% on Mind2Web) and generalize to document, OCR, and chart tasks. Reviewers agree the dataset is large-scale and addresses an important problem, but disagree on the strength of its contributions. One reviewer (rating 8) is notably more positive, praising the clear methodology and strong results; the other reviewers (ratings 5–6) note substantial limitations in data quality validation, baseline comparisons, documentation, and novelty.

### Strengths
- The dataset is large-scale (7.3M samples, 1M websites) and fills a gap in text-rich visual understanding, leveraging naturally structured web UI data. All reviewers recognize its relevance for the VLM community.
- The data collection pipeline (using accessibility trees and LLMs) is clearly described; sub-tasks (captioning, QA, OCR, grounding, action prediction) are well-motivated. One reviewer (rating 8) particularly highlighted this clarity.
- Models fine-tuned on MultiUI achieve strong results across GUI tasks and non-UI domains, demonstrating significant performance gains (e.g., 48% on VisualWebBench, 19.1% on Mind2Web) and robust generalization. This supports the dataset’s utility.
- The paper is easy to follow with substantial illustrative figures.

### Weaknesses
- **Data quality and validation**: The paper lacks rigorous assessment of label quality (accuracy, consistency, noise) from the LLM-based pipeline. Claims of hallucination prevention are not described. Reviewers question whether any human validation was conducted (e.g., for QA tasks) and how harmful/adult content was filtered beyond LLM-based checks. The reliability of using Llama 3 70B and LLaVA for higher-level tasks like question-answering is a concern, as it risks introducing biases.
- **Limited baseline comparisons and evaluations**: The selection of baselines is too narrow, mostly relying on LLaVA. Several cells in Tables 2 and 3 are empty. Missing comparisons with closed models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5) and strong open-source models (MolMo, Gemini, Llama3.2). GPT-4o is not evaluated on GUI understanding/grounding (Table 2) despite showing strong performance on general benchmarks. Screenspot results for SeeClick and CogAgent could be added. The paper also lacks a comprehensive statistical comparison with existing datasets (SeeClick, Ferret-UI, Mind2Web, WebArena) in terms of sample counts, annotation types, and tasks. No experiments compare training on competitor datasets on the same benchmarks.
- **Novelty concerns**: The primary distinction from prior work (SeeClick, Ferret-UI, Mind2Web, WebArena) appears to be dataset scale rather than fundamentally new methodologies or task definitions.
- **Documentation and clarity**: The dataset lacks documentation and a usage guide for the code, making navigation and reproducibility difficult. The grounding task generation description (Section 2.3.3) is unclear—how Llama 3 is used for generating grounding instructions and how element descriptions are extracted from the DOM tree (e.g., is it simply the element’s text as in Fig. 15?) need specification. The high-resolution tiling strategy (Section 3.1) is not detailed; Appendix F lists a “maximum resolution” but it is unclear if this refers to individual tiles. Figures 4 and 6 rely on color, limiting readability in black-and-white.
- **Missing experiments and analyses**: No experiments fine-tuning other architectures beyond LLaVA. Train/test overlap analysis is missing. No analysis of how image resolution impacts performance or patterns across LLaVA variants. The ablation on task type (Section 4.7) shows RefCOCO+ deteriorates with MultiUI Grounding but improves with QA data—why the full MultiUI still benefits is not explained. The exact test splits used for MMMU, RefCOCO+, etc. are not specified. One reviewer questioned whether data points from the same website could appear in both train and test splits.
