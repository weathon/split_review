## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a training-free method called Cyclic One-Way Diffusion (COW) that uses a cyclic “disturb” and “construct” process within a pre-trained frozen diffusion model to preserve low-level pixel information from visual conditions while also following a text prompt. The method is applied to inpainting, attribute editing, style transfer, and other customization tasks. Reviewers found the idea interesting and the method elegant, but opinions varied: one reviewer was very positive (rating 8) about the extensive experiments and high fidelity, two were moderately positive (rating 6), and one was more critical (rating 5), citing a lack of theoretical foundation and insufficient experimental comparisons. All agreed on the potential of the learning-free perspective, but several concerns about practical limitations, analysis depth, and reproducibility emerged.

### Strengths
- Proposes a “diffusion in diffusion” process that enables effective pixel-level and semantic-level visual conditioning, providing a novel perspective to utilize the capabilities of pre-trained diffusion models.
- The method is learning-free (no additional training or fine-tuning) and can be combined with any pre-trained frozen diffusion model.
- The idea is clearly illustrated and well-written, making it easy to follow; the proposed approach is elegant and straightforward.
- Extensive experimental results are provided for various applications (inpainting, text-vision-conditioned generation, style transfer, etc.), demonstrating generation of realistic images with high fidelity to both text and visual conditions.
- Experiments include human evaluation and metrics such as ID-Distance and face detection rates, showing good performance.
- The method can be widely used for personality customization with one or several conditions.

### Weaknesses
- The approach lacks a solid theoretical foundation; it is not intuitively explained why the cyclic one-way diffusion works, which makes it harder to assess its generalizability and limitations.
- The method may have an intrinsic drawback: repeatedly replacing the diffusion latent variable with the visual condition can make the visual condition too strong, potentially conflicting with the text prompt and leading to unrealistic or non-diverse outputs. There is no mechanism to balance the influence of visual vs. textual conditions.
- No quantitative ablation studies or analyses are provided for critical parameters such as the number of cycles, the positions of start and end points, or how these affect quality. The sensitivity of the method to these hyper-parameters is unclear, making it difficult to determine optimal settings for different tasks.
- The inner diffusion cycles (“disturb”, “reconstruct”) introduce additional computational overhead; the trade-off between the number of cycles and generation quality/efficiency is not analyzed. One reviewer noted that the method is inferior to SD inpainting on both performance and efficiency in Table 1, and while it is training-free, the higher inference cost may weaken that advantage and limit practical applicability.
- Most experimental examples are limited to placing an object in a bounding box and adding background; it is not shown whether the method can handle whole-image generation/editing or more general image manipulation tasks.
- The paper would benefit from presenting and analyzing failure cases to clarify the method’s limitations.
- The comparison methods’ results appear too poor (e.g., in Table 1), and more details about their settings are needed. For a comprehensive evaluation, learning-free baselines such as LIVR should be included.
- Sections 3.2 and 3.3 lack a theoretical foundation or pseudo-code, making the explanation harder to follow.
- In Section 3.3, paragraph 4, the difference between “two steps/two ends” and the t-th diffusion step is unclear; how they are used is not explained.
- The evaluation in Table 2 and that described in Section 4.2 are mismatched (the table and text do not align).
- The division of cycles into three phases (page 4) lacks a strict boundary and a clear justification; it is unclear how this phase classification is supported.
- There are typos in the paper (e.g., around eq. (1)) that should be corrected.
- One reviewer questioned the level of changes that occur in the seed image region after the process, noting that some changes are visible in examples but not consistently explained.
- The paper lacks information on reproducibility (e.g., code is not provided) and does not clarify whether the comparison methods are learning-free or fine-tuned.
