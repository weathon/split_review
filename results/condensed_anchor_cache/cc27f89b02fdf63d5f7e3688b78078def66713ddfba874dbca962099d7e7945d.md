## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper analyzes trained depthwise convolution (dwconv) kernels from a range of DS-CNNs (ConvNeXt, ConvNeXtV2, EfficientNet, MobileNet, etc.) using unsupervised clustering (autoencoder + k-means). It reports that over 95% of kernels in ConvNeXtV2 and 90% in ConvNeXt fall into a few clusters whose shapes match Difference-of-Gaussian (DoG) functions and their first- and second-order derivatives. This pattern persists deep into the network, not only in early layers. The work draws parallels to biological vision models and suggests implications for interpretability and architectural design.

### Strengths

- **Novel analysis focusing specifically on depthwise convolution.** To my knowledge, this is the first such study (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- **Insightful visualizations:** Figure 2 convincingly shows DoG-like shapes; Figure 5 identifies a basis of distinct DoG-derivative clusters (R1).
- **Extensive validation across many architectures and layers:** ConvNeXt, ConvNeXtV2, EfficientNet b0–b7, MobileNetV3, Hornet; over 1.6M filters examined; per-layer histograms provided (R1, R3, R4).
- **Observation that DoG-like patterns emerge throughout all layers**, not only in the first layer as previously shown (R3).
- **Correspondence: more performant networks show a higher percentage of identifiable (Gabor-like) filters**, suggesting a link between lower-order filter simplicity and generalization (R3).
- **Raises important questions** about filter complexity, model regularization, and potential canonical visual representations (R3).
- **Practical implications** for DS-CNN design (e.g., kernel size, number of filters per layer) and for biologically-inspired architectures (R4).
- **Robust and insightful results** that connect artificial and biological visual processing (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of concrete architectural improvements or new design principles.** The paper stops at describing the phenomenon; it does not propose how to leverage it (e.g., a loss term encouraging Gabor-like filters, or a kernel basis reparameterization) (R1, R2, R3).
- **Uncertain significance:** The ultimate goal of the analysis remains unclear. While filters cluster into DoG shapes, no deeper lessons (e.g., for network design, regularization, or training dynamics) are extracted (R2).
- **Concern about the autoencoder methodology:**
  - Reviewer 2 questions why a training‑needed autoencoder is necessary when k‑means appears sufficient (e.g., MobileNetV3 analysis). The autoencoder may impose structure and bias results toward DoG shapes; its denoising effect might smooth real learned patterns (R2).
  - Reviewer 1 also asks why an autoencoder is used instead of direct k‑means clustering on the raw kernels (R1).
- **Small kernel size (7×7) limits the claim.** Reviewer 2 notes that 7×7 kernels are small and that verifying the DoG claim on models with much larger kernels (31×31, 51×51; e.g., RepLKNet) is needed to establish generalizability (R2).
- **Incorrect reported accuracy in Table 1:** ConvNeXtV2_tiny top‑1 accuracy on ImageNet‑1k is given as 84.9%, which is wrong (R2; the original model reports ~82.1%).
- **Need for multiple training runs and different datasets:** Only single pre‑trained checkpoints per model are analyzed. Repeating the analysis over multiple random seeds, hyperparameters, and datasets (e.g., Places‑365 for scene classification, CityScapes for segmentation) would strengthen the claim that the patterns are inherent to the architecture (R3, R4).
- **Missing analysis of training dynamics:** How and when these filters form during training (e.g., early vs. late emergence) is not studied. Relatedly, examining over‑fitted or under‑fitted networks could show an increase in “other” (non‑DoG) filters (R3).
- **Unclear role of individual filter basis elements:** The paper does not interpret what each DoG‑derivative pattern (e.g., “off‑center cross”) contributes to the network’s function (R1).
- **Unexplained anomalies in specific layers:** For example, EfficientNet‑b4/b6 layer 10 and Hornet‑tiny layer 14 have high proportions of “square‑off” or other unexpected patterns. The cause (e.g., padding skew, pooling effects, or random seed) is not addressed (R1, R3, R4).
- **Impact of padding and global pooling not considered:** Reviewer 4 suggests that one‑sided padding can skew filters and may explain On‑Square/Off‑Square patterns. Weight banding from global pooling could also affect the results (R4).
- **No comparison with standard (non‑depthwise) convolutions:** It remains unclear whether standard CNNs also learn a similar basis (just less interpretable) or whether the DoG property is unique to depthwise convolutions. A direct comparison (e.g., ResNet vs. ResNeXt/ConvNeXt) is missing (R1).
- **Figure captions are not self‑contained** and often unclear about how visualizations were obtained and what conclusion to draw (R1).
- **Inconsistent notation:** DS, DC, and DSC are used interchangeably; should be unified (R1).
- **Minor language issues:** Incorrect use of \cite, punctuation errors in captions and text (R4).
