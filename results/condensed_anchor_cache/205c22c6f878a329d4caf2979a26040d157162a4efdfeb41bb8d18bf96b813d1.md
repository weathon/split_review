## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes MeZO-A³dam, a memory-efficient zeroth-order Adam variant that adjusts the adaptivity level (the ε term in Adam) as a function of the parameter dimension \(d\). This adjustment is motivated by the authors' “weak adaptivity hypothesis,” which claims that reducing adaptivity in ZO optimization helps mitigate the high variance of ZO gradient estimates. The paper provides dimension-free convergence and generalization bounds and presents experiments on several LLMs (up to 7B) and datasets, showing faster convergence and better final performance than MeZO, MeZO-SVRG, and MeZO-Adam, as well as memory savings when combined with an 8‑bit low‑bit optimizer. The idea is simple and clearly explained. However, multiple reviewers raise significant concerns about the correctness of the theoretical proof (Lemma C.6 appears to miss a dimension factor), the lack of ablation on the scaling function and hyperparameter \(s\), the absence of first‑order baselines, the limited experimental scale (models <7B, no PEFT), and the unclear handling of the hand‑crafted prompt issue. One reviewer is notably more negative, questioning the core theoretical claim and the strength of the empirical validation.

### Strengths

- **Clean motivation and well-written.** The paper is easy to follow, with clear motivation (weak adaptivity hypothesis) and well‑laid‑out illustrations (R1, R2).
- **Weak adaptivity hypothesis and theoretical guarantees.** The paper proposes a plausible hypothesis for why reducing adaptivity helps ZO optimization and provides dimension‑free convergence and generalization bounds (R1, R3, R4). This is a rare theoretical contribution in ZO‑LLM fine‑tuning.
- **Novel adaptivity scaling mechanism.** Adjusting the Adam \(\epsilon\) (denoted \(\delta\)) according to model dimension addresses the high variance of ZO gradients and is a simple, transparent idea (R1, R4).
- **Superior performance over ZO baselines.** The method consistently outperforms MeZO, MeZO-SVRG, and MeZO-Adam in terms of both convergence speed and final accuracy across several models and tasks (up to \(36.6\%\) improvement, up to \(12.6\times\) faster convergence) (R1, R3, R4).
- **Memory efficiency.** Using an off‑the‑shelf low‑bit optimizer yields \(40.3\%\) and \(43.6\%\) memory reduction compared to MeZO-SVRG and MeZO-Adam respectively (R1).
- **Source code provided.** The submitted code aids reproducibility (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Missing first‑order baselines.** No comparison against standard first‑order optimizers (SGD, Adam) is given, making it impossible to gauge the real‑world trade‑off between memory savings and performance loss (R2, R4).
- **Incomplete ablation studies.** 
  - The scaling function \(h(d)\) is central to the method, yet no ablation explores different functional forms (R2).
  - The hyperparameter \(s\) (where \(\delta = \delta_0 \cdot s \cdot d^{2/3}\)) is not studied across models or tasks (R4).
  - No experiment varies \(\delta\) directly to show its effect on performance (R4).
  - It is unclear whether simply tuning \(\alpha, \beta_1, \beta_2\) in MeZO-Adam could achieve the same improvements as the proposed \(\delta\) adjustment (R3).
- **Theoretical proof appears incorrect.** The proof of Lemma C.6 misses the model dimension \(d\) (the result should contain \(d+2\) instead of the claimed constant), which casts doubt on the dimension‑free claim. The same mistake appears in the SubZero paper (arXiv:2410.08989) (R4).
- **Experimental scope is limited.** 
  - The largest model used is small (<7B); given the use of high‑end GPUs, experiments on models above 7B (e.g., 7B or 13B) are expected (R4). 
  - Only full‑parameter fine‑tuning is tested; parameter‑efficient schemes (LoRA, prefix tuning, prompt tuning) are omitted (R1, R4). Since \(\delta\) depends on the dimension of *learnable* parameters, it is unclear how the method would behave with PEFT (R1, R4).
  - The paper claims to overcome the “hand‑crafted prompts” limitation of prior work, but does not demonstrate a prompt‑free pipeline. Prompt‑free is meaningful only for large models (≥7B) where NNs can implicitly understand the task, but the experiments stay with small models and/or use prompt templates (R1, R4).
- **Missing comparison with ZO-SGD.** Empirical work (Zhang et al., ICML 2024; Guo et al., arXiv 2024) suggests ZO-Adam is often not better than ZO-SGD; the paper should compare against ZO-SGD thoroughly (R4).
- **Batch size consistency unclear.** The paper directly cites results from other papers without verifying that all baselines use the same batch size, which can significantly affect gradient variance and fairness (R4).
- **Minor issues and questions.**
  - Figure color schemes are inconsistent between Fig. 2 and Fig. 3, causing confusion (R1).
  - The 8‑bit trick is applicable to any Adam‑based method; a 8‑bit MeZO-Adam baseline should be reported for a fair memory‑efficiency comparison (R1).
  - In Definition 2.1, the vector \(u\) appears both in the denominator and multiplied on the RHS; if \(u(i)\) is very large the gradient estimate could explode (R3).
  - The note about \(m_t, v_t\) construction not being computationally efficient is ambiguous and needs clarification (R3).
  - The paper should discuss differences from (Chen et al., 2019) and why their proof cannot be applied here (R4).
