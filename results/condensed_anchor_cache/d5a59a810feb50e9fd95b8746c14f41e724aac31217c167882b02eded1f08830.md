- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 1, 1, 5, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies universal approximation of continuous functions on \([0,1]^d\) by deep neural networks.  It improves the required width from \(O(d^2)\) (previous work by Shen et al. using the elementary universal activation function, EUAF) to \(O(d)\) by combining super‑expressive activation functions (Yarotsky) with EUAF.  The constructed network uses \(366d+365\) fixed intrinsic neurons (depth not specified in the paper).  The paper also claims that width \(O(d)\) is optimal, providing a lower bound of at least \(d\) width for a family of functions, but this is proved only for single‑layer networks.

### Strengths
- The improvement from required width \(O(d^2)\) to \(O(d)\) is significant (R2; also noted by R3).
- The paper makes the width dependency explicit rather than asymptotic, which is missing from many universal approximation results (R4).
- Including a sharpness test (even if contested) is good practice for a theoretical paper (R4).
- One reviewer (R3) found the proofs sound solid, the paper technically sound, and the construction simple to follow and the theorems easy to understand.  (Other reviewers strongly disagree on clarity and technical depth – see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
- **Incremental contribution / lack of novelty**: Multiple reviewers (R1, R2, R4) state the paper is essentially a straightforward assembly of existing results by Yarotsky and Shen et al., with little new technical innovation.  The originality is unclear, and the proofs are not self‑contained (R4).
- **Poor writing, organization, and proofreading**: Three reviewers (R1, R2, R4) report significant issues: many typos (e.g., “Therefore” misspelled, missing math mode, inconsistent notation), imprecise statements, unclear motivations, and poorly structured sections (e.g., duplicated section titles).  The introduction and related work are hard to follow.  One reviewer (R3) disagreed, calling the paper clearly written and easy to follow.
- **Unpractical / artificial activation functions**: The construction relies on a combination of super‑expressive activation functions (Yarotsky) and EUAF, which are non‑standard and not used in practice.  Even the positive reviewer (R3) notes this is artificial and suggests deriving results for a single fixed activation and more practical architectures.  Moreover, the construction is based on extending a continuous function on a Cantor set to \([0,1]\), which cannot be done stably in practice (R4).
- **Sharpness / lower bound is trivial or mismatched**:  
  * R1 argues the lower bound of width \(d\) is trivial because a network with fewer than \(d\) ridge functions depends on a \(k\)-dimensional projection.  
  * R4 points out that the sharpness is proven for a single‑layer network, whereas the main UAT result (Theorem 3) is for multi‑layer networks; hence sharpness is not actually shown.  The single‑layer lower bound is almost obvious since its level sets form a subspace of dimension \(<d\).  
  * R2 raises a specific counterexample: for \(d=2\), setting \(w_{11}=w_{12}=w\) and \(F_1=F_2=\sin\) leads to \(\sin(x)-\sin(x)=0\), contradicting the claimed lower bound proof.
- **Incomplete or missing details in theorems and proofs**:  
  * Theorem 4’s proof is only given for functions of the form \(\sum_i \sin(x_i)\); the general case is not proven (R2).  
  * The proof of Theorem 2 is only a sketch; a rigorous proof is needed (R2).  
  * The paper does not specify the depth of the network for each theorem (R4).  
  * The NN architecture is not mathematically formulated; it is unclear whether bias terms are used, whether the last layer has an activation function, and whether the activation function is fixed across neurons or varies (R4).  
  * The notation \(\ell^{-1}\) in Section 1.4 may not exist since \(\ell\) is only surjective (R2).  
  * Confusion over which previous result (Yarotsky or Shen) establishes \(N_1\) (R2).
- **Relevance to ICLR community**: R1 and R4 question the practical significance, as the constructed networks are hard to train, non‑standard, and purely mathematical curiosities.
- **Other specific issues**:  
  * The claim of “alleviating the curse of dimensionality” is misleading; the curse of dimensionality involves sampling difficulty, not just approximation (R4).  
  * The quantifiers in Theorem 3 regarding activation functions are unclear (R4).  
  * The condition \(d=2\) is not in math mode in the proof of Theorem 4 (R4).
