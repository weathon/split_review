## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Progressive Autoregressive Video Diffusion Models (PA-VDM), which fine-tunes existing video diffusion models (e.g., Open-Sora) for long video generation by assigning latent frames with progressively increasing noise levels during diffusion, rather than a single noise level. This enables autoregressive conditioning on less noisy previous frames, aiming to generate up to 1-minute (1440 frames at 24 FPS) videos without quality degradation. The method claims easy implementation on top of existing architectures with low additional inference cost.

### Strengths
- PA-VDM extends existing video diffusion models to generate longer videos (up to 1 minute, 1440 frames at 24 FPS) without quality degradation or abrupt scene changes, achieving state-of-the-art results on long video generation. (R1, R3)
- The progressive noise scheduling enables autoregressive generation with fine-grained conditioning, maintaining temporal consistency and smooth transitions (e.g., realistic motion dynamics, preservation of secondary motion attributes). (R1, R2, R3)
- The method can be easily implemented on top of pre-trained video diffusion models without changing architectures, by altering the noise scheduling. (R1, R2)
- The additional computational cost at inference time is not large. (R2)
- Qualitatively, the results are better than other naive baselines. (R4)
- The paper is generally well-written and easy to follow. (R4)

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty due to prior work**: The core idea of a progressive noise schedule across frames was previously proposed in *Rolling Diffusion Models* (ICML 2024), and also addressed in *Diffusion Forcing* (NeurIPS 2024) and *FIFO-Diffusion* (NeurIPS 2024). Reviewers 3 and 4 consider the contribution incremental, with the primary novelty being the extension to high-resolution videos and fine-tuning of existing models. Reviewers 1 and 2 scored 5, finding the method effective, but the split indicates a significant disagreement on novelty and impact. (R3, R4)
- **Missing baselines**: The paper should compare against:
  - A straightforward multi-task training baseline: training the same model on mixed conditions (e.g., 10% text-only, 10% text+1 frame, 10% text+2 frames, etc.) to isolate the benefit of the increasing-noise scheduler. (R1)
  - State-of-the-art video generation baselines such as VideoCrafter2, T2V-Turbo, and Open-Sora (the base model). (R2)
  - For the fine-tuning protocol, comparisons with models fine-tuned using the specific noise schedules from Rolling Diffusion, Diffusion Forcing, and FIFO-Diffusion are needed. (R4)
- **Insufficient evaluation metrics**: The paper relies on VBench but does not report Fréchet Video Distance (FVD), a widely recognized standard metric for temporal video prediction. Including FVD would improve credibility and comparability with prior work. (R3)
- **Superficial treatment of the method**: Section 3 (proposed method) is only about one page; it lacks rigorous or in-depth theoretical analysis. Section 3.3 reads more like an experimental subsection than methodological exposition. (R3)
- **Misinterpretation of related work**: The paper incorrectly claims VDM uses a "replacement"-based autoregressive generation method; VDM’s primary focus is improving temporal consistency via classifier guidance (or reconstruction guidance), with "replacement" only as an ablation study. This inaccuracy should be corrected. (R3)
- **No model complexity analysis**: The paper does not report #params or FLOPs. (R2)
- **Lack of quantitative comparison with alternative noise schedules**: Figure 2 only shows one schedule; the authors should explore different noise-increasing schedules and report their influence on performance. (R2) This also aligns with the request to compare against Rolling Diffusion, Diffusion Forcing, and FIFO-Diffusion noise schedules. (R4)
- **Dataset details insufficient**: The paper does not describe how the fine-tuning data was curated. Reviewers request full details (sources, preprocessing, etc.). (R4)
- **Missing experiments on model generality**: If the main contribution is a fine-tuning scheme, it should be validated on a variety of video diffusion models (different architectures, noise schedulers such as flow-based, EDM, DDPM) to show it is not specific to Open-Sora. (R4)
- **Unclear video sources**: The demonstration videos appear to continue from Sora-generated segments. The reviewers ask for examples generated entirely from Open-Sora or from the fine-tuned model itself. (R4)
