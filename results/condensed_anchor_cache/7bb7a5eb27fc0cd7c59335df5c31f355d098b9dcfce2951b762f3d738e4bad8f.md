## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper proposes Concise and Organized Perception (COP), a prompting method for LLMs in deductive reasoning. COP first creates concept maps highlighting hierarchical relationships among rules/facts, then generates a mind-map based on the question, prunes irrelevant parts, and reconstructs a concise context for chain-of-thought prompting. The method aims to reduce multi-hop reasoning difficulty, improve accuracy, and lower LLM query costs. Experiments on synthetic logical reasoning datasets (ProofWriter, PrOntoQA, PrOntoQA-OOD) show COP outperforms previous SoTA (e.g., LAMBADA, Selection-Inference) for GPT-3.5-Turbo and text-davinci-003.

### Strengths

- **Unique combination of concise and organized strategies**: Integrates mind-map generation and context reconstruction into LLM reasoning, a novel approach compared to prior stage-by-stage decomposition methods (R1). Focuses on distilling relevant knowledge to optimize the number of LLM queries, reducing both cost and error risk (R2).
- **Strong empirical results**: COP significantly outperforms established baselines (e.g., LAMBADA, ProofWriter, Chain-of-Thought) across ProofWriter, PrOntoQA, and PrOntoQA-OOD, especially on multi-hop reasoning problems (R1, R3, R4). Notable improvement on PrOntoQA-OOD with OrIntro rules (R4).
- **Efficiency gains**: COP uses fewer LLM calls than LAMBADA on average (R4) and can adapt to different LLMs (R3).
- **Complementarity to existing methods**: COP can be combined with backward chaining (e.g., LAMBADA+COP) to further boost performance (R1, R4).
- **Reduction of CoT errors**: Manual checks show COP relieves the problem of chain-of-thought predicting correct labels with incorrect reasoning chains (R3). The approach is also more efficient in inference calls (R3).
- **Well-organized and clear presentation**: Comprehensive experiments supporting claims, valuable insights for deductive reasoning (R3). [Note: Some reviewers (R2, R4) found clarity lacking—see Weaknesses.]

### Weaknesses

- **Clarity and methodological exposition**:
  - The methodology for concept graph generation, mind graph generation, and mind graph pruning is hard to follow without a consistent running example and the exact prompts (R2). The overall method is unclear (R4). Pseudo-code includes unnecessary "else – pass" statements (R4).
  - It is unclear what format the input takes after context reconstruction (still rules/pruned or retains mind-map structure) and how terms are unified (exact match?) (R4).

- **Narrow scope and limited generalizability**:
  - Evaluations only use synthetic logical reasoning datasets (ProofWriter, PrOntoQA). It is unclear whether COP can be adapted to real-world data (R3) or what takeaway there is for the broader ICLR audience (R4). Suggested real-world datasets: FOLIO, LogiQA, ReClor (R4).
  - The problem of having many irrelevant premises may be too specific to these synthetic datasets (R4); applicability to a wider set of reasoning tasks is not demonstrated.

- **Insufficient theoretical justification and analysis**:
  - The paper builds heavily on prior work (graph-based reasoning, structured knowledge integration, logical NNs, neuro-symbolic approaches) without clearly distinguishing the novelty or providing a rigorous theoretical framework for why the concise+organized combination is more effective (R1).
  - Lack of detailed error analysis and ablation studies; the improvement of the whole system remains a "mystery" (R1). Missing failure case analysis on each of the three stages (concept graph, mind graph, mind graph pruning) (R2).

- **Missing experimental details and comparisons**:
  - No error analysis; what are the main failure modes? Are they in mind-map construction, rule parsing, or reasoning after both steps? (R4). What exactly are "Incorrect Goal" and "Incorrect Sign" failure modes in Figure 4? (R4).
  - Why did LAMBADA perform so poorly with And rules? This needs discussion (R4).
  - Are the results zero-shot or few-shot? (R4). Why is LogicLM only evaluated in the 5-hop setting? (R4).
  - The paper compares token costs via LLM calls, but token count (which drives API costs) is more important; how does COP compare to LAMBADA in tokens used? (R4).
  - Related work: Scallop (an approach similar to Logic-LM with probabilistic reasoning engine) should be cited or discussed (R2).

- **External symbolic processing**: The method involves hand-written parsing of mind maps (e.g., partitioning by graph edges), so COP is not fully LLM-driven; it shares some characteristics with hybrid neuro-symbolic methods (R4), which the paper contrasts against without clear justification.

- **Clarifications needed**:
  - What "misleading information" (as opposed to irrelevant) is referred to in Section 3? In these problems all premises are true (R4).
  - Request for examples of proofs generated by CoT to show how COP concretely improves proof planning (R3).
