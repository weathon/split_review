## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces RNA-FrameFlow, the first generative model for de novo 3D RNA backbone design. It adapts SE(3) flow matching from proteins, handling RNA’s larger backbone (13 atoms per nucleotide) via an RNA-specific frame representation and auxiliary losses. To address limited 3D RNA data, it uses structural clustering and cropping augmentations. A self-consistency evaluation pipeline (inverse folding via gRNAde, forward folding via RhoFold) and local structural metrics are established. The best model generates backbones of 40–150 nucleotides with >40% validity (scTM ≥ 0.45).

### Strengths

- First generative model for RNA 3D backbone generation, pioneering an RNA frame representation and extending SE(3) flow matching from proteins.
- Comprehensive evaluation protocols: self-consistency pipeline (inverse folding + forward folding) and local structural metrics.
- Technical adaptation to RNA complexity: 13-atom backbone, torsion-based atom placement, and tailored auxiliary losses.
- Achieves >40% validity rate (scTM ≥ 0.45) and is more efficient than diffusion baselines (e.g., MMDiff).
- Clear writing and effective figures (e.g., frame transformations, Ramachandran-like angle plots).
- Transparent about current limitations and provides thoughtful analyses (e.g., impact of cropping augmentations).
- Successful design outcomes on common RNA types (tRNA, 5S rRNA).

### Weaknesses

1. **Self-consistency evaluation pipeline limitations.**  
   - gRNAde inverse folding biases: unknown handling of pseudoknots, non-canonical base pairs, and sequence motif preferences; these may distort self-consistency scores.  
   - Reliance on only RhoFold for forward folding; its length bias and lower accuracy on complex/flexible regions skew results. Suggested: include trRosettaRNA, AlphaFold3, or alternative evaluators.  
   - Cumulative errors from both inverse folding and prediction models limit reliability; no upper bound estimated (e.g., by running the same pipeline on test-set structures).  
   - scTM and pdbTM, being alignment-based, may not capture local variation or novel folds.

2. **Training data limitations and augmentation concerns.**  
   - The small pool of 3D RNA structures biases the model toward over-represented types (tRNA, 5S rRNA) and lengths, leading to memorization rather than generalization to novel folds.  
   - Cropping augmentation, while increasing diversity, introduces unrealistic subsequences; cut points disrupt long-range interactions, producing physically implausible conformations and reducing validity. Structural clustering and cropping are standard techniques (used in AlphaFold, SE(3) diffusion), not novel insights.  
   - Missing analysis: training distribution vs. TM-score distribution across lengths; whether cropping can be adjusted to avoid invalid folds.

3. **Biologically relevant evaluation is insufficient.**  
   - Diversity metric is meaningless: should assess structural novelty across RNA families (e.g., using RNA STRAND classification) rather than only tRNA/rRNA.  
   - Evaluation lacks tertiary interaction motifs: pseudoknots, base multiplets, and non-canonical pairings are not checked, though they are critical for RNA function.

4. **Missing detailed results and ablations.**  
   - No analysis of generation process: effect of number of sampling timesteps on validity, diversity, and novelty; convergence behavior.  
   - No ablation study isolating the contribution of each auxiliary loss term (e.g., clash, bond length, angle losses) to final performance.  
   - EMD values (Table 1) lack scale reference: a 50/50 split of the training set should be included to assess magnitude and sensitivity.  
   - Only validity at scTM ≥ 0.45 is reported; average scTM over all samples is not provided.

5. **Incomplete structural validation.**  
   - Clashes in generated backbones are not quantified: no reporting of average proportion of nucleotides with clashes, types, or locations.  
   - scTM metric evaluates only C4′ self-consistency; rotational and angular accuracy of all backbone atoms (especially C3′, O4′) is not assessed. RMSD between generated and folded structures should be computed.

6. **Methodological novelty and frame representation (minority reviewer – R4, rating 5).**  
   - One reviewer considers the core contribution (applying FrameFlow to RNA) a combination of existing methods (protein FrameFlow, RoseTTAFoldNA’s RNA frames) rather than a significant innovation; data augmentation techniques are commonplace.  
   - Choice of frame atoms (C3′-C4′-O4′) over RoseTTAFoldNA’s P–OP1–OP2 lacks compelling justification or experimental comparison. Although the paper states it is more centrally located, no evidence is given that this improves performance, and neural networks likely can adapt to either offset.  
   - (Minor note) RNA structure prediction accuracy is lower than for proteins; alternative validation (e.g., MD energy estimation) could strengthen results.

7. **Open questions from reviewers**  
   - Could explicit modeling of base pairing/stacking (e.g., hydrogen bonds) improve structural realism?  
   - Would alternative augmentations (beyond cropping) better preserve global fold while increasing diversity?  
   - What are the rigid assumptions in frame atom selection, and how do they impact results?
