## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates whether separating highly conflicting objectives into different optimization levels is more effective than keeping them unified for multilingual multi-task ASR and speech-to-text translation (S2TT). It proposes three Multi-Objective Optimization (MOO) frameworks—VS-ASR, VC-ASR, and VM-ASR—that incorporate multi-level optimization (MLO). Experiments on LibriSpeech, AISHELL v1, and CoVoST v2 datasets show consistent improvements over baselines. Key findings include the importance of optimization order and penalty parameters. One reviewer (score 10) rated the work very highly for novelty and documentation; others (scores 5, 5, 6) were more moderate, citing missing baselines, weak SOTA comparisons, and reproducibility gaps.

### Strengths
1. **First exploration of MOO at different optimization levels for ASR and S2TT across multiple languages**, providing a novel approach.
2. **Directly addresses conflicting objectives using conflict-avoidant update directions**, overcoming limitations of static weighting and constrained optimization.
3. **Comprehensive exploration of three MOO techniques** (VS-ASR, VC-ASR, VM-ASR) with thorough ablation studies on task ordering, penalty parameters, and model sizes (58M and 100M Conformer) across both task-based and language-based MLO settings.
4. **Consistent and significant performance gains over non-MOO baselines** (PT+FT, static weighting) for both ASR and S2TT across multiple languages.
5. **Clear documentation of experimental setup and accessible code** for reproducibility. Memory consumption and training time are also discussed.
6. **Provides reasoning for why optimization order matters** (e.g., UAS sequence superior for ASR, USA for S2TT) and demonstrates the importance of choosing the right penalty parameter in MLO training.
7. **A minority reviewer (score 10) particularly praised** the novelty, well-documented experiments, and the detailed reasoning for optimization order and penalty parameter selection.

### Weaknesses
1. **Baseline WER/BLEU on CoVoST 2 significantly weaker than SOTA.** For a 58M Conformer, the authors report 29.7% WER on English vs. ~23% in the official Fairseq recipe, and Fr→En BLEU of 23.4 vs. 27. This gap requires clarification or justification. *(Reviewer 2)*
2. **Insufficient scope: only two tasks (ASR and S2TT) and two model sizes (58M, 100M Conformer).** Unclear if findings generalize to more tasks or diverse architectures. *(Reviewers 1, 4)*
3. **Missing simpler non-MOO counterparts for each proposed method:**
   - For VC-ASR: a baseline with pretraining followed by finetuning with regularization on the pretraining loss (e.g., KL divergence or L2-based continual learning).
   - For VM-ASR: a baseline that freezes the backbone after pretraining and sequentially finetunes task-specific heads.
   These would isolate whether the MOO framework itself drives improvements. *(Reviewer 4)*
4. **Inadequate motivation for optimization levels and key components:**
   - The intuition behind separating supervised/unsupervised/task-specific/language-specific losses into different optimization levels is insufficiently motivated. *(Reviewer 2)*
   - The claim that the self-supervised loss in VC-ASR has desirable flatness properties is unsupported: Appendix C lacks discussion on loss curvature and how flatness aids generalization across tasks. *(Reviewer 2)*
5. **Missing experimental details for reproducibility:**
   - Learning rates for pre-training and fine-tuning in the Joint PT+FT baseline not specified.
   - Acoustic features (raw audio, MFCCs, log-mel), token vocabulary size per language, and use of data augmentation (e.g., SpecAugment) not reported.
   - LibriSpeech/AISHELL experiment (Section 5.6, Table 5): test-other numbers omitted; AISHELL metrics likely CER, not WER (caption should be fixed); Joint PT+FT baseline missing. *(Reviewer 2)*
6. **Resource efficiency concerns:** Proposed methods are more computationally demanding; fair comparison should include memory- and speed-matched baselines. *(Reviewer 4)* One reviewer asked whether training time or GPU memory could be improved. *(Reviewer 3)*
7. **Organizational and writing issues:**
   - Important conclusions (e.g., Section 5.2) are relegated to appendices, disrupting flow. *(Reviewer 1)*
   - Abbreviations (FT, VC-ASR) used without proper introduction; a glossary would help. *(Reviewer 1)*
   - "Multitask ASR" is confusing since S2TT is not ASR; "multitask speech-to-text" is more accurate. Equations in Sections 4.1–4.3 are difficult to parse; simplification recommended. Section 5 discussion repeats many numbers from tables unnecessarily. *(Reviewer 4)*
   - Related work: Phrase "multi-task learning for joint ASR-S2TT has seen limited exploration" is inaccurate given cited papers and Whisper (Radford et al. 2022), which should be cited. *(Reviewer 4)*
8. **Unaddressed questions that overlap with criticisms:**
   - How does segregating highly conflicting tasks (ASR and S2TT) into different optimization levels reduce overall gradient conflict? *(Reviewer 1)*
   - Why do en, fr, and de have higher WER despite more training data hours in CoVoST2? *(Reviewer 1)*
   - What does "Pareto front tends to be large and spread out" mean in this context, and what are the implications? *(Reviewer 1)*
   - In Tables 1/2, UAS (VM-ASR) does better for ASR and USA (VM-ASR) does better for S2TT. Why does the task right after the unsupervised objective benefit more? Similarly in Table 5, UEC helps En ASR more, UCE helps Zh ASR more. *(Reviewer 2)*
   - Saif et al. 2024 is most related; it should be explained in more detail (Related Work or Section 3.2) and explicitly distinguished from VS-ASR/VC-ASR. *(Reviewer 2)*
9. **Need for theoretical analysis** to understand capabilities and limitations of the proposed methods (noted as future work). *(Reviewers 1, 3)*
10. **Minor issues:** Code needs cleaning for readability. *(Reviewer 1)* Typo on line 185: should read `l_s(θ, φ_t^n)` not `φ_t^N`. *(Reviewer 2)*
