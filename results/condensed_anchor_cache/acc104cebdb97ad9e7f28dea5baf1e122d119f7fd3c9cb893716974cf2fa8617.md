## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper investigates adversarial attacks as protection against diffusion-based image editing/mimicry. It reveals a critical oversight in prior work: all existing protection methods target latent diffusion models (LDMs), ignoring pixel-space diffusion models (PDMs). Through extensive experiments, the authors demonstrate that attacks effective on LDMs fail on PDMs, and even adaptive attacks designed for PDMs are ineffective. They attribute LDM vulnerability to the VAE encoder and conclude that diffusion models themselves are robust. Building on this, they propose PDM-Pure, which uses pre-trained PDMs (via SDEdit) to purify adversarial perturbations from protected images, effectively bypassing protection methods like Glaze, Mist, and Photoguard. The paper is well-motivated and presents fresh, challenging findings against the common belief that diffusion models are highly susceptible to adversarial attacks.

### Strengths

- **Novel contribution**: The paper identifies and fills a significant gap by focusing on PDMs, which have been largely neglected in prior adversarial attack research on diffusion models.
- **Robust experimental validation**: Comprehensive experiments across various architectures (PDMs, LDMs) and datasets demonstrate the superior empirical robustness of PDMs relative to LDMs, using multiple metrics (FID and others reported in Table 2).
- **Evaluation of adaptive attacks**: The authors conduct adaptive attacks, including EOT-based and the recent attack by Shih et al. (2024), which is encouraged and strengthens the claim.
- **Practical implications**: The PDM-Pure framework offers a simple, effective, and clearly explained off-the-shelf purifier that can remove adversarial patterns from protected images, with potential impact on IP protection for digital media.
- **Clear presentation**: The paper is generally well-structured and its findings are accessible; it acknowledges the trade-off between purification effectiveness and image quality.
- **Experiments on state-of-the-art protection methods**: Evaluations on Photoguard, Glaze, and Mist indicate these adversarial-perturbation-based tools cannot consistently prevent generative misuse.

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient acknowledgment of prior work**: The paper’s contributions overlap significantly with existing research on adversarial robustness of diffusion models and diffusion-based purification (e.g., DiffPure by Nie et al., 2022, and GrIDPure). This prior work should be prominently acknowledged and compared to clarify the paper’s uniqueness and value.
- **Incomplete quantitative evaluation for PDM-Pure**: Only FID is reported for the purification results. Since the paper uses three additional metrics in Table 2 for attack evaluation (e.g., LPIPS, CLIP score, etc.), these should also be included for PDM-Pure to provide a complete picture.
- **Weak adaptive attack evaluation**: The experiments in Figures 3 and 4 appear to be rudimentary case studies rather than comprehensive quantitative tests. The claim that PDMs are robust even to adaptive attacks is not convincingly supported. Specific concerns:
  - The perturbation budgets reported for Shih et al.'s attack (ℓ∞ > 150/255) are so large that any image becomes nearly uniform gray (e.g., (127,127,127)), which is not a realistic threat model. This suggests the attack may not have been correctly adapted or applied.
  - The experimental setup for Figures 3 and 4 needs clarification (e.g., whether SDEdit is used to noise and denoise protected images). At first glance, the images appear only restored, not edited, which may confuse the evaluation.
- **Lack of deep explanation for PDM robustness**: It remains unclear why PDMs, which are still neural networks, demonstrate such strong empirical robustness. The paper should provide a more detailed analysis or hypothesis, and discuss whether this robustness could extend to zero-shot classification (e.g., as in Chen et al. ICML 2024 and NeurIPS 2024 suggesting diffusion-based robust classifiers). This would enhance reliability of the findings.
- **Limited scope of tested attacks**: The evaluation primarily focuses on gradient-based pixel-space perturbations. It does not explore semantic adversarial attacks (e.g., those generated by diffusion models themselves) or attacks that manipulate the sampling trajectory, which could reveal vulnerabilities not exposed by current methods.
- **Insufficient explanation of PDM-Pure mechanism**: The rationale for why PDMs remove adversarial patterns is not fully justified (just “should be removed” in line 362). Also, the claim that “LDMs cannot be used to purify protected images” (line 465) is an overstatement; prior work (e.g., using Stable Diffusion) has successfully attacked Glaze via purification.
- **Redundancy in exposition**: The paper spends excessive space (Section 4 and 6.2) explaining that LDM attacks are less effective on PDMs, which is fairly intuitive due to different optimization spaces. More focus should be placed on demonstrating the ineffectiveness of adaptive attacks and why PDM-Pure succeeds.
- **Presentation and formatting issues**: Numerous typos, grammar errors, and formatting inconsistencies detract from quality (e.g., extra space before comma in lines 15/436; redefined “PDM” multiple times; typos in lines 266/267/281/295/469/471). These need correction.
- **Lack of discussion on limitations**: The paper does not address the computational cost of PDM-Pure for real-time applications, potential attacks specifically designed to bypass purification, and scalability issues (PDMs may not scale as well as LDMs for large-scale purification).
- **Missing code release**: The authors are asked to provide code to facilitate reproducibility (Reviewer 3 request).
