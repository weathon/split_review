## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces a signal sampling theory for graphons—a type of graph limit—proving a Poincaré inequality for graphon signals and showing that complements of node subsets satisfying this inequality are unique sampling sets for Paley-Wiener spaces of graphon signals. The authors leverage connections with spectral clustering and Gaussian elimination to prove consistency: unique sampling sets on a convergent graph sequence converge to those on the graphon. They propose a graphon signal sampling algorithm for large graphs and test it empirically on node classification and eigenvalue preservation. The work generalises the notion of a finite sampling set for graphs to a measurable sampling set for graphons and connects the theory to large-graph applications.

### Strengths
- **Novelty and significance**: The approach is original, connecting graph limit theory (graphons) to signal sampling on large graphs, which is both interesting and timely for graph machine learning.
- **Solid theoretical foundations**: The Poincaré inequality for graphon signals and the characterisation of unique sampling sets are rigorously proven, and the convergence results are sufficiently deep for publication.
- **Multiple solution approaches**: The paper shows two different routes to the same sampling problem (via spectral clustering and Gaussian elimination), enriching the discussion.
- **Connection to applications**: Section 5 makes a concrete link between graphon uniqueness sets and large-graph sampling. The paper also provides experimental verifications on node classification and eigenvalue preservation.
- **Clear and logical presentation**: (Reviewer 2) The paper reads very well; background, theory, and results are presented clearly and logically. (Reviewer 4) The technical claims are sound (proofs not checked in detail).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing computational efficiency comparisons**: The paper does not compare its algorithm’s time efficiency with existing graph sampling methods, nor does it discuss how the algorithm scales with graph size (Reviewer 1, Q1; also a missing experiment).  
- **Lack of robustness analysis for imperfect bandlimitedness**: The work does not analyse stability when signals are only approximately bandlimited w.r.t. the cutoff frequency. Real-world signals are rarely perfectly bandlimited; the paper does not address how reconstruction degrades with out-of-band components, nor how the size \(q\) of the uniqueness set might affect stability (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear justification for bandlimitedness in experiments**: It is not obvious that the signals in the datasets used for Section 6 are bandlimited; the authors provide no evidence or argument that the uniqueness sets yield stable representations in those tasks (Reviewer 2, Q2).
- **Ambiguity in Theorem 2**: The theorem refers to \(X\in L^2(S)\) but applies operator \(L\) (presumably the scaled normalised Laplacian of \(\Gamma(S)\)). It is unclear how \(L\) acts on signals defined only on \(L^2(S)\); the relationship between \(L^2(S)\) and \(L^2(D)\) needs clarification (Reviewer 2, Q1).
- **Theory–application gap**: The algorithms and theorems feel more motivated by graphon theory than by concrete application needs. The heavy definitional work can be overwhelming, and it is not clear whether the theorems in Section 4 imply a direct bound on the quality of the algorithm for node classification (Reviewer 3, Q1).
- **Dense presentation and lack of motivation for a general ML audience**: The paper is presented in a very dense manner. For instance, many readers may not be familiar with the Poincaré inequality or why it is relevant; more background would make the work accessible to a broader audience (Reviewer 4).  
- **Missing classical references and a limitation of graphons**: The paper does not cite the original Aldous–Hoover theorem, which is a canonical motivation of graphons from a latent-variable perspective (Reviewer 4). Additionally, many real-world graphs are sparse, yet graphon models are naturally motivated for dense graphs; this limitation is not acknowledged (Reviewer 4).
- **Minority reviewer (score 6)**: This reviewer finds the paper innovative but stresses the lack of scalability/ efficiency comparison as a major weakness.

### Reviewer confidence and disagreement
- Reviewer 1 (score 6, confidence 3) is more critical, particularly on the missing computational benchmarks.  
- Reviewers 2, 4 (score 8, confidence 4) and Reviewer 3 (score 8, confidence 2) are highly positive overall but note the robustness and presentation weaknesses. Their confidence varies, with Reviewer 3 acknowledging limited familiarity with the details.
