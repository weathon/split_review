## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes ODEdit, an unsupervised blind face restoration method that uses a pretrained diffusion model as an ODE solver for image inversion. It aims to eliminate the need for fine-tuning or handcrafted loss functions. The method is evaluated on synthetic degradations and shows advantages in some settings (e.g., severe noise). However, the presentation is unclear, the base method produces blurry outputs, and the experimental evaluation is limited and lacks essential comparisons and details.

### Strengths

- The method is simple in concept.
- When combined with another prior (CodeFormer, GFPGAN), ODEdit produces results that outperform compared methods.
- High FID score is achieved (Table 1).
- The model is robust to strong noise (Figure 3).
- Leveraging the generative capabilities of a diffusion model to achieve high image quality.
- The motivation (addressing faithful and realistic reconstruction) is clear.
- The related works section is well-structured.
- The paper claims to be the first unsupervised approach to use ODE-based inversion in diffusion models for image restoration, which is innovative.
- The method demonstrates advantages over existing methods when the degradation is severe.
- Reviewers 2 and 4 (both score 5) are overall more positive about the performance, especially under severe noise; reviewers 1 and 3 (score 3) are more critical of the clarity and completeness.

### Weaknesses

- **Writing and presentation are poor and impede judgment.** The paper is cumbersome to follow; motivation is hand-wavy, background is imbalanced (irrelevant material included, relevant exposition of SDEdit missing), and the core algorithm (Algorithm 1) is not clearly explained (e.g., what ODE_SOLVER(.) means, how equations are derived). (R1, R3, R4)
- **Base method alone produces blurry reconstructions.** The best results are obtained by plugging ODEdit with another prior (CodeFormer, GFPGAN), making it unclear where the actual gain comes from. (R1)
- **Identity leakage and lack of true restoration.** The method often generates a plausible face from the corrupted reference rather than faithfully restoring the original identity. (R2)
- **Low reconstruction fidelity.** PSNR and SSIM are worse than in previous works. (R2)
- **Evaluation is too narrow.** Only a limited set of degradations (from Hendrycks & Dietterich 2019) is tested; missing exploration of varying levels of blur, compression artifacts, different types of noise, different facial poses, and diverse ethnicities/age groups. (R2, R3)
- **Experiments lack depth and analysis.** The synergy study (Section 4.4) presents only figures without discussion; the ablation study does not isolate individual components or vary parameters systematically. (R3)
- **Missing experimental details.** The testing dataset is not described; the definition of LDM is absent; the degradation process is not provided; implementation details are insufficient. (R3, R4)
- **Unfair comparisons.** The synthetic degradations used (Hendrycks & Dietterich 2019) are not those commonly employed by prior works like CodeFormer, making the comparison unfair. The paper should also compare on real-world degraded face images. (R4)
- **No face identity metric.** Face fidelity should be evaluated with face recognition metrics (e.g., cosine similarity or Euclidean distance with a pre-trained FaceNet model) to directly measure identity preservation. (R3)
- **Advantage of replacing SDE with ODE is not justified.** Why should injection of extra noise be circumvented? Are there disadvantages to using ODE? (R4)
- **Limited applicability.** ODEdit is demonstrated only for face restoration; SDEdit solves many tasks—the reason for this restriction is not explained. (R4)
- **Algorithm derivation unclear.** Algorithm 1 is not derived step by step; the meaning of ODE_SOLVER and its relation to DDIM Eq(9) is ambiguous. (R1, R4)
- **Missing comparison on real-world images.** Performance on real-world blind face restoration is not shown. (R3, R4)
