## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes X²-DFD, a framework that uses Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for explainable deepfake detection. It consists of three modules: Model Feature Assessment (MFA) to rank forgery features, Strong Feature Strengthening (SFS) to fine-tune the MLLM on data built from top-ranked features, and Weak Feature Supplementing (WFS) to integrate external dedicated detectors (EDDs) for low-ranked features. The authors claim this is the first systematic assessment of MLLM capabilities for deepfake detection. Cross-dataset performance improves over pretrained MLLMs, but reviewers raise concerns about explainability evaluation, novelty, reliability of MLLM-generated data, insufficient ablation, and suboptimal performance on unseen attacks. Reviewer scores are 5, 6, 8, 3, with strong disagreement: R3 (score 8) finds the approach valuable and novel, while R4 (score 3) sees limited technical contribution and significant weaknesses.

### Strengths
- The paper is the first to systematically assess the inherent capabilities of MLLMs specifically in deepfake detection, identifying varying discriminative power across different forgery features (R1, R3). This is a novel and valuable contribution to MLLM-based detection (R2, R3).
- Proposes a clear, well-structured framework with three modules (MFA, SFS, WFS) to enhance MLLM detection and explainability (R1, R2, R3, R4). The integration of external dedicated detectors (EDDs) to fill MLLM limitations is reasonable (R3).
- Recognition performance improves, especially in cross-dataset scenarios (R2). Cross-dataset results are good (R4). Experiments are thorough with different protocols and comprehensive comparisons (R1, R2). The paper is well-organized and easy to understand (R2).
- The paper points out limitations of pretrained MLLMs and introduces a pipeline for constructing datasets based on more effective forgery features (R1).

### Weaknesses
1. **Explainability evaluation is lacking and questionable (R1, R3, R4).**  
   - No quantitative metric for explainability is provided; only a single qualitative example in the appendix (R1). A human study is mentioned but lacks details on methodology, statistical analysis, and sample size; human subjects may not have real knowledge of how fake data was created (R3).  
   - Some explanations (e.g., “unusual layout and unnatural skin tone” in Figure 1) are oversimplified and less informative than traditional methods like Grad-CAM; it is unclear if this is a common issue (R4).  
   - MLLMs are not inherently transparent; it is uncertain whether the generated text constitutes “real” explainability or merely describes why an image might be fake (R3).

2. **Reliability and validity of MLLM-generated data for training and evaluation (R1, R2, R3).**  
   - The fine-tuning dataset is constructed using MLLM-generated questions and answers without human verification. This risks the MLLM learning its own biases rather than genuine forgery features (R1).  
   - Using MLLM-generated data to then evaluate the same MLLM creates a circular logic that may not generalize to real-world scenarios (R1).  
   - Pretrained LLMs lack fundamental knowledge about true/false classification for deepfakes; ranking forgery features based on relevance generated by such models may not be meaningful—random selection of other features might produce similar effects (R2).  
   - The forgery feature list is generated by GPT-4o; different LLMs could produce different lists, making the commonality and generalizability of these features uncertain (R2).

3. **Generalizability to other MLLMs and configurations is unexplored (R2, R4).**  
   - Only LLaVA and GPT-4 are tested; results may not hold for other MLLMs (e.g., LLaVA-13B/7B, GPT-3). Sensitivity to different parameter configurations and base models is not examined (R4). The paper does not discuss how conclusions transfer to other MLLMs (R2).

4. **Cross-dataset performance is suboptimal on certain unseen attacks (R2, R4).**  
   - In Table 2, the proposed method is inferior to comparison methods on FSGAN and inswap generation models; a deeper theoretical analysis is needed (R2).  
   - Given the large parameter count and pretrained data, the method does not achieve SOTA on unseen attacks, limiting real-world application (R4).

5. **Ablation study is insufficient and unclear (R1, R2, R4).**  
   - The ablation settings in Table 3 are confusing; the abbreviation GCS is undefined (R2).  
   - Missing ablations: what happens if WFS is applied to strong features or SFS to weak features? Or if both use the same module? (R4).  
   - Only one metric is used for feature selection and evaluation; additional metrics (accuracy, NLP interpretability) are lacking (R4).  
   - The minimal performance difference between the EDD-only and final models in Table 3 suggests limited gains from the full framework (R4).  
   - No in-domain results on FF++ are provided (R2).

6. **Reliance on the blending score introduces vulnerability (R1).**  
   - The blending score (from Sec. 3.3) is used for feature ranking and dataset construction. If this score is wrong (e.g., a real face with low blending, or a fake face not involving blending), the entire SFS and WFS process may be misguided, leading to spurious correlations rather than genuine forgery features. This undermines the claim of improving intrinsic explainability.

7. **Use of prediction probabilities as text input to LLMs is questionable (R2).**  
   - Directly feeding the prediction probabilities of a smaller model as text into the LLM seems unreasonable because LLMs lack numerical reasoning capabilities.

8. **Novelty concerns and over-claiming (R4).**  
   - The idea of using an external model to support LLMs is not novel.  
   - The paper ignores prior work using LLMs for deepfake detection and over-claims contributions (Lines 100-103); the summary in Lines 067-075 misrepresents previous work [r1] by claiming it “fail[s] to provide intuitive and convincing explanations” (R4).  
   - R3 also notes that the “extendable” aspect of the framework is not clearly explained.

9. **External expert model details and baselines are insufficient (R4).**  
   - Training details of the external dedicated detector (EDD) are not provided.  
   - Using an untrained MLLM as a baseline is unreasonable.  
   - The performance impact of a subpar reasoning engine is not discussed.

10. **Missing experiments on low-quality or post-processed images (R4).**  
    - No evaluation on low-quality or post-processed images is conducted, despite their importance in real-world scenarios.

11. **Writing and presentation issues (R1, R4).**  
    - Figure 2 is too small; numbers are barely visible (R1).  
    - Undefined abbreviations: WCS (Sec. 4.2.3, Figure 5) and GCS (Tab. 3) (R1).  
    - Citation inconsistencies (L136 missing, ArXiv references in inconsistent format) (R4).  
    - Missing percentage symbols in metrics (L175-180) (R4).  
    - Metric explanations take excessive space (R4).  
    - Table 4 lacks clarity on which metrics are presented (R4).

12. **Performance of baselines is near-chance on some metrics (R1).**  
    - At Line 176, LLaVA achieves 63.7% and XceptionNet 75.8% on certain datasets; both are almost equally incapable, making analysis of their differences less meaningful (R1).
