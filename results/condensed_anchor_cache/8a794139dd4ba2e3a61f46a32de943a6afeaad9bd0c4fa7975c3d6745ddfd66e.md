- Decision: Accept
- Scores: 8, 1, 6, 8

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proves a formal necessary connection between robust generalization under distributional shifts and learning causal models. Specifically, any agent that achieves bounded regret under a large set of distributional shifts (modelled as interventions on a causal Bayesian network) must have learned an approximate causal model of the environment; for optimal agents this model converges to the true causal graph. The result is argued to have implications for transfer learning, causal inference, and safe AI. Reviewers rated the paper 8, 1, 6, 8, with strong disagreement: most praised the theoretical contribution and significance, while one reviewer (rating 1) found the introduction unclear despite praising the substance; another (rating 6) noted limitations in empirical validation and scope.

### Strengths
- Original and significant theoretical contribution: formally establishes that robust adaptation under distribution shifts implies learning a causal model, going beyond the good regulator theorem.
- Rigorous proofs with clearly stated and well-motivated assumptions; the analysis relaxes the optimality assumption meaningfully.
- Writing is clear, well-structured, and accessible for the technical content.
- Implications for safety and robustness under distribution shifts are important.
- The proof is non-trivial and provides a stepping stone to richer settings (e.g., mediated decision tasks).
- Simple and clear theoretical analysis with broad, powerful implications (one reviewer described it as “a gem”).
- Demonstrates a limitation transfer: limitations in causal structure learning imply limitations in robust decision making.
- Provides an example of inferring causal structure when only one variable is observed under each intervention.
- The problem considered is fundamental; the central idea is described as “cute and clean”.

### Weaknesses
- No empirical validation: even a simple simulation based on the informal overview (Appendix C) would strengthen the paper. Lack of experiments was noted by multiple reviewers.
- Scope is limited to unmediated decision tasks; extending to broader RL settings would increase applicability (acknowledged as a significantly harder challenge).
- The proof is challenging to understand; a more informal/simplified sketch in the main text (beyond Appendix C) is needed. Several reviewers explicitly asked for a simple proof sketch and per-theorem plain-language implications.
- Implications of the assumptions are not discussed. For example, what Assumption 2 implies about the existence of distribution shifts where optimal policies differ should be made explicit.
- Notation and definition issues in the main text: the random variable C is used heavily but not introduced there; the meaning of squares, circles, and diamonds in the CID figure is not explained in the main text; Assumption 1’s condition Desc_D ∩ Anc_U = ∅ excludes trivial settings but this is only mentioned in the appendix – the main text should comment on that.
- Typos and unclear phrasing: “used to derive out results” (missing “our”), “By cCreftheorem: main,theorem: main approx agents” (corrupted cross-reference), and other spelling mistakes.
- The statement of the result in the introduction felt “slippery” (Reviewer 2). Key terms (“distributional shifts”, “distributionally shifted environments”, “target domains”, “causal modelling and transfer learning”) should be defined earlier. The clarification that shifts are modelled as interventions appears only later.
- Only a necessary condition is proved; a sufficient condition (if the agent learns an approximate causal model, it can efficiently learn optimal decisions under shift) is missing and would strengthen the result. This was raised by a reviewer.
- The required assumption of low regret for *all* mixture of local interventions (hard and randomized) is very strong and may not be realistic for many transfer learning tasks, which typically involve interventions on only a subset of variables.
- Minor technical corrections: Definition 6 in the appendix likely needs a subscript on the policy (π_σ) and the condition δ ≥ 0 should be stated; some notation in the proofs is unclear.
- Reviewer disagreement: One reviewer gave a rating of 1 despite praising the work’s content, citing only the unclear introduction and typos; this contrast highlights that the presentation and first section may require significant revision to satisfy all readers.
