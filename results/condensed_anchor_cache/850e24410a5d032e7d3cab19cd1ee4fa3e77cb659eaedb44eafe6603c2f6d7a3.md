## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents NUNO, an offline model-based RL method using neural stochastic differential equations (SDEs). The drift term incorporates physics knowledge as inductive bias, and the diffusion term provides distance-aware uncertainty estimates combining aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties. Rollouts are penalized and adaptively truncated based on uncertainty to mitigate model exploitation. Empirical results on D4RL and NeoRL MuJoCo benchmarks show strong performance on low-quality datasets and competitive results on high-quality datasets. Reviewer 2 finds the problem relevant and the method well-explained with extensive simulations; Reviewers 1 and 3 also praise the novelty and clear presentation.

### Strengths
- **Problem relevance and significance**: Addressing offline RL in low-coverage and low-quality data regimes is a practically important challenge (all reviewers). Reviewer 2 highlights the relevance for real-world applications.
- **Novelty of the approach**: The use of neural SDEs to separate aleatoric and epistemic uncertainties, and the design of the distance-aware uncertainty estimator with desirable mathematical properties (monotonic, bounded, no expensive search) are interesting contributions (R1, R2, R3). Reviewer 2 particularly highlights the distance-aware estimator as interesting and well-founded; reviewers 1 and 3 note the solid theoretic foundation.
- **Experimental results and thoroughness**: The method achieves strong results, especially on low-quality datasets where it outperforms state-of-the-art by up to 93%, and matches or surpasses them on high-quality counterparts (R1, R3). Reviewer 2 praises the extensive simulations, hyperparameter searches, and comparisons with baselines.
- **Presentation quality**: The paper is well-written, logically structured, and easy to follow, with a thorough literature review (R1, R2, R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of ablation studies and toy experiments**: The method has multiple components (physics prior, aleatoric/epistemic decomposition, distance-aware estimator, penalization/truncation), but no ablation isolates their contributions. All reviewers request simplified experiments (e.g., with known ground truth) to validate design choices. Specific missing analyses:
  - Ablating uncertainty components: only aleatoric vs. only distance-aware (R1).
  - Testing alternative uncertainty surrogates: prediction error, visitation counts, or other distance metrics (R2, Q4).
  - Quantifying the effect of the uncertainty estimator and SDE model design in simplified MDPs (R3, also R2, W3).
- **Uncertainty concept underdeveloped**: It is unclear what “distance-aware uncertainty” represents. Reviewer 2 questions whether a high-distance point that is actually stable (low true epistemic uncertainty) would be misclassified due to the monotonic distance estimator (R2, Q2). Also, only the distance-aware (epistemic) uncertainty is used to penalize rollouts in Equation 5; aleatoric uncertainty is not penalized, so highly noisy but well-covered trajectories avoid penalization (R2, Q3). Reviewer 1 echoes the need to understand the interaction between the two uncertainty terms.
- **Scalability and domain limitations**: Experiments are limited to low-dimensional MuJoCo environments; high-dimensional or partially observable/image-based domains are not tested. Scalability of the SDE model and the computational overhead from sampling around each data point for the uncertainty estimator is unclear (R1, R3). Reviewer 1 notes that ensemble-based methods might perform better in higher dimensions.
- **Generalizability of physics prior**: The method assumes decomposing states into position and velocity and relies on rigid-body dynamics (specific parameterization in Equation 8). Reviewers question how to apply this physics-informed approach to environments without such structure, and whether it is more than a convenient state parameterization (R2, Q1; R3).
- **Lack of analysis of inner workings**: While results are promising, there is no investigation into why the method succeeds (e.g., how uncertainty estimates behave in practice). All three reviewers suggest using synthetic environments with ground-truth uncertainty to better understand the method (R2, W2, W3; R1; R3).
- **Specific technical issues and missing clarifications**:
  - Equation 8 contains a typo (variable naming); uncertainty penalization in Figure 1 does not match the main text equation; notation inconsistencies (R2, Q5).
  - Equation 3: how is the denominator μ guaranteed nonzero? (R3).
  - Equation 6: what is the intuition of the log(det(Σ)) term in ℒ_data? (R3).
  - Lines 342–343: provide concrete examples of “unmodeled dynamics” and justify whether penalizing the residual is still beneficial when such dynamics are significant (R3).
- **Claim alignment**: Some main contributions do not accurately reflect the content; the focus could be shifted to better represent the method’s key aspects (R2, W1).

*Note: While Reviewer 2 is generally more positive (score 8) and emphasizes the distance-aware estimator and extensive simulations, they also raise significant concerns about the underdeveloped uncertainty concept and the need for more principled analysis. Reviewers 1 and 3 (score 6) share similar concerns about ablations and scalability.*
