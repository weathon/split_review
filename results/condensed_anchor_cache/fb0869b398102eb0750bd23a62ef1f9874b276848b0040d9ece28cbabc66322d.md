## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces the concept of homologous surrogates to relax the common pure-child assumption in causal discovery with latent variables. It formulates two assumptions (weaker and stronger) that enable partial recovery (ancestor identification) or full recovery (parent identification) of the causal graph. An algorithm leveraging these properties is proposed, and experiments on synthetic data validate effectiveness, though real-world performance is limited. The work is seen as tackling a significant problem, but reviewers raise concerns about presentation clarity, technical contribution (with one reviewer questioning whether homologous surrogates are fundamentally different from pure children), overlooked related work, high computational complexity, small-scale simulations, and missing examples for theorems.

### Strengths
- Addresses a significant and challenging problem: causal discovery with latent variables (R1, R3, R4).
- Introduces the novel concept of homologous surrogates, which relaxes the restrictive pure-child assumption and allows multiple parents for observed children (R1, R2, R3, R4). One reviewer (R1) notes that this concept is “fundamentally equivalent to the notion of pure children,” representing a disagreement with the other reviewers who view it as a distinct contribution.
- Theoretical results are clearly stated with well-defined definitions and complete proofs (R2, R4). Figures are helpful in understanding the method (R2).
- Experiments in various synthetic settings validate the algorithm’s effectiveness (R2). Limitations of the method are explicitly discussed (R2).
- Theories and propositions are explained with examples, crucial for algorithm implementation (R4). The work offers a new perspective for latent variable discovery (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and logical flow:** The manuscript lacks logical coherence, making it difficult to follow (R1). Theories used in a proof appear after the proof itself (e.g., Theorems 1 and 2 are placed after the “Intuition” section), disrupting readability (R4).
- **Technical contribution ambiguity:** One reviewer (R1) argues that the concept of homologous surrogates is fundamentally equivalent to pure children, implying limited novelty. Other reviewers consider it a meaningful relaxation, but this disagreement weakens the paper’s claimed contribution.
- **Missed related work in introduction:** The introduction omits closely related work that relaxes structural assumptions among latent and observed variables, such as [Dong et al., 2024] and [Xie et al., 2024] (R2, R3). The paper also states (line 82) that its setting is new, which may be inaccurate (R2). The claim (line 45) about [Jin et al., 2024] making a pure-children assumption is also potentially imprecise (R2).
- **Poor performance on real-world data:** The method performs unsatisfactorily on the Holzinger & Swineford dataset (R1, R3). The authors attribute this to limited sample size, but no alternative real-world dataset is presented to validate the method (R1). The main text gives only a brief description, and conclusions from the real data experiment are not well motivated (R3).
- **High algorithm complexity:** Algorithm 1 has complexity \(O(|O|^4)\) and Algorithm 2 \(O(|O|^2|L|^2)\), which could hinder real-world applicability. An empirical complexity analysis compared to baselines is missing (R3).
- **Small simulated graphs:** The synthetic graphs used (Figure 5) are small; experiments with more latent variables and sparse latent relations would be informative (R3).
- **Missing intuitive examples for theorems:** While definitions are illustrated, no examples accompany the main theorems, making it hard to grasp the intuitions (R3). The tracking example in figure captions (e.g., Figures 2 and 3) is not explained step-by-step in the main text, reducing clarity (R4).
- **Unclear identification in Equation (7):** The step in which parents \(O_j\) of \(O_i\) are identified is not clear; \(O_j\) might be observed ancestors rather than parents. Algorithm 1 can produce spurious edges, complicating parent identification (R3).
- **Unanswered questions about algorithm behavior:** The authors claim ideal performance with large sample sizes but do not specify how many samples are needed to achieve ideal results (R3). The impact of incorrectly estimating the number of latent variables on final results is not discussed (R3).
- **Missing example of homologous surrogate:** The term “homologous surrogate” should be accompanied by a concrete, immediate example when first mentioned to aid intuitive understanding (R2).
- **Source code link missing from paper:** The review notes the absence of a source code link in the paper itself (R4). (Note: The abstract does include an anonymous link, but the reviewer may have missed it or expects it in the main paper.)
- **Formatting inconsistency:** In Algorithm 1 pseudocode, the font of “Thm.1” in line 3 differs from other instances (R4).
