## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes EMMOE, a benchmark of 100 home robotic tasks in Habitat, with human demonstrations annotated with reasoning steps, subgoal decompositions, and replanning trajectories. It introduces the EMMOE-100 dataset for SFT and DPO fine-tuning, and the HomieBot agent combining a Video-LLaVA high-level planner with low-level navigation and manipulation models. Three metrics are proposed: Task Progress (TP), Success End Rate (SER), and Success Re-plan Rate (SRR). HomieBot achieves 31.8% success on training tasks and 20% on test tasks. Reviewers are split: two (R1, R3, scores 5) value the benchmark and metrics, while two (R2, R4, scores 3) find limited novelty and poor execution.

### Strengths
- Novel evaluation metrics (Task Progress, Success End Rate, Success Re-plan Rate) that offer nuanced assessment of embodied execution and are valuable for the community.
- Human-controlled embodied agent trajectories, unlike PDDL-based datasets (e.g., ALFRED), with deliberate replanning and annotated reasoning for open-ended long-horizon tasks with multiple valid solutions.
- New benchmark of 100 diverse home tasks in Habitat with detailed reasoning annotations (Chain-of-Thought, subgoal decompositions, replanning).
- Provision of the EMMOE-100 dataset in a trainable format for SFT and DPO fine-tuning from egocentric trajectories, beneficial for future embodied AI research.
- Clear statement of objectives and extensive related work coverage.

### Weaknesses
- **Overclaim and limited novelty**: The paper claims to be the first unified benchmark for high-level and low-level evaluation, but previous TAMP literature and simulator benchmarks (e.g., ALFRED, FiLM) have addressed this unification. Two reviewers (R2, R4) consider the work very similar to ALFRED and FiLM with only a few enhancements, while two others (R1, R3) value the new benchmark and metrics. The claimed “trainable data format” contribution is not clearly distinguished from existing datasets.
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons**: No evaluation of existing RL algorithms, LM-based agents, text-only or zero-shot multi-image models (e.g., Qwen2VL, GPT-4o) on the benchmark. Only HomieBot is evaluated, and there is no comparison between modular and end-to-end approaches. This limits the ability to assess relative contributions.
- **Missing failure analysis and error analysis**: The low success rates (31.8% training, 20% testing) are not analyzed. The rule-based error detection (e.g., checking if an object is “closed” before “put”) is brittle – it does not account for locations like “in a sink” or “on a shelf” without open/closed states. No detailed analysis of primary failures, reasoning failure modes, or task-specific bottlenecks is provided.
- **No evaluation before fine-tuning**: The model is not evaluated in its pre-trained state, so the impact of SFT and DPO cannot be assessed. Ablation studies are missing.
- **Lack of individual assessment of modules**: There is no separate evaluation of high-level planning success and low-level execution success. The proposed metrics (TP, SER, SRR) are not clearly linked to these two abilities, making it hard to identify bottlenecks.
- **Methodology details insufficient**: The data processing and augmentation pipeline (fixed-format conversation conversion in SFT, uniform script processing, DPO augmentation algorithm) is not adequately described.
- **Clarity and formatting issues**: The paper contains numerous typos (e.g., “controll”, line 180; missing words between lines 185-186), poor formatting, misplaced content (training data description in Section 3, which should focus on benchmark construction), and lack of a formal problem definition for high-level planning and low-level execution. The relationship between subtask i and subtask i+1 (whether they are different attempts) is unclear.
- **Metric definition errors and inconsistencies**: Equation 1 for Task Progress is likely incorrect – it should take the maximum over keypaths. The Success End Rate equation is unreasonable: if an agent never outputs “End” but completes some tasks, the equation becomes infinite; it should be normalized by the number of successful tasks. There is conceptual confusion between “task” and “trajectory” (M is defined as tasks, but t is called a trajectory). The definition of i in the Success End Rate formula is missing.
- **No discussion of real-to-sim gap**: The paper does not address potential discrepancies between the Habitat simulator and real-world manipulation models, nor how this might affect generalization.
- **Discrete action space not justified**: The work uses a discrete action space, but Habitat supports continuous control; no justification is given for this choice.
- **Unclear measurement of SER and SRR**: How the counts for “End” and replans are computed per trajectory is not explained.
- **Unclear planner-executor feedback**: How the high-level planner receives information about low-level execution success or failure (beyond the rule-based error detection) is not described.
