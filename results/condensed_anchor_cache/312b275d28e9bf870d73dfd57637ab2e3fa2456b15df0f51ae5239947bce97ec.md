## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses knowledge distillation (KD) from strong pretrained models, arguing that inefficiency stems from low mutual information between teacher and student. It proposes two teacher-side preprocessing methods: (1) mutual information‑aware optimization (using Sharpness‑Aware Minimization, SAM) and (2) heuristic MLP block reweighting (called ReMem). The work provides insights into how Transformer components (especially MLP blocks) affect knowledge transfer, and demonstrates results on ViT, ResNet18, MobileNetV2, and EfficientNetV2 across varying dataset sizes and scales. Reviewers gave scores of 3, 6, and 5 (one strongly positive, one very negative, one moderately positive).

### Strengths
- Novel focus on teacher‑side preprocessing for KD, addressing a key limitation of large teacher models.
- The two proposed methods (mutual information optimization with SAM and heuristic MLP reweighting) are intuitive, easy to adopt, and can be directly integrated into existing KD pipelines.
- Shown effective across different model sizes (e.g., ViT, ResNet18, MobileNetV2, EfficientNetV2) and dataset scales, increasing adaptability.
- Provides valuable interpretability insights into how knowledge is preserved in pretrained models and why KD can fail – an under‑studied area.
- The problem statement and mitigation approach are considered novel and relevant.
- One reviewer (score 6) particularly praised the teacher‑side angle, the structure of the paper, and the practical insight; another reviewer (score 3) acknowledged the problem but was more critical of scope and complexity.

### Weaknesses
- **Generalizability concerns**: Validated only on ViT; applicability to other architectures (e.g., large language models) is unclear. The link between “expertiseness” (defined via a bipartite graph) and MLP layers is weak, raising doubts about the generality of the core finding.
- **Limitation on small/imbalanced datasets**: Mutual information‑aware optimization is acknowledged to be ineffective on small or highly imbalanced datasets. The heuristic MLP reweighting supplement may not work in all scenarios.
- **Increased complexity and hyperparameter sensitivity**: Both methods introduce additional hyperparameters (e.g., disturbance size for SAM, weight factor for MLP blocks) with no tuning strategy provided. This complicates model tuning and debugging, and reduces interpretability.
- **Lack of ablation experiments**: No ablation isolating the contributions of mutual information optimization vs. MLP reweighting (e.g., ablation with ReZero instead of reweighting). The overall effect of each component is unclear.
- **Missing comparisons**: The benefit of SAM over alternative mutual information estimators (e.g., MINE) is not demonstrated, either empirically or theoretically. Existing KD methods that avoid teacher fine‑tuning (e.g., Sengupta et al., 2024, “A Good Learner can Teach Better”, ICLR 2024) are not countered analytically or empirically.
- **Unaddressed questions and unverified claims**:
  - Why does ReMem often reduce teacher performance (Tables 2 and 3)?
  - What does “patient” refer to in Table 3?
  - Proposition 5.4 is stated without proof.
  - The effect of ReMem on expertiseness is not shown.
  - No demonstration of expertise levels before and after optimization to confirm that knowledge transfer is indeed enhanced.
  - Criteria for selecting which MLP layers to reweight (especially in large models) are not specified.
  - Concern that weight redistribution may interfere with other domain knowledge the teacher has learned, with no guarantees or tests on unrelated tasks.
- **Contrast among reviewers**: The most positive reviewer (score 6) found the approach insightful and well‑structured, while the most negative reviewer (score 3, confidence 5) highlighted the limited scope, missing ablation, and added complexity; the moderate reviewer (score 5) also noted insufficient experimental rigor.
