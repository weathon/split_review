## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Structural Compression (StructComp), a training framework for Graph Contrastive Learning (GCL) that improves scalability by replacing message passing with a sparse low-rank approximation of the diffusion matrix. The encoder is trained on compressed node embeddings, entirely avoiding message passing during training and drastically reducing the number of contrastive sample pairs. Theoretical analysis shows the compressed loss approximates the original GCL loss and that StructComp acts as an implicit regularizer. Experiments on seven datasets demonstrate large reductions in time and memory consumption while maintaining or improving accuracy over vanilla GCL models and existing scalable training methods.

### Strengths
- The structural compression idea is novel, interesting, and simple in concept.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow; the problem and motivation are clearly presented.
- Experiment results are impressive, particularly the computational savings (time and memory).
- Scalability is a major bottleneck for GCL, and this work makes an important contribution toward addressing it.

### Weaknesses
1. **Theoretical analysis is overly limited.** The proof holds only for a linear GNN and the Erdős–Rényi random graph model, which oversimplifies real-world graphs (e.g., power-law degree distributions, community structures). Without considering non-linearity, the problem reduces to a matrix decomposition. How the theoretical results generalize to non-linear models, heterophilous graphs, and more advanced architectures (GAT, GraphSage, GIN, PNA) is not established. The paper does not explain how the approximation gap in the loss translates to downstream performance. Over-claiming of theoretical contributions (e.g., "theoretical proof") is noted given these strong assumptions.

2. **Scalability claims are not verified on truly large-scale graphs.** The benchmarked datasets (ogbn-arxiv, ogbn-products) are considered medium-scale by OGB standards. Evaluations on larger graphs such as papers100M, Reddit, Yelp, or OGBG-LSC datasets would be necessary to convincingly support the scalability claims.

3. **Robustness and stability claims lack empirical evidence.** The paper states that StructComp acts as an additional regularizer yielding a more robust encoder, but no experiments (e.g., adversarial perturbations, noisy labels, stability demonstrations) are provided to verify this.

4. **Missing comparisons and discussion with related works.** Several recent GCL methods are not discussed or compared: e.g., “Calibrating and Improving Graph Contrastive Learning” (TMLR’23), “Single-Pass Contrastive Learning” (TMLR’23), and “Scaling Up, Scaling Deep: Blockwise Graph Contrastive Learning” (arXiv’23). Efficient GCL baselines such as CCA-SSG and GGD (discussed in the paper) are also not compared. This weakens the positioning of StructComp relative to the current state of the art.

5. **Graph partitioning impact is underexplored.** The method’s efficacy depends on graph partitioning—a classic NP-hard problem. Different strategies (e.g., spectral clustering, METIS, random partitioning) and their trade-offs in computational cost and compressed-graph quality are not analyzed. A more thorough exploration is required.

6. **Applicability concerns:**
   - The exact setting for node classification (transductive vs. inductive) is unclear; it should be specified and evaluated for both.
   - It is not discussed how well StructComp can approximate the variety of augmentations commonly used in GCL (e.g., node dropping, edge perturbation, feature masking).
   - The method’s applicability to graphs with multiple node/edge types (heterogeneous graphs) is not mentioned.

7. **Time and memory cost accounting is ambiguous.** It is unclear whether reported savings include the preprocessing steps (e.g., partitioning, compression). The computation method should be clarified.

8. **Experimental rigor is insufficient.** For example, Figure 4 appears to be based on a single run; multiple independent runs should be reported to assess variance.

9. **No explicit discussion of limitations.** The paper lacks a dedicated limitations section and does not address scenarios where StructComp might underperform or fail.
