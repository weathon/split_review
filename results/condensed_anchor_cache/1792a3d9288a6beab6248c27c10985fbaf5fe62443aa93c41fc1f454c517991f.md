- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 5

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a causal discovery framework (TrendDiff) that identifies trend-influenced variables using CD-NOD and then distinguishes intrinsic trends (real) from measurement trends (false). The authors evaluate on synthetic scenarios (including first-order settings with fixed DAG structures) and one real-world dataset.

### Strengths
- The topic of decoupling intrinsic and measurement trends is engaging and practically important (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The paper is well-written (Reviewer 3).
- Experiments include simulated data and one real-world dataset (Reviewers 1, 2).
- Reviewer 3 found the experimental section well-done, though Reviewers 1 and 2 raised concerns about evaluation and baselines (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of rigorous justification and theoretical guarantees.** The proposed method is motivated by intuition and examples rather than a sound formal derivation (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 1 asks: “Is there any theoretical guarantee of the proposed method that can be made?” Faithfulness is assumed but not justified; its use should be clarified, and the possibility of relaxing it is not explored (Reviewer 3).
- **Vague or missing definitions.** Time trends are defined as “a function concerning time within a given data span,” which is imprecise; they should be defined in SEM language (Reviewer 1). Variable \(C\) (the time index) is not defined when first introduced (Reviewers 1, 2). The statement “\(C\) is a proxy for \(T\)” needs explicit formalization (Reviewer 2). The assumption that “confounders are fixed at each time point” lacks a mathematical/graphical definition (Reviewer 1). An “Assumption” section listing all assumptions in one place is needed instead of scattering them (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing related work and baselines.** There is no dedicated “related work” section, making it hard to assess the contribution relative to prior art (Reviewer 2). No baselines are compared in the experiments, so performance cannot be evaluated (Reviewer 2). The paper heavily relies on CD-NOD, but Section 3 largely summarizes the existing CD-NOD algorithm without clearly distinguishing novel contributions (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited practical scope and unrealistic assumptions.** The algorithm imposes constraints directly on the DAG, but the true DAG is unknown, so the constraints cannot be validated in practice (Reviewer 2). The study is restricted to “first-order scenarios” (Reviewer 2). Reviewers question why accuracy requires this restriction and whether the algorithm fails in higher-order cases (Reviewer 2). Conditional independence tests are difficult to evaluate, especially with high-dimensional conditioning; faithfulness is unrealistic and not verifiable, limiting practical interest (Reviewer 3). For Figure 3(c) the main issue seems to be measurement error rather than intrinsic trends; the authors should discuss whether existing measurement-error causal discovery methods could identify the measurement trend variable (Reviewer 1).
- **Incomplete experimental validation.** Experiments use only one simulated structure (fixed DAG) instead of a broader class of structures (Reviewer 2). The results show identification of 3 intrinsic-trend variables from 9 in that fixed structure, but this is not comprehensive (Reviewer 2). Computational complexity is not reported (Reviewer 2). There is a typo: the main paper lists V1, V5, V7 as intrinsic-trend variables, while the appendix lists X1, X2, X7 (Reviewer 2).
- **Unaddressed methodological questions that expose missing content.**
    - The assumption of IID samples for time series is not justified and appears overly idealistic; both Reviewers 1 and 2 ask for a rationale.
    - “To maintain accuracy” in restricting to first-order scenarios implies the algorithm may not work in other cases; this needs clarification (Reviewer 2).
    - It is unclear how measurement trends are eliminated after identification in the real-world dataset (Reviewer 2).
    - Since Phase 1 also learns the causal graph, the authors could present F1, precision, and recall for the discovered graph on simulated data (Reviewer 2).
    - The claim “reverse is not necessarily true” about changing modules needs explanation (Reviewer 1). The assumption that “trend variables are not positioned as leaf nodes” requires discussion (Reviewer 1).
- **Contrast in reviewer opinion:** Reviewer 3 (score 5) found the paper well-written and the experiments well-done, but still identified the same core weaknesses regarding limited practical interest, dependence on conditional independence tests, and reliance on faithfulness. Reviewers 1 and 2 (score 3 each) were more critical, particularly of the lack of formal justification, missing baselines, and unclear assumptions.
