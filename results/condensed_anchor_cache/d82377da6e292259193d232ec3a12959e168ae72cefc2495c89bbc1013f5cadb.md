## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Latent Consistency Models (LCMs) that adapt consistency models to the latent space of pretrained diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion), enabling few-step (2–4) or even one-step sampling. The method uses an augmented PF-ODE to incorporate classifier-free guidance in a single-stage distillation and introduces a skipping-step schedule to handle the large timestep space (T=1000). Results on LAION-5B-Aesthetics show considerable speed-ups with decent quality. Reviewers generally find the idea interesting and the empirical results impressive, but raise major concerns about technical novelty, missing comparisons, insufficient ablation, and presentation gaps. There is disagreement on writing quality (two reviewers praise it, one criticizes organization). One reviewer notes that the ablation studies provided are helpful, while another points out they are insufficient.

### Strengths
- The idea of viewing guided reverse diffusion as solving an augmented PF-ODE and applying consistency models to the latent space is interesting and intuitive. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Performance is impressive: with 2–4 steps, LCM achieves decent quality and sometimes outperforms other methods with more steps. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- Ablation studies on ODE solvers, skipping-step schedule, and guidance scale are provided to facilitate understanding. (R1; note R3 finds these insufficient – see Weaknesses)
- The method is generally well-written and easy to follow (R2, R4; contrast: R3 finds organization lacking – see Weaknesses)
- The introduction of Latent Consistency Fine-tuning (LCF) for customized datasets is noted, though its effectiveness is questioned. (R1, R2)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited technical novelty**: The adaptation of consistency models to latent space with classifier-free guidance via an augmented ODE is seen as straightforward – CFG within ODE is well-established, and the skipping-step heuristic is a direct consequence of Stable Diffusion’s large timestep (T=1000) rather than a new insight. The paper does not address unique challenges of “latent” consistency models (e.g., choosing a perceptual metric for distillation, which was used in the original pixel-space consistency models). (R2, R4; R1 also notes it is unclear which contributions are most impactful)
- **Missing comparisons**: No comparison to InstaFlow (single-step flow-based model) or the original 50-step Stable Diffusion baseline to clarify the speed–quality trade-off. Performance on other LDMs beyond Stable Diffusion is not shown. (R2, R1)
- **Insufficient ablation studies**: While some ablation is provided (R1), the paper lacks qualitative results for the ODE solvers and skipping-step schedule, as well as both qualitative and quantitative results for the guided consistency distillation component (R3). The choice of DDIM as the main ODE solver is not justified; results with other ODE solvers are missing (R4). The metric used for distillation in latent space (given LPIPS is not applicable) is not clarified (R4).
- **LCF appears ineffective**: On the Simpsons dataset, fine-tuning for 30K steps degrades quality compared to the original LCM (Figure 6). (R2)
- **Lack of realistic photo results**: The qualitative results consist mostly of artistic illustrations; including realistic images would strengthen the evaluation of text-to-image alignment. (R2)
- **Computational details unclear**: The computational complexity of solving the augmented PF-ODE is not given (R1). Exact inference time speedup compared to other methods is not reported (R1). Training time and memory comparisons are missing (R3).
- **Presentation issues**: The paper is not well-organized – Section 1 is too concise, Figure 1 takes too much space, and the overall pipeline is not described in detail (R3). (In contrast, R2 and R4 find the paper well-written, making this a minority criticism.) Text prompts are absent from all visual results (Figures 1, 6), making it hard to assess image-text alignment (R2, R4). There is a typo: “origin data distribution” should be “original data distribution” (R2).
- **Misleading title**: The term “Latent” is unnecessary because the method does not depend on specifics of the latent space – it applies to any diffusion model with a large timestep, not just latent diffusion models. (R4)
