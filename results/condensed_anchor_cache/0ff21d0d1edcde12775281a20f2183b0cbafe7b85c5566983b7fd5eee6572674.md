## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Mamba-CDSP, a new approach for time-varying counterfactual prediction (TCP) using state-space models (specifically the Mamba architecture) combined with a Covariate-based Decorrelation towards Selective Parameters (CDSP) mechanism. The goal is to mitigate the over-balancing problem in sequential confounder adjustment while preserving covariate information and improving efficiency. Experiments on synthetic and real-world (MIMIC-III) datasets are provided. The majority of reviewers (scores 6,6,6,6) focus on this method. One reviewer (score 5) describes a different method (THLTS) using a VAE for shared latent factor learning; that review’s content does not align with the presented paper and is included as-is.

### Strengths
- **Novel combination of Mamba (SSM) with TCP** and design of the CDSP mechanism to address the over-balancing limitation. (Reviewers 2,3,4,5)
- **Well-motivated de-correlation approach** that balances confounding bias correction and covariate information preservation. (Reviewers 2,3,5)
- **Efficient linear-time complexity**, better handling of long sequences, and improved running time compared to Transformers. (Reviewers 3,5)
- **Comprehensive empirical evaluation** across synthetic and real-world (MIMIC-III) datasets, including ablation studies and multiple baselines. (Reviewers 3,4,5)
- **Clear, well-written paper** with easy-to-follow motivation and preliminaries. (Reviewers 1,4)
- **Flexible component** that can be inserted into arbitrary counterfactual outcome forecast models (Reviewer 1, though referring to THLTS, not Mamba-CDSP).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited theoretical analysis**: Lack of rigorous justification for why covariance decorrelation outperforms traditional balancing; no formal bias-variance trade-off or convergence analysis; unclear how selective parameter decorrelation avoids over-balancing. (Reviewers 2,3,4,5)
- **Puzzling gains on MIMIC-III (Table 2)**: Since counterfactuals are not observable in real data, confounding bias should have minimal impact on evaluation, yet CDSP shows large improvements. The source of gains (bias correction vs. other representation benefits) is not explained. (Reviewers 4,5)
- **Novelty concern (Reviewer 3 minority negative)**: The claim of being first to consider step-by-step deconfounding in TCP is disputed; existing works (e.g., [1], [2] – references not specified) already address similar goals. The method is described as “simply replacing the Transformer with Mamba by slightly tailoring the backbone,” lacking significant technical contribution. (Reviewer 3)
- **Small dataset size**: MIMIC-III has only 5,000 patients, which may not adequately reveal the efficiency advantages of Mamba over Transformer. (Reviewer 3)
- **Incomplete literature review**: Missing recent works on temporal counterfactual estimation, such as Chen et al. (Multi-Task Gaussian Process), Wu et al. (Counterfactual Generative Models), Wang et al. (Dual-module Framework), Berrevoets et al. (Disentangled CRN). (Reviewers 4,5)
- **Insufficient implementation details**: Hyperparameters (hidden dimensions, number of layers, decorrelation coefficient, dropout rates) are not specified; the CDSP mechanism is described vaguely; optimization procedure is missing; reference for domain adversarial learning (line 269) is incorrect (Lim 2018 does not use domain-adversarial learning). (Reviewers 4,5)
- **Notation overloading**: Confusion between action variable \(A_t\) and system parameter \(A\). (Reviewer 1)
- **Statistical significance missing**: Table 2 results lack paired t-test or similar; it is unclear if performance gains are statistically significant. (Reviewer 1)
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity**: Need systematic analysis of the decorrelation threshold’s impact on performance across datasets and sequence lengths. (Reviewers 2,3,5)
- **Outstanding questions**: Is “CPSD” on line 316 a typo? Which dataset corresponds to Table 3? (Reviewers 4,5)
- **Missing ablations**: Distribution analysis of counterfactual predictions beyond RMSE would strengthen the evaluation. (Reviewer 1)
- **Unclear baselines**: The role of the “THLTS(v)” baseline and why it is fair needs elaboration; the difference between forecast models \(p_\rho()\) and \(g_\rho()\) in Algorithm 1 should be clarified. (Reviewer 1)
- **Minority reviewer (Reviewer 1) is more positive** about applicability but highlights distinct concerns about notation and statistical rigor, while Reviewer 3 is most critical about novelty and technical depth.
