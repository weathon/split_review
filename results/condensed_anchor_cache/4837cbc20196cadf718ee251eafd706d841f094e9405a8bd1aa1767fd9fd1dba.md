## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a three-part evaluation framework for structure-based drug design (SBDD) models: (1) similarity to known active compounds and FDA-approved drugs using molecular fingerprints, (2) virtual screening ability measured by BEDROC and enrichment factors (EF), and (3) binding affinity estimation via Vina docking, delta scores, and DrugCLIP. Five SBDD models (including MolCRAFT, TargetDiff, Pocket2Mol) are tested; MolCRAFT dominates across multiple metrics. Reviewers acknowledged the importance of addressing inflated docking metrics and the need for more practical evaluation, but raised concerns about validation, overclaim, and scope.

### Strengths
- The problem is of high importance; many generative models rely on inaccurate computational property predictors and wet-lab validation is often infeasible.
- The three proposed assessment techniques capture distinct, non-redundant aspects of generated molecules (similarity, screening enrichment, binding affinity).
- The datasets, especially the test set, are satisfactory.
- The paper explicitly highlights pitfalls of current docking score evaluations (e.g., AutoDock Vina can be exploited by large, low-QED molecules).
- Performance across several 3D SBDD models is systematically compared and contrasted, providing insight for future research.
- The rationale for proposing new metrics is clear and well-motivated.
- The framework represents a novel benchmark addressing a crucial problem in the field.
- The paper is detailed and well-explained in most sections, offering a practical evaluation approach.

### Weaknesses
- **Overclaim regarding wet-lab replacement:** Several reviewers found the statement that the framework “provides a practical alternative to wet-lab experiments” too bold. They noted that metrics do not replace experimental validation; one reviewer (score 8) was less critical but still unconvinced, while others (scores 6) strongly advocated rephrasing to avoid giving a false impression that predictive methods are close to replacing wet-lab tests. More experiments on larger datasets and demonstration of correlation with actual wet-lab results on a subset of compounds are needed.
- **Similarity bias and de novo novelty:** Relying on similarity to known active compounds may limit the search for novel, structurally distinct molecules. Reviewers questioned how the framework would evaluate a molecule very different from known actives but with strong docking affinity, and whether it penalizes genuinely novel scaffolds. Overfitting to the new metrics (e.g., generating molecules similar to known actives to score well) is a risk.
- **Insufficient model coverage:** The evaluation is limited to deep learning (diffusion/flow) models. Reviewers suggested including reinforcement learning (RL) and genetic algorithm (GA) based methods, as well as 1D/2D generative models that have been shown to outperform 3D methods on some benchmarks (e.g., references [1]–[5] from Reviewer 2).
- **Synthesizability not systematically addressed:** While the paper acknowledges the difficulty of synthesizing generated molecules, it does not provide a systematic approach to assess or improve synthesizability. The SA score is imperfect, and similarity to known molecules does not guarantee synthetic accessibility.
- **Virtual screening metrics need experimental validation:** The heavy reliance on virtual screening-based metrics (BEDROC, EF) to indicate practical usefulness may be misleading without experimental validation. One reviewer specifically noted that docking algorithms like AutoDock Vina are too crude (poor sampling/scoring power), and Glide docking shows better enrichment but is proprietary. The inability of current docking to reproduce experimental binding poses (e.g., DiffDock's RMSD thresholds, PoseCheck/PoseBusters for unphysical clashes) undermines confidence.
- **Missing evaluation on novel targets and geometry:** Reviewers asked how the framework performs for targets with no closely related compounds, and whether generated molecules’ 3D geometry (e.g., pose validity, clashes) is considered. Current SBDD models often produce unphysical poses, a challenge not captured by the proposed metrics.
- **Insufficient justification of FDA drug similarity metric:** Table 3 shows that even reference ligands have low similarity to FDA-approved drugs, raising questions about the metric’s relevance. Drugs targeting different proteins can be structurally dissimilar.
- **Minor presentation issues:** Table 1 description is too simplified and hard to interpret (e.g., why using real molecules as template is better). Terminology like FLAPP should be briefly explained. There is a typo in Figure 2 (“actives”).
- **One reviewer was notably more positive (score 8), praising the framework’s novelty and practical potential, while three reviewers (scores 6) were more critical, citing insufficient validation, overclaim, and limited scope.**
