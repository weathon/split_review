## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviews are mixed (scores 5, 5, 3, 5). Reviewer 3 is the most critical (score 3) and finds the C-Net and meta-learning process novel but still identifies major weaknesses. All reviewers converge on insufficient novelty, limited evaluation (only small datasets CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet), missing comparisons and ablations, and insufficient engagement with related work. Reviewer 3 is the only one to positively highlight the novelty of C-Net and its meta-training; other reviewers consider the approach incremental, following standard online distillation with meta-learned weights.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written, well-organized, and effectively communicates key ideas (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- Distilling a pretrained teacher to multiple students simultaneously is an interesting and sound motivation (Reviewers 1, 3). Models with different architectures learn different knowledge, making collaboration beneficial for KD (Reviewer 3).
- The proposed C-Net and its meta-learning training process (bi-level optimization) are considered novel by one reviewer (Reviewer 3); other reviewers acknowledge the approach but find the novelty limited.
- Evaluation covers a wide range of student and teacher architectures and model sizes on CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet (Reviewers 2, 4).
- Detailed implementation and code are provided, aiding reproducibility (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: The paper combines offline and online KD by training multiple students simultaneously while learning loss weights via meta-learning (bi-level optimization). This is seen as incremental and lacking clear improvements over existing dynamic weighting strategies. Several reviewers note that meta-optimized distillation is generally ineffective or difficult to reproduce (Reviewers 1, 2, 4). One reviewer (Reviewer 4) stresses that multi-teacher distillation is not new and that direct comparison to methods like AEKD (NeurIPS2020) that use optimization is missing.
- **Insufficient evaluation on small datasets only**: Experiments are limited to CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet. Standard KD benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet-1K) and downstream transfer learning results are missing, limiting generalizability. The performance on small datasets may not translate to larger, more complex datasets (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Missing related work**: The paper fails to discuss and compare with important prior work on bidirectional KD with multiple models and consensus learning with heterogeneous models. Specifically needed: [A] Deep Mutual Learning (CVPR’18), [B] Dual Learning for Machine Translation (NeurIPS’16), [C] Bidirectional Distillation for Top-K Recommender Systems (WWW’21), [D] Consensus Learning from Heterogeneous Objectives (WWW’22) (Reviewer 3). Additionally, closely related recent KD papers on self-supervised KD, representation matching, offline-online transfer, architecture search for distillation, and automated KD are not engaged with (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of ablation studies on core components**:
  - No thorough comparison of learned weights from C-NET versus simpler manually set weighting schemes (Reviewers 1, 2). The specific advantage of meta-learning over other dynamic weighting strategies is not clarified.
  - No ablation on the PooledStudent logits as supervision; the rationale for this design choice is unclear, and its impact is not explored (Reviewer 1).
  - No ablation on the C-Net architecture and its meta-learning process (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of evaluation on modern architectures**: Experiments use relatively older architectures; no evaluation on modern high-performance CNNs such as ConvNext, VAN, RepLKNet (Reviewer 2).
- **Theoretical analysis missing**: No theoretical insights into how the meta-collaboration process influences student learning patterns (Reviewer 2).
- **Potential technical issue**: In Equation 7, one reviewer believes the optimization of φ should be the outer loop, as Eq.7 is described as the optimization for C-Net; this needs clarification (Reviewer 3).
- **Minority reviewer note**: Reviewer 3, despite finding the C-Net and meta-training novel, gave the lowest score (3) due to the above missing related work and insufficient theoretical/empirical support. The other three reviewers also find novelty very limited despite rating 5.
