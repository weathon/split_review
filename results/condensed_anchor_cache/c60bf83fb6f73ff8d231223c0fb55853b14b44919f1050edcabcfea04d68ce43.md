- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 5, 3, 3, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers acknowledged the importance of a dedicated tool manipulation benchmark and the user-friendly design (Gymnasium API, state/vision support, standard RL algorithms). However, they expressed major concerns about limited realism (floating control point instead of a physical robot), small task size (14 tasks, many pouring variants), lack of novelty compared to existing benchmarks (ManiSkill2, Robosuite), insufficient documentation of action spaces and reward functions, and weak potential for sim-to-real transfer. Reviewer 2 gave a higher rating (5) and specifically emphasized the value of a new benchmark, but still judged the size, complexity, and realism as low. Other reviewers rated 3 and highlighted that the benchmark feels like an engineering project rather than a research contribution. All agreed that the baseline evaluations are useful but incomplete.

### Strengths
- **Well-written and clearly presented.** The paper is well-structured with clear images and a clear picture of related work (Reviewers 1, 2, 5).
- **Important problem motivation.** Tool manipulation is an essential capability for robot learning, requiring multi-step contact-rich interactions. A dedicated benchmark could facilitate reproducible research (Reviewers 2, 3, 5).
- **User-friendly and flexible design.** The benchmark is built on MuJoCo, integrates with Gymnasium API, supports both state-based and vision-based observations, and connects with popular RL libraries, lowering the barrier to entry (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Baseline evaluations with standard RL algorithms.** PPO and SAC are tested on all tasks using both state and vision inputs, providing initial insights into the challenges and limitations of current off-the-shelf methods (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Diverse tool and task offering (within a small scope).** The benchmark includes 12 tools and tasks such as pouring, scooping, stacking, hammering, etc., with multiple tool options per task (Reviewers 1, 4, 5). Reviewer 2 specifically praised the contribution of a new benchmark for robotic skills.

### Weaknesses
- **No physical robot model; tasks use an idealized floating control point.** The agent directly controls the tool’s pose rather than interacting through a robot arm. This abstracts away critical challenges in contact-rich manipulation, such as joint/torque limits, contact forces, and the need to grasp and hold the tool. Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 all raised this as a major limitation, with Reviewer 5 explicitly noting it bypasses tool-object contact dynamics and compromises sim-to-real transfer. Reviewer 3 called this a “point mass robot” and contrasted it with ManiSkill2 and Robosuite which model faithful robot dynamics.
- **Small scale and limited task diversity.** The benchmark contains only 14 tasks, with a heavy emphasis on pouring variations. It lacks common tool-use tasks such as cutting, hammering (beyond a simple version), screwing, or interactions requiring more dexterous manipulation. Reviewers 2, 3, 4, and 5 all criticized this; Reviewer 5 noted the lack of generalization assessment across manipulation primitives.
- **Lack of novelty; feels like an engineering project.** Multiple reviewers (1, 2, 3, 4) considered the contribution incremental, arguing that the tasks could be recreated by importing 3D objects into existing simulators like MuJoCo, and that the APIs and environment features are inherited from MuJoCo and Gymnasium without new technical challenges. Reviewer 1 stated it “feels more like an engineering project.” Reviewer 3 asked explicitly what unique designs or technical contributions differentiate this from prior MuJoCo-based tools.
- **Insufficient details on task specifications.** Reviewer 4 pointed out that the paper omits key information: the action space (joint vs. Cartesian, bounds), the reward function, and the terminal states. This makes it impossible to assess task complexity or reproduce the learning objective. Reviewer 3 also noted that different tasks have different action space dimensions (3–5 DoF) without justification, and asked whether unifying actions would enable multi-task RL.
- **Incomplete comparison with existing benchmarks.** No quantitative or qualitative comparison is made against tool-use tasks in ManiSkill2, Robosuite, or other existing simulators. Reviewers 2 and 4 stressed the need to establish what new features this benchmark adds over those existing environments. Reviewer 2 questioned “If the only contribution here is to package together certain types of tasks that exist across many other benchmarks, that could still be useful, but more work needs to be done to establish the added value.” Reviewer 4 requested a comparison table.
- **Limited object variation and generalizability.** Each task uses only a few fixed objects (e.g., a single container for pouring), restricting study of in-category generalization. Reviewer 4 highlighted this as a barrier for learning robust tool-use policies.
- **Baseline evaluation is insufficient for understanding challenges.** The experiments run only off-the-shelf PPO and SAC; no ablations, reward-shaping analyses, or learning-from-demonstration baselines are provided. Reviewers 2 and 3 noted that mixed success rates could stem from poorly tuned reward functions, and that more reward design exploration and ablation studies are needed to interpret the difficulty. Reviewer 3 also pointed out the counterintuitive result that vision-based policies often outperform state-based ones, which is not explained.
- **Limited realism and computational efficiency.** The simulator runs on CPU MuJoCo, while many recent benchmarks have moved to GPU-based or more realistic simulators. Reviewer 2 noted this benchmark “feels somewhat behind the Pareto frontier” of realism vs. throughput, limiting its utility for large-scale RL or sim-to-real. Reviewer 3 and 5 raised concerns about the embodiment gap between the idealized agent and real robot arms, questioning transfer feasibility.
