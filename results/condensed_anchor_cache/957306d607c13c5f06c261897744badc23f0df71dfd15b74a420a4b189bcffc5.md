## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes IF-MoDGS, a dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting method that does not require precomputed camera poses or point clouds from SfM. It separates static and dynamic scene regions, estimates poses from static background, and uses a deformation network (CSM and HexPlane) to model dynamic motion. Additional losses (high-dimensional feature loss and annealing frequency loss) are introduced to improve spatio-temporal quality. The method is tested on NVIDIA and UCSD datasets. Two reviewers rate it 5 (conf 4 and 5) and two rate it 3 (conf 4), with the main split on novelty, experimental scope, and comparisons.

### Strengths
- First dynamic 3DGS approach that operates without precomputed camera poses and point clouds from SfM (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- Separation of static and dynamic components for independent optimization is a reasonable strategy (Reviewers 1,2,4; but Reviewer 4 notes many SLAM methods already do this).
- High-dimensional feature loss and annealing frequency loss help enhance rendering quality in complex spatio-temporal scenes (Reviewer 3).
- Superior quantitative performance compared to baseline methods reported on the NVIDIA dataset (Reviewers 1,3; but Reviewer 2 notes it outperforms baselines in only 3 of 7 samples).
- The paper is clearly written and easy to follow, with well-structured pipeline illustrations (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- Results demonstrate effectiveness of the proposed pipeline (Reviewers 1,3).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty concerns:** The approach is heavily engineered and combines existing components: static initialization resembles COLMAP-free 3DGS, the warp field parameterization is similar to 4DGS, and the pipeline simply decomposes the problem using monocular predictions (Reviewers 1,3,4). Two reviewers (1 and 3) call it an “engineering implementation” lacking distinct technical novelty; Reviewer 3 explicitly scores Contribution=1. The authors should articulate unique technical contributions beyond engineering combination (Reviewer 1).
- **Over-engineered complexity:** The method uses multiple priors (depth maps, motion masks), loss functions, and separate “initialization” and “reconstruction” modules, despite claiming to be initialization-free. Why can’t these modules be unified? (Reviewer 3). This complexity obscures the core idea (Reviewer 3).
- **Motion mask dependence and robustness:** The method relies heavily on Mask R-CNN for motion masks, but no ablation studies investigate sensitivity to mask accuracy or imperfect predictions (Reviewers 2,3,4). How does performance degrade when masks have errors, especially at boundaries between static and dynamic regions? (Reviewers 1,2,3,4). How is the static region defined from Mask R-CNN output exactly? (Reviewer 1,3). The method also assumes a small static part problem (e.g., object-centric datasets) is handled, but this is not validated (Reviewer 1, question 2).
- **Insufficient experimental scope and comparisons:**
  - Only tested on NVIDIA and UCSD datasets, which are multi-view setups with large camera angles where SfM works well. Should also test on casually captured monocular videos (e.g., DAVIS) where pose estimation is genuinely challenging (Reviewer 4).
  - Did not test on the HyperNeRF dataset (Reviewer 3, question).
  - Missing comparisons against state-of-the-art NeRF-based dynamic methods: DynNeRF, CTNeRF, DynPoint, MonoNeRF (Reviewer 1, weakness 5).
  - Table 1 and Table 2 are redundant: both show quantitative results on the NVIDIA dataset. The proposed method outperforms baselines in only 3 of 7 samples (Reviewer 2). Results for the UCSD dataset are missing (Reviewer 2).
  - No computational efficiency comparison (training time, testing speed) with baselines, which is a key advantage of Gaussian Splatting (Reviewer 2).
  - Rendered videos show only the authors’ method and have visual artifacts (e.g., car with weird moving speed). Videos are from fixed camera positions; analysis of rendering quality from novel viewpoints is needed (Reviewer 4).
  - D3DGS results are unusually poor; provide more details on baseline implementation for Table 2 (Reviewer 4). Also add citations for referenced methods in Table 1 (Reviewer 4).
  - Baseline methods may not use masks; how is fair comparison ensured when the proposed method uses additional supervision (motion masks)? (Reviewer 4).
- **Pose estimation accuracy:** The accuracy of estimated camera poses is not compared to ground truth. How effective and reliable is the pose estimation method? (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing method details:**
  - In Methods, succinct descriptions of employed techniques (COGS, monocular depth) are needed—not just citations (Reviewer 1). Include key features, primary steps, and crucial settings (Reviewer 1).
  - How are Gaussian parameters (e.g., r and s) derived from the point cloud? Missing mathematical formulation or algorithm (Reviewer 1, weakness 3).
  - Canonical Space Mapper (CSM) motivation and training: Why are two warping fields (CSM and HexPlane) necessary? Ablation on CSM is missing (Reviewer 3, question). How is the canonical space defined for each object? Is the static part also mapped to canonical space? How are points converted back to global space before rendering? (Reviewer 1, question 6). Where does ground truth for the Gaussians come from during CSM training? (Reviewer 1, question 5).
  - Outlier removal thresholds (top 5%, bottom 10%) are used when scaling dynamic point clouds from static scale/shift. No justification for these percentages; they appear arbitrary. Since the dynamic part can inherit incorrect scale/shift, simply removing extremes may not fix the underlying scale problem (Reviewer 1, weakness 2). Additional steps for stability are needed or this should be acknowledged as a limitation (Reviewer 1).
- **Redundancy in figures:** Figure 3 and the lower part of Figure 1 share the same framework; recommend removing the lower portion of Figure 1 (Reviewer 1).
- **Optimization difficulty:** Starting pose estimation from scratch under dynamic conditions with inconsistent depth scale/shift risks local optima; no discussion of how this is mitigated (Reviewer 1, question 3).
