## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a prompt-based exposure correction method (CAGGLE) targeting images where under- and over-exposure coexist. It introduces global and local prompts via a Prompt Interaction Module (PIM) and uses a Color Naming model to guide color consistency. The method is evaluated on multi-exposure benchmark datasets and claims state-of-the-art performance.

### Strengths
- Addresses a practical and challenging problem: correction of images containing both under- and over-exposed regions within a single scene, an issue related to inverse tone mapping in HDR.  
- The use of explicit color definition priors from the Color Naming model is a technically valid approach to enforce color consistency and align the latent space with human color perception.  
- The method is explained in a detailed, easy-to-understand manner.  
- Achieves state-of-the-art results both quantitatively and qualitatively, particularly in terms of color consistency.  
- The exploration of prompt learning for mixed-exposure correction is interesting and the effectiveness of global and local prompt learning is justified with results.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty and unclear explanation of core components.**  
  The overall model structure is similar to common existing models. The explanation of how local and global prompts are specifically implemented is not convincing—it is unclear whether existing methods are reused directly and what special designs (if any) were introduced. The claimed novelty of the local prompt conditioned Cross-Attention (LP-CA) is not justified, and no ablation results are provided for it.  
  *Minority reviewer gave a low rating (3) due to these concerns.*

- **Weak motivation and poor illustration.**  
  The introduction and Figure 1 fail to demonstrate the mixed-exposure problem highlighted in the abstract. The example images (one underexposed, one overexposed) do not contain obvious mixed exposure, and the visual differences from existing methods are not significant. This undermines the motivation for color-aware guidance. Including images with both under- and over-exposed regions would strengthen the paper.

- **Missing comparisons and baselines.**  
  The paper uses the Color Naming model (Serrano-Lozano et al., 2024) for intermediate supervision but does not compare against that method itself, even though it is an image enhancement approach. Comparisons to related prompt-based or exposure correction works (e.g., CVPR 2018 HDR transformation, TOG 2018 Exposure white-box framework, ICCV 2023 iterative prompt learning for backlit images) are absent.

- **Insufficient analysis and missing ablations.**  
  - No analysis of the latent space: e.g., using k-means or t-SNE to verify that encoder outputs separate into exposure-level groups, or analyzing the differences captured by the latent spaces in Table 5.  
  - The interaction between prompts and the Color Naming model is not evaluated: e.g., measuring IoU between binary masks derived from local prompt attention and the color-naming-based segmentation.  
  - No visualization of features before and after the PIM (features F and F’).  
  - The rationale for using pixel-shuffle and un-shuffle layers is not explained.  
  - Computational cost (parameters, FLOPs, runtime) is not reported.

- **Methodological concerns and unclear design choices.**  
  - Why is the local prompt learned before the global prompt? Intuitively, global illumination should be easier to learn first; how would performance change if the order were reversed?  
  - Color Naming Loss is computed on input priors, but after exposure correction the ground truth color may shift. It is unclear how color consistency is ensured after correction.  
  - The paper evaluates low-light enhancement on a super-resolution dataset (Aakerberg et al., 2021); this choice is not justified and the compared low-light methods are not state-of-the-art, making the comparison less meaningful.

- **Presentation and reproducibility issues.**  
  - The method section is poorly organized; the pipeline and data flow in Figure 2 are hard to follow (e.g., where P_Local and P_Global come from, which Color Naming model is used).  
  - The introduction contains subjective statements with unclear references (page 1, lines 38–41).  
  - Missing details for reproducibility (e.g., β values of Adam optimizer).  
  - Figure 1 lacks references to the compared methods.
