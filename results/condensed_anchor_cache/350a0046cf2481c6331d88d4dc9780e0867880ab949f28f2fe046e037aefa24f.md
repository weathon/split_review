## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes refusal-free training to produce a Helpful-Only LLM that never refuses harmful prompts. The method filters refusal instances from the Magpie dataset (using LLM-based and rule-based filtering) and trains with SFT followed by DPO. Experiments show a 60.12% reduction in refusal rate on adversarial prompts while preserving Arena-Hard scores. Reviewer opinions diverge: two reviewers (2, 4) rate the work highly (5/5), finding the research question interesting and the method effective; the other two (1, 3) are less enthusiastic (3/5), citing limited contribution, weak motivation, and missing demonstrations. All reviewers note the paper touches an important area but lacks compelling evidence of practical use.

### Strengths
- The research question is interesting and meaningful: understanding what happens when alignment is removed and how to build a model that is helpful but not harmless (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- The paper is well-organized, clearly written, and well-situated within existing literature (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The method is simple (data-level filtering) and effective: results show a dramatic reduction in refusal rates (e.g., 60.12% decrease) while maintaining general problem-solving ability on Arena-Hard (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- The approach would likely outperform baselines like Shadow Alignment and Unalignment on refusal rates and exaggerated safety, because it focuses on being helpful by design rather than subverting safety alignment (Reviewer 1).
- The work can help researchers acknowledge potential failures of LLMs and the consequences of total misalignment (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of clear motivation and problem statement**: The paper does not convincingly explain why a Helpful-Only LLM is needed or how it should be used. No practical applications or use cases are demonstrated (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Limited novelty**: The refusal-free training (filtering) is standard and not technically novel. A reasonable alternative would simply exclude safety-related data from the alignment stage entirely, making the method’s contribution marginal (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Insufficient evaluation**: The model is only evaluated on Arena-Hard for general capability and on an adversarial benchmark for refusal rate. This is not comprehensive; e.g., standard benchmarks like MMLU, HumanEval, BBH, GSM8K are missing (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Unsubstantiated claim about catastrophic forgetting**: The paper asserts that fine-tuning “suffers from catastrophic forgetting,” but prior work (Bhardwaj et al., Yang et al.) shows generic utility can be largely maintained (see Table 5 in Bhardwaj, Section 5.3 in Yang). This claim needs evidence and comparison on utility benchmarks (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of insights about model behavior**: No analysis of where the helpful-only LLM is better than an aligned model, how it fails on safety, or specifically which types of harmful prompts it does not refuse. Results are simply reported as aggregate refusal rates (Reviewer 2).
- **Accuracy of refusal judge model is concerning**: The refusal-rate evaluation relies on a judge model (Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2) whose accuracy under various tasks is not validated. This could make experimental results unreliable (Reviewer 4).
- **Contradiction in method description**: Figure 2 shows keyword filtering in the RL step, but line 254 states “extending this keyword extraction process to RL did not result in significant differences; therefore, we extracted keywords solely during the SFT stage.” This is confusing and needs clarification (Reviewer 4).
- **Unexplained residual refusal**: The final model still refuses 2.68% of prompts. The paper does not explain why (e.g., incomplete filtering, dataset artifacts). A refusal testset should be constructed to evaluate filtering accuracy (Reviewer 4).
- **Results are less exciting than expected**: Refusal-free training does not improve helpfulness (Arena-Hard scores are comparable), and the criteria for helpfulness are unclear. For example, what score does the judge assign to a correct but harmful answer (e.g., bomb-making instructions) vs. a refusal? This needs deeper analysis (Reviewer 4).
- **Filtering vs. rewriting**: The paper filters out refusal responses, which drops many useful prompts. An alternative is to rewrite refusal responses instead. The advantages of filtering over rewriting are not discussed (Reviewer 4).
- **Potential data leakage in keyword extraction**: The evaluation dataset used in keyword extraction is not specified. If it is the same evaluation set used in Table 4, the process is flawed because keywords are extracted from evaluation responses (Reviewer 2). Additionally, in Table 4 row 2, the Magpie align dataset already shows a low refusal rate without any filtering; the paper does not explain why (Reviewer 2).
- **Writing can be improved**: The language and introduction need better motivation and clarity (Reviewer 2).
