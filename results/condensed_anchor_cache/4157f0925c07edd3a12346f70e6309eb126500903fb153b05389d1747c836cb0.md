## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces NIMBA, a Mamba-based approach for 3D point cloud analysis that converts point clouds into 1D sequences via a novel reordering strategy. The claimed contributions are that this conversion preserves spatial structure without positional embeddings or data replication, achieving state-of-the-art results on ModelNet40 and ScanObjectNN while surpassing transformer-based models in accuracy and efficiency and offering improved robustness to transformations. Reviewers acknowledge the simplicity and value of the idea but raise significant concerns about the validity of claims (e.g., removal of positional embeddings), missing comparisons (especially with updated PointMamba, transformer methods like PointBert, and other SSMs), scalability limitations, sensitivity to hyperparameters (threshold *r*), insufficient computational cost analysis, and performance degradation in hybrid architectures.

### Strengths
1. The target of removing positional embeddings and avoiding data replication is valuable and makes the model more principled (R2, R3).
2. Introducing Mamba to point clouds is non-trivial; improving efficiency and robustness is important (R1).
3. The linear complexity of SSMs like Mamba (vs. quadratic attention) gives NIMBA scalability for high-resolution 3D data (R3).
4. NIMBA demonstrates enhanced robustness to common data transformations (rotations, jittering), which is crucial for real-world applications (R1, R3).
5. The idea is simple, the paper is easy to follow, and the overall writing is fluent (R1, R2).
6. The methodology is well-structured with clear comparisons to prior work (R4). The method achieves state-of-the-art results on multiple datasets (R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Positional Embedding Claim Contradiction**  
   The paper asserts that NIMBA does not rely on positional embeddings (PE, line 97), but the ablation study in Section 4.3.1 shows better performance *with* PE, and Table 2 appears to include PE. Since PE is computationally cheap, the claim that NIMBA can contribute without it is undermined; the authors should clarify the role of PE and the impact of omitting it (R1).

2. **Incomplete/Misaligned Comparisons**  
   - PointMamba (the main comparator) has been updated after NeurIPS acceptance; the paper compares against an older version. Comparisons should be made with the latest PointMamba architecture and methodology (R1).  
   - The reordering strategy is not compared with those in Point Cloud Mamba (the prior work discusses many serialization methods). No specific discussion or ablation of alternative strategies is provided (R3).  
   - The paper claims to surpass transformer-based methods but lacks comparisons with key transformer baselines such as PointBert and PointM2AE. A more comprehensive evaluation across both transformer-based and other SSM-based methods (e.g., references [1],[2] in Reviewer 3) is needed to substantiate the claim (R3).  
   - No comparison with other Mamba-based or SSM-based point cloud models beyond a few baselines (R3).

3. **Limited Scaling and Hybrid Architecture Issues**  
   - NIMBA shows limited performance improvement when scaled (larger models), suggesting a potential bottleneck in the sequence conversion or the Mamba architecture itself. Effectiveness should be verified on larger point cloud datasets such as nuScenes and Waymo (R4, also noted by R3).  
   - Performance declines when replacing the Mamba block with the Hydra block (Mamba2) or in hybrid architectures, indicating compatibility issues. The reasons for this drop need investigation (R3, R4).

4. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity and Reordering Concerns**  
   - The proximity threshold *r* (fixed at 0.8) is manually chosen and may not generalize to varying point densities or object scales. An adaptive approach to determine neighborhood relationships would be more robust (R2). The authors should report sensitivity to *r* and the rationale for choosing 0.8 (R3, R4).  
   - Ordering points along a single axis may be suboptimal for 3D data; it risks losing spatial information compared to a method that considers inter-point relationships in 3D space (R2).

5. **Global Modeling with Causal Mamba**  
   - The causal (uni-directional) nature of Mamba processes the sequence sequentially, but point patches may still struggle to interact with each other. The authors should explain how NIMBA achieves effective global modeling despite this limitation (R1).

6. **Lack of Computational Cost Analysis**  
   - The paper claims efficiency improvements from avoiding data replication but provides no detailed inference time analysis, training time, or memory usage comparison against methods that replicate data. The computational cost of the reordering strategy itself should be included (R2, R3). Efficiency claims for large-scale datasets are unsubstantiated without such metrics (R3).

7. **Minor Issues and Typos**  
   - Typos: “positional emebddings” (line 99), “flattered by ordering” (line 352), “environment” (line 377), “conclusion and Fig. 2 show” (line 523) (R1, R4).  
   - Caption in Table 5 appears inaccurate; missing periods at line ends (lines 339, 360) (R1).  
   - Some formatting issues (R4).

8. **Other Weaknesses**  
   - The ablation study could be strengthened by including stronger data augmentation and pre-training fine-tuning to show the upper bound of performance (R1).  
   - The removal of positional embeddings raises questions about model interpretability and whether learned representations are easily understood (R3).  
   - Figure 2 may illustrate feeding wrong ordering centers to Mamba layers; this should be clarified (R2).
