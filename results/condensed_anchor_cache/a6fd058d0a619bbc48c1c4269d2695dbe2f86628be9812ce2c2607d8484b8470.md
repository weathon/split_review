## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies Batched Normalized Stochastic Gradient Descent with Momentum (NSGDM) for nonconvex optimization under heavy-tailed noise where only a finite \(p\)-th moment (\(p\in(1,2]\)) is assumed. It provides convergence guarantees without gradient clipping, achieving the optimal \(\mathcal{O}(T^{(1-p)/(3p-2)})\) rate under \((L_0,L_1)\)-smoothness and a noise bound that can depend on the gradient norm (allowing \(\sigma_1>0\)). A second result gives an \(\mathcal{O}(T^{(1-p)/(2p)})\) rate without prior knowledge of the tail index \(p\), which is the first such result. The proof introduces a new vector-valued martingale inequality (Lemma 4.3) that may be of independent interest. The paper also refines a known lower bound from prior work.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important question: whether gradient clipping is necessary for convergence under heavy-tailed noise. Showing that NSGDM works without clipping provides a new perspective and contribution to the field.
- Theorem 3.6 gives the first convergence result in this setting without knowledge of the tail index \(p\), demonstrating robustness.
- The results are general: they cover \((L_0,L_1)\)-smoothness (relaxing standard smoothness and matching practical neural network training) and a noise bound that can depend on the gradient norm (\(\sigma_1>0\)), going beyond the uniform boundedness assumption of prior work.
- Lemma 4.3 generalizes a known inequality with a simpler proof; this inequality may be useful in other contexts.
- The paper is well written, with intuitive explanations and a clear proof sketch. No mistakes were found in the proofs.
- The lower bound refinement (Theorem 3.3) adds context, though it is largely dependent on prior proofs.
- The unknown-\(p\) result is notable and opens a direction for parameter-agnostic optimization under heavy tails.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of numerical experiments.** Even toy experiments (e.g., dependence on \(\beta\), tightness of the unknown-\(p\) rate) would help illustrate the behavior of NSGDM and support practical relevance. Several reviewers considered this a significant omission, especially since the algorithm is not commonly used and the advantage of normalization over clipping is not empirically demonstrated.
- **Motivation for replacing clipping with normalization is not strongly argued.** One reviewer noted that other procedures (median, sign SGD) could also be considered, and the practical benefit of removing clipping is not compelling.
- **Only in-expectation convergence bounds are provided; high-probability bounds (common in recent heavy-tail literature) are not given.**
- **Batch size restriction.** When \(\sigma_1>0\), the results require \(B>1\); it is unclear if \(B=1\) is fundamentally impossible or just a proof limitation. In the known-\(p\) case (Theorem 3.2), \(B=1\) is used. More discussion or a counterexample is needed.
- **Parameter knowledge requirements.** When \(\sigma_1>0\), Theorem D.1 requires exact knowledge of \(\|\nabla F(x_1)\|\) and all other parameters, not just upper bounds. Can the results be extended to require only upper bounds?
- **The unknown-\(p\) rate \(\mathcal{O}(T^{-(p-1)/(2p)})\) is not optimal; it is unknown whether it is tight for NSGDM.** The paper does not address optimality in this adaptive setting.
- **Insufficient discussion of relation to prior work.** Specific requests: clarify differences between Lemma 4.3 and Lemma 7 of Kornilov et al.; compare to Hübler et al. (2024) on parameter-agnostic optimization under relaxed smoothness; explain how the lower bound construction (Theorem 3.3) differs from Arjevani et al. (2023) and that it depends on \(\varepsilon\); provide reference for the claim in line 334 (“following the existing literature”).
- **The lower bound refinement is largely incremental**, relying heavily on previous proofs.
- **Batched NSGDM is not commonly used in practice; the paper suggests adaptive methods as future work.** The novelty of the algorithm (new or pre-existing) should be clarified. If it is newly proposed, experiments are even more necessary to validate it.
- **The claim of “without gradient clipping” is somewhat misleading** because normalization is a related operation; the practical advantage over clipping is not established.
- **Minor issues:** typos (“initial”→“initiate”, line 325 “smooth”→“smoothness”, duplicate reference in line 48, title of Appendix C says 4.4 instead of 4.3); missing definition of \(m_t\) when \(m_t=0\) in Algorithm 1; unclear step (line 336: how is \(\mathbb{E}\langle \epsilon_t^b,\epsilon_t^u\rangle=0\)?); the assumption in line 809 about \(\|x-y\|\le c/L_1\) vs \(\|x-y\|\le 1/L_1\) needs clarification; missing reference for “following the existing literature” (line 334); AM-GM step in line 855 should be spelled out.
- **Could the results be generalized to convex problems?** (One reviewer indicated this would significantly increase the rating.)
- **The paper does not explore horizon-independent (anytime) parameter choices;** can the results be made independent of total steps \(T\)?
- **Disagreement among reviewers:** While some reviewers praised the theoretical contribution and the novelty of avoiding clipping, others found the practical motivation for replacing clipping with normalization unattractive and noted the absence of high-probability bounds and experiments as significant shortcomings.
