## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces OCCVAR, a generative occupancy world model for autonomous driving that uses a spatial-temporal transformer with temporal next-scale prediction (TNSP) to predict 4D occupancy scenes from coarse to fine scales. It incorporates ego motion into the tokenized occupancy sequence for controllable scene generation and uses a multi-scale tokenizer to capture hierarchical 3D geometry. The model aims to address inefficiency and temporal degradation in long-time generation compared to prior autoregressive approaches. Experiments are conducted on nuScenes and Occ3D datasets.

### Strengths
1. The coarse-to-fine autoregressive mechanism (temporal next-scale prediction) addresses inefficiency and spatial degradation issues in previous autoregressive occupancy world models.
2. Encoding ego-vehicle motion into the occupancy sequence enables prediction of ego motion and controllable scene generation.
3. The multi-scale tokenizer captures hierarchical information of 3D scenes, enhancing fine-grained geometry modeling and reconstruction quality.
4. OCCVAR achieves scalable 4D occupancy world modeling, which is crucial for simulating ego vehicle movement and environment evolution over time.
5. The paper discusses two critical issues in occupancy world models: efficiency and long-term generation.

*Note: Reviewer 2 is more positive (score 6 vs 3 from others), endorsing the overall design and its potential, while other reviewers consider the contributions incremental.*

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited novelty**: The coarse-to-fine approach was already introduced by SurroundOcc [1], OpenOccupancy [2], and Pyramid Diffusion [3]. The multi-scale scene tokenizer is directly adapted from VAR, and OccWorld already uses spatial and temporal attention. Overall, the paper applies VAR to OccWorld without substantial architectural improvements.
2. **Planning performance**: OCCVAR achieves worse motion planning performance than OccWorld and camera-input methods like UniAD and VAD, both with occupancy ground truth input. The paper does not adequately explain why planning is inferior to OccWorld despite similar occupancy representations. A question remains about whether using a continuous ego token would improve planning and surpass OccWorld.
3. **Missing ablation studies**: The paper lacks comprehensive ablation studies to validate the contribution of individual components, such as the impact of different numbers of scales in the tokenizer, the role of next-scale prediction in achieving higher mIoU, or the effect of varying the training regime.
4. **Efficiency concerns**:
   - Table 4 shows that OCCVAR’s latency is 1.6× higher than OccWorld, contradicting the claim of addressing inefficiency.
   - The efficiency comparison in Table 4 is confusing: generation of VAR includes that of direct AR, yet VAR’s inference time is reported shorter than AR’s; further explanation is needed.
   - The parameter comparison in Table 4 is also confusing, as autonomous driving models typically have ~100M parameters.
   - Memory overhead is not reported for the compared methods; it should be included.
5. **Long-term generation**: OCCVAR is compared only to OccWorld for long-term generation, neglecting contemporaries like OccSora and DOME. Both OccSora and DOME also support controllable occupancy generation and long-term forecasting (DOME claims up to 32 seconds). No quantitative comparison is provided, so it is unclear whether OCCVAR offers any advantage in long-term quality or efficiency. Can OCCVAR achieve better long-term generation than OccSora? Can it be comparable to DOME?
6. **Reconstruction and forecasting accuracy**: The slight improvement in L2 prediction accuracy at the 1-second mark over other methods is insufficient to demonstrate effectiveness. Moreover, OCCVAR appears significantly less accurate than DOME in 4D occupancy forecasting, and no direct comparison is made.
7. **Clarity issues**:
   - Figure 1: “Pose Control” is not explained; the scene rotation in the top-right section is unclear.
   - Figure 2 references a “robust tokenizer” without demonstrating how robustness is measured or reflected.
   - The paper claims “scalable” in the title but does not clearly define or demonstrate scalability (e.g., scaling with scene size, number of tokens, or time steps).
8. **Questionable advantage of coarse-to-fine for prediction**: The authors should explain why coarse-to-fine prediction performs better than OccWorld and similar methods only in the first step of motion planning but falls behind in subsequent steps, contradicting the claimed advantage for long-term generation.
9. **Insufficient comparison with OccSora**: OccSora is also capable of controllable occupancy generation and long-term generation, but no quantitative comparison is provided.
