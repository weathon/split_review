## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LucidPPN, a prototypical parts network that decouples color prototypes from shape/texture using two separate branches (ShapeTexNet on grayscale, ColorNet on color), then fuses via element-wise product. A prototypical-object part correspondence loss aligns prototypes with semantic object parts (using pre-trained PDiscoNet). Experiments on CUB-200-2011, Stanford Cars, Stanford Dogs, Oxford Flowers show competitive accuracy, and a user study indicates less ambiguous explanations than PIP-Net.

### Strengths
- **Novelty**: First explicit disentanglement of color from other visual attributes in prototypical parts, reducing prototype ambiguity. (Reviewers 1,2,3)
- **Intuitive architecture**: Two-branch design is well-structured and easy to understand. (Reviewers 1,4)
- **User study**: Empirically demonstrates that LucidPPN explanations are clearer than PIP-Net, increasing persuasiveness. (Reviewer 1)
- **Rich visualizations**: Sufficient cases and visualizations (including appendix) validate the semantic meaning of learned prototypes. (Reviewers 3,4)
- **Reproducibility**: Code is provided. (Reviewer 3)
- **Clarity**: Paper is well-written and easy to follow. (Reviewers 2,3)
- **Significance**: Interpretability is an important research area. (Reviewer 2)

### Weaknesses
- **Performance trade-offs**:
  - Accuracy is lower than baselines on some datasets; on CUB, LucidPPN is below all three compared methods, with no explanation for the gap. (Reviewers 1,4)
  - Missing baseline results on Dogs and Flowers in Table 1 hinder assessment of generalizability. (Reviewer 3)
  - Late fusion of branches may lose crucial color-shape-texture interplay compared to early fusion. (Reviewer 4)
- **Novelty justification insufficient**:
  - Shallow CNN layers already capture Gabor-like filters and color opponency; the method’s added value beyond implicit low-level feature extraction needs stronger justification. (Reviewer 4)
  - Other low-level features (edges, contrast, spatial frequency) are not considered. (Reviewer 4)
  - Relation to concept bottleneck models (CBMs) is not discussed; comparison on human interpretability and causal explanations is missing. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing prototype projection**:
  - No prototype projection step is included; closest training images may be far from prototypes in latent space, potentially making visualizations unrepresentative. (Reviewer 2) *(Note: one reviewer [score 8] was more positive overall but still raised this concern.)*
- **Clarity and organization issues**:
  - Section 3 lacks subheadings, making it hard to follow. (Reviewer 1)
  - Ambiguity whether the prototypical-object part correspondence loss aligns segmentation masks with ShapeTexNet feature maps or aggregated (fused) maps; unclear whether color influences part correspondence. (Reviewer 2)
  - Experimental section unbalanced: excessive content in appendices, main body lacks comprehensive analysis of results. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing experiments and evaluations**:
  - Only CNN backbones are tested; results with Vision Transformers (ViT, CLIP) are needed to demonstrate versatility. (Reviewer 3)
  - Scalability to larger datasets (e.g., ImageNet) is not addressed. (Reviewer 4)
  - No quantitative evaluation of interpretability: temporal stability of prototypes, prototype drift, consistency of explanations are not analyzed. (Reviewer 3)
  - Computational cost (training time, FLOPs, memory) is not compared with baselines. (Reviewer 3)
  - Reason for using binary cross-entropy instead of multi-class cross-entropy is not clarified. (Reviewer 2)
  - Comparative visualizations with other prototypical parts models (e.g., ProtoPNet, PIP-Net) are not shown. (Reviewer 4)
  - No comparison to traditional feature extraction (Sobel for shape, color histograms, Grad-CAM attention). (Reviewer 4)
