## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LLEGO, a framework that integrates Large Language Models (LLMs) into evolutionary genetic programming (GP) for decision tree induction. LLMs are used as conditional generative models to implement fitness-guided crossover and diversity-guided mutation operators, with decision trees represented in natural language. The method is compared against several decision tree induction baselines on classification and regression benchmarks, demonstrating superior generalization performance and more efficient search than traditional GP.

### Strengths
- The integration of LLMs into genetic programming for decision tree induction is novel and promising (R1, R2, R4).
- The paper is well-written, clearly motivated, and easy to follow, with good visualizations (R1, R4).
- Experiments are detailed, fitting, and include comparisons against a solid set of baselines; additional rigorous results on fairness and generalization are provided (R1).
- Appendix D.2 addresses the concern that results might be conditional on solutions existing in the LLM training set (R1).
- The introduction of fitness-guided crossover and diversity-guided mutation is well-motivated (R4).
- The method shows strong empirical performance relative to conventional GP and other tree induction methods (R3: "promising results compared to traditional evolutionary algorithms and optimal decision tree induction methods"; R4: "strong empirical performance").
- The paper explains the LLEGO framework in detail, including the end-to-end algorithm (R2).
- The approach addresses limitations of existing decision tree induction methods (greedy suboptimal, expensive exact optimization) (R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of theoretical analysis**: There is no rigorous explanation of how and why semantic priors from LLMs improve search efficiency and decision tree quality; this weakens the scientific contribution (R2, R3).
- **Over-reliance on LLM priors**: The method depends heavily on the semantic priors in the LLM; performance may degrade if the LLM lacks relevant domain knowledge; the authors do not sufficiently discuss this limitation or mitigation strategies (R2). A minority reviewer (R3) questions whether the mechanism is understood at all, and whether the method will fail if OpenAI changes its models significantly in the future.
- **Motivation for using LLMs at shallow depths**: The paper claims exact methods are computationally complex (exponential in depth and splits), but experiments use depths 3–4, where optimal methods like DL8.5 can find optimal trees in 19/24 cases within 10 minutes. It is unclear why expensive LLMs are needed here (R3).  
- **Unclear why LLMs generalize better**: The paper does not convincingly demonstrate why LLMs can effectively split the feature space recursively (R3). In the spurious correlation example, the LLM was explicitly told which features were spurious—such prior knowledge should ideally be used to remove features before modeling (R3).  
- **Insufficient ablation and baselines**:
  - No ablation study to see if fitness guidance in crossover is necessary (e.g., simply prompting "improve on the above trees") (R4).
  - No baseline using fitness-guided mutation alone (without crossover) to motivate the exploration/exploration split (R1).
  - The term "LLEGO_no_prior" is undefined; no comparison to vanilla LLM evolution without semantic guidance in crossover or diversity guidance in mutation (R4).
- **Population diversity concerns**:
  - Fitness-proportionate (roulette wheel) selection is outdated and known to reduce diversity; tournament or lexicase selection might better balance fitness and diversity (R1).
  - The designed diversity-guided mutation fails to maintain sufficient diversity—LLEGO sacrifices diversity compared to traditional GP, and implementing mutation alone may not preserve it (R4).  
- **Validation of diversity guidance**: It is not experimentally verified that within-population solutions consistently have higher log probabilities than out-of-population solutions (R4).
- **Syntactic validity (~86%)**: The method only produces syntactically valid decision trees ~86% of the time from the LLM operators; the authors do not propose concrete ways to improve this (R1).
- **Limited generalizability assessment**:
  - It is unclear whether LLEGO can be applied to other GP tasks like symbolic regression or program synthesis; no preliminary results are provided (R1, R2, R4).  
  - Only tested on GPT-3.5 and GPT-4; experiments with other models (e.g., Claude) are missing; sensitivity analysis to different LLMs is needed (R3).
- **Novelty concerns from minority reviewer (R3)**: The idea of using LLMs for evolving programs with diversity considerations has been explored before (e.g., GECCO 2024 "LLMatic: Neural Architecture Search via Large Language Models and Quality-Diversity Optimization"), and the paper should discuss this prior work and its overlap.
- **Training accuracy not reported**: It is hard to understand why LLEGO outperforms optimal trees at shallow depths without comparing training accuracy (R3).
- **Table 7 conclusion conflicts**: Table 7 shows that LLMs work well even when semantic priors are removed, which undermines the claim that semantic information is the key to performance. The authors should reconsider their argument (R3).
- **Terminology issue**: The term "semantic information" is used differently than the standard meaning in genetic programming; the authors should either change the term or clarify the distinction to avoid confusion (R3).
- **Missing related work discussion**: Recent work such as ReEvo (NeurIPS 2024), which incorporates semantic priors generated by LLM reflections into evolution, should be discussed in relation to LLEGO (R4).
- **Computational overhead**: No detailed analysis of runtime and memory usage compared to traditional GP; it is unclear when the increased cost is justified (R2).
- **Typo**: Section 3: "buid" should be "build" (R1).
- **Missing experiments**: The method is evaluated only with CART initialization on 25% of the training data; how well would it work without that initialization? (R1). Also, random selection in diversity-guided mutation is insufficient; consider quality-diversity optimization for parent selection (R3).
