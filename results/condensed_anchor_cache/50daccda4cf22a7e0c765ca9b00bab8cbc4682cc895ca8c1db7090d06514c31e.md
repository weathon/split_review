## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces a benchmark covering three PDDL domains (Blocksworld, Logistics, Mini-Grid) and two natural-language domains (Trip Planning, Calendar Scheduling). It evaluates LLMs (Gemma 2 27B, Gemini 1.5 Pro/Flash, GPT-4-turbo) under many-shot in-context learning (1–400 examples), inference-time techniques (ToT, MCTS, Debate on NL domains), and supervised fine-tuning on optimal plans. The work also probes out-of-distribution generalization and failure modes. Reviewers acknowledge the scale of experiments but raise major concerns about novelty, translation quality, overfitting, and missing analyses.

### Strengths
- Conslidated benchmark covering PDDL and NL planning domains with systematic instance generation (R1).  
- First work to analyze fine-tuning on optimal solutions and generalization across problem categories (e.g., training on 3–7 blocks, testing on 8–9) (R1).  
- Extensive experiments: many-shot ICL from 1–400 examples (R3), three inference-time ICL methods (ToT, MCTS, Debate) on NL domains (R3), and ID/OOD fine-tuning splits (R4).  
- Large-scale evaluation with newer models (Gemma 2, Gemini 1.5, GPT-4-turbo) that substantiates and occasionally deviates from past findings (R4).  
- Clear writing (R4).  
- Some experiments suggest that newer models may behave differently from older ones (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty is severely limited.** The benchmark primarily repackages existing PDDL (PlanBench) and travel planning datasets with a few new test problems; it is described as “packaging two domains together” rather than a genuinely new contribution (R1). The main findings—many-shot prompting boosts performance, inference-time techniques help, SFT works, and failure patterns—have all been individually demonstrated in prior work (R4). The three insights in the conclusion (superiority of SFT, curriculum learning effectiveness, limits of hard examples) were already reported by Lehnert et al. (2024) using a better process-supervision approach (R4). Essential related work is omitted: “Thought of Search” (Katz et al., 2024) achieves 100% planning via search steering, and Pallagani et al. (2023) provides similar SFT conclusions and plan generalization analyses (R2). The paper reads as “a summary of well-investigated trends” (R4).
- **Dataset construction and translation are flawed.** The NL translations of PDDL are generated by hand-crafted regular expressions, not human-written text, raising doubts about whether they teach meaningful reasoning (R3). The prompt does **not** include the PDDL domain (actions, preconditions, effects), so it is unclear how an LLM can infer the domain rules (R2). The three classical domains are minimal PDDL (essentially STRIPS with negative preconditions); the work does not address richer PDDL features (R3). If the goal is to treat LLMs as symbolic planners, the problem instances are far too small (R3).
- **Fine-tuning results show overfitting, not generalization.** Near-perfect accuracy in Tables 3 and 4 occurs only when training and evaluation problems have very similar characteristics (e.g., same number of blocks). This is classic overfitting and does not demonstrate valid generalization (R1). The fine-tuning experiments use only 5 instances per setup; details on how initial states, goals, and object names are varied for generalization are missing (R3). For the plan generalization experiments (Table 2, finetuning on fewer blocks and testing on more), no average horizon lengths or per-length performance breakdowns are provided, making it impossible to assess length generalization (R4).
- **Key experiments are missing or incomplete.** Inference-time techniques (ToT, MCTS, Debate) are only applied to NL domains; results on PDDL domains are absent (R3). ReAct experiments are also missing (R4). The “agent-based environment” for TripPlanning is mentioned but never evaluated (R1). Although the paper claims a 5-dataset benchmark suite, most experiments focus on Blocksworld and Logistics; Mini-Grid is only used for many-shot, and TravelPlan/CalendarPlan only for many-shot and inference-time—undermining the claim of a comprehensive benchmark (R4). The overlap between many-shot examples and test examples (e.g., same block configurations) is not analyzed, leaving doubt about whether performance gains come from memorization (R2, R4). No discussion is provided for why smaller models sometimes outperform larger ones (R1).
- **OOD definition is unclear.** The criteria for in-distribution vs. out-of-distribution are not specified; they appear to rely solely on the number of blocks in Blocksworld (R1). In Figure 6, it is unclear what “out-of-distribution” means relative to the original distribution (R3).
- **Methodological details are missing.** The heuristic used by FastDownward to generate optimal plans is not reported (R2). No cost breakdown of experiments is given (R4). The fundamental purpose of using LLMs for planning is questioned when the 400-shot prompt is itself generated by a symbolic planner (R2).
- **Evaluation framing is unjustified.** The paper gives LLMs the benefit of the doubt by relying on careful prompt engineering and data curation, rather than evaluating naive settings. Failure modes under straightforward conditions would be more constructive for improving planning (R1).
- **Contrast between reviewers.** While R3 and R4 assign moderate scores (3) and note some value (large-scale experiments, clear presentation), R1 and R2 are strongly negative (1), citing lack of novelty and serious methodological flaws. Even the more positive minority agrees that novelty is weak and experiments are incomplete.
