- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 6, 1, 3, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a classification-based derivative-free optimization algorithm, RACE-CARS, which adds a random region-shrinking step to the existing SRACOS algorithm. It introduces the concept of hypothesis-target shattering rate to revisit the computational complexity upper bound of such algorithms. A theorem is established showing acceleration due to region-shrinking. Experiments on synthetic functions and black-box tuning for language-model-as-a-service on SST2 are provided, along with an ablation study.

### Strengths
- Well written with rigorous theoretical analysis (Reviewer 1).
- New theoretical bounds showing superiority of RACE-CARS over SRACOS and a new general bound on complexity under the assumption of η-shattering (Reviewer 1).
- Classification-based derivative-free optimization is an interesting alternative compared with Bayesian optimization and zeroth-order optimization (Reviewer 3).
- Empirical results on four synthetic functions and black-box tuning for language-model-as-a-service on the SST2 dataset show faster convergence compared with baselines (Reviewer 3).
- Originality in using the learning theoretic concept of shattering to derive bounds for classification-based DFO (Reviewer 4).
- Quality: experiments on a fairly large-scale LLM problem in addition to standard DFO benchmarks; results confirm theoretical results and RACE-CARS improves significantly over SRACOS (Reviewer 4).
- Significance: zero-order optimization is increasingly popular, especially for hyperparameter optimization, a common practitioner task (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- The paper is very hard to read (Reviewer 2). The reviewer encounters difficulty in comprehending algorithm descriptions due to missing essential sub-procedure details, rendering the paper challenging to read (Reviewer 2).
- The main results (numerical experiments) are placed in the Appendix rather than the main text. Reviewers are not required to read the appendix, which constitutes a primary reason for rejection (Reviewer 2). Reviewers 3 and 4 also note this: experimental graphs should be in the main paper (Reviewers 3, 4).
- For clarity, line numbers are not visible in Algorithms 1 and 2 but are referred to on page 6 (Reviewer 4). The description of the region shrinking process in RACE-CARS could be more detailed, including how shrinking is implemented and how the shrinking rate is chosen (Reviewer 4).
- Theoretical results for RACE-CARS rely on the assumption of dimensionally local Holder continuity to establish the η-shattering property, while SRACOS and its bounds do not require this assumption. This discrepancy limits the generality of the theoretical results. Sufficient conditions for η-shattering are established only for Holder continuous functions, making the results less general than SRACOS (Reviewer 1). It would be beneficial to see evaluation on functions that do not satisfy Holder continuity, such as those with discontinuities or other non-Holder continuous behaviors (Reviewer 1). The experimental evaluation focuses solely on functions satisfying Holder continuity (even if constants are unknown) (Reviewer 1).
- The query complexity in Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 grows exponentially with dimension \(n\). For example, consider \(\Omega = [-1,1]^n\) and \(f(\mathbf{x}) = \|\mathbf{x}\|_1\); then \(|\Omega_\epsilon| = \mathbb{P}(\Omega_\epsilon) \le \epsilon^n\), so the term \(\frac{1}{|\Omega_\epsilon|}\) equals \((\frac{1}{\epsilon})^n\), exponential for \(\epsilon < 1\). This severely limits practical applicability in high-dimensional problems (Reviewer 3).
- The Hypothesis-Target η-Shattering assumption (Definition 3.1) together with the γ-Shrinking assumption (Definition 2.3) are too strong to be practical. The η-Shattering assumption requires the classifier to have at least η overlap with the true ε-solution domain \(\Omega_\epsilon\), which decays exponentially with dimension, demanding very high classifier accuracy. The γ-Shrinking assumption forces the positive-prediction region to decay exponentially with iteration count (\( \gamma^t \)), yet η-Shattering still requires η overlap with the shrinking \(\Omega_\epsilon\). Achieving such a strong classifier with limited training samples in high dimensions is challenging (Reviewer 3).
- No convergence guarantee as \(t \to \infty\). In Theorem 3.3, the left-hand side decays exponentially, but the right-hand side does not guarantee exponential decay. This lack of formal convergence guarantee makes it difficult to assess long-term behavior (Reviewer 3).
- No clear discussion of the computational complexity of the algorithms; only an observation that CMA-ES is more expensive than SRACOS or RACE-CARS. A detailed analysis of time complexity, including dependence on dimensionality and number of iterations, is needed. The number of function evaluations and their cost should be explicitly stated (Reviewer 4). The question about how to sample \((x_t, y_t) \sim \mathbf{Y}_t\) in Algorithm 2 and its computational cost is not addressed (Reviewer 3).
- In the black-box tuning task, only the SST2 dataset is employed. Evaluation on a single dataset is not convincing enough to support general claims. Results may not generalize to other datasets or tasks (Reviewer 3). The reviewer requests comparison with other datasets used in (Sun et al. 2022) (Reviewer 3). The LLM experiment also lacks baselines DE and ZO-Adam, making it difficult to assess relative performance against a broader range of optimization techniques (Reviewer 4). Similarly, no Bayesian optimization baseline was tried (Reviewer 4 question).
- Since Holder continuity is assumed, the reviewer notes that SGLD with 0-th order gradient estimator should theoretically work (Chatterji et al.) and asks for the authors' thoughts on that (Reviewer 1).
- Reviewer 4 questions whether the use of the shattering rate removes all issues of the previous bound discussed in Section 3.1 and whether the bound is now tight; this remains unclear (Reviewer 4).
