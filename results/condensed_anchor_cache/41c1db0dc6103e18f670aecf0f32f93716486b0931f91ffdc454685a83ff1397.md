## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces the Temporal Graph Scaling (TGS) dataset of 84 ERC20 token transaction networks, proposes the TGS-train algorithm for pre-training Temporal Graph Neural Networks (TGNNs) across multiple temporal graphs, and studies transferability to unseen networks. It claims the first empirical demonstration of neural scaling laws in temporal graph learning: pre-training on more networks improves downstream zero-shot performance, with the largest pre-trained model outperforming single-model TGNNs on 13 of 20 unseen token networks.

### Strengths

- **New benchmark for temporal graph scaling**: The TGS dataset provides 84 ERC20 token transaction networks collected from 2017 to 2023, a rich resource for studying scaling and foundation models in temporal graph learning (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Multi-graph training algorithm (TGS-train)**: The proposed method enables pre-training TGNNs on multiple temporal graphs and shows strong effectiveness on the new benchmark, advancing multi-graph generalization (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **First empirical evidence of neural scaling law in temporal graph learning**: Demonstrates that pre-training on a larger number of networks improves downstream performance, following the scaling pattern observed in NLP and CV (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Demonstration of transferability**: The pre-trained model outperforms fine-tuned TGNNs on 13/20 unseen test networks via zero-shot inference, a promising step toward foundation models for temporal graphs (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Clear topic and writing**: The paper is well-written and easy to follow, and its topic is timely for graph foundation model research (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient ablation on scaling effects**: Ablation of memory resetting and data shuffling is only performed on MN-4 and MN-8, not on larger networks (MN-32, MN-64), limiting understanding of scaling robustness (Reviewer 1).
- **Narrow evaluation on prediction tasks**: Only network growth (edge counts) is used as the prediction target. Standard temporal graph tasks like node classification and link prediction are missing, reducing benchmark applicability and generalizability of findings (Reviewer 1).
- **Unclear technical details**: The mechanism and benefit of “random historical embedding” for mitigating catastrophic forgetting are not explained (Reviewer 1). Methodology lacks equations and precise definitions for temporal global efficiency, temporal-correlation coefficient, and temporal betweenness centrality (Reviewers 3, 4). Problem statement in Section 5 is unclear (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited domain transferability**: The method is only evaluated on ERC20 token transaction networks; it is unclear how it would extend to other domains (e.g., social networks, biological interaction networks) with different structural and temporal properties. No analysis of needed adaptations is provided (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited technical novelty in the algorithm**: TGS-train is based on HTGN with only two added mechanisms—shuffling and memory resets. Differences from the original HTGN are not clearly described, and the technical contribution of these mechanisms is considered straightforward (Reviewer 2). No new architecture or algorithm is proposed (Reviewer 3).
- **Dataset comprehensiveness questioned**: The TGS dataset is compared unfavorably to existing temporal datasets like DBLP and Stack Overflow from WinGNN (Reviewer 3). Additionally, different token networks share many nodes (addresses), raising the possibility of merging them into a single large graph, which would undermine the multi-network assumption (Reviewer 4).
- **Overstated claims**: The title and focus suggest neural scaling laws, but the experiments only vary training data quantity—model size and depth are not scaled, making the claim overexaggerated for the scope of work (Reviewer 4). Table 2 alone is insufficient to verify the strong standpoint (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons**: The paper does not include models from TGB and DyGLib, which are standard benchmarks for temporal graphs (Reviewer 4).
- **Problem setting not truly zero-shot**: Because training and evaluation graphs share nodes (addresses), the model gains prior knowledge of node behavior from training networks (e.g., tokens A and B) and applies it to token C. This prior knowledge means the setting is not zero-shot as claimed (Reviewer 4). The paper does not explicitly state early that training and evaluation graphs are disjoint; this is only clarified in Appendix E, which should be in the main body (Reviewer 4).
- **Writing and presentation issues**: Preliminaries and construction of Ethereum networks are unclear (Reviewer 3). Figure 2 (token parse) is unclear (Reviewer 3). Ablation is placed before main results, and the datasets used in ablation are omitted (Reviewer 4). IID training on a timeline is noted as widely used in temporal knowledge graphs, so highlighting it as novel wastes space (Reviewer 4).
- **Minority reviewer contrast**: Reviewers 1 and 2 (scores 6 and 5, confidence 3 and 4) found the work promising and valuable for the field, whereas Reviewers 3 and 4 (both score 3, confidence 4) raised fundamental concerns about novelty, problem formulation, and experimental rigor, indicating significant disagreement on the paper’s contribution and soundness.
