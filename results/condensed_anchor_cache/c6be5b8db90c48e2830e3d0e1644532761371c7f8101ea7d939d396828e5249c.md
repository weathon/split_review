## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper identifies a fundamental issue with the standard maximum mutual information (MMI) criterion for rationale extraction: diminishing marginal returns after partial rationales are found. To address this, the authors propose Norm to Rationale (N2R), which uses the norm of encoder output representations as an alternative objective, based on the idea that norms indicate how much of the input is actually utilized by the network’s weight matrix. Experiments on four text classification datasets and one graph dataset using GRUs, BERT, and GCN show that N2R outperforms MMI and its variants, and competes with llama-3.1-8b-instruct. The method is model-agnostic and falls in the self-explanation category.

### Strengths
- The identified issue of MMI suffering from diminishing marginal returns is meaningful and insightful.
- The proposed norm-based rationale extraction is novel and intuitively sensible; it is a simple approach that seems effective.
- The theoretical analysis of MMI limitations is clear and engaging.
- The method is model-agnostic and has potential for generalization to images and graphs.
- Mixing N2R with MMI improves performance—MMI works better early in extraction; N2R works better after some rationales are detected.
- Experiments are repeated with multiple random seeds and report standard deviations, improving rigor.

### Weaknesses
- **Missing evaluation results**: Task accuracy (predictor accuracy) for rationales extracted by BERT-based methods and llama-3.1-8b-instruct is not reported. The paper only shows rationale quality metrics.
- **Missing dataset coverage for BERT**: BERT-based methods are evaluated only on Beer-Appearance; results on the other three datasets are omitted. No explanation is given.
- **Unclear what the metric improvements mean**: Human annotations for rationales vary. A toy example (line 266) illustrates a case where different annotators might prefer different rationales; the paper does not study whether the numerical improvement translates to qualitatively better rationales. A case study or human analysis comparing extracted rationales would clarify.
- **Inconsistent BERT vs. GRU performance**: Table 1 (GRU) and Table 3 (BERT) show that BERT-based methods perform worse than GRU-based methods on Beer-Appearance, which is counterintuitive since BERT is expected to be stronger. This discrepancy requires explanation.
- **Method description is unclear and potentially wrong**: The paper states that weight matrices are used to estimate input utilization, but weight matrices are input-independent (excluding attention weights) and cannot directly serve that purpose. The description should be rewritten.
- **Code inconsistency**: The function `get_embeddings` in `embeddings.py` is not used elsewhere; `get_glove_embedding` is used but GloVe is never mentioned in the paper. The code and paper text need alignment.
- **Reasoning about matrix rank is vague**: The paper mentions “low rank” and “zero rank” of weight matrices without clear formal definitions or approximations. It is unclear whether weight matrices or embedding matrices are meant. The practical method (Section 4.2) suggests embeddings, but this is not consistent with earlier text.
- **Figure 2 is unclear and appears misordered**: The x-axis is undefined, notations in Equation 2 are not defined, and the figure caption lacks clarity. Figure 2 appears after Figure 3; reordering would help.
- **Limited domain coverage**: The experiments are restricted to text and graphs. The claimed generalizability is not tested on high-dimensional or complex data (e.g., time series, 3D point clouds, speech, images). Absence of results on token-level rationales (e.g., e-SNLI) is a missed opportunity.
- **No discussion of computational resources or architecture sensitivity**: The impact on large models, effect of different activation functions/normalization layers, depth/width scaling, and longer input lengths are not investigated.
- **Theoretical foundation is thin**: The relationship between encoder output norm and input information utilization lacks rigorous mathematical grounding; e.g., using concepts from information theory would strengthen it. The justification for taking the logarithm in Eq. (8) is not explained.
- **Missing hyperparameters**: For example, the hyperparameter in Eq. (5) is not provided.
- **Prior work acknowledged but not fully addressed**: UNIREX already discussed “sufficiency” and “comprehensiveness” metrics related to the MMI problem; the paper does not compare or contrast with that work.
- **Extractor and predictor are not trained end-to-end** due to non-differentiability of the rationale space.
- **No task performance with gold rationales** to indicate maximum achievable performance, and no out-of-distribution evaluation to check reliance on statistical shortcuts.
- **Figure 3 lacks y-axis labels** and panel references (a,b,c) in the caption.
- **Citations in lines 167–173 should be in braces**.
- **Minority critical perspective**: One reviewer (score 5) specifically noted that the method description seems conceptually wrong and that the improvement in numbers may not reflect actual rationale quality, while other reviewers were more positive.
