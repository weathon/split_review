## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses noise in training data for Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) in LLM alignment. Noise is categorized into pointwise noise (low-quality supervised fine-tuning data) and pairwise noise (incorrect preference rankings). The authors show that DPO inherently embeds Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO) principles, conferring pointwise robustness, with the regularization coefficient \(\beta\) inversely related to the robustness radius \(\eta\). To handle pairwise noise, they propose Dr. DPO, which integrates pairwise robustness by optimizing against worst-case pairwise scenarios and introduces a new hyperparameter \(\beta'\) for controlling data pair reliability. Empirical evaluations on IMDB and Anthropic HH demonstrate improved text quality and response accuracy in both noisy and clean settings.

### Strengths
- Clear problem formulation and motivation, with introductory supporting experiments for each claim. The writing is concise and easy to follow even without prior DRO knowledge, aided by a running example and a toy example that help explain the method.
- The paper provides a solid theoretical basis: deriving that DPO is implicitly a form of DRO for pointwise noise, and then extending to pairwise noise via Dr. DPO.
- Extensive experiments and ablation studies support the motivation, main claims, and method characteristics (convergence, KL, reward). Code is provided for reproducibility.
- The idea of applying Distributionally Robust Optimization to LLM alignment is novel and interesting. Dr. DPO has a natural interpretation, does not require explicit noise estimation, and is easy to compute.
- Experimental results are promising: Dr. DPO outperforms DPO and other baselines in both noisy and noise-free environments, with detailed ablation on the new hyperparameter \(\beta'\).
- (Minority positive: Reviewer 1 gave a score of 8, noting the paper’s clarity and extensive experiments; other reviewers scored 5–6 but acknowledged the strengths above.)

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and theoretical gaps:**
  - The discussion on pointwise noise and DRO (Section 3.2) lacks clarity. Equation 7 appears to be a dual form of a PPO objective, yet typical DRO optimizes over distributions \(Q\) close to the sample distribution \((x, \pi_{\theta}(y|x))\), while the authors optimize over distributions \(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot|x)\) near \(\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot|x)\). The connection between Equation 7 and DRO is not clearly explained, and a formal proof connecting DPO to pointwise DRO is missing. The paper states “DPO is Implicitly a Pointwise DRO” but relies on standalone Theorem 3.1 and vague “insights” rather than a rigorous derivation.
  - The objective in Equation (12) may contradict IPO (Identity Preference Optimization). Reviewer 3 questions whether Dr. DPO is simply adding an exponential function \(e^{(\cdot)}\) to the DPO objective, and notes that IPO learns \(h(x,y_w,y_l)\) to a specific value while Dr. DPO maximizes it—this purported contradiction needs clarification.
  - Some declarations are unclear. For example, “Why DPO is Robust to Pointwise Noise” (line 212–215) is stated directly without sufficient justification, whereas Section 4.3 is presented much better.
- **Assumptions and comparability of noise types:**
  - The noise added to pointwise and pairwise datasets in Sections 3.1 and 4.1 is not comparable. Pointwise noise is introduced by generating samples from an untuned GPT-2, which only weakens the probability that a sample is good (reducing \(P(y \text{ is positive})\)), whereas pairwise noise is added by flipping the preference order (setting \(P(y_w > y_l)=0\)). This discrepancy undermines the conclusion that DPO is robust to pointwise noise but not to pairwise noise.
- **Missing experiments and comparisons:**
  - Experiments are limited to IMDB and Anthropic HH. More datasets (e.g., TruthfulQA, GSM8k) should be evaluated to test generalization, as IMDB is a simple task and may not reflect performance on complex tasks.
  - Only one model size (Pythia 2.8B) is used for pairwise experiments, and the model is not explicitly stated in the main text (only mentioned in the appendix). Experiments on more open-source LLMs (e.g., larger models, different families) would strengthen the results.
  - The evaluation compares with too few baselines. Missing comparisons include WPO (arxiv 2406.11827) and other recent reweighting DPO methods, as well as methods listed in arxiv 2409.02795. Also missing related literature on applying DRO to LLMs, e.g., Oren et al., “Distributionally Robust Language Modeling.”
- **Implementation and hyperparameter details:**
  - Dr. DPO requires estimation of \(E_O\) (the worst-case distribution). It is unclear whether this is implemented within a batch or pre-computed on the whole dataset. The batch size likely affects optimization quality, yet no ablation on batch size is provided.
  - When \(\beta' = 1\), the Dr. DPO loss becomes \(-\log E[\exp(h_{\text{DPO}})]\), which differs slightly from the original DPO. A more detailed interpretation of this form would be insightful.
- **Figure and result concerns:**
  - In Figure 4 (Left), the reward-KL trade-off curves appear similar across different flipped-pair rates. Does this imply that at small KL values (e.g., <2), rewards are similar regardless of noise level?
  - Figure 5 (Left) shows that Dr. DPO has higher win *and* loss rates at 0 flips. Does this indicate a trade-off between performance and robustness?
- **Toy example confusion:**
  - The toy example in Section 4.3 passes an inconsistent combination of parameters into the function \(h\), making it hard to follow.
- **Typos:**
  - Line 194: \(\pi_\theta\) (missing subscript formatting?).
  - Section 5.3: “DRDPO” instead of “Dr. DPO”.
- **Minor concerns:**
  - More case studies would help illustrate Dr. DPO’s behavior.
  - Reviewer 3 questions whether the evaluation tasks are too simple; methods effective on IMDB may not generalize well.
