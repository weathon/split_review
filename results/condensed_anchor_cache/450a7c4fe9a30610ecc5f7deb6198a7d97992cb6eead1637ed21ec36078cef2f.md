- Decision: Reject
- Scores: 3, 3, 3, 5, 3

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes using Neural Cellular Automata (NCA) for learned image compression, introducing a Grid NCA (GNCA) strategy that partitions images into patches and trains separate NCA models per patch. Model weights serve as the compressed representation. Experiments on 30 low-resolution images (40×40 to 120×120) from COCO 2017 compare GNCA against JPEG, JPEG-2000, and WebP using PSNR, SSIM, MSE, and compression rate. GNCA achieves competitive reconstruction but very slow encoding (3.2 hours per 40×40 patch) and limited evidence of claimed advantages.

### Strengths
1. **Novel application of NCA to image compression.** The idea is original (first investigation known to reviewers) and the paper introduces NCA as an alternative to autoencoders/VAEs, highlighting potential benefits like parallelization, compactness, and robustness. One reviewer (Reviewer 4, score 5) was notably more positive, finding the attractor / fixed-point property an interesting characteristic reminiscent of Hopfield networks, though noting limited methodological novelty beyond Mordvintsev et al. (2020).
2. **Grid NCA training strategy (GNCA) is a reasonable solution for scaling.** Splitting images into patches and training per-patch NCA models enables handling larger images and exploits NCA parallelism. Multiple reviewers acknowledged this as a sensible, though conceptually simple, approach.
3. **Honest discussion of pros and cons.** The paper includes a dedicated section (6.2) listing limitations, which was appreciated.
4. **Comprehensive evaluation of NCA parameter impact.** The ablation of model size on compression quality is useful for understanding NCA capacity trade-offs.

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient experimental scope for compression evaluation.**  
   - Only one dataset (COCO 2017) used; missing standard compression benchmarks: Kodak, Tecnick, CLIC.  
   - Image sizes are very small (max 120×120) — not “high-resolution” even by standard deep learning standards, and far from real-world use.  
   - Only three compression rates (model sizes) for GNCA and a single quality setting for JPEG / JPEG-2000 / WebP. Rate-distortion curves (PSNR vs. bpp, or MS-SSIM) are not provided — multiple reviewers strongly recommended this as the standard metric in compression literature.  
   - No comparison with state-of-the-art learned compression models (e.g., MLIC++ (Jiang & Wang, 2023), CompressAI models). A baseline with implicit neural representations (COIN) is also notably missing, as INR may be more efficient than the recurrent NCA.  
   - Unclear how many images are averaged in results (e.g., Table 2 caption says “Average results …” but number not given). Figure 3 is shown for a single image rather than averaged over the dataset.

2. **Unsupported or weakly supported claims about advantages.**  
   - *Noise robustness* is claimed as a key benefit but never demonstrated experimentally — no out-of-distribution or adversarial tests.  
   - *Lightweight architecture* argument is unconvincing: the paper only compares parameter count (e.g., 2,600 vs. 83M in MLIC++), ignoring that codec size is amortized over many images. No FLOPs-per-pixel comparison (standard in efficient compression work, e.g., Johnston et al., Yang et al.) is provided, nor comparison of decoding speed or memory.  
   - *Parallelization* is not unique to NCA (all convolutional networks and even JPEG have parallel implementations). The theoretical *asynchronous* property of NCA is not investigated.  
   - *Predictable compression size* is also not unique; standard codecs have deterministic size before entropy coding.  
   - *GNCA vs. traditional codecs*: based on presented rate-distortion results, JPEG, JPEG-2000, and WebP achieve similar or better quality at roughly the same compression rate, while being much faster and not requiring training. One reviewer noted that this undermines the motivation for using GNCA.

3. **Extremely slow training / encoding speed.**  
   - Each patch takes approximately 3.2 hours to train (for 40×40 pixels). Traditional methods require no training; deep learning methods train a single model for all images. Reviewers considered this a serious practical limitation.  
   - No compression or decompression speed comparisons with competing methods are provided.

4. **Limited technical contribution.**  
   - The GNCA strategy is a straightforward patch-wise decomposition with no information exchange between patches. Multiple reviewers called it trivial, noting that splitting into patches is common (e.g., transformers).  
   - The NCA approach itself is borrowed directly from Mordvintsev et al. (2020) without architectural novelty. No effort was made to reduce parameter size beyond using smaller networks or basic 8-bit quantization.

5. **Missing ablations and necessary experiments.**  
   - No ablation over patch size (effect on compression quality and training convergence).  
   - No comparison with an NCA where all cells are alive from the start (all cells seeded), which could eliminate the need for grid-based training.  
   - No results for larger image sizes (HD resolutions) to evaluate scaling.  
   - Training description is unclear: what constitutes an “epoch” when training on a single image? When is backpropagation through time (BPTT) performed? Is the pool-based training technique from Mordvintsev et al. used?  
   - The NCA update rule architecture (layers, dimensions, input/output) is not clearly described; a figure would help verify Equation 4.

6. **Presentation and clarity issues.**  
   - Metrics section (page 5) is overly detailed.  
   - Figure 5 lacks visual comparisons with JPEG, JPEG-2000, and WebP (only GNCA shown).  
   - Figures 4 and 2: it’s ambiguous whether parameter counts are per patch or for the full image. Line 362 suggests total parameters summed over patches, but this should be clarified.  
   - Equation 1 is incorrectly written (convolution of a filter with a pixel rather than the entire grid).  
   - Typographical errors: inconsistent font for cell index “i” in lines 123–126.  
   - Hallucination risk: no discussion of whether NCA can produce outputs vastly different from the target.

7. **Reviewer questions that overlap with missing experiments / criticisms.**  
   - How does GNCA scale with image size and content complexity?  
   - Why choose a central-seed NCA rather than all-cells-alive (Mordvintsev et al.’s self-organizing textures), which would avoid grid training?  
   - What is the meaning of “noise robustness” in compression? Can it be demonstrated?  
   - Could the initial configuration be learned and transmitted as a latent code (instead of starting from a single fixed pixel)?  
   - Are concerns about hallucinations valid?  

*Contrast note:* Reviewer 4 (score 5) was the most positive, highlighting the novel application and the interesting fixed-point property. However, that same reviewer also identified many of the above weaknesses (insufficient experiments, missing baselines, unclear quantization, unconvincing lightweight argument) and rated soundness 2, presentation 2, contribution 2. Thus the higher score appears to reflect more weight on originality rather than a belief that the evidence adequately supports the claims.
