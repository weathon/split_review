## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces VisCon-100K, a dataset for fine-tuning vision-language models (VLMs). It is built by converting 45K interleaved image-text web documents from OBELICS into 100K image conversation samples. The pipeline uses GPT-4V to generate contextual captions from both image and surrounding web text, then OpenChat 3.5 to produce free-form and multiple-choice QA pairs. A key finding is that a "leaky modality mix"—where QA pairs can be answered from either the image or the caption—outperforms non-leaky combinations. The dataset is evaluated by fine-tuning ShareGPT4V-7b and IDEFICS2-8b, and a trained contextual captioner is also released. Two reviewers (3 and 5) find the work promising; two (3 and 3) find the evidence insufficient.

### Strengths
- The paper identifies limitations in existing VLM SFT datasets (e.g., ShareGPT4V using only images to distill GPT-4V, LLaVA relying on captions/detection with purely text LLMs). It proposes using both image and related web text as input to GPT-4V to generate captions with contextual information (time, location, characters) and demonstrates this yields more effective SFT data. (R1)
- The proposed data generation pipeline is scalable and composed of reasonable components: GPT-4V for contextual captioning and OpenChat 3.5 for QA generation. (R2, R3)
- Experimental results on ShareGPT4V and IDEFICS2 show effectiveness of the VisCon-100K dataset and the leaky modality mix. (R3). Ablation studies are included with clear experimental details. (R2, R4)
- The paper is well written and easy to follow. (R4). Note: One reviewer (R1) found the writing quality poor and difficult to read.

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient evidence of effectiveness.** Only one finetune experiment in Section 5.6 on IDEFICS2-8b is provided with caption evaluation; numbers on the other benchmarks used in Figure 4 are missing. Further finetuned experiments on other VLMs are needed. (R2) The evaluation is limited to older models; experiments on recent MLLMs such as MiniCPM-V, InternVL1.5, and LLaVA-Next are missing, which would be crucial to validate the dataset. (R3)
- **Lack of quality analysis for the dataset.** Since all data is model-generated, there is likely considerable noise. No human annotation verification or human evaluation is provided. Improvements in BLEU/ROUGE for the captioner do not necessarily indicate higher quality; human or GPT-assisted evaluation is needed. (R2, R3)
- **Incomplete evaluation of the leaky modality mix.** The motivation is unclear: if answers can be derived solely from the caption, the VLM may learn a shortcut and ignore the image. The paper should evaluate on benchmarks specifically designed to ensure visual reasoning (e.g., MathVerse, MMVP) to confirm the claim that leaky data provides interplay rather than a shortcut. (R4) The improvements in Fig. 3 and Fig. 4 appear marginal or sometimes surpassed by the baseline, casting doubt on the dataset's benefit. (R4)
- **Lack of novelty.** A very similar idea—merging contextual information with image descriptions—is introduced in CapsFusion (Yu et al. 2024, CVPR). The paper lacks any citation or discussion of differences. (R4)
- **Missing comparisons with existing SFT datasets.** The paper (line 291) states VisCon-100K is meant to complement, not replace, but experiments should show the advantage of **complementing with VisCon-100K** compared to complementing with other SFT datasets. Direct qualitative/quantitative comparisons (e.g., diversity metrics, entity statistics, t-SNE of QA text embeddings) are lacking. (R1)
- **Limited scope of main ablation (Table 1).** Table 1 is only conducted on the SEED benchmark; ablations should be applied to other datasets to check consistency of the leaky vs. non-leaky finding. (R2)
- **Concerns about experimental fairness.** The paper should provide direct experimental comparisons between VisCon-100K and other SFT datasets (qualitative illustrations and quantitative statistics). (R1)
- **Writing quality** is poor, making the paper difficult to read and understand. (R1) – This is contradicted by R4 who found it well written.
