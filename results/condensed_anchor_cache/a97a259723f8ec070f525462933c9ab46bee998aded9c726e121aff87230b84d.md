## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents the Spectra LLM suite, a family of ternary language models (TriLMs) ranging from 99M to 3.9B parameters, pretrained on 300B tokens, alongside FloatLM counterparts and post-training quantized (GPTQ) variants at 3/4/6/8 bits. The authors argue that TriLMs scale more efficiently than FloatLMs and QuantLMs when measured by model size in bits, and that a 3.9B TriLM matches a 3.9B FloatLM on most benchmarks despite having fewer bits than an 830M FloatLM. The work examines convergence behavior, scaling laws, downstream reasoning/knowledge/toxicity, and provides theoretical motivation from information theory. The reviewers agree the experiments are extensive and the release is valuable, but there is strong disagreement on novelty and fairness: two reviewers (4,5) rate the paper 5, citing strong similarity to BitNet b1.58, unfair comparisons (QAT vs. PTQ), insufficient training convergence, and lack of actual speedup measurements; three reviewers (1,2,3) are positive (8,10,10), praising the comprehensive study and insights into low-bit scaling.

### Strengths
- **Comprehensive and thorough evaluation**: Models are tested across commonsense reasoning, knowledge, toxicity, and training dynamics. (R1, R2, R5)  
- **Well-written and clearly structured**: The paper is easy to follow, with well-supported claims. (R1, R2, R3)  
- **Extensive and fair experimental settings**: All models are trained with the same recipe, covering multiple sizes and bit-widths. (R2, R3)  
- **High novelty and significant contribution** (R3 – note that R4 and R5 strongly disagree, arguing the approach is very similar to BitNet b1.58 and components are well-known).  
- **Valuable software and model release**: The Spectra LLM suite includes all trained models, enabling future research on low-bit pretraining. (R2, R3, R4)  
- **Insightful scaling-law analysis for low-bit models**: Shows that TriLMs outperform FloatLMs and QuantLMs at equal model size in bits. (R3)  
- **Detailed analysis of training dynamics, convergence, and loss curves** (R1, R2, R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Missing actual inference speedup benchmarks**: Only theoretical/maximal speedup over FP16 is reported. Real kernel measurements (throughput, first-token latency, average latency) are needed; multiple factors (activation quantization, KV cache, kernel implementation) affect realized speed. (R1, R4 – both highlight this as a major gap)  
- **Unfair comparison between TriLM (QAT) and QuantLMs (PTQ-only)**: TriLM is trained from scratch with quantization-aware training (QAT), while the 3/4-bit baselines use only GPTQ – a single PTQ method that degrades at low bit-widths. The paper should compare with QAT-trained 3/4-bit models or more recent PTQ methods (OmniQuant, BiLLM, AffineQuant, SpinQuant, QuaRot). (R2, R4)  
- **Insufficient training convergence and data scalability**: Models are trained on only 300B tokens and may not have converged (especially the 3.9B model). The claim that 300B tokens suffice is unconvincing given modern LLM training scales to >15T tokens (e.g., Llama 3). Further scaling of training data is not studied, and trends might change with more tokens. (R4, R5)  
- **Novelty concerns and similarity to BitNet b1.58**: The architecture and training approach (two-stage weight decay, learning rate scheduling) are nearly identical to BitNet b1.58. The claim that Spectra is “the first to demonstrate the feasibility of pretraining ternary language models” is overestimated. The paper does not clearly explain additional contributions beyond BitNet b1.58. (R5 – R4 echoes limited novelty; R3 finds high novelty – this disagreement is explicit)  
- **Insufficient comparison with BitNet b1.58**: Appendix A.7 lacks experimental details (training data, hyperparameters). No validation perplexity on public datasets (WikiText-2, C4) is reported for either model. (R5)  
- **Lack of hyperparameter and resource transparency**: The pretraining cost (hardware, GPU hours, GPU type) for QuantLMs is not disclosed. Methodology for selecting learning rate, batch size, optimizer settings is absent, hampering reproducibility. (R1)  
- **Missing few-shot results and standard perplexity benchmarks**: No few-shot evaluations on MMLU or LAMBADA to test in-context learning. Generation perplexity on standard datasets (WikiText-2, C4, PTB) is not reported. (R1, R2)  
- **Presentation and organization issues**: Figures 6 and 13 are compressed with unreadable text; Figure 5’s arrow colors are confusing; axis labels are distorted. Content organization could be improved: Sections 2.1 and 2.3 are somewhat redundant and could be moved to appendix; BiLM discussions are only in appendix; deeper discussion of training dynamics (loss reduction halfway) is needed. (R1, R3)  
- **Deeper analysis of training dynamics required**: The loss drop halfway through training is not sufficiently examined. A comparison with the decay-stage analysis in the MiniCPM paper might explain convergence. The paper should also explore why low-bit models scale more efficiently from a training-dynamics perspective. (R3)  
- **Unexplained 3-bit scaling anomaly**: Results show 3-bit models scale less efficiently than 4-bit and TriLMs; no explanation is provided. A discussion of the relationship between number of states and scaling efficiency would be valuable. (R3)  
- **Training cost of QAT vs. FP baseline not reported**: The cost of full QAT training is omitted. Reviewer asks why model parallelism is needed for a 3.9B model. (R4)  
- **Minor questions unresolved**: (a) In Table 5, why is the number of skipped tokens not proportional to the number of skipped batches? (R2) (b) In Figure 6b, why compare TriLM 2.4B with FloatLM 1.1B/1.5B instead of FloatLM 2.4B? (R2) (c) What does “validation loss” in Figure 7 represent? (R1)
