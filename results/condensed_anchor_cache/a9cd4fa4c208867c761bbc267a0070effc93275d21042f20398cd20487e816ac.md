## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces Multi-granularity Diffusion Modeling (MDM), which prioritizes subgoals by difficulty to improve complex reasoning and planning where autoregressive (AR) models struggle. The authors first formalize subgoal imbalance and show that discrete diffusion models avoid the compounding-error pattern that hurts AR models. MDM uses a token-level reweighting mechanism in the diffusion loss to emphasize harder subgoals. On Countdown, Sudoku, and Boolean Satisfiability Problems (SAT), MDM significantly outperforms AR models without search, e.g., 91.5% vs 45.8% on Countdown and 100% vs 20.7% on Sudoku. The method also achieves strong results on Game of 24. The authors argue that each x_t in diffusion acts as a different view of x_0, aiding learning of challenging subgoals.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a novel and important problem: subgoal imbalance in planning tasks. The concept is introduced clearly, and Section 3’s simple metric (planning distance) to contrast AR, Reverse-AR, and diffusion is already a novel contribution.
- The paper is very well motivated, well written, and well structured. Ideas are presented sequentially with evidence and explanations provided soon after assertions.
- The main contribution—theoretical argumentation supported by empirical evidence why diffusion works better than autoregression for planning—is significant, considering the value of such capabilities.
- The proposed method (MDM) is a reasonable and original approach to tackle subgoal imbalance. It replaces AR models with discrete diffusion for planning, introduces a multi-granularity reweighting procedure, and demonstrates superior efficiency (especially on cheaper V100 hardware).
- Experimental results are mind-blowing: MDM almost completely solves Countdown and Game of 24, both major challenges under active investigation. The authors provide code, and a quick glance did not raise immediate red flags.
- The paper is original in linking subgoal imbalance to “multi-view learning” as a possible explanation for diffusion’s superiority.

### Weaknesses
- The evaluation is limited to well-designed, controlled tasks (Countdown, Game of 24, Sudoku, SAT). This restricts general applicability. More realistic RL scenarios and larger domains (e.g., Blocksworld, Mini-Crossword) are needed to show broader impact. Reviewer 3 emphasizes that tasks requiring ‘common sense’ (e.g., Mini-Crossword from Tree-of-Thought) are missing.
- The definition of “subgoal imbalance” is awkward: is it only from an autoregressive perspective or a general aspect of multi-step tasks? The proposition at line 160 makes it sound like the former, but the text leans toward the latter. The proposition should be more rigorous and clearly outline when it applies and when it does not.
- The “multi-view learning” perspective (L243–252) is underdeveloped. What “view” means in this context is not defined, leaving the argument vague and non-rigorous. The transition from observations in 3.2 to the MDM design in 3.3 is not fully clear; the paper might have been better dropping the multi-view angle and focusing entirely on subgoal imbalance.
- Baselines are inconsistent: GPT-4 with Tree-of-Thought is only used on Game of 24; other planning-specific baselines (e.g., PDDL-based solvers) are missing. The paper does not address why one should use MDM over analytic planning approaches.
- Scalability concerns: In Countdown tasks CD4 and CD5, the 85M-parameter model outperforms the 303M-parameter model. This raises questions about how well MDM scales. Additionally, the paper lacks a computational efficiency comparison (training and inference time) against autoregressive models.
- Reviewer 2 questions whether a similar per-token loss reweighting (as in Eq. 8) could be applied to AR models to address the issue in Proposition 1, and whether the “regretful compromise” is simply a reinvention of the “compounding error” concept.
- The paper does not explore whether discrete diffusion models could exhibit in-context learning or emergent generality like AR models, even with more data.
- The work currently targets narrow planning-specific training. It is unclear if the findings generalize to “general” diffusion foundation LMs (i.e., would a foundation diffusion LM still be better at planning than an equivalent AR LM?), and how this narrow case could be incorporated into more general models.
