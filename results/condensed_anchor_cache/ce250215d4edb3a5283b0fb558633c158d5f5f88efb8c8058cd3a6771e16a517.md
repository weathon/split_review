## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LAVO, a linear attention mechanism that compresses context into a fixed-size orthogonal memory, combines it with local attention, and embeds relative position encoding. Evaluated on language modeling, text-to-speech, summarization, point cloud completion, time-series forecasting, and Long Range Arena (LRA). Reviewers (scores 6,5,5,3) agree LAVO achieves strong efficiency on long sequences and shows good extrapolation, but concerns arise about novelty (cumulative mean is a special case of state spaces), performance gaps vs. hardware-optimized attention on shorter inputs, limited baselines (missing S5, Hyena), lack of code, and insufficient hyperparameter analysis. Reviewer 1 (6) is the most positive, highlighting universal applicability and strong empirical results; Reviewer 4 (3) is the most negative, citing omitted strong baselines and poor reproducibility.

### Strengths
- **Good accuracy with linear complexity** – LAVO maintains linear time while achieving strong performance, especially for long-range language modeling (R1, R3). Reviewer 1 (score 6) particularly emphasizes the empirical efficiency and suitability for unbounded contexts.
- **Extrapolation ability** – Longer contexts yield better accuracy; position encoding further improves 0-shot extrapolation to unseen lengths (R1, R3). Reviewer 2 (score 5) commends the comprehensive 0-shot extrapolation evaluation.
- **Simpler implementation than state spaces** – The cumulative‑mean aggregation uses cumsum, which is simpler and faster to implement than learned state‑space models (R2).
- **Effective context compression and local detail** – Orthogonal memory reduces redundancy, and the context‑dissecting technique captures fine‑grained local information (R3). LAVO naturally extends to cross‑attention tasks (R3).
- **Comprehensive and honest experimental evaluation** – The paper covers a wide range of modalities (LM, TTS, summarization, point cloud, time series) and sequence lengths, including 0-shot extrapolation (R2, R4). Reviewer 4 (score 3) acknowledges the paper is well‑written and well‑motivated despite their negative vote.
- **Universal applicability** – LAVO can be used for any task that requires attention, both self‑attention and cross‑attention (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Lags behind FlashAttention on short sequences** – For inputs up to 8k tokens, LAVO is slower and less accurate than hardware‑optimized attention, limiting its practical efficiency in this common regime (R2, R3). Reviewer 1 asks about expected performance when combined with FlashAttention.
- **Limited and outdated baselines** – Strong recent methods (S5, Hyena) are omitted. Reviewer 4 (score 3) considers this vital for acceptance; Reviewer 2 notes that the cumulative mean is a special case of state spaces (S4, DSS, S4D) and similar to MEGA, so the technique is not novel. Performance on LRA is significantly lower than learned global convolutions / state‑space models; Reviewer 4 hypothesizes that S5 would also outperform on long‑sequence language modeling.
- **Lack of theoretical justification** – No explanation is given for why the expressiveness loss from compression/approximation does not substantially degrade accuracy (R1).
- **Positional encoding design** – Additive relative bias (T5/Alibi style) is used, which is IO‑unfriendly and will slow down FlashAttention implementations; Rotary embeddings are more compatible (R2). Reviewer 3 points out that position encoding may not always be beneficial.
- **Missing hyperparameter analysis** – The sensitivity to memory width `w` is not studied; Reviewer 1 asks how to choose `w` and whether it can be made adaptive per context. Reviewer 3 also notes a lack of comprehensive trade‑off analysis.
- **Implementation and numerical stability issues** – Complexity in §3.2 has a typo: parallel prefix sum (cumsum) is O(n log n), not O(n) (R2). The cummean implementation using cumsum may cause overflow in fp16 for inputs >66k; the paper should specify whether bfloat16 or pre‑normalization is used (R2).
- **No source code** – Supplementary material does not include code, making the results difficult to reproduce (R4). This is a key weakness for Reviewer 4.
- **Architecture concerns** – LAVO does not use separate key and value vectors, potentially limiting expressiveness (R1, question). The context‑dissecting step adds computational overhead dependent on window size (R3). Model scaling ability may be limited (R3).
- **Clarity of experimental setup** – It is not stated which tasks use causal vs. non‑causal self‑attention and why (R1). Table 1 could be moved to the appendix if it contains no important detail (R1).
