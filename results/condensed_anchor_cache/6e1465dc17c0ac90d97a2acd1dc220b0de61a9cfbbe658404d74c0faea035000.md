## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes an unsupervised framework for facial attribute analysis that eliminates the need for large labeled datasets. It leverages StyleGAN’s Hierarchical Feature Modulation inductive bias to automatically discover semantically meaningful and disentangled representations of facial attributes at different layers. GAN inversion is used to convert input images into StyleGAN features, and a mutual-information-based feature reduction method is introduced to improve representation quality for downstream tasks. Experiments are conducted on few-shot facial attribute tasks: clustering, classification, and annotation.

### Strengths

*   The paper tackles facial attribute analysis from an unsupervised perspective, reducing dependence on labeled data by exploiting StyleGAN’s feature extraction capabilities.
*   A novel mutual-information-based feature refinement method is proposed to enhance representation quality for unsupervised tasks.
*   Experiments are provided across four classification tasks in three different scenarios (few-shot with true labels, few-shot with pseudo-labels, etc.) to demonstrate framework effectiveness.
*   The general direction of using self-supervised learning to enhance facial feature representation is reasonable and common (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses

*   **Necessity of the unsupervised task not justified.** The paper does not quantify the labeled data requirements for simple supervised classifiers (e.g., ResNet-18 on binary/ternary gender, glasses, age-group tasks). To establish the need for an unsupervised approach, experiments with 10, 50, 100, 500 labeled examples per class should be compared against the proposed method. (Reviewer 1, high confidence)
*   **Claim about MAE data needs is questionable.** The paper states that MAE-based methods still require large amounts of labeled data, but MAE pre-training can significantly reduce downstream labeled data requirements (Reviewer 2, confidence 5).
*   **Task definition and scope are unclear.** The paper defines “facial attribute analysis” but focuses only on simple binary/ternary tasks. The cited related work often treats attribute analysis as an auxiliary component, not a primary task, and the specific attributes addressed do not align with the cited works (Reviewer 1).
*   **Insufficient explanation and experiments on StyleGAN feature superiority.**
    *   In few-shot classification with TrueLabels (Table 3), MAE outperforms StyleGAN (SG2) on some tasks. The authors should explain why and discuss conditions where StyleGAN features are expected to excel over alternatives like MAE (e.g., feature dimensionality, inductive biases).
    *   The generalization claim on AFHQ-Wild is unfair because StyleGAN was pre-trained on that dataset while MAE and VGG were not. Additional experiments on a dataset not used in StyleGAN’s pre-training (e.g., CelebA) are needed for a fair assessment.
    *   Experimental comparison in Fig. 2 is unfair: VGG16 was pre-trained on ImageNet (not face images), so it naturally performs worse on disentangling glasses features. VGG16 should be pre-trained on the same FFHQ dataset for a fair comparison. (Reviewer 2, confidence 5)
*   **Feature reduction method insufficiently justified.**
    *   The reduction step itself needs validation: experiments should compare performance with and without the channel selection (using metrics like ARI or NMI), not just max vs. mean pooling (Reviewer 1).
    *   Selecting feature channels based on only 8 images appears insufficient. Experimental evidence showing that 8 images are enough to select appropriate channels is missing (Reviewer 2, confidence 5).
*   **Method description is overly brief and lacks clarity.** The core of the proposed approach is quantitative justification, but the purpose of the strategy and definitions of key indicators (IntraMI/InterMI) are not sufficiently explained, making the method difficult to comprehend. (Reviewer 3)
*   **Limited novelty and missing comparisons.**
    *   The method primarily relies on StyleGAN’s feature decoupling capability and offers little substantial new insight (Reviewer 3).
    *   The two introduced few-shot tasks (clustering and annotation) lack detailed explanation, academic value justification, and comparisons with other state-of-the-art methods. Many studies on face representation learning are available but not compared. (Reviewer 2, confidence 5; Reviewer 3)
*   **Missing references.** The paper omits relevant works on facial representation learning, such as [1] General Facial Representation Learning in a Visual-Linguistic Manner, and [2] FaceXFormer: A Unified Transformer for Facial Analysis (Reviewer 3).
*   **Typographical error.** Line 260/261: “clustering, annotation, and clustering” is a typo. (Reviewer 2, confidence 5)
