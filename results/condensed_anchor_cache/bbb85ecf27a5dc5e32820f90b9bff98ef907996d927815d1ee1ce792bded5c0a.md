## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses the lack of standardized comparisons in predictive coding network (PCN) research by introducing PCX, a JAX-based library that is much faster than prior implementations (up to 50× via JIT compilation) and provides a unified benchmarking framework. The benchmarks span discriminative tasks (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-100, Tiny ImageNet) and generative tasks (autoencoding, sampling, associative memory) using architectures up to VGG-7 scale. The authors evaluate a range of existing PCN methods (standard PC, positive/negative/centered nudging, incremental PC, Monte Carlo PC) and standard backpropagation, achieving new state-of-the-art results on multiple datasets while analyzing limitations such as scalability and energy propagation.

### Strengths
- Addresses core issues in PC research: inconsistent comparisons, overly simple benchmarks, lack of rigorous standards.
- Provides a comprehensive, standardized benchmark with datasets including CIFAR-100, Tiny ImageNet, Fashion-MNIST, and MNIST.
- PCX library is JAX-based, achieving up to 50× speed improvement over baseline implementations via JIT compilation, and is well-documented with Jupyter Notebook tutorials that are easy to set up and follow.
- Extensive experimental evaluation: thorough hyperparameter studies, evaluation of many existing PCN methods (standard PC, positive/negative/centered nudging, incremental PC, Monte Carlo PC), and comparisons with backpropagation.
- Deep analysis of training mechanics: insights into energy propagation, initialization strategies, stability across optimizers and architectures, the dramatic effect of skip connections, and the poor performance of AdamW as hidden layer size increases.
- Community-centric: open-source library, detailed documentation, and tutorials lower the barrier to entry and encourage collaboration.
- Scales up PCN experiments to larger datasets and architectures than commonly used, improving applicability of methods in the field.
- Shows that PCNs can achieve performance competitive with backpropagation-trained models for small to medium-sized architectures.
- Identifies key scalability limitations (sequential layer updates, linear scaling with depth, slower than backprop for complex models) as crucial findings that guide future research.
- Includes generative tasks (autoencoding, sampling, associative memory) in addition to discriminative tasks, providing broader evaluation.

### Weaknesses
- **Writing and presentation issues**: Inconsistent terminology, grammar/spelling mistakes (e.g., “lied new foundations” should be “laid new foundations”, “split into mini-batches” not “in”, inconsistent capitalization of “just-in-time”), unclear phrasing, and occasional errors in references and captions (e.g., Figure 1 (b) should say “hL is fixed to y”, Figure 5 left/center-left/center-right/right are not sub-indexed, references to Figure 5 right and center-right are inverted). The abbreviation “BP” is used without introduction; citations for well-known methods and datasets (CIFAR-10, MNIST, SGD, Adam, AdamW, VGG) are missing.
- **Limited architecture diversity**: Benchmarks only use MLP and VGG-5/VGG-7 models. Missing evaluations with ResNet, DenseNet, or transformer architectures (even shallow variants) that could reveal further insights into PCN scaling and performance. (Both R1, R2, and R3 note this limitation; R3 specifically questions the choice of VGG-5 (3.8M parameters) vs. VGG-7 (4.5M) given their similar size.)
- **Soundness of specific claims** (highlighted primarily by Reviewer 3; R1 and R2 do not raise these issues):
    - Energy propagation conclusion (L377) that “reducing the energy imbalance between layers” is needed is not supported by Figure 5 and Figures 13–14: the learning rate that minimizes energy imbalance (0.3) gives much worse AdamW performance than learning rates with higher imbalance (e.g., 1e-3). The link between energy imbalance and performance is unclear.
    - Claim in Section 4.1 (L243) that “the best performing algorithms, at least on the most complex tasks, are the ones where the target is nudged towards the real label, that are PN and NN” is contradicted by Table 1: for VGG-5, centered nudging (CN) dominates PN and NN on all tasks except Tiny ImageNet Top-1; for VGG-7, all PC methods are beaten by backpropagation (BP-CE and BP-SE), and PN/NN are often outperformed by PC-CE.
    - Claim (L339) that “PCNs are more flexible and require only half the parameters to achieve similar performance” is only demonstrated on small models (3 layers, 2 hidden layers for associative memory). No experiment on a larger model (e.g., VGG-7 scale) supports this claim.
- **Comparative analysis limited**: The paper would benefit from comparisons with other biologically inspired approaches and broader contextualization within general machine learning.
- **Diversity of tasks/modalities still limited** relative to general deep learning benchmarks; more domains and tasks would strengthen the work.
- **Practical implications** of findings for real-world applications beyond academic benchmarks are not explored.
- **Implementation constraints**: Training time scales linearly with number of layers due to parallelization limitations; PCX is still slower than backpropagation for complex models (noted by R1 as an identified limitation, but also a practical weakness).
- **Questions that overlap with missing analyses**:
    - Why is the library implemented only in JAX and not PyTorch? This may limit adoption (R1).
    - Are there preliminary ideas or suggestions for addressing the scalability issues identified? (R1)
    - Can more model types (e.g., ResNet, ViT) be tested beyond VGG and MLP? (R2, R3)
    - Why are VGG-5 and VGG-7 chosen when their sizes are close (3.8M vs 4.5M)? Can more model sizes provide better data points? (R3)
    - In Table 1, missing data for CIFAR-10 on VGG-7 makes comparison difficult; why are different datasets reported for VGG-5 and VGG-7? (R2)
- **Minor figure issues**: Figure 2 would be clearer if method names were drawn above images (R2). Table 3 caption could be more explicit about comparison of noise and mask modes (R3).
