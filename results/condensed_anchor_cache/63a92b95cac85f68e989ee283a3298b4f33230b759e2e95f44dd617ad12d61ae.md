## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Generate-to-Discriminate (G2D) for domain-incremental continual learning. For each new domain, G2D fine-tunes a domain-specific classifier, adapts a conditional generative model, then trains a domain discriminator on synthetic samples from all past and current domains (generated by the respective models). At inference, the discriminator routes each sample to the corresponding expert classifier. Experiments on three vision benchmarks (including a new real-world dermatology dataset DermCL) and one text QA benchmark show G2D outperforms prior domain-incremental methods by up to 7.6 (vision), 6.2 (language), and 10.0 (dermatology) points. A key empirical finding is that using synthetic data to train a domain discriminator is significantly more effective than using the same synthetic data for generative replay.

### Strengths
1. The core idea is clean and intuitive: decompose domain-incremental learning into domain identification (via a discriminator trained on synthetic data) and expert retrieval, avoiding direct training on potentially low-quality generated samples.
2. The paper makes an important and insightful contribution by demonstrating that the indirect approach (first identify the domain, then solve) can outperform the direct approach (generative replay with the same synthetic data). This provides a fresh perspective on using generative models for continual learning.
3. Extensive evaluation covers multiple modalities (image and text), multiple standard benchmarks, and a challenging real-world medical imaging task (DermCL). A new domain-incremental benchmark (DermCL) is introduced.
4. The related work discussion is well-organized and comprehensive.

### Weaknesses
1. **Fairness and implementation details of the Generative Replay baseline are insufficiently documented.** Reviewers 1 and 2 both question whether the comparison is fair. Specifically:
   - It is unclear how the classifier(s) are fine-tuned in G2D versus Generative Replay. The paper reports “fine-tune only 1.04~2.5% of trainable parameters” (Sec 5.4) without explaining how this percentage was chosen or which parameters are updated, nor whether the same fine-tuning approach is used for both methods.
   - For Generative Replay, the weighting between the loss on replayed data and the loss on current-domain data is not specified (e.g., simply added or balanced to approximate the joint loss over all domains).
   - Full experimental details for Generative Replay are needed to allow proper assessment.
2. **Computational cost and scalability are not discussed.** The paper lacks a breakdown of training cost (number of parameters, training time, memory) for the domain discriminator and the generative model compared to standard expert learning. It is also unclear how the domain discriminator itself is trained (from scratch at each new domain or continually) and how it scales to a large number of domains. Reviewer 1 explicitly notes that the authors may misunderstand PEFT efficiency: the computational savings from LoRA-style fine-tuning are modest relative to forward/backward pass costs, and the real gains are in parameter efficiency and serving.
3. **Presentation and table clarity need improvement.** Reviewer 3 finds it hard to connect the table with the text: for example, in Table 1 it is unclear where ER is, what CaSSLe is, and what the difference between G2D and G2D (Full FT) is (explained much later). Also, different methods are compared across different vision datasets without clear explanation.
4. **Practical relevance of the setting is questioned.** Reviewer 1 doubts the motivating scenario (healthcare institutions may share models but not data): it seems unlikely that an institution would open-source a generative model trained on its data while withholding the data itself.
5. **Specific claims and omissions:**
   - Reviewer 2 points out that the claim “ER with a limited buffer size is an upper bound for generative replay” (p5) appears incorrect.
   - The paper uses S‑iPrompts results from Wang et al. (2022a) but does not report S‑liPrompts. Reviewer 2 asks for an explanation.
   - Reviewer 1 asks: How is synthetic data generated from T5 (encoder‑decoder)—what is fed to the encoder? Is there a separate generator?
   - Reviewer 1 asks: Is the domain-t classifier fine-tuned from task t‑1 or from the pretrained model?
6. **Contrast among reviewers:** Reviewer 3 (score 6) was more positive overall, describing the approach as simple and neat and the experiments as extensive. Reviewers 1 and 2 (score 5) were more critical, focusing on missing experimental details, computational overhead, and questionable comparisons. Reviewer 1 also found the contribution only “fair” (score 2), while Reviewers 2 and 3 rated it “good” (3).
