## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes ZZEdit, a text-driven image editing paradigm that improves editability and fidelity by locating a pivot latent during inversion (not fully to Gaussian noise) and then applying a ZigZag process alternating denoising and inversion to strengthen target guidance while preserving background. The method integrates with existing editing approaches (P2P, PnP, NTI). (All reviewers)

### Strengths
- **Novelty and motivation**: Introduces a novel editing paradigm that addresses the editability–fidelity trade-off by selecting a pivot latent early in the inversion trajectory, moving away from the standard “invert fully to z_T” approach. The motivation is clear, and the pilot experiment provides initial observation. [R1, R2, R3, R4; R2 and R4 highlight clear motivation; R3 finds the idea of encoding each image to a different latent promising.]
- **Presentation**: Writing is generally good and easy to follow; the background on inversion-and-editing pipeline is clearly presented. [R1: very easy to understand; R4: clear presentation. Note: R3 and R2 have concerns about structure and clarity, see Weaknesses.]
- **Experimental results**: Examples demonstrate quantitative improvements in some settings (e.g., CLIP score). The method shows promise over previous approaches in some comparisons. [R1, R4 are positive; R2, R3 are less convinced, see Weaknesses.]
- **Structure**: The approach is well-structured, proceeding from pilot experiment/observation to proposed method. [R2]

### Weaknesses
1. **Pivot selection justification is insufficient**: The condition to determine the pivot timestep (||ε_tgt – ε_null|| > ||ε_src – ε_null||) lacks theoretical analysis and is not empirically verified. Why does this particular condition identify an optimal balance between editability and fidelity? [R1: possibility of small P limiting editing space; R2: hard to distinguish text effects from coarse-to-fine diffusion process; unclear how lines 240–241 relate to method; why should maximum guidance degree be optimal; does the method coincide with that? R3: why use null text as criterion? R4: no verification or theoretical proof.]

2. **ZigZag process motivation and explanation are lacking**: There is no derivation or supervision signal to justify why alternating inversion and denoising around the pivot improves editability while preserving background. Why does this operation work? [R2: lacks theoretical explanation; R3: unclear purpose; R4: no mathematical illustration to prove it handles the problem.]

3. **Experimental results are not consistently promising**:
   - **Table 2 discrepancy**: ZZEdit with P2P performs much worse than PnP-inv, but with PnP it drastically outperforms PnP-inv. Requires detailed explanation. What is actually driving improvement – PnP or ZZEdit? If ZZEdit is effective, why does it fail with P2P? [R1]
   - **Table 1 contradiction**: With zigzag=0, consistency is optimal; introducing zigzag drops 80% of metrics, only minor CLIP score improvement. This does not justify the value of the zigzag component. [R1]
   - **NTI comparison**: ZZEdit (w/ NTI) shows decreased fidelity but increased editability, contradicting the claim that ZZEdit improves both. [R3]
   - **Qualitative failures**: Dog in Figure 1 does not resemble a Pixar style; road lane in ZZEdit+PnP+DDIM is not well-preserved. [R2]
   - **Scope of modifications**: Unclear if the method handles significant changes (e.g., flamingo to giraffe). [R2]
   - **Lack of SOTA comparisons**: Only ablation/baseline comparisons; missing direct comparisons with training-based methods from 2024 (R1 suggests 4–5 SOTA methods) and additional inversion methods (e.g., EDICT, accelerated iterative diffusion inversion) and LLM-based metrics (e.g., TIFA). [R1, R4]

4. **Editing scope limited to object replacement**: Only object replacement is demonstrated. For a method claiming to be general-purpose, it should show object removal, action editing, background replacement, etc. [R1]

5. **Unfair or insufficient comparisons**:
   - **Table 1 guidance scale mismatch**: DDIM reconstruction uses large guidance scale (e.g., 7.5) while ZZEdit uses scale 1. A consistent scale (e.g., 1) or comparison with reconstruction-preserving baselines (e.g., NTI) is needed for fair evaluation. [R3]
   - **Incorrect claim about existing pipelines**: The paper states that the “inversion-then-editing” pipeline requires achieving z_T, but many methods (e.g., P2P) invert only to ~0.7T. This should be corrected. [R4]
   - **Figure 4 zigzag steps**: As pivot nears T, zigzag steps decrease. Keeping zigzag steps constant across all pivots would clarify its effect. [R3]

6. **Pilot experiment and pivot analysis need clarification**:
   - One-step denoising from early latents shows more divergence, but does that directly imply better editability? The intermediate latent preserves more structure, so is it truly better for editability? [R2, R3]
   - Is the stronger response to target text compared to source text consistently observed beyond the initial pivot point, or is it stochastic? [R2]
   - In Figure 6, does the pivot timestep correlate with editing magnitude (e.g., 1.0T for severe modifications, 0.4T for minimal edits)? Such analysis would support the pivot selection method. [R2]

7. **Writing and organization issues**:
   - The structure is difficult to follow; the purpose of the pilot analysis is unclear and not well connected to the method. The related work section could better differentiate the approach. [R3]
   - Minor typos: Algorithm line 13: “executs” → “executes”; line 474: “steadyly” → “steadily”. [R2]

8. **Need for additional validation**:
   - The paper claims improvement in both editability and fidelity, but the experimental evidence is insufficient to support this conclusion. [R3]
   - Combining ZZEdit with the CFG++ editing method (inversion and sampling only) would test if zigzag guidance is truly effective. [R2]

9. **Correction about existing methods**: The paper incorrectly claims that existing pipelines require achieving z_T; many (e.g., P2P) invert only to ~0.7T. This factual error should be corrected. [R4]
