## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes BMC (Bridging and Modeling Correlations), a framework to enhance DPO by (1) generating pseudo-winning responses via targeted modifications of losing responses using a commercial LLM, and (2) applying token-level weighting based on policy model confidence. Experiments on QA, math, and instruction-following tasks show improvements over DPO and other baselines. Reviewers found the idea interesting and experiments extensive, but raised concerns about motivation, computational cost, dependence on strong LLMs, and the modest size of improvements relative to added complexity.

### Strengths

- The idea of using a commercial LLM to rewrite the winning response and introducing confidence-based token-level weighting is novel, simple, and well-motivated (R1, R2, R3).
- The paper is written with high quality, with clear presentation of the two-phase framework (R2).
- Experiments are extensive across multiple tasks (QA, math, instruction following) and include detailed ablation studies, demonstrating clear empirical significance (R1, R2, R3).
- The framework is versatile and can be applied to various DPO variants, showing potential broad applicability (R3).
- Dynamic token-level adjustment based on model confidence is a novel idea for fine-grained credit assignment that could improve learning (R2, R3).

### Weaknesses

- **Motivation for weak correlation is not always valid and insufficiently explained**: The claim that winning and losing responses are generated isolatedly is not typical in practice; a common method is to continue a prefix of the winning response to obtain the losing one, which naturally establishes correlation. The paper does not delve into why weak correlation leads to less informative gradients beyond a high-level argument (R1).
- **Bridging phase may erase non-correctness aspects**: The pseudo-winning response rewriting focuses on semantic correctness but could remove preferred syntax, linguistic organization (e.g., CoT style), or other useful aspects if the original winning response was better in those dimensions. Whether the model can still learn such linguistic features when responses overlap in syntax is unclear, and a better token weighting criterion might be needed instead (R2, with related question).
- **High computational cost and scalability concerns**: The Bridging Phase modifies 60–70% of the dataset using a commercial LLM (GPT-4), which is resource-intensive and potentially infeasible for many practitioners. The paper does not adequately analyze computational overhead, including token-level dynamic programming (edit distance) and its cumulative impact on training time. Scalability is limited; the effect of applying BMC only to a subset of data or designing better token weighting criteria (e.g., based on confidence patterns) should be explored (R2, R3). Additionally, the paper lacks a discussion on using open-source LLMs for rewriting and how their performance compares (R1, R3).
- **Marginal performance improvement does not justify added cost**: Performance gains are modest (e.g., 3.8% on QA tasks) given the significant effort and resources. The paper does not justify the cost-benefit ratio, analyze diminishing returns with proportion of modified data, or provide a time complexity breakdown. **Reviewer 3 in particular emphasizes that the improvements are insufficient**, while Reviewers 1 and 2 also raise related scalability and overhead concerns, indicating a minority stronger criticism on this point (R3, with R1, R2).
- **Token confidence weighting reliability and hyperparameter sensitivity**: The token-level confidence may not always be reliable, and the loss weighting trick hinges on intensive hyperparameter tuning, which undermines the method's practicality (R1).
- **Limited improvement over baselines and unexplained failures**: BMC does not boost SimPO, and the paper offers no explanation for why (R2 question). Comparisons with other state-of-the-art methods that do not use data modification are missing, and the paper should investigate whether performance gains are consistent across tasks and whether larger baseline models would yield different results (R3 questions).
- **Insufficient analysis of rewriting quality**: Beyond Table 3, the paper does not examine semantic equivalence differences between (y_w, tilde y_w) and (y_l, tilde y_l) to understand why LMs struggle to rephrase a winning response into a losing one (R2 question).
- **Potential bias from LLM synthesis**: The data synthesis process may introduce biases from the commercial LLM, but this is not explored (R3).
