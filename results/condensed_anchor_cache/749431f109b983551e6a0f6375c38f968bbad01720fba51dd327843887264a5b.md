## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper investigates prompt embeddings learned through soft prompt-tuning for visual concepts across text-to-image generation, open-set object detection, and zero-shot classification (Stable Diffusion 2, OWL-ViT, CLIP). Using 4,800 embeddings trained for 40 diverse concepts on four standard datasets, the authors find that learned embeddings are model-specific, non-transferable, and act as adversarial perturbations targeting the final layers of text encoders. A linear transformation plus MSE loss fails to transfer prompts between models, and perturbations within an ε-ball to any prior embedding can generate/detect/classify arbitrary concepts. The paper concludes that soft prompt-tuning finds these perturbative solutions, not generalizable representations.

### Strengths

- Novel perspective on prompt tuning across multiple models and tasks, with practical significance for transferring soft prompts.
- Logically structured, clearly presenting methodologies and findings; well-written and easy to follow.
- Large-scale analysis (4,800 embeddings, 40 concepts, four datasets) thoroughly establishes non-transferability.
- Perturbation analysis shows learned embeddings constrained to random prompt anchors perform equally well as those near related concept anchors.
- Simple linear transformation method is straightforward, with implementation details properly explained.

### Weaknesses

- The core motivation (why transfer soft prompts?) is buried at the end, making the flow disjointed; the paper needs reorganization. (Reviewer 2, confidence 3; Reviewer 3, confidence 1, also found motivation hard to understand.)
- No mitigation strategies are proposed despite identifying that prompts resemble adversarial examples and lack transferability. (Reviewers 4 and 5, ratings 6 and 6, confidence 3 and 4.)
- Transfer analysis uses only a linear transformation with MSE loss; nonlinear transformations and/or CLIP loss could offer insights. The failure may be due to the transform/loss, not inherent non-transferability. (Reviewer 4, confidence 3; Reviewer 5, confidence 4, willing to raise score with more insights.)
- Lacks a control setup: tuning on one generative model and testing on another generative model (same task) is missing; only cross-task transfers are shown. (Reviewer 4; also Reviewer 5, question 3.)
- The paper presumes linearity between text embedding spaces; models with different text encoders (e.g., different CLIP variants) may collapse. Even for OpenCLIP-L14 vs. CLIP-L14, learned prompts may exploit quirks of one encoder not present in the other. (Reviewer 3, rating 5, confidence 1: questions the practical utility; Reviewer 2, confidence 3.)
- Dataset diversity limited; four standard datasets are similar (e.g., being common-object biased). Generalizability to more diverse domains (e.g., remote sensing) or more abstract/complex concepts is unclear. Scaling up visual concepts (beyond 40) is needed. (Reviewer 1, confidence 3; Reviewer 2, confidence 3.)
- The linear transfer function’s representativeness is questionable given finite sample size, noise, and quality; penalized least squares or sparse regularization could affect conclusions. (Reviewer 1, confidence 3.)
- Missing analysis of input embedding similarity using metrics like CKA [1] or CKNNA [2] to reveal if semantic alignment exists or if adversarial behavior is the primary non-transferability factor. (Reviewers 4 and 5; both cite Kornblith et al. 2019, Huh et al. 2024.)
- Missing citations for recent work on vision-language representation convergence, e.g., Maniparambil et al. CVPR 2024. (Reviewers 4 and 5.)
- No performance comparison between transferring prompts vs. fine-tuning prompts per task; it’s unclear if the transfer method is more efficient when performance gap is considered. (Reviewer 2, confidence 3.)
- Lacks discussion of limitations (e.g., scaling with task complexity, dataset size, computational cost of learning/transferring prompts). (Reviewer 3, confidence 1.)
- Vocabulary in some sections is unnecessarily complex; description of transfer process could be simplified with concrete examples. (Reviewer 3, confidence 1.)
- Details like derivation of equation (2) are absent and should be in supplementary. (Reviewer 2, confidence 3.)
- Did not fine-tune any model layers; all frozen. (Reviewer 4, question 2.)
- Application of transfer function and its relationship to Section 4 not clearly articulated. (Reviewer 1, confidence 3.)

*Minority contrast*: Reviewer 3 (rating 5, confidence 1) is notably more skeptical about the problem setup if both generative and discriminative models use the same text encoder (OpenCLIP-L14 vs. CLIP-L14), questioning practical utility. Reviewer 5 (rating 6, confidence 4) is more positive but willing to raise score with more insights.
