## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper analyzes how the condition number of the edited matrix affects model sensitivity to perturbations in sequential model editing and proposes the PRUNE framework, which applies constraints to lower this sensitivity. The method uses SVD decomposition to keep singular values of the updated matrix close to those of the original matrix. Experiments on three editing methods (including MEMIT) across three LLMs (GPT-2 XL, LLaMA-2, LLaMA-3) and four downstream tasks show that PRUNE preserves general abilities while maintaining editing performance. The paper provides a theoretically derived upper bound for weight modification, offering a practical guideline. However, the theoretical motivation is questioned by two reviewers, and several experimental and clarity issues are raised.

### Strengths

- Sequential editing is an important and challenging direction in knowledge editing. The paper addresses it from a relatively comprehensive perspective with an excellent solution.
- The literature review is thorough.
- The evaluation scope is extensive, covering three LLMs (GPT-2 XL, LLaMA-2, LLaMA-3) and four representative downstream tasks (reasoning, summarization, open-domain QA, NLI), broadly demonstrating the impact on general abilities.
- The proposed method (PRUNE) effectively mitigates the decline in general abilities caused by sequential edits across multiple metrics, showing considerable performance improvement.
- PRUNE is plug-and-play, applicable to all existing parameter-modifying model editing methods.
- The paper provides an interesting analysis of potential reasons behind loss of general ability, identifying increasing singular values of the edit-matrix as a function of edit count.
- The theoretically derived upper bound offers a quantitative limit beyond which model degradation occurs, deepening understanding of trade-offs in weight modification.

### Weaknesses

**Major concerns about theoretical motivation and explanation (raised by Reviewers 1 and 4, with minority more negative view):**

- The matrix perturbation analysis in §3.2 is not clearly relevant. Since keys (k) are inputs to the edited matrix and remain unchanged before and after editing (the MLP layer is unchanged except for W), the meaning of ∆k is unclear.
- In §3.2 line 241, spectral norm definition is used but line 203 says ||*|| represents 2-norm; clarification needed.
- The hypothetical situation in lines 271–275 is unclear: if it involves N sequential edits about the same fact, each edit matrix will not equal ∆W₁ because the base matrix changes. This appears incorrect.
- **Contradictory evidence:** Table 4 shows singular values increase far more dramatically for MEMIT than for other methods, yet MEMIT is the most stable editing method, contradicting the claim that singular value blow-up causes degradation. Reviewers ask: what is the author's take?
- The choice of restraining function (log function) in Equations 4 and 5 is not well-motivated; more ablation on different actions on larger singular values of ∆W_N is needed.
- **Deep question:** Attenuating the largest singular values (the most significant direction of matrix action) may cause issues; why is it okay to attenuate them? This warrants investigation.
- Experiments in Figures 3 and 4 involve too few sequential edits (≤1000). The method would be more believable with results for at least 1000 edits, preferably more.
- Two reviewers state that while the empirical contribution is interesting, the explanations/motivations for why the approach works are insufficient.

**Missing comparisons and analyses (Reviewers 2 and 3):**

- Comparison with other sequential editing methods is needed in the related work section. Additional discussion on works aimed at preserving general capability after editing would improve the paper.
- Potential negative impacts of PRUNE’s constraints (Equations 3–5) on the efficacy of the most recent knowledge edits are not analyzed. Does the constraint reduce the model's ability to retain other capabilities or historical knowledge? A more detailed discussion is required.
- The model requires using historical ∆W in each editing operation, leading to increased computational resources (memory/compute overhead). This should be discussed.
- The study focuses solely on LLaMA-2 (7B) with a relatively small sample set. Broader evaluations using a smaller GPT series model or a single editing approach with expanded sample size under computational constraints would strengthen evidence.
- The PCA visualization discrepancy is unclear; a modified visualization may clarify.
- Baseline capabilities of LLaMA on general abilities in Table 2 are missing.

**Additional clarity requests (all reviewers):**

- It is unclear why the paper focuses on analyzing changes in kᵢ instead of v or W in the editing setup (where typically only W and v are modified).
- More intuition and explanation for the choice the restraining function (log) in Equations 4 and 5 is needed.
- Some edits may still be lost even with PRUNE; a deeper analysis of this limitation would be valuable (minor).
- Many choices and decisions in the paper appear unmotivated; several questions were listed and require clarification.
