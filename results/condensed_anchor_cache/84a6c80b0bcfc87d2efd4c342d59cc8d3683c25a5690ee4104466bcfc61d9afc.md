## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes ESSformer, a segment-based sparse Transformer for multivariate long-term time-series forecasting, introducing two attention modules: Periodic Attention (PeriA) for temporal dependencies via block-diagonal and dilated attention, and Random-Partition Attention (R-PartA) for feature dependencies by randomly partitioning features and applying attention within groups. Reviewers generally found the paper well-written and clearly presented, with extensive empirical evaluation across seven datasets showing strong performance. However, opinions diverge: three reviewers (scores 6) praised the novelty of the attention mechanisms and the comprehensive ablation and efficiency analyses, while one reviewer (score 3) raised more fundamental concerns about limited novelty, questionable effectiveness, and insufficient theoretical and comparative analysis.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, easy to follow, and uses precise language with meticulous details and beautiful illustrations (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- The two proposed attention modules (PeriA and R-PartA) are novel, intuitive, and address efficiency for segment-based Transformers (Reviewers 1, 4).
- Extensive empirical evaluation on seven real-world datasets demonstrates superior forecasting performance against several leading baselines (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- Comprehensive ablation studies validate the contribution of each module and show efficiency gains in computation (Reviewer 1).
- Theoretical complexity analysis and comparisons of FLOPs and memory usage are provided (Reviewer 3).
- Abundant analyses and visualizations support the effectiveness of the proposed methods (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Ensemble advantage of R-PartA**: R-PartA uses multiple passes with ensemble during inference, which may give an unfair advantage over baselines. The reported results in Table 1 likely benefit from ensembling; performance without ensembling is not reported and could be significantly lower. The average rank of ESSformer without ensembling is not provided (Reviewer 1, including their Question 1).
- **Effectiveness and novelty of sparse attention modules**:
  - ESSformer is not a purely Transformer model; it includes an MLP that encapsulates the two attention modules, making it a simplified version of TSMixer, which already performs well. Ablation experiments show minor performance differences between ablated and non-ablated models, and Table 1 shows no significant gain over TSMixer (Reviewer 3).
  - The overall novelty is limited given the modest empirical improvement over existing methods (Reviewer 2). One reviewer (score 3) was particularly unconvinced, while others (scores 6) considered the contributions novel.
- **Random partition attention mechanism underexplained**:
  - It is unclear why random partitioning of features works. The interpretation that the model "overcomes feature dropping" is unconvincing; reducing the feature count may naturally improve metrics, as univariate forecasting often outperforms multivariate forecasting (Reviewer 3).
  - There is no guidance on choosing the number of groups or the number of ensemble instances during inference, nor a clear relationship between these hyperparameters (Reviewer 4, including their questions). No metrics are provided to determine optimal values before or during training (Reviewer 4).
- **Periodic attention limitations**:
  - PeriA requires a pre-defined periodic interval (block size P) and cannot handle multiple or overlapping periodicities, nor non-stationary periodic patterns common in real-world data (Reviewer 4).
  - The term "periodic" is misleading because block size P is empirically selected and does not reflect intrinsic periodicity of the time series; the title's "periodic and random sparsity" is inaccurate as sparsity applies only to the temporal dimension and randomness only to the feature dimension (Reviewer 2).
- **Theoretical complexity analysis insufficient**:
  - The complexity derivation, e.g., why R-PartA reduces to O(D S_G), is not clearly explained (Reviewer 2, Question 1).
  - The complexity comparison does not include all baseline models from Table 1, such as PatchTST, which is a puzzling omission (Reviewer 3).
