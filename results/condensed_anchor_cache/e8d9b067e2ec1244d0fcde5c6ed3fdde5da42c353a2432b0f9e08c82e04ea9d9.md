## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper addresses privacy-restricted medical QA by using LLMs to generate a knowledge-intensive context from extracted keywords, which is then fed to a fine-tuned SLM. Evaluated on MedQA, HEADQA, MedMCQA, and MMLU-professional medicine, it achieves up to 22.57% absolute accuracy improvement over SLM fine-tuning without context. Experiments cover few-shot, full training, and out-of-domain settings.

### Strengths
- **Timely and important problem**: Extracting useful information from incomplete text under privacy protection using LLM knowledge to boost lightweight SLM performance (R1, R3, R4). Method has important social significance (R4).
- **Novel idea**: Using keywords instead of raw text to query LLM privately for SLM prediction (R2, R3). The demonstrations of synthetic medical data with private information and extracted keywords help understanding (R2).
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: Thorough experiments on multiple medical QA datasets, including few-shot, full training, and out-of-distribution generalization (R1, R2, R4). Significant accuracy gains over baselines (up to 22.57% absolute) (R2). Results compared across different training set sizes (R4).
- **Privacy-preserving focus**: Essential for clinical text; approach demonstrates effectiveness while maintaining privacy (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited algorithmic contribution**: Main work appears engineering-oriented – transferring domain knowledge from LLMs to SLMs; innovation relies heavily on existing methods like the medical NER model from Neumann et al. 2019 (R1, R4). The overall methodology needs improvement (R4).
- **Unclear motivation for SLM**: No evidence provided for a large performance gap between open-source LLMs (e.g., Llama) and ChatGPT on the concerned medical tasks; the premise of using SLM over open-source LLMs is questioned (R2). This weakens the motivation (R2).
- **Privacy definition and evaluation insufficient**:
  - No clear scope of private information; seems limited to PII (Figure 1); cannot address general privacy leakage considered by strict formulations like differential privacy (R2).
  - Keyword extraction may still leak PII (names, dates, addresses) if the NER extracts sensitive tokens or makes errors (e.g., classifying address as symptom). No theoretical guarantee or empirical leakage rate provided; should discuss filtering/postprocessing steps (R2, R3).
  - Privacy budget metric (ratio of words provided to LLM to total words) lacks intuitive explanation linking it to actual privacy risks (R2).
  - Example in Figure 1 is imprecise: privacy issues appear only in the first sentence, and the keywords “man” and “admitted” have almost no impact; simply deleting the first sentence might achieve privacy, making the keyword extraction role unclear (R4).
- **Need for ablation on LLM-generated content**: Unclear which part of the LLM output improves SLM most; e.g., what is the performance if only the LLM’s preliminary decision is fed to the SLM? (R3)
- **Input length issue**: Adding LLM-generated content may cause concatenated input to exceed the SLM’s word window; how is this handled? (R3)
- **Limited task scope**: Datasets are QA test datasets; applicability to medical report datasets requires additional experiments, including how to generate high-quality questions from other LLM interfaces (R1).
- **Risk of incorrect information transfer**: LLMs might introduce incorrect or irrelevant information into SLM predictions; how to ensure only relevant, accurate knowledge is enhanced? (R1)
- **Insufficient related work**: Should discuss more LLM applications in clinical settings, especially knowledge-enhanced models (e.g., “Qualifying Chinese Medical Licensing Examination with Knowledge Enhanced Generative Pre-training Model”) (R3).
- **Annotation error**: Annotation 13 on page 8 appears in the text but is not explained (R4).
- **Suggested improvement**: Enhance NER model to consider age as a crucial keyword for certain diseases (R4).
