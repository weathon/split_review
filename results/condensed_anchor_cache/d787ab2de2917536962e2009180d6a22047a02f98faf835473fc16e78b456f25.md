## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Takin-VC, a zero-shot voice conversion framework that combines a hybrid content encoder (WavLM + HybridFormer quantized via neural codec training) with cross-attention-based context-aware timbre modeling and a memory-augmented conditional flow matching (CFM) module. The method aims to improve speaker similarity and naturalness. Reviewers generally acknowledge the solid engineering and competitive results, but most find the novelty limited and note several missing analyses and comparisons. One reviewer (score 5) is more positive, praising the encoder design and decoupling operations; the other three (score 3) raise significant concerns about novelty, ablation depth, and experimental clarity.

### Strengths
- Figure 5 effectively visualizes speaker similarity between ground truth and converted speech.
- Both subjective (MOS) and objective (SIM, CER, WER) evaluations on small and large datasets demonstrate competitive performance against existing baselines, with clear improvement in speaker similarity and naturalness.
- The paper provides a clear and thorough explanation of the Jointly Hybrid Content and Memory-Augmented Context-Aware Timbre Modeling techniques.
- The proposed method achieves competitive results compared to state-of-the-art systems.
- The encoder design integrating PPG and SSL features is notable; it compensates for the shortcomings of both feature types and better extracts semantic content.
- The writing style is good and the narrative is clear; the entire method is described with sufficient accuracy.
- The decoupling operations of timbre and semantic content prior to CFM hold reference significance in the context of large models.
- The approach addresses timbre leakage through context-aware timbre features and a multi-stage pre-trained content encoder.
- Comprehensive experiments include ablation studies and evaluations on both small and large datasets, showing improvements over SOTA in subjective and objective metrics.

### Weaknesses
- **Notation and figure clarity** (Reviewers 1, 4): Some notation in Sec. 3.1 is hard to understand; figures could be improved to better match the used notations and modules, making the architecture easier to follow.
- **Timbre leakage from SSL features** (Reviewers 1, 3): SSL features are merely quantized (VQ), which does not guarantee complete decoupling of timbre and semantic content. The paper notes PPG features can offer disentangled timbre information, so a comparison of using only VQ vs. using PPG vs. using further purified semantic information (e.g., text) is needed. The current ablation only shows that using PPG is insufficient. Reviewer 3 recommends explicitly testing the effectiveness of VQ in experiments.
- **Inadequate analysis of context-aware timbre modeling** (Reviewer 1): The importance of local context in timbre adaptation is not sufficiently analyzed. No experiment shows the performance when using only a global timbre embedding (from the VP model) instead of fine-grained cross-attention.
- **Missing ablation on key components** (Reviewers 1, 3, 4):
  - For the key-value pair generation in the timbre modeling block, an ablation testing only VP features (without reference Mel) is needed.
  - An ablation on neural codec training is missing (e.g., comparing with/without neural codec loss, or training order).
  - Ablation studies overall do not demonstrate notable improvements in audio quality or robustness (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited novelty** (Reviewers 2, 4): The Jointly Hybrid Content module is a combination of HybridFormer and WavLM representations; Context-Aware Timbre Modeling via Cross-Attention is a common technique (already used in methods like GPT-SoVITS); the Memory-Augmented Timbre Modeling block is relatively simple. Much of the work focuses on combining well-established techniques rather than introducing fundamental innovations.
- **Need comparison with more recent baselines** (Reviewer 2): The paper should compare against recent methods such as CosyVoice-VC and Seed-VC to better showcase advantage over contemporaneous work.
- **Real-time performance claim lacks support** (Reviewer 4): The paper claims improved real-time performance but provides no analysis or experiments to substantiate this.
- **Dataset details insufficient** (Reviewers 1, 2): More explanation about the large-scale dataset (e.g., examples, statistics) is needed; differences in training datasets across compared methods may introduce data bias.
- **Figure 4 input mismatch** (Reviewer 1): The input to the memory module is labeled as x_sctt in Figure 4, but according to Sec. 3.2.2 it should be x_ref.
- **Neural codec training explanation missing** (Reviewer 4): The paper should clarify the objective function and when neural codec training takes place (separately or jointly with other training).
- **Questions requiring further clarification** (Reviewer 4):
  - How to prevent overfitting during neural codec training?
  - For the neural codec involved in Mel and FBank features besides the encoder, how to directly generate speech from the fused features of WavLM and HybridFormer (Figure 2)?
- **Potential audio demonstrations lacking** (Reviewer 1): No demo page or supplementary audio examples are provided to showcase the converted speech.

**Note on reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 5) found the work more compelling, specifically praising the encoder design and the decoupling operations as significant, and considered the narrative clear and accurate. The other three reviewers (score 3) expressed stronger concerns about limited novelty, insufficient ablation depth, and lack of comparisons with recent methods.
