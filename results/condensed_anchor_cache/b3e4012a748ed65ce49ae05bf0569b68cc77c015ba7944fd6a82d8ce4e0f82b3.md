## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies why PLM-based retrievers exhibit source bias (preferring LLM-generated content) and hypothesizes a causal effect between document perplexity and relevance estimation. It proposes an inference-time debiasing method, Causal Diagnosis and Correction (CDC), which diagnoses and separates the perplexity bias. Experiments on three IR domains with MLM-based PLM retrievers and controlled causal analyses support the hypothesis and show debiasing effectiveness. A theoretical analysis relates MLM and IR loss gradients. All three reviewers find the topic important and the causal hypothesis underexplored, but they differ in enthusiasm (scores 8, 6, 5). The main concerns are limited scope (old models, no LLM/CLM retrievers), insufficient validation of the rewriting method used for causal experiments, lack of formalism in the theoretical section, and limited applicability.

### Strengths
- Important and timely topic: the first study to systematically explore the causal mechanism behind source bias in PLM retrievers (a consensual but previously underexplored hypothesis).  
- Clear technical description of the bias, including a causal graph, and a series of experiments (correlation in Sec 3.1, causality in Sec 4.1, mitigation).  
- The linear model fit for perplexity given document and semantic variables strengthens the causal graph argument.  
- Human annotation is used to verify that synthetic documents maintain correctness.  
- Explicit inclusion of document semantic nuance as a variable in the experiments is appreciated.  
- The proposed CDC debiasing method is relevant, novel, and effectively reduces bias across several datasets and LLMs (reviewer 2 finds it “relevant, new and effective”; reviewer 1 calls it “an appreciated contribution”).  
- The analysis of the relationship between MLM and IR loss functions (Sec 4.2) provides additional insights into why the bias occurs (reviewer 3 notes this as interesting).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited model scope and outdated architectures.** The experiments focus exclusively on small, older PLMs (BERT, RoBERTa) and do not include more recent LLMs or SLMs. The analysis is restricted to MLM-based pre-training; models trained with CLM (e.g., GPT-based retrievers) are not considered. The proposed debiasing method shows more effect on older PLMs than on newer ones, raising concerns about whether the phenomenon and CDC generalize to state-of-the-art retrievers. (Reviewers 1 and 3 both raise this; reviewer 3 also notes MLM is “becoming less and less relevant.”)  
- **Insufficient experimental validation of the rewriting method for causal experiments.** The paper assumes that manipulating sampling temperature changes perplexity without altering semantic content. This claim is not rigorously verified: the human evaluation in Appendix E.2.1 only assesses *relevance* (not semantic equivalence) on only 20 triplets, and high temperatures may cause hallucination, altering meaning or relevance. The authors should provide additional evaluation using LLM-based metrics, human eval for semantics, LSA/LDA, or non-LLM embedding similarity. (Reviewer 2, detailed criticism; also affects confidence in the causal analysis of Sec 4.1.)  
- **Theoretical section (Sec 4.2) lacks formalism and contains unclear or potentially incorrect derivations.** Notations are poorly introduced (e.g., dimensions of matrices \(\mathbf{W}\), \(\mathbf{d}_{\text{emb}}\) as an \(L \times N\) matrix with an undefined “l2 norm of 1”, orthogonality assumptions that are unrealistic for non-square PLM projection matrices). Key assumptions (e.g., fine-tuning preserves the “inversion property”) are not motivated. The derivation of the constant \(k\) (line 810) is problematic: dimensionality is unclear, positivity is not guaranteed, the QM-AM inequality is misapplied, and the sign of \(k\) could invert the predicted dependency. These issues make the theoretical argument difficult to follow and potentially flawed. (Reviewer 2, high severity; minor notation and formula issues such as ANCE being introduced late (line 264 vs line 145) and MLM loss division by \(L \times D\) vs \(L\) should also be fixed.)  
- **Limited generalizability and broader relevance.** The study is confined to PLM-based retrievers; some may argue that well-trained retrievers do not suffer from this bias, or that standard training could address it. The paper’s scope may be more suited to SIGIR than to the broader ICLR community. (Reviewer 3; also echoed by reviewer 1’s call for cross-model experiments.)  
- **Potential limitation of CDC for closed models.** The method may not be applicable to closed-source models where training data is unknown. (Reviewer 1.)  
- **Relevance over time.** As retrieval models are increasingly trained on synthetic data, the observed relationship between low perplexity and high relevance (derived from human-trained retrievers) might change, potentially diminishing the continued relevance of this study. (Reviewer 1’s question, reframed as a weakness.)  
- **Causal graph may not fully account for document semantics.** The question “Would not ‘document semantic’ be explained away?” suggests the causal analysis could be incomplete. (Reviewer 3.)  
- **Missing dataset statistics.** The paper does not provide basic statistics (e.g., size, source) for the datasets used, which should be listed. (Reviewer 3’s request, minor.)
