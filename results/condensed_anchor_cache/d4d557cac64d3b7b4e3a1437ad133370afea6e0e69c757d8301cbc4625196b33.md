## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a stepwise internalization method that trains a model initially trained for explicit chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning to internalize those reasoning steps by gradually removing intermediate tokens during fine-tuning. This yields a model that performs implicit CoT, achieving high accuracy on tasks like 9-by-9 multiplication (GPT-2 Small up to 99%) and GSM8K (Mistral 7B over 50%) while significantly reducing inference costs.

### Strengths
- The paper is well written and easy to follow (R1, R2).
- The technical idea is original, well-motivated, and sound (R2).
- Experiments are solid, with compelling results and insightful analysis (R2). The one reviewer (score 8) found the results particularly compelling, while others noted performance shortcomings (see Weaknesses).
- The stepwise internalization approach greatly enhances inference efficiency for reasoning tasks (multi-digit multiplication faster than explicit CoT) and achieves the same or close accuracy as explicit CoT on parity, coin flip, and multi-digit multiplication, outperforming both no-CoT inference and knowledge distillation (R3, R4).
- Demonstrates that smaller models can be trained to perform reasoning tasks effectively by leveraging internal states (R3).
- Interpretability analysis provides insights: the implicit CoT model can replicate partial product steps internally, though internalized reasoning does not yet fully replicate explicit CoT; these findings can motivate future research (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Shortcut concern / genuine internalization questioned.** Figure 3 shows validation accuracy declining as tokens are progressively removed, then recovering after full removal. This suggests the model may have learned a shortcut rather than genuinely internalizing CoT, casting doubt on the core claim (R1, high confidence).
- **Insufficient evidence for internalization.** The probing analysis (Section 6.1) lacks details on probe training, which layer’s hidden states are analyzed, and comparison with a pretrained model to show it does not already internalize CoT. Without these, the claim of true internalization is not adequately supported (R1).
- **Performance behind explicit CoT on most tasks.** The implicit CoT models have worse performance than explicit CoT prompting on the majority of evaluated tasks. Explicit CoT requires no training, is simple to use, and is much more generalizable. Most reviewers considered this a significant limitation, though one reviewer (score 8) found the results solid and the approach a strong contribution (R3, R4). The paper’s framing as a speed–accuracy trade-off was not seen as a major advantage given explicit CoT’s test-time applicability (R4).
- **Weak no-CoT baselines.** The “No CoT” setting for Mistral 7B, GPT-3.5, and GPT-4 in Table 3 uses unclear prompts (e.g., “Do not output intermediate steps”?) that may unfairly depress performance. Also, ICoT-SI is extensively trained on GSM8K, making a direct comparison with few-shot no-CoT versions of those models inappropriate. A more proper baseline would fine-tune CoT-trained models to output only the final answer, with comparable training epochs (R1, R3).
- **Limited task scope and generalization.** Experiments are mostly in-distribution on simple reasoning patterns (multiplication, grade-school math). The method is not evaluated on compositional tasks requiring more intermediate steps or working memory, nor on more complex datasets like MATH. The strong generalizability of explicit CoT is well-established, but the proposed method’s generalization remains unclear (R3, R4).
- **Lack of larger-model and broader comparison.** Only small models are used (largest Mistral 7B); no comparable-size models like LLaMA 3.1 are included. This raises questions about the method’s effectiveness relative to simply scaling model size or data, especially since large models already perform well on simple math (R2, R3, R4).
- **Efficiency of training procedure.** Removing 8 tokens per epoch implies many training epochs are needed; this number increases with longer thought chains and larger models, severely limiting training efficiency (R4).
- **Choice of synthetic CoT data not justified.** The paper does not justify why the specific synthetic CoT steps were chosen; simpler approaches (e.g., deterministic numerical transformations) might be more effective in decreasing latency and increasing accuracy (R3).
- **Unverified inference pipeline for baseline models.** GPT-4 with 5-shot no-CoT achieved only 44% on GSM8K, while publicly reported GPT-3.5 (57.1%) and Mixtral (58.4%) are higher. It is unclear whether the inference pipeline reproduces public scores, making the baseline results questionable (R4).
- **Open questions about working memory loss and motivations.** The paper does not address how losing token-based “working memory” from explicit CoT affects complex compositional reasoning (R3). Moreover, the main motivation beyond latency improvement is not clearly articulated, and whether implicit CoT can match or surpass explicit CoT in all reasoning tasks remains unexplored (R2, R3).
