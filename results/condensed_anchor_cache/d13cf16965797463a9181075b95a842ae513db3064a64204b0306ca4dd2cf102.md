## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents E2LLM, a method for long-context LLM processing that splits long contexts into chunks, compresses each chunk into an embedding vector using a pretrained text encoder (GTE-Large-en), aligns these representations with a decoder-only LLM (LLaMA-2 7B Chat) via a two-layer MLP adapter, and trains with two objectives: reconstruction of the encoder output and long-context instruction fine-tuning. Evaluations are conducted on summarization (QMSum, GovReport), QA (Quality, NarrativeQA, TriviaQA, HotpotQA), Needle-in-a-Haystack, and LongBench, with comparisons to YaRN, LongLoRA, CEPE, StreamingLLM, and LLoCO. The method shows competitive results and lower inference time/memory. Reviewers overall rate the paper between 5 and 6; scores: 6,5,6,6,5.

### Strengths
*   Addresses the important problem of long-context processing in LLMs (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5).
*   Novel encoder-adapter-decoder framework that balances performance, computational efficiency, and compatibility with pretrained models (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5).
*   Achieves strong empirical results on multiple downstream tasks in supervised fine-tuning settings, including good performance on summarization, QA, Needle, and LongBench (R1, R2, R5).
*   Exhibits the lowest inference time and memory usage among compared methods (R1, R2, R5).
*   Includes useful ablation studies (R2) and a comprehensive evaluation (R3, R4).
*   Presentation is clear to some reviewers, with the T1/T2/T3 categorization easy to follow (R3, R4); the method requires minimal retraining beyond the adapter (R3).

### Weaknesses
*   **Benchmark evaluation is insufficient and not up-to-date.**  
    - Most benchmarks have average length far below 128k and are not on par with recent challenging evaluations like RULER or HELMET (R1, R3).  
    - The Needle-in-a-Haystack evaluation is poorly presented: no heat map to show failure positions per depth (R1), and the figure is deceptive (all green despite scores <5) – the actual recall success rate is only ~30%, whereas current long-context LLMs achieve nearly 100%, indicating a significant limitation in precise information retrieval (R5).  
    - There are inconsistencies in dataset reporting (e.g., Sec 4.1 lists HotpotQA but Table 1/4 show Quality) (R2).  
    - Performance breakdown by input length is missing (R1).
*   **Baseline comparisons are unfair, uncontrolled, or missing key methods.**  
    - E2LLM uses LLaMA 2 7B *Chat* as the decoder, while baselines use LLaMA 2 7B (non-chat), and Appendix B calls LLaMA 2 7B the backbone for all methods – a controlled experiment with the same backbone is needed (R2).  
    - Comparison with CEPE uses a different encoder (GTE-Large-en vs. Contriever) and different LoRA rank, so performance gains cannot be attributed to the proposed method alone (R4).  
    - Hyperparameter tuning for all methods is unclear, raising reproducibility and fairness concerns (R4).  
    - No comparison with the mainstream SoTA paradigm *continual pretraining on long texts + long-context SFT* (e.g., LLaMA 3.1, GLM4) is provided, despite the authors’ claim of solving the “impossible triangle” (R5).  
    - E2LLM is only evaluated in supervised fine-tuning settings, unlike prior methods (YaRN, LongLoRA, CEPE) that are studied in continual pretraining with zero-shot or ICL evaluation; such settings should be tested (R2).
*   **Model and training details are unclear or insufficiently analyzed.**  
    - The compression process is poorly explained – chunk size is a key hyperparameter whose effect on results (e.g., negative log-likelihood at chunk boundaries) is not studied; the architecture figure (Figure 1) is too high-level and design choices (e.g., why a two-layer MLP adapter) are not justified (R1).  
    - The complexity analysis is flawed: it does not separate computation for the context part vs. the prompt (which goes directly to the decoder), ignoring the overhead of processing the prompt (R4).  
    - The importance and cost of the two training tasks (reconstruction + reasoning) are not analyzed – reconstruction adds extra cost and it is unclear how much each task contributes (R2).  
    - The paper misses related works, including better position embedding extensions (e.g., [1] and improvements to YaRN [2]) and concurrent work FocusLLM which has a very similar design to E2LLM (R4).
*   **Scalability and real-world applicability are questionable.**  
    - Only small-scale LLMs (7B) are tested; feasibility with larger models like LLaMA 70B is unclear (R3).  
    - Real-time application and inference speed compared to baselines are not addressed (R3).  
    - The claim that E2LLM addresses the challenge of requiring training (streamingLLM point) is inconsistent because E2LLM itself requires training (R4).  
    - The maximum compression rate for lossless recovery is not studied, and it is unclear if performance continues to improve with even longer contexts (R5).
*   **Presentation has issues and disagreements.**  
    - Several reviewers find the presentation unclear: architecture details missing, compression process vague, chunk size heuristic (R1); meanwhile R3 and R4 find it clear – this disagreement indicates room for improvement.  
    - There are citation errors (e.g., “Giorgi et al.”, “Ding et al.”, “Kenton & Toutanova” incorrect references) (R2).  
    - Specific claims about other methods are inaccurate (e.g., YaRN dynamic scaling for attention dispersion, StreamingLLM’s inference time behavior) (R2, R4).
*   **Other specific questions that overlap with criticisms.**  
    - Why is E2LLM better than YaRN and LLoCO on NarrativeQA in Table 1 but not in Table 2? (R2)  
    - How would E2LLM perform on RULER? (R1, R3)  
    - Can chunk size be made learnable? (R3)  
    - What is the max compression rate such that context can be recovered losslessly? (R5)  
    - Does PPL/performance continue decreasing/increasing when context window becomes longer? (R5)
