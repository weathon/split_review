## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper formulates the sequential inference of diffusion models as solving a system of banded nonlinear equations, then applies Newton’s method for rootfinding. Key contributions: (1) proving the Jacobian has a unit-diagonal structure, enabling efficient parallel updates; (2) an initialization procedure that accelerates convergence; (3) a sliding-window batching scheme (ParaSolver). Experiments on StableDiffusion-v2, LSUN Church, and ImageNet show up to 12.1× wall-clock speedup without quality loss. Reviewer 2 (score 8) finds the paper well written with sound claims; Reviewers 1 and 3 (scores 6) raise concerns about theoretical justification and missing baselines.

### Strengths
- Comprehensive literature review of parallel sampling for diffusion models.
- Clear presentation of the unified framework and algorithmic details.
- Large-scale experiments on ImageNet (256×256 and 512×512) and LSUN Church (256×256), including comparisons with DDPM, DDIM, and DPMSolver.
- Key technical contributions noted by Reviewer 1: (a) using Newton’s method (quadratic convergence) instead of Picard iterations; (b) exploiting the banded Jacobian structure for efficient recurrence; (c) a theoretically motivated initialization; (d) sliding-window batching that limits parallel scope.
- The approximation of the Jacobian with the identity matrix in Eq. (12)–(13) keeps computational cost near that of a sequential solver (Reviewer 2’s assessment).

### Weaknesses
- **Convergence analysis of Newton’s method (Reviewer 1, high severity):** Smoothness of the nonlinear system (neural network component) is not analyzed. The paper should discuss the Lipschitz constant of the Jacobian, condition number bounds in relevant regions, and how the initialization ensures the iterates stay within a well-behaved region. Also, the risk of convergence to local minima/saddle points is not examined (loss landscape visualization, eigenvalues of Hessian or NTK would help); a comparison with trust-region or quasi-Newton methods (e.g., BFGS) is recommended.
- **Justification for identity-matrix Jacobian approximation (Reviewers 1 and 3):** The claim that the identity matrix is a good approximation (Eq. (12)–(13)) is insufficiently motivated. The paper should explore alternative cheap approximations (e.g., diagonal Jacobian) and provide empirical convergence comparisons (accuracy vs. cost) against the full Jacobian. Reviewer 3 also asks for mathematical intuition and possible error bounds from numerical analysis.
- **Missing baseline: ParaTAA (Reviewers 2 and 3):** The paper acknowledges ParaTAA (Tang et al., ICML 2024) but does not implement it, citing lack of Diffusers integration. Since the method is closely related, its inclusion is essential for a fair comparison. The authors should implement ParaTAA or justify why direct comparison is infeasible.
- **Missing ablation on iteration counts (Reviewer 2):** The paper should show how the number of required iterations is reduced by: (1) using Newton vs. fixed-point iteration, and (2) approximating vs. fully computing the Jacobian—at least on a controlled toy problem.
- **Scope of comparisons (Reviewer 3):** The paper should also compare with other acceleration approaches that are not purely parallel sampling, e.g., knowledge distillation ([3] Luhman & Luhman, [4] Meng et al., [5] Salimans & Ho), restart sampling ([6] Xu et al.), and self-consistency ([7] Song et al.).
- **Tolerance vs. speedup trade-off (Reviewer 1 question):** A graph showing which tolerance yields the best speedup without compromising visual quality is needed.
- **Presentation issues:**
  - Language clarity: passive voice and awkward phrasing (e.g., lines 296–297: “to fast construct …”) should be revised (Reviewers 1, 3). “Reverse of Jacobian matrix” (Sec. 4.2, below Eq. (9)) likely means “inverse” (Reviewer 1).
  - Figure 1 needs higher resolution, axis labels, and consistent style (Reviewer 2). Figure 5: there is no visible difference between images for different N under DDIM but clear differences under DDPM; the authors should provide quantitative analysis or explain why DDIM is less sensitive (Reviewer 2).
  - Tables 1 & 2 list methods in order DDPM, DDIM, DPMSolver, while figures use DDIM, DPMSolver, DDPM; reorder for consistency (Reviewer 2).
  - Consider adding an illustrative figure of ParaSolver (like prior work [2] Shih et al.) (Reviewer 3).
- **Generic name “ParaSolver” does not convey contributions (Reviewer 1).**
- **Choice of rootfinding over optimization (Reviewer 1):** The paper should better argue why rootfinding is preferable to optimization-based methods (e.g., handling constraints or regularization).
