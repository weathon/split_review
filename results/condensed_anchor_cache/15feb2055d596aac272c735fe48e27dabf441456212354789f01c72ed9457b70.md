## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies stochastic contextual bandits with knapsacks (CBwK) in the small-budget regime (B = Ω(√T)). It proposes the Adaptive and Universal Primal–Dual algorithm (AUPD), which achieves Ṽ((1 + ν*/(δb))√T) regret under strict feasibility without knowing the safety margin, and Ṽ(√T + (ν*/√b) T^{3/4}) regret without strict feasibility—the first such result. The algorithm uses cumulative over-consumption as a Lagrange multiplier and a budget-aware scaling parameter V = b√T. Reviewers 1 and 3 find the contributions significant and the presentation clear; Reviewers 2 and 4 raise concerns about technical novelty, clarity, and practical relevance.

### Strengths
- Addresses an important and natural problem (CBwK) and advances existing work by removing the need to know the safety margin (δ). [Reviewers 1, 3, 4]
- Provides the first regret bound for CBwK without the strict feasibility assumption. [Reviewers 1, 3]
- Algorithm is simple, single-stage, and does not require prior knowledge of whether strict feasibility holds. [Reviewer 3]
- Using cumulative over-consumption as a Lagrange multiplier is a natural and effective idea. [Reviewer 3]
- The setting (small budget, B = Ω(√T)) is interesting and leads to intriguing scientific questions. [Reviewer 2]
- Paper is well-written and easy to follow (Reviewer 1); others find it somewhat difficult to read (Reviewers 2, 4). —*Disagreement: Reviewers 1 and 3 rate presentation higher (3/3), while Reviewers 2 and 4 rate it 2/3.*

### Weaknesses
- The paper is somewhat hard to read; it would benefit from being placed in a larger context with respect to prior/concurrent literature, specifically: Kumar & Kleinberg (NeurIPS 2022), Bernasconi et al. (Adaptive Regret Minimization), Slivkins et al. (COLT 2023), and Bernasconi et al. (ICLR 2024). [Reviewer 2]
- Missing discussion of the relationship between contextual bandits with knapsacks and standard bandits with knapsacks. Since the regret bounds do not depend on the number of contexts, is this due to the learning oracle? Are there technical differences from primal–dual methods for standard BwK? [Reviewer 1]
- No discussion of existing lower bounds for this problem or the gap between the lower bound and the proposed upper bound. [Reviewer 1]
- In the no-strict-feasibility case, the regret bound becomes linear (and thus meaningless) when B = √T (e.g., O(T^{3/4}/√b) with b = B/T = 1/√T gives O(T)). This crucial trade-off between budget B and regret R_T is not adequately addressed. [Reviewer 2]
- It is unclear how the algorithm truly handles the small-budget regime differently from the large-budget regime. What would happen if existing algorithms that do not require knowledge of the Slater parameter (e.g., Slivkins et al. 2023) were applied to the small-budget case? What are the specific technical challenges that prevent adapting those techniques? [Reviewers 2, 4]
- The algorithm and analysis appear standard; the technical differences and challenges with respect to prior work are not clearly explained. Why is the Lyapunov drift method more effective than previous approaches? [Reviewer 4]
- In the experiments, the proposed algorithm is strictly better than alternatives in only 1 out of 3 settings; it performs roughly the same as PGD Adaptive in the other two. More settings would be needed to demonstrate superiority. [Reviewer 3]
- The main text lacks intuition behind the proofs of key lemmas (e.g., Lemma 4, Lemma 5). The proofs in the appendix are followable, but a verbal explanation of the main ideas is missing. [Reviewer 3]
- Several minor typos and presentation issues (lines 314, 337, 346, 363–364, 368, 400–401, 418–419, 483–484, 708–709, 981–982, and unclear use of Assumption 3 in Section B.3.1 vs. B.4). [Reviewer 3]
