## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a training-free method for extending LLM context length via cascading sub-cache buffers that selectively retain tokens, combined with a strided prefill stage. The approach outperforms StreamingLLM on streaming perplexity, QA, book summarization, and passkey retrieval (1M tokens with 65K cache), and reduces prefill latency 6.8× vs. FlashAttention on 1M tokens. Reviewers praise the idea as simple and effective, with strong experiments and careful implementation, but raise concerns about baseline selection, insufficient explanations, and practical memory management. All four assign a score of 6.

### Strengths
- The prefilling observation is sound: bulk processing the prompt avoids the latency pitfalls of StreamingLLM and H2O during the prefill stage.
- Intuitively beneficial to keep middle context in addition to sliding window and attention sink, as demonstrated by passkey retrieval performance.
- The idea is simple and effective, and the design carefully considers implementation to achieve actual speedup.
- Experiment results are very good, and the method is well implemented with careful code optimization.
- Experiments are sufficient (latency and accuracy on multiple tasks and LLMs) with clear figures.
- Builds a new paradigm of KV cache with cascading sub-cache, extending context length without increasing complexity or requiring training, providing a promising solution for edge-side LLM inference.

### Weaknesses
- **Baselines are insufficient.** Most experiments compare only with StreamingLLM; missing comparisons with other dynamic KV-cache methods such as PyramidKV, PyramidInfer, InfLLM, Quest, and FastGen (on LongBench), as well as MInference (for prefill-stage sparsity) and the RULER benchmark. A comparison with MInference on both latency and quality is needed. More evaluation on additional datasets (e.g., more perplexity benchmarks, RULER) would strengthen the results.
- **Memory management is tricky.** The cascading approach may cause memory fragmentation and hurt throughput in practical serving systems (e.g., vllm/sglang). Compatibility with continuous batching is unclear.
- **Lack of clear motivation for cascading structure.** The paper does not adequately explain why a cascading sub-cache is more effective than other eviction strategies for maintaining relevant context. More theoretical underpinnings, key observations, and design rationale are needed.
- **Unclear derivation of linear complexity during prefill (Section 3.1).** Processing fixed-size chunks vs. FlashAttention’s tiling is not clearly differentiated; the paper should explain how the proposed method achieves linear attention complexity and highlight the algorithmic differences.
- **Sequential sub-cache procedure may impact efficiency.** Since each sub-cache depends on the previous one, there is a potential bottleneck. The paper should discuss how to avoid this and achieve higher efficiency compared to StreamingLLM (e.g., parallel processing or optimized data transfer).
- **Token selection algorithm using EMA is similar to H2O’s accumulated attention.** The paper needs an in-depth comparison between EMA and H2O’s method, including advantages/disadvantages of using EMA versus simple accumulation, and clarification on the role of the hyperparameter gamma.
- **Missing implementation details and overhead analysis.** The FlashAttention version used (FA1/2/3, Triton/CUDA) is not stated. The memory and IO overhead for computing, storing, and accessing the EMA for each token, and the resulting memory layout changes, should be quantified. Additionally, pseudocode illustrating the integration with FlashAttention (e.g., fusion into online softmax) would improve reproducibility.
