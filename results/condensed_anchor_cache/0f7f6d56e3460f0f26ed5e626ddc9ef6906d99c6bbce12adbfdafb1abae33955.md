## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an autoregressive generative model (ART and ARC) for knowledge graph (KG) completion that avoids negative sampling by factorizing the joint distribution \(p(S,R,D)=p(S)p(R\mid S)p(D\mid S,R)\) and modeling each factor as a categorical distribution via softmax. The approach is intended to produce global, interpretable probability scores. While reviewers acknowledge the importance of addressing the Open World Assumption and the novelty of learning a joint distribution without negative sampling, significant concerns are raised regarding the modeling assumptions, experiment design, evaluation fairness, empirical performance, presentation clarity, and reproducibility. The paper’s contribution is considered limited given the weak results and lack of rigorous justification.

### Strengths
1. **Addressing the Open World Assumption (OWA)**: The paper tackles an important gap in KGE methods—negative sampling conflicts with the OWA by incorrectly treating unseen facts as false negatives. Avoiding negative sampling is a valuable direction (R1, R2, R3, R4).  
2. **Global probabilistic scores**: The method aims to provide a consistent global score interpretable across different queries, unlike local scores from query-based methods or energy-based scores needing post-hoc normalization. Maintaining a probabilistic interpretation throughout training is notable (R1, R2).  
3. **Novel autoregressive formulation**: The decomposition \(p(S,R,D)=p(S)p(R\mid S)p(D\mid S,R)\) and use of neural networks to model conditional probabilities without negative sampling is a relatively novel approach to KG completion (R3; note R2 finds this trivial conditional probability decomposition lacking novelty).  
4. **Detailed mathematical description**: The paper describes the conditional probability reasoning and model structure in mathematical detail (R3).  
5. **Empirical insights on negative sampling**: The analysis of negative sampling bias is a useful finding (R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Problematic modeling assumptions**:  
   - The categorical distribution assumption for \(p(D\mid S,R)\) is unrealistic: it forces one and only one target entity given a subject and relation, whereas real-world KGs have one-to-many mappings (e.g., (Google, employee, Jeff Dean), (Google, employee, Salar Kamangar)). Adding inverse triples (line 251) converts many-to-one into one-to-many, exacerbating the violation. The model therefore cannot express common relational patterns (R1).  
   - The paper assumes facts are sampled i.i.d. (line 134), which does not hold because entities are shared across facts (e.g., observing (Google, employee, Jeff Dean) makes (Google, owned by, Alphabet) more likely). This assumption needs justification or mitigation (R1).  
   - The method lacks novelty beyond a simple application of the conditional probability formula. The paper does not adequately differentiate from existing autoregressive KGC models or justify why this factorization is a significant architectural innovation (R2; R3 finds it relatively novel, but R2 strongly disagrees).

2. **Confusing or missing evaluation of global vs. local scores**:  
   - The concepts of “local” and “global” scores are not formally defined. The necessity and advantages of global scores for downstream tasks (e.g., complex query answering) are not clearly argued. The paper’s claim that query-based methods cannot support inter-contextual query answering is contradicted by works such as GNN-QE, ULTRA-Query, and others that apply NBFNet to complex logical queries on KGs with unseen entities and relations (R1, R2).  
   - No calibration techniques (Tabacof & Costabello 2019, Silva Filho 2023) are applied to the global scores, undermining the claim that the scores are reliable for downstream decision-making (R1).

3. **Unconvincing and incomplete empirical performance**:  
   - ART only outperforms baselines on FB15k-237 (known to have issues), while being substantially inferior on WN18-RR (F1 gap) and OGBLBioKG (several percentage points behind NBFNet). This selective superiority raises concerns about generalizability (R2, R3).  
   - Only two baseline models (NBFNet, ComplEx) are compared. Many recent stronger methods (RotatE, ConvE, DistMult, SACN, DRUM, Neural-LP, CompILE, GPFL) are omitted, making it impossible to assess relative merit (R3).  
   - ARC results are missing from Table 2; including them would allow a fuller comparison of the two proposed architectures (R4).  
   - The global link prediction evaluation is unfair to query-based methods like NBFNet, which are designed for per-query scoring. Merging all test query scores (line 313) diminishes NBFNet’s expressivity; a fair evaluation would instead measure calibration or consistency of scores (R1).  
   - The claim (page 8, line 395) that the model is “more open to the possibility that unknown triples are true” lacks experimental support—no ablation or quantitative analysis connects this property to improved performance (R4).  
   - Figure 2(c) appears to show negative edges excessively centered around zero, contradicting the text that they are “not excessively concentrated around zero” (R1 Q2).

4. **Poor presentation and reproducibility**:  
   - Figure 1 depicts ARC, not ART, and is too small; font sizes need enlargement (R2, R3).  
   - Best numbers in Tables 2 and 3 are not highlighted; Figure 4 lacks an x-axis label; Figure 2 legends are too small (R2).  
   - The paper contains frequent grammatical and spelling errors (R4).  
   - Model architecture details are insufficient for reproducibility: no diagram for ART; missing specifications for activation functions, number of filters, kernel sizes, embedding dimensions, optimizer, learning rate schedule, batch size (R4).  
   - Missing standard benchmark datasets: Nell-995, YAGO3-10, DBpedia (and for biomedical KGs, Bio2RDF and DRKG are more popular than OGBLBioKG) (R3).  
   - The paper does not specify the structural details of ARC’s 1D convolutions or ART’s transformer layers, leaving the inductive biases unclear (R4).

5. **Unclear contribution and intended purpose**:  
   - The core contribution—learning a joint distribution without negative sampling—is undermined by the model’s poor expressivity (categorical assumption) and weak link prediction results (R4).  
   - The model seems unable to learn a robust distribution over triples (Table 6 results are underwhelming). Since generative capabilities like sampling new triples are explicitly stated as not necessary, what is the intended purpose of this model? (R4; referencing Loconte et al. 2023, who turn KGEs into generative models via MLE without negative sampling).  
   - Reviewer disagreement: R3 finds the approach relatively novel, while R2 and R4 question its novelty and significance given the results.
