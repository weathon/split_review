## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces MathCheck, a framework for evaluating mathematical reasoning in (M)LLMs through a checklist that tests task generalization and reasoning robustness. It generates two datasets: MathCheck-GSM (text-based) from GSM8k and MathCheck-GEO (multi-modal) from GeoQA, UniGeo, Geometry3K. The evaluation covers four tasks (Problem Solving, Answerable Judging, Outcome Judging, Process Judging) and four varieties (Original Problem, Problem Understanding, Irrelevant Disturbance, Scenario Understanding). 26 LLMs and 17 MLLMs are assessed. Frontier models like GPT-4o show robust performance, while many others degrade significantly. The framework correlates well with true mathematical abilities (e.g., linearity with complexity, correlation with private dataset GSM1k) and is extensible to other reasoning tasks.

### Strengths
- Novel evaluation paradigm: The 4x4 matrix (4 tasks × 4 varieties) goes beyond traditional problem-solving and tests whether models truly understand problems, not just solve them. This is a fresh and valuable perspective.
- Comprehensive model coverage: 43 models evaluated (26 LLMs, 17 MLLMs), including open/closed, large/small, generalist/specialized, and multiple prompting strategies, providing strong baselines.
- Methodological rigor: Careful data generation (84.61% pass rate via manual validation), human verification, and validation through correlation with a private high-quality dataset (GSM1k) and compression efficiency metrics. The benchmark better reflects true mathematical abilities and represents mathematical intelligence more linearly.
- Dual modality: Includes both text-only and multi-modal parts, serving multiple research communities.
- Good presentation: Clear writing, illustrative Figure 1, well-structured sections, easy to follow.
- Extensibility: The framework is applied to other domains (data understanding, code generation), and is adaptable to other reasoning tasks (e.g., commonsense reasoning, code) and useful for methods like MCTS and LLM-as-a-judge.
- Significant contribution: Provides a more reliable way to evaluate true mathematical reasoning and identifies stagnation/degradation from fine-tuning.

### Weaknesses
- **Difficulty saturation**: MathCheck-GSM is built on GSM8k (grade-school level). Frontier models (e.g., O1-preview, GPT-4o) achieve >90% accuracy, making it hard to discriminate among top models. Constructing a version from MATH or another harder benchmark would be more informative. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4; Reviewer 1 considers this minor, Reviewer 4 more critical.)
- **Limited seed diversity**: The seed data only includes simple word problems (GSM8k) and geometry problems (GeoQA, etc.), restricting problem diversity. (Reviewer 1)
- **Data generation pipeline**: The 86% pass rate (84.61% with manual validation) implies ~14-15% of generated questions fail quality checks. The paper does not discuss how low-quality questions are handled when detected, nor whether the released benchmark excludes them. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Insufficient model improvement insights**: The evaluation does not yield new insights to guide model improvement; it only diagnoses weaknesses. Further, passing all checklist aspects may not guarantee true reasoning ability. (Reviewer 4)
- **Diversity across question groups**: Since questions are LLM-generated and only human-verified, there is a risk that LLMs produce similar irrelevant disturbances across different seed questions, reducing evaluation comprehensiveness. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing citations / related work expansion**: Should cite papers on functional variation benchmarks that programmatically generate question variants (Reviewer 3). The related work section needs expansion: add paragraphs on AI for math (training, inference, prompting, formal tools) and on similar robustness benchmarks in other domains (e.g., counterfactual tasks; Reviewer 1). Line 431 missing references, Line 463 incorrect quote mark (Reviewer 2).
- **Minor presentation issues**: Inconsistent (M)LLM usage (Reviewer 3). Tables 1 and 2 could be plotted for easier comparison (Reviewer 3). Figure 5 text too small; bar chart recommended (Reviewer 3). Figure 6 missing x-axis label (Reviewer 3). Figure 7 colors hard to differentiate (pink vs. red; Reviewer 3). Unclear meaning of "data statistic" in Appendix C tables 3-5 (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing clarification on human evaluation**: Who are the human annotators (authors or others)? How is "pass" determined? Not explicitly stated. (Reviewer 1)
- **Sample count discrepancy**: For 129 style groups in MathCheck-GSM, expected total samples = 129 × 16 = 2064, but reported as 3096. Please clarify. (Reviewer 4)
- **Questions overlapping with criticisms/missing experiments**:
  - Figure 6: Could accuracy drop be due to increasing difficulty in generating task entries for complex problems? (Reviewer 2)
  - Appendix C2: Pass rates for different complexity levels? Would be useful if not omitted. (Reviewer 2)
  - Pass rate for GPT-4o rewriting for MathCheck-GEO? (Reviewer 3)
  - Are questions that fail the pass rate filtered out of the released benchmark? (Reviewer 3)
