## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper studies decentralized optimization with affine coupled constraints. It establishes lower complexity bounds and proposes a first-order algorithm (based on Chebyshev acceleration and the APAPC method of Salim et al. 2022a) that matches those bounds—the first linearly convergent first-order method for this general setting. The authors derive tight convergence guarantees under smooth and strongly convex objectives and demonstrate the approach on a linear regression experiment.

### Strengths

*   **First lower bound for decentralized optimization under affine constraints** and a matching optimal algorithm, achieving tight analysis (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
*   **Novel reformulation** from problem (1) to problem (20) that provides a strong foundation for the algorithm (Reviewer 1).
*   **Solid discussion of related literature** and clear positioning relative to prior work; the connection to vertical federated learning (VFL) is a useful starting point (Reviewer 2).
*   **Efficient off-the-shelf algorithm** that closes a gap in the literature, using the right tools to provide a well-rounded solution (Reviewer 3).
*   **First-order iterations** with lower computational burden than proximal methods; suitable for different problem types (Reviewers 2, 4).
*   While most reviewers found the contribution incremental (see Weaknesses), **Reviewer 2 (score 8) strongly emphasized the theoretical tightness and the generalizability/significance of the coupled-constraints setting**, and noted the potential for technical understanding of VFL.

### Weaknesses

*   **Incremental contribution**: The algorithm heavily adapts Chebyshev acceleration and APAPC, both well-established techniques. The lower bound proceeds directly from standard lower bounds in the unconstrained case. The reformulation (10) is natural, and the results are not considered technically novel or challenging (Reviewers 1, 3). Reviewer 1 noted that the adaptation of Chebyshev acceleration to problem (20) needs more justification.
*   **Practical applicability**: The algorithm is complex, with multiple algorithmic blocks (Algorithms 2–5) and many hyperparameters (ρ, ν, δ, p) that lack tuning guidelines. Parameter sensitivity is not explored, and the theoretical optimality may not translate to good real-world performance (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 pointed out that the proposed method uses a multi-communication dual-loop approach, while single-loop algorithms (e.g., gradient tracking, ADMM) exist in the decentralized consensus literature and are preferable in practice due to the communication bottleneck.
*   **Insufficient experiments**: The numerical validation is too limited—only linear regression, which does not demonstrate scaling with condition numbers κ_f, κ_A, κ_W, nor test on non-convex objectives, more complex constraint structures, or real-world datasets. Comparisons with baseline methods for specific constraints (e.g., EXTRA for consensus) are missing (Reviewers 1, 4). Reviewer 3 recommended tuning the communication parameter _r_ and showing its impact. Reviewer 2 noted that experiments with real data or neural networks (even if assumptions are not satisfied) would be appreciated for VFL.
*   **Missing analysis without strong convexity**: Existing works (e.g., reference [1]) do not require the strongly-convex assumption, but the paper provides no convergence analysis under a non-strongly-convex setting (Reviewer 4).
*   **Narrow scope of lower bound**: The lower bound is derived for a specific class of algorithms represented by the proposed method; other algorithmic classes are not discussed (Reviewer 2). The question of whether an algorithm can be designed whose operations depend only on each respective condition number (κ_f, κ_A, κ_W) remains open (Reviewer 2's question, seen as a criticism of the bound's scope).
*   **Presentation**: The contribution should be highlighted more clearly; the paper would be more reader-friendly if the main algorithm appeared in Section 4, preceded by the introduction of Chebyshev acceleration and APAPC (Reviewer 4).
*   **Specific missing clarifications**: Results on vertical federated learning should be stated more precisely (e.g., does it simply correspond to κ_A = 1?) and compared with existing model-partitioned distributed optimization (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 asked whether the complexity for the special case of unconstrained consensus can be reduced to match optimal algorithms (Scaman et al., 2017).
