## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces FlexPrefill, a flexible sparse pre-filling mechanism that dynamically adjusts attention patterns and computational budget per input and head. Two key innovations: (1) Query-Aware Sparse Pattern Determination uses Jensen-Shannon divergence to switch between query-specific diverse patterns and predefined "vertical-slash" patterns; (2) Cumulative-Attention Based Index Selection selects query-key indices so the sum of attention scores meets a threshold. Experiments on RULER and Infinite Bench show FlexPrefill achieves comparable results to SOTA on GLM and Yi, and outperforms with a large margin on Llama and Qwen (Reviewer 1). The method is based on solid observations and provides concrete algorithms (Reviewer 3). Reviewers note flexibility and dynamism distinct from MInference (Reviewers 1,3). All four reviewers assign a rating of 8, but Reviewer 3 assigns Contribution 2 (others 3), indicating some concern about novelty relative to existing work.

### Strengths
- **Key idea of dynamic adjustment**: Adaptively optimizing sparse patterns and computational budget per input and attention head is interesting and reasonable (Reviewer 1). This flexibility distinguishes FlexPrefill from MInference (Reviewers 1,3).
- **Strong experimental results**: Comparable to SOTA on GLM/Yi, large improvement on Llama/Qwen (Reviewer 1). Promising improvements over SOTA across multiple context lengths and tasks (Reviewer 4).
- **Clear methodology and algorithms**: The paper gives concrete, simple algorithms for constructing sparse attention patterns (Reviewer 3). The Cumulative-Attention Based Index Selection is convincing, with derivation from a multi-objective optimization problem detailed in Appendix A (Reviewer 3).
- **Quality of experimental setup**: Comprehensive comparisons to StreamingLLM and MInference, ablation studies, and detailed analyses (Reviewer 2). Well-structured and clearly written (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Insufficient comparison with recent related works**: The paper does not compare with other recent sparse attention methods such as Han et al. (2024), Xiao et al. (2024a) (Reviewer 2), SampleAttention, HiP-Attention (Reviewer 3), or methods with automatic selection like Moa (Reviewer 4). Current baselines do not fully demonstrate the advantages of FlexPrefill (Reviewer 3). This is a major gap.
- **Missing detailed computational complexity analysis**: The multi-step decision process (JS divergence calculation, sparse pattern selection, index selection) introduces overhead, but a rigorous time complexity breakdown is missing (Reviewer 2). The costs of pre-calculation on JS divergence are unclear (Reviewer 4). Without this, practical scalability for extremely long sequences is uncertain (Reviewer 2).
- **Lack of detailed comparison of computational savings**: The paper mentions speedups but does not quantify reduction in FLOPs or actual time for attention computation relative to baselines in Tables 1 and 2 (Reviewer 2). A breakdown of computational costs and memory access patterns is needed (Reviewer 2). The overhead from dynamic, head-distinctive computation allocation (Cumulative-Attention) may increase end-to-end latency compared to fixed-budget settings; this should be analyzed (Reviewer 3).
- **Mixed performance benefits and insufficient efficiency reporting**: Reported performance benefits over Full Attention are mixed (Reviewer 2). Table 1 lacks efficiency results; the paper states "accelerating the computational process" (Line 357) but does not include efficiency data in the main results, only in Figure 4 (Reviewer 3). The average attention latency of Full Attention in Figure 4 is not given (Reviewer 2).
- **Unclear justification for fallback pattern**: When the distribution of estimated and true attention diverges, the method defaults to vertical-slash patterns (also used in MInference). It is not clear why vertical-slash is better than block-wise pattern in that scenario; theoretical or empirical guarantee is missing (Reviewer 3). Figure 8 shows only a small portion of heads use Query-Aware patterns, so further analysis of why FlexPrefill outperforms MInference is needed (Reviewer 1).
- **Questions about implementation details**:
  - How is the subset of queries \(\hat{Q}\) selected for representative query selection? Is it randomly sampled? (Reviewer 1, also raised as a missing ablation)
  - In Figure 4, why is there only one point for MInference? A curve adjusting computation budget for MInference and a "Fixed Budget Query-Aware" curve are missing (Reviewer 3).
  - In Algorithm 4, index selection is global (flatten operation). What if query-wise selection is performed? (Reviewer 1)
  - In Algorithm 3, attention scores are not normalized (only means on vertical/slash lines), but Algorithm 4 normalizes. This discrepancy may affect the criterion for the same \(\gamma\). Why is normalization omitted in Algorithm 3? (Reviewer 3)
  - In Appendix A, Step 7, the equivalent form of the dual objective is not intuitive; derivation from Step 6 is unclear (Reviewer 3).
  - How were thresholds (sparse pattern threshold and cumulative attention threshold) chosen? Were they empirically determined? (Reviewer 2)
  - What is the intuition behind using JS divergence vs. W-distance? (Reviewer 4)
  - In formula (2), should JS be the average of KL(a||m)+KL(b||m)? Please clarify (Reviewer 4).
- **Minor presentation issues**: "eacct" → "exact" (Line 1043) (Reviewer 3). Table 5 caption should be above table (Reviewer 3). Figure 4 should show \(\gamma\) values for each point (Reviewer 3). Reference to Figure 4 should be given when discussing acceleration (Line 357) (Reviewer 3).
- **Potential impact of model structure**: The authors do not discuss how model architecture (e.g., number of heads, layers) affects FlexPrefill's performance (Reviewer 4).
