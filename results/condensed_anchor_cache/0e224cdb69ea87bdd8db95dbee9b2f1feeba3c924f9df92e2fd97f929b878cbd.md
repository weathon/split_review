## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces SPA, a representation learning framework that incorporates 3D spatial awareness into Vision Transformers via differentiable neural rendering on multi-view images. Starting with multi-view images and camera poses, SPA constructs feature volumes using deformable attention and employs NeuS-based volume rendering to generate self-supervised RGBD and semantic maps for pretraining. The authors benchmark their approach across 268 tasks in 8 simulators (including VC-1, Franka, Meta-World, RLBench, and LIBERO), covering single-task and language-conditioned multi-task scenarios. SPA consistently outperforms more than 10 state-of-the-art representation methods while using less training data. Real-world experiments confirm practical effectiveness. The self-supervised nature of the training signal reduces the need for expensive labeled data. Reviewers acknowledge the extensive evaluation and effectiveness of the method, but raise concerns about architectural novelty, evaluation scope, missing ablations, and insufficient benchmark documentation.

### Strengths
- **Extensive evaluation**: The paper presents one of the most comprehensive evaluations in embodied representation learning, covering 268 tasks across 8 simulators with thorough comparison against multiple state-of-the-art methods, demonstrating strong empirical rigor (R1, R2, R3). The careful experiment design showcases SPA's versatility and robustness across single-task and language-conditioned multi-task scenarios (R4).
- **Effective use of neural rendering for 3D spatial awareness**: SPA effectively uses neural rendering and multi-view images to enhance the 3D spatial awareness of a ViT, giving the model better understanding of depth and spatial relationships in 3D scenes (R1). The approach addresses an important gap by explicitly incorporating 3D spatial understanding through differentiable rendering (R2).
- **Conceptual contribution**: The paper proposes and validates the spatial awareness hypothesis, which could guide future research in embodied representation learning (R1). *Note: one reviewer (R4) finds this hypothesis not new, arguing many works have already used 3D features for embodied tasks.*
- **Well-written and well-thought-out architecture**: The paper is very nicely written (R3, R4). The architecture is well thought out, effectively tying several components together (R3). The method is simple yet effective (R4).
- **Thorough ablations**: Ablation studies on mask ratio importance, dataset impact, etc., are very informative (R3).
- **Self-supervised training signal**: The method's self-supervised nature reduces the need for expensive labeled data (R2).
- **Results show potential**: The results demonstrate consistent improvements over both vision-centric and embodied-specific baselines, with particularly strong performance on zero-shot camera pose estimation tasks (R2). SPA's potential is clearly shown (R3). The authors greatly invested effort in the evaluation process (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of architectural novelty**: SPA simply extends ViT with neural rendering and 3D spatial features; the underlying architecture remains the same ViT. The paper lacks sufficient groundbreaking innovations at the algorithmic level compared to many new model architectures (R1). *One reviewer (R4) also finds the "significant new hypothesis" claim overstated, as the importance of 3D spatial awareness was already clear from prior work that tried to use 3D features for embodied tasks.*
- **Limited evaluation paradigm**: The evaluation focuses solely on imitation learning and does not explore reinforcement learning or other complex learning paradigms. This limits understanding of generalizability under different real-world conditions (R1).
- **No modeling of temporal dynamics**: SPA is designed for static multi-view images, constraining its ability to model dynamics and temporal changes essential in many embodied AI scenarios. The paper proposes 3D spatial awareness as critical but does not consider temporal relationships (R1). Additionally, the DROID dataset consists of dynamic scenes, which is not explicitly handled by the NeuS volume rendering; no special handling is described (R2).
- **Missing ablation isolating neural rendering’s contribution**: There is a lack of independent comparative experiments to validate the contribution of neural rendering vs. other factors (e.g., better data, training strategies). The specific effects of multi-view learning and 3D spatial understanding are not isolated, making it impossible to determine if improvements come from spatial awareness or other improvements (R1).
- **Counterintuitive performance on LIBERO-spatial**: In Table 4, SPA does not show stronger results on the LIBERO-spatial benchmark, which is an important test of spatial reasoning. Given SPA's neural rendering pretraining objective, stronger results would be expected. The method's visual spatial awareness does not directly translate to performance on tasks requiring linguistic understanding of spatial prepositions and relations (R2).
- **Missing comparison to AM-RADIO**: In Table 3, a comparison to AM-RADIO is missing, despite that feature maps from AM-RADIO are used as supervision during SPA's pretraining. Without this baseline, it is difficult to assess the relative contribution of the proposed approach versus simply using feature map supervision (R2).
- **Insufficient benchmark and table documentation**: Benchmark descriptions are not well written; it is not clear what the tasks are supposed to be (R3). Tables have insufficient captions and it is difficult to understand the metrics from the tables themselves (R3). It is also unclear which methods are adapted from the vision community to solve embodied AI tasks, making it difficult to assess fairness of the comparison (R3).
- **Missing real-world vision-language tasks**: The real-world task setting does not include the most common vision-language tasks that might benefit from spatial awareness, such as those addressed by "Spatial VLM" or the OpenEQA dataset. The paper does not provide results for proper established real-world tasks like object detection or vision-language-based spatial-aware tasks (R3).
- **Methodology concern about volume construction with random masking**: The authors randomly mask patches across multi-view images during volume construction. The quality of the volume construction may be affected, and the training objective could become too easy during volume rendering (R4). It is unclear whether the mask tokens used to fill masked patches are learned (R3).
- **Insufficient discussion of prior work on learning 3D features from 2D images**: In the section "Representation Learning for Embodied AI," there is little discussion of prior work that learns 3D features from 2D images for embodied tasks (R4). Additionally, it is not clear whether Section 2.2 represents a novel contribution or builds on existing methods (R2).
- **Minor presentation issues**: Table 1 appears structured more like an ablation than a main result; moving it to a later section could improve reading flow. Figure 1 is hard to read due to color choice (R2).
