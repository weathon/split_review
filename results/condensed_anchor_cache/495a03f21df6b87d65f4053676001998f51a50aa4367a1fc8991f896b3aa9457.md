## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes an algorithm for constructing prediction sets with PAC guarantees under label shift. It estimates importance weights via the confusion matrix and predicted label distribution, then propagates uncertainty through Gaussian elimination to obtain confidence intervals for the weights, which are used to build the sets. Evaluation is conducted on five datasets: CIFAR-10, ChestX-Ray, Entity-13 (image), CDC Heart (tabular), and AGNews (text). The method satisfies the PAC guarantee while producing smaller prediction sets than baselines.

### Strengths
- Well-written, clear, and well-organized; the core idea is easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- Addresses an important practical problem: calibrated uncertainty quantification under distribution shifts, which is especially relevant in settings like healthcare where outcomes can dynamically change (Reviewers 1, 5).
- Novel combination of importance weighting with Gaussian elimination to propagate uncertainty from the estimated confusion matrix and predicted label distribution into the prediction set construction (Reviewers 1, 4). Reviewer 5 describes this as a “fresh take” that is “potentially groundbreaking.”
- Provides formal PAC theoretical guarantees (Reviewer 1).
- Empirical evaluation covers multiple domains (image, tabular, text) and uses datasets connected to real applications; the ChestX-ray example effectively illustrates practical significance (Reviewers 2, 5).
- Experimental results demonstrate that the PAC guarantee is satisfied (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
*Evaluation and Baselines.*
- The empirical comparison lacks a single metric (e.g., a proper scoring rule) to jointly evaluate prediction set error and size; different methods trade off error and size, making overall comparison difficult (Reviewer 1).
- The claim that traditional algorithms fail to guarantee coverage under label shift is not entirely accurate: the class-specific construction in Sadinle (with per-class cutoffs) guarantees coverage under arbitrary label shift. The paper compares only to a weaker version (LWCP) rather than the stronger, more relevant class-specific baseline. This missing comparison undermines the claimed advantage (Reviewer 3).
- It is unclear why the reference in Figure 2 says “CP” when the context suggests “WCP” (Reviewer 3).
- The analysis of prediction set size and coverage under varying degrees of label shift is not granular enough; explicit quantitative comparisons against other label-shift methods are needed (Reviewer 5).
- Reviewer 2 asks whether simply reducing ε on WCP (as in Corollary 1 of [R3]) would achieve the same PAC guarantee, and what the difference is between that procedure and the optimization in Eq. (16). This indicates a missing theoretical comparison.

*Theoretical Guarantees and Assumptions.*
- Theorem 3.2 lacks a detailed proof; only a discussion of the confusion matrix is provided (Reviewer 2).
- The theoretical novelty is limited: the method primarily combines existing tools (Clopper-Pearson intervals, Gaussian elimination) without introducing significant new theory (Reviewer 2). Contrast: Reviewer 5 considers the Gaussian elimination step “potentially groundbreaking.”
- The PAC guarantee depends on a parameter τ, which seems less meaningful (Reviewer 4).
- The assumption that the confusion matrix is invertible may be too strong for real-world applications (Reviewer 4).

*Methodological Clarity and Practicality.*
- The rejection sampling acceptance probability is defined as accepting with probability \(1 - m_{y_i}/b\) instead of the more common \(m_{y_i}/b\); this divergence is confusing and lacks explanation (Reviewer 2).
- The selection of ε to ensure the average prediction set size exceeds one deviates from standard conformal prediction practice where any desired coverage level can be set. This choice and its implications need justification (Reviewer 4).
- The paper does not discuss practical challenges in estimating importance weights (noise, biases) or how the algorithm handles potential inaccuracies. A more detailed explanation or visual representation of the estimation and application of weights would aid clarity (Reviewer 5).
- The number of iterations t in the Gaussian elimination is in the range 10,000–20,000 even for binary problems, while Section 3 appears to suggest t = K-1. This discrepancy and the implied computational cost are not addressed (Reviewer 4).
- The element-wise confidence interval approach for importance weights can be computationally intensive due to the large number of iterations required (Reviewer 4).
- Definition in §3: \(p(\hat{y}|y) = P_{(X,Y)\sim P_X}[g(X)=\hat{y}|Y=y]\) should use \(P\) instead of \(P_X\) (Reviewer 4).

*Experimental Design and Rigor.*
- Experiments are limited to small-scale datasets with few classes; large-scale experiments (e.g., CIFAR-100) are desired (Reviewer 2).
- Sample sizes for CIFAR-10 and AGNews differ between the “large shift” and “small shift” scenarios without explanation (Reviewer 4).
- In Figure 5, the proposed method “PS-W” is replaced with “Ora” (oracle). It is unclear whether the oracle weight was also used for the other methods in that comparison (Reviewer 4).
- It is unclear how the method performs under extreme label shifts or with limited data; the sensitivity to the magnitude of shift and the impact of limited data on weight estimation reliability should be analyzed (Reviewer 5).
- Could the proposed method serve for outlier detection (i.e., returning empty sets as in Guan and Tibshirani 2022)? This possibility is not explored (Reviewer 4).
