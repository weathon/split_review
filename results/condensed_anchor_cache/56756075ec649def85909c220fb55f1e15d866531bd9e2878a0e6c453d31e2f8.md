## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes MADIFF, the first diffusion-based offline multi-agent reinforcement learning framework. It extends Decision Diffuser (Ajay et al., 2023) to cooperative MARL by adding an attention mechanism for inter-agent coordination during centralized training. MADIFF supports both centralized control (MADIFF-C) and decentralized execution (MADIFF-D) with built-in opponent modeling, where each agent predicts others' joint observations. The inverse dynamics model (IDM) infers joint actions from generated observation sequences. Evaluations are conducted on SMAC (2 maps), MPE (3 environments), and the NBA trajectory prediction dataset (MATP). The method generally outperforms baselines on MPE and MATP and is competitive on SMAC. An ablation confirms the attention modules are helpful.

### Strengths
- **Novel extension**: Diffusion models are applied to offline MARL for the first time, combining trajectory generation with opponent modeling (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5). The combination is novel and the problem is significant for domains like sports, traffic, ecology (R1).
- **Attention mechanism**: The proposed attention-based architecture for information exchange among agents is non-trivial and shown to improve performance in ablation studies (R1, R2, R4, R5).
- **Strong empirical performance**: MADIFF generally outperforms baselines on MPE and MATP, and is competitive on SMAC (R1, R2, R3). The evaluation is relatively diverse (R1, R2).
- **Principled design**: The framework is clear, well-structured, and avoids complicated components. Code is provided for reproducibility (R1, R3). The preliminaries section is well-contained (R1).
- **Impact potential**: The use of diffusion to model agents has great potential for generalization and future work on foundation models (R5).
- **Ablation evidence**: The ablation study (Section 5.5) confirms that the attention modules are essential (R2, R4).

### Weaknesses
**Novelty and relation to prior work**  
- The contribution is limited: MADIFF heavily relies on Decision Diffuser and can be seen as a simple extension with an additional attention layer; adopting attention for MARL is not new (referenced [1,2]) (R2, R4).  
- The benefits of diffusion policies emphasized in Decision Diffuser (stitching, conditioning on constraints/skills) are not addressed in the MARL context (R4).  
- Related work on opponent modeling is missing (e.g., [1] and references therein) (R4).  
- OMAR is excluded from SMAC experiments despite reporting SMAC results (R4).  
- No comparison with MADT-KD [3] (R2).  
- For the centralized controller, it is unclear how MADIFF-C differs from Decision Diffuser operating on the joint observation/action space (R2).  
- The language “decentralized policy and centralized controller” is confusing; the paper essentially proposes CTDE (R5).

**Experimental design and evaluation**  
- SMACv1 has known open-loop issues; results on these easy maps are unconvincing. The community typically reports win rates, which should be near 100% for the tested maps (e.g., QMIX achieves high win rates). The low return values suggest a bug or very limited data (R5). The authors should provide win rates (R1).  
- No SMACv2 [1], Multi-Agent MuJoCo [2], or Hanabi [3] evaluations, where coordination is more challenging due to stochasticity or partial observability. The paper does not acknowledge limitations in such settings (R1, R5).  
- Only 3–5 agents are tested; scalability to many agents is unclear (R1, R4).  
- Figure 3 lacks error bars; no number of seeds is reported for Table 1 or Figure 3 (R1).  
- The purpose of MPE experiments relative to SMAC is unclear, and MATP is niche with uncertain generalization (R5).  
- The NBA dataset is downsampled from 25 Hz to 5 Hz without discussing the effect on MADIFF and baseline performance (R1).  
- The “Valid Ratio” metric in Section 5.4 is poorly explained (R1).

**Clarity and presentation**  
- Writing needs spell-checking and rephrasing; e.g., “high-frequency and less smooth nature of actions” is unclear; conditioning information \(y(\tau)\) (returns and “Other information” in Fig. 1) is not explained (how are returns encoded? what is the extra info?) (R1).  
- Section 3.1’s description of how the attention mechanism incorporates other agents’ information is unclear (R1).  
- The term “opponent modeling” is used despite the cooperative setting (R1, R4).  
- Section 5.4 is unclear overall (R1, R4).  
- Notations are inconsistent: \(s^i\) in Eq. 7 vs. \(o^i\) in Section 2 (R4).  
- Perturbed states \(\tilde{x}\) in \(\hat{\tau}_k\) (above Eq. 4) are not properly defined (R4).  
- Grammar error: “We that..” in Section 5.4 (R4).  
- Figure 2: the planned trajectory of the planning agent (purple) differs from the real sampled trajectory – reason unclear (R2).

**Methodological concerns**  
- The inverse dynamics model (IDM) can be suboptimal when transition dynamics are stochastic. Even in deterministic environments, decentralized execution sees other agents’ stochastic policies as part of the environment, making dynamics stochastic; it is unclear how IDM avoids problems (R2).  
- The inverse dynamics loss (Eq. 7) assumes the next observation depends only on the agent’s current local observation and action, ignoring other agents’ actions. This assumption may break in complex tasks (R4).  
- It is unclear whether MADIFF can handle high-dimensional inputs (e.g., images) since it predicts next observations (Eqs. 8, 9) and experiments use small-dimension inputs (R3).  
- For large \(N\), inferring all other agents’ observations is costly; scalability could benefit from inferring only neighbors (R3).  
- It is unclear whether MADIFF extends beyond cooperative settings to general-sum environments (R3).  
- The curse of dimensionality of the joint action space is not addressed; scaling with \(N\) is unclear (R4).  
- The introduction’s statement about “learning the incorrect centralized value” may be a typo; extrapolation error assumes incorrect value? (R4).  
- Training on combined datasets of mixed quality (Good+Medium+Poor) is not tested; the additional data might benefit IDM (R1).  
- The positive results in MATP may partly come from test trajectories being in-distribution; generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios is uncertain (R5).  
- It is unclear whether the improvement is due to the diffusion aspect or the attention mechanism; a comparison of “method with vs. without attention” (if not already done as the full ablation?) would isolate the effect. One reviewer noted the ablation already shows the attention is helpful, but another questioned whether the diffusion itself is the key (R5).  
- One reviewer found the paper very clear and well-written (R3), while another had multiple clarity issues (R1). One reviewer noted the paper achieves SOTA (R3), while others found the SMAC results underwhelming and the experimental design limited (R1, R5). A minority of reviewers rated the contribution higher (R2 gave 3/3, R5 gave 3/3); others rated it lower (R1 and R4 gave 2/2). Confidence varies: most are fairly confident (3/4), one reviewer is more confident (4/4).
