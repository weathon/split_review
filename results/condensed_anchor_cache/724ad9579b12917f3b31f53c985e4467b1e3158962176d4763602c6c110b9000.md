## Merged Review

### Summary
SimZSS is a simple and efficient framework for open-vocabulary zero-shot semantic segmentation that freezes a self-supervised vision backbone (e.g., DINOv2) and trains only a text encoder. It exploits the spatially aware nature of frozen vision-only models and uses a concept bank to extract discrete local concepts from captions, aligning them with visual tokens via a concept-level objective. Training requires only image-caption pairs (no mask supervision), is data- and compute-efficient (e.g., <15 minutes on 8 GPUs with COCO Captions), and achieves state-of-the-art results on 7 out of 8 benchmark datasets. The approach extends the LiT paradigm to segmentation and adapts to various backbones and datasets with minimal hyperparameters.

### Strengths
- **Strong empirical performance**: Achieves state-of-the-art results on 7 out of 8 zero-shot segmentation benchmarks (reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- **Simple, straightforward, and easy to implement**: The method is clearly explained and requires only a few components; it adapts readily to different backbones and training datasets (reviewers 2,3).
- **No semantic mask supervision required**: Reduces annotation burden; training only uses image-caption pairs (reviewer 2).
- **Clear and well-motivated objectives**: The concept-level objective is more suitable than standard contrastive loss when concepts appear multiple times within a batch (reviewer 2).
- **Well-written with high-quality figures/tables**: The paper is easy to follow, and the visual presentation enhances understanding (reviewer 3).
- **Novel perspective**: Freezing the vision backbone and training only the text encoder effectively leverages pretrained self-supervised models, enabling efficient training and decoupling visual representation learning from cross-modality alignment (reviewers 3,4).
- **Data- and compute-efficient**: Achieves strong results with short training (e.g., <15 minutes) and only caption supervision, scaling to both small curated and large noisy datasets (reviewer 4).
- **Extends LiT to dense segmentation tasks**: Builds on the LiT philosophy while maintaining classification capabilities (reviewer 4).
- **Minimal hyperparameters**: The framework requires little tuning (reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty and deep insights (minority reviewer)**: One reviewer (score 5) found the method lacks theoretical grounding, hypothesis, or mechanism analysis—e.g., why freezing the vision model is beneficial, or how the discrete nature of text helps segmentation. The paper reads as an experiment report rather than providing new insights (reviewer 1: soundness 2, presentation 2, contribution 2). Other reviewers were more positive about the novelty.
- **Unfair comparisons and potential overfitting (minority reviewer)**: The same reviewer noted that SimZSS is trained on COCO Captions and LAION-400M, and the vision encoder is pretrained on LVD-142M, while many baselines use less data. Despite this, on Pascal VOC the method (48.6 with LAION-400M) underperforms CLIP-DINOiser, CLIP-DIY, OVSegmentor, OVDiff, CLIPpy, and TCL. The paper does not address potential overfitting or generalization limitations (reviewer 1).
- **Missing details on inference segmentation**: It is unclear how final segmentation masks are generated from similarity scores. No description of post-processing steps (e.g., threshold, argmax, smoothing) is given, hindering reproducibility (reviewer 2).
- **Lack of analysis explaining performance gains**: There is no thorough ablation study examining contributions of key components (e.g., concept-level objective, similarity metric, concept bank). No qualitative comparison of segmentation masks with other methods (reviewer 2).
- **Missing experiments on vision encoder fine-tuning**: It is not explored whether full or partial fine-tuning of the visual encoder would improve performance. Also, the extreme scenario of entirely different train and test class names is not tested (reviewer 2 questions).
- **Essential details omitted from main paper**: The training data (COCO Captions, LAION-400M) and pretrained vision model (pretrained on LVD-142M) are only described in the supplement; they should be in the main text (reviewer 3).
- **Reliance on predefined concept bank limits open-vocabulary generalization**: The concept bank is derived from the union of class names in evaluation datasets, biasing the model toward those classes. Removing Pascal VOC classes from the bank decreases performance (Table 4). The method may fail to localize objects outside this predefined set. Evaluation on broader datasets (e.g., OpenImagesV7 with 5000+ classes) is missing (reviewers 3 and 4).
- **Tradeoff between localization and recognition capability**: Evaluated only on ImageNet for classification; common practice includes 38 classification datasets. The trained model’s ImageNet accuracy (64.1 for ViT-B/14) is lower than OpenCLIP’s (67.05 for ViT-B/16 trained on the same LAION-400M), indicating that the method hurts recognition performance. This tradeoff is not discussed (reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons with training-free approaches**: Competitive training-free methods (e.g., [a],[b],[c],[d]) that can detect concepts outside a predefined bank are not included in the evaluation (reviewer 3).
- **Scaling bottleneck**: Using a ViT-L backbone does not improve performance over ViT-B, suggesting a bottleneck in architecture or training procedure. The scalability of the approach is unclear (reviewer 4).
- **Sensitivity to noisy captions not evaluated**: The method’s robustness to noisy captions (e.g., from web-scale data) is untested, and no techniques to improve concept bank generation are proposed (reviewer 4 question).
- **Concept bank scaling complexity not analyzed**: The current concept bank covers only evaluation dataset classes; scaling to larger taxonomies (e.g., WordNet) is not discussed, and potential complexities are unexplored (reviewer 4 question).
