## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces a method for forecasting time series governed by ODEs by optimizing cost functions directly on the space of feasible trajectories (solutions of the ODE). It proves that for smooth, Lipschitz ODEs, the solution space on a finite time interval forms a finite-dimensional Riemannian manifold embedded in L². This allows applying ML, MAP, and MMSE estimation directly on the manifold, reducing issues like oversmoothing. Theoretical justification is given, and Monte Carlo simulations on a Lotka-Volterra system (and additional ODEs in appendix) evaluate six different objectives.

### Strengths
- Clear story-line and well-written paper (R1, R2, R4).
- Model formulation is rigorous with robust theoretical proofs (R2, R3).
- Shows the space of trajectories on a finite time interval with finite degrees of freedom is a finite-dimensional manifold embedded in square-integrable functions (R4).
- Summarizes several classic estimation methods for trajectory forecasting and numerically compares their effects on the Lotka-Volterra equation (R4).
- Practical methods for computing trajectory estimations are discussed after the abstract framework (R2).
- One reviewer (R3) was notably more positive, describing the paper as “truly a joy to read”, very well-written, interesting, and containing a nice idea with helpful guidance through proofs and literature. R3 found the contribution relevant for applications and gave a score of 5, contrasting with scores of 3 from others.

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical novelty overstated**: The diffeomorphism between state space and trajectory space is a direct consequence of the existence and uniqueness theorem for ODEs with Lipschitz vector fields; the argument adds no new insight (R1). Theorem 1 and Proposition 1 are not about forecasting; the title’s “provably accurate” is not justified (R4).
- **Standard framing**: The framing of estimation as supervised learning using the diffeomorphism is standard and not novel (R1).
- **Unrealistic assumptions**: Relies on the parameter space Θ, input space U, and initial condition space being finite-dimensional. In practice these are often infinite-dimensional (e.g., unknown initial condition on an open interval, variable coefficients in C^∞). Even when finite-dimensional, the dimension can be large, reducing (13) to standard parameterized minimization; without such prior knowledge one would not use (12) (R4).
- **Missing comparisons to existing methods**: No comparison to standard ODE solvers (e.g., Runge-Kutta) or learning-based approaches (Neural ODE). The evaluation is limited to variants of the proposed methods (R1, R2, R3). The paper does not clearly delineate commonalities/differences with Neural ODE or advantages over them (R2).
- **Insufficient experimental evaluation**: Only one toy ODE (Lotka-Volterra) in the main text; supplement adds minor examples. This falls below ICLR standards (R1). The white noise assumption (Eq. 17) is too simple; the method may perform poorly if noise is correlated with dynamics or fits the trajectory manifold (R4).
- **Computational aspects not addressed**: The optimization problem (Eq. 6) is non-convex, but no discussion of how this is handled (R1). No comparison of computational complexity between estimating initial condition and full trajectory (R2).
- **Lack of discussion on identifiability, sensitivity analysis, adjoint methods** (R1).
- **Clarity issues in experiments**: (R3) It is unclear how det Dψ is computed when only noisy trajectory data is available (assumes exact solver). Also unclear whether the training pipeline uses a single noisy trajectory or multiple trajectories.
- **Figure 2 confusing**: Caption and correspondence to the six objectives is unclear; the mix of maximizing and minimizing objectives makes cross-comparison difficult (R2, R4). The claim that “trajectory MSE illustrates a valley of initializations… not captured by any competing technique” is contradicted by State MSE also showing a valley structure (R2).
- **Minor issues**: φ undefined (R1, R2). Below Eq. 6, R^{K×K} should be R^{N×N} (R1). Inner product in Eq. 8 is L²(I), not RKHS; the Proposition with RKHS should be stated in appendix (R1). Eq. 12 confusion: the empirical estimate equals the regression function as written (R1). Notation inconsistency: x̂ in (13) becomes x̃ in (16) (R4). The metric tensor K is diagonal (zero if time points don’t match), not accommodating time correlations (R4). Typo in Appendix B: ∀x₀∈𝒳, not x (R3). In the proof of Lemma 1, the quantifier should be fixed (R3). The assumption on input set U (a manifold) in main text vs. finite-sum structure in Appendix B is stronger than claimed (R3).
