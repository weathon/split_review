## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes LDReg, a regularization for self-supervised learning (SSL) that penalizes local dimensional collapse. It uses the Fisher-Rao metric on the local intrinsic dimensionality (LID) to encourage higher local dimensionality. The method is motivated by the observation that SSL representations can have high global dimensionality but low local dimensionality. Experiments on standard benchmarks show improved downstream performance, though improvements are often incremental.

### Strengths

- The motivation (local dimensional collapse) is clearly demonstrated and makes sense; it is empirically shown that LID can be low even when global dimensionality is high.
- The method is well-supported by theory, drawing on the Fisher-Rao metric and LID theory from (Houle, 2017a).
- Applying the geometric mean of LID as a regularizer is novel and interesting.
- The regularization is general and can be easily added to existing SSL models (e.g., SimCLR, Barlow Twins) without architectural modifications.
- The experiments show that LDReg indeed increases local dimensionality, and performance improvements are achieved, albeit incremental in some cases, which is understandable given the maturity of SSL.

### Weaknesses

- The implementation details are unclear: equation numbers are missing, and it is difficult to understand how $LID^*_F$ is computed from the limit and derivative of $F(r)$. The derivation makes it hard to implement the loss function from the paper alone.
- Estimating local dimensionality from mini-batches is questionable because samples in a mini-batch are drawn from the full dataset and are not local neighbors. The paper does not address how local neighborhoods are formed from non-local samples.
- The paper lacks comparisons to global dimensionality regularization methods (e.g., Barlow Twins, VICE-Reg) that were mentioned in the introduction. These are natural baselines for understanding the benefit of local vs. global regularization.
- Regularizing local dimensionality may harm the structure of features: Figure 1(c) shows that examples with low LID appear to have more structure, so pushing LID up (toward a random Gaussian distribution) could be detrimental. This point is not discussed.
- No correlation plot between test accuracy and LID is provided; the authors should show that increasing LID improves accuracy. Additionally, both GID and LID could be reported in Table 1 to examine their correlation and the effect of LDReg.
- The computational burden of calculating distance distributions during training (e.g., computing pairwise distances for each mini-batch) may be high, especially for large-scale SSL. The paper does not discuss runtime or complexity, so it is unclear whether the incremental improvements justify the added cost.
- The empirical improvements in Tables 1 and 3 are weak; it is unclear whether they are statistically significant or could be due to random fluctuation.
- The sensitivity to the locality parameter $k$ is not analyzed. Also, the parameter $N$ is ambiguous: is it the dataset size or batch size? If batch size, the sensitivity to batch size (sample density) should be discussed.
- The definition of LID assumes differentiability of $F(r)$ at $r=0$, but $F(r)$ is a probability function (proportion of distances less than $r$). It is unclear how differentiability is justified, and the quality of the estimator (limit as $r\to 0$) is not theoretically or empirically evaluated.
- Novelty concerns: The LID used is defined similarly to IntrDim in (Houle, 2017a), and Theorem 1 may already be from that work. The paper needs to clearly differentiate its contributions and properly attribute the LID definition. Without this, the novelty is limited to the application of the Fisher-Rao metric to LID as a regularizer.
- The experimental analysis is shallow: under-performance cases are not analyzed, and it is not shown how dimensional collapse is actually alleviated (the results do not directly reflect improvement in LID or collapse). The paper should provide a deeper analysis, e.g., visualizations or LID/GID curves.
- Minor issues: missing parenthesis in Definition 1, two variants of $w_k$ ($w^k$) on page 19, and the symbol 'Pr' is undefined.
- A reviewer (score 8) finds the paper strong and well-presented, but others (score 5, 6, 6) have more reservations about significance, novelty, and computational cost. The contrast suggests that the method's practical impact and clarity need improvement.
