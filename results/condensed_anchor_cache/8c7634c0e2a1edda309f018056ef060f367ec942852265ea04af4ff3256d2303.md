## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CrossModalNet, a multimodal medical image segmentation architecture with theoretical analysis (universal approximation, generalization bounds), a Cross-Modal Information Flow (CMIF) metric, and a Joint Adversarial Domain Adaptation (JADA) framework. Experiments are conducted on the MM-WHS dataset. The reviews are sharply divided: Reviewer 1 (score 5, confidence 4) finds the problem important and the theory valuable but criticizes readability and evaluation. Reviewers 2 and 3 (both score 3, confidence 4) acknowledge the theoretical interest and fusion design but point to limited experimental validation and presentation gaps. Reviewer 4 (score 1, confidence 3) is highly critical, arguing the theoretical proofs lack novelty, the experimental validation is insufficient, and the paper contains citation/terminology errors, recommending withdrawal.

### Strengths
- **Important problem addressed** (Reviewer 1, 2, 3): Multimodal medical image segmentation, inter-modality deformation, and domain shift are real challenges.
- **Strong theoretical foundation** (Reviewer 1, 2, 3): Universal approximation capabilities and generalization bounds are derived; the analysis is rigorous. (Reviewer 4 disagrees on novelty, claiming proofs replicate standard sources like PRML or may be AI-generated – this contrast is noted.)
- **Fusion strategy with modern components** (Reviewer 2): Incorporates U-shaped parallel feature network, cross-transformer block, cross-attention, and deformable operator to improve performance.
- **Joint Adversarial Domain Adaptation (JADA)** (Reviewer 1, 2): Aligns marginal and conditional distributions while preserving topological structures.
- **Well-organized** (Reviewer 3; Reviewer 1 and 2 disagree on readability – see Weaknesses).
- **Demonstrates results on one relevant dataset** (Reviewer 1, 4): MM-WHS is challenging and relevant.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited experimental validation** (All Reviewers, high severity): Only one dataset (MM-WHS) is used. Multi-class segmentation datasets like BraTS 2018 and ACDC are recommended. This weakens generalizability claims. (Reviewer 4: “may waste reviewers’ time.”)
- **Missing comparisons with multimodal approaches** (Reviewer 1, 4): All baselines are single-modal, so claimed multimodal advantages are not substantiated.
- **Lack of ablation studies and loss balancing analysis** (Reviewer 1, 4): No ablation for adaptive loss balancing; no training convergence curves; no comparison showing benefit of balancing method. Reviewer 4: the loss balancing approach is not novel and has been previously addressed.
- **No quantitative evaluation of the CMIF metric** (Reviewer 2, 3): The metric is introduced but no results are shown (e.g., how CMIF changes during training or correlates with performance).
- **Missing domain shift evaluation** (Reviewer 2, 3): JADA’s robustness to domain shifts is not specifically tested.
- **Poor presentation and lack of implementation details** (Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 4): 
  - Figure 1 does not clearly illustrate the architecture; connections between visuals and text are unclear (Reviewer 1, 2).
  - Captions are unhelpful (Reviewer 2).
  - No table of numerical results; Figure 2 should be a table with exact metrics, and font is too small (Reviewer 2, 3).
  - Concrete details of U-shaped parallel feature network, Cross Transformer Block, and deformable operator are missing (Reviewer 1, 4).
- **Unclear link between theory and practical performance** (Reviewer 1, 3): It is not shown how generalization bounds or robustness analysis translate into performance gains or guide model selection/preprocessing.
- **Problem setting weakens clinical significance** (Reviewer 3): The MM-WHS data is unpaired (CT and MR from different patients), so complementary information may not benefit understanding of either patient. Recommended application would be paired, well-registered data like BraTS.
- **Originality concerns and citation issues** (Reviewer 4, high severity):
  - Theoretical proofs (e.g., Eq.6–Eq.8) lack novelty and logical progression; a lemma on cross-attention complexity is “weird.”
  - Loss balancing and deformable operator usage are non-innovative; deformable is usually for registration, not segmentation.
  - Missing essential citations; incorrect citation formatting (mixing `\citep` and `\citet`); citations in lines 216, 220, 233 appear invalid.
- **No discussion of computational complexity** (Reviewer 2): Despite discussing transformer complexity, no comparison with baseline models is given.
- **Missing discussion section** (Reviewer 2): Statistical insights and comparison context are absent.
- **Mismatch between problem statement and figure** (Reviewer 4): A classifier is shown in Fig. 1, but the task is segmentation; terms like “jaccard loss” and “graph mapping loss” are undefined in the paper.
- **Presentation issues affecting readability** (Reviewer 1, 2, 3, 4): The paper is difficult to follow; derivations dominate without clear connection to experimental outcomes; definitions/proofs are misleading when applied to general architecture principles (Reviewer 4).
