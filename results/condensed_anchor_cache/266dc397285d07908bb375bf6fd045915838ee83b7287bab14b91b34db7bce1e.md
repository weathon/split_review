## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a Gaussian Mixture Distribution-based Prototype (GMDP) to model complex feature distributions for knowledge distillation-based incremental object detection, aiming to alleviate knowledge shift without storing previous data. It also introduces a Dynamic Adaptive Prototype Optimization (DAPO) strategy and a Dimension Scaling Progressive Learning (DSPL) strategy. Experiments on PASCAL VOC and MS-COCO demonstrate improvements over several baselines. The approach is considered reasonable and flexible, but concerns about innovation, clarity of the “high-dimension” concept, missing implementation details, sensitivity to the number of components, and fairness of comparisons are raised.

### Strengths
- Clear motivation and reasonable solution: the need for more accurate modeling of the complex detection feature space is well justified, and the GMDP approach is a sensible way to capture intra-class and inter-class variability (R1, R3, R4). R4 specifically notes that figures illustrating the differences between classification and detection feature spaces help clarify the motivation.
- GMDP can be readily integrated into existing IOD methods and consistently improves their performance (R1, R4).
- Comprehensive experiments under various incremental settings and thorough ablation studies (including DSPL and DAPO) are provided, with results convincingly showing superiority over baselines and reaching SOTA on PASCAL VOC and MS-COCO (R1, R3, R4). R1 also appreciates the inclusion of training/optimization details and hyper-parameter settings for reproducibility.
- The paper is well-organized, clearly written, and the visualizations (e.g., feature distribution comparisons) are helpful for understanding the approach (R2, R3).
- The Dynamic Adaptive Prototype Optimization (DAPO) strategy is a well-reasoned enhancement that improves the plasticity and stability of class prototypes (R2).
- Leveraging the flexibility of Gaussian mixtures to capture complex distributions alleviates knowledge shift (R2).
- While R2 (score 3) shares these strengths, that reviewer explicitly views the innovation as moderate and the method as not specifically tailored to IOD — this contrast is noted (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
1. **Confusion about "high-dimension":** The paper’s claim of learning “higher-dimension prototypes” conflicts with the DSPL strategy, which transforms the original feature vector into a *lower-dimensional* vector z before computing prototypes (lines 306–310). It is unclear whether “high-dimension” refers to the original feature space, the number of mixture components, or something else. This definition needs clarification (R1).
2. **Missing label assignment details for Eq. (3):** The criteria for assigning the ground-truth label \(y_{i,c}\) to each proposal feature vector are not specified. Is it based on IoU with ground-truth boxes? What thresholds are used? These details are essential for reproducibility (R1).
3. **Sensitivity to the number of Gaussian components (K):** Table 5 shows that performance varies significantly with K. The paper does not analyze *why* this sensitivity exists, nor does it provide guidelines or heuristics for choosing K for a given dataset. Additional ablation on the number of components in both two-stage and multiple-stage incremental settings is needed (R1, R3, R4). R3 also asks whether all classes share the same K, suggesting that easier classes might only need a unimodal distribution while harder classes need multiple components. R4 notes that the claim about “significant challenges to the network’s fitting capability” (line 515) lacks a supporting experiment for larger models.
4. **Notation inconsistencies in Section 3.2:** (a) N is used for both the dimensionality of proposal feature \(x_i\) and the size of the input feature set; (b) the component number of the GMM is denoted K in the text but M in Eqs. (9) and (10); (c) the bold formatting of \(x_i\) is inconsistent across equations. These make the mathematical formulation difficult to follow (R1).
5. **JS divergence calculation for GMMs in Figure 4:** The confusion matrix requires computing Jensen-Shannon divergence between Gaussian mixture distributions. For GMMs, only an upper bound of this metric is typically tractable. The authors should provide details on how this calculation is actually performed (R1).
6. **Integration with CL-DETR (appendix A.2):** Since CL-DETR is query-based and does not generate proposal feature vectors, it is unclear how the GMDP prototypes are produced for it. More details on this integration process are needed (R1).
7. **Limited innovation and lack of IOD specificity (minority reviewer, R2):** Gaussian mixture-based prototypes have already been used in class-incremental learning (see [1–4]). The paper’s emphasis on the complexity of detection features over classification features does not provide substantial new insights, as previous work [1] already notes that embedding distributions are not convex or isotropic. Furthermore, the method is not specifically tailored to object detection; it appears more suited to general class-incremental modeling of class distributions. R2 (score 3) finds the innovation moderate, in contrast to the other reviewers (scores 6, 8, 8) who consider the approach novel and effective.
8. **Potentially skewed comparisons due to exceeding the upper bound:** In some settings (e.g., 19-1), the proposed method (with GMDP and DAPO integrated at the base stage) exceeds the performance of the *Faster R-CNN upper bound* (trained jointly on all classes). This suggests that the base feature representation capacity is already enhanced, making comparisons with existing incremental methods unfair, as those methods are not designed to operate with such a strong backbone (R2).
9. **Lack of discussion on method complexity and training time:** The paper does not analyze the computational cost or training time added by GMDP, DAPO, and DSPL (R3).
10. **Figure quality issues:** Figure 1 has an inconsistent legend, and Figure 3 is blurry/low-resolution (R4).
11. **Imprecise descriptions:** (a) In line 303, “the old class \(C_o\)” should be “the old class *set*”. (b) The variable \(z\) in Eq. (6) is not introduced; it should refer to proposals from the ROI layer. (c) It is unclear how the means and variances of the K GMM components are calculated (are they based on the original features \(x\)?) and whether they are computed before or after training for each incremental step (R4).
12. **Loss weights for DAPO not specified:** The weights of the various losses in the DAPO strategy are not given, nor is it explained how these values are determined across different incremental settings (R3).
13. **Minor presentation issue:** Some best results in Table 4 are not boldfaced (R3).
