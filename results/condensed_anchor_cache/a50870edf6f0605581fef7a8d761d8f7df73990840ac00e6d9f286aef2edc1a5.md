## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper studies the generalization of accelerated SGD (ASGD) for overparameterized linear regression. It establishes an instance-dependent excess risk bound per eigen-subspace of the data covariance matrix. The main findings are: (i) in the subspace of small eigenvalues, ASGD’s bias error decays exponentially faster than SGD, while in the large-eigenvalue subspace the bias decays slower; (ii) ASGD’s variance error is always larger than SGD’s. The authors conclude that ASGD can outperform SGD when the initialization error is mostly aligned with the small-eigenvalue subspace. In the strongly convex specialization, a tighter bias bound than the best-known result is obtained.

### Strengths
- Solid theoretical analysis that yields a clear instance-dependent decomposition of bias and variance error across eigen-subspaces.
- Well-written and easy to follow.
- In the strongly convex regime, obtains a better bias error term than previous work (except for one term the authors claim can be removed).
- Extends the techniques of Jain et al. (2018) to the overparameterized setting, allowing a precise characterization of ASGD behavior.
- Clearly identifies regimes where ASGD is superior (small eigenvalues) and where it is inferior (large eigenvalues, variance).

### Weaknesses
- **Practical value of the main implication is limited.** Reviewer 2 argues that the claimed advantage (ASGD outperforms SGD when the initialization-minus-truth is confined to the small-eigenvalue subspace) is true but “a bit useless,” because that subspace is low-dimensional and most of the signal lives in the large-eigenvalue subspace. Overall the results are described as “essentially negative” and could have been anticipated.
- **Unsubstantiated claim about a removable term.** Reviewer 2 notes that the better bias bound comes with an extra term that the authors claim can be removed, but the removal is not actually carried out.
- **Experimental support is insufficient.** Reviewer 3 states that the experiments do not provide strong support for the theoretical findings. In particular, the claim that ASGD’s variance error is always larger than SGD’s lacks adequate experimental backing, and the experiments are not comprehensive enough.
- **Missing details and clarifications requested by reviewers.** These overlap with criticisms/omissions and are therefore listed here:
  - How is overparameterization explicitly reflected in Theorem 4.1? (Reviewer 3)
  - A more detailed explanation of the challenges encountered in the theoretical analysis and how they are addressed is needed. (Reviewer 3)
  - Provide more instances that satisfy Assumption 3.2. (Reviewer 3)
  - Provide any examples of settings where one can guarantee the initialization error is aligned with the small-eigenvalue subspace (i.e., the “good regime” for ASGD). (Reviewer 2)
- **Disagreement among reviewers on the severity of weaknesses.** Reviewer 1 listed no weaknesses at all, while Reviewer 2 rated the contribution as “fair” (2/4) and Reviewer 3 rated it as “good” (3/4). The more negative assessment from Reviewer 2 is explicitly retained here: the results are characterized as weak and of limited practical use.
