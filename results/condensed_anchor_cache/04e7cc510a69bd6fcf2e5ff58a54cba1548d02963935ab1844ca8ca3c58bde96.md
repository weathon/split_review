## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper investigates the critique ability of LLMs (identifying flaws in their own or other models’ outputs) and introduces CriticBench, a fully automated benchmark of 3K high-quality queries (math problem-solving, code completion, question answering) with annotated response correctness. It evaluates multiple LLMs, finding that critique is challenging, self-critique especially so, and that models are less accurate on problems they are most uncertain about. A simple self-check heuristic that leverages self-critique to improve task performance is proposed. The paper is timely and addresses an under-explored aspect of LLMs, providing a standardized evaluation and noteworthy insights. However, contributions differ: one reviewer found it a solid but not novel heuristic (score 6), another valued the framing but noted scope limitations (score 5), and a third saw significant weaknesses in comprehensiveness and presentation (score 3).

### Strengths
- The paper addresses an important and under-explored aspect of LLMs: their ability to critique their own outputs and self-improve, moving beyond traditional metrics. (Reviewer 2, 3)
- It presents a clear definition of critique ability and distinguishes critique from self-critique, setting proper scope. (Reviewer 2)
- The paper is well-written, easy to follow, with clear details on the fully automated data collection process and good motivation for design choices. (Reviewer 1)
- Evaluation is thorough, covering a wide range of models and revealing scaling trends (e.g., critique emerges only with sufficient size, self-critique is especially hard). (Reviewer 1, 3)
- The self-check heuristic, while not particularly novel, achieves solid improvement on the new benchmark. (Reviewer 1)
- The benchmark provides a standardized way to evaluate critique abilities across diverse tasks. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited granularity and scope**: The benchmark reduces critique quality to a binary decision, offering limited granularity for nuanced flaws; the qualitative questions it can answer are restricted. (Reviewer 1) The evaluation covers only three tasks (math, code, commonsense/QA). Multiple reviewers call for more diverse tasks and domains to improve generalizability. (Reviewer 2, 3)
- **Potential inflation of critique accuracy**: Reviewer 1 questions whether large models’ apparent advantage in critiquing large-model outputs (Figure 4) is inflated by their higher underlying task accuracy rather than genuine critique skill. (Reviewer 1)
- **Lack of deep analysis on why self-critique struggles**: The paper notes that self-critique is difficult but does not delve into why or propose concrete solutions beyond the self-check heuristic. (Reviewer 2) It also lacks an in-depth analysis of the utility and conditions under which self-critique works better. (Reviewer 3)
- **Missing discussion of limitations and biases**: No discussion of ethical implications of reduced human oversight from self-critiquing models. (Reviewer 2) Potential biases in the benchmark construction are not discussed, which could affect validity. (Reviewer 3) The scalability and real-world applicability of the self-check method are not addressed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Presentation issues and missing definitions**: The presentation appears disjointed with inconsistent terminology; key terms such as “policy model” and “critic model” are not defined. (Reviewer 3) Related work is lacking. (Reviewer 3)
- **Benchmark not publicly released**: Despite introducing CriticBench, the authors do not release it, limiting reproducibility and community utility. (Reviewer 3) [Note: Reviewer 1 and 2 did not mention this, so minority view.]
- **Unclear design choices and prompting details**: Questions remain about the rationale for different k values (k=64 for GSM8K and TruthfulQA, k=100 for HumanEval). (Reviewer 3) In Section 5, the phrase “Assume with appropriate prompting” is ambiguous; a detailed explanation of the prompting procedure is missing. (Reviewer 3)
