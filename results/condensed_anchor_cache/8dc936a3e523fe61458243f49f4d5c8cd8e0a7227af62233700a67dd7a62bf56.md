## Merged Review

### Summary
The FLIP framework integrates three modules (multi-modal flow generation, flow-conditioned video generation, and a vision-language value function) to perform model-based planning for robotic manipulation from language and vision inputs. It uses 2D image flow as a general action representation, searches over long-horizon flow and video plans, and can condition a low-level policy. Evaluations cover video plan synthesis, long-horizon video generation, and policy execution on LIBERO-LONG, plus interactive and zero-shot demonstrations.

### Strengths
- **Originality and novelty**: The combination of flow generation, flow-conditioned video prediction, and value-guided planning for manipulation is considered original (Reviewer 1, score 8). Although concurrent works propose generative flow models (e.g., GeneralFlow, Track2Act), the planning framework is novel.
- **Sound design and detailed motivation**: The design of each component is sound and well-motivated. Specific contributions appreciated include:
  - Using a CVAE for the multi-modality of flows (Reviewer 3).
  - The mixed-conditioning mechanism for multi-modal conditional video generation (Reviewer 3).
  - Modifying the LIV value module to use video clips instead of frames to reduce noisy value predictions (Reviewer 3).
  - Video chunking for value prediction and conditioning the video model on flow (Reviewer 1).
  - Action space and conditioning ablations (Reviewer 1).
- **Clarity and presentation**: The paper is clearly written and methodically presented (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Comprehensive experiments and ablations**: The evaluation covers a range of test settings, and ablation studies help understand each component (Reviewers 1, 2). The method outperforms baselines on video plan synthesis, long-horizon video generation, and manipulation tasks (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Demonstrated properties**: Interactive world model capability, zero-shot transfer across objects/robots/tasks, and scalability with model/data budgets are shown (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Significance**: Flow-centric generative planning is regarded as having moderate significance, with potential to be powerful for action representation (Reviewer 1). The framework can inform low-level control policies and support hierarchical planning (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Downstream manipulation performance is limited**: The method does not substantially outperform ATM, a simpler no-planning baseline using a similar generative representation, on the actual LIBERO-LONG manipulation task (Reviewer 1). Conditioning on flow appears to hurt downstream policy compared to video conditioning alone, and the “Ours-FV” results are not well explained (Reviewer 1). This undermines the claim of downstream utility given the overhead. (Reviewer 1, score 8, notes these concerns were partially addressed by modifications after rebuttal, but the performance gap remains a weakness.)
- **Missing stronger baselines and comparisons**: The paper should compare with more recent or powerful methods:
  - UniSim and DIAMOND for video prediction (Reviewer 2). Both use diffusion models without explicit flow extraction, which challenges the flow-centric claim; discussion is needed.
  - For policy learning, compare with OpenVLA and Octo (Reviewer 3).
  - For text-to-video in Section 5.2, IRASim is an unsuitable baseline (uses trajectory, not text); a dedicated text-to-video method should be used (Reviewer 3).
- **Evaluation restricted to single domains**: Experiments are limited to individual simulation domains, even though the method is designed to be task-agnostic. The authors could have leveraged this property, e.g., by training on the full LIBERO benchmark or exploring internet-scale pretraining (Reviewer 1). The policy evaluation only uses LIBERO-LONG; generalisation to LIBERO-Object is not shown (Reviewer 3). No real‑robot experiments are provided (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Emphasis on video prediction over manipulation**: The paper is framed as a manipulation planning method, but the bulk of experiments and analysis focus on video prediction. Video prediction alone does not justify the manipulation claims; metrics such as geometric precision, feasibility, or other downstream criteria should be emphasised more (Reviewer 1).
- **Design and implementation details missing or insufficiently justified**:
  - Why a CVAE is used for the flow generation model instead of a diffusion model; a design choice discussion is needed (Reviewer 2).
  - The quality of video demonstrations (expert vs. random) across all experiments, especially for Section 5.1, is not specified. It is unclear whether the model requires optimal demonstrations or can learn from sub-optimal data (Reviewer 2).
  - Details of how the FLIP‑NC ablation (no value guidance) performs beam search are lacking (Reviewer 3).
  - For the dynamics module experiments (Section 5.4), what information is provided to the LVDM and IRASim baselines when the proposed method uses ground‑truth flow? (Reviewer 3).
  - Are the flow generation and video generation models trained jointly or individually? (Reviewer 3).
- **Practical concerns not evaluated**:
  - Handling of ambiguous language instructions and unexpected environment changes (Reviewer 4).
  - Real-time performance constraints; the computational demands of multi-modal modules may hinder deployment in dynamic environments (Reviewer 4).
  - Limitations of using image flow as an action representation; scenarios where it is not ideal (Reviewer 4).
  - Sensitivity to visual noise or obstructions (Reviewer 4).
  - Quantitative scaling analysis with data size is needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (Reviewer 1, initial score below acceptance raised to 8 after rebuttal) considered the experiments thorough and originally assigned higher scores than the others (all 6), but still raised the primary concern about downstream utility. The other three reviewers (scores 6) shared many of these weaknesses and placed more emphasis on the missing real‑robot results, stronger baselines, and lack of generalisation evaluation.
