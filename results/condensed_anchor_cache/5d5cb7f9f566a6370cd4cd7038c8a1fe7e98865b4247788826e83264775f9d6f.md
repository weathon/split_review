## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper presents OpenMU-Bench, a large-scale benchmark suite for training and evaluating multimodal language models on music understanding, and OpenMU, a model trained on this benchmark. Reviewer scores span 1, 3, 5, 5, indicating strong disagreement. R1 (score 5, confidence 5) and R3 (5, confidence 4) consider the resource and model contributions positive; R2 (3, confidence 4) is moderate; R4 (1, confidence 5) is critical of the benchmark design and claimed contributions. All reviewers note limited novelty in model architecture and dataset construction. Additional points of dissent: R1/R3 stress the value of the dataset and thorough evaluation; R4 argues the benchmark lacks clear ranking capability, task structure, and proper comparisons.

### Strengths
- **Data collection and annotation**: Significant effort in leveraging LLMs (GPT-3.5) to create and augment music-understanding annotations from existing resources; provides a valuable resource for the community (R1, R3).
- **Thorough evaluation and ablation**: Comprehensive comparisons against Mu-Llama, MusiLingo, M2UGen, with detailed ablation studies on music token size/length and LoRA parameters (R1). The exploration of key training factors (e.g., number of music tokens, LoRA settings) offers reusable insights (R2).
- **Standardized text-generation metrics**: OpenMU-Bench standardizes evaluation metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR) for text-generation tasks, improving fairness and consistency across models (R2).
- **OpenMU outperforms existing baselines**: OpenMU demonstrates superior performance over MU-LLAMA (and other baselines) on OpenMU-Bench tasks (R1, R3, R4).
- **Large-scale and comprehensive benchmark**: OpenMU-Bench covers multiple music understanding tasks (captioning, reasoning, lyrics, tool-using, multiple-choice) and includes ~1M data points (R3, R4).
- **Open-sourcing**: Both the dataset and model are publicly released, promoting transparency and reproducibility (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty in model and benchmark**:
  - No new model architecture or evaluation metrics; relies on existing techniques (AudioMAE, Llama 3, projector) and pre-existing datasets (R1, R2, R3, R4).
  - The “tool-using” task is not well-defined—overlaps with other tasks and may not evaluate music understanding (a model could succeed by simply calling an API without comprehension) (R4).
  - The benchmark’s task categories are split by question format rather than music understanding skills (e.g., genre recognition), unlike MMLU; this feels superficial and prevents unified skill-specific metrics (R4).
- **Insufficient evaluation metrics for pure music understanding**:
  - Only NLP metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR) are used; MIR-specific metrics (tempo, chord, melody, genre classification, etc.) are missing, even though OpenMU-Bench could support them (R1).
  - The benchmark lacks an overall ranking metric; multiple metrics per dataset are presented but not aggregated into a single score for model ranking (R4).
- **Dataset construction and quality concerns**:
  - No discussion of training/test set overlap or data selection criteria (R3). R1 also asks whether baselines were evaluated using their pretrained checkpoints or fine-tuned on OpenMU-Bench.
  - No analysis of hallucination or incorrect annotations from GPT-3.5, and no filtering methods described (R1).
  - No quantitative measure of consistency between GPT-3.5-generated text and metadata (R2).
  - Licensing status of the constituent datasets (research-only vs. commercial) is not clarified; licenses may be inconsistent (R1).
  - No anonymous website or repository for practical usability (R1).
- **Missing comparative evaluations**:
  - No baseline model (e.g., Mu-Llama, MusiLingo) is trained on the same OpenMU-Bench training data for a fair comparison; only OpenMU’s own hyperparameter variations are shown (R4).
  - The paper incorrectly claims LLark has not open-sourced its code; LLark’s source is available (https://github.com/spotify-research/llark), and a direct comparison on a subset of OpenMU-Bench is possible (R3).
  - No analysis explaining why OpenMU outperforms other models on certain metrics (R3).
- **Bias in evaluation pipeline**:
  - Processing audio understanding model vectors through Llama for scoring introduces bias compared to direct vector-based distance scoring; this is not analyzed or addressed (R3).
- **Presentation issues**:
  - Section 5.2 (discussion of number of music tokens and LoRA) is placed within Section 4 without sufficient elaboration, confusing the reader (R2).
  - Music is referred to as a modality, but the paper only uses audio; music as a field includes symbolic data as well (R2).
- **Contribution ambiguity**: The abstract emphasizes the benchmark as the main contribution, while the introduction emphasizes the model; it is unclear which is the primary claim (R4).
- **Choice of audio encoder**: AudioMAE is pretrained on only 3K hours of music data, whereas MERT is pretrained on 160K hours—no justification is given for this choice (R2).
