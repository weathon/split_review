## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper addresses binary classification with selective abstention under group fairness and a “no-harm” (no accuracy degradation per group) constraint. It proposes a post‑hoc two‑stage method: (i) an Integer Programming (IP‑Main) procedure assigns abstention and label‑flip decisions per training sample to minimize error subject to fairness disparity, abstention rate, and no‑harm constraints; (ii) surrogate neural networks learn the patterns of the abstention (AB) and flipping (FB) decisions from the IP solutions to generalize to test data. Theoretical feasibility conditions relate disparity tolerance, accuracy, and abstention rate. Experiments on three real‑world datasets compare against LTD and FSCS.

### Strengths

- The paper is clearly structured, well‑written, and most design choices are properly motivated (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- It is the first work to simultaneously consider selective classification under fairness, abstention rate, and no‑harm constraints, which is valuable for real‑world applications (R2, R3).
- The proposed method is flexible w.r.t. the type of fairness constraint (demographic parity, equalized odds, equal opportunity) (R2).
- The theoretical analysis provides insightful feasibility conditions that characterize the trade‑off between accuracy, fairness, and abstention rate, potentially describing the Bayes‑optimal classifier with abstention (R1, R4).
- Experimental results robustly show improvement in fairness disparity without sacrificing accuracy at similar abstention rates, compared to the baselines (R1, R2, R3, R4).

*Note:* One reviewer (R2, score 8, contribution 3) is notably more positive than the others (all score 6; R1 and R4 rated contribution as 2), considering the contribution strong and the evaluation thorough.

### Weaknesses

- **Surrogate models may break hard guarantees.** The paper claims hard constraint satisfaction, but the guarantees are undermined by the use of approximate fairness constraints in IP‑Main and by training surrogate models that only imitate IP solutions. Generalisation of AB/FB surrogates may lead to violations on unseen data; the paper does not discuss when or how the theoretical guarantees still hold (R3, R4). R1 also questions the feasibility of the IP stage and the ability of the surrogate to accurately reproduce AB/FB patterns.
- **Limited evaluation.** Results in Figure 3 and Table 2 are based on only 5 runs, and the error bars in Figure 3 are not specified; Table 2 lacks confidence intervals. More runs would strengthen significance (R2). Training cost (wallclock time and scaling with data size) is not reported, making it hard to judge practicality (R3).
- **Fairness evaluation on abstained samples may be unrealistic.** In practice, abstained decisions often involve human intervention, so the fairness of the entire system (algorithm + human) should be considered. The paper does not present a real‑world scenario that validates its fairness constraints on the classifier alone (R4). Furthermore, the comparison with LTD and FSCS may be unfair because those methods define fairness over the system including human decisions, while the proposed method uses a different fairness objective (R4).
- **Redundancy in objective.** Using both an error‑rate objective and a no‑harm constraint is essentially redundant, as they both aim to maintain accuracy (R4).
- **Unclear or missing motivation for Section 4.2.** It is not clear why additional pairwise constraints on abstention rates between groups are needed when IP‑Main already imposes a desired abstention rate per group. The overhead and vagueness of “performance will become worse” (whether harder to solve or higher error) are not clarified (R2). The variable \(z'\) in (3) is undefined (R2).
- **Lack of guidance for surrogate model design.** The appendix reports different neural network architectures per dataset, but no principle is given for selecting architectures – in particular whether hyperparameter tuning should treat each surrogate in isolation or the whole end‑to‑end pipeline (R3).
- **Missing multi‑group evaluation.** The reviewer asks for comparative performance on multi‑group settings for the Law and Compas datasets (R1). Also, the possibility of extending no‑harm to individual samples (e.g., preventing flips from positive to negative) is not explored (R3).
- **Minor confusions and typos.** (R2) The description “We approximate \(h_A\) and \(h_F\)” is confusing because they are binary parameters; the optimization problem in Section 3.1 defines abstention and no‑harm constraints without the group index \(z\) present in IP‑Main, but no reason for the inconsistency is given; the question whether a classifier is needed at all if FB can use random predictions (R2); a number of typos (“i.e.” → “i.e.,”, “e.g.” → “e.g.,”, “hyperparameter” → “hyperparameters”, “neural network” → “neural networks”, “TBD in 3.1” should be “TBD in 4.1”, an incomplete sentence about excessive abstention, missing period in footnote 2, “DO” → “DP”, “incur” → “incurs”).
- **No discussion of limitations or future work** (R2).
