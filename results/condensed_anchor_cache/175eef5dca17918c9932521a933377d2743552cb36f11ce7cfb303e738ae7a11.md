## Merged Review

### Summary
The three reviewers agree the paper is well-written and addresses a novel problem (lifelong continual adaptation with recurring in-distribution domains). One reviewer (rating 5) finds the problem insightful and the experiments extensive. The other two reviewers (rating 3 each) have strong reservations about the realism and necessity of the new problem setting, question the fairness of the experimental comparisons, and request additional ablations and baselines. There is a clear split: the higher-rated reviewer considers the contributions significant, while the lower-rated reviewers see marginal novelty and incomplete validation.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written; the motivation and idea are clearly explained (R1, R2).
- It addresses the largely overlooked aspect of recurring in-distribution domains in continual adaptation, which is an insightful contribution (R1).
- Extensive experiments are conducted, comparing with state-of-the-art methods in continual adaptation (R1, R2, R3).
- Ablation studies are included (R2, R3).
- The proposed DiffPrompt enables stable and efficient deployment in practical scenarios involving sequential and recurring domains (R1, R3).

### Weaknesses
1. **Problem setting realism and necessity (R1, R2)**:
   - The claim that recurring in-distribution domains are more realistic is disputed. In real-world applications (e.g., autonomous vehicles), new out-of-distribution domains are more likely, and the model could fail in OOD scenarios (R1). No prior knowledge of whether test data is ID or OOD is available (R2).
   - Evidence that test-time adaptation is needed for in-distribution data streams with sequential and recurring domains is missing. Methods from temporal domain adaptation or temporal domain generalization may already perform well without requiring extra training of prompt generators, and are more efficient (R2).
   - A baseline using the same foundation model tested with no prompts is requested (R2).
2. **Missing comparisons (R1, R2)**:
   - No comparison with recent diffusion-based test-time adaptation methods: [A] Turn Down the Noise: Leveraging Diffusion Models for Test-Time Adaptation via Pseudo-Label Ensembling, and [B] Diffusion-TTA: Test-Time Adaptation of Discriminative Models via Generative Feedback (R1).
   - Evaluation of the model’s performance on OOD settings is missing. If the method works only for ID data, its contribution is marginal (R2).
3. **Training data and fairness concerns (R1)**:
   - For the ImageNet-C dataset, did the authors generate 15 noisy domains for training? If so, this uses significantly more data than methods like CoTTA (which rely only on clean data), making the comparison potentially unfair (R1).
4. **Scalability and retraining (R1, R3)**:
   - Introducing a new domain may require retraining each model from scratch, which is impractical (R1).
   - How sensitive is DiffPrompt’s performance to the number of prompt samples collected per domain and the total number of epochs used for training the base model? (R3)
5. **Lack of distinct innovation explanation (R3)**:
   - Further explanation is needed on how DiffPrompt differs from existing prompt tuning techniques, and what key innovations make it suitable for long-term continual adaptation (R3).
6. **Missing ablation studies (R2, R3)**:
   - What is the impact of changing the order of domain recurrence? The algorithm may not rely on the sequence, but this should be tested (R1).
   - For the hypernetwork ablation, what is the input to the hypernetwork – domain embeddings or instance embeddings? (R2)
   - The impact of initializing with different prompts and the consistency of results across initializations should be reported (R3).
   - Additional ablation studies analyzing the contribution of each component (e.g., removing the condition module, using a simpler prompt generation method) are needed (R3).
