## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper introduces 3DGS-Drag, a drag-based 3D Gaussian editing framework. It first binds 3D Gaussians to handle points and moves them to target positions (deformation guidance), then uses a fine-tuned diffusion model with LoRA and Dreambooth for view-consistent content correction (diffusion guidance). A progressive multi-step strategy enables longer edits. Experiments show state-of-the-art performance across motion change, shape adjustment, inpainting, and content extension, requiring 10–20 minutes on a single RTX 4090 GPU.

### Strengths

- Proposes an innovative drag-based 3D Gaussian editing approach, whereas prior 3D editing methods focus on text-guided editing. (R1, R2)
- Uniquely combines 3D Gaussian Splatting for geometric deformation with diffusion-based image correction and iterative dataset updates, providing a practical solution. (R1, R3, R4)
- Introduces a multi-step progressive editing strategy that enables higher-quality edits while maintaining detail and multi-view consistency, addressing limitations of previous 3DGS editing methods such as GaussianEditor and viCA-NeRF. (R1, R4)
- Experiments are thorough and convincing, including qualitative and quantitative comparisons; the method outperforms Instruct-NeRF2NeRF and other baselines in editing quality, user preference, and performance across various tasks. (R2, R3, R4)
- Paper is well-structured and easy to follow. (R2, R4)

**Minority positive view (Reviewer 3, rating 8, vs. other ratings of 6):** The reviewer considers the demo results reasonable and the practical combination of GS-editing and diffusion-based correction effective, with the method clearly outperforming Instruct-NeRF2NeRF.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited to small movements and small objects:** Examples shown involve small movements; failure cases for large object movements (e.g., moving a large object over a large range, repositioning an entire flowerpot rather than elongating it, moving a table or truck) are not provided. Suggest the authors provide failure cases to illustrate the method’s upper bound. (R1, R2, R4)
- **Artifacts and quality issues in outputs:** In the wall-widening example (bottom right, Fig. 1), there is a noticeable color discrepancy between original and widened sections, and leaves near the edited boundary appear blurry. Qualitative figures do not clearly demonstrate the effectiveness of local editing; specific changes should be highlighted. (R2, R4)
- **Inaccurate claim about prior work:** The authors claim “there’s no exact previous work on intuitive 3D drag operation,” but this is inaccurate. ARSP implements drag-based 3D editing using mesh-based deformation, and Interactive3D offers deformable 3D point operations on 3DGS with SDS optimization. (R2)
- **Insufficient explanation of the diffusion guidance step (Sec. 3.4):** The difference from Haque et al. (2023) is only given in equation (5); it is unclear whether the proposed method is very similar to that work. More detailed explanation of the Annealed Dataset Editing (the core contribution) is needed, especially how it differs from Instruct-NeRF2NeRF. (R3)
- **Weaknesses in quantitative evaluation:** The user study (19 subjects) and GPT evaluations have potential biases and insufficient sample size; these limitations are not acknowledged. (R4)
- **Minor presentation issues:** 
  - Line 197: What is \(d\) in \(c \in R^d\)? 
  - Lines 240–241: \(k\) is used with two different meanings (“top-k (k=2)” and as a temporal index in \(\{p_h^k| k \n N_h^i\}\)). 
  - Equations (1) and (2): Minus symbols appear after sigma symbols (summation), which looks odd. 
  - Figure 4 is not referenced from the text. (R3)
