## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a learnable Differential Pyramid Representation Network (DPRNet) for HDR-to-LDR tone mapping. DPRNet integrates a Global Tone Perception (GTP) module, a Local Tone Tuning (LTT) module that learns patch-level LUTs with bilinear interpolation, a Learnable Differential Pyramid (LDP) for multi-scale high-frequency features, and an Iterative Detail Enhancement (IDE) module. Experiments on HDR+, HDRI Haven, HDR Survey, and UVTM video show substantial PSNR gains (2.58 dB on HDR+, 3.31 dB on HDRI Haven over second-best method). One reviewer (score 8) found the method innovative and well-evaluated; another (score 3) raised serious concerns about textual overlap and unclear contribution; two reviewers (scores 5,5) noted moderate strengths but significant weaknesses.

### Strengths
- **Innovative integration of global and local tone mapping**: DPRNet balances global consistency and local contrast harmonization (GTP + LTT), a non-trivial architecture.
- **Effective detail preservation**: The Learnable Differential Pyramid (LDP) and Iterative Detail Enhancement (IDE) capture multi-scale high-frequency components, improving over handcrafted pyramids.
- **Strong quantitative and qualitative results**: Outperforms state-of-the-art methods on multiple datasets (HDR+, HDRI Haven) by large margins in PSNR/SSIM; visual comparisons and a user study support the results.
- **Generalization tested across datasets**: Evaluated on non-homologous images and video tone mapping, demonstrating robustness.
- **Interesting module design**: LTT module learns different LUTs for different regions and uses bilinear interpolation to avoid edge discontinuity.
- **Well-structured and clearly written**: Paper has clear organization and sufficient ablation studies.

### Weaknesses
- **Textual overlap with prior works without proper citation**: Lines 186–199 are identical to Section 3.1 of He et al. (ECCV 2020) except for notations; Line 287–288 is identical to caption of Figure 4 in Zhang et al. (NeurIPS 2024); Lines 363–366 are nearly identical to Section 3.2 of Zhang et al. (NeurIPS 2024). This raises serious originality concerns (reviewer 4).
- **Limited novelty**: Pyramid decomposition has been used in [23] and [4]; the low-resolution enhancement part resembles a combination of CSRNet and 3DLUT. The main contribution is not clearly delineated—LDP may be the core but other components are adapted from existing works (reviewers 2,3).
- **High computational complexity**: The method is computationally intensive, especially for high-resolution images; it is nearly 40× slower than LUT-based methods. The paper provides limited runtime analysis and no discussion of scalability to real-time or mobile applications. Ablation does not offset speed reduction with accuracy gain (reviewers 1,3).
- **Incomplete literature review**: Missing relevant tone mapping works, e.g., Moran et al. “CURL: Neural curve layers for global image enhancement” (ICPR 2021), Zhao et al. “Learning Tone Curves for Local Image Enhancement” (IEEE Access 2022), Unsupervised HDR Image and Video Tone Mapping via Contrastive Learning, Fast global tone mapping for high dynamic range compression, A Real-Time Semi-Supervised Deep Tone Mapping Network, A Low-Latency Noise-Aware Tone Mapping Operator for Hardware Implementation (reviewer 2).
- **Unfair comparison with image enhancement methods**: Several compared baselines are image enhancement algorithms, not dedicated tone mapping methods. It is unclear whether those methods were retrained on the HDR-to-LDR datasets used in this paper (reviewers 2,3). The paper omits comparison with the recent tone mapping method [4] (Zhang et al. 2024) (reviewer 3).
- **Confusing LDP objective**: The high-frequency feature \(H_n\) is extracted from the HDR image and has a corresponding ground truth. If the GT can be directly extracted from the input, the purpose of learning \(H_n\) with a network is unclear (reviewer 4).
- **Unclear figure and network description**: The explanation does not match Figure 2. Specifically, the IDE module inputs are \(H_n\) and \(T_{n+1}\) but Figure 2 shows the first IDE input only as \(H_0\) (same for second); connection paths for \(T\) may be omitted. Also, the circled \(C_c\) in Figure 2 differs from circled \(C\) in Equation 4 (reviewers 2,4).
- **Weak motivation and scope**: The paper’s title implies broad tone mapping, but the method is supervised and focused on HDR-to-LDR. The claim of “generalization ability in unsupervised video TMO” in the conclusion is inconsistent with the supervised training (reviewer 3). The paper does not clearly state the target (LDR display) during reading (reviewer 2).
- **Poor generalization on noisy datasets**: The method struggles on MIT FiveK dataset due to noise and overexposure; no noise-handling component is discussed (reviewer 1).
- **User study limitations**: The user study could include more diverse participants (reviewer 1).
- **Minor errors**: Lines in Table 4 are misaligned; inconsistent uppercase usage of “W/O” in Figure 5; numeric citations do not follow formatting requirements (reviewer 4).
