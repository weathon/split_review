## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Equivariant Graph Neural Operator (EGNO), a method that models 3D dynamics as trajectories using equivariant temporal convolutions in Fourier space, rather than next-step prediction. It combines SE(3)-equivariant graph neural networks with Fourier neural operators to capture temporal correlations while maintaining equivariance. Empirical results on particle simulations, motion capture, and molecular dynamics show superiority over baselines like Tensor Field Networks, SE(3)-Transformers, and E(n)-GNN.

### Strengths
- The idea of extending equivariant graph neural networks with Fourier neural operators for temporal dynamics is well-motivated and original (Reviewers 1,2,3). The work is the first to consider temporal windows as function spaces for neural operators (Reviewer 1).
- The theoretical foundation is solid: incorporating E(n)-equivariance into Fourier transformation is principled (Reviewer 3). The method is general and compatible with any E(n)-equivariant GNN (Reviewer 3).
- Empirical validation is strong: claims are validated theoretically and empirically; the method outperforms baselines across multiple domains (Reviewers 1,2,3,4). Ablation studies demonstrate the utility of each component (Reviewer 1). The operator formulation enables efficient parallel decoding of future states with one inference (Reviewer 4).
- The paper is well-written and organized, making the methodology and background easy to follow (Reviewers 1,3,4).
- The empirical analysis includes varying hyperparameters I and P with results consistent with expectations (Reviewer 1). The work reduces the amount of training data required (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- The definition and implementation of \(M_\theta\) as a complex tensor lacks detailed discussion on its computational implications, such as impact on performance and optimizations needed (Reviewer 1).
- The motivation and implementation of the input processing steps are insufficiently explained: repeating graph \(G(t)\) \(P\) times, time embedding of \(\delta t\), and discrete Fourier transformation. Specific clarifications are needed on: the role of multiplying the state \(G(t)\), how DFT is applied to a single time step from the input sequence, whether the model can operate on an input sequence (not just one time step), and why \(\delta t\) embedding is necessary and how it is implemented (Reviewer 2).
- The method has a strong relation to temporal bundling proposed by Brandstetter et al. (ICLR 2022). A direct comparison in accuracy and computational cost on the same datasets and conditions is strongly recommended to quantify the advantage of the Fourier-based approach (Reviewer 3).
- The ablation study shows two Fourier modes suffice for n-body and mocap-run datasets, raising suspicion that these datasets are too simple to demonstrate the superiority of Fourier transform. A more detailed frequency content analysis is needed to justify the use of Fourier over simpler temporal methods (Reviewer 3).
- EGNO can be regarded as an extension or application of Fourier operator learning; the specific technical contributions beyond established works in equivariant GNNs and Fourier operators need to be clarified (Reviewer 4). One reviewer (R4) is notably more negative on novelty, while another (R1) finds the work highly original.
- Missing baselines: recent N-body simulation methods GCPNet and ClofNet are not mentioned or compared (Reviewer 4). Additionally, comparison to neural ODE / ClofNet's paradigm for trajectory modeling is missing (Reviewer 4).
- The metrics used for motion capture and molecular dynamics tasks are not clearly explained (Reviewer 4).
- The technical details of time embedding are not thoroughly clarified (Reviewer 4).
- Experimental conditions: hyperparameters \(P\) and \(I\) are not specified for each experiment; sometimes only one parameter is given (Reviewer 3). Table 3 caption should specify that it corresponds to \(|\text{Train}| = 3000\) (Reviewer 3).
- The use of Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) typically requires sequence lengths that are powers of two, but the paper tests lengths 2, 5, and 10. This discrepancy is not explained (Reviewer 3).
- Disparity in assessment: Reviewer 4 rates soundness, presentation, and contribution as "fair" (2), while other reviewers rate these aspects as "good" (3), indicating a minority negative view on the paper’s quality.
