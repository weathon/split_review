## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper formalizes the problem of RAG Dataset Inference (RAG-DI), where a data owner detects unauthorized use of their dataset in a RAG system via black-box queries. The authors introduce FARAD, a synthetic dataset designed to avoid LLM training contamination and include fact redundancy. They adapt existing RAG-MIA methods as baselines and propose FACTS (prompting for a single only-answerable question) plus the watermarks-based method WARD. Experiments show WARD consistently outperforms baselines in accuracy, query efficiency, and robustness, with statistical guarantees. The paper is well-written and makes several contributions to the underexplored area of RAG privacy. However, reviewers raise concerns about the realism of experimental settings, the strength of the “provable” claim, lack of countermeasure analysis, and scalability.

### Strengths

1. **Novel Problem Definition and Formalization** – The paper identifies and formalizes RAG-DI, a timely and overlooked issue. This fills a significant research gap and provides a foundation for future work in data privacy for RAG systems (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).

2. **Specialized Benchmark Dataset (FARAD)** – The dataset is built using fictional entity articles from an existing source, rewritten by multiple LLMs to include fact redundancy. It avoids the contamination and lack-of-redundancy problems of previous MIA datasets (EnronEmails, HealthcareMagic). This makes it suitable for realistic RAG-DI evaluation (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4).

3. **Novel Watermarking Approach (WARD)** – Using LLM watermarks for dataset inference is a creative and reasonable idea. WARD provides data owners with rigorous statistical guarantees and consistently outperforms all baselines (including adapted RAG-MIA methods and FACTS) in challenging settings (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3, Reviewer 4). It achieves high true positive rates with zero false positives across diverse settings (Reviewer 2).

4. **Comprehensive Experimental Validation** – The paper conducts extensive experiments with different LLMs, evaluation settings (easy/hard), hyperparameter analysis, modeling of imperfect retrieval, and examination of paraphrased text quality. This strengthens the credibility of WARD’s effectiveness (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 4).

5. **Well-Written and Self-Contained** – The paper is clearly structured, easy to follow, and logically presents contributions from problem definition to dataset creation, baselines, and the proposed method (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

1. **Realism of Experimental Setup**  
   - **Perfect Retrieval Assumption**: Most experiments assume retrieval returns only documents from the target source. Reviewer 1 notes that the hard setting with imperfect retrieval may actually be easier for baselines (distractors are less relevant), yet no comparison of baselines under imperfect retrieval is provided. This is critical because perfect retrieval does not hold in practice.  
   - **Limited Retrieval Scope**: Reviewer 4 questions the effectiveness of WARD when only one of many retrieved documents is watermarked (evaluated only with k=3; unknown for k=10). The claim of “provable” watermark detection lacks empirical support under variable and larger retrieval sizes.  
   - **Artificial Data Redundancy**: The dataset is generated entirely by LLMs. Reviewer 1 doubts whether this pipeline reflects real-world redundancy patterns and suggests human-generated summaries or naturally occurring redundant documents would be more realistic.  
   - **Narrow Scope of Real-World Complexity**: Reviewer 3 notes that real RAG systems may operate in multilingual environments and across diverse domains; the paper does not discuss such scenarios.

2. **Provability and Theoretical Guarantees**  
   - **Missing Rigorous Proof**: Reviewer 4 points out that while the paper claims WARD provides “provable” guarantees, no formal proof is given (unlike standard watermarking works). The term “provable” may only refer to the underlying watermark algorithm, but scalability within this definition is not addressed, which undermines credibility.  
   - **Defense Prompt Rationale Unclear**: Reviewer 1 finds the defense prompt (instructing the RAG model not to answer questions from retrieved documents) counterintuitive. If it is meant to simulate a RAG provider’s countermeasure, why would a provider hinder their own system? The motivation and realism of this defense need clarification.

3. **Countermeasures and Robustness**  
   - **Lack of Discussion on Adversarial Countermeasures**: Reviewer 2 notes that multiple techniques exist to attack watermarking (e.g., paraphrasing, word substitution, response monitoring). The paper does not discuss how WARD would fare against such countermeasures, which is essential for practical deployment.  
   - **Weak Defense Strategy**: Reviewer 4 criticizes the proposed defense as a simple prompt that LLMs may not follow exactly. A more practical defense could involve monitoring vocabulary usage in retrieved documents and penalizing watermarked words in generation, or paraphrasing responses—but such strategies are not investigated. Again, no theoretical guarantee is given for the defense.

4. **Practical Concerns: Scalability, Efficiency, and Applicability**  
   - **Application Range**: Reviewer 2 questions how often this problem arises in practice; if a data owner wants to check all LLMs, that could be prohibitive. The framework assumes the owner can query the same system repeatedly.  
   - **Lack of Computational Complexity Analysis**: Reviewer 2 and Reviewer 3 note that scalability for large datasets is not discussed. Can WARD handle a data owner with millions of documents? The query cost and time are not analyzed.  
   - **Data Update Frequency**: Reviewer 3 asks how frequent data updates (e.g., monthly) would affect WARD’s effectiveness and suggests this needs consideration.  
   - **Black-Box Watermark Generation**: Reviewer 4 asks how watermarked text can be generated with black-box models like GPT-4 or Claude 3.5-Sonnet. The method for this is not elaborated, which is critical given the reliance on watermarking.

5. **Unclear Experimental Details**  
   - **k-Shot Mechanism**: Reviewer 1 is confused about the use of k=3 in the RAG system when the Easy setting assumes only one article per group. The ablation uses k ∈ {3,4,5}, but the Hard setting has only 4 articles per group. The source and reasoning for the extra shots (beyond the number of articles) are not explained, which could affect reproducibility.

**Note on Reviewer Disagreement**: Reviewer 1 (rating 8) is notably more positive than the other reviewers (all rating 6). While sharing many of the same criticisms, Reviewer 1 considers the contributions and writing stronger and finds the watermarking approach particularly novel. The other reviewers, while acknowledging the contributions, have more reservations about the strength of the “provable” claim, lack of countermeasure analysis, and practical applicability.
