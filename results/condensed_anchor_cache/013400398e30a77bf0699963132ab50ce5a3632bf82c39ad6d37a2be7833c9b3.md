## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a data-driven method that combines representation learning (constructing set \(S_k\)) and topological analysis (using Wasserstein distance and Metric Space Approximation & Projection – MSAP) to discover conservation laws in physical systems, including quantum systems. The approach is claimed to be robust to noise. Experiments on four examples illustrate the method. Reviewers generally find the idea novel but criticize the presentation, missing comparisons, and insufficient theoretical grounding. One reviewer was very negative (score 1), finding the paper inaccessible and not suitable for ICLR; another was positive (score 5), while two gave moderate scores (3 each).

### Strengths
- **Novel method**: The combination of representation learning with topological analysis (specifically MSAP) to discover conservation laws is a potentially novel approach (Reviewers 1, 4). The use of the stress function as the loss is interesting (Reviewer 1).
- **Broad applicability**: Demonstrated on both classical and quantum systems (Reviewer 3).
- **Robustness to noise**: The method is claimed to be robust to measurement noise, which is valuable for real-world experimental setups (Reviewer 3). However, other reviewers note this claim lacks substantiation (see Weaknesses).
- **Examples**: The four physical simulations illustrate the potential of the method (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
**Presentation and clarity**
- The paper is not in a suitable form for ICLR; it is inaccessible to a majority of the audience. Subsequent sections lack the contextual information needed for comprehension (Reviewer 2).
- Section 3.3 requires a major overhaul: assumptions are not clearly written or numbered, and their implications should be discussed (Reviewer 1).
- Key notation is missing or undefined:
  * Equation (1): \(\delta_{x(t)}\) is not defined (Reviewers 2, 4).
  * Equation (3): \(T\) is not defined (Reviewer 4).
  * Page 4: \(L_1,\dots,L_n\) are not defined (Reviewer 4).
- The definition of \(S_k\) with “metric \(D_p\)” is confusing; does it imply a Minkowski metric? (Reviewer 4).
- The sentence “This way we force \(S_k\) to contain only manifolds with dimensionality \(\le k\)” is unclear: how is this enforced, and how is the Minkowski metric involved? (Reviewer 4).
- The explanation “the dimensionality of \(C\) will equate to the smallest \(k\) that allows us to identify a suitable \(C'\)” is confusing—is \(k\) a hyperparameter to tune? (Reviewer 4).
- In the latent dynamics model (Figure 1, part v), how does the query time \(t\) work? Does it require an ODE-like iterative solver, or does the network take \(t\) as direct input? (Reviewer 4).
- The algorithm described on page 5 needs more clarification; an appendix could help (Reviewer 1).
- Figures: Figure 2 axes are unclear (Reviewer 1); Figures 2, 3, 4 appear incomplete, and Figure 1 fonts need polishing (Reviewer 4).
- Typo on page 4: “\(S_k\in S_k\)” (Reviewer 4).

**Theoretical and empirical contribution**
- The paper does not provide theoretical guarantees that \(C'\) converges to \(C\) or any error analysis (e.g., as \(k\) equals the dimension of \(C\), does \(S_k\) progressively converge to \(M\)?) (Reviewer 1).
- A clear, distinct set of contributions is missing; the paper fails to specify which specific problem direction it pursues relative to existing work (Reviewer 1).
- Important related works are missing: Hamiltonian Neural Networks (NeurIPS 2019), Lagrangian Neural Networks (ICLR 2020 Workshop), ConCerNet (ICML 2023) (Reviewer 4). Additionally, comparisons with methods using Koopman theory, SINDy, and Neural ODEs are needed, as these can also be used to discover conserved quantities and may be more data-efficient and faster (Reviewer 1).
- The claimed robustness to noise is not substantiated with numerical comparisons to previous methods, nor is the impact of noise strength explored (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The method is not tested on more complex dynamical systems (e.g., higher-dimensional, chaotic, or stiff systems), which would better highlight its advantages (Reviewer 1).
- A key limitation—that variations in different quantities should have approximately the same magnitude—should be introduced in the Methods section, with a specific example of failure when that assumption does not hold (Reviewer 3).
- For quantum systems, the description of “each new experiment” in the data generation is unclear: does it mean a new initial \(x_0\) for each experiment or a new measurement on the same \(x_0\)? Also, sample complexity as a source of measurement noise in quantum systems should be discussed (Reviewer 3).
- The choice of Stress\((M,W)\) as the loss function is not justified; what would happen with other loss functions? (Reviewer 1).
