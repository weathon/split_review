## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces NoRA (Nested Low-Rank Adaptation), a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method that uses a dual-layer nested structure with an Activation-Aware Singular Value Decomposition (AwSVD) initialization. The outer LoRA weights are frozen, and inner LoRA weights are fine-tuned, aiming to reduce tunable parameters while preserving pre-trained knowledge. Evaluations cover commonsense reasoning, vision-language tasks, and subject-driven generation.

### Strengths
- **Rigorous empirical evaluation across diverse tasks**: The paper tests NoRA on multiple benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K, MATH, commonsense reasoning, vision-language) and compares against several LoRA variants, providing robust results.
- **Well-structured and clearly written**: The paper is logically organized, with an intuitive motivation; figures and illustrations support the text cleanly.
- **Innovative technique (AwSVD)**: Leveraging activation distributions for SVD initialization is a clever upgrade from standard SVD; it reduces decomposition errors and facilitates faster convergence (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 5).
- **Significant reduction in tunable parameters and training resources**: NoRA consistently uses far fewer trainable parameters than standard LoRA, reducing memory usage and training time while maintaining or improving performance (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2, Reviewer 5).
- **Strong performance gains over ultra-low-parameter baselines**: On challenging benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH), NoRA substantially outperforms LoRA-XS and VeRA (Reviewer 5).
- **Simple implementation**: The method requires only a few lines of code modification, making it practical for deployment (Reviewer 2).
- **Novel integration of existing ideas**: While components are inspired by prior work, their combination into a nested structure is new and achieves SoTA results with low budgets (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Expressiveness and rank claims lack theoretical/empirical grounding**:
  - The paper claims that NoRA’s rank bound (min(r, r′)) enables more complex non-linear transformations, but this is misleading and unsupported (Reviewers 3, 4, 5). A tighter rank constraint does not imply increased complexity (Reviewer 5). The entire subsection 3.4 on expressiveness and generation is considered nonsensical by multiple reviewers and should be reconsidered (Reviewer 4).
- **Freezing outer LoRA parameters to “maintain stability” is unjustified**:
  - No theoretical or empirical evidence is given to explain why freezing aids stability (Reviewers 3, 4). This design choice needs justification.
- **Missing ablation studies**:
  - The individual contributions of the nested structure and AwSVD are not isolated. Ablation studies are needed to determine which components are most critical (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient comparison with LoRA under similar parameter budgets**:
  - The paper does not directly compare LoRA and NoRA when they have comparable numbers of trainable parameters. Such a comparison is essential to assess whether NoRA genuinely improves performance or simply trades off parameter count (Reviewers 3, 4). Likewise, a holistic figure showing performance across different ranks is missing (Reviewer 5).
- **Comparison scope is too narrow**:
  - The design space evaluation focuses only on VeRA and LoRA-XS, neglecting other significant PEFT methods. Figure 2 resembles an ablation of the proposed techniques rather than a comprehensive comparison (Reviewer 5). A summary table of prior design choices would help (Reviewer 5).
- **No discussion of compatibility with orthogonal methods (DoRA, AdaLoRA, etc.)**:
  - The paper does not explore whether NoRA can complement or integrate with other successful approaches like DoRA or AdaLoRA (Reviewer 5).
- **Vision-language experiments are limited**:
  - Evaluations are conducted only on a few simple classification datasets. More complex vision-language tasks (e.g., referring segmentation/detection, visual captioning) are needed to demonstrate general effectiveness (Reviewer 3). The subject-driven generation results appear selectively reported, possibly cherry-picked; qualitative results on standard benchmarks like DreamBooth should be included (Reviewer 5).
- **Theoretical and intuitive justification for AwSVD is weak**:
  - The paper does not explain why an activation-aware scaling matrix mitigates decomposition errors, nor does it clarify whether a large calibration set is required (Reviewer 5). The design of the activation-weight matrix lacks detail (Reviewer 4).
- **Hyperparameter sensitivity and practical usability concerns**:
  - Optimal configurations vary by task, limiting ease of use (Reviewer 1). Sensitivity to batch size is not explored, and it is unclear how to select input activations from the fine-tuning dataset (Reviewer 5).
- **Claims about addressing LoRA’s limitations are inaccurate in places**:
  - Line 43-44 states that DoRA cannot address slow convergence and overfitting, but DoRA does not aim to address those issues and itself sometimes fails to converge (Reviewer 4, citing DoRA and FLoRA).
  - The connection between experiments in Fig. 1 and the stated problem about decomposition errors is not clear (Reviewer 4).
- **Severe issues with reported results**:
  - Table 1 lists DoRA and LoRA with rank=1, which is not a practical setting (Reviewer 5).
  - The accuracy of DoRA in Table 2 is inconsistent with the original paper (85.3% vs. 83.0%) (Reviewer 5).
- **Novelty is questioned**:
  - The core idea of SVD decomposition has already been explored by LoRA-XS and PiSSA, limiting the novelty of the approach (Reviewer 5).
- **Minor: Notation and definition gaps**:
  - Symbols U, Σ, V, S appear without explanation (Line 224) (Reviewer 4). The definition of “parallel LoRA” and the adapter representation with ReLU are unclear (Reviewer 4). The claim that PiSSA “adjusts matrix ranks” is inaccurate (Reviewer 4, Question 1).
- **Contrast between reviewers**: One reviewer (Reviewer 2, score 6) is more positive, emphasizing the cleverness and practicality of the method, while most others (scores 3, 3, 3) are highly critical, citing weak theory, limited comparison, and unclear motivation. Reviewers 4 and 5 argue the paper is not ready for a top venue.
