## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces LVBench, a long-video understanding benchmark for multimodal large language models (MLLMs), focusing on videos exceeding 30 minutes (average >1 hour). It comprises 103 curated YouTube videos with approximately 1550 human-annotated multiple-choice QA pairs covering six capabilities: temporal grounding, summarization, reasoning, entity recognition, event understanding, and key information retrieval. A total of 15 MLLMs are evaluated, demonstrating the benchmark’s difficulty. While reviewers generally appreciate the focus on longer videos than prior works, the comprehensive evaluation, and the clearly stated motivations, one reviewer (Reviewer 2) was notably more critical, raising significant concerns about the benchmark’s scale, missing baselines, and evaluation methodology.

### Strengths

- **Timely and useful benchmark for long-video QA**, with video lengths exceeding those in existing datasets (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
- **Clear motivation and differentiation from prior work**; Table 1 is particularly effective in highlighting novelty (Reviewer 4).
- **Commendable enumeration of six distinct capabilities** necessary for long-video understanding (Reviewer 2).
- **Comprehensive evaluation of 15 different MLLMs** (Reviewer 2) / multiple baseline methods adopted and tested (Reviewer 1).
- **Well-designed quality control process** to mitigate question-type imbalance and language bias (Reviewer 1).
- **Several interesting analyses on dataset statistics**, including clue-duration analysis (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Paper is well written and motivating** (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited scale and diversity of videos and questions:**
  - Only 103 videos and 1549 QA pairs (second smallest benchmark in QA count); the number of videos is not listed in Table 1, which would highlight the small size. A larger number of videos is necessary for a credible benchmark (Reviewer 2).
  - Video diversity is insufficient: many common categories are missing (e.g., video games, educational, vlogs, health/fitness, technology) and the benchmark is heavily skewed (>80% from Entity and Event recognition – Figure 3). A statistical chart of diversity is needed (Reviewers 1, 2).
  - The lack of diversity in question types limits assessment of broader video understanding (Reviewer 2).
  - Comparison to related datasets (e.g., Short Film Dataset) was omitted (Reviewer 3).

- **Unclear annotation process and quality:**
  - The annotation instructions for generating questions and multiple-choice options are not provided; only one example per type is shown (Figure 2), with no supplementary material (Reviewer 3).
  - The dataset only uses multiple-choice QA, which restricts evaluation flexibility (Reviewer 3).
  - Manual cross-checking for annotation errors is recommended (Reviewer 1).
  - The “multi-stage quality control process” (L072) is not explained in sufficient detail (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).

- **Evaluation methodology and robustness concerns:**
  - Answer extraction uses regex followed by GLM-4; alternative schemes (likelihood selection, cloze-prompting, text-encoder similarity) could improve robustness and should be reported. How GLM-4 is used exactly is not explained (Reviewer 4).
  - Performance may conflate video understanding with instruction following / answer bias: MovieChat and LWM exhibit strong bias towards option A, while InternVL2-40B gives nearly uniform choices. Using the underlying LLM without video as a lower bound is necessary to isolate the video component (Reviewers 2, 4).
  - There is an internal discrepancy: InternVL2-40B score differs between Table 2 (39.8) and Table 3 (39.5) (Reviewer 2).

- **Insufficient baselines and adaptation for long videos:**
  - For models not designed for long videos, sampling only 32 or 96 frames from ~4000-second videos is insufficient; more meaningful adaptation strategies are needed, or these models should be de-emphasized (Reviewer 2).
  - Several important baselines are missing: (1) video caption generation + LLM; (2) text-only (no video) with a state-of-the-art LLM; (3) single center frame with a state-of-the-art VLM; (4) comparison to Short Film Dataset (Reviewers 2, 3, 4).
  - Analysis of few-frame bias (impact of number of input frames) is absent (Reviewer 1).

- **Missing clarity on “Clue Duration” analysis:**
  - How the ground-truth clue duration was collected (human annotation vs. automated) and how “clue length” is determined for free-form questions are not specified (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).

- **Missing experimental details and exposition issues:**
  - Calculation of human scores in Table 3 is not provided (Reviewer 1).
  - LLM filtering (Section 4.4) is not sufficiently explained; consider adding a full row to Table 2 if related to naive baselines (Reviewer 4).
  - Compute/inference time, dataset size (storage), and copyright/licensing details for videos are missing; video license issues (YouTube deletion, distribution, training restrictions) are not addressed (Reviewers 3, 4).
  - Table 3 should report results for all methods, not only the two best (Reviewer 2).
  - Terms “complete video structure” and “coherent logical flow” (L184) are undefined (Reviewer 1).
  - L143: video durations should be given in hours (e.g., “4101 seconds (>1 hour)”) (Reviewer 4).
  - Table 2 caption should repeat abbreviations (ER, EU, KIR, TG, Rea, Sum) for readability (Reviewer 4).
  - Minor typos: missing spaces before parentheses in Section 3.2 enumerated items (Reviewer 2).

- **Counterintuitive result:**
  - Figure 5 shows MovieChat performs better on short videos than long ones, which is surprising and requires explanation (Reviewer 1).

- **Missing discussion and improvement directions:**
  - The paper does not provide discussion on potential directions to improve MLLMs based on the benchmark findings (Reviewer 2).
