## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes UniEEG, an electrode-wise time-frequency pretraining model for EEG that uses CWT and a masked autoencoder framework. It claims to unify representations across datasets with different electrode configurations. While the identified challenge is important and the figures/ablation studies are informative, the paper suffers from poor writing, questionable claims of novelty, insufficient related work, missing critical baselines and implementation details, and fundamental issues with the preprocessing and universality of the approach.

### Strengths
- The addressed challenge (learning universal EEG representations across tasks and electrode configurations) is a key gap in the literature that attracts wide research interest (R2, confidence 4).
- Figures and diagrams are of high quality and informative (R2, R4; confidence 4 each).
- The use of Continuous Wavelet Transform (CWT) to capture time-frequency characteristics of EEG signals is a sensible choice (R3, confidence 5).
- Extensive ablation studies on masking strategies, decoder depths, and the impact of signal domain (time vs. frequency) provide useful insights (R3, confidence 5).
- The topic has practical significance (R4, confidence 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Poor writing and formatting**: The manuscript has multiple errors: two “Introduction” sections on Page 1 (R2, R3, R4); missing spaces (Section 1, 4.1.1); incomplete sentences (Sections 4.1.2, 5.3.1, 5.3.2, 5.3.4); grammatical errors (Section 5.2); extra spaces and missing words (Sections 5.3.1, 5.3.2, 5.3.4); formatting issues in headings (Sections 5.3.2, 5.3.7); Table 1 font too small and unreadable (R2, R4). These errors make the paper difficult to follow and fall short of ICLR standards.
- **False claim of “first”**: The paper claims to be “the first electrode-wise time-frequency pretraining model”, but prior works exist, including BIOT (Yang et al. NeurIPS 2023), Yi et al. (NeurIPS 2024), LaBraM (Jiang et al. ICLR 2024), EEG2Rep (KDD 2024), and Neuro-BERT (IEEE JBHI 2024) (R2, R3, R4). This claim is incorrect and the omission is unacceptable, especially since LaBraM is a known ICLR spotlight paper with highly similar electrode-wise preprocessing and mask-based reconstruction (R4).
- **Insufficient related work and missing baselines**: The paper states that “there are no studies validating MSM in EEG signals”, ignoring recent masked signal modeling works (R3). It only compares against two outdated baselines (2018, 2015), failing to include LaBraM, BIOT, or any other recent foundation model (R3, R4). The claimed SOTA results are therefore unconvincing.
- **Limited novelty**: The approach is a straightforward application of vanilla MAE (Masked Autoencoder) to EEG data, with no significant methodological innovation beyond adapting a pre-existing model (R3).
- **Unclear preprocessing and non-universal signal pipeline**: The “resize” operation for segments of random duration is not explained (padding or resampling? different sampling rates?) (R2). Filtering EEG signals in 2-50 Hz may limit applicability to only motor imagery or steady-state BCIs, contradicting the claimed universality (R1). The use of a simplistic resampling method and standard CWT after dubious bandpass filtering, while neglecting artifact removal, is fundamentally flawed (R1). The authors appear to lack understanding of EEG processing challenges (R1).
- **Missing data split and evaluation details**: The paper uses 16 datasets for pre-training and 12 for evaluation but does not clarify whether splits are based on subject identity or sessions (R2). No standard deviations, significance tests, or holdout validation descriptions are provided (R2). Essential implementation details (training/validation/test split) are absent, compromising reproducibility (R3).
- **Lack of technical depth**: The paper contains no equations, making it impossible to understand how features are extracted and unified across datasets (R4). The methods for unifying frequency-domain features from different acquisition devices are not described (R4).
- **Ignored inter-channel connectivity**: Pretraining is performed channel-by-channel, and representations are fused only after the encoder. This late fusion may miss functional connectivity between channels, unlike approaches such as Yi et al. (2024) or joint encoding (R2, R3). The masking percentage effect on performance is not explored (R2). Unifying frequency features across datasets presents specific challenges that are not addressed (R4).
- **Minority reviewer disagreement**: Two reviewers (R1, R4, scores 1) are highly critical, citing fundamental lack of understanding and severe novelty/omission issues, while two others (R2, R3, scores 3) acknowledge the topic’s importance and the ablation study but still note major flaws. All four reviewers agree on poor writing and insufficient baselines.
