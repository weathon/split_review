## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SWIFT, a self-speculative decoding method that uses layer-skipping of the target LLM as the draft model, with Bayesian optimization for adaptive layer selection, confidence-aware early stopping, and top-k dynamic verification. The method is plug-and-play and requires no additional training or parameters. Reviewers generally acknowledge the clarity and feasibility, but they raise significant concerns about insufficient comparisons with existing self-speculative decoding methods, limited model/task diversity, moderate speedup (1.3–1.6×) relative to methods like EAGLE/Medusa (3–4×), incomplete related work discussion, and lack of evaluation in dynamic settings. One reviewer is more positive (score 8), viewing the Bayesian optimization as a good idea despite limited novelty; others (scores 6, 5, 6) find the innovation insufficient and the speedup less compelling.

### Strengths
- Plug-and-play approach with no auxiliary models, additional parameters, or extensive training required, making it applicable to most LLMs (R1, R2, R3, R4).
- Demonstrates that speculative decoding can accelerate inference even without training a separate draft model, leveraging self-layer sparsity (R2).
- Makes two key observations about task-specific layer sparsity, motivating adaptive layer selection (R2).
- Uses Bayesian optimization to dynamically select layers to skip; reviewer R1 considers this a good idea despite the layer-skipping concept not being novel (R1).
- Incorporates confidence-aware early stopping and top-k per-token predictions during verification to further improve speedup (R2, R4).
- Paper is well-written, flows smoothly, and conveys key ideas in sufficient detail (R2, R4).
- Empirical analysis demonstrates feasibility and speedup over vanilla inference on CNN/DM, GSM8K, and TinyStories with Llama-2 and CodeLlama (R2, R4).

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient comparison with existing self-speculative decoding and layer-skipping methods.** The paper should include self-SD (Draft & Verify, ACL 2024) in Table 2, as SWIFT is essentially an improvement of that approach (R4). Also missing comparisons with other layer-skipping methods (Elhoushi et al. 2024, and another citation at openreview.net/pdf?id=yUmJ483OB0) (R2), and blockwise parallel language models (Kim et al. 2024, Stern et al. 2018, Gloeckle et al. 2024) (R1). The paper should also compare with training-efficient methods like Medusa, EAGLE (EMNLP 2024), and training-free Lookahead (R3, R4) – these achieve 3–4× speedup versus SWIFT’s 1.3–1.6× (R4). The paper claims plug-and-play, but it is unclear if existing methods could also perform well with limited offline training (R2).

2. **Limited model and task diversity.** Experiments are confined to Llama-2 and CodeLlama; testing on larger or differently-architected models (e.g., Llama-3) is needed to demonstrate generalization (R3, R4). Missing results for Llama-2-7B(-Chat) and Llama-2-70B-Chat (R3). Evaluation is also absent on general-purpose chat benchmarks like MT-Bench, and on dynamic settings where the underlying task distribution changes – in such settings, SWIFT may need to interleave optimization and acceleration, raising questions about realized speedup (R2, R3). Reviewer R1 also asks about handling non-stationary input distributions and extreme token count variations or highly domain-specific tasks.

3. **Innovation insufficient relative to prior self-speculative decoding.** The confidence-aware inference strategies are similar to mechanisms already in self-SD (R4). Layer-skipping itself is not new (R1). The use of Bayesian optimization, while potentially useful, needs more discussion and comparison with bandit-based approaches from concurrent work (e.g., “A Unified Framework for Speculative Decoding with Multiple Drafters as a Bandit,” submitted at ICLR 2025) (R1).

4. **Speedup value is modest and requires justification.** SWIFT achieves only 1.3–1.6× speedup, whereas Medusa and EAGLE report 3.05–4.26×, with training overhead reported as only 1–2 days for 70B models (R4). Training-free Lookahead may also perform better (R3). Even within SWIFT, the dynamic verification component shows minimal gain (1.560× vs 1.541× in Appendix D Table 7) (R2). The paper does not clarify if reported speedups include the optimization phase; if so, the number of LLM generations used should be stated (R2). The overhead of layer searching should be compared with training overhead of methods like EAGLE (R3).

5. **Reward design and stability under distributional changes are not well explained.** More discussion is needed on the assumptions and limitations of Bayesian optimization for layer skipping, potentially inspired by bandit approaches (R1).

6. **Related work discussion is incomplete.** The paper should discuss and cite Elhoushi et al. 2024, Kim et al. 2024, Stern et al. 2018, Gloeckle et al. 2024, and the unspecified citation at openreview.net/pdf?id=yUmJ483OB0 (R1, R2).

7. **Method requires different settings for different tasks**, which is not ideal for real-world chat applications where user instructions are unpredictable (R3). This also ties into the lack of evaluation in a dynamic or multi-task setting (R2, R3).
