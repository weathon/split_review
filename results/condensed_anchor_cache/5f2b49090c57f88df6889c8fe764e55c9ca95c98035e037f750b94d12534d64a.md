## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper analyzes memory in deep linear state-space models (SSMs) through three perspectives: autocorrelation function, group delay, and equivalence between narrow deep and shallow wide SSMs. Simplified assumptions (constant B and C, linearity) are used throughout. One reviewer (score 6, confidence 4) found the paper insightful, the theoretical observations important, and the experiments well-designed. Two reviewers (scores 3 each, confidence 4 and 5) raised significant concerns about clarity, novelty, depth of experiments, and practical relevance. The contrasting assessments center on whether the simplified analysis yields meaningful insight beyond existing literature and whether the experiments are sufficiently deep and realistic.

### Strengths
- The problem of understanding how depth affects memory in SSMs is interesting and timely.
- We commend the key theoretical observations: (i) if one layer sets λ≈1 for long-term memory, another layer must learn a λ close to 0 to keep state variance from exploding; (ii) the effect of eigenvectors on the input matrix B in a linear SSM is insightful.
- The setup is simple and effective, using synthetic teacher-student experiments to illustrate the theoretical claims.
- The paper provides a detailed analysis of deep linear SSMs and a thorough review of related work (though one reviewer noted this engagement could be deeper).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and definitions:** Several key terms are not properly defined in the main text. R^(k)(Δ) and Δ (Section 3.1) are only clarified in the appendix; their connection to the autocorrelation function should be explicitly stated. Group delay is loosely defined in words rather than with the standard formal definition (phase delay / group delay). Equation (5) has a typo (k vs l). Section 3.3 describes a general K-layer SSM but the text says K=2, causing confusion.
- **Experiment limitations:**
  - Only 1–3 layers of the LRU model are tested, far from practical depths. At least 12 layers should be explored to see how properties change, and the authors themselves use up to 3 layers in their theory; the limited range weakens the experimental support. Even in Figure 2, deeper networks appear feasible within the computational budget.
  - The teacher-student setup lacks clear motivation and details: is the teacher planted (fixed random parameters) or trained? How are its parameters chosen? This ambiguity affects the validity of the results.
  - Inputs are uncorrelated white noise; it is unclear how the results would change for correlated inputs or more realistic data.
  - The practical importance of the observed phenomena (variance explosion, group delay increase, equivalence) is not demonstrated for real-world architectures that include normalization (e.g., layer norm) or use nonlinearities.
- **Theoretical limitations and novelty:**
  - The autocorrelation derivation for linear SSMs is a well-known result in time-series literature (ARMA models). The paper does not engage with this connection and does not extend the analysis to nonlinear or complex-valued SSMs (most practical SSMs are complex-valued).
  - The equivalence between a narrow deep linear SSM and a shallow wide SSM (Eq. 8) is mathematically straightforward; the authors then claim deep SSMs have different properties (e.g., variance explosion), but since the models are equivalent, there exists a shallow representation reproducing the exact same behavior, making the distinction contradictory. The implication of the P and P⁻¹ transformation is not clearly explained.
  - The key contributions are hard to distinguish from prior work, especially Recurrent Neural Networks: Vanishing and Exploding Gradients Are Not the End of the Story (arXiv:2405.21064). Appendix Lemma 1 appears to duplicate content from that paper without proper adaptation.
- **Cohesion and audience:** The paper reads as a collection of observations rather than a coherent narrative. It lacks a clear separation of takeaways for theoreticians versus practitioners, reducing impact for both groups.
- **Missing comparisons/ablation:** The paper does not compare with or discuss how its results relate to the cited RNN gradient literature (e.g., the RNN arXiv paper). The argument in Appendix Figure 5 about adding depth allowing long-term dependencies with smaller eigenvalues is insufficiently explained.
