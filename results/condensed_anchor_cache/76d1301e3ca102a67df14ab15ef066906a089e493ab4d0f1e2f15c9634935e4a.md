## Merged Review

### Summary
Reviewers characterize this paper as addressing exemplar-free class-incremental learning (EFCIL) by combining a mixture-of-experts (MoE) architecture (similar to SEED, ICLR 2024) with an angular drift compensation method (similar to EASE, CVPR 2024). The method, ProCEED, uses multiple Gaussian-prototypical experts, fine-tunes one per task, applies feature and logit distillation, and realigns old-class prototypes via semantic mapping. All reviewers note strong reported performance on several benchmarks, but raise serious concerns about novelty, textual overlap with prior works, insufficient differentiation, and experiment reliability.

### Strengths
- The experimental results on CIFAR-100, ImageNetSubset, and other datasets validate the author’s claims and demonstrate significant performance gains over baselines (Reviewer 1, confidence 4; Reviewer 2; Reviewer 3; Reviewer 4, confidence 5).  
- Combining feature distillation and logit distillation in the training loss yields better results than using only feature distillation when semantic mapping is not applied (Table 3) (Reviewer 4).  
- On CIFAR-100, the method appears robust even with a lower number of experts (Table 5) (Reviewer 4).  
- The topic of exemplar-free class-incremental learning is important and worth exploring (Reviewer 2, confidence 5; Reviewer 3, confidence 4).  
- The paper proposes prototype consolidation and expert ensemble to tackle the forgetting problem without relying on previous task data (Reviewer 3).  
- The experiments are fairly comprehensive and show superiority in accuracy (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty / heavy reliance on prior works.** All four reviewers highlight that ProCEED is essentially a direct combination of SEED (ICLR 2024) and EASE (CVPR 2024) with minimal changes. The expert ensemble, training loss, Gaussian prototypical components, and inference are essentially the same as SEED; the angular drift compensation via semantic mapping is a copy of EASE (Reviewers 1–4). The only reported difference is the addition of a logit-distillation term, which is already a well-established technique in incremental learning (e.g., XDER, FACIL) (Reviewer 4, confidence 5).  
- **Significant textual overlap / potential plagiarism.** Large portions of Sections 3.2, 3.3, and 3.4 are direct rephrasings of SEED and EASE with only minor notational changes. Examples:  
  - Line 175‑176 copies SEED’s description verbatim (Reviewer 1).  
  - Line 270 copies EASE’s formulation verbatim (Reviewer 1).  
  - Section 3.2 (after Eq.(5)) and Section 3.3 are nearly identical to Section 4.2 of EASE; Section 3.4 mirrors SEED’s algorithm (Reviewer 4, confidence 5).  
  - Even the related work subsection “Gaussian Models in CL” is copied from SEED (Reviewer 2, confidence 5).  
- **Fairness concerns.** The authors do not properly cite SEED and EASE in the sections that directly implement their methodology. The only mention of EASE is a generic “Being inspired by” at the end of the introduction; SEED is only acknowledged for overlapping-class identification (Reviewers 2, 4, confidence 5). Figures 2, 3, and 4 lack acknowledgements of SEED/EASE in the captions (Reviewer 4).  
- **Insufficient differentiation and motivation.** The paper does not clearly state the motivation for using MoE, nor does it contrast ProCEED with other MoE-based approaches upfront (Reviewer 3).  
- **Missing ablations and analyses.**  
  - No ablation on the number of MoE experts (Reviewer 3).  
  - The ablation study (Table 3) would benefit from investigating the effects of angular drift and logit distillation separately for each dataset (Reviewer 3).  
  - The ablation study in Figure 7 on prototype update methods is the same as in EASE (Figure 7(b)), just on different datasets (Reviewer 4).  
- **Lack of discussion on computational cost and memory demands.** Scaling to larger datasets is not addressed (Reviewer 3).  
- **Experimental reliability concerns.**  
  - Standard deviations are not reported for any results (Reviewer 3).  
  - ProCEED appears to have a much higher starting accuracy than compared methods in Figures 5b and 6a (Reviewer 2, confidence 5).  
  - Table 6 contains an unfair comparison: ProCEED is evaluated under a task-incremental learning scenario while other methods are evaluated under a class-incremental learning scenario, making the results doubtful (Reviewer 2, confidence 5).  
- **Writing and presentation issues.** The method description is insufficient; symbols in Eq.(2) (F̂_{k,t}, F_{k,t}, F*_{k,t}) are not explained, and the relationship between F_{k,t} and F is unclear (Reviewer 1, presentation score 1).  
- **Technical questions (weakness / missing explanation).**  
  - Eq.(4) – why can inputs x from all tasks be used simultaneously to build f_k? (Reviewer 1)  
  - Line 248 – how can the model have access to drifted prototypes μ_{2,1} when F2 has not yet been built (first train D1, then D2)? The elements above the diagonal in Eq.(6) are zero (Reviewer 1).  
  - The use of ResNet32 for ImageNetSubset is unusual; ResNet18 is more commonly adopted (Reviewer 1).  
- **Minority positive contrast.** Reviewer 1 (score 5, confidence 4) acknowledges strong experimental results but still notes limited novelty and writing weaknesses. In contrast, Reviewer 4 (score 1, confidence 5) judges the paper to lack any original contribution and raises strong fairness and plagiarism concerns. Reviewers 2 and 3 (score 3) also emphasize the overlap with prior works and experimental flaws.
