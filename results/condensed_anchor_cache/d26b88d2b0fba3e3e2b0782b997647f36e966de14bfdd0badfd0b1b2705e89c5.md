## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Robust Exploration via Clustering-based Online Density Estimation (RECODE), a non-parametric method for novelty-based exploration in deep reinforcement learning. RECODE estimates visitation counts for clusters of states in a learned embedding space, updated via a dynamic memory with discounted counts. The paper also proposes Coupled Action-State Masking (CASM), a transformer-based loss that combines masked sequence modeling with inverse dynamics prediction to learn controllable features over multiple steps. The method is evaluated on hard-exploration Atari games and the DM-HARD-8 suite, where it achieves new state-of-the-art results, including being the first RL agent to reach the end screen in Pitfall! and exceeding human performance on Push Blocks. The paper includes ablation studies on the effect of representation, memory design, and robustness to observation noise.

### Strengths

- The problem of exploration in sparse-reward, large observation spaces is of significant interest. RECODE extends non-parametric novelty estimation (e.g., NGU) by maintaining a memory buffer in a compact embedding space and addresses non-stationary data distributions through careful implementation details (discounted counts, atom update heuristics).
- RECODE simplifies NGU by replacing its separate episodic and global novelty bonuses with a single memory-based mechanism that tracks visitation counts. This removes the need for a separate episodic novelty component, though the simplification adds memory management complexity (noted in Weaknesses).
- The method balances short-term and long-term novelty, avoiding excessive bias toward short-term novelty while integrating long-term memory despite finite memory constraints.
- CASM effectively combines a transformer-based masked sequence model with inverse dynamics prediction to learn a compact embedding space. The overall framework combines robust foundations from multiple fields (clustering, transformers, inverse dynamics) into a high-quality, well-justified algorithm.
- Empirical evaluation shows headline successes: RECODE is the first RL algorithm to reach the end screen in Pitfall! and exceeds human-level performance on Push Blocks in DM-HARD-8. It sets new state-of-the-art results across the DM-HARD-8 suite and performs well on hard-exploration Atari games.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow, with comprehensive descriptions, helpful figures, algorithm pseudo-code, and dedicated experiments beyond task-return analyses (e.g., effect of representation, robustness to noise, ablation of components).
- The contribution to the community is significant, solving challenging long-horizon exploration problems by harnessing powerful representation learning and precise novelty estimation.

### Weaknesses

- **Memory management complexity**: RECODE introduces many hyperparameters (memory size, discount factor for counts, probability of inserting new atoms, heuristics for updating/removing atoms) that require careful tuning and whose interaction is not explored. This undermines the claimed simplification over NGU—R2 explicitly states the simplicity rationale is not apparent and that design space complexity is not clearly lower. The paper lacks a sensitivity analysis and rationale for optimal parameter choices (R1 W1, R2 weakness and question).
- **Overclaimed or insufficiently supported results**:  
  - Claim of state-of-the-art on Atari is not fully supported: RECODE underperforms NGU on hero and venture. The paper should specify which environments it outperforms existing methods (R1 W4).  
  - R2 notes that besides the headline successes on Pitfall! and DM-HARD-8, the experimental figures do not show overwhelming positive evidence in favor of RECODE across the board. Figure 1 lacks definitions of HNS, Capped HNS, and number of frames (R2 question).
- **Missing ablation and comparison experiments** (R1 W2):  
  - Ablate CASM vs one-step action prediction (AP) to determine if CASM only helps under high aliasing/partial observability (as in DM-HARD-8). Provide results for MEME-RECODE and NGU-CASM on Atari.  
  - The use of RND on AP features in Appendix L is arbitrary; a more natural baseline would be RND on the AP features directly or predicting AP features. Compare RECODE vs NGU when NGU uses RND on AP (or RND-like bonus with AP features) for its global bonus to isolate the benefit of RECODE’s memory mechanism.  
  - R2 asks for clarification on how the experimental figures support superiority; a clearer quantitative breakdown is needed.
- **Limited evaluation domains**: The paper lacks evaluation in open-ended environments like Minecraft, which test emergent behavior and less structured exploration. Current evaluation (Atari, DM-Hard-8) does not capture these challenges (R3).
- **Insufficient evidence of robustness across RL algorithms**: Only MEME and VMPO are used. Demonstrating compatibility with widely-used algorithms like PPO or DQN would strengthen the claim that RECODE works with different RL algorithms (R3).
- **Poorly documented computational resources**: Only environment interaction counts are reported; details on memory usage, CPU, and GPU memory are missing. This omission makes it difficult to assess practicality and scalability, especially if RECODE is more resource-intensive than baselines (R3, R3 question).
- **Missing connections to related work**: The paper would benefit from discussing MIMEx (which also uses masked transformers for intrinsic rewards), successor features, and general value functions that incorporate multi-step information for exploration. It should clarify how RECODE relates to these approaches and why it is superior or complementary (R1 W3).
- **Procedural generation concern**: In environments where novel states in the current episode may be common in the global memory, an episodic reset (like NGU) might be preferable. The paper does not discuss when a hard global reset is beneficial versus RECODE’s persistent memory (R1 question).
