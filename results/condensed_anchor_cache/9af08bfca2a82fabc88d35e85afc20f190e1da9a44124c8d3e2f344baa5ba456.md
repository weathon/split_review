## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MATT, a defense against query-based black-box attacks using random patch-wise purification with an ensemble of lightweight purification models that leverage local implicit functions to rebuild the natural image manifold. Theoretical analysis is provided to explain the effectiveness. Experiments on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet demonstrate improvements in classifier robustness.

### Strengths
- First work to introduce purification for black-box defense (R1).
- Makes modifications to existing purification methods (R1).
- Good organization and survey of the task (R1).
- Well written and easy to follow (R2, R3).
- Solid theoretical analysis that effectively supports claims; includes brilliant analysis of Single Deterministic Purifier and Pool of Deterministic Purifiers (Section 4.3), points out vulnerabilities of deterministic transformations, and suggests robustness increases with number of purifiers (R2, R3, R4).
- Experimental results show improvements compared to competing algorithms and are promising (R2, R3). [Note: One reviewer (R4) strongly questioned the experimental validity – see Weaknesses.]

### Weaknesses
- Motivation for the specific design (random patch-wise purification using local implicit functions) is not clearly tied to query-based attacks; the theoretical motivation in Section 4.1 focuses on general defense and could also apply to white-box attacks (R1).
- The proposed method shows limited novelty: local implicit functions have already been used for defense; modifications such as removal of positional encoding, local ensemble inference, and random patch-wise purification appear as incremental improvements on the DISCO paper rather than significant contributions; some design choices seem like workarounds (R2, R3).
- The method degrades clean accuracy; the paper does not adequately study the trade-off between robustness and accuracy. Specific numbers: CIFAR-10 accuracy is 86% (lower than adversarial training), and the reported clean accuracy on "random noise input" is 78%, significantly lower than the original paper's 93.6% (R1, R2). One reviewer (R1) remains unconvinced that the method does not hurt minimum accuracy as claimed.
- Computational overhead and efficiency claims are not sufficiently analyzed. Maintaining and applying multiple purification models introduces significant burden, especially for high-resolution images or real-time applications; training multiple purifiers adds complexity; detailed complexity analysis is missing (R2, R4).
- Experimental evaluation has limitations: baseline attacks appear weak (e.g., NES 80%, Boundary 84.8%, HopSkipJump 86.3% robust accuracy on ImageNet without defense), undermining the validity of robustness comparisons; the proposed method is not always the best compared to baselines; experiments use only WideResNet-28-10 on CIFAR-10 and ResNet-50 on ImageNet; evaluation under transfer attacks is missing; performance under white-box attacks is not reported (R4). (One reviewer (R4) strongly questioned the experimental setup, while R2 and R3 found the results promising – this disagreement indicates the experiments may not be conclusive.)
- Presentation and clarity need improvement: method details are not clear; Figures 3 and 4 lack sufficient explanation (e.g., small batch examples in Fig 3(II), meaning of "Attack during Training" in Fig 4); there are typos (CICAR-10, missing $\gamma$ in Eq(5) where it should be $\lambda$); Section 3 (Preliminaries) with equations is not used and could be replaced with more important content from the appendix (R1, R3, R4).
- The use of randomness to disrupt gradient estimation is well-known and provides limited new insight (R2).
- In Section 4.3, the statement "the robustness of the system is averaged across different purifiers" lacks explanation (R2). [Also a minor typo suggestion: "extremely low" in Section 1 may be intended as "extremely high".]
