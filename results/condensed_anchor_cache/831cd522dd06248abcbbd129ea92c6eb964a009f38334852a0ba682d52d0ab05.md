- Scores: 6, 5, 6, 8

## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes AdaFisher, a second-order optimizer that uses a diagonal block-Kronecker approximation of the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) for adaptive gradient preconditioning. Reviewers were mixed: one (score 8) praised the method’s novelty, stability, and empirical results, while others (scores 5–6) raised concerns about novelty relative to prior work, missing comparisons, computational cost, and insufficient justification of the approximation. Several specific technical questions and requests for additional experiments were raised.

### Strengths
- **Novel methodology:** The diagonal block-Kronecker approximation of the FIM, motivated by the observation that Kronecker factors are diagonally dominant, offers a new balance between computational efficiency and second-order curvature information (Reviewers 1, 3, 4).
- **Thorough experimentation:** Comprehensive empirical results on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet, and WikiText-2 across different architectures (ResNet, ViT, Transformer) show AdaFisher outperforming baselines (Adam, K-FAC, AdaHessian, etc.) (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Stability across hyperparameters:** AdaFisher exhibits strong stability across varying learning rates and batch sizes, reducing the need for extensive tuning (Reviewer 4).
- **Theoretical convergence analysis:** A rigorous analysis is provided for both convex and non-convex cases, establishing a convergence rate of \(O(\log T / \sqrt{T})\) similar to Adam-type methods (Reviewer 4).
- **Well-organized presentation:** The paper is clear and easy to follow (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons:**
  - No comparison with Adafactor, which also uses a Kronecker-factored preconditioner with diagonal factors (Reviewer 1).
  - No comparison with low-rank methods like EVA that have similar complexity (Reviewer 1).
  - No comparison with SGD in image classification tasks (SGD often generalizes better than Adam) (Reviewers 2, 3).
  - No comparison with AdamW, the most widely used optimizer; a direct comparison with AdaFisherW would be informative (Reviewer 4).
- **Novelty and similarity to prior work:** The core idea of using the Fisher information matrix for preconditioning is not novel; the work is very similar to “Kronecker-Factored Second-Order Optimizers Perform First-Order Descent on Neurons” by Frederik Benzing. The specific diagonal approximation needs more justification regarding its impact on preconditioning quality (Reviewer 2). The authors should explicitly differentiate their method from this prior work (Reviewer 2).
- **Computational cost:** The trade-off between computational overhead and performance gains is not clearly addressed. The preconditioner still requires layer inputs and gradients backpropagated through the layer, which may not be feasible for large training systems. Empirical results focus on generalization rather than clearly showing training acceleration (Reviewers 1, 2).
- **Inconsistency in formulation:** Section 2 states \(F_i = \mathcal{H}_{i-1} \otimes \mathcal{S}_i\) but the algorithm uses \(F_i = \mathcal{H}_i \otimes \mathcal{S}_i\). Why is this the case? (Reviewer 3)
- **Hyperparameter tuning and two decay parameters:**
  - Why are two parameters \(\gamma_1\) and \(\gamma_2\) introduced for the exponential moving averages of \(\mathcal{H}\) and \(\mathcal{S}\)? Would one suffice? (Reviewer 3)
  - The specific values of \(\gamma_1\) and \(\gamma_2\) used in different tasks are not reported (only the tuning range). If extensive searching is required, the optimizer may be limited (Reviewers 3, 4).
  - How many epochs were used for hyperparameter tuning (e.g., grid search in Appendix D)? More details are needed for reproducibility (Reviewer 4).
- **Convergence and generalization analysis:**
  - The theoretical analysis considers non-convex optimization, but DNNs are nonsmooth due to ReLU and max-pooling. This limitation should be discussed (Reviewer 4).
  - The experimental comparison is under the same wall-clock time; a comparison under the same number of epochs is needed, as second-order optimizers like AdaHessian may not have converged yet. Does AdaFisher still outperform when all optimizers have fully converged? (Reviewer 3)
  - The paper does not explain why the method enhances generalization significantly, especially given that second-order methods typically generalize worse than first-order methods (Reviewer 2).
- **Regret bound:** AdaFisher has a sub-optimal regret bound \(O(\log T \sqrt{T})\) compared to Shampoo’s optimal \(O(\sqrt{T})\) (Reviewer 1).
- **Reproducibility and questions from reviewers:**
  - Does the Shampoo baseline use grafting? (Reviewer 1)
  - What are the author’s thoughts on using a Kronecker sum approach (e.g., CASPR) to approximate the empirical FIM? (Reviewer 1)
  - Figure 1: The reviewer could not reproduce consistent convergence to similar local minima across multiple runs. Please discuss sensitivity to initialization or provide further insights (Reviewer 4).
  - Table 3: Which hyperparameters were used for Adam on ImageNet ResNet-50? The reported Top-1 accuracy seems surprising (Reviewer 4).
  - The related work omits recent second-order optimizers such as Sophia and INNA; these should be discussed (Reviewer 4).
