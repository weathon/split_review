## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a new class of mixture models (NPC²) that allow negative weights in the linear combination of components and then square the result to ensure non-negativity. This is cast into the probabilistic circuits framework, enabling deep extensions. Theoretical results prove exponential expressive efficiency over standard additive mixtures, and empirical results on distribution estimation tasks demonstrate gains.

### Strengths

- **Originality and simplicity**: The core idea of squaring a linear combination of functions (allowing subtractions) to get a proper density is novel, simple, and intuitive (Reviewers 1,2,3,4,5). The approach avoids difficult Markov chain Monte Carlo normalization because the partition function is tractable under the proposed circuit constraints (Reviewer 4).
- **Theoretical depth**: Extensive theoretical analysis including a proof of conditions for exact normalization, a proof of exponential expressiveness (via a restricted class with unique disjointness), and proofs of connections to related methods (Reviewers 1,4,5). The exponential separation is concretely established for a specific function class (Reviewer 3).
- **Empirical promise**: Empirical results on several real-world density estimation benchmarks show that NPC² often outperforms standard monotonic probabilistic circuits (MPCs) and other competitors given a fixed parameter budget (Reviewers 1,2,3,5).
- **Clarity and presentation**: The paper is generally well written and the figures (especially Figure 1) help illustrate the benefit of relaxing positivity of components (Reviewers 1,5). The related-work section carefully connects to prior work on probabilistic circuits and beyond (Reviewer 5).
- **Code provided**: The authors release the code for experiments (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses

- **Missing limitations and balanced discussion**: The paper oversells the empirical gains; the test-set improvements in Figure 5 are very small (if statistically significant) while the squared model introduces extra computational cost (Reviewer 1). There is no explicit discussion of the approach’s limitations, e.g., which types of distributions might be harder to model with the squaring operation compared to standard mixtures, or the restricted functional form induced by squaring (Reviewer 2). Overfitting is not mentioned despite the increased expressiveness (Reviewer 4). The motivation would be strengthened by a real-world example or a downstream task where better density estimation improves results (Reviewer 3). The paper lacks a balanced discussion of advantages *and* disadvantages of the method (Reviewer 1).
- **Insufficient empirical analysis of computational cost and scalability**: There is no plot of log-likelihood improvement versus CPU time (Reviewer 1). Neither training nor inference time scaling with dimensionality \(D\) is studied (Reviewer 2). A direct runtime comparison with other methods like Masked Autoregressive Flows is missing (Reviewer 2). The extra training cost of using more complex (subtractive) operations is not investigated empirically (Reviewer 3). The paper does not compare squared models (MPC², NPC²) against an MPC of size equal to the unrolled model (Figure 1 right); weight sharing may be a key factor—a parameter-count comparison is needed (Reviewer 5).
- **Missing error bars and experimental details**: Figure 2 lacks error bars, making significance of the results unclear (Reviewer 1). The differences reported in Figure F2(a) are very small and need explanation (Reviewer 1). The cross-comparison issue (NPC²(LT) vs MPC(BT) beats NPC²(LT) in some cases) raises the question of whether the rooted graph (RG) does the heavy lifting; more empirical analysis on RG selection is needed (Reviewer 1). The method for batching (lines 105–106) is unclear: the gradient of \(\log Z\) typically requires sampling from the model, so it is not obvious how batching avoids bias; the exact update rule should be provided (Reviewer 1). Since \(\log c(x)^2 = 2 \log c(x)\), most of the difference between maximizing MM and MM² comes from the gradient of \(\log Z\); this needs better explanation (Reviewer 1). The GPT-2 distillation experiment (in appendix) should compare against other tractable models as well (Reviewer 3).
- **Optimization and practical pitfalls**: There is no discussion of how optimization (SGD) of NPC² compares to standard MPCs—e.g., convergence behavior, stability, tendency to get stuck in local minima (Reviewer 5). The practical issue of how to find a good rooted graph is not addressed; it presumably requires architecture search, which can be an obstacle for practitioners (Reviewer 4). Sensitivity to the rooted graph choice is not explored (Reviewer 4). Sampling is mentioned only in the appendix and appears harder than for standard MPCs (Reviewer 4).
- **Presentation clarity and density**: The exposition in Sections 3 and 4 is very dense; the paper reads like a compressed journal paper (Reviewers 1,4). Section 3 (tensorized circuits) is hard to follow on first reading—Definition 1 uses ambiguous notation where \(\ell\) denotes both a layer and its output tensor, and Figure 2(b,c) blurs the boundary between nodes and edges (Reviewer 4). Some graphics are hard to read: Figure 4 is small and dense with missing x-axis labels; Figure 5 and Figure C1 have axis labels that are not centered (Reviewer 2). The “tensorized circuits” naming may be redundant—they are essentially standard computational graphs (Reviewer 4). Minor typos: “they” repeated on line 227; unnecessary “to” on line 303 (Reviewer 5).
- **Theoretical scope**: The exponential separation is established only for a restricted function class (unique disjointness); it is unclear whether it can be extended to more general distributions (Reviewer 3). A typo in Definition A.1 (Appendix A): sum nodes should use \(\mathrm{sc}(n_i)\) instead of \(\mathrm{sc}(c_i)\) (Reviewer 3). The signed logsumexp trick has been used in previous work (for computing expectations of arbitrary functions with PCs), so the claim of novelty may need qualification (Reviewer 5).
