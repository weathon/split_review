## Merged Review

### Summary
MMDT is a unified platform for comprehensive safety and trustworthiness evaluation of multimodal foundation models (MMFMs), covering safety, hallucination, fairness/bias, privacy, adversarial robustness, and out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization. It includes diverse evaluation scenarios and red teaming algorithms to generate challenging data for both text-to-image and image-to-text models. Evaluations on several MMFMs reveal vulnerabilities and areas for improvement. Reviewers praise the comprehensiveness and practical relevance but raise concerns about missing platform design details, lack of in-depth analysis on underlying mechanisms, limited model selection, potential evaluation bias favoring GPT-4, and insufficient guidance on mitigation and interpretation.

### Strengths
- **Comprehensive evaluation across multiple trustworthiness dimensions** – safety, hallucination, fairness, privacy, adversarial robustness, and OOD generalization – a unique and valuable contribution for identifying vulnerabilities in MMFMs (Reviewers 1, 2, 3).
- **Innovative use of red teaming algorithms** to generate challenging, task-specific data (e.g., object recognition, counting, spatial reasoning, attribute recognition) (Reviewer 1).
- **Evaluation of both text-to-image and image-to-text models** provides a unified assessment (Reviewer 2).
- **Practical relevance** through testing on real-world applications such as autonomous driving and healthcare (Reviewer 1).
- **Detailed appendix** with comprehensive information about MMFM trustworthiness (Reviewer 1).
- **Writing is well-structured and logically clear** (Reviewer 2).
- **Reveals specific vulnerabilities and areas for improvement**, providing actionable insights for researchers and developers (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Lacks in-depth analysis of underlying mechanisms** causing vulnerabilities; it is more an experimental report than a solid research paper. The paper does not explore why problems like hallucination and adversarial robustness manifest differently in multimodal vs. unimodal models, nor does it identify unique MMFM-specific issues beyond those seen in general LLMs (Reviewer 4; echoed by Reviewer 1’s call for explanations of model strengths/weaknesses).
- **Missing detailed description of the evaluation platform’s architecture, modularity, and how it ensures rigorous, scalable, and extensible evaluation** – essential for a trustworthiness-focused benchmark to demonstrate unique contributions beyond a one-time test set (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient detail on model selection criteria** (Appendix A.2 is brief); the set of evaluated models is limited, notably omitting classic models like Claude, raising concerns about generalizability of findings (Reviewer 2).
- **Potential evaluation bias from using GPT-4 to generate data and GPT-4o as an evaluation model** – this may inherently favor the GPT-4 series, as the generated data could reflect GPT-4’s specific strengths/weaknesses (Reviewer 2).
- **No guidance on specific mitigation techniques** for identified vulnerabilities, limiting the paper’s utility for practitioners seeking to improve trustworthiness (Reviewer 1). Also lacks sufficient guidance on interpreting the multiple trustworthiness dimensions for unfamiliar users (Reviewer 1).
- **Unexplained result in Table 1:** jailbreaking yields lower HGR than the Vanilla case despite adversarial optimization; the paper does not explain why HGR was not optimized during adversarial prompt crafting (Reviewer 1, question 1).
- **No analysis of why GPT-4v performs best at defending against harmful text output under image manipulation** (Reviewer 1, question 2).
- **Fairness criteria for “overkill fairness” unclear** – e.g., how to balance fairness goals with historical accuracy and which models are more prone (Reviewer 1, question 3).
- **OOD evaluation lacks specification of which transformations most challenge MMFMs** and which techniques (e.g., in DALL·E 3, GPT-4o) enhance resilience (Reviewer 1, question 4).
- **Privacy benchmark using CLIP embedding similarity for object-level memorization** – potential limitations or improvements, and whether fine-tuning could mitigate memorization without harming performance, are not discussed (Reviewer 1, question 5).
- **Missing discussion of blue team approaches** for defense and future directions (Reviewer 3, question 1).
- **Security threats from multimodal inputs (e.g., multimodal jailbreaks)** not examined; relevant prior work (Arondight) is not discussed (Reviewer 3, question 2).
- **Differences and connections between the proposed red team model and existing work** are not clearly stated (Reviewer 3, question 3).
- **No discussion of how the red team model adapts to dynamically evolving MMFMs** (Reviewer 3, question 4).
- **Diversity of test cases** – a key indicator of red team effectiveness – is not overviewed or discussed (Reviewer 3, question 5).
- **Fairness analysis appears superficial**, lacking discussion of metrics tailored to multimodal data and biases introduced by the data itself (Reviewer 4).
