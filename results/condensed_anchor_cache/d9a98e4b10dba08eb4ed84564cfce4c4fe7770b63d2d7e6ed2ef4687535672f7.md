## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces Proto Successor Measure (PSM), a zero-shot RL method that compresses any MDP to allow optimal policy inference for any reward function without additional environment interactions. The key theoretical contribution is showing that all valid state-action visitation distributions in an MDP form an affine set, and any policy can be represented as an affine combination of policy-independent basis functions and a bias. A practical algorithm learns these basis functions from interaction data (or offline datasets) and solves the downstream optimization via a Lagrangian dual. Experiments are conducted on grid world, maze, and FetchReach, compared to two unsupervised RL baselines. The method is technically sound but has limited evaluation.

### Strengths

- **Novel and impactful theoretical observation** that all state-action visitation distributions in an MDP form an affine set, providing a new perspective for zero-shot RL and having potential impact on RL and unsupervised RL (R1, R3, R4).
- **Mathematical reasoning is well-supported, precise, and clear**, presenting theoretical results that offer new insights (R1, R3, R4).
- **The algorithm can leverage large offline datasets** to learn representations for downstream zero-shot tasks without additional environment interactions, enabling computational efficiency (R1, R2).
- **Policy-independent basis functions are shareable across tasks**, so new policies can be generated by simply adjusting combination weights, reducing retraining and enhancing generalization (R2).
- **Interesting idea with nice visual comparisons** to baselines in a maze environment (R4).
- **Paper is generally well-written and easy to read** (R3, R4), though one reviewer noted sections 4 and 5 could benefit from a clearer high-level flow (R1).

### Weaknesses

- **Limited experimental evaluation**: Only two basic environments (grid world, maze, and FetchReach) are used, lacking complex high-dimensional environments (e.g., Meta-World, MuJoCo) with continuous state/action spaces and realistic dynamics. This limits assessment of scalability and generalization (R1, R2, R4). One reviewer notes that FB (Touati & Ollivier 2021) easily solves FetchReach and suggests adding Ms. Pacman as in prior work to demonstrate outperformance (R4). Another reviewer finds the claim of “outperforming prior baselines” insufficiently proven given the sparse comparisons (R4).
- **Missing technical and reproducibility details**: How Φ is parameterized, optimizer, learning rate, batch size, neural network architecture, and benchmark details (horizon, reward functions) are not provided (R1). No reproducibility statement (R4). Number of seeds not stated (R1). Hyperparameter tuning procedure for algorithm and baselines not described (R1). Handling of continuous state space in the robotic manipulation task (basis function continuity) is not explained (R1, R3).
- **Theoretical gaps for continuous state/action spaces**: Formal theoretical results rely on finite discrete state and action spaces (Theorem 4.1 proof assumes finite actions), contradicting the abstract’s claim of no assumptions. The extension to continuous spaces in Section 5.3 lacks justification for convergence and stability (R3). The computational cost of solving the dual problem (Equation 10) and how it scales with state/action sizes is not analyzed (R3).
- **Discrete policy codebook limitation**: Representing policies as a compact discrete codebook approximates an infinite policy space with a finite set, which may miss optimal policies requiring fine-grained or continuous adjustments, potentially leading to suboptimal results (R2).
- **Insufficient discussion of limitations and failure modes**: No discussion of when the affine set assumption might break down (e.g., stochastic transitions, partial observability) (R1), no discussion or future work section (R4), and no analysis of the dimensionality \(d\) of the affine space (what factors affect it, relation to MDP complexity) (R1).
- **Missing baselines and ablations**: No ablations (e.g., two-player vs. single-player game practical benefits) (R1, R4). No quantitative comparison of learned Q function to oracle optimal Q function (R1). No other quantitative evaluations beyond FetchReach (R4). More summary of baseline methods would help clarify novelty (R3).
- **Clarity and notational issues**:
  - Equations (1)–(3) missing a comma or \(\times\) between \(\mathcal{S}\) and \(\mathcal{A}\) (R3).
  - Lemma 4.1 proof: left bracket misplacement and missing explicit definition of matrix \(S\) (R3).
  - Missing derivation of Equation (5) in Appendix (R3).
  - Theorem 4.4: \(\Phi^{vf}\) not defined, making theorem inaccessible (R3).
  - Equation (7) uses undefined model densities and notations (R3).
  - Missing figure references in lines 257 and 279 (R1, R4).
  - Typo: “toform” should be “to form” (line 432) (R1).
  - Derivation referenced in line 267 is missing from supplementary material (R1).
- **Citation and reference issues**: Citation for world model (“Planning with Diffusion…”) is not directly related; appropriate reference would be “Dream to Control: Learning Behaviors by Latent Imagination” (R2). Many references lack venue (e.g., lines 535, 540, 543, 576, 579, 581, 591, 593, 599); double references of same papers (lines 611, 616); published conference papers given only arXiv references (R4). This, together with other omissions, gives an impression of haste (R4).
- **Minority reviewer concerns**: One reviewer (R4) is particularly critical of the paper’s preparation quality, missing evaluation, and insufficient evidence for claims. Another (R3) finds the theoretical extension to continuous spaces inadequately justified. Reviewers disagree on whether the theoretical observation is entirely novel (R4 unsure; R1 asserts first). R1 and R2 both note the method’s potential but stress the need for broader experiments.
- **Questions overlapping with weaknesses** (incorporated above): Is reward function assumed known in Section 5.3 procedure? (R3). Are Lemma 4.1 and Theorem 4.2 novel? (R3). How is the two-player game practically different from the single-player simplification? (R1). Was the same offline dataset used for all baselines? (R1).
