## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces REFUEL, a novel RLHF algorithm for multi-turn dialogues that addresses covariate shift by training on self-generated data and framing the problem as a sequence of regression tasks. The method avoids a separate critic network by estimating Q-values via a reparameterization trick with relative future rewards. It includes theoretical guarantees and empirical results on UltraInteract and AnthropicHH using Llama-3-8B-it (student) and Llama-3.1-70B-it (simulated user), with GPT-4 as judge. The 8B model fine-tuned with REFUEL outperforms the 70B model on longer turns. Reviewers 1, 2, 3 each scored 6 (confidence 4,2,2); Reviewer 4 scored 8 (confidence 3) and was notably more positive about the method’s novelty and impact.

### Strengths
- Novel and elegant algorithm: applies Q-learning ideas to multi-turn dialogue without actor-critic, addressing the covariate shift problem (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).
- Thorough theoretical analysis with performance guarantees; proves REFUEL requires weaker conditions than Natural Policy Gradient (Reviewers 1,4).
- Strong empirical results: fine-tuning Llama-3-8B-it produces a model that outperforms the larger Llama-3.1-70B-it on later dialogue turns (Reviewers 1,4).
- Computationally efficient: no need for a separate critic network (Reviewer 4).
- Well-written and clearly explained, including an intuitive overview and detailed proofs in the appendix (Reviewers 2,3).
- Reproducible benchmarks (UltraInteract, AnthropicHH) and open-source models (Llama, ArmoRM) (Reviewer 2).
- Important problem: controlling generations over multiple turns is critical for high-quality dialogue agents (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- Evaluation limited to at most 5 turns; does not test longer conversations (10–20 turns) where benefits should be larger. Unclear if error accumulation or long-range dependency issues arise (Reviewers 2,3,4).
- Performance is degraded on turns 0 and 1 compared to raw Llama-8B and most baselines; the paper provides no explanation for this drop (Reviewers 2,3).
- Missing ablation studies: no analysis of the individual contributions of relative future reward regression vs. on-policy data generation, so the driving mechanism is unclear (Reviewer 4).
- No comparison with PPO (omitted due to computational constraints), making it hard to contextualize REFUEL’s gains against a well-tuned actor-critic baseline (Reviewer 4).
- Models evaluated only on Llama 3 family (instruction-tuned versions); not tested on other pretraining schemes (e.g., supervised LM only, Gemma, Phi) (Reviewer 1).
- Evaluation datasets lack true goal-oriented (task-oriented) dialogues that require multi-step planning to achieve a specific goal, weakening the demonstration of long-term planning (Reviewer 1).
- Simulated user (Llama-3.1-70B-it) may not capture real human interaction patterns; human evaluation or comparison with human-generated dialogues would strengthen practical applicability (Reviewer 4).
- Utility beyond dialogue (e.g., multi-step reasoning, tool use) is not explored (Reviewers 2,4).
- Only GPT-4 win-rate is used as metric; additional quantitative measurements (beyond qualitative examples showing reduced repetition) would be helpful (Reviewer 2).
- Not evaluated on common single-turn tasks (e.g., QA) to check whether the method degrades performance on those (Reviewer 3).
