## Merged Review

### Summary
PointRecon is an online 3D reconstruction method from posed monocular RGB videos. It maintains a global point cloud scene representation, continuously updating point features and 3D locations as new frames arrive. The core is a ray-based 2D-3D feature matching technique used to adjust existing points and predict depth for new points. New points are added and redundancies removed via learned confidence. Evaluated on ScanNet, the method is compared against offline and online MVS approaches, achieving comparable quality.

### Strengths
- The motivation is sound: addressing monocular depth estimation limits by introducing multi-view matching to improve depth prediction and ensure view-consistent reconstruction (reviewers 3, 4).
- Using point clouds as the scene representation is scalable, memory-efficient compared to volumetric methods, and free from fixed resolution/voxel size constraints (reviewers 2, 4).
- Loosening epipolar constraints has potential to improve performance (reviewer 2).
- Extensive experiments benchmark the method against both offline and online approaches with different scene representations (reviewer 3). Global consistency across views is maintained (reviewer 4). Competitive performance in depth and mesh quality on ScanNet (reviewer 4).
- **Minority dissent (reviewer 1):** One reviewer found no obvious strengths, citing unjustified steps, weak experiments, and baselines outperforming the method on many metrics.

### Weaknesses
- **Strong dependency on accurate camera poses:** The ray-based 2D-3D matching assumes high pose precision; minor angular deviations significantly affect rays, especially for distant points. The method appears applicable only after pose estimation is complete, undermining the rationale for incremental/online processing (reviewer 1). Integration with SLAM or imperfect pose scenarios is not addressed; an experiment with noisy poses is needed (reviewer 1).
- **High latency / not truly online:** The method runs at ~1 FPS (0.6 s/frame), which is 6× slower than alternatives like SimpleRecon and VisFusion, making it unsuitable for real-time online reconstruction (reviewers 1, 2, 4). It is outperformed in speed and often in accuracy by significantly faster methods (reviewer 1, Table 1 & 2).
- **Depth updates rely on a single new image per step:** Using only one view at a time reduces robustness in homogeneous regions, limits co-visibility for matching, and fixes the baseline, which may explain less smooth reconstructions (reviewer 3). This contradicts typical multi-view methods that integrate multiple views simultaneously (reviewer 3).
- **Lacks critical ablations and comparisons:** No ablation comparing ray-based matching to point-based or traditional epipolar line matching (reviewers 2, 4). No detailed study on the relaxation of epipolar geometry constraints (reviewer 2). No experiment on different geometric metadata choices (reviewer 4). No comparison with NICER-SLAM (reviewer 1). Missing evaluation on other datasets (e.g., outdoor or multi-room) to test generalization and scalability (reviewers 1, 4). Need hardware details and memory usage comparison (reviewer 2).
- **Noisy outputs and edge artifacts:** Individual offset prediction introduces local noise (Figure 5, reviewer 2). Meshes are noisier due to lack of advanced smoothing/post-processing (reviewer 4). Gaps appear at edges between visible and non-visible regions (reviewer 2, Line 243). The final surface reconstruction via TSDF Fusion inherits its accuracy and resolution limitations (reviewer 3).
- **Unclear advantage over prior methods:** The quality is similar to SimpleRecon or VisFusion while being slower; no clear aspect where the proposed method has stronger potential is demonstrated (reviewer 4).
- **Minor issues:** The conclusion claims state-of-the-art performance, which is inaccurate (reviewer 1). The assumption of known camera poses should be stated in the abstract (reviewer 1). Paper is hard to read and would benefit from a high-level overview of initialization and iterative refinement (reviewers 2, 3). Figure 2 is too dark (reviewer 1). Missing related work on volumetric methods, ray matching, and photometric stereo (reviewer 1, references [a–i]).
- **View-dependent confidence and occlusion handling unclear:** The learned confidence does not appear to be view-dependent; occlusions may cause depth averaging between points (reviewer 4). Visibility determination and handling of aligned points along the line of sight needs clarification (reviewers 3, 4). View-independent color jittering may harm stereo matching (reviewer 3).
