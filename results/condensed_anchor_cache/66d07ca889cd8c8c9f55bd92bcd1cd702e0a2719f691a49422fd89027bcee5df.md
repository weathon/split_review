## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes pFedVMP, a personalized federated learning method that uses variational message passing (VMP) to model distributions of both model parameters and global feature centroids (via a Gaussian mixture model). Clients learn a shared base model and a local head; global aggregation updates the base and GMM distributions. Experiments on vision datasets show improved accuracy and fairness over several baselines.

### Strengths
- Promising experimental results showing better performance than the reported baselines, with a thorough numerical study including interpretation, ablation, and fairness analysis.  
- The overall approach is intuitive and well-motivated, combining ideas from personalized federated learning (global shared base + local head) with a Bayesian perspective to model both parameter and feature spaces.  
- The motivation and contribution are clearly stated.

### Weaknesses
1. **Novelty and missing comparisons** – The core idea of splitting into a global shared base and local head and using feature centroids is not new (e.g., FedRep [Collins et al., 2021]; MOON [CVPR 2021] already made similar claims about global feature centroids). The Bayesian perspective is also well-explored in federated learning (e.g., BNFed, pFedGP, pFedBayes, FedPA, FedEP, QLSD). The paper does not clearly articulate the specific advantages of the Bayesian approach over optimization-based methods (e.g., FedRep) and does not compare against many closely related Bayesian FL baselines. Missing comparisons: FedRep, MOON, PRIOR, and other works after 2023 (only FedPAC from ICLR 2023 is included). This weakens the claim of being state-of-the-art. *Reviewer 2 (R2) and Reviewer 4 (R4, higher confidence 4) particularly emphasize this limitation; R2 questions whether prior Bayesian FL methods could already solve the problem studied via convex optimization.*

2. **Presentation and derivation clarity** – Section 4 mixes conceptual optimization goals (P1–P3) with practical simplifications without a clear delineation, making the derivation hard to follow. Many quantities (e.g., local GMM parameter update equations) are not explicitly derived in the main text or appendix. Specific issues:  
   - The support of distributions (p vs. q) and how the KL-divergence is evaluated is unclear. (R1 Q1)  
   - The relation between (P2) and (P3) is ambiguous (lines 269–270). (R1 Q4)  
   - The derivation from (P3) to Equation (8) (“low-cost implementation of SG-MCMC”) is not explained. (R1 Q5)  
   - (P2) appears imprecise: the second KL term is not a normalized distribution, and some prior distributions are missing. (R1 Q6)  
   - Better labeling (e.g., (P1a,P1b)) and separation of parameter updates vs. distribution updates (e.g., splitting Section 4.3.1) would improve readability. (R1 Q2, Q3)  

3. **Lack of Bayesian benchmark baselines** – The experiments do not include standard Bayesian federated learning methods such as BNFed, pFedGP, pFedBayes, FedPA, FedEP, and QLSD. This limits the evaluation of the claimed effectiveness in a Bayesian context. (R3 Q1)

4. **Computational cost** – The algorithm involves numerous matrix inversions per communication round (especially in Equation 12), leading to a significant computational burden, particularly with high-dimensional features. An evaluation of computational cost relative to other methods and proposed solutions are needed. (R3 Q2)

5. **Hyperparameter sensitivity and justification** – The algorithm has several hyperparameters (e.g., in Equations 8 and 10) and relies on prior distributions. The paper lacks a detailed ablation study and justification for these choices, which is critical for Bayesian methods. (R2, R3 Q3)

6. **Theoretical guarantees** – No theoretical analysis (e.g., convergence guarantees, consistency) is provided for the proposed method. (R3 Q4, also implied by R2)

7. **Ambiguous terminology** – The phrase “second-order statistical information” is confusing; it could refer to covariance matrices, second-order moments, or second-order gradients/Hessians. Clarification is needed. (R4)

8. **Minor issues** – Title typo (“Massage” should be “Message”); references cut off; subscript formatting (e.g., “max” with full under-set); typo in `Z_{k,n}` (line 160 should have k instead of y_k); misuse of \cup vs. \bigcup; inconsistency between (1) and (2) on lines 42–44; missing bracket in denominator of line 240; equation numbering should be on RHS; Figure 3 missing space after “Upper:”. (R1, R3)
