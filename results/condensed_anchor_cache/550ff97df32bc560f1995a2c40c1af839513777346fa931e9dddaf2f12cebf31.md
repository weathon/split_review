## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes LDINet for single-image FMO deblatting. It introduces a Decomposition-Interpolation Block (DIB) that breaks latent feature maps into discrete time-indexed parts, decomposes each part into scalar and gradient fields, and applies an affine transformation (AffNet) to interpolate features at arbitrary time indices. The encoder-decoder framework and many losses are adopted from DeFMO. The method is evaluated on three real-world datasets (TbD, TbD-3D, etc.) and reports improvements over prior methods on two of them.

### Strengths
- The idea of using decomposition into time-indexed parts and interpolation via affine transformation in latent space is promising for recovering the appearance of FMO at arbitrary times during exposure.
- The Decomposition-Interpolation Block (DIB) is well-designed and elegantly combines ideas from DeFMO and TbD-3D.
- The decomposition of latent features into scalar and gradient fields to handle non-linearity of affine transformation is a non-trivial observation.
- Extensive evaluation on three real-world datasets with ablation studies (Table 2, Table 3, Table 4) that highlight many design choices.
- The paper provides detailed training settings and comprehensive experimental results, including analysis of parameter effects and visualizations.
- Experimental results show the proposed method outperforms existing methods on two of the three datasets.

### Weaknesses
**Novelty and Relation to Prior Work**
- The contribution is incremental: the encoder-decoder framework and most training losses are reused from DeFMO (R1, R4). The paper lacks a clear discussion of the differences from DeFMO and why the proposed method is better, beyond stating it (R1). Reviewer 3 notes that many parts and loss functions are reused, and some modifications (e.g., reconstruction loss comparing I_t with background instead of F_t as in DeFMO) are not justified.

**Motivation and Justification of Key Design Choices**
- **Scalar/gradient fields and AffNet:** Multiple reviewers question why the latent space needs to be split into scalar and gradient fields, and why an AffNet is necessary.
  - R2: The motivation for decomposing convolution into summation and directional derivatives is unclear; no ablation study justifies this design choice. The connection to affine transformations is not well-established.
  - R3: An ablation comparing to simple linear interpolation of feature maps (without AffNet) is missing. The improvement in Table 4 is marginal; it is unclear if the gain comes from the decomposition or from added model complexity.
  - R3: The relationship between P'(x), P''(x), and S(x) is confusing and potentially contradictory.
  - R4: An ablation removing the decomposition and only using affine transformation would be beneficial.
- **Affine transformation redundancy and invertibility:**
  - R2: Predicting two affine transforms (I_t→I_t' and I_t'→I_t) is redundant; one should be the inverse of the other. This introduces extra parameters and potential error accumulation without justification.
  - R2: There is no guarantee that the predicted affine transformation is invertible; only soft losses guide learning. Hard constraints are needed to avoid degenerate transformations.
- **Feature consistency loss (Eq. 9):**
  - R3: This loss penalizes differences between adjacent latent maps, encouraging all maps to be identical, which contradicts the goal of capturing motion. Unlike DeFMO’s normalized cross-correlation loss, there is no mechanism to allow movement, risking collapse to a trivial solution.
- **Architecture ablation (bi-branched):**
  - R3: The bi-branched architecture ablation is unclear: what does “separate appearance and mask estimation” mean? How does the single-branch version work? The description is vague.

**Mathematical and Presentation Issues**
- R1: The process from Eq. (3) to Eq. (5) is unclear and not well illustrated in Fig. 2.
- R2: Eq. (10) should use the Frobenius norm (not standard MSE) for computing relative distance between matrices.
- R3: Eq. (6) normalization is unclear: the sum $\sum_D \sum_{\tau} (M_{\tau} > 0)$ needs explanation; why is the first term normalized differently (without $>0$)?
- R2: All equations lack proper indexing; the domain D is not defined consistently, making it hard to understand how losses are computed.
- R1, R3: Many typos (e.g., “with backgrounds” → “with background”, “feature maps… is” → “are”, “experiments has shown” → “have shown”, etc.).
- R3: The claim “Conventional methods mostly recover a clear image at the median of the motion” is inaccurate.
- R4: The weight formula at the bottom of page 4 (unlabeled) is not motivated.

**Experimental Evaluation Concerns**
- R4: There are no PSNR/SSIM improvements on the TbD dataset; the improvement on TbD-3D is marginal. The significance is unclear without mean/std over multiple runs.
- R3, R4: Multiple runs with mean/std are needed to assess whether improvements are due to randomness. For example, in Table 3, performance drops when the number of parts increases to 20.
- R3: The reconstruction loss modification (comparing I_t with background) is not ablated to show its impact.
- R2: Qualitative results (Fig. 6) show masks similar to DeFMO; no supplementary video demonstrates temporal reconstruction.
- R3: It would be useful to see SfB results when using silhouettes from the proposed method.
- R4: The training cost compared to DeFMO is not reported.
- R4: The motivation for choosing the direction with smaller relative error rate (when determining trajectory direction) is not explained.

**Presentation Quality Disagreement**
- R1 finds the paper “poorly written” and the presentation “too poor to be fully understood.” R3, in contrast, finds it “mostly well-written and well-structured.” This disagreement is preserved.
