## Merged Review

### Summary

Four reviewers evaluated the paper, which proposes a method to transform a dense transformer into a sparse variant by training binary gates that allow tokens to skip attention or feed-forward sub-layers. Reviewers rated the paper 3, 3, 1, and 3. Three reviewers found the motivation interesting but the experiments too limited for a top conference; one reviewer (R3) was strongly negative, citing major gaps in writing, motivation, and analysis. Common weaknesses include insufficient evaluation scope, lack of baselines, and novelty concerns.

### Strengths

- Addresses the important problem of reducing the computational cost of obtaining sparse LLMs, which normally require training from scratch (R1).
- Supports fine-grained sparsity by gating at the sub-layer level (attention or feed-forward) rather than skipping entire layers (R1).
- The use of L1 and KL-divergence losses to prevent deviation from pretrained weights is interesting (R2). R2 also enjoyed reading sections 4.8, 4.9, and 4.10.
- The motivation is clear – exploiting sparsity inherent in LLMs to improve efficiency (R4). (Note: R3 contradicted, saying motivation is not clearly laid out.)
- The writing is clear (R4). (Note: R1 and R3 found gaps and typos; see Weaknesses.)
- R3 acknowledged that the paper provides limited experiments showing the layer skip algorithm can sparsify a dense transformer via training.

### Weaknesses

- **Title error**: The PDF uses the template title (R1, R3, R4). R1 and R3 also noted multiple typos and citation mistakes (e.g., line 35 “which is present by Bengio” instead of “presented by Bengio (2013)”). R3 pointed out an overly long unpunctuated sentence at lines 247–249 and a typo in line 208. R1 suggests better proofreading and correct use of \citet vs \citep.
- **Limited model scale and variety**: Experiments use only Gemma 2B, which is small and less common. Reviewers request evaluation on at least one larger model (7B–8B) and on open-source families like LLaMA (R1, R2, R3, R4). R2 found the choice of context lengths (288, 576, 1152) odd and insufficient.
- **Insufficient evaluation**: Only MT-Bench is used for assessing performance. R1 notes MT-Bench has a limited number of samples; R2 suggests adding GSM-8k or MMLU-Pro; R4 adds MMLU, GSM8K, and HumanEval. R3 notes no explanation for why SlimPajama was chosen or any dataset description. R4 also stresses that acceptable performance loss must be shown through broader benchmarks.
- **No baselines compared**: No comparison to other sparse LLMs, layer pruning techniques, or Mixture-of-Depths (MoD). R1 notes no baselines at all; R2 suggests a baseline that skips entire layers; R4 points out the novelty is limited since the only difference from MoD is skipping sublayers instead of the whole layer – a minor improvement that needs deeper justification.
- **Concerning behavior at longer contexts**: The matching coefficient (MC) drops when switching from context length 288 to 1152. R2 finds this concerning and requests a deeper investigation of skip patterns and performance at longer contexts, not just a one-line explanation. The paper should also provide a table showing actual time savings and performance delta for the best model (R2).
- **Limited novelty and shallow analysis**: R4 states the improvement over MoD is minor; the authors should analyze why sublayer skipping is chosen. R3 finds Section 4.10 (case study) not comprehensively analyzed, with shallow conclusions. R3 also notes that notations in Section 4.8 need clear definitions.
- **Reviewer disagreement**: R3 is the most negative – scoring 1 – and found the motivation and background unclear, the experiments insufficient across all dimensions, and the writing poor. This contrasts with R4’s view that motivation and writing are clear. R3’s additional criticisms (poor notations, shallow case study, missing dataset explanation) are not disputed by other reviewers.
