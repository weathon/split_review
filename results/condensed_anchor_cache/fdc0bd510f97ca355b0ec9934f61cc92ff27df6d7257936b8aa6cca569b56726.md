## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes TAVRNN, a temporal attention-enhanced variational graph recurrent neural network for modeling time-varying neural connectivity. It is applied to two datasets: in vivo calcium imaging from rat hippocampus (position decoding) and in vitro electrophysiology from DishBrain neurons playing pong. Ratings were 3, 3, 5, 1, with one reviewer very positive about the dataset contribution and ablation studies, and one reviewer extremely negative, accusing the paper of having text lifted from Yap et al. (IJCAI 2024) without citation and finding the work inscrutable and poorly organized. The remaining two reviewers gave weak reject scores, citing marginal innovation, unclear presentation, and insufficient validation.

### Strengths
- Combines variational GRNNs with temporal attention, a novel modeling approach for a challenging neuroscience problem.
- Introduces a novel dataset and benchmark from the DishBrain system to study self-organization during learning, which is an exciting application area.
- Extensive comparisons against multiple graph representation baselines are performed.
- Outperforms baselines in clustering patterns on the DishBrain dataset and demonstrates advantages in time complexity and scalability.
- Comprehensive ablation studies are included (Table 2).
- The model’s ability to link connectivity changes to performance variations (e.g., alignment of sensory/motor channels correlating with high pong performance) is a potentially interesting insight.
- One reviewer (score 5) emphasized the importance of the dynamic graph embedding perspective and the novelty of the DishBrain dataset.

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and organization are severely lacking.** The paper switches confusingly between data types (electrophysiological vs. calcium imaging). Figure 2, the model diagram, is vague and lacks sufficient caption information to be interpretable.
- **Potential plagiarism.** Reviewer 4 states that much of the methods section appears to have been directly lifted from “A Deep Probabilistic Spatiotemporal Framework for Dynamic Graph Representation Learning with Application to Brain Disorder Identification” by Yap et al., IJCAI 2024, with no reference to that work.
- **Innovation is marginal.** The core addition—temporal attention—yields only very small improvements over VGRNN in the ablation study (Table 2). The necessity of attention for the two datasets is not justified. One reviewer (score 5) also acknowledges limited predictive performance in classifying rat position.
- **Benchmarking is weak and incomplete.** On the rat dataset, TAVRNN is outperformed by GraphERT in classification accuracy (Table 1). No comparison to much simpler, standard methods (e.g., linear decoding, PCA, static VAEs) is provided. Direct evaluation on reconstruction of the thresholded adjacency matrices (the training objective) is missing. Generative quality when running the dynamics in forward mode is not assessed. Alternative dynamical modeling approaches (e.g., LFADS, CEBRA, self-supervised contrastive methods) that take raw time series as input are not compared, so the benefits of the graph representation approach remain unclear.
- **Conceptual goals and mathematical setup are hard to follow.** The graphical model (Z_t → X_t → A_t) is not explicitly written. It is unclear why learning the binary adjacency structure is preferable to learning the full correlation matrix, since thresholding can be applied after. The roles of the two latent spaces, Z_t and H_t, are obfuscated; the dynamics function operates on H_t rather than Z_t (Eq. 8a), yet both are described as “learned embedding spaces.” It is unclear which is used for downstream tasks (clustering, classification) and how they are visualized (e.g., Fig. 5). One reviewer asks: “What is the dimensionality of Z or H, and how is it chosen?”
- **Evaluation details are missing.** How train/val/test splits are done (cross-session, single- or multi-animal) is not specified. It is unclear whether downstream tasks evaluate Z_t, H_t, or both. For clustering in the DishBrain dataset, the reviewers ask whether evaluations are performed directly on Z_t or on further t-SNE reduced coordinates, and why not evaluate classification with a linear readout of the features instead.
- **Weak connection to learning and behavior.** The paper claims that TAVRNN provides insight into reorganization of neural networks during learning, but classification/clustering on place or sensorimotor encoding does not directly measure adaptive learning. No evaluation of single-node connectivity dynamics learned by TAVRNN compared to GRNN is provided.
- **Line 440-444 makes a claim without supporting figure or data.**
- **The most scientifically interesting result** (Supp. Fig. S4 showing network structure underlying variable performance) is hidden in the supplement and not discussed in the main text.
- **Limitation of Pearson correlation.** The graph construction uses zero-lag Pearson correlation, which only captures linear relationships and ignores nonlinear dependencies and time-lagged effects crucial in neural data. This may bias results and limit applicability.
- **Minority reviewer disagreement.** One reviewer (score 5) was more positive, noting the novel dataset and comprehensive ablation as strengths, but still acknowledged limited predictive performance. Another reviewer (score 1) was strongly negative, citing potential plagiarism and poor organization, and found the evaluations inscrutable. The two mid-range reviewers also raised serious concerns about clarity and validation.
