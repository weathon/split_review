## Merged Review

### Summary

Reviewers acknowledge the novelty of using multi-modal foundation models (MMFMs) to construct domain-specific languages (DSLs) for molecular generation via tree decomposition, but they raise substantial concerns about clarity, experimental rigor, missing details, and unsubstantiated claims. Confidence varies: one reviewer rated 6 (positive), one 5 (borderline), one 3 and one 1 (negative). There is strong disagreement on presentation and completeness.

### Strengths

- **Novel direction:** Casting DSL construction as a tree-decomposition process guided by an MMFM is an unusual and original research direction (R1, R3, R4). Using rendered molecular images as input to the MMFM cleverly bypasses LLM limitations with SMILES/SELFIES, and the approach works well for describing motifs and suggesting combinations (R3, R4).
- **Ablation study:** The paper includes a thorough ablation where the FM components are replaced with reasonable heuristics (R1, R2). The ablation of the MMFM module is explicitly noted (R2).
- **Promising experimental results:** The generated molecules perform as well as domain expert annotations (R4).
- **Presentation clarity (partial):** Some reviewers found the writing relatively clear, easy to follow, and the concept figure helpful (R1, R2).

### Weaknesses

- **Lack of clarity and missing definitions:** Abbreviations (DSL, FM, MMFM, FMG) are not defined in the introduction; FMG’s full name appears only in the abstract (R2, R4). The paper’s goal is unclear—molecular generation or DSL generation? (R3). The introduction omits background on molecular language, which first appears in Related Work, creating a confusing structure (R4). Methods lack explanations and examples of key terms (R4).
- **Insufficient experimental scope and baselines:** Only grammar-based and VAE methods are compared; state-of-the-art SMILES-based language models (e.g., REINVENT 2.0, structured state-space models, Özçelik et al. 2024) and recent sample-efficient methods (Guo & Schwaller 2023) are omitted (R3). Modern motif-based models like MoLeR are not considered (R1). Only small/medium datasets (e.g., Isocyanates (11), Acrylates, chain extenders) are used; no evaluation on larger benchmarks (ZINC250k, MOSES) for broader comparison (R2). The meaning of numbers in parentheses (e.g., “Isocyanates (11)”) and column headers in Tables 1 and 2 are not explained (R3). The number of generated molecules is unspecified (R3).
- **Missing comparison to SMILES-based models and sample efficiency:** Claims that SMILES/SELFIES cause generation issues ignore that SELFIES was designed to guarantee validity (Krenn et al. 2019) (R3). The statement that GNNs or LLMs require “extensive training resources” is unsubstantiated and ignores recent sample-efficient methods (R3). The paper provides no analysis of computational resources used by FMG (R3). Introduction lacks citations for many claims (R3).
- **Incomplete experimental and method details:** Which MMFM is used? (e.g., gpt-4o?) – temperature, image resolution, size, prompt specifics, and whether fine-tuning or zero-shot inference are all missing (R3, R4). Dataset descriptions, training/inference procedures, and how baselines are evaluated are unclear (R1, R4). Code is not provided to reproduce results (R2).
- **Interpretability advantage not demonstrated:** Although interpretability is claimed as a major advantage, no case study or detailed examples of generated molecules or the learned DSL are provided (R2, R3). A dedicated interpretability analysis is recommended (R2).
- **Real-world relevance questioned:** It is unclear whether simply generating/sampling molecules from a given subdomain is useful without pairing with molecular optimization (R1).
- **Presentation and consistency errors:** Analysis refers to “dimensions 2) and 3)” without definition (R3). Several sentences are incomplete or contain typos (e.g., “surprising considering,” line 426; missing reference in line 403; “u, v share an atom” unclear on line 189–191) (R1, R3). The VAE baseline setup (pretrained + fine-tuned vs. trained from scratch) is ambiguous; alternatives like steering a frozen pretrained VAE or constraining motif sets are not explored (R1).
