## Merged Review

### Summary
The four reviewers are split: two assign score 3 (confidence 4, 3) and two assign score 5 (confidence 4, 4). All acknowledge the zero-shot, visual-only nature of ZCTG and its combination of scene graphs with LLMs. However, the lower-scoring reviewers raise substantial concerns about novelty, missing baselines, and insufficient evaluation. The higher-scoring reviewers find the framework practical and easy to follow, but also identify several missing experiments and metric issues. The main points of disagreement are whether the method’s zero-shot capability compensates for its direct reuse of existing modules and whether the reported comparisons are fair and complete.

### Strengths
- **Zero-shot solution for video chaptering and title generation (R1, R2, R3, R4).** The method requires no training on annotated chapter data and relies solely on visual frames, avoiding the need for speech transcripts or human-annotated titles. R4 notes it is the first zero-shot approach for this specific task, although R4 also notes that the task itself was defined by VidChapters-7M.
- **Creative combination of existing technologies (R1).** The use of scene graphs with LLMs is seen as a novel integration.
- **Experimental results show superiority (R2).** The paper reports competitive results compared to some baselines.
- **Paper is easy to follow (R4).** The framework is clearly described.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty / direct reuse of existing modules (R1, R2, R4).** The framework simply chains together existing pretrained components (I3D, TW-FINCH, Vicuna v1.5, scene graph generator) without task-specific design. R2 specifically notes no special treatment of LLM hallucination or scene graph noise. R4 considers it more engineering than academic, and R1 calls it an incremental improvement (despite being creative). R4 also points out that the task was previously defined by VidChapters-7M, undermining the claim of being the first solution.
- **Insufficient baseline comparisons and missing metrics (R1, R3, R4).**
  - R1: Baselines from the VidChapters-7M paper (Table 3, without fine-tuning) achieve better results than ZCTG on metrics B1–B4 (Table 1).
  - R3: Table 3 (LLM-based evaluation) does not compare to baselines such as Vid2Seq (zero-shot and finetuned). Also, the paper does not report the chapter segmentation metrics (MoF, IoU) used in the VidChapters paper; having them would enable fair comparison (R4 also misses these metrics).
  - R3: No study validating that LLMs can reliably evaluate chapter segmentation outputs (timestamp accuracy), as opposed to title quality.
- **Lack of crucial ablations (R2, R4).**
  - R2: No experiments with other LLMs, video chapter generators, or spatio-temporal feature extractors. No test of directly generating frame captions by a VLM and summarizing them into chapters.
  - R2: How do different prompts affect performance?
  - R4: Suggests trying other spatio-temporal feature extractors and scene graph generators.
- **CLIPScore is not validated for this task (R3).** The VidChapters-7M paper notes that >25% of videos have speech-only chapters, where CLIPScore (image-text similarity) is likely inaccurate. The paper does not exclude such videos or analyze CLIPScore’s relevance. This undermines the main reported metric.
- **No use of speech modality (R1, R3).** R1 asks why speech (which improves performance in VidChapter7M) is not included. R3 notes that many chapters are speech-based, so relying solely on visual cues is a limitation.
- **Presentation clarity issue (R2).** Figure 1 lacks a caption and the meaning of the dotted line is unclear.
- **High computational demand (R1).** The integration of scene graphs and LLMs may limit scalability and accessibility.
- **Missing discussion of failure cases (R1, R3).** R1 asks for failure cases where the framework does not work. R3 implicitly raises this via concerns about speech-only videos.
- **No comparison to straightforward caption-based approach (R2, R3).** Both suggest comparing scene‑graph-based titles to those generated from per-frame captions from a state-of-the-art VLM (e.g., BLIP2 or other). R2 specifically asks whether such a simple pipeline would work better.
- **Not positioned relative to video captioning+grounding (R4).** R4 asks how this task differs from video captioning and grounding, and whether a strong captioning+grounding model could already perform the same segmentation and titling.
