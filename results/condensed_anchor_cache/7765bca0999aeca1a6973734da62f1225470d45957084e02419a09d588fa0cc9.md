## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces PathGen-1.6M, a dataset of 1.6 million high-quality pathology image-caption pairs constructed from TCGA Whole Slide Images using a multi-agent pipeline: an LLM-based description agent generates captions for representative patches, a revision agent refines them, and a summarization agent produces final captions. The authors train PathGen-CLIP (a pathology-specific CLIP model) and PathGen-LLaVA (a multimodal model with Vicuna) on this data, achieving improvements across nine zero-shot classification tasks, few-shot tasks, and three WSI-level tasks compared to existing pathology VLMs and general-purpose CLIP models. The dataset, code, and models are publicly released.

### Strengths
- **Originality and scalability**: A novel, scalable pipeline for generating high-quality pathology image-caption pairs from WSI-only data using a multi-agent collaboration (description, revision, summarization). This approach is not explored before in pathology.
- **Dataset size and impact**: The largest publicly available pathology patch-caption dataset (1.6M pairs), enabling training of better vision-language models.
- **Strong evaluation**: Extensive experiments on zero-shot patch classification, few-shot learning, and WSI-level tasks, with comparisons against multiple baselines (e.g., GigaPath, CONCH, PLIP, BiomedCLIP, etc.). Ablations on dataset construction and scaling (size and model capacity) are included.
- **Representative patch selection**: Use of GPT-4 prompts based on organ morphology to guide patch selection, combined with clustering (cluster count as sqrt of slide size) to ensure diversity.
- **Human validation**: Pathologist evaluation of generated captions reports 88–90% accuracy for correct/partially correct findings.
- **Public release**: Dataset, code, and models are made available, benefiting the community.
- **All reviewers (scores 8) found the work solid and well-executed**; one reviewer (score 6) acknowledged scalability and dataset contribution but raised more concerns.

### Weaknesses
- **Overstated “agent-based” claim**: The pipeline is a sequential series of model calls, not truly autonomous agents. (R1)
- **Missing literature on multi-agent architectures** (R1)
- **Methods section lacks detail** and clarity: motivations for design choices (e.g., combining prompt-based and clustering-based retrieval, threshold selection 0.88) are not explained. (R1, R2)
- **Clarity issues**: Numbers of prompts per WSI, how many prompts match each patch (report vs. attribute), and whether similarity is computed globally across all patches are unspecified. (R1)
- **Revision agent limitations**: The revise LMM primarily performs surface-level textual edits (additions, deletions, modifications) and is ineffective at correcting deeper pathological inaccuracies (e.g., misidentified tissue types, missed subtle diagnostic features). (R3) Its ability to handle correct descriptions (no-op capability) is unclear. (R1)
- **Quality and scale of initial dataset**: Unclear how the 10K initial pairs were expanded to 30K (3 captions per image?), and whether scaling this initial dataset or refining its quality would help. (R2) No human quality check on GPT-4 captions for the 30K set is provided. (R2)
- **Missing baselines**: 
  - No comparison using original pathology reports (from TCGA) directly with patch images to isolate the benefit of generated descriptions. (R4)
  - No comparison of PathGen-CLIP trained *without* the revision agent’s data or without the generated data altogether (i.e., using only the original 700K selected patches with reports). (R2, R4)
  - No fully supervised baselines to contextualize CLIP’s performance gap with supervised methods. (R1)
- **Missing SOTA comparisons**:
  - CONCH (publicly available) was not compared in zero-shot and few-shot tasks. (R2, R4)
  - H-Optimus (publicly available WSI encoder) missing from WSI tasks. (R2)
  - UNI and mSTAR (CLIP-style on TCGA) are missing. (R4)
  - Few-shot comparison should include CLIP-style models (e.g., CONCH) rather than only vision-only GigaPath. (R4)
- **Limited model generalizability**: Only CLIP architecture is evaluated; no experiments with other VLM architectures or training objectives to confirm the dataset’s broad utility. (R3)
- **Out-of-domain generalizability not validated**: All downstream tasks derive from TCGA/TCIA, raising potential data leakage and homogeneity concerns. (R3, R4)
- **Missing details on instruction-tuning data curation** (R1)
- **Expert evaluation missing criteria**: Definition of “correct” vs. “incorrect” findings and how majority voting was performed are absent. (R4)
- **Potential data leakage**: Many downstream benchmarks are from TCIA, which shares source with TCGA used for dataset construction. (R3)
- **Writing and formatting issues**: Typos (e.g., “PathGen-LLaVAdesp”), model naming inconsistencies (PathGen-LLaVA omitted from conclusion), low-contrast colors in figures, missing citation (GigaPath). (R3, R4)
- **Minor concerns**: No ablation on effectiveness of each data construction step (e.g., filtering, summarization). (R4) How essential details are preserved in step 5 is unclear. (R4)
- **Reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 6) raised multiple missing baselines and comparisons, while three reviewers (score 8) found the evaluation strong; the positive consensus is not unanimous.
