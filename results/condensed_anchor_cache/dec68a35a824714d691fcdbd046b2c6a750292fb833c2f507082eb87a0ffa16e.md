## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes pseudo \(f\)-divergences, where the generating function \(f\) is concave on \([0,a)\) and convex on \([a,\infty)\) for some \(a>0\), rather than convex everywhere. It shows that a specific choice of \(f\) yields a pseudo divergence equal to the squared norm in a Bayes Hilbert space plus an entropy term. A variational lower bound for pseudo \(f\)-divergences is derived and applied to \(f\)-GANs. Experiments on image generation report FID scores comparable to Wasserstein GAN and improved over standard \(f\)-GANs (e.g., Pearson). One reviewer (Score 8) found the presentation clear and the connection practically useful, while the other three reviewers (Score 6) expressed concerns about the experimental validation, clarity of the contribution, and the scope of the generalization.

### Strengths
- The idea of generalizing \(f\)-divergences to locally non-convex functions is interesting and a step toward a more general family of divergences.
- The connection between pseudo \(f\)-divergences and Bayes Hilbert spaces (BHS) is neat, provides useful theoretical insight, and is promising (Section 4.2). Multiple reviewers noted this connection as a highlight.
- The paper is well-motivated; the first three sections are well-written and pedagogical. One reviewer specifically praised the clarity of definitions and theorems.
- The illustration in GANs demonstrates soundness of the established results and the framework provides a flexible variational estimation tool.
- Code is provided and seems reproducible. The paper rediscovers and theoretically motivates the log-variance loss used in Richter et al.
- The generalization links BHS (which is not naturally good at sampling) with \(f\)-divergence optimization, which is more flexible for estimation.

### Weaknesses
- **One reviewer considered the weaknesses only minor (presentation improvements), but other reviewers raised significant concerns about the experimental validation, clarity of the contribution, and the scope of the generalization.**

- **Experimental evaluation is weak:** The results do not show a clear advantage of pseudo \(f\)-divergences; WGAN still outperforms BHS-GAN, and the difference from standard \(f\)-GANs is marginal (e.g., 2 FID from Pearson). The interpretation of results is overly bold given the marginal improvements. The setup does not exploit the flexibility of the framework—no synthetic examples demonstrate the benefit of local concavity. The nature of the experiment is poorly described and unclear. The paper does not study whether the BHS-GAN loss alleviates common GAN problems such as mode collapse or unstable training.

- **Missing experiments motivated by the introduction:** The paper motivates the work by noting that Wasserstein distance is unsuitable for heavy-tailed distributions, yet the experiments use standard image data (not heavy-tailed). A toy experiment where WGAN fails and the proposed method succeeds would strengthen the work. Similarly, concrete examples where the generalization significantly improves or robustifies performance (even on simple tasks) are lacking.

- **Theoretical contribution is unclear and overstated:** The generalization only considers a specific type of non-convexity (concave on \([0,a)\), convex on \([a,\infty)\)). It does not address functions with multiple intervals of convexity/concavity. The claim of “bridging the gap between \(f\)-divergences and Bayes Hilbert spaces” is questionable because the paper does not encompass true \(f\)-divergences (the case \(a=0\)); it only handles pseudo divergences. It is unclear whether the \(a=0\) case has already been linked to BHS in the literature—if so, the contribution is an extension to pseudo-divergences, not a direct bridge.

- **Limited additional insight from the formalism:** While the connection is interesting, the paper offers no further insights beyond defining that specific pseudo divergence. The entropy term in Lemma 4.5 is left without interpretation.

- **Writing and presentation issues:** Incorrect equation reference on line 201 (should refer to line 196). Missing subscript \(c_A\) for constant \(c\). Jump from Eq. 2 to Corollary 4.4 is surprising—need explanation of the relation between \(T\) and \(\tilde{T}\) (see appendix). Double equation on line 431. Fig. 2(a) images are barely distinguishable (change size). Overlapping paragraphs in Sections 1 (lines 64-72, 124-131, 156-160) could be summarized. Missing prepositions before references (e.g., “including” before a list). Fenchel conjugate definition should use \(x^\top y\), not \(xy\).

- **Unanswered questions that limit impact (each touches on missing analysis or missing experiments):**
  - How does the framework help with high-dimensional sampling for Bayes Hilbert spaces? The connection appears to yield an alternative divergence, but a sampling procedure is not clarified.
  - Can the connection be leveraged for other generative models (e.g., diffusion models) beyond GANs?
  - Can the distance between posterior and generation measure be reformulated in terms of BHS losses, leveraging the Bayes-theorem structure?
  - Is it possible to consider functions with multiple areas of non-convexity (e.g., convex on \([0,a],[b,c],[d,\infty)\) and concave on \((a,b),(c,d)\))?
