## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Invariant-Content Feature Reconstruction (IFR) for cross-domain few-shot classification, using cross-attention between original and augmented images to combine "high-level" backbone features with "fine-grained invariant-content" features. Experiments on Meta-Dataset show average accuracy gains of 1.6% and 6.5% under two settings. Reviewer 1 (score 6, confidence 4) finds the approach novel, the problem statement clear, and the experiments comprehensive. Reviewer 2 (score 3, confidence 4) and Reviewer 3 (score 3, confidence 3) agree that the presentation is clear and results are good, but they strongly criticize the limited novelty, insufficient comparisons with recent state-of-the-art (TSA, TriM), poor motivation relying only on a qualitative activation map, and unclear definitions of key terms. Reviewer 3 also questions the significance of the feature reconstruction given the tiny α value (≈1e-4) and the reliance on borrowed theory. The main disagreement is between the positive assessment of contribution by Reviewer 1 and the negative assessments by Reviewers 2 and 3.

### Strengths
- **Clear motivation and presentation**: The paper clearly articulates the challenge of cross-domain few-shot classification and why prior methods are insufficient (Reviewers 1, 2).  
- **Novel (from Reviewer 1 perspective) approach**: IFR introduces invariant-content feature reconstruction via cross-attention between original and augmented images, addressing a critical gap (Reviewer 1). However, Reviewers 2 and 3 note limited novelty—see Weaknesses.  
- **Extensive experiments on Meta-Dataset benchmark with good results**: IFR improves average accuracy by 1.6% and 6.5% under two CFC settings (Reviewers 1, 2, 3). Reviewer 1 considers this comprehensive and credible.  
- **Theoretical analysis**: The paper presents Lipschitz continuity analysis of the attention heads (Reviewer 3), though this is later criticized as borrowed and insufficient.  
- **Simple yet effective method** (Reviewers 2, 3 – noted positively despite novelty concerns).  

### Weaknesses
- **Limited novelty**: IFR only performs cross-attention between original and augmented images; transformer-based cross-attention has been widely used (Reviewers 2, 3). The single attention head and scaled dot-product attention are not novel, making the technical contribution insufficient (Reviewer 3).  
- **Borrowed and insufficient theory**: Theorem 2 is not proposed in this paper – it is borrowed from existing work (Reviewer 3). The Lipschitz continuity claim (“we can reliably leverage IFR to find good representations”) is too vague; a more detailed analysis is needed (Reviewer 3).  
- **Insufficient comparative analysis**: The baseline for the toy example in Figure 1 (URL, 2020) is outdated. Missing comparisons with recent state-of-the-art methods TSA and TriM, which achieve higher performance on the Meta-Dataset leaderboard (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 also asks for a detailed discussion of why IFR outperforms existing methods and why methods [1][2] (presumably TSA and TriM) are not compared.  
- **Unclear definitions**: It is not clearly explained what “high-level features” and “invariant-content features” denote; from Figure 2 one can only guess that the backbone output is high-level and the attention output is invariant-content (Reviewer 3). The distinction between “informativeness” and “discriminativeness” is also unclear (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 asks for a more detailed explanation of the attention module and how it specifically contributes to invariant-content feature extraction.  
- **Poor motivation and lack of credibility**: The claim that prior works cannot capture representative key features is supported only by a qualitative activation map comparison (Figure 1). This is not sufficient to point out shortcomings, and the significant performance gains in Tables 1 and 2 therefore lack credibility (Reviewer 3). Reviewer 1 raises the potential overfitting risk in few-shot scenarios and asks for mitigation strategies – this is not addressed.  
- **Redundant or unnecessary content**: Figure 4 is too simple to be included as a figure. Theorem 2 is unnecessary since it is not proposed in this paper (Reviewer 3).  
- **Questionable feature reconstruction**: The method effectively performs scaled dot-product attention; given that the α value in Equation (2) is about 1e-4, it is questionable how significant the reconstruction is (Reviewer 3 question).  
- **Missing experiments**: How well does IFR generalize to domains significantly different from those in Meta-Dataset? No experiments provided (Reviewer 1 question). Computational efficiency and scalability compared to existing methods are also not addressed (Reviewer 1 question).  
- **Citing inconsistencies**: Some citations are within parentheses, others are not (Reviewer 3).
