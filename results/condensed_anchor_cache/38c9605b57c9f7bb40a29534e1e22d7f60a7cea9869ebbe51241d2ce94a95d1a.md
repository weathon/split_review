## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces ART-FR, a face restoration method that reformulates the problem as a conditional generation task in discrete latent space using a Masked Auto-Regressive Transformer. A VQGAN visual tokenizer obtains discrete representations, then an auto-regressive model conditioned on low-quality features iteratively samples high-quality latent codes, which are decoded by a pretrained decoder. The method is inspired by language model success in discrete modeling. Reviewers broadly acknowledge the technical soundness and competitive performance, but express strong concerns about novelty, motivation clarity, experimental rigor, and presentation quality.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written and technically sound; the method is detailed (R1).
- The auto-regressive formulation for face restoration is a new approach that effectively reformulates restoration as a generative task in discrete latent space, inspired by language models (R3, R4). Reviewer 2 also acknowledges it as a new AR model for face restoration (R2).
- The method shows consistent performance improvement across diverse face restoration tasks and degradation levels, including extreme noise, with strong capability in identity preservation and perceptual metrics such as LPIPS and FID (R3, R4). It exhibits competitive scores and outperforms several state-of-the-art methods (R2, R4).
- The comparison with existing methods is comprehensive, with additional visual results in the supplementary file (R1).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and contribution**: The method is seen by some reviewers as a direct improvement to the transformer module in CodeFormer with limited methodological contribution (R2). Another reviewer finds the method not innovative enough and the visual effect unsatisfactory (e.g., Figure 4 comparisons are not obvious) (R1). Reviewer 4, however, views the approach as unique and effective. The authors are asked to clearly differentiate from the CVPR2023 paper “Not All Image Regions Matter: Masked Vector Quantization for Autoregressive Image Generation” (R2).
- **Motivation unclear**: Multiple reviewers question why an auto-regressive model (good at causal generation) benefits face restoration, as the mapping problem is more related to transformer frameworks (R3). The claimed “struggle to accurately capture mapping” between LQ and HQ images is not measured, and the mechanism of domain gap influence on latent representation is not stated (R2). Reviewer 4 also asks for clarification on how the auto-regressive manner benefits SR and whether it can generate realistic facial images regardless of LR degradation severity (R4). Reviewer 3 suggests a sequence-to-sequence method might also be valid (R3).
- **Insufficient and unconvincing experiments**: The ablations are not rigorous: they lack numerical controls for each module (R1), are not comprehensive (missing comparison with model taking Ground Truth tokens as inputs) (R2), and lack analysis of hyperparameters like inference step \(T\) and mask prediction order (R3, R4). The main quantitative comparison in Table 1 is not convincing (R3). Qualitative results in Figure 5 are comparable with VQFG (R3). The comparison with DnCNN is unreasonable because DnCNN has lightweight parameters and is not designed for face restoration; alternative comparisons should be used to show robustness (R4). The comparison methods only go up to 2022; the paper should include RestoreFormer (2023), DAEFR (2024), DifFace (2024) or more recent works (R2). Improvements are not consistent across all metrics, and selection bias is not stated (R2). The use of LPIPS in realness metrics is not reasonable, and some listed methods show abnormal results (R2).
- **Presentation and layout issues**: The writing structure and context are too similar to CodeFormer and poorly organized; Figure 3 is confusing and the training pipeline is unclear (R2). Layout problems include missing units for PSNR and misaligned fonts in Tables 2 and 3 (R1). There are typos (e.g., reversed quotation marks in line 434) (R3).
- **Computational overhead and inference speed**: The auto-regressive process may incur significant computational overhead during inference, limiting deployment on resource-limited devices (R4). Inference speed is not discussed compared to non-auto-regressive methods (R3).
- **Robustness and overfitting**: The paper does not fully address potential overfitting or instability when training degradation does not match real-world data (R4). Failure cases are only shown for occlusion by hand or non-face regions; it is unclear whether the auto-regressive model can generate realistic facial images under light or severe degradation from the LR image alone (R4).
