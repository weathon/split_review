## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper analyzes the diversity of zero-shot classification logits across different CLIP-trained vision backbones (ViTs and ResNets) pre-trained on the same dataset (WIT-400M). It finds that backbones exhibit different error patterns and that per-sample oracle selection could improve accuracy by over 40 percentage points. Based on this, the authors propose Neural Logit Controller (NLC), a learnable ensemble module that uses a one-layer MLP to predict per-backbone temperature values from concatenated features, trained with cross-entropy using as few as one labeled example per class. NLC is evaluated on a large collection of classification datasets, achieving up to 39.1% improvement over the best single backbone (average 9.1%) and outperforming other ensemble methods. The method can also be combined with Tip-Adapter.

Reviewers agree the paper provides a systematic investigation of CLIP backbone diversity and a simple, effective ensemble method. One reviewer (score 8) was highly positive, praising the thorough analysis and strong results. Other reviewers (scores 5–6) raised concerns about novelty, practical overhead, incomplete comparisons, and potential bias from the supervision signal.

### Strengths
- **In-depth analysis of CLIP backbone diversity**: The paper systematically studies logit diversity, complementarity, robustness, and oracle performance across different CLIP models (ViT-S/B/L, R-50/101/50x4, etc.) on many datasets. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Interesting observation**: Smaller models can correctly predict samples that larger models get wrong, highlighting the need for careful model selection. (R4)
- **Simple and data-efficient method**: NLC uses a learned temperature scaling conditioned on the input, trained with only one labeled example per class, making it practical for few-shot settings. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- **Strong empirical results**: Up to 39.1% improvement over the best single backbone (average 9.1%), and outperforms other ensemble methods (e.g., Super Learner). Results are consistent across many datasets. (R1, R3)
- **Comprehensive evaluation**: Large collection of datasets; ablation studies; combination with Tip-Adapter; analysis of inference FLOPs and cascade efficiency. (R2, R3)
- **Clear writing**: Well-structured and easy to follow. (R1, R2, R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Concerns about generalizability and bias from supervision**:
  - The NLC training requires labeled examples per class for each target dataset, which may harm zero-shot generalization to unseen classes, concepts, or compositions (e.g., ARO, SugarCrepe). (R1 Q1.1–1.3)
  - The large performance gain in Figure 7 (zero-shot setting) may be partly due to the injection of supervised signal, making the comparison with regular ensembles biased. This is supported by the drop from 9.1% (Figure 8) to 1.6% (Figure 9) when linear heads are trained—suggesting the supervision signal, not architecture diversity, drives much of the gain. (R3 W1, Q1)
  - No standard deviation reported for few-shot accuracy when using different subsets of training samples; robustness of the method to different support sets is unclear. (R4 Q1)
- **Novelty concerns**:
  - The core finding that more diverse models enable better ensembles is not surprising, especially given prior work showing similar diversity in supervised models (e.g., [1, 2, 3] in reviews). The claim of non-obviousness (line 200) is overstated. (R1, R4)
  - Ensemble on logits with learned temperature is closely related to Super Learner; the main addition is image conditioning, but improvement over SL is not large. (R1)
  - Late fusion of logits is not a novel approach; the method still relies on output of each individual model. (R2)
- **Incomplete comparisons**:
  - Few-shot experiments (Section 4.3, Figure 10, Table 1) compare NLC only with individual backbones or Tip-Adapter per backbone, but not with other ensemble methods combined with Tip-Adapter. Any ensemble method could be applied in that setting. (R4)
  - MoE is missing from Figure 8; linear heads in Figure 9 may serve as a MoE baseline but are not discussed. (R3 Q2)
  - Cascade combination lacks training details and comparison with the original cascade method. (R4)
- **Practical limitations**:
  - Inference time is 5× to 34× that of a single backbone; GPU memory and inference time compared to other ensemble methods (SL, MoE) are not reported. The high cost raises doubts about practical applicability, especially given the modest gains over SL. (R1 Q3.1–3.2, R2, R4)
  - The pipeline requires post-learning (training the ensemble module) and loading all backbones, making it less straightforward than simply using one model. (R2)
  - No improvement observed when increasing number of training shots; more analysis is needed. (R2)
- **Limited scope of evaluation**:
  - Only classification tasks are studied; no retrieval or other vision-language tasks, even though CLIP is a general model. (R2, R4)
  - The method adds inference overhead without a corresponding reduction; fusing models at training stage (as in prior work [1]) could avoid this. (R4)
- **Minor issues**:
  - Line 134: “train a pair of vision and text encoders φ_l and φ_v” appears to swap language and vision labels. (R3)
  - Figure 4: dashed lines are unclear; their setup should be described. (R3)
  - L815: OpenCLIP is developed by LAION, not used for the main models (which are OpenAI’s on WIT-400M). (R1)
  - Clarification: “one-layer MLP” – does this mean exactly one hidden layer? (R3 Q3)
