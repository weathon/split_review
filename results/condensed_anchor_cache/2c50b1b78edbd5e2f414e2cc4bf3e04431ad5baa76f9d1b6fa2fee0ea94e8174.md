## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents AddressVLM, a model for fine-grained street-level address localization within cities using Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). It proposes a two-stage training protocol: cross-view alignment tuning (integrating perspective-invariant satellite images via a grafting mechanism and automatic alignment label generation) and address localization tuning. The authors construct two new street-view VQA datasets (Pitts-VQA and SF-Base-VQA) from existing address localization datasets. AddressVLM outperforms baseline LVLMs and GeoReasoner by over 9% and 12% average accuracy on these datasets, respectively. The model uses only 4B parameters, suggesting feasibility for deployment. One reviewer (score 8) found the paper very well-written with clear figures and a thorough ablation study, while the other three reviewers (scores 5) raised several concerns about experimental scope, replication, theoretical depth, and missing comparisons.

### Strengths
- **Clear motivation and problem framing:** The work addresses a clear gap – while LVLMs work at city/country level, they struggle with precise street-level address localization. Figure 1 effectively puts existing methods side-by-side with AddressVLM (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
- **Effectiveness of cross-view alignment tuning:** The proposed method of aligning sparsely collected street-view images with globally consistent satellite images enhances the LVLM's global understanding of urban street distributions, addressing limitations that cannot be solved by address localization tuning alone. The image grafting mechanism and automatic label generation are novel (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Thorough ablation study:** The ablation reports results on different model variants during each training stage (Reviewer 2).
- **Practical efficiency:** Only 4B parameters, highlighting feasibility for future device deployment and updates (Reviewer 3).
- **New datasets for the community:** Two VQA datasets (Pitts-VQA and SF-Base-VQA) adapted from existing address localization datasets are introduced; one reviewer hopes they (and trained models/weights) will be made public (Reviewers 1, 4).
- **Paper clarity:** Well-written, clear figures, straightforward presentation (Reviewers 2, 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Error amplification in label generation:** The pre-trained LVLM used to generate textual labels for cross-view alignment may introduce errors or biases that become training data and could be amplified during the second address localization tuning stage (Reviewer 1).
- **Absolute performance still below specialized models:** Despite improvements, absolute accuracy remains below specialized discriminative models like AddressCLIP; on the A_ds indicator the proposed method does not surpass AddressCLIP (Reviewer 1).
- **Limited geographical evaluation:** Evaluated only on US cities (Pittsburgh and San Francisco); does not verify applicability and scalability to diverse urban landscapes, architectural styles, or street layouts worldwide. No analysis across different urban types (residential, commercial, industrial) (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Lack of comparisons with relevant methods:** Missing comparisons with UrbanCLIP, UrbanVLP, and other traditional geo-localization methods like Pigeon and GeoCLIP (Reviewer 4). Also missing evaluation on other geo-localization benchmarks adapted for VQA (e.g., OpenStreetView-5M, Geoguessr data) to show how the method affects coarse-level geo-localization or other related tasks (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient ablation on key design choices:** The optimal overlap ratio δ=0.5 is concluded without extensive ablation on values >0.5; quantitative experiments on the three grafting methods are missing (Reviewer 4). The ablation table (Table 3) is confusing and needs redesign to better demonstrate the effectiveness of each module (Reviewer 1).
- **Lack of quantitative evidence for first-stage impact:** No quantitative experiments to demonstrate whether performing the first stage (cross-view alignment tuning) significantly impacts final performance (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing theoretical analysis and mathematical derivation:** The paper lacks explanation of how the cross-view alignment loss is formulated, how gradients propagate during training, and the impact of geometric distortions or occlusions in street-view images on the alignment process (Reviewer 3).
- **Replication of GeoReasoner not justified:** The experimental results lack evidence that the replicated GeoReasoner's performance at city/country level is comparable to the original paper, making it difficult to assess the replication's reasonableness (Reviewer 4).
- **Limited backbone study:** Only CLIP and Phi-3.1-mini used; no study with alternatives like SigLIP (better on VQA) or DINOv2 (better at object localization) to establish a stronger case for the chosen backbones (Reviewer 2).
- **Figure and notation clarity issues:** In Figure 3(b,c), the colors and lengths are not introduced (Reviewer 1). The meaning of indicators A_ds and A_sd (line 364) is unclear; do they represent the same? (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing details on dataset construction and formatting:** No visual or tabular comparison highlighting differences of the new datasets relative to existing ones (Reviewer 1). It is unclear what datasets were used in each stage, whether any additional datasets were included, and whether the formatted VQA data in the second stage impacts the model's performance on other tasks or dialogue capabilities (Reviewer 4).
- **Concerns about model output and question flexibility:** It is unclear whether the final LVLM can output only brief district names or can also provide reasoning/analyses; whether it returns accurate locations for free-form (non-template) questions (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear design choices:** Why is CutMix-style grafting used instead of other mixup augmentation methods? Why is the street-view image grafted onto the upper right corner of the satellite view? Would other positions work? (Reviewers 3, 4). The difference between Visual Place Recognition and Cross-view Geo-localization is not briefly explained (Reviewer 3). The paper does not indicate whether predecessors have done work on a question-answering-based approach (Reviewer 3).
- **Unfreezing vision encoder inconsistency:** In Section 4.3, unfreezing the Vision Encoder (VE) during the second stage generally improves performance (comparing CD vs. (E, AddressVLM)), but the improvement is not clear when comparing AB; the reason is unexplained (Reviewer 4).
- **Lack of discussion on limitations and edge cases:** No analysis of failure patterns (e.g., bias toward one street at intersections, low-light conditions, occlusions) or comparison with existing model failure patterns (Reviewer 2). The use of an LVLM (e.g., GPT-4) for automatic alignment label generation is not confirmed (Reviewer 4).
