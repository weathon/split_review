- Scores: 10, 6, 6

## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper generalizes Graph Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (GNODE) to a stochastic counterpart, Latent Graph Neural Stochastic Differential Equations (LGNSDE), by embedding Brownian motion to quantify uncertainty. The model uses an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck prior and a posterior with drift parameterized by a GCN, trained via variational inference. Two theoretical results are provided (bounds on output variance and on solutions under node feature perturbations). Experiments on five standard graph datasets evaluate OOD detection, noise robustness, and active learning. The three reviewers agree the idea is interesting and the approach potentially promising, but they differ strongly in their assessment of novelty, evaluation rigor, and experimental thoroughness (scores: 10, 6, 6).

### Strengths
- The idea of extending GNODE to its stochastic counterpart via SDEs is sound, innovative, and of high utility to the community (Reviewer 1).
- The mathematical framework is simply elegant and the model description is clear, concise, and intuitive (Reviewers 1, 3).
- Two theoretical results are important and original: a bound on model output variance (Proposition 1) and a bound on solutions under node feature perturbations (Reviewers 1, 3). The robustness of the model is clearly demonstrated by theoretical and experimental results (Reviewer 1).
- The experiments empirically show promising uncertainty quantification performance, including OOD detection, test noise perturbation, and active learning (Reviewers 2, 3). Post rebuttal, Reviewer 2 found the experimental evaluation good.
- The framework can be of high utility to the community (Reviewer 1).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with SOTA methods**: The paper lacks comparisons with strong related works, especially those using SDEs (e.g., GNSD, Lin et al., 2024) and other high-performing methods (e.g., GPN, Stadler et al., 2021; energy-based methods) (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 3 specifically notes the absence of comparisons with GNSD and with specialized OOD detection methods like GNNSafe (Wu et al., 2023). The few baselines used do not follow a stochastic approach, limiting fair comparison (Reviewer 3).
- **Insufficient differentiation from Lin et al. (2024)**: The method shows significant similarities to Lin et al. (2024), which also proposes an SDE-based GNN. The authors acknowledge this with only one sentence in related work; a detailed mathematical comparison and empirical study (even on a synthetic dataset) are needed to highlight unique merits (Reviewer 2). The method is not compared against Lin et al. at all (Reviewer 2).
- **Limited novelty in model definition**: While the application to graphs is creative, the model itself is very similar to Calvo-Ordoñez et al. (2024) and Xu et al. (2022), who leverage SDEs in a similar way but not on graphs. This reduces the contribution (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear interpretation of uncertainty from theory**: It is not immediately clear how the bounded output variance in Proposition 1 translates to effectively quantified uncertainty (Reviewer 2).
- **Dependence on integration methods**: The results may be heavily dependent on the integration method; no study akin to that in GRAND (Chamberlain et al., 2021) is provided (Reviewer 1).
- **Accuracy and cost concerns**: The accuracy is not as performant as many cheaper variants (Reviewer 1). Speed and memory benchmarks are missing; the model is expected to be expensive (Reviewer 1). Walltime comparison with other methods is needed (Reviewer 3).
- **Lack of experimental details and reproducibility issues**: The experimental section lacks clarity on number of epochs, early stopping criteria, and specific data splits (Reviewer 3). The hyperparameter selection process is vague: “highest validation accuracy” metric is not defined, grid search setup is not detailed, and uniform hyperparameters were used across all models—potentially leading to suboptimal performance compared to per-model tuning (Reviewer 3).
- **Choice of constant drift/diffusion in OU prior not explored**: The implications of using a constant drift and diffusion function on prior expressiveness and posterior distribution are not discussed; no ablation study on alternative drift/diffusion functions is performed (Reviewer 3). Could a non-constant drift be allowed? (Reviewer 3 question)
- **Theoretical claim about perturbation resistance not tested**: The paper claims resistance to small perturbations in graph structure, but this is not experimentally validated (Reviewer 3). Even though the feature perturbation bound is shown, the structural perturbation claim is untested.
- **Missing experiments**: Varying GNN backbones and the impact of graph rewiring are not tested (Reviewer 1 question). An ablation study on the effect of hyperparameters (e.g., constant drift/diffusion choices) on training and convergence is missing (Reviewer 3 question). Practical limitations of the method are not discussed (Reviewer 3 question).
- **Minor writing issues**: In the introduction, “Before the widespread of neural networks” is missing a word (e.g., “success”). In Section 6, “Hence, constructing a different method.” is an incomplete sentence (Reviewer 3).
