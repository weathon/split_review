## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes DP-ZeRO, a differentially private version of the ZeRO optimizer for distributed training of large models (e.g., GPT-100B, ViT-10B). It aims to match the communication and computation efficiency of standard ZeRO, enable mixed-precision DP training, and scale to models with hundreds of billions of parameters. The authors evaluate throughput under various settings and show minimal overhead compared to non-DP ZeRO, and they promise to open-source the codebase. Reviewers acknowledge the importance of the problem and the timeliness of enabling private training of very large models, but raise significant concerns about clarity, novelty, experimental choices, and the strength of certain claims. There is notable disagreement: one reviewer (score 1) finds the algorithm description insufficient and the practical motivation weak, while two other reviewers (score 5) consider the contributions solid and the method promising.

### Strengths
- Tackles the important problem of private training of large models (R1).
- Extends DP fine-tuning to very large models (GPT-100B, ViT-10B) for the first time, which is timely given the rapid growth of model sizes (R2, R3).
- Empirical evaluation shows computational efficiency comparable to non-DP ZeRO across many architectures and high scalability with the number of GPUs (R2).
- Provides analysis of time complexity and practical solutions for loss scaling and integration with multiple clipping methods (R3).
- Promises to release the codebase as the first DP distributed learning library (R3).
- Addresses the challenging distributed learning setting where model partitioning is necessary; focuses on model parallelism via ZeRO rather than pipeline parallelism, avoiding the pipeline bubble inefficiency (R4).

### Weaknesses
**Novelty and Contribution Clarity**  
- The algorithm description is unclear: it is not specified how the challenge of per-sample gradient norms in the backpropagation step is overcome, beyond stating that it has a small cost (R1).  
- The claimed contribution lacks novelty – incorporating the Gaussian mechanism into ZeRO (with mixed precision) appears similar to prior work that incorporated DP into pipeline parallelism, e.g., He et al. (2022) (R4).  
- The claim that DP-ZeRO enables DP deep learning with more than 1B trainable parameters for the first time is too strong. Ghost Clipping (Li et al., 2022) can train all parameters of GPT-2-large on a smaller GPU, and Beltran et al. (2024) train a ResNet of almost 1B parameters with Opacus on 40 GB VRAM; line 072 also mentions that 2B is possible without the contribution. The claim should be rephrased as “far more than 1B” (R3).  
- Figure 1 suggests no other works have achieved such large trainable parameters, but appropriate citations (e.g., Beltran et al., 2024 for ViT-Huge) are missing; the figure is never referenced in the text (R3).

**Clarity and Presentation**  
- A summary of the full pipeline would greatly improve readability; currently the components are spread across the paper, making it difficult to assess privacy properties and the overall procedure (R2).  
- The ZeRO optimizer is not covered in a dedicated section, making the paper hard to follow as a stand-alone work (R4).  
- The threat model is not mentioned: what quantity is private, why, and from whom are not clarified (R4).  
- The discussion of forward/backward propagation in Section 3.1 is confusing; its purpose is not clear and it does not appear to be novel analysis (R2).  
- Figure captions (especially Figures 4 and 6) lack comprehensive information; Figures 2, 6, and 8 use a compressed format that impairs understanding; subfigures would be helpful (R3).  
- The discussion about super-linearity forces the reader to frequently consult Rajbhandari et al. (2020) (R4).  
- The usage of $\Psi$ is confusing; the unit (bytes) should be added, and the claim that total $\Psi_{\text{model}} = 16$ with three components is unclear (R3).  
- Minor typos: “states. and” on page 4 / line 192 (R2, R3, R4); misplaced period and “and”.

**Experimental Design and Missing Baselines**  
- For large models that require model parallelism, per-core batch sizes are already very small (e.g., batch size 1) and/or PEFT is used, which makes the additional cost of per-example gradients negligible; the larger practical challenge of private training – very large batch sizes – is not mentioned (R1).  
- Missing comparison to existing distributed DP baselines: throughput against DataP (data parallelism) and PipeP (pipeline parallelism) should be shown. Remark 4.2 states that the usefulness can be demonstrated by comparing ZeRO to PipeP, but this comparison is not provided (R2).  
- In experiments (Figures 4, 6, 7), the GPUs’ VRAM is not fully utilized; maximum batch size should be used to improve speed, as noted by Beltran et al. (2024). Figure 8 confirms a gap between maximum B and fixed B=2 (R3, Q1).  
- For PEFT, only 25% of GPU memory is used in Figure 6; more memory usage would likely yield higher speedups (R3, Q7).  
- How does the scaling of DP-ZeRO compare to naively scaling DP with DDP (e.g., Beltran et al., 2024, Figure 7) for smaller models? Is DP-ZeRO beneficial only for very large models? (R3, Q3).  
- Recent work (De et al., 2022; Räisä et al., 2024) shows that large logical batch sizes are beneficial under DP; how does this influence the relative cost of DP in the method? (R3, Q6).  
- The provided anonymous git repository is unstructured and it is unclear how the code relates to fastDP or if it is integrated (R3, Q2).

**Technical and Formal Issues**  
- In the memory cost derivation, it is not clear where $16\Psi$ for DataP comes from (R2).  
- In line 197, $2\Psi_{\text{model}}$ is said to be fp16 parameters – does this mean $2\Psi_{\text{model}}$ bytes of memory? (R2).  
- At the end of page 5, the clipping bound does not appear in the noise scaling; is it assumed that the maximum gradient norm is 1? (R2).  
- Equation (3) appears to have the numerator and denominator reversed (R2, R4).  
- Line 308 (eq. 3) same reversal noted (R2).  
- In Section 3.2.3, the claim that local computation can accelerate by 50% (Table 2) is not clearly justified: treating training_params as almost zero, the computation for PEFT is $O(BT^2) + 4BT \Phi_{\text{model}}$, which is more than half of full training $O(BT^2) + 6.666BT \Phi_{\text{model}}$ (R3).  
- Line 155 states “With $\sigma_{DP} = 0$ leading to $\epsilon = 0$ (not private)” – this should emphasize that it is not private only in terms of noise, but clipping still differs from non-DP (R3).  
- The sentence “These techniques improve the relative speed of DP-ZeRO but worsens the absolute speed” is unclear and needs rephrasing (R3).  
- The phrase “DDP cannot train models that exceed the capacity of one GPU” (line 68) should be clarified to refer to cases where the model weights do not fit (R3).  
- “Post-processing” in line 128 is incorrect; post-processing in DP usually refers to a property that cannot be reversed (R3).  
- “$2B model training” in line 72-73 is ambiguous; “B” is not defined (R4).  
- Line 222 mentions “bf16” without defining “bf” (R4).  

**Minor Issues**  
- Line 192: “optimizer states. and the half-precision…” – typo and missing clarity on “fp” (R4).  
- Line 456 implies SGD leads to worse accuracy than Adam universally, which is not true (R3).  
- Figure 1 is not referenced in the text (R4).
