## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes KrwEmd, an algorithm for hand abstraction in poker that uses a novel k-recall winrate feature and Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) to cluster signal infosets, addressing excessive abstraction from imperfect recall. Reviewers’ assessments diverge: one finds the method novel and experimentally solid (score 6), while the other two raise serious concerns about clarity, reproducibility, and overlap with an unreviewed arXiv paper (scores 3 each). All agree that presentation and connection to prior work need improvement.

### Strengths
- Revisits and advances early-2000s research topics on hand abstraction in poker (R1).
- Proposes a novel infoset feature, the k-recall winrate, which integrates historical information with future signals to distinguish infosets (R2, R3).
- Combines this feature with Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) to create a practical clustering algorithm (R2, R3).
- Solid experimental validation: tested on variants of Hold’em and compared against state-of-the-art methods such as POI and other imperfect recall algorithms (R1, R3); results consistently show performance improvements at reasonable computational cost (R3).
- Algorithm details are provided in the appendix (R1).

### Weaknesses
- Major overlap with the unpublished Fu et al. (2024) arXiv paper in ideas, formulation, description, and experimental setup; the soundness of the approach is hard to evaluate because the reference is not yet peer-reviewed (R1).
- Prohibitively difficult to read: Section 2.1 presents a wall of notation and definitions that makes it impossible to track (R2). Notation should be introduced gradually, e.g., first covering game notation then signals separately (R2).
- The relationship between Section 4 and Section 5 is not explained (R3). The KrwEmd algorithm itself is not clearly introduced; a step-by-step breakdown with examples or diagrams is missing, and core algorithmic details are relegated to the appendix, diminishing visibility (R3).
- Experimental details are insufficient: the paper does not specify the number of games per training iteration, the meaning of a “training iteration,” the number of seeds, or whether significance testing was performed. Without these, the results do not fully support the claim of outperforming other algorithms (R2).
- Questions that indicate missing experiments or discussion:
  - Why is KrwEmd described as the “first practical algorithm” in this context? (R1)
  - What is the relationship to Fu et al. (2024)? Is the current work significantly enhanced by that arXiv paper? (R1)
  - The algorithm is tested only on Numeral211 Hold’em; why was a broader set of games not included, and what adaptations would be required for other domains (e.g., other game types or ML applications)? (R1, R3)
  - The k-recall feature is manually tuned; is an adaptive or automated hyperparameter selection mechanism possible? (R3)
  - The trade-off between adding more history and increasing complexity is not discussed; how is k chosen, and how does that choice affect performance across games? (R3)
  - In games with deeper decision trees, does too much historical information overcomplicate late-stage abstraction? (R3)
- Reviewer confidence is low (R2: 2, R3: 2; R1: 3) due to readability issues and missing evaluation details, making it difficult to judge the soundness of the algorithm and the robustness of the experimental claims (R2, R3).
