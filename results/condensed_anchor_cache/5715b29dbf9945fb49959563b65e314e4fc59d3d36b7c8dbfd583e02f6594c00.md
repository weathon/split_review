## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper analyzes limitations in SNN-based Vision Transformers, identifying a mismatch between vanilla self-attention and spatio-temporal spike trains that leads to degraded spatial relevance and limited temporal interactions. To address these issues, the authors propose a biologically inspired Saccadic Spike Self-Attention (SSSA) mechanism that uses a distribution-based method for Q-K correlation and a saccadic temporal interaction module. Building on SSSA, they develop SNN-ViT, which achieves state-of-the-art performance with linear computational complexity, highlighting its potential for energy-critical edge vision applications.

### Strengths

1. **Performance and efficiency**: SNN-ViT outperforms previous SNN-based methods on various vision tasks (e.g., ImageNet classification, object detection, segmentation) while achieving linear computational complexity (O(D) for SSSA-V2), enhancing viability for power-critical applications.  
2. **Clear analysis of correlation degradation**: The paper provides a thorough theoretical and experimental analysis of why vanilla self-attention fails with binary spike trains, offering a plausible explanation for the performance gap between SNN-based and ANN-based ViTs.  
3. **Biological inspiration**: The approach is well-motivated by biological saccadic attention mechanisms, a strong point given the effectiveness of biological visual systems.  
4. **Temporal interaction**: The saccadic interaction module explicitly addresses the underexplored benefit of using SNN temporal structure for static image processing, enhancing whole scene understanding.  
5. **Thorough presentation**: The paper is well-written with a detailed appendix that explains the design and operational logic of all modules; the method section is specifically tailored to address the issues raised.  
6. **Existing literature understanding**: The authors demonstrate a deep understanding of common shortcomings in current SNN+Transformer work and summarize them clearly.

### Weaknesses

1. **Insufficient theoretical derivations and clarity**:  
   - The proof of equivalence for the parallel training and serial inference formulation (Eq. 8) is missing; the claimed equivalence is questionable and requires detailed derivation.  
   - Equations are incomplete in places (e.g., Eq. 7).  
   - Symbols are ambiguous: in Eq. 1 the QK subscripts are unclear; in Eq. 8 the bolded \(V_{th}\) vs. non-bolded version is not explained; the relationship between membrane potential \(H[t]\) and \(H[t-1]\) and the dynamic adjustment of \(V_{th}\) during inference are not clarified in the main text.  
   - The paper states that asynchronous inference does not require dependence on historical membrane potentials, but the update mechanism is not explained, which is crucial for understanding the complexity reduction.  
   - Figure 1 lacks axis labels and units; the simulation-reality gap and whether real datasets would yield similar distributions are not discussed.  

2. **Missing ablation studies and experimental validation**:  
   - No ablation on the internal components of SSSA: the effects of SSSA-V1 vs. V2, the choice of saccadic neuron vs. standard LIF neuron, and the patch subset selection mechanism in the saccadic interaction module are not analyzed.  
   - The rationale for selecting a subset of QK matrices in the patch design is not justified; it is unclear whether using all values would improve performance or how this design generalizes across datasets/tasks.  
   - Results are not reported with multiple trials or error bars, reducing reliability.  
   - Only the SSSA and GL-SPS modules are ablated; more comprehensive ablation is needed to support the effectiveness of all new methods.  

3. **Incomplete comparisons and overlooked related work**:  
   - The paper does not include performance results of SOTA ANN-based ViTs in Tables 1–3, making it difficult to assess the true performance gap between SNN-based and ANN-based models.  
   - The Saccadic Neuron exhibits high similarity to the Parallel Spiking Neuron (PSN) [Fang et al., 2024], yet the paper does not discuss the relationship, differences, or why PSN is not used directly.  
   - Other closely related methods are not compared or discussed; the paper lacks a thorough analysis of how SSSA relates to existing SNN self-attention approaches.  

4. **Concerns about SNN understanding and fairness of analysis**:  
   - The assertion that decay and reset mechanisms cause significant loss of historical information is questionable; biological neurons use these mechanisms to avoid excessive firing and retain memory, so this claim raises doubts about the authors’ understanding of spiking neurons.  
   - The analysis of LayerNorm’s incompatibility with spike-driven characteristics may be unfair: many SNN studies replace LayerNorm with BatchNorm (which merges into Conv during inference), and LayerNorm itself can be merged into Linear during inference, so the distribution analysis of Q and K may be biased.  
   - The paper argues that previous work did not introduce temporal modeling in self-attention, but SNNs inherently model temporal features through spiking neurons; the need for an additional temporal component is not sufficiently justified.  

5. **Energy consumption and complexity claims**:  
   - The claimed energy efficiency advantage appears theoretical. In Table 2, comparing with SpikingResformer at 13.7M vs. 17.8M parameters, SNN-ViT yields 14.28 mJ energy and 74.66% accuracy vs. 3.37 mJ and 75.95%; at 30.4M vs. 35.5M parameters, 20.83 mJ (76.87%) vs. 5.46 mJ (77.24%). These results show no significant advantage at smaller scales and larger energy consumption, contradicting the efficiency claim.  
   - SSSA-V1 introduces outer product operations and SSSA-V2 introduces Hadamard products, both of which involve floating-point (MAC) operations, undermining the purely spike-driven claim.  

6. **Inadequate biological grounding**:  
   - The introduction to biological saccadic mechanisms is insufficient; no mathematical formulation of the biological process is given, making the connection between the methodology and its inspiration appear tenuous.  
   - The paper should include research history, related works on algorithms inspired by other visual mechanisms, and concrete examples linking the mathematical formulation to biological saccades.  

7. **Unclear interpretability and experiment details**:  
   - Attention heatmaps in Figures 5 and 7 are noisy and lack interpretability; the binary focus/ignore operation likely contributes to this, making it difficult to understand which features the network uses.  
   - The remote sensing target detection experiments with YOLO-v3 are not clearly described: missing details on backbone integration with the classification head, feature extraction layers, YOLO-v3 configuration (anchor boxes, input size), training environment, and hardware requirements.  
   - Appendices A and B need expansion: e.g., why dot products effectively measure Q-K correlation in ANNs (with mathematical proof) and why cross-entropy is not used for similarity in ANNs while dot products are preferred; a discussion of limitations of cross-entropy for attention is missing.  

8. **Training overhead**: The training time cost and resource requirements are not reported, which is important given the substantial training overhead typical of SNNs.
