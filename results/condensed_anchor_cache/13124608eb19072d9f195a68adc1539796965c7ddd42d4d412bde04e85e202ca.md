## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes HVSA (Hyperbolic Visual-Semantic Alignment) for structural visual recognition. Two components are introduced: hierarchy-agnostic visual-semantic alignment (using a Gaussian mixture VAE) and hierarchy-aware semantic learning (using triplet metric learning over labels). Experiments on classification and segmentation across six datasets demonstrate competitive improvements.

### Strengths
- Compelling motivation for incorporating hierarchical structure into visual recognition.
- Novelty: visual-semantic alignment from a probabilistic perspective in hyperbolic space; the hierarchy-agnostic idea is innovative.
- Comprehensive experiments covering multiple tasks (classification and segmentation) and six datasets, with convincing/competitive results.
- Well-written and easy to follow; the concept of encoding hierarchical label embeddings and aligning visual embeddings is reasonable, and the triplet loss in label space is sound.

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of ablation for hyperbolic vs. Euclidean space (Reviewer 1):** No direct comparison when all loss terms are performed in Euclidean space. Theoretical advantages of hyperbolic space are not experimentally isolated or supported by ablation studies.
- **Insufficient details on taxonomy acquisition (Reviewer 1):** No explanation of how label taxonomies are obtained (manual vs. automatic, criteria), and no tree structures are provided to validate hierarchical relationships.
- **Missing definitions of evaluation metrics (Reviewer 1):** Metrics CV and CMAP are not introduced, making results hard to interpret.
- **Method presentation is incomplete and contains errors (Reviewers 2 and 3):**
  - Logic flow between sections 3.1 and 3.2 is broken; the method is very difficult to reproduce.
  - Missing definition of \(q_\theta\) in Eqs. (7) and (8).
  - Incorrect statements: “the mean of the wrapped normal mixture distribution is calculated by Möbius addition” is not logically correct; “\(y_{l,i} = \mu_l + \Sigma_l\)” adds a covariance matrix to a mean vector.
  - Reconstruction loss is neither explained nor referenced.
  - Typos: Section 3.2 Task Setting says “label i” instead of “label j”; Section 3.2.1 says “align visual embedding with visual embedding” instead of “with label embedding”.
- **Equations essentially copied from prior work without proper contextualization (Reviewer 2):** Eqs. (9)–(10) are the same as Eqs. (32)–(33) in Ganea et al. (2018), and Eq. (11) is exactly Eq. (28) in Ganea et al. (2018), where those equations define hyperbolic cones—the core contribution of that work. In this submission, those cone definitions are not connected to the claimed novel contributions (probabilistic perspective, hierarchy-agnostic alignment), undermining novelty and attribution.
- **Compositional label modeling not new and not properly evaluated (Reviewer 2):** The example of compositional labels (bus → vehicle/large-vehicle/bus) is the same approach as Liu et al. (2020) on WordNet, with no fundamental differences. The claimed core contribution “Probabilistic Label Embedding” is not ablated against non-probabilistic embeddings in any of the 10 tables. The term “compositional labels” is not explicitly used or explored in Section 4.1, despite the claim.
- **In-depth analysis lacking (Reviewer 2):** Tables only show “we have better numbers” without understanding of why each component works. No ablation is provided to support the effectiveness of probabilistic over non-probabilistic embeddings.
- **Missing computation efficiency (Reviewer 3):** Training and inference computation cost is not reported.
- **Motivation for hierarchy-agnostic component questioned (Reviewer 2):** What is the aim of hierarchy-agnostic alignment when hierarchy information is already available? Why is the “agnostic” part important? This is not justified.

*Note: Reviewer 2 (score 3) is significantly more critical than Reviewers 1 and 3 (both score 6), raising fundamental issues about novelty, reproducibility, and evaluation depth.*
