## Merged Review

### Summary
HIEROS proposes hierarchical world models with S5 layers (S5WM) and a novel time-balanced sampling strategy (ETBS) for sample-efficient RL. Two reviewers (scores 3,3) judge the paper as insufficiently validated: they cite missing key baselines (S4WM, a strong model-free method), incomplete ablations (4 games, 1 seed, no confidence intervals), and writing that obscures contributions. The third reviewer (score 8) considers the paper strong—with high-quality descriptions, well-motivated experiments, and SOTA on several Atari 100k games—but notes limited originality, clarity issues, and poor performance on simpler games. All agree the idea of hierarchical world models is promising.

### Strengths
- **Promising direction**: Hierarchical world models for RL are a natural and promising extension of prior work (Reviewers 1,2,3).
- **Novel S5 layer application**: Introducing the S5 sequence model into model-based RL is a valuable domain test of its generality (Reviewer 1).
- **Novel sampling approach (ETBS)**: The method for non-uniform training-data sampling is a potentially beneficial community tool (Reviewer 1).
- **Strong positive assessment from minority reviewer**: Reviewer 3 calls the paper “strong” with excellent descriptions, well-motivated experiments, clear discussion, and SOTA on several Atari 100k games.
- **Code release**: Authors commit to releasing code (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing key baseline comparisons**:
  - No comparison to S4WM—the most natural SSM-based world model. The authors’ justification is insufficient; alternatives include waiting for code release or contacting the S4WM authors (Reviewer 1; question 7).
  - No comparison to the recent model-free method [1] (high replay ratio, policy reset), which outperforms HIEROS on Atari 100k (Mean 1.27 vs. 1.20, Median 0.68 vs. 0.56, IQM 0.63 vs. 0.53). This invalidates the SOTA claim (Reviewer 2).
- **Insufficient ablation and analysis**:
  - Ablation experiments (Appendix F) cover only 4 games (Krull, Breakout, Freeway, Battle Zone) and appear to use only 1 seed, with no error bars. High RL variance makes conclusions unreliable (Reviewers 1,2; question 9).
  - Main results (Table 1) lack confidence intervals; significance of differences vs. baselines is unclear (Reviewer 2).
  - The free-bit loss (Kingma et al. 2016) is included without experimental justification (Reviewer 1).
  - Several ablation environments show no benefit; the authors’ hypothesized reasons remain untested (Reviewer 1).
  - The effect of ETBS sampling is not discernible from the provided figure (Figure 9) (Reviewer 1, question 11).
- **Writing and clarity issues**:
  - No dedicated background section; related work is fused into methods, obscuring contributions. The methods section should focus solely on the authors’ work (Reviewer 1).
  - Core concept “subgoals” is not defined until several paragraphs in (Reviewer 3).
  - The sampling contribution is introduced as a footnote in the introduction, which lacks motivation, making the paper feel disjointed (Reviewer 1).
  - Important details (ablations) are relegated to the appendix; less essential figures (Figures 2, 4) remain in the main paper. The hierarchy depth ablation should be in the main paper (Reviewers 1,3).
  - Overstated claim: “0.6 days is on par with 0.5 hours” (Reviewer 3).
  - Minor language errors: “usurally” (Reviewer 3).
- **Combination of existing components**: The algorithm mostly combines DreamerV3 hierarchy, S5 layers, and time-balanced sampling from prior work; it does not constitute a fundamental advance (Reviewer 2; Reviewer 3 agrees originality is the weakest part).
- **Poor performance on simpler games**: HIEROS struggles on simpler games (e.g., Pong), limiting practical significance. The paper does not explore automatic hierarchy depth selection to address this (Reviewer 3; question 2). Reviewer 1 also notes a lack of benefit on some environments.
- **Missing experimental details**: Number of trials/steps for ablations not reported (Reviewer 1, question 9). Unclear whether authors attempted to contact S4WM authors (Reviewer 1, question 7). Related questions that overlap with criticisms: whether world models are just model-based RL (q1), whether ETBS is a third contribution (q2), confusion between Director and DreamerV3 (q3), whether the S5 block is from prior work (q4), normalization in Eq 2 (q5), how sub-goals are distinguished (q6), prior work on MLE issues (q8), and choice of ablation environments (q10). These reinforce concerns about clarity and validation.
