## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes two implicit functional BDL approaches, ifBNN and ifBDE, which use the 2-Wasserstein distance in function space instead of KL divergence in weight space. The key idea is to transform the BDL posterior into a Gaussian process via the neural tangent kernel (NTK) to compute the Wasserstein distance tractably. The authors provide promising results on regression, classification, and contextual bandit tasks. While two reviewers find the method novel, well-motivated, and clearly written, two others have major reservations about experimental rigour, computational cost, reproducibility, and comparison with existing methods, especially more scalable function-space approaches.

### Strengths
- **Novel, well-motivated idea.** Using the NTK to enable tractable 2-Wasserstein distance computation in function-space Bayesian deep learning is a novel contribution, addressing known issues with weight-space priors and KL divergence. The ifBNN method is considered elegant and simple (R1, R2, R4).  
- **Clear exposition.** The paper is well-written and easy to follow (R2: “pleasure to read”, R3, R4).  
- **Applicable to two popular BDL frameworks.** The idea is extended to both Bayesian neural networks (ifBNN) and deep ensembles (ifBDE) (R1).  
- **Relevant research direction.** Functional BNN methods are increasingly important for uncertainty quantification (R2, R3).  
- **Diverse set of experiments.** The paper evaluates on regression, classification, and bandit tasks, showing competitive or superior performance against several weight-space and function-space baselines (R1, R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with recent function-space and related methods.**  
  – Latest functional-space works: Rudner et al. (2023), Cinquin et al. (2024 – FSP-Laplace), Wu et al. (UAI 2024), Haddouche & Guedj (2023) (R1).  
  – Linearized Laplace approximation and its functional extension (references in R4); Last Layer Laplace and regular deep ensembles (R2).  
  – The state-of-the-art function-space VI method (reference 5 in R4) is ignored.  
- **Insufficient experimental validation and reproducibility concerns.**  
  – Only simple 2-layer MLP models are used; behaviour at larger scales is not evaluated (R3: “insufficient evidence”, R4: small-scale datasets not useful anymore).  
  – Classification results lack in-distribution uncertainty metrics: NLL and ECE are missing (R2).  
  – UCI regression results differ from those in the GWI paper; no explanation for the discrepancy (R1).  
  – Contextual bandit experiment is minimal (only one dataset); better benchmarks exist (R4, citing ref. [6] 2310.00137).  
  – No ablation studies on key hyperparameters, e.g., number of measurement points (R3).  
  – Many implementation details missing: prior specification and tuning, optimizer details, activation functions (R2). For example, GWI uses tanh while authors use rbf—why? (R1).  
  – The authors mention scaling to GPT‑4 and Gemini, but the method already underperforms on small-scale datasets (e.g., ifBDE: 95% on MNIST, 85% on FMNIST, 48.26% on CIFAR-10) (R4).  
- **Computational cost and scalability concerns.**  
  – NTK covariance matrix inversion scales quadratically with dataset size, making the method prohibitively expensive for large deep learning datasets (R3).  
  – Linearization must be performed at every ELBO iteration, unlike functional Laplace which only requires Jacobian at test time (R4).  
  – Scalability issues common to NTK methods are not discussed (R2).  
- **Theoretical justification and validity concerns.**  
  – The approximation of the functional posterior via the NTK is shaky because even large models are not in the NTK regime (R4, citing ref. [6]).  
  – Obtaining GP behaviour requires special NTK initialization; it is unclear how a general neural network can converge to the needed GP (R3).  
  – It is not clear whether the proposed loss is a valid ELBO (i.e., a valid VI objective) (R4).  
- **Ambiguities and definition issues.**  
  – The motivation for ifBDE is unclear; why does jointly training an ensemble with Wasserstein regularisation not cause functions to collapse? (R2).  
  – The specification of BDEs (ensembling KLBBB models) is not referenced and is unfamiliar; how can BDE perform worse than an individual KLBBB model? (R2).  
  – Notation in Theorem 1 (f_in, m_in, Σ_in) is hard to follow (R1). Section 3.1 and 3.2 need rewriting for accessibility (R1).  
  – Line 309: “Wasserstein” typo; Algorithm 1 and 2 should say “training” not “inference” (R1).  
  – Line 359: what does “achieve accuracy of 10^{-10}” mean? (R2).  
- **Visualization of uncertainty quantification lacking.** No 2D classification examples or similar visualisations to illustrate how well the methods capture uncertainty (R1, citing Tran et al. 2022).  
- **Disagreement in reviewer assessments.** Two reviewers rated the paper highly (5/5) and two much lower (3/5), reflecting a strong split on whether the experimental evidence and computational burden are acceptable. The negative reviewers found the experimental section “highly lacking” and the performance “sub-par” given the cost.
