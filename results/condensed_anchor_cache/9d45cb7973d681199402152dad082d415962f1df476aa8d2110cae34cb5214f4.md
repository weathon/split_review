## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates backdoor attacks and defenses on Vision Transformers (ViTs), finding that existing defenses underperform due to improper adaptation from CNNs (e.g., using SGD instead of AdamW). With corrected optimizers, existing attacks are easily defended. The authors propose a new attack, Channel Activation Trigger (CAT), which adds a small adversarial perturbation to the trigger to bypass defenses. CAT is shown to be effective on CIFAR-10 and transferable to other ViT architectures. The paper includes comprehensive experiments and ablation studies.

### Strengths
- The paper is easy to read and understand.
- The observation that ViT defenses often use the wrong optimizer (SGD instead of AdamW) reveals that the perceived vulnerability of ViTs to backdoor attacks may be overstated (reviewer 1, 3, 4).
- The proposed CAT attack appears effective against ViTs (reviewer 1).
- CAT demonstrates transferability to other ViT architectures (Table 4, CIFAR-10) (reviewer 2).
- The paper conducts comprehensive experiments and ablation studies (reviewer 3).
- The authors revisit backdoor defense methods and identify optimizer misuse as a key factor in their inadequacy (reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- The defense improvements rely on experimental trials (optimizer, epochs, pruning granularity) without a methodology for selecting good hyperparameters (reviewer 1).
- Experimental variability is concerning: in Section 3.2, the fine-tuning curve for the first 20 epochs differs significantly when the experiment is run for 100 versus fewer epochs (left plot (a) left), raising reliability concerns (reviewer 1).
- The improvement from CAT is limited; Table 4 shows that most unsuccessful attacks remain unsuccessful after adding CAT (reviewer 1).
- Some symbols are unexplained, and Appendix C Figure 7 should refer to Table 7 (reviewer 1).
- The study is limited to a single dataset (CIFAR-10) and a single ViT architecture (ViT-B), making conclusions unconvincing (reviewer 2). Lack of evaluation on more complex datasets like ImageNet (reviewer 4).
- The threat model is unclear: the paper does not specify whether the adversary has access to the model during training, and distinguishes between source/target being the same or different models. In ImageNet experiments, both source and target are ViT-B, giving the adversary full access to architecture and parameters, which is impractical and limits impact (reviewer 2).
- The hypothesis that backdoor attacks are easy to defend with proper optimizer inheritance lacks supporting citations. No reference is provided for using SGD to mitigate backdoors in ViTs; the most straightforward approach would be to use the same optimizer as during training (AdamW for ViT) (reviewer 3).
- Only CNN-specific backdoor attacks (e.g., BadNets, Blend) are tested in Table 2; the paper does not evaluate ViT-specific attacks [2,3,4]. This is critical because the study claims to investigate backdoor defense on ViTs (reviewer 3, reviewer 4).
- The paper only considers purified-based backdoor defenses (fine-tuning, pruning), not detection-based defenses like Neural Cleanse [1] that rely on reverse engineering and outlier detection. It is unclear whether CAT can bypass detection (reviewer 3, reviewer 4).
- The proposed attack is based on differences in intermediate-level representations (channel activation). The authors do not show whether it is effective only on ViT or also on CNNs (reviewer 3).
- The paper lacks sufficient baselines to demonstrate the superiority of CAT: no comparison with SOTA backdoor attacks, especially advanced ViT-specific attacks (reviewer 4).
- There is insufficient evaluation of CAT against SOTA backdoor defenses designed for ViT (reviewer 4).
- [1] B. Wang et al., Neural Cleanse, IEEE S&P 2019.
- [2] Zheng et al., TrojViT, CVPR 2023.
- [3] Zheng et al., Data-free backdoor removal based on channel lipschitzness, ECCV 2022.
- [4] Subramanya et al., Backdoor attacks on vision transformers, arXiv 2022.
