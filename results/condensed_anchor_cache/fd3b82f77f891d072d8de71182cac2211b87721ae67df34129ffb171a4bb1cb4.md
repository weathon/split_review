## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes LEGO, a self-supervised pretraining method for 3D molecules that segments structures into tetrahedra and learns to reconstruct them via orientation prediction and arrangement generation, building upon TokenGT by adding 3D attributes. Evaluated on MoleculeNet and OGBLSC-PCQM4Mv2. Reviewers found the concept interesting and original but identified major concerns about experimental results, clarity, chemical validity of tetrahedra, ablation studies, and missing evaluations on other 3D benchmarks. All reviewers rated 3.

### Strengths
1. **Novel conceptual approach**: Segmenting 3D molecular structures into local components (tetrahedra) and learning to reconstruct them is an insightful and original idea for pretraining (R1, R3, R4). The combination of graph token representation with decomposition into smaller 3D structural units is interesting (R3). The method is well-motivated by the concept of local geometric units (R4).

2. **Intriguing reconstruction method**: The two-step reconstruction (spatial orientation prediction and internal arrangement generation) presents a unique strategy for leveraging 3D structure (R1). The approach shows good novelty despite the base model being a straightforward extension of TokenGT with 3D coordinates (R1).

3. **Acceptable performance on some tasks**: Empirical results are OK for some fine-tuning tasks on MoleculeNet, though not consistently solid across all tasks (R2). The downstream application on MoleculeNet is significant (R3). However, reviewers note that results are not strong enough to demonstrate clear superiority (R4).

4. **Mostly clear presentation (minority view)**: One reviewer found the presentation mostly clear and the method easy to follow (R4). This contrasts with clarity concerns raised by other reviewers (see Weaknesses).

### Weaknesses
1. **Insufficient experimental results to demonstrate superiority**:
   - On OGBLSC-PCQM4Mv2, performance lags behind state-of-the-art methods (R1). Table 2 omits the top-performing models from the OGB-LSC challenge; LEGO would rank 9th in validation MAE with a substantial gap to Uni-Mol (ICLR 2023) (R3). The authors should submit their model to the benchmark for test set evaluation (R3).
   - Evaluation is limited to MoleculeNet and OGBLSC-PCQM4Mv2; many key 3D benchmarks (QM9, GEOM-drug, MD17) are missing, which would better demonstrate the value of 3D pretraining (R1, R4). The claim that 3D information is beneficial on MoleculeNet tasks is questionable because these tasks are graph properties, not 3D properties; it is unclear what 3D structure is being used (R2).
   - The provided explanation for underperformance on liposome compounds (“different 3D structures molecules exhibit”) is vague and unsubstantiated (R2, R3).
   - Overall, results are not sufficiently strong to support empirical superiority, and the experimental quality may not meet the ICLR bar (R4).

2. **Ablation studies are unconvincing and incomplete**:
   - They do not evaluate key innovations: tetrahedra vs. point-wise generation, role of edge information (R2), or the contribution of the 3D TokenGT backbone (R4).
   - The perturbation range tested is too limited, and Table 3 suggests the chosen parameters are not optimal (R3).
   - The random perturbation pretraining in Table 4 is not described clearly enough (R3).
   - It is also unclear whether the proposed SSL objective can be combined with other backbones or pretraining objectives, which would demonstrate extensibility (R4).

3. **Chemical validity of tetrahedra as local units is problematic**:
   - The geometrical justification of tetrahedra as the simplest polyhedron does not align with real molecular geometries. Figure 1b shows benzene, which has a planar-trigonal (120°) bonding pattern that is not a tetrahedron and has different local symmetries (R2).
   - The method’s handling of atoms with more than four bonds (e.g., sulfur, phosphorous) is not discussed (R2).

4. **Clarity issues in method description and writing**:
   - The training objective and reconstruction process are not clearly explained: it is unspecified how mask indicators for center atoms, edges, and leaf atoms are used to generate reconstruction targets, nor how the loss is computed across the different tasks or whether they are weighted (R1).
   - The exact input format for TokenGT-3D (original molecules, masked local structures, etc.) and how learned representations are used for downstream tasks are not specified (R1).
   - The paper contains numerous rushed or unclear sentences: undefined `d_p` on page 5, “way to increase the and generalizability” on page 6, “is enough to valid our method”, “graph representation an pass it”, “an important properties”, “proved to be close related” on page 7 (R3). The first sentence on page 8 does not make sense as written (R3).
   - One reviewer noted the presentation is mostly clear (R4), but the above issues indicate a need for significant rewriting, especially in the methods and experimental results sections (R3).

5. **Missing reconstruction accuracy metric**:
   - The authors do not report the reconstruction accuracy on the pretraining dataset, which would demonstrate whether the model is effectively learning 3D structural information (R1).

6. **Equivariance concern**:
   - The base model’s simple strategy of appending 3D coordinates to node embeddings may compromise the essential equivariance of 3D molecules, as the model could be sensitive to arbitrary rotations and translations, which is critical for molecular property prediction (R1).

7. **Table/result presentation errors**:
   - Table 1 does not correctly highlight the best and second-best results; notably, in the BACE column, two 2D models perform better than LEGO (R3).
   - Table 2 misses the vast majority of best-performing models from the OGB-LSC large-scale challenge; if counted correctly, LEGO would rank 9th with a substantial gap to Uni-Mol (R3).

8. **Missing related work discussion**:
   - The paper does not discuss fragment-based molecular reconstruction works such as Flam-Shepherd et al. (2022), which is closely related to the core idea of reconstructing molecular geometries from 3D fragments (R3). The discussion of the atom-bond inconsistency problem could be shortened without losing insights (R3).

9. **Layout issue**:
   - The Algorithm should be positioned before page 10 or placed in the appendix, rather than appearing after the references on page 12 (R1).
