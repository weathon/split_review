## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper applies mechanistic interpretability methods (probing, pruning, reverse fine-tuning) to study how fine-tuning alters pretrained model capabilities. Using synthetic settings (PCFG with Tracr solutions) and a more realistic setup (TinyStories dataset), the authors find that fine-tuning with a small learning rate typically adds a minimal transformation (“wrapper”) on top of existing capabilities rather than modifying them, and that further fine-tuning can revive these wrapped capabilities sample-efficiently.

### Strengths
- Experimental setup gradually steps from synthetic to realistic, with meticulous linking of claim consistency across experiments (Reviewer 1).
- Provides an exact definition of “capability” and a clear dichotomy into strong and weak relevance (Reviewer 1).
- Addresses a critical problem (understanding fine-tuning dynamics) with compelling mechanistic tools: probing, pruning, reverse fine-tuning (Reviewer 2).
- TinyStories experiment: pretraining capability is recovered via small-learning-rate fine-tuning; in the high-learning-rate deletion setting, capability loss matches the control model. Figure 79 in the appendix is particularly interesting (Reviewer 2).
- Comprehensive hyperparameter search and many alternatives reported in the appendix (Reviewer 2).
- Novel synthetic tasks designed to probe capability evolution during fine-tuning (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation is extremely unclear across multiple sections.**  
  * Figures 4 (i)–(iv) disagree between text and figure; Figure 5 has no legend; Figure 6 has no x-axis; plots are out of order (Fig. 7 referenced before Fig. 5) (Reviewer 2).  
  * Notation is overloaded: \(R_{FT, TR}\) and \(R_{FT, TE}\) are stated to contain three elements but these are not explained; \(L, M, H\) in \(P_C^L\) are undefined; the relationship between \(O_{pre}\) and \(O_{ft}\) is confusing (if \(O_{pre}\) already contains \(b\), why fine-tune on counting \(O_{ft}b\)?); the sentence “The probability of embedding a spurious correlation…” is grammatically incorrect (Reviewer 3).  
  * These issues make it hard or impossible to understand the paper’s main claims (Reviewer 3) and force the reader to parse effortfully (Reviewer 2).

- **Wrapper formalization is inconsistent.** Definition 2 defines a wrapper \(g\) as a function composed with the capability \(\mathcal{C}\), independent of parameterization. In experiments, the wrapper is always a *subset of weights to be pruned*. Probing therefore cannot be used as evidence that the model learns a wrapper under the given definition. It is non-trivial to find a definition that is both captured by pruning and captures the spirit of a lightweight modification. The paper should provide an example of a fine-tuning capability that *cannot* be obtained by a wrapper under the (revised) definition—otherwise the claim may be true by construction (Reviewer 2).

- **Claim of sample-efficient revival is not demonstrated.** Experiment 3 claims revival via reverse fine-tuning is sample-efficient, but provides no comparison to the time needed to learn the pretrained capability from scratch (the capabilities are compiled via Tracr, so no baseline exists). The fine-tuned model could simply be a good initialization; sample-efficiency analysis is critical (Reviewer 2).

- **Experiment 4 (probing analysis) is unclear and seems redundant.** Figure 8 does not concern probing; perhaps Figure 5 is meant? The new information beyond Experiment 2 is not grounded in data (Reviewer 2).

- **Missing baseline: fine-tuning with a mixture of pretrained data.** This is the most common fine-tuning technique; conclusions drawn without this baseline are less applicable (Reviewer 1).

- **Missing related work.** No discussion of how semi-controllable LMs (PCFGs, Tracr) have been used in interpretability (Reviewer 1). Two-stage LLM fine-tuning [1] and data-distributional properties and in-context learning [2] are absent (Reviewer 3).

- **Safety motivation is unsupported.** The introduction and conclusion raise safety and jailbreaking, but no experiments address this (Reviewer 1).

- **One reviewer (score 8) notes that the main findings—small learning rates only change the model a little, changes are reverted by retraining on pretraining data, large learning rates cause capability loss—are well-established.** The authors should highlight newer results, such as how the strong/weak relevance distinction could benefit fine-tuning new capabilities (Reviewer 1; a minority more positive reviewer who still found the paper solid).  
  *A separate reviewer (score 6) adds that the definition of “capability loss” or “recoverable” remains unclear (Reviewer 2).
