## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes a 3D self-supervised learning method (MultiView-ML) that takes only 3D point clouds as input and reconstructs both the original point cloud and multi-view depth images from different viewpoints. It uses an asymmetric encoder-decoder with a Multi-Scale Multi-Head (MSMH) attention mechanism and a two-stage teacher-student training strategy to align 2D and 3D representations. The method is evaluated on 3D classification, few-shot learning, part segmentation, and object detection, reporting improvements over several baselines.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, well-organized, and easy to follow, with high-quality figures.
- The unsymmetric encoder-decoder design is interesting; enforcing the autoencoder to decode multi-view representations from 3D-only input is a reasonable way to encourage multi-view geometric understanding.
- The Multi-Scale Multi-Head (MSMH) attention mechanism, which facilitates local-global information interaction across attention heads at different scales, is a notable component.
- The two-stage training strategy (teacher-student) for aligning 2D and 3D representations is feasible, challenging, and a significant contribution to multimodal self-supervised learning.
- The experiments cover a good range of tasks (classification, few-shot, part segmentation, object detection) and ablation studies demonstrate the effectiveness of MSMH, token-level reconstruction, and intra/inter-modality design choices.
- Promising experimental results: the method outperforms several state-of-the-art counterparts on multiple downstream tasks.

### Weaknesses
- **Motivation and claims:** The paper claims that incorporating both 2D and 3D modalities as input is redundant and inefficient, but provides insufficient evidence or ablation studies to support this claim. Notably, prior works (e.g., ReCon) already use only 3D input and link 2D modalities through contrastive learning. The core issue is not whether 2D is explicitly provided, but whether the method leverages multi-view information effectively – a justification that is missing. Furthermore, the paper's own method depends on 2D depth images for reconstruction, which appears to contradict the stated drawback. More rigorous justification is needed.
- **Technical novelty and similarity to existing work:** The overall pipeline (MAE + cross-modal distillation) closely resembles prior works like ReCon (ICML 2023). Replacing images with projected depth images does not seem a challenging extension. The MSMH attention is very similar to Grouped Vector Attention in Point Transformer V2 (NeurIPS 2022). Similarly, the use of point-only input and multi-scale attention has been explored in I2P-MAE (CVPR 2023), TAP (ICCV 2023), and Point-M2AE (NeurIPS 2022). The paper needs to clearly differentiate its contributions from these methods.
- **Incomplete and out-of-date baselines:** Comparisons are missing with several recent and stronger methods such as PointGPT (NeurIPS 2023), ReCon (ICML 2023), ACT (ICLR 2023), and ShapeLLM. Comparisons with ReCon and I2P-MAE are relegated to the supplementary and are incomplete (e.g., few-shot results for ReCon are missing). The claimed state-of-the-art results are not fully supported given these omissions.
- **Ablation and analysis gaps:** 
    - The contribution of each loss term (point cloud reconstruction vs. multi-view depth reconstruction) and the teacher-student setup are not ablated separately. A direct comparison with a single-stage training of the same total epochs is needed to isolate the benefit of the two-stage approach (currently epoch distribution across stages is unspecified).
    - No ablation is performed using other types of 2D representations (e.g., silhouettes, contours) as reconstruction targets.
    - No experiment where the input modality is 3D and the output modality is only 2D (to isolate the effect of multi-view supervision).
    - The impact of the number or resolution of multi-view images on performance is not studied.
    - Training efficiency and parameter count are not reported; the added computational cost from 3D-to-2D projection, MSMH, and two-stage training should be discussed.
- **Part segmentation details and gains:** No per-category IoU results or qualitative visualizations (e.g., segmentation maps) are provided in the main paper, unlike prior works such as Point-MAE and Point-M2AE. The reported improvement on part segmentation and few-shot learning is marginal, requiring further explanation.
- **Need for larger-scale evaluation:** Results on saturated datasets (ScanObjectNN, ModelNet40) may not demonstrate scalability; experiments on more complex and diverse datasets like Objaverse would be valuable.
- **Presentation and typos:** The title contains an obvious typo: "leaner" should be "learner". Figure font sizes are too small. Additional typos include 'pertaining' on line 396. In Supplementary Table 1, "Ours (Point-M2AE)" should likely be labeled "Ours (Recon)".
- **Missing qualitative visualizations:** The paper should provide complementary maps or reconstruction visualizations (e.g., input vs. output point clouds and depth images) to illustrate self-supervised learning quality, as done in Point-MAE and Point-M2AE.
- **Role of depth images unclear:** The paper uses 2D depth images as reconstruction targets but does not clearly explain how these differ from rendered images in the dataset or what specific role they play in the model. The feature-level multi-view projection is also not clearly described.
- **Lack of per-category results and visualizations for part segmentation:** As noted, no per-class IoU or segmentation visualizations are presented.
