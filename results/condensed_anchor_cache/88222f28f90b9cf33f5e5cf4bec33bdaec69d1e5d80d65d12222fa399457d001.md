## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper presents STAR (Synthesis of Tailored Architectures), a framework for automated neural architecture search that combines a search space grounded in the theory of linear input-varying systems (LIVs) with hierarchical numerical genome encoding and evolutionary optimization. The authors claim STAR can discover architectures that improve over highly-optimized Transformers and striped hybrid models on quality, parameter size, and inference cache frontiers for autoregressive language modeling. Two reviewers (scoring 8) find the method well-motivated, the hierarchical search space theoretically grounded, the presentation clear, and the experimental results convincing. Two other reviewers (scoring 6) acknowledge promising results and a novel encoding, but note that the use of evolutionary algorithms for architecture search is not new, the comparison baselines are limited (only Transformer++ and StripedMamba), and several design choices and implementation details remain underspecified. All reviewers agree that the approach shows potential, but they express varying degrees of concern about novelty, generality, and reproducibility.

### Strengths
- **Novel search space and encoding**: The LIV-based hierarchical design space is grounded in theory and provides a solid foundation for expressing a wide variety of operators (attention, recurrence, convolutions) as special LIV-cases, enabling a systematic and generalizable search space that extends beyond predefined backbones. The hierarchical numerical encoding into architecture genomes is novel and lends itself to evolutionary optimization. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4)
- **Straightforward evolutionary optimization**: The genome representation is interpretable and easy to manipulate; constraints ensure robustness to edits. The use of evolutionary algorithms (e.g., NSGA-2) is well-justified given the complexity of the search space, and it facilitates multi-objective optimization across quality, size, and inference cache simultaneously. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **Clear presentation and thorough evaluation**: The paper is well-written, logically structured, and presents key findings accessibly. The experimental evaluation is extensive, comparing STAR-optimized architectures against Transformer++ and StripedMamba baselines on perplexity, downstream benchmarks, model size, and inference caching, providing convincing evidence of effectiveness. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)
- **Promising results**: STAR demonstrates improvements over highly-optimized Transformer and hybrid baselines, and the results are well-analyzed with respect to multiple metrics. (Reviewers 1, 4)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited comparison baselines**: The evaluation only compares with Transformer++ and StripedMamba. Comparison with additional state-of-the-art LLMs (e.g., Llama series, BitNet, Falcon-Mamba) on standard benchmarks (RedPajama, LAMBADA, HellaSwag, ARC-e, PiQA) is missing; also absent are comparisons with dedicated KV cache optimization methods. A more comprehensive evaluation under consistent training conditions is needed to assess STAR’s effectiveness and generality. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Insufficiently novel core idea**: Using evolutionary algorithms for architecture search is not new, and the method appears geared toward tweaking transformer-based stacks rather than discovering fundamentally new designs. For instance, the pre-norm residual structure is fixed and cannot be varied by the optimizer, which relies on manual expertise. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- **Design choices and constraints lack justification and ablation**:
  - The distinction and implementation of channel vs token mixing across different LIVs, along with associated constraints, are not clearly explained. (Reviewer 3)
  - Mutation constraints in Section 4.2 suggest unconstrained genomes are not robust to random edits; an ablation study comparing constrained vs unconstrained mutations (e.g., distribution of valid/invalid architectures, impact on convergence) is needed. (Reviewer 2)
  - The fixed backbone length of 24 LIVs is chosen without discussion of variable-length search spaces; challenges and benefits of allowing arbitrary numbers of LIV modules should be addressed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Incomplete specification of evolutionary operators and handling of invalid designs**: The types of mutation (uniform, polynomial?) and recombination (uniform, fixed-point?) used are not specified. The framework’s mechanism for handling invalid designs generated after mutation (replacement, alteration, correction, or removal) and the frequency of such invalid designs are not reported. (Reviewer 3)
- **Inadequate detail on genome encoding and convergence**:
  - The mapping between integer values in the genome and specific architectural choices (e.g., LIV classes, parameter ranges) is unclear. A table listing all search parameters, their definitions, possible values, and architectural implications would aid reproducibility. (Reviewer 3)
  - The use of a single-genome representation raises concerns about optimization convergence, especially for complex tasks. The paper does not discuss pros and cons of one-shot vs multi-stage optimization strategies. (Reviewer 3)
- **Misleading claim about gradient-free optimization**: The abstract states STAR’s optimization framework is gradient-free, but the evaluation of each architecture involves training network parameters via gradient descent. This should be clarified: architecture search is gradient-free, but fitness evaluation requires gradient-based training. (Reviewer 3)
- **Notation confusion**: Equation L128’s underbrace suggests the summation is part of \(T_{ij}^{\alpha \beta}\), but Eq. L138 repeats the summation; also \(T_{ij}\) appears overloaded. Clarifying the distinction between the general and special cases would improve readability. (Reviewer 2)
- **Selection of presented architectures not explained**: In Table 5.1, the difference between STAR-1 to STAR-8 is not described. How were these eight models selected from the Pareto front? Architectural diversity and selection criteria should be provided. (Reviewer 2)
- **Reproducibility**: Reproducing STAR without source code is challenging. The authors are encouraged to share the code to facilitate verification and further investigation. (Reviewer 3)
