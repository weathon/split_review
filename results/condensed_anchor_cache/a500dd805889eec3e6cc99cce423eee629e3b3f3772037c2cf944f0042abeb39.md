## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces CipherChat, a framework that uses cipher prompts (character encodings, common ciphers, and a proposed SelfCipher) to bypass the safety alignment of LLMs. Experiments on GPT-3.5, GPT-4, and Llama2 across 11 safety domains in English and Chinese show that certain ciphers can achieve nearly 100% success in eliciting unsafe responses from GPT-4, and that SelfCipher often outperforms human-designed ciphers. Two reviewers (scores 8) found the work novel, well-written, and insightful, emphasizing the important finding that smarter models are more vulnerable. A third reviewer (score 6) acknowledged effectiveness for OpenAI models but raised concerns about generalizability and presentation. A fourth reviewer (score 5) criticized missing experimental details, limited technical contribution, and insufficient robustness of the evaluation. All reviewers agreed on the topic’s interest and the paper’s clarity.

### Strengths
- The idea of using encrypted/non-natural language prompts (ciphers) to attack LLM safety alignment is novel and important; it reveals a new security vulnerability. (R1, R2, R3, R4)
- The paper is well-written, clearly organized, and easy to follow; the related work is thoroughly described. (R1, R2, R3)
- The finding that GPT-4 is more vulnerable than smaller models, and that SelfCipher can evoke a "secret cipher" without explicit rules, is particularly noteworthy and interesting to the community. (R1, R2)
- The experimental results and analysis are convincing and insightful for GPT-3.5 and GPT-4. (R2, R4)
- Two languages (English and Chinese) and multiple cipher types are considered, adding breadth. (R3)
- SelfCipher is highlighted as a potentially powerful attack even without a predefined cipher. (R1, R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Dataset and generalizability concerns**: The experiments use only one Chinese dataset (with a translated English version). This raises concerns about bias toward a single language and cultural context; the vulnerability may not generalize to other languages or settings. (R2) The translation from Chinese to English may be unreliable because safety has cultural and linguistic implications. (R3)
- **Missing experimental details and requirements**:
  - Performance with *safe* demonstrations should be reported (e.g., in the "Impact of Unsafe Demonstration" section). How much does the unsafe rate degrade? (R1)
  - For the "Impact of Fundamental Model" section, unsafe rates for both Llama2 models (13B and 70B) are requested to allow community benchmarking. (R1)
  - Interpretation of the trend: GPT-4 is more unsafe than ChatGPT, but the same trend does not hold for Llama2. Can we claim that "smarter = unsafer"? (R1)
  - Human evaluation details are insufficient: who were the evaluators, how many annotations per sample, what was the inter-annotator agreement, and what was the exact setup? (R3) The sample size for human evaluation is small (50 per case). (R3)
  - The self-cipher section lacks enough explanation and details on actual input and evaluation. (R3, R4)
  - Many rows in experimental results are empty because success rates are low in most cases, making it hard to draw robust conclusions. (R3)
- **Technical contribution**: The method is essentially in-context prompting with demonstrations, which is relatively simple; the technical novelty is limited. (R3)
- **Defense and practical impact**: The vulnerability appears easy to defend (e.g., perplexity detection for ciphers, content filtering for SelfCipher). While the paper argues that the vulnerability is important, the ease of defense may reduce real-world relevance. (R2) An ethics statement discussing societal impact and potential misuse is missing. (R3)
- **Generalization to other LLMs**: The method is effective for OpenAI models but does not easily generalize to other LLMs (as shown in Table 4). (R4)
- **Presentation issues**:
  - The "Case Study" section is left blank. (R1, R4)
  - The term "ICL" is not defined. (R4)
  - Several sentences are unclear (e.g., "directly translate the cipher input into natural language" on page 4; "solve the problem of generating invalid responses without unsafe demonstrations" on page 8). (R1)
  - It is unclear what the actual input to SelfCipher is and how the unsafe rate is measured in that setting. (R4)
- **Unclear or conflicting results**: In Table 3, why does +SafeDemo lead to a *higher* unsafe rate for UTF-8? (R4) Why was a subset of the data sampled rather than using the full set? (R2)
- **Lack of robustness**: The experimental setup needs improvement; the conclusions are not fully supported by the data given the many empty entries. (R3)

*Note: There is some variation among reviewers. One reviewer (score 8) considered the findings "convincing and insightful" while another (score 5) viewed the technical contribution as "lacking" and the evaluation as "not robust." The majority (three out of four) found the topic important and the paper clearly written, but concerns about dataset diversity, missing details, and presentation were raised by multiple reviewers.*
