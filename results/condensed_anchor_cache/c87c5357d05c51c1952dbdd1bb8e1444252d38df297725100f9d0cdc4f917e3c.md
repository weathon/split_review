## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper provides finite-particle convergence rates for Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) in the Kernelized Stein Discrepancy (KSD) and Wasserstein-2 metrics. The key insight is that the time derivative of the relative entropy between the joint particle density and the N-fold product target measure splits into a dominant negative term proportional to N times expected KSD² and a smaller positive term (Equation 9). This yields KSD rates of order 1/√N in continuous and discrete time, a double exponential improvement over the only previous finite-particle result (Shi & Mackey, 2024). Under mild assumptions the bounds grow polynomially in dimension. Adding a bilinear component to the kernel gives Wasserstein-2 convergence in continuous time; with bilinear+Matérn kernels, rates show curse-of-dimensionality similar to i.i.d. setting. Marginal convergence and long-time propagation of chaos for time-averaged particle laws are also obtained.

### Strengths
- Significantly improves particle dependence over Shi & Mackey (2024) in KSD, achieving near-optimal rates (matching i.i.d. rates) for finite-particle discrete-time SVGD. This is arguably the first satisfactory convergence bound for an algorithm that has attracted considerable theoretical attention.
- The main novel insight (Equation 9) is remarkably clean and allows an intuitive proof strategy, bypassing challenges faced by previous attempts.
- First convergence rate in Wasserstein-2 distance for SVGD (albeit with curse of dimensionality).
- The discussion of related works is extensive and informative.
- Paper is extremely well written and appears formally correct (Reviewer 4); presentation is clear though technical from Section 4 onward.

### Weaknesses
- **Assumptions and comparisons unclear.**  
  - Conditions in main results are not easy to verify (Reviewer 2). The growth condition in Assumption 2, while presented as a generalization, lacks sufficient explanation of its implications and relation to more standard conditions used in sampling (e.g., log-Sobolev, Poincaré, Talagrand inequalities). A table comparing assumptions and convergence rates with previous work (especially Shi & Mackey 2024) would greatly enhance clarity and allow rigorous assessment of the contribution (Reviewer 3).  
  - The feasibility of the initial distribution assumption (\(\limsup_{N\to\infty} \frac{1}{N} \mathrm{KL}(p^N(0)\|\pi^{\otimes N})<\infty\)) is not discussed; it is unclear how to choose such an initial distribution in practice (Reviewer 2).
  - The smoothness constant from condition (c) is not included in Theorem 3; its effect on the convergence rate is not characterized.

- **Weak discussion of Wasserstein-2 results.**  
  - The curse of dimensionality for Wasserstein-2 convergence is mentioned but lacks sufficient intuition or analysis, especially given the dimension-independent results for KSD. Practical implications in high dimensions are not explored (Reviewer 3).  
  - The restriction to Matérn-family kernels (eq. 12) in Theorem 5 is justified only as “computational clarity”; it is not clear whether the result extends to the broader kernel class defined in eq. (11). A remark clarifying whether the proof strategy can be generalized is needed (Reviewer 4).  
  - A simulation comparing Matérn SVGD to standard choices (e.g., Gaussian RBF) would help assess practical relevance (Reviewer 3).

- **Missing results and motivation.**  
  - There are no convergence results for SVGD in KL divergence (even in infinite-particle/continuous-time); it is unclear if the proof approach could yield such a result (Reviewer 1).  
  - Only the time-averaged particle law is analyzed for propagation of chaos; it is not shown whether SVGD also exhibits long-time propagation of chaos for the “last-iterate” occupancy measure (Reviewer 1).  
  - The KSD results are also given for time-averaged distributions; whether the averaging can be removed (as in LMC) is not addressed (Reviewer 2).  
  - Section 5 on propagation of chaos lacks motivation: it is not explained why propagation of chaos would be a desirable property for SVGD (Reviewer 1).

- **Notation and presentation issues.**  
  - No section collecting all notations, making certain parts hard to read (Reviewer 3).  
  - Several notation problems:  
    * In Theorem 2, \(P(x_1(t)\in dx)\) is mathematically vague; the intended meaning should be stated explicitly (Reviewer 2).  
    * In Lemma 1, \(p^N(t,\underline{z})\) is defined, but later the notation changes to \(p(t,\underline{z})\) (Reviewer 2).  
    * In the proof of Theorem 1, \(H(0)\) should be \(H^N(0)\) to keep notation self-explanatory (Reviewer 2).  
  - The time-average integration range \(\frac{1}{N}\int_0^N\) (from 0 to \(N\)) is confusing; why not integrate from 0 to \(T\)? (Reviewer 3).  
  - What is \(\mathcal{L}\) in Assumption 4? (Reviewer 3).

- **Mathematical gaps and potential errors.**  
  - The derivations starting at line 281 are not well explained: it is unclear why the first term on the right-hand side vanishes on the second line and how the third line is derived (Reviewer 2).  
  - The inequality to obtain (17) upper-bounds \((\sum_j V(x_j)/N)^{2\alpha}\) by \(\sum_j V(x_j)/N\), which may not hold unless \(\sum_j V(x_j)/N \le 1\); this seems incorrect even under the assumption \(2\alpha<1\) (Reviewer 2).  
  - At line 927, “Routine computations give…” would benefit from more details to ease reading (Reviewer 2).  
  - In the proof of Theorem 3, the bounds on the magnitude and Lipschitzness of the update operator (Lemmas 3 and 4) grow with the iteration count \(n\). It is not shown whether these quantities can be related back to the distance between \(\mu_n^N\) and \(\pi\), which would allow bounding the discretization error uniformly for long times (Reviewer 1).

- **Typos and minor inaccuracies.**  
  - In Assumption 1(b) and Lemma 1, \(p_0^N\) needs to be \(C^2\) (not \(C^1\)) for \(p^N(t,\cdot)\) to be \(C^2\) (Reviewer 1).  
  - In Assumption 2(d), perhaps “=” should be “\(\le\)” (Reviewer 1).  
  - Typos on line 430 (also noted by Reviewer 4), line 271 “equation equation”, line 291 missing period, line 674 extra square bracket, lines 747/749 missing sum sign, page 8 “literature literature” (Reviewers 1, 2).  
  - The definition of the \(N\)-fold product target measure \(\pi^{\otimes N}\) should be formally given (Reviewer 4).

- **Mixing time and comparison questions.**  
  - For the Gaussian target with \(\alpha=1/2\) (Remark 3), Theorem 3 requires \(N \asymp 1/\varepsilon^2\) and \(T = O(N^4) = O(1/\varepsilon^8)\). It is unclear whether this mixing time is comparable to other sampling algorithms for Gaussian targets (Reviewer 3).
