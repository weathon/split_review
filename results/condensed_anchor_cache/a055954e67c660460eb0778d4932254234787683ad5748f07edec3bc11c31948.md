## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Tree-Sliced Wasserstein distance on Systems of Lines (TSW-SL), replacing the 1D lines in Sliced Wasserstein (SW) with tree systems (metrizable by a tree metric) to better preserve topological information. A full theoretical analysis defines tree systems, splitting maps as projection mechanisms, and a novel Radon transform with injectivity. TSW-SL is shown to be a metric and can be approximated in the same complexity as SW. Experiments on gradient flows, image style transfer, and generative models (SWGAN, DDGAN) compare TSW-SL with SW and variants, reporting better and more flexible performance.  
Reviewers 1, 2, and 3 are moderately positive to skeptical (scores 6,5,5), while Reviewer 4 is strongly positive (score 8). All agree the paper is clear and well-structured, but there is disagreement on significance and evaluation rigor.

### Strengths
- **Clarity and structure:**
  - Well-written, logically structured, easy to follow.  
  - Effort on clear illustrations (Figures 1,2) that help understand the tree constructions.
- **Originality and novelty:**
  - Novel approach of using tree-based priors for Sliced OT; replacing 1D lines with tree systems is original and more flexible.  
  - The construction leverages the closed-form OT solution on trees, preserving computational efficiency.  
  - Provides a full theoretical analysis: definition of tree systems, splitting maps, Radon transform, metric property.
- **Significance:**
  - Important advancement for efficient closed-form Wasserstein distance computation.  
  - Generalizes Sliced-Wasserstein distance and can be approximated in the same sample complexity.
- **Experimental results:**
  - Consistently shows better performance than SW across gradient flows, color transfer, and generative modeling (SWGAN, DDGAN).  
  - Code is provided.  
  - Experiments include baseline comparisons on both low-dimensional (2D) and high-dimensional (image) data.  
  - Maintains lightweight computation relative to SW.

### Weaknesses
- **Evaluation quality and depth:**
  - *Major weakness:* Poor evaluation – only Tables 1 and 2 offer expressive justification of the method (R1).  
  - Figure 5 (color transfer) appears identical to baselines; more expressive examples needed (R1).  
  - Table 4 (FID) does not demonstrate that the proposed method gives better results (R1).  
  - Qualitative results in Section 6.2 are not sufficiently compelling to support claims (R3).  
  - No evaluation showing the computed distance is actually closer to the true Wasserstein distance (R1).
- **Unclear advantages over SW and its variants:**
  - The paper is a straightforward/incremental extension of prior work; skeptical about significance and impact (R2).  
  - TSW-SL achieves similar or only slightly better performance than SW while retaining the same limitations (e.g., difficulties in sampling meaningful lines in high dimensions, Table 2) and adding complexity (tree structure, splitting maps) – it is unclear when TSW-SL should be preferred (R3).  
  - No thorough analysis of the impact of the number of lines per tree (a critical parameter; using one line per tree recovers SW) (R3).  
  - Influence of splitting maps is not addressed; central to the new Radon transform but choices and impact on the distance are unexplored (R3).  
  - The claim that tree systems avoid loss of topological information is not strongly argued – is this related to number of lines? (R3).  
  - It is stated that improved performances are expected by adapting advanced sampling schemes from SW, but extension may not be straightforward (R3).
- **GAN experiment concerns:**
  - GAN training details insufficient: e.g., why the optimization problem (Appendix lines 1501-1505) is valid, and how DDGAN conditioning on time/point fits the TSW-SL framework is not explained (R2).  
  - GAN tuning is known to be more about hyperparameters than loss functions; most practical works still use vanilla losses, so the contribution of TSW-SL as a GAN loss may be minor (R2).  
  - Missing training time comparison (time per iteration, epoch, to convergence) and convergence plots (FID vs epoch) for TSW-SL vs. DDGAN vs. SW baselines (R2).  
  - Number of projections used in SW generator experiments (50 and 500) seems very low compared to the original paper (~100K) – impact on results? (R4).  
  - Gradient flow experiments use different ground costs (L2 vs. tree metric) across methods, making comparison at fixed iterations difficult – effect of proposed distance is not isolated (R3).
- **Missing theoretical analysis:**
  - No statistical analysis (sample complexity) nor topological analysis for TSW-SL (R3, R4).  
  - No derivation of additional properties (e.g., metrization of weak convergence, statistical properties) (R3).  
  - How does TSW-SL relate to other distances? Is it a lower bound of Sliced-Wasserstein or of Wasserstein? (R4).  
  - Impact of root position (stated as “root is positioned near the mean of the target distribution” in Section 6) not explored (R3).  
  - Algorithm 1 only samples chain trees – were trees with varying child counts tested? (R4).
- **Clarity and presentation errors:**
  - Section 3.2 is challenging to read, though Figures help (R4).  
  - Equation (2): abuse of notation – Radon transform of a density yields a density, not a measure (R4).  
  - Line 467: loss should not be denoted as $\mathcal{L}$ (already used for lines) (R3).  
  - Appendix: line 870 $\overline{L}$ should be $\overline{\mathcal{L}}$ (R3).  
  - Example A.14 unclear: $n_i$ lengths, meaning of arrows, missing arrow on line 1035 (R3).  
  - Page 25 transition from eq. (46) to eq. (47) insufficiently justified (R3).  
  - Table 1: by “Wasserstein distance” do you mean $W_2$? (R3).  
  - Table 2: timing description unclear (R3).  
  - Line 102: “(Helgason & Helgason, 2011)” should be “(Helgason, 2011)” (R4).  
  - Line 322: “If tree systems in $\mathbb{T}$ consists only one line” – typo (R4).  
- **Missing references:**
  - Missing references [1,2,3] when citing work projecting on lower-dimensional subspaces (R4).  
- **Reviewer disagreement:**  
  Reviewer 4 (score 8) sees the construction as well-motivated, original, and consistently outperforming, with only minor weaknesses. In contrast, Reviewers 2 and 3 (scores 5) consider the contribution incremental and question the practical significance, particularly in GAN training.
