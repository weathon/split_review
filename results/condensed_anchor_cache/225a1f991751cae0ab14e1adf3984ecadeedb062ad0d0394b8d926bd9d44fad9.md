## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces SymmetricDiffusers, a discrete diffusion model for learning distributions over the symmetric group \(S_n\). It proposes forward processes using card shuffling methods (riffle shuffle, random transpositions, random insertions) and reverse processes using a generalized Plackett-Luce (GPL) distribution. A theoretically grounded denoising schedule merges reverse steps to improve efficiency. The model is trained via a variational lower bound estimated with Monte Carlo samples. Experiments on sorting 4-digit MNIST, jigsaw puzzles (CIFAR-10, MNIST), and TSP-20 show state-of-the-art or comparable results.

### Strengths

- **Originality:** First discrete diffusion model over \(S_n\); novel use of classical card shuffling methods (riffle shuffle, random transpositions, random insertions) as forward processes; introduction of the generalized Plackett-Luce distribution for the reverse process, which is provably more expressive than the standard PL distribution (e.g., can represent delta distributions).
- **Thorough mathematical treatment:** Enumeration and rigorous analysis of forward and reverse processes, grounded in the theory of random walks on finite groups with careful treatment of mixing times and transition probabilities. This adds mathematical credibility.
- **Clear writing:** The paper is well-organized, structured, and generally easy to follow. The survey of Markov chains and the presentation of the forward/reverse processes are clear and aided by helpful figures.
- **Comprehensive experiments:** Validation on diverse tasks (sorting, jigsaw puzzles, TSP) with good performance, especially at increased sequence lengths. The code is shared.
- **Significance:** Opens new possibilities for generative modeling in permutation-based domains (ranking, combinatorial optimization, sequence alignment). The findings can potentially inspire future work on discrete diffusion for structured data.

### Weaknesses

- **Scalability concerns:** Experiments are small-scale (4-digit MNIST sorting, 20-node TSP, CIFAR-10 jigsaw puzzles) and do not explore scalability to larger \(n\). The factorial growth of the state space and quadratic scaling with \(n\) remain open challenges. The paper lacks clear insights into practical scalability limits or potential optimization strategies (e.g., sparse transitions, modular architectures).
- **Lack of strong motivation for generative modeling:** The paper does not adequately justify why learning a full distribution over permutations (rather than a single permutation per input) is necessary for the tested tasks. The claim that “learning a distribution over \(S_n\) through the lens of modern machine learning is both natural and beneficial” feels unjustified. A discussion of trade-offs compared to differentiable sorting methods would help.
- **Insufficient comparative analysis:** No direct comparison against other discrete diffusion models such as D3PMs or standard diffusion models operating on the product space \([n]^n\). The simple baseline of treating permutations as categorical vectors in \([n]^n\) is not considered. This makes it unclear whether the proposed shuffling-based approach offers practical advantages over existing general-purpose discrete diffusion frameworks. The claim of state-of-the-art on TSP-20 is too bold given the small instance size and the existence of many specialized TSP solvers.
- **Expressiveness limitations of the reverse process:** The reverse process for random transpositions may not be expressive enough. For example, a target distribution that assigns probability 0.5 to transposition \((1,2)\) and 0.5 to \((2,3)\) cannot be represented by the model. This raises concerns about the model’s ability to capture arbitrary permutation distributions.
- **Limited theoretical depth:** While the parameterization is novel, the paper lacks deeper theoretical analysis, e.g., convergence properties of the Markov chains or a rigorous analysis of the expressiveness of the reverse process parameterization.
- **Experimental clarity issues:** Several implementation details are unclear: exact parameterization of transition probabilities, how the neural network parameterizes the reverse process, specific architectures and hyperparameters, and what the sequence length \(n\) is in the 4-digit MNIST task. The difference from differentiable sorting baselines in Table 1 is not explained.
- **Denoising schedule not ablated:** The impact of the denoising schedule on computational efficiency and performance is not benchmarked via ablation. Sensitivity to the choice of schedule is unexplored.
- **Broader applicability not discussed:** The paper does not discuss whether the model can be adapted to non-permutation discrete structures (e.g., graphs, hierarchical data) or how it might generalize beyond \(S_n\).
- **OOD performance not evaluated:** No experiments on out-of-distribution data (e.g., colored or font-shifted MNIST) are provided.
- **Minor issues:** Typographical errors in abstract (line 009) and line 472. Proposition 1 should state that the GPL distribution can represent a delta distribution “in the limit” rather than “exactly.”

- **Reviewer disagreement:** One reviewer (R4) is more critical regarding expressiveness and theoretical depth, while others (R1, R2, R3, R5) are more positive overall.
