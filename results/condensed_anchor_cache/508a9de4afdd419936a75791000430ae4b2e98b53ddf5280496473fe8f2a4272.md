## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MADiTS, a diffusion-based data augmentation pipeline for offline multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) that addresses dataset imbalance in both temporal and spatial dimensions. It trains a multi-agent trajectory diffusion model (conditional on the first joint observation) to generate segments, applies a bidirectional dynamics constraint (forward dynamics model together with an inverse dynamics model) to filter out dynamics-inconsistent segments, and uses Integrated Gradients to identify underperforming agents and regenerate their actions while keeping others fixed. The process iterates until a satisfactory augmented episode trajectory is produced. Experiments on MPE and SMACv1 datasets show improvements when applied to BC, OMIGA, and CFCQL.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, clear, and easy to follow.
- The diffusion-based approach for data augmentation is sensible and compatible with any offline MARL algorithm.
- The idea of using Integrated Gradients to identify underperforming agents is novel and may have broader potential in multi-agent learning.
- The experimental results are impressive, achieving SOTA performance on all evaluated datasets (MPE, SMACv1).
- An ablation study demonstrates the impact of different components of MADiTS.
- The method addresses the important real-world problem of data imbalance in offline MARL.
- The overall structure is complete and the experimental evidence is convincing.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited comparison to existing diffusion-based offline MARL methods** – The paper does not compare MADiTS against MADiff [1] (which also develops diffusion models to enhance offline datasets in MARL), DOM2 [3], or SIT [2]. MADiff is used as the architectural backbone (mentioned in appendix) but not as a baseline in main experiments. Clarification on whether “w/o dc + pn” in Figure 4a is equivalent to MADiff is needed. Discussions and experimental comparisons with these methods are missing.
- **Narrow evaluation scope** – Only MPE and SMACv1 are tested; more challenging tasks like MAMuJoCo and SMACv2 are ignored. SMACv1 has known issues, making SMACv2 more appropriate. Also, experiments are limited to environments with up to five agents; scaling to larger numbers is not demonstrated.
- **Missing hyperparameter sensitivity analysis** – The method uses several environment-independent thresholds (e.g., δ_recon) with no analysis of how they interact with the diffusion generation process or the bidirectional dynamics constraint. For instance, the paper does not explore how different values of δ_recon affect the diversity, quality, and final performance of augmented datasets. This makes it difficult to assess robustness and generalizability.
- **Computational complexity unaddressed** – MADiTS involves multiple models and steps (diffusion model training, trajectory generation, dynamics constraint verification, credit assignment) but provides no runtime analysis, breakdown of computational costs, or comparison with other augmentation methods. Vague descriptors like “until a satisfactory trajectory is obtained” obscure efficiency. Scaling of Integrated Gradients with the number of agents is not discussed.
- **Straightforward extension from single-agent** – Most techniques (except Integrated Gradients in Sec 4.2) appear to be directly adapted from single-agent diffusion-based RL, with states and actions replaced by global observations and actions. The naming “Stitch” is confusing because, unlike DiffStitch, the diffusion model conditions only on the first state, not both the first and last trajectory segments.
- **Unrealistic dataset generation** – The imbalanced dataset is created by perturbing expert trajectories with random noise. It is unclear why this approach is better than using a dataset collected by mixed policies (e.g., combining medium and expert trajectories), which would be more realistic. The impact of MADiTS on trajectory diversity is not discussed; the dynamics constraints and behavior correction may harm diversity.
- **Insufficient analysis of Integrated Gradients** – The paper lacks a quantitative demonstration of a general correlation between Integrated Gradients values and actual underperformance degrees across different environments. This makes the behavior correction step less grounded. Moreover, a concrete concern about regeneration effectiveness is raised: if the training dataset lacks joint trajectories where both agents have high contributions (e.g., a two-agent scenario with trajectories τ₁ and τ₂ where each agent has high contribution in only one trajectory), conditioning on one agent’s high-contribution trajectory to regenerate the other agent’s actions may not yield a better joint trajectory because the model was never trained on such combinations. The paper should discuss such limitations and requirements for the training data.
- **Missing scope** – The method is not evaluated on cooperative-competitive settings or on balanced-but-bad datasets (e.g., uniformly low rewards). The paper does not explicitly clarify which technique (e.g., temporal vs. spatial imbalance) solves which specific imbalance problem.
