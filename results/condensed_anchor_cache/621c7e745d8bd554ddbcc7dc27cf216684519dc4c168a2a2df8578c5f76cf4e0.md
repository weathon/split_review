## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper applies Mask R‑CNN to detect and segment malaria parasites in microscopic blood‑smear images across four Plasmodium species (P. falciparum, P. malariae, P. ovale, P. vivax). The authors collected and annotated a dataset of 971 images from Rwandan healthcare facilities and report good mean average precision (mAP) scores, notably mAP 0.9575 for P. vivax and mAP 0.9459 for P. malariae.

### Strengths
- Tackles a relevant societal problem (malaria diagnosis) and is easy to read.
- The dataset is quite large for a medical dataset and has been carefully annotated; this is valuable work that could be useful to others (though privacy concerns may prevent open release).
- Detection results appear to be good (decent mAP scores).
- The application of Mask R‑CNN to this multi‑species detection task may be new (reviewer 3’s positive perspective, though outweighed by severe novelty concerns).

### Weaknesses
- **Lack of novelty**: The paper is a straightforward application of a 7‑year‑old architecture (Mask R‑CNN) without any methodological adaptation. It resembles a project report rather than a conference‑ready contribution. The introduction over‑explains basic concepts (CNN, R‑CNN) and shows no awareness of recent advances (e.g., YOLOv5 is mentioned as if current).
- **No comparisons with state‑of‑the‑art or baseline methods**: There are no empirical comparisons against prior detection/segmentation approaches. The literature review mentions some methods, but no quantifiable results are provided; verbal claims that this approach shows “notable advances” are unsubstantiated. Without comparisons, it is impossible to judge whether Mask R‑CNN outperforms other models or to place the results.
- **No ablation studies or domain‑specific analyses**: No experiments to isolate the effect of different components. The paper provides no insights into why the dataset is challenging, where Mask R‑CNN succeeds or fails, or why other models might struggle. Only common‑knowledge justifications (e.g., “end‑to‑end”) are given.
- **Dataset description is insufficient**: The distribution of classes, distribution of parasites per image, and overall task difficulty are not made clear.
- **Missing experimental details**: The GPU used is not reported. Abbreviations (e.g., PM, PO) are not defined in the tables. Intersection‑over‑Union (IoU) results are not provided, even though Mask R‑CNN outputs instance‑level segmentation.
- **Reference issues**: Some references are missing, such as the WHO (World Health Organization) report.
- **Overall assessment**: The work may be valuable as engineering practice for medical professionals, but is not of interest to the machine‑learning community due to lack of novelty, missing evidence, and insufficient analysis. (Reviewer 2 is somewhat more positive about the dataset and results, but agrees on low novelty and lack of comparisons.)
