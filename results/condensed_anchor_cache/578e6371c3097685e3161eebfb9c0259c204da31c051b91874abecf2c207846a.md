## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes FEI, a faithfulness-guided ensemble interpretation method that targets faithfulness to hidden-layer representations using a differentiable approximation of perturbation metrics and ensemble over fractiles. Reviewer 1 (score 3, confidence 2) found the paper difficult to understand due to notation issues and reported mixed results: Q_P improved but Q_D not improved, and defense mechanisms worse than baselines. Reviewer 2 (score 5, confidence 2) found the method sensible and qualitative results reasonable but criticized outdated baselines and insufficient quantitative comparison, and noted limited familiarity with interpretable deep learning. Reviewer 3 (score 6, confidence 3) found the overlooked problem worth exploring and the ensemble method effective, but noted lack of in-depth visualization analysis and that EP could locate objects better than FEI.

### Strengths
- The method improves the Q_P metric over selected baselines (Reviewers 1, 3; Reviewer 2 notes reasonable qualitative results).
- Focus on faithfulness to hidden-layer representations is an overlooked problem worth exploring (Reviewer 3).
- The ensemble method effectively removes the need for hyper-parameters (Reviewer 3).
- Gradient clipping maintains internal faithfulness as shown with visualization results (Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- Section 3 is difficult to understand due to notation issues: misuse of dot product vs. Hadamard product in Eq. (2), inconsistent use of \(p\) and \(\alpha\), missing dependencies in Eq. (3) (Reviewer 1).
- The relation between faithfulness metrics and faithfulness optimization is unclear; specifically, how \(l_{faith}\) relates to Q_P and Q_D – it appears to only optimize perturbation based on Table 1 results (Reviewer 1).
- Results are mixed: Q_P improves, but Q_D does not improve, and defense mechanisms are worse than baseline (lower is better? text unclear) (Reviewer 1).
- Implementation details unclear: (a) consistency constraint \(\alpha_1 \le \alpha_2\) – could be parameterized as cumulative sum? (b) what is \(\bar{x}\) in Eq. (5)? Same as \(\tilde{x}\)? (c) Are gradients clipped at every single layer? If not, how is decomposition defined? (Reviewer 1).
- Baselines are outdated (Extremal Perturbation Fong et al. 2019, RISE Petsiuk et al. 2018, FGVis Wagner et al. 2019, GradCam Selvaraju et al. 2017, Integrated Gradient Sundararajan et al. 2017); quantitative comparison is insufficient and should include recently proposed competitors (Reviewer 2; note: Reviewer 2 is not fully familiar with the area but finds the comparison insufficient; willing to raise rating if others disagree – minority less confident negative).
- Lack of in-depth visualization analysis: EP can locate the Submarine better than FEI in qualitative examples; the paper does not analyze why certain regions are highlighted as ‘hot’ or ‘cold’, whether ‘hot’ areas consistently correspond to semantically meaningful parts crucial for the model’s decision, or whether ‘cold’ areas are truly irrelevant or play a subtle role. A more thorough investigation of attribution maps’ relationship with model’s internal representations is needed (Reviewer 3).
