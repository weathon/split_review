## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Adaptive Resolution Residual Networks (ARRNs) that use Laplacian residuals and Laplacian dropout to allow a single network to process signals at different resolutions. Laplacian residuals structure the network so that lower-resolution signals require fewer residuals for exact computation, reducing cost without retraining. Laplacian dropout is a training augmentation that randomly lowers the number of residuals, improving robustness of the compressed low-resolution versions. Reviewers broadly praise the clarity and motivation of the idea, but the majority strongly criticize the experiments for being limited to CIFAR at resolutions ≤32 × 32, question the novelty (calling it an extension of previous Laplacian pyramid work), and point to missing higher-resolution evaluations and comparisons with resolution-adaptive networks. One reviewer (score 6) is considerably more positive, finding the paper well-written and the comparisons adequate, while three reviewers (score 3 each) are much more critical.

### Strengths
- Having a single network process images at various resolutions is an important and well-motivated research topic (R1).
- The paper is well-written with clear figures; the idea that lower resolution requires fewer Laplacian residuals is natural and intuitive (R1).
- The proposed method is compared to many different prior architectures (R1).
- Using Laplacian residuals to replace standard residuals in CNNs is an interesting and reasonable approach (R2).
- The idea is simple and clear (R4).
- Experiments show effectiveness of the proposed algorithm on CIFAR datasets (R4).
- The algorithm has low computational cost and does not require retraining after compression (R4).
- Laplacian dropout improves robustness of compressed ARRNs (R3).

### Weaknesses
- **Resolution severely limited:** Experiments only show results for images at resolution below 32×32; the largest resolution tested is 32×32 (R1, R2). Standard ImageNet uses 224×224 and many state-of-the-art works evaluate at 384×384, so the lack of evaluation at higher resolutions (e.g., 256, 512) makes the method’s scalability and practical applicability unclear (R1, R2). One reviewer (R1) questions what challenges exist to extend the method to more practical resolutions.
- **Dataset insufficient:** Experiments are mainly on CIFAR‑10/100; results are not convincing without evaluation on more diverse datasets (e.g., ImageNet) (R1, R3). The method’s performance on datasets with different characteristics (complex textures, varying object sizes, noise) is unknown (R1). One reviewer (R3) explicitly requests experiments on ImageNet with more neural networks and also asks whether ARRNs can be used in other tasks like super-resolution.
- **Novelty questioned:** The method is seen as merely an extension of Lai 2017 and Singh 2021, applying Laplacian pyramids to signal resolution; the core idea of using Laplacian pyramids for resolution adaptation is not novel, and the paper does not sufficiently demonstrate a significant departure from previous works (R2). Another reviewer (R3) notes that related work on networks that do not assume fixed resolution (e.g., continuous implicit representations, papers [1-4] listed) is not discussed; this oversight undermines the claim that most methods assume fixed resolution.
- **Inference time concerns:** The inference time reported in Figure 7 is overwhelming given that the resolution is at most 32×32; it is unclear whether this is an implementation or hardware issue (R2). A comparison between standard residuals and Laplacian residuals is missing, making it difficult to assess true efficiency (R2).
- **Dependence on Laplacian dropout:** Without Laplacian dropout, performance drops quickly (R4). It is not clear whether the improved performance comes from the dropout itself or from the Laplacian residuals introduced in Section 4.2 (R4). This raises questions about the robustness of the core architecture.
- **Missing comparisons with resolution-adaptive networks:** The paper only compares with the approach of upsampling low-resolution inputs, not with other methods that adapt to resolution (R4). The experiments cannot show the superiority of ARRNs over existing resolution-adaptive networks (R4). One reviewer (R3) asks for examples of application scenarios where a single network must handle various resolutions.
- **Practicability concerns:** The algorithm can only deal with the resolutions that are contained in the feature maps, and performance degrades without Laplacian dropout (R4). The application scenario is unclear, especially since the method is only verified on very small images (e.g., 32×32) (R4).
- **Missing details and writing issues:**
  - How to set the filter kernels \(\phi^{low}_n\) is not explained (R4).
  - Typo on page 1, third paragraph of the introduction: “illustrated in ??” (R1, R3).
  - Missing reference in the first paragraph of Section 3.1 (two successive instances of “ideal”) (R4).
  - Page 1, bottom: “illustrated in ??” (R1, R2).
  - One reviewer (R3) asks for examples of application scenarios where a single network must handle various signal resolutions.
