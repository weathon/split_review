## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper studies layer pruning in LLMs, finding that large fractions of deeper layers (up to half) can be removed with minimal performance loss on multiple-choice QA benchmarks (e.g., MMLU). The pruning strategy identifies consecutive blocks via angular distance between last-token representations, then heals the model with QLoRA on a single A100. All reviewers agree the topic is relevant and the presentation is clear, but assessments diverge: one reviewer (rating 8) finds the findings novel and practically useful, while the other three (rating 6) have substantial reservations. The most critical reviewer (soundness 2) judges the ablations incomplete and the conclusions unconvincing. Common concerns center on the narrow evaluation scope (limited to single-token QA), the absence of control ablations (random/pruning of earlier layers), insufficient treatment of reasoning tasks (GSM8K relegated to the appendix), and lack of comparisons to established similarity metrics (CKA, SVCCA) and geometric analyses.

### Strengths
- The paper is clearly written and well-organized, with intuitive figures and a comprehensive appendix. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- The main observation—that large fractions of deeper layers (up to half) can be pruned without significant loss on knowledge-based benchmarks (e.g., MMLU)—is relevant and interesting. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- The use of simple, intuitive similarity metrics (angular distance between last-token representations) to identify prune-able layers is valuable and leads to an effective pruning strategy. (Reviewers 1, 2)
- The findings are consistent across multiple model families (e.g., Llama, Mistral, Phi-3) and show systematic differences between families, such as the fraction of layers at which “collapse” occurs. (Reviewer 4)
- With QLoRA-based healing, the approach achieves substantial reductions in memory and inference time using only a single A100 GPU, with potential practical impact. (Reviewers 1, 3)
- One reviewer (rating 8) highlighted the novelty and potential utility of the findings for reducing LLM memory footprint, considering the problem setup well-defined and the claims supported by a wide set of models. (Reviewer 3, minority positive view)

### Weaknesses
- **Limited task scope**: Experiments are almost entirely on multiple-choice QA benchmarks requiring a single generated token. The paper does not adequately evaluate more complex tasks requiring reasoning, planning, or open-ended generation. (Reviewers 2, 3, 4) Specifically, GSM8K results (showing deeper-layer pruning is not always beneficial) are relegated to the appendix, discussed only briefly (line 357, Fig. 7), and shown for only one model (Llama-70b), while other appendix figures use different models (e.g., Fig. 8 Llama-13b). (Reviewer 4) Reviewers request tests on text summarization, open-ended generation, and other reasoning/planning tasks. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Missing control ablations**: The claim that later layers are ineffective lacks convincing control comparisons. No random-layer pruning or pruning of earlier layers is performed to verify that resilience is specific to deeper layers; removing an equal proportion of random layers might yield similar results. (Reviewer 4) Additionally, the consecutive-block strategy is not compared to non-consecutive (e.g., greedy iterative) pruning. (Reviewer 1)
- **Incomplete comparison to existing methods**: The cosine-similarity metric is not compared to other established metrics for layer similarity (e.g., CKA, SVCCA). (Reviewer 1) Also, no quantitative efficiency comparison of the pruning method itself with representative methodologies is provided. (Reviewer 2)
- **Lack of connection to related geometric analyses**: The paper does not relate its layer-similarity findings to recent work on intrinsic dimensionality (Valeriani et al., NeurIPS 2023; Cheng et al., ACL 2023) and linearity (Park et al., NeurIPS 2023; Engels et al., 2024) in LLM representations. (Reviewer 1)
- **Overstated claims**: The title and some claims could be interpreted as “LLMs don’t require deep layers,” overemphasizing results given the limited evaluation. (Reviewer 2) Conclusions about the role of deeper layers (memorization vs. reasoning) are unclear and rushed, with the most interesting difference left under-explored. (Reviewer 4)
- **Missing qualitative analysis**: No qualitative generation examples are shown for the C4 perplexity results, which could help reveal when semantic generation collapses relative to QA performance degradation. (Reviewer 2)
- **Practical implications underexplored**: The paper does not discuss practical implications for improving LLM pre-training schemes beyond downstream efficiency. (Reviewer 2) The section on the role of deeper layers (Section 4.2) is underdeveloped and would benefit from more detailed discussion of Fig. 7 (GSM8K). (Reviewer 1)
