## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper addresses multi-task perception (drivable area segmentation and object detection) in unstructured environments using two techniques: Anti-Degradation Complementary (ADC) Learning, which alternates training between labeled and pseudo-labeled data across non-overlapping task annotations, and SAMEnhancer, which refines segmentation masks by fusing a trained model’s output with a Mobile SAM mask prompted by selected points. Experiments are conducted on IDD and BDD datasets. Reviewer opinions diverge: two reviewers (scores 1,1) view the paper as having minimal novelty, inadequate experiments, and flawed exposition; two reviewers (scores 3,3) acknowledge practical significance and some performance gains but still raise major concerns about missing comparisons, insufficient novelty, and lack of thorough evaluation.

### Strengths
- The problem of multi-task learning with non-overlapping task annotations in unstructured environments is novel and practically important. (R1, R2, R4)
- The ADC learning method in Section 3.1 is simple, logical, and easy to implement. (R1, R2)
- The experimental results on IDD and BDD datasets show some performance improvements for the joint segmentation and detection task. (R3, R4)
- The proposed techniques improve generalization performance. (R3)

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty and contribution**  
  - The approach is mostly a direct combination of existing methods (self-training with pseudo-labels and SAM) with no additional analysis. (R1)  
  - No comparison is made to prior multi-task learning or semi-supervised learning works. Many such approaches exist, so the authors cannot claim novelty. (R1, R2, R3)  
  - The observed improvement can be attributed simply to training on more data (unlabeled fold or SA-1B), which is not surprising. (R3)

- **Experiments are severely incomplete**  
  - No comparisons with state-of-the-art multi-task perception methods (e.g., YOLOP, YOLOPv2, HybridNets) on BDD or IDD are provided. (R4)  
  - No comparisons with SAM adapter/enhancement methods (e.g., Sam-adapter, Medical SAM adapter, Classwise-SAM-adapter, PA-SAM). (R4)  
  - No ablation studies on the key components (ADC learning, SAMEnhancer) are performed. Baseline comparison to simple semi-supervised learning without alternating pseudo-label training is missing. (R1, R2, R4)  
  - The two methods (ADC and SAMEnhancer) are never evaluated together, even though they are compatible. (R1)  
  - Only one primary experiment on IDD is presented, which is insufficient for comprehensive evaluation. (R2)  
  - Improvements over simply using non-overlapping training data appear marginal at best. (R1)  
  - Computational complexity (FLOPs, parameters, runtime, memory) is not reported, which is critical for autonomous driving. (R4)

- **Method explanations are unclear or unsupported**  
  - The motivation for the SAMEnhancer point‑prompting approach is not clear, and no supporting experiments justify the complexity. (R1, R2)  
  - The pseudo‑labeling in ADC learning lacks confidence filtering, which can lead to many erroneous predictions. (R2)  
  - The alternating optimization in ADC is not clearly differentiated from joint training (the encoder is still jointly trained while task heads are frozen). (R2)  
  - The “strong structural confidence” claim in Section 3.2.3 is not well explained. (R2)  
  - SAM was trained on the fully labeled SA-1B dataset, not self‑supervised; this should be clarified, and the role of SA-1B in the learning setup should be discussed. (R3)  
  - The SAMEnhancer effectively is post‑processing similar to CRF (DeepLab, PAMI 2018); a direct comparison to CRF in terms of performance and efficiency is missing. (R3)  
  - Visual examples (Fig. 4) still show imprecise predictions at road boundaries and shallows; no analysis of these failure cases is provided. (R4)

- **Missing related work and references**  
  - The prior work section is inadequate; it omits literature on semi‑supervised learning, multi‑task representation learning, SAM integration in domain‑specific pipelines, and relevant references on IDD and multi‑task architectures. (R1, R2, R4)

- **Writing and presentation**  
  - The methods section is poorly structured; the paper is well written only up to Section 3, and later parts appear rushed and incomplete. (R1, R2)  
  - The paper appears to be an incomplete submission. (R2)

- **Minority reviewer disagreement**  
  - Two reviewers (scores 1,1) consider the overall contribution and evaluation insufficient for acceptance, while two reviewers (scores 3,3) see some practical value but still require major improvements.
