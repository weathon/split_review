## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper proposes a method to visualize the evolution of scientific discourse over time using UMAP and text embeddings of paper abstracts from arXiv (cs.CL, 55k articles, 1995–2024). Embeddings are generated with GISTEmbed, projected into 3D with UMAP where time forms an additional axis, followed by qualitative exploratory analysis. All three reviewers gave a score of 1 (strong reject) with confidence 4–5.

### Strengths

- The text embedding–based approach to studying scientific discourse and evolution is a promising direction.
- The use of word embeddings and UMAP is valid, especially for large-scale textual data visualization.
- The approach could potentially serve as a useful tool for meta-scientific analysis or historiography (though this potential is not realized in the current paper).

### Weaknesses

- **Anonymity violation (minority, stronger negativity):** One reviewer noted that the paper is not anonymous and should be desk-rejected; the other two reviewers did not comment on this issue.
- **Lack of significant contribution:** The paper presents a qualitative exploratory analysis that does not represent a significant advancement beyond common practice of analyzing embeddings, which is performed routinely in various contexts.
- **Inadequate literature survey:** The manuscript misses essentially all important literature in science of science and scientometrics, including extensive prior work using embeddings to study scientific discourse (e.g., Nomic.ai, AllenAI, groups at Northwestern, UChicago, Indiana, Northeastern, Stanford). A thorough survey of existing work is required.
- **No justification for embedding model choice:** The selection of GISTEmbed is not justified, nor are comparisons provided with other embedding models to strengthen findings.
- **Unclear visualizations:** The visualizations lack clarity, making it difficult to extract meaningful information or concretely support claims about the evolution of research topics. Clearer examples are needed.
- **Misinterpretation of UMAP axes:** The paper uses UMAP axes as meaningful coordinates, which has been extensively critiqued (e.g., Pachter et al. 2021). The axes do not carry intrinsic semantic meaning.
- **Lack of comparison with alternative dimensionality reduction techniques:** The authors did not compare UMAP results with PCA or t-SNE, which is necessary to justify the choice.
- **No actionable insights or quantitative analysis:** The results fail to provide concrete, actionable insights; the paper would need a clearer research question and rigorous quantitative evaluation.
- **Missing contributions:** The paper does not make a meaningful contribution to representation learning or machine learning, and it is not a good fit for ICLR even if the above issues are addressed.
