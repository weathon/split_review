## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a probabilistic framework for generalizable Implicit Neural Representations (INRs), specifically neural radiance fields (NeRF), called Geometric Neural Processes (GeomNP). It formulates INR generalization from sparse observations as a probabilistic modeling problem, introducing learnable geometric bases (mixtures of 3D Gaussians with semantic latents) to align 2D context features with 3D target points, and hierarchical latent variables for multi-level modulation. Experiments on ShapeNet and DTU-MVS datasets show improved reconstruction over deterministic baselines like PixelNeRF, and the method extends to 2D image regression.

### Strengths
- **Novel probabilistic framing**: Formulating generalizable NeRF as a probabilistic problem is interesting and novel, enabling uncertainty estimation for downstream tasks (Reviewers 1, 2, 4, 6). 
- **Geometric Bases module**: The use of learnable mixtures of 3D Gaussians as geometric priors effectively aligns 2D-3D information and induces structure. Ablations (Table 3: sensitivity to number of bases; Table 4: significant impact) demonstrate its importance (Reviewers 2, 3, 5, 6).
- **Hierarchical latent variables**: Incorporating both local and globally averaged latent features enables modulation at different spatial levels, improving generalization (Reviewers 1, 3, 5).
- **Strong quantitative results**: Superior results on ShapeNet objects and DTU-MVS dataset compared to PixelNeRF (Reviewers 3, 5).
- **Extension to 2D signals**: The method seamlessly extends to 2D image regression, demonstrating broader applicability (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Presentation**: The paper is generally well-written and easy to follow (Reviewers 2, 3, 6).
- **Informative ablation study**: Key components and design choices are ablated (Reviewers 2, 5).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing comparisons with state-of-the-art methods**: The paper only compares with PixelNeRF (ICCV 2021) on DTU. Several recent generalizable NeRF methods are absent, making it difficult to assess relative performance. Missing baselines include: Splatter-Image, pixelSplat, MVSplat, LRM (Reviewer 2); IBRNet, MVSNeRF, and their subsequent works (Reviewer 5); PointNeRF (Reviewer 4); GPF (ICLR 2024), GeFu (CVPR 2024) (Reviewer 6); Sparsenerf, Freenerf (Reviewer 6); also probabilistic NeRF methods like DiffRF (2023) and Tewari et al. (2023) (Reviewer 4). Multiple reviewers (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6) strongly urge comparisons with these methods to validate contributions.
- **Evaluation on limited and small datasets**: Experiments are restricted to ShapeNet and DTU. Missing evaluations on larger, more diverse benchmarks like Objaverse, Google-Scanned Objects, Co3D (Reviewer 2), and NeRF synthetic / MipNeRF-360 datasets (Reviewer 5). This limits assessment of scalability and generalization to complex scenes (Reviewers 2, 5).
- **Clarity and paper structure issues**: 
  - The paper’s main goal is not immediately clear: is it a generic framework for INR generalization or primarily a NeRF reconstruction method? The focus on a single downstream application conflicts with the claimed generality (Reviewers 1, 4).
  - Technical terms like “2D context sets,” “target sets,” “3D NeRF fusing” are misleading; using “camera rays” and “2D pixels” consistently would improve clarity (Reviewer 5).
  - The modulation layer implementation needs clearer explanation, e.g., a pseudocode or more detailed illustration of training and inference (Reviewer 5).
  - The experimental setup for uncertainty estimation (L.522) is not described, making it unclear whether the predicted uncertainty is reasonable (Reviewer 4).
  - Pre-training details for the 2D part are not clarified—is it pre-trained on a variety of objects? (Reviewer 5)
- **Insufficient experimental evidence for claimed contributions**:
  - No cross-category evaluation to test if geometric priors transfer to unseen categories (Reviewer 4).
  - Qualitative results for hierarchical ablation are missing (Reviewer 4).
  - The ablation on geometric bases (L.485) is conducted on 64×64 images, which is too low resolution to be convincing (Reviewer 4).
  - Only single novel view is shown per object; multi-view qualitative comparisons are needed (Reviewer 5, 6).
  - Individual results for each DTU object are needed (Reviewer 5).
  - No videos of 3D reconstructions or novel view synthesis are provided (Reviewer 6).
- **Computational cost analysis missing**: No comparison of training time, inference cost, or model parameters with baselines, despite the probabilistic framework likely being more resource-intensive (Reviewers 2, 3). Reviewer 3 specifically asks for comparison of computational cost with other probabilistic methods.
- **Missing discussion of related work**: Misclassifies Erkoc et al. 2023 as deterministic, though it uses a diffusion approach (Reviewer 4). Several relevant 2024 works (GPF, GeFu) are omitted (Reviewer 6). The discussion of how existing methods handle structural information is insufficient (Reviewer 1).
- **Potential unnecessary complexity for 2D tasks**: Extending the probabilistic framework to simpler 2D tasks may introduce unnecessary complexity when less resource-intensive models could suffice (Reviewer 3).
- **Table 4**: The best PSNR should be highlighted, and it is unclear which subset of Lamps is used (Reviewers 4, 5).
- **Reviewer 1 (minority negative)**: The paper’s goal is confusing (reconstruction vs. generation); single-view evaluation does not match claims of leveraging multiple signals; the claim that prior methods do not consider structural information is not fully accurate and lacks experimental support.
- **Remaining reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms**:
  - Can the method be applied to Gaussian splatting representations? (Reviewer 3)
  - How does diversity of training scenes affect generalization? (Reviewer 3)
  - Effect of partial occlusions or noise? (Reviewer 3)
  - How exactly is the method incorporated into PixelNeRF? (Reviewer 4, L.420)
  - Which types of geometric descriptors (implicit vs. explicit) are superior? (Reviewer 6)
  - If more views are used, can the method match performance of conventional generalizable NeRF methods? (Reviewer 6)
