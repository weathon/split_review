## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Magnetic Mirror Descent Self-Play Preference Optimization (MDSPO/MPO) for RLHF, formulating preference learning as a two-player constant-sum game to handle non-transitive preferences. It introduces a magnetic term in mirror descent to achieve linear last-iterate convergence to the Nash equilibrium (NE) of the original game (not just the regularized one). Practical implementations (MDSPO and MDSPO-RT) are given, and experiments on MixEval-Hard show improvement over the SFT baseline. Reviewers noted the novelty of last-iterate convergence guarantees and practical relevance, but raised concerns about presentation, missing baselines, unclear theory–practice connection, and lack of analysis for convergence to the original NE.

### Strengths
- Achieves last‑iterate convergence to the NE of the original game under general (non‑transitive) preferences, overcoming the average‑iterate convergence of prior work (Reviewers 1,2,3,4).  
- First theoretical result for last‑iterate convergence to the original NE (previous results were limited to the regularized game) (Reviewer 4).  
- Linear convergence rate makes the method suitable for fine‑tuning LLMs (Reviewers 2,4).  
- Practical benefits: reduced inference time and lower variance (Reviewer 4).  
- Empirical performance on MixEval‑Hard is significantly better than the SFT baseline (Reviewers 2,3).  
- Extension from SPO to MDSPO is well‑motivated; the idea of periodically updating the magnet policy is novel and comes with convergence proofs (Reviewer 3).  
- Provides two algorithms (MDSPO and MDSPO‑RT) and empirical comparison (Reviewer 3).  
- Studies the Nash learning setting, which is broader than the Bradley‑Terry assumption (Reviewer 4).  

*Minority contrast:* One reviewer (score 8) was particularly positive about the last‑iterate guarantee and performance improvement, while the other three reviewers (score 6 each) were more measured in their assessment.

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and clarity:** The mathematical/technical exposition needs improvement (Reviewer 1).  
- **Uniqueness assumption (line 145):** The paper states the constant‑sum game has a unique symmetric NE; this does not hold for arbitrary constant‑sum games. The role of uniqueness in the proofs should be clarified, and the need for this assumption should be justified (Reviewer 1).  
- **Motivation for magnetic term:** The reason for choosing magnetic mirror descent (MMD) over other last‑iterate methods (e.g., optimistic mirror descent) is unclear. A discussion of the technical challenge that MMD solves would deepen understanding (Reviewers 2,3).  
- **Experiment baselines:** Only the SFT baseline is used. For a comprehensive evaluation, comparisons with RLHF‑PPO should be added (Reviewer 2).  
- **Connection between theory and implementation:** It is unclear how the MMD objective in equation (6) is derived from the theoretical update. Specifically, the first term on the right‑hand side seems unrelated to the policy \(\pi\) in the MMD update and might be ignored; a more detailed derivation or intuitive explanation is needed (Reviewer 3).  
- **Guidance for MDSPO vs. MDSPO‑RT:** The paper states the choice depends on task requirements, but no concrete guidelines are given. The only empirical result shows MDSPO‑RT performing worse; more insight into when each algorithm excels would be beneficial (Reviewer 3).  
- **Missing empirical analysis of average‑iterate issue:** Since the paper is motivated by overcoming average‑iterate convergence, empirical observations of this phenomenon and a direct comparison between SPO and MDSPO are essential. The work would be significantly strengthened by demonstrating how MDSPO alleviates the problem (Reviewer 3).  
- **Attribution of Theorem 3.2:** This theorem closely follows the derivation of Theorem 3.4 in [1]. The citation should be given explicitly before presenting the theorem (Reviewer 3).  
- **Proof details in Lemma 3.3 and Theorem 3.4:** Several steps are unclear or contain typos. For example, line 976 has an ill‑defined inner product, line 992 misapplies the first‑order optimality condition, and lines 998‑999 contain incorrect variable substitutions (Reviewer 3).  
- **Convergence rate to the original NE:** The rate of convergence to the original NE is not studied. The authors should either provide such an analysis or explain why it is challenging (Reviewer 4).  
- **Approximation error in Section 3.2:** The approach requires the exact NE of the regularized game, which is not available in practice; MMD only gives an approximation. The convergence result under approximation error is not addressed (Reviewer 4).  
- **Comparison to Nash‑MD:** Can the Nash‑MD algorithm from Munos et al. be adapted to the sequential scheme of Section 3.2 to achieve last‑iterate convergence to the original NE? This comparison is missing (Reviewer 4).  
- **Minor:** Typographical error in line 116: should be \(y_{<t}\) instead of \(y_{<k}\) (Reviewer 2).
