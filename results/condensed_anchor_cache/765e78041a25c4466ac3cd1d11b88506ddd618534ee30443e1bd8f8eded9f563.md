## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes AnomalyCLIP, a zero-shot anomaly detection (ZSAD) method that adapts CLIP via object-agnostic text prompt learning. It uses learnable prompt tokens for normality and abnormality, a DPAM (V-V self-attention) mechanism, and both global and local (glocal) context optimization. Evaluated on 17 real-world datasets (industrial defect and medical imaging), it outperforms prior ZSAD baselines. Reviewers note strong empirical breadth, a sound motivation, and clear writing, but raise concerns about the object-agnostic assumption, DPAM’s rationale, ablation fairness, missing comparisons, and computational overhead.

### Strengths
- The paper is the first to apply prompt learning for CLIP-like VLMs in zero-shot anomaly detection, a relatively under-explored direction.
- The object-agnostic prompt design is a novel and creative departure from traditional semantic-based methods, enabling generalization across diverse object classes without prompt redesign.
- The methodology is robust and the empirical evaluation is extensive: validated across 17 diverse datasets (industrial and medical), with comprehensive ablation studies for each component.
- The approach achieves remarkable ZSAD performance, outperforming existing baselines by clear margins.
- The contribution is clearly motivated and well-organized; the paper is easy to follow.
- The work is a notable advancement in the field, with potential real-world impact in domains where training data is unavailable.

### Weaknesses
- **Object-agnostic assumption may be too optimistic.** The paper assumes anomalies are object-agnostic, which can fail in cases where normal patterns resemble anomalies (e.g., scratch-like textures that are normal for a product). A discussion of successful vs. failure cases is missing (Reviewer 4).
- **Prompt template “damaged [cls]” is restrictive.** It may not represent all anomaly types (e.g., missing components) or generalize well beyond defect inspection to other domains (e.g., medical). The paper does not justify why this template is suitable for diverse scenarios (Reviewer 1, also a question from Reviewer 4 about why two prompts vs. one).
- **DPAM (V-V attention) lacks theoretical justification.** It is unclear why replacing Q-K attention with V-V attention (without updating visual encoder parameters) preserves more localized information. Relying on a single example (Figure 3) is insufficient; the paper should show results of original Q-K attention and consider an ensemble of Q-Q, K-K, V-V, and Q-K (Reviewers 1, 3, 5). The similarity of Q-Q, K-K, V-V variants is not explained (Reviewer 3).
- **Computation overhead not discussed.** Feeding the same image into two visual encoders (Figure 2) introduces additional cost; training cost is also not discussed (Reviewers 1, 6).
- **Ablation studies are unclear and potentially unfair.**
  - Incremental addition (T1–T4, Table 4) is less informative than removal; e.g., a setting with T2+T3+T4 without T1 (DPAM) is needed. The reported AUC increase from 47.9 to 54.8 for T1 (DPAM alone) is not significant (both near random for a two-class problem) (Reviewer 5).
  - In Table 5, it is unclear how the model is optimized when both global and local losses are absent (Reviewer 5).
  - The comparison to CoOp uses a different prompt template for normality and abnormality (e.g., [normal][cls] vs. [anomalous][cls]), making it an unfair comparison (Reviewer 3).
- **Missing comparisons with concurrent/related work.** The paper should compare with AnomalyGPT (Gu et al., 2023) and other recent zero-shot AD methods (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Missing details and clarifications.**
  - The term “glocal” is introduced without explanation (Reviewer 2).
  - Symbols Sn and Sa (used in L_local) are defined after first use (Reviewer 4).
  - Figure 2 caption lacks explanation; Figure 6 feels confusing and unnecessary (Reviewer 6).
  - No discussion on why Focal and Dice losses are necessary (Reviewer 6).
  - No comparison of different ViT backbones of CLIP (Reviewer 6).
- **Minor reviewer questions that overlap with criticisms:**
  - Why are two text prompts (normal vs. anomalous) needed instead of one? (Reviewer 4)
  - Why are object-agnostic prompts better than object-aware prompts for a specific class? (Reviewer 6)
  - Are hidden layer visual embeddings used only for the segmentation map, while the final output embedding only for the image-level score? (Reviewer 6)
