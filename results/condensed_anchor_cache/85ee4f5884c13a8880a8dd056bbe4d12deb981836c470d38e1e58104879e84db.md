## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper investigates compositionality failures in text-to-image diffusion models (Stable Diffusion v1.4, v2). The authors identify two sources: (1) CLIP text encoder’s attention module assigns erroneous attention contributions from unrelated tokens, and (2) CLIP’s output space is sub‑optimal for driving the UNet. They propose two patches: a zero‑shot attention reweighting and a window‑based linear projection (WiCLP) fine‑tuned on a small set of compositional image‑text pairs. The approach aims to improve composition of attributes (color, texture, shape) without harming FID score.

### Strengths
- The problem is well motivated and practically important.
- The analysis of CLIP text embeddings is detailed and correctly pinpoints the root cause of compositionality issues in SD 1.4 / 2.0.
- The proposed methods are simple and easy to implement.
- The methods are validated to be effective, showing clear improvements in color, texture, and shape (Table 1) while maintaining FID.
- Figures (e.g., attention maps) clearly illustrate the core ideas.
- The paper is easy to follow.
- The zero-shot attention reweighting and the window-based linear projection both contribute to improved compositional alignment.

### Weaknesses
- **Limited innovation and justification**: Fine-tuning a linear projection on top of CLIP text embeddings is not new. The paper does not sufficiently justify why this specific approach is the most effective compared to other fine‑tuning strategies (e.g., fine‑tuning the U‑Net). The aggregation of neighboring tokens lacks theoretical depth. [R3]
- **Simplistic locality assumption**: The window‑based projection assumes semantically related tokens always appear within a local window. This fails for complex sentences with long‑distance relations (e.g., “three cubes, the color of which are red, green, blue”). The method may not fundamentally solve the attention assignment problem and a more general parsing method is needed. [R4]
- **Struggles with numerical composition**: The method often generates correct attributes but the wrong number of objects, even for simple prompts like “one A one B”. No failure‑case visualizations are provided. [R4]
- **Outdated baselines and missing comparisons**: The paper uses SD v2 as baseline, which is now outdated. No qualitative or quantitative results are compared with SDXL, SD 3.5, or FLUX. Comparisons with recent compositional generation methods (e.g., RPG [1], Realcompo [2]) are absent. This makes it difficult to gauge the relative merit of the approach. Several reviewers noted that newer models handle composition much better, suggesting the root issue is in the old models’ design/training rather than a fundamental CLIP limitation. [R1, R3]
- **Limited scope of experiments**: The evaluation is restricted to a few attribute types (color, texture, shape). Handling more diverse composition types would require more manual data collection. Fine‑tuning only text embeddings (fixing U‑Net and text encoder) leaves open whether adjusting other components could yield similar or better improvements. [R1, R3]
- **Practical concerns**: Training WiCLP requires 25K steps on one RTX A5000 GPU (Appendix D.1) and access to the original training data. The trained layer may not generalize to other fine‑tuned/stylized diffusion models. A training‑free alternative (e.g., reweighting tokens with “:1.21” syntax) is widely used and should have been discussed. [R2]
- **Limited impact for modern architectures**: The proposed method focuses on CLIP‑based models, but most current models (e.g., SD3, Flux) use T5‑based encoders. It is uncertain whether the approach can be adapted to T5 or would produce comparable results. [R3]
- **Presentation issues**: Quotation marks are inconsistently formatted; Figure 5 lacks labels on the left; Figure 7 is low resolution. [R3]
- **Minority reviewer disagreement**: One reviewer (score 3) was significantly more critical about innovation, comparisons, presentation, and impact, while three other reviewers (score 5) found the paper acceptable despite noted limitations. [R3 vs. R1, R2, R4]
- **Missing discussions and questions**: The paper does not compare with alternative fine‑tuning strategies or examine whether fine‑tuning other components (e.g., U‑Net) could be more effective. The question of how to adapt the method to T5‑based diffusion models (e.g., Pixart‑Alpha) is raised but left unanswered. [R3, R4]
