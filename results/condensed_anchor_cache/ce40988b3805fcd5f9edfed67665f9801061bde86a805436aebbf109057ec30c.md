## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces CL-MFAP, an unsupervised contrastive learning-based multimodal foundation model for predicting antibiotic properties of small molecules. It uses three molecular modalities (SMILES strings, molecular graphs, and Morgan fingerprints) processed by dedicated encoders (transformer with RoPE, graph transformer encoder with bi-level routing attention, and MLP, respectively), jointly pretrained on 1.6 million bioactive molecules from ChEMBL. The model is evaluated on five downstream datasets and outperforms three molecular language model baselines on three of them. The reviewers note that the problem is important and the idea of adapting bi-level routing attention to molecular graphs is interesting, but raise concerns about limited baselines, lack of standard deviations, incomplete ablation studies, unclear justification for antibiotic specificity, and presentation clarity.

### Strengths
- **Integration of RoPE and BRA** enhances the model's ability to capture long-range dependencies and molecular structure in a multimodal contrastive learning setting. The BRA is used jointly with MPNN and graph transformer encoder, and ablation studies of these sub-model combinations are performed and included as baselines.
- **Adaptation of bi-level routing attention to molecular graphs** is an interesting and potentially beneficial contribution, originally from computer vision.
- **Exploration of the architecture space** within the contrastive learning framework for molecular property prediction: different architectures were tested to select the best model.
- **Evaluation on multiple downstream tasks** with notable improvement over the chosen baselines; the datasets are well-studied and relevant for antibiotic discovery.
- **Clear motivation** in the introduction and related work; Figure 1 effectively explains the proposed model.
- **Publicly available code** (though see weaknesses).

### Weaknesses

**Experimental rigor and baselines**
- **Limited baselines:** Out of 7 baselines, 4 are variants of the proposed model; only 3 are from other authors (MolFormer, MolBERT, ChemBERTa-2). None of the baselines are multimodal, and no GNN-only, fingerprint-only, or contrastive learning pre-trained model (e.g., MolCLR) is compared. Adding such baselines (including an MLP on fingerprints, a GNN for graphs, and a contrastive model) is needed.  
- **No standard deviations or confidence intervals** reported for any results; multiple independent runs (3–5) should be performed and reported to assess statistical significance.  
- **Ablation study incomplete:** It only varies components of the molecular graph encoder (CL-BL1–4). Missing ablations on (i) the contribution of each modality (removing SMILES, graphs, or fingerprints), (ii) whether pre-training helps over training from scratch for each downstream task, and (iii) whether more general models (MolFormer, ChemBERTa-2) fine-tuned on the same pre-training data would match performance.  
- **Data splitting method not described; data collection method not explained** (e.g., what does “collected bacterial-related compounds targeting the specific domain of antibiotics from ChEMBL” mean?).  
- **Concern about label leakage / dataset overlap** between pre-training and downstream datasets.

**Relevance and justification**
- The architecture and contrastive learning framework are general, not specific to antibiotics. The paper lacks justification for why this method is especially suited for antibiotic property prediction (vs. any other small molecule drug). If no antibiotic-specific inductive bias exists, evaluation on general benchmarks like MoleculeNet or Therapeutic Data Commons is expected.  
- Most downstream datasets (beyond MIC) are not antibiotic-specific, weakening the claim that the model is “tailored for discovering small molecules with potential antibiotic properties.”

**Presentation and clarity**
- The “Proposed Approach” section is difficult to follow. Notation in equations is undefined or inconsistently introduced (e.g., \(m,n\) in Eq.1, \(k_n\) in Eq.2, \(R_{\Theta,i}^d\) in Eq.3; symbol \(\varphi\) introduced but unused). If these cannot be explained, they should be removed and replaced with references to original works.  
- The bi-level routing attention equations are copied from Dong et al. / Zhu et al. without proper explanation or definition in the context of molecular graphs. Typos exist (e.g., “feature map \(x\), \(X\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times C}\)”).  
- Algorithms (especially Algorithm 2) use undefined functions (e.g., `computeLoss`, `Windowing`, `Routing`, etc.) and appear to differ from Equation 9. The two `if` conditions in Algorithm 2 lines 4 and 6 are identical (typo). Parameter values like window size \(S\) are not explained.  
- Figures 2 and 3 have very small font sizes; Figure 2 would be more readable as a 2D barplot.  
- The paper uses the ICLR 2024 template, not the latest 2025 version.  
- A graphical depiction of the Window-to-Window Level Routing and Pixel-to-Pixel Level Attention, as well as the combination of BRA, GTE, and MPNN substructures, would help understanding.  
- Jargon is used without sufficient definitions; a glossary or more contextual explanations would benefit non-expert readers.

**Technical depth and novelty**
- The model is a combination of known techniques (multimodal molecular representations + contrastive learning). The technical novelty is limited.  
- The contrastive loss is the vanilla form of classical contrastive learning; the paper does not explain how this framework uniquely applies to antibiotic property prediction compared to existing methodologies.  
- The explanation of bi-level routing attention is vague; its adaptation to graphs is not clearly described (e.g., how the concepts of 2D feature map and pixel apply to graph topology).  
- It is unclear why BRA should perform well for molecular graphs, given that it was designed for large images and most antibiotic small molecules are small; the advantage over graph transformers for capturing long-range dependencies is not argued.

**Code and reproducibility**
- The GitHub repository lacks instructions on how to run the code or install dependencies; no license file is provided.  
- Code may not be intuitive; including easy-to-test data or a Jupyter Notebook would help replicability.

**Additional missing details**
- The fingerprint type and critical parameters (number of bits, radius) are not described (ECFP mentioned only in appendix as a baseline, not for the model’s fingerprint encoder).  
- No biological justification or visualization of the results (e.g., case studies of compounds correctly/incorrectly predicted).  
- RePRA scores in the results lack proper contextualization and interpretation.

**Minor issues**
- Positive pairs on page 6 should be [x1, y1] instead of [x1, y2].  
- Some statements make sweeping claims about model performance without adequate evidence or context.
