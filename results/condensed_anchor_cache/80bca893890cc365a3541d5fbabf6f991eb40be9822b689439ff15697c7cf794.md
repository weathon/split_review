## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes Distance-Aware Noise-Sample Matching (DANSM) to speed up training of rectified flow and diffusion models by lengthening inter-path distances. DANSM is motivated by the observation that well-trained models use preferable noise-sample pairs with fewer path crossings in 2D visualizations, indicating longer inter-path distance in high dimensions. The method derives a closed-form formula from rectified flow models and simplifies optimization by relating inter-path distance to path length. Experiments on CIFAR and LSUN-BEDROOM (image and latent space) show 30%–40% training speedup without sacrificing generation quality.

### Strengths
- The idea is theoretically motivated and sound, with a simple and straightforward implementation. Claims are backed by empirical evaluations.
- A clear diagram (reviewers highlight figure clarity) illustrates how different diffusion models produce similar outputs from the same noise, identifying “preferable noise-sample pairs.”
- DANSM significantly speeds up training by 30%–40% without quality loss, offering new insights into diffusion model efficiency.
- The approach leverages closed-form formulas from rectified flow models, enabling efficient optimization without architectural changes, making integration easy.
- The optimization surrogate relating inter-path distance to path length is a helpful simplification.

### Weaknesses
- **Presentation and reproducibility:** One reviewer finds the main idea unclear—the paper heavily emphasizes “consistent model reproducibility” then abruptly transitions to training speed, making the core contribution ambiguous. The same reviewer states that Algorithm 1 cannot be effectively reproduced with the provided steps; mathematical formulations in Sections 3.3 and 4.2 are needed to rigorously explain how the permutation σ is optimized and how this relates to the overall training objective. The computational cost and complexity of the algorithm are not discussed.
- **Experimental scope and visual results:** 
  - FID evaluations use very few sampling steps; multiple reviewers request comparisons across a range of sampling steps (including longer ones) to verify that quality does not degrade at convergence. Visualizations (e.g., decoded samples from SD) are also desired.
  - Validation is limited to CIFAR and LSUN-BEDROOM. Reviewers request broader datasets (ImageNet, FFHQ) and higher-resolution experiments, including comparisons with more SD models. 
  - No visual comparison of generated images is provided; this is needed to complement FID, especially when quantitative differences are small.
  - Ablation studies analyzing the impact of different components of DANSM are absent.
- **Missing comparison to related method:** The paper lacks empirical comparison to “Immiscible Diffusion” (though differences are discussed in text), a closely related approach.
- **Plot quality issues:** One reviewer notes that Figure 6(b), Figure 7(b), and another figure (likely Fig. 6b) have lines starting from arbitrary locations, making interpretation difficult. In Figure 6(b) (CIFAR? unclear) the method shows no clear training speed gains, raising questions about its effectiveness on that dataset.
- **Conceptual questions raised by reviewers:** 
  - Why is it necessary to learn noise-sample pairs that are well-aligned in t-SNE space? The reasoning for preferring straight flow matching is not fully explained.
  - Whether “consistent model reproducibility” is beneficial or detrimental for diffusion models is not addressed—it might indicate limited generation patterns due to Gaussian isotropy.
- **Minor:** One typo noted: line 198/199 “To analysis” (should be “analyse”), line 309/310 “have not been evaluation” (should be “evaluated”).
