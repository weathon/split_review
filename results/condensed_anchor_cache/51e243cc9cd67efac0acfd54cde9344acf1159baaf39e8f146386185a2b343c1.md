## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SparseLGS, a feed-forward method for constructing 3D semantic fields from sparse (e.g., two) viewpoints using 3D Gaussian Splatting (based on MVSplat), video tracking for consistent SAM segmentations, and a multi-view language memory bank (MV-LMB) that links low-dimensional 3D IDs to high-dimensional CLIP features for efficient open-vocabulary querying. It is evaluated on the LERF and 3D-OVS datasets for object localization and segmentation.

### Strengths
- Well-written and easy to follow. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- First feed-forward method for 3D semantic field learning, avoiding per-scene multi-view optimization and enabling direct inference. (Reviewer 2)
- Impressive quantitative results: achieves SOTA under sparse-view configurations, outperforming existing methods in chosen IoU, localization accuracy, and mIoU on LERF and 3D-OVS. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- Fast inference: scene construction in under 30 seconds and open-vocabulary querying in 0.011 seconds per query. (Reviewer 2)
- The multi-view language memory bank improves query efficiency. (Reviewer 3)
- Integration of MVSplat, SAM, video object tracking, and LangSplat is effective. (Reviewer 3)
- Evaluation on 3D-OVS shows promising results. (Reviewer 3)

### Weaknesses
**Limited Technical Novelty (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)**  
The method is largely a combination of existing components (MVSplat, SAM, video tracking, CLIP, LangSplat) without significant modifications. The sparse-view capability entirely derives from MVSplat; mask association uses standard video tracking (similar to GaussianGroup). The only novel part—the multi-view language memory bank—is considered incremental. The core idea of projecting 2D features into 3D has been explored previously. A dissenting reviewer (score 3) found the technical contributions limited and the ablation experiments unconvincing.

**Insufficient and Potentially Unfair Comparisons (Reviewers 2, 3, 4)**  
- Only one Gaussian-based method (LangSplat) is compared; many recent open-source baselines are omitted: OpenGaussian, SplatLoc, FastLGS, LEGaussians, GaussianGroup. (Reviewers 3, 4)  
- The comparison with LangSplat may be unfair: in the provided video, LangSplat’s RGB rendering is poor (appearance and semantics are coupled), so SparseLGS’s advantage may come from MVSplat’s better geometry/appearance rather than from the semantic embedding contribution. (Reviewer 4)  
- The number of views used to train the compared LangSplat baseline is unclear; the reported performance is much lower than in the original LangSplat paper. (Reviewer 2)

**Confusing and Incomplete Ablation Study (Reviewers 3, 4)**  
- In Table 5, adding the feed-forward model (MVSplat) to LangSplat degrades performance drastically; the authors attribute this to “semantic feature inconsistencies” but do not explain clearly. The Mask Association module (designed to address this) makes results even worse. (Reviewer 3)  
- The ablation lacks a baseline without MVSplat (i.e., using only the feed-forward model without MA and MV-LMB) to isolate contributions. (Reviewer 4)  
- It is unclear what the base configuration is and how metrics are computed in other ablation conditions. (Reviewer 4)

**Unclear Experimental Setup and Metrics (Reviewer 4)**  
- The reported metrics are inconsistent with the official metrics of comparison methods, but no details are given on how new metrics were obtained; presumably they were re-measured on two views, but this is not documented. (Reviewer 4)  
- Table 6 comparing time efficiency with NeRF methods is unnecessary; speed advantage over NeRF has already been demonstrated for Gaussians in prior work. (Reviewer 4)

**Robustness and Scope Concerns (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)**  
- The method relies on a video tracking model; robustness to tracking errors is not verified, and incorrect tracking could severely affect performance. (Reviewer 2)  
- The method is demonstrated only on two views; it is unclear whether it can support more views or if learning semantic fields from only two views is fundamentally limited. (Reviewer 2)  
- In Table 6, 2D Query achieves higher Chosen IoU than the proposed method; no intuition is provided for this result. (Reviewer 1)  
- In Figure 7, “chopsticks and napkins” are treated as one object; it is unclear whether this is due to the manually set parameter N (number of objects) and how sensitivity to N affects results. (Reviewer 4)  
- The need to train a Res Block and MLP for semantic parameters instead of simply dividing RGB space into N parts is not justified. (Reviewer 4)

**Overall:** While the paper demonstrates strong empirical performance and speed, the technical novelty is considered limited by multiple reviewers. The comparison and ablation studies are insufficient to convincingly isolate the contributions. The method relies heavily on pre-trained models without significant adaptation, and several experimental details remain unclear.
