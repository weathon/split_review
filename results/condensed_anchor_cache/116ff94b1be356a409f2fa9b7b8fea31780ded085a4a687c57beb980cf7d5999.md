## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Spectraformer, a unified framework for kernel-based linearized attention that recombines existing component functions and weight matrices from random-feature methods (e.g., Trig, ReLU, exp, softplus, and various weight matrix constructions including Gaussian, Orthogonal, QMC, learnable). It tests combinations on three tasks from the LRA benchmark, concluding that no single combination dominates and kernel choice is task-dependent.

### Strengths
- **Unification exposes gaps**: The framework systematically covers past work (e.g., Performer, FAVOR+, PRF, etc.) and reveals previously untested combinations that show promise on some LRA tasks (R1, R3, R4).
- **Neat idea of learnable projections**: Representing weight vectors as learnable (rather than fixed-sampled) is a sound extension (R2).
- **Comprehensive comparison**: The experimental section includes a wide range of related methods (Performer, FLASH, etc.) and several kernel variants (R2).
- **Sound extension**: The mathematical formulation (Eq. 11) is general and covers many prior low-rank attention mechanisms (R2).
- **Well-written and easy to follow** (R1).
- **Interesting observation**: No single random-feature method excels across all tasks, highlighting task-specific sensitivity (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty is limited**: The work mainly recombines component functions and weight matrices from prior work (Chowdhury et al. 2022 and others) without introducing new theoretical results or novel attention mechanisms (R1, R3, R4). One reviewer (Score 6) found the extension sound but acknowledged the limited novelty.
- **Empirical evaluation insufficient**:
    - Only evaluated on the LRA benchmark, which is outdated and insufficient to draw general conclusions (R1, R2, R4).
    - Missing evaluation on more recent benchmarks, multiple modalities, and tasks such as vision or language modeling (R1, R2).
    - No direct comparison of how well the kernel approximations approximate the true softmax kernel (e.g., approximation error) (R4).
    - No quality-gap analysis compared to full-attention transformers (e.g., Performer-ViT vs. standard ViT) (R4).
    - The paper does not show that any combination closes the quality gap with standard softmax attention (R4).
- **Vague and uninformative conclusions**: The main takeaway (“different kernels are good at different tasks”) provides no actionable guidance for practitioners. No clear recommendation emerges (R1, R2, R3, R4). Specifically, there is no guideline for selecting the best combination before training, nor is there analysis of when/why certain combinations might be advantageous (R3, R4).
- **Missing computational cost analysis**: The additional parameters introduced by learnable weight matrices may increase memory and compute footprint; this is not discussed (R2). The question of extra computational and memory footprint (R2) is unaddressed.
- **Unbiasedness of Eq. 11**: The paper does not clarify whether a subset of the new instantiations (beyond those already known) still provides unbiased estimation of the softmax kernel (R2).
- **QMC components are incremental**: The use of Quasi-Monte Carlo and structured matrices (Orthogonal, QMC) is presented without theoretical guarantees on variance reduction compared to prior orthogonal random features (R4). Related work (Avron et al. 2016, Choromanski et al. 2022) is cited but not leveraged for new theory.
- **Writing and presentation issues**:
    - Section 3.4 describes pseudocode but should provide a clear pseudocode block instead of referring to specific code lines (R4).
    - Key concepts (e.g., “valid component function”) are mentioned before being formally defined (R4).
- **Missing analysis of softmax mimicry**: The paper does not investigate whether training the kernel to mimic the spikiness of softmax (as in Hedgehog & Porcupine, Zhang et al. 2024) would improve performance (R4).
- **Minority reviewer contrast**: One reviewer (Score 6) was more positive, emphasizing the practical importance of fast inference and the neatness of learnable projections, but still noted the vague conclusions, insufficient benchmarks, and missing computational cost discussion. The other three reviewers (Scores 3, 3, 3) found novelty marginal and evaluation insufficient.
