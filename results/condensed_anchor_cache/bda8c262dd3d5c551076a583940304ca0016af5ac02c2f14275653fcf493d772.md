## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces Model Folding, a data-free and fine-tuning-free compression method that merges structurally similar neurons (rows/channels) via k-means clustering and repairs activation statistics using two data-free procedures (Fold-AR and Fold-DIR, the latter based on DeepInversion). Experiments on ResNet, VGG, and LLaMA-7B show that at high sparsity levels (≥40%) the method outperforms prior data-free techniques (IFM, INN) and achieves competitive results with data-driven methods on vision benchmarks, but on LLaMA-7B the perplexity gap is large (13.33 vs. LLM‑Pruner 10.53, Wanda 8.22). Most reviewers rated the paper 6 (accept); one reviewer rated 5, citing limited novelty and poor LLM performance.

### Strengths
- **Conceptually connects neuron alignment / network symmetry (e.g., Ainsworth et al., 2023) to compression** – a direction that deserves more attention (R1 notes novelty is somewhat novel but incremental; R4 praises rigorous theoretical analysis).
- **Fully data-free and fine-tuning-free**, making it practical for resource-constrained deployment (R1, R2, R3).
- **Two data-free activation-repair variants** (Fold-AR and Fold-DIR) derived from approximate and inversion-based techniques (R2).
- **Strong empirical results on vision models**: on ResNet18 and VGG (ImageNet, CIFAR‑10/100) the method outperforms existing data-free methods (IFM, INN) especially at sparsity >40% (R1, R2, R3). Theoretical justification accompanies empirical support (R2, R4).
- **Clear writing and presentation**: pipeline figure (Fig. 1), reproducible experimental setup (R1, R3, R4). Visualizations provide detailed insights (R3).
- **Effective for large-scale models** (LLaMA‑7B) despite lower competitive performance (R1, R2).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing related literature and baselines**:
  - Model quantization works (Martínez et al., CVPR 2021; Nagel et al., 2021) are not discussed (R1).
  - Other data-free compression papers (including those using DeepInversion) are not cited or compared (R2, R3, R4).
  - SliceGPT, a merging‑based method, is omitted (R3).
  - For LLaMA‑7B, a model‑merging compression method [5] is not compared (R4).
- **Limited technical novelty**: the method combines existing techniques (k-means clustering, DeepInversion, REPAIR) without a fundamentally new approach (R3). R1 describes the contribution as “fairly incremental” (though conceptually connecting is noted). One reviewer (R3) scored 5 and rated contribution 2; the other reviewers gave contribution 3 or 2, reflecting modest novelty.
- **LLaMA‑7B results are not competitive** – Wiki PPL 13.33 vs. LLM‑Pruner (10.53) and Wanda (8.22); the claim “comparable performance to data-driven methods” is overstated (R3). R1 also notes “comparable/somewhat worse” for LLMs.
- **Lack of sufficient experimental results**:
  - Compression ratio and speedup ratio are not reported (R2).
  - No inference speed comparison on different devices after compression (R2, question).
- **Writing clarity issues**:
  - The Fold‑AR section is hard to follow; an algorithm box would help (R1).
  - The term “merging similar channels” is not precisely defined for architectures beyond CNNs (R1). Typos: “l-the” (line 278), unfinished sentence (line 414) (R1).
- **Unclear why tuning‑free is necessary** when fine‑tuning cost is acceptable but performance drops are significant (R4). The data-free aspect relies on DeepInversion, a known method, diminishing novelty (R3).
- **Missing analysis of the connection to weight matching** – whether clustering achieves alignment/mode connectivity as in (Ainsworth et al., 2023) is not explored (R1, question).
