## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes CAMBranch, a data-efficient imitation learning framework for branching in B&B algorithms. It generates Augmented MILPs (AMILPs) via variable shifting to expand limited Strong Branching demonstrations, and employs contrastive learning to better capture MILP features. Experiments show CAMBranch achieves strong performance with only 10% of the full training data. Reviewers find the motivation practical, the augmentation strategy theoretically grounded, and the approach simple and effective, with significant gains in low-data settings.

### Strengths
- Practical motivation: generating expert demonstrations for Strong Branching is extremely time-consuming, especially for industrial-level datasets (Reviewers 1,2,4).
- The augmentation strategy via variable shifting has a theoretical guarantee and is insightful; it explicitly uses the shifting equivalence property of MILPs (Reviewers 2,4).
- The method is well-motivated, concise, and effective, with significant performance improvements in low-data situations (Reviewer 1).
- Clear writing and easy-to-follow flow (Reviewer 4).

### Weaknesses
- **Missing CAMBranch (100%) result**: The paper focuses on low-data settings, but understanding CAMBranch’s behavior with the full dataset is critical. If it cannot outperform the baseline (e.g., GCNN) using full data, that limitation should be discussed. Without this result, it is hard to assess whether the method limits the potential of imitation learning (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 also asks: what sample ratio with CAMBranch leads to comparative results with the model trained on full data? What if the full training dataset is also augmented—would that still bring improvement?
- **Inconsistent or marginal improvements**: On many medium and hard datasets, CAMBranch does not consistently outperform the GCNN baseline. For example, in Table 8 the improvement is not significant; in Figure 2 (MIS dataset), GCNN (10%) sometimes even outperforms GCNN (more data) (Reviewer 2). Reviewer 4 also notes marginal improvement in Figure 1, suggesting a lighter GNN with fewer hidden layers might achieve similar accuracy with less expert data.
- **Presentation of results needs improvement**: Figures 2–4 and Table 5 are not optimal – bars lack detailed values, histograms are less compact than tables. Table 8 (data efficiency comparison) deserves a place in the main text given the paper’s goal. Across tables, it is hard to compare metrics for models using the same amount of data (Reviewers 1,4). Additionally, the shifted geometric mean is used without explaining the intuition or the \( s \) value (Reviewer 1).
- **Fidelity of imitation learning**: Strong Branching involves nuanced decisions considering many factors and future states; replicating this complexity through imitation learning is challenging. If the model fails to capture the depth of Strong Branching, branching decisions could be significantly less efficient (Reviewer 3).
- **Computational overhead**: Training contrastive learning models requires significant resources, and real-time application within B&B may introduce computational overhead that offsets efficiency gains (Reviewer 3).
- **Sensitivity to hyperparameters**: Performance may be highly dependent on learning rate, batch size, etc., requiring extensive tuning (Reviewer 3).
- **Generalizability and robustness**: The diversity of MILP problems (size, structure, complexity) poses a challenge. The model’s robustness to adversarial or unusual problem structures remains unaddressed, and the augmentation method is tailored specifically to branching and not easily transferable to other B&B algorithms (Reviewers 2,3).
- **Missing comparisons to other ML-based approaches for training efficiency**: For example, simple auxiliary tasks (as in prior work, e.g., [1]) can efficiently promote training efficiency; a comparison to such methods is needed (Reviewer 4).
- **Unclear expert data collection details**: How many MILP instances are needed to generate 20k expert samples? Does “10%” refer to 10% of MILP instances or 10% of expert samples? How many expert samples can one instance produce? The accumulative time for collecting expert samples should be reported (Reviewer 2).
- **Unaddressed exploration‑exploitation trade-off**: The paper does not discuss how CAMBranch navigates the balance between exploring new branches and exploiting known promising paths, or whether mechanisms exist to prevent premature convergence (Reviewer 3).
- **Interpretability**: The interpretability and explainability of branching decisions made by the learned model are not addressed (Reviewer 3).
