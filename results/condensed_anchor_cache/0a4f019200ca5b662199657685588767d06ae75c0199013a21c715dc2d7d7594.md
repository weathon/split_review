## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes Bayesian Policy Distillation (BPD), a method for compressing deep RL policies via offline RL. BPD combines behavior cloning, value optimization, and sparsity-inducing Bayesian neural network training to produce lightweight, fast-inference student policies from larger teacher networks. Experiments in simulation and on a real inverted pendulum show reductions in inference time (78%) and memory (98%). Reviewers are split: one finds the idea smart and well-structured (rating 6), while two are critical of significance, clarity, and experimental strength (ratings 3 and 3). Confidence levels range from 3 to 4.

### Strengths
- The introduction is well written, and Section 2 introduces relevant concepts clearly (Reviewer 1).
- Experiments are carried out rigorously across different environments and repetitions, including a real-world inverted pendulum demonstration that provides concrete evidence of inference speed and performance (Reviewers 1, 3).
- The core idea of leveraging an existing behavior cloning loss in offline RL (e.g., TD3+BC) and transforming it via variational training to achieve controllable sparsity is clever and well-motivated (Reviewer 3).
- Figure 5 shows that sparsity vs. performance trade-off is monotonic and well-behaved (except Hopper-v3-medium), allowing practitioners to choose a suitable threshold (Reviewer 3).
- The paper is well-structured overall, with equations that usefully illustrate the progression (Reviewer 3).
- The method is designed for scenarios with limited computation and expensive data collection, which is practically relevant (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
**Significance & Positioning:**
- The “distillation” framing is not fitting: the method is essentially batch-constrained offline RL. If the teacher is near-optimal, one would just clone it (Eq. 8-9) without value optimization (Eq. 10-11). If the teacher is suboptimal, distilling imperfect information is questionable (Reviewer 1).
- The paper lacks a related works section and a clear contributions statement to delineate improvements over existing methods, especially TD3+BC (Reviewer 3).
- The biggest computational bottleneck in RL is usually the critic (Q-function) or transition model, not the policy actor; this weakens the motivation for policy-only compression (Reviewer 1).
- The method is very close to TD3+BC, differing only in the training and form of the student policy. A clearer comparison with TD3+BC is needed (Reviewer 3).

**Clarity & Writing:**
- Section 3 writing is unclear:  
  - The terms “student policy,” “target policy,” and “teacher” are used inconsistently and without proper introduction; it appears the behavior policy is the teacher, but this is never stated (Reviewer 1).  
  - In Eq. (9), it is said that “L_RL-ELBO includes a term for BC,” but Eq. (9) *is* the BC term under a Bayesian/VI perspective (Reviewer 1).  
  - The claim that “a general behavior for states not in the dataset is considered when training the Q-function” is inaccurate: the behavior policy is fixed in offline RL; it is not learned (Reviewer 1).  
  - Describing the action distribution from a BNN as a “random perturbation” is misleading—it arises from epistemic uncertainty over parameters, not from a stochastic policy (Reviewer 1).
- The framework diagram (Figure 1) is too simplistic and does not explain how BPD learns the student network (Reviewer 2).
- The symbol “p” is used for both transition probability (Sec 2.1) and posterior (Sec 2.2) without distinction (Reviewer 2).
- In Table 1, the environment names are inconsistent (“Ant-v3” vs. “Ant”) (Reviewer 2).
- The paper refers to “Table 4” in Sections 4.1 and 4.2, but Table 4 does not exist (likely meaning Table 1) (Reviewers 2, 3).
- The order of Figure 4 and Figure 5 is inconsistent with the order they are referenced in the text (Reviewer 2).
- The naming of the “sparsity” metric (Eq. L358) is confusing because it is relative to the teacher network; a dense student without any sparsification can have high “sparsity.” A better name like “teacher compression ratio” is suggested (Reviewer 3).
- The introductory paragraph (lines 42-62) is confusing, especially lines 56-62: the argument about online algorithms not tuned and performing poorly due to environment interaction is unclear (Reviewer 3).
- Minor: missing dash in “Kullback-Leibler” (L55); line about “increase their zero-weights to the maximum” (L374-375) needs rephrasing (Reviewer 3).

**Experimental Issues & Missing Comparisons:**
- In Table 1, BPD does not achieve optimal Return or Sparsity in most tasks (Reviewer 2).
- The performance improvements over TD3+BC with a comparably small network are modest: at similar sparsity levels, BPD is only noticeably better in 3 out of 8 environments, and TD3+BC gets higher mean score in half the environments (Reviewer 3).
- The real-world experiment (Sec 4.3) compares BPD to a teacher, but does not include a small TD3+BC student at a similar sparsity level. Such a comparison would strengthen the claim that BPD is superior for compression (Reviewer 3, also implied by Rev1’s criticism).
- The second experiment in Sec 4.3 (learning a small network without teacher guidance) is not persuasive; it does not show performance degradation due to compression. Comparisons should be between student networks of different sizes *under teacher guidance* (Reviewer 2).
- The selection of “2 highest performances” in reporting is arbitrary; methods whose confidence intervals intersect could also be considered. Using rliable or similar tools is recommended (Reviewer 3).
- The choice of hyperparameter C=2 is not well justified, while the literature “widely uses” 3 (Reviewer 3).
- The method of setting the student policy network size a priori is unclear: Sec 3.1 says it does not need to be set, but experiments use a specific smaller size (Reviewer 3). Similarly, TD3+BC uses a separate student network size—why and how was that chosen? (Reviewer 2).

**Reviewer Questions that Overlap with Weaknesses (missing experiments/comparisons/ablation):**
- In Eq. (10), is R(s,a) obtained from environment interaction or from the offline dataset? (Reviewer 2) → This points to a missing clarification about the offline RL setup.
- In Table 1, why is the student network size set separately for TD3+BC? Is it reasonable to use the same size for Expert and Medium settings in that method? Does the low Return of TD3+BC Expert in Ant result from this setting? (Reviewer 2) → Suggests an ablation or justification is missing.
- Can you perform an ablation like Fig. 5 with increasing network size for TD3+BC? How do the curves compare? (Reviewer 3) → Missing comparison.
- How should one choose the size of the policy network trained with BPD? (Reviewer 3) → Missing guidance.
- Could a small TD3+BC student be tested in the real-world inverted pendulum to further demonstrate BPD’s benefits? (Reviewer 3) → Missing experiment.
- Do you anticipate instances where BPD would be much more performant than TD3+BC? (Reviewer 3) → Open question that suggests limited evidence of advantage.
