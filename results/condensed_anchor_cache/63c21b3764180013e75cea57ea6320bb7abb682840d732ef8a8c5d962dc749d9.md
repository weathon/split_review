## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper introduces dEBORA, a bilevel optimization-based method for dynamic rank selection in low-rank adaptation (LoRA) for fine-tuning large neural networks. It avoids implicit differentiation by using an efficient hypergradient approximation and integrates a stochastic away-step Frank-Wolfe variant to eliminate projection and ensure identifiability of the optimal rank structure. Theoretical convergence guarantees are provided, and experiments on DeBERTa and ResNet show competitive or superior performance with fewer parameters.

### Strengths
- The paper addresses a long-standing problem in LoRA: selecting the optimal rank per layer, which previously required manual tuning or heuristics. The proposed optimization-based criterion is a novel contribution to PEFT.
- Combining bilevel optimization with dynamic rank selection is a fresh approach, and the avoidance of implicit differentiation via hypergradient approximation reduces computational cost and complexity.
- The use of a stochastic away-step Frank-Wolfe algorithm is a relatively fresh application to the LoRA problem, providing projection-free updates and identifiability guarantees.
- Experimental results are robust, covering a wide range of benchmarks (DeBERTa, ResNet). With fewer parameters, dEBORA shows competitive or superior performance compared to LoRA and AdaLoRA.
- Theoretical analysis is provided, including convergence guarantees (e.g., Theorem 4.1, 6.2, 6.5).
- Reviewers generally found the contribution solid (scores 6,6,8; Reviewer 3 rated 8, noting a fresh application of Frank-Wolfe).

### Weaknesses
- **Clarity and notation issues** (Reviewer 1, confidence 2): Several symbols and variables are undefined or defined late, hindering readability. Examples: $\otimes$ first appears line 141, $\odot$ line 215, $\Delta$ in Equation (8) defined only in Theorem 6.2, variable $V$ undefined in Equation (21), $\mu$ missing in Theorem 6.5. Equations (30) and (31) are noticeably smaller and contain undefined variables. Excess equation numbering for unreferenced equations adds clutter. Typographical errors (missing commas lines 208,240,319,401,407; extra period line 686; “By” should be “by” line 679). Notation error in line 755: $||\partial_{\mathcal{B}}f_2^*||$ should be $||\partial_{\mathcal{B}}f_1^*||$; origin of boundedness of this term needs clarification.
- **Missing comparisons** (Reviewer 1): No experimental comparison with BiLoRA (Qiang et al., 2024) despite mention. Results for Pfeiffer adapter (Pfeiffer et al., 2021) and Houlsby adapter (Houlsby et al., 2019) are stated as compared but not clearly reflected.
- **Memory consumption not adequately addressed** (Reviewer 2, key issue, confidence 4): The paper claims parameter reduction, but peak memory consumption during training (especially for large language models) and overhead of storing intermediate gradients/sensitivity measures are not compared. The practical impact on memory remains uncertain.
- **Scalability and computational cost for large models** (Reviewer 2, key issue): Applicability to larger models such as LLaMA2 is unclear. The time complexity of the Frank-Wolfe direction search and hypergradient computation (iterative rank selection) is not sufficiently detailed. The claim that finding the smallest entry in the hypergradient vector is low-cost needs clearer justification; a theoretical and empirical breakdown (wall-clock time) is requested.
- **Assumptions in Theorem 4.1** (Reviewers 1,2): The condition $K\ge 0$ alone appears insufficient to ensure gradient is locally approximately constant; the relationship to Hessian terms needs clarification (Reviewer 1, Q2). The assumptions about uniform invertibility and boundedness of the bilinear operator are not discussed for practical feasibility in deep neural networks (Reviewer 2).
- **Rank selection and parameter $\tau$** (Reviewer 1 Q1, Reviewer 2): Unclear how rank $r$ is adjusted after determining optimal $s$; is $r$ recalibrated automatically? The parameter $\tau$ is tied to rank selection, but its practical setting and impact on memory are not clarified; guidelines for choosing $\tau$ are needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Manifold constraints and first-order optimality** (Reviewer 1 Q3): Experiments mention Oblique and Stiefel manifolds for $\mathcal{B}$, but it is unclear if the first-order optimality condition in Equation (3) still holds under manifold constraints. This could affect approximations and key results.
- **Invertibility in Equation (35)** (Reviewer 1 Q4): The invertibility of product $AB$ needs justification for the equation to hold.
- **Data splitting and label balance** (Reviewer 3): Intuition for splitting the dataset into two parts (line 159) is not explained. In line 472, “randomly partitioned the dataset into equally sized subsets” does not ensure label balance for both parts; this should be clarified.
- **Batch size consideration** (Reviewer 3 Q3): The method uses stochastic Frank-Wolfe with mini-batches; the authors did not consider full batch size or discuss its effect.
- **Visualization** (Reviewer 3 Q1): It would be better to include a figure illustrating the bypass architecture in the neural network (line 150-151).
- **Positivity of $s$** (Reviewer 2): In line 154, it is unclear whether parameter $s$ should be restricted to nonnegative values.
- **Minor formatting** (Reviewer 1): Equations not referenced should not be numbered; uniform formatting is needed.
