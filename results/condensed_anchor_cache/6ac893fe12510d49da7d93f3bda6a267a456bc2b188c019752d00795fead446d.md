## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper extends the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) framework to training setups with additional derivative loss terms (e.g., Jacobian regularization, Physics-Informed Neural Networks) by proposing a paradigm using Gâteaux derivative labels. It provides theoretical derivations from both inductive and geometrical perspectives and conducts experiments on synthetic data.

### Strengths
- Provides a unified explanation for NTK with additive derivative loss items, expanding the utility of NTK to more diverse tasks (all reviewers).
- Offers both inductive and geometrical perspectives, giving a comprehensive understanding of the hard convergence phenomenon (Reviewer 1).
- Tackles a novel and substantial direction of modern ML research by extending NTK to losses of generic interest; the ambition is commendable (Reviewer 2).
- The paper is well organized with empirical support (Reviewer 1); Reviewer 2 notes the work’s ambition is impressive despite other flaws.

### Weaknesses
- Literature summary is misleading: the claim that NTK analysis is limited to least-square loss is false, as NTK has been applied to cross‑entropy and other losses (Reviewer 1).
- Experiments are limited to simple synthetic data; no validation on real-world datasets. Numerical experiments are not impressive enough to compensate for mathematical concerns (Reviewers 1, 2). Another reviewer states the paper lacks empirical validations of its theory (Reviewer 3).
- No algorithm or implementation guidance for real data; the provided code is specific to the synthetic distribution. It is unclear how to extend the approach to finite-width networks or non‑smooth activations like ReLU, and whether the additional derivative loss drives weights far from initialization, breaking NTK assumptions (Reviewer 1).
- The paper does not discuss implications or potentials of the proposed change, leaving it unclear how this improves understanding of NTK or training of neural networks (Reviewer 3).
- Significant mathematical and presentation flaws (Reviewer 2, with high confidence – 5): poor grammar (e.g., “Neural $Tangents$ Kernel”), Proposition 2 self‑reference (“Solution of (11) is (11)”), no proof that $\Theta'$ is symmetric, proof of Proposition 1 merely restates the proposition. Handwaved switches between stochastic and deterministic gradient descent; no proof that the newly defined NTK‑like objects ($\Theta', \Theta''$) retain the static kernel property or that Jacot’s approach extends seamlessly. The Gâteaux derivative is incorrectly described as an element of the reals (it maps to reals but is an operator). PINNs are mentioned but scant numerical evidence is provided. Overall, the mathematical treatment is considered careless and unconvincing.
- Only the infinite‑width regime is considered; finite‑width implications are unclear (Reviewer 1).
