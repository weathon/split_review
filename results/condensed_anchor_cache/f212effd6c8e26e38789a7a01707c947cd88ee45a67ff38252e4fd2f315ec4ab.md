## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MEDIC, a training-free zero-shot music editing method using pretrained text-to-audio diffusion models. It extends DDIM inversion with a Disentangled Inversion Technique that separates the reverse process into triple branches and a Harmonized Attention Control framework that unifies mutual self-attention (from MasaCtrl), cross-attention (from Prompt-to-Prompt), and a new Harmonic Branch. The method aims to improve edit fidelity and content preservation for both rigid and non-rigid music edits. The paper also introduces ZoME-Bench, a benchmark of 1,100 samples across ten editing categories. Two reviewers rated it 5 (confidences 3 and 5) praising novelty and thoroughness, while two rated it 3 (confidences 5 and 4) raising serious concerns about evaluation validity, benchmark design, and clarity.

### Strengths
- The main idea of incorporating mutual self-attention, cross-attention control, and a Harmonic Branch is sensible. Even though each module is not entirely novel, the combination appears effective and produces good zero-shot editing performance compared to previous state-of-the-art, with the demo page showing controllability of some music concepts that previous models failed to control (Reviewer 2, Reviewer 1).
- The methodology of Harmonized Attention Control and Disentangled Inversion is reasonably novel and well-motivated; graphical comparisons to existing methods like MusicMagus are appreciated (Reviewer 4, Reviewer 1).
- Ablations of the proposed method are solid and thorough, clearly demonstrating the strengths of the design choices (Reviewer 4).
- The paper is thorough in experimental design, including both subjective evaluations and a variety of objective experiments that effectively demonstrate the validity of the chosen methods (Reviewer 2).
- The proposed ZoME-Bench is a useful resource for future researchers on music editing, providing a comprehensive set of editing categories and prompts (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 2).
- The discussion of related work is comprehensive (Reviewer 2).
- The Disentangled Inversion Technique and Harmonic Branch are original contributions that could help zero-shot editing in other domains (Reviewer 1, Reviewer 3).

### Weaknesses
- **Definition of rigid and non-rigid tasks**: The paper does not clearly define what constitutes rigid vs. non-rigid editing in the music context, and these terms are introduced without explanation. This leaves the validity of claims about the method’s effectiveness in doubt, and the example in Section 3.3.3 is not intuitive. Porting image-domain definitions (region-based vs. global edits) to audio is not directly applicable because audio lacks region-concept co-occurrence. Even the example prompt differences in Figure 1 are hard to distinguish (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Insufficient validation of objective metrics**: The metrics chosen (FAD, LPAPS, CLAP Score) are for general audio editing and lack music-specific metrics such as chroma distance. More critically, the checkpoints used are questionable: FAD with VGG-ish is known to be inappropriate for music; using LAION-CLAP or DAC/EnCodec embeddings is recommended. LPAPS was trained on VGGSound (not music) and the repository's authors acknowledge training procedure issues. No intuitive explanation is given for why LPAPS measures “consistency.” The CLAP model checkpoint used is not clearly cited (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Inconsistency with baseline reproductions**: LPAPS values for DDPM-friendly on the same dataset are about 10× lower than those reported in Manor and Michaeli. Similar inconsistencies appear for FAD. It is unclear how hyperparameters (especially \(T_{start}\) for editing strength) were chosen for baselines compared to the proposed method (Reviewer 4).
- **Subjective evaluation lacks rigor**: No error bars, hypothesis tests, or statistical significance are reported. Demographic information (gender, age, music background, training distribution of subjects) is missing, which is important due to potential biases from AMT. The number of subjects and samples per task is not stated (Reviewers 2, 4).
- **Benchmark limitations**: ZoME-Bench lacks ground truth audio samples defining the correct edit, relying instead on CLAP similarity (which can be gamed by a model that changes the CLAP embedding without actually editing the content). Many tasks (genre, mood, rhythm, background, melody) are ill-defined because they do not disentangle content vs. style changes (e.g., what changes when “blues” goes to “rock”?). Instrument-change tasks (0/1/2/8) are similar to source separation and could have used ground truth separated tracks (e.g., from MedleyDB) but do not. The benchmark does not follow the growing practice of using pretrained discriminative models to assess edit fidelity. The overall utility for standardizing music editing evaluation is limited (Reviewers 3, 4).
- **Questionable motivation for inversion**: Since a source prompt \(\mathcal{P}\) is provided, it is unclear why the inversion process in Algorithm 2 line 3 is needed; simply using attention control during reverse sampling might suffice. The paper claims “substantially fewer computational resources” but Table 7 shows higher compute than baselines (Reviewer 3).
- **Unclear hyperparameters**: Values and effects of hyperparameters \(k, \tau_c, L, S\) are not explained, and no ablation or case study varying them is provided. Equation 9 uses an undefined \(l\). Threshold function definitions in Equations 3/4/6 are inconsistent (Reviewers 1, 3).
- **Presentation and clarity problems**: Overuse of acronyms (Disentangled Inversion Control, Harmonized Attention Control, Disentangled Inversion Technique) makes it hard to know what is a subset of what. Sections 3.3.1 and 3.3.2 are difficult to follow without intimate knowledge of image-domain works. Global Attention Refinement and Local Attention Blend are given special names but it is unclear if they are novel or from existing work. Equation 2 has an ambiguous lone \(M_t\). The term “plug-in-plus” (line 143) appears to be a typo for “plug-and-play.” “ZoMo-bench” appears (line 369). “MelodyDB” should be “MedleyDB.” MusicCaps citation is wrong (should be Agostinelli et al.). The term “forward” in Algorithm 2 is used inconsistently with standard diffusion terminology (forward = data→noise, not model forward pass). Repeated content appears in Appendix C (lines 874-892). The main text uses “audio editing” inconsistently with the title’s “music editing.” The acronym “MEDIC” is never explicitly defined (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 4).
- **Disentangled control not proven**: The paper claims “disentangled” control but does not demonstrate that the different control mechanisms (self-attention, cross-attention, harmonic) do not interfere with each other. They are not intuitively orthogonal, so experiments or different terminology is needed (Reviewer 2).
- **Qualitative analysis is heuristic**: The visual inspection of spectrograms in the “Qualitative Results” section does not convincingly show structural relevance to the source audio. Audio demo samples often sacrifice either edit fidelity or content preservation rather than achieving both (Reviewer 4).
- **Missing experiment for computational efficiency**: The paper claims efficiency but does not compare to all baselines (e.g., Null-Text Inversion) and does not provide timing details relative to other methods (Reviewers 2, 3).
- **Other missing details**: Table 4 lacks results for [0,0,0]; Table 6 is not referenced; the MedleyDB subset usage and how text prompts were generated (from Manor and Michaeli?) is not explained; the ground truth for the benchmark in terms of audio files is not clarified (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
