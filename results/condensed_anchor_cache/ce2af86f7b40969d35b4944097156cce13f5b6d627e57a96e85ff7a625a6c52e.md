## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes a recurrent neural network (RNN) based image reconstruction method for spike cameras, with a focus on low-light environments. It introduces a light-robust representation (LR-Rep) that aggregates temporal spike information across a longer, bidirectional window, and a fusion module to extract temporal features. A synthetic benchmark dataset (LLR) is constructed by dimming scenes from SPCS [Hu et al. 2022] to simulate low-light conditions. The method is evaluated on both synthetic and real spike streams, achieving a noticeable but slight improvement over the state-of-the-art WGSE. The paper also includes an ablation study to validate architectural choices.

### Strengths
- Low-light imaging with neuromorphic cameras is an interesting and important problem.
- The paper covers both dataset simulation and architectural proposals for low-light reconstruction.
- The proposed method noticeably outperforms the state-of-the-art (WGSE). Reviewer 4 also notes that performance seems good.
- Extensive ablation studies and validation are provided.
- Sound logic from problem statement to pipeline development.
- The method is demonstrated to work on both real and synthetic data (noted by Reviewer 3, who gave the highest score of 8).
- The key idea extends the local interspike interval (LISI) transform to incorporate the release time of forward and backward spikes, which is a reasonable approach for low-light conditions.

### Weaknesses
- **Benchmark and low-light definition:** The paper does not establish clear benchmarks for light robustness. The LLR dataset contains only two lighting conditions (normal and low) and five motions, which is insufficient for thorough benchmarking. The claim that light sources are "consistent with the real world" lacks technical details and justification (Reviewer 1).
- **Missing quantification of performance vs. light intensity:** No analysis of how performance degrades as light intensity decreases, making it unclear how "low light" the method is robust to (Reviewer 1).
- **Motion artifacts not addressed:** As the method extracts information over a longer temporal window in low light, motion may significantly affect reconstruction quality, but this is not studied or demonstrated (Reviewer 1).
- **Noise modeling is insufficient:** The forward model only accounts for quantization noise; it does not model Poisson noise. It is unclear how the method would perform in photon-starved regimes with significant shot noise (Reviewers 1, 2). Reviewer 2 specifically asks for behavior under Poisson noise.
- **High PSNR values seem inconsistent:** In Figure 1, the S2I reconstruction appears noisy compared to the ground truth, yet Table 1 reports PSNR >40 dB, which does not seem credible. Reviewer 3 also questions how RGB ground truth scenes are converted to grayscale (Figure 2).
- **Temporal window length (delta T) not investigated:** How varying the temporal window length affects reconstruction performance and whether the number of forward paths in LR-Rep changes with delta T are unanswered (Reviewer 4).
- **Memory and training cost:** The pipeline appears to require large memory; the paper does not report memory usage or training time (Reviewer 4).
- **Significance and novelty concerns:** The bidirectional attentive approach is well-established in video frame interpolation and event-based vision. The proposed GISI is a minor extension of the previous LISI transform, and the marginal improvement shown in Table 2 raises questions about its contribution (Reviewer 1). Reviewer 2 also asks why the wavelet structure from WGSE was not leveraged.
- **No reconstructed videos provided:** Without videos, it is impossible to evaluate whether the method introduces flickering artifacts (Reviewer 2).
- **Presentation and clarity issues:**
  - Figure 5 is uninformative and uses confusing notation (e.g., “LISI_ti = 21” should have subscript, math expressions not in LaTeX). Figures 1, 3, 4, and 9 have various display problems (color indistinguishability, arrows not drawn correctly, GISI example shows nothing, dynamic range inconsistencies in Figure 9 making STP look saturated) – noted by Reviewers 1, 2, 4.
  - LISI and GISI are introduced suddenly in Section 4.3 without prior explanation; the LISI transform is only described in a caption (Reviewer 4).
  - Grammar errors, typos (e.g., “camrea” at end of page 4, incomplete sentences like “A fusion module.”), and extra capitalizations (Reviewers 1, 2, 4).
  - The statement “The algorithm is in appendix” does not specify which appendix (Reviewer 2).
- **Figure 11 inconsistency:** The method converges well at 5 frames, but using 21 frames yields worse PSNR than 13 frames – this contradicts the choice of frame number and needs explanation (Reviewer 1).
