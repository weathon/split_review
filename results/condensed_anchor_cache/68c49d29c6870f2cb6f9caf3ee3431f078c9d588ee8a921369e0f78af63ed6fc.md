## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper implements a deep RL framework with predictive auxiliary objectives (positive sampling loss for future state prediction and negative sampling loss for distinguishing non-consecutive states) for representation learning. In a gridworld environment, a convolutional encoder processes visual observations, a prediction layer produces auxiliary predictions, and a Double DQN agent outputs Q-values. The authors demonstrate that predictive objectives improve and stabilize learning, prevent representational collapse, and enhance transfer learning when the transition structure remains unchanged. They relate the encoder, predictive module, and value network to the sensory cortex, hippocampus, and striatum respectively, showing that representation changes in the model mirror those observed in neural recordings.

### Strengths
- Clear presentation of methods and approach, with clean figures that effectively illustrate key results.
- Experiments are sound and rigorously done, testing multiple perspectives (representation collapse, transfer, splitting, adaptation to novel transitions).
- The normative, multi-region model is developed without fitting to neural data, making it extendable to other tasks and testable against new biological evidence.
- Strong link to the brain: the work grounds specific phenomena (e.g., place cells, splitting, predictive coding) into model predictions and connects them to real neural observations.
- Demonstrates that predictive auxiliary losses benefit downstream representations even when the predictive module does not directly provide information to the valuation/action selection area.
- The work uses an exciting framework (auxiliary losses in deep RL) to model interactions across multiple brain regions simultaneously.
- The authors explicitly discuss the limitation that predictive auxiliary objectives may be less helpful when the transition structure or policy changes.

### Weaknesses
- The paper does not describe the gridworld environments used (complexity, size, layout). Example environments should be provided, and the effect of varying environment complexity on learning rate, transfer, and representation quality should be investigated.
- The description of the auxiliary losses (page 4) needs improvement: the role of τ in the positive loss term is unclear; the third term likely should read τ(z_{t+1}, a_{t+1}) instead of τ(o_{t+1}, a_{t+1}); the motivation for choosing these two specific loss terms (positive sampling and negative sampling) should be stated explicitly (they appear inspired by pattern completion vs. pattern separation in hippocampus). Also, define z_i and z_j as latent representations of non-consecutive states for the negative sampling term.
- Related works section is sparse. Missing key references that use auxiliary predictive losses in RL to model cognitive/neural phenomena: Kumar et al. 2022 NeurIPS (predicting abstractions for human biases), Jensen et al. 2023 bioRxiv (predictive loss for replay patterns), and Binz & Schulz 2022 NeurIPS (regularization for exploration quirks).
- Section 4.4 on value learning in the encoder feels somewhat disconnected from the primary focus on predictive objectives and brain representations.
- The memory component introduced on page 8 is not explained. This context is necessary to interpret Figure 4’s results.
- Figure 2D: the meaning of the colors is not stated.
- Figure 5c: the reported changes in firing rates appear very small; are these changes statistically significant?
- Design decisions are not fully justified: (a) Why use an off-policy learner (Q-learning) rather than an on-policy or actor-critic method, which may be more biologically realistic for striatum? (b) Why is there no recurrence in the model? The striatum projects back to sensory cortex via thalamus, and hippocampus can influence visual cortex (Hindy et al. 2016 Nature Neuroscience). Recurrent loops could provide normative principles.
- Minor text errors: page 4, "the predictive model is trained with..." missing verb; page 5, "is used" at end of sentence; page 7, "to" in "undergo to experience-dependent changes"; page 8, "remembering whether the previous trial type" (remove "whether") and empty reference.
- The paper lacks an explicit limitations section discussing scope and potential extensions (e.g., partial observability, recurrent connections, biological realism of off-policy learning).
