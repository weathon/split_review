## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes dataset alignment for detecting fake images from latent diffusion models (LDMs) by reconstructing real images using the LDM's autoencoder, creating a set of fake images that are similar to real ones in size, aspect ratio, and semantic content. This alignment forces detectors to focus on LDM decoder artifacts rather than spurious correlations (e.g., resolution, compression, content) that previous methods can exploit. The approach is computationally efficient because it avoids the denoising process. Experiments show improved robustness and performance over the major baseline (Corvi), and the paper provides some analysis of pitfalls in prior work. All four reviewers rated the paper 6.

### Strengths
1. Proposes a simple and efficient way to collect training data for fake image detection, sidestepping the computationally expensive full generation process.
2. Provides analysis of common pitfalls in prior works, such as the influence of image resolution differences or compression artifacts (R1).
3. Well-written with a clear research motivation and a well-identified critical problem (dataset alignment causing spurious correlations) (R2).
4. Provides reasonable evidence validating the findings, especially the insightful experiment on downsampling before vs. after resizing (Table 5) (R2).
5. The idea of using only the LDM autoencoder to generate fakes is interesting, and achieves better performance than using complete generated images (R3).
6. Reveals the impact of scaling factors on detection methods, contributing valuable insights (R3).
7. The method is simple and can serve as a useful baseline for LDM-based fake image detection (R4).
8. More effective and efficient than the major baseline Corvi, with extensive experiments supporting the superior performance (R4).
9. (R4 found the paper somewhat clear and well-written, but note disagreement from R3 on presentation quality – see Weaknesses.)

### Weaknesses
1. **Limited generalization to other architectures and fake types**: The method is mainly effective for LDM-based generation, not for GANs, traditional diffusion models, or models with very different autoencoder architectures (e.g., VQ-VAE, VQ-GAN, FLUX.1-dev) (R1, R2, R3). A systematic evaluation across a spectrum of autoencoder designs is missing (R1). The scope excludes deepfakes (face-swapping), talking-head videos, and other manipulations beyond full image synthesis; this limits practical applicability and needs discussion (R2). The detector may primarily learn LDM-specific patterns rather than broader fake patterns, and generalization across the LDM family (e.g., changes in VAE or UNet) is unclear (R2). The method also ignores semantic inconsistencies (e.g., incorrect hands) that some generators create (R3).

2. **Insufficient robustness evaluation**: Only one randomized post-processing technique is tested (Tables 2,3) (R1). Missing robustness tests against noise, JPEG compression, rotation, scaling, and different noise types (R1, R3). The proposed method and Corvi are both easily disturbed by lossy compression, limiting real-world applicability (R4). A separate, granular analysis of robustness to various transformations (like Figure 2 and the figure on page 10) is needed; the figure on page 10 should be properly numbered and referenced (R1).

3. **Weak comparison baselines and limited context**: Only two methods are compared, and neither is state-of-the-art on complex public datasets like GenImage (R3). The comparison datasets are not from AIGC detection benchmarks, making it difficult to gauge fair performance improvement (R3). The alignment issue is not entirely new; prior deepfake detection works (refs [2],[3]) addressed alignment – the paper should discuss differences and novel contributions (R2). A more robust evaluation with more baselines and standard benchmarks would strengthen the paper (R3).

4. **Missing analysis of method variants and underlying mechanisms**: Two variants "Ours" and "Ours-Sync" are introduced but not explained, and "Ours-Sync" outperforms "Ours" in most cases without analysis (R4). Why "Ours" occasionally outperforms "Ours-Sync" is unexplained (R4). The paper provides only shallow comparative experiments; it lacks deep exploration of reasons behind observed phenomena and how the method could benefit existing detection approaches (R3). The technical contribution feels insufficient (R3).

5. **Metric and evaluation issues**: Accuracy is sensitive to thresholds; threshold-less metrics like average precision (AP) would give a more accurate picture of performance degradation (R1). For example, Cozzolino-LDM’s accuracy in Tables 1/2 improves after post-processing, which is misleading without a threshold-independent measure (R1). The rationale for calibrating the threshold in Table 3 is unclear, and what happens with the default threshold (0.5) is not explained (R1).

6. **Scope/title mismatch and missing definitions**: The title implies general fake image detection, but the paper focuses on LDMs; qualifiers should be added (R4). The definition of "fake image" is limited to entire image synthesis, excluding other manipulations (R2).

7. **Omitted discussions and experiments**: No comparison of online vs. offline reconstruction (computational cost, memory, detection performance) (R2). Limited analysis of pre-trained models (CLIP vs. ResNet-50) and how pre-training objectives affect detection (R2). No experiments on different network architectures beyond ResNet-50 (R4). Performance drops for fake images with small scaling factors or real images with large factors are unexplained (R4). A larger batch size might improve performance but was not tested (R4). The combination of the proposed method with Corvi was not attempted (R4).

8. **Presentation quality disagreement**: One reviewer (R3) rated presentation 1, citing weak paper structure, unclear diagrams, disorganized dataset descriptions, and missing figure captions (conclusion figure missing caption also noted by R4). Other reviewers (R1, R2, R4) found the paper somewhat clear or well-written. The figure in the conclusion lacks a caption (R4).
