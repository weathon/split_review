## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes SREF, an entropy-guided approach that uses rephrased questions as self-references to improve answer consistency for known facts and encourage refusal on uncertain questions. Experiments across 12 LLMs and 3 datasets (NQ, TQ, HQ) show average improvements of 16.01% over baselines and 15.10% in consistency. Reviewers are split: two rate the paper 3 (weak acceptance) and one rates it 5 (strong acceptance), disagreeing on novelty, completeness of baselines, evaluation rigor, and performance on strong models.

### Strengths
- Experiments cover a broad range: 12 LLMs (including Llama, Mistral, GPT-4o) across 3 datasets (NQ, TQ, HQ), providing extensive evidence for factuality and consistency.
- The method is simple and straightforward, using question rephrasing sampling to generate relevant references; it is applicable to both open-source and closed-source models.
- The paper is the first to correlate using LLMs as self-references with entropy, establishing a connection to consistency and KL divergence; experimental results show a positive correlation between decreased entropy and increased consistency.
- Theoretical support from entropy perspective is provided.
- The experimental design is relatively comprehensive, and the discussion is interesting.
- The paper is well-written and easy to follow.

### Weaknesses
- **Novelty disagreement**: One reviewer praises the entropy-guided framing as novel, but another argues the method lacks novelty given prior works (2023.findings-emnlp.1032, 2406.02543, 2402.00367); these works should be discussed as related baselines.
- **Missing critical baselines**: The paper omits comparison with established methods from 2023.findings-emnlp.1032 and 2210.01296.
- **Unclear motivation and reasoning**: The introduction does not clearly articulate why limitations in intrinsic self-correction led to leveraging internal knowledge for references. The explanation of Eq.7 (determining “Unsure”) is insufficient: if reference R is contradictory, will the LLM definitely output “Unsure”? More explicit reasoning is needed (typo: “inconsistency” should be “inconsistent”).
- **Uncertainty evaluation is insufficient**: A higher frequency of “Unsure” outputs does not necessarily indicate better-calibrated uncertainty; the paper should verify that accuracy is higher when the model does not output “Unsure.”
- **Performance degradation on strong models**: On NQ and TQ, the vanilla setting outperforms SREF for strong models like GPT-4o. The mean metric across all models in Tables 1 and 2 downplays this decline. The reason for SREF’s failure on models with broader self-knowledge should be explained.
- **Limited dataset scope**: Only common-sense QA datasets (NQ, TQ, HQ) are evaluated. Reasoning datasets (e.g., GSM8K, MATH) are recommended to assess scalability and robustness, as self-correction methods are typically applied to reasoning tasks [S3c-Math, DotaMath].
- **Computational cost not addressed**: SREF uses multi-round generation; comparison of FLOPs budgets with vanilla and other self-correction methods is missing.
- **Ambiguity in example and consistency measurement**: The example in Figure 2 is itself ambiguous and requires more than factual knowledge; unambiguous examples where SREF shows improvement should be provided. The use of distractors and multiple-choice to measure consistency is questioned—a more direct approach would be to measure consistency of multiple generated answers.
- **Hallucination risk for genuinely unknown knowledge**: In Figure 3, some points originally marked “Unsure” now appear inaccurate. LLMs lack human-like self-knowledge; for genuinely unknown facts, adding references may increase hallucinations. SREF may effectively address “known knowns” and “unknown unknowns” but could worsen ambiguous situations, hindering broader application.
- **Cross-dataset error analysis needed**: To understand why SREF diverges from vanilla, a cross-dataset error analysis would help identify specific successes/failures and clarify generalizability across datasets.
