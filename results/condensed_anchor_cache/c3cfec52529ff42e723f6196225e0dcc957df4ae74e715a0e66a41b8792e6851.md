## Merged Review

### Summary

The paper introduces CHOPINLLM, a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) tailored for chart comprehension, and proposes a three-stage training strategy: (1) incorporating raw data values during alignment pre-training, (2) randomly replacing images with textual chart representations during end-to-end fine-tuning, and (3) requiring data extraction before answering in fine-tuning. It also presents an automatic data generation pipeline using text-only LLMs (e.g., GPT-4) to produce 5M chart-QA pairs and a new benchmark covering diverse chart types and question levels. Reviewers acknowledge the relevance of chart understanding and commend aspects of the pipeline and training analysis, but raise major concerns about performance relative to existing methods, insufficient baselines, unclear novelty, missing evaluations on unannotated charts and other tasks, and questionable validity of the new benchmark. Reviewer 2 (score 6) is more positive about the pretraining perspective and clear presentation, while others (scores 5) find the contributions incremental and the experimental support weak.

### Strengths

- The paper explores the rarely investigated pretraining stage (alignment with chart-specific data) for improving chart understanding, setting it apart from works focusing only on supervised fine-tuning. Controlled experiments show that curated chart pretraining data significantly boosts later fine-tuning performance on the same QA set. (Reviewer 2)
- The data generation pipeline using text-only LLMs to produce raw data, code, and QA is fully automatic, cost-effective, and clearly documented in the supplementary material. (Reviewers 1, 2, 4)
- The paper provides a comprehensive summary and recommendations on training strategies for the chart domain, including the effectiveness of different data types (e.g., textual representation of charts, data-first reasoning). The findings are clearly presented and easy to follow. (Reviewers 1, 2, 3)
- A new benchmark with diverse chart types and question-answering levels is established to evaluate MLLMs’ chart comprehension, which may aid future research. (Reviewers 1, 4)
- The training techniques (raw data in alignment, text-only chart representation, data-first QA) demonstrate strong performance across various chart types, including unannotated charts, and show practical utility. (Reviewers 1, 4)

### Weaknesses

- **Performance not state-of-the-art**: CHOPINLLM does not achieve SOTA in Table 4 (e.g., ChartQA 71.39 vs. TinyChart 82.88). The authors attribute this to other models using more/annotated data, but provide no controlled experiment showing that combining the proposed synthetic data with existing datasets improves results. Without such evidence, the benefit of training an underperforming model solely on synthetic data is limited. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Missing baselines**: The paper omits crucial comparisons with general MLLMs (InternVL2, Phi-3.5-V), proprietary models (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet), and recent chart-specific models (TinyChart, ChartGemma, OneChart, ChartReformer). Based on numbers from those papers, CHOPINLLM’s performance is not competitive. (Reviewers 1, 3, 4)
- **Unsupported claims about unannotated charts**: The paper claims less reliance on numerical annotations (L73, L478) but provides no controlled ablation. Stronger performance on unannotated images is not evidence; questions may be easier. No specific design for unannotated charts is introduced, and results on unannotated examples are not reported. Benchmarks with high-quality unannotated charts (MMC, ChartBench, ChartX) are mentioned in Table 1 but not used for evaluation. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Novelty and contribution questioned**: Training with raw data alignment has been adopted in ChartAst, ChartReformer; extracting data before QA is explored in OneChart. The three-stage strategy is seen as common (stage-1: adding chart data to alignment, stage-2/3: adding chart QA data) and offers unclear significance. The paper reads more like a technical report than a novel contribution. (Reviewers 3, 4)
- **Data generation pipeline inefficiency**: TinyChart uses ~1M synthetic data and achieves higher ChartQA (82.88) than CHOPINLLM’s 5M data (71.39), raising doubts about the pipeline’s efficiency. (Reviewer 4)
- **Lack of controlled data scaling and cost-effectiveness analysis**: No ablation shows how model performance changes when matching data volumes of baselines (e.g., reducing ChartAst data to 5M, or reducing CHOPINLLM data to match ChartLlama/MMC/ChartInstruct amounts). The paper does not compare the cost-effectiveness of synthetic data per unit compute. (Reviewer 2)
- **Potential task bias and generalization failure**: Adding chart-specific data to pretraining makes chart understanding data over-represented; it is unknown whether the model degrades on other MLLM tasks (e.g., MME, SEED). No such evaluations are provided, leaving doubt about whether CHOPINLLM is a general MLLM or chart-only. (Reviewers 2, 3)
- **Benchmark validity concerns**: The largest performance gains on the new benchmark come from adding same-type synthetic QAs in stage-2, yet gains on ChartQA are very small, suggesting a narrow, biased distribution. This makes the benchmark’s real-world utility questionable; scaling up such synthetic data can inflate scores without improving actual chart understanding. (Reviewer 2)
- **Missing specifics**: The base model fine-tuned is unspecified (only described as LLaVA-like), making it hard to evaluate the training approach. (Reviewer 3)
- **Minor issues**: Wrong reference at L300; typo at L522; ambiguous terminology “chart variation” (L274); resolution bottleneck (LLaVA 1.5 only supports 336²) not discussed. (Reviewer 2)
- **Generalization to other domains not demonstrated**: The proposed data generation method’s broader applicability is not shown, which would justify its use when annotated data is scarce. (Reviewer 1)
