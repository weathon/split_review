## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes MULAN, a learned diffusion process that uses a multivariate noise schedule, instance-conditional diffusion, and auxiliary latent variables to increase the ELBO. The authors show that the ELBO for continuous-time diffusion models is invariant to the noise schedule only for univariate noise; under multivariate noise the ELBO becomes a path integral that varies with the schedule, enabling joint optimization of model and schedule. MULAN achieves improved negative log-likelihood on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet and approximately 2× faster convergence to state-of-the-art likelihoods. The method is grounded in Bayesian inference, treating the learned diffusion process as an approximate variational posterior.

### Strengths
- The paper is well-written, clearly organized, and easy to follow (all reviewers).
- The theoretical insight that ELBO invariance breaks under multivariate noise schedules is novel and could open further research on diffusion processes (R1, R4).
- MULAN surpasses the previous strong likelihood benchmark set by VDM, which is impressive (R1); the improvements in likelihood and convergence are solidly demonstrated (R2, R3).
- Conditioning the noise schedule on an auxiliary latent variable is a valuable contribution that enhances model likelihoods (R1).
- The ablation studies show synergy between the multivariate schedule and the auxiliary variable (R3); experiments are thorough and the reference list is complete (R2).
- The approach of learning an adaptive noise schedule is somewhat novel (R4).

### Weaknesses
- **Theoretical justification for the adaptive schedule is lacking.** The intuition from a frequency/perceptual perspective is not convincing, especially because likelihood often does not correlate with sample quality (R3). No solid theorem or rigorous explanation is given for why an instance-dependent, pixel-wise polynomial schedule is superior to constant/linear/exponential schedules (R2, R3).
- **Individual contributions of components are unclear.** The multivariate noise schedule does not work alone—MULAN without the auxiliary variable performs worse than standard VDM (Figure 1a) (R1, R4). A more detailed ablation isolating the effect of multivariate noise vs. auxiliary variables, with a fair comparison (e.g., VDM + auxiliary variable), is needed to assess the value of each component (R1).
- **Role and necessity of the auxiliary latent variable are questionable.** Equation (7) raises a concern: if the actual objective is the ELBO w.r.t. (x,z), this could act as a bottleneck on the ELBO w.r.t. (x₀, x₁..T, z), potentially not narrowing the posterior gap (R3). The paper does not clearly delineate the architectural and algorithmic differences from prior work that uses auxiliary latent variables (e.g., Wang et al. 2023), making it hard to assess novelty and effectiveness (R4).
- **Motivation–objective mismatch.** The paper motivates learning noise schedules by citing manual tuning for perceptual quality in high-resolution diffusion models, yet optimizes ELBO—a metric unrelated to perceptual quality. It is unclear whether the method is beneficial when the goal is sample quality, especially since likelihood does not correlate with visual appearance (R1, R3).
- **Performance improvements are marginal given added complexity.** Compared to VDM (the codebase and settings used), the gains are modest. Moreover, MULAN performs worse than i-DODE* (Zheng et al., 2023) on the same metrics (Table 1), limiting the claim of state-of-the-art (R3, R4). (Note: R1 considered the improvement over VDM very impressive, so there is disagreement among reviewers.)
- **Missing comparisons and clarification.** The paper frequently references continuous-time diffusion models but omits citation [1] (origin of such models) and concurrent works [2],[3] that proposed the same ELBO (R1). No generated image samples are shown to visually demonstrate improvements (R2).
- **Additional technical concerns.** The method for computing bits-per-dimension (BPD)—whether using stochastic VLB or ODE-based exact likelihood—is not stated; variance of the stochastic VLB and the effect of log-SNR parameterization on variance are not discussed, while VDM explicitly minimizes VLB variance with a learned schedule (R3). Running time comparison with baselines is not provided (R4). A typo exists in Appendix C.1: “prexisting” → “pre-existing” (R3).
- **Novelty is disputed.** While R1 and R4 found the learned schedule or the theoretical insight somewhat novel, R3 argues that multivariate non-isotropic noise and auxiliary variables in diffusion are not new, and the combination lacks a significant theoretical contribution; the polynomial parameterization is not fundamentally different from prior parameterizations (R3).
