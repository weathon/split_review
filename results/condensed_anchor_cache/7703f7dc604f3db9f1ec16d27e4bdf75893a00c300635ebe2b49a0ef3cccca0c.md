## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper introduces LEPA, a self-training method that prompts an LLM (Llama 3.1-8B-Instruct) to first generate an anticipatory plan before producing a solution, then refines that plan via self-reflection until it leads to a correct answer. The resulting plan-and-solution pairs are used to fine-tune the LLM. Experiments on four reasoning benchmarks (HellaSwag, MATH, BoolQ, PIQA) show improvements over baselines. All reviewers agree the idea is simple and addresses an important question about self-generated data, but they raise significant concerns about limited generalizability, missing comparisons, and insufficient justification.

### Strengths
- Novel self-improvement method that addresses the problem of what essential information self-generated data should contain (R1, R2).
- Simple and straightforward idea, clearly presented with figures and examples; easy to implement and generalizable to other tasks (R3).
- Experiments demonstrate superiority over baselines with sufficient analysis, including inference computation (R3).
- Ablation studies validate the usefulness of key components (R2).
- Results show planning data improves performance, with continuous improvement even after 15 iterations (Figure 3; R4).
- Paper is easy to follow (R4).

### Weaknesses
- Evaluation limited to a single LLM (Llama 3 8B Instruct); no experiments on other LLMs (R1, R2, R3). (R3 notes this is not a major issue but still a limitation.)
- No out-of-distribution (OOD) experiments; only in-domain performance tested. Concerns about generalization to different datasets or tasks (e.g., trained on MATH, tested on MMLU-pro math) (R1, R3, R4).
- Zero-shot CoT performance for Llama3-8B-Instruct on MATH is reported as only 19%, which seems low; one reviewer recommended re-evaluating with simple-eval (R3).
- Data generation pipeline is not clearly illustrated (R1).
- Justification of anticipatory plans in Section 2.3 is not solid theoretically; relies only on cognitive science references and a single example (R2).
- Related work missing: the idea of generating a plan before solutions has been explored in embodied agents (e.g., [1][2] in review) and LUMOS (specifically LUMOS-O) already showed planning data improves LLMs (R2, R4). Also missing discussion of self-reflection literature (R2).
- Claim of compatibility with RL is unsupported; current method is limited to prompting and self-reflection (R2).
- Comparison in Table 2 may be unfair: LEPA’s training data is likely larger than the “Without Plan / Without Self-Reflection” settings, if aligned with lines 349-360 (R4).
- Only outcome judgment in data selection; no quality evaluation of the plan or the chain-of-thought before the answer (R4). The claim of preventing false-positive data (line 224) needs a standard for planning quality (R4).
- Method may negatively affect experience for simple problems by forcing unnecessary planning, incurring extra cost compared to ReSF (R3).
- Concern that LEPA merely replaces the solution with a memorizable plan; unclear if it provides sufficient improvement in few-shot or OOD settings (e.g., trained on few MATH instances, tested on others) (R3).
- Handling of questions with no correct answer after data creation is not addressed (R1).
- Guidance on how to better choose data is missing (R1).
- No discussion of how to optimize for open-ended solutions where correctness is hard to evaluate (R2).
- Table 3 is confusing; metrics and their relation to methods are not clearly explained (R2).
- Missing comparisons with other baselines mentioned in related work, such as those using MCTS (R4).
- Lack of discussion on why planning data improves reasoning ability (R4).
- Only four reasoning benchmarks; experiment section not convincing with so few benchmarks (R1).
