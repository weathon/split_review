## Merged Review

### Summary
This paper proposes DSpodFL, a unified algorithmic framework for decentralized federated learning (DFL) that explicitly models both per-iteration local gradient descent and inter-client model exchanges as arbitrary indicator random variables. This captures heterogeneous and time-varying computation/communication scenarios without a central server. The framework subsumes existing methods such as DGD, DFedAvg, and RG. Convergence is analyzed for strongly convex and nonconvex objectives under constant and diminishing learning rates, with mild assumptions on graph connectivity, data heterogeneity, and gradient noise. Experiments across various system settings show improved training speed over baselines.

### Strengths
- **Unified algorithmic framework**: The formulation in eqs. (2)–(3) with doubly stochastic mixing matrices simultaneously handles sporadicity in local updates and communication, subsuming existing decentralized optimization methods (Reviewers 1, 4, 5).
- **General stochasticity**: Captures varying and time‑varying computation/communication capabilities and heterogeneity across clients (Reviewers 1, 2, 3, 5).
- **Rigorous convergence analysis**: Provides convergence guarantees for both convex and nonconvex losses under mild assumptions (Reviewers 1, 4, 5). The proof techniques align with standard analysis in the literature (Reviewer 1).
- **Novelty**: The combined investigation of sporadic local computations *and* inter‑client communication is previously unexplored in DFL (Reviewer 5).
- **Clean exposition**: The algorithm is presented clearly (Reviewer 3); the paper is well‑written with justified theoretical results (Reviewer 5).
- **Sufficient experiments**: Numerical results effectively demonstrate the advantages of DSpodFL over baselines (Reviewer 5). The framework is compared with several baselines (Reviewer 2).
- **General applicability**: The framework allows varying resource availability (Reviewer 2).

### Weaknesses
- **Source of gain unclear**: The improved performance in total latency is observed, but the benefit over other algorithms is not quantified or discussed – is it simply that frequent communication is unnecessary? (Reviewer 1, strength direction: unclear)
- **Practicality of mixing weights**: The weights \(r_{ij}\) (e.g., in Metropolis‑Hastings) may require knowledge of future graph topology; in dynamic FL networks that assumption may not hold. How are they chosen in simulations? (Reviewer 1, missing explanation)
- **Correlation ignored**: The independence assumption between communication and computation events (Assumption 4.3) rules out delays in gradient computation – if a node computes for a long time and receives a model update, it must discard its gradient and restart from scratch. Allowing correlation or delayed gradients is a major limitation (Reviewer 2, minority negative, confidence 4).
- **Privacy and communication anonymity**: It is unclear whether clients need to know the identity of communicating neighbors (blind aggregation). The paper lacks discussion of privacy implications, and recent work on communication sporadicity with privacy constraints is omitted (Reviewer 1).
- **Only asymptotic convergence**: The analysis holds as \(K \to \infty\); frameworks such as Even (2024) give bounds for any finite \(K\) (Reviewer 2).
- **Discrepancy between described and analyzed algorithm**: The paper initially presents algorithm (2), but the actual analysis uses (3)–(4) which differ due to re‑normalization of the diagonal element \(p_{ii}^k\) (Reviewer 2).
- **Non‑convex rate expressed with opaque parameters**: The convergence bound for non‑convex functions depends on \(w_1,\dots,w_5\) defined only in the appendix, making it hard to interpret (Reviewer 2).
- **Learning rate tuning**: The same learning rate is used for all algorithms without tuning, potentially favoring some methods (Reviewer 2).
- **Missing baseline**: Experimental comparison to Even (2024) is absent; what is the reason? (Reviewer 2)
- **Convergence to a suboptimal point**: The algorithm does **not** converge to the original optimal solution. Unlike distributed optimization methods that incorporate error correction to achieve (almost) exact convergence, DSpodFL’s limit point has a bias; this raises concerns about its usefulness under large heterogeneity (Reviewer 3, confidence 4).
- **Insufficient detail in proofs**: The appendix lacks step‑by‑step explanation for many derivations (Reviewer 3). A formal filtration for conditional expectations should be set up (Reviewer 3).
- **Information needed for learning rates**: It is not specified what information each agent requires to compute the learning rate, nor why all agents must use the same learning rate (Reviewer 3).
- **Recovering existing results**: It is unclear whether Theorems 4.11 and 4.12 directly recover the convergence rates of DGD, DFedAvg, and RG, and whether those recovered rates match the original analyses – detailed steps are requested (Reviewer 4).
- **Incomplete experimental results**: Figure 2(b) shows test accuracy only up to \(\tau=10000\) (accuracy ~0.6); larger horizons (e.g., 20000–30000) should be shown. The same applies to Figure 2(c). Performance in wall‑clock time is not reported (Reviewer 4).
- **Justification of matrix properties**: Section 3.3 does not clearly explain why the random matrix \(P^k\) is doubly stochastic and symmetric; a proof and example would help (Reviewer 5).
- **Different gradient diversity measures**: Assumption 4.1 uses \(\|\nabla F(\theta) - \nabla F_i(\theta)\|\) while Assumption 4.2 uses \(\|\nabla F_i(\theta)\|\); the rationale for this distinction is not given (Reviewer 5).
- **Unrealistic dependence on learning rate**: In the diminishing‑learning‑rate case, exact convergence requires the frequency of SGDs to increase over time, making SGD probabilities depend on the stepsize – this seems unrealistic and is not required for constant stepsize (Reviewer 5).
- **Possible typo in equation (4)**: The expression for \(p_{ii}^k\) may incorrectly omit \(r_{ii} \hat v_{ii}^k\) (Reviewer 5).
- **Implementation details of baselines**: It is unclear how baseline algorithms are adapted to the heterogeneous communication/computation setting and how \(d_i, b_{ij}\) map to node speed (Reviewer 2).
- **Clarification on definitions and recovery**: Several technical points need clarification: (i) whether indicator variables in Def. 4.6 are Bernoulli, (ii) how result (10) recovers Koloskova (2020) (including the local‑step case \(d_{\min}=0\)), (iii) the meaning of “average total delay up to iteration \(k\)”, (iv) why the mixing matrix \(R\) must be doubly stochastic when \(P\) is later renormalized, (v) whether division by zero occurs if \(d_{\max}^k=0\), and (vi) the sufficiency of \(\lim_{k\to0} \Phi(k:0)=0\) for consensus/average error convergence (Reviewers 1, 2).
