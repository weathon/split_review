## Merged Review

### Summary

This paper addresses Multi-Source Domain Adaptation (MSDA) for Vision-Language Models (VLMs). It proposes a fine-tuning framework combining category-specific prompts with multimodal Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). Learnable prompt tokens serve as shared features across domains, while domain-specific multimodal LoRA matrices capture domain-specific features; a shared projection layer aggregates features from different modalities. All source LoRA modules are combined via coefficients into an integrated module adapted to the target domain. Experiments on Office-31, Office-Home, and DomainNet are reported.

### Strengths

- The problem of MUDA for VLMs is practical and worth solving (R1, R2, R4).
- The framework integrates prompts with multimodal LoRA for MSDA, considering class-specific prompting and a shared projection layer to aggregate features across modalities (R2, R4).
- Experimental results show clear performance gains on popular benchmarks, especially the challenging DomainNet dataset (R1, R4).
- The methodology section is clearly organized, explaining the prompting mechanism and LoRA matrix integration process (R3, R4).
- The approach is flexible, adaptable to different datasets/domains, and has potential for applications where training data is scarce or multiple distinct domains are involved (R4).
- Reviewer 4 (score 5) highlights originality in bridging domain-invariant and domain-specific features, which could inspire further work (R4).

### Weaknesses

- **Novelty and contribution are limited**. The interaction mechanism is borrowed from the MMA paper (Yang et al., CVPR 2024a) and thus cannot be considered a unique contribution (R1). Figure 1 appears plagiarized from Figure 2 of MMA (R1). Equations (11) and (13) resemble those in the MMA paper (R1). The overall novelty is constrained (R2).  
  *(Note: Reviewer 4 (score 5) disagrees, viewing the combination of category-specific prompting and multimodal LoRA as original and creative.)*

- **Insufficient experiments and missing comparisons**:
  - Should compare with MMA [1] (Yang et al.) (R1, R3).
  - Should compare with recent state-of-the-art methods such as Du et al. (2024) "Domain-agnostic mutual prompting" (R3).
  - Ablation studies are absent or minimal: need detailed ablation on contribution of each component (prompt tuning vs. LoRA only, varying LoRA ranks/configurations, etc.) (R2, R3, R4). The paper only touches on prompt length and pseudo-label thresholds but lacks rigorous component-wise ablation (R3, R4).
  - Baseline methods are limited (R2).
  - No metrics demonstrating overfitting mitigation (e.g., validation accuracy trends across epochs, performance under varied training data sizes, comparison of overfitting rates between prompt tuning alone vs. prompt+LoRA) despite claiming to address overfitting (R4).

- **Presentation is poor** with numerous errors and unclear notation:
