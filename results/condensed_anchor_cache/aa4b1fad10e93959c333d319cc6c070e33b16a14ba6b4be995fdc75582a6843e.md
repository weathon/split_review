## Merged Review

### Summary
The paper proposes the Smooth Min-Max (SMM) network module, which replaces the hard min and max operations in min-max (MM) monotonic networks with LogSumExp-based smooth approximations. The goal is to alleviate the “silent neuron” problem caused by zero partial derivatives in MM nonlinearities, thereby improving the learning signal. The authors present theoretical approximation guarantees (as β → ∞) and provide empirical comparisons on toy examples and real datasets (including ChestXRay, Table 3) against alternative monotonic models such as LMNs and XGBoost.

### Strengths
- The paper is very clear, well-written, and easy to follow (Reviewers 1, 2).
- It addresses an important problem: sometimes “worse” models can be empirically better because they are easier to optimise, and silent neurons in MM networks cause undesired local optima (Reviewer 1).
- The SMM architecture is simple, intuitive, and well-motivated; Reviewer 3 specifically found it innovative and a well-motivated transition from hard min-max to a LogSumExp-based approach.
- Extensive comparisons against other monotonic models help readers understand the potential advantages of SMM (Reviewer 2).
- Theoretical guarantees (Theorem, Corollary) establish that SMM can approximate MM arbitrarily well as the smoothness parameter β becomes sufficiently large, supporting asymptotic approximation properties (Reviewer 3).
- Empirical results (according to Reviewer 3) demonstrate the effectiveness of SMM on multiple datasets, confirming its practicality and potential. (Note: this positive assessment contrasts with the weaker view of the empirical evidence by Reviewers 1 and 2.)

### Weaknesses
- Reviewer 1: Theorem 1 is not very meaningful because the required network size grows very quickly as ε decreases.
- Reviewers 1 and 2: The empirical results do not convincingly demonstrate a significant advantage of SMM over competing methods. For example, differences in Table 3 / ChestXRay appear likely statistically insignificant (Reviewer 1); even after accounting for error bars, SMM does not show a clear advantage over LMNs or XGBoost (Reviewer 2). (Reviewer 3, in contrast, found the experimental results a major strength.)
- Reviewer 2: The novelty of smoothing non-smooth neurons with LogSumExp is questionable, as this approach is popular in statistical modeling; however, Reviewer 2 defers to others on the monotonic-network literature.
- Reviewers 2 and 3: The choice and training of the scaling factor β is not adequately addressed. Reviewer 2 asks how β should be chosen in practice, noting that tuning to ensure monotonicity may require retraining the entire network. Reviewer 3 notes a lack of exploration of β’s behavior during training (its trajectory, relationship with loss, final values after training), and the paper does not explain how the optimization landscape changes with β.
- Reviewer 3: The potential for β to get stuck in local minima—especially in relation to the vanishing gradient problem—is not fully addressed.
- Reviewer 3: The observation that test errors vary significantly with different initial β values raises the question of whether the optimization process may not fully converge or whether β plays a more complex role in training dynamics than currently understood.
- Reviewer 1: The paper does not explore whether different types of smooth relaxations (other than LogSumExp) could be used.
